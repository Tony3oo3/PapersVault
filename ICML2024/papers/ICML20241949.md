## Problem

Diffusion model sampling is inherently sequential, requiring T autoregressive neural network evaluations that become a bottleneck for large models like Stable Diffusion. While Shih et al. (2023) first proposed parallelizing this process via Picard-Lindelof iterations, their formulation lacks a principled framework for analyzing convergence, limiting the development of further acceleration techniques. This paper addresses the gap by providing a rigorous triangular nonlinear equation formulation that enables systematic improvements to the parallel solving process.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained diffusion model (DiT at 256×256 or Stable Diffusion v1.5 at 512×512) with a conditioning signal (class label or text prompt) and a Gaussian noise vector, producing a generated image identical to what the corresponding sequential solver (DDIM or DDPM) would yield. A window size parameter w controls the memory-computation tradeoff: larger w allows more parallel evaluations per step but requires more GPU memory, while smaller w reduces memory at the cost of more sequential window shifts. The method is entirely training-free and requires only the existing ε_θ network, with no architectural modifications.

## Method

ParaTAA reformulates the autoregressive diffusion sampling procedure as a system of k-th order triangular nonlinear equations, where the order k determines how many future timesteps inform each update and thus shapes the convergence landscape. The system is solved via fixed-point iteration, where all T neural network evaluations are executed in parallel per step. The key acceleration is Triangular Anderson Acceleration (TAA), which constrains the inverse Jacobian approximation matrix to be block upper triangular, preventing unconverged earlier-timestep variables from corrupting already-converged later ones—a critical fix that standard Anderson Acceleration lacks for triangular systems. Two additional practical tricks further reduce steps: early stopping when perceptual quality is sufficient (often 7 steps vs. 13–17 for full residual convergence), and initializing from a previously computed trajectory of a similar prompt, which enables smooth image interpolation in as few as 3 steps.

## Results

ParaTAA reduces the inference steps required by sequential sampling by a factor of 4–14× across all tested scenarios while maintaining comparable image quality. For the headline SD DDIM-100 scenario, ParaTAA produces images matching sequential sampling in only 7 parallel steps (vs. 100 sequential steps), achieving a CLIP Score of 24.2 identical to the sequential baseline. On DiT DDIM-100 (ImageNet), ParaTAA requires 11 steps with FID 20.0 and IS 448.3, compared to 100 steps with FID 20.6 and IS 446.9 for sequential sampling. Wall-clock time speedup ranges from 1.5× to 2.9× depending on the scenario and hardware configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaTAA outperforms the FP baseline (equivalent to Shih et al. 2023) on every scenario, requiring substantially fewer inference steps: e.g., 11 vs. 23.0 steps on DiT DDIM-100, 21 vs. 42.3 on DiT DDPM-100, and 7 vs. 15.8 on SD DDIM-100. Against the FP+ baseline (fixed-point iteration with grid-searched optimal order k), ParaTAA still wins on the DiT scenarios (11 vs. 19 on DDIM-100; 21 vs. 31 on DDPM-100) and ties on the SD scenarios (7 vs. 7 on DDIM-100; 15 vs. 21 on DDPM-100). Compared to sequential DDIM and DDPM sampling, ParaTAA achieves a 4–14× reduction in inference steps while producing images of equal or slightly better quality (e.g., FID 20.0 vs. 20.6 on DiT DDIM-100; identical CLIP Score 24.2 on SD DDIM-100). The only scenario where ParaTAA shows a slight quality trade-off is DiT DDPM-100, where IS drops from 464.8 (sequential) to 457.8 (ParaTAA), though FID improves from 22.7 to 22.1. Wall-clock speedup is more modest (1.5–2.9×) than the step reduction due to parallelization overhead and memory constraints, but the authors note this can be further improved with optimized hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parallel sampling framework introduced by Shih et al. (2023), which applied Picard-Lindelof iterations to solve the diffusion ODE/SDE in parallel. The key departure is a reformulation of the autoregressive sampling procedure as a system of triangular nonlinear equations, which subsumes the PL iteration as a special case (k = window size) and enables rigorous convergence analysis. From the optimization literature, the authors adapt Anderson Acceleration (Anderson, 1965; Walker & Ni, 2011) into a novel Triangular Anderson Acceleration (TAA) that respects the triangular structure, avoiding the numerical instability of standard AA. The work also draws conceptual parallels to parallelizing autoregressive inference in LLMs (speculative decoding) and feedforward networks (Song et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParaTAA provides a training-free, universal parallel sampling algorithm that reduces inference steps by 4–14× while producing (nearly) identical images to sequential sampling, making it immediately applicable to any existing diffusion model and solver. The smooth interpolation capability when initializing from a similar trajectory opens practical avenues for image editing, variation, and prompt optimization without additional training. Key limitations include the requirement for multiple GPUs to realize wall-clock speedup, restriction to first-order solvers (DDIM/DDPM), and the need for empirical tuning of the order k. Open questions include extension to higher-order solvers (DPM-Solver, Heun), theoretical convergence guarantees for TAA, and applicability to video diffusion models.
