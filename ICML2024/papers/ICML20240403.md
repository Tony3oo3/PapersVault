## Problem

Selecting high-loss samples for gradient updates is known to reduce training steps, but the overhead of computing exact per-sample losses negates the gains, and no prior work provides a theoretical characterization of how much speedup is achievable when using approximate (rather than exact) losses for selection. This paper fills that gap by analyzing greedy SGD with approximate losses and proposing early exiting as a lightweight mechanism to obtain them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240403/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the method receives R i.i.d. samples from the data distribution along with their approximate loss values computed by applying the linear classification head to an intermediate layer's representation (rather than the final layer's). It selects the single sample (or top 50% in the mini-batch variant) with the largest approximate loss and performs one full forward-backward pass on that sample to produce the parameter update. The approximate losses require only a partial forward pass up to the chosen exit layer, making them substantially cheaper than exact losses for deep models.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary results. Theoretically, it proves that Greedy SGD (GSGD) with approximate losses converges to a constant factor of the optimal loss F* in fewer iterations than vanilla SGD for smooth convex objectives, while also showing GSGD may fail to converge to F* asymptotically; it further quantifies how the noise level σ in the approximate losses degrades the speedup, showing a threshold σ_max below which GSGD still outperforms SGD. Practically, it introduces SIFT, which uses early exiting at an intermediate layer to compute approximate MLM losses (or prediction entropies) for all samples in a batch, then backpropagates only on the top 50% by that criterion. Evaluated on a 110M-parameter 12-layer BERT base model (pretrained from scratch on BookCorpus + Wikipedia) and a modified ResNet-50 (on CIFAR-100 and Food-101), SIFT achieves significant wall-clock and backpropagation-step savings—e.g., reaching 64% validation accuracy in ~43 hours versus ~57 hours for vanilla training—without any optimized implementation of the selection forward pass.

## Results

On BERT base pretraining (110M params, 12 layers), entropy-based SIFT with exit at layer 6 achieves 66.80% validation accuracy vs. 64.66% for the vanilla baseline, while loss-based SIFT at layer 12 reaches 66.40%. In wall-clock time, SIFT with first-layer exit reaches 64% validation accuracy in ~43 hours compared to ~57 hours for vanilla training. On CIFAR-100 with the same backpropagation sample complexity (100 epochs), SIFT with last-layer exit achieves 76.93% test accuracy vs. 69.02% for the baseline at the best learning rate (5e-3). On Food-101 under the same setting, SIFT with last-layer exit reaches 66.64% vs. 58.88% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla training with no sample selection (uniform random sampling). SIFT consistently outperforms the baseline across all experiments and metrics. On BERT pretraining, entropy-based SIFT (layer 6) improves validation accuracy by 2.14 percentage points over the baseline (66.80% vs. 64.66%), while also reducing wall-clock time by ~25% to reach the same accuracy threshold. On CIFAR-100 with matched backpropagation complexity, SIFT with last-layer exit wins by 7.91 points (76.93% vs. 69.02% at lr=5e-3), and with matched training time, SIFT with early exit wins by 4.41 points (73.45% vs. 69.04% at lr=1e-2). On the harder Food-101 dataset, SIFT with last-layer exit achieves 66.64% vs. 58.88% baseline (same backprop complexity, lr=5e-3), a 7.76-point gain. Notably, SIFT maintains its advantage even when learning rates are tuned for both methods, confirming the gains are not merely an artifact of suboptimal hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the greedy sample-selection line of work (OSGD by Kawaguchi & Lu, 2019; OHEM by Shrivastava et al., 2016) by replacing exact per-sample losses with cheap approximate losses obtained via early exiting from intermediate layers. While early exiting has been widely studied for inference acceleration (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016; Schwartz et al., 2020), the authors repurpose it as a lightweight filtering mechanism for training, a use they note is novel. The theoretical contribution is the first convergence analysis of greedy SGD under approximate (noisy) loss values, going beyond the argmax-preserving assumption that prior work implicitly requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when and to what extent greedy sample selection with approximate losses outperforms random SGD, revealing both a speedup (converging to O(F*) faster) and a fundamental limitation (GSGD may not converge to F* asymptotically). Practically, SIFT yields meaningful wall-clock savings (~25% for BERT) without any optimized implementation, suggesting further gains are possible with pipelining. Key open questions include extending the theory to non-convex objectives, testing on much larger transformer models, and whether the early-exit-based selection generalizes beyond the specific architectures studied.
