## Problem

Standard Plug-and-Play (PnP) algorithms apply a pre-trained denoiser to image iterates that become progressively cleaner during optimization, creating a domain shift from the noisy images on which the denoiser was trained. Diffusion-based restoration methods avoid this by always re-noising before denoising, but lack rigorous convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether one can inherit the 'noisy-input' principle of diffusion models within a PnP optimization framework while retaining provable convergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image y (either blurred by a known convolution kernel or with a random mask of missing pixels) and produces a single restored image x_N after N iterations of stochastic gradient descent. A pre-trained Gradient-Step denoiser (trained on natural images) is used as the implicit prior, and the noise level σ is annealed from a high initial value (e.g., 1.8σ_y for deblurring) down to a small final value (e.g., 0.5σ_y) across m=16 annealing levels. No clean reference image is needed at test time; the denoiser is frozen and used as a black-box regularizer.

## Method

SNORE defines a new regularization R_σ(x) = −E_{x̃~N(x,σ²I)}[log p_σ(x̃)], the expected negative log of the noisy prior evaluated at Gaussian-perturbed versions of x, which is equivalent to a KL divergence between a local Gaussian and the noisy prior. Its gradient (x − E[D_σ*(x̃)])/σ² is estimated unbiasedly by sampling a single noise realization ε~N(0,I) per iteration, yielding the update x_{k+1} = x_k − δ∇F(x_k,y) − (αδ/σ²)(x_k − D_σ(x_k + σε)). This injects noise only into the denoiser input (not the data-fidelity term), keeping the denoiser always operating within its training distribution. An annealing schedule (Algorithm 3) progressively decreases σ and α across m levels, motivated by a critical-point analysis showing that cluster points of the annealed sequence lie in the critical-point set of the ideal MAP problem. Convergence to a critical point is proved for the exact MMSE denoiser (Proposition 3.3), and the bias from using a learned denoiser is bounded (Proposition 3.5).

## Results

On deblurring (CBSD10, 10 kernels, 3 noise levels), Ann-SNORE achieves PSNR of 29.92 / 27.91 / 25.61 dB at σ_y = 5/10/20 per 255, competitive with RED Prox (30.64 / 28.50 / 26.31 dB) but trailing by ~0.7 dB on distortion metrics. On inpainting (CBSD68, p=0.5), Ann-SNORE Prox achieves the best PSNR (31.94 dB), SSIM (0.93), and LPIPS (0.04), outperforming RED (31.26 dB), RED Prox (30.31 dB), and DiffPIR (29.57 dB). On perceptual quality (BRISQUE, lower is better), Ann-SNORE scores 28.24/27.89/28.88 for deblurring, well below RED Prox (45.27/51.41/55.48) but above DiffPIR (17.87/21.12/33.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RED Prox (the strongest distortion-metric baseline), Ann-SNORE consistently trails by 0.6–0.7 dB in PSNR and 0.03–0.05 in SSIM across all three deblurring noise levels, but substantially outperforms it on the no-reference perceptual metric BRISQUE (e.g., 28.88 vs. 55.48 at σ_y=20/255). Against DiffPIR, Ann-SNORE wins on PSNR and SSIM at 10/255 and 20/255 (e.g., 27.91 vs. 27.47 dB at 10/255) but DiffPIR achieves lower BRISQUE scores, likely because the shared denoiser was not trained for the high noise levels DiffPIR requires. Against PnP SGD, Ann-SNORE wins on all four metrics at every noise level (e.g., 27.91 vs. 27.61 dB PSNR at 10/255). On inpainting (p=0.5), Ann-SNORE Prox clearly dominates: it achieves the best PSNR (31.94 dB), SSIM (0.93), and LPIPS (0.04), beating RED by 0.68 dB and RED Prox by 1.63 dB in PSNR, while DiffPIR wins only on BRISQUE (4.17 vs. 8.35). Overall, SNORE is competitive on deblurring (trailing RED Prox on full-reference metrics but matching or beating on perceptual quality) and superior on inpainting across distortion metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SNORE extends the RED/PnP framework (Romano et al., 2017; Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013) by injecting Gaussian noise into the denoiser input at each iteration, motivated by the observation that diffusion models (DDM) apply denoisers only to re-noised images. It departs from PnP SGD (Laumont et al., 2023) by restricting stochasticity to the regularization term only, and from DDM-based restoration (DiffPIR, Chung et al.) by adopting an optimization rather than sampling perspective. The annealing schedule draws on annealed importance sampling (Neal, 2001) and the decreasing-noise schedules in diffusion models, while the KL-divergence interpretation of the regularization connects to the score-matching literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNORE provides the first PnP-style regularization with provable convergence guarantees that explicitly matches the denoiser's training distribution, bridging the theoretical gap between convergent PnP methods and empirically strong but theoretically ungrounded DDM-based restoration. The biased-denoiser error bound (Proposition 3.5) is practically important since real denoisers are never exact MMSE. Key limitations include higher computational cost than RED for deblurring (requiring many annealing levels) and the boundedness assumption in the convergence proof; open questions include whether adaptive optimizers (ADAM, INNA) can reduce cost while preserving convergence, and quantifying output uncertainty for safety-critical applications like medical imaging.
