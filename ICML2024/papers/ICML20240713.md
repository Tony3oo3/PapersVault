## Problem

Despite the empirical success of LLM-empowered agents in robotics, autonomous driving, and personal assistance, no theoretical framework explains why pretrained LLMs—trained solely for next-token prediction—can effectively solve decision-making problems in the physical world. This paper addresses four open questions: what is the correct theoretical model for LLM agent performance, how do pretrained LLMs plan via prompting, how should the exploration-exploitation tradeoff be handled, and how do pretraining errors of the LLM and Reporter propagate to overall agent performance.

## Input / Output

The Planner receives a natural language task, a sequence of language observations (translated from physical states by the Reporter), and historical episode context, all concatenated into a single prompt of the form H_t ∪ {ω^t, τ_h^t}. It outputs a sequence of language subgoals that the Actor decomposes into low-level physical actions over H_a steps per subgoal. The system operates as a hierarchical POMDP: the Planner never directly observes physical states but only their language descriptions, and the high-level transition kernel is implicitly determined by the fixed Actor policy and environment dynamics, both hidden from the Planner as a latent variable z.

## Method

The paper formalizes LLM agents as a Planner-Actor-Reporter (PAR) system within a Hierarchical MDP, where the LLM Planner navigates a high-level POMDP by generating subgoals via in-context learning. The central theoretical result is that a pretrained LLM performs Bayesian Aggregated Imitation Learning (BAIL): it implicitly constructs a posterior over the hidden environment parameter z (transition kernel and Actor policy) and aggregates expert policies weighted by this posterior, a mechanism encoded by the transformer architecture and activated through prompting. The authors prove that naively following LLM recommendations yields linear regret, and introduce an ε-greedy exploration strategy with η-distinguishable exploration policies that achieves Õ(√T) regret under a perfect setting. Under practical settings, the regret decomposes into the exploration term plus a pretraining error term combining the LLM's cross-entropy estimation error and the Reporter's contrastive learning error, both converging at O((N_p T_p)^{-1/2}).

## Results

The paper establishes that under a perfect pretraining setting, the LLM Planner performs Bayesian Aggregated Imitation Learning (BAIL) via in-context learning (Proposition 4.2), but naively following LLM recommendations incurs linear regret Ω(T) (Proposition 4.3). With an ε-greedy exploration strategy using an η-distinguishable exploration policy, the Planner achieves a sublinear regret of Õ(H^{3/2} √(T/η · log(c_Z|Z|√T))) (Theorem 4.6). Under the practical setting with pretrained LLM and Reporter, the regret decomposes into the same √T exploration term plus a pretraining error term H²T·Δ_p, where Δ_p combines the LLM's TV error (converging at O((N_p T̄_p)^{-1/2})) and the Reporter's TV error (converging at O((N_p T_p H)^{-1/2})) (Theorem 5.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The closest theoretical comparison is to Lee et al. (2023), who showed that a perfect decision-pretrained transformer attains Õ(H^{3/2} √T) Bayesian regret via ICL; this paper targets the more challenging frequentist regret and achieves a comparable rate with additional exploration. The paper also extends Liu et al. (2023)'s world-model-based LLM planning framework by incorporating pretraining error analysis and multi-agent coordination, yielding a regret of Õ(√(H³TK/η)) for K cooperating actors under the perfect setting (Corollary B.4). No head-to-head empirical benchmarks are provided, as the contributions are entirely in the form of regret bounds and convergence guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bayesian interpretation of in-context learning (Xie et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023) and the decision-pretrained transformer framework (Lee et al., 2023) to formalize LLM agents within a hierarchical RL structure. Unlike Lee et al. (2023), which analyzes a flat MDP and achieves Bayesian regret, and Liu et al. (2023), which treats the LLM as a world model, this work introduces a Planner-Actor-Reporter (PAR) architecture where the LLM navigates a high-level POMDP with a latent variable encoding both the environment dynamics and the low-level Actor policy. The key departure is proving that the LLM's ICL-based planning is equivalent to Bayesian Aggregated Imitation Learning (BAIL) and that this alone yields linear regret, necessitating an explicit ε-greedy exploration strategy with η-distinguishability to achieve sublinear regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework that rigorously explains why LLM agents work (BAIL via ICL) and why they can fail (insufficient exploration without explicit deviation from LLM recommendations), providing a principled foundation for designing more reliable LLM-driven autonomous systems. The practical regret bound (Theorem 5.7) quantifies how pretraining errors in both the LLM and the multimodal Reporter propagate into planning performance, offering concrete guidance on data requirements. Open questions include whether the finite latent space assumption |Z| < ∞ can be relaxed, whether the η-distinguishability condition is practically verifiable, and how the framework extends to continuous action spaces and non-stationary environments. The theoretical nature of the work means no empirical validation is provided, leaving a gap between the provable guarantees and real-world LLM agent performance.
