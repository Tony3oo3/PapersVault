## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers for high-dimensional Poisson equations (PINNs, Deep Ritz, diffusion loss, BSDE-based methods) suffer from high computational costs due to spatial derivative computation, slow propagation of boundary information to the interior, or prohibitively long SDE simulation times. While Walk-on-Spheres (WoS) methods efficiently estimate solutions pointwise via Monte Carlo, they cannot amortize this cost across the domain, making them impractical for applications requiring many sequential evaluations such as PDE-constrained optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a domain Ω ⊂ ℝ^d, a source term f, a Dirichlet boundary condition g, and a neural network v_θ as input. During training, it samples interior points ξ ∈ Ω and boundary points ζ ∈ ∂Ω, simulating WoS trajectories from each ξ to generate regression targets. The output is a trained feedforward network (6 layers, width 256, GELU, residual connections) that approximates the PDE solution u at arbitrary points in Ω via a single O(1) forward pass, with the key property that the loss requires zero spatial derivatives of v_θ—unlike PINNs (Laplacian) or Deep Ritz (gradient).

## Method

NWoS derives a recursive stochastic representation of the Poisson solution by decomposing it over nested balls B_{r_k}(ξ_k) inscribed in the domain, where each step samples the next point uniformly on the sphere boundary (exploiting Brownian motion isotropy) rather than time-discretizing the SDE. The source term is accumulated at each step using the Green's function for the ball, yielding a single-trajectory estimator WoS(ξ) = g(ξ̄_κ) − Σ|B_{r_k}|f(γ_k)G_{r_k}(γ_k, ξ_k). The training loss L_NWoS[v] = E[(v(ξ) − WoS(ξ))²] is a simple regression objective requiring no spatial derivatives, with theoretical guarantees that its minimizer approximates the true solution in the uniform norm up to O(ε) where ε is the stopping tolerance. Practical improvements include: (i) a maximum step count K where the network itself evaluates the terminal value (enforcing the mean-value property on subdomains), (ii) control variates using ∇v_θ·(ξ_1 − x) detached from the computation graph to reduce variance, and (iii) a buffer that caches WoS estimates and periodically evicts/replaces points to balance diversity and accuracy.

## Results

NWoS achieves the lowest relative L²-error across all four main benchmarks: 4.29e-5 on Laplace(10d), 6.56e-4 on Committor(10d), 2.60e-3 on Poisson Rect.(10d), and 4.82e-4 on Poisson(50d). These represent improvements of roughly 3.7×, 1.9×, 4×, and 1.2× over the best competing method on each problem, respectively. NWoS also requires zero spatial derivatives in its loss (vs. 1–2 for all baselines), uses only one loss term, and reduces peak GPU memory by orders of magnitude compared to PINNs. In the PDE-constrained optimization application, NWoS converges to a 1.30% relative L²-error for parameter estimation within 20 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NWoS outperforms all five baselines on every one of the four main benchmarks. Against PINNs [Raissi et al., 2019], NWoS achieves 17× lower error on Laplace(10d) (4.29e-5 vs. 7.42e-4) and 16× lower on Poisson(50d) (4.82e-4 vs. 7.70e-3), while also requiring no second-order derivatives and using far less GPU memory. Against the Deep Ritz method [E et al., 2017], NWoS is 4× more accurate on Poisson Rect.(10d) (2.60e-3 vs. 1.06e-2) and 2× on Poisson(50d) (4.82e-4 vs. 1.05e-3). Against the diffusion loss [Nüsken & Richter, 2021], NWoS is 3.7× better on Laplace(10d) (4.29e-5 vs. 1.57e-4) and dramatically better on Committor(10d) (6.56e-4 vs. 4.48e-2) and Poisson Rect.(10d) (2.60e-3 vs. 9.69e-2), where the diffusion loss struggles with its short-horizon SDE truncation. Against the Neural Cache [Li et al., 2023], NWoS is 1.9× better on Committor(10d) and 6.3× better on Poisson(50d), with the paper attributing the Neural Cache's weakness to overfitting on a fixed buffer of points. Against standard Walk-on-Spheres [Muller, 1956] without a neural network, NWoS is 25× more accurate on Poisson Rect.(10d) (2.60e-3 vs. 2.32e-1) and 9× on Laplace(10d), demonstrating that amortizing WoS into a learned model is essential for domain-wide accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

NWoS directly extends Muller's (1956) Walk-on-Spheres Monte Carlo algorithm by replacing pointwise estimation with a neural network trained via a WoS-based regression loss, leveraging the theoretical framework of Grohs & Herrmann (2022) on neural network approximation of elliptic PDEs. It departs from the dominant PINN/Deep Ritz paradigm (which requires spatial derivatives and separate boundary losses) and from SDE-based losses (Feynman-Kac, BSDE, diffusion loss) that incur high simulation costs, by exploiting the isotropy of Brownian motion to jump directly to sphere boundaries without time-discretization. The method also builds on the Neural Cache idea from Li et al. (2023) for graphics applications but generalizes it to high-dimensional parametric PDEs with a buffer that evicts and replaces points to avoid overfitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NWoS provides a derivative-free, single-term loss that achieves orders-of-magnitude reductions in GPU memory and error compared to PINNs for high-dimensional Poisson equations (up to 500d), making previously intractable problems in molecular dynamics (committor functions) and PDE-constrained optimization practically solvable. The key limitation is that the method currently handles only Poisson equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions and requires a fixed parametrization for parametric problems, leaving extensions to Neumann/Robin conditions, spatially varying coefficients, and neural operators as important open questions. The approach also highlights a broader principle: stochastic representations can eliminate the need for expensive automatic differentiation in PDE losses, potentially informing future solver design beyond Poisson-type problems.
