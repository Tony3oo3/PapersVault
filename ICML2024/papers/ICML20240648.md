## Problem

When the contraction map Φ in a parametric fixed-point equation is nonsmooth (e.g., involving ReLU activations or proximal operators), the standard chain rule used by iterative differentiation (ITD) and approximate implicit differentiation (AID) no longer holds, leaving these widely used methods without rigorous convergence guarantees. While Bolte et al. (2022) established an asymptotic rate for nonsmooth ITD, no non-asymptotic rates existed for AID, and the stochastic composite setting (where Φ = G∘T with T accessible only via unbiased estimators) was entirely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The methods consume an approximate fixed-point iterate w_t(λ) (obtained from any convergent solver), a direction vector y ∈ ℝ^d, and either direct evaluations of Φ or, in the stochastic case, access to G and a stochastic estimator T̂_ξ of the inner map T. The output is a single element of the conservative Jacobian-vector product D_w(λ)^T y (a vector in ℝ^m), or in the bilevel setting, a mini-batch gradient estimate of the upper-level objective f(λ). In the stochastic NSID algorithm, two independent sample sequences are used: one (J samples) to form a mini-batch estimator T̄ of T, and another (k samples) to drive the stochastic fixed-point iteration solving the adjoint linear system.

## Method

The paper leverages the conservative derivative framework of Bolte & Pauwels (2021), which restores a valid chain rule for piecewise-smooth definable maps, and introduces a piecewise Lipschitz smoothness condition (Lemma 3.2) that replaces global L-smoothness and governs the error propagation through the set-valued derivative iterations. For deterministic ITD and AID-FP, this yields non-asymptotic linear rates O(κt·e^{-t/κ}) and O(κ²·e^{-t/κ}) respectively, where κ = (1−q)^{-1} is the contraction-condition number, with an additional indicator term δ_λ(t) that captures the transient cost of traversing nondifferentiable regions. For the stochastic composite case, the paper introduces NSID, which replaces the exact linear system with a mini-batch estimator T̄ built from J samples of T̂_ξ, then solves the resulting stochastic fixed-point equation via a diminishing-step-size iteration over k additional samples, achieving mean-square error O(κ⁵/k + κ⁴/J + κ^{4+α}/t) and matching the O(ε^{-1}) sample complexity of SGD for strongly convex smooth objectives.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic linear convergence rates for nonsmooth implicit differentiation: AID-FP converges as O(κ²e^{-t/κ}) while ITD converges slightly slower as O(κte^{-t/κ}), where κ=(1-q)⁻¹ is the condition number. For the new stochastic method NSID, the mean square error of the Jacobian-vector product estimate is O(κ⁵/k + κ⁴/J + κ^(4+α)/t), yielding a sample complexity of O(κ^(5+α)ε⁻¹) to achieve error ≤ε. Empirically, AID-FP shows a wide performance gap over ITD after support identification in the elastic net problem, and NSID converges faster than deterministic AID-FP in the stochastic setting while the smooth SID baseline diverges in the data poisoning experiment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Bolte et al. (2022), the paper improves the nonsmooth ITD rate from O((√q+ε)^t) to O((q+ε)^t), matching the best available smooth ITD rate and providing a more explicit bound without arbitrary ε. Compared to Grazzi et al. (2020) (smooth AID/ITD), the nonsmooth rates fully recover the smooth bounds as a special case (r=1) and additionally quantify the degradation due to nondifferentiable regions via the indicator terms δ_λ(t) and δ̄_λ(t). Against the SID algorithm of Grazzi et al. (2023), NSID handles a general nonsmooth outer map G (e.g., soft-thresholding) rather than requiring G=identity; empirically, SID fails to converge on the elastic net problem for a specific λ choice and diverges entirely in the data poisoning experiment, while NSID converges in both settings. The paper also notes that AID-FP is theoretically and empirically faster than ITD, with the gap widening near nondifferentiable regions of Φ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conservative derivative framework of Bolte & Pauwels (2021) and the nonsmooth ITD analysis of Bolte et al. (2022) to cover both iterative and approximate implicit differentiation in the nonsmooth setting. It recovers the smooth rates of Grazzi et al. (2020) as a special case while improving upon Bolte et al.'s O((√q+ε)^t) ITD rate to O((q+ε)^t), and provides the first linear convergence rate for nonsmooth AID. The stochastic method NSID generalizes the smooth SID algorithm of Grazzi et al. (2023) to handle composite maps Φ = G∘T where T is only accessible through stochastic estimators, a setting where no unbiased estimator of Φ itself is available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the missing theoretical guarantees for implicit differentiation methods that practitioners already deploy via PyTorch when the contraction map is nonsmooth (e.g., ReLU networks, proximal operators, LASSO/SVM solvers). The empirical finding that AID-FP substantially outperforms ITD after support identification in elastic-net problems is practically relevant for hyperparameter optimization. A key open question remains: establishing convergence rates for solving the full nonsmooth bilevel problem (not just computing the hypergradient) is left for future work, as the piecewise-smoothness constant M_λ/R_λ becomes unbounded near nondifferentiable regions, preventing uniform control over the parameter space.
