## Problem

The vanilla LIF spiking neuron model, the standard unit in SNNs, suffers from three critical limitations for spatio-temporal prediction: its fixed exponential leakage causes monotonic decay of historical membrane potential, preventing long-term dependency capture; its simple feedforward connectivity restricts spatial information interaction within a layer; and its static parameters cannot adapt to dynamically changing data distributions during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242133/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a temporal sequence of T input frames (e.g., 10 frames of 64×64 grayscale for Moving MNIST, 4 frames of 2×32×32 for TaxiBJ traffic flow, 10 frames of 128×128 for KTH) that are resized into patch-based tensors (e.g., 4×32×32 or 16×32×32) and processed step-by-step by spiking neurons over time steps. It outputs T′ predicted future frames (10, 4, 20, or 40 depending on the dataset) via a final convolutional restoration layer, or a gesture class label for the DVS128 event-based recognition task. The KTH evaluation additionally tests recursive prediction by extending output length from 20 to 40 frames at test time without retraining.

## Method

The STC-LIF model introduces two learnable, input-dependent regulatory factors—β (temporal/axon-soma circuit) and γ (spatial/axon-dendrite circuit)—computed as Tanh of a 16-group convolution applied to the neuron's own spike output at the previous time step. β dynamically scales the historical membrane potential (amplifying when positive to preserve long-term memory, attenuating when negative to forget irrelevant history), while γ modulates the incoming synaptic current (enhancing or suppressing spiking). This replaces the fixed α-leakage of vanilla LIF with a data-driven, per-neuron, per-timestep modulation that simultaneously addresses long-term dependency, spatial interaction, and parameter adaptability. During backpropagation, gradients through the spatio-temporal circuit are truncated so that the circuit weights only optimize the modulation factors without interfering with spike output learning. The design is modular and extends directly to PLIF and LM-H models (STC-PLIF, STC-LM-H), and the group convolution keeps the parameter overhead to ~9% while capturing local spatial neighborhood effects.

## Results

On Moving MNIST, STC-LIF achieves MSE 47.0, MAE 136.4, and SSIM 0.863, reducing MSE by 55.8 compared to vanilla LIF (102.8) and by 32.7 compared to the best adaptive baseline GLIF (79.7). On TaxiBJ, STC-LIF attains MSE 56.4 (×100) and SSIM 0.973, outperforming TC-LIF (MSE 59.2) and all other baselines. On KTH with 10→40 frame prediction, STC-LIF reaches SSIM 0.815 and PSNR 22.63, surpassing the next-best GLIF (SSIM 0.795, PSNR 21.90) and substantially outperforming LM-H (SSIM 0.708, PSNR 16.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STC-LIF decisively outperforms all four adaptive spiking neuron baselines across every dataset and metric. On Moving MNIST, the gap is largest: STC-LIF (MSE 47.0) beats GLIF (79.7), LM-H (93.2), PLIF (99.2), and TC-LIF (105.1) by 32.7–58.1 MSE points, with SSIM jumping from 0.685 (GLIF) to 0.863. On TaxiBJ, STC-LIF achieves the lowest MSE (56.4 vs. TC-LIF's 59.2) and ties TC-LIF on SSIM (0.973), though TC-LIF edges it slightly on MAE (19.3 vs. 19.5). On KTH, STC-LIF leads all baselines on both SSIM and PSNR in both 10→20 and 10→40 settings, with the advantage widening at longer prediction horizons (e.g., PSNR 22.63 vs. GLIF's 21.90 at 10→40). The paper further demonstrates that the spatio-temporal circuit generalizes: STC-PLIF and STC-LM-H each outperform their respective base models (PLIF, LM-H) by a wide margin on all three prediction benchmarks, confirming the circuit's plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of adaptive spiking neuron models (PLIF, GLIF, TC-LIF, LM-H) that introduced learnable parameters to overcome the fixed-dynamics limitation of vanilla LIF. Its key departure is drawing on biological autaptic synapses to introduce a spatio-temporal self-connection circuit with dynamic, input-dependent modulation factors (β and γ) that remain active during inference, unlike prior adaptive models whose parameters freeze after training. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in extension, demonstrated by its application to both PLIF and LM-H, positioning it as a general enhancement layer rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The STC-LIF model addresses a genuine gap in SNN research: the inability of existing adaptive neuron models to dynamically adapt during inference to changing spatio-temporal data distributions. The DVS128 experiment revealing that vanilla LIF learns only spatial positional information while STC-LIF captures temporal structure is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, all experiments are conducted on relatively small datasets (Moving MNIST, TaxiBJ, KTH) with SNN-only baselines, leaving open questions about scalability to larger video prediction benchmarks, deeper networks, and practical neuromorphic hardware deployment. The modest 9.3% parameter overhead makes the approach attractive, but whether the dynamic modulation factors remain effective at scale or in real-time inference on spiking hardware remains untested.
