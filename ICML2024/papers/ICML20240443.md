## Problem

Gradient-based optimization over Riemannian manifolds requires careful tuning of the learning rate, where poor choices lead to divergence or slow convergence. While learning-rate-free methods (e.g., DoG, DoWG, coin betting) have been developed for Euclidean optimization, no comparable tools existed for the Riemannian setting. This paper introduces the first comprehensive study of learning-rate-free algorithms for stochastic Riemannian optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240443/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms require only a geodesically convex objective on a Riemannian manifold, an initial point, a stochastic gradient oracle, and a small initial distance estimate ε (e.g., 10⁻⁸ to 10⁰). The sole tunable parameter ε is shown to be insensitive in practice—the algorithms adapt within a few hundred iterations regardless of its magnitude. Output consists of a sequence of iterates on the manifold plus a weighted (or uniform) average iterate, accompanied by high-probability optimality gap bounds that are optimal up to logarithmic factors in both Lipschitz and smooth settings.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the intractable maximum distance to the optimum d̄_T with a proxy r̄_t (maximum geodesic distance from the starting point), yielding adaptive step sizes of the form η_t = r̄_t / √(ζ_κ(r̄_t) · G_t), where G_t is the cumulative squared gradient norm and ζ_κ is a geometric curvature function. Three algorithms are proposed: RDoG (unweighted gradient normalization), RDoWG (weighted gradient normalization using r̄_s² weights for improved geometric adaptation), and NRDoG (normalized gradient descent providing scale-free adaptivity to both Lipschitz and smoothness constants). A 'tamed' variant (T-RDoWG) further reduces step sizes by a polylogarithmic factor to guarantee bounded iterates with high probability. Convergence is established via a Riemannian Jensen's inequality for weighted geodesic averaging and a martingale concentration bound, achieving O(1/√T) rates in the non-smooth case and O(1/T) in the smooth case, matching the best-known optimally tuned deterministic rates up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper's experimental results are presented primarily through figures rather than a single numerical table. Across all three tasks (Rayleigh quotient maximization, PCA on Grassmann manifolds, and Poincaré ball word embeddings), RDoG, RDoWG, and NRDoG are insensitive to the initial distance estimate ε across eight orders of magnitude (ε ∈ [10⁻⁸, 10⁰]), consistently achieving negligible geodesic distance to the numerical solution. In contrast, RSGD and RADAM require learning rates within a narrow window (η ∈ [10⁻⁸, 10⁶]) to perform well. On the Poincaré ball WordNet embedding task, RDoG and RDoWG achieve competitive mean average precision without any burn-in heuristic, while RSGD and RADAM performance significantly degrades without it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RSGD (Bonnabel, 2013), the proposed learning-rate-free methods (RDoG, RDoWG, NRDoG) demonstrate clear robustness advantages: RSGD is highly sensitive to the learning rate, overshooting with too-large steps and converging slowly with too-small steps, while the proposed methods adapt automatically within a few hundred iterations regardless of the initial distance estimate. Against RADAM (Becigneul & Ganea, 2019), the comparison is similarly favorable: RADAM requires careful learning rate tuning across all three experimental tasks, and on the Poincaré ball word embedding task, RADAM's performance significantly degrades without a burn-in heuristic (reduced learning rate for the first ten epochs), whereas RDoG and RDoWG achieve competitive mean average precision without any such heuristic. The paper does not report a single unified numerical table; instead, results are shown as geodesic distance curves, regret trace plots, and mean average precision bar charts, all consistently showing the learning-rate-free methods matching or exceeding the best-tuned baselines while eliminating the need for hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to systematically develop learning-rate-free algorithms for stochastic Riemannian optimization, explicitly noting that no prior work addresses this gap. It adapts two recent Euclidean parameter-free methods—DoG (Ivgi et al., 2023) and DoWG (Khaled et al., 2023)—to the Riemannian setting by incorporating the geometric curvature function ζ_κ into step-size schedules and weighted averaging, while building on the foundational RSGD framework of Bonnabel (2013). A third algorithm (NRDoG) extends normalized gradient descent (Shor, 2012; Levy, 2016) to manifolds, providing scale-free adaptivity to both Lipschitz and smoothness constants without user-specified parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Riemannian optimization is widely used in PCA, matrix completion, tensor factorization, and hyperbolic embeddings, yet learning-rate tuning remains a major barrier to robust deployment. The paper's high-probability convergence guarantees (optimal up to log factors) and empirical insensitivity to the initial distance estimate ε across 8 orders of magnitude make these methods genuinely drop-in replacements for tuned RSGD/RADAM. Open questions include extending to retraction-based updates for large-scale problems, handling manifolds with intractable geodesic distances (Stiefel, Fisher-Rao), and integrating with Riemannian momentum/acceleration schemes.
