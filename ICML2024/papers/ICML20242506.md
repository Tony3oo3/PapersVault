## Problem

Despite the widespread use of diffusion models, the fundamental question of why different diffusion models trained on the same dataset produce nearly identical outputs from the same initial noise has remained unexplored. This 'consistent model reproducibility' phenomenon was noted anecdotally in early work but lacked systematic quantification, theoretical grounding, or an explanation of what distribution the models are actually learning. The paper also asks whether reproducibility persists across architectures, training procedures, and sampling methods, and how it relates to the model's ability to generalize versus memorize.

## Input / Output

The evaluation protocol feeds 10K i.i.d. Gaussian noise samples (N(0,I), scaled by σ_max for VE kernels) into multiple independently trained diffusion models and compares their outputs pairwise using SSCD cosine similarity (threshold 0.6) and pixel-space MAE (threshold 15.0). For conditional models, a class label is additionally provided; for inverse problems, a partial observation (masked image) is supplied. The primary outputs are generated 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10) or 256×256 images (ImageNet), along with three scalar metrics: RP Score (probability of high SSCD similarity between model pairs), MAE Score, and GL Score (probability that a generated sample is dissimilar to all training samples).

## Method

The paper introduces a systematic framework for measuring reproducibility across 20+ diffusion model variants spanning U-Net and Transformer architectures, VP/VE/sub-VP SDEs, DPM-Solver/Heun/DDIM samplers, and training-from-scratch vs. distillation vs. consistency-training procedures. It identifies two distinct regimes governed by the ratio of model capacity to training data size: a memorization regime where models converge to the closed-form score function of the empirical multi-delta distribution (derived in Proposition 3.2), and a generalization regime where models converge to the score function of the true underlying distribution (validated via a mixture-of-Gaussians experiment in Proposition 3.3 and a model-recovery experiment). The theoretical analysis uses Tweedie's formula to connect the learned score function to the data distribution, and the empirical study extends reproducibility analysis to conditional generation (showing structured cross-class reproducibility) and inverse problem solving via diffusion posterior sampling (where reproducibility holds only within the same architecture family).

## Results

Across 20 unconditional diffusion model configurations trained on CIFAR-10, all pairwise reproducibility (RP) scores exceed 0.68, with the majority above 0.80. The highest scores (1.0) occur between models differing only in ODE sampler (DDPMv4 vs. DDPMv5), while the lowest (0.68) is between UViT (Transformer) and CT (consistency training). In the generalization regime, RP scores remain consistently high (0.70–0.91) regardless of architecture (U-Net vs. Transformer), training procedure (from scratch vs. distillation), or perturbation kernel (VP, VE, sub-VP). In contrast, GANs (wGAN, SNGAN) and VAEs (VAE, VAMP) show negligible reproducibility, confirming this phenomenon is unique to diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new model to beat baselines; instead, it demonstrates that all 20 diffusion model variants (spanning U-Net, Transformer, EDM, DDPM++, Multistage, Consistency Training, and Progressive Distillation architectures) are mutually reproducible with RP scores of 0.68–1.0. The key contrast is with GANs (wGAN, SNGAN) and VAEs (VAE, VAMP), which show no meaningful reproducibility, confirming the phenomenon is specific to diffusion models. Among diffusion models, the lowest cross-architecture scores (0.70–0.74) occur between Transformer-based (UViT, DiT) and EDM/CT models, while same-architecture pairs (e.g., DDPMv4 vs. DDPMv5) achieve perfect 1.0 scores. For inverse problem solving (inpainting via DPS), reproducibility holds strongly within the same architecture family (U-Net vs. U-Net, Transformer vs. Transformer) but drops notably when comparing U-Net to Transformer models, suggesting the gradient term in DPS disrupts cross-architecture consistency. Conditional models on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet exhibit structured reproducibility: high RP_cond among conditional models and high RP_between only when the unconditional model's output class matches the conditional label.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SSCD image-similarity metric (Pizzi et al., 2022) and the optimal-denoiser theory from Karras et al. (2022) to formalize and systematically study a phenomenon previously noted only anecdotally in Song et al. (2020b): that different diffusion models trained on the same data produce nearly identical outputs from the same noise. It extends the memorization/generalization regime taxonomy of Yoon et al. (2023) by showing that reproducibility is the observable signature distinguishing the two regimes, and positions itself as an empirical complement to the growing body of convergence analyses (Li et al., 2023a; Chen et al., 2022; Benton et al., 2023) that typically assume L²-accurate score estimation or simplified architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that U-Net, Transformer, distillation-based, and consistency-trained models all converge to the same noise-to-image mapping has direct practical consequences: it implies that commercial black-box diffusion models can be replicated by training on their own outputs (a significant privacy risk), and that training efficiency can be improved by exploiting the shared score function across model sizes. Key open questions include why reproducibility breaks across architectures in inverse-problem settings (DPS), whether the phenomenon persists at the scale of Stable Diffusion-class models, and what theoretical properties of the optimization landscape guarantee this robustness. The evidence is strongest on CIFAR-10, and the theoretical analysis is limited to toy distributions (multi-delta, low-rank MoG), leaving the natural-image case primarily empirical.
