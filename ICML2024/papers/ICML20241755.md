## Problem

Commonly used information-theoretic measures for quantifying total, aleatoric, and epistemic uncertainty from second-order predictive distributions (Shannon entropy, conditional entropy, mutual information) violate natural axiomatic properties such as invariance under spread-preserving location shifts and monotonicity under mean-preserving spreads. Wimmer et al. (2023) identified these failures but did not propose a replacement, leaving a gap in principled uncertainty quantification for safety-critical ML applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a second-order distribution Q over the (K−1)-dimensional probability simplex (e.g., a Dirichlet distribution parameterized by α ∈ ℝ₊ᴷ) and produces three non-negative scalars: TU, AU, and EU, each bounded in [0, (K−1)/K]. For Dirichlet inputs, TU admits a closed form (1 − max_y α_y/α₀), AU requires Monte Carlo estimation of an expectation, and EU is obtained by solving a convex constrained optimization over the simplex with a guaranteed unique solution solvable in ~30 bisection iterations.

## Method

The paper defines each uncertainty measure as the minimum second-order Wasserstein distance from Q to a fixed reference set: TU measures distance to second-order Dirac measures on first-order Dirac measures (no uncertainty at all), AU measures distance to mixtures of such Dirac measures (no aleatoric uncertainty), and EU measures distance to second-order Dirac measures on the simplex (no epistemic uncertainty). The first-order ground metric is the total variation distance (½‖p−q‖₁) induced by the trivial 0/1 distance on the label space, making the second-order Wasserstein distance computable via the trivial coupling. The authors prove that all eight axioms (A0–A8) are satisfied, including the previously violated mean-preserving spread and spread-preserving location shift properties. For Dirichlet distributions, they derive explicit formulas and show EU reduces to a convex optimization with a unique solution solvable via Lagrange multipliers and bisection.

## Results

This is primarily a theoretical paper; the headline result is Theorem 4.8, which proves that the Wasserstein-distance-based uncertainty measures (TU, AU, EU) satisfy all eight axioms (A0–A8) proposed in Section 3.1. Proposition 4.6 establishes that all three measures are bounded above by (K−1)/K, with the bound reached at natural extreme cases (e.g., AU maximized at δ_Unif, EU maximized at the discrete uniform on Dirac measures). For Dirichlet second-order distributions, TU admits the closed form 1 − max_y α_y/α₀ (Eq. 20), while EU reduces to a convex constrained optimization (Proposition 4.9) solvable in ~30 bisection iterations. Figure 3 (Appendix B) provides a qualitative numerical comparison on six Bernoulli-parameter second-order distributions, confirming that the distance-based measures respect A5 (mean-preserving spread) and A6 (spread-preserving shift) where the entropy-based measures fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Wasserstein-distance-based measures against the standard entropy-based approach (Shannon entropy for TU, conditional entropy for AU, mutual information for EU) that was criticized by Wimmer et al. (2023, UAI). The entropy-based measures are shown to violate A5 (mean-preserving spread should increase EU) and A6 (spread-preserving location shift should leave EU unchanged): specifically, comparing Unif[0.3,0.7] vs. Unif[0.6,1.0] in Figure 3, the entropy-based EU increases under a pure location shift, while the distance-based EU remains constant. The Schweighofer et al. (2023) variant (Eqs. 8–9) is also discussed: it avoids the dependence on the mean p̄ but violates A4 (TU is not maximal for the continuous second-order uniform) and can produce unbounded values. The proposed Wasserstein-based measures satisfy all axioms A0–A8 simultaneously, which neither baseline achieves, and additionally yield naturally bounded values in [0, (K−1)/K] without requiring ad-hoc normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly responds to Wimmer et al. (2023), who demonstrated that the standard information-theoretic uncertainty measures (Shannon entropy for TU, conditional entropy for AU, mutual information for EU) violate natural axioms when applied to second-order predictive distributions. It also engages with the alternative proposed by Schweighofer et al. (2023), which uses expected cross-entropy between i.i.d. copies of Q but still lacks a clean reference-set interpretation and can be unbounded. The paper's core methodological move is to import the Wasserstein distance from optimal transport theory (Villani, 2009; 2021) into the second-order probability space, framing each uncertainty measure as the minimal transport cost from Q to a reference set of 'least uncertain' second-order distributions. The axiomatic framework itself draws on the broader literature on uncertainty measures for imprecise probabilities and credal sets (Bronevich & Klir, 2008; Sale et al., 2023a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first distance-based uncertainty measures for second-order distributions that provably satisfy a comprehensive set of axioms (A0–A8), filling a gap left by Wimmer et al. who identified the problems but offered no replacement. The Wasserstein instantiation yields computable formulas for Dirichlet distributions (the workhorse of Evidential Deep Learning), with TU in closed form and EU reducible to a one-dimensional convex optimization. However, the paper is purely theoretical: no empirical comparison in actual EDL pipelines is provided, and the authors themselves flag the need for systematic experimental validation. Open questions include whether other metrics (beyond Wasserstein) satisfy the axioms, what general metric properties are sufficient, and whether these measures lead to different (and better) training dynamics in practice.
