## Problem

Existing graph representation learning methods, including both message-passing GNNs and Graph Transformers, are confined to internal information within a single graph and neglect correlations between different graphs. The paper argues that graphs sharing structural motifs (e.g., benzene rings in molecular data) should have similar representations, and that exploiting these inter-graph correlations is a critical but unexplored dimension for improving graph learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241191/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes graphs with node features (projected to a d-dimensional hidden space) and edge features, optionally augmented with positional encodings (Laplacian PE or RWPE) added to node embeddings. Graphs are variable-sized (7–479 nodes on average across datasets) and are processed in mini-batches where all graphs in a batch share the same external key-value units. Outputs are node-level embeddings that support graph-level classification/regression (via readout), node-level classification, and link prediction scoring, with the model achieving SOTA on all three prediction granularities.

## Method

The core mechanism is Graph External Attention (GEA), which replaces intra-graph self-attention with attention between input graph nodes/edges and a set of learnable external key-value units (U ∈ R^{S×d}) shared across all graphs in the batch. This captures inter-graph correlations implicitly: nodes in different graphs that attend to the same external unit effectively share information. GEA applies double-normalization (row and column softmax) to the attention matrix for numerical stability, and uses separate external units for nodes, edges, and a shared unit linking edges to nodes. The full GEAET architecture stacks L layers, each running three parallel branches—GEANet (inter-graph), a message-passing GNN (local structure), and a Transformer with self-attention (global intra-graph interaction)—whose outputs are summed and passed through an FFN. Unlike self-attention's O(n²) cost, GEA scales linearly in the number of nodes and edges, and the paper demonstrates it is less dependent on positional encoding and more interpretable than self-attention.

## Results

GEAET achieves state-of-the-art results on 5 out of 6 main benchmark datasets: CIFAR-10 (76.63% accuracy), MNIST (98.51% accuracy), Peptides-Struct (0.2445 MAE), PascalVOC-SP (0.4585 F1), and COCO-SP (0.3895 F1). The most striking margins are on PascalVOC-SP and COCO-SP, where GEAET surpasses the next-best method (Exphormer) by 6.2 and 4.7 F1 points respectively. On PATTERN, GEAET (86.99%) ranks second behind GRIT (87.20%). The method also achieves the best link prediction MRR (0.3518) on PCQM-Contact, outperforming Graph Diffuser (0.3388) and SAN (0.3341).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEAET outperforms all 18 baselines on 5 of 6 main datasets, with the largest gaps on PascalVOC-SP (0.4585 F1 vs. Exphormer's 0.3966, a 6.2-point margin) and COCO-SP (0.3895 vs. 0.3430, a 4.7-point margin), where no prior method approaches its performance. On CIFAR-10, GEAET (76.63%) narrowly edges out GRIT (76.47%) and Exphormer (74.75%), while on MNIST it leads Graph-ViT/MLPMixer (98.46%) by 0.05 points. The sole loss is on PATTERN, where GRIT achieves 87.20% versus GEAET's 86.99%, though GEAET's variance is tighter (±0.026 vs. ±0.076). On Peptides-Struct, GEAET (0.2445 MAE) marginally beats Graph-ViT (0.2449) and GRIT (0.2460). Among message-passing GNNs, GEAET's integrated GEANet module boosts GCN from 0.1268 to 0.2250 F1 on PascalVOC-SP and from 0.3496 to 0.2512 MAE on Peptides-Struct, demonstrating that the external attention component alone provides substantial gains over standard neighborhood aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the external attention mechanism from vision (Guo et al., 2022) to graph representation learning, replacing the quadratic self-attention of Graph Transformers with a linear-complexity attention against shared external key-value units. It further incorporates the double-normalization technique from PCT (Guo et al., 2021) and extends the external attention to handle both node and edge features via a shared connection unit. The resulting GEAET architecture sits within the hybrid GNN+Transformer lineage (GraphGPS, SAT, GraphTrans) but departs from all prior graph Transformers by explicitly modeling inter-graph correlations through the external units, a dimension of information that existing methods confine to intra-graph interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GEANet substantially reduces dependence on positional encoding (MAE 0.2512 without PE vs. 0.3871 for Transformer on Peptides-Struct) is practically significant, as positional encoding design has been a persistent source of hyperparameter sensitivity in graph Transformers. The plug-and-play nature of GEANet—simply adding it to GCN, GIN, or GatedGCN yields large gains without architectural redesign—makes it an accessible tool for practitioners. Open questions remain around the lack of theoretical expressivity bounds, the high memory cost of the external units at scale, and whether the inter-graph correlation signal generalizes beyond the batch-level setting used here.
