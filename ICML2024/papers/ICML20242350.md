## Problem

Overparameterization in deep factorizations and adapters provides well-documented benefits—improved optimization landscapes, implicit low-rank regularization, and reduced overfitting—but at the cost of dramatically increased computational and memory requirements. The paper asks whether these benefits can be retained while training on a fraction of the parameters, and answers affirmatively by showing that gradient descent dynamics in deep overparameterized factorizations are confined to invariant low-dimensional subspaces that can be identified and exploited at initialization.

## Input / Output

For deep matrix completion, the method takes a partially observed matrix (a ground-truth matrix Φ masked by Ω) and produces a recovered low-rank matrix, optimizing only 4Lr² compressed parameters plus slowly-updated d×2r subspace factors instead of Ld² full-width parameters. For language model fine-tuning (Deep LoRA), the method takes frozen pretrained BERT/T5 weights and a small downstream dataset, and produces per-layer low-rank weight updates ΔW_k parameterized by 3r² compressed factors per layer (vs. 2dr for vanilla LoRA), with r typically 8 and d in the thousands. The subspace factors U_{L,1}, V_{1,1} are constructed once at initialization via a single gradient computation and SVD/QR decomposition, adding only O(d³) overhead equivalent to one GD step.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) proves that under orthogonal initialization, each weight matrix W_l(t) in an L-layer deep factorization admits a block-diagonal decomposition W_l(t) = U_l [W̃_l(t) 0; 0 ρ_l(t)I_m] V_l^T where W̃_l ∈ R^{2r×2r} carries all learning dynamics and ρ_l(t)I_m is a scalar multiple of identity on an invariant (d−2r)-dimensional subspace that remains stationary throughout GD. The compression procedure constructs the invariant subspace factors at initialization by computing the first-layer gradient, finding an orthonormal basis for its nullspace intersection, and propagating via the orthogonal initialization structure, then trains only the 2r×2r compressed weights. For matrix completion, where the observation mask perturbs the initial subspace estimate, the method introduces discrepant learning rates (γη on subspace factors vs. η on compressed weights) to allow gradual subspace correction. For Deep LoRA, this compression is applied per-layer to a 3-layer overparameterized adapter, yielding fewer hyperparameters (no per-layer rank or scale α tuning), less overfitting in few-shot regimes, and robustness to the choice of rank r, while maintaining comparable wall-time to vanilla LoRA.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with 1024 training samples, Deep LoRA improves over vanilla LoRA by an average of +0.028 (±0.002) across all tasks, with the largest gains on CoLA (+0.090) and QNLI (+0.048). In the few-shot NLG setting on E2E (16 samples), Deep LoRA improves BLEU by +0.033 and ROUGE-L by +0.061 over vanilla LoRA, though METEOR is marginally worse (−0.00036). On STS-B with as few as 16 training samples, Deep LoRA consistently outperforms vanilla LoRA across all sample sizes (n ∈ {16, 64, 256}), with the gap most pronounced at n=16. Deep LoRA also finds lower numerical-rank solutions (most layers at rank 0–4 vs. saturated rank 8 for vanilla LoRA) and is robust to overestimating the rank r.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), the standard two-layer low-rank adaptation method. Deep LoRA wins on all eight GLUE tasks with 1024 samples, achieving an overall improvement of +0.028, with the most substantial gains on CoLA (+0.090) and QNLI (+0.048). In the few-shot NLG setting on E2E (16 samples), Deep LoRA outperforms vanilla LoRA on BLEU (+0.033) and all ROUGE variants (ROUGE-2: +0.056, ROUGE-L: +0.061), while being marginally worse on METEOR (−0.00036). On STS-B few-shot fine-tuning, Deep LoRA consistently surpasses vanilla LoRA at every sample size tested (16, 64, 256), with the advantage being largest at n=16. Additionally, Deep LoRA demonstrates superior robustness to the rank hyperparameter r: while vanilla LoRA degrades when r is overestimated, Deep LoRA maintains stable performance, and it adaptively finds lower-rank solutions (numerical rank 0–4 vs. saturated rank 8), providing implicit regularization that prevents overfitting in limited-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by replacing its two-layer low-rank update with a compressed three-layer overparameterized factorization, grounded in a new theoretical result: rigorous proof that gradient descent on deep overparameterized matrix factorizations confines each weight matrix's dynamics to an invariant low-dimensional subspace of dimension 2r (where r is the target rank). This departs from prior implicit-bias work (Arora et al., 2019; Gunasekar et al., 2017) that characterized *what* solution GD finds, by showing *how* it gets there and enabling practical compression. The compression technique also generalizes the deep matrix completion setting, where the mask Ω perturbs the invariant subspace and necessitates slow co-adaptation of the projection factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Deep LoRA offers a practical drop-in improvement over vanilla LoRA for few-shot fine-tuning with minimal parameter overhead (~0.01% more than LoRA), reducing overfitting and eliminating the need to carefully tune rank r and scale α per layer. The theoretical invariant-subspace result provides a principled compression mechanism that could extend to other overparameterized settings, though the current proof is restricted to the linear (matrix factorization) case and the non-linear extension remains conjectural. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to diffusion model fine-tuning, whether second-order methods along the rank-r subspace can further accelerate training, and whether the low-rank bias connects to broader representation-learning phenomena like neural collapse.
