## Problem

Worst-group risk minimization (Group DRO / min-max fairness) has been well-studied in non-private settings, but no formal study existed for this problem under (ε,δ)-differential privacy. The central question is whether one can privately learn a model that approximately minimizes the maximal population risk across p sub-populations while guaranteeing that no individual data point's influence on the output is bounded, and at what rate.

## Input / Output

In the online (stochastic) setting, the algorithm accesses p group distributions through sample oracles, drawing a total of K data points across all groups during training; in the offline setting, it receives a fixed collection of p datasets each of size K/p. The loss is assumed convex, L-Lipschitz, and bounded in [0,B] over a compact parameter space W ⊂ R^d with L2 diameter M. The output is a single parameter vector w_T whose expected excess worst-group population risk (or empirical risk in the offline case) is bounded, with the bound depending on p, K, d, ε, and δ.

## Method

The paper's primary algorithm, Minimax Phased ERM, iteratively solves a sequence of regularized minimax objectives (with quadratic regularization on w and entropy regularization on the group weights λ), doubling the w-regularization each round and adding Gaussian noise calibrated to the solution's sensitivity. The key technical contribution is a new stability-based generalization argument: Δ-uniform argument stability of the regularized minimax solution implies Õ(Δ + 1/√n) generalization error for worst-group risk, circumventing the √Δ bottleneck known for stochastic saddle-point problems. A second framework casts the objective as a two-player zero-sum game, running any DP-OCO algorithm (e.g., DP-FTRL) for the w-player and EXP3 with Laplace-noised loss estimates for the λ-player. For the offline empirical setting, a private multiplicative group reweighting scheme combines Noisy-SGD model updates with multiplicative-weights updates on Laplace-privatized per-group losses, achieving a nearly optimal Õ(p√d/(Kε)) excess empirical risk.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical guarantees for differentially private worst-group risk minimization. (1) The Minimax Phased ERM algorithm (Theorem 2) achieves excess worst-group population risk of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{p/K} + p\sqrt{d}/(K\epsilon))$, which is nearly optimal in the offline setting where each group has $K/p$ samples. (2) A DP-OCO framework (Corollary 8) yields excess risk of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d^{1/2}/(\epsilon K)} + \sqrt{p/(K\epsilon^2)} + \sqrt{p/K})$, which dominates the Phased ERM bound when $p \geq \max\{\sqrt{d}, K/d\}$. (3) For offline worst-group empirical risk minimization (Theorem 10), the Noisy-SGD-MGR algorithm achieves nearly optimal excess risk of $\tilde{O}(p\sqrt{d}/(K\epsilon))$, matching the DP-ERM lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against known lower bounds and prior DP-SSP results. The Phased ERM rate matches the non-private lower bound $\Omega(\sqrt{p/K})$ of Soma et al. (2022) up to logarithmic factors when $d = O(K/p)$, and the full bound $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{p/K} + p\sqrt{d}/(K\epsilon))$ is shown to be nearly optimal via a matching lower bound construction in the offline setting. The DP-OCO framework is more favorable than Phased ERM in the regime $p \geq \max\{\sqrt{d}, K/d\}$ or $\sqrt{K/d^{1/2}} \leq p \leq \sqrt{d}$, particularly for $\epsilon = \Theta(1)$. Both methods circumvent a known bottleneck: directly applying existing DP-SSP results (Yang et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2022; Bassily et al. 2023) would yield suboptimal rates because the $L_2$ Lipschitz constant of the objective scales with $p$. The offline Noisy-SGD-MGR algorithm matches the DP-ERM lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt{d}/(n\epsilon))$ (Bassily et al. 2014) up to log factors, while a private active-group-selection variant (Theorem 14) achieves a suboptimal rate of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{MLBp/(K\epsilon)} + MLp\sqrt{d}/(K\epsilon))$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Phased ERM framework from Feldman et al. (2020) for private stochastic convex optimization into a minimax setting, adding a new stability-based generalization analysis that circumvents the known √Δ bottleneck for argument-stable algorithms in stochastic saddle point problems (Ozdaglar et al., 2022). It also builds on the zero-sum game decomposition of worst-group risk from Soma et al. (2022) and Haghtalab et al. (2022), and on the multiplicative group reweighting scheme from Abernethy et al. (2022) and Diana et al. (2021), by providing private versions of these approaches. The key departure from prior work is the use of uniform argument stability rather than uniform convergence for generalization, which yields near-optimal rates for general convex losses where uniform convergence-based analyses (Mohri et al., 2019; Abernethy et al., 2022) are suboptimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of worst-group risk minimization under differential privacy, filling a clear gap between the well-developed non-private Group DRO literature and the DP optimization literature. The stability-based generalization argument is a reusable technical tool that may apply beyond this specific problem, and the side product of a non-private regularization-based method matching the non-private lower bound (without requiring single-pass structure) is of independent interest. The main open question is closing the gap between the two upper bounds (Phased ERM vs. DP-OCO framework) to establish the optimal rate for worst-group population risk in general, and extending the results to non-convex losses or more complex group structures.
