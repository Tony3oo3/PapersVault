## Problem

RLHF aligns LLMs with human preferences but requires expensive human annotation at scale. While RLAIF (Bai et al., 2022b) proposed using LLM-generated preferences, it was never directly compared against RLHF, leaving open whether AI feedback can serve as a viable, scalable substitute for human feedback in reinforcement learning alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241071/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a text context (Reddit post or conversation history) paired with two candidate responses, which are fed to an off-the-shelf LLM labeler (PaLM 2 family) to produce a soft preference distribution via softmax over the log-probabilities of tokens '1' and '2'. In canonical RLAIF this distribution trains a reward model; in d-RLAIF the LLM instead scores a single generation on a 1–10 scale during RL, with the final reward computed as a probability-weighted sum normalized to [-1, 1]. The end output is an RL-trained policy that generates summaries or dialogue responses, evaluated by human win rates and harmless rates.

## Method

Canonical RLAIF trains a reward model on AI-generated soft preference labels using cross-entropy loss on the RM's softmax scores, then runs REINFORCE with a KL penalty against the SFT policy. To mitigate position bias, each candidate pair is scored twice with reversed order and the distributions are averaged. Chain-of-thought reasoning is elicited via a two-step procedure: the LLM first generates a free-form rationale, which is concatenated back into the prompt before extracting the preference distribution. The key novel contribution, d-RLAIF, eliminates the reward model entirely by directly prompting the LLM to assign a 1–10 quality score to each generated response during RL, computing the reward as the expected value over score-token probabilities. This avoids RM staleness as the policy drifts out of distribution and removes the separate RM training step. The paper further demonstrates that RLAIF improves over SFT even when the AI labeler is the same size as (or the exact same checkpoint as) the policy, constituting strict LLM self-improvement.

## Results

On summarization, RLAIF and RLHF are preferred over the SFT baseline 71% and 73% of the time respectively, with no statistically significant difference between them (50% head-to-head win rate). On helpful dialogue generation, RLAIF and RLHF achieve 63% and 64% win rates over SFT, again with no significant head-to-head difference (52%). On harmless dialogue generation, RLAIF achieves an 88% harmless rate, substantially outperforming RLHF (76%) and SFT (64%). The d-RLAIF variant further improves to 74% win rate over SFT on summarization and 66% on helpful dialogue, demonstrating strict LLM self-improvement when the labeler and policy share the same checkpoint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RLHF (Stiennon et al., 2020; Bai et al., 2022a), RLAIF achieves statistically indistinguishable performance on summarization (50% head-to-head win rate) and helpful dialogue generation (52% head-to-head), confirming that AI-generated preferences are a viable substitute for human labels. However, on harmless dialogue generation, RLAIF clearly outperforms RLHF with an 88% harmless rate versus 76%, a 12-point gap. Both RLAIF and RLHF substantially outperform the SFT baseline across all three tasks (71–73% vs. SFT on summarization, 63–64% on helpful dialogue, 88%/76% vs. 64% on harmless dialogue). The d-RLAIF variant, which bypasses reward model training entirely, further outperforms same-size canonical RLAIF by 6 points (74% vs. 68% win rate over SFT on summarization), suggesting that directly querying the LLM for rewards during RL avoids the reward model staleness problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLAIF concept introduced in Bai et al. (2022b, Constitutional AI) by providing the first direct, controlled comparison of RLAIF versus RLHF across multiple tasks, which the original work left unanswered. It builds on the standard RLHF pipeline (Stiennon et al., 2020; Ouyang et al., 2022) and the helpful/harmless dialogue setup (Bai et al., 2022a), but departs from prior work in two key ways: introducing d-RLAIF, which eliminates the reward model entirely by querying an LLM directly during RL, and demonstrating that the AI labeler can be the same size or even the same checkpoint as the policy being trained. The systematic study of prompting techniques (chain-of-thought, in-context learning, preamble specificity) for AI preference labeling is also novel relative to prior RLAIF work, which used a fixed prompting recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing RLAIF achieves performance on par with RLHF at over 10x lower labeling cost, this paper offers a practical path to scaling alignment beyond the bottleneck of human annotation, with direct implications for making RLHF-style training accessible to smaller labs. The self-improvement result (same-checkpoint labeler still yields gains) opens a potentially important direction for autonomous model improvement, though it is demonstrated only on a single model family (PaLM 2) and small model sizes. Key open questions remain: whether combining human and AI feedback in a curriculum (RLAIF warm-up → RLHF refinement) can exceed either alone, how RLAIF adapts to model-based RL settings, and whether the prompting findings (e.g., in-context learning hurting alignment) generalize beyond PaLM 2. The paper also notes that RM accuracy does not perfectly predict downstream RL effectiveness, suggesting the reward model training step may be less critical than previously assumed.
