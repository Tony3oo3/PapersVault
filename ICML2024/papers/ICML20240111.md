## Problem

Relational DNN properties such as universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) robustness and worst-case hamming distance require reasoning about multiple executions of the same network under a shared perturbation, yet existing verifiers either treat executions independently or only capture input-level dependencies while ignoring cross-execution dependencies at hidden layers. The SOTA relational verifier (Zeng et al., 2023) computes linear approximations independently per execution and adds input-layer constraints, losing precision because hidden-layer activations are not jointly constrained across executions. This paper addresses the gap by developing a scalable method that leverages cross-execution dependencies at all layers during bound refinement.

## Input / Output

RACoon takes a ReLU DNN N, an input specification Φ defining L∞ perturbation regions (‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε) for k executions plus a cross-execution constraint (e.g., a common perturbation δ shared across all k inputs), and an output specification Ψ expressed as a conjunction of linear inequalities over the network outputs. It produces a sound lower bound on worst-case k-UAP accuracy (larger is more precise) or a sound upper bound on worst-case hamming distance (smaller is more precise). The final MILP formulation introduces only O(k × n_l) integer variables—encoding the output specification—rather than O(k × n_r) for a naive encoding, where n_l ≪ n_r is the output dimension versus total ReLU count. The method is sound but incomplete and operates on small-to-medium networks (ConvSmall, ConvBig, IBPSmall, IBPMedium) on MNIST and CIFAR-10.

## Method

RACoon's core mechanism is cross-executional bound refinement: instead of optimizing parametric α-CROWN bounds independently per execution, it jointly refines the α parameters across a subset of executions using a Lagrangian dual formulation. Specifically, it (1) formulates an LP relaxation of the relational property, (2) derives the Lagrangian dual with multipliers λ_i that couple linear approximations from different executions, (3) exploits the L∞ norm structure to obtain a closed-form G(λ) = Σ λ_i·a_i(α_i) − ε·Σ_j|Σ_i λ_i·L_i(α_i)[j]| for the inner minimization over δ, and (4) optimizes G(λ) jointly over all α parameters and λ via projected gradient descent. The absolute-value term in G(λ) is the key structural element that preserves cross-execution dependencies, making the joint optimum provably at least as good as independent refinement (Theorem 4.2). RACoon then feeds the refined bounds into a MILP with O(k × n_l) integer variables solved by Gurobi, and uses a greedy heuristic to select which subsets of executions (up to size k₁ from the top-k₀ most-violated) to refine, avoiding the 2^k − 1 combinatorial explosion.

## Results

On MNIST, RACoon improves worst-case UAP accuracy by up to +16.5% over the SOTA I/O formulation (e.g., 85.5% vs. 69.0% for ConvSmall COLT at ε=0.15) and up to +14.0% over the non-relational verifier. On CIFAR-10, the improvement reaches +22.0% (39.0% vs. 17.0% for IBPSmall IBP at ε=6/255). For worst-case hamming distance on MNIST (k=20), RACoon reduces the bound by up to 8 (e.g., 3.0 vs. 11.0 for IBPMedium IBP at ε=0.30), a 40% reduction. Runtime remains practical: under 8 seconds for MNIST ConvBig (20 executions) and under 12 seconds for CIFAR-10 ConvBig (10 executions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RACoon is compared against two baselines: the non-relational verifier (autoLiRPA, Xu et al. 2020) and the SOTA relational verifier I/O formulation (Zeng et al. 2023). RACoon outperforms both baselines on every single DNN configuration for both relational properties (k-UAP and worst-case hamming distance). Against the I/O formulation, RACoon gains up to +16.5 percentage points in UAP accuracy on MNIST (ConvSmall COLT: 85.5% vs. 69.0%) and up to +22.0 points on CIFAR-10 (IBPSmall IBP: 39.0% vs. 17.0%). The non-relational verifier, which treats each execution independently, is consistently the weakest baseline, with RACoon achieving 10–22 points higher UAP accuracy in the hardest settings. For hamming distance, RACoon reduces the worst-case bound by up to 8 (IBPMedium IBP: 3.0 vs. 11.0), while the I/O formulation only achieves a 1-point reduction over the non-relational verifier in the same setting. RACoon is slower than the I/O formulation (e.g., 5.2s vs. 2.65s for MNIST ConvSmall Standard) but remains well within practical limits (<8s for MNIST, <12s for CIFAR-10 on the largest networks tested).

## Relation to Prior Work

RACoon extends the parametric bound refinement framework of α-CROWN (Xu et al., 2021), which optimizes ReLU bound parameters per execution via differentiable optimization, to a cross-executional setting where parameters from multiple executions are jointly optimized. It builds on the relational verification framework of Zeng et al. (2023) (I/O formulation), which encodes input-level dependencies between executions in a MILP but uses static linear approximations computed independently per execution. The key departure is the Lagrangian dual formulation with a closed-form G(λ) that couples parametric bounds across executions at all hidden layers, enabling joint optimization that exploits cross-executional dependencies the SOTA baseline ignores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Relational properties like UAP robustness and worst-case hamming distance are practically important for safety-critical deployments, yet existing verifiers lose significant precision by treating executions independently. RACoon provides a general, provably correct framework that can serve as a bounding subroutine for branch-and-bound complete verifiers, and its cross-executional refinement alone (without MILP) can already prove properties that non-relational verification cannot. Key limitations include inapplicability to large-scale networks (e.g., ImageNet) and incompleteness; combining RACoon with ReLU-splitting or input-splitting branching strategies for complete relational verification is left as future work, as is extension to properties over different DNNs (local equivalence) and using relational properties as training objectives.
