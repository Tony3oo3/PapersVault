## Problem

Tensor network structure search (TN-SS) requires selecting optimal hyperparameters (ranks, topology, permutation) in a high-dimensional discrete space, and existing sampling-based algorithms are manually crafted heuristics that struggle with the exploration-exploitation tradeoff and local convergence. The paper asks whether large language models can automatically discover novel TN-SS algorithms, replacing the labor-intensive cycle of human experts repeatedly designing new heuristics for different downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242406/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes existing TN-SS algorithms (TNGA, GREEDY, TNLS) as Python code implementing a standardized GenerateSamples interface, along with task-specific training data (e.g., 256×256 grayscale images reshaped to order-8 tensors, or order-4/6 Gaussian process mean tensors) and an LLM (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, or Claude). It outputs novel TN-SS algorithms as executable Python code that, when run, produce optimal tensor network adjacency matrices minimizing a combined complexity-and-reconstruction objective. Generated code must pass a sandbox environment (small-scale data, error checking) before entering formal numerical evaluation.

## Method

tnGPS is an iterative prompting pipeline that mimics a human expert's innovation workflow through five LLM-driven phases: Knowledge Categorization (clustering algorithms by methodological similarity), Idea Dropout (roulette selection with logarithmic probability to sample diverse algorithms), Knowledge Recombination (merging multiple algorithms into new ones), Incremental Innovation (slight modifications to individual algorithms), and Diversity Injection (generating methodologically orthogonal new algorithms without performance constraints). Each phase uses a structured prompt composed of an interface description, in-context algorithm code with scores, and format restrictions. The discovered algorithms are evaluated on local hardware, and their scores feed back into subsequent iterations to guide further refinement. Unlike conventional automated algorithm discovery that searches a handcrafted finite-dimensional space, tnGPS leverages LLMs to explore an infinite-dimensional code space, enabling the discovery of genuinely novel mechanisms such as inverse annealing schedules and Gaussian perturbation mutations.

## Results

On the BSD500 image compression benchmark (test set), the best tnGPS-discovered algorithm Ho-2 achieves a log compression ratio of 1.352 with RSE 0.130, outperforming all four baselines (TNGA: 1.332/0.132, TNLS: 1.335/0.131, GREEDY: 1.331/0.132, TnALE: 1.328/0.132). On the out-of-domain Gaussian process model compression task, Ho-2 is the only algorithm that beats the baseline on MG+random initialization (3.01k vs. 3.36k parameters), while TNGA and TNLS produce structures 4–5× larger and GREEDY/TnALE fail entirely. In the ablation study, the full tnGPS pipeline achieves an objective of 0.1102 versus 0.1558 for the best baseline, a 29% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the in-domain BSD500 test set, Ho-2 achieves the highest log compression ratio (1.352) among all methods, beating TNGA (1.332), TNLS (1.335), GREEDY (1.331), and TnALE (1.328) by 0.02–0.021 in log CR, while maintaining a competitive RSE of 0.130. On the out-of-domain CCPP+random setting, Ho-2 finds a structure with 2.24k parameters, outperforming TNGA (2.36k), TNLS (2.50k), GREEDY (2.60k), and TnALE (2.36k). The most striking result is on MG+random, where Ho-2 achieves 3.01k parameters (beating the 3.36k baseline), while TNGA (12.69k) and TNLS (17.25k) produce structures 4–5× larger, and GREEDY and TnALE fail to converge at all. Under TT initialization, all methods converge to similar parameter counts (2.24k for CCPP, 3.01k for MG), with GREEDY and TnALE requiring the fewest samples (21 and 18 for CCPP respectively). Overall, tnGPS-discovered algorithms consistently match or exceed SOTA baselines, with Ho-2 being the standout performer particularly in challenging random-initialization and out-of-domain scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) sampling-based tensor network structure search (TN-SS) algorithms such as TNGA, TNLS, TnALE, and GREEDY, which all follow a common 'sampling-evaluation' paradigm differing only in their GenerateSamples function; and (2) LLM-driven automatic algorithm discovery (AAD), where prior work (Liu et al., 2023; 2024) used LLMs to evolve algorithms for the traveling salesman problem. The key departure is the explicit modeling of human expert innovation workflows—knowledge categorization, idea dropout, knowledge recombination, incremental innovation, and diversity injection—as a structured prompting pipeline, rather than relying on generic evolutionary operators. The paper positions itself as a 'meta-method' that generates TN-SS algorithms rather than solving TN-SS directly, distinguishing it from both the TN-SS literature and the broader AutoML/AAD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs can discover genuinely novel algorithmic ideas (non-Markovian sampling, inverse annealing, Gaussian perturbation mutation) that outperform hand-crafted heuristics, and that these discovered algorithms generalize to out-of-domain tasks (image compression → Gaussian process compression). However, the evidence is limited to small-scale benchmarks (14 images, two small regression datasets) and a handful of discovered algorithms, and performance is explicitly dependent on the underlying LLM's capabilities. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger search spaces, whether the discovered algorithms' novelty is reproducible across LLM versions, and whether the human-expert-mimicking workflow is truly necessary versus simpler evolutionary prompting strategies.
