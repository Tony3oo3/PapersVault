## Problem

Existing adversarial example (AE) detection methods such as DkNN, kNN, LID, and LNG rely on external reference AEs for training detectors or calibrating thresholds, making them vulnerable to unseen attacks and easily bypassed by adaptive attacks. No prior method leverages the feature-invariance property of self-supervised learning (SSL) models to detect AEs without any prior knowledge of adversarial attacks or reference adversarial samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240724/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BEYOND takes a single RGB image as input and generates 50 augmented neighbors via fixed-seed transformations (horizontal flip, crop, color jitter, grayscale). The target classifier predicts a class label for the input, while the SSL classification head predicts labels for all 50 neighbors and the representation head encodes 51 representations (input + neighbors). The final output is a binary reject/accept decision: the sample is flagged as an AE if either the label consistency count or the representation similarity count falls below a threshold calibrated solely on clean samples at FPR=5%.

## Method

BEYOND employs an off-the-shelf SSL model (e.g., SimSiam) as a shared feature extractor, with a supervised classification head g(·) for label consistency detection and a three-layer FC representation head h(·) for representation similarity detection. The core insight is that SSL models are trained to project an input and its augmentations onto the same submanifold, so clean samples maintain high label agreement and cosine similarity with their 50 augmented neighbors, while adversarial perturbations break this invariance. Two complementary detection mechanisms operate in parallel: label consistency counts how many neighbor predictions (from the SSL head) match the target classifier's prediction, and representation similarity counts how many neighbor representations exceed a cosine similarity threshold relative to the input. The two mechanisms have contradictory optimization directions—label consistency requires larger perturbations while representation similarity requires smaller ones—causing gradient cancellation that makes simultaneous adaptive evasion intractable. A theoretical analysis using first-order Taylor expansions and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality formalizes why the AE-to-neighbor distance in SSL feature space is significantly amplified relative to clean samples.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BEYOND achieves AUC scores of 98.89% (FGSM), 99.28% (PGD), 99.16% (AutoAttack), and 99.27% (Square) under unseen attacks, consistently outperforming all baselines by a large margin. On ImageNet, BEYOND reaches 97.59% AUC (FGSM) and 96.26% (PGD) on unseen attacks, surpassing the previous best (LNG at 96.85% and 89.61% respectively). Under the Orthogonal-PGD adaptive attack at L∞=0.01, BEYOND achieves 88.38% robust accuracy at FPR 5%, compared to the best baseline (DLA) at 62.60%; with ATC, BEYOND reaches 94.50% at L∞=8/255 versus 0.00% for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEYOND dominates all ten baselines on CIFAR-10 unseen attacks, with the largest gaps over DkNN (61.55–59.46%), kNN (61.83–73.39%), LID (71.08–66.18%), and Hu (84.51–95.82%), which all rely on external reference AEs or layer-wise neighborhood comparisons. Against the strongest baseline LNG (98.51% FGSM, 94.71% Square), BEYOND leads by 0.38 and 4.56 AUC points respectively, and the gap widens dramatically on PGD (99.28 vs. 63.14) and AutoAttack (99.16 vs. 58.47). On ImageNet, BEYOND outperforms LNG on unseen FGSM (97.59 vs. 96.85) and PGD (96.26 vs. 89.61), though LNG edges ahead on seen FGSM/PGD (99.53/98.42 vs. 97.59/96.26); however, BEYOND surpasses LNG by 9.41 AUC on seen C&W (95.46 vs. 86.05). Under Orthogonal-PGD adaptive attacks, BEYOND achieves 88.38% RA@FPR5% at L∞=0.01 versus DLA's 62.60%, Trapdoor's 0.00%, SID's 6.90%, and SPAM's 1.20%; at the harder L∞=8/255 setting, BEYOND+ATC reaches 94.50% while all baselines drop to 0.00–8.00%. Against the adaptive purification method Mao, BEYOND with ATC achieves 93.20% robust accuracy versus Mao's 75.09% on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEYOND departs from the established neighborhood-based AE detection paradigm (DkNN, kNN, LID, LNG) by replacing external reference samples with self-generated augmentations of the input, eliminating the need for prior knowledge of adversarial examples. It repurposes the core invariance property of self-supervised learning models—trained to project an input and its augmentations to the same representation submanifold—as a detection signal, which is a novel application of SSL to the AE detection problem. The theoretical analysis connects this to the observation that adversarial perturbations cause large feature-space displacements that augmentations partially undo, formalized through first-order Taylor expansions and Cauchy-Schwarz bounds. The dual-mechanism design (label consistency + representation similarity) is positioned as a defense against adaptive attacks by creating conflicting optimization gradients, a property not exploited in prior neighborhood-based detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEYOND offers a practically attractive plug-and-play solution: it requires no retraining of the target classifier, no reference AE set, and no knowledge of the attack type, while achieving ~99% AUC on CIFAR-10 across all tested attacks including unseen ones. The gradient-cancellation argument for adaptive attack resistance is a meaningful step toward defenses that survive when the attacker knows the detection strategy, a setting where most prior methods (LNG, DkNN, Mao) fail. Limitations include degradation under large perturbations (>16/255), the additional inference cost of an SSL model plus 50 augmentations, and evaluation restricted to image classification. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modalities (text, audio, time-series) and whether the SSL backbone choice or augmentation set can be further optimized for specific deployment constraints.
