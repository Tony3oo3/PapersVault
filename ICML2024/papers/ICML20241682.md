## Problem

Prevailing methods for non-stationary online learning rely on two-layer ensembles maintaining O(log T) base learners to achieve optimal dynamic regret, incurring substantial computational and storage overhead. This paper asks whether near-optimal dynamic regret can be attained with a single-layer model, and introduces the notion of underlying dynamic regret—a special form of universal dynamic regret where the learner observes an unbiased empirical estimation of the ground-truth comparator—as a tractable target that still encompasses key applications such as online label shift.

## Input / Output

The method operates in two stages: an offline initialization phase with a labeled dataset S0 to train a base classifier h0, followed by an online adaptation phase where only unlabeled data S_t arrives each round. The learner constructs an unbiased estimator of the true label distribution via BBSE (combining h0's soft predictions on unlabeled data with the offline confusion matrix), and outputs a single classifier's prediction rather than an ensemble combination. The wavelet detection module determines when to reset the model, and the decision vector is updated via OGD or reweighting within each stationary interval.

## Method

The core contribution is a detection-restart framework that replaces model ensembles with wavelet-based non-stationarity detection. A novel streaming wavelet operator organizes CDJV wavelet coefficients in a binary indexed tree, lazily updating only O(log T) coefficients per round (versus O(T) for traditional matrix computation) and performing implicit padding to handle non-power-of-2 sequence lengths. The soft-threshold operator on wavelet coefficients separates high-frequency sample noise from low-frequency environmental trends, triggering a model restart when the Frobenius norm of thresholded coefficients exceeds a variance-based threshold. Applied to online label shift, this yields Wav-O (OGD update) and Wav-R (reweighting update), both achieving the optimal O(T^{2/3}(P_T^0)^{1/3}) dynamic regret with a single classifier and reducing per-round computational complexity from O(N_0·d·log T) to O(N_0·d + log T).

## Results

On the five benchmark OLS datasets (CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, EuroSAT, Fashion-MNIST, MNIST), Wav-O achieves average errors of 15.52% (CIFAR-10 Linear), 26.11% (CINIC-10 Linear), 7.15% (EuroSAT Linear), 8.34% (Fashion Linear), and 1.09% (MNIST Linear), consistently outperforming the OGD baseline by 0.4–2.0 percentage points. Under Square Shift, Wav-R achieves the best or near-best results on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 7.32% on Fashion, 1.03% on MNIST). In the general OCO setting, Wav-O with k=2 achieves 5.58% error on Square Shift, matching or beating ensemble methods like ATLAS (5.65%) and FLH-FTL (5.64%). The streaming wavelet operator processes ~5× more items per second and uses ~half the storage of the ATLAS ensemble.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wav-O consistently outperforms the OGD baseline (Zinkevich, 2003) across all datasets and shift types, with margins of 0.4–2.0 pp (e.g., 15.52% vs 15.92% on CIFAR-10 Linear; 7.15% vs 9.13% on EuroSAT Linear). Wav-R outperforms the Reweight baseline (Wu et al., 2021) on most datasets (e.g., 15.68% vs 18.25% on CIFAR-10 Linear; 7.32% vs 8.28% on Fashion Square). Against the ensemble-based ATLAS (Bai et al., 2022), Wav-O matches or beats it on 4 of 5 datasets under Linear Shift and 3 of 5 under Square Shift, while using a single model instead of O(log T) base learners. Compared to Effi-Dyn (Zhao et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art two-layer method, Wav-O achieves comparable accuracy (24.03% vs 25.45% on Locomotion) with substantially lower per-round computational cost (O(N₀d + log T) vs O(N₀d log T)). Against FLH-FTL (Baby et al., 2023), Wav-O is competitive on CIFAR-10 (15.52% vs 17.32% Linear) and EuroSAT (7.15% vs 11.35% Linear) but slightly trails on Fashion (8.34% vs 7.84% Linear). LAME (Boudiaf et al., 2022), a test-time adaptation method, is only evaluated on the Locomotion dataset where Wav-O (24.03%) outperforms it (24.92%). Overall, the single-layer wavelet-based methods match or exceed ensemble-based competitors while reducing storage by ~50% and increasing throughput by ~5×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the wavelet-based detection framework from online trend filtering (Baby & Wang, 2019; 2020) into a general online convex optimization setting, replacing the standard two-layer ensemble approach for dynamic regret minimization (Zhang et al., 2018; Cutkosky, 2020) with a single-layer adaptive restart scheme. The key technical departure is the streaming wavelet operator, which uses a binary indexed tree to update wavelet coefficients in O(log T) per round, overcoming the O(T) recomputation bottleneck of prior wavelet methods. For the online label shift application, the paper builds on BBSE (Lipton et al., 2018) for unbiased label distribution estimation and the risk-rewriting technique from Bai et al. (2022), but replaces their O(log T) base-learner ensemble with a single classifier guided by wavelet-based restarts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the ensemble structure long considered necessary for near-optimal dynamic regret can be eliminated for a well-motivated subclass of problems (underlying dynamic regret), yielding a 5× speedup and ~50% storage reduction in practice. However, for general convex functions the achieved rate of Õ(T^{2/3}(P_T^0)^{1/3}) remains suboptimal compared to the ensemble-achievable Õ(√T·P_T^0), and the authors explicitly connect closing this gap to the open problem of reducing dynamic regret to strongly adaptive regret. The paper also raises the fundamental question of whether the O(log T) multi-resolution overhead is inherently necessary for non-stationary online learning, which would be an interesting lower-bound direction.
