## Problem

Blum & Stangl (2019) showed that equal opportunity constraints on a stylized distribution with i.i.d. label noise can recover the Bayes optimal classifier even from extremely biased data, but their proof relied on a tailored iterative case analysis that does not generalize. The gap this paper addresses is whether this recovery phenomenon extends to other noise models (Massart/malicious), other fairness constraints (demographic parity), arbitrary data distributions, arbitrary hypothesis classes, and time-varying bias pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241812/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original data distribution D over (X, A, Y) with binary sensitive attribute A ∈ {0,1} and binary labels Y ∈ {0,1}, a data bias model (e.g., under-representation with survival probabilities β_p, β_n and label flip rate ν), and a fairness constraint (equal opportunity or demographic parity). It produces the optimal fair classifier on the biased distribution D̃ and, crucially, characterizes the exact necessary and sufficient conditions on the bias parameters under which this classifier coincides with the Bayes optimal (or optimal-in-class) classifier on D. The output is structured as group-dependent thresholds t_a applied to the original regression function η(x,a), with the recovery region visualized as a 2D region in (β_p, β_n) space.

## Method

The key insight is that for all studied data bias models, the regression function on the biased distribution η̃(x,a) is a linear fractional transformation of the original η(x,a), and since optimal fair classifiers are characterized by group-dependent thresholds on the regression function (via Lagrange duality), the optimal fair classifier on D̃ reduces to thresholds on the original η(x,a). The proof then shows that under the stated bias parameter conditions, the only Lagrange multiplier λ* that satisfies the fairness constraint yields thresholds t_0, t_1 ∈ (δ, 1−δ), which by Lemma A.5 forces the threshold classifier to coincide with the Bayes optimal classifier. For arbitrary distributions, reject option classifiers (which abstain on inputs with η ∈ (δ, 1−δ)) create an analogous separation, and for arbitrary hypothesis classes, an ε-robustness assumption (invariance under small near-identity linear fractional perturbations) replaces the threshold argument entirely. The technique further composes over time steps for time-varying bias, yielding upper bounds on the time horizon for recovery.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental results tables. The headline results are recovery theorems: Theorem 4.1 shows that under Massart noise (δ < 1/2), the optimal equal opportunity classifier on the biased distribution recovers the Bayes optimal classifier whenever (1−r)(1−2δ) + r((1−δ)β_p(1−2ν) − δβ_n) > 0 and (1−r)(1−2δ) + r((1−δ)β_n − δβ_p(1−2ν)) > 0. Theorem 4.2 strengthens Blum & Stangl (2019) by proving these conditions are both necessary and sufficient. Theorem 6.2 extends recovery to arbitrary hypothesis classes under an ε-robustness assumption, with conditions r((1−ν)β_p − (1−ε)β_n) + ε(1−r) ≥ 0 and r((1+ε)β_n − (1−ν)β_p) + ε(1−r) ≥ 0. Figure 1 (r=0.25, ν=0.05, δ=0.45) and Figure 2 (r=0.2, ν=0.1, ε=0.05) illustrate that recovery is possible even for extreme under-representation (β_p, β_n near 0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical baselines or comparison tables; all results are theoretical. The primary comparison is with Blum & Stangl (2019), whose recovery theorem is shown to be a strict special case of Theorem 4.1 (i.i.d. noise is a special case of Massart noise with δ(x,a)=δ). The paper strengthens their result in three ways: (1) proving the bias-parameter conditions are necessary and sufficient rather than merely sufficient (Theorem 4.2), (2) extending from i.i.d. label noise to general Massart noise, and (3) providing an analog for demographic parity (Theorem 4.3) where Blum & Stangl only showed a single counterexample. The paper further generalizes beyond their stylized distribution to arbitrary distributions via reject option classifiers (Theorem 5.2) and to arbitrary hypothesis classes via an ε-robustness assumption (Theorem 6.2), neither of which is addressed in the prior work. No other competing methods are benchmarked, as the contribution is entirely in the theoretical characterization of when fairness constraints implicitly rectify data bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the recovery result of Blum & Stangl (2019), who showed that equal opportunity constraints on a stylized distribution with i.i.d. label noise can recover the Bayes optimal classifier from extremely biased data. The key technical departure is the observation that all studied data bias models induce a linear fractional transformation on the regression function, which, combined with the threshold-based characterizations of optimal fair classifiers from Menon & Williamson (2018) and Chzhen et al. (2019), yields a unified proof framework. The paper further draws on the reject option classifier literature (Bartlett & Wegkamp, 2008; Cortes et al., 2016) to handle arbitrary distributions, and introduces an ε-robustness assumption to handle arbitrary hypothesis classes where threshold-based arguments no longer apply.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fairness constraints can serve as an implicit bias-correction mechanism without requiring explicit knowledge or estimation of the bias, which is a practically appealing property for deployment settings where bias parameters are unknown. The extension to arbitrary distributions and hypothesis classes, along with the necessary-and-sufficient characterization of recovery conditions, significantly broadens the applicability of the Blum-Stangl result. However, the results remain conditional on the optimal classifier being fair and robust, the bias models are still stylized (under-representation plus label flip), and the time-varying analysis yields only necessary conditions with finite-horizon upper bounds. Open questions include connecting to performative prediction where bias parameters depend on model performance, studying sample complexity implications, and extending to other fairness constraints like predictive parity.
