## Problem

Existing local feature selection methods for high-dimensional, low-sample-size (HDLSS) tabular biomedical data suffer from three key limitations: they neglect globally important features, they are susceptible to the co-adaptation problem (achieving high accuracy with uninformative features), and they lack an inductive bias aligned with the clustering assumption that is well-suited to biomedical data. ProtoGate addresses all three by introducing a global-to-local selection paradigm, a non-parametric predictor that structurally prevents co-adaptation, and prototype-based prediction that encodes the clustering assumption.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single tabular sample x ∈ R^D (with D ≫ N in the HDLSS regime, e.g., 2,000–5,966 features for 62–200 samples) and produces three outputs: a predicted class label, a continuous instance-wise feature mask s_local ∈ [0,1]^D (where values > 0 indicate selected features), and K nearest prototypes serving as prototypical explanations. During training, the prototype base is constructed from the current batch's masked samples; during inference, it uses the full training set's masked samples, making the predictor non-parametric and fixed at test time.

## Method

ProtoGate consists of a trainable gating network S_W and a non-parametric prototype-based predictor F_θ. The gating network performs global-to-local feature selection: L1-regularization on the first layer's weights W^[1] induces a soft global mask (shared across samples), which is then refined by a differentiable L0-regularization (via Gaussian noise relaxation with σ=0.5) to produce instance-wise local masks. The predictor is a differentiable KNN that sorts prototypes by Euclidean distance using NeuralSort during training (for backpropagation) and QuickSort during inference (for speed — the 'hybrid sorting' strategy), then predicts via majority vote over K nearest prototypes. Because the predictor has zero trainable parameters, it cannot co-adapt with the feature selector, and the only optimised parameters are W in the gating network, making the training objective fully differentiable and end-to-end optimisable with standard gradient descent.

## Results

On seven HDLSS biomedical datasets, ProtoGate achieves an average balanced-accuracy rank of 2.00±1.00, consistently placing in the top three across all datasets. It outperforms the best local feature selection method (REAL-X, rank 5.86±3.18) and the best global method (Lasso, rank 5.00±2.89) by a clear margin, while also surpassing the strongest baseline (MLP, rank 3.86±3.34). ProtoGate achieves its highest gains on 'colon' (83.95%), 'meta-pam' (95.96%), and 'toxicity' (92.34%), and also selects fewer features per sample while maintaining shorter inference times than competing local methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoGate outperforms all 15 baselines in average rank (2.00 vs. 3.86 for the best baseline MLP and 5.00 for the best global method Lasso). Among local feature selection methods, ProtoGate surpasses REAL-X (the strongest local competitor) by a margin of 3.86 rank positions on average, with notable gains on 'colon' (+7.20 pp), 'meta-dr' (+0.42 pp), and 'tcga-2y' (+1.88 pp). ProtoGate also beats LSPIN (rank 8.29) and LLSPIN (rank 9.00), which suffer from the co-adaptation problem and fail to maintain consistent accuracy across datasets (e.g., LSPIN drops to 76.92% on 'lung' vs. ProtoGate's 93.44%). Simple baselines like MLP and Lasso, which lack instance-wise feature selection, are also outperformed by ProtoGate on average, confirming that the global-to-local selection strategy provides a meaningful advantage over both global-only and purely local approaches. On the non-HDLSS generalisability datasets (100-plants-texture, cnae-9, mfeat-fourier, vehicle), ProtoGate achieves competitive accuracy against gradient boosting trees, demonstrating that the method is not limited to the HDLSS regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoGate extends the local feature selection line (LSPIN, INVASE, L2X, TabNet) by introducing a 'disjoint in-model selection' paradigm that sits between joint in-model selection (where selector and predictor co-adapt) and disjoint post-hoc selection (REAL-X, which sacrifices in-model explainability). It builds directly on LSPIN's exact Gaussian-noise relaxation for differentiable ℓ₀-regularisation and on NeuralSort for differentiable sorting, but repurposes them in a novel architecture: a trainable gating network (with ℓ₁ global sparsity on the first layer) feeds a non-parametric KNN predictor that has no trainable parameters. The clustering assumption from case-based reasoning and prototype-based learning is encoded as an inductive bias through the prototype-based prediction mechanism, a design choice absent from prior local feature selection methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the co-adaptation problem is more damaging than previously quantified: methods like LSPIN can achieve top classification accuracy while selecting features with rank 6/10 fidelity, undermining the interpretability claims of local feature selection. ProtoGate's non-parametric predictor elegantly eliminates this failure mode while preserving in-model explainability, and the finding that simple baselines (Lasso, MLP) outperform complex local methods on HDLSS data challenges the assumption that architectural complexity is necessary in this regime. Open questions include whether the clustering assumption generalises beyond biomedical data (Syn3 shows it can fail), whether the non-parametric predictor limits expressiveness on tasks with complex decision boundaries, and whether the approach scales to larger sample sizes where the prototype base becomes expensive.
