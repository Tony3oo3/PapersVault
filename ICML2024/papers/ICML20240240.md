## Problem

IPF (Sinkhorn's algorithm) has been widely used to infer dynamic networks from their time-varying marginals and a time-aggregated adjacency matrix, with strong empirical results in mobility and transportation settings. However, no generative model existed under which IPF is a principled maximum likelihood estimator, no statistical error bounds were available for its parameter estimates, and there was no principled algorithm to handle IPF's frequent non-convergence on sparse network data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a time-aggregated adjacency matrix X̄ (the sum of all time-step adjacency matrices over a period), along with time-varying row marginals p^(t) and column marginals q^(t) for each target time step t. It outputs the inferred adjacency matrix X̂^(t) = D⁰X̄D¹ and the biproportional scaling factors (u, v), where the output is constrained to preserve the zero pattern of X̄—only entries where X̄_ij > 0 can be nonzero. Zero marginals (common in nighttime data) are handled by a modified IPF that sets corresponding scaling factors to zero, effectively reducing the problem to a submatrix.

## Method

The paper introduces the biproportional Poisson model, where each entry X_ij^(t) is independently drawn from Poisson(e^{u_i} X̄_ij e^{-v_j}) when X̄_ij > 0, and proves that the MLEs of this model are exactly recovered by IPF because the log-likelihood equals the negative of the dual potential function g(u,v) that IPF minimizes. This connection yields structure-dependent error bounds on the MLEs quantified by the Fiedler eigenvalue of the bipartite graph induced by X̄, and a high-probability guarantee that finite MLEs exist when the Hessian at the true parameters has a sufficiently large second-smallest eigenvalue. To address IPF non-convergence on sparse data, the paper introduces ConvIPF, a polynomial-time algorithm that iteratively identifies blocking row sets via max-flow and minimally adds edges (either minimizing the number of new edges or the change in the largest eigenvalue λ₁) to unblock them, guaranteeing convergence with far fewer structural changes than the standard practice of replacing all zeros with ε.

## Results

On CitiBike ground-truth hourly networks, IPF achieves a 78% improvement in cosine similarity over the doubly constrained gravity model (even with only month-aggregated input), a 214% improvement over the ablation that removes the time-aggregated network X̄, and ~31% improvement over ablations removing either row or column marginals. On SafeGraph mobility data, ConvIPF resolves IPF non-convergence by adding only 2 edges (vs. 10,012,193 for the standard ε-replacement fix) and changes the spectral radius λ₁ by 5.60×10⁻⁹ (vs. 30.04 for the standard fix). Under correct model specification on synthetic data, the cosine similarity between the true network and IPF's estimate is 0.911, degrading gracefully to 0.855 (exponential) and 0.843 (negative binomial) under misspecification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IPF against two main baselines. Against the doubly constrained gravity model (Navick & Furth, 1994), which uses a distance matrix instead of the time-aggregated network, IPF wins decisively with a 78% improvement in cosine similarity to ground-truth CitiBike hourly networks, demonstrating that the time-aggregated network provides substantial information beyond geographical distance assumptions. Against the standard IPF non-convergence remedy of replacing all zeros with a small ε (Lomax & Norman, 2015), the proposed ConvIPF algorithm wins by orders of magnitude: it adds only 2 new edges versus 10,012,193, and changes the epidemic-relevant spectral radius λ₁ by 5.60×10⁻⁹ versus 30.04, while still guaranteeing convergence. The paper also includes ablation baselines (removing X̄, p^(t), or q^(t)), all of which IPF outperforms, confirming that all three inputs contribute meaningfully to inference quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the missing statistical foundation for applying IPF (Sinkhorn's algorithm) to dynamic network inference, a problem where prior work (Liang et al. 2006; McCord et al. 2010; Chang et al. 2021a) used IPF empirically without formal justification. The key intellectual lineage runs from the KL-divergence interpretation of IPF (Bregman 1967; Ireland & Kullback 1968) through Qu et al. (2023), who connected IPF to choice-model MLEs, to the present paper's identification of a biproportional Poisson generative model whose MLEs are exactly the IPF solution. The paper also positions itself relative to collective graphical models (Sheldon & Dietterich 2011) and entropy-regularized optimal transport (Cuturi 2013), distinguishing its setting by the availability of a time-aggregated network and the focus on disaggregation over time rather than over populations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying the biproportional Poisson model as the canonical generative process for which IPF is the MLE, the paper makes previously implicit assumptions (biproportional form, stationarity, no row-column interactions) explicit and testable, and enables structure-dependent error bounds via the Fiedler eigenvalue. ConvIPF addresses a critical practical gap—IPF non-convergence on sparse real-world data—by adding minimally (e.g., 2 edges vs. 10 million) to guarantee convergence, with direct implications for epidemic modeling and transportation planning. Open questions include how IPF's inference quality varies with temporal dynamics (periodicity), whether variants of IPF can outperform the standard procedure, and whether the biproportional Poisson model extends naturally to richer interaction structures or non-Poisson count distributions.
