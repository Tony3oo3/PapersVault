## Problem

Existing offline safe RL methods rely on Markovian cost functions or cost-to-go conditioning, which cannot express complex temporal and logical constraints such as 'if velocity exceeds a threshold, slow down within the next 5 steps.' Constrained optimization baselines fail when the relabeled cost becomes non-Markovian, and no prior work incorporates formal temporal logic specifications into the offline safe RL setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240676/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes five token streams per timestep: prefix STL robustness values (computed online from the observed trajectory prefix), suffix STL robustness values (set as a fixed target rather than autoregressively updated, since robustness values lack the additive property of return-to-go), return-to-go, state, and action sequences. The output is a Gaussian-distributed action at each step. A key design choice is that the suffix target is specified per-timestep in advance because the recursive min/max structure of STL semantics prevents simple autoregressive updates like C_{t+1} = C_t - c_t.

## Method

SDT extends Decision Transformer by inserting two new token types—prefix and suffix STL robustness values—into the causal transformer's input sequence, enabling the policy to condition on both achieved past safety and desired future safety. The prefix (ρ_pre = ρ(τ_{1:t}, 1, φ)) and suffix (ρ_suf = ρ(τ_{t:T}, 1, φ)) provide complementary information that addresses the sparsity problem inherent in STL robustness values, where a single critical state can dominate the entire trajectory's satisfaction. Training is purely supervised: the objective maximizes the log-likelihood of dataset actions under a Gaussian policy plus an entropy regularizer, with no value function or constrained optimization. At evaluation, the suffix is set as a configurable target (enabling alignment to different safety levels without retraining), while the prefix is computed online from observed states and the return-to-go is updated autoregressively from received rewards.

## Results

On the DSRL Bullet-Safety-Gym benchmarks, SDT achieves the highest STL specification satisfaction rate across all three environment groups: 0.97 (Run), 0.86 (Circle), and 0.76 (Reach), while maintaining competitive normalized rewards (0.96, 0.89, 0.75 respectively). SDT also attains the lowest cumulative relabeled cost in the Run environments (0.33) and Circle environments (0.94), substantially outperforming constrained-optimization baselines whose costs range from 5.7 to 60.3. The offline dataset satisfaction rates are only 20.0%, 8.52%, and 3.47% for Run, Circle, and Reach respectively, indicating that SDT generalizes well beyond the quality of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDT outperforms all six baseline categories on the primary safety metric (STL satisfaction rate) across every environment group. Among conditioned RL methods, CDT is the closest competitor on Run (0.92 rate, 0.32 cost) but drops sharply on Circle (0.49 rate vs. SDT's 0.86) because its cost-to-go conditioning cannot capture the temporal structure of the specification; RvS-Rρ, which also uses prefix/suffix tokens but with an MLP policy, achieves 0.79/0.80/0.65 rates, consistently below SDT's 0.97/0.86/0.76, confirming the benefit of the transformer's sequential modeling. All four constrained-optimization baselines (BCQ-Lag, BEAR-Lag, CPQ, COptiDICE) fail to meet the safety threshold set by BC-safe in most environments, with satisfaction rates as low as 0.00–0.41 and costs 10–60× higher than SDT, because their value-function-based formulations assume Markovian costs that are violated by the temporal STL constraints. On the Reach environment, where no cost function can be defined, SDT is the only method (besides RvS-Rρ and BC-safe) that can be evaluated, and it achieves a 0.76 satisfaction rate versus BC-safe's 0.34, demonstrating the advantage of using robustness values directly rather than requiring a scalar cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) and the reward-conditioned supervised learning (RCSL) framework to offline safe RL by replacing the standard cost-to-go conditioning with Signal Temporal Logic (STL) robustness values. Prior offline safe RL methods (CDT, SaFormer) condition on Markovian cost-to-go, which cannot capture non-Markovian temporal constraints like 'if velocity exceeds threshold, slow down within 5 steps.' The paper also connects to the broader STL-in-RL literature (Balakrishnan & Deshmukh 2019; Bozkurt et al. 2020), but those are online methods; SDT is the first to bring STL into the offline setting. The key conceptual move is framing prefix and suffix robustness values as complementary forms of hindsight information matching (Furuta et al., 2022), where the suffix encodes desired future compliance and the prefix encodes achieved past compliance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a path toward specifying complex, non-Markovian safety requirements in offline safe RL without hand-crafting cost functions, which is a practical bottleneck in domains like autonomous driving and robotics. The alignment property—adapting to different satisfaction levels at inference time without retraining—mirrors the appeal of return-conditioned RL and could simplify deployment. Limitations include evaluation only on relatively simple Bullet-Safety-Gym environments with short-horizon temporal operators (G, F, U over 5 steps), and the method currently handles only safety specifications rather than combining safety and performance objectives in a single STL formula. Open questions include scaling to longer-horizon or nested specifications, handling stochastic environments where RCSL methods are known to struggle, and whether the prefix/suffix design generalizes to other formal logics (e.g., LTL).
