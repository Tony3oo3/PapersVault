## Problem

Existing offline RL methods for confounded POMDPs are either model-free (requiring policy-dependent confidence regions that restrict the policy class) or model-based but limited to tabular settings. No model-based offline RL algorithm exists for confounded POMDPs with continuous state and observation spaces under the milder partial coverage assumption, leaving a gap between the restrictive full-coverage model-based methods and the policy-class-limited model-free approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240751/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N i.i.d. offline trajectories of the form (o_t, a_t, r_t)_{t=1:T} generated by a behavior policy that may depend on unobserved states, along with pre-collected baseline covariates O_0 that carry information about the initial hidden state. State and observation spaces are continuous while the action space is finite, necessitating function approximation. The output is a history-dependent policy π̂ mapping (O_t, H_{t-1}) → Δ(A), together with estimated reward-emission and dynamic-emission bridge functions in tensor-product RKHS spaces, and a conservative (pessimistic) policy value estimate for any candidate policy.

## Method

The core contribution is a model-based identification of policy value via two sets of bridge functions—reward-emission b_R and dynamic-emission b_D—that solve conditional moment restrictions E[b(W,y)|X] = p(y|X) for all y, a new type of problem distinct from standard conditional moment restrictions. A two-stage nonparametric estimation procedure in RKHS is used: Stage 1 learns conditional mean embeddings of p(W|X) and conditional densities p(y|X) via kernel ridge regression; Stage 2 estimates bridge functions by empirical risk minimization in the tensor-product RKHS H_W ⊗ H_Y, yielding closed-form solutions. Confidence regions are constructed around the estimated bridge functions using the empirical risk functional, and critically, these regions are policy-independent (unlike model-free counterparts), enabling an unrestricted policy class. The learned policy is obtained by maximizing the most pessimistic policy value (minimum over the confidence regions) within the policy class, extending the pessimism principle to the model-based confounded POMDP setting. The resulting suboptimality bound depends polynomially on horizon T and sample size N, with a rate of N_2^{-γ/(4γ+4)} governed by the RKHS eigenvalue decay rate γ.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or datasets. The main result (Theorem 4.2) establishes a finite-sample upper bound on the suboptimality of the learned policy: SubOpt(π̂) ≲ (Σ_{t=1}^{T} (√M_R + (T−t)√M_D) C_t^{π*}) √(log(T/δ)) N_2^{−γ/(4γ+4)}, where γ is the eigenvalue decay rate of the RKHS kernels, C_t^{π*} are concentrability coefficients of the optimal policy, and M_R, M_D are uniform bounds on the bridge function classes. The bound depends polynomially on the horizon T and decays at rate N_2^{−γ/(4γ+4)} in the sample size, requiring only partial coverage (finite concentrability of π* rather than all policies).

## Baselines & Comparisons

No empirical baselines are compared; the paper provides theoretical comparisons. Against Lu et al. (2022), the first model-free pessimistic method for confounded POMDPs, the proposed model-based method removes the √log(|Π|) term from the suboptimality bound, allowing an unrestricted policy class rather than requiring the optimal policy to lie in a pre-specified finite class. Against Hong et al. (2023), the first policy gradient method for confounded POMDPs, this work relaxes the full coverage assumption (requiring coverage of all policies) to partial coverage (requiring coverage only of the optimal policy). Compared to model-free OPE methods (Bennett & Kallus 2021; Shi et al. 2022; Miao et al. 2022), the model-based identification extracts reward and transition models independently of the policy, and as a by-product identifies the full marginal reward distribution p^π(r_t), enabling applications to risk-sensitive and multi-objective RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage kernel estimation framework from Singh et al. (2019) and Mastouri et al. (2021) to a new conditional moment restriction problem where the response is a conditional density rather than a scalar, and applies it to identify reward and transition models in confounded POMDPs. It departs from the model-free line of work (Shi et al. 2022, Bennett & Kallus 2021, Miao et al. 2022) that solves Bellman-type backward moment equations to directly identify policy values, instead extracting policy-independent reward and dynamic models via bridge functions. The pessimism principle and confidence region construction are adapted from Lu et al. (2022), but the key structural difference is that the resulting confidence regions are policy-independent, unlike their model-free counterparts which depend on the policy being evaluated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy-independence of the confidence regions eliminates the √log(|Π|) term present in model-free bounds, allowing an unrestricted policy class—a meaningful advantage when the global optimum lies outside a pre-specified policy family. The model-based identification also yields the marginal reward distribution as a by-product, opening the door to risk-sensitive and multi-objective extensions. However, the paper provides no empirical evaluation, assumes exact realizability of bridge functions in the RKHS, and the sequential integration over bridge functions may be computationally expensive for long horizons. Relaxing the realizability assumption to accommodate approximation error and designing practical algorithms remain important open questions.
