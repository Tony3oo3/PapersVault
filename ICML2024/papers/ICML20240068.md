## Problem

Deep learning models frequently rely on spurious correlations in training data, producing brittle predictions on subgroups where those correlations break down. Existing mitigation methods (GDRO, DFR, AFR, etc.) require explicit group annotations to partition data and rebalance training, but such annotations are expensive, often unavailable, and may not be well-defined in real-world settings. This paper asks whether unsupervised object-centric representation learning can discover data-driven proxies for subgroups without any human-labeled group information.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces two types of output: (1) a set of discrete concept assignments per image, where each image is mapped to multiple concepts from a learned codebook of K vectors (e.g., K=8 for most datasets), and (2) a class label from a separately trained robust classifier. Unlike subgroup-based methods that assign each sample to exactly one group, CoBalT associates each image with a set of concepts, reflecting that a single image can contain multiple semantic elements (e.g., a bird AND a water background). The concept dictionary is learned entirely unsupervised and is not constrained to match ground-truth subgroup boundaries.

## Method

CoBalT follows a two-stage procedure. In Stage 1, a DINO-style teacher-student network with slot attention (following Wen et al., 2022) decomposes each image into N spatially-grounded slot representations, which are then discretized via vector quantization into a learned concept dictionary of K entries. The teacher branch uses hard argmax assignment to the dictionary while the student uses soft softmax assignment, with a cross-entropy distillation loss (L_vq) ensuring consistency; the dictionary itself is updated by exponential moving average of teacher slot representations. In Stage 2, a separate classifier (architecture-agnostic, typically ResNet-50) is trained using concept-aware importance sampling: within each concept cluster, samples are drawn with probability inversely proportional to their class frequency raised to a power λ, ensuring rare concepts are oversampled and classes are balanced within each concept. This sampling strategy contrasts between samples from the same concept but different classes, rather than between groups within the same class as in prior work.

## Results

On Waterbirds (no group annotation), CoBalT_ig achieves 89.0% worst-group accuracy, outperforming ULA (86.1%), XRM (86.1%), and MaskTune (86.4%) by approximately 3%. On Urban Cars with multiple spurious correlations, CoBalT_ig reaches 80.0% worst-group accuracy, surpassing SPARE (76.9%) by 3%. On the ImageNet-9 Background Challenge, CoBalT_ig improves over MaskTune by 1.1% (Mixed-same), 1.5% (Mixed-rand), and 1.9% (Only-FG) while maintaining 97.9% on the Original test set. On CelebA without validation group labels, CoBalT_ig achieves 89.2% worst-group accuracy versus 78.0% for MaskTune and 47.7% for ERM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the no-group-annotation setting (the paper's primary contribution), CoBalT_ig outperforms all group-agnostic baselines on Waterbirds worst-group accuracy: 89.0% vs. ULA (86.1%), XRM (86.1%), and MaskTune (86.4%), a ~3% margin. On CelebA without validation group labels (Table 4), CoBalT_ig (89.2%) substantially exceeds MaskTune (78.0%) and all group-inference methods (LfF 24.4%, JTT 40.6%, CVaR DRO 36.1%), while CoBalT_avg (81.1%) still beats MaskTune by 3.1%. On Urban Cars with two simultaneous spurious correlations, CoBalT_ig (80.0%) surpasses SPARE (76.9%) by 3% and GDRO (75.2%) by 4.8%, where most baselines exhibit 'whack-a-mole' degradation. On ImageNet-9 Background Challenge, CoBalT_ig improves over MaskTune by 1.1–1.9% across all shifted test sets while matching ERM on the Original set (97.9%). On CMNIST, CoBalT's worst-group accuracy (73.5–74.5%) trails ULA (75.1%) slightly, which the authors attribute to limited spatial separability of color-based concepts. Overall, CoBalT matches or exceeds group-annotated methods (e.g., GDRO 89.9% on Waterbirds) while requiring no human group labels at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: object-centric representation learning (slot attention, Wen et al. 2022, Locatello et al. 2020) and robust classification under spurious correlations (GDRO, DFR, SPARE, XRM). Rather than directly inferring subgroups as prior group-inference methods do (GEORGE, JTT, SPARE), CoBalT discovers general-purpose discrete concepts via vector quantization of slot representations, then uses concept occurrence statistics for importance-weighted sampling. The teacher-student architecture and EMA updates follow DINO (Caron et al., 2021), while the vector-quantized concept dictionary draws on VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al., 2017). The key departure from prior spurious-correlation work is that concepts are dataset-independent abstractions (e.g., 'water background', 'blue bird') rather than task-specific subgroup labels, and the sampling strategy contrasts across classes within a concept rather than across groups within a class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for human-annotated group labels, which is a major bottleneck in real-world deployment of robust classifiers. The method's ability to handle multiple simultaneous spurious correlations (Urban Cars 'whack-a-mole' scenario) and attribute generalization (ImageNet-9 background challenge) without group annotations is a meaningful step toward scalable robustness. However, the reliance on spatially separable concepts limits effectiveness on datasets where spurious attributes are not spatially localized (CMNIST, CelebA), and the 1–3% gains over strong baselines, while consistent, do not constitute a paradigm shift. Open questions include extending the approach to non-spatially-separable concepts, NLP/multimodal settings, and concept-aware data augmentation.
