## Problem

Existing conformal methods for multi-series trajectory forecasting either rely on Bonferroni corrections that become overly conservative for long horizons, or use fixed-width calibration that cannot adapt to trajectories with varying intrinsic unpredictability (heteroscedasticity). This paper addresses the gap of constructing prediction bands that simultaneously guarantee finite-sample marginal coverage of the entire trajectory while automatically adjusting their width to the difficulty of each new trajectory, without requiring pre-specified difficulty labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LSTM forecaster, a set of exchangeable calibration trajectories, and the initial position of a new test trajectory, operating in an online one-step-ahead fashion where each prediction at time t is made after observing Y_{t-1}. It outputs a sequence of per-step prediction intervals that together form a simultaneous band covering the full trajectory with probability at least 1−α. The bands are constructed by expanding ACI-generated intervals by a conformal margin (additive or multiplicative), and the ACI component adapts its width in real time as the trajectory unfolds, so the output band is not a fixed shape but evolves with observed data.

## Method

CAFHT combines Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) with split-conformal calibration: ACI generates trajectory-adaptive prediction bands that narrow or widen based on recent coverage performance, while a conformal quantile computed from calibration trajectories provides a finite-sample margin ensuring simultaneous marginal coverage. The key conformity score is the maximum margin by which the ACI band must be expanded (in both directions) to cover the entire calibration trajectory, and the final band at each step is the ACI interval plus/minus the empirical quantile of these scores. A data-driven grid search over the ACI learning rate γ is performed on a held-out calibration subset to select the most informative (narrowest average width) bands without invalidating the exchangeability guarantee. The method extends to multi-dimensional trajectories via an infinity-norm reduction and supports multiplicative scores that scale the conformal margin proportionally to the ACI band width, making it more natural for trajectories with varying noise levels.

## Results

On synthetic heterogeneous AR trajectories (dynamic noise, 10% hard samples), CAFHT achieves the narrowest average prediction band width (0.163 at n=2000, 0.151 at n=10000) while maintaining 90% simultaneous marginal coverage. Critically, CAFHT achieves 65.6% conditional coverage on hard-to-predict trajectories at n=2000, compared to only 6.0% for NCTP and 99.5% for CFRNN (whose bands saturate at the maximum width of 2.0, rendering them uninformative). On ORCA pedestrian trajectories (2D, T=20), CAFHT similarly produces the most informative bands with the lowest average width and highest conditional coverage among the three methods, all achieving the guaranteed 90% marginal coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAFHT clearly outperforms NCTP (Yu et al., 2023) on both key metrics: it achieves a substantially narrower average band width (0.163 vs. 0.308 at n=2000) and a dramatically higher conditional coverage on hard trajectories (65.6% vs. 6.0%), because NCTP's fixed normalization cannot adapt to per-trajectory heteroscedasticity. Against CFRNN (Stankeviciute et al., 2021), CAFHT trades a lower conditional-hard coverage (65.6% vs. 99.5%) for a far more informative band width (0.163 vs. 2.000, the maximum possible after normalization to [−1,1]), making CFRNN's bands practically useless for motion planning. The gap between CAFHT and NCTP widens as the prediction horizon T increases (Figure 4), where CFRNN's Bonferroni correction becomes increasingly conservative while CAFHT maintains narrow, adaptive bands. On ORCA pedestrian trajectories, the same pattern holds: CAFHT produces the narrowest bands with the highest conditional coverage, while CFRNN bands remain saturated at maximum width. CopulaCPTS (Sun & Yu, 2023), tested in the appendix, performs comparably to NCTP and is similarly outperformed by CAFHT on conditional coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAFHT fuses two previously separate lines of conformal prediction: the online Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) of Gibbs & Candès (2021), designed for single-series forecasting with asymptotic average coverage, and the split-conformal calibration framework of Romano et al. (2019), which provides finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability. The key conceptual departure is from prior multi-series trajectory forecasting methods (CFRNN of Stankeviciute et al., 2021; NCTP of Yu et al., 2023), which either rely on conservative Bonferroni corrections or use fixed normalization constants that cannot adapt to per-trajectory unpredictability. By wrapping ACI's adaptive bands inside a split-conformal calibration step, the paper inherits ACI's heteroscedasticity-awareness while recovering the finite-sample simultaneous marginal coverage guarantee that ACI alone cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a concrete and practically important gap: in motion planning (autonomous driving, pedestrian prediction), marginal coverage guarantees can be misleadingly satisfied by covering easy trajectories well while failing on hard ones, as the paper demonstrates with NCTP achieving only ~6% conditional coverage on hard trajectories. Open questions include whether the method can achieve asymptotically optimal band widths, whether stronger conditional guarantees (beyond the approximate ones observed empirically) are attainable, and how to reduce the algorithmic randomness introduced by the data-splitting step for hyperparameter tuning. The paper also notes that real-world deployment in motion planning remains to be demonstrated.
