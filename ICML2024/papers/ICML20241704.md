## Problem

Existing causal inference algorithms require access to the full joint distribution, which is intractable for high-dimensional variables like images, while existing deep causal generative models either assume causal sufficiency (no latent confounders), are restricted to specific graph topologies, or only handle low-dimensional variables. Moreover, none of these methods can incorporate large pre-trained generative models without retraining their weights, making them impractical for leveraging state-of-the-art image generators. The paper asks: can we train deep causal generative models with latent confounders in a modular fashion that both improves convergence and allows plug-in of pre-trained components?

## Input / Output

The method requires a fully specified ADMG (with bidirected edges encoding semi-Markovian latent confounders) as prior knowledge, an observational dataset D ~ P(V), and optionally pre-trained conditional generative models for individual variables. The output is a trained DCM (a collection of neural networks arranged per the causal graph) that implicitly models the joint distribution and can generate samples from any identifiable interventional distribution P_x(Y) via forward propagation with intervened variable values manually set. The semi-Markovian constraint limits each latent confounder to affecting exactly two observed variables, and the data distribution must be strictly positive.

## Method

Modular-DCM decomposes training into two phases: (1) constructing an H-graph that arranges c-components into h-nodes with a valid partial training order, where an edge H_s → H_t exists when do-calculus rule-2 fails on the parents of H_t within H_s; and (2) training each h-node sequentially via adversarial training (WGAN-GP), freezing previously trained modules. The key insight is that Tian's factorization decomposes P(V) into c-factors P_pa(C_j)(C_j), and for each h-node H_k, the algorithm finds the smallest ancestor set A_k satisfying the modularity condition (do-calculus rule-2 applies), so that the observational conditional P(H_k, A_k | Pa(H_k, A_k)) serves as a valid proxy for the interventional c-factor. This allows training each module against real observational data while only updating the current h-node's parameters, reducing the loss complexity at each step and enabling pre-trained generators to be frozen and reused without weight corruption.

## Results

On the Colored-MNIST frontdoor graph, Modular-DCM achieves TVD=0.08 for P(D,A) and TVD=0.13 for P(A|do(D=0)) at epoch 300, substantially outperforming NCM (TVD=0.17 and 0.43 respectively) and the non-modular ablation DCM-Rep (TVD=0.16 and 0.36). For image quality, Modular-DCM with a pre-trained generator achieves FID=27.20 versus NCM's 59.59 and DCM-Rep's 80.65. On the diamond graph with two image variables, Modular-DCM achieves FID(I₁)=32.88 and FID(I₂)=11.80 compared to NCM's 80.54 and 65.17. On CelebA-HQ invariant prediction, the Augmented classifier (trained with Modular-DCM-generated interventional data) reaches 0.901 accuracy on the Sex=0, Eyeglass=1 sub-population, versus 0.810 for the standard TrainDomain classifier and 0.600 for IRM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is NCM (Xia et al., 2023), which trains all mechanisms jointly with a single loss. Modular-DCM decisively outperforms NCM on causal inference accuracy: TVD for P(A|do(D=0)) drops from 0.43 to 0.13 (a 3.3× improvement), and FID for generated images drops from 59.59 to 27.20. NCM also fails to produce intervention-consistent images (classified as [0.19, 0.81] instead of the true [0.5, 0.5] uniform), while Modular-DCM achieves 99% accuracy in matching the intervention. The non-modular ablation DCM-Rep confirms that modularization itself (not just the architecture) drives the gains, as it converges slower (TVD=0.36 vs 0.13) and produces lower-quality images (FID=80.65 vs 27.20). On CelebA-HQ, the Augmented classifier leveraging Modular-DCM interventional samples outperforms IRM (Arjovsky et al., 2019) by a wide margin on the target sub-population (0.901 vs 0.600 for Sex=0, Eyeglass=1), demonstrating that causal interventional data generation is more effective than environment-based invariance regularization for this domain-shift setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep causal generative model (DCM) framework initiated by Kocaoglu et al. (2018, CausalGAN) and extended to semi-Markovian models with latent confounders by Xia et al. (2021, 2023). The key departure is the introduction of modular training: prior methods train all mechanism networks jointly against the full joint distribution, which is empirically difficult for high-dimensional variables and incompatible with pre-trained models. By leveraging Tian's factorization into c-factors and a novel H-graph structure that encodes a valid partial training order, Modular-DCM identifies which sub-networks can be trained independently and which must be trained together, while preserving the identifiability guarantees of the underlying SCM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to plug in large pre-trained generative models (e.g., StyleGAN-based InterFaceGAN) into a causal inference pipeline without retraining them is practically significant, since such models require industrial-scale compute to train from scratch. The modular approach also reduces training complexity by minimizing a less complex loss at each phase, which the paper demonstrates leads to faster convergence and better image quality. Key limitations include the assumption of a fully known causal graph, the semi-Markovian restriction (each confounder affects exactly two variables), and the lack of conditional sampling support. Open questions include extending to non-Markovian models, learning partial causal structure, and applying the framework in federated or continual learning settings.
