## Problem

Empirical work has consistently shown that large batch sizes (high subsampling rates) improve DP-SGD performance, yet theoretical explanations for this benefit were heuristic at best. The central unresolved question is how the required noise level σ scales with the subsampling rate q under tight privacy accounting, which determines whether the effective injected noise variance σ²/q² increases or decreases with q.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241706/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on the Poisson subsampled Gaussian mechanism parameterized by subsampling rate q, noise standard deviation σ, number of iterations T, and privacy targets (ε, δ). The primary output is the effective noise variance σ(q,T)²/q² and its decomposition into a subsampling-induced component (decreasing in q) and a noise-induced component. Numerical verification uses Google's PLD accountant to compute σ(q,T) for δ=10⁻⁵ across grids of ε, q, and T values.

## Method

The paper decomposes total gradient variance in DP-SGD into subsampling-induced variance (proven decreasing in q) and effective DP noise variance σ²/q², then focuses on how σ(q,T) scales with q. The main theorem proves that as T→∞, the Poisson subsampled Gaussian mechanism is asymptotically equivalent (in total variation) to a standard Gaussian mechanism with noise σ/q, establishing the linear relationship σ(q,T) ≈ q·σ(1,T) and hence invariance of the effective noise variance to q. The proof proceeds by showing the KL divergence between the subsampled and Gaussian mechanisms is O(1/T²) per iteration via a fourth-order Taylor expansion of the log-likelihood ratio, combined with Pinsker's inequality. For the single-iteration case (T=1), the paper derives an explicit formula for σ'(q) in terms of erf functions and identifies a sufficient condition (a < b) guaranteeing σ(q)/q is decreasing, which is verified numerically across a broad grid of practical (ε, q, σ) values.

## Results

The paper proves that in the limit of infinite iterations (T→∞), the noise standard deviation σ(q,T) scales linearly with the subsampling rate q, i.e., σ(q,T)/(q·σ(1,T)) → 1 (Corollary 5.4). This means the effective DP noise variance σ²/q² is invariant to q, while the subsampling-induced variance (1−q)/q · Σgᵢ² strictly decreases with q, so the total gradient variance always decreases with larger batch sizes. For the single-iteration case (T=1), Theorem 6.2 shows σ_eff is monotonically decreasing in q under the condition a < b, and Conjecture 6.3 (verified numerically for δ=10⁻⁵, ε ∈ [4δ, 4.0]) confirms this condition holds across a broad practical parameter range. Empirically (Figure 1), σ_eff converges to σ(1,T) as T grows, with faster convergence for smaller ε values, and σ_eff stays above σ(1,T) for all q < 1, indicating even larger variance reductions from large batches outside the asymptotic regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without direct method-to-method benchmark comparisons. The paper's findings provide a rigorous justification for the linear σ ∝ q rule-of-thumb previously suggested heuristically by Li et al. (2021) and Sander et al. (2023) to explain empirical observations from McMahan et al. (2018), De et al. (2022), and Mehta et al. (2023) that large batch sizes outperform heavy subsampling in DP-SGD. The paper also contrasts with Rényi DP-based analyses of the subsampled Gaussian mechanism (Abadi et al. 2016, Bun et al. 2018, Mironov et al. 2019), noting that those works do not provide tight privacy bounds and therefore do not imply the linear relationship holds asymptotically under tight accounting. The key contribution is showing that subsampling amplification's benefit is purely computational (saving compute by processing fewer samples per step) rather than a source of additional utility, since the effective noise level does not decrease with smaller q in the asymptotic regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the tight privacy accounting framework for the Poisson subsampled Gaussian mechanism established by Koskela et al. (2020) and the PLRV composition machinery of Sommer et al. (2019), applying them to a question that prior work had only addressed heuristically. The linear relationship σ = cq between subsampling rate and noise standard deviation was previously suggested as a rule-of-thumb by Li et al. (2021) and Sander et al. (2023) to explain empirical observations from McMahan et al. (2018) and De et al. (2022), but no rigorous proof existed under tight accounting. The paper departs from Rényi DP-based analyses (Abadi et al. 2016, Mironov et al. 2019) that also exhibit this linearity but do not provide tight bounds, instead working with the hockey-stick divergence and dominating pairs to prove the asymptotic result formally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why large batch sizes improve DP-SGD performance, resolving a gap that had only empirical and heuristic accounts. The conceptual reframing—that subsampling amplification saves compute rather than achieving fundamentally better utility—has practical implications for how practitioners allocate training resources under privacy constraints. Key open questions include interpolating the theory between T=1 and T→∞ (the T=1 result relies on a numerically verified conjecture rather than a proof), and understanding how these variance results translate into convergence guarantees for specific loss landscapes. The paper also notes that the analysis is limited to Poisson subsampling, leaving WOR and WR schemes unaddressed due to unresolved issues in their privacy accounting.
