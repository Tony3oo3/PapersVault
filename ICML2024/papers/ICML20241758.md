## Problem

Predictive coding (PC) graphs can compute conditional probabilities (correlations) on Bayesian networks but cannot model interventions or counterfactuals, which require mutilating the causal graph—a computationally expensive and structurally invasive operation. Additionally, fully connected PC graphs underperform hierarchical models on classification because conditional queries allow bidirectional information flow and excessive model complexity. The paper asks whether PC graphs can natively support all three levels of Pearl's causal hierarchy and learn causal structure from observational data without graph modification.

## Input / Output

For causal inference, the model takes observational data generated from a known DAG (linear or nonlinear SCMs with additive Gaussian noise) plus an intervention value and, for counterfactuals, factual endogenous values; it outputs estimated interventional or counterfactual distributions over descendant nodes. For classification, inputs are 784-dimensional MNIST/FashionMNIST images, pairs of MNIST images (2-MNIST), or MNIST images paired with a rotation angle and a hidden color variable; outputs are class labels, a learned sparse adjacency matrix, or a counterfactual image (e.g., a rotated or recolored digit). Notably, interventions are applied 'at runtime' by zeroing a single error node rather than restructuring the graph, and the counterfactual image is produced by fixing the hidden exogenous node (color) while swapping the treatment (rotation).

## Method

The key insight is that in a PC graph, the only signal propagating opposite to causal arrows is the prediction error; therefore, performing a do-operation on node x_j is equivalent to fixing x_j to the intervention value while setting its error node e_j to zero at every time step, which is proven equivalent to the standard mutilated-graph computation (Proposition 3.1). Counterfactuals follow a three-step abduction–action–prediction protocol using this interventional query. For structure learning, the adjacency matrix A becomes a continuous, learnable parameter whose entries are updated by gradient descent on the PC variational free energy, regularized by an L1 (Laplace) sparsity prior and the NOTEARS acyclicity constraint h(A)=tr(exp(A∘A))−d; a novel contrastive training scheme with negative examples prevents convergence to degenerate self-predicting structures. The entire framework operates on the standard PC energy (sum of squared value-to-prediction errors under mean-field and Laplace approximations), requiring no architectural changes beyond the error-node manipulation and the learnable adjacency matrix.

## Results

On MNIST and FashionMNIST classification, interventional queries reduce test error from 9.87% (correlation) to 7.93% and from 18.32% to 16.6%, respectively. Adding an acyclicity prior further reduces error to 3.17% (MNIST) and 14.08% (FashionMNIST), while negative examples achieve 3.51% and 13.98%. On the colored-MNIST counterfactual robustness benchmark, PC graphs achieve an MSE of 0.0008±0.0002, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art (De Brouwer, 2022) which reports 0.001±0.001, while also generalizing to 40° rotations absent from training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For counterfactual robustness on colored-MNIST, PC graphs achieve a lower test MSE (0.0008±0.0002) than De Brouwer (2022) (0.001±0.001) while using a simpler MLP architecture without convolutional layers or ad hoc training techniques, and additionally generalize to 40° rotations not seen during training. For causal structure learning on Erdős-Rényi and scale-free random graphs (10–20 nodes), PC graphs are compared against PC (Kalisch & Buhlman, 2007), GES (Chickering, 2002), NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018), and ICALiNGAM (Shimizu et al., 2006). The paper reports that PC graphs sustain stable performance across all graph setups as measured by SHD and F1 score, and notably perform very well on scale-free graphs where most baselines struggle due to irregular degree distributions. For classification, the paper does not compare against standard backpropagation-trained networks directly but notes that fine-tuning the learned sparse structure can reach test errors below 2%, matching standard neural network performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predictive coding graph framework of Salvatori et al. (2022a), which demonstrated that PC can perform variational inference on arbitrary graph topologies, by showing that the same architecture can natively compute interventional and counterfactual queries without graph mutilation. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 3.1) that zeroing a node's prediction error is equivalent to removing its incoming edges bridges Pearl's do-calculus with the local update dynamics of PC. For structure learning, the paper adapts the NOTEARS acyclicity prior (Zheng et al., 2018) to the PC energy function and introduces a contrastive negative-examples mechanism to prevent degenerate self-predicting solutions, while the counterfactual robustness experiments directly compare against De Brouwer (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, biologically plausible route to end-to-end causal inference: a single architecture can learn structure from observational data, then answer associational, interventional, and counterfactual queries without explicit structural equations. However, the practical impact is currently limited: classification gains are modest (~2% on MNIST), the causal inference experiments use small synthetic graphs with known structure, and the no-hidden-confounders assumption restricts real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the interventional-query trick generalizes to soft interventions, whether structure learning scales beyond 20-node graphs, and whether the approach can handle unobserved confounders or temporal causal structures.
