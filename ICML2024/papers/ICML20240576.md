## Problem

Existing RMDP methods either require full access to the nominal transition model (limiting them to tabular settings) or embed robustness via regularization tied to a specific RL algorithm (e.g., policy gradient or Q-learning), making them hard to scale to high-dimensional domains. No prior approach allows a practitioner to simply plug robustness into an arbitrary off-the-shelf deep RL algorithm without modifying its learning dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each environment step, the method requires N next-state samples drawn from the nominal transition kernel (the unmodified environment dynamics), in addition to the standard state observation and action. The resampled next state is then fed into any standard RL algorithm's replay buffer as if it were a real environment transition. The output is a robust policy and value function that degrade gracefully under transition-kernel perturbations (noise, physical parameter changes) beyond the nominal setting, without requiring the perturbation to conform to the KL uncertainty set used during training.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that for a KL-rectangular uncertainty set, the worst transition kernel is an exponential tilt of the nominal kernel: P_worst(s'|s,a) = P̄(s'|s,a)·exp(−(v_robust(s')−ω)/κ), where ω is a threshold and κ a temperature parameter both implicitly determined by the uncertainty radius β. Practically, EWoK approximates this by sampling N next states from the nominal kernel, then resampling one with probability proportional to exp(−(v(s')−mean(v))/κ), where v is a neural-network value function trained online. This resampled transition is injected into any standard RL algorithm (DDQN, PPO, SAC, TD3) with no modification to the algorithm's update rules. A convergence theorem (Theorem 3.5) guarantees that the estimated worst kernel converges geometrically to the true worst kernel, and the robust value converges to the true robust value, making the approach both theoretically grounded and algorithm-agnostic.

## Results

EWoK is evaluated on Cartpole (DDQN, PPO) and three DeepMind Control tasks (Walker-run, Walker-stand, Walker-walk with SAC and TD3), reporting IQM with 95% stratified bootstrap CIs across 20–40 random seeds. Across all environments, EWoK consistently achieves higher average reward than both the non-robust baseline and Domain Randomization when the transition kernel is perturbed (noise or environment parameters), while being trained exclusively on the nominal environment. A notable trade-off appears in Walker-run, where EWoK achieves slightly lower reward under nominal dynamics but substantially better reward under perturbed ones. Training overhead is modest: EWoK adds roughly 1 minute over DDQN (~5 vs ~4 min), is comparable for PPO (~60 min), and adds ~1 hour for SAC/TD3 on DeepMind Control (~6 vs ~5 hours, ~7 vs ~6 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Domain Randomization (Tobin et al., 2017), EWoK outperforms DR on all perturbation settings (noise scale, pole mass, pole length, cart mass, gravity, joint damping, thigh length, torso length) in both Cartpole and DeepMind Control environments, despite DR having the privileged advantage of training on diverse perturbed environments while EWoK trains only on the nominal one. Against the non-robust RL baselines (DDQN, PPO, SAC, TD3), EWoK shows clear performance gains under perturbation, with the gap widening as the perturbation magnitude increases. In Appendix C, EWoK with Q-learning is compared to the Robust DP bisection algorithm of Nilim & El Ghaoui (2005) on a simple MDP and a modified Cliff-Walking environment; EWoK converges to a very similar robust return and achieves the same optimal robust policy as the model-based DP approach, despite being a sample-based method that does not require access to the true transition kernel. The paper notes that existing RMDP methods (e.g., Kumar et al. 2022, 2023; Wang & Zou 2022) are tied to specific RL algorithms and have not been validated in high-dimensional settings, making direct apples-to-apples comparison infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RMDP framework of Iyengar (2005) and Nilim & El Ghaoui (2005), which established the KL-rectangular uncertainty set and robust Bellman operator. It departs from the dominant line of work (Kumar et al. 2022/2023, Wang & Zou 2022, Derman et al. 2021) that achieves robustness by regularizing the policy or value updates, instead proposing to leave the RL algorithm untouched and simulate the worst transition kernel via a resampling mechanism. The key theoretical contribution is a closed-form characterization (Theorem 3.2) showing the worst kernel for KL uncertainty sets is the nominal kernel reweighted by an exponential of the negative robust value, which enables practical approximate sampling. This positions EWoK as a complementary paradigm to both model-based robust DP (Wiesemann et al. 2013) and regularization-based model-free methods, and is distinct from domain randomization (Tobin et al. 2017) in being theoretically grounded in RMDPs rather than heuristic perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The algorithm-agnostic design is the paper's most practically significant contribution: any existing deep RL implementation (SAC, TD3, PPO, DQN) can be made robust with a single resampling step, removing the need to derive algorithm-specific robust variants. This directly addresses the scalability bottleneck that has limited RMDP methods to tabular or small-scale settings. Key limitations include the requirement to sample next states multiple times from the nominal kernel (ruling out single-sample environments without a learned world model), the gap between the KL-theory and the non-KL perturbations used in experiments, and the observed trade-off where robustness under perturbation comes at the cost of nominal-kernel performance (especially in walker-run). Open questions include combining EWoK with learned world models, extending to coupled (non-rectangular) uncertainty sets, and applying it in offline or safe RL settings.
