## Problem

Selecting the optimal pre-trained LLM to fine-tune for a downstream task is infeasible under resource constraints, since fine-tuning all candidates is prohibitively expensive. Existing scaling laws derived for pre-training fail to model fine-tuning behavior because they cannot capture a previously unobserved 'pre-power phase' at low data regimes, where the loss curve's slope gradually decreases before entering the linear power phase. This theoretical gap means no principled method can accurately predict full-fine-tuning performance from a small data budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of m pre-trained LLMs (ranging from 100M to 7B parameters, including decoder-only, encoder-decoder, MoE, and instruction-tuned architectures) and a fine-tuning dataset subset S_sub of size γ|S| (where γ ∈ {1/512, ..., 1/8} controls the data budget). It consumes the input by iteratively halving the subset and fine-tuning each model on each size, collecting (data-size, test-loss) pairs. The output is a predicted full-fine-tuning cross-entropy loss for each model, from which the model with the lowest predicted loss is selected as the best candidate.

## Method

The paper introduces the Rectified Scaling Law L̂(D) = B/(D_l + D^β) + E, which augments the standard fine-tuning scaling law with a 'pre-learned data size' D_l representing the equivalent amount of downstream data the model has already absorbed during pre-training. This single additional parameter makes the log-log derivative non-monotonic, theoretically unifying the pre-power phase (where slope decreases) and the power phase (where slope is roughly constant) into one expression, and empirically reducing fitting RMSD from 0.036 to 0.007. Built on this law, the 'Accept then Stop' (AtS) algorithm fine-tunes each model on S_sub, then repeatedly halves the subset and checks whether the new (log D, log L) point deviates from a linear fit of previously accepted points by more than a threshold δ (default 5σ). Once the deviation exceeds δ—signaling entry into the pre-power phase—the algorithm stops and extrapolates the full-dataset loss from the accepted power-phase points via linear regression, achieving near-optimal model selection with hundreds of times less compute than full fine-tuning.

## Results

AtS achieves average Pearson correlation of 62.7% (FLAN), 84.6% (WMT19), and 93.8% (Gigaword) across all budget ratios, with average Relative Accuracy of 92.1%, 99.2%, and 95.1% respectively. Even under the most constrained setting (γ=1/512), AtS maintains 45.6%, 61.5%, and 91.0% PearCorr on FLAN, WMT19, and Gigaword, while ZeroShot and ModelSize produce negatively correlated predictions (−10.7% and −20.9% on FLAN; −49.2% and −24.4% on Gigaword). At γ=1/256, AtS selects the near-optimal model with RelAcc >95% on all three datasets, a level no baseline reaches even at γ=1/8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AtS outperforms all three baselines (ZeroShot, SubTuning, ModelSize) on every dataset and every budget ratio for both PearCorr and RelAcc. ZeroShot and ModelSize produce negatively correlated predictions on FLAN (−10.7% and −20.9% PearCorr) and Gigaword (−49.2% and −24.4%), meaning they actively select worse models as the budget shrinks. SubTuning is the closest competitor, reaching 93.5% PearCorr on WMT19 at γ=1/8, but degrades rapidly to 34.5% at γ=1/512, while AtS maintains 61.5%. On RelAcc, AtS achieves >95% on all three datasets at γ=1/256, whereas no baseline exceeds 85.3% (ZeroShot on FLAN) or 99.1% (SubTuning on WMT19) at any budget ratio. The gap is most dramatic on FLAN, where AtS's advantage over the second-best method grows from ~30 points at γ=1/8 to ~29 points at γ=1/512 in PearCorr.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling law literature from pre-training (Kaplan et al., 2020) and transfer learning (Hernandez et al., 2021) to the fine-tuning regime, where it discovers a previously unobserved 'pre-power phase' that the standard power-law form provably cannot capture (Theorem 3.1). The Rectified Scaling Law introduces a single 'pre-learned data size' parameter D_l to unify both phases, departing from prior approaches that either model phases separately with extra parameters or ignore the transition entirely. On the selection side, the paper positions LLM selection for fine-tuning as a new resource-constrained prediction task, arguing that existing model selection methods (designed for classification/regression) are fundamentally incompatible with generative LLMs, and that intuitive heuristics (model size, zero-shot score, sub-tuning loss) can even yield negatively correlated rankings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AtS algorithm's ability to select near-optimal models (RelAcc > 95%) with γ=1/256 while all baselines fail even at γ=1/8 has direct practical impact on compute costs for LLM fine-tuning pipelines. The pre-power phase discovery also connects to the emerging literature on emergent abilities (the ~2.2 loss threshold), suggesting a deeper structural transition in LLM learning that remains unexplained. Key open questions include: whether the law extends to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) or RLHF, how to handle the regime where all observations fall in the pre-power phase (extremely small γ), and whether the phase transition appears in non-standard training settings beyond full fine-tuning.
