## Problem

Vector-quantized image models suffer from codebook collapse: VQGAN uses only ~36% of its tokens, and even usage-improving methods like Reg-VQ exhibit highly imbalanced token training (high active frequency variance), leading to artifacts and poor generalization. The paper asks what the ideal codebook distribution should be and derives a principled answer by casting codebook training as a sphere packing problem inspired by Kepler's Conjecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes 256×256 RGB images and compresses them via a CNN encoder into a 16×16 grid of discrete tokens (downsampling factor f=16), where each token is one of K=1024 codebook vectors of effective dimension n_z/d=64 (with d=4 partitions). For conditional generation, a semantic segmentation map of the same spatial size serves as input to an autoregressive transformer; for super-resolution, a low-resolution image is fed into a latent diffusion model whose autoencoder is replaced by the Kepler codebook. Outputs are full-resolution reconstructed, generated, or super-resolved images alongside the discrete latent representation.

## Method

The paper derives that an optimally trained codebook should follow a compact, hexagonal-lattice structure (θ=60° basis vectors) whose per-dimension token distribution is an n_z-dimensional Irwin-Hall distribution (sum of n_z independent uniforms). Training is regularized by a KL divergence loss between the codebook's empirical posterior and this Irwin-Hall prior, encouraging all tokens to be used uniformly and reducing active frequency variance. To make the KL computation tractable under the curse of dimensionality, a codebook partition strategy reshapes the encoder output from h×w×n_z into h×w×d groups of n_z/d dimensions, quantizing each group independently before reshaping back. The overall architecture follows VQGAN (CNN encoder-decoder + vector quantization + GAN training), with the IHD regularization and partition as the only modifications to the codebook learning stage.

## Results

On ADE20K, the Kepler codebook achieves 21.34 PSNR and 16.87 rFID for reconstruction, and 33.84 FID for generation, outperforming all baselines. On CelebA-HQ, it reaches 24.85 PSNR, 8.59 rFID, and 14.96 FID. The method achieves 100% codebook usage (vs. 35.9% for VQGAN and 87.6% for Reg-VQ) while significantly reducing active frequency variance, indicating balanced token training across the entire codebook.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Kepler codebook outperforms VQGAN by 2.45 dB PSNR and 11.3 rFID on ADE20K reconstruction, and by 2.41 dB PSNR and 4.15 rFID on CelebA-HQ. Against Reg-VQ, it gains 2.9 dB PSNR and 6.82 rFID on ADE20K, and 2.8 dB PSNR and 1.5 rFID on CelebA-HQ. Compared to the recent FSQ method (evaluated only on ADE20K), the Kepler codebook improves by 1.03 dB PSNR and 1.43 rFID for reconstruction, while also achieving a lower generation FID (33.84 vs. 35.03). In the downstream super-resolution task on DRealSR within the LDM framework, the Kepler codebook autoencoder is compared against the original LDM autoencoder, with the paper reporting advantages in FID for the SR task. Cross-domain experiments (trained on ADE20K, tested on MS-COCO, LS-DIR, DIV2K) and multi-resolution evaluation on DIV2K further confirm consistent gains over VQGAN and Reg-VQ across all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, rFID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQGAN architecture (Esser et al., 2021) and addresses the codebook collapse problem that plagues VQ-based methods, including VQ-VAE, VQGAN, and Reg-VQ. Rather than adopting prior remedies like Gumbel-Softmax stochastic quantization (Reg-VQ) or codebook reset strategies (HVQ-VAE, Jukebox), it reframes codebook training as a sphere packing problem inspired by Kepler's Conjecture, deriving an Irwin-Hall distribution as the ideal prior for token placement. The codebook partition technique further departs from prior work by exploiting the i.i.d. structure across token dimensions to reduce the effective dimensionality for KL-divergence-based regularization, a practical step not present in earlier VQ methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric justification for why compact, structured codebook distributions outperform uniform or stochastic alternatives, which could guide future codebook design beyond image generation to other VQ-based modalities (audio, language). However, the derivation relies on idealized assumptions (rational number field for tokens, exact hexagonal lattice in 2D extended by induction), and the practical IHD regularization is an approximation whose tightness in high-dimensional settings remains unproven. Open questions include whether the 60° hexagonal structure generalizes to non-CNN architectures (e.g., ViT-based tokenizers) and whether the approach scales to the very large codebooks (K > 65536) used in modern generative models.
