## Problem

Current single-pass uncertainty estimators enforce feature preservation by constraining distances in the network output, which the paper shows theoretically and empirically opposes the information compression objective of learning—particularly under class imbalance, deep architectures, and distributional shift. The paper identifies that preserving distances is only beneficial when the preserved features carry label-relevant information, and that blindly enforcing output-level constraints degrades generalization performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240139/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 ImageNet) or 2D CT scan slices (organA/C/S from MedMNIST) and produces two outputs: a class label from the main feed-forward network and a scalar uncertainty score computed as a weighted sum of Dempster-Shafer distances from approximate Gaussian Process layers atop internal classifiers. Fitting the combination weights requires only a small unlabeled validation subset drawn from the training distribution—no OOD samples or additional labels are needed.

## Method

TULIP implements transitional feature preservation by placing internal classifiers (shallow linear heads with GP output layers) at intermediate layers of a standard ResNet backbone, forming a Shallow-Deep Network. All internal classifiers are trained jointly with the main network using a weighted multi-task loss, and during inference the uncertainty score is a weighted linear combination of individual GP-based distance scores from each internal classifier. The combination weights are fitted post-hoc via logistic regression on proxy labels derived from prediction switches between consecutive internal classifiers on a small validation set. The key theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) proves that a linear combination of intermediate-layer distances is feature-preserving whenever the first layer is collapse-resistant, eliminating the need for explicit output-level constraints like spectral normalization or gradient penalties while still maintaining input-distance information for uncertainty estimation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50, TULIP achieves 94.88% accuracy with OOD AUROC of 0.738 (CIFAR-10-C), 0.936 (CIFAR-100-C), and 0.946 (SVHN), outperforming all single-pass baselines on corruption and cross-dataset detection. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50, TULIP reaches 78.44% accuracy with AUROC of 0.868 (CIFAR-10-C), 0.738 (CIFAR-100-C), and 0.955 (SVHN), surpassing SNGP by 3.4–5.5 AUROC points across all OOD benchmarks. TULIP requires only 1x runtime and 1.5x space (vs. 5x for ensembles) while using no explicit feature preservation constraints, and uses only 90% of training labels for the combination head fitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TULIP outperforms all single-pass baselines on CIFAR-100-C and CIFAR-10-C AUROC across all three ResNet depths. Against SNGP (the strongest single-pass competitor), TULIP gains +0.017 AUROC on CIFAR-10-C and +0.008 on CIFAR-100-C (ResNet-50, CIFAR-10), while SNGP retains a +0.030 advantage on SVHN; on CIFAR-100, TULIP dominates SNGP by +0.047 to +0.055 AUROC across all OOD sets. TULIP substantially outperforms DUQ (+0.120 AUROC on CIFAR-10-C) and DUN (+0.025 on CIFAR-10-C), which rely on gradient penalty and diversity loss constraints respectively. Compared to the 5-model ensemble (5x runtime/space), TULIP matches or exceeds ensemble AUROC on CIFAR-10-C (0.738 vs 0.725) and CIFAR-100-C (0.936 vs 0.914) at 1x runtime, though it trails on clean accuracy (94.88% vs 96.33%). On the medical CT scan benchmarks (organA/C/S), TULIP achieves the highest accuracy (95.25% on organA) and best or near-best AUROC, while SNGP degrades significantly under the class imbalance inherent to cross-plane evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TULIP departs from the dominant paradigm in single-pass uncertainty estimation (SNGP, DUQ, DUN) that enforces feature preservation directly in the network output via spectral normalization, gradient penalties, or diversity losses. Instead, it repurposes shallow-deep networks (originally designed for computation reduction) as a mechanism for 'transitional feature preservation,' where uncertainty is encoded in the spread of intermediate-layer representations rather than in a single output. The theoretical analysis connects this to the information bottleneck framework, showing that output-level distance preservation can actively oppose the compression objective, while intermediate representations naturally retain input-distribution information before it is collapsed. The method also positions itself as a single-pass alternative to deep ensembles, which achieve similar transitional preservation but require multiple forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a fundamental tension in single-pass uncertainty estimation: the same feature preservation that enables reliable OOD detection can degrade classification accuracy, particularly under class imbalance or distributional shift. TULIP's practical advantage is that it requires no OOD validation data, no architectural modification of the backbone (demonstrated on ImageNet), and operates in a single forward pass with only 1.5× space overhead. Key limitations include reduced effectiveness on shallow architectures with few intermediate layers, sensitivity to the number and placement of internal classifiers, and the need for a validation set to fit combination weights. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very deep or non-residual architectures, whether the GP-based scoring can be replaced with lighter alternatives, and whether the theoretical guarantee (Proposition 4.1) holds under the collapse-resistance assumption in practice without explicit first-layer constraints.
