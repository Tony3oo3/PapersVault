## Problem

GNN model editing has been largely overlooked, with only one GNN-specific method (EGNN) available, and even that method suffers catastrophic performance degradation (up to 49.3% accuracy drop) under sequential edits. The paper identifies that the root cause of GNN editing sensitivity is not solely neighborhood aggregation (as previously attributed) but primarily overfitting to individual editing targets, which no existing method adequately addresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242553/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained GNN model, a misclassified node as the editing target, a pool of training samples, and the set of all previously edited targets. During each edit, it constructs a batch comprising the current target, its k-hop neighbors present in the training set (fraction α of β extra samples), randomly selected training samples (fraction 1−α of β), and all historical editing targets. The output is an updated MLP head (with the GNN frozen) that correctly classifies the target while preserving general test accuracy, with gradient descent terminating when all targets in the batch are corrected or a step budget (500 steps) is exhausted.

## Method

SEED-GNN builds on EGNN's frozen GNN + active MLP ensemble for efficiency (caching GNN outputs, updating only MLP weights) but introduces three key mechanisms to combat overfitting and catastrophic forgetting. First, edit-aware training sample mix-up batches the editing target with its k-hop neighbors in the training set (protecting them from neighborhood corrosion) and random training samples, controlled by hyperparameters α (neighbor ratio, default 0.25) and β (total extra samples, default 100). Second, incremental batching includes all previously edited targets in every edit operation, preventing later edits from overwriting earlier patches. Third, a stoppage criterion halts gradient descent once all targets in the batch are correctly classified or the step budget is spent. The core insight is that overfitting to low-dimensional editing targets—not just the GNN aggregation mechanism—is the primary driver of editing sensitivity, and simple batching-based regularization resolves it.

## Results

SEED-GNN achieves near-perfect Average Success Rate (Avg SR ≈ 0.99–1.0) across all 7 datasets and 4 GNN backbones while maintaining Average Test Drawdown (Avg DD) ≤ 10% in every setting. On the large-scale ogbn-products dataset (2.4M nodes), SEED-GNN with GCN achieves Avg DD of 8.3 and Avg SR of 1.0 after 50 sequential edits, compared to EGNN's Avg DD of 30.4 and Avg SR of 0.72. On small-scale Cora, SEED-GNN with GraphSAGE achieves Avg DD of 1.0 and Avg SR of 1.0, while the next-best method (EGNN) reaches only Avg DD of 10.8 and Avg SR of 0.62. The method also achieves negative Avg DD (i.e., improved general accuracy) on several configurations, such as GIN on Amazon Computers (Avg DD = −8.0) and GIN on Coauthor-CS (Avg DD = −1.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEED-GNN decisively outperforms EGNN (Liu et al., 2023), the only prior GNN-specific editing method, on both key metrics: SEED-GNN achieves Avg SR of 0.99–1.0 versus EGNN's 0.35–0.72, and Avg DD of 1–8% versus EGNN's 3–30% across all dataset/backbone combinations. Against ENN (Sinitsin et al., 2020), the gap is even more extreme — ENN collapses to Avg SR of 0.01–0.21 and Avg DD of 48–76% on large graphs, rendering it essentially unusable for sequential editing. Adapter-tuning (Houlsby et al., 2019) similarly fails, with Avg SR of 0.17–0.34 and Avg DD of 54–75%, confirming that generic PEFT methods are incompatible with GNN editing. LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) presents the most interesting comparison: it achieves low Avg DD (1–4%) but at the cost of near-zero Avg SR (0.08–0.30), meaning it barely corrects the target errors while preserving general performance — the opposite of what model editing requires. SEED-GNN is the only method that simultaneously achieves high SR (≥0.93) and low DD (≤10%) across all reported settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EGNN (Liu et al., 2023a), the sole prior GNN-specific model editing method, which proposed a frozen GNN + active MLP ensemble to sidestep neighborhood aggregation's editing sensitivity. SEED-GNN retains EGNN's efficient architecture but departs fundamentally in its diagnosis: rather than treating neighborhood aggregation as the root cause of editing fragility, the authors identify overfitting of individual editing targets as the primary culprit, and address it through edit-aware batching (mixing in neighbors and random training samples) plus incremental batching of all prior edit targets. The method also draws on broader model-editing literature (ENN, MEND, LoRA, Adapter-tuning) as baselines, positioning GNN editing as a previously neglected subfield that inherits lessons from NLP and CV editing research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEED-GNN is the first GNN editing method that maintains near-perfect success rates (≈1.0 Avg SR) and low drawdown (≤10% Avg DD) across 50 sequential edits on multiple architectures and datasets, making GNN model editing practically viable for the first time. The reframing of the editing-sensitivity problem from an architectural limitation (neighborhood aggregation) to a training-dynamics issue (overfitting) opens new directions for GNN editing research beyond the frozen-ensemble paradigm. Key limitations include restriction to node classification, the gap between Max DD and Avg DD suggesting occasional large performance dips, and untested compatibility with modern GNN training techniques. The formalization of sequential GNN editing evaluation criteria (i-th Edits SR/DD, Max/Avg DD) provides a reusable framework for future work in this nascent field.
