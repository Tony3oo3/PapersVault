## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for LLMs require white-box access to model parameters to perform gradient updates, making them infeasible for black-box LLMs or computationally prohibitive for models with billions of parameters. This paper asks whether unlearning can be achieved purely at inference time, without any parameter modification, by exploiting the in-context learning capability of LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method constructs a prompt from three components: the forget points (training instances targeted for deletion) with their labels randomly flipped to different values, L correctly-labeled examples sampled from the training distribution (L ∈ {2, 4, 6}), and the query input. The model then predicts the next token (a class label for classification tasks or a text span for question-answering) with temperature t=0. No parameter updates occur; the entire unlearning effect is mediated through the prompt structure alone.

## Method

ICUL is a three-step prompt construction procedure performed at inference time: (1) the K forget points are included in the context with their labels randomly flipped to different values, (2) L correctly-labeled training examples are appended to prevent the model from overcorrecting on the forget points, and (3) the query input is added for prediction. The key insight is that presenting the model with the forget points under incorrect labels diminishes its confidence specifically on those instances to levels matching a model that was never trained on them, while the correctly-labeled padding examples anchor overall task performance. The method requires no access to model parameters, runs on a V100 (32 GB) where gradient-ascent baselines require an A100 (80 GB), and is evaluated via LiRA-Forget, a likelihood-ratio-based test adapted from the LiRA membership inference attack that uses shadow models to distinguish unlearned points from held-out points.

## Results

On the SQUAD question-answering task, ICUL reduces LiRA-Forget TPR at FPR=0.01 from 0.0241 (Baseline) to 0.0183 for 5 deletions and from 0.0247 to 0.0140 for 10 deletions, a 24% and 43% reduction in privacy leakage respectively, while test accuracy drops from 72.0% to 68.7% (5 deletions) and 72.2% to 60.3% (10 deletions). Across all four classification datasets (SST-2, AG-News, Amazon, and SQUAD) and deletion sizes (1, 5, 10, 20), ICUL bests the Gradient Ascent baseline in 14 out of 16 cases on TPR at FPR=0.01. On Llama-2 7B, ICUL requires only 29.90–34.16 GB GPU RAM versus 64.80–68.91 GB for GA, and completes model updates in 0.00 s versus 1.44–15.94 s for GA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Gradient Ascent (GA) from Jang et al. (2023), which performs sequential gradient ascent on the forget set and requires white-box parameter access. ICUL outperforms GA on the primary unlearning metric (LiRA-Forget TPR at FPR=0.01) in 14 out of 16 dataset–deletion-size combinations, with ICUL curves tracing closer to the random-guessing benchmark. However, GA achieves slightly higher test accuracy than ICUL for small deletion requests (1–5 points); the gap narrows at 10 deletions, and at 20 deletions GA's test accuracy drops significantly while ICUL maintains a stable level. Computationally, ICUL is dramatically more efficient: on Llama-2 7B it uses roughly half the GPU RAM (≈30 GB vs ≈65–69 GB) and requires zero model-update time (inference-only) versus 1.4–16 s for GA. On the SQUAD task, ICUL reduces privacy leakage by 24% (5 deletions) and 43% (10 deletions) relative to the no-unlearning Baseline, approaching the random-guessing benchmark of 0.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the in-context learning paradigm (Brown et al., 2020) — originally a mechanism for *learning* new tasks at inference time — as a mechanism for *unlearning* specific training points, a conceptual inversion that no prior work had explored. For evaluation, it adapts the LiRA likelihood-ratio membership inference statistic (Carlini et al., 2022) into a new unlearning-specific metric (LiRA-Forget), moving beyond the heuristic loss-comparison and test-error metrics used in prior unlearning work. The method is positioned as complementary to white-box unlearning approaches like gradient ascent (Jang et al., 2023) and descent-to-delete (Neel et al., 2021), which require parameter access and expensive gradient computations that are infeasible for billion-parameter LLMs or black-box API-only models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICUL offers a practically important alternative for organizations that only have query access to LLMs or face GPU memory constraints (running on a 32GB V100 vs. 80GB A100 for gradient ascent), making unlearning feasible in settings where prior methods simply cannot run. However, significant limitations remain: the context-length constraint makes large deletion requests infeasible, test accuracy drops substantially with more deletions (16.7% on SQUAD for 10 deletions), and the method is vulnerable to prompt-inversion attacks that could expose the unlearning context. Open questions include scaling to larger deletion sets, reducing inference-time cost as prompts grow, and whether the approach generalizes to open-ended generation tasks beyond classification and QA.
