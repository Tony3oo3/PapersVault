## Problem

Graph Attention Networks (GATs) are designed to assign learnable importance weights to neighbors, yet in practice they cannot effectively switch off neighborhood aggregation when a node's own features are sufficient for the task. This structural limitation, rooted in a conservation law of GAT gradient flow dynamics that constrains attention parameter norms, causes unnecessary oversmoothing and degrades performance on heterophilic graphs where neighbors are often uninformative or misleading.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with self-loops and d-dimensional node feature vectors, and produces per-node class labels along with interpretable self-attention coefficients α_vv that quantify the relative reliance on a node's own features versus its neighborhood at each layer. The key interpretability signal is that α_vv→1 indicates MLP-like behavior (no aggregation) while α_vv→0 indicates pure neighborhood aggregation, with real-world tasks typically requiring intermediate values that vary across nodes and layers.

## Method

GATE modifies GAT by replacing the single attention parameter vector **a** with two separate vectors **a_s** (applied to neighbor nodes) and **a_t** (applied to the self-node via indicator functions in the attention score, Eq. 4), enabling independent control over neighborhood and self-feature contributions. The theoretical core is a new conservation law (Theorem 4.3) showing that **a_s** and **a_t** can interchange their budget for relative parameter change, so switching off neighborhood aggregation requires only small attention norms (the well-trainable regime) rather than the large norms that GAT's conservation law forces. In the weight-sharing variant GATE_S, all feature transformation matrices are shared (W=U=V), adding only d parameters per layer over GAT. The architecture is initialized with zero attention parameters and orthogonal looks-linear feature matrices, and trained with standard Adam without regularization, allowing it to flexibly interleave perceptron-like layers (α_vv≈1) with aggregation layers (α_vv<1) as the task demands.

## Results

On five heterophilic benchmark datasets, GATE outperforms GAT by large margins: 75.55% vs 26.10% accuracy on roman-empire, 66.14% vs 50.83% AUC on minesweeper, and 62.95% vs 57.72% AUC on questions. On the large-scale OGB-arxiv dataset (169K nodes), GATE achieves a new SOTA test accuracy of 79.57±0.84% with 12 layers, surpassing GAT's 71.87% (3 layers) and the previous SOTA of 78.03% from a language-model-based method. On OGB-products, GATE reaches 86.24% vs GAT's 80.63%, and on OGB-mag, 35.29% vs 32.61%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GATE decisively outperforms GAT on all five heterophilic datasets, with the largest gap on roman-empire (75.55% vs 26.10%, a ~49-point improvement) and minesweeper (66.14% vs 50.83% AUC). Against FAGCN, GATE wins on four of five datasets (roman-empire, amazon-ratings, questions, tolokers) but loses slightly on minesweeper (66.14% vs 63.38% AUC in the simple setting; in the full Platonov et al. setup, GATE achieves 92.82% vs FAGCN's 88.17%). In the extensive 14-baseline comparison (Table 4), GATE outperforms GCN (e.g., 89.51% vs 73.69% on roman-empire) and GraphSAGE (89.51% vs 85.74%) across all datasets, and matches or slightly exceeds GAT-sep (89.51% vs 88.75% on roman-empire) while using an order of magnitude fewer parameters (d vs d² additional parameters per layer). On OGB-arxiv, GATE's 79.57% with 12 layers substantially exceeds GAT's 71.87% with 3 layers, demonstrating that GATE benefits from depth without suffering oversmoothing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GAT (Veličković et al., 2018) by identifying a structural limitation in its attention mechanism: the inability to effectively switch off neighborhood aggregation. The theoretical foundation draws directly on the conservation law for GAT gradient flow dynamics established by Mustafa & Burkholz (2023), which the authors update to the GATE architecture (Theorem 4.3). Unlike prior oversmoothing remedies (skip connections, normalization, graph rewiring) that work around the problem, GATE addresses the root cause by separating attention parameters for self-features versus neighbor features, enabling the model to emulate MLP behavior in layers where aggregation is unhelpful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GAT—a widely used baseline architecture—cannot fulfill its design intent of learning to ignore irrelevant neighbors is a meaningful correction to the field's understanding of attention-based GNNs. GATE's interpretable per-node attention coefficients open a path toward answering the long-debated question of how much graph structure is actually useful for a given task. Open questions include whether the conservation-law framework generalizes to other attention mechanisms (e.g., multi-head, cross-attention), whether the approach extends to edge-level or subgraph-level aggregation, and whether the synthetic test bed can serve as a standard benchmark for adaptive aggregation schemes.
