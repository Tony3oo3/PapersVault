## Problem

The hypergeometric distribution models sampling without replacement from a finite discrete population, yet no effective method exists for inferring its parameters (total population size N and per-category counts N_i) when both are unknown, especially in high-dimensional settings with intrinsic low-rank structure. This gap is critical in domains like single-cell genomics and text modeling where counts are severely undersampled and exhibit dependence between categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse count matrices (e.g., bag-of-words token counts for ~4700 reading passages over 34k tokens, or gene transcript counts for 1126 cells over 43k genes) where each row is an undersampled observation with n << N. It produces continuous estimates of per-category population counts N_i (roundable to integers), the inferred total population size N, and a low-dimensional latent representation z (10–16 dimensions) usable for clustering. A violation penalty enforces N_i ≥ c_i per observation, and the decoder output uses ReLU to guarantee non-negative counts.

## Method

The core contribution is a VAE whose decoder outputs estimated category counts N_i and whose observation model is the multivariate hypergeometric likelihood, replacing factorials in binomial coefficients with the gamma function to enable continuous differentiable optimization. The ELBO objective combines the KL divergence between the approximate posterior and a diagonal Gaussian prior, the expected hypergeometric log-likelihood under the posterior, and a violation penalty for infeasible estimates (N_i < c_i). This differs from standard count-data VAEs (multinomial or Poisson likelihoods) by explicitly modeling the dependence between category counts induced by sampling without replacement, which is essential when the sample fraction f_max is non-negligible. For a single ground-truth distribution, the method reduces to direct MLE via gradient descent on the gamma-relaxed log-likelihood; for mixtures, the continuous latent variable allows information sharing across similar observations to overcome sparsity from severe under-sampling.

## Results

On simulated count-data benchmarks (K=100–10,000 categories, 3–10 mixture components, 20–60% under-sampling), the hypergeometric (HG) VAE achieves near-perfect clustering (ARI 0.99–1.00) and dramatically lower count-estimation error than Poisson: MPE of 1.6–5.2% vs. 6.3–64% and MAE of 27–573 vs. 6–7,309 depending on dataset scale. On the CLEAR readability corpus (4,724 passages), the inferred latent vocabulary size correlates strongly with all nine established readability indices, whereas raw token count shows near-zero association. On the SPIKE single-cell genomics dataset, the model recovers the known ground-truth spike-in RNA count (~4,000) and estimates total human RNA at a median of 260,000 transcripts per cell, consistent with prior estimates of ~200,000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed hypergeometric (HG) likelihood against two baselines: a multinomial (MN) VAE following Liang et al. (2018) and a Poisson (P) VAE following Inouye et al. (2017), using identical architecture and training procedure. HG decisively outperforms both on count-estimation accuracy: MPE is 1.6–5.2% for HG versus 6.3–64% for Poisson, and MAE is 27–573 for HG versus 6–7,309 for Poisson across the simulated benchmarks. On clustering (ARI), HG matches MN at 1.00 in most settings but pulls ahead in the hardest case (3 distributions with 2 unique, 10³–10⁴ categories) where MN and P both plateau at ARI ≈ 0.45 while HG reaches 1.00. The key differentiator is that HG is the only method able to distinguish distributions with similar relative category frequencies but different absolute population sizes, because it models both absolute and relative counts. On the real-world SPIKE dataset, HG recovers the known ground-truth spike-in RNA counts to within a narrow band around 4,000, whereas the raw measured counts show large variance and a shifted mean when human cells are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational autoencoder framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014) by replacing the standard Gaussian or Poisson likelihood with a continuous-relaxation hypergeometric likelihood, enabling direct estimation of absolute category counts rather than relative frequencies. It departs from the classical hypergeometric MLE literature (Oberhofer & Kaufmann, 1987; Darroch, 1958), which either assumes the total population size N is known or requires a capture-recapture protocol with tagging and resampling, by formulating the problem as a mixture of distributions with both N and all N_i unknown and high-dimensional. The gamma-function relaxation of the binomial coefficient and the violation penalty for the N_i ≥ c_i constraint are the key technical additions that make continuous optimization feasible within the VAE's ELBO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: no prior method could estimate a mixture of discrete population sizes under severe under-sampling with high-dimensional, low-rank structure, a setting that arises naturally in single-cell genomics and text modeling. The SPIKE-in genomics result—recovering known ground-truth transcript counts from heavily undersampled measurements—is a compelling validation, and the CLEAR corpus application shows the inferred latent vocabulary size correlates with established readability indices where raw token counts do not. Open questions include scalability to populations with millions of categories, theoretical guarantees for the MLE under the gamma relaxation, and whether the violation penalty can be replaced by a more principled constrained optimization approach. The method's reliance on the under-sampling assumption (n << N) limits applicability to settings where sampling is near-complete.
