## Problem

Existing meta-RL methods (policy-gradient, black-box, and task-inference approaches) generalize poorly when the test task distribution shifts from the training distribution: PPG methods degrade to vanilla RL requiring millions of interactions, and task-inference methods rely on simple feedforward policy networks that cannot perform online inference over unseen task distributions. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether a single pretrained network with frozen parameters can continuously improve its policy through interaction with an OOD environment, without any gradient updates.

## Input / Output

The LILTrans network consumes a dynamic window of the most recent b episodes of interaction sequences (observations, actions, rewards, episode-reset flags) augmented with a relative time embedding (t/H⁺) that distinguishes exploration from exploitation phases. It outputs three quantities per time step: an action sampled from the approximate PPD of the Bayes-optimal policy, a predicted reward, and a predicted next observation. At meta-test time the network parameters are frozen; the only mechanism for improvement is the rolling dynamic window that feeds new interaction data back into the same transformer, enabling progressive uncertainty reduction over an arbitrarily long horizon L.

## Method

PSBL meta-trains a transformer variant (LILTrans) to directly approximate the Predictive Posterior Distribution (PPD) of the optimal policy, rather than first sampling an MDP from a posterior and then acting greedily under it. Training combines three losses: a supervised-learning cross-entropy on predicted rewards/observations, a KL-divergence actor loss against a Bayes-optimal policy obtained via simulated annealing on a learned Q-function, and a TD-error critic loss. A dynamic window of b episodes (smaller than the training horizon of N episodes) plus a relative time embedding lets the same fixed-length transformer process an unbounded test horizon by rolling the window forward, which is the key mechanism enabling lifelong in-context learning. At test time, the agent samples actions from the approximate PPD, and each new observation-reward pair updates the context, progressively sharpening the posterior toward a near-deterministic optimal policy—across episodes for sparse-reward tasks and within one or two episodes for dense-reward tasks.

## Results

Results are reported as average returns over 64 parallel environments (3 seeds) across four benchmarks: Gridworld (5×5→8×8), Dark-Key-to-Door (9×9→12×12), HalfCheetah-Vel (0–3→4–4.5 m/s), and Walker-2D-Param (scale 3→6). In sparse-reward discrete tasks, PSBL is the only method that continues to improve beyond the training horizon, demonstrating lifelong in-context learning across episodes, while all baselines plateau or decline. In dense-reward continuous tasks, PSBL converges to near-optimal performance within 1–2 episodes and its OOD advantage grows with increasing distribution shift. PSBL is also more sample-efficient than AMAGO, VariBAD, and RL² in discrete tasks, and comparable to PEARL in continuous tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSBL is compared against four baselines: AMAGO (transformer-based in-context RL), VariBAD (variational Bayes-adaptive meta-RL), RL² (recurrent context-based RL), and PEARL (probabilistic context variable meta-RL, continuous tasks only). PSBL wins decisively on all OOD evaluations across both discrete and continuous tasks, with the gap widening as the test distribution diverges from training. VariBAD adapts slightly faster to in-distribution tasks in Gridworld but its performance collapses under distribution shift because its feedforward policy lacks online inference capability. AMAGO is outperformed by PSBL in both asymptotic performance and training data efficiency. RL², the oldest baseline, is consistently the weakest in OOD settings. PEARL is the closest competitor in continuous tasks (comparable sample efficiency) but still trails PSBL in OOD returns, particularly at the largest distribution shifts (e.g., Walker-2D-Param scale 6, HalfCheetah-Vel 4–4.5 m/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

PSBL sits at the intersection of three research threads: posterior sampling in RL (Osband et al., 2013; Rakelly et al., 2019), transformer-based amortized Bayesian inference (Muller et al., 2021; Ortega et al., 2019; Mikulik et al., 2020), and in-context learning for decision-making (Laskin et al., 2022; Grigsby et al., 2023). The key departure from prior posterior sampling methods is bypassing the intermediate step of sampling an MDP and instead directly sampling actions from the approximate PPD of the optimal policy. The closest prior work is AMAGO, a transformer-based in-context RL method; PSBL differentiates itself by adding a supervised learning loss for reward/observation prediction, a simulated-annealing-derived actor loss for policy regression, and a dynamic window with time embeddings that enable evaluation over an extended (near-infinite) horizon rather than a fixed one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in meta-RL: most methods are evaluated only on in-distribution tasks, yet real-world deployment requires robustness to distributional shift. The 'lifelong in-context learning' framing—where a frozen transformer progressively reduces uncertainty over many episodes—is a useful conceptual contribution that could inform future work on adaptive agents. However, the OOD shifts tested are parameter-level (grid size 5×5→8×8, velocity 0–3→4–4.5 m/s) rather than structural, and the environments are relatively simple; whether this approach scales to complex, high-dimensional OOD scenarios with novel task structures remains an open question. The limited ablation (only window size) and the absence of comparison against more recent OOD-robust meta-RL methods (e.g., Mendonca et al., 2020) leave some uncertainty about which components drive the OOD robustness.
