## Problem

In generative models based on dynamical transport (flows, diffusions, stochastic interpolants), the base density is conventionally chosen to be a simple, data-agnostic distribution such as a standard Gaussian, representing an absence of prior knowledge about the target structure. This paper formalizes how to construct data-dependent couplings in which base samples are computed conditionally given target samples, enabling more efficient transport with lower cost and better conditional generative performance.

## Input / Output

For inpainting, the model receives a masked image where unmasked pixels retain their true values and masked pixels are initialized with independent Gaussian noise, along with an ImageNet class label; it outputs a complete image with the masked region filled by a conditionally valid sample. For super-resolution, the input is an upsampled low-resolution image (64×64 or 256×256) plus a class label, and the output is a high-resolution image (256×256 or 512×512). The conditioning signal (mask or low-res image) is appended as extra input channels to the U-Net velocity model, and the base density x_0 is constructed directly from the target x_1 via a task-specific corruption operator plus Gaussian noise, so the initial state at t=0 is already spatially proximal to the target.

## Method

The core contribution is a general formulation of stochastic interpolants with dependent couplings: instead of the standard independent pairing ρ(x_0,x_1)=ρ_0(x_0)ρ_1(x_1), the joint density is factored as ρ(x_0,x_1)=ρ_1(x_1)ρ_0(x_0|x_1), where the conditional base is generated by a task-specific corruption (e.g., masking or downsampling) plus Gaussian noise. The interpolant I_t = α_t x_0 + β_t x_1 + γ_t z defines a time-dependent density ρ_t that solves a transport equation with velocity field b_t(x)=E(Ė_t|I_t=x), which is learned via the same simulation-free square-loss regression as standard flow matching. A key theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) shows that the transport cost E[|X_1−x_0|²] is bounded by E[|Ė_t|²], and data-dependent couplings tighten this bound by making x_0 already close to x_1. The velocity model is a U-Net that takes the interpolant state and conditioning as input, with the output masked to enforce structural constraints (e.g., fixed unmasked pixels in inpainting). Sampling proceeds by initializing X_0 from the corrupted observation plus noise and integrating the probability flow ODE to t=1.

## Results

On ImageNet super-resolution (64×64 → 256×256), the data-dependent coupling method achieves FID-50k of 2.13 (train) and 2.05 (valid), substantially outperforming all prior methods. On ImageNet inpainting (256×256 and 512×512), the dependent coupling reduces FID-50k from 1.35 (uncoupled baseline) to 1.13. The method uses a U-Net velocity model trained via square-loss regression on the stochastic interpolant, with the low-resolution or masked image provided as conditioning at all timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's data-dependent coupling method outperforms all five baselines on the 64×64→256×256 super-resolution task. Against the strongest prior method, I²SB (Liu et al., 2023), it improves validation FID-50k from 2.70 to 2.05 (a 24% relative reduction). Compared to Cascaded Diffusion (Ho et al., 2022), the gap is even larger: 2.05 vs. 4.63 on validation FID. Against diffusion-based baselines SR3 (5.20) and ADM (3.10), the method achieves roughly 2.5× and 1.5× lower FID respectively. The inpainting comparison (Table 2) shows a more modest but consistent gain: FID-50k drops from 1.35 (independent Gaussian coupling) to 1.13 (data-dependent coupling), confirming that the coupling design itself—rather than the conditioning variable—drives the improvement. Notably, the method requires no inference-time corrections (e.g., replacement or MCMC), unlike prior conditional diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2022; Albergo et al., 2023) by replacing the standard independent coupling between base and target densities with a data-dependent one, where base samples are computed conditionally given target samples. It explicitly distinguishes this coupling mechanism from the separate (and orthogonal) act of conditioning the velocity field on external information such as class labels or low-resolution images, a separation that prior conditional diffusion and flow works (Saharia et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2022a) treated as a single undifferentiated operation. The approach is positioned as simpler and more flexible than minibatch optimal transport couplings (Pooladian et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2023), learned couplings with independent sampling (Lee et al., 2023), and Schrödinger bridge formulations (De Bortoli et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023a), while subsuming them as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clean, simulation-free training recipe (standard square-loss regression) for conditional generative models within the stochastic interpolant formalism, with a theoretical transport-cost bound showing that data-dependent couplings provably reduce the upper bound on expected displacement. The practical impact is demonstrated through competitive FIDs on ImageNet inpainting and super-resolution, but the coupling design remains task-specific and hand-crafted, leaving open the question of whether learned or adaptive couplings could further improve performance. The paper also highlights potential applications in molecule generation and autoencoder error correction, suggesting the coupling-vs-conditioning distinction could generalize beyond image tasks.
