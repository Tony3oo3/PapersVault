## Problem

Existing non-stationary RL methods either explicitly model environmental change factors (requiring impractical prior knowledge) or rely on meta-learning that struggles with complex, unpredictable dynamics. This paper argues that non-stationarity propagates and accumulates through causal relationships during state transitions, and that tracing the causal origin of changes—rather than explicitly modeling them—offers a more robust path to stable policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242448/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw state vector s ∈ R^{d_s} from the environment, transforms it via an MLP into N node features (each of dimension d_f), and constructs a weighted adjacency matrix from pairwise node similarities. It outputs a causal-origin graph representation G(s) ∈ R^{N×2d_g} (concatenation of core-GAT and general-GAT node embeddings), which is further compressed through a VAE into a latent h ∈ R^{d_h} that conditions the policy π(a|s,h) to produce an action. The graph structure is not fixed a priori; the number of nodes N and feature dimensions are environment-dependent hyperparameters.

## Method

COREP learns a causal-origin representation via a dual Graph Attention Network: a core-GAT that captures the stable, environment-shared union graph (a MAG over state, hidden state, action, and reward nodes) and a general-GAT that continuously adapts to compensate for information the core-GAT overlooks. The core-GAT's updates are gated by a TD-error detection mechanism—its weights are frozen when recent TD errors fall within a confidence interval of the buffer mean, and unfrozen only when a significant environmental shift is detected. The causal-origin representation is embedded in a VAE framework for efficient learning, with regularization terms enforcing MAG structural constraints (symmetry penalty for bidirected edges) and sparsity. Theoretically, the paper proves that the environment-shared union graph across sub-environments is itself a valid MAG, providing identifiability guarantees for the learned structure.

## Results

COREP is evaluated on 12 environments from the DeepMind Control Suite with non-stationary noise injected via periodic and random coefficient perturbations (Eq. 4.1). Across all environments, COREP achieves the highest mean return and lowest variance among all baselines. In the 6 environments with tabulated final returns, COREP outperforms the SOTA non-stationary baseline FN-VAE by 33.1 (Cartpole Swingup: 743.4 vs 710.3), 51.3 (Reacher Easy: 964.6 vs 913.3), 19.1 (Reacher Hard: 947.2 vs 928.1), 26.2 (Cup Catch: 877.5 vs 851.3), 44.6 (Cheetah Run: 651.1 vs 606.5), and 64.6 (Hopper Stand: 645.5 vs 580.9) points. The performance gap between COREP and an Oracle with full non-stationarity information is notably narrow, indicating effective mitigation of non-stationarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COREP consistently outperforms all three baselines across all 12 evaluated environments. Against FN-VAE (the SOTA non-stationary RL method), COREP achieves higher mean returns in every environment, with the largest margins in complex high-dimensional tasks (e.g., +64.6 on Hopper Stand, +44.6 on Cheetah Run) and substantially lower variance (e.g., ±21.2 vs ±64.5 on Cartpole Swingup), indicating greater stability. Against VariBAD (a meta-RL method with some non-stationarity handling), COREP shows both higher returns and much tighter variance, whereas VariBAD exhibits large performance swings across seeds. Against vanilla PPO (no non-stationarity handling), COREP achieves dramatically higher returns, confirming that the causal-origin representation is essential for coping with the injected non-stationarity. The narrow gap between COREP and an Oracle with full access to non-stationarity information further validates that COREP effectively recovers the relevant environmental structure without requiring explicit change detection or prior knowledge of sub-environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

COREP departs from the dominant line of non-stationary RL work (FN-VAE, LILAC, VariBAD) that explicitly models environmental change factors, instead proposing to implicitly trace the causal origin of non-stationarity through a learned graph representation. The theoretical foundation draws on causal structure discovery from mixtures of DAGs (Saeed et al., 2020), extending the environment-shared union graph concept to RL dynamics with hidden states. Architecturally, it repurposes GATs in a dual-structure (core + general) with a TD-error-guided freezing mechanism, a design not previously applied to non-stationary RL, and wraps the representation in a VAE for efficient learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal-origin representation offers a modular, plug-in approach to non-stationarity that avoids the impractical requirement of prior knowledge about environmental changes, and the TD-error-guided update elegantly eliminates explicit change-point detection. However, scalability in high-dimensional state spaces remains a significant limitation due to the graph-based representation, and all experiments use synthetic non-stationarity on DeepMind Control Suite rather than genuinely complex real-world dynamics. Open questions include whether the causal-origin representation generalizes to partially observable settings, multi-agent non-stationarity, and whether integrating with normalizing flows or probabilistic graphical models (as the authors suggest) can overcome the scalability bottleneck.
