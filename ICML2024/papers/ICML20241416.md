## Problem

Learning contextualized optimal transport plans—predicting how a source distribution shifts under an unseen perturbation—has relied on Brenier's theorem (restricting to squared Euclidean cost), unstable min-max training, or architectures that cannot generalize to new contexts. No quantum computing formulation existed for amortized optimization of contextualized transportation plans, and parametrically producing doubly stochastic matrices (DSMs) remains an open challenge in classical ML, typically requiring iterative non-parametric methods like Sinkhorn.

## Input / Output

The method takes a context vector p (e.g., drug dosage in [0,1]) and an initial quasi-probability distribution μ over d cell-type clusters (d must be a power of 2 to match circuit qubit count) as input, and produces a d×d transportation plan T whose row marginals equal μ and whose column marginals give the predicted target distribution ν. The circuit naturally emits a doubly stochastic matrix; for generic transport maps, a right stochastic sub-matrix is extracted via an 'atop' aggregation over the top quadrants of a 2d×2d DSM and rescaled by diag(μ) at inference. The relaxed assignment problem variant outputs the DSM directly without rescaling.

## Method

The core insight is that for any unitary U, the Hadamard product U⊙U̅ is a doubly stochastic matrix, providing a 'quantum inductive bias' that enforces row/column sum constraints by construction. A parametric quantum circuit U_p(p, θ) on m+n qubits is applied to maximally entangled states |b_m⟩⊗|b_n⟩; tracing out the m auxiliary qubits yields a density matrix ρ whose projective measurements give DSM entries as a convex combination of unistochastic matrices (via the Operator-Schmidt decomposition of U_p). For transport maps, the right stochastic matrix is embedded into the top quadrants of a 2d×2d DSM using a checkerboard ansatz that enforces Q₃=Q₂ and Q₄=Q₁, then rescaled by the user-provided μ. Training uses gradient-free COBYLA optimization over the Frobenius distance between predicted and ground-truth plans, with 2(n+m+1) qubits total (e.g., 24 qubits for 8×8 DSMs on IBM hardware).

## Results

On the SciPlex dataset (real scRNA-seq data with drug dosages as context), QontOT-LT achieves SAE 0.78 and Frob 0.61 on Mocetinostat, while CellOT (unconditional, 5 separate models) achieves SAE 0.46 and Frob 0.41, and CondOT achieves SAE 0.45 and Frob 0.40. However, on marginal metrics, QontOT-LM matches or exceeds both: L2 0.16 vs. 0.17 (CellOT) and R2 0.57 vs. 0.52 (CellOT) on Mocetinostat. In the headline 24-qubit hardware experiment on the contextual relaxed assignment problem (predicting 8×8 DSMs), QontOT with 124 gradient-free-optimized parameters outperforms all 15 tested NeuCOT configurations (ranging from 3k to 5M parameters trained with ADAM) on Frobenius distance, despite device noise, no error mitigation, and only 8192 shots per iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CellOT (Bunne et al., 2023), QontOT loses on transportation plan metrics (SAE and Frobenius norm) by a notable margin (e.g., SAE 0.78 vs. 0.46 on Mocetinostat), but this comparison is somewhat unfair since CellOT is an unconditional model with five separately trained networks (one per dosage condition). When CellOT is forced to aggregate across conditions (CellOT-homo), its performance drops below QontOT-LT on SAE (0.68 vs. 0.78) and substantially on R2 (0.37 vs. 0.49). Against CondOT (Bunne et al., 2022), which uses partial ICNNs and is parameterized by dosage, QontOT again trails on OT plan metrics (Frob 0.61 vs. 0.40) but is competitive or superior on marginal metrics: QontOT-LM achieves R2 0.57 vs. CondOT's 0.56 on Mocetinostat and L2 0.17 vs. 0.32 on Pracinostat. The most striking comparison is in the 24-qubit hardware experiment on the contextual relaxed assignment problem, where QontOT (124 parameters, gradient-free COBYLA optimization) outperforms all 15 NeuCOT configurations (3k–5M parameters, ADAM with backpropagation) on Frobenius distance, suggesting a quantum inductive bias advantage for DSM prediction specifically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying an algebraic link between unitary operators and doubly stochastic matrices (DSMs), thereby bridging quantum computing with optimal transport for the first time. It departs from the dominant neural OT lineage (CellOT, CondOT, Makkuva et al.) that relies on Brenier's theorem and input-convex neural networks, instead exploiting the fact that the Hadamard product of a unitary with its conjugate yields a DSM — a 'quantum inductive bias' that enforces row/column stochasticity by construction. The contextual OT task itself builds on CondOT (Bunne et al., 2022) and the Monge Gap (Uscidda & Cuturi, 2023), but the quantum formulation is cost-agnostic and produces explicit transport plans rather than push-forwarded measures. The paper also introduces NeuCOT, a classical quantum-inspired baseline that uses row-softmax to produce right-stochastic matrices, filling a gap between Brenier-based methods and the quantum approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24-qubit hardware experiment showing QontOT outperforming all 15 NeuCOT configurations on contextual DSM prediction — with only 124 gradient-free parameters versus up to 5M in the classical models — is a compelling early signal that the quantum inductive bias for DSMs may confer a genuine structural advantage. However, the evidence is limited: the problem is classically simulable at 24 qubits, uses a small dataset of 40 8×8 DSMs, and lacks error mitigation, so it remains unclear whether the advantage persists at scale. Open questions include whether the quantum advantage is robust to larger qubit counts, whether the approach extends to unbalanced or multidimensional OT, and whether the DSM-prediction framing (more natural for the quantum ansatz) translates to the broader transport-map prediction task where classical methods currently lead. The connection to Sinkformers and other architectures where DSMs emerge naturally suggests a broader applicability beyond single-cell biology.
