## Problem

End users who download pre-trained language models from third parties face backdoor threats where injected triggers cause misclassification even after fine-tuning on clean data. Existing defenses either require knowledge of the trigger pattern, depend on access to a guaranteed clean PLM, or rely on maximum entropy loss that fails against strong attacks like layer-wise weight poisoning. The paper tackles the more general and practical scenario where the defender has no information about the attack and no clean reference model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a potentially poisoned encoder-only PLM (BERT_BASE, RoBERTa, or DistilBERT) and a private clean text dataset split into training, validation, and test subsets. It produces a purified pre-trained model whose attention heads have been pruned and whose [CLS] attention has been regularized, ready for standard fine-tuning on the user's downstream classification task. The critical constraint is that the defender has zero knowledge of the trigger, target class, or poisoning details, and no access to any clean PLM for weight mixing.

## Method

PURE operates in two stages. First, it greedily prunes attention heads by iteratively removing the head with the lowest [CLS] attention variance on clean validation data, stopping when clean accuracy drops below a threshold c; the key insight is that trigger-hijacked heads exhibit near-uniform (low-variance) [CLS] attention on clean inputs while showing extreme focus drifting on poisoned inputs. Second, it fine-tunes the pruned model with an additional L2-norm regularization term on [CLS] attention weights across all remaining heads, using layer-specific coefficients λ_l = -log₂(Mean_head(var_h^l)) to apply stronger normalization to layers that originally contained more low-variance heads. This prevents surviving heads from over-concentrating on specific tokens during fine-tuning, complementing the pruning step to eliminate residual backdoor effects.

## Results

PURE achieves the lowest Label Flip Rate (LFR) across all rare-word-based attacks on BERT, RoBERTa, and DistilBERT in both Domain Shift and Full Data Knowledge scenarios, while maintaining clean accuracy (ACC) within ~1-2% of vanilla fine-tuning. For example, on BERT with BadNet (IMDB→SST-2), PURE reduces LFR from 83.18% (FT) to 9.53% with 91.46% ACC. Against the strongest attack LWP (IMDB→SST-2), PURE achieves 14.57% LFR versus 98.27% (FT), 85.96% (FTH), and 88.46% (MEFT). Statistical significance is confirmed with p < 0.01 for BERT and p < 0.05 for DistilBERT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PURE consistently outperforms both FTH (Kurita et al., 2020) and MEFT (Liu et al., 2023) on LFR across all three rare-word-based attacks and all three scenarios for BERT. Against FTH, the gap is most dramatic under LWP: PURE achieves 14.57% LFR versus 85.96% for FTH on IMDB→SST-2, a reduction of over 70 percentage points. Against MEFT, PURE also leads substantially on LWP (14.57% vs. 88.46% on IMDB→SST-2), where MEFT essentially fails to neutralize the backdoor. On BadNet and RIPPLe in the Domain Shift scenarios, PURE and MEFT are closer (e.g., 9.53% vs. 20.14% for BadNet IMDB→SST-2), but PURE still wins. In the Full Data Knowledge scenario, both baselines degrade severely (LFR > 85% for LWP), while PURE still reduces LFR to 71.07%, though this remains the weakest setting for all methods. Clean accuracy is preserved within 1-2% of vanilla fine-tuning across all comparisons, confirming that the defense does not sacrifice utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-anomaly-based defense of Lyu et al. (2022), who showed that trigger tokens hijack [CLS] attention in specific BERT heads and that pruning those heads mitigates backdoors when triggers are known. PURE's key conceptual departure is replacing trigger knowledge with a variance-based greedy pruning criterion: heads that exhibit attention focus drifting on poisoned inputs are identified by their anomalously low [CLS] attention variance on clean inputs, enabling defense without any prior knowledge of the attack. The method also departs from weight-mixing approaches (Fine-Mixing, Fine-Purifying) that require a guaranteed clean PLM, and from maximum-entropy fine-tuning (Liu et al., 2023), which the authors show is ineffective against layer-wise poisoning and syntactic/style triggers. The L2-norm attention regularization is a new complementary term that addresses heads with high clean variance that nonetheless remain susceptible to triggers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PURE addresses a realistic and under-served threat model: end users downloading PLMs from third parties with no trigger knowledge and no access to clean reference models, a scenario where most prior defenses (trigger inversion, clean-model mixing) are inapplicable. The method is practical and lightweight (head pruning + a single regularization term during fine-tuning), making it deployable without architectural changes. However, its effectiveness degrades notably against layer-wise weight poisoning in full-data-knowledge scenarios (LFR ~71% vs. ~10% for BadNet) and against syntactic/style-based triggers, where even a clean model shows elevated LFR due to semantic distortion from paraphrasing tools. Open questions include generalization to decoder-only or generative models, multi-class or sequence-level tasks, and whether the variance-based pruning criterion remains reliable under more adaptive or multi-trigger attacks.
