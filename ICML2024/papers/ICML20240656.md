## Problem

A large class of kernel methods for density ratio estimation (KuLSIF, logistic regression, exponential, and square loss approaches) suffers from error saturation: their convergence rates plateau at (m+n)^(-2/3) regardless of how regular the underlying problem is, preventing them from achieving the optimal rates available for highly smooth density ratios. This paper identifies this saturation as a fundamental limitation of single-step Tikhonov regularization in the Bregman-divergence framework and shows how to provably overcome it.

## Input / Output

The method takes two i.i.d. sample sets drawn from probability measures P and Q (m samples from P, n from Q) and produces a density ratio estimate β̂ = g(f_z^{λ,t}) in an RKHS with a continuous bounded kernel. In the domain adaptation application, the input is a labeled source dataset and an unlabeled target dataset, and the output is a set of ensemble weights c* that combine pre-trained domain adaptation models via importance-weighted functional regression. The regularization parameter λ is chosen a priori from the regularity index r, and the iteration count t (up to 10 in experiments) is selected by cross-validation on the Bregman objective.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the standard Tikhonov penalty ‖f‖² with an iterated penalty ‖f − f^{λ,t}‖², where f^{λ,0} = 0 and each iterate f^{λ,t+1} is the empirical risk minimizer of the Bregman divergence plus the shifted quadratic penalty. This iterated Tikhonov regularization, inspired by the Bregman method and Bregmanization from inverse problems, allows the solution to progressively refine toward the true density ratio across t steps. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves a non-saturating error bound of (m+n)^(-2sα/(2sα+1)) with s = min{r+1/2, t}, which is optimal for square loss and strictly improves upon the saturating (m+n)^(-2/3) rate when t ≥ r+1/2. Computationally, each iteration is solved via the representer theorem and nonlinear conjugate gradient (Polak-Ribière line search), except for KuLSIF which admits a closed-form coefficient update. The approach is validated on synthetic datasets with known regularity, Gaussian mixture benchmarks, and large-scale domain adaptation experiments (9000+ trained networks) across text, image, and sensor modalities.

## Results

On the Amazon Reviews domain adaptation benchmark (12 tasks, 11 DA methods), iterated regularization improves all four density ratio estimators: KuLSIF from 0.787 to 0.790, Exp from 0.563 to 0.565, LR from 0.785 to 0.788, and SQ from 0.219 to 0.221 average target accuracy. On geometrically constructed datasets with known density ratios, iterated SQ reduces the Bregman divergence error from 11.810 to 11.400, and iterated Exp from 18.775 to 12.890 (average over 10 datasets). The iterated KuLSIF also surpasses the SOTA aggregation method of Dinu et al. (2023) (0.788) with 0.790 average accuracy on Amazon Reviews.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-iterated KuLSIF (Kanamori et al., 2009), the iterated version consistently wins: 0.790 vs. 0.787 on Amazon Reviews, 0.530 vs. 0.528 on MiniDomainNet, and 0.779 vs. 0.737 on HHAR (a 4.2-point gain on sensory data). Against non-iterated LR (Bickel et al., 2009), iteration improves accuracy on Amazon Reviews (0.788 vs. 0.785) and MiniDomainNet (0.525 vs. 0.523) but ties on HHAR (0.772 both). Against non-iterated Exp and SQ (Menon & Ong, 2016), iteration improves Exp on Amazon Reviews (0.565 vs. 0.563) and HHAR (0.700 vs. 0.694), and improves SQ on Amazon Reviews (0.221 vs. 0.219), while MiniDomainNet and HHAR SQ results are essentially tied. Against the SOTA aggregation method of Dinu et al. (2023), iterated KuLSIF achieves 0.790 average accuracy on Amazon Reviews versus 0.788 for the SOTA, while non-iterated KuLSIF (0.787) falls slightly below. In 9 out of 12 averaged comparisons across all datasets and DA methods, iterated regularization strictly improves over its non-iterated counterpart, and it never causes overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterated Tikhonov regularization framework from supervised learning (Beugnot et al., 2021) to density ratio estimation, leveraging the Bregman divergence formulation of Sugiyama et al. (2012b) and the duality between density ratio estimation and binary classification established by Menon & Ong (2016). The core technical tool—replacing the standard Tikhonov penalty ‖f‖² with the iterated penalty ‖f − f^{λ,t}‖²—comes from inverse problems literature (King & Chillingworth, 1979; Scherzer, 1993), and the error analysis builds on the self-concordance-based fast rates of Marteau-Ferey et al. (2019b). The practical application setting is the importance-weighted ensembling framework of Dinu et al. (2023) for unsupervised domain adaptation, where density ratio estimates serve as importance weights for model aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably non-saturating error bounds for a broad class of kernel density ratio estimation methods, which is a meaningful theoretical gap since saturation prevents optimal convergence rates on highly regular problems. Practically, the iterated methods yield consistent (if modest) improvements in domain adaptation accuracy across text, image, and sensor benchmarks, and the approach is computationally cheap (t ≤ 10 iterations). Key open questions include: how to select the number of iterations without a priori knowledge of the regularity index r, whether the theory extends to finite-width deep networks (where additional implicit regularizations break the RKHS analysis), and whether time-dependent Bregman divergences (Kim et al., 2023) can be combined with iteration for time-series domain adaptation.
