## Problem

Existing protein language models face a fundamental trade-off: masked LMs (e.g., ESM2) excel at sequence understanding but lack a principled generative formulation, while autoregressive LMs can generate but suffer from unidirectional context that limits their ability to capture global residue interactions in proteins. The paper argues that a powerful generative model that learns the underlying data distribution should simultaneously acquire deep understanding, motivating a unified framework that combines scalable generative modeling with bidirectional receptive fields.

## Input / Output

The model consumes protein sequences over a 20-amino-acid vocabulary, truncated to 1024 tokens, and operates in a discrete state space where each position is either an amino acid or an absorbing mask token [X]. For conditional generation, it accepts partial peptide sequences (with fixed motif positions), 3D backbone structures encoded by a frozen GVP-Transformer, or secondary structure annotations via a plug-and-play classifier. Outputs include full amino acid sequences (sampled over 500 denoising steps), per-residue embeddings (L×d) for downstream prediction, and structure-conditioned sequences evaluated by AAR, scTM, and pLDDT.

## Method

DPLM employs absorbing discrete diffusion (specifically the reparameterized discrete diffusion framework of Zheng et al., 2023a) as its generative pre-training objective, where the forward process progressively masks tokens toward a stationary [X] distribution and the backward process denoises via a weighted cross-entropy loss that subsumes both MLM and AR-LM as special cases. The architecture is a standard transformer (150M/650M/3B parameters, matching ESM2) trained on ~45M UniRef50 sequences using a two-stage strategy: initial MLM pre-training followed by diffusion fine-tuning to avoid instability from extreme masking ratios. For conditional generation, the paper introduces three mechanisms: (1) sequence conditioning by fixing observed tokens during denoising, (2) cross-modal conditioning via a lightweight cross-attention adapter tuned on paired data while freezing both DPLM and the modality encoder, and (3) a novel discrete classifier guidance that approximates the continuous score-based guidance via first-order Taylor expansion on the probability simplex, enabling plug-and-play steering toward arbitrary user-defined properties without retraining.

## Results

On protein predictive downstream tasks (Table 1), DPLM-3B outperforms ESM2-650M across all 10 tasks, e.g., HumanPPI 90.00% vs. 84.78%, Metal Ion Binding 75.94% vs. 71.88%, and GO CC 0.463 vs. 0.402 Fmax, while closely approaching structure-aware SaProt (e.g., Thermostability 0.704 vs. 0.724 Spearman's ρ). For unconditional generation, DPLM achieves the highest average pLDDT (~81.18) across lengths 100–1000, surpassing both EvoDiff and UniRef50 natural sequences, while also producing more novel (higher pdb-TM) and diverse (lower inner-TM) structures. On inverse folding (CATH 4.2/4.3), DPLM-3B with GVP-Transformer encoder attains 59.44% AAR, 0.86 scTM, and 77.12 pLDDT, outperforming LM-DESIGN (56.49% AAR, 0.85 scTM, 74.89 pLDDT). For motif-scaffolding, DPLM solves more problems and achieves higher average success rate than EvoDiff, and with structure conditioning matches or exceeds RFDiffusion on 6 of 17 problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPLM-3B outperforms ESM2-650M (the dominant masked-LM baseline) on every predictive task in Table 1, with the largest gains on HumanPPI (+5.22 Acc%) and GO CC (+0.061 Fmax), and also surpasses the AR-LM baseline by wide margins (e.g., HumanPPI 90.00% vs. 68.48%). DPLM-3B closely approaches structure-aware SaProt (which uses explicit Foldseek structural embeddings), trailing by only 0.020 on Thermostability and 0.23% on DeepLoc Subcellular, suggesting DPLM implicitly captures structural information from sequence alone. For unconditional generation, DPLM achieves higher pLDDT than EvoDiff at all lengths and produces more novel structures (higher pdb-TM) while maintaining greater diversity (lower inner-TM). On inverse folding, DPLM-3B (59.44% AAR, 0.86 scTM, 77.12 pLDDT) outperforms LM-DESIGN (56.49%, 0.85, 74.89) and all structure-based baselines (ProteinMPNN, GVP, PiFold, GVP-Transformer). For motif-scaffolding, DPLM solves more of the 17 benchmark problems than EvoDiff with higher average success rate, and with structure conditioning beats RFDiffusion on 6 problems, though RFDiffusion retains an edge on motifs with rigid helical structures. The AR-LM baseline (same 650M architecture with causal masking) is substantially worse on all predictive tasks, confirming the paper's argument that unidirectional context limits protein understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPLM sits at the intersection of discrete diffusion probabilistic models (Austin et al., 2021; Hoogeboom et al., 2021b) and protein language modeling (ESM2, Lin et al., 2022), adopting the reparameterized discrete diffusion framework of Zheng et al. (2023a) for its training and sampling. It departs from EvoDiff (Alamdari et al., 2023), the closest prior protein diffusion model, by using a more principled absorbing diffusion formulation (rather than order-agnostic autoregressive diffusion), demonstrating strong representation learning, and introducing discrete classifier guidance for controllable generation. The cross-modal conditioning strategy follows the adapter-tuning approach of Zheng et al. (2023b), while the plug-and-play classifier guidance adapts the first-order Taylor expansion technique from continuous diffusion (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) to the discrete setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is showing that a generative diffusion pre-training objective yields better protein representations than the standard masked language modeling objective, supporting the intuition that 'what you cannot create, you do not understand.' This positions DPLM as a unified foundation model for both generation and prediction, reducing the need for separate specialized models. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (MLM warmup then diffusion) is truly necessary or an artifact of training instability, whether DPLM can natively integrate 3D structure (as SaProt does explicitly), and how the discrete classifier guidance extends to more complex user preferences like binding affinity or thermostability. The 1024-token limit and the need for ESMFold as an external folding oracle for evaluation also constrain practical applicability to very long or de novo proteins.
