## Problem

While Vision Transformers are well-known for their strong robustness and large kernel convnets (e.g., RepLKNet with 31×31 kernels) have recently matched or exceeded ViT performance on standard benchmarks, it remains unclear whether large kernel convnets are inherently robust and what mechanisms underlie their robustness. This gap is critical for practical deployment in safety-critical applications where robustness is a prerequisite.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240300/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluated models consume standard RGB images at 224×224 resolution (384×384 for the RepLKNet-31L variant) and produce ImageNet-1K class labels. Robustness is quantified through diverse metrics: top-1 accuracy on adversarial/corruption/semantic-shift datasets, mean corruption error (mCE), mean flip rate (mFR), mean top-5 distance (mT5D), AUPR for out-of-distribution detection, and background-gap scores. Occlusion experiments operate at the patch level (16×16 patches, 196 patches per image), masking subsets of patches to zero, while adversarial attacks apply l∞-bounded pixel perturbations with budgets ranging from 0.002 to 0.4.

## Method

This is a comprehensive empirical study centered on RepLKNet-31B (31×31 kernels), evaluated against ViT-B/L, BiT, and ResNet-50 across six robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-A, -C, -O, -P, -R, -9). The paper then designs nine targeted experiments to attribute robustness to specific architectural properties: (1) occlusion invariance under random, salient (foreground), and non-salient (background) patch-drop; (2) adversarial robustness under FGSM, PGD, and the transferable TAIG attack; (3) a lesion study removing blocks to probe redundancy; (4) frequency-domain noise sensitivity; (5) a kernel-size ablation replacing all 31×31 convolutions with 3×3; (6) kernel attention distance analysis showing large kernels attend to both local and global information at shallow layers and predominantly global information at deep layers—mirroring ViT's self-attention pattern; (7) feature map variance stability across depth; (8) scaling validation with ConvNeXt and larger RepLKNet variants; and (9) progressive kernel-size scaling (3→51) on ConvNeXt-T. The central finding is that large kernel size itself—not self-attention—is the critical factor enabling ViT-comparable robustness, operating through a combination of local-global receptive field coverage, stable early-layer feature maps, and uniform frequency-domain noise resistance.

## Results

RepLKNet-31B (31×31 kernels) achieves 29.4% top-1 on ImageNet-A, 49.2% mean top-1 on ImageNet-C (mCE 36.5%), 78.9% AUPR on ImageNet-O, 43.9% top-1 on ImageNet-R, and 97.3% origin accuracy on ImageNet-9, consistently matching or exceeding ViT-L/16 across all six robustness benchmarks. On ImageNet-P, RepLKNet-31B achieves mFR 29.2% and mT5D 49.8%, outperforming ViT-L (33.1/50.2) and AugMix (37.4/N/A). Scaling to RepLKNet-31L (384×384 pretraining) further improves to 39.6% on ImageNet-A and 49.1% on ImageNet-R, while ConvNeXt-L (7×7 kernels) reaches 38.7% and 47.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against typical small-kernel CNNs, RepLKNet-31B dominates: it outperforms BiT-m r101×3 by 2.8× on ImageNet-A (29.4% vs. 10.5%) and reduces mCE from 58.3% to 36.5% on ImageNet-C. Compared to ViT-L/16, RepLKNet-31B matches or slightly exceeds on ImageNet-A (29.4% vs. 28.1%) and ImageNet-O (78.9% AUPR), while ViT-L retains a small edge on ImageNet-C (49.9% vs. 49.2% mean top-1) and ImageNet-R (40.6% vs. 43.9% — actually RepLKNet wins here). Against ViT-B/16, RepLKNet-31B wins on every benchmark by clear margins (e.g., 29.4% vs. 26.7% on ImageNet-A, 43.9% vs. 38.0% on ImageNet-R). RepLKNet-31B also surpasses dedicated robustness-enhancing methods: its mCE of 36.5% beats DeepAugment+AugMix (53.6%) and its mFR of 29.2% beats AugMix alone (37.4%). Among large-kernel CNNs, ConvNeXt-L (7×7) slightly edges RepLKNet-31B on ImageNet-A (38.7% vs. 29.4%) and ImageNet-C (56.5% vs. 49.2%), but RepLKNet-31L (384×384) recovers the lead at 39.6% and 52.6%, confirming that kernel size and model scale jointly drive robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the robustness analysis of Vision Transformers (Bhojanapalli et al. 2021; Shao et al. 2021; Paul & Chen 2022), which established ViTs as inherently more robust than CNNs, and the recent revival of large kernel convnets (RepLKNet, ConvNeXt) that demonstrated competitive accuracy with ViTs. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper repurposes the established robustness evaluation framework (six ImageNet benchmark suites) and applies it to large kernel convnets, revealing that the robustness advantage previously attributed to self-attention can be replicated through large receptive field convolution. The mechanistic analyses—kernel attention distance, frequency-domain noise sensitivity, and occlusion invariance—draw on tools from prior ViT interpretability work (Raghu et al. 2021; Park & Kim 2022) but apply them to a fundamentally different inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that pure CNNs with large kernels achieve robustness comparable or superior to ViTs across six diverse benchmarks—challenges the prevailing narrative that self-attention is the necessary ingredient for strong robustness, opening the door to more efficient and deployable robust models. The kernel attention analysis (local at shallow layers, global at deep layers) and the uniform frequency-domain robustness provide mechanistic explanations that could guide future architecture design. Limitations include the absence of theoretical proofs and the kernel-size ablation being conducted only on ImageNet-1K rather than ImageNet-21K, leaving open whether the robustness gains fully transfer under stronger pre-training. A key open question is whether the robustness stems purely from receptive field size or from specific properties of the large-kernel parameterization, and whether this extends to non-ImageNet domains and downstream tasks like detection and segmentation.
