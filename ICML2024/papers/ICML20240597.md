## Problem

Online Vision-and-Language Navigation requires an agent to sequentially execute instructions in dynamically changing environments, creating an adaptability-stability dilemma: frequent per-step model updates cause cumulative errors and catastrophic forgetting, while infrequent updates leave the model unable to track environmental shifts. Existing TTA methods, designed for single-shot classification, do not account for VLN's dual structure of inter-sample online execution and intra-sample multi-step action decisions, and simply applying them yields negligible or even negative gains.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a natural language instruction, a 36-image panoramic view encoded by a pre-trained ViT, a progressively constructed undirected navigable graph, and a history encoding of prior observations and actions. The model outputs a probability distribution over currently navigable nodes (including a STOP node), from which the next action is selected. For adaptation, only the affine parameters of the last four layer-norm layers (768-dim each) are updated via entropy minimization on the action distribution, with batch size 1 and samples fed sequentially to simulate online execution.

## Method

FSTTA performs a unified decomposition-accumulation analysis on both gradients (fast phase) and parameters (slow phase) using SVD-derived local coordinate systems. In the fast phase, gradients from M consecutive steps are centered and decomposed; an adaptive coefficient (inverse eigenvalue) suppresses divergent components while preserving concordant directions, and the resulting update direction is length-calibrated and scaled by a dynamic learning rate that shrinks when the gradient covariance trace deviates from its running mean. In the slow phase, the model reverts to a historical checkpoint, and the parameter trajectory across N samples is decomposed via SVD; an exponentially weighted reference direction (Eq. 8) orients the principal axes, and the slow update directly modulates parameters along these axes with a fixed learning rate. The two phases alternate during testing, balancing short-term adaptability against long-term stability without requiring any labeled data.

## Results

On REVERIE Val Unseen, FSTTA applied to DUET achieves SR=54.15% and SPL=36.41%, improving over the base DUET (SR=46.98%, SPL=33.73%) by 7.2% and 2.7% respectively, and outperforming the best TTA baseline SAR (SR=48.00%, SPL=33.92%) by 6.2% and 2.5%. On R2R Val Unseen, DUET-FSTTA reaches SR=75% and SPL=62% (vs. DUET's 72%/60%), while on SOON Val Unseen, HM3D-FSTTA sets a new SOTA with SR=42.44% and SPL=31.03%. The method also generalizes to continuous environments (R2R-CE), where DUET-FSTTA improves SR from 46% to 48% and SPL from 40% to 42%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSTTA decisively outperforms all six TTA baselines (EATA, CoTTA, NOTE, SAR, ViDA, TENT) on REVERIE Val Unseen, achieving 54.15% SR vs. the best baseline SAR at 48.00% (+6.2%) and 36.41% SPL vs. SAR's 33.92% (+2.5%), while also reducing inference time by 7% compared to SAR. Against VLN model baselines, FSTTA consistently improves its base models: DUET-FSTTA beats DUET by 7.2% SR on REVERIE Val Unseen and 3% SR on R2R Val Unseen, while HM3D-FSTTA surpasses HM3D by 1.1% SR on REVERIE and 1% SR on R2R. On SOON, HM3D-FSTTA establishes new SOTA (SR=42.44%, SPL=31.03%), substantially outperforming GridMM (SR=37.46%, SPL=24.81%) and AZHP (SR=40.71%, SPL=26.58%). On R2R-CE, FSTTA improves base models (DUET: 46→48% SR; BEVBert: 59→60% SR) but remains below the strongest non-TTA methods like ETPNav (57% SR) and GridMM (49% SR) on Val Unseen. The method also demonstrates no catastrophic forgetting: after adapting on unseen data, the model retains comparable performance on seen data without further TTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time adaptation (TTA) from the standard single-sample classification setting to the sequential, multi-step structure of online Vision-and-Language Navigation. It builds directly on TENT's entropy-minimization objective and the DUET dual-scale graph transformer as the base model, but departs from prior TTA work (EATA, CoTTA, NOTE, SAR, ViDA) by addressing the unique inter-sample online execution and intra-sample multi-step action decision that VLN requires. The gradient decomposition-accumulation analysis draws on SVD-based techniques from domain generalization (e.g., PGrad, FISH), but repurposes them within a two-phase fast-slow framework that has no direct analogue in prior TTA or DG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of online TTA for VLN, demonstrating that naive application of existing TTA methods either fails to help or actively degrades performance, while a principled fast-slow decomposition yields substantial gains (7.1% SR on REVERIE val unseen) across four benchmarks and two base models. Key limitations include restricting adaptation to normalization layers only, simulating the online setting by shuffling test samples rather than using a true streaming environment, and using fixed periodic update frequencies. Open questions include whether adaptive (non-periodic) update invocation, adaptation of non-normalization layers, and more realistic online learning datasets would further improve or alter the conclusions.
