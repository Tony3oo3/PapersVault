## Problem

SAM cannot autonomously segment specific objects without manual prompts (points or bounding boxes), and existing finetuning approaches like Med-SA and SAMed suffer from severe overfitting when labeled data is extremely limited (fewer than 10 examples), as is common in medical imaging. No prior method simultaneously eliminates the need for manual prompts and prevents overfitting in such ultra-low-data regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242449/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, BLO-SAM takes a single RGB or medical image as input with no prompts required, and outputs one binary segmentation mask per semantic class (multi-class tasks are decomposed into multiple binary tasks). During training, fewer than 10 labeled image-mask pairs are randomly split into two equal subsets D1 and D2. The model uses SAM's ViT-B image encoder by default, and the learnable prompt embedding serves as a fixed-size vector input to the prompt encoder, replacing any manual point or box prompts.

## Method

BLO-SAM finetunes SAM via a bi-level optimization framework where LoRA layers (added to query and value projections in the mask decoder's self-attention, image-to-token, and token-to-image attention blocks), transposed convolutions, and MLP heads constitute the lower-level parameters W, while a learnable prompt embedding vector A serves as the upper-level parameter. The training set is split into D1 and D2: the lower level optimizes W on D1 with A fixed, and the upper level optimizes A on D2 using the resulting W*(A), analogous to tuning hyperparameters on a validation set while training weights on a training set. Gradients for the upper level are computed via a one-step unrolled approximation with first-order (default) or second-order finite-difference correction. This separation of parameter updates across disjoint data subsets is the key mechanism that mitigates overfitting, and the end-to-end iterative optimization allows W and A to mutually influence each other for joint convergence.

## Results

On the Kvasir-SEG polyp segmentation task with only 4 training examples, BLO-SAM achieves a Dice score of 59.7%, substantially outperforming SAMed (42.7%), Med-SA (33.5%), and vanilla SAM (14.7%). On the JSRT lung segmentation task (4 examples), BLO-SAM reaches 93.7% Dice, surpassing SAMed (91.8%) and Med-SA (91.7%). On CelebAMask facial component segmentation (4 examples), BLO-SAM achieves 65.9% overall Dice versus 58.2% for SAMed and 32.9% for vanilla SAM. Across all six primary tasks, BLO-SAM consistently achieves the highest Dice score while requiring no manual prompts during inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLO-SAM outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of tasks. Against vanilla SAM, BLO-SAM achieves dramatic gains (e.g., 59.7% vs. 14.7% on Kvasir-4, 93.7% vs. 31.4% on Lung-4), confirming that LoRA-based finetuning with a learned prompt embedding effectively adapts SAM to domain-shifted data. Compared to the closest SAM-based competitors, BLO-SAM surpasses SAMed by 17.0 points on Kvasir-4 (59.7 vs. 42.7) and by 12.5 points on Human Body-4 (76.3 vs. 63.8), while matching or exceeding Med-SA on all tasks (e.g., 73.2 vs. 69.8 on Teeth-4). Against supervised baselines DeepLab and SwinUnet, BLO-SAM leads by 16–38 points on medical tasks (e.g., 59.7 vs. 31.9 on Kvasir-4), demonstrating the advantage of leveraging SAM's pretrained representations in few-shot regimes. Few-shot methods HSNet and SSP are competitive on Lung segmentation (SSP reaches 90.1% at 4 examples vs. BLO-SAM's 93.7%) but fall significantly behind on Kvasir and Teeth. LISA, which uses text prompts via a large language model, is outperformed by BLO-SAM by 10–25 points across all tested tasks (e.g., 73.2 vs. 47.8 on Teeth-4). UNet, included as an additional baseline in the appendix, is also surpassed by BLO-SAM on both body and teeth segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAM as a segmentation foundation model and LoRA for parameter-efficient finetuning, but departs from prior SAM-adaptation methods (Med-SA, SAMed) in two key ways: it eliminates the need for manual prompts by learning a prompt embedding, and it introduces a bi-level optimization framework inspired by hyperparameter tuning (Franceschi et al., 2018) and DARTS-style unrolled gradient computation (Liu et al., 2018). The conceptual reframing of the prompt embedding as a 'hyperparameter' that should be validated on a separate data split, rather than co-optimized with model weights on a single training set, is the paper's central methodological departure. This positions BLO-SAM as a principled overfitting-prevention strategy for foundation model adaptation, complementing rather than replacing parameter-efficient techniques like LoRA or Adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: finetuning large foundation models like SAM on extremely limited labeled data (fewer than 10 examples), a common scenario in medical imaging where expert annotation is expensive and privacy constraints limit data availability. The consistent improvements over SAMed and Med-SA across six diverse datasets, combined with the strong out-of-domain generalization results (e.g., a model trained on polyp segmentation achieving ~68% Dice on human body, car body, teeth, and lung tasks), suggest the BLO framework produces more robust, less overfit models. Limitations include evaluation only on the smallest SAM variant (ViT-B), testing exclusively in ultra-low-data regimes (<10 examples), and the added computational overhead of the two-level optimization. Open questions include whether the BLO framework generalizes to other foundation models (e.g., for language or video), whether it remains beneficial as training data scales up, and whether the specific choice of prompt embedding as the 'upper-level variable' is optimal versus other architectural choices.
