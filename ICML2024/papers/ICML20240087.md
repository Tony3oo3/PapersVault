## Problem

While SGD is known to avoid saddle points, no prior work characterizes the full long-run distribution of SGD over all critical components of a general non-convex objective—specifically, which minima, saddles, or maxima are visited most often and with what relative probability. Existing diffusion-approximation approaches fail in the small step-size regime relevant to SGD because the convergence rate of the limiting SDE to its invariant measure degrades exponentially as η→0, rendering the bounds vacuous.

## Input / Output

The framework requires a C² smooth, coercive objective f with a finite number of smoothly connected critical components, a stochastic gradient oracle whose noise is zero-mean, sub-Gaussian, and has positive definite covariance at every point, and a step-size η. The output is a complete characterization of the invariant measure μ∞^η: for each critical component K_i, the probability mass μ∞^η(U_i) in a small neighborhood is given by exp(−E_i/η) up to normalization, where E_i is an energy level computed from the quasi-potential (minimum action cost) of transitioning between all pairs of critical components. For Gaussian noise, the energy simplifies to E_i = 2f_i/σ², recovering the classical Boltzmann-Gibbs form.

## Method

The paper extends the Freidlin-Wentzell large deviations theory—originally for continuous-time diffusions on compact manifolds—to discrete-time SGD evolving on unbounded ℝ^d with general (possibly discrete) noise. The key construction is an action functional S_T[γ] = ∫ L_G(γ(t), γ̇(t)) dt built from the cumulant-generating function of the stochastic gradient, which quantifies the exponential cost of a trajectory deviating from the gradient flow. An LDP is established for the piecewise-linear interpolation of SGD iterates, then transferred to a subsampled (accelerated) sequence to handle the discrete-time setting. Transition costs B_ij between critical components are defined as infima of the action over connecting paths, and the energy E_i of each component is the minimum total weight of a spanning tree directed toward i in the complete weighted graph of transition costs. This yields the main result: the invariant measure over critical components follows an ε-approximate Boltzmann-Gibbs distribution at temperature η, with the ground state determined jointly by f and the noise statistics rather than by f alone.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that the invariant measure of SGD satisfies μ_∞^η(U_i) ∝ exp(−E_i/η), i.e., an ε-approximate Boltzmann-Gibbs distribution over critical components with temperature equal to the step-size η and energy levels E_i given by the minimum-weight spanning tree in the transition graph. For Gaussian noise with variance σ², the energy simplifies to E_i = 2f_i/σ² (Eq. 11), recovering the classical Gibbs measure. Theorem 3 shows the critical region is visited with probability ≥ 1 − e^{−c/η}, and Theorem 4 shows exponential concentration around the ground state K_0 = ∪_{i∈argmin E_i} K_i with the same rate. Theorem 2 further guarantees that every non-minimizing component (saddle or local max) is exponentially dominated by some minimizing component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or comparison tables. The paper positions its contribution against three prior analytical approaches to the long-run distribution of SGD. (1) The SGLD/Langevin approach (Raginsky et al. [54]) recovers SGD by setting β ∝ 1/η, but the convergence rate of the SDE to its invariant measure is exponential in β, making the discretization error bounds vacuous in the small-η regime relevant to SGD. (2) The diffusion approximation approach (Li et al. [39,40]) tracks SGD over finite time intervals, but the convergence speed of the induced SDE to its invariant measure degrades exponentially as η→0, precluding a global description. (3) The Markov chain approach (Dieuleveut et al. [11], Lu et al. [45], Gurbuzbalaban et al. [21]) characterizes ergodicity and heavy-tailed behavior near infinity but provides no information on which critical regions are visited. The present paper's large-deviations framework is the first to give a global, component-wise characterization of the invariant measure over all critical components of a general non-convex objective, without requiring strict-saddle assumptions or log-concavity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Freidlin-Wentzell large deviations framework for randomly perturbed dynamical systems [18] and its discrete-time extensions by Kifer [32, 33] to the setting of SGD on unbounded domains with general noise profiles. It departs from the dominant SDE-approximation approach (Langevin dynamics, diffusion approximations) which fails for constant step-size SGD because the convergence rate to the invariant measure degrades exponentially as η→0, rendering bounds vacuous. It also goes beyond Markov-chain ergodicity results [11, 45] that only quantify bias to the global minimum, and heavy-tail characterizations [21, 23, 52] that describe behavior only near infinity, by providing a full distributional characterization over all critical components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first characterization of the invariant measure of SGD over components of critical points in general non-convex settings, establishing a Boltzmann-Gibbs structure where the step-size acts as temperature and energy levels depend on both the objective and noise statistics. A key practical implication is that the ground state (most-visited critical component) need not coincide with the global minimum, and that flatness of a minimum (small Hessian eigenvalues) can dominate depth in determining relative visitation probabilities. The main open challenge is estimating the energy levels E_i in practice for high-dimensional problems, which would be needed to connect these theoretical predictions to generalization behavior.
