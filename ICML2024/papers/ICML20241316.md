## Problem

Deploying LLMs on mobile devices requires sub-billion parameter models that fit within 6–12 GB of DRAM, yet existing small models (OPT-125M, GPT-Neo-125M, Pythia-160M) are simply downscaled variants of larger architectures and are not optimized for this regime. The paper challenges the prevailing scaling-law assumption that architecture is negligible at fixed parameter count, showing that for sub-billion models, architectural choices—particularly depth versus width—have a decisive impact on quality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes natural language text prompts (up to a few hundred tokens for on-device inference) and autoregressively generates text tokens at up to 50 tokens/s on an iPhone 13 for the 125M variant. For the API-calling downstream task, a user utterance such as 'Help me set an alarm at 7:30 AM' is mapped to a structured JSON API call (e.g., {API: "alarm(time=7:30 am)"}) plus a natural-language agent response. The hard constraint is that the entire model plus runtime must occupy less than ~10% of device DRAM, which rules out 7B-class models at 8-bit precision.

## Method

MobileLLM combines four architecture-level techniques for sub-billion transformers: (1) a deep-and-thin topology (30–32 layers with reduced embedding dimension, e.g., 576-dim for 125M) that outperforms the conventional 12-layer wide design by 2–4 points; (2) SwiGLU feed-forward networks; (3) input–output embedding sharing, which frees ~16M parameters (≈12% of a 125M model) that are reallocated to extra layers; and (4) grouped-query attention with a 3:1 or 5:1 query-to-KV-head ratio, reducing KV redundancy while preserving accuracy. On top of this baseline, MobileLLM-LS introduces immediate block-wise weight sharing: every two adjacent transformer blocks share the same weights, effectively doubling the number of sequential layers (e.g., 30 distinct blocks → 60 forward passes) with zero additional parameters and only ~2.6% execution-time overhead on-device, because the shared weights remain in SRAM cache between the two computations. The models are trained from scratch on 1T tokens with Adam (lr 2e-3, cosine decay) on 32 A100 GPUs, and are compatible with W8A8 post-training quantization with <0.5-point accuracy loss.

## Results

On zero-shot common sense reasoning (8-task average), MobileLLM-125M achieves 46.3%, surpassing the previous best 125M model (GPT-Neo-125M at 42.9%) by 3.4 points, and MobileLLM-350M reaches 51.3%, exceeding the prior best sub-500M model (RWKV-430M at 47.0%) by 4.3 points. With immediate block-wise layer sharing (MobileLLM-LS), accuracy further improves to 47.0% (125M) and 52.1% (350M) with no additional parameters. On the API calling task, MobileLLM-350M achieves 65.3% EM_intent and 48.8% EM_structure, matching or exceeding LLaMA-v2 7B (62.8% / 50.9%) despite being ~20× smaller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileLLM-125M (46.3 avg) outperforms all same-size baselines: GPT-Neo-125M (42.9), OPT-125M (42.6), Galactica-125M (40.9), and LaMini-GPT-124M (41.9), and also beats larger models Pythia-160M (42.5) and RWKV-169M (43.6) while being 22–26% smaller. In the 350M class, MobileLLM-350M (51.3) surpasses RWKV-430M (47.0), Pythia-410M (46.6), OPT-350M (43.9), BLOOM-560M (44.2), and Cerebras-GPT-590M (43.0) by 4.3+ points. At the 1B scale (Appendix Table 8), MobileLLM-1B (57.3) beats Falcon-1B (56.3), TinyLlama-1.1B (54.2), and MobiLlama-1B (55.2), and MobileLLM-1.5B (59.4) outperforms Qwen1.5-1.8B (56.5) despite having fewer parameters. On the API calling task, MobileLLM-350M achieves 65.3% EM_intent vs. LLaMA-v2 7B's 62.8%, and 48.8% EM_structure vs. 50.9%, demonstrating near-parity with a 20× larger model. On chat benchmarks (AlpacaEval), MobileLLM-LS-350M reaches 48.2% win rate vs. text-davinci-001, approaching GPT-3's self-win rate of 50%, while all sub-200M baselines score below 4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes several techniques originally designed for large LLMs—grouped query attention (Ainslie et al., 2023) for KV-cache reduction and input-output embedding sharing (Zhang et al., 2022, OPT)—for the sub-billion regime where these components constitute a disproportionate share of parameters. It departs from the scaling-law orthodoxy (Kaplan et al., 2020) that architecture is negligible given fixed parameter count, instead demonstrating through 19 controlled experiments that deep-and-thin configurations dominate shallow-wide ones at 125M–350M scale. The immediate block-wise weight sharing extends the weight-sharing line (Reid et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2022) by showing that naive adjacent-block repetition, chosen for SRAM cache locality, outperforms more elaborate sharing topologies in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-ablated recipe for building the strongest sub-billion LLMs, directly addressing the memory (6–12 GB DRAM) and energy (0.035 J/token for 350M vs. 0.7 J/token for 7B) constraints of mobile deployment. The finding that a 350M model matches a 7B model on API-calling exact-match scores suggests that many common on-device tasks do not require large models, potentially reshaping deployment decisions. Open questions include whether the depth-over-width advantage persists with substantially more training data (models here use only 1T tokens), whether the block-wise sharing benefit generalizes to non-autoregressive or speculative decoding, and whether knowledge distillation from larger teachers could close the remaining gap on open-ended generation tasks.
