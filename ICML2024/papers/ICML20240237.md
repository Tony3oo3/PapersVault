## Problem

In standard RLHF, the language model generates tokens autoregressively but receives only a single scalar reward at the end of the completion, creating a sparse-reward credit-assignment problem that makes PPO training slow, unstable, and especially fragile for long generations. The reward model, being a transformer, computes attention maps over all tokens during its forward pass, yet this information is discarded when only the scalar output is used. The paper asks whether this free attention information can be exploited to densify the reward signal without additional modelling or computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the reward model's last-layer attention weights (averaged over heads) and the scalar reward score r_φ computed on the full completion, redistributing the reward proportionally to each token's attention weight. The output is a per-token reward sequence fed into PPO to train the language model policy. A key constraint is that the reward model and generator must share the same tokenizer so both operate over the same MDP; the attention weights are already computed during the standard forward pass, so no extra inference cost is incurred.

## Method

ABC (Attention Based Credit) replaces the sparse episodic reward with a dense token-level reward by multiplying the final scalar reward r_C by the attention weight α on the current token, yielding R̂_φ(s,a,s') = α_{I(s')} · r_C + R_φ(s,a,s'). The paper proves this is exactly potential-based reward shaping with potential Φ(s) = r_C Σ_t α_t, guaranteeing via Ng et al. (1999) that the optimal policy is unchanged. In practice, a convex combination with hyperparameter β ∈ [0,1] controls the trade-off between the shaped and original reward, and a standard KL penalty against the SFT reference model is added as a regularizer. The approach is agnostic to the choice of RL algorithm, reward model architecture (provided it uses attention), or preference modelling framework, requiring only a small modification to the PPO trainer to accept per-token rewards instead of a single scalar.

## Results

On the three evaluation tasks, ABC achieves the highest average reward across all settings: 8.32±0.16 on Positive Generation (vs. 7.21±0.39 for RLHF and 6.60±0.19 for Uniform), 9.18±0.17 on Summarisation (vs. 9.03±0.13 for RLHF), and 6.55±0.04 on Single-turn Dialogue (vs. 5.78±0.14 for RLHF). ABC also reaches its optimum in fewer than half the training steps required by vanilla RLHF and exhibits substantially lower variance across seeds. On AlpacaEval 2.0, ABC-generated responses are preferred over RLHF responses 69.32±1.62% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABC outperforms both baselines on all three tasks. Against vanilla RLHF (Christiano et al., 2017), ABC gains +1.11 reward on Positive Generation, +0.15 on Summarisation, and +0.77 on Single-turn Dialogue, while also reducing the standard deviation by roughly 2–3×, indicating more stable training. Against Uniform smoothing (Gangwani et al., 2020), ABC leads by +1.72 on Positive Generation, +0.15 on Summarisation, and +0.11 on Single-turn Dialogue; Uniform is competitive on longer-generation tasks but still trails ABC. In the reward-KL frontier analysis, ABC achieves higher reward at nearly every KL divergence level compared to RLHF, and traverses the trade-off space more smoothly without the 'hook' instability seen in vanilla RLHF. The ABC-D ablation (using the generator's own attention rather than the reward model's) performs roughly on par with Uniform, confirming that the reward model's attention is the critical signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard RLHF pipeline (Christiano et al., 2017) by exploiting an underutilized byproduct of the reward model's transformer architecture: its attention weights. Rather than learning a separate surrogate model for credit assignment (as in SECRET, Rudder, or process supervision methods), ABC redistributes the scalar episodic reward across tokens using the attention map already computed during the forward pass. The theoretical grounding draws directly from potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999), proving that the attention-weighted redistribution preserves the optimal policy. This positions ABC as a minimal, zero-overhead intervention within the existing PPO-based RLHF recipe, distinct from alternatives that either abandon RL entirely (DPO, SLiC-HF) or require additional human annotation (process supervision).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'for free' dense reward signal addresses a well-known practical pain point in RLHF—sparse terminal rewards causing unstable and slow training—without any architectural changes or extra computation, making it immediately adoptable in existing pipelines. The potential-based shaping guarantee eliminates concerns about objective mismatch, a critical property for production systems. Key limitations remain: the method requires shared tokenizers between reward and policy models, attention weights are strictly positive (cannot distinguish beneficial from harmful tokens), and all experiments use models under 7B parameters on a single GPU, leaving open whether the benefits persist at industrial scale. The interaction with reward model ensembles and over-optimization risk is flagged but unexplored.
