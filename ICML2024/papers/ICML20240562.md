## Problem

Tactile sensing has not been incorporated into multimodal generative language models, largely because natural language labels for tactile data are scarce and aligning tactile readings with both visual observations and language descriptions is complex. No existing tactile dataset contains open-vocabulary language annotations, and prior work on tactile-vision association is limited to closed-vocabulary classification tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tactile encoder takes 224×224 RGB tactile images (from the DIGIT sensor's internal deformable surface) and 224×224 visual images (from a webcam), producing aligned embeddings in the OpenCLIP latent space for zero-shot cross-modal classification. The TVL-LLaMA model takes a visual image, a cropped visual image centered on the contact point, and a tactile image as input, and generates a tactile description of up to five sensory adjectives in natural language. During encoder training, 10% of batches use non-contact (background) tactile frames labeled with the text 'background' to regularize the tactile representation.

## Method

The key architectural departure from ImageBind is computing pairwise contrastive (InfoNCE) loss across all three modality pairs—tactile-vision, tactile-language, and vision-language—rather than binding only to vision, which provides direct supervision for tactile-language alignment. The tactile encoder is a randomly initialized ViT (Tiny/Small/Base) trained against frozen OpenCLIP vision and language encoders, with projectors removed so the tactile encoder projects directly into the CLIP latent space. To mitigate overfitting on the 44K-pair dataset, 10% of training samples are non-contact frames and language labels are randomly shuffled and subset per image. For text generation, the paper follows the ImageBind-LLM recipe: tactile and vision latents are averaged into a single token, fused via a zero-initialized gate, and LLaMA-2 7B is fine-tuned with LoRA on a mix of TVL, Alpaca, and LLaVA instruction data. The dataset itself is labeled 10% by humans (using a 400-word tactile vocabulary) and 90% by GPT-4V pseudo-labels generated from both full and contact-cropped visual images.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary tactile classification task, the TVL encoder (ViT-Tiny) achieves 36.7% top-1 and 70.3% top-5 tactile-text accuracy, surpassing OpenCLIP's vision-text alignment (28.4% top-1) and dramatically outperforming the SSVTP encoder (0.2% top-1 tactile-vision). On the TVL Benchmark for tactile description generation, TVL-LLaMA (ViT-Base) scores 5.03/10, outperforming GPT-4V (4.49) by +12% and the best open-source VLM (LLaVA-1.5 13B, 3.62) by +32%. All TVL-LLaMA variants (4.89–5.03) significantly outperform GPT-4V at α=0.05, while SSVTP-LLaMA (3.54), which lacks tactile-language alignment, underperforms even GPT-4V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVL-LLaMA outperforms all eight compared baselines on the TVL Benchmark. Against GPT-4V (the pseudo-label generator and strongest non-tactile baseline at 4.49), TVL-LLaMA (ViT-Base) achieves 5.03 (+12%), with the improvement being statistically significant (p=3.46×10⁻⁶). Against open-source VLMs, the gap is larger: LLaVA-1.5 13B (3.62), ViP-LLaVA 13B (3.80), LLaMA-Adapter (3.02), BLIP-2 OPT-6.7B (2.64), and InstructBLIP 13B (1.24) all score 1.2–3.8 points below TVL-LLaMA, with the best open-source model (LLaVA-1.5 13B) trailing by +32%. The SSVTP-LLaMA baseline (3.54), which uses tactile-vision encoders but omits tactile-language contrastive loss, underperforms GPT-4V, confirming that explicit tactile-language alignment is necessary. On the classification task (Table 1), the TVL encoder's 36.7% top-1 tactile-text accuracy exceeds OpenCLIP's 28.4% vision-text accuracy, while its 79.5% top-1 tactile-vision accuracy dwarfs SSVTP's 0.2%, demonstrating that the in-the-wild HCT data substantially improves generalization over the lab-collected SSVTP dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from ImageBind's vision-centric binding strategy (where all modalities are contrastively aligned only to the visual modality) by introducing pairwise contrastive losses across all three modalities—tactile-vision, tactile-language, and vision-language—providing direct supervision for tactile-language alignment. For the language model alignment stage, it follows the two-stage recipe from ImageBind-LLM (pretrain a zero-initialized gate, then LoRA-finetune LLaMA-2), substituting the ImageBind encoders with the newly trained TVL tactile encoder. The pseudo-labeling strategy draws on the broader trend of using VLMs (GPT-4V) to generate training data, analogous to how LLaVA and Alpaca leverage GPT-generated instructions, but applied here to the cross-modal setting where a vision-only model generates tactile descriptions from visual observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-vocabulary tactile-vision-language dataset, addressing a fundamental gap: prior tactile datasets used closed-vocabulary labels and no prior work aligned touch with natural language in a generative setting. The finding that a model trained on 10% human labels + 90% GPT-4V pseudo-labels outperforms GPT-4V itself on tactile understanding suggests a viable path to scaling tactile-language data without prohibitive annotation costs. Key limitations include the small dataset scale (44K pairs), the inherent ceiling on vision-derived tactile labels (touch captures information invisible to cameras), and camera occlusion of the contact patch. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other tactile sensors, whether larger-scale data collection can close the remaining gap to human-level tactile understanding, and how to handle the distribution shift between VLM pseudo-labels and human annotations more gracefully.
