## Problem

Offline RL methods require reward-labeled data and typically produce a single policy biased toward one behavior, while unsupervised skill learning methods lack explicit mechanisms to encourage behavioral diversity. The paper addresses how to learn a diverse, controllable set of skills from a mixture of label-free offline data without suffering from out-of-distribution issues that plague model-based offline approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241252/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a state observation s_t and a continuous skill vector z ∈ R^d (drawn from a prior p(z)) and produces a single action a_t via one forward pass through the sequence model π_θ(τ_t|s_t, z). The policy internally predicts a full trajectory τ_t = [s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, …, s_T, a_T], but only the first action is executed. The pre-trained diffusion prior π_ψ is used exclusively during training as a regularization signal and is not invoked at test time, distinguishing DIDI from Diffuser-style iterative denoising at inference.

## Method

DIDI first trains an unconditional diffusion model π_ψ on the offline dataset to approximate the data distribution, then freezes it as a prior. The contextual policy π_θ(τ_t|s_t, z) is a single sequence model (serving as both policy and dynamics) trained jointly with a skill discriminator q_φ(z|τ_t) under the objective J_DIDI, which combines mutual-information-based diversity (empowerment), optional reward maximization, and a diffusion-guided regularization term that penalizes deviation from the pre-trained prior via a noise-prediction loss. The key mechanism is that the diffusion forward noising process q(τ_t^{1:N}|τ_t) conditions the latent variables on the policy's own output, preserving the chicken-and-egg connection necessary for diversity while the large number of diffusion steps N provides training stability by approximating a fixed Gaussian prior at the terminal step. This design avoids the OOD problem of model-based offline RL (no separate dynamics model to compound errors) and the instability of VAE-prior alternatives (where sampling from a fixed prior collapses diversity), while enabling single-forward-pass inference unlike iterative diffusion-based planners.

## Results

On D4RL tasks (Table 3), DIDI achieves average returns of 75.6, 74.8, and 75.7 across three learned skills, competitive with CQL (77.6), Decision Transformer (74.7), and Diffuser (77.5). On the behavioral diversity metric (Table 1), DIDI outperforms all baselines across Push T (7.2 vs. 6.8/2.5/1.6), Push F (12.2 vs. 10.4/6.5/7.1), Push 7 (10.2 vs. 8.1/7.7/5.4), and Kitchen (9.8 vs. 8.7/8.3/9.0). In downstream fine-tuning with obstacles (Table 2), DIDI's diverse skill space yields substantially higher success rates than a single optimal policy, e.g., 0.82 vs. 0.14 on Push7 with 5 demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On D4RL tasks, DIDI's three skills achieve average returns of 75.6–75.7, slightly below CQL (77.6) and Diffuser (77.5) but above Decision Transformer (74.7). The gap to CQL and Diffuser is most pronounced on halfcheetah-medium-expert (83.0–86.6 vs. 91.6/88.9) and halfcheetah-medium-replay (33.2–34.8 vs. 45.5/37.7), while DIDI skill1 actually surpasses Diffuser on walker2d-medium (80.3 vs. 79.6) and matches or exceeds CQL on hopper-medium-replay (96.1 vs. 95.0). The critical distinction is that CQL, DT, and Diffuser each produce a single policy, whereas DIDI simultaneously yields three distinct skills with comparable performance, enabling downstream flexibility. On the diversity metric (Table 1), DIDI dominates all baselines (k-means-DI, VAE-DI, VAE-DI-fixed) by 1–5 points across all four domains, confirming that the diffusion-guided regularization preserves behavioral diversity without sacrificing task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIDI sits at the intersection of offline RL, unsupervised skill discovery (empowerment/DIAYN), and diffusion probabilistic models. It departs from Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022), which uses a diffusion model as the inference-time planner, by instead pre-training the diffusion model as a fixed regularization prior and distilling its guidance into a single-forward-pass contextual policy. The empowerment objective from DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., 2018) is inherited directly, but the key novelty is the three-step relabeling mechanism (pseudo-behavior → diffusion noising/denoising → proxy-target) that avoids out-of-distribution issues without requiring a separate dynamics model. The paper also explicitly contrasts with VAE-based priors, arguing that diffusion's multi-step structure naturally balances the 'chicken-and-egg' dependency needed for diversity against training stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is in enabling agents to extract a controllable, diverse skill repertoire from messy, label-free offline data without any reward supervision — a common real-world scenario. The downstream task results (Table 2) showing that fine-tuning only skill embeddings outperforms fine-tuning a single policy on obstacle-avoidance tasks is a concrete demonstration of why diversity matters for adaptability. However, on D4RL (Table 3), DIDI achieves competitive but not clearly superior returns compared to CQL, DT, and Diffuser, suggesting the method's primary value is in behavioral diversity and controllability rather than peak performance. Open questions include how the skill dimensionality should be chosen, whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous control, and whether the single-forward-pass policy retains the full expressiveness of the diffusion prior in complex long-horizon tasks.
