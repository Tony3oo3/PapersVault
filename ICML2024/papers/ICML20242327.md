## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pre-training methods rely solely on image-text pairing as supervision, ignoring two rich signal types naturally present in clinical datasets: multi-view spatial information (frontal and lateral chest radiographs) and temporal information (chronological sequences of a patient's diagnoses). Lateral views are either discarded or treated identically to frontal views, and temporal structure is at best exploited via a single prior image without a dedicated optimization objective, leaving fine-grained spatial and temporal semantics unlearned.

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired frontal and lateral chest X-ray images (patchified at 16×16 for ViT-B/16; lateral views are zero-padded when absent) and the corresponding radiological report text (encoded by BioClinicalBERT). Data is reorganized into patient-level sequences of length 4 (or 1 for single-visit patients), so the encoder processes multi-view images and text at each timestep. Downstream, the learned representations are adapted to produce disease-progression labels (improving/stable/worsening), sentence-similarity scores for temporal-semantic paraphrase detection, zero-shot pneumonia classification logits, and multi-class chest X-ray labels.

## Method

Med-ST introduces a Mixture of View Experts (MoVE) image encoder that replaces the standard ViT feed-forward block with two view-specific experts (Frontal-FFN and Lateral-FFN), allowing complementary spatial features from frontal and lateral patches to be extracted and concatenated before global and local contrastive alignment. For fine-grained spatial alignment, a modality-weighted local alignment loss computes per-token attention over image patches and reweights each patch–token contrastive pair by a softmax score derived from the element-wise product of the two features, so pathological regions receive higher loss weight. For temporal modeling, the paper proposes a cross-modal bidirectional cycle consistency objective that progresses from simple to complex: a Forward Mapping Classification (FMC) loss treats the text sequence as classes and classifies each image feature, while a Reverse Mapping Regression (RMR) loss fits a Gaussian prior over the similarity distribution of the mapped-back image features, penalizing temporal misalignment with a piecewise quadratic/linear objective. The total training loss combines global image–text contrastive loss, the modality-weighted local alignment loss, and the bidirectional temporal cycle consistency loss, all optimized jointly on MIMIC-CXR sequences.

## Results

On the MS-CXR-T temporal image classification benchmark (10-fold CV), Med-ST achieves 61.12% average accuracy across five findings, surpassing the strongest temporal baseline BioViL-T (59.02%) by 2.10% and the best non-temporal baseline MGCA (57.31%) by 3.81%. On the RadGraph temporal sentence similarity task, Med-ST reaches 83.76% accuracy and 84.60 AUROC, outperforming BioViL-T by 4.95% in accuracy. On zero-shot RSNA pneumonia classification, Med-ST attains 68.37% accuracy and 77.14 AUROC, the best among all compared methods. On COVIDx medical image classification, Med-ST achieves 93.0% at 100% training data and 87.7% at 10%, though it trails MGCA (74.8%) at the 1% data regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Med-ST outperforms all eight baselines on the primary temporal image classification task, with the largest margin over BioViL-T (+2.10% avg. accuracy) and MGCA (+3.81%), and achieves the best per-finding scores in Consolidation (+3.64% over BioViL-T), Edema (+5.80%), and Pleural Effusion (+4.53%). On the RadGraph temporal sentence similarity task, Med-ST leads BioViL-T by 4.95% in accuracy and 3.21 in AUROC, and MGCA by 8.34% in accuracy, notably without using masked language modeling. On zero-shot RSNA pneumonia classification, Med-ST edges out MGCA by 1.12% accuracy and 3.17 AUROC points. On COVIDx, Med-ST wins at 10% (87.7% vs. MGCA 84.8%) and 100% (93.0% vs. MGCA 92.3%) training data but underperforms MGCA at 1% (71.2% vs. 74.8%), which the authors attribute to the model absorbing a wider range of supervision signals that require more data to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

Med-ST extends the medical vision-language pre-training line (MGCA, MRM, BioViL, BioViL-T) by explicitly exploiting two supervision signals that prior methods largely ignored: multi-view spatial information (frontal + lateral chest radiographs) and temporal sequence structure across a patient's historical records. The spatial component draws on Mixture-of-Experts architectures to replace the standard ViT FFN with view-specific experts (MoVE), while the temporal component adapts the cycle-consistency framework of Dwibedi et al. (2019) to a cross-modal, bidirectional setting with a novel 'simple-to-complex' curriculum (classification in the forward pass, Gaussian-prior regression in the reverse pass). The modality-weighted local alignment departs from prior single-modality attention weighting (e.g., MGCA) by jointly considering information content in both visual and textual tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that off-the-shelf medical datasets contain substantial unused supervision (lateral views, chronological ordering) that can be exploited without any additional annotation, yielding consistent gains across temporal and static classification tasks. However, the evaluation is confined to chest X-rays with sequences capped at length 4, and the 1%-data result underperforms baselines, suggesting the richer supervision signal requires sufficient data to be beneficial. Open questions include whether the multi-view MoVE and temporal cycle-consistency objectives generalize to other modalities (CT, MRI, ultrasound), whether longer patient histories improve temporal modeling, and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse medical datasets beyond MIMIC-CXR.
