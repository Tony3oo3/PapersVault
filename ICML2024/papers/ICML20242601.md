## Problem

Transformers exhibit sub-optimal performance on long-range tasks compared to specialized layers such as state-space models and long convolutions, despite being theoretically expressive enough to replicate them. The paper argues this gap is not due to expressiveness or optimization pathology but rather a generalization failure caused by the absence of appropriate inductive bias—specifically, smoothness and locality—within the attention mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method operates on causal (unidirectional) sequences of variable length, ranging from 1K to 16K tokens in the LRA benchmark, and accepts text, vectorized images (e.g., 28×28 MNIST flattened to 784-dim vectors), and numerical sequences. Outputs are task-specific: classification accuracy on LRA and Sequential/Permuted MNIST, bits-per-byte for next-token prediction on Text8/Enwik8/Wikitext-103, and R² scores on synthetic atomic tasks. The LaS-Chunk variant further constrains each attention head to a fixed local block of 128 tokens to achieve linear complexity while preserving local information.

## Method

LaS-Attention modifies standard self-attention with two parameter-free operators applied to the attention matrix: (1) a smoothness operator that performs 1-D average pooling (window size P) along each row, and (2) a locality operator that element-wise multiplies the attention scores by an Exponentially Locally Decaying (ELD) matrix exp(−α_c · D_L), where D_L is the causal distance matrix. The decay rate α_c is set per head via exponential-uniform initialization in [0, B], with α_0 = 0 preserving one vanilla head, so different heads attend at different spatial scales, creating a hierarchical composition of local dependencies into global long-range interactions. A LaS-Chunk variant segments the sequence into blocks of 128 to reduce complexity from quadratic to linear while retaining the same inductive bias. The paper also proves (Theorem B.1) that a single attention head with sufficient hidden dimension can express any state-space convolution kernel, establishing that the bottleneck is inductive bias rather than capacity.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, LaS-Attention achieves an average accuracy of 73.99%, outperforming the best prior transformer variant (Luna-256 at 61.95%) by 12.04% and the vanilla Transformer (54.39%) by 19.6%. The linear-complexity LaS-Chunk variant reaches 65.73% average, surpassing all full-attention transformers on all tasks except Pathfinder. On the Image task specifically, LaS-Attention scores 70.44%, exceeding all transformer variants by at least 22%. However, LaS-Attention still trails global convolution layers such as MEGA (88.21%) and S4-v2 (86.09%), indicating remaining room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaS-Attention decisively outperforms all transformer-based baselines on the LRA benchmark: it beats Luna-256 (61.95%) by 12.04%, XFM (59.24%) by 14.75%, BigBird (55.01%) by 19%, and the vanilla Transformer (54.39%) by 19.6%. The linear-complexity LaS-Chunk variant (65.73%) also surpasses every full-attention transformer on all tasks except Pathfinder, where it drops to 54.61% versus Luna-256's 78.55%. Against global convolution layers, LaS-Attention underperforms: MEGA (88.21%) leads by 14.22%, S4-v2 (86.09%) by 12.1%, and SG-Conv (87.17%) by 13.18%, with the gap attributed partly to LaS's unidirectional (causal) processing versus the bidirectional processing in most convolution-based methods. On Sequential MNIST, LaS improves over the vanilla Transformer from 98.90% to 99.18% (sMNIST) and from 97.90% to 98.05% (pMNIST), though S4 still leads at 99.63% and 98.70% respectively. In the ablation against Alibi (Press et al., 2021), the exponential decaying structure of ELD provides a 6.69% average improvement over linear Alibi bias on the four-task LRA subset, confirming that the specific exponential decay shape matters beyond simple positional biasing. On language modeling (Text8/Enwik8), LaS-Attention achieves lower bpb than vanilla attention (1.3332 vs 1.3557 on Text8; 1.2826 vs 1.2885 on Enwik8), but on the larger Wikitext-103 with 12 layers, LaS shows a small perplexity degradation (21.87 vs 20.20), suggesting the inductive bias is most beneficial for smaller-scale or long-range-heavy settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer attention mechanism by incorporating two inductive biases—exponential positional decay (ELD) and attention smoothing—drawn from the design principles of state-space layers (S4, S5), long convolutions (SGConv, FlashButterfly), and linear RNNs. It positions itself as a conceptual bridge between standard transformers and these specialized long-range layers, showing that the key properties making the latter effective (decaying kernels, smoothness) can be injected into attention with no additional parameters. The method is distinct from Alibi, which also uses distance-based bias but targets length extrapolation rather than long-range inductive bias, and from hierarchical local-global attention approaches (Luna, MEGABYTE) that combine separate local and global mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central diagnostic—that transformers' long-range weakness stems from generalization (insufficient inductive bias) rather than expressiveness or optimization—provides a clear target for future transformer improvements. However, LaS-Attention still trails SOTA long-range layers (73.99 vs. 88.21 average LRA for MEGA), and its failure on inherently non-smooth tasks like Select (0.783 vs. 0.971) reveals that the smoothness bias is a double-edged sword. Open questions include whether bidirectional processing, learned decay rates, or more sophisticated smoothing could close the remaining gap, and whether the locality principle generalizes beyond 1-D causal settings to multi-dimensional or bidirectional architectures.
