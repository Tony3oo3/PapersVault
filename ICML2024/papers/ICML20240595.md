## Problem

First-order online linear programming (OLP) algorithms are computationally efficient but have been stuck at O(√T) regret, far from the O(log T) achievable by LP-based methods. The paper identifies a fundamental dilemma that explains this gap: good decision-making requires a large stepsize for adaptability to newly arriving samples, while good learning of the dual optimum requires a small stepsize—no single first-order method can satisfy both simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240595/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequential stream of resource requests (c_t, a_t) ∈ R × R^m over horizon T, where each request must be irrevocably accepted or rejected before the next arrives, subject to an initial inventory vector b ∈ R^m. Decisions are made via a dual-based threshold rule: accept if c_t ≥ ⟨a_t, y^t⟩, where y^t is a maintained dual estimate updated by subgradient steps. Performance is measured by regret (gap from the offline LP optimum) and constraint violation (norm of excess resource consumption), both of which the paper bounds at O(T^{1/3}).

## Method

The paper introduces a two-path, two-phase framework that decouples learning from decision-making by running two independent first-order methods in parallel. During an exploration phase (t = 1 to T_e), a learning path A_L (SGD with 1/(μt) stepsize, ASSG, or SADAGRAD) converges quickly to the dual optimum y*, while a decision path A_D (subgradient method with stepsize α_e) makes online decisions. At the transition, the decision path restarts from the learning path's estimate y_L^{T_e+1} and switches to a larger stepsize α_p for the exploitation phase, preserving adaptability to new samples. This one-time restart strategy lets the algorithm combine fast learning (small stepsize) with responsive decision-making (large stepsize) in separate components, achieving O(T^{1/3}) regret and constraint violation with SGD with known μ, and O(T^{1/3} log T) with parameter-free ASSG/SADAGRAD—breaking the O(√T) barrier for first-order OLP methods under continuous distributions.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is that first-order OLP algorithms can achieve O(T^{1/3}) regret and constraint violation (instance M2, using SGD with known μ as the learning path), breaking the previous O(√T) barrier for first-order methods under continuous support. With parameter-free algorithms (ASSG or SADAGRAD), the bound is O(T^{1/3} log T) (M3). Empirically, M2 achieves up to 5× lower regret than the no-exploration baseline M0 (e.g., 24.00 vs 123.03 at T=10⁵) while running in 0.064 seconds versus 4742.9 seconds for the LP-based MLP algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its first-order methods (M0, M1, M2) against the LP-based algorithm MLP from Li and Ye (2022) in Table 3. On regret, MLP consistently outperforms all first-order methods: at T=10⁵, MLP achieves 5.91 average regret versus 24.00 for M2, 47.86 for M1, and 123.03 for M0. However, the computational cost difference is dramatic: MLP requires 4742.9 seconds at T=10⁵, while M2 completes in 0.064 seconds — a speedup of over 70,000×. Among the first-order variants, M2 (with exploration using SGD with known μ) achieves up to 5× lower regret than M0 (no exploration) at the same T, and M1 (basic SGD exploration) sits in between. The paper positions its contribution not as beating LP-based regret but as closing the gap from O(√T) to O(T^{1/3}) while retaining the computational efficiency that makes first-order methods practical for time-sensitive applications like online advertising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dual-based OLP framework of Li and Ye (2022) and Li et al. (2020), which established that first-order subgradient methods achieve O(√T) regret. The key departure is the identification of a fundamental 'dilemma': algorithms that learn the dual optimum faster (via smaller stepsizes, e.g., SGD with known μ, ASSG, SADAGRAD) actually perform worse at online decision-making due to reduced adaptability. The paper's two-path, two-phase framework decouples a fast-converging learning algorithm (A_L) from a larger-stepsize decision algorithm (A_D) with a one-time restart, a structural idea not present in prior first-order OLP work where learning and decision-making are tightly coupled in a single update rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing first-order methods can break the O(√T) regret barrier in the continuous-support OLP setting, achieving O(T^{1/3}) regret and constraint violation. The dilemma characterization—where better learning (smaller stepsizes) actively harms decision-making—provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding why prior first-order approaches were stuck. However, a significant gap remains: O(T^{1/3}) is still far from the O(log T) achieved by LP-based methods, and it is open whether first-order methods can close this gap further. The framework's generality (flexible choice of A_L and A_D, multi-stage restarts) suggests potential applicability to broader online decision-making problems beyond OLP.
