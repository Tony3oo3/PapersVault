## Problem

Topological Neural Networks (TNNs) process higher-order relational structures on simplicial complexes, while persistent homology (PH) captures multiresolution topological features like loops and voids. Although PH has been shown to boost GNN expressivity (e.g., TOGL, RePHINE), the benefits of integrating PH into TNNs remained unexplored, and existing equivariant methods (EGNN, IMPSN) fail to capture topological structures in geometric simplicial complexes. The paper also lacks a continuous-time (Neural ODE) formulation for PH-augmented topological networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes attributed simplicial complexes where each simplex carries a feature vector, and in the geometric variant, 0-simplices additionally carry 3D coordinates. At each layer, the complex is refined via message passing, then persistence diagrams are extracted from color-based or geometric filtrations over the refined complex. Outputs are complex-level representations fed to task-specific heads: class labels (graph classification), scalar molecular properties (QM9), antibody amino-acid sequences with 3D Cα coordinates (CDR-H3), and per-atom energy/force vectors (MD17).

## Method

TopNets defines a per-layer pipeline: (i) a general TNN message-passing step refines simplex embeddings, (ii) persistence diagrams are computed from i-simplex-color filtrations (generalizing vertex-color and edge-color filtrations to arbitrary dimensions) and vectorized via a PersLay-style scheme, (iii) a dimension-dependent topological aggregation function merges PH embeddings with MP features, and (iv) interleaved readout functions pool across layers and dimensions. The framework provably subsumes TOGL, PersLay, and RePHINE as special cases (Proposition 1 shows SWL+PH strictly exceeds SWL expressivity). The E(n)-equivariant variant replaces standard filtrations with geometric i-simplex-color filtrations using E(n)-invariant functions of coordinates, preserving equivariance while incorporating spatial information. The continuous variant parameterizes the message-passing dynamics as a Neural ODE over the simplicial complex, with derived discretization error bounds (Propositions 3–4) relating N-layer discrete networks to their ODE counterparts.

## Results

On CDR-H3 antibody design, TopNets with RePHINE diagrams achieve 44.80% AAR and 1.75 Å RMSD, surpassing the previous SOTA AbODE (39.8% AAR, 1.73 Å RMSD) by ~5 percentage points on sequence recovery. On QM9 molecular property prediction, continuous E-TopNets with RePHINE diagrams achieve MAE of 0.070 bohr³ (α), 25 meV (ε_LUMO), and 1.37 meV (ZPVE), competitive with or surpassing SOTA equivariant methods like IMPSN and EGNN on several targets. On 3BPA MD17, TopNets achieve 2.5±0.2 meV RMSE for energy at 300 K, outperforming NequIP (3.3±0.1) and matching MACE (3.0±0.2), with similar trends at 600 K and 1200 K. On graph classification, continuous TopNets consistently improve over their discrete counterparts across all five datasets (e.g., GIN+RePHINE: 81.60% vs 79.23% on NCI109).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CDR-H3 antibody design, TopNets decisively outperform all baselines on AAR: 44.80% (RePHINE) vs. 39.8% (AbODE), 36.38% (MEAN), and 21.13% (RefineGNN), while matching AbODE on RMSD (1.75 vs. 1.73 Å). On QM9, continuous E-TopNets with RePHINE diagrams are competitive with SOTA equivariant methods (IMPSN, EGNN, DimeNet++) but do not uniformly dominate; they surpass IMPSN on ZPVE (1.37 vs. 1.44 meV) and ε_LUMO (25 vs. 25 meV) but trail DimeNet++ and SphereNet on dipole moment (μ) and polarizability (α). On 3BPA MD17, TopNets outperform NequIP across all temperatures and metrics (e.g., 29.2 vs. 38.5 meV energy RMSE at 1200 K) while essentially tying MACE (29.2 vs. 29.8 meV at 1200 K). On graph classification, continuous TopNets consistently beat their discrete counterparts by 1–4 percentage points across NCI109, IMDB-B, NCI1, MOLHIV, and PROTEINS, and Cont.TopNets outperform TOGL on Enzymes (69.7% vs. 53.0% with GCN; 58.3% vs. 43.8% with GIN). The paper positions TopNets as a strict superset of TOGL, PersLay, and RePHINE, with the continuous extension providing consistent gains over discrete versions across all tested TNN backbones (GCN, GIN, MPSN).

## Relation to Prior Work

TopNets unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) message-passing TNNs on simplicial complexes (MPSN, CWN), (2) PH-augmented GNNs (TOGL, PersLay, RePHINE), and (3) equivariant/continuous neural networks (EMPSN, Neural ODEs). The paper explicitly shows that TOGL, PersLay, and RePHINE are special cases of the TopNets framework (Appendix C), and extends the TNN backbone with geometric i-simplex-color filtrations to achieve E(n)-equivariance. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 1: SWL + PH ≻ SWL) generalizes known results about PH enhancing GNN expressivity to the simplicial TNN setting, while the continuous extension derives Neural ODEs over simplicial complexes with discretization error bounds analogous to Sander et al. (2022) for ResNets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework gives the topological deep learning community a single language for combining PH descriptors with higher-order message passing, equivariance, and continuous dynamics — reducing the fragmentation across TOGL, RePHINE, PersLay, and EMPSN. The consistent empirical gains from continuous TopNets over discrete counterparts across all datasets and TNN backbones suggest that the ODE formulation is not merely a theoretical curiosity but a practical improvement. However, the paper acknowledges significant computational overhead from PH computation and higher-order message passing, and is restricted to simplicial complexes (not combinatorial complexes), leaving open whether the framework extends to more general topological structures. The discretization error bound also reveals that convergence to the ODE solution is not guaranteed without additional regularity assumptions, a limitation shared with the broader Neural ODE literature.
