## Problem

The Online Bayesian Bipartite Matching (OBBM) problem requires making irrevocable matching decisions online without knowing future arrivals. The optimal online algorithm (OPT_on) computes value-to-go (VTG) via dynamic programming, but this requires exponential space even for constant-factor approximations. The paper asks whether a GNN can efficiently approximate VTG to compete with OPT_on, and provides theoretical justification for why GNNs are the right architecture for this task on spatially-structured graphs.

## Input / Output

The GNN receives the current matching state encoded as an attributed bipartite graph: node features include a positional encoder, a one-hot skip-node indicator, an online/offline mask, a one-hot for the currently arriving node, and a dynamic arrival-probability vector; edge features carry the edge weight. The GNN outputs a predicted VTG scalar for each feasible action (match to each available neighbor or skip), and the algorithm selects the action with the highest predicted VTG. Despite being trained exclusively on 16-node graphs, the model generalizes to inputs up to 18× larger, and a meta-GNN variant selects between regime-specific GNNs based on the online-to-offline node ratio.

## Method

MAGNOLIA replaces the intractable VTG computation in OPT_on with a GENConv message-passing GNN whose update rule mirrors the Bellman equation: h_v^(k) = MLP(h_v^(k-1) + max_{u∈N(v)}{ReLU(h_u^(k-1) + w_vu)}). The GNN is trained via supervised learning with teacher forcing on 2000 instances of 16-node graphs, where targets are exact VTG values computed by solving the exponential DP. The key theoretical contribution proves that for bipartite random geometric graphs (b-RGGs) with β-smooth node distributions, VTG is O(β log N)-locally approximable—matching the k-hop neighborhood structure inherent to GNN message passing—by showing b-RGGs decompose into small connected components under random hypercube partitions. A meta-GNN layer selects between GNNs trained on different online/offline regimes to improve generalization across problem configurations.

## Results

MAGNOLIA achieves an average competitive ratio of 0.937–0.978 across 12 graph configurations (ER, BA, GEOM, Rideshare, gMission) at size (10×20), consistently outperforming the best baseline by 2–5%. Despite being trained exclusively on 16-node graphs, MAGNOLIA generalizes to graphs up to 18× larger with minimal performance degradation. A meta-GNN variant further improves regime generalization across varying online-to-offline node ratios, and MAGNOLIA remains the most robust model under input noise (ρ up to 0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGNOLIA outperforms both baselines across all 12 graph configurations. Against LP-rounding (Braverman et al., 2022), which achieves a 0.632 approximation guarantee, MAGNOLIA leads by 1–4% in most settings, with the largest gap on GEOM graphs (up to 2.2% at q=0.15) and Rideshare graphs (~2.1%). LP-rounding ties MAGNOLIA on gMission (both at 0.951) and is competitive on ER p=0.25 (0.929 vs. 0.945). Against greedy-t (Alomrani et al., 2022), MAGNOLIA's advantage is more pronounced, reaching 6–8% on BA b=4 (0.937 vs. 0.875) and 15% on gMission (0.951 vs. 0.802), where the threshold-based heuristic struggles significantly. The paper notes that Alomrani et al.'s full RL framework is not directly comparable since it requires per-configuration training, larger training graphs, and more compute, making a fair head-to-head comparison infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes message-passing GNNs (specifically GENConv) for a fundamentally different ML task than prior work: rather than learning a matching policy end-to-end via RL (as in Alomrani et al., 2022) or optimizing worst-case guarantees, it uses supervised learning to approximate the value-to-go of the known-but-intractable optimal online algorithm for OBBM. The theoretical contribution carves new ground by proving that VTG is locally approximable on bipartite random geometric graphs, establishing a formal correspondence between the local message-passing structure of GNNs and the local decomposability of VTG. This positions the work at the intersection of the OBBM approximation literature (Krengel & Sucheston, Papadimitriou et al., Braverman et al.) and the ML-for-combinatorial-optimization program, but with a distinct inductive bias: the GNN is trained to mimic a specific algorithmic structure (greedy VTG maximization) rather than to discover one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that VTG is O(β log N)-locally approximable on b-RGGs provides the first principled justification for why GNNs should work for online matching, moving the field beyond purely empirical validation. Practically, the 2–5% competitive-ratio improvement over LP-rounding and the demonstrated size generalization (16-node training → 18× larger test graphs) suggest a viable path toward deploying learned matching in spatial crowdsourcing. Key open questions include whether the locality guarantee extends beyond b-RGGs to other graph families, whether the approach scales to the thousands-of-nodes regime typical of real marketplaces, and how to handle the practical issue that exact arrival probabilities are rarely known.
