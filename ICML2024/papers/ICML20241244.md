## Problem

Large-scale unified vision models have predominantly targeted high-level tasks (segmentation, detection), leaving low-level image processing—restoration, enhancement, and feature extraction—largely unaddressed by in-context learning approaches. Existing visual prompting methods (MAE-VQGAN, Painter) suffer from inadequate prompt organization and limited task coverage, failing to handle cross-domain tasks with fundamentally different output representations (natural images vs. edge maps vs. stylized outputs).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a prompt image pair (degraded→clean, or input→output) that implicitly specifies the task, followed by a query image to be processed. All images are resized to 512×512 and encoded as 16×16 patches; the four images are concatenated into a single 4×256×256 sequence. The output is a pixel-level image in whatever domain the prompt dictates—restored RGB, enhanced RGB, or a single-channel edge map—determined entirely by the prompt pair without any task-specific head or fine-tuning.

## Method

PromptGIP reformulates image processing as a visual prompting QA problem by arranging images in a Q-A-Q-A sequence: the first Q-A pair is the task prompt (context), the second Q is the query image (question), and the second A is the target output (answer) to be predicted. A vanilla ViT-large backbone processes the concatenated sequence, and during training both answer images are randomly masked at 85% ratio (unlike Painter's Q-Q-A-A layout or direct prediction of only the final answer), forcing the model to learn the Q→A mapping from both prompt and query contexts. The key architectural insight is that the explicit Q-A-Q-A ordering preserves the question-to-answer correspondence, enabling precise pixel-level prediction across 15 diverse tasks with a single training phase and no task-specific modules.

## Results

On 10 image restoration tasks (PSNR/SSIM), PromptGIP trained with all 15 tasks (♠) achieves competitive performance: 26.22/0.8167 on Gaussian noise, 27.29/0.8590 on Poisson noise, and 24.32/0.9020 on haze, consistently outperforming Painter♠ (24.17–24.63 PSNR range) and ViT-large* across all degradations. On image enhancement, PromptGIP♠ reaches 20.30 PSNR on low-light enhancement (LOL) and 26.11 PSNR on local Laplacian filtering, surpassing Painter♠ (19.47 and 23.87 respectively). For edge detection, PromptGIP♠ achieves 21.44 MAE on Canny and 3.79 MAE on Laplacian, substantially outperforming Painter♠ (33.72 and 5.45 MAE). The model handles up to 15 cross-domain tasks in a single framework without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Painter♠ (the closest in-context learning baseline), PromptGIP♠ wins on all 10 restoration tasks, with the largest margins on haze (+3.72 PSNR), complex rain (+1.27 PSNR), and simple rain (+2.11 PSNR). Compared to ViT-large★ (same backbone, single-task training), PromptGIP★ outperforms on 9 out of 10 restoration tasks, confirming that the Q-A-Q-A prompting paradigm itself provides a meaningful boost over direct prediction. Against Restormer★ (state-of-the-art restoration architecture, single-task), PromptGIP★ trails by 1–8 dB on most degradations (e.g., 26.48 vs. 28.66 on Gaussian noise), which the authors attribute to Restormer's purpose-built architecture; however, PromptGIP★ surpasses Restormer★ on haze (24.56 vs. 14.83) and complex rain (24.33 vs. 24.26). When both are trained on all tasks (♠), PromptGIP♠ clearly outperforms Restormer♠ on every degradation, with the gap widening dramatically on haze (24.32 vs. 7.22 PSNR) and complex rain (24.08 vs. 20.39 PSNR). On enhancement and edge detection (Table 2), PromptGIP♠ dominates Painter♠ across all four metrics (LLE PSNR 20.30 vs. 19.47, LLF PSNR 26.11 vs. 23.87, Canny MAE 21.44 vs. 33.72, Laplacian MAE 3.79 vs. 5.45), while ViT-large★ achieves the best Laplacian MAE (1.47) but fails catastrophically on Canny (36.53 MAE) and LLE (13.37 PSNR), highlighting that no single-task model can span these output domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptGIP builds on the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., 2022) and the visual in-context learning line initiated by MAE-VQGAN and Painter, but departs from both in its encoding structure: it replaces Painter's Q-Q-A-A sequence with a Q-A-Q-A arrangement and, unlike MAE-VQGAN, predicts pixels directly rather than VQGAN tokens. Conceptually, it repurposes the NLP question-answering framing—treating an input-output image pair as a 'question-answer' sentence—to unify low-level vision tasks that prior multi-task restoration methods (Real-ESRGAN, Restormer, PromptIR, ProRes) handled only within the single output domain of natural images. The key extension is crossing output-domain boundaries: the same model produces restored RGB images, enhanced images, and binary edge maps depending on the visual prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single ViT-Large model can be steered across 15 tasks spanning three fundamentally different output representations (natural images, enhanced images, edge maps) purely through visual prompts, without task-specific fine-tuning—a capability no prior restoration or multi-task model achieved. However, the practical impact is tempered by the use of a vanilla ViT backbone (which the authors acknowledge limits low-level fidelity), modest training data, and the absence of true emergent generalization (e.g., colorization fails). Open questions include whether scaling data and compute, or adopting stronger low-level backbones (e.g., Restormer, SwinIR), would unlock the in-context learning potential the paper hints at, and whether the Q-A-Q-A paradigm generalizes beyond the 15 tasks tested.
