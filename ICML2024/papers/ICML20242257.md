## Problem

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting substantially improves LLM reasoning performance, but crafting effective CoT recipes requires human experts who understand both the task and prompting mechanics, limiting scalability. Moreover, the CoT prompt that works best for one model may perform poorly on another, making fair cross-model comparisons infeasible without per-model prompt optimization. No prior method automatically discovers model-specific CoT recipes from a small set of labeled examples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of 20 training question-answer pairs (with optional k∈{1,3} clones for diversity) and a generic instruction message, and produces a set of N=20k CoT recipes (free-text step-by-step solutions). At test time, K=5 recipes are selected based on individual training accuracy and inserted as few-shot demonstrations alongside the test question; the LLM then generates a reasoning chain followed by a final answer extracted between <answer> tags. The entire pipeline operates on text with no gradient-based training of the LLM.

## Method

Reprompting frames CoT recipe discovery as sampling from a joint distribution p(z₁,…,z_N | {x_i,y_i}, m) using Gibbs sampling. Initialization draws each recipe z_j independently via zero-shot prompting from LLM₁, with rejection sampling (p_rej=0.99) to occasionally retain imperfect solutions. Iterative sampling then picks a random index j, selects K other recipes as in-context examples, and prompts LLM₂ to generate a new recipe for problem x_j; the new recipe is accepted if it yields the correct answer or passes a stochastic rejection test. This process is equivalent to an evolutionary algorithm where recipes are recombined (crossover via multi-example prompting) and mutated (LLM generation) across up to 20,000 iterations until convergence. A key design choice is decoupling LLM₁ (initialization) from LLM₂ (sampling), enabling a stronger model to bootstrap a weaker one by providing diverse initial strategies that the weaker model then recombines and refines.

## Results

On 5 BBH tasks, Reprompting with ChatGPT achieves 83.0% average accuracy, outperforming human-written CoT (70.1%), CoT+Self-Consistency (71.2%), APO (50.1%), and Auto-CoT (72.2%). Across all 20 tasks (5 BBH + 7 additional BBH + 8 MATH/GSM8K), Reprompting achieves +9.4 points over human-written CoT on average. The combo model (ChatGPT for initialization + InstructGPT for sampling) reaches 81.2% on the 5 BBH tasks, and Reprompting with ChatGPT alone reaches 99.6% on Object Counting and 93.2% on Temporal Sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reprompting (ChatGPT) consistently outperforms human-written CoT prompts (Suzgun et al., 2022) on all 5 BBH tasks, with the largest gap on Temporal Sequences (93.2 vs. 66.8, +26.4) and Geometric Shapes (72.8 vs. 58.0, +14.8). Against CoT+Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022b), Reprompting wins on all 5 tasks by 2–26 points (83.0 vs. 71.2 average). Against APO (Pryzant et al., 2023), Reprompting achieves a dramatic +32.9 point margin (83.0 vs. 50.1), with APO underperforming even zero-shot on two tasks. Against Auto-CoT (Zhang et al., 2022), Reprompting leads by +10.8 points on average (83.0 vs. 72.2), winning on all 5 tasks. On the broader 15-task evaluation (Table 2), Reprompting still beats CoT by +8.2 points on average and notably outperforms CoT on tasks where human-written CoT degrades below zero-shot (e.g., Movie Recommendation: 78.4 vs. 25.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Gibbs sampling (Geman & Geman, 1984) as an iterative framework for discovering Chain-of-Thought recipes, building directly on the CoT prompting formalism of Wei et al. (2022) where step-by-step solutions z_i are inserted between questions and answers. It departs from prior automated CoT methods like Auto-CoT (Zhang et al., 2022), which performs a single zero-shot generation pass without iterative refinement, and APO (Pryzant et al., 2023), which edits a single prompt via textual gradients. The key conceptual move is treating the set of CoT recipes as a joint distribution to be sampled, where each recipe is updated conditioned on the others, enabling recombination and evolution of reasoning strategies across training examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CoT prompts optimized for one LLM can underperform by 18–19 points on another (Table 4) challenges the common practice of using a single human-written CoT prompt across models for fair comparison, and the combo-model result (ChatGPT bootstrapping InstructGPT for up to +71 points) suggests practical value in cross-model prompt transfer. Limitations include evaluation on only two proprietary LLMs and 20 tasks, reliance on OpenAI API access (costing ~$80–$800 per task), and no theoretical convergence guarantee for the Gibbs sampler in this non-standard setting. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-source models, longer reasoning chains, or tasks where the answer space is open-ended rather than exact-match.
