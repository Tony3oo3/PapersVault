## Problem

In standard Multi-Head Attention, heads operate independently, causing a low-rank bottleneck in attention score matrices that limits expressiveness and head redundancy that wastes parameters and computation. Existing head-composition approaches either only combine head outputs (surface-level), use static projection composition (computationally expensive and input-agnostic), or lack the combination of true compositionality, dynamicity, and efficiency simultaneously.

## Input / Output

DCMHA is a drop-in replacement for MHA that consumes the same Q, K, V projection matrices and produces the same-shaped attention output, but inserts two Compose functions: one on the attention score tensor A^S ∈ R^{H×T×S} before softmax and one on the attention weight tensor A^W after softmax. Each Compose call takes the attention tensor plus the full query matrix Q ∈ R^{T×D_m} and key matrix K ∈ R^{S×D_m} to generate input-dependent H×H transformation matrices per (i,j) pair, without materializing the full 4-D tensor. For language modeling the model outputs next-token logits; for the vision experiments it outputs ImageNet-1K class labels from ViT patch embeddings.

## Method

DCMHA dynamically composes attention heads by transforming each attention vector A_{:ij} through five branches: a static base (replaced by a skip connection in practice), query-wise low-rank projection (rank R=2), query-wise tanh gating, key-wise low-rank projection, and key-wise tanh gating, whose outputs are summed. The dynamic projection and gating weights are generated from Q_i and K_j via small FFNs (GELU-activated) and linear projections respectively, making the composition input-dependent. The key efficiency insight is a two-level tensor decomposition: a row-plus-column split that separates query-wise and key-wise transformations (avoiding materialization of the full T×S×H×H tensor), followed by a low-rank-plus-diagonal decomposition that reduces each H×H transformation to 2HR+H parameters. Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 establish that static attention-score composition is equivalent to H-fold QK projection expansion and static attention-weight composition is equivalent to H-fold OV projection expansion, confirming fundamental expressiveness gains over output-level composition.

## Results

DCFormer matches the performance of models with ~1.7×–2.0× compute: DCFormer-834M matches Transformer with 1.87× compute, and DCFormer++-834M matches Transformer++ with 1.67× compute. At large scale, DCPythia-6.9B achieves Pile validation perplexity of 5.95 (vs. 6.29 for Pythia-6.9B and 6.01 for Pythia-12B) and 56.7% average zero-shot accuracy across 11 downstream tasks, outperforming both Pythia-6.9B (55.1%) and Pythia-12B (56.5%). On the Flan Collection dataset, DCPythia-6.9B achieves 7.13 ppl vs. 7.85 for Pythia-6.9B and 7.26 for Pythia-12B, showing larger gains on instruction-following and emergent abilities. For vision, DCViT-S/16 (22M params) matches ViT-M/16 (39M params) on ImageNet-1K, achieving 80.1% top-1 accuracy at epoch 300.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pythia (Biderman et al., 2023), DCPythia-6.9B outperforms Pythia-12B (a model ~74% larger) on Pile perplexity (5.95 vs. 6.01), Flan perplexity (7.13 vs. 7.26), and average zero-shot accuracy (56.7% vs. 56.5%), while DCPythia-2.8B beats Pythia-2.8B by 0.27 ppl on Pile and +1.4% average accuracy. Against the GPT-3-style Transformer (Brown et al., 2020), DCFormer-834M achieves the same validation loss as a Transformer trained with 1.87× more compute. Against the LLaMA/Transformer++ architecture (Touvron et al., 2023), DCFormer++-834M matches Transformer++ with 1.67× compute, and the relative improvement of DCMHA over Transformer++ decreases less with scale than RoPE+SwiGLU alone. For vision, DCViT-S/16 (22M params) achieves 80.1% top-1 on ImageNet-1K at epoch 300, matching ViT-M/16 (39M params, 80.3%) while using only 56% of the parameters, and outperforming the same-size ViT-S/16 baseline (79.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCMHA extends the line of head-composition work on Multi-Head Attention, most directly building on Talking-Heads Attention (Shazeer et al., 2020), which introduced static cross-head projections before and after softmax. The paper's key departure is making this composition dynamic (input-dependent) and applying it to both attention scores and weights simultaneously, while maintaining efficiency through a two-level tensor decomposition (row-plus-column, then low-rank-plus-diagonal). Theoretically, the paper proves that static attention-matrix composition is equivalent to H-fold-expanded projection composition (Theorems 2.1–2.2), but shows that dynamic composition has no such projection equivalent, establishing it as strictly more general. This positions DCMHA as the first method to satisfy true compositionality, dynamicity, and efficiency simultaneously (Table 1), filling the gap between lightweight output-level composition and expensive projection-level composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCFormer's ability to match ~1.7×–2.0× compute of baseline Transformers as a drop-in MHA replacement makes it a practically attractive architectural upgrade for foundation model training, with DCPythia-6.9B outperforming the 1.7×-larger Pythia-12B on both perplexity and downstream tasks. The synthetic-task analysis and head-diversity measurements provide mechanistic evidence that dynamic composition genuinely recombines QK and OV circuits, addressing the low-rank bottleneck and head redundancy problems. Key limitations include 10–25% training throughput overhead (I/O-bound compose operations), the difficulty of adapting pretrained Transformers to DCFormer via fine-tuning, and the fact that gains are most pronounced at larger scales. Open questions include combining DCMHA with linear-attention or SSM variants, developing FlashAttention-style kernel fusion to reduce the I/O bottleneck, and deeper interpretability of the learned composition patterns.
