## Problem

Existing federated learning platforms and benchmarks are limited to simple image classification with supervised learning and label distribution shifts, overlooking the broader spectrum of practical CV tasks (detection, segmentation, pose estimation), diverse data patterns (semi-supervised, unsupervised, feature shifts, continual learning), and model configurations (split learning, heterogeneous architectures). No single platform provides comprehensive, out-of-the-box support for these practical FL scenarios across task, data, and model levels simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242596/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform consumes configuration files (YAML or Python dictionaries specifying dataset, split strategy, model, optimizer, and FL hyperparameters) along with RGB images or 3D point clouds partitioned across clients, and produces trained model parameters, task-specific predictions (class labels, bounding boxes, segmentation masks, keypoint coordinates, identity embeddings), and dual evaluation metrics covering both algorithmic performance (accuracy, mAP, mIoU, PCK) and system metrics (computation time, communication time, memory consumption). A distinguishing feature is the three-tier customization interface: users can modify only configuration parameters, register custom components (datasets, models, client/server logic) as plugins, or fully override the FL workflow execution logic.

## Method

COALA is a vision-centric FL platform organized around three benchmarking levels (task, data, model) and three degrees of customization (configuration, component, workflow). The system architecture comprises a task scheduler, resource manager, data manager, model manager, communicator, aggregator, and tracker that automate the full FL lifecycle from task submission through training rounds to output delivery. At the task level, it supports 15 CV tasks including federated multiple-task learning with shared backbones and task-specific decoders. At the data level, it provides out-of-the-box simulations for label shifts (Dirichlet-based), feature/domain shifts (multi-domain datasets), semi-supervised and unsupervised FL, federated continual learning with a drift detector, and test-time distribution shift. At the model level, it benchmarks full-model training, split learning (MocoSFL), clustered/personalized FL with multiple models (IFCA, FedRep), and federated parameter-efficient fine-tuning of foundation models (Fed-LoRA on ViT-B/16). The platform is validated through systematic experiments using FedAvg as the primary algorithm across all scenarios, with comparisons against specialized methods like FedICON, TARGET, SemiFL, and FedMTL.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 100 clients, FedAvg achieves 90.90% accuracy under IID partitioning but drops to 84.08% under Dir(0.5) label shift; on CIFAR-100 the corresponding numbers are 63.52% and 56.80%. For test-time covariate shift, FedICON substantially outperforms vanilla FedAvg (e.g., 76.62% vs. 53.43% on CIFAR-10 Dir, 95.27% vs. 89.63% on Digits-Five). In federated PEFT of ViT-B/16, Fed-LoRA (r=1) reaches 97.89% on CIFAR-10 and 90.57% on CIFAR-100, surpassing Fed-Linear (95.36% / 81.30%) while using only 0.05%–0.13% of trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedICON (Tan et al., 2023) consistently outperforms vanilla FedAvg under covariate shift, with the largest gap on CIFAR-10 Dir (76.62% vs. 53.43%, a 23.2-point improvement) and Office-Caltech (76.27% vs. 65.02%); even without shift, FedICON's contrastive feature learning yields modest gains (e.g., 92.38% vs. 89.22% on CIFAR-10 Dir). In clustered/personalized FL (Table 7), IFCA (Ghosh et al., 2020) leads on Digits-5 with feature shift (95.60% vs. FedAvg 94.74%), while FedAvg-FT (local fine-tuning) is strongest on CIFAR-10 with label shift (91.16% vs. FedAvg 90.17%); FedRep (Collins et al., 2021) underperforms both on CIFAR-10 (85.20%). For federated continual learning, TARGET (Zhang et al., 2023b) with synthetic data mitigates catastrophic forgetting more effectively than FedAvg across incremental tasks on CIFAR-10/100. In semi-supervised FL, the label-in-server setting (SemiFL, Diao et al., 2022) outperforms the label-in-client setting, as server-side fine-tuning produces more reliable pseudo-labels. For federated multiple-task learning, FedMTL (Zhuang et al., 2023c) surpasses both all-in-one and one-by-one baselines by leveraging task affinity grouping.

## Relation to Prior Work

COALA carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of FL benchmarks (LEAF, FedCV, OARF, FedReID, FedScale) into a single vision-centric platform that simultaneously addresses task diversity, data heterogeneity patterns, and model configuration variations. Prior platforms like FedML/FedCV supported a handful of vision tasks under supervised, single-model, static-data assumptions, while others (FLUTE, FLGO) focused on system flexibility without comprehensive vision coverage. COALA departs from this by treating the three levels—task (15 CV tasks including pose estimation and multi-task learning), data (semi/unsupervised, feature shifts, continual learning), and model (split learning, heterogeneous architectures, PEFT of foundation models)—as a unified design space rather than isolated concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COALA provides the first comprehensive benchmarking infrastructure for practical FL scenarios in vision, filling gaps that no single prior platform addressed (e.g., federated pose estimation, federated multiple-task learning, split learning at scale, PEFT of ViT in FL). The benchmark results reveal open questions: whether feature shifts truly harm detection performance (they appear not to, possibly due to data quantity confounds), why larger backbones yield negligible gains in federated pose estimation, and how to develop fairer global models under domain shift. Limitations include reliance on FedAvg as the primary baseline, limited dataset coverage per task (e.g., only BDD100K for detection), and the absence of privacy mechanisms (differential privacy, secure aggregation) that are critical for real deployment.
