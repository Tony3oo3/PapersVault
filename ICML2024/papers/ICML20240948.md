## Problem

While GNNs operating on covariance matrices derived from input data alone (C_XX) have been studied, no theoretical framework existed to determine the optimal graph structure for a supervised learning task. This paper asks: what should the graph shift operator (GSO) be when the goal is to predict outputs from inputs, and can the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) provide a principled answer?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240948/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series (e.g., 100 fMRI features × 4500 time points per subject) where the input is the signal vector z^(t) at the current time step and the target is z^(t+Δt) at a future time step. The GSO is constructed as the symmetric cross-covariance C_XY = ½(XY^T + YX^T) between the input and output data matrices, rather than the input-only covariance C_XX. The output is the predicted multivariate signal at the future time step, and the framework also supports transfer across different feature dimensionalities (e.g., 50→100 features) by simply replacing the GSO.

## Method

The paper defines 'alignment' A = ỹ^T Θ ỹ as a scalar measuring how well the output vector aligns with dominant NTK eigenvectors, and proves that gradient descent convergence rate is proportional to A. For graph filters, maximizing a lower bound on A under a Frobenius-norm constraint yields the optimal GSO as a scaled version of C_XY (Theorem 3.5). For two-layer GNNs with tanh activation, a Hermite polynomial expansion decomposes the alignment into a leading linear term and a smaller correction, and the paper proves (Theorem 3.13) that the linear alignment lower-bounds the full alignment up to constants, so the same cross-covariance optimality carries over to the non-linear case. Empirically, GNNs and graph filters using C_XY as the GSO achieve faster training convergence and lower test error than C_XX-based counterparts on HCP-YA resting-state fMRI time series prediction, with the advantage persisting under cross-dimensional transfer.

## Results

On the HCP-YA resting-state fMRI dataset (100 features, 4500 time points, 1003 subjects), GNNs and graph filters using the cross-covariance matrix C_XY as the GSO consistently achieve faster training convergence and lower test error than those using the input-only covariance C_XX, across all prediction horizons Δt ∈ {1,2,3,4,5}. The test-error gap between C_XY and C_XX predictors remains positive and consistent even as prediction accuracy diminishes with increasing Δt. In the transferability setting (training on 50-feature data and testing on 100-feature data for the same individual), the relative advantage of C_XY over C_XX is preserved, confirming that the cross-covariance benefit is not an artifact of in-distribution evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the coVariance Neural Network (VNN) from Sihag et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which uses the input-only covariance matrix C_XX as the GSO. Across all experimental settings—graph filters, two-layer GNNs, multiple prediction horizons (Δt = 1 through 5), and the transfer scenario (50→100 features)—the C_XY-based predictors uniformly outperform the C_XX-based VNNs on both training loss convergence speed and final test error. The paper reports that the gap in test error between C_XY and C_XX is consistent across the full HCP-YA dataset (averaged over all 1003 individuals) and does not vanish at larger Δt values. In the transfer setting, the relative decrease in loss from using C_XY versus C_XX is similar in both the in-distribution (100→100) and cross-dimensional (50→100) scenarios, indicating the cross-covariance advantage is robust to the GNN transferability mechanism. The paper also notes that C_XX is representative of a broader class of input-only graph constructions (Pearson correlation, Euclidean distance with Gaussian kernel), so the C_XY advantage generalizes beyond the specific covariance baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of NTK theory (Jacot et al., 2018), graph filter-based GNN architectures (Gama et al., 2020), and Covariance Neural Networks (Sihag et al., 2022). Prior NTK analyses of GNNs (Du et al., 2019; Sabanayagam et al., 2021, 2022; Krishnagopal & Ruiz, 2023) used NTKs to *explain* empirical trends in GNN performance, whereas this paper inverts the direction: it uses NTK alignment analysis to *prescribe* a specific architectural choice—namely, that the cross-covariance matrix C_XY between input and output data should serve as the Graph Shift Operator. The connection to PLS regression (maximizing cross-covariance between latent spaces) is noted but the GNN formulation avoids artificial dimensionality reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification (via NTK alignment optimization) for using cross-covariance graphs in supervised GNN learning, moving beyond the purely empirical motivation of prior covariance-based GNN work. Key limitations include the restriction to equal-dimensional input/output vectors, the reliance on lower bounds whose tightness is not characterized, and theoretical guarantees only for two-layer GNNs with tanh activation. Open questions include extending the analysis to different input/output dimensionalities, other GNN architectures (GAT, transformers), and whether the alignment-based GSO selection generalizes beyond the graph filter convolution operator.
