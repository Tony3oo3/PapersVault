## Problem

Prior work on Bayesian offline bandits relies on LCB-style pessimism, selecting actions with the highest lower confidence bound on reward. The paper argues this approach is inherently flawed for minimizing Bayesian regret because penalizing uncertainty also penalizes upside potential, and proves that LCB is guaranteed to increase regret even in simple problems. No prior algorithm explicitly minimizes analytical upper bounds on Bayesian regret.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian posterior (mean μ ∈ R^d, covariance Σ ∈ R^{d×d}) derived from a logged dataset via Bayesian linear regression, a feature matrix Φ ∈ R^{d×k} mapping k actions to d-dimensional features, a risk tolerance δ ∈ (0, 1/2), and a number of tightening iterations m. It outputs a randomized policy π ∈ Δ_k — a probability distribution over the k actions rather than a single deterministic choice — along with a scalar regret upper bound ρ that certifies the policy's worst-case performance at confidence level 1−δ. The optimization is solved via SOCP solvers (e.g., MOSEK) with worst-case complexity O(k⁶).

## Method

BRMOB reformulates the high-confidence Bayesian regret as a Value-at-Risk (VaR) of the epistemic uncertainty and derives two complementary upper bounds: an action-set bound (Theorem 4.3) that grows sub-logarithmically with the number of actions k via a union bound over per-action VaRs, and a parameter-space bound (Theorem 4.5) that grows with the parameter dimension d via a χ² credible region. The algorithm jointly minimizes both bounds by solving a second-order cone program, then iteratively tightens the action-set bound by optimizing the probability allocation ξ across actions using exponential conic constraints. A key design choice is that BRMOB returns a randomized policy rather than a deterministic one, enabling hedging that significantly reduces regret compared to point selections. The paper proves a matching lower bound showing the upper bounds are tight, and constructs a family of problems where LCB's uncertainty penalty is provably counterproductive for regret minimization.

## Results

Theoretically, BRMOB achieves a regret bound of $\mathfrak{R}_\delta(\hat{\pi}) \leq 2\eta$ where $\eta = \sqrt{\min\{2\log(k/\delta),\, 5d\log(1/\delta)\} / (\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_0)^{-1} + \gamma n \bar{\sigma}^{-2})}$, which grows as $\sqrt{\log k}$ in the number of actions and $\sqrt{d}$ in the parameter dimension. In contrast, FlatOPO's bound grows as $d$ in the parameter dimension (a factor of $\sqrt{d}$ worse). Numerically, on synthetic domains with $k=d=5$, $k=d=50$, and $d=4$ with $k\in\{10,50,100\}$, BRMOB consistently achieves the lowest Bayesian regret across all data sizes, with the gap widening as posterior uncertainty increases. The only challenging regime for BRMOB is when $k \gg d$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRMOB outperforms FlatOPO (Hong et al., 2023) on all synthetic domains tested, both in regret magnitude and in the tightness of its theoretical guarantee. FlatOPO's $\beta = \sqrt{5d\log(1/\delta)}$ coefficient grows rapidly with $d$, making its LCB penalty overly conservative; the paper shows that no tuned value of $\beta$ allows FlatOPO to beat even the simple Greedy baseline, consistent with the theoretical result that LCB is inherently unsuitable for Bayesian regret minimization. Against the scenario-based method (Nemirovski & Shapiro, 2006), BRMOB achieves lower regret with far fewer samples (the scenario method required 4000 posterior samples) and provides a provable regret guarantee, whereas the scenario method offers no theoretical bound. BRMOB also matches or slightly exceeds Greedy (which sets $\beta=0$), with the advantage coming from its ability to randomize over actions to hedge regret. The theoretical lower bound in Theorem 4.4 confirms that BRMOB's upper bound is tight up to a constant factor in the coefficient $\kappa_u(k)/\kappa_l(k-1) < 10$ for $\delta \leq 1/2$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LCB (lower confidence bound) paradigm in Bayesian offline bandits and offline RL (Hong et al. 2023, Delage & Mannor 2010, Petrik & Russel 2019), which selects actions by maximizing reward minus an uncertainty penalty. Instead, BRMOB directly minimizes analytical upper bounds on Bayesian regret derived through connections to Value-at-Risk and Entropic Value-at-Risk, solved via second-order and exponential cone programming. The closest prior result is the Bernstein technique for bounding linear chance-constrained programs (Nemirovski & Shapiro 2007), which the paper identifies as a special case of its action-set bound. The paper also draws on robust optimization over credible regions (Gupta 2019, Petrik & Russel 2019) but applies it to the non-concave maximum-of-Gaussians structure that arises in regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that penalizing reward uncertainty (the core of all LCB algorithms) is provably counterproductive for Bayesian regret minimization—challenges a foundational assumption in offline decision-making and suggests that explicit regret minimization is a more principled direction than pessimism. Open questions include whether these insights transfer to frequentist offline RL settings (where similar concerns have been raised by Xie et al. 2022, Xiao et al. 2021), extension to contextual bandits, and whether the parameter-space bound (Theorem 4.5) can be generalized to sub-Gaussian posteriors without additional assumptions. The empirical validation is limited to synthetic domains, and the practical impact on real-world offline bandit applications remains to be demonstrated.
