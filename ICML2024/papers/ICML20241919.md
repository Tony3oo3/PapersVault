## Problem

Applying federated learning to fine-tune pre-trained language models on edge devices is hampered by three simultaneous challenges: model parameters are inaccessible due to encapsulation in mobile frameworks, local backpropagation is infeasible on resource-constrained devices, and exchanging millions-to-billions of parameters creates prohibitive communication overhead. Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, adapter tuning, p-tuning) reduce communication but still require clients to access model parameters and perform gradient-based optimization, leaving them impractical for real-world edge deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw text inputs (movie review sentences for SST-2, Yelp reviews, or news articles for AG's News) and produces discrete class labels (binary sentiment or 4-way topic). The trainable artifact is a 500-dimensional continuous prompt vector in a low-dimensional subspace, projected to the full embedding space via a fixed random projection matrix; this vector is concatenated to input embeddings before PLM inference. Only forward passes of the frozen PLM are required at clients—no gradients or parameter access—making the entire local training loop a sequence of inference API calls evaluated by CMA-ES.

## Method

FedBPT adapts Black-Box Tuning (BBT) to a federated setting by replacing gradient-based local training with CMA-ES, a gradient-free evolutionary optimizer that searches for optimal prompts in a low-dimensional space (d=500) using only forward inference. The key server-side innovation is a server-level CMA-ES that treats uploaded local search results as a population of samples rather than averaging distribution statistics (which is mathematically invalid for covariance matrices), with a theoretically derived corrected step size σ′ that accounts for multiple local search iterations per communication round. To counter non-IID overfitting where a client's dominant class causes the prompt to collapse to a single prediction, each client applies a random token-masking perturbation and optimizes the ratio of real-input loss to perturbed-input loss, ensuring the prompt generalizes beyond the local label distribution. The entire system communicates only 500 scalar values per client per round (≈4 KB), reducing communication by over 500,000× compared to full-model FedAvg while maintaining competitive accuracy with RoBERTa and Llama 2.

## Results

FedBPT trains only 500 parameters (a low-dimensional vector optimized via CMA-ES) and achieves 86.47% accuracy on SST-2 (non-IID, RoBERTa), 81.03% on AG's News, and 90.8% on Yelp. Compared to gradient-based baselines requiring 51K–355M trainable parameters, FedBPT reduces per-device per-round communication from up to 120 MB (FedP-tuning) to 4 KB — a >500,000× reduction — while cutting memory footprint by over 3× (1.8 GB vs. 5.8–7.2 GB). With Llama 2 (7B), the same 500-parameter budget reduces communication from ~2 GB to 4 KB per round, and FedBPT improves accuracy by 12–13% over manual prompts under non-IID settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedPrompt (51K params), FedBPT (500 params) wins on SST-2 non-IID (86.47% vs. 85.55%) but trails on AG's News non-IID (81.03% vs. 85.62%) and Yelp non-IID (90.80% vs. 91.47%), a gap the paper attributes to FedPrompt's gradient-based optimization having access to model internals. Against FedAvg (355M params) and FedLoRA (786K params), FedBPT wins on all three datasets under non-IID (e.g., SST-2: 86.47% vs. 82.40% and 84.53%; AG's News: 81.03% vs. 76.54% and 75.90%), demonstrating that full-model or LoRA fine-tuning degrades under few-shot federated settings. Against In-Context Learning (Brown et al., 2020), FedBPT improves SST-2 non-IID accuracy by 6.77 points (86.47% vs. 79.70%) and AG's News by 4.07 points, while slightly underperforming on Yelp (90.80% vs. 89.65% — actually a 1.15-point win). The paper also shows that simply combining BBT with FedAvg (FedAvg-BBT) yields only marginal gains over manual prompts, validating the need for the proposed server-level CMA-ES aggregation and perturbation-based overfitting mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedBPT adapts Black-box Tuning (BBT, Sun et al., ICML 2022), which uses CMA-ES to optimize prompts without backpropagation, into a federated learning setting. It departs from standard FL algorithms (FedAvg, FedPrompt, FedLoRA, FedP-tuning) that communicate model parameters or gradients by instead exchanging only CMA-ES distribution statistics (mean vector, covariance matrix, step size), reducing communicated variables from millions/billions to ~500. The key methodological departure is the server-level CMA-ES aggregation, which treats local search results as sampled solutions rather than directly averaging distribution parameters as FedAvg would, and a perturbation-based regularization to counter non-IID overfitting that is more severe under gradient-free optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedBPT offers a practical pathway for federated adaptation of LLMs on resource-constrained edge devices where backpropagation and full parameter access are infeasible, with a 500k× communication reduction and 3× memory savings while matching gradient-based accuracy. The scalability property—trainable parameters remain at 500 regardless of model size (RoBERTa 356M to Llama 2 7B)—is particularly relevant as PLM scales continue to grow. Limitations include evaluation only on few-shot classification tasks with 10 clients, and the framework's reliance on a fixed random projection matrix that may not capture task-specific structure as well as gradient-based methods. Open questions include extension to generative tasks, larger client populations, and whether the CMA-ES search quality degrades in higher-dimensional prompt spaces.
