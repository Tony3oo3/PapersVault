## Problem

Flow Matching (FM) and its variants rely on a single neural ODE to transport mass from a source to a target distribution, but the existence and uniqueness theorem for ODEs makes it impossible to resolve singularities arising from the inherent (joint) heterogeneity of multi-modal source or target distributions. The paper proves that even optimally trained FM cannot transport between certain simple distributions (e.g., a point mass to a two-point mixture) without producing discontinuous flow maps or large Lipschitz constants, leading to degraded sample quality and slow inference requiring many NFEs.

## Input / Output

The method takes source distribution samples (e.g., a two-mode Gaussian mixture rather than a standard Gaussian) and a discrete switching signal s = (y₀, y₁) formed by pairing cluster labels from K₀ source clusters and K₁ target clusters, drawn from a coupling matrix P satisfying mass-conservation constraints. The shared neural network v_t(x; θ|s) receives the linearly interpolated point (1−t)x₀ + tx₁, the time t, and the switching signal s, and outputs a velocity vector. On CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB), the output is a generated image; on synthetic 1-D/2-D tasks, it is a point in the target distribution.

## Method

SFM replaces the single ODE of FM with a family of conditional ODEs u_t(x|s) indexed by a switching signal s, where the source and target distributions are decomposed as mixtures q_i(x) = ∫ q_i(x|s) q°(s) ds. Training uses the Switching Conditional FM (SCFM) objective, which regresses the simple conditional vector field u_t(x|z,s) = x₁ − x₀ with a single shared network v_t(x;θ|s) across all switching signals, and is shown to have the same gradient as the intractable SFM objective. The switching mechanism partitions empirical source and target data into K₀ and K₁ clusters (via standard clustering), then samples s from a coupling matrix P ∈ ℝ^{K₀×K₁} that satisfies row/column mass constraints; the extremal-solution property of optimal transport guarantees P has at most K₀+K₁−1 nonzero entries, reducing the number of distinct ODEs from K₀K₁ to linear complexity. The framework composes orthogonally with minibatch optimal transport (OT-SFM) to further straighten each conditional flow, and theoretically permits trajectory intersection across different ODEs—something a single ODE forbids—thereby eliminating the singularities proven unavoidable in FM.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a two-mode Gaussian mixture source, the best SFM variant (I-SFM two2ten, extremal) achieves a FID of 4.22 at adaptive NFE, outperforming OT-CFM (4.91) and I-CFM (94.55). At a fixed NFE of 20, I-SFM (two2ten, mixed) reaches 15.60 FID versus 26.15 for OT-CFM, and at NFE=40 it achieves 6.98 versus 10.90. The paper also demonstrates on synthetic 1-D and 2-D Gaussian mixture distributions that switching ODEs eliminates the singularities inherent to single-ODE flow matching, producing smoother and more regular transport trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: I-CFM (independent coupling flow matching, Lipman et al. 2022) and OT-CFM (optimal transport coupling flow matching, Pooladian et al. 2023). Because the experiments use a two-mode Gaussian mixture as the source distribution (rather than a standard Gaussian), I-CFM performs very poorly (FID 94.55 at adaptive NFE), as the mode separation of the source creates singularities that a single ODE cannot resolve. OT-CFM handles this better (FID 4.91) but is still outperformed by the best SFM variants: I-SFM (two2ten, extremal) achieves 4.22 and I-SFM (two2ten, mixed) achieves 4.27 at adaptive NFE. At lower NFE budgets (e.g., NFE=20), the gap widens further, with I-SFM (two2ten, mixed) at 15.60 versus OT-CFM at 26.15. Interestingly, OT-SFM variants do not consistently outperform their I-SFM counterparts, which the authors attribute to the switching mechanism already alleviating the singularities that OT coupling would otherwise address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching / Conditional Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2022) by replacing the single ODE with a pool of ODEs selected via a discrete switching signal, motivated by a theoretical demonstration that a single ODE fundamentally cannot transport mass between certain simple distributions (e.g., a point mass to a two-point mass) due to the existence-and-uniqueness theorem for IVPs. The switching mechanism is orthogonal to and composable with existing coupling strategies such as minibatch optimal transport (Pooladian et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2023a,b), yielding the OT-SFM variant. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard CFM formulation by introducing a latent switching variable s that partitions both source and target into conditional sub-distributions, each governed by its own vector field, thereby allowing trajectory intersections across different ODEs while preserving the marginal probability path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical results (Propositions 3.1, 3.5, 3.7, Corollary 3.3) provide a clean and rigorous characterization of when and why single-ODE flow matching fails, which is a useful diagnostic for practitioners working with multi-modal or disconnected distributions. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the switching mechanism relies on explicit clustering of source and target data, which the authors acknowledge is challenging in high-dimensional settings like images, and the only real-dataset experiment uses CIFAR-10 with a deliberately non-standard two-mode Gaussian source. Open questions include whether the switching mechanism can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on hand-designed clusterings, how it scales to high-dimensional data where singularity identification is non-trivial, and whether the modest FID improvements over OT-CFM (e.g., 4.22 vs. 4.91 at adaptive NFE) persist on larger or more complex benchmarks.
