## Problem

Existing street-view geo-localization methods (retrieval-based and classification-based) operate as black boxes without providing interpretable reasoning, and no high-quality street-view dataset with reasoning annotations exists to train large vision-language models for this task. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a new LVLM-based paradigm that produces geo-localization predictions accompanied by human-interpretable reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single street-view RGB image (resized and patchified for the ViT encoder) along with a text question prompt (e.g., 'which country is the image located? Explain the reason') and produces a structured JSON output containing the predicted country, predicted city, and a natural-language reasoning explanation. The reasoning granularity is limited to country-level in the first training stage due to the coarseness of the game-derived text-image pairs, while city-level prediction is achieved in the second stage.

## Method

GeoReasoner is built on Qwen-VL (ViT vision encoder + VL Adapter + 9.6B LLM decoder) and is fine-tuned in two LoRA stages: first, reasoning tuning on ~3K text-image pairs harvested from GeoGuessr and Tuxun geo-localization games to inject human inference knowledge; second, location tuning on 70K+ high-locatability Google Street View images to learn fine-grained city-level prediction. A key enabling component is a CLIP-based locatability quantization network that uses MaskFormer semantic segmentation masks and Sentence-BERT similarity between game-derived textual clues and segmentation labels to compute a weighted locatability score per image, filtering 130K+ raw GSV images down to those with a score above 0.4. This data-quality filter, combined with the two-stage LoRA training, allows GeoReasoner to outperform StreetCLIP (trained on 1.1M images) while using only 70K training images and additionally providing step-by-step reasoning for each prediction.

## Results

On the primary GSV test set, GeoReasoner achieves 82.37% country-level accuracy (F1 0.9033) and 75.21% city-level accuracy (F1 0.8585), outperforming the best LVLM baseline Qwen-VL by 25.02% and 38.61% in F1, respectively. GeoReasoner also slightly surpasses StreetCLIP (F1 0.8854 country, 0.8543 city) while being trained on only 70K images versus StreetCLIP's 1.1M. On the Im2GPS benchmark, GeoReasoner fine-tuned on just 10K filtered Flickr images reaches 0.86 country-level accuracy, comparable to GeoCLIP (0.87) and ISNs (0.78) trained on millions of images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoReasoner outperforms all LVLM baselines substantially: over Qwen-VL (F1 0.7225→0.9033 country, 0.5270→0.8585 city), LLaVA (F1 0.5744→0.9033 country), and GPT-4V (F1 0.4923→0.9033 country, where GPT-4V's low recall of 0.34 stems from frequent refusals to answer). Against the specialized classification model StreetCLIP, GeoReasoner achieves a marginal F1 advantage (0.9033 vs. 0.8854 country; 0.8585 vs. 0.8543 city) despite using ~16× fewer training images (70K vs. 1.1M). On the Im2GPS and Im2GPS3k benchmarks, GeoReasoner fine-tuned on only 10K filtered Flickr images matches or exceeds GeoCLIP (0.86 vs. 0.87 country on Im2GPS; 0.83 vs. 0.83 on Im2GPS3k) and ISNs (0.86 vs. 0.78 country on Im2GPS), while trailing them on fine-grained Street1km accuracy (0.13 vs. 0.25/0.22). GeoReasoner clearly surpasses PlaNet, CPlaNet, Translocator, and GeoDecoder on country-level accuracy across both Im2GPS benchmarks, though it lags on the 1km street-level metric where coordinate-precision methods have an inherent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in image geo-localization—retrieval-based (matching against a geo-tagged gallery) and classification-based (partitioning the Earth into cells)—by introducing an LVLM-based approach that produces interpretable reasoning alongside location predictions. Architecturally, it builds on Qwen-VL as the backbone and repurposes CLIP-style visual-text alignment (via MaskFormer segmentation and Sentence-BERT) to construct a novel 'locatability' scoring network for data curation. The two-stage LoRA fine-tuning (reasoning tuning on 3K game-derived text-image pairs, then location tuning on 70K curated street views) is a practical adaptation strategy that distinguishes it from prior geo-localization classifiers that treat the task as a pure classification problem without inference traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that carefully curated, high-locatability data (70K images) can match or exceed models trained on an order of magnitude more data (StreetCLIP's 1.1M images), suggesting data quality and reasoning augmentation are underexplored levers in geo-localization. The locatability metric and the game-derived reasoning dataset are reusable assets for the broader street-view analysis community. Key open questions remain: the model fails on architectural replicas (e.g., Eiffel Tower copies), the reasoning dataset lacks landscape and vegetation cues, and GPT-4V's near-refusal to perform geo-localization (recall ≈ 0.31–0.34) highlights unresolved tensions between safety training and spatial reasoning in frontier LVLMs.
