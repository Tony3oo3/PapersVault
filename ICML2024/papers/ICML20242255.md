## Problem

Most provably sample-efficient RL algorithms assume fully adaptive policy updates after every episode, which is unrealistic when switching costs are high or data arrives in parallel. While prior work addressed rare policy switch and batch learning for specific structures (tabular, linear MDP, low eluder dimension), it remained open whether these adaptivity constraints could be handled under general function approximation classes such as Bellman Eluder dimension, linear mixture MDP, kernelized nonlinear regulator, and POMDPs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an episodic MDP or zero-sum Markov Game with a hypothesis class F (and auxiliary class G satisfying F⊆G), a decomposable loss function ℓ, and either a total episode budget K (for rare policy switch) or a pre-specified number of batches B (for batch learning). It outputs a sequence of policies π^k with a logarithmic switching cost Õ(dH log K) and sublinear regret Õ(κH√(dβK)), where d is the EC-class complexity parameter and β is a logarithmic confidence parameter. The key structural constraint is that the policy is only recomputed when the in-sample discrepancy between the current optimistic model and all historical data exceeds a threshold, rather than after every episode.

## Method

The paper introduces the Eluder-Condition (EC) class, which imposes a slightly stronger eluder assumption than standard Bellman Eluder dimension: the cumulative squared in-sample loss must be bounded by O(dβ log k) rather than O(√(dβk)), enabling tighter control over when new data genuinely improves estimation. For the rare policy switch problem, Algorithm 1 (ℓ₂-EC-RS) uses optimistic planning within a confidence set but employs a lazy switching strategy: the confidence set and policy are updated only when the in-sample discrepancy L_h^{1:k}(D_h^{1:k}, f^k, f^k) − inf_g L_h^{1:k}(D_h^{1:k}, f^k, g) exceeds 5β for some step h, reducing oracle calls from O(K) to O(log K). For batch learning, Algorithm 2 (ℓ₂-EC-Batch) uses a uniform grid of B batches and a Good/Bad batch decomposition: batches with small in-sample error (c_j ≤ 5) contribute O(√K) regret via standard optimism, while the number of Bad batches is bounded by O((log K)²) using the EC condition, yielding regret Õ(√(dHK) + dHK/B). The framework extends to zero-sum Markov Games and an ℓ₁-type EC class covering undercomplete POMDPs.

## Results

Theoretically, the proposed ℓ₂-EC-RS algorithm achieves a Ō(H√(dK)) regret with a Ō(dH log K) switching cost on the EC class, while the ℓ₂-EC-Batch algorithm achieves a Ō(√(dHK) + dHK/B) regret for B batches. Empirically, on the Linear Mixture MDP (T=2000), EC-RS reduces the average number of policy switches from 2000 (OPERA) to 92.8 over 10 simulations, cutting execution time from 321.6 s to 15.9 s while maintaining comparable cumulative reward. On Mujoco tasks (100,000 episodes), MEX_MF_low_switch reduces policy switches by 90–97% relative to MEX_MF (e.g., 10,052 vs. 100,000 on HalfCheetah-v2) while preserving similar reward performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OPERA (Chen et al., 2022) on the Linear Mixture MDP, EC-RS converges to the optimal value slightly slower in cumulative reward but reduces the average number of policy switches from 2000 to 92.8 (a ~21× reduction) and cuts wall-clock execution time from 321.6 s to 15.9 s (~20× speedup) over 10 simulations. Against MEX_MF (Liu et al., 2024) on three Mujoco tasks, MEX_MF_low_switch achieves comparable or even slightly better reward curves while reducing policy switches by 90–97%: 10,052 vs. 100,000 on HalfCheetah-v2, 29,930 vs. 100,000 on Hopper-v3, and 10,742 vs. 100,000 on Walker2d-v3. The authors attribute the occasional reward improvement to the conservative switching criterion preventing premature, potentially detrimental policy changes. In terms of theoretical coverage, the EC class subsumes all models handled by prior low-switching-cost algorithms (tabular MDP, linear MDP, low eluder dimension, generalized linear Bellman complete) and additionally covers new classes such as low D_Δ-type Bellman eluder dimension, linear mixture MDP, KNR, and undercomplete POMDP, none of which were previously addressed under adaptivity constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the eluder-dimension lineage (Russo & Van Roy, 2013; Jin et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022) by introducing a slightly stronger eluder condition (the EC class) that specifically enables controlling policy-switching frequency under general function approximation. Prior rare-policy-switch and batch-learning results were restricted to tabular MDPs, linear MDPs, or low-eluder-dimension classes with additional assumptions (e.g., value-closeness in Kong et al., 2021); the EC class unifies and extends these to linear mixture MDPs, kernelized nonlinear regulators, undercomplete POMDPs, and zero-sum Markov games. The lazy switching strategy builds on optimistic planning (GOLF, OPERA) but replaces per-episode confidence-set updates with a threshold-based update rule, and the ℓ₁-type EC class adapts the OMLE framework (Liu et al., 2022b) with a similar lazy mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of both rare policy switch and batch learning under a single general function-approximation framework, closing a gap left by prior work that could only handle specific model classes. The exponential reduction in oracle calls (O(K) → O(log K)) has direct practical implications for large-scale deployment where policy recompilation is expensive. Open questions include extending the ℓ₁-type EC class to multi-agent settings, clarifying the relationship between the EC class and the general eluder dimension, and whether the slightly stronger eluder condition (3.2) is truly necessary or an artifact of the analysis. The framework's coverage of POMDPs and zero-sum Markov games suggests a path toward provably efficient multi-agent RL under practical constraints.
