## Problem

Existing federated AUC maximization methods (e.g., LocalSCGDAM) only handle two-level compositional minimax optimization, where inner-level weights are updated once before AUC loss optimization. Preliminary experiments show that multi-level compositional learning (multiple inner-level updates) significantly improves AUC on highly imbalanced data, but the federated multi-level compositional minimax problem has never been studied: the multi-level structure coupled with distributed inner-level functions introduces biased stochastic gradients for both primal and dual variables, and it is unclear whether the number of levels K can be made level-independent in the convergence rate.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images for binary classification across five datasets (CatvsDog, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, Melanoma) with a positive-to-total imbalance ratio of 0.05, distributed across N=8 devices (N=4 for Melanoma due to GPU memory). Each device holds a local partition of the imbalanced training set; the output is a binary class label. The inner-level functions consume mini-batches (size 8 for STL-10, 16 otherwise) to perform K-step cross-entropy pretraining, while the outer-level consumes separate mini-batches for the minimax AUC loss, with all components synchronized every τ communication rounds.

## Method

LocalSMCGDAM formulates deep AUC maximization as a K-level compositional minimax problem where K steps of cross-entropy gradient descent (inner levels) precede the AUC minimax optimization (outer level). To handle the biased multi-level stochastic gradients, the algorithm employs a STORM-like recursive estimator at each inner level k to approximate the compositional function value, and a momentum-based moving-average technique to reduce variance in both the primal gradient (via chain rule across K levels) and the dual gradient. Communication occurs every τ iterations, synchronizing model parameters, momentum vectors, and all K inner-level function estimators across devices. Theoretically, the paper proves an O(K²/√(NT)) convergence rate for the nonconvex-strongly-concave problem, establishing linear speedup in N and level-independent complexity (K affects only a polynomial coefficient, not the order), yielding O(1/ε⁴) sample complexity and O(1/ε³) communication complexity.

## Results

The paper evaluates test AUC score across five highly imbalanced binary classification datasets (imbalance ratio 0.05) under three communication periods (τ=4, 8, 16). LocalSMCGDAM achieves the best AUC on every dataset and period, with headline results of 0.918 on CATvsDOG, 0.900 on CIFAR-10, 0.794 on STL-10, 0.676 on CIFAR-100, and 0.903 on Melanoma (τ=16). The method also achieves O(1/ε⁴) sample complexity and O(1/ε³) communication complexity with linear speedup in the number of devices N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocalSMCGDAM consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and communication period. The largest gains are over CoDA (Guo et al., 2020) and LocalSGDM-RL (Wang et al., 2021), which do not use a compositional framework: on CIFAR-10 (τ=4), LocalSMCGDAM achieves 0.900 AUC versus 0.791 (CoDA) and 0.793 (LocalSGDM-RL), a gap of over 10 points. Against the closest competitor LocalSCGDAM (Zhang et al., 2023), which uses only a single inner-level update (K=1), the multi-level approach (K=3) yields a consistent 2–4 point AUC improvement (e.g., 0.918 vs. 0.885 on CATvsDOG, 0.794 vs. 0.753 on STL-10). FedProx and Scaffold, which assume a balanced global distribution, are also surpassed, confirming the advantage of the AUC-based compositional formulation under global imbalance. The advantage over LocalSGDAM (Sharma et al., 2022) is moderate (3–5 points), as both use minimax AUC optimization but LocalSMCGDAM adds the multi-level compositional structure for better feature pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: multi-level stochastic compositional optimization (Yang et al. 2019; Balasubramanian et al. 2022; Jiang et al. 2022), which had only been studied for minimization problems, and federated minimax optimization for AUC maximization (CoDA by Guo et al. 2020; LocalSCGDAM by Zhang et al. 2023), which was limited to two-level compositional structure. The key departure is formulating the inner cross-entropy pretraining as a K-level compositional function within a federated minimax framework, requiring a STORM-like estimator at each level plus momentum-based gradient tracking for both primal and dual variables. The algorithmic design inherits the moving-average and variance-reduction ideas from single-machine multi-level compositional methods but must additionally handle per-device biased gradients and periodic communication, a combination not addressed in any prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence analysis for the federated multi-level compositional minimax problem, establishing O(1/ε⁴) sample complexity and O(1/ε³) communication complexity with linear speedup in N devices and level-independent rate in K. For practitioners handling highly imbalanced federated classification (e.g., medical imaging), the multi-level pretraining intuition—using multiple cross-entropy steps before AUC fine-tuning—provides a practical recipe that consistently outperforms the two-level baseline by 2–4 AUC points across five datasets. A notable limitation is that the convergence constant still scales as C̃^K, and the empirical finding that excessively large K degrades performance (because cross-entropy overfits to the majority class) suggests the practical benefit of multi-level structure is bounded. Open questions include whether truly K-independent constants are achievable and whether the approach extends to non-iid or asynchronous federated settings.
