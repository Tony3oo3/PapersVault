## Problem

Estimating probability divergences (1-Wasserstein, Sobolev IPMs, MMD, density) suffers from the curse of dimensionality, yet many data-generative models (graphs, point clouds, molecules) exhibit group invariances that should reduce sample complexity. Prior work (Chen et al., 2023) established gains only for finite groups acting on full-dimensional submanifolds of R^d, leaving the case of positive-dimensional Lie groups and general manifolds completely open. This paper proves that invariances yield a two-fold gain: a multiplicative factor in effective sample size (vol(M)/vol(M/G)) and, for positive-dimensional groups, an improved convergence exponent from 1/dim(M) to 1/dim(M/G).

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from a G-invariant Borel probability measure μ on a compact, connected, boundaryless smooth Riemannian manifold M, together with the smooth isometric action of a Lie group G on M. It outputs a G-invariant estimated measure μ̃ (or density dμ̃/dx) and a convergence rate bound of the form E[D(μ, μ̃)] ≲ (δ_G/n)^{κ_G}, where δ_G and κ_G depend on the quotient space volume vol(M/G), its dimension d = dim(M/G), the smoothness parameter s of the density, and the divergence-specific parameter α. The quotient space M/G need not be a manifold; the paper defines its dimension and volume via the principal part (the dense open subset with manifold structure).

## Method

The core technique is a Fourier approach using the Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunction basis on M, combined with a new local Weyl's law for G-invariant functions (Theorem 7) that quantifies the sparsity of the Fourier series: only eigenfunctions in the invariant subspace V_{λ,G} carry nonzero coefficients, reducing the effective eigenvalue counting from dim(M) to d = dim(M/G). For arbitrary (non-smooth) measures, the estimator applies a heat-kernel mollifier with exponential tail decay to the empirical Fourier coefficients, then optimizes the smoothing parameter σ to balance the W_1(μ, μ_⋆) ≤ C√σ bias against the variance term, yielding the (vol(M/G)/n)^{1/d} rate. For smooth densities (dμ/dx ∈ H^s(M)), the estimator truncates the invariant Fourier series at a cutoff T and optimizes T to balance the bias (decaying as λ_T^{-(s+α)}) against the variance (growing as λ_T^{d/2-α}/n), producing the improved exponent (s+α)/(2s+d). For the MMD regime (α > d/2), the kernel trace Tr(K;G) = Σ m(λ;G)|ξ(λ)| directly quantifies the sample complexity gain, which can depend on the full spectral structure of the group action rather than just dim(G) and vol(M/G).

## Results

The paper establishes that group invariances yield a two-fold sample complexity gain: (1) a multiplicative factor δ_G = vol(M/G) (or Z(α;G) in the MMD regime) that effectively multiplies the number of samples, and (2) an improved convergence exponent κ_G that depends on the quotient space dimension d = dim(M/G) rather than the full manifold dimension. For the 1-Wasserstein distance, the rate is (vol(M/G)/n)^(1/d); for smooth densities with Sobolev regularity s, it improves to (vol(M/G)/n)^((s+1)/(2s+d)). In the MMD regime (α > d/2), the rate saturates at O(n^{-1/2}) with a kernel-dependent constant Z(α;G). The single experiment on a 6D torus with circular-shift invariance on two coordinates confirms the theoretical gain, showing the invariant estimator converges faster than both the non-invariant estimator and full data augmentation.

**Table 1: Summary of the main convergence rate bounds under group invariances.**

| Divergence Measure | δ_G | κ_G |
|---|---|---|
| 1-Wasserstein (Thm 1) | vol(M/G) | 1/d |
| 1-Wasserstein, s-smooth (Thm 2) | vol(M/G) | (s+1)/(2s+d) |
| Sobolev IPMs, α < d/2 (Thm 3) | vol(M/G) | (s+α)/(2s+d) |
| Sobolev IPMs, α > d/2, MMD regime (Thm 4) | Z(α;G) | 1/2 |
| Density estimation in L² (Thm 5) | vol(M/G) | s/(2s+d) |
| Density estimation in L∞, s > d/2 (Thm 6) | (vol(M/G))^(2s/(s−d/2)) | (s−d/2)/(2s+d) |

*For any divergence D, the bound is E[D(μ, μ̃)] ≲ (δ_G/n)^κ_G, where d = dim(M/G), s is the Sobolev regularity of dμ/dx, and Z(α;G) is the zeta function restricted to G-invariant Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against the general (non-invariant) case and prior work rather than competing algorithms. Setting G = {id_G} recovers the known tight rates for estimating divergences without invariances (e.g., (vol(M)/n)^(1/dim(M)) for 1-Wasserstein), confirming the bounds are consistent. For finite groups, the result extends Chen et al. (2023), which was limited to 1-Lipschitz actions on full-dimensional submanifolds of R^d, to arbitrary smooth compact manifolds and arbitrary Lie groups (including positive-dimensional groups). The key new contribution is the exponent improvement for positive-dimensional groups: the exponent depends on d = dim(M/G) rather than dim(M), which can be substantially smaller. In the single experiment (6D torus with 2D circular-shift invariance), the proposed invariant estimator outperforms both the non-invariant estimator and full data augmentation, demonstrating that regularization (balancing bias and variance via the truncation parameter T) is crucial and that simply augmenting data is insufficient to capture the full theoretical gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample complexity analysis of probability divergences under group invariances from Chen et al. (2023), who proved bounds only for finite groups with 1-Lipschitz actions on full-dimensional submanifolds of R^d, to arbitrary Lie groups (including positive-dimensional groups) acting on arbitrary compact smooth manifolds. Methodologically, it builds on the Laplace-Beltrami spectral framework from Tahmasebi & Jegelka (2023) for kernel regression, repurposing a new local Weyl's law for G-invariant eigenfunctions to bound estimation error in Wasserstein, Sobolev IPM, MMD, and density estimation settings. The key departure from prior work is the use of a Fourier/mollifier approach rather than covering-number arguments, which is essential for handling positive-dimensional groups where the quotient space may not be a manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-fold gain characterization (improved exponent via reduced quotient dimension, plus a multiplicative volume factor) provides a clean, general theoretical framework for understanding how invariances reduce sample complexity across multiple divergence measures simultaneously. This is particularly relevant for generative modeling of invariant data (point clouds, molecules, graphs) where practitioners can now quantify the benefit of exploiting symmetry. However, the empirical validation is minimal (a single synthetic experiment on a 6D torus), and the L^∞ density estimation bound is acknowledged to be suboptimal even in the non-invariant case, leaving room for tighter results. The results are entirely theoretical and do not address computational feasibility of the proposed estimators for high-dimensional manifolds.
