## Problem

Language models confidently hallucinate in long-form generations, leading users to make suboptimal downstream decisions, yet existing calibration methods (temperature scaling, Platt scaling, etc.) are restricted to classification or short outputs and cannot calibrate the many claims embedded in a single long-form generation. This paper defines linguistic calibration for long-form generations—requiring that an LM's outputs enable users to make calibrated probabilistic forecasts on related questions—and provides the first end-to-end training framework to achieve it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an open-ended text query (e.g., 'Write a paragraph about {question}') and produces a long-form natural language generation that interleaves factual claims with explicit confidence statements in both numerical form ('I estimate a 70% likelihood that...') and linguistic form ('I am fairly sure that...'). Unlike prior calibration work that outputs a single probability vector or short answer, the confidence is distributed across multiple claims within the paragraph, each with its own calibrated uncertainty, and is evaluated by how well a downstream reader can forecast answers to related question-answer pairs conditioned on the generation.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, summary distillation bootstraps the base LM (Llama 2 7B) by sampling many responses to each query, using an API-based LLM to produce a consensus paragraph with confidence statements reflecting claim frequency across samples, and finetuning on these (query, summary) pairs. Second, decision-based RL optimizes a proper scoring rule (negative log loss) as the reward, where the reward is computed on a trained surrogate reader's forecast of the correct answer given the generation; the surrogate decomposes forecasting into ExtractAnswers (a finetuned RedPajama 3B) and ForecastProbs (a cross-entropy head on the SFT model), with a regularization term enforcing probability normalization and a KL penalty from the SFT policy to prevent over-optimization. The key insight is that defining linguistic calibration through downstream user forecast calibration reduces the problem to a standard proper scoring rule objective, making end-to-end calibration of long-form text tractable without requiring access to real-world decision rewards.

## Results

On in-distribution TriviaQA, LC RL (Llama 2 7B) achieves 64.74% accuracy with 0.108 ECE, substantially outperforming Factuality RL (63.33% accuracy, 0.367 ECE) and LC SFT (60.98% accuracy, 0.166 ECE). Under significant domain shift, LC RL maintains strong calibration: 0.088 ECE on Jeopardy, 0.213 ECE on SciQ, and 0.342 ECE on BioASQ Task B, while matching or exceeding the accuracy of all Llama 2 7B-based baselines. On the held-out biography generation task (FactScore), LC RL achieves 46.77% claim-level accuracy with 0.266 ECE, compared to 39.86% accuracy and 0.601 ECE for Factuality RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4 (OpenAI et al., 2023), LC RL achieves substantially lower accuracy (64.74% vs. 78.00% on TriviaQA; 56.85% vs. 66.20% on SciQ) but significantly better ECE (0.108 vs. 0.220 on TriviaQA; 0.213 vs. 0.338 on SciQ), demonstrating that a smaller model with the right calibration objective can be better calibrated than a much more factual larger model. Against the JAFU prompting strategy (Tian et al., 2023), LC RL again trades accuracy for calibration: 64.74% vs. 77.70% accuracy but 0.108 vs. 0.212 ECE on TriviaQA, and 0.213 vs. 0.304 ECE on SciQ. Notably, LC RL's ECE is competitive with GPT-4 Summary ICL 8-Shot (0.108 vs. 0.119 on TriviaQA) despite the 15-point accuracy gap, and on BioASQ Task B, LC RL (0.342 ECE) outperforms GPT-4 0-Shot (0.379) and GPT-4 JAFU 0-Shot (0.356). Among all Llama 2 7B-based methods, LC RL strictly dominates on the accuracy-ECE frontier, improving ECE by 0.259 over Factuality RL while simultaneously improving accuracy by 1.41 points on TriviaQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mielke et al.'s (2022) definition of linguistic calibration from single-claim utterances to long-form, multi-claim generations by reframing the problem through the lens of decision theory (Zhao et al., 2021; Zhao & Ermon, 2021). Rather than calibrating the LM's text directly, it constructs an objective in the space of downstream user forecasts and applies standard proper scoring rules (negative log loss) as an RL reward. The two-step training framework—summary distillation inspired by Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) followed by decision-based RL—departs from prior LM calibration work (Kadavath et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2023; Kuhn et al., 2023) that is restricted to classification or short-answer settings where a single confidence score suffices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end method for calibrating long-form LM generations, addressing a gap where established recalibration techniques (temperature scaling, Platt scaling) simply do not apply. The zero-shot transfer from trivia QA to an entirely held-out biography generation task suggests the learned calibration is a general property of the model rather than task-specific, which is promising for real-world deployment. Key limitations include the white-box requirement (no API-only LLMs), the use of QA datasets as a proxy for real-world decision-making, and the observation that most emitted confidence statements are unambiguous percentages rather than nuanced linguistic hedges. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger models, how to handle ambiguous linguistic confidence for diverse user populations, and whether more representative decision-making datasets would improve generalization.
