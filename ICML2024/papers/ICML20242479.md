## Problem

Existing graph neural architecture search (GNAS) methods assume graph tasks remain static during the search process, failing in real-world scenarios where sequential graph tasks arrive continually. The paper identifies and formally proves the critical challenge of 'architecture conflicts': the optimal architecture for a new task can be sub-optimal or even harmful for past tasks, and mainstream differentiable search methods (e.g., DARTS) provably cannot resolve this due to their entangled search process, leading to catastrophic forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242479/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of graphs arriving sequentially, where each graph provides an adjacency matrix and node feature matrix with a node classification task over two distinct classes. Crucially, only the current graph is accessible during training and architecture search (past graphs are unavailable), following the classIL continual learning setting. The output is a set of K searched GNN architectures (one per module, specifying operation choices per layer) plus node class predictions; at test time the model must classify across all previously seen categories without task indicators.

## Method

GASIM disentangles the task-architecture relationship through three components. First, a modular supernet with K independent DARTS-style modules, where hard routing ensures only one module is updated per incoming task, preventing cross-module interference. Second, a factor-based task-module router learns latent graph factors via MMD distribution matching and maintains a task-module relation graph to route each new task to the module with the most similar historical factors. Third, an invariant architecture search mechanism computes an invariance loss (variance of classification losses across all factors previously routed to the same module) that constrains the searched architecture to remain effective for past tasks sharing that module, balancing new-task performance against forgetting via a weighted combination with the routing loss.

## Results

GASIM achieves the best Average Performance (AP) on all three datasets: 76.03% on CoraFull, 65.03% on Arxiv, and 98.40% on Reddit. On CoraFull, GASIM improves AP by 8.3% over the second-best baseline (GIN at 67.70%) while also achieving the lowest Average Forgetting (AF = -5.22 vs. -3.62 for GIN). On Arxiv, GASIM leads by 2.4% AP over GCN (62.64%) and reduces AF from -13.78 to -12.19. On Reddit, GASIM achieves the best AP (98.40%) and the lowest AF (-0.17) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GASIM outperforms all 10 baselines on AP across all three datasets. The largest margin is on CoraFull, where GASIM (76.03 AP) surpasses the best hand-designed GNN (GIN, 67.70) by 8.3% and the best NAS method (DARTS, 66.02) by 10.0%. On Arxiv, GASIM leads GCN (62.64) by 2.4% AP and DARTS (61.45) by 3.6%, while also reducing AF from -13.78 (GCN) to -12.19. On Reddit, gains are smaller but consistent: GASIM (98.40 AP, -0.17 AF) edges out GIN (97.77 AP, 0.04 AF) and DARTS (97.55 AP, -0.25 AF). Notably, DARTS achieves competitive AP on Arxiv (61.45) but suffers severe forgetting (AF = -16.59), confirming the architecture conflict problem; GASIM mitigates this through modular routing. GraphNAS shows the worst AF on CoraFull (-18.85) and Arxiv AP (52.94), indicating that reinforcement-learning-based GNAS is particularly vulnerable to continual settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of graph neural architecture search (GNAS) and continual learning, building directly on differentiable search methods like DARTS and graph-specific variants (GraphNAS, GASSO) while departing from their core assumption of static tasks. The modular supernet design draws on Mixture-of-Experts routing, but repurposes it not for capacity scaling (the standard MoE objective) but for disentangling task-architecture relationships to resolve conflicts. The invariant architecture search mechanism adapts principles from out-of-distribution generalization literature (invariant risk minimization) to the architecture parameter space, and the factor-based router leverages disentangled representation learning via MMD-based distribution matching. A key conceptual departure is the theoretical demonstration that DARTS's entangled search space provably fails under architecture conflicts, motivating the modular decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of continual GNAS, identifying architecture conflicts as a fundamental obstacle that standard differentiable search cannot overcome. The practical implication is significant for real-world graph systems (citation networks, drug discovery) where new node types and tasks emerge continuously and a fixed architecture is suboptimal. However, the scope is limited to homogeneous graphs and node classification; extending to heterogeneous graphs, graph-level tasks, or larger-scale continual settings remains open. The modular supernet + routing paradigm could potentially transfer to other continual NAS domains beyond graphs, but this is unexplored.
