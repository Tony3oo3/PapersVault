## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for minimax optimization algorithms (GDA, SGDA, GDmax, SGDmax) rely on Lipschitz smoothness, but the minimax structure itself can break this condition—as shown in distributionally robust optimization where the objective is not Lipschitz smooth even when individual loss components are. Without Lipschitz smoothness, vanilla gradient-based minimax methods can diverge, as demonstrated by a simple counterexample where the primal gradient grows super-linearly. The paper addresses this gap by proving convergence under a relaxed generalized smoothness condition.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax loss f(x,y), initial values x₀ and y₀ (where y₀ must satisfy ‖y₀ − y*(x₀)‖ ≤ C₀G_x/l_x(2G_x), obtained via a preliminary strongly-convex minimization subproblem), and a fixed learning rate η. The adaptive stepsize S_t is computed at runtime from gradient norms (current, time-averaged, or exponentially averaged), making the effective x-stepsize η/S_t rather than a constant. The output is an ε-first-order stationary point of the primal function Φ(x) = f(x, y*(x)), with the same gradient or SFO complexity as the Lipschitz smooth counterparts.

## Method

The core mechanism is a suitable stepsize strategy applied to the x-update: instead of x_{t+1} = x_t − η∇_x f(x_t, y_t), the algorithm uses x_{t+1} = x_t − (η/S_t)∇_x f(x_t, y_t), where S_t is one of four options (current gradient norm, time-averaged norm, a precomputed constant, or exponential moving average). This controls per-iteration displacement when gradients are large, preventing the divergence observed under non-Lipschitz smoothness. The paper introduces l_x-l_y-smoothness (Definition 2.5), a minimax generalization of l-smoothness where the smoothness constant depends on the local gradient norm, and proves via simultaneous induction on ‖∇Φ(x_t)‖, ‖∇_x f(x_t,y_t)‖, ‖∇_y f(x_t,y_t)‖, and ‖y_t − y_t*‖ that all four generalized algorithms (GDA, SGDA, GDmax, SGDmax) achieve O(κ²ε⁻²) or O(κ³ε⁻⁴) complexity—matching their Lipschitz smooth counterparts. The stochastic variants are proven with high probability (1−δ) using a stopping-time argument combined with Markov's inequality and union bounds.

## Results

The paper evaluates its Generalized SGDA (G-SGDA) with gradient normalization stepsize against conventional constant-stepsize SGDA on 9 LIBSVM binary classification datasets (a9a, covtype, diabetes, german, gisette, ijcnn1, mushrooms, phishing, w8a) for the robust logistic regression task. Results are presented as convergence curves of the primal objective Φ(x) versus iterations (Figure 1) rather than a numerical summary table. The paper reports that G-SGDA 'improves the convergence speed or stability of SGDA algorithm significantly on all datasets,' with the conventional SGDA requiring two different (smaller) learning rates to converge at all, while G-SGDA converges more reliably and faster across all 9 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole empirical baseline is conventional SGDA with a fixed constant stepsize, evaluated at the two largest learning rates (from {0.1, 0.01, 0.001, 0.0001, 1e-5, 1e-6}) that still allow convergence (denoted SGDA-1 and SGDA-2). The proposed G-SGDA (using gradient normalization, i.e., S_t = ‖v_t‖) outperforms both SGDA variants on all 9 datasets in terms of convergence speed and/or stability of the primal objective Φ(x). The advantage is particularly notable on datasets where conventional SGDA is unstable or requires very small learning rates to avoid divergence, consistent with the paper's theoretical motivation that non-Lipschitz-smooth minimax objectives can cause vanilla gradient methods to diverge. No quantitative gap (e.g., percentage improvement in final loss) is reported numerically; the comparison is qualitative via convergence plots. The mini-batch size is fixed at 50 for all methods, and learning rates are selected by grid search per dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of classic minimax algorithms (GDA, SGDA from Lin et al. 2020; GDmax, SGDmax from Jin et al. 2020) from the standard Lipschitz smoothness assumption to the weaker l_x-l_y-smoothness condition, which generalizes the l-smoothness framework of Li et al. (2023) and (L0,L1)-smoothness of Zhang et al. (2019) to the minimax setting. The key technical departure is a 'suitable stepsize' strategy (gradient normalization or its variants) that controls per-iteration movement when gradients are unbounded, analogous to gradient clipping in nonconvex minimization. A concurrent work by Hao et al. (2024) addresses unbounded smoothness in bilevel optimization but assumes Lipschitz smoothness in the lower-level function, making the present paper's treatment more general for minimax problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that many important minimax applications—distributionally robust optimization, phase retrieval, adversarial training—violate Lipschitz smoothness, meaning existing convergence guarantees simply do not apply. By proving that the same O(κ²ε⁻²) and O(κ³ε⁻⁴) complexity bounds hold under generalized smoothness, the paper removes a theoretical barrier that could discourage practitioners from applying these algorithms to such problems. Limitations include the restriction to nonconvex-strongly-concave settings, the need for an initialization phase to approximate y*(x₀), and the fact that stochastic guarantees are high-probability rather than in-expectation. Open questions include whether the suitable stepsize strategy composes with variance reduction (e.g., SREDA) and whether analogous results extend to non-strongly-concave or saddle-point settings.
