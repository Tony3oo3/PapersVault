## Problem

Existing federated learning methods address distribution shifts (label, feature, or concept) in isolation, yet real-world deployments face all three simultaneously. The paper identifies that existing clustered FL methods (IFCA, FeSEM, FedEM, FedSoft) fail to separate concept-shifted clients into distinct clusters while keeping label/feature-shifted clients together, resulting in models that overfit local distributions and exhibit large global-local performance gaps.

## Input / Output

The method takes K pre-specified cluster models, client image-label pairs (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet), and a number of clusters K matching the number of concepts. It outputs K global model parameter vectors θ_k and per-client soft clustering weights ω_{i;k} (summing to 1 across k), which together define an ensemble prediction. A key design choice is that the objective uses the ratio P(y|x;θ_k)/P(y;θ_k) rather than raw likelihood, requiring only the loss f(x,y;θ_k) and a running estimate of per-class proportions C_{y,k} to compute in practice.

## Method

FedRC formulates clustering as a bi-level optimization over model parameters Θ and soft assignment weights Ω, with a novel objective L(Θ,Ω) = (1/N)Σ ln(Σ_k ω_{i;k}·Ĩ(x,y;θ_k)) where Ĩ(x,y;θ_k) = exp(−f(x,y;θ_k))/C_{y,k} approximates the likelihood ratio P(y|x)/P(y). The key insight is that this ratio is small only under concept shifts (where P(y|x) drops), while label or feature shifts (which reduce P(y) or P(x)) do not significantly penalize the objective, thereby decoupling concept shifts from other shift types. Optimization alternates an E-step (updating per-sample weights γ_{i,j;k} and per-client weights ω_{i;k}) with an M-step (updating θ_k via weighted gradient descent), and the federated version (FedRC) performs T local SGD steps per round before FedAvg aggregation. The paper proves O(1/T) convergence for the centralized version and O(1/√T) for the federated version under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions.

## Results

FedRC achieves the highest global accuracy across all four datasets: 59.00% on FashionMNIST (CNN), 63.83% on CIFAR-10 (MobileNetV2), 27.79% on Tiny-ImageNet (MobileNetV2), and 47.80% on CIFAR-10 / 28.10% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18). FedRC also exhibits the smallest global-local performance gap among all methods, indicating superior robustness to unseen global distributions. The fine-tuned variant FedRC-FT matches the local accuracy of personalized methods (e.g., 91.02% vs. FedSoft's 91.35% on FashionMNIST) while retaining 62.37% global accuracy, far exceeding FedSoft's 19.88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRC dominates all clustered FL baselines on global accuracy: it outperforms FeSEM (47.63%) by 11.4 points on FashionMNIST, FedEM (43.35%) by 20.5 points on CIFAR-10, and FedEM (17.33%) by 10.5 points on Tiny-ImageNet. FedSoft achieves the highest local accuracy (91.35% on FashionMNIST, 83.08% on CIFAR-10) but suffers catastrophic global degradation (19.88% and 22.00% respectively), confirming its overfitting to local distributions. IFCA and CFL show limited global gains over FedAvg, with IFCA actually underperforming FedAvg on global accuracy for CIFAR-10 (26.62% vs. 30.47%) and Tiny-ImageNet (12.54% vs. 14.27%). FedRC-FT bridges the personalization-generalization gap, matching FedSoft's local accuracy within 0.3 points while achieving 62.37% global accuracy versus FedSoft's 19.88% on FashionMNIST. On ResNet-18 (Table 2), FedRC similarly leads with 47.80% global on CIFAR-10 and 28.10% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the next-best FedEM by 13.6 and 3.3 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bi-level optimization framework of EM-based clustered FL methods (particularly FedEM/Marfoq et al., 2021) but departs fundamentally in its objective function design. While prior clustered FL methods (IFCA, FeSEM, FedSoft, CFL) aim to group clients by local distribution similarity, FedRC introduces a novel objective that explicitly decouples concept shifts from label and feature shifts, targeting the shared decision boundary rather than local data overlap. The paper also extends the problem setting from single-shift-type handling (label shift via SCAFFOLD, feature shift via domain generalization, concept shift via label noise correction) to the simultaneous occurrence of all three, which it argues is the realistic deployment scenario.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously unexamined failure mode: existing clustered FL methods overfit to local label distributions rather than separating by concept, leading to large local-global performance gaps (e.g., FedSoft achieves 91% local but only 20% global accuracy on FashionMNIST). This has direct practical implications for FL deployments across geographically and culturally diverse client populations. Open questions include handling non-participating clients that themselves experience concept shifts (requiring OOD detection or test-time adaptation), providing deeper theoretical explanation for why the proposed objective achieves the desired clustering, and extending the approach to scenarios where the number of concepts is truly unknown and dynamic.
