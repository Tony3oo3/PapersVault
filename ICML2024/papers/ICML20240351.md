## Problem

Tempering (annealing) is a standard technique in SMC and AIS for sampling from targets known up to normalization, but its connection to optimization theory was unexplored: unlike Langevin dynamics, which are known to discretize the Wasserstein-2 gradient flow of the KL, no analogous characterization existed for tempering. This left adaptive tempering schedules without a principled optimization-theoretic justification and convergence rates for the tempering iterates unavailable in general.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target density π and an initial distribution μ₀ (both defined on ℝᵈ), and produces a weighted particle approximation of π via SMC, an adaptive tempering schedule (λₙ)ₙ₌₀ᵀ, and an explicit convergence bound KL(μₙ|π) ≤ (1/λ₁)(1−λₙ)KL(π|μ₀). The tempering schedule is determined online by the Fisher information I(λ) of the tempered exponential family, and the convergence rate is independent of the convexity or smoothness properties of the target π, unlike Langevin-based rates.

## Method

The paper establishes that the tempering sequence μₙ ∝ μ₀^(1−λₙ)π^(λₙ) is exactly the entropic mirror descent iterate on the reverse KL with step sizes γₙ = (λₙ−λₙ₋₁)/(1−λₙ₋₁), making SMC importance weights wₙ(x) = (π(x)/μₙ₋₁(x))^γₙ a particle approximation of the multiplicative mirror descent update. A convergence rate is derived by extending the mirror descent analysis of Aubin-Frankowski et al. (2022) to varying step sizes, yielding KL(μₙ|π) ≤ (1/λ₁)(1−λₙ)KL(π|μ₀). The popular ESS-based adaptive rule is then justified as keeping the KL (equivalently χ², up to a constant) between consecutive tempered distributions fixed, which by the Bregman descent lemma guarantees a constant lower bound on objective decrease per step. This leads to the principled schedule λₙ − λₙ₋₁ = c·I(λₙ₋₁)^(−1/2), which for i.i.d. targets implies O(√d) tempering steps and is shown to be more numerically stable than alternatives that target the full KL(μₙ|π) directly.

## Results

The paper establishes that entropic mirror descent on the reverse KL yields the tempering sequence, with convergence rate KL(μ_n|π) ≤ (1/γ₁)∏(1−γ_k)·KL(π|μ₀), which is independent of the target's convexity properties. For i.i.d. targets, the optimal tempering schedule scales as O(d^{1/2}) steps, confirmed numerically. In the narrow Gaussian experiment (π = N(1_d, 0.1²Id), μ₀ = N(0_d, Id), d=2, N=10⁴ particles), adaptive SMC achieves ESS=0.79 in only 5 tempering steps, while the constant-rate AIS of Goshtasbpour et al. (2023) requires 6,257 steps with ESS=0.38.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is against the constant-rate adaptive AIS of Goshtasbpour et al. (2023), which selects the next temperature to decrease KL(μ_λ|π) at a constant rate (ODE: λ̇ = c[I(λ)(1−λ)]^{-1}). On the narrow Gaussian target, adaptive SMC (which keeps KL between successive iterates constant, i.e. λ_n − λ_{n−1} = c·I(λ_{n−1})^{-1/2}) requires only 5 tempering steps with ESS=0.79, while Goshtasbpour et al.'s method needs 6,257 steps with ESS=0.38 — a 1,250× reduction in steps and a substantially better approximation. The paper attributes this gap to numerical stability: Goshtasbpour et al.'s weights target π directly (higher variance), whereas SMC weights target μ_{λ_n} with proposal μ_{λ_{n−1}} (lower variance). Against constant-step-size SMC and AIS (γ=0.05), adaptive SMC uses 146× fewer steps (5 vs. 730) while maintaining comparable ESS (0.79 vs. 0.99 for SMC; 0.04 for AIS). The O(d^{1/2}) scaling of tempering steps is also confirmed numerically, contrasting with the O(d) scaling suggested by Beskos et al. (2014) for i.i.d. targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the SMC/AIS tempering literature (Del Moral et al., 2006; Neal, 2001; Jasra et al., 2011) and the mirror-descent-on-measures framework of Aubin-Frankowski et al. (2022) and Lu et al. (2018). It shows that the tempering sequence is exactly the entropic mirror descent iterate on the reverse KL, which is a forward-Euler discretization of the Fisher-Rao gradient flow (the discrete-time analogue of the continuous-time connection noted by Domingo-Enrich and Pooladian, 2023). The convergence proof extends Aubin-Frankowski et al.'s constant-step-size result to varying step sizes, and the adaptive tempering rule is derived from the Bregman proximal inequality rather than from variance-control heuristics. The paper also positions itself against Goshtasbpour et al. (2023), whose constant-rate AIS schedule solves a different ODE and is shown to be numerically unstable in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is that the widely-used ESS-based adaptive tempering rule in SMC is now shown to be equivalent (up to higher-order terms) to keeping the KL between consecutive iterates constant, giving it a principled optimization justification and suggesting more numerically stable alternatives (log-weight-based KL estimation, Fisher-information-based step selection). The convergence rate for the tempering iterates is independent of the target's convexity, in contrast to Langevin MCMC rates that require log-concavity or log-Sobolev conditions. An open tension remains: Beskos et al. (2014) prove stability of SMC with O(d) tempering steps for i.i.d. targets, while this paper's theory and experiments suggest O(d^{1/2}) suffices; reconciling these scalings is left for future work. The framework also clarifies why SMC outperforms PMD (Dai et al., 2016) and SRAIS (Korba and Portier, 2022) in both cost and numerical stability.
