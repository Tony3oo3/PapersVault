## Problem

Jacobi decoding can parallelize LLM inference by iteratively updating an n-token sequence to a fixed point, but vanilla LLMs achieve only ~1.05× speedup because they rarely predict more than one correct token per iteration when preceding tokens are wrong. Existing remedies like Medusa and speculative decoding require extra architectural components or auxiliary draft models, increasing memory cost and system complexity. The paper asks: can we adapt the target LLM itself to converge in fewer Jacobi steps without adding any extra parameters or components?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text prompt concatenated with a randomly initialized n-token sequence (n is a hyperparameter, typically 16–64) and produces the same n-token sequence that greedy AR decoding would generate, using a single forward pass with a causal attention mask. During training, the input is any intermediate state y^(j) sampled from a collected Jacobi trajectory, and the target is the fixed point y* of that trajectory. The n-token sequence length at inference need not match the training length, providing deployment flexibility, though longer sequences increase per-step FLOPs.

## Method

CLLMs fine-tune a pre-trained LLM with a global consistency loss (L_GC) that minimizes the KL divergence between the model's output conditioned on the fixed-point context and its output conditioned on an arbitrary trajectory point, effectively training the model to map any Jacobi trajectory state directly to the fixed point in one step. This is combined with a standard AR loss (weighted by ω) to prevent distributional drift and preserve generation quality. Training data consists of Jacobi trajectories collected by running the target LLM's Jacobi decoding on domain-specific prompts, augmented by randomly correcting erroneous tokens to diversify the 'correct-wrong' patterns. The approach draws a direct analogy to consistency models in diffusion: just as CMs map any noisy state on a probability-flow ODE to the clean image, CLLMs map any intermediate Jacobi state to the AR fixed point. The resulting acceleration stems from two learned phenomena—fast forwarding (2–6 consecutive tokens correctly predicted per pass) and stationary tokens (correctly predicted tokens that remain stable despite preceding errors)—which together reduce the number of Jacobi iterations needed for convergence.

## Results

On domain-specific benchmarks, CLLMs achieve 2.4×–3.4× speedup via Jacobi decoding with minimal accuracy loss: 3.0× on GSM8K (56.4 vs 59.1 test@1), 3.4× on Spider (69.3 vs 70.0 execution accuracy), and 2.5× on CodeSearchNet-Python (59.2 vs 60.4 pass@1). On the open-domain MT-bench (ShareGPT), CLLMs achieve 2.4× speedup with a score of 6.4, matching the fine-tuned baseline's 6.5. Language modeling perplexity remains competitive (9.5 on WikiText2 vs 8.0 baseline; 15.3 on PTB vs 15.6 baseline) while delivering 2.1×–2.3× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla Jacobi decoding on the fine-tuned baseline, CLLMs deliver a dramatic improvement: 3.0× vs 1.1× speedup on GSM8K and 3.4× vs 1.0× on Spider, confirming that the consistency loss is the key enabler. Compared to Medusa-2 (which adds 1.6B extra parameters for a 6.7B model), CLLMs match or exceed speedup (3.0× vs 1.6× on GSM8K; 3.4× vs 2.7× on Spider) while using 6.7B parameters instead of 8.3B, and achieve substantially better accuracy on CodeSearchNet-Python (59.2 vs 48.3 pass@1) and Spider (69.3 vs 66.4). Against speculative decoding with a distilled LLaMA-160m draft model, CLLMs win on speedup (3.0× vs 1.7× on GSM8K; 3.4× vs 1.8× on Spider) with comparable accuracy, and avoid the need to manage two models in a single system. CLLMs also outperform lookahead decoding applied to the unmodified baseline (2.5× vs 1.6× on ShareGPT; 3.6× vs 1.5× on Spider), and the two methods compose additively—CLLM + lookahead yields the highest throughput in most settings. The only trade-off is a small accuracy drop (e.g., 56.4 vs 59.1 on GSM8K, 69.3 vs 70.0 on Spider) relative to the fine-tuned AR baseline, which the authors attribute to the consistency loss pulling the model slightly off the exact AR distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: Jacobi decoding for LLMs (Santilli et al., 2023; Song et al., 2021a), which reframes autoregressive generation as parallel fixed-point iteration, and consistency models for diffusion (Song et al., 2023), which accelerate sampling by mapping any intermediate denoising state directly to the final output. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the Jacobi trajectory in LLM decoding plays the same role as the probability-flow ODE trajectory in diffusion, enabling a consistency loss that trains the LLM to jump from any trajectory point to the fixed point in one step. Unlike speculative decoding (which requires a separate draft model) or Medusa (which adds extra decoding heads and ~1.6B parameters), CLLMs require no architectural modification or auxiliary components—only a moderate fine-tuning pass with a combined consistency and AR loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that CLLMs deliver 2.4×–3.4× inference speedup with zero additional memory overhead and no architectural changes, making them drop-in replacements for existing LLMs in latency-sensitive serving scenarios. However, the method is currently restricted to greedy decoding and 7B-scale models, and its effectiveness depends heavily on the quality and domain coverage of the collected Jacobi trajectory dataset. Open questions include whether the consistency-training recipe transfers to non-greedy sampling (nucleus, top-p), whether it scales to 70B+ models, and whether the on-policy variant (using the model's own trajectories) could enable CLLM-style acceleration during pre-training itself, as the authors speculate.
