## Problem

In standard RLHF, any monotone transformation of a learned reward model preserves preference rankings, yet no principled criterion exists for choosing which transformation to use in the alignment step. Similarly, when aligning to multiple properties (e.g., helpfulness AND harmlessness), the standard weighted sum of raw rewards allows strong performance on one property to compensate for poor performance on another. The paper asks whether a theoretically grounded transformation and aggregation scheme can simultaneously mitigate reward hacking, reduce underfitting, and make multi-reward combination behave as logical AND.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Bradley-Terry reward model (a T5-base trained on pairwise preference data), a prompt-specific reference reward value (e.g., the 85th quantile of rewards sampled from the SFT policy, estimated via Gaussian quantile prediction), and an SFT-finetuned base language model. It produces a transformed scalar utility u(x,y) = log σ(r(x,y) − r^ref(x)) that replaces the raw reward in the KL-regularized PPO objective. For multi-property alignment, each property's reward is independently centered and log-sigmoid-transformed, then summed into a single utility; the reference values serve as the effective weighting mechanism in place of explicit coefficients.

## Method

The paper derives the LSC-transformation (log-sigmoid-centered transformation) by interpreting alignment as sampling from the posterior conditional on outputs being 'good' on a target property. Connecting the KL-regularized RLHF optimum (an exponential tilting of the base policy) to the log-probability of goodness, the correct utility is u(x,y) = log p(G=1|x,y), which for Bradley-Terry rewards yields log σ(r(x,y) − r^ref(x)). The centering by a prompt-specific reference reward is essential because BT rewards are only identifiable up to arbitrary prompt-dependent shifts, making any fixed constant reference meaningless. Mechanistically, the log-sigmoid caps marginal utility for already-high-reward outputs (reducing both reward hacking and underfitting), and under a conditional independence assumption, summation of transformed rewards corresponds to the logical AND of 'good' properties. The alignment procedure itself (PPO with KL penalty) is unchanged; only the utility signal fed to the optimizer is transformed.

## Results

The LSC-transformation (log-sigmoid of centered Bradley-Terry rewards) consistently outperforms the raw-reward baseline across all KL regularization levels in both single-reward and multi-reward alignment. In the aggregation setting (helpful AND harmless), the transformed sum yields substantially higher win rates over the SFT model than the weighted sum of raw rewards, with the gap widening at higher KL values where reward hacking is most severe. Mechanistically, the transformation reduces the probability ratio between a marginally better and a very good response from ~10× (raw) to ~1.01× (transformed), and in the two-property aggregation case, the transformed sum upweights the response that is good on both properties by ~1.8× versus 1× for the raw sum. Reward distributions under the transformed alignment are more concentrated (fewer extreme high and low rewards), and shortcut exploitation (list formatting, 'consult a professional' responses) is substantially reduced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the LSC-transformed reward against the standard raw Bradley-Terry reward (the dominant baseline in RLHF practice) in both single-reward and multi-reward settings, using both PPO-based RLHF and best-of-k sampling. In single-reward alignment (helpfulness or harmlessness), the transformed reward dominates the raw reward at every KL level under both evaluation strategies (T5-XXL reward-model win rate and PALM-2 zero-shot judge win rate). In the aggregation setting (helpful AND harmless), the sum of transformed rewards outperforms the weighted sum of raw rewards (swept over weights w=0.1–0.9) across all KL values and in best-of-k (k up to 191). The advantage is most pronounced at high KL, where the raw-reward baseline suffers severe reward hacking (performance decays) while the transformed reward maintains or improves performance. The ablation in Section 6 shows that neither centering alone nor log-sigmoid alone (with a fixed reference of 0) improves alignment; the full LSC-transformation (centering by a prompt-specific reference plus log-sigmoid) is necessary for the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard RLHF pipeline (Bradley-Terry reward learning + KL-regularized PPO alignment) and the exponential-tilting characterization of the optimal aligned policy (Korbak et al., 2022) to derive a principled reward transformation. It departs from prior reward-hacking mitigation work (ensembles, constrained optimization, regularization) by proposing a simple monotone transformation of the reward itself, and from prior multi-objective alignment work (weighted sums of raw rewards) by showing that summing log-sigmoid-transformed rewards corresponds to logical AND under an independence assumption. The derivation is motivated by a probabilistic reinterpretation of alignment as posterior sampling conditional on 'goodness,' connecting the utility function to log-probability of a binary semantic variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LSC-transformation offers a drop-in, theoretically grounded modification to the standard RLHF pipeline that simultaneously mitigates reward hacking and provides a principled multi-objective aggregation rule, without requiring ensembles, constrained optimization, or additional data collection. Open questions include whether the Independent Judgements assumption (Assumption 1) holds in practice for correlated properties, whether the transformation transfers to direct preference optimization methods (DPO) that bypass explicit reward models, and whether the reference-reward choice (e.g., 85th quantile) generalizes across model scales and domains. The paper notes the technique is complementary to existing reward-hacking mitigations and could be combined with them.
