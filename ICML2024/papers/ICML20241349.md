## Problem

Reconstructing global ocean deoxygenation over a century (1920–2023) is critically important for understanding the 'breathless ocean,' yet existing approaches are inadequate: expert-dominated numerical simulations (CMIP6) ignore in-situ observations and propagate biases, while spatial interpolation methods disregard temporal dynamics and coupled biogeochemical factors. The core difficulty is that over 96% of dissolved oxygen observations are missing across an irregular 4D spatio-temporal domain, making precise reconstruction extremely challenging.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sparse dissolved oxygen observations from past and future T timesteps, 4D geographical coordinates (longitude, latitude, depth, time), and environmental factors (temperature, salinity, nitrate, phosphate, silicate, chlorophyll) at a 1°×1° spatial resolution, 33 depth levels (0–5500 m), and annual temporal resolution (T=104 years). Because the ocean is an irregular cube constrained by land plates and seabed topography, the data is organized as a 4D graph rather than a regular grid, with edge features encoding distance, density, and pressure between connected nodes. The output is the reconstructed dissolved oxygen concentration (μmol/kg) at every 4D coordinate, yielding a complete global deoxygenation field from 1920 to 2023.

## Method

OXYGENERATOR constructs a 4D graph over irregular ocean boundaries using two neighbor types—proximity neighbors within a local spatial window and information hubs identified by observation completeness—to enrich message passing under extreme sparsity. A Spatio-Temporal Graph HyperNetwork generates node-specific rotation matrices and bias vectors from context information (coordinates + physical elements), enabling adaptive, continuous ocean zoning that replaces pre-determined partition rules; these zoning-varying parameters modulate GNN message passing so that nodes in similar oceanographic regimes share effective parameters. A BiLSTM encodes bidirectional temporal DO dynamics while an MLP embeds multi-factor environmental features, and the two are concatenated as node representations. A chemistry-informed gradient variance regularization term penalizes the variance of ∂DO/∂N and ∂DO/∂P across a batch, encoding the Redfield thermodynamic equilibrium (O:N:P = 138:16:1) as an inductive bias to calibrate reconstruction uncertainty. Training minimizes a combined loss of reconstruction MSE and the two chemical regularization terms, with area-based batching and per-region adaptive iteration counts to handle spatially varying complexity.

## Results

OXYGENERATOR achieves a mean MAPE of 14.28±1.16 across 4-fold cross-validation, reducing MAPE by 38.77% compared to the best CMIP6 numerical simulation (GFDL-ESM4 historical at 25.28±1.31). It also attains R² of 0.9026±0.0072, RMSE of 26.31±1.23, and MAE of 17.57±1.10, outperforming all three CMIP6 baselines (CESM2 omip1, CESM2 omip2, GFDL-ESM4 historical) on every metric. The reconstruction covers 104 years (1920–2023) at 1°×1° spatial resolution and 33 depth levels, with only 3.735% of the 4D grid observed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OXYGENERATOR outperforms all three CMIP6 expert-dominated numerical simulations (CESM2 omip1, CESM2 omip2, GFDL-ESM4 historical) by a wide margin, achieving 38.77% lower MAPE than the best simulation (GFDL-ESM4 historical at 25.28%) and 9.70% higher R². Against classical graph neural network baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE), OXYGENERATOR's advantage is even more dramatic: MAPE drops from 42.70/39.75/42.53 to 14.28, a ~36% improvement, because standard GNNs cannot handle the spatio-temporal heterogeneity and extreme sparsity (96.265% missing) of ocean observations. The zoning-varying hypernetwork mechanism is critical — removing it (w/o Zoning-Varying ablation) degrades MAPE from 14.28 to 23.84, nearly matching the CMIP6 level. The chemistry-informed gradient variance regularization contributes a smaller but consistent gain (14.28 vs. 14.85 MAPE without it), while temporal DO observations via BiLSTM provide the largest single-component contribution (17.01 MAPE without them). Overall, OXYGENERATOR wins on every metric against every baseline tested, with the largest absolute gains in regions of extreme deoxygenation (e.g., Black Sea) where numerical simulations fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven Earth system line (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, ClimaX) to the specific problem of ocean deoxygenation reconstruction, departing from both expert-dominated numerical simulations (CMIP6) that ignore in-situ observations and simple spatial interpolation methods (Schmidtko et al., 2017) that neglect temporal and multi-factor correlations. Architecturally, it repurposes hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2017) to generate node-wise zoning parameters for graph message-passing, and adapts physics-informed neural network ideas (Raissi et al., 2019) to encode Redfield-ratio chemical equilibrium as a gradient-variance regularizer rather than a PDE constraint. The 4D irregular graph construction with 'information hub' neighbors is a novel departure from standard GNN neighborhood definitions, motivated by the extreme 96.265% missing rate that makes local receptive fields insufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep learning reconstruction of global ocean deoxygenation over a full century, quantifying the OMZ30 expansion from 3.54% to 10.67% and providing a data-driven alternative to simulation-only analyses. Key limitations include the irreproducibility of historical observations (only 3.735% observed, making evaluation inherently uncertain), the need for oceanographer validation of physical plausibility (e.g., ENSO response), and the extreme sparsity in regions like the South Pacific (0.027% complete observations in 1920–1950). Open questions include whether the chemistry-informed regularization generalizes beyond O-N-P to other biogeochemical cycles, and how the method scales to higher spatial-temporal resolution as new observation platforms (e.g., Argo-BGC) accumulate data.
