## Problem

Dyna-style MBRL methods like MBPO schedule model-based rollout lengths based on training time, implicitly assuming uniform model accuracy across the state space. This assumption breaks down in complex systems where model accuracy is inherently local—accurate near observed data but unreliable in extrapolated regions. The paper reframes the question from 'When to trust your model?' to 'Where to trust your model?', seeking a spatially-aware rollout mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240558/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes physics-state vectors from MuJoCo environments, a probabilistic ensemble (PE) dynamics model with E Gaussian PNNs, and replay buffer data from environment interaction. It produces control actions via a SAC policy, per-step rollout termination decisions based on whether GJS uncertainty exceeds a self-tuned threshold κ, and an adaptive κ updated each training round as the mean of scaled ζ-quantiles of first-step uncertainty. The single tunable hyperparameter ξ scales the base uncertainty to control how aggressively rollouts are truncated, while T_max=10 and ζ=95% remain fixed across all tasks.

## Method

MACURA restricts branched model-based rollouts to a subset E of the state space where model uncertainty is below a threshold κ, providing a monotonic improvement guarantee (Theorem 4.1) that bounds the return gap by accumulated dynamics misalignment over E. The set E is constructed using a Geometric Jensen-Shannon (GJS) divergence between pairwise PNN predictions, which yields a closed-form, efficient uncertainty measure unlike the intractable KL-based formulation in prior work. The threshold κ is self-tuned each round as the average of scaled ζ-quantiles of uncertainty measured at the first rollout step (where states are guaranteed to be in the data support), making the mechanism transferable without environment-specific scheduling. Rollouts terminate immediately when GJS uncertainty exceeds κ, and the number of SAC gradient steps is proportionally scaled to the volume of generated model data to maintain a stable update-to-data ratio.

## Results

On the MuJoCo benchmark (Humanoid, Ant, Halfcheetah, Walker, Hopper), MACURA achieves substantially faster learning than both MBPO and M2AC, with the most pronounced gains in high-dimensional environments such as Humanoid and Ant. In terms of asymptotic performance, MACURA considerably outperforms both model-based baselines and frequently matches or exceeds the model-free SAC baseline. All model-based methods (MACURA, MBPO, M2AC) learn substantially faster than SAC, confirming the data-efficiency advantage of Dyna-style MBRL. The results are presented as learning curves (Figure 4) rather than a single numerical table, with MACURA consistently reaching higher returns earlier and achieving the best final performance across most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), MACURA wins on both data efficiency and asymptotic performance in most MuJoCo tasks, with the largest gaps in Humanoid and Ant where MBPO's fixed rollout schedule is most limiting. Against M2AC (Pan et al., 2020), MACURA similarly outperforms on data efficiency and final performance; the paper notes that M2AC's One-vs-Rest uncertainty estimate is brittle and its rank-based rollout heuristic substantially underperforms MACURA's threshold-based mechanism (confirmed in ablation, Figure 7). Against the model-free SAC baseline, MACURA frequently matches or exceeds SAC's asymptotic return while learning substantially faster, demonstrating that the uncertainty-aware rollout mechanism extracts more value from the learned dynamics model. MBPO with fine-tuned per-task rollout schedules can compete with M2AC, but MACURA's single hyperparameter ξ (tuned per task) provides a simpler and more robust alternative. The paper also shows that MACURA's advantage is amplified by pink noise exploration, which yields the best combination of data efficiency, asymptotic performance, and training stability across all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), the state-of-the-art Dyna-style MBRL method that combines a SAC agent with a probabilistic ensemble dynamics model. The key conceptual departure is reframing the rollout scheduling question from 'when to trust your model?' (a time-based, uniform-accuracy assumption) to 'where to trust your model?' (a spatial, local-accuracy perspective), using the ensemble's epistemic uncertainty to define a region E of sufficient model accuracy. The closest prior work is M2AC (Pan et al., 2020), which also adapts rollout length via uncertainty but uses a rank-based filtering heuristic and a brittle One-vs-Rest uncertainty estimate; MACURA replaces both with a threshold-based mechanism and a closed-form GJS divergence measure. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) extends improvement bounds from Luo et al. (2018) and Janner et al. (2019) to the specific setting of branched rollouts restricted to a subset of state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MACURA substantially reduces the tuning burden of Dyna-style MBRL from multiple schedule hyperparameters to a single interpretable scaling factor ξ, while achieving better data efficiency and asymptotic performance than MBPO and M2AC on MuJoCo. The finding that MACURA frequently matches or exceeds the model-free SAC baseline is practically significant, suggesting that properly scheduled model usage can fully recover the data-efficiency advantage without sacrificing final performance. Limitations include evaluation only on state-vector MuJoCo tasks (no vision-based MBRL), the theoretical bound requiring an approximation for the true MDP's exit time from E, and the single tunable hyperparameter ξ still requiring per-task calibration. Open questions include whether the GJS-based spatial trust mechanism transfers to world-model-based (vision) MBRL and to other model classes beyond probabilistic ensembles.
