## Problem

Deep learning models, including deep MIL methods, lack a principled way to incorporate quantitative domain knowledge because their internal parameters and layer outputs have no a priori association with tangible domain entities. This paper shows that the structured nature of MIL—where a bag-level label depends on latent instance-level labels—permits a direct mapping between attention module outputs and instance class probabilities, opening a pathway for injecting probabilistic domain knowledge (PDK) into the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240686/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes bags of protein sequences (immune receptor sequences, ~25,000 per bag) encoded via a 1D CNN, along with instance-level prior annotations that assign each sequence a binary high/low prior value (φ^h or φ^l) indicating its estimated disease-relevance from an external, noisy source. The model outputs a bag-level binary classification (healthy vs. diseased) via an MLP on the pooled bag embedding, and the attention module simultaneously produces per-instance attention weights that are interpretable as witness probabilities. The prior annotations are intentionally noisy: only a fraction of true witnesses receive high priors, and some non-witnesses are incorrectly assigned high priors.

## Method

Building on DeepRC (a Modern Hopfield Network–based attention MIL model), the paper proposes three strategies to incorporate PDK at the attention layer, exploiting the fact that attention outputs are monotonic to instance class probability. Direct Prior Injection (DPI) removes the learned attention network entirely and uses the prior values directly as attention scores. Attention Modulation (AM) retains the attention network but scales its softmax-normalized weights by the prior values (φ^h for high-prior, φ^l for low-prior instances) before computing the bag embedding, effectively up-weighting high-prior witnesses in the forward pass. Attention Training (AT) retains the attention network unchanged but adds an auxiliary cross-entropy loss at the instance level (a multi-task formulation) that trains the attention output to match the prior distribution, with a weighting hyperparameter η to handle class imbalance. All three strategies add no trainable parameters; AM requires only an extra vector–vector dot product, and DPI actually reduces model size by removing the attention network.

## Results

On the immune repertoire MIL dataset, the Attention Modulation (AM) strategy achieves a bag-level ROCAUC of 0.84 at a witness rate of just 0.05%, whereas the Vanilla DeepRC baseline requires a witness rate of 1% (ROCAUC 0.78) to perform comparably — a twenty-fold reduction in required signal strength. AM also reaches ROCAUC 1.00 with only 12 training bags (vs. 360 for Vanilla) and converges to ROCAUC 1.00 within 1,000 gradient steps (vs. 100,000+ for Vanilla). At WR=2%, both AM and AT achieve ROCAUC 1.00±0.00, outperforming Vanilla (0.81±0.12), Average Pooling (0.75±0.07), and Direct Prior Injection (0.93±0.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Vanilla DeepRC baseline (Widrich et al., 2020), both AM and AT substantially outperform it at low witness rates: at WR=0.05%, AM achieves ROCAUC 0.84 vs. Vanilla's 0.53, and at WR=0.2%, AM reaches 0.99 vs. Vanilla's 0.54. On the MNIST-MIL benchmark, AT (using the ADMIL architecture from Ilse et al., 2018) achieves ROCAUC 0.92–1.00 across witness rates of 0.5%–3%, while the Vanilla ADMIL baseline only reaches 0.57–0.87. The Conjunctive pooling baseline (Early et al., 2023) performs poorly at low witness rates (ROCAUC 0.47–0.54 at WR≤0.2%), well below AM and AT. The Additive MIL variant (Javed et al., 2022) on MNIST-MIL is outperformed by AM at all witness rates (e.g., 0.73 vs. 0.52 at WR=0.5%). Simple logistic regression baselines (L1/L2 LogReg, L2 SVC) also fail at low witness rates (ROCAUC ≤0.63 at WR=0.2%), confirming that the gains come from the structured integration of PDK into the attention mechanism rather than from the prior signal alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeepRC (Widrich et al., 2020), an attention-based deep MIL model using Modern Hopfield Networks for immune repertoire classification, and on ADMIL (Ilse et al., 2018) for the MNIST-MIL experiments. The key conceptual departure is from standard deep learning, where learned representations lack a priori association with domain entities, and from Bayesian neural networks, which inject priors at the parameter level. The authors identify that MIL's bag-instance structure, combined with attention-based pooling, creates a natural mapping between attention outputs and instance-level class probabilities, enabling three distinct strategies (Direct Prior Injection, Attention Modulation, Attention Training) for incorporating probabilistic domain knowledge at the intermediate-feature level rather than the parameter level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that even noisy, two-level probabilistic domain knowledge can reduce the required witness rate by 20× and dataset size by 30×, with faster convergence and no additional trainable parameters—practically significant for data-scarce biomedical settings like immune-based diagnostics. A notable finding is that prior injection not only improves bag-level predictions but also enhances the attention module's own ability to identify witnesses, suggesting the prior amplifies gradient flow through relevant instances. Limitations include reliance on semi-synthetic data, a single application domain, and only two-level (high/low) priors; open questions include which other problem structures admit similar component-to-entity mappings, how richer priors (e.g., beta-distributed hyper-priors) would behave, and whether the benefits transfer to real-world noisy settings at scale.
