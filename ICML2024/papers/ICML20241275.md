## Problem

Existing scalable BLO algorithms rely on lower-level convexity or the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition to control approximation errors, leaving large-scale nonconvex-nonconvex BLO problems without efficient single-loop, Hessian-free solutions carrying non-asymptotic guarantees. This paper addresses that gap by tackling BLO where both upper- and lower-level objectives are nonconvex (and the LL objective may be nonsmooth), without requiring any PL or convexity assumption on the LL problem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only first-order gradient evaluations of the smooth components F and f, plus the nonsmooth LL component g accessed through its proximal mapping, along with a proximal parameter γ and a growing penalty sequence c_k = c̲(k+1)^p. It produces the BLO solution pair (x*, y*) satisfying the first-order optimality conditions of the LL subproblem, and in applications yields hyperparameters, data-cleaning weights, or neural network architectures. No Hessian or Hessian-vector products are required at any point, and the nonsmooth LL term is handled entirely through proximal operators.

## Method

MEHA reformulates the BLO problem via the Moreau envelope v_γ(x,y) of the LL objective, converting the nested constraint y ∈ S(x) into the inequality φ(x,y) − v_γ(x,y) ≤ 0, which is then handled through a penalty approximation with parameter c_k. At each iteration, a single proximal gradient step generates θ^{k+1} as an approximation to the exact proximal LL solution θ*_γ(x^k, y^k), and this approximation is substituted into the gradient formula of v_γ to construct inexact update directions for x (projected gradient) and y (proximal gradient). The key insight is that the proximal LL subproblem φ(x,·) + (1/2γ)‖·−y‖² can be strongly convex even when the original LL problem is nonconvex, providing the contraction mechanism needed to control approximation errors without any PL condition. This yields a single-loop, Hessian-free algorithm with O(1/√K) non-asymptotic convergence to a stationary point of the penalized problem, and O(1/K^p) convergence of the constraint violation when c_k grows.

## Results

On few-shot learning (Omniglot), MEHA achieves 91.17% accuracy on 10-way 1-shot and 89.96% on 20-way 1-shot, the best or near-best accuracy while being the fastest (130.81s and 235.77s respectively). On data hyper-cleaning, MEHA attains the highest accuracy of 83.19% (F1: 89.94) on FashionMNIST and 89.55% (F1: 91.26) on MNIST. For neural architecture search, MEHA achieves 96.070% test accuracy, outperforming DARTS (95.569%), P-DARTS (95.710%), and PC-DARTS (95.540%). On large-scale synthetic nonconvex problems (1000 dimensions), MEHA completes in 47.45s versus 68.65s for BOME and 3537.19s for IAPTT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEHA outperforms all 13 baselines across the evaluated tasks. On few-shot learning, MEHA achieves the best 10-way accuracy (91.17%) and is the fastest method (130.81s), beating BAMM (90.57%, 180.48s) and V-PBGD (90.28%, 315.59s); on 20-way it is competitive (89.96%) while being the fastest (235.77s vs. IAPTT's 3450.80s). On data hyper-cleaning, MEHA leads on both FashionMNIST (83.19% vs. BOME's 82.16%) and MNIST (89.55% vs. V-PBGD's 89.10%), with the highest F1 scores. For NAS, MEHA's 96.070% test accuracy surpasses DARTS (95.569%), P-DARTS (95.710%), and PC-DARTS (95.540%). On synthetic nonconvex problems, MEHA is 24× faster than IAPTT at dimension 100 (12.26s vs. 290.82s) and 75× faster at dimension 1000 (47.45s vs. 3537.19s), while also beating BOME (68.65s). On the group lasso task (Table 6), MEHA achieves the lowest test error (71.9 at m=600, 89.5 at m=3600) and fastest runtime (0.3s, 2.5s) compared to VF-iDCA (74.7, 90.0) and IGJO (96.7, 120.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Moreau envelope-based reformulation of BLO introduced by Gao et al. (2023) from convex LL settings to general nonconvex and nonsmooth LL objectives. It departs from the dominant line of nonconvex-nonconvex BLO work (BOME, IAPTT, V-PBGD, GALET, SLM) by eliminating the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition requirement, which all prior Hessian-free methods relied upon to control approximation errors in single-gradient-step LL updates. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the proximal LL subproblem within the Moreau envelope formulation inherits strong convexity from the quadratic regularization term, even when the original LL objective is nonconvex, thereby enabling error control without PL. This positions MEHA as the first method simultaneously achieving single-loop, Hessian-free, and PL-free guarantees for nonconvex-nonconvex BLO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the PL condition is practically significant because many real-world LL objectives in hyperparameter optimization, NAS, and meta-learning (e.g., those involving nonconvex regularizers or neural network losses) do not provably satisfy PL, yet prior methods required it for convergence guarantees. The single-loop Hessian-free design makes the method scalable to large-dimensional problems, as demonstrated by the 30x+ speedup over double-loop competitors at dimension 1000. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic settings (acknowledged as future work), understanding whether the O(K^(-(1-2p)/2)) rate is tight, and whether the penalty parameter schedule can be made adaptive. The method's reliance on proximal mappings of the nonsmooth LL component may limit applicability to problems where such mappings are not readily available.
