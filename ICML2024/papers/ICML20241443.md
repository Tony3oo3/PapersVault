## Problem

The BBP phase transition criterion for spiked Wigner models is well understood in the homogeneous noise case, but for inhomogeneous (block-structured) noise, the spectral phase transition of the optimal transformed matrix had only been conjectured (Pak et al., 2023) without a rigorous proof. This paper provides the first exact asymptotic characterization of both the top eigenvalue outlier and the signal overlap for general K×K block-structured spiked Wigner models, proving the conjectured threshold and establishing optimality of the associated PCA method.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy matrix observation Y = (1/√N)xx^T + H ⊙ (Δ^{1/2}) where Δ is a block-constant variance profile with K communities of proportions ρ_k and noise levels s_{kl}, and produces the top eigenvector of the transformed matrix Y~/√N = (Y ⊙ Δ^{-1} − (1/√N)Diag(Δ^{-1}1))/√N. The output includes the per-community overlap vector q ∈ R^K (measuring alignment between the top eigenvector and the signal in each block) and the phase transition threshold λ₁(Ω_K) = 1, where Ω_K = D_{ρ^{1/2}} S_K D_{ρ^{1/2}} is a K×K matrix encoding all model parameters. The analysis is universal: it holds for any noise distribution with finite moments, not just Gaussian.

## Method

The core technique combines three ingredients: (1) a quadratic vector equation (QVE) characterizing the Stieltjes transform of the variance-profile Wigner bulk, (2) an eigendecomposition showing the small-rank signal matrix Z/√N is a rotation of Ω_K up to vanishing error, and (3) a secular equation det(I_K − D_{g(z)} Ω_K) = 0 whose roots determine outlier positions. The key insight is that the effective signal-to-noise ratio is λ₁(Ω_K), the top eigenvalue of a K×K matrix, and the phase transition occurs precisely when this eigenvalue crosses 1. For λ₁(Ω_K) > 1, the outlier sits at z=1 and the overlap vector is proportional to the right Perron-Frobenius eigenvector of D_{g(1)} Ω_K; for λ₁(Ω_K) ≤ 1, no outlier exists and the overlap vanishes. The overlap magnitude is computed via resolvent analysis using the Woodbury identity and first-order perturbation theory for the top eigenvalue of D_{g(1+ε)}^{1/2} Ω_K D_{g(1+ε)}^{1/2}.

## Results

The main theorem (Thm. 2.6) establishes a sharp phase transition for the top eigenvalue of the transformed matrix Y~/√N at the critical value λ₁(Ω_K) = 1, where Ω_K = D_(ρ^(⊙1/2)) S_K D_(ρ^(⊙1/2)) encodes all block-structure parameters. Below the threshold (λ₁(Ω_K) ≤ 1), the top eigenvalue converges to the rightmost edge of the bulk r(μ_X) and the overlap vector q → 0. Above the threshold (λ₁(Ω_K) > 1), an outlier appears at exactly 1 and the overlap vector converges to C^(-1/2) D_(ρ^(⊙1/2))(1_K − g(1)) ≻ 0, where g(1) is the solution of a quadratic vector equation. This proves Conjecture 2.6 of Pak et al. (2023) and establishes that PCA on the transformed matrix Y~ is optimal for detection in the inhomogeneous spiked Wigner model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform empirical baseline comparisons, as it is a purely theoretical work. However, it provides a critical comparison between three spectral methods: PCA on the original matrix Y, PCA on Y ⊙ (Δ^(⊙-1/2)), and PCA on the proposed transformed matrix Y~. Citing Prop. 2.18 of Guionnet et al. (2022), the paper shows that the detection thresholds for spectral methods on Y or on Y ⊙ (Δ^(⊙-1/2)) are always strictly worse than the optimal algorithmic recovery threshold, while PCA on Y~ achieves exactly this optimal threshold (matching the AMP phase transition from Pak et al., 2023). The result also generalizes the classical BBP phase transition (Baik et al., 2005) from homogeneous to inhomogeneous block-structured noise, with the scalar SNR parameter replaced by the matrix quantity λ₁(Ω_K). The analysis is universal in the noise distribution (only requiring finite moments, not Gaussianity), going beyond the original conjecture which assumed Gaussian entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the seminal BBP (Baik–Ben Arous–Péché, 2005) phase transition criterion from homogeneous spiked Wigner models to block-structured (inhomogeneous) noise, proving a conjecture stated by Pak et al. (2023) that the spectral threshold is governed by the top eigenvalue of a K×K matrix Ω_K encoding all community proportions and noise levels. The analysis builds on the quadratic vector equation (QVE) framework for variance-profile Wigner matrices developed by Ajanki, Erdős, and Krüger (2017a,b; 2019), and connects to the information-theoretic and algorithmic limits characterized by Guionnet et al. (2022) and Behne & Reeves (2022). Prior partial spectral analyses by Bigot & Male (2020) and Lee & Lee (2023) addressed restricted regimes; this work provides the first complete characterization for general fixed K blocks, including the exact overlap formula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that PCA on a specific transformed matrix achieves the optimal algorithmic detection threshold for inhomogeneous spiked models, closing a gap between conjectured AMP performance and provable spectral guarantees. This provides a rigorous warm-start for AMP algorithms, making them provably optimal in MMSE within the class of iterative methods. Open questions include extending the analysis to general (non-block) variance profiles, understanding eigenvalue fluctuations in the inhomogeneous setting, and whether the statistical-to-computational gap identified by Guionnet et al. (2022) can be bridged by more sophisticated spectral methods.
