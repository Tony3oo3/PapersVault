## Problem

Zeroth-order optimization on Riemannian manifolds was only recently made possible by Li et al. (2023a), but their Riemannian zeroth-order gradient descent achieves only O(d/ε²) query complexity, inferior to the O~(d/ε^{7/4}) achievable in Euclidean space. Moreover, existing accelerated zeroth-order methods (Zhang & Gu, 2022) require very small smoothing parameters (Õ(ε^{13/8}d^{-1/2})), which in practice cause instability due to noise dominance in finite-difference estimates. The paper asks whether one can simultaneously achieve the accelerated O~(d/ε^{7/4}) complexity on Riemannian manifolds while allowing a substantially larger, more robust smoothing parameter.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only point-wise function value evaluations f(x) on a d-dimensional Riemannian manifold M (no gradient or Hessian access), using a coordinate-wise finite-difference estimator with smoothing parameter μ to approximate the gradient of the pullback function in the tangent space. With Option I and no perturbation, it outputs an ε-approximate Riemannian first-order stationary point in O((f(x₀)−f_low)·d/ε^{7/4}) queries; with a small random perturbation of radius r = θB/(6K) added to the tangent-space initialization, it outputs an ε-approximate second-order stationary point with probability ≥ 1−δ in Õ((f(x₀)−f_low)·d/ε^{7/4}) queries. The retraction mapping (e.g., normalization on the sphere, exponential map on the simplex) is the only manifold-specific component required.

## Method

RAZGD alternates between two subroutines based on the magnitude of the zeroth-order estimator at the current iterate: when ‖g_{x_t}(0;μ)‖ ≥ lB, it performs a standard Riemannian zeroth-order gradient descent step (Subroutine 1) yielding O(B²) function decrease; otherwise it enters a tangent space step (Subroutine 2) that applies Nesterov-style accelerated zeroth-order updates to the pullback function f̂_x(·) = f∘Retr_x over K iterations, achieving O(ε^{3/2}) decrease per outer step. The key mechanism is working in the tangent space T_{x_t}M where the pullback function is locally Euclidean, enabling the use of the accelerated gradient descent potential-function analysis from Li & Lin (2022) adapted to inexact (zeroth-order) gradients. The smoothing parameter is chosen as μ = Õ(ε^{7/8}d^{-1/2}) for the second-order case—improving over Zhang & Gu's Õ(ε^{13/8}d^{-1/2})—by carefully balancing approximation error against the acceleration potential via Young's inequality. For asymptotic almost-sure convergence to strict second-order stationary points, Option II (Subroutine 3) decays μ geometrically (or via a slower time-varying factor β_k = 1−1/(k+2)), making the zeroth-order estimator converge to the true gradient and invoking the Stable Manifold Theorem to show the set of initial points converging to saddle points has measure zero.

## Results

RAZGD with Option I achieves a function query complexity of O(ε^{-7/4}d) for finding an ε-approximate first-order stationary point, improving upon the prior Riemannian result ZO-RGD (O(d/ε²)) by a factor of O(ε^{-1/4}). The perturbed variant achieves O~(ε^{-7/4}d) for second-order stationary points while requiring a smoothing parameter of only O~(ε^{7/8}d^{-1/2}), a factor of O~(ε^{3/4}) larger than the best prior Euclidean result (Zhang & Gu, 2022). In experiments on the empirical hypervolume problem over the unit sphere, RAZGD reaches target accuracy in ~30 seconds versus over 300 seconds for PZGD, and on the quartic saddle-point benchmark it maintains convergence even at μ=0.3 where competing methods fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior Riemannian zeroth-order method ZO-RGD (Li et al., 2023a), RAZGD improves the query complexity from O(d/ε²) to O(d/ε^{7/4}) for first-order stationary points, a factor of O(ε^{-1/4}) gain. Compared to the best Euclidean accelerated zeroth-order method ZO-PAGD (Zhang & Gu, 2022), RAZGD matches the O~(d/ε^{7/4}) query complexity for second-order stationary points but requires a substantially larger smoothing parameter (ε^{7/8} vs. ε^{13/8}), translating to better practical robustness. In the quartic saddle-point experiment (Figure 4), RAZGD exhibits smaller run-to-run variance than both ZO-Perturbed-AGD and ZO-Perturbed-AGD-ANCF at μ=0.01, and uniquely converges at the much larger μ=0.3 where both baselines fail. On the simplex least-squares and sphere hypervolume benchmarks (Figures 5–6), RAZGD requires fewer function queries than both RZGD (Li et al., 2023a) and PZGD, reaching target accuracy in ~30 s versus >300 s for PZGD. All other baselines in Table 1 (PAGD, ZO-GD, ZO-GD-NCF, ZOPGD) are strictly dominated by RAZGD in query complexity, with the gap ranging from O(ε^{-1/4}) to O(ε^{-13/4}d).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Riemannian zeroth-order optimization (initiated by Li et al., 2023a), Euclidean accelerated zeroth-order methods (Zhang & Gu, 2022), and the tangent-space-step mechanism for nonconvex acceleration (Li & Lin, 2022; Criscitiello & Boumal, 2022). It is the first work to combine Nesterov-style acceleration with zeroth-order estimation on Riemannian manifolds, using the pullback function framework to reduce the problem to Euclidean accelerated gradient descent in the tangent space. A key departure from the Euclidean counterpart is the use of Young's inequality in a way that yields a larger admissible smoothing parameter in the Riemannian setting, which the authors explicitly note is counterintuitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically relevant for applications where gradient computation is infeasible and the feasible set has manifold structure (robot learning, Bayesian optimization, optimal predictor combination), reducing query complexity from O(ε^{-2}d) to O(ε^{-7/4}d) while simultaneously improving robustness through a larger smoothing parameter. The almost-sure saddle-point avoidance via the Stable Manifold Theorem closes a gap between high-probability and probability-1 guarantees. Limitations include the reliance on a deterministic coordinate-wise estimator (requiring 2d function evaluations per gradient estimate), local Lipschitz assumptions that may not hold globally on curved manifolds, and the exponential decay of μ in the asymptotic subroutine, which the authors acknowledge makes the method less attractive from a purely zeroth-order perspective.
