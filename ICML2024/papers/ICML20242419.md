## Problem

In nonstationary online learning, the widely used constant-LR OGD baseline achieves minimax-optimal discounted regret but only under worst-case assumptions on the domain diameter and Lipschitz constant, and it cannot adapt to the actual loss sequence or comparator. The paper asks whether one can achieve instance-optimal discounted regret that depends on the realized gradient variance and comparator norm, while simultaneously establishing a principled link between regularization design and adaptive OCO theory for continual/lifelong learning where forgetting is necessary.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives per-round loss gradients g_t and discount factors λ_t (provided as side information by an external expert, not chosen by the algorithm itself), along with a hyperparameter ε that serves as a prior guess of the comparator magnitude. In the OCP application, it additionally receives a nested set-valued function C_t: [0,∞) → 2^Y and outputs a radius r_t that determines the prediction set C_t(r_t); the discount factors encode the forgetting schedule, with λ_t < 1 causing exponential decay of past losses and λ_t ∈ {0,1} reducing to a hard-restart scheme. The 1D magnitude learner operates on [0,∞) with the full R^d extension handled via polar decomposition.

## Method

The core technique is a rescaling trick that converts an undiscounted adaptive OCO algorithm into a discounted one by applying the base algorithm to upweighted surrogate gradients ĝ_t = (∏_{i<t} λ_i^{-1}) g_t, effectively 'forgetting' older losses while preserving the scale-free property. The base algorithm is a 1D FTRL-based magnitude learner built on an erfi-potential function (from Zhang et al., 2024), combined with hint-and-clipping for online Lipschitz guessing and an unconstrained-to-constrained projection onto [0,∞). The extension to R^d uses polar decomposition, separating direction learning (an RMSProp-like rule) from magnitude learning. Crucially, FTRL is used instead of OGD because it 'memorizes' the initialization (the inductive bias x*) while forgetting online data, and in the OCP application the FTRL structure makes coverage guarantees follow directly from iterate stability, avoiding the complex grouping arguments required for OGD-based analyses.

## Results

On the online conformal prediction task (image classification with time-varying corruption, target miscoverage α=0.1), the paper's three algorithm variants (MAGL-D, MAGL, MAGDIS) achieve the lowest local coverage error (LCE₁₀₀) of 0.08, 0.09, and 0.07 respectively, outperforming all baselines. MAGL-D also achieves the lowest average prediction set width (118.5), while all three variants run at approximately 1.0× the speed of Simple OGD, compared to 11.07× for the aggregation-based SAOCP baseline. The average coverage of the proposed methods (0.884–0.894) is slightly below the 0.9 target but competitive with baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bhatnagar et al. (2023), the paper's methods achieve lower LCE₁₀₀ (0.07–0.08 vs. 0.09 for SF-OGD and 0.10 for SAOCP), indicating better local coverage under distribution shift. The computational advantage is dramatic: SAOCP requires 11.07× the runtime of Simple OGD due to its bilevel aggregation, while the proposed FTRL-based algorithms run at ~1.0×. Against Gibbs & Candes (2021) FACI, the proposed methods again win on LCE₁₀₀ (0.07–0.08 vs. 0.12) and runtime (1.0× vs. 2.98×), though FACI achieves slightly higher average coverage (0.889 vs. 0.884–0.894). The simplified MAGDIS variant, which drops the Lipschitz-tracking parameter h_t, achieves the best LCE₁₀₀ (0.07) despite lacking a formal guarantee, suggesting the core FTRL structure is the key driver of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads in online learning: discounted regret for nonstationary environments (Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi, Kapralov & Panigrahy, Brown & Sandholm) and scale-free adaptive OCO (Cutkosky, Mhammedi & Koolen, Zhang et al. 2024). The key methodological contribution is a rescaling trick that converts scale-free undiscounted regret bounds into discounted regret bounds, combined with the erfi-potential FTRL learner from Zhang et al. (2024) as the base algorithm. For the OCP application, the paper departs from the standard OGD-based analysis (Gibbs & Candes 2021; Bhatnagar et al. 2023) by showing that FTRL's explicit dependence on the cumulative gradient sum S_t^* makes coverage guarantees follow directly from iterate stability, avoiding the complex grouping arguments required for OGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a principled quantitative connection between adaptive OCO theory and regularization design in continual/lifelong learning, showing that the 'right' regularizer for a forgetting algorithm can be derived from instance-optimal regret bounds rather than heuristics. The FTRL-over-OGD analytical advantage for OCP coverage is a genuinely new tool that could simplify future conformal prediction analyses. Open questions include the online selection of discount factors (deferred to future work) and the connection to deep learning optimizers like ADAM, which the authors note as a promising direction. The practical impact is somewhat limited by algorithmic complexity, though the simplified MAGDIS variant shows the core ideas are empirically robust.
