## Problem

Denoising diffusion models are widely used for generative modeling, but extending them to Monte Carlo sampling from unnormalized densities is non-trivial because one cannot draw samples from the target to train a score network. Existing diffusion-based samplers either minimize a reverse KL objective (mode-seeking, no normalizing constant), rely on importance sampling that scales poorly in high dimensions, or use MCMC to estimate scores without providing normalizing constant estimates. The paper asks: can one obtain asymptotically consistent samples and unbiased normalizing constant estimates from a diffusion-based particle scheme that only requires pointwise evaluation of the unnormalized density?

## Input / Output

The method requires only pointwise evaluation of the unnormalized target density γ(x) (no samples from π are needed), a diffusion schedule β_t over [0,T], and a number of particles N. In practice, a variational Gaussian approximation N(μ,Σ) of π is first computed to reparameterize the target so that the guidance potential g₀ is well-behaved. The output is a set of N particles approximating π along with a product-of-weights estimate of the normalizing constant Z, which is unbiased for any finite N and consistent as N→∞.

## Method

PDDS reformulates the denoising diffusion as a guided diffusion with tractable base score (−x_t) and an intractable guidance potential g_t, then approximates g_t with a neural network ĝ_θ(k,x_k) and corrects the resulting approximation error via a Sequential Monte Carlo particle scheme: at each backward time step, particles are moved using an approximate reverse diffusion kernel, reweighted by the ratio of true-to-approximate potentials, resampled (systematic or sorted stratified), and optionally perturbed with a MALA/HMC kernel invariant to the approximate target. A key novelty is a Novel Score Matching (NSM) loss that regresses the score against ∇log g₀(X₀) rather than ∇log p(X_t|X₀), provably avoiding the variance blowup of standard Denoising Score Matching as the discretization step δ→0. Theoretical results establish a CLT for fixed discretization and show that with sorted stratified resampling the asymptotic error remains bounded as K→∞, scaling with the integrated squared error of the score approximation.

## Results

PDDS-MCMC achieves the lowest bias and variance in normalizing constant estimation (log Z) across posterior tasks (Sonar, LGCP), outperforming all baselines except CRAFT at 4 steps on Sonar. On the challenging 40-mode GMM in 20 dimensions, PDDS-MCMC significantly outperforms CRAFT in both bias and variance, while PIS and DDS fail to produce competitive estimates. Both PDDS variants uniformly outperform the diffusion-based baselines (PIS and DDS) on all tasks. In terms of sample quality, PDDS-MCMC achieves a lower entropy-regularized Wasserstein-2 distance to the GMM20 target than CRAFT, recovering a far greater proportion of target modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMC (Del Moral et al., 2006) with HMC kernels, PDDS-MCMC achieves lower bias and variance on both posterior tasks (Sonar and LGCP), while PDDS without MCMC is competitive. Against CRAFT (Matthews et al., 2022), PDDS-MCMC wins on most tasks but CRAFT is best on the 10D Funnel and at 4 steps on Sonar; on the 40-mode GMM in 20D, PDDS-MCMC significantly outperforms CRAFT in both bias and variance, and also achieves a lower Wasserstein-2 distance to the target. Against PIS (Zhang & Chen, 2022) and DDS (Vargas et al., 2023), both PDDS variants uniformly outperform these diffusion-based samplers across all tasks, with the gap widening in higher dimensions and on multimodal targets. Notably, PIS and DDS fail to capture all modes of the Mixture target due to the mode-seeking behaviour of the reverse KL objective, and both fail to produce competitive results on the GMM task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of denoising diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021), guided diffusion for inverse problems (Chung et al., 2023; Song et al., 2023), and Sequential Monte Carlo samplers (Del Moral et al., 2006). It departs from prior diffusion-based samplers (Vargas et al., 2023; Zhang & Chen, 2022; Berner et al., 2022) that rely on reverse-KL objectives or importance sampling by instead wrapping the guided diffusion within an SMC framework, which yields asymptotically consistent estimates. The novel score matching loss (NSM) extends the classical DSM identity (Hyvärinen, 2005) by exploiting the fact that in the sampling setting one has access to the unnormalized target density, enabling a loss whose gradient variance remains finite as the discretization step shrinks—something impossible in the generative modeling setting where only samples are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDDS provides the first diffusion-based Monte Carlo sampler with formal asymptotic consistency guarantees (CLT for fixed discretization, bounded error as K→∞ with sorted stratified resampling), addressing a key theoretical gap in prior diffusion-based sampling work. The practical impact is demonstrated by competitive or superior normalizing constant estimates versus CRAFT, SMC, PIS, and DDS across multimodal and high-dimensional targets. A stated limitation is the reliance on g₀ being a well-behaved potential, requiring a variational reparameterization that may not always be effective; the authors note that more sophisticated transport techniques (Hoffman et al., 2019) could help. An open question is quantifying the individual contributions of the guidance loss, the neural network parametrization, and the SMC mechanism to the overall variance reduction.
