## Problem

Despite the urgent need for reliable uncertainty quantification in large-scale foundation models and LLMs—as evidenced by overconfident yet incorrect predictions—Bayesian deep learning (BDL) remains underutilized in the current AI landscape. The primary impediment is the computational scalability of BDL methods, which have not kept pace with the rapid adoption of billions-of-parameters models. This position paper argues that BDL's untapped potential can address critical gaps in safety, interpretability, and decision-making that deterministic deep learning leaves unaddressed.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, the work does not define a single input-output pipeline but instead surveys BDL methods across multiple modalities (text, RGB images, time series) and application domains (healthcare, drug discovery, climate science, autonomous driving). The 'input' is the current state of BDL literature and the challenges of foundation-model-scale deployment; the 'output' is a structured taxonomy of strengths, a critical analysis of computational bottlenecks (posterior computation, prior specification, scalability), and a set of concrete research directions for making BDL competitive with deterministic deep learning in speed, memory, and predictive utility.

## Method

The paper structures its argument in three parts: (1) a review of BDL strengths—uncertainty quantification, data efficiency, adaptability to evolving domains, and model misspecification handling via Bayesian model averaging; (2) a critical examination of current challenges, including the local/multimodal limitations of Laplace and variational approximations, the cost of deep ensembles and SG-MCMC, the difficulty of prior specification in high-dimensional weight spaces, and the symmetry-induced redundancies that hinder scalability; and (3) a proposal of future research directions spanning new posterior sampling algorithms (tempered posteriors, normalizing flows, score-based diffusion, flow matching), hybrid Bayesian-deterministic architectures, deep kernel processes and machines, semi-/self-supervised Bayesian learning, probabilistic numerics for mixed-precision computation, compression via sparsity-inducing priors, and Bayesian approaches to LLM fine-tuning (Bayesian LoRA, Bayesian reward modeling). The central thesis is that simultaneously achieving time efficiency, memory efficiency, and high model utility (predictive performance plus calibrated uncertainty) is the 'holy grail' of approximate Bayesian inference that must be solved for BDL to become mainstream in the foundation-model era.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not present quantitative experimental results or benchmark comparisons. The central empirical illustration (Figure 1) demonstrates that popular LLMs (GPT-4 via Bing Chat and LLaMA-2-70B) produce factually incorrect answers with very high stated confidence (100% and 90%, respectively), motivating the need for calibrated uncertainty quantification that BDL can provide. The paper's contribution is a structured argument for why BDL matters (uncertainty quantification, data efficiency, adaptability, interpretability) and a roadmap of research directions to address scalability challenges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not conduct head-to-head experimental comparisons between methods. Instead, it qualitatively contrasts BDL approaches against each other and against non-Bayesian alternatives: Laplace and variational approximations are noted as computationally cheap but fundamentally local (single-mode) and parametrization-dependent; deep ensembles are effective but costly for large models; SG-MCMC methods (SG-LD, SG-HMC) offer better posterior approximations but converge slower than SGD; and conformal prediction is acknowledged as a competitor for UQ but requires an exchangeability assumption that BDL does not. The paper argues that no single existing BDL method yet matches the speed of standard SGD while delivering high-quality posterior approximations, identifying this as the 'holy grail' of approximate Bayesian inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes the entire Bayesian deep learning literature — from Laplace approximations (MacKay, 1992; Ritter et al., 2018) and variational inference (Blundell et al., 2015) through stochastic gradient MCMC (Welling & Teh, 2011; Chen et al., 2014) and deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) — and repositions it as urgently needed for the foundation-model era. Rather than proposing a new method, it carves new ground by arguing that BDL's underutilization in LLMs is a community-level gap, not a technical impossibility, and by connecting disparate BDL subfields (deep kernel processes, probabilistic numerics, singular learning theory) to concrete LLM applications like Bayesian LoRA and reward modeling. The paper departs from the typical BDL contribution (a new approximation or prior) by making a programmatic case for scalability research directions that bridge the gap between BDL's current million-parameter ceiling and billion-parameter foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central open question — whether BDL can match the computational efficiency of deterministic deep learning while providing calibrated uncertainty — remains unresolved, and the authors explicitly identify this as the 'holy grail of approximate Bayesian inference.' The Figure 1 demonstration of LLMs producing confidently wrong answers (e.g., misidentifying sulfuric acid as osmium tetroxide) underscores the practical urgency, yet no existing BDL method has been validated at LLM scale. The proposed research directions (subspace inference, hybrid Bayesian-deterministic architectures, deep kernel machines, symmetry-aware priors) are promising but largely aspirational, and the paper acknowledges that BDL's adoption has been 'sluggish' for two decades. For practitioners, the paper serves as a comprehensive roadmap but offers no immediately deployable technique; its impact will depend on whether the community follows through on the scalability challenges identified.
