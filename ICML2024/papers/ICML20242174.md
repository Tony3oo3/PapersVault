## Problem

While discrete-state Markov jump processes and continuous-state SDE-based diffusion models are both widely used for generative modeling, the precise relationship between their time-reversals—the quantities actually learned during training—remained elusive. This paper establishes that the time-reversed Ehrenfest process (a discrete birth-death process) converges to the time-reversed Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in the large state-space limit, thereby creating a direct bridge that allows methods, loss functions, and pretrained models to be transferred between the two settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sample from the approximate equilibrium distribution (a standard Gaussian in the scaled setting) and a neural network that predicts the conditional expectation (or equivalently, the score) at each time step, and produces a discrete image by running the time-reversed birth-death process from t=T to t=0. For image generation, each pixel/channel is a discrete state in {0,…,255} (S=255), so CIFAR-10 images are 3×32×32=3072 dimensions and MNIST images are 32×32=1024 dimensions. The forward process factorizes independently across dimensions, but the learned backward rates couple all dimensions through the shared conditional expectation, and sampling uses τ-leaping to efficiently simulate the many small jumps.

## Method

The core contribution is showing that the backward rates of the time-reversed scaled Ehrenfest process depend on a conditional expectation of a ratio of forward transition probabilities, which in the large-S limit reduces to 1 ± (2/√S)·∇log p_t(x)—exactly the score function used in continuous SDE-based diffusion. This yields a family of training losses: L_Gauss approximates the ratio with a Gaussian closed form, L_Taylor uses a first-order Taylor expansion requiring only one network output, and L_OU directly matches the OU conditional score, making it identical in form to the DDPM denoising objective. Because L_OU is structurally the same as the continuous denoising score matching loss, a U-Net pretrained in the continuous DDPM setting can be directly repurposed (or fine-tuned) for the discrete Ehrenfest process without architectural changes. The forward process is simulation-free (each dimension is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables per Lemma 3.1), and the reverse process is sampled via τ-leaping with Poisson-distributed jump counts per dimension per time step.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (50,000 samples), the best from-scratch Ehrenfest model using L_Taylor achieves IS 9.66 and FID 5.12, while L_OU achieves IS 9.50 and FID 5.08. The transfer-learning variant (using a pretrained DDPM U-Net without further discrete training) reaches IS 8.75 and FID 11.57, and the short-finetuned pretrained variant reaches IS 9.14 and FID 6.63. On MNIST (32×32, S=255), the paper demonstrates qualitative sample quality with L_OU but does not report quantitative metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's best from-scratch model (L_Taylor, IS 9.66, FID 5.12) outperforms τ-LDR(0) (IS 8.74, FID 8.10) and D3PM Gauss (IS 8.56, FID 7.34) on both metrics, and substantially outperforms D3PM Absorbing (IS 6.78, FID 30.97). Against the strongest baseline τ-LDR(10) (IS 9.49, FID 3.74), the Ehrenfest process achieves a slightly higher IS (9.66 vs 9.49) but a worse FID (5.12 vs 3.74), indicating a trade-off between sample diversity and fidelity. The transfer-learning setting (no discrete training) yields IS 8.75 and FID 11.57, which is competitive with τ-LDR(0) but clearly below the from-scratch results, suggesting that the discrete-specific training is important for full performance. The authors note that they did not invest significant time in hyperparameter tuning or sampling strategy optimization, so the gap to τ-LDR(10) on FID may be closable with further tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: continuous-time Markov jump processes for discrete-state generative modeling (Campbell et al., 2022) and SDE-based score matching for continuous-state diffusion models (Song et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020). While prior work by Santos et al. (2023) investigated the correspondence between these two frameworks, it did not identify a direct relationship between the learned models. This paper fills that gap by showing that the time-reversed Ehrenfest process (a birth-death process) converges to the time-reversed Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, and that the conditional expectation needed for backward rates in the discrete setting corresponds exactly to the score function in the continuous setting, enabling direct transfer of loss functions and pretrained models between the two regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical bridge between discrete-state and continuous-state generative models opens a pathway for cross-pollination: loss functions, architectures, and pretrained weights can now be transferred between the two settings, as demonstrated by the DDPM-to-Ehrenfest transfer learning experiments. The conditional-expectation loss is also practically simpler than modeling the full reverse transition distribution, reducing the learning problem to function approximation. However, the convergence results are stated as expectations rather than rigorous theorems, the empirical FID scores on CIFAR-10 (5.08 best) are competitive but not dominant compared to τ-LDR(10) at 3.74, and the generalization beyond birth-death processes remains an open question that limits the immediate applicability to non-lattice discrete data.
