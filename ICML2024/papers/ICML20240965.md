## Problem

Existing two-timescale gradient methods (GDA and extragradient) for nonconvex-nonconcave minimax optimization face two critical shortcomings: they can be unstable at non-strict local minimax points even with sufficiently large timescale separation, and computing the required timescale separation is infeasible in practice as it depends on unavailable second-order information. This paper addresses both issues to enable convergence to non-strict local minimax points that are prevalent in modern overparameterized settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a smooth nonconvex-nonconcave minimax objective f(x,y) and produces iterates converging to a stationary point satisfying the second-order necessary condition of a local minimax point. A key design constraint is that the two timescale parameters τ_k and γ_k are allowed to differ (specifically γ_k = √τ_k), which is essential for the stabilizing effect; in the increasing-timescale-separation variant, τ_k grows as k^(1/(2+2c)) while γ_k grows as k^(1/(4+4c)) for any positive constant c.

## Method

The proposed Alt2-EG method incorporates a double-step alternating update where the y-gradient is evaluated after an x-update, creating an implicit regularization that shifts problematic type (i) eigenvalues from the left-half plane into the stability region P_η. The critical choice γ = √τ (rather than γ = τ) produces eigenvalue shifts of order Ω(√ε) sufficient for stability at non-strict local minimax points while preserving the correct asymptotics for type (ii) and (iii) eigenvalues tied to the second-order necessary condition via the restricted Schur complement. To eliminate the need for a priori knowledge of a sufficiently large τ, the Alt2-EG-ITS variant increases the timescale separation indefinitely, analyzed via non-autonomous dynamical system theory to show asymptotic stability with rate O(k^(-1/2) e^(-2√k)). Under a star-convex-star-concave setting, both variants globally find first-order stationary points, and combined with local convergence and escape results, globally converge to local minimax points almost surely.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical convergence guarantees rather than empirical benchmarks. Theorem 5.4 establishes that Alt2-EG-FTS with (τ, √τ) is exponentially stable at any stationary point satisfying the second-order necessary condition (S_res ⪰ 0, B ⪯ 0) for sufficiently large τ, under Assumptions 1 and 3′. Theorem 6.1 extends this to Alt2-EG-ITS with (k^{1/(2+2c)}, k^{1/(4+4c)}), showing asymptotic stability with rate O(1/√k · e^{−2√k}) without requiring a pre-specified τ. A numerical experiment on f(x,y) = −x² + 2xy (a non-strict local minimax point at (0,0)) confirms that Alt2-EG-FTS with τ = 20 and Alt2-EG-ITS converge to the optimum, while GDA-FTS, EG-FTS, and the standard alternating variant (Alt1-EG-FTS) all fail to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two existing two-timescale methods: the two-timescale GDA (GDA-FTS, from Heusel et al. 2017) and the two-timescale extragradient (EG-FTS, from Chae et al. 2024). On the non-strict local minimax example f(x,y) = −x² + 2xy, both GDA-FTS and EG-FTS fail to converge to the local minimax point (0,0) even with τ = 20, due to instability caused by type (i) eigenvalues that lie outside the stability region P_η. The proposed Alt2-EG-FTS (with the double-step alternating update and γ = √τ) and Alt2-EG-ITS (with increasing timescale separation) both successfully converge to this point. The key theoretical distinction is that the double-step alternating update shifts the problematic type (i) eigenvalues into the right-half plane (by Ω(√ε)), resolving the instability that plagues EG-FTS at non-strict local minimax points. Additionally, Alt2-EG-ITS eliminates the need to pre-specify a sufficiently large τ, which is infeasible in practice since it depends on unavailable second-order information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the two-timescale extragradient method of Chae et al. (2024b), which was the first method proven to converge to non-strict local minimax points (removing the non-degeneracy assumption on ∇²yy f required by Jin et al., 2020). It addresses two specific limitations identified in that work: (i) instability at certain non-strict local minimax points due to type (i) eigenvalues approaching zero from outside the stability region, and (ii) the infeasibility of computing the required timescale separation in practice. The double-step alternating update draws on the broader literature showing alternating GDA is more stable than simultaneous GDA (Mescheder et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2024), while the increasing timescale separation scheme connects to adaptive/parameter-free methods like Tiada (Li et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-strict local minimax points are ubiquitous in overparameterized settings (Cooper, 2021; Liu et al., 2022), yet existing two-timescale methods provably fail to converge to some of them. This paper closes that gap by showing that a simple alternating update implicitly regularizes the dynamics (shifting problematic eigenvalues into the stability region) without altering stationary points, and that increasing the timescale separation indefinitely eliminates the need to compute a problem-specific threshold. Key open questions remain: the escaping behavior at strict non-minimax points for the increasing-timescale variant is unproven (the stable manifold theorem used for the fixed case does not extend to non-autonomous systems), and the global convergence guarantee for Alt2-EG-ITS is weaker than for Alt2-EG-FTS due to the non-autonomous nature of the dynamics.
