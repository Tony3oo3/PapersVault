## Problem

Group Equivariant CNNs enforce a fixed equivariance group across all data, but real-world datasets exhibit varying levels of partial symmetry per instance (e.g., digit 6 breaks under 180° rotation while digit 7 does not). Partial G-CNN relaxes this by restricting the output group space, yet still applies a uniform equivariance level to every input and suffers from training instability in discrete groups. The paper addresses both gaps: making equivariance level input-specific and stabilizing discrete group training.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 28×28 MNIST digits, 64×64 Flowers102) and lifts them to group feature maps over SO(2) or T(2)×H_m. It outputs class labels for image classification along with calibrated uncertainty metrics (NLL and Brier score). The input-aware distribution q(u|f) is conditioned on intermediate layer features rather than the raw image, making it memory-efficient for continuous group convolutions where retaining per-layer group elements would be prohibitive.

## Method

VP G-CNN modifies the group convolution integral by inserting an input-aware distribution q(u|f^(l)) over output group elements, conditioned on the previous layer's feature map. Training uses variational inference: group elements are treated as latent variables, and the ELBO (classification log-likelihood minus KL divergence to a uniform prior representing full equivariance) is maximized, so the model regularizes toward full equivariance but deviates when it harms classification. For continuous SO(2), a reparametrizable encoder e_φ(f) outputs θ∈[0,1] and samples r_φ(f,ε)=επ·e_φ(f); for discrete H_m, a novel softmax-weighted threshold distribution replaces the unstable Gumbel-Softmax, selecting group elements whose importance weight exceeds 1/m−η. Only a subset of layers (e.g., lifting and final convolution) need the input-aware partial convolution for the entire network to inherit partial equivariance, keeping the parameter overhead minimal.

## Results

On Flowers102 (color-shift partial equivariance), VP G-CNN with 6 group elements achieves 69.3±0.4% test accuracy, outperforming the full G-CNN (65.0±0.7%), Partial G-CNN (66.8±0.7%), and InstaAug (62.2±0.8%). On ColorMNIST (fully equivariant color-shift task), VP G-CNN reaches 88.4±1.1%, competitive with the full G-CNN (88.7±0.3%) while Partial G-CNN collapses to 63.5±0.6% due to training instability. On CIFAR-10 (rotation equivariance), VP G-CNN with 8 elements achieves 87.6±0.2%, marginally exceeding G-CNN (86.8±0.6%) and Partial G-CNN (87.3±0.4%). VP G-CNN also achieves the best uncertainty metrics on Flowers102: NLL 0.0121 and Brier score 0.0035, versus 0.0171/0.0042 for G-CNN and 0.0346/0.0086 for Partial G-CNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VP G-CNN decisively outperforms Partial G-CNN on Flowers102 (69.3% vs. 66.8% with 6 elements), where Partial G-CNN suffers from unstable training of its Gumbel-Softmax discrete distribution; the gap is even more dramatic on ColorMNIST, where Partial G-CNN collapses to 63.5% while VP G-CNN reaches 88.4%. Against the full G-CNN baseline, VP G-CNN gains 4.3 points on Flowers102 (69.3% vs. 65.0% with 6 elements), demonstrating that input-aware partial equivariance is beneficial when some flowers (e.g., Barberton Daisy) are confused by color shifts. VP G-CNN also beats InstaAug by 7.1 points on Flowers102 (69.3% vs. 62.2%), as InstaAug's augmentation-based approach destabilizes training in the equivariant setting. On CIFAR-10, where partial equivariance is less critical (no rotated test images), VP G-CNN (87.6%) ties or slightly exceeds Partial G-CNN (87.3%) and G-CNN (86.8%). In the additional AdaAug comparison on Flowers102, VP G-CNN (69.40%) outperforms Partial CEResNet w/ AdaAug (68.45%) and ResNet w/ AdaAug (64.70%), and does so without requiring a validation set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Partial G-CNN (Romero & Lohit, 2022), which broke full group equivariance by restricting the output group element distribution to a fixed subset, by making that distribution input-conditioned and training it via variational inference (ELBO with a uniform prior representing full equivariance). The group convolution framework itself builds on Cohen & Welling (2016) and the color-equivariant CEConv of Lengyel et al. (2023). A key technical departure is replacing the Gumbel-Softmax sampling used by Partial G-CNN for discrete groups with a novel softmax-threshold distribution that resolves training instability, while the continuous SO(2) case retains a reparametrized uniform distribution with input-dependent range.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of G-CNNs—fixed equivariance levels that mismatch the heterogeneous symmetries present in real data (e.g., digit 6 vs. 7 under rotation, or Barberton Daisy vs. Snapdragon under color-shift)—and provides a principled variational mechanism to adapt equivariance per instance. However, the empirical gains are modest: on CIFAR10, VP G-CNN essentially ties Partial G-CNN, and the most compelling results (Flowers102) are partly attributable to fixing the discrete-group training instability rather than the input-awareness itself. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger or higher-dimensional groups (beyond SO(2) and H_m), whether the variational regularization is necessary versus simpler input-dependent parameterization, and how the method behaves on larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet.
