## Problem

Score-based generative models (SGMs) suffer from the curse of dimensionality when applied to high-cardinality data such as 3D point clouds with 10^4–10^5 points, where performance degrades sharply as the number of data elements grows. Existing macroscopic approaches that operate in infinite-dimensional function spaces impose strong structural assumptions that limit practical applicability, while conventional SGMs lack a principled mechanism to model inter-particle associations at scale. The paper asks how to restructure diffusion models so that robustness is preserved as cardinality N → ∞.

## Input / Output

The model begins with a small set of N₀ Gaussian-noise particles (e.g., 1.25×10³ points in R^d) and progressively branches them via a learned particle-branching function at K denoising steps, doubling the count each time (branching ratio b=2) until reaching the target cardinality (e.g., 2×10⁴ points for MedShapeNet). The score network at each step receives both the individual particle coordinates and the empirical measure of all particles as input, producing a per-particle velocity field. The final output is an unordered set of N 3D points representing a shape, evaluated via sliced 2-Wasserstein distance (synthetic) or EMD/CD (real datasets).

## Method

MF-CDMs recast high-cardinality data generation as a mean-field N-particle system governed by exchangeable stochastic differential equations, then exploit the propagation of chaos property to show that a small number of particles suffices to approximate the infinite-cardinality limit. The training objective is a mean-field score-matching loss derived via the Itô-Wentzell-Lions formula on Wasserstein space, featuring a 1/√(Nd) Sobolev-norm scaling that makes score estimation robust to large N. To handle computational intractability, the denoising trajectory is subdivided into K sub-intervals with geometrically increasing cardinality (N_k = b^k·N₀), and a learned particle-branching function glues the piecewise Wasserstein gradient flows at each cardinality transition. The score network combines a per-particle component A_θ with a truncated convolution B_θ * ν over a local Euclidean ball of radius R, encoding mean-field interactions with geometrically proximate neighbors.

## Results

On the real-world 3D point-cloud generation task, MF-CDMs achieves the best EMD and CD on both ShapeNet (EMD 2.627, CD 1.877) and MedShapeNet (EMD 4.046, CD 2.764), with metrics scaled by 10². On the synthetic scalability experiment at extreme cardinality (N=10⁵, d=32), MF-CDMs attains a sliced 2-Wasserstein distance of 4.059, substantially outperforming the best baseline LION (6.105) by ~33%. The advantage grows with cardinality: at N=10³ the gap is modest, but at N=10⁵ MF-CDMs leads all baselines by a wide margin, confirming its design goal of robustness under high-cardinality data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MF-CDMs outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both real-world datasets. On ShapeNet (N≈10⁴ points), MF-CDMs reduces EMD by 14% over the next-best DPM (2.627 vs 3.058) and CD by 42% (1.877 vs 3.269). The gap widens dramatically on MedShapeNet (N≈2×10⁴ points), where task-oriented methods like PVD and LION—optimized for low-cardinality 2048-point settings—suffer severe degradation (PVD CD jumps to 5.902, LION CD to 5.135), while MF-CDMs maintains CD of 2.764, a 54% improvement over PVD. On the synthetic scalability benchmark at N=10⁵, d=32, MF-CDMs (SW2=4.059) beats LION (6.105), DPM (7.448), CLD (7.131), and VP-SDEs (7.542) by 33–86%, demonstrating that the propagation-of-chaos formulation specifically addresses the curse of dimensionality that causes conventional SGMs to degrade at high cardinality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VP-SDE score-matching framework (Song et al., 2021) by reinterpreting high-cardinality data (e.g., point clouds) as large systems of interacting particles governed by mean-field stochastic differential equations, rather than as points in a high-dimensional Euclidean space. The theoretical backbone draws on Kac's propagation of chaos and Wasserstein gradient flow geometry (Santambrogio, 2017), while the training objective generalizes conventional score matching via the Itô-Wentzell-Lions formula. It departs from macroscopic infinite-dimensional diffusion approaches (Lim et al., 2023; Hagemann et al., 2023) that require strong function-valued representations, and from point-cloud-specific architectures (DPM, LION, PVD) that impose rigid localization constraints, instead relying on a flexible truncated-convolution interaction that only requires exchangeability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/√(Nd) scaling in the MF-SM objective provides a principled theoretical guarantee that score estimation remains stable as cardinality grows, addressing a fundamental scalability limitation of conventional SGMs that prior work identified but did not resolve. The particle branching subdivision strategy offers a practical divide-and-conquer scheme for training and sampling at extreme cardinalities (10^4–10^5 points), though the method loses its advantage at small N (10^3) where simpler baselines suffice. Open questions include whether the mean-field interaction design generalizes beyond unstructured point sets to structured data (molecules, graphs), and whether the computational overhead of the Wasserstein-space formulation limits real-world adoption at scale.
