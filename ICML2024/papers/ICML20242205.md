## Problem

Dwork et al. (ITCS 2010) showed that a profile can be reconstructed from a noisy histogram by solving a linear program, but left the privacy-utility trade-off unclear because they did not analyze for which error levels the LP is solvable with high probability. This paper closes that gap by replacing the LP with a near-linear-time algorithm and providing explicit ℓ₁, ℓ₂, and ℓ∞ error bounds, while also proving an information-theoretic lower bound showing the O(1/√d) domain-size dependence is optimal for updatable private sketches.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a d-dimensional private histogram h̃ (each coordinate being a true count plus independent discrete Laplace noise, possibly clipped to [0,n]) and a norm parameter p ∈ {1,2,∞}, and returns an approximate profile vector r ∈ [0,1]^{n+1} where r[t] estimates the fraction of domain items appearing exactly t times. A key practical detail is that the algorithm first 'unfolds' clipped histogram entries by adding geometric noise at the boundaries, so that both clipped and unclipped histograms are handled identically. The entire pipeline runs in O(d + n log n) time, with the dominant cost being FFT-based multiplication by the inverse circulant matrix.

## Method

The core insight is that the expected noisy profile E[f̃] equals A·f for a circulant matrix A whose entries are determined by the truncated discrete Laplace distribution, so inverting the noise reduces to computing A⁻¹f̃. Because A is circulant, A⁻¹ is diagonalized by the DFT matrix, making each matrix-vector product an O(n log n) FFT operation. The algorithm (Algorithm 2) computes u = A⁻¹f̃ and then projects u onto the hyperplane {r : Σr[t]=1} along the direction that minimizes the induced ℓ_p norm, yielding the optimal solution to a relaxed problem (P₃). A subsequent rounding step (Algorithm 3) clips coordinates to [0,1] and redistributes mass to restore the sum-to-1 constraint, provably increasing ℓ₁/ℓ₂ error by at most a constant factor. Error analysis decomposes into the matrix-norm amplification of A⁻¹ (bounded via eigenvalue analysis) and concentration of f̃ around its expectation (via McDiarmid's and Bernstein's inequalities), yielding the O(1/√d) dependence on domain size in all three norms.

## Results

The paper establishes that the profile can be reconstructed from a discrete-Laplace-privatized histogram in O(d + n log n) time (vs. polynomial time for the prior LP-based approach of Dwork et al., 2010). The estimation error scales as O(1/√d) in all three norms (ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞), with an additional factor of ((e^ε+1)/(e^ε−1))². An information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 1.2) shows that Ω(1/√d) error is unavoidable for any updatable ε-DP sketch, making the d-dependence asymptotically optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical baselines or experimental comparisons, as it is a purely theoretical contribution. The primary comparison is with Dwork, Naor, Pitassi, Rothblum, and Yekhanin (ITCS 2010), whose pan-private streaming algorithm also reconstructs the profile from a noisy histogram but formulates the inversion as a linear program with polynomial-time complexity and unspecified exponents in the sketch size. This paper replaces the LP with a circulant-matrix / FFT-based inversion, reducing the time to O(d + n log n), and provides explicit, tight error bounds in ℓ₁, ℓ₂, and ℓ∞ norms where Dwork et al. left the privacy-utility trade-off unclear (they did not analyze for which error levels the LP is solvable with high probability). The lower bound in Theorem 1.2 applies broadly to all updatable ε-DP algorithms (not just the discrete Laplace mechanism), showing that no method in this class can beat the Ω(1/√d) error floor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dwork et al. (ITCS 2010), who formulated profile estimation from a noisy histogram as a linear program in the pan-private streaming setting. The key departure is replacing that LP with a circulant-matrix inversion solvable via FFT, reducing runtime from polynomial to O(d + n log n), and switching from Dwork et al.'s nearly-uniform-mod-p noise to the standard discrete Laplace mechanism of Ghosh et al. (STOC 2009). The information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 1.2) is established via a reduction to the two-party differential privacy inner-product lower bound of McGregor et al. (FOCS 2010) and Haitner et al. (STOC 2022), connecting profile estimation to a well-studied impossibility result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap left by Dwork et al. (2010), who did not analyze for what error levels their LP is solvable, by providing explicit ℓ₁/ℓ₂/ℓ∞ error bounds and proving the O(1/√d) domain-size dependence is information-theoretically optimal for any updatable ε-DP sketch. A notable open question is the gap between the ℓ∞ upper bound's O(1/ε^1.5) dependence and the Ω(1/ε) lower bound for releasing a single counter, suggesting room for tighter analysis. The result is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and its practical impact is confined to privacy-preserving frequency analysis in distributed or streaming settings rather than broader ML applications.
