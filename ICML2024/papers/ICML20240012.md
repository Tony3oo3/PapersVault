## Problem

Existing EEG seizure detection models (LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM, DCRNN, Transformers) achieve high accuracy but rely on computationally expensive gating mechanisms or deep stacked layers, resulting in inference latencies of 2–23 ms and memory footprints of 0.25–30 MB. This makes them impractical for real-time deployment on resource-constrained edge devices such as Responsive Neurostimulation (RNS) and Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) implants, which require sub-millisecond predictions and minimal memory to trigger timely stimulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a preprocessed EEG clip X ∈ R^{T×M×N} where T spans 4–14 seconds of 1-second windows, M=100 (TUSZ) or M=128 (CHB-MIT) log-amplitude frequency features obtained via FFT on 19-channel raw EEG resampled to 200/256 Hz, and N=19 electrodes arranged in the standard 10/20 system. A static distance-based graph (Gaussian kernel, k=0.9) over the 19 electrode nodes is constructed once and reused across all time points. The model outputs a binary seizure/no-seizure label for detection or a 5-class seizure type label (general non-specific, tonic-clonic, absence, focal, complex parietal) for classification.

## Method

REST replaces the gating mechanisms of RNNs with a residual state update: the input is linearly mapped to a latent state H^t = WX^t + US^{t-1}, and the state is updated as S^t = H^t + δS^t, where δS^t is computed by a two-layer graph convolution (Morris et al. 2019) over the static EEG distance graph. A binary random mask (Bernoulli with probability p) is applied element-wise to δS^t, serving as continuous dropout during both training and inference while skipping zero-masked computations to accelerate inference. To increase effective depth without additional parameters or memory, REST reuses the same weights W, U, and Θ across multiple sequential state updates at each time point, with the random mask ensuring each iteration updates a different subset of state features. The residual connection S^t = H^t + δS^t provides a direct gradient path that provably prevents vanishing gradients, and the shared-weight multi-update design keeps the total parameter count under 10K (37 KB) while matching or exceeding the accuracy of models with 126K–7.5M parameters.

## Results

On the TUSZ dataset (10-s clip), REST(RM) achieves 83.6% AUROC for seizure detection with only 8.4K parameters (37 KB) and 1.292 ms inference time, matching DCRNN w/SS (82.1%) and approaching the Transformer (85.5%) while being 20× faster and 38× smaller. On CHB-MIT, REST(RM) dominates all baselines, reaching 96.7% AUROC at 4-s clip length and 91.6% at 12-s. For seizure classification (10-s), REST(RM) attains a weighted F1 of 0.60, second only to DCRNN w/SS (0.62) but with 39× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REST(RM) outperforms all six baselines on the CHB-MIT dataset across every clip length, achieving 96.7% AUROC at 4-s versus DCRNN's 88.7% and the Transformer's 80.1%. On TUSZ at 10-s, REST(RM) (83.6%) surpasses DCRNN (80.0%), CNN-LSTM (75.4%), ResNet-LSTM (74.3%), GRU (73.5%), and LSTM (70.4%), but trails the Transformer (85.5%) by ~2% and DCRNN w/SS (82.1%) is slightly below REST. In inference speed, REST(RM) at 1.292 ms is 9× faster than DCRNN (9.67 ms), 20× faster than DCRNN w/SS (23.25 ms), and 3× faster than the next-fastest baseline LSTM (3.254 ms). In model size, REST(RM) at 37 KB / 8.4K parameters is 14× smaller than the smallest baseline (Transformer, 0.80 MB / 120.3K) and over 600× smaller than CNN-LSTM (22.8 MB / 6M). For seizure classification, REST(RM) (F1 = 0.60) is second only to DCRNN w/SS (0.62) while using 39× fewer parameters, and substantially outperforms LSTM (0.39), GRU (0.44), CNN-LSTM (0.47), and Transformer (0.54).

## Relation to Prior Work

REST sits at the intersection of three research threads: residual learning (He et al., 2016), graph neural networks for EEG spatial structure (Tang et al., 2021; Ho & Armanfard, 2023), and recurrent state evolution for temporal dependencies. The key conceptual departure is replacing the gating mechanisms of LSTM/GRU with a residual state update (S^t = H^t + δS^t) where the increment δS^t is computed via a lightweight graph convolution, and replacing learned update gates with binary random masking inspired by neural cellular automata (Mordvintsev et al., 2020). Multiple updates with shared weights substitute for stacked RNN layers, eliminating the per-layer memory overhead that makes deep recurrent architectures impractical for edge deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9× inference speedup and 14× size reduction (37KB, <10K parameters) while maintaining competitive accuracy directly addresses the unmet need for on-device seizure detection in closed-loop neurostimulation systems (RNS, DBS), where sub-millisecond latency and minimal memory are hard constraints. The paper acknowledges that real-world validation on diverse patient populations, energy efficiency assessment, and neurologist sign-off remain open before clinical deployment. A broader open question is whether the residual-update-plus-graph-convolution paradigm generalizes beyond EEG to other spatially-structured time-series signals on resource-constrained hardware.
