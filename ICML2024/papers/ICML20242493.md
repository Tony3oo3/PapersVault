## Problem

Existing LLM-based jailbreak detection methods, such as prompting the model to self-classify toxic content, suffer from a domain shift: alignment training imparts a self-censorship format (refusing harmful requests) while classification-based safeguards require a different format (labeling content as harmful/benign). This mismatch limits detection efficacy, particularly in the critical regime of high true positive rate and low false positive rate.

## Input / Output

PARDEN takes the full LLM-generated output text (not the user prompt) and feeds it back into the same LLM with a repeat prompt containing in-context examples. The output is a binary harmful/benign decision derived from a BLEU score threshold between the original output and the model's attempted repetition; if classified as harmful, the refusal/repeat output is returned to the user instead of the original. The method requires no white-box access or finetuning, and supports partial repeats (first n tokens) to bound computational cost to a constant number of forward passes regardless of output length.

## Method

PARDEN prompts the LLM to repeat its own sampled output by constructing a repeat prompt with in-context examples of faithful repetitions, then computes the BLEU score between the original output and the model's repetition. The key insight is that repetition is a self-censorship task the model is already trained on during alignment, avoiding the domain shift that plagues classification-based safeguards, while presenting the entire output at once circumvents the autoregressive trap. For benign outputs, the model faithfully repeats (BLEU ≈ 0.95), while for harmful outputs it triggers a refusal response (BLEU ≈ 0.2–0.6), creating a clear distributional separation. A threshold on the BLEU score induces a family of classifiers, and in-context examples are particularly important for short partial repeats (5–20 tokens) to maintain high repetition fidelity. The method is input-agnostic, operates purely in the output space, and requires no finetuning or gradient access.

## Results

On the aggregated harmful behaviours dataset (Llama-2-7B), PARDEN achieves an AUC of 0.958±0.0066 and an FPR of 2.0%±0.86% at TPR=90%, compared to 0.922 AUC and 24.8% FPR for the Helbling et al. classifier baseline and 0.660 AUC and 64.5% FPR for the perplexity filter. For Claude-2.1 on harmful strings, PARDEN reaches (TPR, FPR) = (90.0%, 1.09%) with AUC 0.9875, versus (69.2%, 2.72%) for the baseline. Across 10 transferability scenarios where PARDEN's safety-alignment premise holds, it reduces FPR at fixed TPR=90% in 9 out of 10 cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARDEN is compared against two baselines: (1) the binary self-classification method of Helbling et al. (2023), which prompts the LLM to label outputs as harmful/benign, and (2) the perplexity filter of Jain et al. (2023), which flags high-perplexity text. PARDEN decisively outperforms both on the primary Llama-2-7B evaluation: it improves AUC from 0.922 to 0.958 and reduces FPR at TPR=90% from 24.8% to 2.0% (an ~11× reduction) over the Helbling et al. classifier, and from 64.5% to 2.0% over the perplexity filter. The perplexity filter is particularly weak because AutoDan-style attacks are specifically designed to evade perplexity-based detection. In the broader transferability study (Table 3), PARDEN wins in 9 of 10 applicable scenarios across Llama-2-7B, Claude-2.1, and Mistral-7B as defender models, with the sole exception being a near-tie for Llama-attacked/Claude-defended on harmful behaviours (FPR 1.27% vs 1.09%). The one scenario where PARDEN underperforms is when Mistral-7B (an unaligned model) is used as the defender, which violates PARDEN's core assumption of a safety-aligned base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM-as-classifier paradigm (Helbling et al., 2023; Inan et al., 2023) by identifying a domain shift problem: alignment trains models in a self-censorship format (refuse harmful content), yet classification-based safeguards ask the model to perform an untrained labeling task. PARDEN instead repurposes the model's trained self-censorship behavior by prompting it to repeat its own output, using BLEU score as a detection signal. It also circumvents the autoregressive trap identified by Zou et al. (2023) and Zhu et al. (2023) by re-processing the full output at once, and operates in the output space rather than the input space targeted by perplexity filters (Jain et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARDEN offers a practical, zero-finetuning, black-box-compatible defense that achieves an 11× FPR reduction at TPR=90% over the prior best classifier, making it immediately deployable alongside existing LLMs. A key limitation is its dependence on a safety-aligned base model (Mistral-7B, being unaligned, fails entirely), which underscores that defense methods cannot substitute for robust alignment. Open questions include whether alignment procedures can be modified to reduce domain shift more broadly, how PARDEN composes with input-space filters for 'misleading' attacks (harmful intent, benign-looking output), and what other operations on 'high-order LLMs' (compositions of autoregressive models) yield useful fixed-point characterizations.
