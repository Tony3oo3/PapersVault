## Problem

Predicting antibody humanness—a proxy for immunogenicity risk—is critical for therapeutic antibody development, yet existing methods (Hu-mAb, OASis, AbNatiV) rely almost exclusively on the Observed Antibody Space (OAS) database, which lacks functional characterization of sequences. The Patented Antibody Database (PAD) offers noisy but informative associations between sequences and their potential targets, yet this resource has not been leveraged for humanness prediction, leaving a gap in how functional context can improve sequence representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes unpaired antibody amino acid sequences (heavy, light, kappa, lambda, or VHH/nanobody chains) of variable length up to 180 residues, with no alignment or explicit chain-type/species conditioning. Each residue is encoded via a trainable amino-acid-type embedding, a positional embedding, and 18 pre-computed biophysical features (hydrophobicity, charge, volume, etc.), with Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) injected for robustness. The output is a binary humanness score (human vs. non-human) via a softmax head, plus a fixed-dimensional sequence embedding (128-dim) that can be used for downstream tasks or interpretability analysis via attention maps.

## Method

SelfPAD employs a two-stage, multi-loss training process on the PAD. In the pre-training stage, a 4-layer transformer encoder (dim 32, 16 heads) is trained with a SimCLR-style contrastive objective: at each iteration, k targets are sampled, two sequences per target form positive pairs, and the remaining sequences in the batch serve as negatives, using a normalized temperature-scaled cross-entropy loss. In the fine-tuning stage, the top four encoder layers are re-initialized, an MLP head (128→512→128→2 with residual connections, GELU, batch norm, dropout) is appended, and the model is trained with cross-entropy loss (label smoothing 0.5) for 25 epochs. Two noise mechanisms—shift noise (randomly offsetting sequence start) and swap noise (replacing embedding entries with values from the same column)—are applied during fine-tuning to improve robustness. The key distinction from prior work is using noisy patent-derived functional labels as weak supervision for contrastive representation learning, rather than relying on OAS-based self-supervised sequence completion.

## Results

On the PAD test set (29k sequences), FT-SelfPAD achieves 97.59 F1, 99.12 ROC AUC, and 98.54 PR AUC for humanness prediction, substantially outperforming AbNatiV (91.59 F1, 91.52 ROC AUC), OASis (88.67 F1, 88.30 ROC AUC), and Hu-mAb (70.35 F1, 74.83 ROC AUC). On the 25 humanization dataset, FT-SelfPAD achieves perfect scores (100 F1, 100 ROC AUC, 100 PR AUC) for both paired and single-chain evaluation. On the 553 therapeutics dataset (paired), FT-SelfPAD reaches 94.47 ROC AUC and 98.16 PR AUC, and on the 217 immunogenicity dataset (paired) it achieves 82.86 ROC AUC and 93.79 PR AUC, establishing state-of-the-art on five of six external inference tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FT-SelfPAD outperforms all three baselines on the PAD test set across every metric, with the largest gap over Hu-mAb (27.24 F1 points) and a 7.6-point ROC AUC advantage over AbNatiV. On the 553 therapeutics dataset (paired), FT-SelfPAD leads with 94.47 ROC AUC vs. 90.02 (AbNatiV), 89.48 (OASis), and 77.68 (Hu-mAb); Hu-mAb's ROC AUC drops sharply here due to its known weakness on light chains. On the 217 immunogenicity dataset (paired), FT-SelfPAD achieves the best ROC AUC (82.86) and PR AUC (93.79), though Hu-mAb edges it slightly on F1 (88.96 vs. 87.50) and Accuracy (82.95 vs. 80.65). On the 25 humanization dataset, FT-SelfPAD achieves perfect 100% scores on all metrics for both paired and single-chain settings, compared to 92.00 F1 (AbNatiV), 96.00 F1 (OASis), and 96.15 F1 (Hu-mAb) for paired sequences. The one task where FT-SelfPAD does not achieve the best score is the 217 immunogenicity single-chain setting, where AbNatiV leads on PR AUC (91.25 vs. 90.44), though FT-SelfPAD still wins on ROC AUC (78.94 vs. 77.86).

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfPAD departs from the dominant OAS-centric paradigm for antibody representation learning (OASis, Hu-mAb, AbNatiV) by exploiting the Patented Antibody Database (PAD), which provides noisy functional annotations (potential targets) absent from OAS. Methodologically, it adapts the SimCLR-style contrastive learning framework to a weakly-supervised multi-task setting where positive pairs are defined by shared patent targets, then transitions to supervised cross-entropy fine-tuning on the same data—a pre-training/fine-tuning lineage borrowed from NLP and vision. The incorporation of 18 biophysical amino-acid features, swap noise, and shift noise distinguishes it from prior antibody language models that rely purely on sequence-completion pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Humanness prediction is a practical bottleneck in therapeutic antibody development, where immunogenicity-driven attrition carries substantial cost; demonstrating that a smaller (~136k), noisier patent dataset with functional labels can outperform methods trained on billions of OAS sequences suggests that signal quality (functional grounding) can outweigh raw data volume. Key limitations include PAD's bias toward lab-optimized sequences, the noise in target annotations, and the small evaluation sets (especially the 25-pair humanization data where perfect scores are less informative). Open questions include whether incorporating OAS natural sequences would further improve generalization, whether the framework extends to other developability properties (aggregation, viscosity), and how to better curate patent data to reduce noise.
