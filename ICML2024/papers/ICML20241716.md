## Problem

While Garg et al. (2023) provided empirical evidence that input loss curvature (trace of the loss Hessian w.r.t. inputs) correlates with Feldman's stability-based memorization score at ~3 orders of magnitude lower compute cost, no theoretical framework explained this connection. This paper addresses that gap by deriving formal upper bounds linking memorization, input loss curvature, and differential privacy, completing a triangle of theoretical relationships among all three quantities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241716/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on a trained DNN, a training sample z_i = (x_i, y_i), and the loss function, producing closed-form upper bounds on memorization in terms of curvature and privacy parameters. For empirical curvature estimation, the method requires a trained model and computes the trace of the input Hessian via Hutchinson's estimator using n=10 Rademacher vectors and a finite-step perturbation h=1e-3, avoiding full Hessian computation. Experiments use pre-trained ResNet50/Small Inception models from Feldman & Zhang (2020) for memorization-curvature validation, and DP-SGD-trained ResNet18 models (via Opacus, 20 epochs, max gradient norm 1.0, δ=1e-5) for privacy-curvature validation on CIFAR-10/100 (32×32) and ImageNet.

## Method

The paper establishes three theorems forming a complete theoretical triangle. Theorem 5.1 bounds memorization by input loss curvature using Nesterov-Polyak cubic regularization on the loss, a ν-adjacency assumption (two samples within a ball B_p(ν)), and Lipschitz Hessian, yielding |mem| ≤ (1/L)·E[tr(∇²ℓ)] + c₁ where c₁ aggregates stability, generalization, and model bias terms. Theorem 5.3 bounds expected input loss curvature by the privacy parameter via an improved stability bound (Lemma 5.2) showing ε-DP implies L(1-e^{-ε})-stability, tightening the prior L(e^ε-1) bound from Wang et al. (2016). Theorem 5.4 directly bounds memorization by 1-e^{-ε} for any ε-DP algorithm. Empirically, the linear memorization-curvature relationship is validated by binning 1000 CIFAR-100 and 100 ImageNet models' scores, with improved fits when modeling the loss bound L as proportional to m_sub^{-0.5} per sub-population (Bousquet-Elisseeff rate).

## Results

The paper establishes three theoretical theorems linking memorization, input loss curvature, and differential privacy, validated empirically on CIFAR-100 (1000 Small Inception models), ImageNet (100 ResNet50 models), and CIFAR-10. The key quantitative finding is a strong linear relationship between memorization scores and input loss curvature (Theorem 5.1), with curvature providing ~3 orders of magnitude compute savings over Feldman's memorization score. The privacy-curvature link (Theorem 5.3) is validated by training DP-SGD ResNet18 models with ε ranging from 5 to 100, showing curvature decreases as privacy strengthens. The improved stability bound L(1−e^{−ε}) (Lemma 5.2) tightens the previous L(e^{ε}−1) bound from Wang et al. (2016).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares input loss curvature against three previously proposed memorization proxies: Model Confidence (Carlini et al., 2019), Adversarial Distance (Stock & Cisse, 2018), and Learning Time (Jiang et al., 2020). On CIFAR-100, all three proxies show mostly linear trends with memorization score, though with some nonlinearity at the extremes. On ImageNet, the comparison is more decisive: Adversarial Distance and Model Confidence become quite non-linear with respect to memorization, while Learning Time remains linear. Input loss curvature maintains a strong linear relationship across both datasets, and unlike the other proxies, it is the only one with a formal theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.1) bounding memorization. Additionally, the paper improves upon Wang et al. (2016) by tightening the privacy-to-stability bound from L(e^{ε}−1) to L(1−e^{−ε}), which is strictly smaller for all ε > 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the missing theoretical framework connecting three previously studied but separately analyzed quantities: Feldman's (2019) stability-based memorization score, Garg et al.'s (2023) empirical observation that input loss curvature (trace of the input Hessian) correlates with memorization, and differential privacy. While Garg et al. demonstrated ~3 orders of magnitude compute savings using curvature as a memorization proxy, they offered no theoretical justification. The paper also improves upon Wang et al. (2016)'s privacy-stability bound from L(e^ε−1) to the tighter L(1−e^{−ε}), and leverages the Nesterov-Polyak cubic Hessian bound to bridge curvature and memorization. Unlike influence-function approaches that require convexity and positive-definiteness assumptions, the framework here applies to non-convex DNN settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: input loss curvature becomes a theoretically grounded, ~1000× cheaper proxy for the expensive Feldman memorization score, enabling memorization analysis at scale on datasets like ImageNet. The unified triangle of links (memorization ↔ curvature ↔ privacy) gives practitioners a principled way to reason about how much a model memorizes and how privacy guarantees constrain that memorization. However, the theoretical bounds are loose in practice (empirical memorization scores sit well below the Theorem 5.4 upper bound), and the framework relies on assumptions (uniform stability, generalization, Lipschitz Hessian) that are reasonable but not always verifiable. Open questions include whether the linear curvature-memorization relationship holds for non-vision tasks, whether the bounds can be tightened, and whether the PSD Hessian assumption needed for practical trace estimation limits generality.
