## Problem

For directed densest subgraph discovery, prior semi-streaming algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: Bahmani et al. (2012) achieve O(n polylog n) memory but require O(log n) passes, while Esfandiari et al. (2015) make a single pass but need O(n^{3/2}) memory. This paper closes the gap by showing that a (2+ε)-approximate directed densest subgraph can be found in a single pass with O(n polylog n) memory on randomized streams, and extends the result to MPC with quadratic round-complexity improvements over prior work.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a directed graph as a single-pass edge stream (randomized for the theoretical guarantee, though experiments show robustness to non-randomized orderings) along with an approximation parameter ε and a ratio parameter c = |S*|/|T*| (which is handled by trying O(n) multiplicative guesses). It outputs a pair of vertex sets (S*, T*) maximizing the density |E(S,T)|/√(|S||T|) up to a (2+ε) factor. In the MPC setting, the same graph is partitioned across machines with per-machine memory of n^{1+δ} (super-linear) or n polylog n (near-linear), and the algorithm runs in O(1) or O(√log n) synchronous rounds respectively.

## Method

The core mechanism is a peeling algorithm (VSETS-UPDATE) that iteratively removes vertices whose degree to the opposite set falls below a (1+ε)·average threshold, combined with a novel single-pass sampling procedure (SET-SAMPLE) that maintains an approximately uniform edge sample between the current candidate sets S and T without re-reading the stream. SET-SAMPLE merges edges already stored in memory with a binomially-determined number of new edges drawn from the remaining stream, exploiting the stream's randomization to produce negatively correlated samples amenable to Chernoff-style concentration. For MPC, two key observations enable faster algorithms: (1) only (|S|+|T|)·ξ edges are needed rather than nξ, so once the candidate sets shrink, per-machine memory exceeds sample size; and (2) repeated peeling of one set (e.g., T in a star-like structure) requires no fresh sampling since S is unchanged, collapsing what would be O(log n) rounds into O(√log n) by batching same-side peels within a single round.

## Results

Algorithm 4 achieves a (2+ε)-approximate directed densest subgraph in a single pass with O(n·poly(log n)) memory, improving over the O(log n) passes required by Bahmani et al. (2012). Empirically, it is at least 2× faster than the baseline (reaching ~2.5× on LiveJournal and Twitter) while achieving densities within 3% of the baseline across all six datasets. On large graphs, the paper's algorithm actually attains higher density: 5.5% better on LiveJournal and 1.5% better on Twitter. In the MPC near-linear memory regime, the algorithm uses at least half the number of phases compared to Bahmani et al. (2012).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: the multi-pass streaming algorithm of Bahmani et al. (PVLDB 2012), which requires O(log n) passes and O(n·poly(log n)) memory. The proposed Algorithm 4 wins decisively on runtime, being at least 2× faster overall and ~2.5× faster on the two largest real-world graphs (LiveJournal with 4.8M nodes and Twitter with 41.6M nodes). On accuracy, the two methods are essentially tied: maximum densities are within 3% of each other for every dataset, and the paper's method actually achieves slightly higher density on large graphs (5.5% on LiveJournal, 1.5% on Twitter). Notably, these results hold even when the input stream is non-randomized (e.g., edges sorted by endpoint), despite the theoretical guarantee requiring a randomized stream. In the MPC setting, the paper's O(√log n)-round algorithm uses at least half the phases of Bahmani et al.'s O(log n)-round algorithm, with no visible correlation between phase count and graph size in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical peeling algorithm of Charikar (2003) for the (2+ε)-approximation of directed densest subgraphs, adapting it to sampling-based frameworks. It directly addresses a gap identified by prior work: Bahmani et al. (2012) achieved O(n poly log n) memory but required O(log n) passes, while Esfandiari et al. (2015) achieved single-pass but needed O(n^1.5) memory. The key technical contribution is the SET-SAMPLE subroutine, which enables degree estimation from a partially-seen stream without additional passes, and the use of a generalized Chernoff bound for negatively correlated variables to handle non-independent edge sampling. For MPC, the paper extends these ideas to achieve O(1) rounds in the super-linear memory regime and O(√log n) rounds in the near-linear regime, improving upon the O(log n) round complexity of Bahmani et al. (2012, 2014).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap between directed and undirected densest subgraph algorithms in MPC (reducing round complexity from O(log n) to O(1) or O(√log n)), which is practically important given that many real-world graphs (social networks, web links) are directed. Empirically, the algorithm is 2× faster than the state-of-the-art baseline while matching or slightly exceeding its accuracy, even on non-randomized streams where theoretical guarantees do not formally apply. Key open questions remain: whether a (1+ε)-approximation is achievable in o(log n) passes or in the sublinear-memory MPC regime, and whether the (2+ε) approximation can be tightened to (1+ε) in the near-linear MPC setting without increasing round complexity.
