## Problem

Existing unsupervised concept extraction methods (PCA, KMeans, NMF, dictionary learning) find concept vectors that are individually discriminative but fail to compose: adding the representation of 'white birds' to 'small birds' does not yield 'small white birds.' The paper identifies that compositionality is essential for explaining predictions, editing model behavior, and training on composed concepts, yet no prior method enforces the structural properties (orthogonality across attributes, non-orthogonality within attributes) that make concept representations compositional.

## Input / Output

CCE takes frozen embeddings from a pretrained model (CLIP ViT for image datasets, Llama-2 13B Chat for text datasets) and a user-specified number of attributes M and concepts per attribute K. It outputs a set of concept vectors grouped by attribute, where vectors within the same attribute are non-orthogonal (capturing correlated concepts like different colors) while vectors across different attributes are orthogonal (enabling clean composition). The method operates entirely in the embedding space without modifying the pretrained model, and the number of concepts is a tunable hyperparameter that significantly affects downstream performance.

## Method

CCE discovers compositional concepts by iteratively learning entire subspaces (one per attribute) rather than individual concept vectors. Within each subspace, it jointly optimizes a projection matrix P ∈ R^{d×S} and spherical K-Means centroids V: LearnSubspace performs gradient ascent on the differentiable Silhouette score of the projected data ZP given fixed cluster assignments, while LearnConcepts re-clusters ZP with spherical K-Means. A regularization term matches the projected centroids back to the original space to prevent overfitting. After converging on one attribute's subspace, CCE removes it from the data via orthogonal rejection (Z ← Z − ZPP^T), guaranteeing that subsequent attributes are orthogonal to prior ones by construction. This two-property enforcement—orthogonality across attributes and non-orthogonality within—matches the theoretical conditions proven necessary for compositionality in Theorem 3.1.

## Results

On the controlled CLEVR dataset, CCE achieves a compositionality score of 3.163, matching the ground-truth score of 3.162 (lower is better), while the best baseline (DictLearn) scores 3.367. On CUB-sub and Truth-sub, CCE also achieves the best compositionality scores (0.459 and 3.689 respectively), comparable to ground truth (0.462 and 3.743). For downstream classification in the full setting, CCE achieves the highest accuracy on CUB (76.49%), Truth (87.89%), and HAM10000 (80.05%), outperforming all unsupervised baselines. CCE also achieves a MAP of 1.000 on CLEVR for predicting concept compositions, matching ground truth exactly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCE outperforms all five baselines on compositionality scores across all three controlled datasets. PCA (RepE) achieves the highest individual concept discrimination (ROC-AUC up to 0.981 on CLEVR) but its compositionality score (3.684) is significantly worse than CCE (3.163), confirming that good individual concepts do not imply compositional ones. DictLearn produces highly accurate individual concept representations (cosine similarity 0.745 on CLEVR) yet still fails to compose correctly (compositionality score 3.367 vs. CCE's 3.163). ACE (KMeans) and SemiNMF show moderate compositionality but consistently underperform CCE by 0.2–0.5 in compositionality score. Concept Transformer (CT) performs near-random on compositionality (4.929 on CLEVR, matching the Random baseline at 4.925), indicating its learned representations are essentially non-compositional. On downstream classification, CCE achieves 76.49% on CUB, 87.89% on Truth, and 80.05% on HAM10000, beating the best baseline (SemiNMF at 75.81%, 87.36%, and 76.30% respectively) by 0.7–3.8 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised concept extraction literature (PCA/RepE, KMeans/ACE, Dictionary Learning, NMF/CRAFT, Concept Transformer) by identifying compositionality as a critical but previously unenforced property of extracted concepts. While prior methods optimize for individual concept discriminability or faithfulness, none simultaneously enforce the two properties the authors prove necessary for compositionality: orthogonality across attributes and correlation within attributes. CCE departs from the individual-concept paradigm by jointly learning entire subspaces via orthogonal rejection, drawing on subspace learning and spherical K-Means clustering rather than the linear algebra or factorization approaches used by baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Compositionality is a prerequisite for practical uses of concept-based interpretability—model editing, debugging via conceptual counterfactuals, and concept bottleneck models all assume that concepts can be combined linearly. The paper's demonstration that even high-quality individual concepts (e.g., NMF with 0.907 ROC-AUC on CLEVR) fail to compose reveals a gap that practitioners building on top of extracted concepts may not have recognized. Key limitations include the assumption of a flat concept hierarchy (no handling of '(small blue) car' vs 'small (blue car)'), no treatment of genuinely non-compositional concepts like 'hot dog,' and the need for manual concept naming. Open questions include whether the subspace approach scales to deeply hierarchical or relational concept structures and whether the orthogonality assumption holds in more complex, real-world embedding spaces.
