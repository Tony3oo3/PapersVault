## Problem

Existing tree-search methods for LLM reasoning (Tree-of-Thought, RAP) rely on prompting a pre-trained LLM as the value function, making them dependent on well-designed prompts and large, capable models, and limiting search depth to 7–10 steps. They also cannot benefit from in-domain training, so they fail in domains where the pre-trained LLM lacks sufficient knowledge or where long-horizon planning is required. The paper asks whether a learned value function combined with AlphaZero-style deep tree-search can make LLM decoding and training generally applicable, scalable to much greater depths, and iteratively improvable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (question, query, or state description) and operates with three learned components: a policy LLM (LLaMA2-7B or GPT-2-small), a value function (decoder-only transformer with an MLP scalar head per token position), and an outcome reward model. The action space is either sentence-level (each reasoning step is one node, for tasks like GSM8K, Game24, PrOntoQA, Chess Endgame) or token-level (each token is one node, for RLHF alignment), which determines tree depth (up to 64 for token-level) and width (5–50 child nodes per expansion). Output is a generated reasoning chain with a final answer, an aligned text response, or a sequence of chess moves, optionally aggregated from multiple tree-search trajectories via majority vote or ORM-weighted selection.

## Method

TS-LLM replaces prompt-based self-evaluation with a learned LLM-based value function trained via TD-λ or Monte-Carlo value targets on sampled rollouts, and an outcome reward model trained on final-step rewards. Five tree-search algorithms are implemented: BFS-V, DFS-V (value-pruned beam/depth search), classic MCTS (Monte-Carlo backup only at terminal nodes), MCTS-α (AlphaZero-style with value-function backup at every leaf, non-reversible step-by-step search), and MCTS-Rollout (repeated full-tree search from the root with value backup at intermediate nodes). The key architectural insight is treating tree-search as a policy improvement operator within a generalized policy iteration loop: (1) tree-search over the training set produces augmented trajectories, (2) the policy is improved by supervised distillation on positive trajectories, and (3) the value function and ORM are retrained on the augmented data. This iterative cycle simultaneously refines all three networks, and the framework scales from 125M to 7B parameter models and from depth-4 to depth-64 search trees.

## Results

On Path@1, MCTS-α (the AlphaZero-like variant) achieves 51.9% on GSM8k, 63.3% on Game24, 99.4% on PrOntoQA, 2.221 reward on RLHF, and 96.90% win rate on Chess Endgame, substantially outperforming CoT-greedy (41.4%, 12.7%, 48.8%, 0.318, 58.14%) across all five tasks. MCTS-Rollout achieves the best Game24 score (71.3%) and Chess Endgame score (98.76%). Under the fairer Equal-Token comparison, TS-LLM's advantage narrows on GSM8k (BFS-V at 52.5% vs CoT-SC_ORM at 52.3%) but remains dominant on Game24 (70.9% vs 50.6%), PrOntoQA (99.6% vs 83.2%), and Chess Endgame (94.26% vs 73.80%). The learned value function (LLaMA-V) also outperforms prompt-based GPT-3.5 evaluation on Game24 (64.8% vs 21.0% with the same LLaMA-SFT policy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToT (Yao et al., 2023), TS-LLM's learned value function (LLaMA-V) dominates the prompt-based GPT-3.5 value on Game24 (64.8% vs 15.5% with GPT-3.5 policy; 64.8% vs 21.0% with LLaMA-SFT policy) and GSM8k (52.5% vs 45.8% with LLaMA-SFT policy), demonstrating that a small learned value model is more reliable than prompting a much larger LLM for self-evaluation. Against CoT (Wei et al., 2022), all TS-LLM variants substantially outperform CoT-greedy on Game24 (63–71% vs 12.7%), PrOntoQA (94–99% vs 48.8%), RLHF (2.221 vs 0.318), and Chess Endgame (96.9–98.8% vs 58.1%), with a more modest gain on GSM8k (51.9–52.5% vs 41.4%). Against CoT-SC (Wang et al., 2022), the Equal-Token comparison reveals that CoT-SC_ORM is competitive on GSM8k (52.3% vs 52.5%) but TS-LLM clearly wins on Game24 (70.9% vs 50.6%), PrOntoQA (99.6% vs 83.2%), and Chess Endgame (94.26% vs 73.80%), confirming that tree-search with value back-propagation is essential for deep or wide search spaces. Against RFT (Yuan et al., 2023), one iteration of TS-LLM policy distillation (π_θ1 greedy at 47.9%) slightly exceeds RFT-50 (47.0%) and RFT-100 (47.5%) on GSM8k. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), TS-LLM's single-iteration MCTS-α with updated policy and value (2.67) marginally beats PPO (2.53) on RLHF, though PPO conducts multiple iterative updates and is noted as not fully comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AlphaZero framework (Silver et al., 2017) to LLM generation by replacing prompted self-evaluation (as in Tree-of-Thought and RAP) with a learned value function and outcome reward model, enabling tree search to scale from depth ~10 to depth 64. It departs from prior tree-search-for-LLMs work in two key ways: (1) the value function is trained rather than obtained via prompting, making the approach applicable to small models (125M–7B) without requiring GPT-4-class evaluators, and (2) tree search is repurposed as a policy improvement operator within an iterative training loop (policy distillation + value learning), not merely an inference-time decoding strategy. The MCTS-α and MCTS-Rollout variants are direct adaptations of AlphaZero's select-expand-evaluate-backup loop to language-space action nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a learned value function from a 7B model outperforms prompted GPT-3.5 as a tree-search guide, challenging the assumption that stronger LLMs are needed for reliable self-evaluation. However, the Equal-Token comparison reveals that on shallow tasks (GSM8K), simple CoT-SC with ORM aggregation nearly matches tree-search when computation is controlled, suggesting the tree-search advantage is most pronounced in deep-search settings (RLHF depth 64, chess endgame depth 50). Key open questions include: whether the iterative training loop converges over multiple updates (only one iteration is shown), how to reduce the 2–3× wall-time overhead of tree search, and whether aggregation benefits scale with more search paths. The framework's practical impact will depend on resolving these engineering and scalability challenges.
