## Problem

Adapting pre-trained LLMs to new domains requires expensive finetuning and suffers from catastrophic forgetting, while existing post-training pruning methods (SparseGPT, WANDA) cannot be applied sequentially across datasets without either freezing previously pruned weights (weight stasis) or degrading performance on earlier datasets (forgetting). No prior method enables training-free pruning under a continual model adaptation setting where the model must remain effective across all encountered datasets without retraining or storing past data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full pre-trained LLM weight matrix, a target sparsity ratio (e.g., 50% unstructured or 2:4/4:8 semi-structured), and a small calibration set of 16 segments × 2048 tokens drawn from each new dataset in the sequence. It outputs a pruned weight matrix with a binary mask applied element-wise (or in N:M blocks for semi-structured sparsity) to all linear layers (~99% of parameters), excluding the embedding and classification head. Crucially, no past datasets need to be stored; the accumulated importance scores in W*_k carry all necessary meta-information from prior datasets.

## Method

COPAL introduces a sensitivity-analysis-based importance metric that accumulates across datasets to enable continual, training-free pruning. For each new dataset, it computes finite-difference sensitivity terms S_W (output change under weight perturbation ΔW) and S_x (output change under input perturbation Δx), then derives a per-sample loss gradient ∇_{dW} L_j^i = 2·dy_j^i·(∂f/∂W). The key mechanism is the recursive importance update W*_k = Σ_j |W·∇_{dW} L_j^k| + W*_{k-1}, which combines the current dataset's sensitivity with the accumulated importance from all prior datasets, preventing both weight stasis (previously pruned weights can be re-evaluated) and forgetting (past-dataset sensitivity is preserved in the running sum). A threshold T_s is set from the sorted importance values at the target sparsity level, and a binary mask is applied to produce the pruned weights. The entire process is layer-wise and single-step, requiring no gradient backpropagation or weight updates—only forward passes with small perturbations on calibration data.

## Results

On unstructured 50% sparsity continual pruning across Wikitext-2, PTB, and C4, COPAL achieves average Backward Transfer (A-BWT) of 0.016 on LLaMA-7B, 0.029 on LLaMA-13B, 0.007 on LLaMA-30B, and 0.001 on LLaMA-65B, compared to 0.569–0.606 for the best baselines (Wanda/SparseGPT). Average Perplexity (A-PPL) ranges from 9.728 (7B) to 6.791 (65B), consistently matching or exceeding the best baseline. For semi-structured 2:4 pruning on LLaMA-65B, COPAL reduces A-BWT to 0.038 versus SparseGPT's 2.128 (98.2% improvement) while improving A-PPL to 9.161 from 9.333.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPAL is compared against three baselines: Magnitude pruning, SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023), and Wanda (Sun et al., 2023). Magnitude pruning completely fails in the continual setting, exhibiting Weight Stasis (WS) across all model sizes and sparsity configurations, rendering it non-competitive. Against SparseGPT, COPAL achieves a 97.2% reduction in A-BWT on LLaMA-7B (0.016 vs. 0.591) and a 99.7% reduction on LLaMA-65B (0.001 vs. 0.334), while also improving A-PPL by 4.4% (7B) and 6.0% (65B). Against Wanda, the best-performing baseline, COPAL reduces A-BWT by 97.2% on 7B (0.016 vs. 0.569) and 99.4% on 65B (0.001 vs. 0.172), with A-PPL improvements of 2.7% (7B) and 10.4% (65B). The advantage is consistent across all four model scales (7B, 13B, 30B, 65B) and all sparsity types (unstructured, 2:4, 4:8), with the BWT gap widening as model size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the calibration-guided post-training pruning paradigm (WANDA, SparseGPT) into a sequential, multi-dataset setting, introducing the concept of 'continual pruning' as distinct from both single-shot pruning and pruning-enabled continual learning (which requires retraining). The key departure is a sensitivity-based importance metric that accumulates across datasets, replacing the per-dataset calibration criterion used by WANDA and SparseGPT. The authors formally identify two failure modes—weight stasis and catastrophic forgetting—that arise when existing pruning methods are naively applied sequentially, and design their method to address both simultaneously without any gradient-based updates or replay of past data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COPAL offers a practical path to adapting large LLMs to new domains while maintaining efficiency, without the computational cost of fine-tuning or the storage burden of retaining past datasets. However, the evaluation is limited to perplexity on three text corpora (Wikitext-2, PTB, C4) and the LLaMA family only, leaving open whether the sensitivity metric generalizes to more diverse domain shifts, downstream task performance, or non-LLaMA architectures. An open question is how the accumulated importance metric behaves under very long dataset sequences or highly dissimilar domain transitions, and whether the approach extends to structured pruning at larger sparsity levels.
