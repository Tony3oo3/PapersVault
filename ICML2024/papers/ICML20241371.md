## Problem

Item-level locally differentially private (LDP) sparse linear regression suffers a minimax rate of O(s*·d/nmε²), making it infeasible when the feature dimension d is large. This paper asks whether user-level LDP (ULDP), where each user holds m samples, can eliminate the linear dependency on d beyond the trivial effective-sample-size gain, and provides an affirmative answer by establishing both a matching lower bound and an algorithm achieving O(s*²/nmε²).

## Input / Output

The method takes n users, each holding m i.i.d. sub-Gaussian samples (X_i, y_i) ∈ (R^d × R)^m, along with a privacy budget ε, and outputs a sparse coefficient vector β ∈ R^d with at most s* non-zero entries. The protocol is sequential-interactive: each user's private message can depend on all previously received messages, and the full procedure requires log(d) + 2 communication rounds with each user sending only 1 bit per round. The selected support set is guaranteed to contain the true non-zero coordinates with probability at least 1 − 4/n².

## Method

The framework operates in two phases. In the selection phase, the first n/2 users each run a local variable selector (Lasso or SCAD) on their m samples and report one selected variable; a private HeavyHitter protocol (based on Bassily et al., 2020, using randomized response over a binary prefix tree with Hadamard-transformed hash buckets) then identifies the s* most frequently reported variables, yielding a candidate set of size O(s*) that contains the true support. In the estimation phase, the remaining n/2 users fit local estimators on the reduced s*-dimensional subspace. The two-round protocol (2-SLR) uses a ULDPMean procedure inspired by Girgis et al. (2022): a first group of users defines a histogram bin around the expected estimator values, and a second group projects their local estimates onto this bin before adding Laplace noise, reducing sensitivity and achieving the O(s*²/nmε²) error bound. A multi-round variant (M-SLR) instead solves a private accelerated mini-batch gradient descent (ULDP SCO) on the selected subspace, borrowing the methodology of Bassily & Sun (2023) but improving the analysis to exploit strong convexity of the squared loss.

## Results

On six real datasets, the proposed M-SLR (multi-round ULDP) achieves the best overall rank sum of 19 (vs. 24 for 2-SLR, 31 for Lasso, 50 for LDPIHT, and 56 for LDPPROX) at both ε=1 and ε=4, measured by MSE ratio relative to non-private Lasso. On the Taxi dataset, M-SLR achieves an MSE ratio of 0.01 (i.e., 100× better than non-private Lasso), while LDPPROX and LDPIHT score 1.20 and 1.02 respectively at ε=1. In synthetic experiments, 2-SLR achieves lower ℓ₂ error than Lasso for all tested (n, m, ε) settings when n ≥ 400, and its error is independent of dimension d when m is sufficiently large, confirming the theoretical rate O(s*² log³n / nmε²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-SLR dominates both LDP baselines (LDPPROX and LDPIHT) across nearly all datasets and privacy levels, with particularly large margins on Taxi (MSE ratio 0.01 vs. 1.02–1.20) and Loan (0.97 vs. 2.00–5.27 at ε=1). Against the non-private Lasso baseline fitted on each user's local samples, M-SLR wins on 4 of 6 datasets at both ε=1 and ε=4 (Airline, Loan, MIP, Taxi), while Lasso retains an edge on Wine (1.14 vs. 1.37) and Yolanda (1.19 vs. 1.22). The two-round 2-SLR is slightly behind M-SLR on real data but wins on Yolanda at ε=4 (1.11 vs. 1.18), and in synthetic experiments 2-SLR consistently outperforms M-SLR, especially at smaller n. Both LDP baselines (LDPPROX, LDPIHT) are uniformly the worst performers, confirming the paper's theoretical claim that item-level LDP sparse regression suffers a linear d-dependency that ULDP eliminates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LDP sparse regression literature (Wang & Xu 2019; Zhu et al. 2023), which established that item-level LDP sparse regression suffers a minimax rate linear in dimension d, to the user-level LDP (ULDP) setting where each user holds m samples. The algorithmic framework composes three existing building blocks: the heavy-hitter protocol of Bassily et al. (2020) for private candidate variable selection, the ULDP stochastic convex optimization of Bassily & Sun (2023) for a multi-round estimation protocol, and the two-stage private mean estimation of Girgis et al. (2022) for a more efficient two-round protocol. The key conceptual departure is showing that the selection-estimation strategy, well-known in non-private high-dimensional statistics (Wang et al. 2011; Liang et al. 2023), becomes viable under ULDP precisely because local samples enable each user to perform meaningful variable selection before privatization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that ULDP sparse regression achieves O(s*²/nmε²) rather than the LDP rate O(ds*/nmε²)—removes a fundamental barrier that made private sparse estimation infeasible for high-dimensional data, with direct implications for data collection design (prioritizing users who can provide multiple samples). The framework generalizes to arbitrary sparse estimation problems via reduction to non-private learners (Theorem 3.7), making it a reusable template. Open questions include establishing a tight minimax lower bound for ULDP, handling the regime where m is small (m ≤ s* log d), and determining whether the advantage persists under non-interactive ULDP, since most existing ULDP methods require sequential interactivity.
