## Problem

Schrödinger Bridge (SB) diffusion models offer optimal transport guarantees but require estimating intractable forward score functions, forcing costly implicit training via simulated trajectories and dependence on warm-up checkpoints. This sacrifices the simulation-free property that makes standard score-based generative models (SGMs) scalable. The paper asks whether one can retain efficient transport plans while restoring simulation-free training for the backward score.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240417/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes standard Gaussian noise and produces 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), with the forward process parameterized by a learnable diagonal matrix Λ that controls per-dimension diffusion rates. For time series forecasting, an LSTM encodes the multivariate history into a conditioning vector h_n, and a U-Net-based score network generates probabilistic forecasts of the next P steps. The forward process is a multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with closed-form mean and covariance, so sampling x_t given x_0 requires only a matrix exponential (or elementwise exponentials in the diagonal case) plus a Cholesky factor.

## Method

VSDM applies variational inference to linearize the intractable forward score ∇log ψ_t(x) ≈ A_t x, turning the forward SDE into a multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with a positive-definite matrix D_t = I − 2A_t. This closed-form forward process restores simulation-free explicit score matching (ESM) for training the backward score network, eliminating the costly implicit loss of standard SB. The variational score A_t is then adaptively optimized via stochastic approximation: at each stage, backward trajectories are simulated with the current score, and A_t is updated by a gradient step on a simplified loss. Convergence of A_t to a local equilibrium is proven using SA theory under local strong convexity, and the variational gap relative to the true SB solution is bounded via the Girsanov theorem. In practice, D_t is restricted to a diagonal time-invariant form for computational efficiency, and EMA is used to stabilize the adaptive updates.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (unconditional, 32×32), VSDM achieves an FID of 2.28, outperforming all other optimal-transport-based baselines (SB-FBSDE: 3.01, DOT: 15.78, DGFlow: 9.63) and most score-based models (SDE: 2.92, ScoreFlow: 5.7, VDM: 4.00), though trailing LSGM (2.10) and EDM (1.97). On time series forecasting (CRPS-SUM, lower is better), VSDM achieves 0.038 (Electricity), 0.008 (Exchange Rate), and 0.395 (Solar), beating DDPM on Exchange Rate and Solar and SGM on all three datasets. The convergence study shows VSDM reaches FID 2.28 from scratch (no warm-up) after ~200K images, with FID dropping from 406 at 0K to 3.01 at 200K images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among optimal-transport-based methods, VSDM (FID 2.28) clearly outperforms SB-FBSDE (3.01), DGFlow (9.63), and DOT (15.78) on CIFAR-10, demonstrating that the variational linearization preserves transport efficiency while restoring simulation-free training. Against standard score-based generative models, VSDM beats SDE (2.92), ScoreFlow (5.70), and VDM (4.00) but is slightly behind LSGM (2.10) and EDM (1.97), which benefit from more aggressive architectural choices. On time series forecasting, VSDM achieves the best CRPS-SUM on Exchange Rate (0.008 vs. 0.012 for both DDPM and SGM) and Solar (0.395 vs. 0.506 for DDPM and 0.413 for SGM), while DDPM retains a slight edge on Electricity (0.026 vs. 0.038). In the Gaussian Schrödinger Bridge comparison (Figure 1), VSDM trajectories closely align with the closed-form GSB solution, with deviations attributed to score estimation and discretization. On synthetic anisotropic data (spiral-8Y), VSDM-10 accurately reconstructs boundary regions where SGM-10 fails, and achieves ~40% improvement in trajectory straightness over SGM-10 without requiring the excessive β_max values (20–30) that SGMs need.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSDM sits at the intersection of two established lines: Schrödinger Bridge (SB) diffusion models (Chen et al., 2022b), which provide entropic optimal transport guarantees but require costly simulation-based training of intractable forward scores, and score-based generative models (SGM, Song et al., 2021b), which are simulation-free but lack transport optimality. The key departure is applying variational inference to linearize the forward score into a multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, recovering closed-form forward transitions and simulation-free backward score training while retaining a sub-optimal transport structure. This positions VSDM between the two extremes: it inherits the ESM training paradigm of SGM but augments it with an adaptively optimized linear drift (the variational score) that encodes transport information, drawing on stochastic approximation theory for convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that VSDM removes the warm-up initialization dependency that has plagued SB-based methods in large-scale training, making efficient-transport diffusion models more accessible for from-scratch training on high-dimensional data. The theoretical contribution—proving convergence of the variational score via stochastic approximation and bounding the variational gap via Girsanov's theorem—provides a principled framework for understanding how much transport optimality is sacrificed by the linear approximation. Open questions include whether the sub-optimal transport gap can be further reduced (e.g., via higher-order or nonlinear variational families), whether the Gaussian prior restriction can be lifted, and whether the 'ADAM-like' acceleration via critically damped dynamics and Hessian approximations mentioned as future work can close the remaining gap to full SB optimality.
