## Problem

Standard Axial 2D RoPE decomposes 2D spatial positions into horizontal and vertical components, concentrating all frequency support on the coordinate axes and leaving diagonal or oblique spatial relationships only indirectly encoded. This directional bias is a meaningful limitation for vision transformers, where natural images contain rich spatial structures in all orientations, and it manifests as axis-aligned artifacts in Fourier-domain reconstruction and diffuse, axis-biased attention patterns.

## Input / Output

Spiral RoPE operates on patchified RGB images at resolutions ranging from 224×224 (classification) to 512×512 (segmentation) and 256×256 (generation), where each patch's 2D grid position (p_x, p_y) is projected onto K uniformly spaced directions to compute rotation angles. The method introduces no additional learnable parameters or computational overhead relative to Axial 2D RoPE, and is combined with the original absolute positional embedding (APE) following prior practice. Outputs are standard task-specific: class logits for ImageNet-1k, per-pixel class maps for ADE20k segmentation, and 256×256 images for DiT-based generation.

## Method

Spiral RoPE partitions the d-dimensional embedding into K equal groups (K=16 for classification/segmentation, K=8 for generation), each associated with a direction φ_k = kπ/K uniformly distributed over [0, π). Each group is rotated by the scalar projection of the patch position onto its direction, t_k(p) = p_x·cos(φ_k) + p_y·sin(φ_k), enabling explicit encoding of positional relationships along oblique orientations. A grouped interleaved frequency assignment strategy distributes the same d/4 distinct base frequencies as Axial 2D RoPE across all K directions: adjacent frequency pairs are assigned round-robin to perpendicular direction pairs (k, k+K/2), so each direction receives a mix of low and high frequencies while preserving multi-scale encoding capacity. The resulting frequency pattern forms a spiral in the 2D frequency plane, and the relative-position property is preserved since t_k(p) − t_k(p′) = t_k(p − p′). A base frequency scaling factor of 1.5 (θ_base = 15000) is used to compensate for the reduced per-direction frequency count.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification, Spiral RoPE achieves 83.97% top-1 accuracy with DeiT-Large at 224×224, outperforming Axial 2D RoPE (83.61%) and RoPE-Mixed (83.51%). On ADE20k semantic segmentation, it reaches 49.12% mIoU with DeiT-Large, a +1.22% gain over Axial 2D RoPE. For class-conditional image generation with DiT-XL/2, Spiral RoPE achieves FID 15.55 (400k steps) and FID 1.74 (7M steps), surpassing both the original DiT (FID 2.27) and SiT (FID 2.06) in the extended training setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spiral RoPE consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task and model size. Against APE (ViT), the gains are largest: +0.73% top-1 for DeiT-L on ImageNet-1k and +2.21% mIoU on ADE20k, confirming that relative positional encoding substantially improves over absolute embeddings. Against Axial 2D RoPE (Su et al.), Spiral RoPE wins by +0.36% top-1 (DeiT-B) to +0.36% (DeiT-L) on classification, +0.53% to +1.22% mIoU on segmentation, and 0.7–1.1 FID points on generation. Against RoPE-Mixed (Heo et al.), which uses learnable per-head frequencies, Spiral RoPE achieves a clear margin of +0.44% top-1 (DeiT-L, 224) and +0.57% mIoU (DeiT-L), suggesting that pre-specified uniform directions generalize better than learned frequencies. In the extended 7M-step DiT-XL/2 generation setting, Spiral RoPE (FID 1.74) beats both the original DiT (FID 2.27) and SiT (FID 2.06), a gap of 0.32–0.53 FID points that is substantial in the sub-2 FID regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Axial 2D RoPE (which partitions embedding dimensions into x- and y-axis halves) by distributing positional encoding across K uniformly spaced directions in the 2D plane. The closest prior work is RoPE-Mixed (Heo et al., 2024), which makes per-head frequencies learnable to implicitly capture diagonal information, whereas Spiral RoPE uses pre-specified geometric directions with a grouped interleaved frequency assignment that preserves the same total frequency count as the axial baseline. The method inherits the relative-position property of 1D RoPE (Su et al., 2024) and the 2D decomposition strategy used in SAM 2 and Qwen-Image, but departs from the axis-aligned assumption that underlies all prior 2D RoPE formulations for vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The directional-bias diagnosis is clean and the fix is parameter-free, making Spiral RoPE a drop-in replacement that could be adopted broadly in vision transformer pipelines without architectural changes. The consistent (if modest) gains across classification, segmentation, and generation, combined with notably better resolution extrapolation and rotation robustness, suggest that multi-directional positional encoding is a meaningful inductive bias for 2D spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether the optimal K and frequency scaling transfer across architectures and task types, whether the approach extends naturally to 3D (e.g., video or point clouds), and whether the gains persist at much larger model scales where positional encoding may become less bottlenecking.
