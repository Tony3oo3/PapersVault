## Problem

Spectral GNNs are universal for approximating node signals but their graph-discrimination power is bounded by the 1-WL test, mirroring the expressivity ceiling of standard message-passing GNNs. The paper asks: what is the spectral counterpart of lifting message passing to the node-pair domain that would let spectral GNNs systematically surpass 1-WL while retaining the efficiency and universality of spectral filtering?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264595/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes node features X ∈ R^{n×d₁}, optional node-pair features E ∈ R^{n×n×d₂}, and the graph Laplacian L. Node and node-pair features are first lifted into a joint node-pair representation H ∈ R^{n²×d} via an encoder φ, which is then filtered by a bivariate spectral operator. Outputs include node-level classification scores (primary task), node-pair signal predictions, and graph/node/edge-level homomorphism or cycle counts for expressivity benchmarks. The key structural difference from standard spectral GNNs is that the filter acts on n²-dimensional node-pair signals with a bivariate response g(λᵢ, λⱼ) over eigenvalue pairs, rather than on n-dimensional node signals with a univariate response g(λᵢ).

## Method

FSPECGNN generalizes classical spectral convolution by (1) lifting signals from the node domain V to the node-pair domain V×V, and (2) replacing the univariate filter g(λᵢ) with a bivariate filter g(λᵢ, λⱼ), yielding the operator (U⊗U) diag(vec(G_λ)) (U⊗U)ᵀ where G_λ = (g(λᵢ,λⱼ)). Classical spectral GNNs are recovered as the diagonal restriction g(λᵢ,λᵢ) (Proposition 3.3). For scalability, the bivariate polynomial filter is decomposed via a low-rank tensor factorization into S pairs of univariate polynomial filters, reducing the full-spectrum convolution to the sandwich form h(L)·ε·f(L)·H·W (Eq. 5), where ε = I + α·GAT provides feature-dependent off-diagonal mixing and f, h are standard polynomial spectral filters (ChebConv, ChebIIConv, or BernConv). This avoids explicit n²×n² matrix construction, achieving O(Kmd) complexity comparable to classical spectral layers. Theoretically, FSPECGNN is upper-bounded by Local 2-GNN expressivity (Theorem 3.7) and can match it under simple-spectrum conditions (Theorem 3.8); for heterophilic learning, the paper proves that the optimal class-separability convolution necessarily requires off-diagonal spectral components that classical diagonal spectral filters cannot realize (Theorems 4.1–4.2).

## Results

On heterophilic node classification, the best FSPECGNN variant consistently outperforms all five spectral baselines across all eight datasets. Headline gains include 39.57% accuracy on Squirrel (vs. 25.94% for the best baseline BernNet), 77.11% on Questions (vs. 65.41% for BernNet), and 88.30% ROC-AUC on Minesweeper (vs. 87.40% for JacobiConv). On substructure-counting benchmarks, FSPECGNN achieves MAE comparable to Local 2-GNN (e.g., 0.005 vs. 0.008 on triangle graph-level cycle counting) while running ~5× faster with lower peak GPU memory (9,532 MB vs. 11,532 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSPECGNN outperforms all five spectral baselines (ChebNet, ChebNetII, GPRGNN, JacobiConv, BernNet) on every one of the eight heterophilic datasets, with the largest margins on Squirrel (+13.6 pp over BernNet) and Questions (+11.7 pp over BernNet). Each FSPECGNN variant also beats its corresponding base filter (e.g., FSPECGNN(Cheb) > ChebNet, FSPECGNN(Bern) > BernNet), confirming that the off-diagonal node-pair interaction adds value beyond the univariate spectral filter. On substructure-counting tasks, FSPECGNN is broadly competitive with Local 2-GNN and Local 2-FGNN, matching or exceeding them on several templates (e.g., 0.005 vs. 0.008 MAE on triangle graph-level cycle counting) while being substantially faster (1.11 s/it vs. 7.57 s/it) and using less peak GPU memory (9,532 MB vs. 11,532 MB). Compared to Spectral Invariant GNN, FSPECGNN achieves lower MAE on both homomorphism and cycle counting benchmarks. The ablation (Table 4) shows that removing the off-diagonal interaction degrades accuracy by 0.6–5.0 pp, and removing both the in-filter and off-diagonal component reduces the model to ChebNet-level performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSPECGNNs extend classical spectral GNNs (Wang & Zhang, 2022; Defferrard et al., 2016; He et al., 2021, 2022) from univariate Laplacian filters g(λᵢ) on node signals to bivariate filters g(λᵢ, λⱼ) on node-pair signals, via the Kronecker-product structure of the Cartesian product graph G□G. This is the spectral counterpart of the spatial 'lifting' to V×V that underlies 2-WL and Local 2-GNN (Morris et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2024), but operates entirely in the eigenbasis rather than through neighborhood aggregation. The paper departs from Specformer (Bo et al., 2023), which remains diagonal in the eigenbasis, and from CITRUS (Einizade et al., 2024) and STSGNN (Chen et al., 2025), which use product graphs or multiple Laplacians for different purposes, by studying a single graph's Laplacian applied to both coordinates of a node-pair signal and analyzing the resulting expressivity and scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that heterophily is inherently a second-order spectral phenomenon: Theorem 4.2 proves that no classical (diagonal) spectral filter can realize the optimal class-separating convolution, while FSPECGNNs can. This provides a principled, spectral explanation for why GCN-style models fail on heterophilic graphs and why off-diagonal eigenspace coupling is necessary, not incidental. Practically, the low-rank tensor decomposition (Proposition 3.9) yields implementations with O(Kmd) complexity matching classical spectral GNNs, achieving ~5× lower runtime than spatial second-order baselines like Local 2-GNN while matching their expressivity. Open questions include extending the universal approximation to multi-channel node-pair signals, exploring richer initial convolutions beyond ε = I + α·GAT, and developing the framework for higher-order (k-tuple) domains and non-graph combinatorial structures.
