## Problem

Large-scale language model training is constrained by a fixed token budget T rather than a fixed number of optimization steps, yet no explicit optimization error laws exist as functions of batch size B, sequence length S, and token budget T for projection-free (conditional gradient) methods. While empirical studies identify critical batch sizes and μP-style frameworks ensure local per-step stability, neither provides a principled, token-budget-aware characterization of when increasing batch size becomes provably detrimental or how stepsize should co-vary with the effective batch-sequence scale.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a model architecture with a norm-constrained parameter space (e.g., per-layer spectral or infinity-norm balls), a token stream from FineWeb, and the triple (B, S, T) as its operating configuration. The key structural constraint is that the number of parameter updates is K = T/(BS), so the token budget directly couples batch size, sequence length, and the number of optimization steps. The output is a non-monotone optimization error bound ε(B,S,T) with three identifiable regimes, from which the paper extracts explicit scaling rules: BS ∝ T^{2/3}, β ∝ T^{-1/3}, and α = constant, along with a two-stage restart protocol for delayed-data training.

## Method

The paper establishes in-expectation convergence guarantees for a momentum-based stochastic conditional gradient algorithm (Scion) under a μ-Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz error bound in a general normed geometry, where the conditional gradient step is computed via a linear minimization oracle (LMO) over a norm ball. The analysis explicitly tracks the interplay between the momentum parameter α, the Frank-Wolfe stepsize β, the gradient Lipschitz constant L, the norm-equivalence constant ρ, and the stochastic noise level σ² = σ★²/(BS). Translating the iteration complexity into token complexity via T = KBS yields a three-regime error landscape: a noise-dominated regime where larger BS helps, a batch-independent plateau where error scales as T^{-1/3}, and an iteration-starved regime where error grows linearly in BS. Balancing the dominant terms at the crossover between the second and third regimes produces the BST scaling rule BS ∝ T^{2/3}, which in turn fixes β ∝ 1/K and shows that the momentum parameter α is invariant under scaling. The paper validates these predictions empirically on NanoGPT (124M and 1B) trained on FineWeb, demonstrating that the BST rule predicts optimal stepsizes across token budgets and model sizes, and that a two-stage restart strategy (small BS early, large BS later) outperforms both fixed-μP and fixed-large-batch baselines.

## Results

Under a fixed token budget of 1.3B (TPP 10.8), the 124M NanoGPT model achieves a best validation loss of 3.3706±0.0019 at B=256, S=1024, β=2.4×10⁻⁴, with performance nearly flat across B∈{256, 512} (consistent with the predicted intermediate regime). The BST scaling rule (BS ∝ T^{2/3}, β ∝ T^{-1/3}) accurately predicts the optimal Frank-Wolfe stepsize: for the 124M model, predicted β values of 3.0×10⁻⁴, 2.4×10⁻⁴, and 2.1×10⁻⁴ at T=2.7B, 5.3B, and 8.0B tokens respectively all match the empirically optimal β within one grid step. For the 1B model at T=10.8B, the BST-predicted β≈1.95×10⁻⁴ and BS≈1024×1024 also align with the best-performing configuration, while the μP baseline (fixed B=256, β=3.6×10⁻⁴) degrades by ~0.01–0.03 in validation loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BST-scaled Scion against three baseline families on a 1B model (T=10.8B): (1) μP-fixed Scion (B=256, S=1024, β=3.6×10⁻⁴ throughout), (2) fixed large-batch Scion trained from scratch (B=512/S=2048 or B=1024/S=1024, with either μP or BST stepsize), and (3) BST-restarted Scion (small B for first 1.3B tokens, then increased BS with rescaled β). The BST-restarted strategy achieves the best validation loss, with the B=1024/S=1024 restart outperforming the B=512/S=2048 restart by ~0.01. The μP-fixed baseline (which keeps all hyperparameters unchanged) performs worst, confirming that local per-step stability alone is insufficient for long-horizon token-budget optimization. Among fixed large-batch baselines, the BST-rescaled stepsize (β₁=1.8×10⁻⁴) consistently outperforms the μP stepsize (β₀=3.6×10⁻⁴) by 0.005–0.01 in validation loss. Further increasing BS beyond the BST prediction (to B=2048 or 4096) degrades performance by 0.005–0.04, validating the iteration-starved regime predicted by Corollary 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of stochastic conditional gradient (SCG) methods from the star-convexity setting of Kovalev (2025) to the more general μ-KL condition, and builds on the norm-constrained LMO framework of Pethick et al. (2025a) that underlies modern optimizers like Scion and Muon. It positions itself explicitly against the μP hyperparameter-transfer framework (Yang et al.), arguing that μP ensures only local per-step stability while leaving global token-budget-dependent scaling of (B, S, β) unaddressed. The paper also connects to the critical-batch-size literature (McCandlish et al., Zhang et al., Bergsma et al.) by providing an explicit optimization-error mechanism for the empirically observed 'too-large batch hurts' regime, and to the PL/KL condition literature (Karimi et al., Bolte et al.) by extending the μ-KL assumption beyond the Euclidean norm to the LMO-induced dual norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BST scaling rule (BS ~ T^{2/3}, β ~ T^{-1/3}) gives practitioners a concrete, theory-derived recipe for jointly adapting batch size, sequence length, and stepsize as the token budget grows—something that prior empirical studies identified as necessary but could not formalize. The finding that μP-style fixed-hyperparameter transfer becomes provably suboptimal at larger token budgets challenges a widely used practical assumption and motivates stage-wise or restart-based training schedules. Open questions include whether the μ-KL condition and the fitted power laws for L, μ, ρ generalize beyond the NanoGPT/FineWeb setting, whether the analysis extends to relaxed smoothness assumptions, and whether the BST exponents hold for architectures or optimizers outside the SCG/Muon family.
