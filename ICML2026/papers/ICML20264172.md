## Problem

RL with verifiable rewards (e.g., GRPO) suffers from sparse terminal supervision: when all sampled responses in a group are incorrect, the group-normalized advantage collapses to zero, eliminating the learning signal entirely. Existing textual-feedback approaches densify supervision but treat the critic as fixed or auxiliary, producing feedback that may be fluent or locally correct yet fails to improve the actor's downstream policy. The paper identifies that feedback quality should be judged by its effect on the actor after adaptation, not by standalone correctness, making learnable feedback an inherently bilevel problem.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text problem (math, code, or science question) as input. The actor LLM first generates an initial response y₀; the critic LLM then produces a short natural-language feedback instruction z conditioned on (x, y₀); the actor conditions on (x, y₀, z) to produce a refined response y₁, which is scored by a binary verifiable reward R(x, y₁) ∈ {0,1}. The feedback is constrained to be a brief corrective instruction (not a full solution), and the entire pipeline operates in a fixed 2-turn setup during training, though inference can extend to additional refinement turns.

## Method

Bi-NAC formulates the actor-critic interaction as a Stackelberg bilevel program: the lower-level actor maximizes expected task reward conditioned on the critic's feedback, while the upper-level critic is optimized through the actor's learning dynamics to generate feedback that maximizes the actor's final reward after adaptation. Following the BOME Lagrangian reformulation, the paper derives closed-form policy-gradient updates for both levels: the actor gradient is a standard REINFORCE-style update on both y₀ and y₁ scaled by (1+λ), while the critic gradient contains two terms — a positive term from current-actor rollouts and a negative regularization term from 'optimal-actor' rollouts (approximated via a recent checkpoint θᵏ). This bilevel coupling ensures the critic learns to produce task-specific, actionable feedback rather than generic or merely plausible critiques. The framework is instantiated with LoRA fine-tuning (rank 16) on LLaMA-3.2 and Qwen-2/3 backbones, and a single-model variant is shown to match the two-model version's performance.

## Results

Bi-NAC consistently outperforms all baselines across MATH-500, MBPP, and GPQA at 1B, 3B, and 8B scales. At the 1B scale, Bi-NAC achieves 46.6% on MATH-500, 66.4% on MBPP, and 40.6% on GPQA, surpassing the strongest baseline SCoRe (39.8%, 62.3%, 34.4%) by 6.8, 4.1, and 6.2 points respectively. In terms of parameter efficiency, the 2B Bi-NAC model (1B actor + 1B critic) outperforms the 3B GRPO baseline by 5.1 points on MATH-500 (46.56% vs 41.43%), and the 6B Bi-NAC surpasses the 7B GRPO by 3.0 points on MATH-500 (51.40% vs 48.40%) and 5.7 points on GPQA (49.30% vs 43.60%). On the extremely difficult AIME 2024 benchmark, Bi-NAC doubles the base model's accuracy from 6.7% to 13.3% after a single critic-guided refinement turn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-NAC dominates all five baselines across every benchmark and model scale. Against Behavioral Cloning (BC), the largest gap appears on GPQA at 8B (56.3% vs 39.0%, +17.3 points), confirming that RL with learnable feedback substantially exceeds supervised fine-tuning. Against ArCHer (RL-trained actor only, no RL-trained critic), Bi-NAC gains 16.0 points on MATH-500 at 1B (46.6% vs 30.6%), showing that training the critic is critical beyond merely training the actor. Against SCoRe (RL-trained actor with fixed, task-agnostic feedback), Bi-NAC improves by 6.8 points on MATH-500 at 1B (46.6% vs 39.8%) and by 11.7 points on GPQA at 8B (56.3% vs 44.6%), demonstrating that learnable, actor-aligned feedback outperforms static guidance. Against GRPO (the standard group-based RL baseline), Bi-NAC achieves superior parameter efficiency: the 2B Bi-NAC model beats the 3B GRPO model by 5.1 points on MATH-500 and 5.2 points on MBPP, while the 6B Bi-NAC beats the 7B GRPO by 3.0 points on MATH-500 and 5.7 points on GPQA. Against Hier-NFT (zero-shot, no fine-tuning at either level), Bi-NAC gains 20.8 points on MATH-500 at 1B (46.6% vs 25.8%), underscoring the value of reward-based fine-tuning at both hierarchical levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL with verifiable rewards for LLM reasoning (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1), which suffers from sparse terminal supervision and advantage collapse; (2) textual feedback and critique-guided refinement (SCoRe, ArCHer, Critique-GRPO, Reflexion, Self-Refine), which densifies supervision but typically treats the critic as fixed or trained with objectives decoupled from the actor's downstream performance; and (3) bilevel optimization and meta-learning (Finn et al., Franceschi et al., Stadie et al.), which provides the mathematical machinery for coupled optimization. The key departure from prior textual-feedback work is the explicit Stackelberg bilevel formulation: the critic is not trained to produce plausible or locally correct feedback, but is optimized *through* the actor's learning dynamics so that feedback is rewarded only insofar as it improves the actor's verified task reward after adaptation. This reframes the design question from 'how to produce correct feedback' to 'how to produce feedback the actor can learn from.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—actor-critic misalignment in textual feedback RL—that prior work has noted anecdotally but not resolved with a principled training objective. The parameter-efficiency results (2B Bi-NAC > 3B GRPO, 6B > 7B GRPO across MATH, MBPP, GPQA) suggest that aligning the feedback signal can substitute for raw model scale, which is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, several open questions remain: the bilevel gradient is approximated via a recent checkpoint for θ*, the single-model variant performs comparably to the two-model setup (raising questions about whether architectural separation is truly necessary), experiments are limited to ≤8B models, and the framework has not been tested on multimodal or agentic reasoning. Whether the bilevel structure provides genuine advantages over simpler joint-training objectives at larger scales is an important open question.
