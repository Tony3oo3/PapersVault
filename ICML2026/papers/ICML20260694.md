## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving approaches for LLM agents rely on external, inference-time interventions (prompt engineering or agent-based gatekeepers) that are brittle, scenario-specific, and paradoxically expand the privacy attack surface by exposing sensitive information in intermediate reasoning traces. No prior work has internalized contextual privacy preservation directly into the model's generation policy to enable privacy-compliant agentic actions, leaving the learning-to-act-in-a-contextually-private-manner problem unexplored.

## Input / Output

The system receives a context c, a user instruction u, a set of sensitive information items S defining contextual privacy constraints, and a tool-use trajectory (scratchpad of prior actions and observations). It produces a privacy-compliant tool action (e.g., a structured JSON message to send via MessengerSendMessage or GmailSendEmail) that fulfills the instruction while avoiding disclosure of any item in S under the given context. The multi-agent pipeline operates in a tree-structured search where each agent branches into B candidate responses, and the final leaf actions are scored by an LLM-as-a-judge for both leakage and helpfulness.

## Method

PrivAct internalizes contextual privacy through a two-part multi-agent preference training framework. First, a tree-structured generator-verifier-refiner pipeline explores the response space, and a reward propagation mechanism assigns values to intermediate responses by averaging descendant leaf rewards, enabling per-agent preference pair construction via threshold-based partitioning. Second, a Leakage-Conditioned Asymmetric Reward Shaping (LC-ARS) function creates two separated optimization regimes: a penalized regime (L>0) where both leakage severity L^α and helpfulness H^β are compounded as penalties, and a positive-reward regime (L=0) where convex H^β shaping rewards high-quality responses. The concave α<1 exponent disproportionately penalizes even minor leakage, while β>1 emphasizes high helpfulness only when privacy is fully preserved. Each agent is then fine-tuned via DPO on its respective preference pairs, embedding privacy preferences directly into generation behavior without requiring explicit inference-time privacy analysis.

## Results

On PrivacyLens, PrivAct achieves up to a 12.32% reduction in average privacy leakage (11.41% vs. 23.74% for AIFC on Mistral-7B) while maintaining comparable helpfulness (0.863 vs. 0.869 avg). Across all four backbones (Llama-8B, Llama-1B, Mistral-7B, Qwen3-4B), PrivAct consistently lies on a more favorable privacy–helpfulness Pareto frontier than Vanilla LM, PPE, and AIFC under both average and worst-case (leak@K) privacy metrics. On ConfAIde (zero-shot transfer), both the fine-tuned refiner and verifier outperform the base model across all backbones on Tier 3 and Tier 4 metrics. On the Compositional Privacy Risks dataset, PrivAct-integrated defenders improve Overall Success over the CoT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPE (Shao et al., 2024), PrivAct strictly dominates on both privacy and helpfulness for Llama-8B (6.26% vs. 9.50% avg leak; 0.737 vs. 0.690 avg helpfulness) and Mistral-7B (9.39% vs. 17.27% avg leak; 0.856 vs. 0.832 avg helpfulness at b₂=0.0). Against AIFC (Li et al., 2025a, 1-2-3 Check), PrivAct achieves substantially lower leakage—12.32% lower average leak on Mistral-7B (11.41% vs. 23.74%) and 14.85% lower on Llama-8B (6.26% vs. 21.11%)—while sacrificing only a small amount of helpfulness (e.g., 0.863 vs. 0.869 avg on Mistral-7B). On the Compositional Privacy Risks dataset (Patil et al., 2025), PrivAct-integrated defenders outperform the CoT baseline (42.39% Overall Success), demonstrating that internalized privacy awareness also mitigates multi-turn compositional leakage. On ConfAIde, PrivAct's zero-shot transfer shows consistent gains over the base model across all four backbones on both Tier 3 (Theory-of-Mind) and Tier 4 (professional multi-party) metrics, confirming that acting-level training transfers to probing-level privacy understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

PrivAct builds on the multi-agent preference learning paradigm introduced by MALT (Motwani et al., 2024), which established the generator-verifier-refiner architecture and distributed DPO training for collaborative LLM systems, and on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the underlying optimization algorithm. It departs from the dominant line of contextual privacy work—prompt-based enforcement (Shao et al., 2024) and agent-based information flow control (Li et al., 2025a; Wang et al., 2025a)—by shifting privacy preservation from inference-time external interventions to training-time internalization, and from explicit PII protection (Zhang et al., 2024a) to the subtler, context-dependent norms of contextual integrity. The novel LC-ARS reward shaping mechanism is the paper's key methodological departure, treating privacy as a hard prerequisite rather than a competing objective in the reward function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that internalized privacy training outperforms explicit inference-time privacy reasoning (AIFC) challenges the prevailing assumption that structured chain-of-thought privacy analysis is necessary for compliance, and the zero-shot transfer from acting tasks to probing benchmarks suggests that behavioral alignment can induce reasoning improvements. However, the approach requires fine-tuning open-weight models, limiting applicability to closed-source LLMs, and the LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (Fleiss' Kappa of 0.56 for helpfulness) introduces measurement uncertainty. Open questions include whether the method scales to more complex, non-linear multi-agent topologies, whether it generalizes to longer-horizon agentic tasks with more sensitive data, and whether the LC-ARS formulation can be extended to multi-party or dynamic privacy contexts beyond the two-party settings tested here.
