## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts models require caching only a subset of experts in limited GPU memory, but existing caching policies (LRU, prefetching, eviction, miss handling) have each been evaluated in isolation with 1-2 policy combinations, leaving cross-policy interactions and optimal configurations across hardware constraints uncharacterized. Additionally, traditional temporal eviction policies like LRU and LFU are fundamentally mismatched to MoE's deterministic sequential layer access pattern, causing premature eviction of soon-needed experts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261576/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt and MoE model weights quantized to int8/int4/int2 (via bitsandbytes and HQQ), producing generated text while making real-time cache management decisions. GPU cache capacity is software-limited to 1%, 5%, or 25% of full model size (e.g., 0.6 GB to 21 GB for Mixtral), and CPU-to-GPU bandwidth is throttled to 5 GB/s to emulate constrained interconnects. The benchmark operates on a single A100 GPU with PyTorch forward hooks intercepting gate outputs, requiring no model modification or specialized hardware.

## Method

SpecMD decomposes the MoE cache policy space into four orthogonal dimensions—routing, prefetch, eviction, and miss handling—enabling systematic cross-policy comparison across four MoE architectures (OLMoE, Mixtral, Phi-3.5-MoE, Qwen1.5-MoE) and three capacity regimes. The key technical contribution is Least-Stale (LS) eviction, which partitions the cache into a stale queue (experts from previous forward passes) and a current queue (experts accessed in the current pass or prefetched), evicting stale experts first via FIFO layer-position ordering to prevent collision misses where soon-needed experts are evicted prematurely. LS achieves up to 85× fewer collision misses than LRU at 1% capacity and 88-92% hit rates. A second key finding is that score-based prefetching (filtering by gate-score percentile) outperforms fixed top-k prefetching despite lower prediction accuracy, because it dynamically adapts the number of prefetched experts per layer to better utilize available bandwidth.

## Results

At 5% cache capacity (0.6 GB for OLMoE), Least-Stale eviction achieves 1.6–1.9% collision miss rates versus 4.5–12.6% for LRU and 42.6–60.9% for Score-Based, reducing collision misses by 2.6–8.6× over LRU and 58–91× over Score-Based. As a drop-in replacement in existing systems, Least-Stale delivers 10.7–34.7% TTFT reduction on OLMoE-1B-7B and 14.8–44.1% across diverse baselines (Table 3). Score-based prefetching (80th percentile) outperforms top-k prefetching in hit rate and synchronous overhead for 3 of 4 models despite lower raw prediction accuracy. On the full quality-speed trade-off (Table 1), no single configuration dominates: Config #5 (Score+LS+Fetch) achieves the best TTFT among quality-preserving settings (2220 ms on OLMoE, 3044 ms on Mixtral) while maintaining GSM8K accuracy within 1–2% of the 8-bit baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Eliseev & Mazur (2023, Config #1: Spec+LRU+Fetch), the proposed Config #5 (Score+LS+Fetch) achieves lower TTFT on all four models (e.g., 2220 vs 3077 ms on OLMoE, 3044 vs 4238 ms on Mixtral) while matching or slightly improving GSM8K accuracy (0.691 vs 0.677 on OLMoE). Against HOBBIT (Tang et al., 2024, Config #3: Spec+LHU+Cascade), Config #5 is slower in TTFT (2220 vs 1590 ms on OLMoE) but substantially better on quality: GSM8K improves from 0.606 to 0.691 on OLMoE and from 0.217 to 0.497 on Qwen, indicating HOBBIT's precision cascading trades accuracy for speed. Against MixtureCache (Skliar et al., 2025, Config #4: LRU+Fetch+cache-aware routing), Config #5 wins on TTFT across all models (2220 vs 2665 on OLMoE, 2893 vs 3489 on Qwen) and on GSM8K for OLMoE (0.691 vs 0.664) and Qwen (0.497 vs 0.296), though MixtureCache retains a slight edge on Mixtral and Phi-3.5 GSM8K. The paper also shows that Least-Stale as a pure drop-in eviction replacement (keeping each baseline's own prefetch and routing) yields 10.7–34.7% TTFT reduction on OLMoE and up to 44.1% on Phi-3.5 (HOBBIT), confirming its portability independent of the full system.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying benchmark (SpecMD) over a fragmented landscape where prior MoE caching systems—Eliseev & Mazur's speculative prefetching with LRU, HOBBIT's precision cascading with LHU eviction, Zhu et al.'s score-based prefetching with substitution, and MixtureCache's cache-aware routing—each evaluate only 1–2 policy combinations under specific hardware assumptions. Conceptually, it departs from the temporal-locality assumption underlying LRU/LFU by showing that MoE expert access follows a deterministic front-to-back layer sequence, and introduces Least-Stale as a spatial-temporal eviction policy that no prior work has attempted. The cache-aware routing formula is directly adopted from Skliar et al. (2025), and the score-based prefetching mechanism follows Zhu et al. (2025), but the cross-policy interaction study across four architectures and three capacity regimes is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard temporal caching assumptions (LRU, LFU) are fundamentally mismatched to MoE's sequential layer access pattern provides practitioners with a clear diagnostic for why naive caching underperforms and a drop-in fix (Least-Stale) that yields 10–44% TTFT reductions across diverse baselines. The SpecMD benchmark lowers the barrier to reproducible MoE caching research by requiring only consumer-grade GPUs and PyTorch hooks, enabling the community to explore policy interactions that were previously studied in isolation. Key limitations include single-GPU evaluation with software-emulated bandwidth/capacity constraints, exclusion of multi-GPU and pipeline-parallel settings, and restriction to training-free policies, leaving open how these findings transfer to distributed deployments and learned routing modifications.
