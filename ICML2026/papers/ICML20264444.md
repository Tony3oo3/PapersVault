## Problem

Current LLM evaluations treat the model in isolation, ignoring that measured performance arises from the entire evaluation system—dataset, prompting, decoding, and software-hardware stack—whose underspecification alone can induce accuracy swings of up to 70%. Prior analyses target individual components in isolation without quantifying their joint effects or disentangling intrinsic model capability from dataset-induced confounding (e.g., pretraining-evaluation overlap), leaving benchmark scores unreliable and attribution unprincipled.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an LLM (or LLM service) and a benchmark dataset as input, then systematically samples from a 10-component configuration space (Language, Question Format, Question Paraphrase, Shot, CoT, Multi Turn, temperature, top_p, presence_penalty, max_tokens) with predefined value ranges. Under A-SCS, each original question is augmented into seen, analogical, and novel variants via five automated pipelines, and evaluation samples one instance per question with balanced 1/2–1/2 probability between analogical and novel layers. Outputs include mean accuracy with 95%/99% confidence intervals, per-component and interaction effect sizes (η²) with permutation-test p-values, and model rankings that can reverse relative to single-configuration results.

## Method

LLM evaluatology integrates four steps: (1) a Self-Contained System (SCS) that explicitly specifies the evaluated object and 10 indispensable evaluation-condition components with value ranges; (2) an SCM-oriented causal DAG identifying a backdoor path D→O_M→Y through dataset-related confounding, motivating instance-level treatment of dataset effects; (3) an Augmented SCS (A-SCS) that constructs analogical variants (distractor insertion, numeric substitution, conditional recomposition) and novel variants (recent-source adaptation, conceptual synthesis) via five script-driven LLM pipelines with automated verification; and (4) factorial decomposition under design of experiments, where ANOVA over a 2¹⁰ subspace quantifies main and interaction effects with permutation tests for significance. Systematic sampling with dual stopping criteria (normal-approximation floor and running-mean convergence) keeps evaluation cost-effective while producing accuracy estimates that closely approximate exhaustive ground truth.

## Results

Under the proposed SCS/A-SCS framework, the same model's accuracy on AIME ranges from 0 to 0.93 across 500 sampled configurations, with evaluation choices alone inducing accuracy swings of up to 70% (162 distinct conditions). ANOVA on DeepSeek identifies Question Format (η²=0.400) and CoT (η²=0.080) as the dominant main effects, with their interaction (η²=0.161) as the largest single factor; max_tokens (η²=0.028), Shot, and Multi Turn follow. Comparing default-configuration scores against interval estimates from systematic sampling reveals 18 systematic mismatches across model pairs: 9 sign reversals, 7 missed differences, and 3 illusory gaps, demonstrating that single-setting evaluations are unreliable for ranking claims.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between LLM evaluatology (systematic sampling over the full 10-component configuration space) and traditional single-configuration evaluation (the 'default setting' used in most benchmark reports). On AIME, the default-configuration accuracy for each model falls far outside the 95% and 99% confidence intervals obtained from 500 random configurations, indicating that a single point estimate is a poor proxy for true performance. For pairwise model comparisons, 18 out of 36 model pairs show systematic mismatches: in 9 cases the default configuration favors one model while the confidence interval supports the opposite ranking (e.g., Doubao vs. GPT4.1), in 7 cases the default shows no gap but the interval excludes zero (e.g., Kimi vs. MistralL), and in 3 cases the default shows a sizable gap but the interval includes zero (e.g., DeepSeek vs. Qwen2.5). A 'restricted-space' verification—exhaustively evaluating the top-5 most influential components per model—confirms that the exhaustive ground truth falls within the confidence intervals from random sampling, validating the framework's accuracy. The same qualitative pattern (default results as poor proxies) extends to SWE-bench, MMBench, and Arena-Hard, confirming the phenomenon is not benchmark-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Evaluatology framework (Zhan et al., 2024, 2025) from general evaluation science to the specific and qualitatively different setting of LLMs, where open-ended usage and pretraining-evaluation overlap introduce dataset-induced confounding absent in traditional benchmarking. It departs from prior single-component studies (prompting, decoding, language effects) by jointly modeling ten evaluation components under a structured causal model (Pearl, 2009) and factorial design of experiments (Montgomery, 2017), quantifying main and interaction effects simultaneously. The Augmented SCS (A-SCS) with its five script-driven augmentation pipelines goes beyond the small-scale manual manipulations (distractors, numeric edits) in prior work by systematically generating seen, analogical, and novel variants at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that evaluation choices alone can induce up to 70% accuracy swings and reverse model rankings challenges the widespread practice of reporting single-configuration benchmark scores as stable model properties, with immediate implications for how the community interprets and compares LLMs. The framework is benchmark-agnostic and automatable, offering a reproducible path toward more honest attribution of model capability. Open questions include whether the ten-component space captures all relevant confounders (e.g., hardware, API latency, context-window truncation), how the causal structure generalizes to truly open-ended or agentic evaluation, and whether the A-SCS augmentation pipelines introduce their own biases that could confound the very effects they aim to probe.
