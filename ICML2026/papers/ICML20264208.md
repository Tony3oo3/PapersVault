## Problem

Existing WSI-to-RNA-seq prediction methods rely on deterministic one-to-one regression, producing single point estimates that suppress biologically meaningful variability and fail to capture inter-patient and intra-tumoral heterogeneity. Additionally, genome-wide RNA-seq spans over 20,000 genes, making it computationally infeasible to model each gene as an independent token without structured prior knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264208/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input is a histopathology WSI at ×20 magnification (0.5 μm/pixel), partitioned into 256×256 non-overlapping tiles (up to 4,000 after background filtering), encoded by a pretrained ResNet-50 or UNI backbone, then aggregated into 100 k-means clusters forming a 100×d slide-level representation. Output is a genome-wide bulk RNA-seq expression vector over 20,820 genes (log₂(FPKM-UQ+1) transformed), generated as a conditional distribution via 20-step Euler ODE integration, with uncertainty quantified by sampling 100 predictions per input.

## Method

RNA-FM formulates transcriptomic prediction as a continuous-time conditional transport problem, learning a velocity field v_θ(x_t, t | y) that maps a standard Gaussian prior to the target RNA-seq distribution conditioned on WSI features, using a linear interpolant (α=1−t, σ=t) and minimizing a flow-matching L2 objective. To handle genome-scale dimensionality, genes are tokenized into Gene Ontology Biological Process pathway tokens via pathway-specific MLPs, with inter-pathway dependencies modeled through graph-constrained multi-head self-attention (edges defined by gene overlap between pathways). A 7-block DiT backbone (8 heads, 512-dim latent) processes pathway tokens conditioned on the WSI cluster representation and flow time, and pathway-specific heads decode tokens back to gene-level predictions with overlap averaging. Inference uses classifier-free guidance (scale s=2) to strengthen morphology conditioning, and the model is trained with AdamW (lr=1e-4, batch 32, up to 2000 epochs) with EMA parameters.

## Results

On TCGA cross-validation, RNA-FM (UNI) achieves PCC of 0.687/0.712/0.716 on LUAD/BRCA/COAD (T1000) and 0.784/0.790/0.860 on T50, competitive with or surpassing deterministic baselines. On external CPTAC validation, RNA-FM clearly dominates: PCC of 0.362/0.456/0.396 (T1000) and 0.500/0.572/0.543 (T50) across LUAD/BRCA/COAD, outperforming tRNAsformer and SEQUOIA by 5–15 PCC points. RNA-FM uniquely provides calibrated uncertainty estimates (e.g., 80% interval coverage of 0.791 on T500, Spearman variance–error correlation of 0.597) that deterministic regression baselines cannot offer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNA-FM is compared against three deterministic regression baselines: HE2RNA (ResNet-50, 2020), tRNAsformer (UNI, 2023), and SEQUOIA (ResNet-50, 2024). On TCGA cross-validation, RNA-FM is broadly competitive with tRNAsformer (the strongest baseline), trailing by 0.5–7 PCC points on LUAD and BRCA but matching or exceeding it on COAD (e.g., T100: 0.836 vs. 0.824; T50: 0.860 vs. 0.856). SEQUOIA collapses on COAD (PCC ≈ 0.14–0.19), where RNA-FM achieves 0.72–0.86. On the external CPTAC validation, RNA-FM wins decisively across all three cancer types and all gene subsets: on CPTAC-BRCA T1000 it reaches PCC 0.456 versus 0.380 (tRNAsformer) and 0.375 (SEQUOIA), and on CPTAC-COAD T50 it reaches 0.543 versus 0.373 and 0.457. The generative formulation also enables calibrated uncertainty quantification (interval coverage, Gaussian NLL, variance–error correlation) that none of the deterministic baselines can provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

RNA-FM departs from the dominant deterministic regression paradigm in WSI-to-RNA-seq prediction (HE2RNA, tRNAsformer, SEQUOIA) by reframing the task as a conditional generative problem via flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024), learning a continuous-time velocity field rather than a single point estimate. The classifier-free guidance mechanism (Ho & Salimans, 2021) is adapted to strengthen morphology conditioning during sampling. A key architectural departure is the pathway-aware tokenization of 20,820 genes into Gene Ontology Biological Process tokens with inter-pathway graph attention, which addresses the dimensionality challenge that makes direct genome-scale generation intractable and distinguishes RNA-FM from prior diffusion-based approaches in single-cell or spatial transcriptomics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By modeling the full conditional distribution p(x|y) rather than collapsing to a point estimate, RNA-FM captures inter-patient and intra-tumoral heterogeneity that deterministic methods suppress, and provides calibrated uncertainty estimates (validated via interval coverage and NLL) that are practically useful for clinical decision-making. The pathway-aware structure makes genome-scale generation both computationally tractable and biologically interpretable, a design principle that could transfer to other high-dimensional omics prediction tasks. Key limitations include evaluation on only three cancer types (LUAD, BRCA, COAD), reliance on bulk RNA-seq, and the use of a fixed GO-BP pathway set; extending to single-cell or spatial transcriptomics and more diverse anatomical sites remains open.
