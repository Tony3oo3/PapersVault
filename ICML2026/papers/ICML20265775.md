## Problem

Despite rapid progress in neural network wavefunctions for quantum many-body problems, there is no unified dataset or benchmarking protocol to systematically compare how different architectures represent different classes of strongly correlated quantum states. The field lacks a consistent framework to quantify expressivity scaling with system size and architectural parameters across physically distinct regimes such as topological order, superconducting pairing, and Wigner crystallization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265775/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes analytically defined target wavefunctions (Laughlin, Moore-Read, BCS geminal-power, and Wigner crystal Slater determinants) parameterized by filling factor, pairing symmetry, and lattice geometry, and trains an NN wavefunction Ψ_θ(R) over many-body electron positions R via Monte Carlo sampling. The output is a fidelity series {F(N_e)} across system sizes, from which power-law parameters (α, β) in F = 1 − α(N_e−2)^β are extracted, along with ablation curves over determinant count N_det and layer depth N_layer. F(8) serves as a compact summary metric of representability for each target.

## Method

The paper introduces WF-Bench, a dataset of 31 target wavefunctions spanning topological states (Laughlin m=1,3,5 with 0–3 quasiholes; Moore-Read m=2,4,6 with 0–3 quasiholes), superconducting states (11 BCS geminal-power wavefunctions with s-, p-, d-, f-wave pairing in singlet/triplet spin channels), and Wigner crystals (8 states with Gaussian, squeezed-Gaussian, and moiré orbitals on hexagonal lattices). The benchmarking protocol optimizes a fidelity loss L_F = −log F via Monte Carlo sampling, but for topological states with complex phase windings, a two-stage pretraining loss is first applied: a normalization-invariant probability-matching term L_1 computed under a mixed sampling distribution p_mix ∝ (p_θ + p_t)/2 with a learned offset c to prevent self-trapping, plus a current-matching term L_2 penalizing per-particle phase gradient discrepancies. Training uses transfer learning across system sizes (parameters from N_e initialize N_e+2) and the KFAC optimizer with a decaying learning rate. The protocol is applied to two architectures—Ferminet (permutation-equivariant one- and two-body feature streams with SchNet-style inter-stream mixing) and Psiformer (self-attention blocks with MLP)—both producing complex neural orbitals φ_θ^k and forming Ψ_θ as a sum of N_det determinants, with an exponential envelope factor imposed for topological targets.

## Results

Across all 31 target wavefunctions in WF-Bench, fidelity F follows an empirical power-law decay F = 1 − α(N_e − 2)^β with increasing electron number. At N_e = 8, Psiformer achieves F(8) ranging from 0.9340 (BCS_FXX2Y2_TP) to 0.9999 (WC_GAU_V1A), while Ferminet ranges from 0.8401 (BCS_FXX2Y2_TP) to 0.9998 (WC_GAU_V1A). Wigner crystal states are the easiest to represent (F(8) > 0.996 for both architectures), while topological states with large m (e.g., Laughlin_m5, Moore_m6) are the hardest, with Ferminet dropping to F(8) = 0.7068 for Moore_m6. Ablation studies show diminishing returns: fidelity saturates beyond N_det ≈ 32 and N_layer ≈ 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two architectures—Psiformer (self-attention) and Ferminet (permutation-equivariant one/two-body features)—on the same 31 target wavefunctions under identical training protocols. Psiformer consistently outperforms Ferminet across all 31 wavefunctions at comparable parameter counts (e.g., ~7.3×10⁵ vs ~8.3×10⁵ parameters for N_e=10 topological states). The gap is most pronounced for topological states: for Laughlin_m5, Psiformer achieves F(8)=0.9966 versus Ferminet's 0.8737 (a 12.3-point gap), and for Moore_m6, Psiformer reaches 0.9681 versus Ferminet's 0.7068 (a 26.1-point gap). For superconducting states, the advantage is moderate (e.g., BCS_FP_TP: 0.9498 vs 0.8902). For Wigner crystals, both architectures perform near-perfectly and the gap is minimal (e.g., WC_GAU_v1A: 0.9999 vs 0.9998). The paper does not introduce a new architecture but establishes a unified evaluation framework, so the comparison is architecture-vs-architecture rather than method-vs-baseline in the traditional sense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Nazaryan et al. (2025), which introduced a density-current pretraining loss and studied fidelity scaling for Laughlin, Moore-Read, and BCS wavefunctions, by expanding the scope to 31 target wavefunctions across three physical classes (topological, superconducting, Wigner crystal) and adding Wigner crystals as a new category. It also builds on the broader NN wavefunction architecture literature (Ferminet, Psiformer) and theoretical expressivity analyses (Deng et al. 2017, Carleo et al. 2018, Lu et al. 2026), but departs from prior numerical scaling studies (Jiang et al. 2025, Rende et al. 2026) by introducing a unified, architecture-agnostic benchmarking protocol with a modified mixed-sampling pretraining loss designed to prevent self-trapping. The key conceptual contribution is framing expressivity evaluation as a dataset-driven, reproducible benchmark rather than a per-wavefunction ad hoc study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WF-Bench provides the first comprehensive, multi-class, multi-architecture benchmark for NN wavefunction representability, giving the community a standardized reference point for evaluating new architectures and guiding design choices (e.g., the practical finding that N_det saturates around 32 and N_layer beyond 2 yields diminishing returns). The consistent power-law fidelity decay F = 1 − α(N_e−2)^β across all 31 wavefunctions offers a compact empirical characterization that can inform theoretical scaling analyses. Open questions include whether the observed scaling exponents β can be predicted from physical properties of the target state, how the results change with improved optimization protocols, and whether the framework extends to lattice models, molecules, and other strongly correlated systems beyond the continuum electron gas setting studied here.
