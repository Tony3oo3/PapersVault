## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on sensitive data amplifies memorization and enables training data extraction (TDE) attacks, yet existing defenses either lack formal guarantees (deduplication, modified losses) or incur substantial utility loss (differential privacy). While Near Access Freeness (NAF) provides provable closeness to constituent models, the paper shows that the standard CP-Δ NAF bound becomes numerically large in realistic fine-tuning settings because memorization induces sharp probability spikes on a small number of tokens, rendering existing NAF-based methods practically vulnerable to TDE.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, two fine-tuned constituent models (trained on disjoint 3000-example partitions), and a fixed base model (either the pre-fine-tuning LLM or a constant-logits vector), along with a smoothing parameter m (default 10). It produces a single next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary (~32,000 tokens) that is NAF-close to both constituents under relative max-divergence. All models are constrained to assign probability ≥ e^{-20} to every output token, and the three forward passes at inference are independent and can be executed in parallel.

## Method

SCP-Δr combines two complementary mechanisms to enforce the Sparse Relative Probability Shift (SpaRPS) property, which the paper shows does not naturally hold in fine-tuned LLMs. First, it operates on relative probability distributions (RPDs) rather than raw probabilities, removing global normalization effects so that sparse token-level shifts yield dramatically smaller NAF bounds (orders of magnitude smaller than CP-Δ). Second, it applies base-model smoothing: for each constituent model, only the top-m tokens ranked by relative information gain score (p(y|x)·log(rp(y|x)/rb(y|x))) retain their fine-tuned relative probabilities, while all remaining tokens are replaced with the base model's relative probabilities up to a global rescaling. The final output is CP-Δr applied to the two smoothed RPDs. The paper further establishes the first formal link between NAF guarantees and TDE resistance by bounding the likelihood ratio r₁(y|x)/r₀(y|x) that underlies optimal membership inference (LiRA), showing that for smoothed (rare) tokens the extraction bound is at most ~1.053·(tr₁/tr₀), whereas CP-Δ bounds do not meaningfully constrain TDE.

## Results

SCP-Δr-b achieves a Normalized Attack Score (NAS) of 0.11, substantially lower than CP-Δ (0.47), CP-ΔKL (0.76), CP-Fuse (0.50), and CP-Δr (0.22), approaching the No-CP validation baseline (0.002). On the canary attack, SCP-Δr-b reduces mean exposure to 1.4 (95th percentile: 3.2) versus 8.8 (17) for CP-Δ and 3.6 (8.0) for CP-Δr. On PII extraction (Names2IDs), SCP-Δr-b achieves an average extracted length of 0.43 and a full-extraction rate below 0.001, compared to 2.4 and 0.15 for CP-Δ. The 99th-percentile k_x NAF bound drops from 11.6 (CP-Δ) to 0.98 (CP-Δr) to 0.004 (SCP-Δr-b) on MathAbstracts, reflecting orders-of-magnitude improvement. Utility is preserved: next-token accuracy on MathAbstracts, StoryTelling, and CodeInstructions remains within 0.01–0.03 of the No-CP baseline, and MMLU/IFEval scores are unchanged by smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CP-Δ (Vyas et al., 2023), SCP-Δr-b dominates on every security metric: canary mean drops from 8.8 to 1.4, PII AEL from 2.4 to 0.43, and the 99th-percentile k_x bound from 11.6 to 0.004, while utility is maintained. Against CP-Δr (Segal et al., 2025), SCP-Δr-b improves canary exposure (1.4 vs. 3.6 mean) and PII extraction (AEL 0.43 vs. 1.9, FER <0.001 vs. 0.06), with TTE AUC/ACC remaining comparable (e.g., Math AUC 0.76 vs. 0.80), consistent with Proposition 4.8 which predicts the largest gains on smoothed (rare) tokens. Against CP-Fuse (Abad et al., 2025), SCP-Δr-b achieves lower canary scores (1.4 vs. 3.8) and substantially lower PII AEL (0.43 vs. 4.1); the paper notes that CP-Fuse's fusing mechanism has no effect on the first generated token, so under adaptive attacks it reduces to CP-ΔKL. Overall, SCP-Δr-b is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-baseline security (NAS 0.11 vs. 0.002 for the validation reference) without measurable utility degradation, whereas all non-RPD-based CP-style methods (CP-Δ, CP-ΔKL, CP-Fuse) remain highly vulnerable with NAS values of 0.47–0.76.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Near Access Freeness (NAF) framework introduced by Vyas et al. (2023) for copyright protection and the relative probability distribution (RPD) aggregation of Segal et al. (2025) for access control, repurposing both for training data extraction (TDE) defense. The key conceptual departure is identifying that CP-Δ's raw-probability bounds become vacuously large under memorization-induced probability spikes, and that SpaRPS (Sparse Relative Probability Shift) does not naturally hold in fine-tuned LLMs. The paper introduces base-model smoothing as a mechanism to enforce SpaRPS and establishes the first formal link between NAF divergence bounds and TDE adversary information gain via likelihood-ratio analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first defense against TDE for fine-tuned LLMs that provides provable guarantees without the substantial utility loss typical of differential privacy, making it a practical option for deploying models on sensitive data. The finding that previously proposed NAF-based methods (CP-Δ, CP-ΔKL, CP-Fuse) are empirically vulnerable to straightforward extraction attacks is important for the broader NAF community, as it shows that copyright-protection guarantees do not automatically transfer to privacy. Open questions include whether the partition assumption (at least one constituent model excludes each sensitive example) can be relaxed for near-duplicate data, whether the approach extends to pre-training at scale, and whether attacks stronger than LiRA exist outside the optimality assumptions.
