## Problem

Existing precipitation nowcasting systems treat storm evolution as 2D radar echo extrapolation, which fails under rapid intensification because flattened reflectivity lacks the vertical structure and environmental context needed to anticipate convective transitions. Two specific gaps are identified: (C1) cross-layer feedbacks propagate on mismatched, state-dependent timescales that break locality assumptions in multi-layer fusion, and (C2) the impact of local precursors is non-stationary and conditioned on the evolving synoptic-scale environment, requiring dynamic gating rather than static side information.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 13 historical frames of heterogeneous multi-source observations—satellite (VIS, IR channels), radar (VIL or CR), and sparse weather-station measurements—projected onto a shared 128×128 lat-lon grid via SetConv, alongside ERA5 reanalysis fields (65 variables across 5 pressure levels and single-level quantities) on a 0.25° grid. It produces 25 future radar reflectivity frames at 5-minute cadence (120-minute horizon), decoded from a VAE latent space. The US subset uses event-based SEVIR samples (384 km × 384 km, 2017–2019) while France uses continuous MeteoNet time series (550 km × 550 km, 2016–2018), with all modalities temporally aligned to a 5-minute grid.

## Method

StormInsight decomposes convective evolution into three complementary encodings: a Convective State Encoder (SetConv + Multi-Scale Inception + VAE + FiLM fusion) capturing within-layer morphology, a Vertical Interaction Encoder using a 4-expert Mixture-of-Experts (directional M→H, H→M, M→L, L→M pathways) with a Gate-of-Time module to handle asynchronous cross-layer feedbacks, and an Atmospheric Environment Encoder built on Multi-mesh Message Passing over a spherical mesh to encode synoptic-scale thermodynamic and dynamical constraints. The Convective System Decoder performs conditional flow matching (DiT backbone) where the velocity field is hierarchically modulated: a Global Adapter injects environmental forcing via AdaLN at the trajectory level (analogous to external forcing in atmospheric equations), while a Local Adapter gates frame-level cross-layer expert features into every attention block. The VAE is pre-trained separately, experts are trained with directional reconstruction losses, and the full model is trained with a flow-matching objective using optimal-transport linear interpolation paths, decoded via Euler ODE integration (20 steps) and the frozen VAE decoder.

## Results

On StormBench, StormInsight achieves the best performance across all metrics in both the USA and France regions. In the USA, it reduces MAE to 12.608 (vs. 14.176 for the best baseline SimVP) and improves mCSI to 0.540 (vs. 0.403), a 34.0% relative gain in mCSI. In France, it achieves MAE 0.980 and mCSI 0.258, outperforming the next-best SimVP (MAE 1.600, mCSI 0.106) by 38.8% in MAE and 143% in mCSI. The paper reports an overall 12.4% MAE reduction and 34.0% mCSI improvement over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StormInsight outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric in both the USA and France regions. The closest competitor is SimVP, which achieves the best baseline MAE (14.176) and mCSI (0.403) in the USA; StormInsight improves mCSI by 34.0% (0.540 vs. 0.403) and reduces MAE by 11.1%. In France, the gap is even larger: StormInsight's mCSI of 0.258 nearly triples SimVP's 0.106, and its MAE of 0.980 is 38.8% lower. Recurrent architectures (ConvLSTM, PredRNN-V2) show the weakest performance, particularly on high-threshold CSI (e.g., ConvLSTM scores 0.142 on CSI₂₁₉ in the USA), indicating poor detection of intense convection. Transformer-based methods (Earthformer, Earthfarseer, AlphaPre) and physics-informed models (PastNet, NowcastNet) perform better than recurrent baselines but still trail StormInsight by 10–20% in mCSI. Probabilistic baselines evaluated under radar-only input (DGMR, PreDiff in Table 7) also lose to StormInsight's multi-source MISO setting, with DGMR achieving only mCSI 0.199 in the USA versus StormInsight's 0.540.

## Relation to Prior Work

StormInsight departs from the dominant paradigm of treating precipitation nowcasting as 2D radar echo extrapolation (ConvLSTM, SimVP, Earthformer, AlphaPre, etc.) by reframing it as a multi-source, vertically structured, environment-conditioned dynamical modeling problem. Architecturally, it composes several established components in a novel configuration: a VAE latent space with conditional flow matching for generative decoding (Lipman et al., 2022), multi-mesh message passing on a spherical graph for encoding reanalysis fields (Lam et al., 2023), and SetConv-based projection for unifying heterogeneous observation densities (Allen et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is the explicit decomposition of convective dynamics into within-layer states, directional cross-layer interactions (via a Mixture-of-Experts), and synoptic-scale environmental forcing, rather than treating all inputs as a flat multi-channel video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Kerrville, Texas 2025 tragedy (130+ fatalities from a rapidly intensifying storm) underscores that the critical failure mode in nowcasting is not detection but anticipation of evolution, particularly during intensification transitions. StormBench provides a much-needed multi-source, multi-region benchmark that goes beyond the radar-only SEVIR/MeteoNet paradigm, enabling the community to evaluate environment-aware models. Key open questions remain: the framework still operates on multi-layer 2D slices rather than full volumetric 3D radar, polarimetric variables (Z_DR, K_DP) are untapped, and the France PSNR degradation (30.1 vs. ~40 for baselines) suggests a trade-off between structural accuracy and pixel-level smoothness that warrants further investigation. Whether the environmental conditioning generalizes to regions with different convective regimes (e.g., tropical monsoon) is untested.
