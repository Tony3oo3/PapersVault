## Problem

Existing hypergraph contrastive learning methods implicitly assume that nodes sharing a hyperedge are semantically homogeneous, but real-world hypergraphs contain heterogeneous node-hyperedge interactions that introduce noisy signals during both view augmentation and message passing. Random masking strategies in view generation and indiscriminate aggregation in propagation fail to distinguish homogeneous from heterogeneous connections, leading to feature contamination and false-positive contrastive alignment that degrade representation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a hypergraph specified by its binary incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{|V|×|E|} and per-node feature vectors x_v as input. Node-hyperedge heterogeneity scores δ(v,e) are computed once from raw features via cosine distance to the hyperedge centroid, then used to guide both view construction and propagation reweighting. The trained encoder outputs node embeddings evaluated by a frozen linear probe for node classification, with the three-level contrastive objective (node, hyperedge, structural) providing the self-supervised training signal without any labels.

## Method

H²CL introduces two coupled heterogeneity-aware mechanisms. First, a Heterogeneity-Aware View Generator converts cosine-distance-based heterogeneity scores into retention probabilities and uses multi-round Bernoulli sampling to preferentially mask high-heterogeneity incidences, producing contrastive views biased toward homogeneous structure rather than uniform random masking. Second, a Heterogeneity-Aware Hypergraph Encoder applies softmax reweighting of (1−δ(v,e)) in both the node-to-hyperedge and hyperedge-to-node aggregation stages, so that homogeneous connections receive larger propagation weights while heterogeneous ones are suppressed. The encoder is proven to perform coordinate descent on a heterogeneity-weighted Dirichlet energy, formally guaranteeing semantic smoothness within homogeneous clusters and boundary sharpening at heterogeneous interfaces. Training uses a three-level InfoNCE objective combining node-level, hyperedge-level, and structural-level (bilinear node-hyperedge compatibility) contrastive losses with learned temperature parameters.

## Results

H²CL achieves the best node classification accuracy on all seven main benchmark datasets: 81.67% on Cora-CC, 82.02% on Cora-CA, 72.17% on Citeseer, 80.05% on 20News, 99.84% on Mushroom, 75.27% on NTU, and 97.40% on ModelNet40. On the larger-scale DHG-Bench hypergraphs, H²CL reaches 86.22% (Actor), 78.57% (Walmart), and 85.72% (Trivago), substantially outperforming supervised HGNN baselines by 8–28 percentage points. The method also achieves 87.92% on PubMed, surpassing the strongest recent baseline (Adaptive Expansion, 86.82%) and TriCL (84.26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H²CL outperforms all 14 baselines across the seven main datasets. Against the strongest contrastive baselines, H²CL beats TriCL by 0.30 pp (Cora-CC), 0.02 pp (Cora-CA), 0.35 pp (Citeseer), 0.21 pp (20News), 0.21 pp (Mushroom), 0.31 pp (NTU), and 0.32 pp (ModelNet40), and beats CHGNN by 1.87 pp (Cora-CC), 3.12 pp (Cora-CA), 2.67 pp (Citeseer), 0.18 pp (20News), 0.14 pp (Mushroom), 1.37 pp (NTU), and 0.30 pp (ModelNet40). Against supervised hypergraph methods, H²CL surpasses UniGCN (the best supervised HGNN) on six of seven datasets, with the largest gap on Citeseer (72.17 vs 66.40, +5.77 pp) and 20News (80.05 vs 80.24, roughly tied). Against graph-based GNNs (GCN, GAT), H²CL gains 4–10 pp on most datasets and avoids OOM on 20News where GCN and GAT fail. On the larger DHG-Bench hypergraphs, H²CL leads HGNN by 8.39 pp (Actor), 1.45 pp (Walmart), and 28.05 pp (Trivago), and leads HNHN by 5.02 pp, 13.36 pp, and 31.97 pp respectively, indicating that the heterogeneity-aware design scales effectively to graphs with over 170k nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

H²CL extends the standard two-stage hypergraph message-passing framework of HGNN (Feng et al., 2019) by inserting heterogeneity-aware reweighting coefficients into both the node-to-hyperedge and hyperedge-to-node aggregation steps, and adapts TriCL's (Lee & Shin, 2022) three-level contrastive objective (node, hyperedge, structural) to the hypergraph setting. Its key departure from prior hypergraph contrastive methods like HyperGCL (Wei et al., 2022) is replacing topology-agnostic random masking with a heterogeneity-guided view generator that preferentially suppresses high-heterogeneity incidences. The theoretical contribution positions the encoder as a coordinate-descent step minimizing a heterogeneity-weighted Dirichlet energy, a framing not present in prior hypergraph propagation analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation—the implicit homophily assumption in hypergraph learning—by providing both a practical filtering mechanism and a principled energy-minimization interpretation. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (TriCL) are modest (typically 0.2–0.5% accuracy), and the robustness advantage under injected structural noise is the more compelling evidence. Open questions include whether heterogeneity scores computed once from raw features remain valid as embeddings evolve during training, and whether the noise-suppression framing generalizes to tasks where heterogeneous connections are informative signals (e.g., influence propagation, community bridging).
