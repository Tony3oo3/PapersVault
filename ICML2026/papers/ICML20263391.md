## Problem

Most neural PDE backbones rely on Fourier mixing, convolutions, or attention, which either treat all long-range interactions as equally plausible a priori or use fixed linear stencils with weak expressivity at the receptive-field fringe. For conservative and wave phenomena, the true solution operator routes information along state-dependent characteristics or rays, suggesting that adaptive, sparse, nonlocal sampling is the natural primitive. The paper asks whether learned coordinate warps alone—without Fourier multipliers, dot-product attention, or convolutional mixing—can constitute a sufficient and scalable backbone for state-of-the-art PDE solvers.

## Input / Output

In the primary 4→1 unconditioned setting, the model receives four consecutive PDE state frames stacked along the channel axis on a structured 2D or 3D grid and predicts the next time-step solution field; simulation parameters are not provided and must be inferred implicitly from the short history. A conditioned 1→1 variant additionally receives simulation parameters as a separate tensor for FiLM conditioning. The architecture operates on structured grids with periodic or zero-padded boundary handling at displaced sample points, and supports autoregressive rollouts where the predicted frame is fed back as the latest input frame.

## Method

FLOWERS is built from a multihead SELFWARP layer in which each head predicts a displacement ρ^(h)(x) using only pointwise information at x (a small MLP on the local channel vector), then warps the input by bilinearly sampling features at the displaced coordinate x + ρ^(h)(x); all nonlocality enters through these sparse per-head pullbacks, keeping cost linear in grid points. Heads are mixed pointwise via a linear value projection, and the multihead SELFWARP is embedded in a residual FlowerBlock (GroupNorm → GELU → 1×1 skip) that is scaffolded in a U-Net-like multiscale hierarchy with stride-2 down/up convolutions. The design is theoretically motivated by three complementary lenses: (i) for scalar conservation laws the short-time solution operator is a pullback by the characteristic flow map, whose first-order displacement depends only on the local state; (ii) for variable-speed waves the geometric-optics solution is a superposition of pullbacks along rays indexed by an angular variable, naturally discretized by heads; and (iii) in the continuum limit of many heads and deep composition, the architecture yields a self-adaptive kinetic transport equation in lifted phase space (x, η). A compact 17M-parameter FLOWER outperforms FNO, ConvNeXt U-Net, and scOT baselines across 18 diverse 2D/3D benchmarks, while a 156M-parameter variant surpasses the 628M-parameter POSEIDON-L foundation model on Euler dynamics.

## Results

On the 18-dataset The Well/PDEBench/PDEGym suite (4→1 unconditioned next-step prediction), the 17M-parameter FLOWER achieves the lowest VRMSE on 17 of 18 datasets, with margins ranging from 2× to 20× over baselines (e.g., 0.0064 vs. 0.1454 on Acoustic Maze; 0.0491 vs. 0.1714 on Rayleigh-Taylor). On long-horizon 1:20 autoregressive rollouts, FLOWER remains best on most datasets, with particularly large gaps on Rayleigh-Taylor (0.586 vs. 3.006 FNO, 5.389 CNUNET) and Rayleigh-Bénard (2.166 vs. 39.038 FNO). Scaling to 156M parameters, FLOWER-Medium achieves 0.0108 one-step and 0.0739 rollout VRMSE on the Euler dataset, outperforming the 629M-parameter POSEIDON-L foundation model (0.0194 / 0.1114) with roughly 4× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOWER (17M params) outperforms FNO on 17 of 18 next-step datasets, with the largest relative gains on transport-dominated problems (e.g., 3.5× lower VRMSE on Rayleigh-Taylor, 23× on Planet SWE). Against CNEXTU-NET, FLOWER wins on all 18 datasets, with particularly strong margins on Rayleigh-Taylor (0.0491 vs. 0.1351) and Viscoelastic Instability (0.0624 vs. 0.1623). SCOT (the attention-based architecture from POSEIDON) is outperformed on all 2D datasets where it is available, though it does not support 3D. In the scaling study on the Euler dataset, FLOWER-Medium (156M) beats the much larger POSEIDON-L (629M) on both one-step (0.0108 vs. 0.0194) and 1:20 rollout (0.0739 vs. 0.1114) VRMSE, while FLOWER-Tiny (17M) already matches POSEIDON-L's rollout performance (0.1114 vs. 0.1114). The one exception where FLOWER does not lead is the Neutron Star Merger 1:20 rollout, where FNO is marginally better (0.5980 vs. 0.6223).

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOWERS extract the pullback (coordinate warp) primitive from earlier wave-imaging networks (Kothari et al., 2020) and spatial transformer / deformable convolution architectures, but make it the sole adaptive nonlocal mixing mechanism rather than an augmentation layered on top of Fourier or attention blocks. The theoretical grounding through conservation-law characteristics, geometric-optics ray sums, and a kinetic-theory continuum limit is new; prior warp-based PDE work lacked this multi-perspective justification. The U-Net-like multiscale scaffold and multihead organization follow standard deep-learning engineering, but the claim that no Fourier multiplier, dot-product attention, or convolutional mixing is needed for state-of-the-art PDE solving is a deliberate departure from the FNO/ConvNeXt/transformer paradigms that dominate the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 17M-parameter FLOWER outperforms Fourier, convolutional, and attention-based baselines of similar size across 18+ diverse 2D/3D PDE benchmarks, and a 150M variant surpasses the 628M-parameter POSEIDON-L foundation model, suggesting warps are a highly parameter-efficient primitive for PDE solution operators. The learned displacement fields are interpretable (visibly aligning with fluid velocity), offering a diagnostic window into what the network has learned. Open questions include: when do learned warps track physically meaningful transport modes on diffusive or multi-physics problems, how to further stabilize long-horizon autoregressive rollouts, and whether the architecture extends naturally to unstructured meshes and complex boundary conditions.
