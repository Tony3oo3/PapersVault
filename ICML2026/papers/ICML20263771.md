## Problem

Existing patch-based time series forecasting methods (e.g., PatchTST, SRSNet) treat all patches equally during prediction, failing to dynamically identify and remove low-quality patches. In real-world data collection, missing values, distribution shifts, anomalies, and white noise cause certain patches to carry harmful information that degrades forecast accuracy, yet no prior method provides a unified mechanism to handle all four degradation types simultaneously at the patch level.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} (N channels, T timestamps) or univariate series, which is first instance-normalized and then divided into patches of size p (typically 16–24). For multivariate forecasting, the look-back window is fixed at 96 with horizons F ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}; for univariate forecasting, input/output lengths follow the TFB benchmark specifications across 8,068 series. The output is a dense tensor of predicted future values Ŷ ∈ R^{N×F} produced by a linear flatten head applied to compressed token representations.

## Method

SEER introduces two coupled modules. The Augmented Embedding Module replaces the single linear patch projection with a Mixture-of-Experts layer using noisy top-k gating, so each patch token is routed to k of M experts plus M shared experts, creating heterogeneous linear representation spaces that enrich patch-wise semantics; simultaneously, a channel-adaptive perception mechanism (stochastic pooling over channel embeddings followed by repeat-concat and MLP) produces per-channel series-wise prototypes. The Learnable Patch Replacement Module then operates in two stages: (1) an Automated Token Filter scores each patch via sigmoid(Linear) and masks those below a learnable threshold τ, and (2) a Replaced Causal Self-Attention substitutes filtered patches with their channel's series-wise prototype, prepends the global prototype as a leading token, and applies multi-head causal self-attention so that even identically replaced patches acquire distinct position-specific representations. A final standard MSA layer and a linear dimensionality-reduction projection (scaling ratio ~0.5–0.75) compress tokens before a linear flatten head produces the forecast.

## Results

On multivariate forecasting (look-back 96, horizons 96/192/336/720), SEER achieves first place in 34 out of 45 MSE settings and 39 out of 45 MAE settings across 8 benchmark datasets. Compared to the second-best baseline xPatch, SEER delivers a 7.3% average MSE reduction and a 4.9% average MAE reduction. In robustness experiments under four synthetic perturbations (white noise, anomalies, missing values, distribution shift) on ETTh2, SEER achieves the lowest MSE (0.293–0.296) and MAE (0.335–0.340) across all corruption types, outperforming SRSNet, Amplifier, iTransformer, and DLinear.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEER outperforms all 8 multivariate baselines (SRSNet, DUET, xPatch, Amplifier, FredFormer, iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear) on average MSE across all 8 datasets, with the largest margins over DLinear (e.g., 0.356 vs 0.563 MSE on ETTh2) and PatchTST (0.221 vs 0.270 on Solar). The closest competitor is xPatch, which SEER beats by 7.3% in average MSE and 4.9% in average MAE; SRSNet and DUET trail by roughly 4–5% MSE. In the robustness comparison (Table 5, synthetic perturbations on ETTh2), SEER achieves the best MSE and MAE under all four corruption types (white noise, anomalies, missing values, distribution shift), with the gap over SRSNet most pronounced for missing values (0.290 vs 0.299 MSE) and white noise (0.293 vs 0.297). On real-world low-quality datasets (Table 6), SEER leads on all 8 datasets (ILI, NASDAQ, FRED-MD, Wiki2000, SMD, PSM, USHCN, Human Activity), with particularly large gains on anomaly-affected SMD (0.520 vs 0.603 MSE vs SRSNet) and white-noise-affected FRED-MD (54.27 vs 61.25 MSE). DLinear consistently ranks last across both accuracy and robustness benchmarks, while SRSNet is the strongest runner-up in nearly every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEER extends the patch-based forecasting lineage (PatchTST, xPatch, Crossformer) by introducing dynamic, learnable patch selection rather than treating all patches uniformly, and it builds on the robust forecasting line (SRSNet, DUET, RevIN, RobustTSF) by addressing multiple degradation types—missing values, distribution shifts, anomalies, and white noise—within a single unified framework rather than targeting one in isolation. The MoE-based augmented embedding draws on noisy gating from NLP to create heterogeneous linear representation spaces for patches, while the series-wise prototype construction borrows the stochastic pooling mechanism from SOFTS. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage Learnable Patch Replacement Module, which couples a differentiable scoring/filtering step with a causal-attention-based substitution of low-quality patches by global series-wise tokens—a mechanism absent from both the patch-based and robust-forecasting literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world time series pipelines routinely encounter mixed degradation (sensor noise, gaps, drift, anomalies) simultaneously, yet prior robust methods handle only one type at a time. SEER's unified treatment and consistent SOTA across all four perturbation categories make it a strong candidate for deployment in monitoring and forecasting systems where data quality is uncontrolled. Open questions remain: whether the learned filter can reliably distinguish genuinely informative abrupt changes (e.g., financial crashes) from noise, how the method behaves under severe global distribution shifts where the global prototype itself becomes unrepresentative, and whether the threshold hyperparameter can be made fully adaptive without manual tuning.
