## Problem

Existing EEG-based auditory attention decoding methods predominantly rely on amplitude or power in the frequency domain, treating spectral representations as intensity maps while overlooking phase information. Neuroscientific evidence indicates that the brain tracks dynamic speech streams primarily through phase-locking (neural entrainment) rather than mere power modulation, so ignoring phase limits the ability to distinguish structured neural patterns from random noise, especially under low-SNR conditions and ultra-short decision windows.

## Input / Output

The model takes multi-channel EEG signals (64 or 32 channels) segmented into decision windows of 0.1 s, 1 s, or 2 s, which are first preprocessed (band-pass filtering, re-referencing, artifact removal) and then projected via Common Spatial Patterns into a C×T feature tensor (e.g., 64×128 for KUL at 128 Hz). The output is a binary classification label indicating the attended speaker (left or right), produced by a fully connected layer after spatial-temporal convolutional aggregation and adaptive average pooling.

## Method

PCRNet comprises two core modules: a Temporal Context Calibration (TCC) module that applies multi-scale self-attention with a sigmoid gating mechanism to refine Q/K/V projections before standard attention computation, and a Dual-Domain Integration (DDI) module that processes features in parallel temporal (cascade of dilated convolutions with rates {1,2,4}) and spectral branches. The spectral branch employs a Residual-Spectral Interface (RSI) block that performs 2D FFT on the input, computes an amplitude importance gate to generate a spectral mask, then independently refines the real and imaginary components through a dual-branch complex feature mixer modulated by the mask before recombining and applying inverse FFT. This phase-guided complex-domain processing is the key distinction from prior amplitude-only spectral methods, enabling dynamic suppression of noise-dominated frequencies while preserving phase structure. The two branch outputs are concatenated, fused through a transition block, and combined with a global residual connection; training uses standard supervised cross-entropy with Adam (lr=4e-3) and early stopping.

## Results

PCRNet achieves state-of-the-art auditory attention decoding accuracy across three public datasets (KUL, DTU, AVED) at three decision window lengths (0.1 s, 1 s, 2 s). On KUL, it reaches 98.4% / 98.5% / 98.8% accuracy (±2.83 / ±2.35 / ±1.70 SD) for 0.1 s / 1 s / 2 s windows, surpassing the previous best (MHANet) by 2.8 / 2.7 / 2.2 percentage points. On AVED (audio-only), it achieves 70.2% / 89.7% / 94.3%, and on AVED (audio-visual), 69.2% / 89.5% / 94.2%. The model contains only ~30.86 k trainable parameters (0.03 M) and requires 95.05 MFLOPs per 1-second window, making it suitable for low-latency deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCRNet outperforms all six baselines on KUL across every window length, with the largest margin (2.8 pp) over MHANet at 0.1 s (98.4% vs 95.6%). On AVED (both audio-only and audio-visual), PCRNet consistently leads, beating MHANet by 2.3 pp (AO, 0.1 s) to 2.2 pp (AV, 2 s) and SSF-DST by up to 17 pp (AO, 2 s: 94.3% vs 57.0%). On DTU, PCRNet wins at 1 s (85.3% vs 84.1% SSF-DST) and 2 s (85.6% vs 85.0% SSF-DST), but narrowly trails SSF-DST at 0.1 s (78.6% vs 79.7%), a gap the paper does not explicitly acknowledge. Compared to the older CNN-based baselines (SSF-CNN, MBSSFCC), PCRNet gains 12–22 pp across all settings. The most direct competitor is MHANet (Li et al., 2025), which also uses multi-scale attention; PCRNet's additional phase-aware spectral refinement (RSI) and dual-domain integration yield 1.5–3.5 pp improvements over MHANet on most dataset–window combinations, with the exception of DTU 0.1 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCRNet extends the dual-branch temporal-frequency paradigm established by DBPNet, MHANet, and SSF-DST, which model EEG in parallel time and frequency streams but operate exclusively on amplitude or power representations. Its key departure is introducing a phase-aware spectral refinement stage (the RSI block) that processes real and imaginary FFT components separately with an amplitude-gated complex feature mixer, grounded in neuroscientific evidence that phase-locking, not power modulation, is the primary mechanism of neural speech tracking. The multi-scale temporal attention with gating in the TCC module is directly inspired by MHANet's MTA design, while the dilated convolution cascade for long-range temporal context follows standard practice in temporal modeling. Overall, the paper repurposes the well-established dual-domain fusion architecture by adding a complex-domain spectral filtering step that prior AAD methods had omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strong performance under ultra-short 0.1-second decision windows (98.4% on KUL, 70.2% on AVED-AO) is practically significant for low-latency applications such as neuro-steered hearing aids, where sub-second response times are essential. The extremely low parameter count (~31K) makes the model deployable on resource-constrained hardware. However, several open questions remain: all experiments use within-subject evaluation with binary left/right classification, cross-subject and cross-domain generalization is untested, and the ablation reveals that the RSI (phase-aware) component contributes only marginally over the temporal branch alone on KUL (98.3% vs 98.4%), suggesting the phase-awareness claim may be less critical than the paper's framing implies. Broader validation across languages, channel counts, and multi-speaker scenarios would be needed before clinical deployment.
