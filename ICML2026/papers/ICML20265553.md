## Problem

Online infinite hidden Markov models (iHMMs) are highly sensitive to outliers and model misspecification: a single anomalous observation can corrupt the posterior over a regime's parameters and simultaneously trigger the creation of spurious latent states. Existing robust methods address either observation-space or state-space sensitivity in isolation, but no online iHMM provides provable robustness guarantees in both spaces simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265553/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of d-dimensional observations y_t paired with exogenous covariates x_t under a linear-Gaussian emission model with known noise covariance R_t. It produces one-step-ahead point predictions with uncertainty bounds, discrete latent state (regime) assignments, and full posteriors over per-regime parameter vectors θ_ℓ ∈ ℝ^m. State inference is performed over batches of B consecutive observations rather than at every time step, so regime-switching decisions are delayed by up to B−1 steps, trading a small detection delay for robustness against transient outliers.

## Method

BR-iHMM combines two complementary robustness mechanisms within a particle-learning framework. In the observation space, it replaces the standard Gaussian likelihood with a weighted likelihood using an inverse-multiquadratic (IMQ) weight on the Mahalanobis residual (WoLF), which downweights extreme observations while preserving conjugacy and closed-form Kalman-style updates. In the state space, it introduces a degenerate sticky HDP prior that enforces deterministic intra-batch state persistence (self-transition probability 1 within a batch, standard HDP at batch boundaries), preventing a single outlier from dominating the path posterior and inducing spurious regime creation. The key theoretical contribution is showing that observation-space robustness alone is provably insufficient (Theorem 4.1: state-space PIF remains unbounded), while the joint combination yields a uniformly bounded posterior influence function in both spaces (Theorem 4.2). The algorithm is implemented via Rao-Blackwellised particle learning with ESS-based resampling and a state-pruning heuristic for scalability.

## Results

BR-iHMM achieves the best one-step-ahead RMSE among all online methods across all three datasets: 46.1 on the synthetic high-dimensional regression (vs. 101.7 for iHMM and 103.8 for WoLF-iHMM, a ~55% reduction), 0.47 on hourly electricity demand (vs. 0.57 for iHMM), and 0.616 on order flow imbalance. In regime detection, BR-iHMM attains a positive predictive value of 0.963 and true positive rate of 0.996, far exceeding iHMM (PPV 0.551, TPR 0.363) and WoLF-iHMM (PPV 0.533, TPR 0.344), at the cost of a small detection delay of 3.4 time steps. The offline beam-sampling iHMM remains the overall best RMSE (e.g., 2.9 on synthetic) but is not robust to outliers and requires full data access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the offline beam-sampling iHMM (Van Gael et al., 2008), BR-iHMM is the best *online* method on all three datasets, with the gap largest on the synthetic task (46.1 vs. 2.9 RMSE); however, the offline model is not outlier-robust and creates spurious states assigned to outliers. Against Bayesian online changepoint detection (Adams & MacKay, 2007), BR-iHMM dominates on every dataset: 46.1 vs. 123.12 RMSE on synthetic, 0.47 vs. 0.80 on electricity, and 0.616 vs. 0.733 on OFI. Against the robust DSM-BOCD (Altamirano et al., 2023) in the well-log segmentation task, BR-iHMM is more resistant to spikes between observations 1000–1500 and reverts to previously encountered states at short regimes (e.g., near t=2500), whereas DSM-BOCD requires a recalibration period and misses the changepoint at t=1500. Internally, the ablation from iHMM → WoLF-iHMM → BR-iHMM shows that observation-space robustness alone (WoLF) does not fix the state-creation pathology (Theorem 4.1), and the batched state-space mechanism is what delivers the large RMSE and PPV gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate robustness mechanisms for online iHMMs: the WoLF weighted-likelihood update from Duran-Martin et al. (2024) for observation-space robustness, and a novel batched state-inference mechanism with a degenerate sticky HDP prior for state-space robustness. It builds directly on the particle-learning online iHMM of Rodriguez (2011) and the Generalised Bayes framework of Bissiri et al. (2016), but departs from prior work by proving (Theorem 4.1) that observation-space robustness alone is provably insufficient—outliers can still trigger spurious regime creation through the latent state posterior. The batched mechanism, drawing on the generalised posterior framework of Duran-Martin et al. (2025), is the key new architectural component that closes this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first online iHMM with provable bounded posterior influence in both observation and state spaces simultaneously, providing a principled robustness-adaptivity trade-off controlled by the batch size B. The 67% forecasting error reduction over competing online Bayesian methods across finance, energy, and synthetic high-dimensional settings demonstrates practical value for real-time regime-switching applications. Key open questions include adaptive (data-driven) selection of B, extension of the PIF bounds beyond linear-Gaussian emissions, and an analytical characterisation of the adaptivity-robustness trade-off rather than the current empirical one.
