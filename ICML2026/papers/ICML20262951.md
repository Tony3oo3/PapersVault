## Problem

Embodied agents replan frequently to recover from execution drift and partial observability, but each LLM-based replanning call carries an accumulated textual context that grows over time and across agents, causing replanning latency to develop heavy tails that miss real-time deadlines even when task success remains high. Most embodied evaluations report only task success or mean latency, masking persistent per-call deadline violations, and replanning cost is rarely treated as a first-class systems object with explicit budgets and auditability.

## Input / Output

The controller consumes a growing replanning context (task specification, recent observations/actions, failure traces, and multi-agent coordination messages), trigger signals (periodic, failure-driven, or hazard-driven), and a small controller state (cooldown/commit counters, consecutive-failure flags). It outputs a per-call replanning decision (replan or skip), a token budget B_t constraining the planner input length after compression, a latency SLO for the end-to-end replanning call path (including pruning, retrieval, and planning phases), the pruned context fed to the planner, the resulting plan, and phase-level audit logs attributing latency to each module.

## Method

BRACE is a budgeted replanning controller that applies a stability gate (cooldown and commit windows) to decide whether a trigger should invoke the planner, then selects a replanning mode and per-call token/latency budgets, with failure-aware overrides that temporarily relax constraints after repeated failures. E-RECAP is a trained progressive token-pruning module that scores each token's importance via a lightweight MLP predictor operating on intermediate transformer hidden states, prunes across a set of selected layers while always preserving head and tail tokens, and fills the remaining per-layer budget with top-scoring tokens. The predictor is trained with a combined objective preserving generation quality, matching gradient-based saliency magnitudes, and maintaining relative token ordering. The entire replanning call path is instrumented with phase-level accounting so that overhead from pruning, retrieval, and planning is attributed separately, enabling budget-matched comparisons where alternative context-reduction strategies are constrained to the same planner-input token budget.

## Results

Across three embodied platforms (Meta Habitat, RoboFactory, AirSim), BRACE + E-RECAP reduces replanning-call token counts by 62–92% (e.g., 235→20 tokens on Habitat, 2,934→1,114 on AirSim) and cuts SLO violation rates from 85.5–100.0% down to 4.7–50.0%, while maintaining 100% task success in the saturated regime. On the harder RoboFactory Pass-Shoe setting where open-loop, frozen-plan, and No BRACE all achieve 0% success, BRACE + E-RECAP reaches 80.0% success with only 4.6% SLO violations. E-RECAP alone yields 2.1–2.6× replanning-latency speedups with 71–76% token reduction under multi-agent context growth (K=1 to K=8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the budget-matched RoboFactory Pass-Shoe anchor (B=128), E-RECAP achieves the lowest P95 replanning latency (207.09 ms) and lowest SLO violation rate (0.49%) among all 12 compared methods. It outperforms the nearest quantization baselines KVTC (210.33 ms, 1.01%) and TurboQuant (221.57 ms, 1.73%) by 3–14 ms in P95 latency and by 0.5–1.2 pp in SLO violations. Selector-based methods (ReST-KV 231.95 ms / 2.27%, DefensiveKV 239.09 ms / 3.49%, Gated Attention 248.14 ms / 4.27%) and adapted compression/distillation methods (Robust Compression Boundary 244.21 ms / 3.74%, Cross Distillation 239.74 ms / 3.52%) all trail E-RECAP on both latency and SLO compliance. The weakest baseline, Sub-LIME, lags by 56.7 ms in P95 and 9.5 pp in SLO violations. All methods achieve 100% task success at this budget, so the differentiation is purely in tail-latency and deadline-miss behavior, confirming that learned progressive pruning preserves quality better than heuristic or quantization-based alternatives under matched token constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical metareasoning and resource-bounded rationality (Russell & Wefald, 1991; Lin et al., 2015) for the specific setting of LLM/VLA-based embodied replanning, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on plan quality and task success rather than the systems cost of repeated planner invocations. E-RECAP extends the token-pruning literature (which has targeted per-frame visual tokens or single-step queries) to the long-horizon, multi-agent replanning context that accumulates over time, while BRACE introduces a controller layer with cooldown/commit stability policies and phase-level accounting that has no direct analogue in prior embodied planning systems. The paper explicitly positions itself against the dominant evaluation paradigm (success + mean latency) by arguing that per-call tail latency and SLO violation rates are necessary first-class metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that embodied evaluations reporting only success and mean latency can mask near-universal deadline misses (85–100% SLO violations at 100% success), which would destabilize real-time closed-loop control in deployment. The harder-setting RoboFactory result—where open-loop, frozen-plan, and un-budgeted replanning all fail while BRACE+E-RECAP reaches 80% success with 4.6% SLO violations—demonstrates that budgeting is not merely an efficiency optimization but a correctness requirement under execution drift. Open questions include whether the controller policies generalize beyond the tested thresholds, how tightly BRACE can integrate with retrieval/caching backends without breaking the accounting contract, and whether the single-arm real-robot validation (PICKFRUIT/PUSHT) extends to dexterous or multi-robot physical settings.
