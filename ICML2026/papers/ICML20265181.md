## Problem

Tabular foundation models (e.g., TabPFN) achieve strong accuracy but incur heavy inference costs that scale poorly with context size, while lightweight DNNs can match their performance if the architecture is well-configured. However, existing tabular NAS methods rely entirely on expensive training-based evaluations, making progressive, budget-aware architecture search impractical, and no tabular-specific zero-cost proxy existed to enable fast training-free architecture scoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265181/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

pTNAS takes a tabular dataset (with heterogeneous numerical and categorical features) and a total time budget T_max as input. It outputs a dataset-specific DNN architecture defined as a tuple of hidden-layer widths (e.g., (h₁, h₂, h₃, h₄) drawn from a candidate set H), which is then trained and deployed for classification (AUC) or regression (MAE). The search space is discrete with fixed depth L=4 and variable widths, yielding |H|^L candidate architectures (up to ~160K across the benchmark).

## Method

pTNAS employs a filter-and-refine strategy with a budget-aware coordinator that holistically allocates time between two phases. In the coarse-grained filtering phase, a novel tabular-specific zero-cost proxy called pTProxy scores architectures via neuron-level saliency (|∂L/∂zₙ| ⊙ zₙ) recalibrated by a depth/width weight K_l/d_l, where d_l is a finite-difference layer sensitivity estimate; this captures both trainability and expressivity and is used to guide an evolutionary search over the architecture space. In the fine-grained refinement phase, the top-K candidates are trained under a Successive Halving schedule (η=3) that progressively eliminates weaker architectures while increasing per-architecture training budget. The coordinator solves a constrained optimization (Eq. 6) to choose M (filtering budget) and K (refinement budget) that maximize validation performance under T_max, enabling pTNAS to deliver a viable architecture within seconds and continuously improve as more budget is available.

## Results

On eight benchmark datasets (4 classification, 4 regression), pTNAS (T_max=10s) achieves the best overall average rank of 1.38 and average normalized score of 0.9946, outperforming all 15 baselines across both task types. In the progressive NAS comparison on Frappe, Diabetes, and Criteo, pTNAS reaches the globally best architecture up to 82.75× faster than EA-NAS and TabNAS. In end-to-end efficiency, pTNAS is 4.78× faster than TabPFN and 12.25× faster than FT-Transformer on regression tasks, while maintaining inference speed comparable to optimized tree-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pTNAS (T_max=10s) achieves the best overall average rank (1.38) and normalized score (0.9946) across all 15 baselines, winning on both classification (rank 1.00) and regression (rank 1.75). Among tree-based CMs, LightGBM (rank 6.31) and CatBoost (rank 6.25) are the strongest but still trail pTNAS by ~5 rank positions; TabPFN (rank 7.44) and TabICL (rank 4.50, classification-only) are competitive on classification but weaker on regression. Among deep tabular models, FT-Transformer (rank 6.06) and ARM-Net (rank 6.13) are the closest competitors, yet pTNAS leads by roughly 4.7–4.7 rank positions. LLM-based methods (TP-BERTa, Nomic, BGE) underperform substantially, with TP-BERTa achieving a normalized score of only 0.3993. Among NAS baselines, TabNAS (rank 6.25) and EA-NAS (rank 7.00) are significantly outperformed because the 10s budget allows them to explore only a limited number of architectures, whereas pTNAS's filter-and-refine strategy with pTProxy enables rapid exploration of hundreds of candidates before refining the top-K via Successive Halving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NAS literature from vision to tabular data by introducing the first progressive, budget-aware search strategy for tabular DNNs. It builds on Successive Halving/HyperBand for the refinement-phase scheduling and adapts the neuron-saliency concept from SynFlow into a tabular-specific zero-cost proxy (pTProxy) that jointly captures trainability and expressivity at the neuron level rather than the parameter level used by vision proxies. The key departure from prior tabular NAS work (TabNAS, EA-NAS, AgEBO-Tabular) is the filter-and-refine decomposition: a fast training-free evolutionary filtering phase followed by a lightweight training-based refinement, coordinated by a budget allocator—whereas all prior tabular NAS methods rely exclusively on expensive training-based evaluation and cannot deliver progressive improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

pTNAS provides a practical path to competitive tabular predictive performance without incurring the quadratic inference cost of Tabular Foundation Models, which is significant for latency-sensitive production settings. The introduction of NAS-Bench-Tabular (160K+ precomputed architecture-dataset pairs) fills a reproducibility gap that has hindered systematic tabular NAS research. However, the current search space is limited to fixed-depth MLPs with variable widths, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that incorporating deployment constraints (memory footprint, model size) remains open. Whether the neuron-level proxy generalizes to richer macro-topologies (attention, transformers) beyond the BlockMixed space in the appendix is an important open question.
