## Problem

Standard time-delay embedding in Convergent Cross Mapping (CCM) applies a rigid linear transformation that directly propagates measurement noise into the embedding space, producing geometrically rough shadow manifolds with unreliable distance-based neighborhoods. This geometric degradation undermines CCM's core mechanism of identifying causal drivers via k-nearest-neighbor cross-mapping, particularly under complex or high-level noise where Takens' theorem guarantees no longer hold in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262848/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multivariate time series (N variables over T time points) as input. During training, the global embedder consumes full multivariate snapshots x_t ∈ R^N, while the delay embedder consumes univariate temporal windows of length τ from each individual series. At test time, only the learned univariate shadow-manifold embeddings are used in the CCM pipeline, preserving CCM's fundamental premise that the shadow manifold is reconstructed solely from a single series. The output is a causal adjacency matrix derived from CCM convergence scores (AUC + monotonicity trend of cross-map correlation curves across increasing library sizes), binarized via an empirical threshold.

## Method

TopoDistill employs a two-stage training procedure. In stage 1, a global embedder (MLP encoder + single linear decoder) is trained on multivariate snapshots under a one-step-ahead prediction objective, learning a Koopman-like representation that implicitly denoises the system dynamics. In stage 2, the frozen global encoder provides a topological teacher distribution (tSNE-style conditional neighborhood probabilities), and a shared delay embedder (TCN or MLP with tanh activations) is distilled to match this distribution via KL divergence, while a temporal contrastive loss (InfoNCE with positive/negative temporal windows) enforces trajectory continuity and prevents manifold collapse. A manifold-smoothness-based early-stopping criterion (sum of squared consecutive embedding differences) halts training before the student overfits noise to match the teacher. At inference, the learned delay embeddings replace rigid TDE in the standard CCM cross-mapping pipeline, yielding smoother neighborhoods that better reflect true dynamical proximity.

## Results

TopoDistill achieves the best performance across all four benchmark datasets on all three metrics (AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, SHD). On the Double Pendulum dataset, TopoDistill-MLP reaches AUC-ROC 0.912, AUC-PR 0.908, and SHD 0.050, substantially outperforming the closest baseline CUTS+ (ROC 0.850, SHD 0.117). On the challenging real-world Causal Rivers dataset, where all baselines struggle, TopoDistill-MLP achieves AUC-ROC 0.795 and SHD 0.150, compared to the next-best CUTS (ROC 0.753, SHD 0.180). On fMRI, TopoDistill-TCN attains AUC-ROC 0.945 and SHD 0.084, surpassing PCMCI (ROC 0.920, SHD 0.099).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoDistill outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the most direct baseline CCM (which uses standard TDE), TopoDistill-MLP improves SHD from 0.167 to 0.050 on Double Pendulum and from 0.200 to 0.150 on Causal Rivers, while boosting AUC-ROC from 0.875 to 0.912 and 0.594 to 0.795 respectively. PCMCI is competitive on fMRI (ROC 0.920 vs. 0.945) but lags significantly on Double Pendulum (ROC 0.750 vs. 0.912) and Causal Rivers (ROC 0.663 vs. 0.795). CUTS+ is the strongest non-CCM baseline on Double Pendulum (ROC 0.850, SHD 0.117) but degrades sharply on UUMC (ROC 0.527) and Causal Rivers (ROC 0.512), where TopoDistill maintains ROC above 0.79. On the particularly difficult Causal Rivers dataset, all baselines cluster between ROC 0.51–0.75, while TopoDistill achieves 0.794–0.795, indicating that topology-guided manifold reconstruction is most beneficial under high noise and long-range dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CCM (Sugihara et al., 2012) framework by replacing its rigid time-delay embedding with a learnable neural delay embedder, guided by topology distillation from a multivariate global embedder trained under a Koopman-inspired one-step-ahead prediction objective. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of knowledge distillation (teacher-student neighborhood alignment à la t-SNE), contrastive learning (InfoNCE for temporal continuity), and dynamical-systems state-space reconstruction. It departs from prior CCM improvements (Latent CCM, deep Gaussian process CCM) by explicitly preserving CCM's univariate shadow-manifold premise while using multivariate observations solely as a supervisory signal, rather than incorporating them into the causal test itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCM is a widely used tool for causal discovery in chaotic and nonlinear dynamical systems, but its sensitivity to noise-induced manifold degradation has limited its reliability in real-world settings. TopoDistill offers a principled, theoretically grounded fix that maintains the method's interpretability and univariate premise. Key open questions include: whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional systems (the Causal Rivers subgraphs are still limited), whether the two-stage training and smoothness-based early stopping generalize without manual tuning, and whether the weak-to-moderate coupling assumption of CCM can be relaxed. The paper also acknowledges the lack of an automated threshold selection procedure for binarizing the causal adjacency matrix.
