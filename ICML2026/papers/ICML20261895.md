## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is the standard mechanism for steering conditional diffusion, but at high guidance scales it causes oversaturation, texture artifacts, and structural collapse because it performs Euclidean extrapolation that drives sampling trajectories off the high-density data manifold. Existing remedies such as dynamic thresholding, projection-based filtering, and schedule-based interventions address symptoms with hand-crafted heuristics rather than providing a single geometric objective that jointly determines the optimal guidance direction and its appropriate strength.

## Input / Output

MOG is a training-free, drop-in replacement for the CFG guidance step inside any standard diffusion sampler. At each denoising iteration it consumes the same unconditional and conditional score estimates already computed by the model, applies a matrix-free Riemannian metric correction to the conditional increment, and feeds the resulting guided score back into the sampler. It operates at native resolution (256×256 for ImageNet class-conditional, 512×512 for COCO text-to-image) and is architecture-agnostic, validated across UNet (SD-2.1, SD-XL), Transformer (DiT, SD-3.5), and Rectified Flow (FLUX.1) backbones with negligible added latency (≤1.08×).

## Method

MOG reformulates each guidance step as a local optimal control problem: minimize a quadratic transport cost under a positive-definite Riemannian metric M_t plus a linear alignment term that decreases the conditional energy. Solving the resulting convex subproblem yields a closed-form natural-gradient update s_MOG = s_0 + β(t) M_t^{-1} Δs, which preconditions the conditional increment to suppress off-manifold components while preserving efficient descent along the manifold. The practical MOG-Score instantiation constructs M_t as a rank-one anisotropic update to a scaled identity, using the normalized unconditional score as the local normal direction (λ_⊥/λ_∥ = 10), with the inverse applied via Sherman-Morrison in O(d) time. Auto-MOG further derives an adaptive β(t) from an energy-balance principle—setting the guidance energy proportional to the prior energy under M_t—so that guidance strength decays naturally with improving signal-to-noise ratio and compensates for weak conditional signals, eliminating manual scale tuning.

## Results

Auto-MOG achieves the best FID across all six evaluated models, with headline results of 8.78 on DiT-XL/2 (vs. 19.14 for CFG), 4.30 on EDM2-XXL (vs. 8.65 for CFG), and 17.84 on FLUX.1 (vs. 20.80 for CFG). On SD-XL at w=15, Auto-MOG reduces saturation from 0.28 (CFG) to 0.17 and contrast from 0.24 to 0.16, while simultaneously achieving the highest HPSv2 (29.00) and CLIP score (34.20). The method adds negligible computational overhead (1.08× latency vs. CFG) and requires no retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Auto-MOG outperforms all four baselines on FID across every model, with the largest margins over standard CFG (e.g., 8.78 vs. 19.14 on DiT-XL/2, a 54% reduction) and consistent 0.3–0.5 FID improvements over the strongest baseline APG (e.g., 4.30 vs. 4.94 on EDM2-XXL, 17.84 vs. 18.15 on FLUX.1). Against CFG++, Auto-MOG wins on FID in all six settings and additionally avoids CFG++'s tendency to wash out contrast (Auto-MOG maintains higher dynamic range while achieving lower saturation on SD-XL: 0.17 vs. 0.25). Compared to APG, Auto-MOG achieves higher HPSv2 and CLIP scores in 5 of 6 models, indicating better text-image alignment without the hazy appearance APG introduces. Against LF-CFG, Auto-MOG consistently achieves lower FID (e.g., 18.10 vs. 19.45 on SD-3.5) and higher alignment scores, showing that geometric direction correction is more effective than frequency-domain filtering. In the user study, Auto-MOG achieves 62.0% win rate over CFG (p<0.001) and 60.3% over CFG++ in expert evaluation, with the strongest advantage in Color Fidelity and Realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by Ho & Salimans (2022), reframing its Euclidean linear extrapolation as a local optimal control problem under a Riemannian metric. It unifies and subsumes several prior stabilization strategies—CFG++ (radial projection), APG (orthogonal projection to the unconditional score), and LF-CFG (spectral filtering)—as special cases of a single variational objective with different quadratic cost matrices, while arguing that the principled choice is the data-manifold geometry. The Auto-MOG adaptive schedule extends prior schedule-based interventions (rectified guidance, truncated CFG) by deriving the guidance strength from an energy-balance principle rather than hand-designed decay curves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOG provides a geometrically grounded explanation for the well-known failure of high-scale CFG (off-manifold drift) and offers a training-free, O(d)-overhead correction that consistently improves fidelity and alignment across UNet, DiT, MMDiT, and Rectified Flow architectures without retraining. Auto-MOG's elimination of manual guidance-scale tuning is practically significant for production systems. Key limitations include the rank-1 anisotropic metric (capturing only a single normal direction), dependence on the quality of the unconditional score as a manifold proxy, and a static anisotropy ratio; extending to curvature-adaptive, higher-rank, or spatio-temporal metrics for video remains open.
