## Problem

Standard DPO treats each preference comparison as a flat winner-versus-loser label over whole sequences, providing no mechanism to reward how an answer is derived (its reasoning structure) or to modulate learning pressure when preferences are noisy or logically brittle. This limitation is most acute on reasoning- and factuality-sensitive tasks, where fluent but structurally incoherent responses can be preferred over coherent ones, and where a small fraction of mislabeled or fragile pairs can destabilize training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard pairwise preference data (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) and, for each candidate, elicits a small directed graph of 3–6 atomic sub-claim nodes and support/dependency edges using a frozen auxiliary LLM and an offline NLI classifier. Node-level correctness probabilities from a calibrated verifier and K=3 re-elicited perturbed graphs supply the uncertainty signal. The output is an aligned LLM policy (7–8B or 70B) trained with a modified DPO objective; no online rollouts, value head, or separately trained reward model are required, and the reference policy is either frozen or updated via EMA (ρ=0.995).

## Method

TUR-DPO augments the DPO logit with a shaped reward r_φ that linearly combines a semantic score (NLI-verified factuality + task metric − hallucination penalty) and a topology score (minimal path coverage − cycles − dangling nodes − contradictions), minus an uncertainty penalty. Epistemic uncertainty is estimated as the variance of topology scores plus Jensen-Shannon divergence across K re-elicited graphs, while aleatoric uncertainty uses coverage-corrected binary entropy over node verification probabilities; the average pair uncertainty maps to a per-pair weight w ∈ [w_min, 1] that attenuates noisy comparisons. The final loss is −w · log σ(β[Δlog π_θ − Δlog π_ref] + γ Δr_φ), which reduces exactly to vanilla DPO when γ→0. Theoretically, the objective is shown to be a weighted Bradley-Terry negative log-likelihood with a shaped margin, and a bias bound (Lemma 2.1) demonstrates that clipping weights to [w_min, 1] limits worst-case estimator bias linearly in (1−w_min)·ε under label-flip noise. Graph extraction is fully offline and decoupled from the trainable policy to avoid circular self-reinforcement, and the method is modular: any component (topology, uncertainty, shaped reward) can be disabled by zeroing its coefficient.

## Results

Across six benchmarks spanning mathematical reasoning, factual QA, summarization, and dialogue, TUR-DPO consistently outperforms DPO by 2.6–4.1 points (e.g., GSM8K EM 62.8 vs. 58.7; MATH EM 36.0 vs. 33.4; BBH Acc. 46.7 vs. 43.9). On reasoning-centric tasks, TUR-DPO matches or exceeds PPO-RLHF (GSM8K human eval: 63.1 vs. 62.0; MATH: 36.4 vs. 35.5), while PPO retains a narrow 0.7-point edge on stylistic HH dialogue (67.9 vs. 67.2 human win-rate). TUR-DPO also reduces Expected Calibration Error from 0.101 (DPO) to 0.087, and retains 89% of its clean-data win-rate under 20% label corruption versus only 81% for DPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), TUR-DPO wins on every task and every evaluation protocol, with the largest margins on multi-step reasoning (GSM8K +4.1 EM, MATH +2.6 EM, BBH +2.8 Acc.) and a consistent +3.6–4.1 point win-rate gain on summarization and dialogue. Against IPO (Azar et al., 2023), TUR-DPO outperforms by 2.6–4.1 points across all benchmarks, confirming that the structured topology reward and uncertainty weighting provide benefits beyond the Bradley-Terry classification formulation. Against PPO-RLHF (Schulman et al., 2017), TUR-DPO matches or exceeds PPO on all reasoning and QA tasks (GSM8K +1.1, MATH +0.9, BBH +1.2 human win-rate) while PPO retains a marginal 0.7-point advantage on stylistic HH dialogue; however, TUR-DPO requires no online rollouts, value head, or KL scheduling. Among RL-free baselines (Table 10), TUR-DPO leads ORPO by 3.4 EM, SimPO by 2.7 EM, KTO by 4.1 EM, and IPO by 3.9 EM on GSM8K, while also achieving the lowest ECE (0.087 vs. 0.101–0.108) and highest structural coherence score (70.4 vs. 58.3–61.2), indicating that the gains stem from explicit topology and uncertainty signals rather than architectural differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

TUR-DPO extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by injecting two signals absent from all prior RL-free preference objectives: a lightweight reasoning-topology score (drawing on graph-based explanation analysis from Da et al., 2025) and a calibrated uncertainty weight (building on semantic entropy and finite-sample bias corrections from McCabe et al., 2025 and Lamb et al., 2025). Unlike ORPO, SimPO, KTO, and IPO—which all treat responses as flat sequences—TUR-DPO shapes the DPO logit margin with structural coherence (acyclicity, path coverage, contradiction) and down-weights brittle preference pairs via instance-weighted Bradley-Terry estimation (Huang et al., 2006). The method remains RL-free and rollout-free, positioning it as a drop-in augmentation of existing DPO pipelines rather than a replacement for PPO-based RLHF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners who want PPO-competitive reasoning performance without the operational complexity of online rollouts, value heads, and KL scheduling, TUR-DPO offers a practical middle ground with only ~15% compute overhead over vanilla DPO. Key limitations include dependence on graph extraction quality (formatting errors become the dominant failure mode at 41%), weaker signals on creative or non-deterministic tasks where support links are absent, and a limited re-elicitation budget (K=3) that may miss edge-case structural failures. Open questions include whether topology-aware signals transfer to video-language models and MoE architectures, whether conformal prediction can replace the current heuristic uncertainty estimator, and how the method behaves under very long multi-turn conversations where graph extraction reliability degrades.
