## Problem

Existing neural architecture search and hyperparameter optimization methods require users to manually design search spaces (e.g., context-free grammars), and categorical models of machine learning such as the Para-construction have not been leveraged to automate the construction of model classes. This paper asks whether search spaces for parametric functions can be automatically synthesized from typed component specifications, with the categorical structure providing compositional guarantees of well-formedness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a typed FCLP repository (combinators with parametric intersection types encoding PyTorch modules and their valid hyperparameters), a target parameterized intersection type that uses a special 'any' literal to control structural variance and bound search space size, and a scoring function (test loss on held-out data). It produces a parameterized tree grammar (the search space) equipped with sampling and search procedures, from which optimal combinatory terms are extracted and interpreted as executable PyTorch modules. The type system guarantees that all synthesized terms are structurally well-formed with respect to the Para-construction, and the literal collections L0/L1/L2 encode the full string diagram structure (nodes, parallel layers, sequential layers) as data.

## Method

The paper formalizes the Para-construction as an algebraic theory of string diagram constructors (node, edges, swap, repara, before, beside, ε) with term equations encoding categorical laws, then encodes this theory as an FCLP repository using stratified intersection types (D0/D1/D2) and a term rewriting system that forbids left-hand sides to enforce unique normal forms. A key design choice is stratifying the type of string diagrams into three levels—atomic constructors, parallel compositions, and sequential compositions of parallel compositions—which enforces the interchange law and eliminates the repara constructor (reparameterizations are expressed by reusing combinators with different arguments). The CoSy synthesizer is extended with top-down sampling (adapted from property-based testing) and two search methods: evolutionary operators (crossover/mutation on parameterized tree grammars) and Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian Process surrogate. The central empirical finding is that kernels operating on the directed acyclic multigraph (DAMG) structure induced by the categorical string diagram representation achieve stronger alignment with the objective function than kernels on the syntactic tree of combinatory terms, demonstrating that the categorical structure provides a useful inductive bias for surrogate-based optimization.

## Results

Across all synthesized search spaces, the Weisfeiler-Lehman kernel applied to the directed acyclic multigraph (DAMG) representation consistently achieves the best surrogate modeling performance: mean NMSE of 0.781 (Global) and 0.874 (Refined), and mean R² of 0.367 (Global) and 0.431 (Refined), outperforming the subtree kernel (mean NMSE 0.837/0.946; mean R² 0.158/0.244) and the WL kernel on tree structure (mean NMSE 1.035/1.046; mean R² −0.394/−0.124). Bayesian optimization using the DAMG kernel identifies better candidates than random search in all configurations within the same evaluation budget. The best discovered model approximates the target piecewise-affine function f*(x) = 10 − |x| with coefficients 1.001x + 10.0011 (x ≤ 0) and −0.9996x + 10.0011 (x > 0), using only a Copy, ReLU, and two Linear layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external NAS or HPO baselines; instead, it ablates over kernel choice and search-space refinement. The DAMG kernel (WL kernel applied to the categorical string-diagram structure) dominates both the subtree kernel and the WL-on-tree kernel on mean NMSE and R² across all search spaces, with the largest gap on R² (0.367 vs. 0.158 for Tree-Global, and 0.431 vs. −0.124 for WL-Refined). Refined search spaces (with restricted structural variance) generally improve surrogate learnability over their global counterparts, though the effect is kernel-dependent. In the optimization comparison (Appendix B), Bayesian optimization with the DAMG kernel consistently outperforms random search under the same evaluation budget across all synthesized search spaces, confirming that the categorical structure provides a useful inductive bias for surrogate-based exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of categorical deep learning (the Para-construction of Gavranović et al. and Cruttwell et al.) and component-oriented program synthesis (FCLP and CoSy of Dudenhefner et al.). It departs from prior NAS search-space approaches (BOHNAS, Einspace, Negrinho et al.) that require users to hand-write context-free grammars or domain-specific languages, instead automatically deriving search spaces from typed component specifications via intersection types and term rewriting. The key conceptual move is formalizing the Para-construction's string diagrams as an algebraic theory whose constructors become FCLP combinators, thereby making the categorical compositional structure directly exploitable by the CoSy synthesizer and its newly added sampling/evolutionary/Bayesian-optimization procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, language-agnostic route to automated search-space design that guarantees structural well-formedness of synthesized models, potentially reducing the manual effort in NAS and HPO. The finding that kernels operating on the categorical DAMG representation outperform purely syntactic tree kernels suggests the Para-construction's structure encodes functionally relevant inductive bias, though this is demonstrated only on a single 1D piecewise-affine regression toy. Major open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or multi-modal settings, whether the finite-search-space restriction (required for fast sampling) is too limiting for practical architectures, and the absence of a categorical semantics for FCLP that would allow formal soundness/completeness guarantees for the synthesized models.
