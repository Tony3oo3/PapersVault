## Problem

Stochastic mirror descent (SMD) is a foundational first-order method for constrained convex optimization, but its convergence guarantees presume light-tailed gradient noise with finite variance. In practice, gradient noise in deep learning is empirically heavy-tailed with potentially infinite variance, and it remains unclear whether SMD retains its convergence properties in this regime. This paper asks whether SMD remains robust under heavy-tailed noise and characterizes precisely how the convergence rate degrades as the noise tail index decreases below 2.

## Input / Output

The method takes a convex (or relatively strongly convex) objective over a compact convex set, a Bregman regularizer that may be steep (so gradients can be unbounded at the boundary), and a stochastic gradient oracle whose noise has bounded p-th central moments for p ∈ (1,2]—explicitly permitting infinite variance. The output is a suite of guarantees with explicit dependence on noise intensity parameters: ergodic convergence rates for time-averaged iterates, concentration bounds on the fraction of time spent near the optimum, first-passage (hitting time) estimates, and last-iterate convergence rates, each decomposed into tame (finite-variance) and heavy (infinite-variance) noise contributions.

## Method

The paper introduces the Lévy mirror flow (LMF), a continuous-time SDE for stochastic dual averaging driven by a centered Lévy process with finite p-th moments, whose Lévy-Itô decomposition separates the noise into a diffusion component, a short-jump component, and an unbounded long-jump component. The key technical contribution is a weak Itô formula for Lipschitz-smooth convex functions that are not C², which is necessary because standard Itô's formula does not apply to Lévy processes with unbounded jumps and the Fenchel coupling is only Lipschitz-smooth. Applying this formula to the η-deflated Fenchel coupling yields explicit energy bounds that decompose into tame and heavy noise terms, giving O(ε^{-p/(p-1)}) ergodic rates in the convex case and geometric convergence to an uncertainty ball in the relatively strongly convex case. These continuous-time results then percolate to matching discrete-time guarantees for SDA, lazy mirror descent, and SMD, where each discrete-time bound decomposes into a continuous-time term plus a discretization term, confirming the Lévy model as a faithful proxy for the heavy-noise regime.

## Results

In the convex case, the Lévy mirror flow (LMF) attains ε-optimality within Õ(ε^{-p/(p-1)}) time with learning rate η(t) = 1/t^{1/p}, yielding the rate E[f(X̄(t))] − min f = O(1/t^{(p-1)/p}). In the relatively strongly convex case, LMF orbits converge geometrically to an uncertainty ball of radius δ_η = √(2/μ · [ηV_tame² + η^{p-1}V_heavy^p]), and the hitting time to a δ-ball scales as Õ(δ^{-2p/(p-1)}). The discrete-time SDA algorithm achieves the matching rate Õ((σ^p/ε)^{1/(p-1)}) iterations for ε-optimality under relative strong convexity, with the fraction of time spent δ-far from the optimum bounded by O(δ_η²/δ²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without empirical baselines, so comparisons are made against existing convergence rate results in the literature. In the tame regime (p = 2), the paper recovers the classical O(1/√t) ergodic rate for convex SMD under Brownian noise established by Mertikopoulos & Staudigl [70], and the O(1/ε) rate for strongly convex optimization. In the heavy-noise regime (p < 2), the convex rate O(t^{-(p-1)/p}) matches the optimal lower bound of Nemirovski & Yudin [77] and the results of Vural et al. [108] for SMD under infinite variance. The discrete-time strongly convex rate Õ((σ^p/ε)^{1/(p-1)}) is comparable to Liu [57] for dual averaging, but the paper notes it does not match the Ω(ε^{-p}/(2(p−1))) lower bound of Zhang et al. [117]; the authors attribute this gap to their setting allowing unbounded gradients (steep regularizers) whereas [117] assumes bounded gradients. The key qualitative contribution is that the Lévy SDE model faithfully reproduces the discrete-time behavior, with discrete bounds decomposing into a continuous-time term plus a discretization term, validating the continuous-time analysis as a principled proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time stochastic mirror flow (SMF) of Mertikopoulos & Staudigl [70]—which models SMD under Brownian noise—by replacing the driving martingale with a centered Lévy process, yielding the Lévy mirror flow (LMF). The discrete-time analysis builds on the SMD/SDA/LMD template of Nemirovski & Yudin [77] and Nesterov [81], but departs from prior heavy-noise results (e.g., Vural et al. [108], Liu [57]) by working under general Bregman divergences with steep regularizers rather than Euclidean norms, and by providing concentration and first-passage estimates alongside ergodic convergence. A key technical departure is the introduction of a weak Itô formula for Lipschitz-smooth but non-C² convex functions driven by Lévy integrators, which is new in the stochastic analysis literature and necessary because the Fenchel coupling energy function is not twice differentiable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Lévy mirror flow provides a principled continuous-time surrogate for SMD under heavy-tailed noise, and the fact that discrete-time bounds decompose into a continuous-time term plus a discretization term validates this model as a faithful proxy—opening a new analytical pathway for studying heavy-noise optimization. The robustness result (SMD retains convergence with only a smooth rate deterioration as p decreases below 2) is reassuring for practitioners, but the derived rates for strongly convex objectives do not match the Ω(ε^{-p}/(2(p-1))) lower bounds of Zhang et al. [117], a gap the authors acknowledge and conjecture can be closed with a different energy function. Open directions include sharpening the relative strong convexity analysis and extending the LMF framework to sampling from constrained spaces.
