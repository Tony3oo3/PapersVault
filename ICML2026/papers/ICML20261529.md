## Problem

Existing Efficient Distillation (EDistill) methods for LLMs achieve state-of-the-art general ability benchmarks but suffer from severe multi-step reasoning degradation, a phenomenon the authors term reasoning collapse. The paper identifies the geometric root cause: randomly initialized projection matrices in width-reduced EDistill undergo a winner-take-all singular value dynamic that collapses the effective rank (eRank) of hidden representations, rendering tokens indistinguishable and undermining reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen teacher LLM (e.g., Llama3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B/7B), a tiny calibration set of only 15 samples (from Nemotron-Pretraining-Dataset, processed in under 3 minutes on a single GPU) to estimate per-channel activation importance, and a 10B–20B token training corpus. It outputs a hardware-friendly student model with a natively reduced hidden dimension D′ < D, where all teacher weights are frozen and only the inserted projection matrices are trained before being merged into the final architecture.

## Method

RED replaces the standard small random initialization (N(0, 0.02²)) of width-reduced projection matrices with an activation-aware channel-selection initialization: the down-projection Q(0) is set to a binary matrix H that selects the D′ most important channels (ranked by mean absolute activation across layers on the calibration set), and the up-projection O(0) = Hᵀ. This makes the product M(0) = Q(0)O(0) a symmetric projection matrix whose singular values are all in {0, 1} with zero initial time-derivatives, provably preventing the exponential singular-value divergence (ṡσ ∝ σ) that random initialization triggers under gradient flow. The theoretical analysis formalizes how this avoids eRank collapse and bounds the resulting token distinguishability via Total Variation distance, while empirically RED recovers reasoning to 2–4× the level of LRC while matching or exceeding SOTA general ability at the same 10B–20B token budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RED dominates its closest competitor LRC across all three model sizes on both Gen.Avg. and Reas.Avg., with the largest gap at 1.7B (Reas.Avg. 0.25 vs 0.06, a 4.2× margin) and 1.5B (0.28 vs 0.09, 3.1×). Against full-parameter baselines, RED-1.5B (Reas.Avg. 0.28) exceeds SmolLM2-1.7B (0.22) and MiniCPM-1.0B (0.26) on reasoning while matching or beating them on Gen.Avg. (0.61 vs 0.61 and 0.57), using only 10B tokens versus their trillions. RED-1.7B (Gen.Avg. 0.64, Reas.Avg. 0.25) outperforms InternLM2-1.8B (0.59, 0.16) and Llama3.2-1.2B (0.55, 0.17) on both metrics. Among LoRA-style and depth-reduced EDistill baselines (SliceGPT, LLM-Pruner, LLMStreamline, ReplaceMe), all show Reas.Avg. below 0.10, confirming that RED's activation-aware initialization is critical for preserving reasoning. At the 4B scale (Table 5), RED-4B (Reas.Avg. 0.33, Gen.Avg. 0.69) surpasses LRC-4B (0.18, 0.69) and Minitron-4B (0.21, 0.64), approaching Gemma3-4B (0.40, 0.66) despite a 200× smaller training corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the width-reduced Efficient Distillation (EDistill) paradigm introduced by LRC (Hao et al., 2025), which inserts learnable projection matrices to reduce hidden dimension while freezing teacher parameters. RED replaces LRC's small random initialization with activation-aware channel-selection matrices, borrowing the importance estimation strategy from Minitron (Muralidharan et al., 2024). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior EDistill work by reframing the evaluation criterion: rather than treating general-ability benchmarks as sufficient, it identifies and diagnoses a previously overlooked failure mode (reasoning collapse) through the lens of representation geometry (eRank) and singular value dynamics, positioning the contribution as both a theoretical analysis and a targeted initialization fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of reasoning collapse in SOTA EDistilled LLMs is a critical diagnostic for the model-compression community: methods that appear competitive on general benchmarks may be fundamentally broken for multi-step reasoning, code generation, and mathematical tasks. The proposed fix (activation-aware initialization) is trivially simple to implement and likely to be adopted as a default in any width-reduced distillation pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to depth-reduced EDistill, whether it interacts favorably with post-training (RL, instruction tuning), and whether the linear autoencoder proxy faithfully captures the nonlinear dynamics of full Transformer training. The paper also notes the need to evaluate RED on strong reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) and multimodal architectures.
