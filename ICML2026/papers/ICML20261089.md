## Problem

Existing low-variance surrogate losses for generative model training (KL_sq, Vargrad, Trajectory Balance) are inherently tied to the reverse KL divergence, producing mode-seeking behavior where the model collapses onto a few high-probability modes. In generative tasks like drug discovery or exploratory agents, mode-covering behavior is preferred, but no principled extension of these off-policy-valid, low-variance losses to other f-divergences existed. This paper constructs a complete family of surrogate losses parameterized by arbitrary f-divergences, enabling continuous control over the mode-seeking/mode-covering trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method takes model parameters θ, an unnormalized reward/energy function R(y), and a batch of B samples, operating on the log-probability difference Δ_θ(y) = log p_θ(y) − R(y). It produces a scalar batch-wise loss and gradient estimates, with the intractable partition function Z estimated via a batch-wise generalized deviation (DevGrad) that solves for a scalar shift C minimizing the batch loss, avoiding a separate forward pass. A tempering parameter β rescales the loss argument by β and the output by 1/β to prevent exponential overflow when rewards are large relative to β (e.g., β=0.005 with rewards in [0,1] would otherwise produce magnitudes of e^200).

## Method

The core contribution is a bidirectional correspondence between translation-invariant loss functions on log-probability differences and f-divergences: given any convex generator f with f(1)=0, f'(1)=1, f''(1)=1, the loss L_f(Δ) = ∫₀^Δ (f'(exp(t)) − f'(1))dt has on-policy auto-differentiated gradients matching the f-divergence gradient, while off-policy it retains the same global minimizer (Propositions 4.2 and 4.4). To handle unnormalized targets, DevGrad generalizes Vargrad by replacing the batch-wise variance with a batch-wise generalized deviation, estimated by minimizing the loss over a scalar shift C, which also centres the score-function coefficients for variance reduction. A tempering mechanism (Definition 4.7) scales the loss by 1/β and its argument by β, preventing numerical overflow in high-reward regimes and recovering the KIMI K2 loss as the tempered reverse-KL special case. The α-divergence family provides a continuous knob between mode-covering (α→0, Forward KL) and mode-seeking (α→1, standard Trajectory Balance), with annealing α during training empirically achieving the best diversity-reward trade-off across SynFlowNet molecule discovery, conditional diffusion sampling, and asynchronous LLM fine-tuning.

## Results

The paper's results are primarily qualitative, presented through training curves and sampling visualizations rather than numerical tables. On SynFlowNet (DRD2), α=0.75 yields more diverse high-reward molecules than standard Trajectory Balance (α=1), while α=1.2 causes mode collapse; annealing α from 0.75 to 1.2 achieves the best diversity-reward trade-off. On conditional MNIST diffusion sampling, standard TB oversamples digits 0 and 6 for even digits, whereas f-divergence losses (particularly annealed α-divergences) sample target digits more evenly. On asynchronous LLM fine-tuning (GSM8k + Hendrycks MATH, 50-step delay, 300 steps, 3 seeds), the Reverse KL, Pearson χ², and Forward KL tempered DevGrad losses all exhibit predictable entropy-reward trade-offs across Qwen 2.5 (3B–14B) and OLMo-2-7B models, while the optimized PPO baseline (Intellect) produces inconsistent results across model classes due to off-policy instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Trajectory Balance (Malkin et al., 2022; α=1), the f-divergence loss family wins on mode-covering metrics: in SynFlowNet, α=0.75 produces measurably more diverse molecule sets at comparable reward levels, and in conditional diffusion sampling, f-divergence losses distribute probability more evenly across target digits where TB collapses onto a subset (oversampling 0s and 6s for even digits). The annealed α-divergence (0.75→1.2) outperforms both pure TB and pure low-α losses by combining early exploration with late exploitation. Against the optimized PPO implementation from Intellect (2025, incorporating CISPO clipping and DAPO), the tempered DevGrad f-divergence losses (used without clipping, importance weighting, or masking) produce consistent, reproducible entropy-reward trade-offs across all tested model sizes and families, whereas PPO exhibits significant variance and instability under the 50-step asynchronous setting, failing to maintain consistent performance across the Qwen 2.5 and OLMo-2 model classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) the Trajectory Balance squared-error loss for GFlowNets (Malkin et al., 2022), (2) the VarGrad batch-wise variance normalization for unnormalized targets (Richter et al., 2020), and (3) the KIMI K2/K1.5 squared-KL loss for async LLM tuning (Team et al., 2025a,b; Bartoldson et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2025). The key theoretical bridge is the bidirectional correspondence between translation-invariant log-probability losses and f-divergences established by Hu et al. (2025) in the GFlowNet setting, which this paper adapts to policy optimization with unnormalized targets. The main departures from prior work are the DevGrad generalization (replacing batch variance with generalized deviations for arbitrary f-divergences) and the tempered loss formulation that prevents numerical overflow at small β, neither of which appears in the GFlowNet or LLM-tuning literature separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that this loss family gives practitioners a single knob (the f-divergence choice, e.g., the α parameter) to control mode-seeking vs. mode-covering behavior across GFlowNets, diffusion models, and LLMs, while retaining off-policy validity critical for the now-dominant asynchronous RL training paradigm. The demonstration that standard PPO/GRPO becomes unstable at 50-step delays while these losses remain stable is practically important for large-scale LLM post-training. Open questions include: the Jensen-Shannon divergence requires numerical root-finding for batch normalization (no closed form), the on-policy backward-policy gradients provably do not correspond to any f-divergence (Appendix B.1.3), and it remains unclear how these losses interact with other practical LLM training techniques like clipping and importance weighting at scale.
