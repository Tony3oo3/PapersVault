## Problem

Prior work promoting forward-mode AD (FMAD) and zero-order (ZO) optimization as memory-efficient alternatives to backpropagation for LLM fine-tuning typically benchmarks only against vanilla BP, omitting activation checkpointing—a widely supported BP variant that already reduces memory substantially. Additionally, critical metrics such as computational cost and wall-clock time to convergence are frequently absent, leaving the practical trade-offs of these methods inadequately characterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study fine-tunes pre-trained LLMs and vision-language models (LLaMA 3.1 8B, OPT 1.3B/6.7B/13B, BERT 110M/340M, RoBERTa 125M/355M, Qwen2-VL 7B) using QLoRA adapters (rank r=1, scale α=1) on 7 tasks spanning text classification, generation, reading comprehension, and visual question answering. FMAD and ZO variants additionally consume random perturbation vectors sampled from N(0, I_d) and require multiple forward passes per gradient estimate (two for ZO finite differences, one extra jvp pass for FMAD), whereas BP variants use standard autograd. Outputs are evaluated on four axes: test accuracy, wall-clock convergence time (minutes), peak GPU memory (GB), and total compute cost (TFLOPs until convergence).

## Method

The paper establishes a unified theoretical framework deriving convergence error bounds, memory costs, and per-iteration compute complexity for BP, checkpointed BP, FMAD, and ZO as functions of trainable dimensionality d and perturbation count n, showing that FMAD/ZO incur O(nd) compute and stricter learning-rate constraints that degrade convergence in high dimensions. Empirically, 16 gradient computation methods (including variance-reduced variants: gradient accumulation, multiple perturbations, adaptive sampling, SVRG, and sparse updates) are benchmarked across 7 tasks and 5+ model families, revealing that BP with activation checkpointing achieves up to 31.1% higher accuracy, 34.8% faster convergence, and 3.8× lower total compute at comparable memory. The authors also design two new variance reduction strategies for FMAD and ZO (improving accuracy by 7.5–14.0% but still falling short of BP) and identify a critical failure mode where adaptive optimizers like ADAMW trigger cascading jvp amplification, creating a positive feedback loop that destabilizes training and produces NaN gradients.

## Results

On LLAMA 3.1 (8B) fine-tuned with QLoRA (rank 1), BP-CHECKPOINTING achieves the highest accuracy across all 7 tasks: 94.2% (AGNews), 88.3% (BoolQ), 85.2% (MultiRC), 54.3% (GSM8K), 60.3% (MMLU), 87.1% (VQAv2), and 98.5% (TextVQA). FMAD-VANILLA and ZO-VANILLA lag behind BP by 4.5–27.5% and 5.6–31.1% respectively, with the largest gaps on BoolQ (−27.6% FMAD, −31.1% ZO) and TextVQA (−20.2% FMAD, −25.6% ZO). In terms of compute, BP-CHECKPOINTING requires only 65.2×10⁴ TFLOPs to converge versus 251.2×10⁴ for ZO-VANILLA (3.8× more) and 207.4×10⁴ for FMAD-VANILLA (3.2× more), while converging 21.4–34.8% faster in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BP with activation checkpointing (Chen et al., 2016) is the strongest baseline and the paper's central finding: it dominates all FMAD and ZO variants on accuracy, convergence speed, and total compute while using comparable or even less memory than ZO. Standard backpropagation (Rumelhart et al., 1986) in its vanilla form achieves the same accuracy as BP-CHECKPOINTING but runs out of memory at larger batch sizes, making checkpointing the practical choice. Among ZO methods, ZO-VANILLA (Chen et al., 2019) suffers the largest accuracy drops (up to −31.1% on BoolQ), while ZO-MULTIPLE (Feng et al., 2024) narrows the gap to −7.4% on AGNews but at 9.7× the per-iteration compute cost. ZO-SVRG (Liu et al., 2018) provides modest gains over ZO-VANILLA (e.g., 84.7 vs. 73.6 on AGNews) but still trails BP-CHECKPOINTING by 9.5%. ZO-SPARSE (Guo et al., 2025) performs worst overall, lagging BP by up to −35.1% on BoolQ. MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023) achieves 80.5% on AGNews (−13.7% vs. BP) and is limited to classification tasks. FMAD-VANILLA (Baydin et al., 2022) consistently outperforms ZO-VANILLA by 1.1–6.9% due to access to analytic Jacobian-vector products, but still trails BP-CHECKPOINTING by 4.5–27.5%, with its best variant (FMAD-ACCUMULATE) reaching only 88.0% on AGNews (−6.2% vs. BP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a corrective to the body of work on FMAD and ZO optimization for LLMs (MeZO, MeZO-SVRG, Revisiting ZO, ZO-Sparse, SPRY, FOMOH), which the authors argue suffered from a one-sided benchmarking narrative by comparing only against vanilla BP while omitting activation checkpointing and compute-cost metrics. Rather than proposing a new gradient estimation method, the paper carves new ground by (a) unifying BP, FMAD, and ZO under a single theoretical framework with explicit convergence, memory, and compute bounds as functions of dimensionality d and perturbation count n, and (b) introducing two new variance-reduction variants (gradient accumulation and adaptive perturbation sampling) for FMAD and ZO to test whether these methods can close the gap. The failure-mode analysis of jvp amplification under adaptive optimizers (ADAMW) is a genuinely new diagnostic that prior FMAD/ZO work did not explore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that BP with checkpointing dominates FMAD and ZO across accuracy (up to 31.1% higher), convergence speed (up to 34.8% faster), and total compute (up to 3.8× lower) at comparable memory—corrects a widely circulated narrative that perturbation-based methods are practical alternatives for LLM fine-tuning. The jvp amplification failure mode under ADAMW (a positive feedback loop where spikes inflate weights, which further amplify jvps) provides a mechanistic explanation for why these methods behave unpredictably in practice and may inform future optimizer design. Limitations include a single checkpointing granularity (library-dependent), default LORA rank of 1, and no full-fine-tuning comparison, leaving open whether finer-grained checkpointing or larger trainable parameter sets would narrow the gap. The work is most impactful as a methodological correction: it establishes that memory-efficient BP variants must be the baseline against which any 'memory-saving' gradient method is evaluated.
