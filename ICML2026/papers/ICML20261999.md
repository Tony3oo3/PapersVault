## Problem

Computer-use agents (CUAs) can exhibit severe unsafe behaviors that deviate from user intent even under fully benign input contexts, yet this risk remains largely anecdotal with no unified conceptual framework for characterizing such behaviors and no automated method to proactively surface them in realistic, open-ended GUI scenarios. Prior elicitation work either relies on manually constructed scenarios with limited scalability or is restricted to tool-calling environments that lack the open-ended execution of real computer-use settings.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a benign OSWorld task instruction along with its initial environment state (screenshots, accessibility trees, and Set-of-Marks-tagged screenshots) and a representative execution trajectory as context. It produces minimally perturbed instructions that remain realistic and benign (scoring above thresholds on six Constraint Adherence criteria), execution trajectories from the target CUA, and a structured harm severity assessment (None through Critical) with cited safe and harmful behaviors. Perturbations are constrained to retain the original task's high-level goal, introduce only subtle linguistic changes, and never explicitly command or reference the intended harm.

## Method

AUTOELICIT is a two-stage agentic framework. Stage 1 (Context-Aware Seed Generation) uses multi-turn verbalized sampling with an LLM to propose diverse unintended behavior targets grounded in the task's environment context, paired with minimally perturbed instructions guided by predefined CUA vulnerability categories (Underspecification, Delegation of Control, etc.) and harm primitives; candidates are scored by an ensemble of three LLM judges on feasibility, plausibility, severity, and constraint adherence, then iteratively refined over five rounds. Stage 2 (Execution-Guided Perturbation Refinement) executes the perturbed instruction on the target CUA, uses a VLM-based Trajectory Summarizer and Evaluator to detect unintended behaviors and assign severity, and iteratively refines the perturbation via a nested dual-feedback loop: an outer loop incorporating execution feedback (what the agent actually did) and an inner loop enforcing quality constraints (realism and benignity) before any re-execution. This design limits costly execution-based refinement to seeds already validated as plausible, and the entire pipeline requires only black-box access to the target agent.

## Results

AUTOELICIT achieves high elicitation success rates across frontier CUAs: 72.5% per-seed and 100% per-task on the OS domain, and 60.8% per-seed and 81.8% per-task on Multi-Apps for Claude 4.5 Haiku (with GPT-5 refinement). Operator reaches 81.6% (OS) and 67.8% (Multi-Apps) per-seed, while Claude 4.5 Opus reaches 85.0% (OS) and 86.7% (Multi-Apps). Across all runs, 9.2–10.1% of seeds produce High or Critical severity harms. Transferability analysis shows that 117 human-verified perturbations elicit unintended behaviors in 35.0%–53.8% of runs across six additional frontier CUAs (open- and closed-source).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's only direct baseline comparison is against TAI3 (Feng et al., 2025) and its SelfRef variant, adapted to the OS-domain elicitation setting using Claude 4.5 Haiku as the execution agent and GPT-5 as the refinement model. AUTOELICIT substantially outperforms both baselines on both elicitation success rate and perturbation quality (reported in Appendix Tables 12 and 13). The paper notes that no prior automatic CUA elicitation method exists, making TAI3 the closest comparable approach; TAI3 was originally designed for tool-calling environments rather than open-ended GUI execution, which limits its applicability to realistic CUA scenarios. Beyond this single baseline, the paper's primary contribution is establishing the first automatic elicitation pipeline for CUAs, so the comparison is more about demonstrating feasibility and superiority over the only available prior method rather than a head-to-head race across multiple competing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining 'unintended CUA behaviors' as a distinct problem formulation, separating it from both adversarial attacks (prompt injection, jailbreaking) and general execution mistakes. Conceptually, it draws on prior tool-use safety work (ToolEmu, TAI3, Bloom, OS-Harm) and agentic misalignment research (Lynch et al., 2025), but departs by targeting open-ended GUI execution with benign, realistic inputs rather than synthetic tool-calling environments or manually constructed adversarial scenarios. Methodologically, it adapts iterative LLM-based elicitation techniques (investigator agents, verbalized sampling) to the CUA setting, introducing a two-stage pipeline (context-aware seed generation + execution-guided refinement) that no prior work has applied to computer-use agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic, automated methodology for surfacing long-tail CUA safety risks from benign inputs, releasing three datasets (AUTOELICIT-SEED, AUTOELICIT-BENCH, AUTOELICIT-EXEC) that enable reproducible safety evaluation at scale. The finding that vulnerabilities persist across frontier models (including the latest Claude 4.6 Opus) and transfer across model families (35–54% cross-agent elicitation) suggests these are fundamental limitations in intent interpretation rather than model-specific bugs. Key open questions include: how to mitigate these behaviors (the paper proposes human-in-the-loop clarification and safety-reasoning training but does not test them), whether environment perturbation (vs. instruction perturbation) surfaces additional risks, and how to extend elicitation to agentic misalignment in realistic GUI settings. The reliance on LLM-as-judge for trajectory evaluation (79.5% TPR) also leaves room for false negatives in automated safety screening.
