## Problem

Amodal instance segmentation in retail is hindered by the absence of a large-scale, geometry-aware benchmark: existing datasets (COCOA, KINS, D2SA) cover generic or tabletop scenes, lack depth/occlusion-order/camera metadata, and provide no unified sim-to-real protocol. On the modeling side, prior methods either use separate query sets for amodal and visible tasks (fragmenting the representation) or rely on decoder-heavy architectures, making it difficult to jointly reason about occlusion under severe retail clutter.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image resized to 640×640 via shortest-edge scale jittering with aspect-ratio-preserving padding. It outputs, per detected instance, an amodal mask (complete silhouette including occluded regions), a visible mask (only the unoccluded portion), and a class label from a shared classification head. The synthetic 3D-IRAIS training set additionally provides depth maps, per-object occlusion order, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics as geometry-aware supervision signals, though the network itself is RGB-only at inference.

## Method

EUREKA is a streamlined encoder-only ViT (DINOv2/v3 ViT-L pretrained) that introduces a Unified Query mechanism: a single set of 200 learnable queries is injected into the final 4 encoder layers and jointly encodes visible appearance and amodal structural context in a shared latent space, avoiding the feature fragmentation of dual-query designs. Two independent mask heads (amodal and visible) project the refined queries onto an upsampled patch feature map, while a shared classification head exploits visibility-invariant category prediction. A conditional masked self-attention mechanism constrains query-to-patch interactions using intermediate masks whose source (Visible, Amodal, or pixel-wise Union) is selectable per dataset, enabling adaptive receptive fields for occlusion reasoning. Training uses joint Hungarian matching that combines classification and both mask losses into a single bipartite assignment, ensuring each query consistently models both the visible and amodal extent of the same instance. The companion MaviGen pipeline generates the 3D-IRAIS training set via physics-based or geometry-constrained 3D placement, stratified hemispherical viewpoint sampling, and Blender rendering that yields paired amodal/visible masks, depth, and occlusion-order annotations at scale.

## Results

On the primary in-domain benchmark 3D-IRAIS, EUREKA with DINOv3 achieves 89.75% amodal AP and 90.57% visible AP, surpassing the best CNN-based baseline (AISFormer, 82.67% amodal AP) by over 7 AP. On the public D2SA benchmark, EUREKA-DINOv3 sets a new state of the art with 84.84% amodal AP and 89.78% visible AP, exceeding the previous best (AISFormer+SAMEO, 74.1%/79.8%) by 10.7 and 10.0 AP respectively. For sim-to-real transfer (trained on 3D-IRAIS, tested on Real-IRAIS), EUREKA-DINOv2 achieves 37.10% amodal AP and 41.95% visible AP, dramatically outperforming prior methods that typically score 10–23% AP. On COCOA-cls, EUREKA-DINOv3 reaches 39.50% amodal AP and 40.59% visible AP, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EUREKA outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every evaluated dataset. On D2SA, the largest margin is against AISFormer+SAMEO (Tai et al., CVPR 2025), the previous SOTA, which EUREKA-DINOv3 surpasses by 10.7 AP (amodal) and 10.0 AP (visible); against the strongest CNN-based method VRSP-Net (Xiao et al., AAAI 2021), the gap is 14.6 AP amodal. On COCOA-cls, EUREKA-DINOv3 leads Shapeformer (Tran et al., IJCNN 2024) by 3.67 AP amodal and AISFormer (Tran et al., 2022) by 3.73 AP amodal, while also providing visible-mask metrics that most prior methods (Mask R-CNN, ORCNN, ASN, BCNet, Mask2Former) do not report. On the in-domain 3D-IRAIS benchmark, EUREKA-DINOv2 exceeds AISFormer (ResNet-101) by 6.43 AP amodal and 6.11 AP visible. For sim-to-real transfer to Real-IRAIS, EUREKA-DINOv2 (37.10% amodal AP) nearly doubles the best prior method APSNet (29.19%) and outperforms VRSP-Net (16.09%) by over 20 AP, confirming that the unified query + dual-mask-head design generalizes far better than single-task or decoupled-query architectures under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

EUREKA builds directly on the encoder-only ViT segmentation framework of Kerssies et al. (2025), adopting their mask-guided self-attention and mask annealing within the final M transformer layers, but departs from the single-task design by introducing a unified query mechanism with dual amodal/visible mask heads and a conditional attention mask that can be sourced from visible, amodal, or union predictions. On the dataset side, the paper positions IRAIS as filling a gap left by existing amodal benchmarks (COCOA, KINS, D2SA, MUVA) that lack retail-specific scenes, geometry-aware supervision (depth, occlusion order, camera parameters), and a controlled sim-to-real protocol. The MaviGen pipeline extends prior synthetic data generation approaches by combining physics-based stacking with geometry-constrained arrangement and stratified multi-view sampling, producing annotations that are richer than any prior amodal dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IRAIS benchmark and MaviGen pipeline provide the first scalable, geometry-aware retail amodal segmentation resource, enabling rigorous sim-to-real transfer studies that were previously infeasible due to the absence of matched synthetic/real splits with unified annotation protocols. The demonstrated transfer (37% amodal AP on real images from synthetic-only training, versus 10–23% for prior methods) suggests that geometry-aware synthetic supervision can substantially reduce reliance on expensive real-world amodal annotation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond retail to other dense-occlusion domains, how to handle the acknowledged failures on transparent/translucent objects, and whether the sim-to-real gap can be further closed with domain adaptation or larger real-data splits.
