## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) scales LLM reasoning but incurs prohibitive compute because it must sample G completions per prompt to estimate intra-group advantages, making forward-pass cost scale linearly with group size. Existing selective-utilization methods (e.g., GRESO, CPPO) reduce this cost by discarding low-value prompts or completions, but they alter the underlying sampling distribution and introduce systematic gradient bias, leading to suboptimal convergence. The open question is how to prune samples for efficiency while provably preserving an unbiased gradient estimator of the full-batch GRPO objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of variable-length text prompts (math word problems from GSM8K or MATH) and, for each retained prompt, generates G completions (reasoning chains, max 1024 tokens) from the behavior policy π_θ_old. A key practical detail is that DPPO reorganizes the dataset at initialization via Dense Prompt Packing: a window-based greedy algorithm aggregates multiple shorter prompts into compact sequences of length ≤ L_max, so the effective batch contains fewer but denser sequence slots. The output is the updated policy model π_θ, evaluated by Pass@1 accuracy on held-out mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

DPPO formulates selective data utilization as a two-level importance sampling process. At the completion level, it prunes responses whose absolute advantage |A_i| falls below the per-prompt mean absolute advantage threshold with probability r_o; at the prompt level, it uses a carry-forward historical difficulty score H_t(q) (mean absolute advantage from the previous epoch) to rank prompts and prune the lowest-scoring 50% with probability r_q. Retained samples are reweighted by analytically derived rescaling factors γ(o,q) = C_o(q)/(1−P_t^o(o)) and γ(q) = C_q/(1−P_t^q(q)), where C_o and C_q are normalization constants computed from the retained-to-total sample ratios, guaranteeing that the expected gradient under the pruned distribution equals the full-batch GRPO gradient. A variance analysis (Appendix D) shows that under score-based pruning the estimator variance does not exceed the full-batch variance for moderate pruning rates. To counteract the data sparsity and memory fragmentation introduced by pruning, DPPO additionally applies Dense Prompt Packing, a window-based greedy strategy that maximizes valid token density per sequence slot and sustains GPU saturation comparable to the unpruned baseline.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution evaluation (Table 2), DPPO with r_q=0.9, r_o=0.9 achieves 36.80% average accuracy across six math benchmarks (MATH500, AIME25, AIME24, AMC, Minerva, Olympiad) on Qwen3-4B with a 2.37× training speedup over GRPO, and 36.87% on Qwen3-8B with 1.90× speedup. On the in-distribution MATH training set, the most aggressive pruning configuration yields 80.15% accuracy (+3.15% over GRPO's 77.00%) on Qwen3-4B while reducing GPU-hours from 137.04 to 57.88. DPPO also generalizes as a plug-in to other RL algorithms, achieving 1.98× speedup over DAPO and 2.45× over GSPO on GSM8K with negligible accuracy change.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPPO outperforms all three heuristic pruning baselines on average accuracy across both model scales. Against GRPO (the full-batch baseline), DPPO improves average accuracy by +3.36% (Qwen3-4B) and +3.32% (Qwen3-8B) while achieving 2.37× and 1.90× speedup respectively, with the largest single-benchmark gain on AIME24 (+10.00% over GRPO). Against CPPO (completion-level pruning), DPPO leads by +5.22% (4B) and +5.16% (8B) in average accuracy with roughly 1.7× greater speedup, indicating that CPPO's uncorrected pruning introduces significant gradient bias. Against GRESO (prompt-level selection), DPPO gains +1.70% (4B) and +1.42% (8B) in accuracy at comparable speed (2.37× vs 2.31× for 4B; 1.90× vs 1.88× for 8B), confirming that importance-sampling-based rescaling provides a meaningful advantage over heuristic filtering. As a plug-in to other RL algorithms, DPPO accelerates DAPO by 1.98× (4B) / 1.92× (8B) and GSPO by 2.45× (4B) / 2.46× (8B) on GSM8K with accuracy changes within ±0.3%, demonstrating algorithm-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeek-R1) and addresses a gap left by recent selective data utilization methods such as GRESO (prompt-level) and CPPO (completion-level), which prune low-value samples heuristically without correcting the resulting distributional shift. The key conceptual departure is reformulating dynamic pruning as a hierarchical importance sampling process, where retained samples are reweighted by mathematically derived rescaling factors to preserve unbiased gradient estimation. The Dense Prompt Packing component draws on standard sequence-packing ideas from inference serving but applies them specifically to counteract pruning-induced sparsity during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3× training speedup with maintained or improved accuracy is practically significant for the increasingly compute-intensive RL training of reasoning LLMs, and the plug-and-play compatibility with GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO broadens its applicability. The counterintuitive finding that aggressive pruning (90%) can outperform full-batch GRPO by ~3% on math benchmarks suggests an implicit curriculum effect, though this is validated primarily on mathematical reasoning tasks. Open questions include whether the variance increase at high pruning rates (factor ~3×) degrades convergence in longer training runs, whether the carry-forward heuristic for prompt-level difficulty estimation remains valid in non-stationary training regimes, and whether the accuracy gains generalize to non-mathematical reasoning domains.
