## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods—whether token-dropping or head-reallocation—degrade substantially on reasoning LLMs because they cannot identify which attention heads are critical for maintaining chain-of-thought consistency and controlling generation termination. Token-dropping uniformly evicts tokens across all heads, inevitably discarding reasoning-critical information, while head-reallocation methods target retrieval heads optimized for recall tasks rather than the distinct heads that sustain generative reasoning. No prior method directly observes how each head's compression affects actual reasoning outcomes during autoregressive generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (mathematical word problems, Python coding tasks, or knowledge QA questions) and produces a generated text response alongside a per-head binary KV cache allocation: a top-k subset of heads receives full KV cache while remaining heads use a compressed circular buffer of 16 sink tokens plus 64 recent local tokens. Training consumes 3,000 filtered mathematical problems from DeepScaleR, curated via curriculum sampling by output token length (0–8K tokens), with all LLM parameters frozen and only L×H gating scalars optimized. The compressed-head window is configurable at inference (e.g., 32+128 or 64+256) to trade memory for robustness at high sparsity.

## Method

RLKV introduces L×H learnable gating adapters that, after scaled dot-product attention, linearly interpolate between full-attention output and local (sink+window) attention output for each head, reducing the optimization space to a single scalar per head. These adapters are trained via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on verifiable-reward mathematical problems, where the reward signal (answer correctness) directly reflects reasoning quality and the L1 penalty drives compressible heads toward zero. Two stabilization techniques prevent training collapse: self-distillation sampling (curriculum-filtered problems matched to model capability for stable rewards) and adaptive penalty weighting (dynamic β that decays when mean reward drops below a threshold, breaking the vicious cycle between sparse rewards and dense regularization). At inference, the learned continuous gating scores are discretized into a top-k binary selection: the highest-scoring heads retain full KV cache while all others use the compressed sink+local buffer, yielding 20–60% cache reduction with near-lossless accuracy and up to 2.06× end-to-end speedup under SGLang continuous batching.

## Results

RLKV achieves near-lossless reasoning performance with 20–60% KV cache reduction across three models (Llama-3.1-8B-R1, Qwen-2.5-7B-R1, Qwen-3-4B-Thinking) and eight benchmarks. At the near-lossless threshold (sparsity 0.2–0.6 depending on task/model), accuracy drops are within ±3 points of the full-KV baseline, and on several benchmarks (e.g., AIME24 on Llama-3.1-8B-R1: 40.0% vs. 36.7% full; Math500: 84.6% vs. 83.0%) RLKV actually exceeds the uncompressed model. Under SGLang continuous batching, RLKV delivers up to 2.06× end-to-end speedup at 60% sparsity (504 s vs. 1,036 s on Math500) while retaining 73.8% accuracy versus 79.4% for full attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLKV outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and sparsity level, with the largest margins on harder reasoning tasks. At 40% sparsity on Llama-3.1-8B-R1, RLKV leads DuoAttention by 13.0 points on AIME24 (40.0 vs. 20.0) and by 7.8 points on Math500 (84.6 vs. 81.6); it leads R-KV by 26.7 points on AIME24 (40.0 vs. 13.3) and by 34.4 points on MBPP (63.8 vs. 29.4). H2O, the classic token-dropping method, collapses catastrophically on reasoning tasks (e.g., 27.0% on Math500 vs. 83.0% full), confirming that uniform token eviction is fundamentally incompatible with extended CoT. KVZip is competitive at low sparsity (0.2) but degrades sharply at 0.6–0.8, while RLKV maintains the smallest accuracy gap to full attention at every level. On the long-context LongReason benchmark (70K context), RLKV also leads all baselines, achieving 52.5% at 40% sparsity on Llama-3.1-8B-R1 versus 48.75% (DuoAttention) and 49.0% (KVZip), with R-KV collapsing to 0.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the head-reallocation paradigm (DuoAttention, KVZip, RazorAttention) by replacing static retrieval-head identification with an RL-driven discovery of 'reasoning-critical heads.' It adopts the mixed-attention gating mechanism from DuoAttention and the sink+local window from streaming attention, but departs fundamentally in using GRPO with verifiable rewards to optimize gating adapters against actual generation outcomes rather than proxy metrics or teacher-forced objectives. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods target retrieval heads for long-context recall, whereas RLKV directly observes how each head's compression affects reasoning quality during autoregressive CoT generation, revealing a distinct set of heads essential for CoT consistency and generation termination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLKV provides the first empirical evidence that reasoning-critical heads are a distinct population from retrieval heads, with different error modes (repetitive loops vs. over-length generation) when compressed. The practical impact is significant: 20–60% KV cache reduction with near-lossless accuracy and up to 2.06× end-to-end speedup under continuous batching, making extended-CoT reasoning models more deployable. Open questions include whether static gating scores generalize to query-adaptive settings, how to push compression beyond 80% sparsity (likely requiring combination with quantization), and whether the RL-as-probe paradigm transfers to other efficiency methods (pruning, sparse attention, token compression) and multimodal/agentic reasoning.
