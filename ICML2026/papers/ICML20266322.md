## Problem

Existing speculative decoding draft models are trained with token-level cross-entropy on a single greedy trajectory, yet practical decoding samples and ranks multiple stochastic draft paths in a tree before verification. This training-decoding distributional discrepancy means the draft model optimizes a deterministic single-path distribution that diverges from the multi-path, path-level-utility distribution actually used at inference, limiting achievable acceptance length and speedup.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text context prefix (or image + text context for multimodal settings) and produces a generated text sequence via the standard draft-verify speculative decoding loop. During training, the draft model generates S latent path proposals per prefix, which are scored by a path-level validity oracle comparing cumulative acceptance probability κ against a greedy target baseline; rejected proposals are replaced with corrective samples from the target model. The output is a retrained draft model that, at inference, proposes draft paths whose distribution better matches the multi-path verification posterior, yielding longer accepted spans without altering the target model's output distribution.

## Method

VSD reformulates draft training as variational inference over latent draft paths, deriving an ELBO with two terms: a path-level validity term E[log κ(x,z)] that rewards proposals likely to survive verification, and a KL divergence to the target path distribution as a regularizer. The intractable ELBO is optimized via a Monte Carlo EM procedure: the E-step draws S proposals from the draft model, filters them through a path-level utility oracle (retaining paths whose κ exceeds a greedy target baseline), and substitutes rejected paths with target-model samples; the M-step updates the draft model using a variance-reduced gradient estimator incorporating Adaptive Rejection Weighting (a leave-one-out control variate that modulates rejection signal strength based on draft reliability) and Confidence-Aware Regularization (a softmax-weighted penalty on overconfident rejected paths inspired by hard-negative mining). Theoretical analysis proves that maximizing the VSD objective increases a provable lower bound on expected accepted length by a multiplicative factor, and that VSD strictly dominates KL-only draft training whenever path validity is non-uniform.

## Results

On LLM benchmarks (MT-Bench, HumanEval, GSM8K), VSD integrated with EAGLE-3 achieves an average speedup ratio of 4.22× and acceptance length of 7.00 tokens at T=0, improving over EAGLE-3 (3.95×, 6.55) by +6.7% SR and +6.8% τ. At T=1, the gains are +7.1% SR and +4.9% τ. On MLLM benchmarks (VQAv2, AI2D, SQA Image, ChartQA, TextVQA, Hallusion), VSD improves MSD's average speedup by 7.9% and ViSpec's by 7.3% at T=0, with consistent gains at T=1 as well. Across all settings, VSD accepts 6–7 tokens per draft-verification cycle versus 5–6 for EAGLE-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSD consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across LLM and MLLM settings. Against the strongest LLM baseline EAGLE-3, VSD improves average speedup by 9.6% (T=0) and 7.3% (T=1), with the largest gains on Vicuna-13B (+12.6% SR) and DeepSeek-R1-8B (+11.6% SR) where greedy-path alignment is weakest. GRIFFIN, the second-best LLM baseline, is surpassed by VSD by 12.5% SR at T=0 on LLaMA-3.1-8B (4.22 vs 3.75). For MLLMs, VSD beats MSD by 7.9% SR and ViSpec by 7.3% SR on LLaVA-1.5-7B at T=0, with similar margins at T=1. Compatibility experiments confirm VSD also improves GRIFFIN (+6.3% SR) and HASS (+7.6% SR) when used as the initialization backbone, demonstrating that the variational objective is orthogonal to the underlying draft architecture. Weaker baselines (PLD, Lookahead, SPS, Medusa, EAGLE, EAGLE-2, Hydra) are all surpassed by 50–100%+ in speedup ratio, confirming that the gains come from the variational training objective rather than architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the speculative decoding lineage (Leviathan et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2023) and its recent draft-training refinements—EAGLE/EAGLE-2/EAGLE-3 (Li et al. 2024a,b; 2026), GRIFFIN (Hu et al. 2025b), HASS (Zhang et al. 2024), and multimodal variants ViSpec (Kang et al. 2025) and MSD (Lin et al. 2025)—all of which train the draft model via token-level cross-entropy or KL distillation along a single greedy trajectory. The key conceptual departure is reframing draft training as variational inference over latent draft paths, deriving an ELBO whose two terms (path-level acceptance utility minus KL to the target) explicitly target the multi-path, ranked verification distribution used at decoding time. The optimization via a Monte Carlo EM procedure with Adaptive Rejection Weighting and Confidence-Aware Regularization is a practical contribution layered on top of this variational objective, and the theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–3) provides the first formal link between a draft-training objective and a lower bound on expected acceptance length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the training-decoding distributional mismatch that has been an implicit but unaddressed limitation in all prior draft-training methods, VSD offers a principled drop-in improvement (adding one term to the existing loss) that yields 7–12% speedup gains across LLMs and MLLMs without changing model architecture or decoding procedure. The variational framing is general enough to potentially transfer to other sequential-decision settings where training optimizes a single trajectory but inference explores a distribution. Open questions include whether scaling the number of latent proposals S beyond 40 (limited by compute) yields further gains, whether the approach extends to speech or other modalities, and whether the EM procedure can be made more sample-efficient for very long draft horizons.
