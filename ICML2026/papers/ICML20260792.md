## Problem

Prediction-powered inference (PPI) provides valid statistical inference by debiasing ML model imputations, but deriving PPI methods with additional guarantees—such as differential privacy, robustness to outliers, or validity under continuous distribution shift—has required ad-hoc, case-by-case constructions. This paper resolves the fragmentation by showing that a calibrated conformal set-predictor can serve as a general imputation mechanism that inherits any guarantee from the conformal calibration procedure used to fit it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260792/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabelled features (X_i)_{i=1}^n, a conformal set-predictor C: X → 2^Y calibrated on a separate hold-out set (with known or estimated miscoverage rate Err(C)), and for e-value procedures, a predictable sequence of bounded e-value components e_i(Y) ∈ [a_i, b_i]. It outputs valid (1−α) confidence intervals for means, confidence sets for Z- and M-estimation parameters, and anytime-valid e-values with test supermartingale structure. A key practical property is that a single (potentially differentially private) conformal calibration can be reused for multiple post-hoc inferences, unlike standard PPI which requires access to labelled calibration data for each inference.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces point imputation in PPI with set-valued imputation via a calibrated conformal predictor: for mean estimation, E[φ(Y)] is bounded by [E[inf φ(C(X))] − M·Err(C), E[sup φ(C(X))] + M·Err(C)], where M = b − a is the range of φ, and standard one-sided CIs on the inf/sup expectations yield a valid two-sided CI. For Z-estimation (and by extension M-estimation via the first-order condition), the confidence set is {θ : 0 ∈ [L̂_θ − M_θ·Err(C), Û_θ + M_θ·Err(C)]}, with size governed by the average predictive set width, the miscoverage rate, and the steepness of the estimating equation at θ*. For e-values, the construction E_n^{ppi-(C)} = ∏ rescale_{η_i}(inf e_i(C_i(X_i)) − (b_i−a_i)Err(C_i)) preserves the test supermartingale structure, making it the first general PPI procedure valid without active data collection. Additional guarantees (privacy, robustness, shift-validity) are obtained simply by choosing the corresponding conformal calibration method, leveraging the existing conformal prediction literature without new case-specific proofs.

## Results

On the phishing prevalence task (mean estimation), the conformal PPI method produces the tightest confidence intervals among all compared methods, outperforming PPI++, FAB, and the labelled-only baseline, while also achieving the smallest variance across data splits. On the gene expression task (median estimation), the method surpasses PPI++ but is slightly wider than FAB. In the private thyroid cancer setting, differentially private conformal calibration yields only minor increases in interval size compared to non-private calibration. For continuous risk monitoring on forest cover type, the conformal PPI e-values reject a false null hypothesis substantially faster and more confidently than using only the occasional labelled samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: PPI++ (Angelopoulos et al., 2023b), FAB-PPI (Cortinovis & Caron, 2025), and a labelled-only classical CI. On the phishing mean-estimation task, the proposed conformal PPI method wins, producing the tightest confidence intervals and the smallest variance across all methods. On the gene expression median-estimation task, the method loses to FAB (which uses a Bayesian prior over model quality to tighten intervals) but beats PPI++. In the private thyroid cancer experiment, no prior PPI method is applicable since none guarantee differential privacy, making the conformal PPI approach the first valid procedure in that setting. For the online risk monitoring task on forest cover type, the only prior applicable method (Csillag et al., 2025) requires active data collection, whereas the conformal PPI e-values operate in a purely observational regime and reject false nulls much more quickly than the labelled-only baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established but previously disconnected research threads: prediction-powered inference (PPI), which debiases statistical inference using ML predictions of missing labels, and conformal prediction, which provides distribution-free uncertainty quantification via calibrated set-predictors. The key conceptual move is replacing point imputation in PPI with imputation through a calibrated conformal set-predictor, so that all additional guarantees available in the conformal literature (differential privacy, robustness to adversarial shift, validity under continuous distribution shift) are inherited for free by choosing an appropriate calibration method. This departs from prior PPI work on additional guarantees (e.g., performativity, federation, interference), which required ad-hoc, case-by-case analyses, and from prior conformal prediction work, which had not been applied to nonparametric statistical inference. The e-value-powered extension builds directly on the authors' own prior work on prediction-powered e-values (Csillag et al., 2025), extending it to the conformal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, general mechanism for obtaining PPI with arbitrary additional guarantees, replacing the fragmented landscape of case-specific constructions and making previously inapplicable settings (private healthcare inference, continuous risk monitoring without active data collection) tractable. The boundedness assumption on the estimand is a practical limitation, though the e-value pathway partially circumvents it. Open questions include extending the framework to unbounded or high-dimensional estimands, tightening the power analysis (which depends on average conformal set size), and whether the approach can handle more complex statistical models beyond Z/M-estimation. For practitioners deploying ML models in regulated or safety-critical settings, this offers a principled path to valid inference with privacy or robustness guarantees that prior PPI methods simply could not provide.
