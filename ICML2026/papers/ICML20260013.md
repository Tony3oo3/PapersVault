## Problem

Existing representation alignment methods are inherently pairwise, scaling quadratically with the number of models and failing to enforce cycle consistency when composing translations through intermediate models. Moreover, a fundamental trade-off exists: strict isometric alignment (Procrustes) preserves internal geometry needed for model stitching but underperforms on retrieval tasks where agreement-maximizing methods like GCCA prevail, and no prior method reconciles both objectives within a single shared reference space.

## Input / Output

The method takes N matched (or padded) standardized embeddings of dimension d from each of M≥3 models, with correspondences defined by sample identity on a training split. It produces one orthogonal map Ω_m per model into a shared universe, a consensus centroid U, and (for GCPA) a shared lightweight MLP correction T_θ applied to row-normalized universe directions. Any-to-any translation is obtained by composition through the universe (Ω_{m←n} = Ω_m Ω_n^⊤), reducing the number of learned maps from O(M²) to O(M) and guaranteeing cycle consistency by construction. For heterogeneous feature dimensions, PCA fitted on the training split projects all spaces to a common dimension d before alignment.

## Method

GCPA operates in two stages. First, it solves Generalized Procrustes Analysis (GPA) via alternating minimization to obtain orthogonal maps {Ω_m} and a consensus centroid U that minimize global dispersion, yielding an isometric universe that preserves each model's internal distances and angles. Second, it trains a single shared residual MLP T_θ on the universe coordinates to minimize the negative cosine similarity between each corrected direction and the per-sample consensus direction c_i (the normalized average of all M unit directions for that sample), regularized by a trust penalty that activates only when angular drift from the original GPA direction exceeds a threshold τ. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 3.2) is that increasing each view's agreement with the consensus monotonically increases the sum of all pairwise cosine similarities, so the correction provably improves global retrieval agreement. By sharing T_θ across all models and anchoring it to the GPA scaffold via the trust penalty, GCPA achieves GCCA-level retrieval performance while retaining a geometrically consistent, composable reference space that supports model stitching and efficient incremental addition of new models.

## Results

On TED-MULTI cross-lingual retrieval (M=10 languages), GCPA achieves 0.503 average rank-1 accuracy and 0.314 worst-pair accuracy, outperforming GCCA (0.487/0.288), GPA (0.433/0.236), and pairwise Procrustes (0.430/0.230). On MASSIVE intent clustering, GCPA boosts Adjusted Rand Index from 0.198 (GPA/NA) to 0.300 and NMI from 0.509 to 0.617. On FLICKR8K multimodal retrieval, GCPA nearly doubles HuBERT→DINOv2 retrieval from 14–15% (GPA) to 29%, and on FLICKR30K achieves R@1 0.640 and mAP 0.733 versus GCCA's 0.626/0.709.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCPA consistently outperforms GCCA (Kettenring 1971; Horst 1961) on all retrieval benchmarks: on TED-MULTI M=10 it gains +1.6 points average and +2.6 points worst-pair rank-1, and on FLICKR30K it improves R@1 from 0.626 to 0.640 and mAP from 0.709 to 0.733. Against GPA (Schonemann 1966; Gower 1975), GCPA achieves substantially higher retrieval accuracy (e.g., 0.503 vs 0.433 on TED-MULTI M=10) while GPA retains an edge in pure geometric tasks like model stitching where isometric fidelity is critical. Compared to pairwise Procrustes (Maiorca 2023), GCPA improves both average and worst-case retrieval by 7+ points at M=10 and also provides cycle-consistent translations and O(M) rather than O(M²) maps. On MASSIVE clustering, GCPA is the only method that meaningfully improves over no alignment (ARI 0.300 vs 0.198), while both GPA and GCCA fail to exceed the unaligned baseline. GCPA also degrades more gracefully under 75% correspondence noise on TED-MULTI, retaining higher absolute retrieval accuracy than both GCCA and GPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends pairwise Procrustes alignment (Hurley & Cattell, 1962; Maiorca et al., 2023) to the M≥3 model regime by adapting Generalized Procrustes Analysis (GPA) from psychometrics to neural representation spaces, introducing a shared 'universe' reference that reduces alignment complexity from O(M²) to O(M) and enforces cycle consistency by construction. It positions itself against GCCA (Horst, 1961; Kettenring, 1971), which already handles M-way alignment but via a shared-basis formulation that distorts internal geometry. The key departure is the GCPA correction: using the GPA orthogonal frame as a geometric scaffold and applying a lightweight shared MLP to nudge directions toward a per-sample multi-model consensus, thereby bridging the gap between isometric fidelity (needed for stitching) and agreement maximization (needed for retrieval).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(M) scaling and single-map model addition make multi-model alignment practically viable for growing model collections, and the explicit characterization of the geometry-vs-agreement trade-off gives practitioners a principled choice: GPA when geometric fidelity is paramount, GCPA when retrieval or clustering performance matters. A notable limitation is that GCPA's consensus correction can be harmful when the aligned spaces are too weakly related (e.g., only two corrupted models), in which case GPA is the safer reference. Open questions include extending to M-way functional (nonlinear) alignment for highly heterogeneous collections, and whether the universe framework can support decentralized model merging or continual learning where a coherent global state must be maintained from fragmented views.
