## Problem

NVIDIA's 2:4 Sparse Tensor Cores deliver 2× throughput but enforce a rigid 50% pruning ratio that causes severe accuracy loss in LLMs (14.9–16.5 points on Qwen2.5-7B/14B). Milder (2N-2):2N patterns such as 6:8 (25% pruning) preserve accuracy within 0.4–1.8 points of dense, yet receive zero hardware support and fall back to dense execution in all major inference engines. This paper bridges the gap by enabling Sparse Tensor Core acceleration for the entire (2N-2):2N family on existing GPUs without hardware modifications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a (2N-2):2N sparse weight matrix (produced by post-hoc pruning methods like Wanda or SparseGPT) and a per-token activation vector. The weight matrix is expanded by a factor γ = 2 − 2/N (e.g., 1.5× for 6:8) into concatenated 2:4-compliant windows, while the activation undergoes a pure index-remapping (no arithmetic) that replicates elements across overlapping windows. The output is a mathematically identical GEMM result computed entirely through hardware-accelerated 2:4 sparse operations, integrated into vLLM's linear layer pipeline with a single configuration flag.

## Method

The core mechanism is Sliding Window Decomposition: any (2N-2):2N block is losslessly decomposed into N−1 overlapping windows of size 4 with stride 2, each satisfying the 2:4 constraint (at most 2 non-zeros per 4 positions). Theorem 1 proves N−1 windows are both necessary and sufficient, yielding the optimal expansion factor γ = (N−1)·4/(2N). The corresponding activation lifting operator Ψ is a pure index remapping that replicates input elements according to window coverage, enabling fusion with per-token quantization at low marginal cost. The system comprises three phases: an offline greedy weight packer (O(K) per row), cuSPARSELt compression at model load, and an online Triton fused kernel that combines quantization and sliding in a single pass. The theoretical speedup bound is S_eff = N/(N−1) (e.g., 1.33× for 6:8), which the paper confirms is achieved on compute-bound workloads across six GPU architectures and five precisions.

## Results

On A100 (INT8, M=16384, compute-bound), SlideSparse at 6:8 sparsity achieves 1.46× kernel speedup over dense cuBLASLt, exceeding the theoretical N/(N−1)=1.33× bound because native 2:4 itself reaches 2.18×. End-to-end on vLLM (Qwen2.5-7B, A100, INT8, M=8192), 6:8 achieves exactly 1.33× speedup, matching the theoretical upper bound. In terms of accuracy, 6:8 sparsity stays within 0.4–1.8 average points of dense across 9 benchmarks (Qwen2.5-7B/14B, both Wanda and SparseGPT), while 2:4 sparsity degrades by 14.9–16.5 points. Efficiency (measured speedup / expected from 2:4 baseline) exceeds 100% on all datacenter GPUs: 115% (A100), 119% (H100), 134% (B200) for INT8 6:8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense cuBLASLt baseline, SlideSparse at 6:8 delivers 1.46× kernel speedup and 1.33× end-to-end speedup on A100 INT8, confirming that moderate sparsity (75% density) yields real acceleration previously unavailable. Against native 2:4 via cuSPARSELt (2.18× kernel speedup), SlideSparse at 6:8 achieves ~67% of the raw speedup (1.46×/2.18×), which is the expected ratio given the 1.5× expansion factor (2.18/1.5≈1.45); however, the accuracy trade-off is dramatically better: 6:8 loses only 0.4–1.8 points versus 14.9–16.5 points for 2:4. Against Wanda and SparseGPT as pruning methods, the paper shows that both methods produce comparable accuracy at 6:8 (within 0.4–1.8 pts of dense), validating that the sparsity pattern—not the specific pruning algorithm—drives the accuracy-speedup trade-off. The efficiency metric (measured/expected speedup) consistently exceeds 100% on A100, H100, and B200, indicating that SlideSparse introduces no hidden overhead and can even unlock additional Sparse Tensor Core utilization beyond what native 2:4 workflows achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on NVIDIA's 2:4 Sparse Tensor Cores and cuSPARSELt, which provide hardware-accelerated sparse GEMM but only for the rigid 50%-pruning 2:4 pattern. It departs from prior structured-sparsity-for-LLMs work (SparseGPT, Wanda, Sheared LLaMA) by showing that milder (2N-2):2N patterns—previously treated as dense by inference engines like vLLM and TensorRT-LLM—can be losslessly decomposed into overlapping 2:4-compliant windows via a Sliding Window Decomposition, unlocking the same hardware path without any architectural modification. The activation-side rearrangement is fused into per-token quantization kernels (in the SmoothQuant/LLM.int8() lineage), making the expansion nearly free at runtime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SlideSparse fills a concrete deployment gap: practitioners who choose 6:8 or 4:6 sparsity to preserve LLM accuracy (within 0.4–1.8 points of dense vs. 14.9–16.5 for 2:4) previously received zero latency benefit. By establishing sparsity as a second optimization axis orthogonal to quantization, the paper opens a continuous accuracy–speedup trade-off spectrum rather than a binary dense/2:4 choice. Key limitations include evaluation only with one-shot post-hoc pruning (no sparse-aware training), modest absolute speedups (≤1.33× for 6:8), and dependence on cuSPARSELt's 2:4 backend. Open questions include whether training LLMs with explicit (2N-2):2N constraints from initialization would push accuracy at higher sparsity levels (e.g., 4:6), and whether the approach generalizes to non-NVIDIA hardware or non-2:4 sparse cores.
