## Problem

Contrastive learning for audio spectrograms remains underexplored compared to masked reconstruction approaches, largely because effective augmentation design is domain-dependent and unstructured masking—while ideal for reconstruction—preserves too much local structure in positive pairs, making the contrastive task trivial and leading to dimension collapse. This paper asks whether a structured masking strategy can serve as a principled view-generation mechanism for contrastive pre-training on spectrograms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 10-second mono audio clips at 16 kHz, converts them to log-Mel spectrograms (128 Kaldi-compatible Mel bands, 25 ms Hann window, 10 ms hop), and partitions them into 16×16 patches. Two augmented views per sample are constructed via waveform-level augmentations followed by independent time-frequency masking, reducing each view to roughly 24% of the full token sequence. The encoder outputs a single utterance-level embedding (via average pooling) used for classification, deepfake detection, or alignment with an LLM for captioning.

## Method

AudioMosaic uses a ViT-B/16 (AST) backbone trained with a contrastive objective where positive pairs are constructed by applying independent structured masking along the time (ρt=0.6) and frequency (ρf=0.4) dimensions to two augmented views of the same utterance. Unlike reconstruction-based methods that use unstructured masking to preserve local context, this structured masking deliberately reduces shared local redundancy between views, forcing the encoder to rely on global temporal-spectral patterns. Masked patch tokens are randomly shuffled before encoding to reduce spatial bias, and only visible patches are processed, cutting quadratic attention cost from O(N²) to O((1−ρ)²N²). A lightweight two-layer MLP projection head replaces the Transformer decoder used in masked autoencoders, reducing pre-training parameters to ~86M. The paper further provides an effective-rank analysis showing that structured time-frequency masking yields higher-dimensional, less collapsed representations than unstructured masking under the same contrastive objective.

## Results

On the primary fine-tuning benchmarks, AudioMosaic achieves 42.5 mAP on AS-20K, 50.2 mAP on AS-2M, 97.5% on ESC-50, 98.4% on SPC-2, and 99.0% on SPC-1, setting new state-of-the-art on AS-20K, ESC-50, and SPC-1. Under linear probing (frozen encoder), AudioMosaic reaches 29.4 mAP on AS-20K, substantially outperforming the next best method SSLAM (15.0 mAP) and Audio-MAE (18.3 mAP). On the EnvSDD deepfake detection benchmark, AudioMosaic+Linear achieves an average EER of 1.30% (TTA) and 0.02% (ATA), far below BEATs+AASIST (5.81% / 1.33%) and Wav2Vec 2.0+AASIST (17.43% / 23.17%). When aligned with LLaMA-7B for audio-language tasks, AudioMosaic improves zero-shot ESC-50 accuracy from 82.0% to 86.5% and VGGSound from 38.4% to 54.6% over the original LTU/CAV-MAE setup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioMosaic outperforms the strongest in-domain SSL baseline SSLAM on AS-20K (42.5 vs. 40.9 mAP), ESC-50 (97.5% vs. 96.2%), and SPC-1 (99.0% vs. 98.8%), while matching it on AS-2M (50.2 mAP). It also surpasses EAT (40.2 mAP on AS-20K), BEATs (38.3), A-JEPA (38.4), and Audio-MAE (37.0) on AS-20K, with the largest margin over Audio-MAE at +5.5 mAP. The only metric where AudioMosaic trails a baseline is SPC-2, where ASiT achieves 98.8% versus AudioMosaic's 98.4%. Under linear probing, the gap widens dramatically: AudioMosaic reaches 29.4 mAP on AS-20K versus 15.0 for SSLAM and 18.3 for Audio-MAE, indicating substantially more discriminative frozen representations. On deepfake detection (EnvSDD), AudioMosaic+Linear reduces average EER to 1.30% (TTA) and 0.02% (ATA), compared to 5.81%/1.33% for BEATs+AASIST and 17.43%/23.17% for Wav2Vec 2.0+AASIST. For audio-language tasks, replacing CAV-MAE with AudioMosaic in the LTU framework improves zero-shot ESC-50 from 82.0% to 86.5%, VGGSound from 38.4% to 54.6%, and AudioCaps SPICE from 16.0 to 17.1, with only a minor drop on DCASE (50.5→48.9 Mi-F1).

## Relation to Prior Work

AudioMosaic builds on the AST (ViT-B/16) backbone and the SimCLR-style contrastive framework, but repurposes structured time-frequency masking—previously used in SpecAugment for supervised learning and in Audio-MAE/BEATs/EAT as reconstruction noise—as a mechanism for constructing positive pairs in a contrastive objective. This departs from two dominant lineages: (1) masked spectrogram modeling, where unstructured masking preserves local context for reconstruction, and (2) waveform-based contrastive methods (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT) that contrast short temporal segments. The paper's effective-rank analysis provides a representation-theoretic bridge, showing that unstructured masking creates overly easy contrastive tasks (shared local redundancy → dimension collapse), while structured time-frequency masking forces the encoder to rely on global, utterance-invariant structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that contrastive pre-training with structured masking can match or exceed the dominant masked-reconstruction paradigm on fine-tuning benchmarks while substantially outperforming it under linear probing (29.4 vs. 18.3 mAP on AS-20K), suggesting reconstruction-based objectives may produce representations that are less linearly separable and more dependent on fine-tuning to unlock their utility. The efficiency gains (no Transformer decoder, ~24% visible tokens per view, enabling 6144 batch sizes on a single 48 GB GPU) make large-batch contrastive pre-training practical for audio. Open questions include whether the specific masking ratios (ρt=0.6, ρf=0.4) generalize to longer or non-speech audio, whether combining contrastive and reconstruction objectives yields further gains, and whether the linear-probing advantage persists at larger model scales or with different encoder architectures.
