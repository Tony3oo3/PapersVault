## Problem

Existing LLM-based Time Series Question Answering (TSQA) systems suffer from two fundamental limitations: they either treat time series as raw text tokens or perform only shallow multimodal alignment (simple embedding concatenation), failing to ground semantic reasoning in the specific temporal patterns (trends, seasonalities) that queries actually require. Additionally, when a general TSQA model is trained on a heterogeneous mix of simple discriminative and complex generative tasks, optimization imbalance causes the model to overfit to easier objectives, stalling the development of deep temporal reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series set (padded to uniform length, instance-normalized, and split into patches) together with a natural language question as input. The time series is consumed not as raw values but as embedded patch tokens that undergo latent decomposition into trend and seasonal components before cross-modal alignment. Output is a structured natural language response containing a chain-of-thought reasoning block (within think tags) followed by a final answer, formatted as a boxed label for multiple-choice/true-false tasks or free text scored by Rouge-L for open-ended generation tasks.

## Method

PATRA introduces a Pattern-Aware Alignment Module that decomposes time series embeddings in latent space into full, trend (via average pooling), and seasonal (residual) components, then uses three sets of Learnable Alignment Tokens to extract corresponding text features via cross-attention. Paired text and time series tokens are concatenated and processed through self-attention to fuse textual semantics into temporal representations, which are then spliced into the text sequence via placeholder replacement before being fed to a Qwen2.5-7B LLM backbone. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss for alignment, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining format compliance and a task-aware balanced reward that normalizes all task-type rewards to [0,2], preventing the model from exploiting easier tasks and ensuring stable cross-task optimization of reasoning chains.

## Results

On the TSQA benchmark, PATRA-7B achieves the best performance across all four task types: 56.03% accuracy on Comprehension, 64.69% on Recognition, 44.59% on Reasoning, and 52.78% on Prescience, with Rouge-L scores of 25.67, 25.46, 27.36, and 27.06 respectively. These results surpass the strongest open-source textual baseline (Qwen2.5-7B) by up to 19.18% (Recognition accuracy) and the best multimodal baseline (ChatTS-7B) by up to 28.69% (Recognition accuracy). On out-of-domain MTBench evaluation, PATRA achieves SOTA in both Weather (60.46% MCQA, 52.04% trend prediction) and Finance (63.33% 3-way, 60.80% 5-way for 7-day; 54.39% 3-way, 43.70% 5-way for 30-day) domains, outperforming proprietary models including GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PATRA-7B outperforms all open-source baselines across every metric on TSQA. Against the strongest textual baseline Qwen2.5-7B, PATRA gains +13.79% on Comprehension accuracy, +19.18% on Recognition accuracy, +8.11% on Reasoning accuracy, and +25.93% on Prescience accuracy. Against the best multimodal baseline ChatTS-7B, the margins are even larger: +11.20% (Comp.), +28.69% (Recog.), +21.62% (Reason.), and +26.86% (Presc.) in accuracy, with Rouge-L improvements of +12.37, +12.23, +11.52, and +13.07 respectively. PATRA also surpasses ITFormer-7B by +15.51% (Comp.), +19.45% (Recog.), +14.19% (Reason.), and +8.34% (Presc.) in accuracy. Compared to the proprietary GPT-4o upper bound, PATRA exceeds it on Comprehension (56.03 vs. 50.86) and Prescience (52.78 vs. 66.66 is lower, but Rouge-L is 27.06 vs. 6.78), while GPT-4o retains an edge on Recognition accuracy (69.65 vs. 64.69) and Reasoning accuracy (50.00 vs. 44.59). On the out-of-domain MTBench, PATRA achieves SOTA in Weather MCQA (60.46 vs. 59.78 Claude) and Finance 7-day 3-way (63.33 vs. 53.11 Gemini-3-Pro), beating all proprietary models on most Finance metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal LLM paradigm (originally developed for vision-language tasks) to time series by introducing a pattern-aware alignment mechanism that decomposes temporal embeddings into trend and seasonal components in latent space, then aligns each with corresponding text tokens via learnable alignment tokens and cross-modal self-attention. It departs from prior time-series-LLM works (TimeLLM, ChatTS, ITFormer, TimeOmni-1) that either treat series as text tokens or perform shallow embedding concatenation, arguing that explicit pattern decomposition is necessary for 'deep alignment.' On the training side, it builds on GRPO-based RL (as in DeepSeek-R1) but introduces a task-aware balanced reward to address multi-task optimization imbalance, a gap not addressed by prior single-task or SFT-only approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pattern-aware alignment mechanism offers a concrete architectural recipe for grounding LLM reasoning in physical time series structure, and the balanced reward design is a practical solution to a known multi-task RL problem that could transfer beyond TSQA. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 7B backbone (Qwen2.5) and a single training dataset (TSQA), and the paper itself acknowledges attention bias issues on highly volatile, non-stationary series (Figure 5). Open questions include whether the latent decomposition generalizes to irregular or multivariate series with strong cross-channel dependencies, whether the balanced reward scales to more than four task types, and whether the approach transfers to larger model scales where the alignment module's relative capacity may shift.
