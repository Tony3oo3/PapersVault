## Problem

Transformer inference applies a fixed number of layers to every input regardless of complexity, wasting computation on simple queries. Existing early exit methods use output confidence (softmax entropy, top-1 probability) as a proxy for internal convergence, which inverts the causal relationship and incurs O(d×V) per-layer overhead from vocabulary projection that erodes the theoretical speedup. The paper asks whether convergence can be detected directly from the geometry of hidden state trajectories with vocabulary-independent cost.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text prompts fed through a pre-trained LLaMA-2 model (7B or 13B). During the prefill pass, it monitors only the last token's d-dimensional hidden state at each layer, computing two O(d) scalar quantities per layer. At the determined exit layer, it applies the standard LayerNorm and LM head once to produce logits for next-token prediction. Subsequent autoregressive decoding proceeds at the fixed truncated depth with a standard KV-cache, requiring no auxiliary classifiers or learned exit heads.

## Method

GCEE (Geometric Convergence Early Exit) exploits a discovered two-phase structure in hidden state trajectories: early layers produce large, volatile updates (construction phase) while later layers produce small, nearly collinear updates (propagation phase), with a sharp transition at an input-dependent saturation layer. At each layer, the method computes the normalized step size s̃^(l) = ||u^(l)||/||h^(l)|| and the directional stability c^(l) = cos(u^(l), u^(l-1)), where u^(l) = h^(l) − h^(l-1). Exit triggers when both quantities simultaneously indicate convergence (s̃ ≤ τ_s and c ≥ τ_c) for K consecutive layers beyond a warmup depth l_min, requiring only O(d) operations per layer with no learned components. Theoretical analysis establishes that propagation-phase updates follow geometric decay with contraction factor ρ ≈ 0.86, yielding explicit bounds on the depth-tail residual, logit perturbation through LayerNorm, and conditions under which argmax predictions are preserved.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B, GCEE retains 97.1–98.6% of full-depth accuracy across TriviaQA, Natural Questions, HellaSwag, and WinoGrande while reducing FLOPs by 34% (average exit layer 21.2 vs. 32). The same pattern holds at 13B scale (97.5–98.8% retention, 34% FLOPs reduction, avg layer 26.5 vs. 40). GCEE achieves a 1.45× end-to-end wall-clock speedup, closely tracking its 1.51× theoretical FLOPs reduction, whereas entropy-based methods achieve only 1.14× due to O(d×V) per-layer projection overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCEE outperforms all four baselines on every task and both model scales. Against Entropy (Xin et al., 2020), GCEE gains 4.6 points on TriviaQA (63.5 vs. 58.9), 2.7 on NQ, 4.0 on HellaSwag, and 3.0 on WinoGrande (7B); the gap is largest on TriviaQA where confidence is most miscalibrated. Against Top-1 Confidence (Schuster et al., 2022), GCEE leads by 5.3 points on TriviaQA and 3.7 on HellaSwag, with similar margins elsewhere. Against Hidden State Similarity (Shan et al., 2024), GCEE gains 7.0 points on TriviaQA and 6.3 on HellaSwag, confirming that cosine similarity between consecutive hidden states is a weaker convergence signal than the combined step-size + directional-stability criterion. Against Unified Skip (Liu et al., 2024), a static 30% layer-removal method, GCEE leads by 6.7 points on TriviaQA and 7.3 on HellaSwag at comparable average depth (~21–22 layers), validating the value of input-adaptive depth allocation over fixed truncation. Notably, GCEE's end-to-end speedup (1.45×) substantially exceeds that of Entropy (1.14×) and Top-1 Conf (1.16×) despite similar layer savings, because its O(d) decision overhead (<0.1 ms/layer) avoids the ~2.5 ms/layer vocabulary projection cost that erodes confidence-based methods' theoretical gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the early exit problem through the lens of numerical analysis, treating Transformer depth as a non-stationary fixed-point iteration and applying classical convergence criteria (vanishing step size, stabilizing direction) to the hidden state trajectory. It departs from the dominant confidence-based paradigm (DeeBERT, CALM, PABEE) by arguing that output confidence is a downstream symptom of representational convergence rather than a criterion for it, and from auxiliary-module approaches (SkipDecode, ConsistentEE) by requiring no learned components or architectural modifications. The geometric framing builds on the emerging observation of internal state saturation (Shan et al., 2024) and the ODE/fixed-point interpretation of residual networks (Lawson & Aitchison, 2025), but adds the directional stability component, a formal cone-coherent contraction analysis, and a vocabulary-independent O(d) decision overhead that directly addresses the scalability bottleneck of logit projection in large-vocabulary LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vocabulary-independent O(d) per-layer overhead is a practically significant property for LLM serving, where the O(d×V) logit projection at every candidate exit layer erodes the theoretical FLOPs savings of confidence-based methods (demonstrated as 1.14× vs 1.45× end-to-end speedup). The training-free, drop-in nature makes it immediately deployable without retraining or kernel modifications. Open questions include whether the two-phase structure and its sharpness generalize beyond LLaMA-2 to other architectures (e.g., GPT, Mistral, MoE models), whether the criterion remains effective for complex multi-turn generation or long-context tasks where the last-token trajectory may behave differently, and whether the theoretical cone-coherent contraction assumption holds universally or only in the propagation regime the method targets.
