## Problem

Safety alignment for vision-language models has focused almost exclusively on the text modality, treating visual inputs as a passive or descriptive channel. This paper identifies a cross-modality alignment gap: text-based safety training does not automatically generalize to harmful intent conveyed through visual structure, context, or analogy, leaving a large and exploitable attack surface unaddressed in frontier VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260152/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each attack pairs a crafted visual image (glyph legend + encoded sentence, object-replaced scene, text-replaced artifact, or analogy riddle) with a neutralized text prompt in which all harmful referents have been replaced by abstract slot variables (X₁–X₄), making the text alone benign. The VLM is prompted with a 'decode-first' instruction to interpret the visual encoding before executing the implied task, and responses are sampled K=5 times per behavior. Outputs are scored by a three-model judge ensemble (Grok-4.1-Fast, Gemini-3-Flash, Claude-Haiku-4.5) on a four-level rubric (refusal / misunderstanding / partial / compliance), with Best-of-K success defined as any attempt reaching full compliance.

## Method

The paper introduces four visual jailbreak attacks sharing a common principle: encoding or implying prohibited intent through visual structure while keeping surface-level textual and visual content ostensibly benign. (1) Visual Cipher maps each word of a harmful instruction to a unique geometric glyph and presents a legend plus encoded sentence as images; (2) Visual Object Replacement swaps a harmful object for a benign surrogate (e.g., bomb→banana) while preserving scene context that implies the original referent; (3) Visual Text Replacement edits harmful text inside culturally recognizable artifacts (book covers, posters) to benign placeholders while retaining typography and surrounding imagery; (4) Visual Analogy Riddle decomposes harmful intent into individually benign three-row analogies whose joint solution recovers the prohibited concept. All attacks are evaluated on 159 HarmBench behaviors across six frontier VLMs (GPT-5.2, Claude-Haiku-4.5, Gemini-3-Flash, Gemini-3.1-Pro, Qwen3-VL-235B, Qwen3-VL-32B) against five prior VLM jailbreak baselines. Preliminary interpretability via Logit Lens and refusal-direction analysis on Qwen3-VL-32B reveals that replacement attacks suppress late-layer refusal activation while dangerous semantics persist in mid-to-late semantic layers, and a lightweight output-side guard (Qwen3Guard-Stream-0.6B) flags most compliant outputs, suggesting text-based output classifiers can mitigate these visual attacks without modifying the VLM stack.

## Results

The paper reports Attack Success Rate (ASR) under Best-of-5 sampling across six frontier VLMs on 159 HarmBench behaviors. The headline finding is a cross-modality alignment gap: Visual Cipher achieves 40.9% ASR on Claude-Haiku-4.5 versus only 10.7% for an equivalent textual cipher, while Gemini-3-Flash is most vulnerable overall (97.5% visual cipher ASR) and GPT-5.2 is most robust (8.2%). Across all four attack types, visual attacks outperform their textual counterparts on most models, with Visual Text Replacement reaching 58.1% on Qwen3-VL-32B and Visual Analogy Riddle reaching 67.9% (textual) on Gemini-3-Flash.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's four visual attacks substantially outperform all five prior VLM jailbreak baselines on most models. HADES (Li et al., 2024) averages only 13.2% ASR across models, while Visual Cipher alone reaches 97.5% on Gemini-3-Flash and 87.4% on Qwen3-VL-32B. FigStep (Gong et al., 2025) is highly model-selective, peaking at 49.1% on Qwen3-VL-235B and 45.9% on Claude-Haiku-4.5 but dropping to 10.1% on Gemini-3-Flash; Visual Cipher outperforms it by 87.4 points on Gemini-3-Flash (97.5% vs. 10.1%) and 76.1 points on Qwen3-VL-32B (87.4% vs. 11.3%). The MM-SafetyBench baselines (TYPO, SD, SD+TYPO from Liu et al., 2024b) are the weakest, with SD+TYPO reaching at most 60.8% on Qwen3-VL-32B, well below Visual Cipher (87.4%) and Visual Text Replacement (58.1%) on the same model. Notably, the paper's attacks target a complementary threat model: they produce semantically meaningful, interpretable images rather than optimized adversarial perturbations, while still achieving higher or comparable ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM jailbreaking literature (Qi et al. 2024, Shayegani et al. 2024, FigStep, MM-SafetyBench) by introducing attacks that are semantically meaningful and require no gradient access, departing from both adversarial-perturbation and typography-based approaches. Most directly, the visual object replacement attack is framed as the visual analog of Doublespeak (Yona et al. 2025), which showed that textual in-context examples can hijack internal representations; here, visual scene context performs the same semantic overwrite. The visual analogy riddle attack has no direct precedent in VLM safety literature, exploiting implicit multi-step reasoning over benign visual components to reconstruct prohibited concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence for a cross-modality alignment gap—text-based safety training does not automatically generalize to harmful intent conveyed visually—which has direct implications for the growing deployment of VLMs in multimodal assistants and document understanding. The interpretability analysis (refusal-direction suppression, Logit Lens showing dangerous semantics persisting in semantic layers despite benign appearance) offers a mechanistic account that could guide targeted safety interventions. Key open questions include whether these attacks become more potent as visual reasoning improves (current high misunderstanding rates may mask true vulnerability), how they transfer across models or compose across modalities, and whether output-side classifiers like Constitutional Classifiers can serve as a reliable defense-in-depth layer.
