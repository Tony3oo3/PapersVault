## Problem

Static PEFT methods like LoRA impose fixed low-rank constraints that cannot adapt to the varying complexity of specialized downstream tasks, while standard MoE fine-tuning causes parameter explosion by duplicating full neural blocks. Existing dynamic rank and routing heuristics rely on gradient-based signals that are prone to local optima and routing collapse, leaving no mechanism to jointly evolve adapter topology and expert routing in an online, parameter-efficient manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263080/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces pixel-level binary segmentation masks via a U-Net-style decoder. The frozen SAM2-Hiera-Large encoder processes the image, with ACO-ConvLoRA modules injected at both encoder and decoder layers; the decoder aggregates multi-scale features to output the final mask. No special input preprocessing or prompt engineering is required beyond standard image resizing to the backbone's expected resolution.

## Method

ACO-MoE-LoRA introduces an 'Evolving-while-Training' paradigm with three coupled mechanisms. First, a Latent Space MoE embeds lightweight experts strictly within the LoRA bottleneck (r_t ≪ d), using a Project-Route-Reconstruct workflow where shared matrices A and B project features into a compressed subspace, route them through top-k experts, and reconstruct the output—decoupling parameter cost from model width. Second, a Pheromone-Guided Gating mechanism adds log(τ) evolutionary priors and a frequency penalty to the routing logits, explicitly counteracting the 'Rich-get-Richer' dynamic that causes routing collapse in standard MoE. Third, a dual-path ACO meta-controller dynamically evolves per-layer rank (r_t ∈ {4,8,16,32}) and scaling factor α via Elastic Weight Slicing over a pre-initialized SuperLoRA structure, using validation IoU minus a computational cost penalty as the fitness signal, with cosine-annealed exploration that transitions to deterministic selection in the final 10% of training.

## Results

Across 16 datasets spanning four specialized domains, ACO-MoE-LoRA (Dynamic) achieves state-of-the-art performance: 93.3% mDice on Kvasir-SEG, 0.951 E_φ on CAMO, 0.950 S_α on ECSSD, and 0.818 mIoU on MAS3K, while using only 8.24M trainable parameters (3.74% of the SAM2 backbone). The Fixed-Rank variant matches or exceeds standard LoRA (r=16) with a ~4.7× parameter reduction (3.73M vs. 17.62M), achieving 92.3% mDice on Kvasir-SEG. Full Fine-Tuning (227.1M params) paradoxically underperforms all PEFT methods (e.g., 69.5% vs. 93.3% mDice), confirming catastrophic forgetting as the dominant failure mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACO-MoE-LoRA (Dynamic) outperforms all 28 baselines across the four domains. Against the strongest static PEFT baseline, LoRA+Conv-LoRA (4.74M params), the Dynamic variant gains +1.9% mDice on Kvasir-SEG (93.3% vs. 91.4%) and +0.012 E_φ on CAMO (0.951 vs. 0.937), while the Fixed variant (3.73M) already surpasses it on Kvasir-SEG mDice (92.3% vs. 91.4%). Compared to standard LoRA (r=16, 17.62M params), the Fixed variant achieves +0.5% mDice with 4.7× fewer parameters. Full Fine-Tuning (227.1M params) catastrophically underperforms all PEFT methods (69.5% vs. 93.3% mDice), attributed to destructive updates to the SA-1B pre-trained weights. Among domain-specific SOTAs, the method surpasses MEGANet by +2.1% mDice on CVC-ColonDB (81.4% vs. 79.3%), SAFE-Net by +1.0% on ETIS (80.4% vs. 79.4%), Dual-SAM by +2.9% mIoU on MAS3K (81.8% vs. 78.9%), and RUN by +5.0% S_α on NC4K (90.1% vs. 85.1%). Against dynamic-rank baselines DyLoRA and AdaLoRA, the ACO-driven evolution achieves +0.8% and +0.4% mDice respectively, confirming that global evolutionary search outperforms random sampling and greedy importance pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning of foundation models (LoRA, Conv-LoRA, Adapters), Mixture-of-Experts for dynamic capacity scaling, and bio-inspired optimization (ACO) for discrete structural search. It departs from prior PEFT-MoE hybrids (MoLE, MixLoRA) by embedding experts strictly within the low-rank bottleneck rather than duplicating full adapter modules, and from gradient-based or ALF routing by introducing a pheromone-guided gating mechanism that injects evolutionary memory into the forward pass. The 'Evolving-while-Training' framing distinguishes it from NAS-style offline architecture search by coupling ACO's discrete topology decisions (rank, alpha, sparsity) with continuous gradient descent in a single training loop, building on the SAM2-UNet baseline architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 3.73M-parameter fixed-rank variant can match or exceed 17.62M-parameter LoRA, and that the dynamic variant adds only ~3% training overhead while achieving SOTA across 16 datasets in four specialized domains. The 'Less is More' observation—that Full Fine-Tuning (227M params) catastrophically underperforms the PEFT approach—reinforces the value of structural regularization in foundation model adaptation. Key open questions include generalization beyond SAM2-Hiera-Large to other backbones (ViT, CLIP), robustness under noisy or non-monotonic validation signals that drive the pheromone updates, and whether the ACO meta-controller's benefits persist at larger model scales or in non-segmentation tasks. The single-backbone evaluation and the dependence on a clean validation IoU signal are the most significant practical limitations.
