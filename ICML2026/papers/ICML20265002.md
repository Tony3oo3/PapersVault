## Problem

Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) are commonly assumed to produce their best samples at the final diffusion timestep, yet empirical observations show that latent-space samples visibly stabilize before full denoising, unlike pixel-space diffusion. This paper identifies that the final steps of LDM diffusion can actually degrade sample quality due to the intrinsic dimensionality reduction, and provides the first principled characterization of how latent dimension interacts with the optimal stopping time.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the framework takes a centered Gaussian data distribution with covariance Σ, a latent dimension d, a semi-orthogonal projection matrix P, and a stopping time t, and outputs the Fréchet (Wasserstein-2) distance between the generated and target distributions, from which optimal (d, t) pairs are derived. Practically, the method consumes a trained autoencoder (linear or U-Net-based) and its corresponding LDM, and produces FID trajectories over diffusion timesteps. A key practical output is the 'noisy AE proxy': by simply encoding a real image, adding Gaussian noise at level t in the latent space, and decoding, one can predict the LDM's FID curve without running the full backward diffusion, enabling model selection at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Method

Under a Gaussian framework with linear autoencoders, the paper shows that the backward latent diffusion is exactly equivalent to a 'noisy autoencoder' that encodes data, injects Gaussian noise into the latent representation, and decodes it. By explicitly computing the Fréchet distance between the true data distribution and this decoded noisy latent, they derive a time-dependent trade-off: lower-dimensional projections are optimal at early diffusion times, while higher dimensions become necessary as t approaches T, with the crossover times determined by the data covariance eigenvalues. For data supported on a d₀-dimensional subspace, they prove that projecting onto that subspace combined with early stopping (strictly before T) is jointly optimal. They further extend the analysis to score matching with weight-norm constraints (capped at C), showing that the optimal projection dimension scales logarithmically with C for exponentially decaying covariance spectra. Empirically, they validate these predictions on GMM data with exact scores, linear AEs on MNIST, and U-Net AEs on ImageNet-256, demonstrating that the noisy AE proxy faithfully tracks LDM FID curves and that intersection points across latent dimensions coincide between the proxy and the full model.

## Results

The paper's key empirical finding is that FID curves for LDMs exhibit a characteristic U-shape, meaning the optimal generation quality is achieved at an intermediate stopping time t < T rather than at full denoising. On ImageNet-256 with custom-trained U-Net AEs (latent dimensions 32×32×4, 64×64×3, 32×32×16, all with 500M total parameters), the noisy AE proxy FID curves closely track the corresponding LDM FID curves, with intersection points across latent dimensions occurring at identical timesteps. On MNIST with linear AEs (latent dims 64, 256, 400), the dimension–stopping-time trade-off predicted by Proposition 4.2 is confirmed: lower-dimensional latents achieve their best FID earlier in the diffusion, while higher-dimensional latents require later stopping. The same U-shape behavior is observed with pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE and FLUX-AE on ImageNet-256, confirming the phenomenon is architecture-agnostic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so there are no traditional baselines to beat. The primary empirical comparison is between LDMs and their noisy AE proxies: the FID trajectories of the two are nearly indistinguishable in shape and ranking, with crossing points between different latent dimensions occurring at the same timesteps for both. A secondary comparison is between LDMs and pixel-space diffusion (Figure 1), where LDM-generated samples visibly stabilize in the final diffusion steps while pixel-space models still undergo significant refinement, motivating the early-stopping hypothesis. Across latent dimensions on ImageNet-256, the paper confirms that larger latent dimensions (e.g., 32×32×16) require later stopping times to reach their optimal FID, while smaller dimensions (32×32×4) peak earlier — consistent with the theoretical prediction that early generation steps favor lower dimensions. The noisy AE proxy is shown to be a valid substitute for full LDM training in model selection, potentially saving the computational cost of training a diffusion model for each candidate latent dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimal stopping analysis for diffusion models (Hurault et al., 2025; Achilli et al., 2025) by incorporating the two-stage LDM architecture of Rombach et al. (2022), where the autoencoder's latent dimension becomes a coupled hyperparameter with the stopping time. The VP-SDE formulation follows Song et al. (2020), and the key conceptual departure is the reinterpretation of the backward diffusion as a 'noisy autoencoder'—encoding, injecting latent noise, then decoding—which enables a clean Gaussian analysis via the Fréchet distance. Unlike prior optimal stopping work that considered only standard pixel-space diffusion, this paper explicitly characterizes how the latent dimension d interacts with time t to determine the optimal (d, t) pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for early stopping in LDMs beyond numerical stability, showing that the final diffusion steps can actively degrade sample quality due to the dimensionality reduction. The noisy AE proxy is a practically valuable tool: it allows practitioners to predict optimal stopping times and latent dimensions without training full LDMs, potentially saving substantial compute. Key limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to Gaussian data and linear autoencoders, with the extension to non-linear architectures supported only empirically. Open questions include extending the theory to non-Gaussian distributions, other noise schedules (e.g., EDMs), and characterizing the optimal stopping time for non-linear score functions learned by neural networks.
