## Problem

Existing graph-based OD flow methods encode inter-area relationships solely through pairwise adjacency and straight-line distance matrices, which fail to preserve fundamental geometric properties such as collinearity, relative orientation, and geodesic (free-flow) distances. This omission limits the model's ability to capture long-range and multi-area dependencies that are critical for accurate OD flow prediction and generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261774/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a region-level data instance comprising per-area feature vectors (POI counts, demographics), PCA-aligned and normalized geographic coordinates, a road network adjacency matrix, and three distance matrices (Euclidean, k-hop, geodesic), all packaged into a per-pair geographic descriptor tensor G ∈ R^{n×n×d_g}. For prediction, it outputs a single n×n non-negative OD flow matrix; for generation, it samples from a learned distribution via flow matching, producing diverse n×n OD matrices conditioned on the same geographic and intrinsic context plus a random seed.

## Method

GeoFlow augments each area pair with relative position (PCA-aligned, normalized to [-1,1]), k-hop distance, and geodesic distance alongside straight-line distance, then encodes them through a geometric-intrinsic fusion encoder: an MLP maps pairwise geometric attributes to embeddings, while a graph-attention module—conditioned on the geographic tensor via a learned score matrix—aggregates intrinsic area attributes (POIs, demographics) across neighbors. The decoder alternates origin-wise and destination-wise self-attention (axial attention, O(n³)) with a compact global-attention pathway that pools OD-pair embeddings to area-level representations, applies full self-attention (O(n²)), and expands back to the n×n grid via a Hadamard product, capturing competitive dependencies among areas sharing an origin or destination. For generation, GeoFlow is paired with continuous-time flow matching (the first application to OD flow), which learns a velocity field over a linear-Gaussian interpolation path conditioned on the fused geometric-intrinsic embeddings, yielding more stable training and faster inference than diffusion-based alternatives.

## Results

On the prediction task, GeoFlow achieves the best CPC, NRMSE, and JSD across all three OD regimes (Commuting, Freight, Air Travel), with headline numbers of CPC 0.604 / NRMSE 1.727 / JSD 0.171 on Commuting, CPC 0.645 / NRMSE 0.606 / JSD 0.048 on Freight, and CPC 0.808 / NRMSE 0.360 / JSD 0.013 on Air Travel. On the generation task, GeoFlow attains the highest diversity (Div. 0.069) and the best JSD on all three datasets, while also achieving the best CPC on Commuting (0.566) and Freight (0.485) and the best NRMSE on Freight (0.883). Compared to the strongest transformer baseline TransFlower, GeoFlow improves Commuting CPC by 12.4 percentage points (0.604 vs. 0.486) and reduces NRMSE by 19.5% (1.727 vs. 2.146).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On prediction, GeoFlow dominates all ten baselines on every metric across all three datasets; the largest gap is against the strongest transformer baseline TransFlower, where GeoFlow improves Commuting CPC by 12.4 pp (0.604 vs. 0.486), Freight NRMSE by 41.8% (0.606 vs. 1.042), and Air Travel JSD by 18.8% (0.013 vs. 0.016). Classical methods (Gravity, RF, GBRT, SVR) and early deep models (DGM, GMEL) are consistently outperformed by wide margins, with GeoFlow's Commuting CPC exceeding the best classical baseline (RF, 0.403) by 20.1 pp. On generation, GeoFlow achieves the best CPC on Commuting (0.566 vs. WEDAN's 0.527) and Freight (0.485 vs. WEDAN's 0.480), the best NRMSE on Freight (0.883 vs. WEDAN's 1.183), and the best JSD on all three datasets. The sole metric where GeoFlow trails a baseline is Air Travel generation CPC (0.457 vs. WEDAN's 0.486), though GeoFlow still leads on NRMSE (1.213 vs. 1.485) and JSD (0.031 vs. 0.036) for that dataset. GeoFlow also achieves the highest diversity score (0.069) among all generative baselines, indicating it avoids the mode collapse observed in NetGAN (0.065), DiffODGen (0.048), and WEDAN (0.053).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoFlow extends the graph-based OD flow modeling lineage (GMEL, DiffODGen, WEDAN) by explicitly augmenting area representations with geospatial descriptors—relative position, k-hop distance, and geodesic distance—that prior methods encode only implicitly through adjacency and straight-line distance matrices. The encoder adapts graph attention (Veličković et al., 2017) by conditioning inter-area attention on a geographic score matrix, while the decoder repurposes axial attention (Ho et al., 2019) to the n×n OD grid and complements it with a compact global-attention pathway. For generation, the paper is the first to apply continuous-time flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) to OD flow synthesis, replacing the diffusion-based approaches used by DiffODGen and WEDAN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly providing geospatial features that are difficult to recover from pairwise matrices (confirmed by a 51% angle-reconstruction error in their inference experiment) yields substantial accuracy gains over strong transformer baselines, offering a practical recipe for OD flow modeling in data-scarce regions. The axial-global decoder reduces attention cost from O(n⁴) to O(n³) while maintaining competitive accuracy, making the approach tractable for city-scale regions on a single GPU. Open questions include how well the method transfers when reliable network-geodesic or k-hop data are unavailable (as the Impact Statement acknowledges), whether the flow-matching advantage over diffusion generalizes to other structured generative tasks, and how performance scales beyond the ~1,300-area regions tested.
