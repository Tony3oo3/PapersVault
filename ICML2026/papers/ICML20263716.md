## Problem

PPO's ratio clipping mechanism, the de facto trust-region safeguard in LLM RL fine-tuning, is structurally ill-suited to the large, long-tailed vocabularies of LLMs. Because the probability ratio of a single sampled token is a noisy Monte Carlo estimate of true policy divergence, PPO aggressively over-penalizes updates to low-probability tokens (slowing learning) while under-penalizing potentially catastrophic shifts in high-probability tokens (risking instability). This paper asks whether the trust region can be redefined to directly constrain distributional divergence rather than a single-sample ratio.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a standard off-policy LLM RL loop: an inference engine samples rollout sequences from a behavior policy μ, a reward function (verifiable for math, or a learned reward model for RLHF) assigns a scalar score per response, and a trainer engine computes per-token gradients. The trust region is anchored to the original rollout distribution μ (not a recomputed on-policy distribution), and the divergence is computed per token position over the full vocabulary. The output is updated policy parameters that, when deployed, produce a fine-tuned LLM with improved task performance.

## Method

DPPO replaces PPO's ratio-clipping mask with a divergence-based mask that blocks an update only when the estimated policy divergence D(μ(·|s_t)‖π(·|s_t)) exceeds a threshold δ and the update is moving away from the trust region (r_t > 1 for positive advantage, r_t < 1 for negative advantage). To avoid the prohibitive cost of computing exact TV or KL divergence over a 100k+ vocabulary, DPPO introduces two lightweight approximations: a Binary approximation that collapses the distribution to a Bernoulli (sampled token vs. all others), and a Top-K approximation that tracks the K highest-probability tokens plus an 'other' bucket. Both are proven to be principled lower bounds on the true divergence. The paper further derives policy improvement bounds for the finite-horizon, undiscounted LLM setting (Theorems 3.1–3.2), showing that the average token-level TV divergence provides a tighter, linear-in-horizon guarantee than the quadratic max-divergence bound, directly justifying the per-token divergence constraint used in the algorithm.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0 (RLHF with Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507), DPPO-Binary-TV achieves 80.90 length-controlled win rate and 79.93 raw win rate, outperforming GRPO (77.05 / 72.20) and establishing a new SOTA on the community leaderboard. Across five large-scale AIME24/AIME25 experiments (MoE Base, MoE Base w/ R3, MoE Thinking, Dense Base, MoE Base w/ LoRA), DPPO consistently reaches higher final scores faster than GRPO-ClipHigher and CISPO, while maintaining stable training-inference mismatch. Notably, DPPO without rollout router replay (R3) outperforms R3-enhanced baselines, and anchoring the trust region to the behavior policy (rather than a recomputed distribution) reduces training cost by ~25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO-ClipHigher (the primary PPO-style baseline), DPPO wins on both training efficiency and final AIME24/AIME25 scores across all five large-scale experiments, with GRPO-ClipHigher suffering a significant training collapse in the MoE Thinking setting and consistently exhibiting excessively high entropy. Against CISPO (PG-TIS), DPPO clearly wins: CISPO collapses prematurely in the MoE Base w/o R3 experiment and underperforms its untruncated counterpart PG-IS, which the paper attributes to TIS systematically down-weighting low-probability token gradients. Against MiniRL (which anchors the trust region to a recomputed policy distribution), DPPO wins decisively: MiniRL fails to control training-inference mismatch and collapses, while DPPO anchored to the original behavior policy remains stable. Against unconstrained PG-IS, DPPO wins on stability (PG-IS accumulates mismatch and collapses), though the paper notes that only a minimal divergence-based mask on negative samples is needed to restore stability, confirming that a small fraction (≤0.5%) of 'bad updates' drives the collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PPO's ratio-clipping mechanism and TRPO's divergence-based trust region theory, arguing that PPO's clipping is a noisy single-sample Monte Carlo estimate of true policy divergence that is structurally ill-suited to LLMs' large, long-tailed vocabularies. It departs from prior heuristic fixes (Clip-Higher, CISPO) that identify the over-penalization of low-probability tokens but do not address the root cause, and from MiniRL's use of a recomputed policy ratio as the trust-region anchor. The theoretical contribution derives finite-horizon, undiscounted policy improvement bounds specific to LLM generation, bridging the gap between classical TRPO theory (which assumes a discount factor) and the sequence-level, sparse-reward setting of LLM RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPPO offers a principled, low-overhead drop-in replacement for PPO/GRPO in LLM RL fine-tuning, with strong empirical validation across model scales (1.5B–30B), architectures (dense, MoE, LoRA), and tasks (math reasoning, RLHF alignment). Two findings have broad practical impact: the trust region must be anchored to the original rollout policy rather than a recomputed distribution (contradicting common open-source practice and saving ~25% compute), and truncated importance sampling—a widely adopted variance-reduction technique—actually destabilizes training by systematically down-weighting low-probability tokens. Open questions include whether the binary approximation remains sufficient for even larger or multimodal vocabularies, and how the divergence threshold δ should be adapted dynamically during training.
