## Problem

Reinforcing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) to leverage their intrinsic understanding for improved generation remains largely unexplored. Prior approaches rely on proprietary teacher models to synthesize cold-start SFT data, risking that gains stem from distillation rather than the model's own capabilities, while existing visual-generation RL reward models chase training-aligned metrics and fail to provide stable, discriminative signals for diverse real-world prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Reasoning T2I, the model takes a text prompt and produces an autoregressive reasoning sequence followed by a diffusion-generated image; for Self-Reflective Refinement, it takes an initial (deliberately low-quality) generated image plus the prompt and outputs a corrected image. Training operates at 512×512 resolution with a group size of 14 trajectories per prompt and 32 prompts per step, while evaluation extends to 1024×1024 without high-resolution fine-tuning. The unified trajectory τ = (y, z₁→z₀) concatenates discrete AR tokens with the continuous flow-matching latent path, and the reward is computed solely on the final image.

## Method

AlphaGRPO formulates multimodal generation as a single unified trajectory and applies GRPO end-to-end, propagating the image-based advantage to update both the autoregressive reasoning policy and the flow-matching generation policy simultaneously, without any cold-start SFT stage. The key reward mechanism, DVReward, uses an LLM to decompose each prompt into atomic verifiable rubrics across 10 semantic dimensions and 8 perceptual-quality aspects, then scores each rubric via the Yes/No token probability ratio of a general MLLM (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B), combining semantic and quality scores via geometric mean. A False-Positive Rectification step assigns the group-minimum reward to any refinement trajectory that fails to improve over the initial image, preventing spurious positive advantages. The framework is implemented with LoRA (r=32) on Bagel over 380 steps on 64 A100 GPUs, using SGLang-based decentralized asynchronous reward serving to eliminate verification latency.

## Results

At the 512×512 training resolution, AlphaGRPO (trained on Self-Reflective Refinement) achieves 79.1/79.5 on TIIF-Bench (Short/Long) versus BAGEL's 75.2/78.6, 86.3 on DPG-Bench versus 85.0, and 85.0 on GenEval versus 84.0. At 1024×1024 (no high-res fine-tuning), AlphaGRPO (RT2I) reaches 87.4 on GenEval and 78.7/79.5 on TIIF-Bench, outperforming BAGEL (86.6, 76.4/76.2). On GEdit-Bench (image editing, no editing training data), AlphaGRPO attains an overall score of 7.08, surpassing BAGEL (6.52) by +0.56 and all open-source editing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the BAGEL backbone (the same architecture before RL), AlphaGRPO yields consistent gains across all four T2I benchmarks: +3.7 on TIIF-Bench Short, +0.9 on TIIF-Bench Long, +1.0 on DPG-Bench, and +1.0 on GenEval at 512px; at 1024px the RT2I variant improves GenEval from 86.6 to 87.4. Compared to generation-only models (FLUX.1 dev, SD3 Medium), AlphaGRPO + Inf. SRR leads on TIIF-Bench (79.1 vs. 71.1) and GenEval (84.2 vs. 82.0), while unified models Show-o and JanusPro trail by 10–20 points on TIIF-Bench. On GEdit-Bench (image editing, where no editing data is used during training), AlphaGRPO (7.08 overall) surpasses all open-source editors including Step1X-Edit (6.70), OmniGen2 (6.41), and BAGEL (6.52), though it remains below GPT-4o (7.53) and Gemini 2.0 (6.32 on G_O but 7.85 on G_SC). The paper also shows that training on Self-Reflective Refinement generalizes to editing (+0.52 over BAGEL) without any editing-specific data, whereas RT2I training alone yields a smaller +0.33 gain on GEdit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from LLM reasoning and Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) from standalone diffusion models to the AR-Diffusion Unified Multimodal Model setting, formulating multimodal generation as a single hybrid trajectory (autoregressive text + diffusion latents) optimized end-to-end. It deliberately departs from prior UMM alignment work (e.g., UniRL, Nie et al. 2025) that relies on cold-start SFT with proprietary teacher models, arguing instead that RL alone can activate latent reasoning primitives already present in pretrained UMMs. The reward design (DVReward) contrasts with both holistic scalar scorers (VIEScore) and fine-tuned preference models (HPSv3, UnifiedReward), positioning a general MLLM used in a decompositional question-answering mode as a more stable and generalizable reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for applying RL to unified multimodal models without the costly cold-start SFT stage, and demonstrates that the resulting alignment generalizes to downstream tasks (editing) never seen during training. DVReward offers a reusable pattern for constructing interpretable, fine-grained rewards from general MLLMs without domain-specific fine-tuning, which could be adopted broadly. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (Bagel), 512-resolution training, reliance on outcome-only rewards (no process rewards for self-reflection), and the acknowledgment that the base model's noisy 512px outputs can compromise training stability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger UMMs, whether process rewards for self-reflection yield further gains, and whether the no-cold-start claim holds when the base model's latent reasoning is weaker.
