## Problem

Existing structure-augmented RAG systems suffer from two compounding failure modes: potential retrieval suspension, where missing relations in OpenIE-extracted knowledge graphs break search paths, and cumulative semantic drift, where embedding-based matching propagates irrelevant associations through graph traversal. These issues worsen as corpus scale increases, causing structure-augmented RAG to degrade faster than even naive embedding-based retrieval.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query and a pre-built ParseKG—a directed hypergraph whose nodes are named entities and whose hyperedges are entity-masked sentence residues (e.g., '##(born ##)is an ##memoirist.')—and produces a set of graph-level clues, each a (translated_entity, translated_relation_span) pair. These clues are backtracked to their source sentences and passages, then reranked by a lightweight embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to yield Top-5 supporting passages for a general LLM to generate the final answer. The number of clues is controlled by beam sizes (α, β), which define how many entity entries and relation spans are decoded per query.

## Method

KG-Translator reframes passage retrieval as a constrained translation task over a parsing-based knowledge graph. First, a lightweight NLP pipeline (spaCy en_core_web_trf) performs sentence segmentation, NER, and syntactic parsing to build ParseKG, where hyperrelations are the raw text residues after masking all entities—preserving complete relational semantics without OpenIE. Second, a small LLM (Llama-3.2-1B or Qwen-2.5-1.5B) is fine-tuned to autoregressively translate the query into clues, with decoding constrained at each token by a Trie (prefix constraint over head entities) and an FM-index (substring constraint over adjacent hyperrelation spans), ensuring every generated clue is faithful to the graph. A bidirectional mechanism (forward then reverse translation) captures both outgoing and incoming hyperrelations. Third, the decoded clues are traced back to their originating passages, and a lightweight ranking model selects the Top-5 contexts. The entire graph construction incurs zero token cost, and the translation runs in constant time relative to corpus size.

## Results

On four multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue, ScaleQA), KG-Translator with Llama-3.2-1B achieves an average F1 of 68.45% and EM of 60.83%, outperforming the strongest baseline GFM-RAG (61.84% F1, 54.08% EM) by 6.61% and 6.75% respectively. The Qwen-2.5-1.5B variant achieves nearly identical performance (68.22% F1). KG-Translator also demonstrates zero-shot generalizability on PopQA, improving over NaiveRAG by 1.20% EM and 1.02% F1 without additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KG-Translator outperforms all 14 baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest structure-augmented RAG baseline GFM-RAG, it gains 6.61% F1 on average, with the largest margin on 2WikiMQA (+9.16% F1) and MuSiQue (+7.87% F1). Notably, RAPTOR and GraphRAG actually underperform NaiveRAG (Top-5) by 2.42% and 12.62% F1 respectively, indicating that their structural overhead introduces more noise than signal. Among the HippoRAG family, KG-Translator beats HippoRAG by 9.10% F1 and HippoRAG2 by 10.03% F1, with the gap widening on ScaleQA (large corpus) where HippoRAG2 degrades to 54.85% F1 while KG-Translator reaches 68.33%. The method also surpasses PER-QA (the closest structural competitor at 61.41% F1) by 7.04% F1, and achieves this with only 3.74 average retrieved contexts versus 5 for all baselines, while incurring zero token cost for graph construction (288s vs. 3,365–4,789s for OpenIE-based methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant structure-augmented RAG paradigm (HippoRAG, GFM-RAG, LightRAG, RAPTOR, GraphRAG) that relies on OpenIE-based knowledge graph construction and embedding-driven graph traversal (PPR, GNNs) for retrieval. Instead, it repurposes constrained decoding techniques from generative retrieval (Trie for prefix constraints, FM-index for substring constraints, as used in autoregressive entity retrieval and code search) to frame passage retrieval as a query-to-clue translation task over a parsing-based hypergraph (ParseKG). The graph construction itself draws on classical NLP (spaCy NER and syntactic parsing) rather than LLM-based extraction, positioning the work at the intersection of constrained generation and lightweight structural indexing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates that structure-augmented RAG systems degrade more sharply than NaiveRAG as corpora scale, attributing this to retrieval suspension (missing relations) and cumulative semantic drift (embedding noise amplified by graph propagation). The translation paradigm offers a practical alternative: zero token cost for graph construction, constant-time clue generation, and robustness to corpus scaling, all achievable with sub-2B models. Open questions include whether the entity-constrained indexing bottleneck (dominating latency at scale) can be further mitigated, how to combine translation-based and matching-based retrieval for complementary gains, and whether the approach extends to non-English or domain-specific corpora where NER quality may degrade.
