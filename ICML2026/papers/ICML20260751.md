## Problem

In online decentralized MARL, federated-style convex aggregation of Q-values (e.g., FedAvg) causes a 'learning rollback' phenomenon: when agents have heterogeneous uncertainty due to non-uniform state-action visitation, convex averaging degrades confident estimates toward uncertain ones, slowing or reversing overall learning progress. Existing convergence guarantees for federated Q-learning rely on uniform ergodicity or i.i.d. sampling assumptions that are fundamentally violated in online learning, leaving no principled aggregation rule that preserves the accuracy of well-trained agents while still incorporating information from less-experienced ones.

## Input / Output

Each agent's input consists of its local Q-table (|S|×|A| matrix), its visitation difference matrix D_i^t (per state-action count of local updates since the last consensus step), and the corresponding Q-tables and D matrices received from neighbors over the communication graph. The output is an updated consensus Q-table per agent plus per-state-action adaptive weights W_{i,m}^t that are deliberately non-convex (can be negative or exceed 1), enabling extrapolation toward more confident estimates rather than interpolation. Weights are computed element-wise over the state-action space, so different (s,a) pairs receive different aggregation coefficients within the same consensus step.

## Method

The core contribution is the Adaptive Global Consensus (AGC) mechanism, which replaces convex averaging with uncertainty-aware weights constructed from the visitation difference matrices via the operator Pow(D) = γ^D, where γ^k quantifies the residual Bellman-contraction error after k local updates. For a target agent m and neighbor i with D_m^t ≠ D_i^t, the weight W_{i,m}^t is derived by solving a two-equation system that estimates the true policy value and the initialization offset from the pair's Q-values, yielding weights that extrapolate toward the more confident agent (W > 1 or W < 0) while maintaining the normalization constraint ΣW = 1. A fully decentralized recursive algorithm (Algorithm 1) computes these weights using only pairwise neighbor-to-neighbor communication every E steps, requiring no central coordinator. Theoretically, one AGC update is equivalent to augmenting the target agent with all neighbors' local updates, accelerating convergence from γ^{t_m} to γ^{t_m + Σ t_i} while bounding the variance of the aggregated estimate, and the method reduces to standard averaging when all agents share equal visitation counts.

## Results

On the MPE cooperative navigation task (2 agents, 10×10 grid, H=10, γ=0.9), FedAGC achieves a validation mean score of 8.485 (near the global optimum of ~8.5), compared to 2.243 for FedAVG and 3.909 for direct data sharing (Table 2). In the scalability experiment, all agent counts M∈{2,…,12} reach 100% collective task completion, with convergence episodes decreasing approximately exponentially as M grows. Under stochastic reward noise (M=8), FedAGC maintains near-optimal mean scores (28.19–28.88) for std(ε)≤0.4, degrading gracefully to 24.41 at std(ε)=0.6 (Table 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is FedAVG (Li et al., 2019), which uses uniform convex averaging for Q-value aggregation. FedAGC outperforms FedAVG by a wide margin: 8.485 vs. 2.243 mean validation score (~3.8× improvement) on the MPE task, and FedAVG fails to converge to the optimal policy at all, exhibiting the learning rollback phenomenon that motivates the paper. FedAGC also substantially outperforms direct experience data sharing (8.485 vs. 3.909), demonstrating that parameter sharing with adaptive weights is both more sample-efficient and more communication-efficient than raw data exchange. In the symbolic MDP rollback experiment (Fig. 3), FedAVG shows clear degradation of the well-trained agent's estimate upon aggregation, while AGC eliminates the rollback entirely. Additional Fed-style adaptive baselines (FedADP_lin, FedADP_exp) also suffer from residual rollback, though FedADP_exp approaches AGC performance; the zoomed-in error comparison confirms AGC consistently maintains lower aggregation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated Q-value aggregation paradigm (Fed-Q, Zhuo et al. 2019) and convex parameter averaging (FedAvg, Li et al. 2019) to the online decentralized MARL setting, where the uniform ergodicity assumption underlying their convergence guarantees is violated. The key departure is identifying that convex aggregation weights are fundamentally insufficient when agents have heterogeneous update depths, and proposing non-convex adaptive weights (AGC) that can be negative or exceed one. The theoretical framework builds on Bellman contraction properties for policy evaluation and draws on federated optimization literature on heterogeneity (Reddi et al. 2020; Jin et al. 2022), but applies these ideas to the specific structure of online Q-learning with per-state-action visitation counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal characterization of learning rollback as an inherent consequence of convex aggregation over heterogeneous-uncertainty estimates provides a clear diagnostic for why federated-style MARL methods underperform in practice, and the AGC mechanism offers a principled fix with convergence and variance guarantees. However, the current treatment is restricted to tabular state-action spaces, homogeneous agents, and fully connected networks, leaving open whether the approach extends to function approximation, non-linear operators, and sparse or dynamic topologies. The paper also raises the broader question of whether more agents always help in decentralized learning, suggesting that naive parameter sharing can be counterproductive without uncertainty-aware weighting.
