## Problem

Safety evaluation in spoken dialogues remains text-centric, ignoring prosodic cues and transcription failures that are critical in real voice-agent deployments. Existing speech toxicity datasets annotate isolated utterances rather than multi-turn dialogue structure, and no systematic study has examined how large audio-language models (LALMs) behave as safety judges across modalities, transcription sources, and turn positions in multi-turn spoken interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261832/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each judge receives a multi-turn spoken dialogue (3–10 turns) in one of three modalities: audio-only, transcription-only, or audio+transcription, with transcriptions sourced from ground-truth labels or Whisper-Large/Base ASR. The judge produces a single scalar safety score in [0,1] (higher = safer) under a fixed prompting interface. The benchmark comprises 24,000 unsafe variants derived from 100 base dialogues, where each variant differs from its safe counterpart in exactly one replaced turn, spanning 8 unsafe categories and 5 ordinal severity levels, enabling fine-grained attribution of safety judgments to localized content.

## Method

The paper constructs a controlled benchmark by taking 100 safe multi-turn dialogues from DEEPDIALOGUE, using GPT-4o to rewrite a single target turn into unsafe content conditioned on a specified category and severity level while preserving conversational coherence, then synthesizing the revised turn with speaker-conditioned XTTS-v2 TTS to produce spoken variants. Six LALMs (Qwen2-Audio, Audio Flamingo 3, MERaLiON, Qwen2.5-Omni, MiniCPM-o, Gemini-2.5-Flash) plus an LLaMA text-only baseline are evaluated as zero-shot safety judges across 24 configurations (model × modality × transcription source) using five prompting strategies selected via dialogue-level 5-fold cross-validation. Three complementary metrics are defined: Sensitivity (minimum score drop across severity levels), Specificity (fraction of correctly ordered severity pairs), and Position Bias (difference in metric when the unsafe turn is last vs. first), with 95% confidence intervals computed via cluster bootstrap over dialogues. A human anchor study with 5 raters validates construct validity (κ≈0.84 for safety, κ_w≈0.80 for severity), and masked-audio prosody controls isolate non-lexical acoustic contributions to safety judgments.

## Results

Under GT transcriptions, the text-only LLaMA baseline achieves the highest Sensitivity (0.457, 95% CI [0.436, 0.480]) and Specificity (0.941, 95% CI [0.930, 0.953]) across all 24 configurations. Among the six LALM judges, Audio Flamingo 3 reaches the best Sensitivity (0.280, 95% CI [0.221, 0.337]) in multimodal mode, while MERaLiON attains the best Specificity (0.937, 95% CI [0.919, 0.952]) in transcription-only mode. Position bias (AAPB_sens) ranges from 0.029 (Qwen text-only) to 0.310 (LLaMA GT), revealing a stability–recall trade-off where the highest-sensitivity configurations are also the most position-unstable. Modality effects are model-dependent: adding audio improves Qwen's Spec from 0.667 to 0.800 but increases its Sens position bias, while MERaLiON's highest Sens occurs in audio-only (0.229) despite its best Spec being in text-only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LLaMA-3.1-8B text-only baseline dominates both Sensitivity (0.457) and Specificity (0.941) under GT transcriptions, outperforming all six LALMs by a wide margin on Sens (next best: Flamingo at 0.280) and narrowly on Spec (next best: MERaLiON text-only at 0.937). Among the LALMs, no single model wins across all metrics: Audio Flamingo 3 achieves the highest LALM Sensitivity (0.280 multimodal) but its audio-only mode is the weakest (Sens −0.064), while MERaLiON shows the most consistent Spec (≈1.0 across all modalities) but lower Sens than Flamingo in multimodal. Qwen2-Audio benefits most from adding audio to transcriptions for Spec (0.667→0.800) but suffers a large negative Sens in multimodal (−0.114), indicating audio interferes with its detection. Qwen2.5-Omni is audio-dominant for Sens (0.089 audio vs. 0.028 text) yet has the weakest Spec among competitive models (0.1–0.3), Gemini-2.5-Flash is conservative and stable (Spec ≈1.0 across modalities, Sens ≈0.07–0.08), and MiniCPM-o-4.5 is the clear negative case with negative Sens and near-zero Spec in all modalities. Replacing GT with Whisper transcriptions reduces LLaMA's Sens from 0.457 to 0.282–0.288 while largely preserving Spec (0.918–0.924), and the effect is model-specific: Gemini loses text-only Spec under Whisper, Qwen2.5-Omni gains multimodal Sens, and MiniCPM inverts its text-only Sens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024) for large audio-language models in a setting where no prior systematic study exists. It extends the line of speech toxicity work (DeToxy, ADIMA, MuTox, ToxicTone) from single-utterance classification to multi-turn dialogue-level safety judgment, and goes beyond text-centric guardrail datasets (Aegis, Nemotron Content Safety Audio) by incorporating audio-native cues. The controlled single-turn perturbation design draws on the dialogue-quality evaluation methodology of DEEPDIALOGUE, while the Pareto and position-bias analyses adapt robustness-testing ideas from the LLM-judge literature to the spoken domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open benchmark enabling reproducible, fine-grained evaluation of LALM safety judges in multi-turn spoken dialogue, filling a gap that limits deployment of voice agents in safety-critical settings. The finding that text-only LLaMA outperforms all LALMs on sensitivity, and that audio can degrade rather than improve severity ordering for some models, challenges the default assumption that multimodal is strictly better and provides concrete configuration guidance. Key open questions include whether these trade-offs hold for natural (non-TTS) speech, multilingual settings, and compounding multi-turn harms, all of which the authors acknowledge as limitations of the current synthetic, English-only, single-turn-perturbation design.
