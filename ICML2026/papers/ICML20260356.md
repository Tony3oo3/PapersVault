## Problem

Existing spatial gene expression prediction methods impose Cartesian or geometry-agnostic locality assumptions that mismatch the hexagonal spot layout of platforms like Visium, producing anisotropic receptive fields. Additionally, standard point-wise regression objectives encourage predictions to regress toward local means, over-smoothing gene-specific spatial heterogeneity such as sharp tumor-stroma boundaries and immune niches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260356/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes spot-centered H&E image patches (extracted from whole-slide images) and their 2D Cartesian spatial coordinates. Patches are encoded by the pretrained UNI pathology foundation model into fixed-dimensional visual tokens, while coordinates are mapped to integer axial/cube coordinates on a pointy-top hexagonal lattice using a median-neighbor-distance normalization. The output is a per-spot gene expression vector of dimension 128 (dataset-specific target gene panel), predicted by a gene head operating on the final token representations.

## Method

HEXST is a 4-stage × 3-block Transformer that partitions spots into multi-scale hexagonal windows (radius K_l increasing per stage) and performs shifted-window multi-head self-attention (HexMSA) within each window, with window centers offset by half-basis-vector shifts across successive blocks to enable cross-window information flow. Relative spatial geometry is encoded via HexRoPE, a rotary positional embedding that distributes per-head channels across three cube-coordinate axes (u, v, w) and rotates query/key pairs by angles proportional to the lattice offset along each axis, preserving translation equivariance within windows. To counter over-smoothing, a deviation-matching loss aligns per-gene standardized deviations (expression minus batch mean, normalized by gene-wise std) between predictions and ground truth, computed in embedding space via a separate deviation head. A transcriptomic feature alignment loss additionally regularizes intermediate representations toward scFoundation embeddings of the ground-truth expression, injecting single-cell transcriptomic priors during training only.

## Results

Across seven SpaRED spatial transcriptomics datasets, HEXST achieves the best average performance on all five evaluation metrics: PCC_F = 0.4227, PCC_S = 0.7697, MI_F = 0.1540, AUC_0vNZ = 0.6820, and AUC_Q50 = 0.7227. Compared to the strongest baseline (PEKA), HEXST improves PCC_F by +0.0364, PCC_S by +0.0558, MI_F by +0.0431, AUC_0vNZ by +0.0312, and AUC_Q50 by +0.0348. HEXST ranks first in 5 out of 7 datasets for PCC_F, PCC_S, and AUC_0vNZ, and in 6 out of 7 for MI_F and AUC_Q50. On downstream clinical tasks, HEXST-guided representations yield 48.55% accuracy (κ = 0.6743) on TCGA-PRAD Gleason grading and 0.5970 C-index on TCGA-LUAD survival prediction, both outperforming PEKA-guided representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEXST outperforms all seven baselines on every metric averaged across the seven SpaRED datasets. The closest competitor is PEKA (2025), which uses parameter-efficient fine-tuning with knowledge distillation; HEXST leads PEKA by +0.0364 in PCC_F and +0.0558 in PCC_S, with the largest relative gap in MI_F (+0.0431, a ~39% relative improvement). NH2ST (2025), a dual-branch contrastive learning method, is the second-strongest baseline but trails HEXST by 0.095 in PCC_F and 0.061 in PCC_S. Earlier methods show a clear progression: EGNv2 (2024) and TCGN (2024) achieve moderate PCC_F (~0.20–0.25), while CNN- and attention-based methods from 2020–2023 (STNet, Hist2ST, EGNv1) remain near-chance on gene-wise metrics (PCC_F < 0.06). Notably, HEXST also wins on per-dataset rankings in 5–6 of 7 datasets for most metrics, whereas PEKA only achieves the top rank on AUC_0vNZ in VMB and AUC_Q50 in EHPCP1. On downstream TCGA tasks, HEXST-guided representations consistently outperform PEKA-guided ones (e.g., +0.027 in κ for Gleason grading, +0.0066 in C-index for survival), though both fall slightly short of the bulk RNA-seq upper bound (0.6042 C-index).

## Relation to Prior Work

HEXST extends the shifted-window Transformer paradigm (originally designed for Cartesian image patches) to the hexagonal spot geometry of Visium-based spatial transcriptomics, introducing hexagonal window partitioning and a novel HexRoPE that generalizes rotary positional encoding from 1D/2D Cartesian offsets to hexagonal cube-coordinate offsets. It builds on the spatial gene expression prediction-from-histology line of work (STNet, Hist2ST, EGNv1/v2, TCGN, NH2ST, PEKA) but departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) encoding the non-Cartesian lattice structure as an architectural inductive bias rather than leaving it to be learned from data, and (2) complementing point-wise regression with a deviation-matching objective and transcriptomic feature alignment from scFoundation to counteract over-smoothing. The visual backbone (UNI) and the MIL aggregation (CLAM) for downstream tasks are adopted from existing pathology and spatial omics toolkits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By aligning the attention geometry with the actual sampling lattice of widely used ST platforms, HEXST addresses a concrete mismatch that prior Cartesian or graph-based methods ignore, and the deviation-matching loss tackles the practical problem of over-smoothed predictions that obscure biologically meaningful spatial boundaries (e.g., tumor-stroma interfaces). The transfer to TCGA-PRAD Gleason grading and TCGA-LUAD survival prediction without architectural modification demonstrates that the learned transcriptomic representations carry clinically relevant signal beyond the training distribution. Open questions include whether the hexagonal inductive bias generalizes to continuous-coordinate assays (e.g., MERFISH, Slide-seq), how the method scales to larger gene panels beyond the 128-gene SpaRED benchmark, and whether the observed trade-off between correlation-based (PCC) and distribution-based (MI, AUC) metrics under different loss configurations can be resolved more elegantly.
