## Problem

Unrolled transformers, trained to mimic optimization descent algorithms, exhibit non-monotonic loss along their layers during training—behavior inconsistent with a true optimizer. This arises because there is no guarantee that learned parameters satisfy the descent conditions, and the bilevel structure inherent in training unrolled architectures is not accounted for. The paper asks whether explicitly enforcing layerwise descent constraints during training can restore the monotonic-descent property and yield transformers that are more robust to input perturbations and distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

For text classification, the input is a sequence of T token embeddings in R^N with additive Gaussian noise (σ = γ·σ_x), and the output is a class label produced by a shared linear-softmax readout applied to the [CLS] token (DistilBERT/RoBERTa) or average-pooled layer outputs (UT) at every intermediate layer. For video denoising, the input is a sequence of 160×160 grayscale frames split into 16×16 patches with Gaussian noise, and the output is the reconstructed frame sequence; DUST and UT use tied attention parameters (Q=K=V=D) with N<D, while ViT processes each frame independently. For LLM fine-tuning (Llama 3.1 8B), the input is a perturbed instruction sequence and the output is a token-level completion evaluated by mean token accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is a constrained learning formulation (Eq. 4) that requires each transformer layer's expected loss to be at most (1−α_l) times the previous layer's expected loss, turning standard ERM into a nonconvex constrained program. Training proceeds via a primal-dual algorithm (Algorithm 1) that alternates SGD on the transformer parameters T (primal) with projected gradient ascent on non-negative Lagrange multipliers λ (dual), with negligible overhead over standard ERM. To handle feasibility, the paper employs resilient constrained learning, introducing slack variables with a quadratic penalty that is equivalent to applying weight decay (factor 1−1/β) to the dual update. Theoretical analysis establishes that the constrained transformer converges asymptotically in depth to a near-optimal statistical loss (Theorem 2, via a supermartingale argument) and retains descent behavior under distribution shifts bounded by an asymmetric distance metric (Corollary 3, Theorem 4), providing OOD generalization guarantees. The framework is applied to both unrolled architectures (UT, DUST) and conventional pretrained transformers (DistilBERT, RoBERTa, ViT, Llama 3.1 8B), demonstrating monotonic layerwise descent, improved OOD robustness under Gaussian, uniform, and discrete perturbations, and preserved in-distribution performance.

## Results

On video denoising (γ_train=0.13), constrained DUST with L=5 on CUHK Avenue achieves an average RMSE of 14.72 versus 28.27 for the unconstrained model (a 47.9% reduction), and constrained DUST with L=7 on Avenue drops to 14.52 vs. 63.31 unconstrained. On text classification (IMDb, RoBERTa L=24), the constrained model exhibits monotonic layerwise loss descent and degrades more gracefully under OOD embedding perturbations (γ up to 1.0), with the gap widening for larger models. On Llama 3.1 8B fine-tuned on Alpaca, the constrained model achieves a 69.93% length-controlled win rate over the unconstrained model at γ=1.5 perturbation, while maintaining 50% (parity) at γ=0.0, confirming preserved in-distribution performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between constrained (descent-constrained) and unconstrained (standard ERM) versions of the same architecture, rather than against external baselines. In video denoising, constrained models outperform their unconstrained counterparts in 5 out of 9 model–dataset configurations at γ_train=0.13, with the largest gains on CUHK Avenue (up to 47.9% RMSE reduction for DUST L=5) and UCSD (all constrained models consistently lower RMSE for γ≥1). In three cases performance is comparable, and in one case (UT-Avenue) OOD performance is slightly degraded. For text classification on IMDb, constrained RoBERTa and DistilBERT show notably better OOD accuracy under increasing Gaussian embedding noise, with the advantage growing with model size; on MNLI the effect is less pronounced. On Llama 3.1 8B, the constrained model shows virtually no in-distribution degradation (50% win rate at γ=0) while gaining a 69.93% win rate at γ=1.5, confirming that descent constraints improve robustness without sacrificing standard performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic unrolling literature (Gregor & LeCun, 2010; Yang et al., 2022; De Weerdt et al., 2023) by moving from architectural resemblance to enforced behavioral descent: prior unrolled transformers are trained via standard ERM and exhibit non-monotonic layer losses, whereas this work adds explicit layerwise descent constraints to the training objective. The training algorithm is built directly on the constrained learning framework of Chamon et al. (2023) and Chamon & Ribeiro (2020), with the resilient relaxation of Hounie et al. (2023) to handle feasibility. The OOD generalization theory adapts the supermartingale convergence argument from Hadou et al. (2024), which established descent-constraint robustness for non-transformer unrolled networks, to the transformer setting with attention-specific assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical convergence and OOD generalization guarantees specifically for transformers trained with descent constraints, and validates them across a wide range of architectures (DistilBERT, RoBERTa, Llama 8B, ViT, DUST, UT) and tasks (text classification, video denoising, instruction fine-tuning). The negligible computational overhead (only L additional scalar multipliers) makes the approach practically attractive. However, the robustness gains are inconsistent in video denoising (5/9 cases improved, some constrained runs diverge), the MNLI results show weaker effects, and the Llama 8B evaluation relies on a single GPT-5-judged AlpacaEval run. Open directions include combining descent constraints with primal-dual neural algorithmic reasoning, exploring gradient-based constraint formulations, and applying the framework to diffusion or latent reasoning models.
