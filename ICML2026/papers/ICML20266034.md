## Problem

Semi-supervised hyperspectral change detection relies on confidence-based pseudo-label selection, which creates a dilemma: high-confidence samples aggravate overfitting under limited labeled data, while low-confidence samples introduce noisy supervision that propagates errors. Existing influence-based selection methods are unstable in low-confidence regions where volatile gradients degrade reliability, and pre-trained foundation models lack decoders adapted to the discontinuous, sparsely distributed change patterns characteristic of HSI-CD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes bi-temporal hyperspectral image pairs from the EO-1 Hyperion sensor (242 spectral bands, 30 m spatial resolution) under a transductive semi-supervised setting where only 1% of pixels are labeled for training and 1% for validation, with the remaining 98% serving as unlabeled test samples. Inputs are tokenized into 5×5 patches with a batch size of 256, and the model outputs a per-pixel binary change detection map (changed vs. unchanged). The USA and China datasets are further cropped to simulate severe class imbalance (e.g., ~3,989 changed vs. 33,125 unchanged pixels on USA).

## Method

IAF-CDNet freezes the SpatialSigma ViT encoder and applies LoRA for lightweight adaptation, paired with a novel Adaptive Fusion Change Decoder (AFCD) that fuses bi-temporal difference features through a dual-path progressive strategy: a semantic path propagates global context from deep to shallow layers while a detail path aggregates local change cues from shallow to deep, with gated fusion blocks adaptively combining both paths. The key innovation is Influence-Aware Semi-supervised Fine-tuning (IA-SFT), which replaces confidence thresholds with an empirical influence measure: each low-confidence pseudo-label undergoes 3 iterations of sample-wise rapid fine-tuning, and its influence is quantified by the prediction shift it induces on labeled data, weighted by a sigmoid-based boundary-aware term and a binary inconsistency bonus. High-value pseudo-labels are retained if both the mean and variance of their influence scores across all labeled samples fall within the top 30%, followed by class-distribution calibration using high-confidence samples and progressive weight scheduling (0.2→1) during final semi-supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On the three benchmark HSI-CD datasets, IAF-CDNet achieves the best performance across all metrics. On River (most challenging), it reaches 97.28% OA, 83.11% KC, and 84.61% F1, outperforming the second-best PUL-DCD (82.14% F1) by 2.47 points. On China, it attains 98.95% OA, 93.41% KC, and 93.99% F1, surpassing DCENet (92.65% F1) by 1.34 points. On USA, it achieves 97.04% OA, 84.41% KC, and 86.06% F1, leading DCENet (83.48% F1) by 2.58 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAF-CDNet outperforms all nine HSI-CD baselines (DIEFEN, DCENet, GlobalMind, MSCSCNet, CSCANet, MS2FN, AIWSEN, PUL-DCD, SpikeHCD) on every metric across all three datasets. The largest margins appear on River, where IAF-CDNet's 84.61% F1 exceeds PUL-DCD (82.14%) and MS2FN (82.10%) by over 2.4 points, and on USA, where it beats DCENet (83.48%) by 2.58 F1 points. Among the pseudo-label strategy baselines (Table 5), IA-SFT achieves the highest pseudo-label accuracy (PLA) on all three datasets (94.73/97.24/95.12%), surpassing eFUMI (76.75/97.92/88.94%), ISAL (92.31/95.83/82.36%), and SemiReward (88.63/96.73/80.35%). Notably, eFUMI achieves the highest PLA on China (97.92%) but yields a lower F1 (92.95%) than IA-SFT (93.99%), confirming that PLA alone does not guarantee detection quality. The transferability experiments (Table 6) show that plugging IA-SFT into AIWSEN and SpikeHCD improves their F1 by 1.08 and 2.01 points respectively on River, validating the plug-and-play nature of the strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant confidence-based pseudo-label selection paradigm (FixMatch, FlexMatch, FreeMatch) and the gradient-based influence function literature (Koh & Liang, ISAL) by proposing an empirical, prediction-shift-based influence metric grounded in the mirrored influence hypothesis. It repurposes the foundation-model fine-tuning paradigm (SpatialSigma/HyperSigma + LoRA) for HSI-CD, but introduces a novel task-specific decoder (AFCD) with dual-path semantic/detail fusion and gated merging that is absent from the fixed decoders of existing hyperspectral foundation models. The training-coupled pseudo-label strategies (SemiReward, CGMatch, CPL) share the insight that pseudo-label quality should be judged by learning dynamics rather than confidence, but IA-SFT distinguishes itself by measuring influence through actual prediction shifts on labeled data rather than reward models or consistency losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of IA-SFT (demonstrated by integrating it into AIWSEN and SpikeHCD with consistent gains) makes it a potentially reusable component for the broader semi-supervised remote sensing community, not just HSI-CD. However, the per-sample rapid fine-tuning required for influence evaluation introduces non-trivial computational overhead that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve, and the transductive setting (unlabeled data drawn from the test set) limits direct applicability to real-world deployment where future images are truly unseen. Open questions include whether group-level or batch-level influence estimation can approximate per-sample results at lower cost, whether the approach extends to inductive or cross-domain scenarios, and whether the boundary-aware weighting and mean-variance selection criteria generalize beyond binary change detection to multi-class or multi-temporal settings.
