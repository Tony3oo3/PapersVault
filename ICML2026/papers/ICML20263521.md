## Problem

Existing Byzantine-robust federated learning methods assume all clients participate every round, an assumption that breaks down under the partial participation required for communication efficiency at scale. When only a subset of clients is sampled each round, the sampled set can contain a Byzantine majority even if the global Byzantine ratio is below 1/2, causing robust aggregators to fail immediately. The paper addresses this Byzantine majority round problem, which prior work either ignores or handles with impractical requirements such as large minibatches.

## Input / Output

At each round, the server receives fresh local momentum vectors from the sampled clients (computed from a single stochastic gradient with coefficient α) and reuses server-side cached momentum for non-sampled clients, reweighting them by a product of (1−α_s p_s) factors over the delay period. The robust aggregator then operates on the full set of n momentum vectors (sampled + cached), producing a single aggregated update that is subtracted from the global model parameters. No additional communication is required beyond the standard partial-participation protocol: only sampled clients transmit, and the server maintains one momentum vector per client in memory.

## Method

DeMoA introduces the delayed momentum aggregation principle: the server applies a (δ,c)-robust aggregator (e.g., Krum, coordinate-wise median, centered clipping, RFA) not only to fresh momentum from sampled clients but also to cached momentum from non-sampled clients, ensuring the aggregator always sees all n clients and thus the global Byzantine ratio δ < 1/2 is preserved at every round. A key design choice is using (1−αp) as the momentum decay coefficient for both sampled and non-sampled clients rather than the standard (1−α), which decouples the randomness of client sampling from the momentum recursion and avoids an extra variance term proportional to the norm of delayed momentum. The preprocessing function P reweights a client's cached momentum by the product of (1−α_s p_s) over its delay, removing the implicit momentum bias that would otherwise accumulate from stale updates. Theoretically, DeMoA achieves a convergence rate of O(cδζ² + √((LF⁰ + cδ(σ²+ζ²))(σ²+(1−p)ζ²)/(pGT))) under (ζ,B)-heterogeneity, matching full-participation lower bounds and eliminating the non-vanishing error term under overparameterization (0,B)-heterogeneity.

## Results

With a 20% Byzantine ratio (δ=0.2) and only 10% partial participation rate (p=0.1) on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), DeMoA achieves the best test accuracy across all five Byzantine attacks (ALIE, Bit-Flipping, IPM, Label-Flipping, Mimic), while FedAvg and FedCM collapse in the very first epoch due to an immediate Byzantine majority round. On MNIST (ConvNet) with p=0.5, DeMoA similarly attains the highest and most stable accuracy, whereas FedAvg and FedCM collapse by epoch 4. In the non-Byzantine baseline (δ=0, p=0.5), DeMoA also outperforms FedCM on both IID and non-IID splits, suggesting delayed momentum aggregation acts as an implicit regularizer under heterogeneity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), DeMoA wins decisively: under partial participation with δ=0.2, FedAvg encounters a Byzantine majority round almost immediately (first epoch on CIFAR-10 with p=0.1; epoch 4 on MNIST with p=0.5) and diverges, while DeMoA remains stable and converges to the highest accuracy. Against FedCM (Xu et al., 2021), DeMoA also wins on both Byzantine and non-Byzantine settings; even without any Byzantine clients, FedCM's explicit momentum gains are negated by the implicit momentum effect from partial participation, whereas DeMoA's preprocessing function corrects this and yields better test accuracy. Against Byz-VR-MARINA-PP (Malinovsky et al., 2024), DeMoA achieves higher final test accuracy: Byz-VR-MARINA-PP remains stable under Byzantine attacks but suffers from a persistent bias introduced by its clipping mechanism, resulting in consistently lower accuracy than DeMoA across both IID and non-IID data on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: Byzantine-robust FL under full participation (robust aggregation + local momentum, per Karimireddy et al. 2021/2022, Gorbunov et al. 2023) and communication-efficient FL with partial participation (FedAvg, FedCM, MIFA). The key departure is the 'delayed momentum aggregation' principle: by reusing cached momentum from non-sampled clients alongside fresh updates, the server always sees the global Byzantine ratio δ<1/2, preventing the Byzantine-majority rounds that break existing robust aggregators under partial participation. Conceptually, this repurposes the client-caching idea from MIFA (Gu et al., 2021) for Byzantine robustness, but adds a critical momentum correction (using coefficient 1−αp rather than 1−α) to eliminate an implicit-momentum variance term that would otherwise degrade convergence. The two closest prior works—Allouah et al. (2024) and Malinovsky et al. (2024)—either lack variance reduction (vulnerable to time-coupled attacks like ALIE) or require impractical large minibatches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Partial participation is essential for scaling FL to thousands of clients, yet the paper shows that with only 10% participation and 20% Byzantine ratio, standard methods (FedAvg, FedCM) collapse in the very first epoch on CIFAR-10—making this a practically urgent gap. The theoretical guarantee that the non-vanishing error term O(cδζ²) is not amplified by communication sparsity (unlike decentralized settings) is a clean and useful result, and under overparameterization the term vanishes entirely. Open questions include: dedicated adaptive attacks targeting the delayed-momentum mechanism, extension beyond Bernoulli sampling to other client-selection strategies, and integrating communication compression into the preprocessing function. The server-side memory requirement (one momentum vector per client) may be a concern at very large scale.
