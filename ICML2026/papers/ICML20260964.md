## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs trained on facts of the form "A is B" systematically fail to answer the inverse query "B is A" — the reversal curse. This paper argues the failure is not solely a data-coverage issue but a representation-access problem: causal masking induces a chiral asymmetry between subject- and object-oriented latent states, and standard autoregressive readout cannot expose inverse relational structure that may already be present in the embedding space.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes the last-token hidden state of an object entity (extracted from a frozen Llama-3-8B backbone) as the query, applies a learned Householder reflection to produce a reflected subject-oriented estimate, and retrieves the matching subject entity from a fixed bank of 100,000 model-derived entity representations using a temperature-scaled cosine score. The output is a Top-1 retrieved entity, not a generated token sequence; multi-token entities are handled via last-token pooling, and the identical knowledge bank is shared across all compared architectures to isolate structural effects from embedding variance.

## Method

The Chiral Transformer augments each Transformer block with a Chiral Projection Layer (CPL) — a single Householder reflection parameterized by one learnable normal vector v — inserted as a parallel residual stream after multi-head self-attention, gated by a learned sigmoid scalar. Training uses LoRA (rank 16) on the Llama-3-8B backbone plus full optimization of the CPL, with a dual objective combining standard cross-entropy and a chiral regularization term that minimizes the commutative gap between the forward-mapped state T(f(z_A, r)) and the stop-gradient adjoint state f(z_B, r⁻¹). At inference, Adjoint-Induced Retrieval (AIR) replaces autoregressive decoding with a single isometric projection of the object embedding followed by k-NN search over the entity bank, avoiding the cumulative causal drift that degrades sequential inverse generation. The Householder form guarantees T²=I and norm preservation by algebraic construction rather than through a learned penalty, providing a strict Z₂ inductive bias at negligible computational cost (O(d) per block versus O(d²) for the surrounding attention and FFN layers).

## Results

On the Fact-Inv-300 diagnostic benchmark, the Chiral Transformer (A1) achieves 65.07% inverse-relation retrieval accuracy under the AIR protocol, compared to near-zero (0.27%) for standard Llama-3 autoregressive readout. On the larger Wiki-Inverse-10K generalization set, A1 reaches 61.31% inverse accuracy while maintaining 90.48% forward fidelity. The method trades a modest forward-accuracy drop (82.13% vs. 88.47% on Fact-Inv-300) for a large inverse-access gain, and AIR inference is 3.18× faster than standard 5-token autoregressive decoding (68.4 ms vs. 217.2 ms per example).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A1 substantially outperforms both data-centric baselines on inverse accuracy: it beats semantic-aware permutation training (Guo et al., DA) by 32.6 points (65.07% vs. 32.47%) on Fact-Inv-300 and by 30.1 points (61.31% vs. 31.18%) on Wiki-Inverse-10K. Against bidirectional training (Golovneva et al., BDP), A1 gains 24.0 points on Fact-Inv-300 (65.07% vs. 41.13%) and 15.6 points on Wiki-Inverse-10K (61.31% vs. 45.76%). In the attribution analysis (Table 4), A1 also surpasses the DPR dense-retrieval baseline (Karpukhin et al.) by a wide margin (61.31% vs. 14.28% inverse accuracy on Wiki-Inverse-10K), indicating that the Householder adjoint map contributes beyond what a generic dual-encoder retriever can achieve. The trade-off is a 6.3-point forward-accuracy drop relative to standard Llama-3 on Fact-Inv-300, though on Wiki-Inverse-10K A1 retains 90.48% forward accuracy, only 0.76 points below the standard model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the reversal curse literature (Berglund et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2024) and geometric deep learning / group-equivariant modeling (Cohen & Welling 2016; Bronstein et al. 2021), departing from prior data-centric mitigations (reverse training, semantic permutation) by reframing the problem as a representation-access issue rather than a data-coverage gap. The core architectural ingredient — a Householder reflection as an involutive isometry — is repurposed from classical linear algebra (Householder 1958) into a lightweight inductive bias for enforcing Z₂ equivariance in Transformer hidden states. The Adjoint-Induced Retrieval (AIR) protocol draws on dense retrieval and nearest-neighbor language modeling (Karpukhin et al. 2020; Khandelwal et al. 2020) but applies the learned adjoint map as a query transformation over a model-derived entity bank rather than an external corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central contribution is a conceptual reframing: the reversal curse is not merely a memorization failure but a readout-access problem where causal masking creates a geometric orientation mismatch between subject- and object-oriented latent states. This opens a research direction of using explicit symmetry constraints (beyond data augmentation) to make latent relational structure more accessible. However, the practical scope is narrow — the method targets symmetric relations with well-defined inverses, relies on a fixed 100K-entity retrieval bank, and the single global Householder reflection may be under-expressive for heterogeneous or non-symmetric relations. Open questions include whether multi-head or relation-specific adjoint maps can generalize, whether the approach extends to open-ended generation without a candidate bank, and whether the representation-access framing holds at larger scales or with more diverse relation types.
