## Problem

MLLMs perform poorly on high-resolution images, and prior work attributes this to small object size, motivating 'zoom-in' (crop + upscale) strategies. This paper shows through hierarchical decoupling that the true bottleneck is background interference—both semantic distractors and token-level redundancy—rather than object size, and that upscaling alone provides no benefit. The key question becomes: how to selectively remove irrelevant background while preserving the spatial layout of query-relevant regions, without costly training or iterative search?

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (4K or 8K) and a natural-language question as input. It internally extracts cross-attention maps from a specific mid-layer of the MLLM (layer 15 for Qwen2.5-VL, layer 17 for InternVL3), identifies semantic tokens in the question, and produces a compact layout-preserving image where only query-relevant regions are retained on a transparent canvas with their relative spatial positions maintained. Both the original image and this compact image are fed to the MLLM alongside the question to generate the final VQA answer. The entire pipeline is training-free and reduces peak GPU memory from >96 GB to ~20 GB via targeted tensor offloading with FlashAttention.

## Method

HiDe consists of two modules. Token-wise Attention Decoupling (TAD) decomposes the question into individual semantic tokens (nouns, adjectives) versus non-semantic tokens, extracts each semantic token's cross-attention map over image patches at a chosen mid-layer, smooths the maps with a Gaussian kernel to combat visual-sink noise, and subtracts a background noise prior estimated from a generic 'search' prompt to purify the signal. Layout-Preserving Decoupling (LPD) then binarizes the purified maps at a threshold, extracts connected components as bounding boxes, and reconstructs a compact image via a grid-based mapping that stitches only content-bearing cells while discarding empty background space, strictly preserving the relative spatial configuration of all identified objects. The key insight is that semantic tokens naturally focus attention on compact, informative regions while function words disperse it, and mid-layers provide the strongest visual-semantic grounding signal before deeper layers shift toward reasoning. An engineering integration with FlashAttention computes attention weights only for the n semantic query tokens with CPU offloading, enabling the full pipeline to run within ~20 GB VRAM in a single forward pass.

## Results

HiDe achieves state-of-the-art results among training-free methods on high-resolution VQA benchmarks. With Qwen2.5-VL 7B, it reaches 92.1% average accuracy on V*Bench (94.8% Attr, 88.2% Spatial), 77.5% on HRBench-4K, and 75.4% on HRBench-8K. With InternVL3 8B, it achieves 91.6% on V*Bench and 77.1% on HRBench-8K. Through targeted tensor offloading with FlashAttention, HiDe reduces peak GPU memory from >96 GB (ViCrop OOM) to ~20 GB (a 75% reduction) while halving inference latency compared to ViCrop*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViCrop (the primary training-free baseline), HiDe wins on every benchmark and model: on V*Bench with Qwen2.5-VL 7B, HiDe scores 92.1% vs. ViCrop's 82.2% (+9.9), and on HRBench-8K with Qwen3-VL 8B, HiDe reaches 83.9% vs. 75.6% (+8.3). Against DeepEyes (RL-based), HiDe with Qwen2.5-VL 7B surpasses it on V*Bench (92.1% vs. 90.1%) and HRBench-4K (77.5% vs. 75.1%), while also improving HRBench-8K average (75.4% vs. 72.6%), despite DeepEyes having a slight edge on HRBench-8K FCP (58.5% vs. 57.3%). Against GPT-4o, HiDe substantially outperforms on all high-resolution benchmarks (e.g., 92.1% vs. 66.0% on V*Bench). The only method that exceeds HiDe is OpenAI o3, which reports 95.7% on V*Bench, though no HRBench results are provided for o3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'locate-then-zoom-in' framing of high-resolution MLLM perception by showing that the zoom-in operation conflates two distinct effects (upscaling and cropping) and that the latter—specifically background removal—drives most of the gain. Conceptually, it builds on attention-based cropping methods like ViCrop (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025) and tree-search approaches (ZoomEye, DyFo), but reframes the problem through a hierarchical decoupling analysis that isolates semantic-token attention from non-semantic noise and foreground from background redundancy. The resulting HiDe pipeline (TAD + LPD) is a training-free, architecture-agnostic repurposing of cross-attention signals already present in pretrained MLLMs, positioned as an orthogonal complement to dynamic-resolution encoders (Qwen-VL, InternVL3) rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that background token redundancy and semantic distractors—not object size—are the primary bottleneck for high-resolution MLLMs reframes how the community should think about fine-grained visual understanding, suggesting that reducing input clutter may be more effective than increasing resolution. Practically, the 75% peak-memory reduction (96 GB → 20 GB) and one-shot inference (vs. multi-pass search) make high-resolution MLLM deployment feasible on commodity hardware without any fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or ultra-high-resolution (>8K) settings, whether the fixed layer-selection heuristic transfers to future architectures, and whether the compact representation can be further compressed without sacrificing spatial fidelity.
