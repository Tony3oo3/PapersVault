## Problem

Riemannian generative models (diffusion and flow matching on manifolds) require tens to hundreds of neural network evaluations at inference, creating a computational bottleneck in scientific design pipelines where the model serves as a proposal mechanism within iterative optimization loops. While Euclidean few-step methods like consistency models and MeanFlow have addressed this for flat geometry, no principled framework existed for learning flow maps directly on curved manifolds such as SE(3)^N or the probability simplex.

## Input / Output

The method takes a random point on the target manifold as input (e.g., a noisy SE(3)^N frame for proteins or a point on the 4-dimensional simplex per position for DNA) and produces a single manifold-valued sample in one forward pass. For DNA promoter design, the model is additionally conditioned on a target transcription signal profile; for reward-guided generation, a differentiable reward function is evaluated via x1 look-ahead (predicting the terminal state from an intermediate step) to steer the flow without retraining. The x1-prediction parameterization means the network outputs a manifold-valued point directly, making it compatible with existing architectures like FrameDiff and FrameFlow that already predict SE(3) frames.

## Method

RMF learns a parameterized average velocity u_{s,t}^θ on the manifold, from which the flow map is recovered via the exponential map Φ_{s,t}(x) = exp_x((t−s)·u_{s,t}^θ(x)). The paper derives three equivalent characterizations of the manifold average velocity (Eulerian, Lagrangian, and semigroup identities) and converts each into a self-consistent regression objective. A critical design choice is the placement of the stop-gradient operator: derivative-dependent quantities are placed inside the stopped target, eliminating the need for higher-order derivatives during backpropagation and reducing training memory by nearly 2× compared to the concurrent GFM formulation. For parameterization, x1-prediction (network outputs a manifold point, average velocity recovered via the log map) is shown to be most stable at high dimensions (up to D=2048), and the semigroup objective combined with x1-prediction yields the most reliable training on high-dimensional manifolds like SE(3)^N with N up to 128.

## Results

On protein backbone generation (SCOPe), RMF with the semigroup objective and x₁-prediction achieves 82% designability (<2 Å scRMSD) at just 5 NFE, compared to 9% for FrameDiff and 4% for FrameFlow at the same budget; even at 1 NFE, RMF produces 35% designable samples while baselines collapse to 0%. At 100 NFE, RMF reaches 94% designability, matching or exceeding FrameFlow (93%) and FrameDiff (74%). On promoter DNA design (FANTOM5), 1-step RMF matches the 100-step Fisher FM baseline (MSE 0.030 vs 0.030; k-mer correlation 0.96 vs 0.96) while outperforming Dirichlet FM (MSE 0.030 vs 0.034).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FrameDiff and FrameFlow on protein backbone generation, RMF dominates in the few-step regime: at 5 NFE, RMF achieves 82% designability versus 9% (FrameDiff) and 4% (FrameFlow), and at 1 NFE it still yields 35% designable samples while both baselines produce 0%. At 100 NFE, RMF (94%) matches FrameFlow (93%) and substantially outperforms FrameDiff (74%), while also achieving the best scRMSD (1.01 vs 1.16 and 1.78). GENIE, which requires 500–1000 NFE, achieves only 22% designability at 1000 steps, far below RMF's 94% at 100 steps. On DNA promoter design, 1-step RMF (Eulerian, x₁-pred) matches 100-step Fisher FM exactly (MSE 0.030, k-mer corr. 0.96) and outperforms Dirichlet FM (MSE 0.034), demonstrating that the 10× NFE reduction comes at no quality cost. The paper also shows that reward-guided inference with x₁ look-ahead consistently improves downstream metrics (e.g., β-sheet fraction from 0.18 to 0.24 at 5 NFE) over both unguided generation and naive current-state guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Euclidean flow map learning (MeanFlow, Geng et al. 2025; Boffi et al. 2025) to Riemannian manifolds, deriving three equivalent characterizations of the manifold average velocity (Eulerian, Lagrangian, semigroup) that generalize the flat-space identities. It departs from the concurrent GFM work (Davis et al. 2025) in a critical design choice: placing the stop-gradient operator to encompass all derivative-dependent terms, thereby eliminating higher-order derivative evaluations and halving training memory. The x1-prediction parameterization adapts endpoint prediction from flow matching to the two-time flow map structure, enabling direct reuse of existing manifold-valued architectures (FrameDiff, FrameFlow, FoldFlow) without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing inference from hundreds to as few as one forward pass while matching multi-step quality on high-dimensional scientific tasks (SE(3)^N protein backbones, R^{1024×4} DNA simplices), RMF removes a key computational bottleneck in iterative scientific design pipelines where generative models serve as proposal mechanisms. The reward look-ahead technique enables test-time steering on manifolds without retraining, opening a path to property-guided design that was previously infeasible at scale. Open questions include whether the semigroup + x1-prediction combination generalizes to non-embedded or non-symmetric manifolds, and whether the efficiency gap over multi-step methods narrows as model scale increases further.
