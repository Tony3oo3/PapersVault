## Problem

Maximum likelihood world models in MBRL suffer from two core limitations: indiscriminate reconstruction wastes modeling capacity on task-irrelevant distractors, and the statistical objective is misaligned with decision-making utility. Existing remedies—explicit value prediction branches or heavy external priors like object detectors—introduce gradient dominance, prior-posterior value gaps, or significant computational overhead. The paper asks whether value awareness can be injected into the latent space intrinsically, without auxiliary modules or external dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261936/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as the underlying world model: 64×64 RGB frames (Atari 100k) or higher-resolution images / proprioceptive vectors (DMC Suite), paired with discrete or continuous actions. The key architectural detail is that the value network's full categorical distribution output (not a scalar) is used as the alignment anchor between prior and posterior latent states, and the method is designed as a plug-and-play regularization term that adds no new prediction heads or external models. Outputs remain standard: policy actions, reconstructed observations, predicted rewards, and continuation flags, with the latent space now implicitly biased toward decision-critical features.

## Method

The core contribution is a Latent-Space Value-Alignment Regularization (L_var) that minimizes the KL divergence between value distributions V_θ(v_t|s_t) inferred from the posterior state and V_θ(ṽ_t|s̃_t) inferred from the prior state, structured identically to the dynamics loss with stop-gradient operators to prevent gradient interference. An adaptive weighting mechanism sets β_var = 1/max(1, sg(KL[q||p])), using the inverse dynamics loss as a self-regulating curriculum: when dynamics are unstable (high KL), value alignment is suppressed; as the model matures, alignment strength increases. A 10,000-step warm-up phase deactivates L_var entirely during early exploration when value estimates are unreliable. The method integrates into both RSSM-based (DreamerV3) and TSSM-based (STORM) backbones with only a few lines of code, adding ~1.5% GPU memory and ~1% training time overhead while improving DreamerV3's Atari 100k human-normalized mean from 1.10 to 1.34 and median from 0.58 to 1.00.

## Results

On Atari 100k (26 games, human-normalized), DreamerV3+Var improves the mean score from 1.10 to 1.34 (+21.8%) and the median from 0.58 to 1.00 (+72.4%), with 24/26 games showing gains. STORM+Var similarly raises the mean from 1.14 to 1.36 and the median from 0.51 to 0.81. On the DeepMind Control Suite, improvements are more moderate: visual-input tasks improve from 792 to 827 mean (15/20 tasks), and proprioceptive tasks from 805 to 817 mean (13/18 tasks). The added computational overhead is negligible (~1.4% GPU memory, ~1% training time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the explicit value-prediction baseline MuDreamer, DreamerV3+Var achieves a substantially higher median (1.00 vs. 0.43) and mean (1.34 vs. 1.26) on Atari 100k, indicating that implicit latent-space alignment avoids the gradient-dominance and prior-posterior gap issues of auxiliary value branches. Against the external-prior baseline OC-STORM, STORM+Var matches the mean score (1.36 vs. 1.35) while nearly doubling the median (0.81 vs. 0.44), demonstrating superior robustness without the computational cost of object detectors. Against the pure value-aware baseline VaGraM, both DreamerV3+Var and STORM+Var dominate across all settings, confirming that a robust RSSM/TSSM backbone is essential for stable performance. On the DMC Suite, DreamerV3+Var outperforms TD-MPC2 (827 vs. 721 visual mean) and MuDreamer (827 vs. 784.7), though gains over the vanilla DreamerV3 backbone are more modest (4.4% visual, 1.5% proprioceptive) due to the inherently lower signal-to-noise ratio in continuous control. The two regressions (Frostbite, PrivateEye) are attributed to extreme reward sparsity where noisy value targets suppress exploration, a trade-off the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established MBRL paradigms: maximum likelihood world models (PlaNet, DreamerV3, STORM) that capture stochastic dynamics via RSSM/TSSM but treat all observation dimensions indiscriminately, and value-aware world models (VAML, VaGraM, CVAML) that prioritize decision-critical features but suffer from training instability and sub-optimal performance. Prior attempts to bridge these paradigms—MuDreamer's explicit value prediction branch, OC-STORM's external object-detector priors, and TEMPO's bi-level meta-weighting—each introduce auxiliary modules or heavy external dependencies. The key departure here is a minimalist latent-space KL alignment between prior and posterior value distributions, requiring no additional prediction heads, external models, or meta-architectures, and an adaptive weighting schedule that uses the dynamics loss itself as a self-regulating curriculum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a few lines of code (a KL term plus an adaptive weight) consistently improve both RNN-based (DreamerV3) and Transformer-based (STORM) world models across 46 environments with negligible computational overhead (~1.5% extra GPU memory, ~1% extra training time). The median Atari 100k score jumping from 0.58 to 1.00 for DreamerV3 suggests the method particularly helps the long tail of games where reconstruction fidelity alone is insufficient. However, regressions in extremely sparse-reward games (PrivateEye, Frostbite) and the modest DMC gains (1.5–4.4%) highlight an open trade-off between task-specific focus and exploratory breadth. Future work could explore whether the alignment principle extends to diffusion-based world models (DIAMOND) or whether the adaptive schedule can be replaced by learned, environment-aware weighting.
