## Problem

When real data are scarce but abundant synthetic data are available, practitioners face a dilemma: ignoring synthetic data yields overly conservative inference, while blindly pooling them risks uncontrolled error rates since the synthetic distribution may differ arbitrarily from the real one. No general framework existed that could safely leverage synthetic data across a broad class of inference procedures (conformal prediction, hypothesis testing, multiple testing) with distribution-free, finite-sample error rate guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small real dataset D_n (n samples), a large synthetic dataset D̃_N (N ≫ n samples, from any distribution Q with no assumptions), a base inference algorithm Alg_α that already controls risk at level α, and a user-specified tolerance ε > 0. The base algorithm is treated as a black box and invoked three times: on real data at level α, on real data at level α+ε, and on the pooled data at level α. The output is the same type as the base algorithm (prediction set, binary rejection decision, or rejection set), but with improved sample efficiency and a guaranteed worst-case error bound of α+ε.

## Method

GESPI aggregates the outputs of three runs of the base algorithm using a min (∧) operation: the synthetic-powered run Alg_α(D_n ∪ D̃_N) is combined with the guardrail run Alg_{α+ε}(D_n), so that the final output is the 'more conservative' of the two under a partial order on the action space. A two-sided variant additionally takes the max (∨) with the base run Alg_α(D_n), ensuring the output is deterministically sandwiched between the base method at levels α and α+ε (Theorem 3.4). The key theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.3) bounds the risk by α + min{ε, c·d_{ℓ,Alg}(P,Q)}, where the TV-distance-type term d_{ℓ,Alg}(P,Q) measures how well the synthetic distribution Q matches the real distribution P: when synthetic data are high quality the risk approaches α, while in the worst case the guardrail caps it at α+ε. The framework is distribution-free, requires no assumptions on the synthetic data, and applies uniformly to conformal prediction (via set intersection/union), one-sided hypothesis testing (via AND/OR logic), and multiple testing (via set union/intersection of rejection sets).

## Results

Across all applications, GESPI achieves risk/coverage close to the nominal target α with substantially lower variability than the Guardrail baseline, while never underperforming the OnlyReal base method. In protein structure prediction (CASP-14, α=5%), GESPI reduces the abstention rate from 100% (OnlyReal) to 85.6% for protein T1029 and from ~91% to ~48% for T1078, while maintaining risk at or near the target level. In image classification (ImageNet, 95% target coverage), GESPI achieves coverage near 95% with low variance, whereas Guardrail's realized coverage can drop as low as 60% for the Magpie class due to small sample size. In outlier detection (Shuttle, Credit Card, KDDCup99), GESPI attains FWER close to the target α with lower variability than Guardrail and power approaching that of an Oracle that has access to perfectly labeled inliers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GESPI against four internal baselines (OnlyReal, Guardrail, OnlySynth, Synth+Real) and one external method (SPI, Bashari et al. 2025a). Against OnlyReal, GESPI is guaranteed to dominate in utility (Theorem 3.4): it always produces prediction sets no wider, tests with no less power, and rejection sets no smaller, while achieving risk closer to the target α rather than the conservative level of OnlyReal. Against Guardrail (the same base method at the relaxed level α+ε), GESPI matches or exceeds its utility while achieving risk closer to α and with lower empirical variance—critical in practice where only a single dataset is available. Against SPI, GESPI is more sample-efficient for small synthetic sample sizes N (achieving coverage closer to 1−α with smaller prediction sets), while the two methods become comparable as N grows large; additionally, GESPI offers greater methodological flexibility since it treats the base inference procedure as a black box. In outlier detection, GESPI's power approaches that of an Oracle with perfectly labeled inliers, substantially outperforming OnlyReal on the ROC curve while maintaining FWER at the target level.

## Relation to Prior Work

GESPI is directly inspired by SPI (Bashari et al., 2025a), which incorporated synthetic data into conformal prediction via a score-space transportation. The key departure is architectural: rather than modifying the internal mechanics of the inference procedure (as SPI does for conformal prediction), GESPI treats the base method as a black-box and wraps around it with a three-run aggregation (base, guardrail, synthetic-powered), yielding a unified framework applicable to conformal prediction, hypothesis testing, and multiple testing simultaneously. The paper explicitly contrasts with prediction-powered inference (Angelopoulos et al.), semi-supervised learning, and robust statistics under distribution shift, noting that all of these impose structural assumptions on the relationship between real and auxiliary data, whereas GESPI requires none—synthetic data may come from an arbitrary distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, assumption-free recipe for any practitioner who has limited real data and abundant synthetic data: set α and ε, run the base method three times, and aggregate. The automatic adaptation to synthetic data quality (no a-priori knowledge needed) and the variance-reduction benefit beyond nominal risk control make it broadly applicable, as demonstrated across protein structure prediction, LLM comparison, and outlier detection. A key limitation is that the power gain is entirely contingent on synthetic data quality, and the paper acknowledges that adaptive selection of which synthetic data to use is an open direction. The framework's generality also raises the question of whether the ε relaxation is truly necessary or whether tighter constructions exist for specific problem classes.
