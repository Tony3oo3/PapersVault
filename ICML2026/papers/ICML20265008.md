## Problem

Existing multi-behavior recommendation models integrate auxiliary behaviors indiscriminately, allowing noisy or task-irrelevant interactions to contaminate graph message passing and mislead target-behavior learning. Prior information bottleneck approaches operate only at the representation level after noisy edges have already propagated, failing to suppress spurious auxiliary structures at their source. GCIB addresses this gap by jointly denoising auxiliary graphs structurally and enriching target representations semantically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265008/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous multi-behavior bipartite graph where users and items are shared nodes and each behavior type (e.g., click, cart, purchase) defines a separate edge set, along with a designated target behavior graph. It produces user-item recommendation scores via inner product of fused embeddings, where each user/item embedding is the average of its target-behavior and denoised-auxiliary-behavior representations. The edge retention masks learned during training are applied before graph propagation, so the denoised auxiliary subgraph is what feeds into the LightGCN layers rather than the raw interaction graph.

## Method

GCIB performs dual-level denoising: at the structural level, it learns a differentiable edge-retention mask for each auxiliary behavior graph using a Concrete-relaxed Bernoulli parameterized by target-behavior user-item embeddings, guided by a graph information bottleneck objective that maximizes a variational lower bound of I(R; G'_k) (equivalent to BPR loss) while minimizing HSIC(G'_k, G_k) to suppress redundancy with the original auxiliary graph. At the feature level, a cross-behavior InfoNCE contrastive loss aligns the denoised auxiliary representations with target-behavior representations for both users and items, transferring complementary semantics to sparse target supervision. A global LightGCN encoding over the full multi-behavior graph provides shared initialization, and the final prediction averages target and auxiliary domain embeddings. The total loss combines BPR recommendation loss, the HSIC-based IB regularizer, the contrastive alignment loss, and L2 weight decay, all optimized end-to-end.

## Results

GCIB achieves the best HR@10 and NDCG@10 across all four datasets: 0.1617/0.0944 on Tmall, 0.1815/0.1199 on Taobao, 0.0746/0.0358 on Yelp, and 0.0916/0.0429 on ML-10M. Compared to the strongest baseline, GCIB improves HR@10 by up to 40.5% on the sparse Yelp dataset and by 13.1% on the dense ML-10M dataset. The gains are most pronounced on NDCG@10, reaching +19.4% on Taobao and +37.2% on Yelp, indicating that GCIB not only retrieves relevant items but also ranks them more accurately.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCIB outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitors are the recent denoising/self-supervised methods: on Tmall, NSED (HR@10=0.1502) and BCIPM (NDCG@10=0.0831) are the strongest, with GCIB improving HR@10 by 7.7% and NDCG@10 by 13.6%. On Taobao, MBLFE is the nearest baseline (HR@10=0.1577, NDCG@10=0.0907), and GCIB surpasses it by 15.1% and 19.4% respectively. On the sparse Yelp dataset, MBLFE leads among baselines (HR@10=0.0531), yet GCIB achieves a 40.5% relative gain, the largest improvement across all settings. On ML-10M, BCIPM is the strongest baseline (HR@10=0.0810, NDCG@10=0.0392), with GCIB improving by 13.1% and 9.4%. Notably, cascaded models like CRGCN and MB-CGCN suffer from negative transfer on Yelp and ML-10M (e.g., CRGCN drops to HR@10=0.0370 on Yelp), whereas GCIB maintains consistent gains, demonstrating that its structural-semantic co-optimization effectively mitigates noise-induced degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCIB extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) principle from Wu et al. (2020) and the HSIC-based denoising framework of Yang et al. (2024, GBSR) by applying the bottleneck directly to auxiliary behavior graph structures via learned edge-retention masks, rather than compressing node representations after message passing has already propagated noise. It builds on LightGCN as the propagation backbone and introduces a cross-behavior InfoNCE contrastive objective that treats denoised auxiliary and target features as complementary views, a combination not jointly optimized in prior multi-behavior methods like BCIPM, NSED, or MBLFE. The key conceptual departure from recent IB-based recommenders (e.g., HGIB, Zhang et al. 2025) is the structural-level formulation: GCIB decides which auxiliary edges to retain before propagation, whereas prior IB methods operate at the embedding level post-propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode in multi-behavior recommendation—noisy auxiliary interactions degrading target-behavior prediction—by coupling structural pruning with semantic alignment in a single end-to-end framework. The dramatic ablation drop when both components are removed (Tmall HR@10: 0.1617 → 0.0356) suggests strong synergy between the two denoising levels, though the large dataset-dependent variation in the IB weight β (1 on Yelp vs. 80 on Tmall) raises practical concerns about transferability and tuning sensitivity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large industrial graphs, whether it generalizes to non-cascading or temporally dynamic behavior structures, and whether the Concrete-relaxation-based edge masking remains stable under high sparsity or adversarial perturbations beyond the 20% synthetic noise tested.
