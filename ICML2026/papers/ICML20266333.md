## Problem

Existing vector quantization methods treat codebooks as unstructured sets of independent vectors, leading to codebook collapse where many codes remain permanently inactive ('dead codes') and significant representational redundancy. Rather than optimizing the internal structure of the codebook, prior work simply scales up codebook size to compensate, incurring high computational cost and limiting fine-grained adaptation. This paper asks whether a compact, intrinsically structured codebook can match or exceed the representational quality of large unstructured ones.

## Input / Output

For audio, raw waveforms (32 kHz or 48 kHz) are encoded into 50 or 75 tokens per second and reconstructed; for visual, 256×256 RGB images are patchified into 64 tokens and reconstructed; for video-to-music, video frames at 1 fps are quantized into discrete IVQ codes that are directly dequantized in a music codebook to produce generated audio. A key design property is that the same IVQ codebook is shared across modalities—quantization is modality-agnostic while dequantization is modality-specific—so codes extracted from a visual encoder can be fed directly into a music decoder without any cross-modal transformation layer.

## Method

IVQ is a Residual-Product Quantization scheme organized as a 4-layer hierarchical codebook where each layer i uses n_i codes (2, 4, 8, or 64) to quantize K_i = 64/n_i blocks of the residual, so that all layers jointly span 64^4 combinations while the total codebook contains only 78 vectors (or as few as 8 in the Yin-Yang variant). Two structural losses enforce the IChing-inspired geometry: a hierarchical consistency loss (InfoNCE-style) aligns the same hexagram index across different quantization granularities, and a relational consistency loss encodes zong (reversal), cuo (negation), and cuo-zong (contrapositive) symmetries so that non-activated codes receive gradient through their geometrically related counterparts. This group-wise gradient propagation across 20 relational groups of 64 hexagrams eliminates dead codes without any restart or re-initialization trick, achieving 100% codebook utilization with codebooks 50–100× smaller than baselines.

## Results

On the MTG-Jamendo dataset, IVQ with an ultra-compact 4×2 codebook achieves the best reconstruction quality among all quantization methods: KLD 0.37, LSD 1.54, PSNR 24.21 dB, SSIM 0.56, and CS 0.77, outperforming RVQ (4×64) and PVQ (4×64) while using 32× fewer codes. On LibriSpeech, IVQ similarly leads with KLD 0.22, PSNR 23.47 dB, and CS 0.57. In the application-level comparison (Table 2), Encodec+IVQ achieves 100% codebook utilization (vs. 79% for Encodec, 32% for MuCodec, 21% for SemantiCodec) and the best PSNR (22.51 dB) and LSD (1.54) among all music codecs, though FAD (2.56) and FD (1.34) remain slightly behind WavTokenizer (1.44/0.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVQ (4×2) outperforms RVQ (4×64) and PVQ (4×64) on all five MTG metrics (e.g., PSNR 24.21 vs. 23.79/23.84; KLD 0.37 vs. 0.50/0.42) while using 32× fewer codes, and similarly dominates on LibriSpeech (PSNR 23.47 vs. 23.05/23.09). Naive VQ (1×4096) and FSQ (1×4375) suffer severe degradation (PSNR 16.61 and 15.47 on MTG), confirming that large unstructured codebooks with dead codes are inferior to compact structured ones. In the application comparison (Table 2), Encodec+IVQ beats Encodec (4×2048) on PSNR (22.51 vs. 21.25), LSD (1.54 vs. 1.67), and KLD (0.15 vs. 0.17) with 100% code utilization vs. 79%, but trails WavTokenizer (1×4096) on FAD (2.56 vs. 1.44) and FD (1.34 vs. 0.96). For video-to-music generation (Table 3), IVQV2M achieves the best KLD (1.05), FAD (3.15), CS (0.11), OMQ (3.21), and MVC (3.46) among all baselines including VidMuse, M²UGen, GVMGen, and Control V2M, despite not using any pretrained large-scale backbone. In visual reconstruction (Table 4), ViT-IVQVAE (78 codes) achieves the best FID (2.77) and IS (159.23) compared to ViT-VQVAE (4096 codes, FID 2.95, IS 151.33) and ViT-VQGAN (8192 codes, FID 23.15), with only a slight PSNR gap (17.85 vs. 21.79 for VQGAN).

## Relation to Prior Work

IVQ is positioned as a structured hybrid of Residual VQ (Chen et al., 2010) and Product VQ (Jegou et al., 2010), but departs from both by sharing a single codebook across all subspaces within a layer and by imposing explicit geometric symmetry constraints (zong/cuo/cuo-zong) inspired by IChing hexagram relations. The fundamental VQ-VAE framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) with its argmin quantization, straight-through estimator, and commitment loss is retained, but the unstructured codebook is replaced with a hierarchical binary-composition codebook and two new regularizers: a hierarchical consistency loss aligning codes across granularities and a relational consistency loss enforcing symmetry among codes. This contrasts with prior work that addresses codebook collapse through random reinitialization, EMA updates, or simply enlarging the codebook, rather than through intrinsic structural dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a codebook of 8 codes (4×2 Yin-Yang) can match or exceed the reconstruction quality of codebooks 1000–2000× larger in an Encodec-style audio codec, while achieving 100% code utilization versus 21–79% for baselines. This suggests that codebook structure, not size, is the primary determinant of VQ effectiveness, which could reshape how practitioners design quantization modules for deployment-constrained settings. However, the evidence is limited to a single codec architecture and moderate-scale datasets; the FAD/FD gap in music reconstruction, the acknowledged insufficiency of the Yin-Yang variant for high-fidelity image reconstruction, and the absence of evaluation on large-scale pretrained backbones leave open whether the approach scales to the multi-million-code regimes used in production systems like MusicGen or large VQ-VAE-2 models.
