## Problem

Real-world preference datasets for LLM alignment contain 20–40% noisy preference pairs, causing standard RLHF and DPO to overfit spurious signals. Existing robust methods either fail to achieve full noise-tolerance or require precise knowledge of the dataset noise rate, which is unobservable in practice. This paper asks whether one can provably recover unbiased alignment objectives from purely noisy data without clean supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264739/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard noisy preference triplets (prompt x, chosen y_w, rejected y_l) drawn from a corrupted distribution P^η, a frozen reference SFT model π_ref, and a single tunable scalar a ∈ [0,1) that upper-bounds the true noise rate. It outputs either an unbiased reward model r_φ* (for the RLHF pipeline) or an unbiased policy model π_θ* (for the DPO pipeline), both trained end-to-end via a single modified loss. A key practical property is parameter downward compatibility: if the assumed a exceeds the true noise rate, the loss still recovers the unbiased optimum, so practitioners can set a conservatively high (e.g., 0.8) without knowing the exact corruption level.

## Method

The core insight is that label-flip noise with rate η induces a known affine distortion on the Bradley-Terry preference probability, and this distortion is analytically invertible. By expressing the noisy reward/policy in terms of the unbiased counterpart and substituting into the standard RM or DPO loss, the authors derive two closed-form objectives: the URM loss −log((exp(Δ)+a)/(exp(Δ)+1)) for reward models and the UDPO loss with the same functional form applied to the DPO log-ratio margin. Both losses share a unified margin-based representation that preserves the RLHF–DPO equivalence. Gradient analysis shows the unbiased loss's gradient vanishes for highly misranked samples (where noisy pairs concentrate), preventing overfitting to corrupted labels, while peaking at intermediate margins to focus on genuinely ambiguous pairs. The paper further proves classification calibration and derives an explicit excess-risk bound, establishing that minimizing the unbiased loss recovers the Bayes-optimal classifier.

## Results

On supervised preference learning (Table 2), UDPO and α-UDPO achieve the highest average win rates across all settings: 85.9% and 86.1% with Llama-3.2-3B, and 79.4% and 79.6% with Qwen-3-1.7B, exceeding standard DPO by approximately 15% in average win rate. On reward model training (Table 1), URM and α-URM achieve 79.4% and 79.9% average win rate with Llama-3.2-3B, outperforming the best baseline (rRM at 75.7%) by roughly 4%. On the more complex UFB dataset with 8B models, UDPO reaches 74.3% (Llama-3.1-8B) and 60.6% (Qwen-3-8B), surpassing the strongest baseline (IPO at 72.7% and rDPO at 58.3%) by 1.6% and 2.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDPO/α-UDPO consistently outperform all six baselines in average win rate across both model families. Against standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), the improvement is the largest: UDPO exceeds DPO by 14.5% (Llama-3.2-3B: 85.9% vs 71.4%) and 13.5% (Qwen-3-1.7B: 79.4% vs 65.9%), confirming that DPO severely overfits noisy preferences. Against the strongest noise-robust baselines rDPO (Chowdhury et al., 2024) and Dr.DPO (Wu et al., 2025), UDPO leads by 1.2% (85.9% vs 84.7%) and 0.9% (85.9% vs 85.0%) on Llama-3.2-3B, and by 4.9% (79.4% vs 74.5%) and 2.8% (79.4% vs 76.6%) on Qwen-3-1.7B. Label smoothing (cDPO, Mitchell 2023) and IPO (Azar et al., 2024) are clearly outperformed, with UDPO exceeding IPO by 4.1% and 7.4% on the two models respectively. For reward model training, URM outperforms rRM (Chowdhury et al., 2024) by 3.7% (Llama-3.2-3B: 79.4% vs 75.7%) and 4.2% (Qwen-3-1.7B: 70.1% vs 65.9%), while label smoothing (cRM) provides no meaningful gain over standard RM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and RLHF reward-model (Christiano et al., 2017) frameworks by analytically inverting the statistical distortion that symmetric label-flip noise introduces into the Bradley-Terry preference model. Unlike prior noisy-preference methods—rDPO (Chowdhury et al., 2024) requires exact noise-rate estimation, while ROPO and Dr.DPO achieve only partial noise-tolerance—the proposed URM and UDPO losses correct noise through a closed-form transformation of the reward margin, requiring only a single tunable hyperparameter. The key conceptual departure is treating noise as an invertible pointwise transformation on the log-odds scale rather than as a distributional robustness constraint, which yields a unified margin-based loss shared by both the RLHF and DPO paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter downward compatibility property (overestimating noise is safe, underestimating is not) makes the method practically deployable without precise noise-rate measurement, addressing a key limitation of rDPO and label smoothing. The classification-calibration and excess-risk-bound results provide the first such guarantees for a noise-corrected preference loss, connecting the practical objective to Bayes-optimal classification. Open questions include whether the symmetric flip-noise model captures real-world annotation errors (which may be instance-dependent or asymmetric), and whether the single global parameter a suffices for highly heterogeneous datasets. The method's simplicity—a constant rescaling of the standard DPO/RM loss—makes it easy to adopt in existing training pipelines.
