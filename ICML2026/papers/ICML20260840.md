## Problem

Tokenization in LLMs remains a hardcoded, hand-crafted compression step (e.g., BPE) despite the broader trend toward end-to-end architectures. Prior attempts to learn token boundaries inside the model relied on straight-through estimators that relax discrete boundary decisions into continuous surrogates, lacking theoretical guarantees for the actual discrete optimization problem. This paper asks whether token boundaries can instead be learned via score function (policy gradient) estimates, which directly optimize the discrete boundary-placement objective.

## Input / Output

The model consumes fixed-length UTF-8 byte sequences (4096 bytes) and autoregressively predicts the next byte at each position. Simultaneously, a lightweight boundary policy outputs a Bernoulli probability at every byte position, determining where to place token boundaries for downsampling. The architecture is an autoregressive U-net: byte-level encoder → stochastic downsampling at learned boundaries → token-level transformer → upsampling back to byte level → byte-level decoder, with all operations strictly causal.

## Method

The core contribution is a score function estimator (REINFORCE) applied to the discrete token-boundary decisions, decomposing the total gradient into a standard conditional cross-entropy term and a policy gradient term that directly optimizes boundary placement. To make the high-variance policy gradient practicable with a single sample per sequence, the paper applies three variance-reduction techniques from RL: (1) early-exit relative rewards that subtract a baseline next-byte probability computed from early byte-level embeddings, (2) time discounting (γ=0.99) to decouple reward attribution across the sequence, and (3) batch-relative advantage centering. The boundary policy is parameterized as a sigmoid over linear projections of byte representations with a sliding window of prior boundary decisions, and a separate target loss applies pressure on the batch-mean logit to maintain a desired downsampling rate. The total loss combines the autoregressive cross-entropy, the policy gradient loss, the downsample-rate target loss, and an early-exit auxiliary loss, all trained jointly with standard gradient descent.

## Results

On 147M-parameter models trained on FineWeb, the proposed score-function method (Ours) achieves the lowest bits-per-byte on PIQA (1.557), HellaSwag (1.212), and FineWeb Test (1.297), outperforming all dynamic tokenization baselines. The w=1 ablation variant is even stronger, reaching 1.280 bits-per-byte on FineWeb Test. On CodeParrot (90M parameters), the method achieves 0.568 bits-per-byte validation loss versus 0.769 for H-Net at 0.95×10¹⁸ FLOPs, a substantial gap. The learned policy matches BPE-guided downsampling (1.299 vs. 1.297 bits-per-byte on FineWeb Test) without any external tokenizer priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the straight-through estimator of Nawrot et al. (2023), the proposed method wins on all five benchmarks, with the largest gaps on PIQA (1.557 vs. 1.737) and HellaSwag (1.212 vs. 1.340). Against H-Net (Hwang et al., 2025), the method wins on PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, and FineWeb Test, but H-Net retains an advantage on LAMBADA (2.130 vs. 1.737 bits-per-byte); on CodeParrot the gap is even more pronounced, with the proposed method reaching 0.568 vs. 0.769 bits-per-byte. Compared to the BPE-guidance baseline, the learned policy matches FineWeb Test performance (1.297 vs. 1.299) and beats it on PIQA, HellaSwag, and ARC-Easy, while BPE guidance leads on LAMBADA (1.645 vs. 1.737). The w=1 ablation (reduced boundary-logit window) performs comparably to the full model, indicating the sliding-window context is not essential at this scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the autoregressive U-Net architecture of Nawrot et al. (2022), which processes bytes through a downsample-mid-upsample pipeline, and applies the stochastic computation graph framework of Schulman et al. (2015) to derive a REINFORCE-style policy gradient for discrete token boundary decisions. It departs from prior end-to-end tokenization work (Nawrot et al. 2023, Hwang et al. 2025, Godey et al. 2022) that relies on straight-through estimators—relaxing discrete boundaries to continuous surrogates—by instead using score function estimators with RL variance-reduction techniques (time discounting, early-exit baselines, batch-relative advantages). The paper positions itself as providing the theoretical guarantee (local optimality in the large-compute limit) that STEs lack, while matching or exceeding their empirical performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tokenization remains a hardcoded, artisanal step in LLM pipelines, and demonstrating that score function estimators can learn semantically meaningful boundaries from pure cross-entropy optimization (without inductive bias toward whitespace or entropy) offers a principled path toward fully end-to-end architectures. However, all experiments are at the 90–147M parameter scale, well below the >10²¹ FLOP threshold where U-Net advantages over token-based models have been observed, leaving open whether the learned tokenization strategy retains its edge at frontier scale. Key open questions include how the optimal tokenization aspect ratio scales with model size, whether a single model can adapt to variable downsample rates, and whether the approach generalizes beyond English text and Python code.
