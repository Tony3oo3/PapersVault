## Problem

Existing visual tokenizers (VAEs, RAEs, pointwise-matching encoders) constrain the latent space only at the per-sample level, leaving the aggregate posterior distribution unregulated and making it unclear which latent geometry is optimal for downstream generative modeling. This paper asks: can we explicitly control the global structure of the latent distribution, and which reference distribution best balances reconstruction fidelity with generative tractability?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265523/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images and produces a 16×16 spatial latent map with 32 channels per location, using a frozen DINOv2-ViT/L encoder followed by an MLP projection head and a convolutional decoder (Flux-style). For conditional generation, class labels from ImageNet are provided to the downstream diffusion model. The reference distribution p_r(z) is arbitrary and can be derived from SSL features (DINOv2), supervised features (ResNet-34), text embeddings (SigLIP), diffusion noise states, or synthetic distributions (Gaussian, GMM).

## Method

DMVAE replaces the standard per-sample KL-divergence in VAEs with a distribution-level matching constraint that aligns the aggregate posterior q(z) = ∫q(z|x)p(x)dx with an arbitrary reference distribution p_r(z). The mechanism follows Distribution Matching Distillation: a frozen teacher score network (trained via flow matching on p_r(z) samples) provides the target score s_real, while an online student score network s_fake tracks the evolving q(z) during training. Each iteration performs three coupled updates: (i) reconstruction loss updates encoder and decoder, (ii) the student score model is trained on stop-gradient encoder outputs, and (iii) the encoder is updated via the gradient ∇_θ L_DM ≈ E[w_t(s_fake − s_real) · dE_θ(x)/dθ], which pulls latents toward high-density modes of p_r(z) while the −s_fake term prevents mode collapse. This distributional constraint cannot be deceptively minimized the way per-sample losses can, as it enforces holistic structural alignment of the entire latent manifold. The paper further demonstrates that SSL-derived (DINO) reference distributions yield the best trade-off, achieving gFID 3.22 on ImageNet 256×256 in only 64 diffusion training epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DMVAE achieves a gFID of 3.22 after only 64 training epochs, outperforming AlignTok (3.71) and VA-VAE (5.14) at the same budget. With 400 epochs, DMVAE reaches gFID 1.82, surpassing RAE (1.87 at 800 epochs), and at 800 epochs achieves a final gFID of 1.64 with IS 216.3, the best among all compared methods. The reconstruction quality (PSNR 21.5, rFID 0.64) is lower than VA-VAE (PSNR 27.6, rFID 0.28) and AlignTok (PSNR 25.8, rFID 0.26), reflecting the trade-off between generative efficiency and pixel-level fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 64-epoch budget, DMVAE (gFID 3.22) clearly outperforms both AlignTok (3.71) and VA-VAE (5.14), demonstrating that distribution-level matching with a DINO prior accelerates convergence more effectively than per-sample feature alignment. At 800 epochs, DMVAE (1.64) surpasses all baselines including AlignTok (2.04), VA-VAE (2.17), RAE (1.87), and MAR (2.35), achieving the best gFID and IS (216.3). However, DMVAE trades off reconstruction quality: its PSNR of 21.5 and rFID of 0.64 are notably worse than VA-VAE (27.6 / 0.28) and AlignTok (25.8 / 0.26), indicating that the distribution matching constraint sacrifices some pixel-level fidelity. Against autoregressive baselines (LlamaGen, MAR), DMVAE achieves substantially better gFID at comparable or lower parameter counts. The comparison with RAE is particularly instructive: while RAE uses a frozen VFM encoder and achieves competitive gFID (1.87), it suffers from poor reconstruction (PSNR 18.9), whereas DMVAE maintains a learnable encoder with better reconstruction while still achieving superior generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMVAE extends the VAE framework by replacing the per-sample KL-divergence regularizer with a distribution-level matching objective, adapting the score-based distribution matching mechanism from Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) to the tokenizer training setting. It departs from pointwise alignment methods (VA-VAE, AlignTok) that constrain individual latent codes to match SSL features, and from RAE-style frozen encoders that sacrifice reconstruction for structure, by operating on the aggregate posterior q(z) rather than individual samples. The conceptual lineage also connects to Adversarial Autoencoders, which similarly aim to shape the latent distribution but suffer from GAN instability; DMVAE replaces the discriminator with a diffusion/flow score model for more stable matching to complex reference distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical comparison of latent distribution structures (Gaussian, GMM, text embeddings, supervised features, SSL features, diffusion noise) for downstream diffusion modeling, yielding the practical finding that DINO-derived distributions offer the best reconstruction-generation tradeoff. The 64-epoch gFID of 3.22 demonstrates a substantial training efficiency gain that could reduce compute costs for latent diffusion pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that matching distributions with large initial divergence remains unstable, meaning the reference acts more as a regularizer than a precise target, and robust optimization for distant-matching is left as open work. The framework's applicability to audio, video, and 3D generation is noted but not yet demonstrated.
