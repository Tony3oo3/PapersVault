## Problem

Deep learning precipitation nowcasting faces a fundamental trade-off: regression models (e.g., ConvLSTM, Earthformer) minimize pixel-wise loss by averaging under uncertainty, producing oversmoothed forecasts that erode convective details and violate atmospheric turbulence power laws, while diffusion models recover high-frequency realism but introduce stochastic hallucinations—spatially misplaced or physically inconsistent rain cells. No existing deterministic framework simultaneously preserves spectral fidelity and structural reliability across the full 0–2 hour forecast horizon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of historical radar reflectivity frames (15 frames at 6-min intervals for CIKM at 101×101, 25 frames at 6-min for Shanghai at 501×501, or 25 frames at 5-min for SEVIR at 384×384), standardized to 128×128 or 256×256 for training. It produces a future reflectivity field over a 120-minute horizon, generated through 8 iterative refinement steps where each step progressively unlocks a wider frequency band, starting from a zero low-frequency condition and ending with the full-resolution forecast.

## Method

SDIR reformulates nowcasting as a coarse-to-fine spectral refinement process via a dual-path architecture. The SFG-Former (Stage I) uses Scale-Adaptive Transformer blocks with 3D Rotary Position Embeddings and a Frequency Scale Embedder that modulates attention via a (γ, β, α) triplet conditioned on a scalar spectral-depth signal s, producing a stable low-frequency synoptic skeleton. The FR-Refiner (Stage II) is a U-Net-like residual generator with Scale-Conditioned Fourier Neural Operator blocks at the bottleneck that synthesize high-frequency textures in the spectral domain, with s injected via Adaptive Normalization. Training follows a spectral curriculum: s is sampled from Beta(1.0, 3.0) to bias toward low frequencies, the low-frequency condition C_s is constructed by DCT truncation of the ground truth, and a Physically Consistent PSD loss with dynamic frequency masking (weighting unlocked bands at 1.0 and stabilized bands at 0.2) enforces turbulence-consistent energy distributions. At inference, the model starts from s=0 and iteratively re-truncates its own prediction to form the next low-frequency condition over 8 steps, deterministically unlocking higher-frequency bands without stochastic sampling.

## Results

On the Shanghai dataset (501×501), SDIR achieves an average HSS of 0.5882, CSI of 0.4497, SSIM of 0.8548, and MAE of 1129.1, outperforming all eight baselines across every reflectivity threshold (20–40 dBZ). On CIKM (101×101), SDIR reaches HSS 0.4724 and CSI 0.4043, surpassing the strongest deterministic baseline Earthformer by 13.6% and 14.1% respectively. On the large-scale SEVIR benchmark (384×384), SDIR attains HSS 0.4401, CSI 0.3499, SSIM 0.7544, and MAE 1897.9, maintaining its lead over all competitors including the diffusion-based DiffCast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDIR outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Among deterministic models, Earthformer is the strongest competitor on CIKM (HSS 0.4159, CSI 0.3544) and Shanghai (HSS 0.5015, CSI 0.3711), but SDIR surpasses it by 13.6%/14.1% (CIKM) and 17.3%/21.1% (Shanghai) in average HSS/CSI. PhyDNet leads among physics-informed baselines on Shanghai (HSS 0.5203, CSI 0.3892) yet still trails SDIR by 6.8%/6.1%. The diffusion-based DiffCast achieves the best spectral slope error (SSE 0.2302 vs. SDIR's 0.4592) but is 12.5× slower at inference (14.68 s vs. 1.17 s per sample) and loses to SDIR on all spatial metrics (HSS, CSI, SSIM, MAE). Recurrent baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN) and SimVP degrade sharply at the 40 dBZ threshold, while SDIR maintains robust performance (CSI 0.3089 on Shanghai), confirming its advantage in capturing extreme precipitation events.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDIR adapts the progressive spectral decoupling paradigm from Zhao et al. (TPAMI 2026) on UHD image restoration to precipitation nowcasting, repurposing the coarse-to-fine frequency-unlocking strategy for a meteorological forecasting task. Architecturally, it borrows Scale-Adaptive Transformer blocks from FLUX.1 Kontext (Labs et al., 2025) for the SFG-Former and combines them with Fourier Neural Operators (SFNO) in a U-Net-like FR-Refiner, creating a hybrid that departs from both the pure-regression lineage (ConvLSTM → Earthformer) and the stochastic-generation lineage (DiffCast, PreDiff). The key conceptual departure is framing nowcasting as deterministic multi-stage spectral refinement anchored by a low-frequency synoptic skeleton, rather than either single-pass regression or iterative denoising from noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical resolution to the long-standing blurring-vs-hallucination trade-off in operational nowcasting, with a 12× inference speed advantage over diffusion-based methods (1.17s vs 14.68s) while achieving superior categorical accuracy across three benchmarks. The PCPSD loss with dynamic frequency masking provides a principled physics-informed regularizer that could transfer to other spectral-fidelity-sensitive forecasting tasks. However, the authors acknowledge that fine-scale texture realism still lags behind generative models, and the approach's reliance on DCT-truncated conditions introduces a mild exposure bias that the standard training strategy only partially mitigates. Open questions include whether the spectral curriculum generalizes to sub-hourly nowcasting at even higher resolutions and whether the deterministic refinement can be extended to multi-modal inputs (satellite, lightning).
