## Problem

Despite the empirical success of deep neural networks, the geometric mechanisms by which feature representations evolve through layers remain poorly understood. Prior work on Ricci flow in deep learning (Baptista et al., 2024) operated only at a global scale and used the simplistic Forman curvature, while no theoretical results existed on how geometric graph structures encoding local feature geometry evolve as data propagates through network layers. This paper asks whether the local, curvature-driven dynamics of feature geometry during training align with discrete Ricci flow, and what role nonlinear activations play in enabling such transformations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained feedforward network (ReLU hidden layers, sigmoid output) and a finite sample set X from a binary classification task, constructing symmetric k-NN graphs on the feature representations Φ_ℓ(X) at every layer ℓ. It outputs per-vertex local Ricci evolution coefficients (Pearson correlations between Ollivier-Ricci curvature and neighborhood distance change across layers), per-layer Ricci coefficients aggregating over all vertices, and community structure metrics (modularity, normalized cut, curvature gap) tracking class separability. The framework is agnostic to the specific discrete curvature used—exact Ollivier, the Tian et al. arithmetic-mean approximation, or augmented Forman curvature can all be plugged in, with the latter two offering O(E·deg_max) or O(deg_max) per-edge cost versus O(deg_max³) for exact Ollivier.

## Method

The core contribution is the local Ricci evolution coefficient ρ(x), which for each vertex x computes the Pearson correlation across layers between the Ollivier-Ricci scalar curvature of its one-hop neighborhood and the average change in shortest-path distances to its neighbors, capturing whether positively curved regions contract and negatively curved regions expand as predicted by Ricci flow. Theoretically, the paper proves via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma that wide linear networks preserve k-NN graph structure with high probability (Theorem 3.1), while showing that even composing an exact isometry with ReLU can rewire the k-NN graph (Theorem 3.4), establishing that nonlinear activations are necessary for genuine geometric transformation. Empirically, over 20,000 networks trained on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and synthetic datasets consistently exhibit strongly negative local Ricci evolution coefficients (e.g., −0.65 ± 0.04 on MNIST 1-vs-7), with the proportion of negative-coefficient vertices increasing with network width and depth. The framework further reveals that the emergence of class separability corresponds to growing community structure (increasing modularity, decreasing normalized cut), that local Ricci coefficients decline during productive training and rise during overfitting (suggesting an early-stopping heuristic), and that layer Ricci coefficients identify a critical depth beyond which additional layers cease to yield meaningful curvature-driven changes.

## Results

Across more than 20,000 feedforward networks trained on binary classification tasks, local Ricci evolution coefficients are consistently negative, ranging from −0.27 (FMNIST-SvD, width 15, depth 7) to −0.76 (CIFAR-10, width 50, depth 15), with 73–98% of vertices exhibiting negative coefficients. The effect strengthens with both network width and depth: for example, on CIFAR-10 the mean coefficient improves from −0.44 (width 15, depth 7) to −0.76 (width 50, depth 15). Community structure metrics (modularity, normalized cut) increase monotonically across layers, confirming that class separability emerges alongside Ricci flow-like geometric smoothing. Layer-Ricci coefficients reveal a critical depth beyond which additional layers no longer yield meaningful curvature-driven changes, and this depth coincides with the depth maximizing test accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct comparison is against the global Ricci coefficient of Baptista et al. (2024), which uses Forman-Ricci curvature and all-pairwise distances at a global scale. On randomly initialized, untrained networks, the global Ricci coefficient already shows strongly negative values (−0.20 to −0.39 across four synthetic datasets, with 89–99% of networks negative), indicating spurious Ricci flow-like behavior that is an artifact of the global aggregation. In contrast, the proposed local Ricci evolution coefficients remain near zero for untrained networks (−0.037 to +0.039, with only 21–76% of vertices negative), correctly reflecting the absence of curvature-driven dynamics before training. This demonstrates that the local framework is sensitive to genuine learning-induced geometric changes, whereas the global metric cannot distinguish trained from untrained networks. The local approach also has a practical advantage: it remains computable even when k-NN graphs are disconnected, while the global coefficient requires connected graphs at every layer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ricci flow interpretation of deep learning initiated by Baptista et al. (2024), who analyzed geometric transformations at a global scale using Forman-Ricci curvature. The key departures are (1) shifting to a local framework that captures the pointwise nature of Hamilton's Ricci flow, and (2) adopting Ollivier-Ricci curvature, which has consistency guarantees relative to the underlying manifold's curvature, rather than the simpler Forman-Ricci curvature. The theoretical results build on the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma to show that wide linear networks act as approximate isometries preserving k-NN graph structure, while ReLU activations are necessary for genuine geometric change. The empirical analysis connects to the broader literature on geometric simplification (Ansuini et al., 2019; Cohen et al., 2020) and topological simplification (Naitzat et al., 2020) of feature representations, but reframes these observations through the specific lens of curvature-driven flow dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local Ricci evolution coefficient provides a principled, computable metric for evaluating whether a network's feature transformations follow curvature-driven regularization, with practical implications for early stopping (coefficients reverse when overfitting begins) and depth selection (a critical depth beyond which additional layers cease to yield geometric benefit). The finding that untrained networks show no local Ricci flow-like behavior while the global metric of Baptista et al. does (even at random initialization) validates the local framework as a more sensitive diagnostic tool. Key open questions include deriving exact evolution equations for graph geometry under nonlinear activations, extending the analysis to convolutional architectures and larger-scale datasets, and understanding the connection to double descent. The 'manifold' framing remains heuristic, and the study is limited to binary classification on relatively small datasets with feedforward architectures.
