## Problem

Option-based hierarchical RL benefits from longer options to reduce deliberation costs, but committing to long options under an approximate model causes small errors to compound and degrade plan quality. While deliberation costs have been introduced heuristically (Harb et al., 2018), no prior work formally characterizes the tradeoff between re-planning cost and compounding model or input-drift error as a function of option duration, leaving the choice of optimal option length without theoretical grounding.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a finite MDP or IDMDP (state space, action space, transition kernel, bounded rewards, discount factor), a deliberation cost function C, a finite set of admissible termination probabilities B, and a sample size n (for the model-error setting). It produces a closed-form upper bound on the commitment loss and an explicit optimal termination probability β*_eval as a function of the problem parameters (C_max, R_max, n, L_dyn, σ, γ). The output is a theoretical recommendation rather than a learned policy, though it is validated empirically via value iteration on a Ring MDP and DQN training on a Cat-and-Mouse IDMDP.

## Method

The paper introduces the commitment loss, decomposed via a triangle inequality into a deliberation-cost term (growing linearly with the termination threshold β_eval, bounded by γC_max(β_eval − β_min)/(1−γ)) and a model-variance term (decreasing with β_eval). For statistical model error from MLE on n samples, the variance scales as 1/β_eval, yielding a PAC bound whose minimizer gives β*_eval = Θ(√(R_max/C_max)·n^{−1/4}). For input drift in an IDMDP under a frozen-input approximation (inputs held constant within an option, updated only at boundaries), the variance scales as 1/β_eval² under Lipschitz dynamics and bounded per-step drift assumptions, yielding β*_eval = Θ((R_max·L_dyn·σ/C_max)^{1/3}). The proofs rely on coupling arguments for the deliberation cost and perturbation bounds on option-level SMDP kernels for the variance terms, with the key technical step being the geometric-series evaluation of compounded one-step deviations over the random option duration.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contributions are theoretical bounds rather than benchmark scores. The commitment loss is upper-bounded by a deliberation cost term (γC_max/(1−γ)·(β_eval − β_min)) plus a variance term that scales as 1/β_eval (model error) or 1/β_eval² (input drift). Solving these bounds yields closed-form optimal termination rates: β_eval* = Θ(√(R_max/C_max)·n^(−1/4)) for the model-error setting (Corollary 3.3) and β_eval* = Θ((R_max·L_dyn·σ/C_max)^(1/3)) for the input-drift setting (Corollary 4.7). Empirically, the Ring MDP experiments (1000 random configurations, N=10, p=0.125) confirm the predicted U-shape of normalized commitment loss versus β_eval, with the optimum shifting to lower β_eval as C_max increases from 0.01 to 0.3 and to higher β_eval as sample size n decreases from 100 to 1. The Cat-and-Mouse DQN experiments (10×10 grid, 40,000 state-input pairs, 5 seeds) confirm that average optimal β* increases monotonically with input drift rate p_turn ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} and decreases with deliberation cost C_max ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.4}, matching Corollary 4.7's prediction.

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-to-method comparisons; the 'compares_to' list is empty. The experiments serve as validation of theoretical predictions rather than benchmarking against competing algorithms. In the Ring MDP setting, the empirical commitment loss curves qualitatively match the U-shaped bound from Theorem 3.2, and the location of the empirical optimum tracks the theoretical prediction from Corollary 3.3 (higher C_max → lower optimal β_eval; lower n → higher optimal β_eval). In the Cat-and-Mouse setting, the DQN-learned optimal termination probabilities reproduce the monotonic dependence on p_turn (proxy for σ) and C_max predicted by Corollary 4.7, extending the known deliberation-cost effect from Harb et al. (2018) with a new input-drift interaction. The paper positions its contribution as complementary to Harutyunyan et al. (2019) on per-decision option discounting and to Lee et al. (2024) on uncertainty-aware action repetition, providing the first theoretical framework that explicitly trades deliberation cost against compounding model or drift error as a function of option duration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the options framework (Sutton et al., 1999) and the deliberation-cost formulation of Harb et al. (2018) by introducing a formal 'commitment loss' that decomposes the performance gap into a deliberation-cost term and a model-variance term as a function of option duration. It further builds on the Input-Driven MDP (Mao et al., 2018) to define an IDSMDP and a frozen-input approximation, enabling analysis of information drift as a second source of compounding error. The closest prior work is Harutyunyan et al. (2019), who study per-decision discounting in options; this paper instead holds the discount factor fixed and varies the termination probability, yielding explicit PAC-style bounds and closed-form optimal termination rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, closed-form recommendations for option duration in HRL as a function of deliberation cost, reward scale, sample size, and input-drift parameters, which could guide hyperparameter selection in practical HRL systems that currently treat deliberation cost as a heuristic. Key limitations include the state-independent termination assumption, the model-based perspective (most modern HRL is model-free), and the idealized error models; extending to function approximation, correlated data, and state-dependent termination probabilities are natural next steps. The slow n^{-1/4} convergence rate for the model-error case implies that even large increases in sample size only modestly shift the optimal commitment length, a practical insight for data-limited settings.
