## Problem

Existing contrastive multi-view clustering methods require a pre-defined cluster number K, limiting their applicability in open-world scenarios lacking prior structural knowledge. Prior unknown-K approaches (e.g., DeepDPM, scUNC, MMCVA) suffer from non-end-to-end cascade designs, high computational costs, or an inability to dynamically adjust the K search based on the quality of resulting partitions, leaving no unified framework that jointly optimizes feature learning and cluster number estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes V heterogeneous views (V ranges from 2 to 6 across datasets, with per-view dimensions from 3 to 6144) through view-specific autoencoders, requiring no ground-truth K at inference. It autonomously outputs the optimal cluster number K (searched over {2, …, K_max}), soft cluster assignments via Gaussian-based centroid probabilities, and a consensus representation U that fuses structure-enhanced features across views with learnable view weights. The K_max threshold of 20 determines whether a single-agent or multi-agent divide-and-conquer configuration is instantiated.

## Method

GROK formulates optimal-K search as a Markov decision process and applies GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), originally designed for LLM reasoning, to the unsupervised clustering domain. The perception module uses view-specific autoencoders with self-attention-based manifold propagation and a structure-guided contrastive loss to produce discriminative consensus representations as state observations. The decision module samples a group of N_S candidate K values, computes relative advantages via group mean/std normalization, and updates the policy with a clipped surrogate objective; an adaptive reward function applies dual-scale normalization (linear for low-K, logarithmic for high-K) to prevent reward collapse, while a divide-and-conquer strategy partitions the action space into 20-unit intervals handled by specialized agents when K_max > 20. The feedback module converts the predicted K into differentiable geometric constraints (isotropic Gaussian assignment probabilities to K-means centroids) that reshape the feature manifold, closing the perception-decision-feedback loop and enabling end-to-end co-optimization of representations and cluster structure.

## Results

GROK achieves the best or near-best ACC, NMI, and ARI across all 8 multi-view datasets while autonomously predicting K without ground-truth input. Headline numbers include ACC 0.993 / NMI 0.981 on CIFAR-10, ACC 0.977 / NMI 0.895 on Synthetic3d, and ACC 0.636 / NMI 0.626 on Caltech7. On the challenging DHA dataset (K=23), GROK predicts K=22 (Δ=−1) and achieves ACC 0.816 / NMI 0.867, while the non-parametric DeepDPM severely underestimates K (predicting 8, Δ=−15) and drops to ACC 0.256. The predicted K matches ground truth exactly (Δ=0) on Synthetic3d, Hdigit, and CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROK outperforms the non-parametric DeepDPM by large margins on every dataset (e.g., +32.7% ACC on Synthetic3d, +34.2% on CIFAR-10, +56.0% on DHA), primarily because DeepDPM's split-merge heuristic severely misestimates K (Δ ranges from −15 to +8). Against the seven parameterized multi-view baselines that require ground-truth K, GROK achieves the highest ACC on 7 of 8 datasets, with the sole exception being DHA where AdaM edges ahead by 0.9% ACC (0.825 vs. 0.816) due to its exact K prior. On the class-imbalanced Prokaryotic and Caltech7 datasets, GROK's ACC (0.632, 0.636) surpasses the best parameterized baselines (AdaM 0.525, PMIMC 0.540) by 10.1% and 9.6% respectively, despite predicting K=3 and K=4 instead of the true K=4 and K=7. GROK matches or exceeds AdaM (the strongest parameterized baseline) on NMI and ARI across all datasets, including DHA (NMI 0.867 vs. 0.859) and YouTubeFace (NMI 0.325 vs. 0.314). The plug-and-play experiments further confirm that attaching the GROK agent to MFLVC, GCFAggMVC, or SEM improves their ACC by up to 13.6% on Caltech7, validating the agent's generalizability beyond the full pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes GRPO, a group-relative policy optimization strategy originally developed for LLM mathematical reasoning (Shao et al., 2024), into the unsupervised multi-view clustering domain for the first time. It departs from prior unknown-K clustering methods (DeepDPM's split-merge, scUNC's community detection, RGC/ICGR's Q-network bandits) by formulating K estimation as an end-to-end MDP with a geometric feedback loop that co-optimizes feature learning and structure discovery, rather than relying on non-differentiable cascade pipelines. The perception backbone draws on contrastive multi-view clustering (CMC, GCFAggMVC, SEM) and the divide-and-conquer action space strategy is novel within the RL-for-clustering lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration—where the cluster decision agent, when grafted onto three existing parameterized methods, yields up to 13.6% ACC improvement while eliminating the need for a prior K—suggests a broadly applicable module for the multi-view clustering community. However, the method still lags slightly on high-K datasets (DHA, YouTubeFace) where a one-cluster prediction error imposes an inherent ACC penalty, and the framework's scalability beyond K_max ≈ 100 remains untested. Open questions include whether the geometric feedback mechanism generalizes to non-Euclidean or graph-structured data, and whether the GRPO sampling strategy remains efficient as the action space grows further.
