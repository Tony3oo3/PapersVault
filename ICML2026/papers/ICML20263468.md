## Problem

Exploration in reinforcement learning is typically promoted by adding explicit bonus terms (entropy regularization, count-based bonuses, RND) or by posterior sampling, both of which require auxiliary models or hand-tuned coefficients. This paper asks whether exploration can emerge purely from optimizing a reward-based objective that accounts for the agent's ability to retry: if an agent will encounter similar states multiple times, the rational policy is to maximize the best-of-M returns rather than the single-trial return, and this objective naturally induces stochastic, uncertainty-aware exploration without any bonus.

## Input / Output

The method takes an environment state (RGB frames for MinAtar/Atari, symbolic state for Craftax) and a K-dimensional Q-value vector covering all discrete actions at that state, which is sorted to compute the closed-form Expected Improvement in O(K log K). It outputs a discrete action and a policy gradient update whose advantage is the EI-transformed return minus an action-independent baseline. A continuous retry parameter m > 0 (typically 1.2–1.4) controls the exploration–exploitation trade-off, and a Q-replacement step substitutes the sampled action's Q-value with the observed return before computing EI to prevent spurious self-improvement from critic underestimation.

## Method

ReMax defines the policy objective as the expected maximum Q-value over M i.i.d. action draws, with an outer expectation over a distribution of Q-functions capturing epistemic uncertainty. The key technical contribution is a policy-gradient reformulation: by inserting a leave-one-out baseline (the max Q-value over the other M−1 draws), the coupled max operator decomposes into an action-specific Expected Improvement term, yielding a single-action expectation estimable from one trajectory return. The EI admits a closed-form expression via sorting the Q-value vector, and the integer retry count M is generalized to a continuous parameter m > 0 for fine-grained control. RePPO instantiates this as a PPO variant with a Q-critic (instead of a V-critic) and an EI-based advantage, requiring no entropy bonus or intrinsic reward; the only changes from standard PPO are the critic type and the advantage computation.

## Results

On Craftax, RePPO(m=1.2) achieves 11.87% of max reward (std 0.16) without any entropy or intrinsic bonus, matching PPO-Q+Ent (11.85%) and outperforming PPO-V+Ent (11.66%) and RND+Ent (11.68%). On MinAtar, RePPO with m∈[1.2, 1.4] outperforms PPO-V, PPO-Q+Ent, and PPO-V+RND on median, IQM, and mean normalized scores across four games, while remaining comparable to PQN on median/IQM and consistently outperforming it on mean. Peak performance on MinAtar occurs around m=1.2–1.4, and RePPO maintains higher policy entropy than PPO with an entropy bonus throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO-V and PPO-Q without entropy bonuses, RePPO clearly wins on both MinAtar and Craftax (e.g., 11.87% vs. 9.31% on Craftax), confirming that the retry objective itself drives exploration. Against entropy-regularized PPO (PPO-V+Ent, PPO-Q+Ent), RePPO matches or slightly exceeds performance on Craftax (11.87% vs. 11.85% for PPO-Q+Ent) and outperforms on MinAtar, all without any entropy term. Against RND (Random Network Distillation), RePPO outperforms both RND alone (9.62%) and RND+Ent (11.68%) on Craftax, and also outperforms PPO-V+RND on MinAtar; notably, RND degrades significantly when entropy is disabled, whereas RePPO does not rely on it. Against PQN (a strong Q-learning baseline), RePPO is comparable on median and IQM but consistently outperforms on mean normalized score on MinAtar, indicating better tail performance. In the bandit setting, ReMax achieves empirically sublinear cumulative regret comparable to Thompson sampling and UCB, while a Softmax baseline incurs higher regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the earlier tree-based ReMax formulation (Koyamada et al., 2022) into a Q-function-based objective that avoids costly explicit trajectory-tree construction, and adopts the baseline-insertion technique from Tang et al. (2025b) to derive a single-trajectory-estimable policy gradient. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant exploration paradigms—entropy regularization, count-based bonuses, RND, and posterior sampling—by showing that exploration can emerge purely from maximizing a retry-based reward objective (expected max over M draws) without any auxiliary bonus term. The paper also positions itself relative to the concurrent pass@K/max@K literature in LLM reasoning, distinguishing ReMax by its explicit modeling of reward uncertainty rather than assuming fixed, verifiable rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RePPO offers a principled, bonus-free exploration mechanism with a continuous parameter m that gives practitioners fine-grained control over the exploration-exploitation trade-off, and it matches or exceeds entropy-regularized PPO and RND on MinAtar and Craftax without additional models or hyperparameters. Key limitations include restriction to discrete action spaces (the closed-form EI requires sorting all action values), lack of theoretical regret bounds in the main paper (deferred to follow-up work), and the observation that on harder Atari exploration tasks the benefit over plain PPO diminishes. Open directions include extending to continuous actions, explicitly modeling Q-value uncertainty via ensembles or Bayesian critics to enable deeper (structured) exploration, and connecting the retry objective to LLM post-training where pass@K-style objectives are already in use.
