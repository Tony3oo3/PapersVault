## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation construct contrastive logits by externally perturbing the input image (e.g., adding noise, flipping, or generating new images), which fails to reflect the model's internal degree of visual reliance during decoding and requires an additional full forward pass. The paper identifies that the optimal point for visual perturbation varies across layers, inputs, and decoding steps, yet prior methods either use fixed input-level modifications or ignore the layer-wise structure of visual information processing entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail' or a yes/no question) as input, with no image preprocessing or modification required. It produces a single autoregressive text response, identical in format to standard LVLM output. The key operational detail is that the contrast branch is constructed entirely within the decoder by masking visual attention weights, so the input image is consumed exactly once through the vision encoder and projection module, and no second image-level forward pass is needed.

## Method

DEGAP performs contrastive decoding by masking visual attention inside the LVLM decoder rather than perturbing the input image. At each decoding step, it computes the layer-wise logit entropy via a logit lens and identifies the 'descending knee point'—the layer where entropy transitions from a flat regime to a sharp drop—using a two-segment piecewise-linear fit (Muggeo's breakpoint method) on the entropy curve truncated at its minimum. Visual attention scores over visual tokens are then set to −∞ from that dynamically selected layer onward, producing a contrast branch whose logits are dominated by language priors. To avoid a full second forward pass, the method reuses the hidden state cached at the layer immediately before the masking point from the original branch and performs partial inference only on the remaining layers. The final token distribution is computed as Softmax[(1+α)f_original − αf_masked], penalizing tokens that are favored without visual grounding.

## Results

On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5), DEGAP achieves the lowest hallucination rates of C_S=19.1 and C_I=6.3 while maintaining the highest recall (65.2), outperforming the best baseline (HALC: C_S=21.2, C_I=6.9) by 2.1 and 0.6 respectively. On MME hallucination, DEGAP scores 631.11 overall (vs. 605.54 for DeGF and 573.33 for regular decoding), with notable gains of +20.00 on Count and +3.33 on Position. On POPE, DEGAP achieves up to +7.09 accuracy and +6.73 F1 over regular decoding, with average accuracy gains of +1.50 (LLaVA-1.5), +3.51 (InstructBLIP), and +2.69 (mPLUG-Owl2). Inference latency is 5.09s per sample on LLaVA-1.5, roughly 20–57% faster than prior contrastive methods (VCD: 6.41s, DeGF: 11.86s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEGAP outperforms all nine baselines (VCD, M3ID, RITUAL, ICD, OPERA, HALC, DROPOUT, DeGF, DeCo) across every reported benchmark. On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5), DEGAP reduces C_S by 2.1 over the best baseline (HALC, 21.2) and by 8.1 over regular decoding (27.2), while simultaneously improving recall by 8.2 tokens. On MME, DEGAP leads the strongest baseline DeGF by +25.57 in overall score and by +4.34 on the Count sub-task, where most prior methods struggle. On POPE, DEGAP is the only method to achieve the best accuracy on all three backbones (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl2) across Random, Popular, and Adversarial splits; ICD marginally edges DEGAP on a few LLaVA-1.5 Popular/Adversarial F1 values but DEGAP provides more consistent gains overall. On general tasks, DEGAP surpasses DeGF by +2.0 on MMBench and +1.8 on MM-Vet, confirming that hallucination suppression does not degrade broader multimodal reasoning. In terms of efficiency, DEGAP's 5.09s latency on LLaVA-1.5 is ~20% faster than VCD/M3ID/ICD (~6s) and ~57% faster than DeGF (11.86s), thanks to its partial-inference design that avoids a full second forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEGAP extends the contrastive decoding lineage initiated by VCD, M3ID, RITUAL, and ICD, which all construct contrastive logits by externally perturbing the input image (noise, transforms, text-only conditioning). The key departure is moving the perturbation from the input level to the model's internal visual attention, eliminating the need for image preprocessing or auxiliary generative models. It also distinguishes itself from DeCo (Wang et al., 2025), which uses layer-wise analysis to *reinforce* reliable intermediate signals, whereas DEGAP uses layer-wise entropy to identify where to *suppress* visual grounding in order to expose hallucination-prone language priors. The partial-inference mechanism—reusing cached hidden states before the masking layer and re-running only subsequent layers—further differentiates DEGAP from prior contrastive methods that require a full second forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEGAP offers a practical, training-free hallucination mitigation strategy with lower latency than existing contrastive decoding methods, making it more deployable in real-world LVLM pipelines. Its generalization across six architectures (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl2, Qwen2.5-VL 7B/32B, Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B) suggests the entropy-guided layer-selection signal is a robust property of transformer-based VLMs rather than an artifact of a specific model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based VLMs, whether the 100% masking ratio remains optimal under different decoding temperatures or beam settings, and whether the entropy-knee detection degrades in very deep or very shallow architectures where the entropy curve shape may differ.
