## Problem

MLLM-based reward models for image editing suffer from 'Attention Collapse,' where the model's focus degenerates into a blind sink state, effectively rendering the source image invisible and reducing cross-image comparison to single-image evaluation. This perception gap produces miscalibrated scores that diverge from human preference, undermining the reliability of reward signals needed for Online RL in image editing. The paper argues that explicit spatial grounding is essential to anchor perception and enable precise pixel-level verification between corresponding regions.

## Input / Output

The SC stream consumes the original image, the edited image, and a text editing instruction, while the PQ stream uses input isolation by feeding only the edited image to enforce reference-free perceptual evaluation. The model outputs a structured tuple of bounding boxes (normalized to [0, 1000] coordinates), a textual rationale with interleaved special tokens (<|bbox_id|> for region-specific descriptions, <|global|> for holistic assessment), and scalar scores on a 0–25 VIEScore scale. The final reward is a weighted geometric mean R = (S_SC)^0.80 · (S_PQ)^0.20, where SC and PQ are themselves weighted sums of sub-dimension scores.

## Method

SpatialReward introduces a 'Think-with-Boxes' mechanism that breaks attention collapse by first predicting edit-region bounding boxes, then injecting them as interleaved tokens into the reasoning chain to anchor textual verification to specific pixel regions, compelling the model to perform explicit cross-image comparison. The architecture splits into two streams: an SC stream (both images, spatially grounded) and a PQ stream (edited image only, reference-free). Training data (SPATIALREWARD-260K) is constructed via a Spatial-Prior-Guided pipeline with Category-Specific Expert Routing—Qwen-3-VL-235B generates grounding boxes, Gemini-2.5-Pro handles human-centric edits with crop-focused prompts, and GPT-5 handles general objects with bounding-box overlays—followed by a consistency check that discards hallucinated rationales. The model (Qwen-3-VL-8B-Instruct) is trained in two stages: SFT on 260K samples, then GRPO on 7K hard samples using Gemini-3.0-Flash as an online consistency supervisor to suppress ungrounded reasoning. A weighted geometric-mean aggregation (α=0.80, w_SC={0.6, 0.4}) is chosen over arithmetic mean or bucket-principle min-aggregation to provide dense yet sensitive reward gradients for RL optimization.

## Results

SpatialReward (8B) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three reward benchmarks: 0.803 Overall on EditReward-Bench (+11.3% over EditScore-8B), 0.661 Overall on MMRB2 (+9.1% over EditScore-8B), and 0.483 Overall on MER-Bench (surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro's 0.462). In Online RL, SpatialReward boosts OmniGen2 by +0.90 on GEdit-Bench and +0.28 on ImageEdit-Bench, nearly doubling the gain of GPT-4.1 (+0.45) and outperforming the leading discriminative model EditReward (+0.77). On the challenging 4-Pair MER-Bench subset, SpatialReward attains the highest accuracy of 21.5%, surpassing even Gemini-3.0-Flash (19.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialReward outperforms all four baselines on the primary Overall metric of each benchmark. Against EditScore-8B (the direct generative baseline), SpatialReward gains +11.3% on EditReward-Bench (0.803 vs. 0.690) and +9.1% on MMRB2 (0.661 vs. 0.570), validating that explicit spatial grounding activates the reasoning potential of 8B models. Against EditReward (the leading discriminative model), SpatialReward wins on EditReward-Bench (0.803 vs. 0.792) and MMRB2 (0.661 vs. 0.657), while also achieving superior Online RL gains (+0.90 vs. +0.77 on GEdit-Bench); however, EditReward retains an edge on MER-Bench Overall (0.448 vs. 0.483 is actually SpatialReward winning) and Kendall's τ (0.490 vs. 0.549, SpatialReward wins). Against Gemini-2.5-Pro, SpatialReward surpasses it on EditReward-Bench (0.803 vs. 0.722), MMRB2 (0.661 vs. 0.534), and MER-Bench Overall (0.483 vs. 0.462), though Gemini-3.0-Flash slightly edges out SpatialReward on MER-Bench Overall (0.508 vs. 0.483) and Kendall's τ (0.594 vs. 0.549). Against the raw Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, SpatialReward shows dramatic improvements across all benchmarks (e.g., 0.803 vs. 0.562 on EditReward-Bench), confirming that the spatial reasoning training pipeline is the primary source of gains rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialReward extends the VIEScore evaluation framework (Ku et al., 2024) and the generative reward-modeling paradigm of EditScore (Luo et al., 2025) by injecting explicit spatial reasoning—bounding-box prediction followed by interleaved region-reference tokens—into the reasoning chain. This draws conceptually from the visual grounding literature (Shikra, Qwen-VL, Ferret) where coordinate prediction strengthens attribute binding, but repurposes it for cross-image verification in editing evaluation. The paper departs from discriminative reward models like EditReward (Wu et al., 2025d) by arguing that an explicit reasoning path with spatial anchors is necessary to prevent 'Attention Collapse,' and positions its contribution within the broader Online RL-for-editing pipeline (Flow-GRPO, Dance-GRPO) where reward quality is the bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Attention Collapse' diagnosis, supported by quantitative attention-map analysis, provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why MLLM evaluators fail at cross-image comparison—a problem that has been observed anecdotally but not formally characterized. The +0.90 GEdit-Bench gain (nearly double GPT-4.1's +0.45) demonstrates that spatially-grounded reward signals materially improve downstream editing quality, making the approach practically relevant for teams deploying Online RL pipelines. Open questions include whether the Think-with-Boxes mechanism generalizes beyond 8B-scale backbones, whether region-level credit assignment (mentioned as future work) can further densify the reward signal, and whether the approach transfers to video editing or other multi-frame comparison tasks.
