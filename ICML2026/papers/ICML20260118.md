## Problem

Existing high-probability (HP) convergence guarantees for decentralized SGD require strong assumptions such as uniformly bounded gradients or asymptotically vanishing noise, creating a significant gap with the weaker conditions needed for mean-squared error (MSE) convergence. Moreover, no prior HP result for decentralized methods achieves the linear speed-up in the number of users that is well-established in the MSE literature. This paper closes both gaps by showing DSGD converges in HP under the same cost conditions as MSE, with order-optimal rates and linear speed-up for both non-convex and strongly convex costs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a network of n users communicating over a static, doubly stochastic graph, where each user queries a stochastic first-order oracle to obtain local gradient estimates at their current model. The analysis produces tail bounds of the form P(X^t > ε) ≤ exp(−Ct^γ₁ ε^γ₂) for the average gradient norm-squared (non-convex) or the last-iterate optimality gap (strongly convex), with leading terms scaling as O(1/√(nT)) and O(1/(nT)) respectively. A mild logarithmic dependence on dimension d appears in the bounds, stemming from the MGF analysis of the averaged sub-Gaussian noise across users.

## Method

The core technique is to simultaneously bound the moment-generating function (MGF) of the quantity of interest and the MGF of the consensus gap, rather than working with second moments as in MSE analysis. For non-convex costs, an 'offset trick' absorbs the gradient-noise inner product into the left-hand side, removing the need for bounded gradients; for strongly convex costs, a novel MGF bound on the consensus gap (Lemma 3.5) eliminates the heterogeneity assumption entirely. A key enabler is Lemma 3.2, which proves the network-averaged noise is O(σ/√n)-sub-Gaussian, establishing that the variance-reduction benefit of decentralized learning persists in the HP sense and directly yielding linear speed-up. For strongly convex costs, a new result on the MGF of 'almost decreasing' processes (Lemma 3.6) provides a sharper bound than existing centralized results, which is essential for recovering the 1/n factor in the leading rate term and achieving a transient time strictly sharper than MSE-based bounds.

## Results

For non-convex costs, DSGD achieves the HP rate O(σ√L(Δ_f + log(2d/δ))/(√(nT)) + (Δ_f + log(1/δ))/(CT) + Δ_x L²/((1−λ²)T) + nλ²L(A²+σ²)/(σ²(1−λ)²T)) with a fixed step-size, where the leading term shows linear speed-up in n. For strongly convex costs, the last-iterate HP rate is O(ν⁻¹ log(2/δ) + σ²κ log(2d)/μ)/(n(T+t₀)) + O(λ²L(nσ²+‖∇f*‖²(1+κ²))/(1−λ)²n(T+t₀)²), again with linear speed-up. Numerical experiments on synthetic strongly convex problems (Erdős-Rényi graph, n=25/50/100) confirm exponentially decaying tail probabilities with faster decay for larger networks, and non-convex logistic regression on LIBSVM datasets (mushroom, a9a, ijcnn1) validates the gradient-norm tail bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest prior work, Lu et al. (2024), studies HP convergence of DSGD but requires path-wise uniformly bounded gradients and (for PL costs) asymptotically vanishing noise, and does not achieve linear speed-up in n. This paper removes both restrictive assumptions, replacing bounded gradients with the standard heterogeneity condition (A5) and standard sub-Gaussian noise, while establishing linear speed-up via a novel variance-reduction result in the HP sense (Lemma 3.2). Compared to centralized SGD HP results (Ghadimi & Lan 2013; Harvey et al. 2019; Bajović et al. 2023; Liu & Zhou 2024), the decentralized rates match the centralized leading-order terms (O(1/√T) non-convex, O(1/T) strongly convex) while adding the 1/√n and 1/n speed-up factors. The transient time for strongly convex costs is O(max{n/(1−λ), 1/(1−λ)²}), which is strictly sharper than the O(n/(1−λ)²) transient time implied by MSE rates (Alghunaim & Yuan 2022), demonstrating the tightness of the HP analysis. No direct numerical benchmarking against competing algorithms is performed; the experiments validate the theoretical predictions (exponential tail decay, linear speed-up) rather than comparing against baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the centralized high-probability (HP) convergence analysis of SGD (Ghadimi & Lan 2013; Harvey et al. 2019; Liu & Zhou 2024) to the decentralized setting, while simultaneously matching the assumptions and rates of the well-developed MSE convergence literature for DSGD (Koloskova et al. 2020; Jakovetić et al. 2018; Xin et al. 2021). The closest prior work, Lu et al. (2024), established HP convergence of DSGD but required uniformly bounded gradients and asymptotically vanishing noise for PL costs, and could not achieve linear speed-up. The paper departs from this by using the 'offset trick' (Li & Orabona 2020) to remove bounded gradients, a novel MGF bound for the consensus gap (Lemma 3.5) to eliminate the heterogeneity condition for strongly convex costs, and a variance-reduction result in the HP sense (Lemma 3.2) to establish the first linear speed-up for DSGD in the HP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant gap between HP and MSE convergence guarantees in decentralized optimization, showing that the same cost-function conditions suffice for both, which is important for applications like federated LLM training where only a single run is feasible and exponential tail bounds are needed. The technical results—particularly the HP variance-reduction lemma and the sharp MGF bound for 'almost decreasing' processes—are of independent interest beyond DSGD. Open questions include extending to PL-condition costs, incorporating gradient tracking to remove heterogeneity bounds, handling heavy-tailed noise, and generalizing to time-varying networks. The dimension-dependent log(d) factor in the bounds, inherited from sub-Gaussian vector concentration, remains a minor unresolved issue.
