## Problem

Existing self-supervised molecular pretraining methods rely on hand-crafted augmentations, contrastive negatives, or generative reconstruction in input space, and most operate solely on 2D graph topology, leaving 3D conformational information underutilized. No simple predictive framework existed that could jointly leverage 2D and 3D modalities without these costly or ambiguous design choices, particularly in low-data regimes where labeled molecular datasets are scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260116/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D molecular graph (atoms as nodes, covalent bonds as edges) together with an ensemble of 3D conformer coordinates (three conformers in practice, sourced from GEOM or generated via RDKit/Auto3D). During pretraining, the basic modeling unit is not the whole molecule but a pair of complementary k-EgoNet subgraphs (k∈{3,4}), each encoded independently. At fine-tuning, the output is either a single whole-molecule CLS embedding (C-FREE_MOL) or a DeepSets aggregation of multiple subgraph embeddings (C-FREE_SUB), both fed to a lightweight task head for classification or regression.

## Method

C-FREE segments each molecule into complementary context and target subgraphs via fixed-radius k-EgoNets, then learns to predict the target's latent embedding from the context's. Each subgraph is encoded by a GINE (2D) and a PaiNN or SchNet (3D, processing all conformers), with node-level embeddings concatenated into a single multimodal token sequence (with CLS, SEP, and modality tokens) processed by a Transformer. A lightweight Transformer+MLP predictor maps the context CLS embedding toward the target CLS embedding, while the target encoder is maintained as an EMA of the context encoder (BYOL/JEPA-style), and training minimizes L2 distance between predicted and target embeddings—no negatives, augmentations, positional encodings, or clustering required. At fine-tuning, the DeepSets subgraph aggregation head simulates ESAN, making the model provably more expressive than 1-WL, and the framework inherits the invariance properties of its 3D encoder (e.g., E(3) invariance for PaiNN).

## Results

On MoleculeNet with frozen backbones and linear probing, C-FREE_MM-MOL achieves the best average ROC-AUC of 71.07, outperforming the strongest contrastive baseline JOAO (65.05) by over 6 points and the random-init baseline (59.60) by 11.5 points. In the 2D-only setting, C-FREE_2D-SUB reaches 67.27 average ROC-AUC, still surpassing all 2D baselines. Under full fine-tuning (Table 2), C-FREE_PaiNN-3C delivers the best overall average on MoleculeNet, matching or exceeding UniMol (pretrained on 19M molecules vs. C-FREE's 0.33M) on several tasks. On QM9, C-FREE_MM outperforms the randomly initialized PaiNN on 5 of 6 targets and beats UniMol-2 on 4 of 6, while on ZINC it achieves 0.204 MAE vs. GraphJEPA's 0.434.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-FREE dominates all 2D-only contrastive and generative baselines on MoleculeNet linear probing: it beats JOAO (65.05) and GraphCL (65.04) by 4–6 points in average ROC-AUC, and outperforms generative methods like GPT-GNN (60.74) and ContextPred (60.36) by 6–10 points. Against the multimodal foundation models in the full fine-tuning setting (Table 2), C-FREE_PaiNN-3C surpasses MoleBlend (Yu et al., 2023) and 3D InfoMax (Stark et al., 2022) on 5 of 8 MoleculeNet tasks, and matches or exceeds UniMol (Zhou et al., 2023) on several tasks despite being pretrained on 0.33M molecules versus UniMol's 19M. On QM9, C-FREE_MM outperforms the randomly initialized PaiNN (Schütt et al., 2021) on 5 of 6 targets (e.g., 0.057 vs. 0.042 MAE on Mu, 0.107 vs. 0.149 on Alpha), confirming gains come from pretraining rather than architecture alone, and beats UniMol-2 on 4 of 6 targets. On ZINC, C-FREE achieves 0.204 MAE, nearly halving GraphJEPA's (Skenderi et al., 2025) 0.434. On the EXP expressiveness benchmark, C-FREE (3-Ego) reaches 99.08% accuracy, slightly above GraphJEPA's 98.77% and far above GINE's 50.69%, while avoiding GraphJEPA's costly METIS clustering. The one notable weakness is SIDER, where conformer generation fails for larger molecules, causing C-FREE to underperform baselines on that specific dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-FREE synthesizes three prior threads: the JEPA predictive framework (context encoder, target encoder via EMA, and a predictor network), the ESAN ego-net subgraph decomposition with DeepSets aggregation, and the MolMix multimodal sequence construction (CLS/SEP tokens with modality embeddings over GINE + PaiNN/SchNet encoders). It departs from contrastive molecular pretraining (GraphCL, JOAO, GraphMVP) by eliminating negatives and hand-crafted augmentations, from generative methods (GROVER, GPT-GNN, MoleBlend) by operating entirely in latent space, and from GraphJEPA by removing METIS clustering, positional encodings, and hierarchical objectives. The key conceptual move is using fixed-radius ego-nets as the natural 'patch' unit for molecules, replacing the costly or ambiguous subgraph-construction procedures used by prior latent-predictive and contrastive approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically relevant finding is the data efficiency: C-FREE matches or surpasses UniMol (pretrained on 19M molecules) and other baselines (pretrained on 3M+ molecules) while using only 0.33M molecules from GEOM, and shows clear gains in low-label regimes (1–10% of labels). This suggests that the quality of the pretraining objective and the integration of 3D conformer ensembles can partially compensate for pretraining scale, which is important for domains where large labeled or even unlabeled 3D-annotated datasets are unavailable. Limitations include the small model size (9.1M parameters), the failure of conformer generation for larger molecules (affecting SIDER), and the exclusion of 1D/SMILES modality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 100M-molecule collections, whether adding chirality-aware or SMILES modalities further improves representations, and whether the ego-net radius can be made adaptive per molecule.
