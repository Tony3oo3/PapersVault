## Problem

Existing order learning methods for rank estimation assume deterministic, error-free ordinal annotations, but in practice labels are noisy due to subtle distinctions between adjacent ranks, subjectivity, and inter-observer variability. This paper identifies that treating noise as a marginal perturbation to deterministic supervision is insufficient: an instance with a noisy label inherently relates to multiple neighboring ranks with varying plausibility, requiring a fundamentally probabilistic reformulation of the instance-rank relationship.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262348/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder (VGG16 for images at 224×224×3, or BERT for text) maps each instance to a feature vector in an embedding space. At inference, the rank is estimated by minimizing the stochastic dissimilarity D_h(x,r) = Σ_s p_s · d²(h_x, μ_{r+s}) over all candidate ranks r, where p_s follows a discrete Gaussian with a fixed σ_test. The output is a discrete rank from {1,…,n}; for continuous-valued tasks like aesthetic scores, the rank is mapped back to the original scale (e.g., 101 quantized levels in [0,1]).

## Method

SOL reformulates rank estimation under label noise as a stochastic ordering problem: each instance x with noisy rank r_x is associated with centroid μ_{r_x+s} with probability p_s (discrete Gaussian), rather than deterministically with a single centroid. Training minimizes two complementary losses: (1) a discriminative loss ℓ_disc that attracts h_x toward centroids within a noise-aware neighborhood (|s| < δ) while repelling it from distant centroids, derived as a discrete second-derivative surrogate of the desideratum; and (2) a stochastic order loss ℓ_order that enforces pairwise ordering relations between instances using margin losses weighted by the probabilities Pr(x≺y), Pr(x≈y), Pr(x≻y) computed from the noise model. Centroids are updated via a weighted-mean rule after each epoch, and an optional outlier detection and relabeling step iteratively refines extreme label errors by estimating per-instance noise and adjusting ranks.

## Results

On MORPH II (facial age estimation), SOL achieves the best MAE and CS across all noise settings, reaching MAE 2.489 / CS 91.35% under Gaussian noise at κ=0.2, and maintaining MAE 2.826 / CS 87.70% at κ=0.4. On the real-world WMT2020 textual regression task, SOL attains PCC 0.680 and SRCC 0.649, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (RES) by 2.0 and 1.9 points respectively. Across all five datasets (MORPH II, CLAP2015, AADB, RSNA, WMT2020) and four noise types (Gaussian, Laplacian, Uniform, Skewed), SOL consistently achieves the best or near-best performance, with the single exception of MAE under Laplacian noise on RSNA where MWR edges it slightly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOL outperforms all 12 baselines on MORPH II across every noise type and level. The closest competitor is GOL (Lee et al., 2022), which also learns an embedding space with centroids but assumes clean labels; SOL improves over GOL by 0.027 MAE at κ=0.2 and by 0.035 MAE at κ=0.4, with the gap widening as noise increases (e.g., 0.103 MAE gap on CLAP2015 at κ=0.4 vs. 0.065 at κ=0.2). Noise-robust regression methods (ROR-CE, C-Mixup, ConFrag) trail SOL by 0.37–0.57 MAE on MORPH II, while noise-robust classification methods (SPR, ACL) are far behind (6+ MAE worse) because they treat all label errors identically and cannot exploit ordinal structure. On the real-world WMT2020 task, SOL surpasses the best prior method (RES from Wang et al., 2022a) by 2.0 PCC points (0.680 vs. 0.660) and 1.9 SRCC points (0.649 vs. 0.630). On RSNA (medical bone age), SOL leads the second-best MWR by 0.254 MAE at κ=0.1, and on AADB (aesthetic scoring) it beats the runner-up ConR by 2.5% relative MAE reduction at κ=0.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOL extends the geometric order learning (GOL) paradigm, which learns an embedding space where instances are arranged by rank relative to class centroids, by replacing the deterministic instance-to-centroid association with a stochastic one governed by a discrete Gaussian noise model. This departs from prior noise-robust ordinal regression methods (ROR-CE, C-Mixup, ConFrag) that modify loss functions or sampling strategies but treat all label errors identically, and from order learning methods (MWR, GOL) that assume clean annotations. The key conceptual shift is reformulating rank estimation under noise as a stochastic ordering problem where each instance is probabilistically linked to multiple neighboring ranks, rather than applying a robustness patch to a deterministic formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stochastic ordering reformulation provides a principled, unified treatment of ordinal label noise that naturally captures the structure of annotation errors (nearby ranks more plausible than distant ones), and the resulting two-loss framework (discriminative + stochastic order) is modular and interpretable. The consistent improvements across five diverse datasets and four noise types, including real-world WMT2020 annotations, suggest broad applicability to any ordinal prediction task where labels are inherently noisy. Limitations include reliance on a discrete Gaussian noise model (though tested on alternatives), use of a VGG16 backbone that is not state-of-the-art, and the added complexity of the outlier detection and relabeling loop. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large rank spaces (e.g., thousands of discrete levels), whether the fixed σ_test can be made fully adaptive without the performance degradation observed in the paper's ablation, and whether the method integrates naturally with modern transformer-based encoders.
