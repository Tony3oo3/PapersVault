## Problem

Graph Prompt Learning (GPL) methods are increasingly applied in cross-domain settings where pre-training and downstream tasks differ substantially in data distribution, yet the mechanism behind their effectiveness under such domain shift remains unexplored. Empirically, the authors find that simple baselines—linear probing and full fine-tuning—are competitive with or even surpass representative GPL methods in cross-domain few-shot scenarios, raising the question of what actually drives GPL's performance and how to design principled fusion of pre-trained and task-specific knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target graph (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}) along with a frozen pre-trained GNN encoder (2-layer GCN, 128 hidden dims) and produces node or graph class labels. It operates in a cross-domain few-shot regime where the source pre-training domain is unavailable at adaptation time, and only N-way K-shot labeled samples (K ∈ {1, 3, 5, 50}) are drawn from the target domain. A learnable MLP projector aligns input feature dimensions to the encoder's hidden space before dual-branch encoding.

## Method

GP2F instantiates a dual-branch architecture grounded in a theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) proving that an affine fusion of two unbiased noisy estimators of the same latent representation achieves strictly smaller MSE than either branch alone. The frozen branch passes the target graph through the pre-trained GNN unchanged, while the adapted branch injects residual LoRA-style adapters (down-projection → ReLU → up-projection with learnable scaling β) at each layer for parameter-efficient task-specific adaptation. The two branches are fused via a learnable scalar α: H_mix = α·H_pre + (1−α)·H_adp. Two auxiliary losses regularize the fusion: a structural contrastive loss L_ctr treats the two branch outputs as positive views of each node (with neighborhood nodes as additional positives) to align their semantics, and a topology-consistent loss L_fus applies BCE between the fused self-similarity matrix and the target adjacency matrix to preserve structural consistency. The total objective is L_cls + λ₁·L_ctr + λ₂·L_fus, with only adapters, projector, classifier, and scaling factors (α, β) being trained.

## Results

On 1-shot cross-domain node classification (pre-trained on Cora), GP2F achieves the best accuracy on all seven target datasets, with an average improvement of 1.66% over the strongest baseline (GraphLoRA-S), reaching 57.80% on Cora, 51.55% on CiteSeer, 68.33% on CS, and 67.60% on Photo. On 50-shot graph classification (pre-trained on PROTEINS), GP2F achieves the best or second-best AUROC/ACC on all six datasets, with the largest gains on COX2 (+3.02%) and BZR (+1.83%). On large-scale benchmarks, GP2F reaches 68.76% on ogbn-arxiv and 76.91% on ogbn-products, outperforming GraphLoRA by 0.15% and 1.86% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP2F outperforms all GPL baselines (GPPT, GPF, GraphPrompt, EdgePrompt, DAGPrompT) across every dataset in the 1-shot node classification setting, with the largest margins over GPPT (e.g., +30.26% on Cora) and GraphPrompt (e.g., +10.45% on CiteSeer). Against the strongest cross-domain baseline GraphLoRA-S, GP2F wins on all seven datasets by an average of 1.66%, with the largest gaps on CiteSeer (+3.45%) and CS (+5.61%). Notably, simple Linear Probing baselines (e.g., GraphMAE+LP) remain competitive with GPL methods, achieving 64.06% on CS and 44.57% on WikiCS, while Full Fine-Tuning consistently underperforms LP in the 1-shot regime due to overfitting. Against Graph Foundation Models (GCOPE, MDGPT, MDGFM), GP2F achieves the best accuracy on all three datasets, with a particularly large margin on PubMed (64.62% vs. 46.84% for MDGFM). On large-scale ogbn benchmarks, GP2F surpasses GraphLoRA on both ogbn-arxiv (68.76% vs. 68.61%) and ogbn-products (76.91% vs. 75.05%), while GraphPrompt runs out of memory on ogbn-products.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph prompt learning (GPL) line of work (GPPT, GPF, GraphPrompt, EdgePrompt, DAGPrompT) and cross-domain GPL methods (GraphLoRA, MDGPT, MDGFM, GCOPE) by providing the first theoretical analysis of why GPL is effective under domain shift. It departs from prior GPL methods that rely on a single adaptation pathway by explicitly instantiating two complementary branches—a frozen pre-trained encoder and a lightweight adapter-based adapted branch—and proving via a control-variate argument that their affine fusion strictly reduces estimation error. The topology-consistent fusion loss and structural contrastive alignment are novel regularization mechanisms not present in prior cross-domain GPL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that linear probing and full fine-tuning are competitive or superior to GPL methods in cross-domain few-shot settings challenges the field's working assumption that prompt learning is necessary for effective adaptation, and the theoretical analysis provides a principled explanation for when and why dual-branch fusion helps. The practical implication is that cross-domain GPL design should explicitly balance transferable pre-trained knowledge against task-specific adaptation rather than relying on a single prompting mechanism. Open questions include whether the theoretical assumptions (linear separability, unbiased errors) hold under severe domain shift, how the approach extends to truly heterogeneous graph types (e.g., molecular to social), and whether the adaptive fusion coefficient α can be made more interpretable or task-aware.
