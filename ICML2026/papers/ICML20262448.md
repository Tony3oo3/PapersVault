## Problem

While cross-domain knowledge distillation is widely used in practice, the theoretical conditions under which a student model benefits from teacher supervision in the presence of domain shift remain poorly understood. Prior high-dimensional analyses (e.g., Emrullah Ildiz et al., 2025) required the source and target covariance matrices to be jointly diagonalizable and were restricted to pure teacher supervision (ξ=1), leaving the trade-off between distillation and learning from observed target labels unexplored.

## Input / Output

The framework operates in the high-dimensional regime where dimension M scales proportionally with both source sample size N₁ and target sample size N₂. The imitation parameter ξ is allowed to take any real value (not restricted to [0,1]), enabling both over-reliance on the teacher (ξ>1) and anti-learning against the teacher (ξ<0). The output is a closed-form asymptotic bias-variance decomposition of the excess risk expressed in terms of Stieltjes transforms of the Marchenko-Pastur-type laws associated with each domain's covariance structure.

## Method

The paper derives precise high-dimensional asymptotics for the excess risk of a linear student model trained via cross-domain KD by leveraging deterministic equivalents from random matrix theory. The key technical tool is a Stieltjes-transform-based deterministic equivalent for products of resolvent matrices Q(z₁)AQ(z₂) across two different domains, yielding explicit bias and variance terms that depend on the eigenvector overlap between the source and target covariance matrices. The analysis covers both deterministic and random (i.i.d. prior) parameter settings, as well as ridge and ridgeless regression. A central finding is a 'crossed double descent' phenomenon where the excess risk is non-monotonic in both the teacher's and student's dimension-to-sample-size ratios simultaneously, revealing an interaction between the two models' overparameterization that has not been previously characterized.

## Results

The paper derives a precise high-dimensional asymptotic bias-variance decomposition of the excess risk for cross-domain KD (Theorem 3.1), showing the risk depends on the geometry of Σ₁, Σ₂, β₁, β₂, and the imitation parameter ξ. A key guarantee (Proposition 3.9) establishes that even under substantial domain shift, there exists ξ ∈ ℝ such that the student model's excess risk is strictly less than the student-only baseline (ξ=0) almost surely. The paper further identifies a novel 'crossed double descent' phenomenon where the asymptotic excess risk is non-monotonic in both the teacher's and student's dimension-to-sample-size ratios (γ₁ = M/N₁, γ₂ = M/N₂), and shows the optimal ξ can be negative (anti-learning) when λ_s λ_t m̲₁ · SNR > M/N₂ (Corollary 3.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the student-only baseline (ξ=0), where it proves the existence of ξ values yielding strictly lower excess risk even under covariate and model shift. Compared to Emrullah Ildiz et al. (2025), this work relaxes the joint diagonalizability requirement on Σ₁ and Σ₂, captures eigenvector overlap effects, and extends from pure teacher supervision (ξ=1) to general ξ ∈ ℝ. Relative to Moniri & Hassani (2025), which analyzes W2S generalization under isotropic features and ξ=1, this paper handles anisotropic covariates, arbitrary ξ, and both deterministic and random parameter settings. The framework also generalizes Hastie et al. (2022) on single-domain ridge regression by incorporating the teacher's influence through the cross-domain terms involving Π₁ and Π₂. Numerical simulations (Figure 1) confirm theoretical predictions match Monte Carlo estimates (averaged over 100 trials) for both model shift and covariate shift scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional analysis of cross-domain knowledge distillation initiated by Emrullah Ildiz et al. (2025), which required joint diagonalizability of source and target covariance matrices and was restricted to pure teacher supervision (ξ=1). It also generalizes the single-domain ridge regression asymptotics of Hastie et al. (2022) (the ξ=0 student-only case) and the isotropic W2S analysis of Moniri & Hassani (2025) (no covariate shift, ξ=1) by simultaneously handling both model and covariate shift with a general imitation parameter ξ ∈ ℝ. The analytical toolkit—Stieltjes transforms, deterministic equivalents, and Marchenko-Pastur laws from random matrix theory—follows the standard RMT framework used in high-dimensional statistics, but the paper introduces new composite quantities (the S_i operators and E_1–E_5 matrices) to capture the coupled teacher-student interaction across two distinct covariance structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous high-dimensional asymptotic guarantee that cross-domain KD can outperform student-only training even under substantial domain discrepancy, with the existence of a beneficial ξ depending on the geometry of the covariance matrices and parameter vectors. The identification of a 'crossed double descent' phenomenon—where excess risk is non-monotonic in both the teacher's and student's dimension-to-sample-size ratios simultaneously—reveals a previously uncharacterized interaction between the two models. Key limitations include the restriction to linear models (ridge and ridgeless regression); the authors note that extending to nonlinear architectures (e.g., two-layer networks) remains open, and the practical relevance to deep learning will depend on whether these linear-model insights transfer. The finding that the optimal ξ can be negative (anti-learning against the teacher) even in the absence of domain shift challenges the common heuristic that teacher supervision should always be weighted positively.
