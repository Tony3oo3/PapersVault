## Problem

The score matching loss used to train diffusion models is an upper bound on the KL divergence between data and model distributions, but the gap between the two is known to be tight only in the worst case and is not descriptive of actual sample quality. This makes the score matching objective an unreliable proxy for model evaluation, since a lower loss does not necessarily imply a better approximation. The paper asks whether this gap can be tightened by exploiting structural properties of the score estimator.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a parametric score network s^θ (assumed locally bounded measurable), a data distribution μ on R^d, and a time horizon T, and produces explicit upper bounds on forward KL, reverse KL, and Wasserstein distances between μ and the model distribution. The bounds involve time-decaying weightings λ_T(t) or C_T(t) that are decreasing in t, meaning the score matching loss at low noise scales (small forward times) is weighted more heavily. The forward KL bounds require regularity assumptions on s^θ (one-sided Lipschitz or dissipativity at distance), while the reverse KL bounds instead require a log-Sobolev inequality on μ, and the Wasserstein bounds require dissipativity at distance on the drift.

## Method

The core technical insight is to exploit the contraction properties of the backward Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, linking the score matching gap to ergodicity of the underlying Langevin diffusion. For KL bounds, the authors develop an entropy flow analysis (bounding the time-derivative of KL between true and estimated backward marginals) combined with log-Sobolev inequalities, yielding time-decaying weightings that depend on the one-sided Lipschitz constant of s^θ. For Wasserstein bounds, they adapt Eberle's reflection coupling technique to the diffusion model setting, coupling the true and estimated backward SDEs with a reflection coupling at distance and synchronous coupling when close, under a dissipativity-at-distance condition. A negative result (Proposition 3.1) via the dynamic Schrödinger bridge problem shows that without additional regularity on the score, the gap cannot be tightened in general, motivating the use of structural assumptions. The resulting bounds decouple the time-weighting for training (which downweights small noise scales) from the time-weighting for evaluation (which upweights them), providing a theoretical explanation for why small noise scales matter more for generalization.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a set of tightened theoretical bounds replacing the classical ELBO (Equation 1) with time-decaying weightings. Under a one-sided Lipschitz condition with constant M < 1, the forward KL bound gains an exponential decay λ_T(t) ≤ exp(−(1−M)/(2−M) · t), making the bound time-uniform of order O(sup_t E[ε_t^θ(X_t)²]). For the reverse KL, the decay λ̄(t) = √(ρ₀/(ρ₀ + e^{2t} − 1)) is at least exponential and depends only on the data distribution's LSI constant ρ₀, not on the score network. Empirically on CIFAR-10, the paper's time-decaying weighting tracks the KL divergence sensitivity to score perturbations more closely than the standard ELBO weighting while remaining a valid upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the classical ELBO bound from Song et al. (2021a) (Equation 1), which uses a uniform 1/4 weighting on the score matching loss with no time decay. The paper's Theorem 3.7 recovers this bound in the limit of infinite LSI constants (α ≡ 0) but systematically improves the time-dependence whenever the LSI constant ρ(t) is finite, yielding a strictly smaller bound for all t > 0. In the empirical CIFAR-10 experiment (Figure 5), the paper's time-decaying weighting with a crudely estimated regularity constant better predicts the KL divergence sensitivity to score perturbations at different noise levels than the ELBO weighting, while faithfully remaining an upper bound. The paper also improves upon the time-continuous convergence bounds of Conforti et al. (2025) and Benton et al. (2024a) as a byproduct, and its Wasserstein bound (Corollary 4.9) has improved dependence on T compared to recent results from Gentiloni-Silveri & Ocello (2025), Bruno & Sabanis (2025), and Wang & Wang (2025). The reverse KL bound (Theorem 4.3) is described as new to the authors' knowledge for continuous-time DMs, with no direct prior baseline to compare against.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ELBO bound for diffusion models from Song et al. (2021a), which uses Girsanov's theorem to relate KL divergence to the score matching loss, and on the convergence bounds literature (Conforti et al., 2025; Benton et al., 2024a) that upper-bounds various discrepancies by the score matching loss. The key technical departure is exploiting the contraction properties of backward processes via entropy flow and log-Sobolev inequalities (building on Monmarché et al., 2024 for perturbative LSI estimates) to derive time-decaying weightings that tighten the classical uniform bound. For Wasserstein bounds, the paper introduces reflection coupling (Eberle, 2016) into the DM setting, a technique previously used for Langevin ergodicity but not for score-based generative model analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The score matching gap is a fundamental disconnect in DM theory: the training objective (score matching loss) is an upper bound on sample quality with no guarantee of tightness, making it an unreliable evaluation proxy. The paper's time-decaying weightings suggest that small noise scales are disproportionately important for sample quality, which partially explains why standard training schedules that downweight small noise (Ho et al., 2020) may inadvertently hurt generalization. Open questions include whether tighter LSI estimates can yield even sharper decay, whether the results extend to discrete-time samplers, and whether the theoretical weightings translate into practical training or evaluation protocols beyond the low-dimensional and CIFAR-10 experiments shown.
