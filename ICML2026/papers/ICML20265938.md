## Problem

Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) generate text through multi-step denoising over partially masked states, so estimating the probability of a full generation trajectory requires many forward passes over intermediate states—unlike autoregressive models where token-level factorization enables a single forward pass. This computational bottleneck makes offline preference optimization (e.g., DPO) for dLLMs prohibitively expensive and limits the scalability of their post-training pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265938/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference pairs (prompt, preferred completion y⁺, dispreferred completion y⁻) and produces an updated dLLM policy. During training, each completion is packed into a single sequence with a custom block-attention mask that lets tokens in each block attend to clean tokens from prior blocks and partially masked tokens within the same block, simulating N_B distinct diffusion states in one forward pass. A top-k confidence scheduler (k=0.1×block size) selects which masked coordinates to unmask at each sampled step, aligning the training-time unmasking with the inference-time decoding strategy.

## Method

dTRPO combines two theoretical reductions applied to the DPO objective. First, State Reduction (Theorem 3.1) shows the trajectory log-probability equals an expectation over a single uniformly sampled time step per block, reducing required evaluations from T total steps to N_B blocks. Second, Ratio Reduction (Theorem 3.2) proves that when taking the ratio of policy to reference policy on the same transition, all schedule-dependent coefficients (involving α_t) cancel, leaving only the product of model-predicted categorical probabilities at newly unmasked positions. Together, these yield a loss that requires exactly 4 forward passes per training example (policy and reference on both y⁺ and y⁻), matching ARM DPO cost, and is implemented with a single forward pass per completion via block attention. The approach is purely offline, uses parameter-efficient updates (MLP and output projection layers only), and generalizes to both block-wise dLLMs (Fast-dLLM-v2) and long-block dLLMs (LLaDA).

## Results

On 7B dLLMs (Fast-dLLM-v2 backbone), dTRPO achieves 30.30 on GPQA, 85.97 on GSM8K, 64.3 on MATH, 15.17 on LiveCodeBench v6, 63.4 on HumanEval+, and 65.06 on IFEval, delivering gains of up to 9.6% on STEM (GPQA), 4.3% on coding (HumanEval+), and 3.0% on instruction-following (IFEval) over the untrained backbone. On LLM-as-a-judge benchmarks, dTRPO reaches 7.41 on Arena-Hard and 6.53 on MT-Bench, narrowing the gap to the source ARM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: 10.43 and 8.08) to within 3.02% and 1.55% respectively. Training requires only 4 forward passes per example (matching ARM DPO cost) on 64 A100 GPUs for ~5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest same-backbone baseline (Fast-dLLM-v2), dTRPO wins on every benchmark: +9.59 on GPQA, +3.63 on GSM8K, +4.04 on MATH, +3.61 on LCBv6, +4.3 on HumanEval+, and +2.95 on IFEval. Among alternative post-training estimators on the same backbone, dTRPO outperforms VRPO on all benchmarks (e.g., GPQA 30.30 vs 24.24, HumanEval+ 63.4 vs 57.3) and DPO w/ MF on all but IFEval (65.06 vs 65.62, a 0.56-point loss). Compared to from-scratch dLLMs (LLaDA, Dream), dTRPO leads by large margins on reasoning (MATH 64.3 vs 42.5/50.2) and coding (LCBv6 15.17 vs 6.07/12.61), though Dream edges out on MBPP+ (54.5 vs 51.6). In the cross-backbone comparison (Table 6, Qwen3-initialized models), dTRPO (Qwen2.5-based) shows a more balanced degradation profile (avg. −8.46) than SDAR (−12.36) and TraDO (−8.88), particularly on GPQA and IFEval where the Qwen3-based dLLMs drop 20+ points. Relative to the source ARM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), dTRPO retains most capability with gaps of only 1.90 on GSM8K, 6.32 on IFEval, and 3.02 on Arena-Hard, while the 1.9× sampling speed advantage of dLLMs is preserved (38.80 TPS vs 38.90 TPS on GSM8K).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the MDP formulation of reverse diffusion (Black et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024) to derive two exact reduction theorems for dLLM trajectory probability ratios: a state reduction (sampling one step per block suffices) and a ratio reduction (schedule-dependent coefficients cancel, leaving only categorical probabilities of newly unmasked tokens). It departs from prior dLLM post-training work (d1, TRaDO, d2, DiffPO, VRPO), which relied on heuristic estimators such as mean-field approximations, ELBO-based Monte Carlo, or importance sampling, by providing a principled, unbiased derivation grounded in the discrete diffusion theory. The block-attention mechanism for implementing the estimator in a single forward pass is a practical contribution that connects the theoretical reduction to an efficient training procedure analogous to causal attention in ARMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

dTRPO enables offline preference optimization for dLLMs at the same computational cost as ARM DPO (4 forward passes per example), removing the primary scalability bottleneck that has limited dLLM post-training to small-scale or online-only settings. The theoretical results are architecture-agnostic (validated on both block-wise Fast-dLLM-v2 and long-block LLaDA), suggesting broad applicability as dLLM adoption grows. Limitations include evaluation only at 7B scale, a residual independence assumption in the state reduction, and the top-k scheduler's mild violation of conditional independence (shown empirically negligible). Open questions include whether the approach extends to online RL at scale, whether it transfers to larger dLLMs, and whether the single-sample-per-block estimator remains stable as block sizes grow.
