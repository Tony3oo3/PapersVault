## Problem

Existing system identification theory for nonlinear systems relies on restrictive assumptions such as Gaussian inputs, i.i.d. zero-mean disturbances, or adversaries with limited information access. This paper addresses the gap of identifying input-output mappings of general nonlinear, partially observed systems under correlated, nonzero-mean adversarial disturbances that exploit the full information history, where attacks occur probabilistically at each time step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262100/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of control inputs (u_t, ..., u_{t-τ}) of length τ+1 and a single partial observation trajectory (y_0, ..., y_{T-1}) from a nonlinear dynamical system. It produces an estimated matrix G* ∈ R^{r×M} that linearly maps basis function evaluations Φ(U_t^(τ)) to observations, with a guaranteed Frobenius-norm estimation error of O(ρ^τ) where ρ is the contraction factor of the dynamics. The learner selects the input memory length τ, which simultaneously governs the permissible attack probability (p < 1/(2τ)), the required sample complexity, and the approximation fidelity of the basis function representation.

## Method

The paper reformulates the nonlinear input-output mapping as a linear combination of M basis functions applied to a truncated input history, reducing identification to estimating the coefficient matrix G* via an ℓ₂-norm estimator rather than standard least-squares. The key insight is that when the attack probability satisfies p < 1/(2τ), the probability of τ consecutive attack-free periods exceeds 0.5, ensuring that the ℓ₂-norm estimator's objective landscape retains a strict minimum at G* despite adversarial corruption. The proof proceeds by first establishing uniqueness of G* under clean conditions via a covering-number argument over the unit Frobenius-norm sphere, then extending to the general case with nonzero past-state and approximation-error terms using a perturbation bound. A matching lower bound of Ω(ρ^τ) is constructed by designing two indistinguishable system instances under probabilistic adversarial attacks, proving the ℓ₂-norm estimator's error rate is information-theoretically optimal and superior to ℓ_α estimators for α ≠ 2.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that the ℓ₂-norm estimator achieves an estimation error of O(ρ^τ) in Frobenius norm with probability at least 1−δ, where ρ ∈ (0,1) is the contraction factor of the dynamics and τ is the input memory length, provided the attack probability satisfies p < 1/(2τ). A matching lower bound of Ω(ρ^τ) (Theorem 3.5) proves this rate is optimal for any estimator. The required time horizon scales as T = Ω(τν⁸/(2(1−p)^τ − 1)² · [rM log(τν/(2(1−p)^τ − 1)) + log(1/δ)]), where ν = √(Mτ)·L_φ·σ_u/λ. In experiments on a 100-dimensional nonlinear system with tanh activation, the ℓ₂-norm estimator converges to the true mapping while least-squares fails completely under adversarial attacks with mean vectors of magnitude 300–1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed ℓ₂-norm estimator against three baselines: (1) standard least-squares, (2) ℓ₁-norm estimator, and (3) ℓ∞-norm estimator. In Experiment 1, least-squares completely fails to recover the system under adversarial attacks (with attack means of 300 or 1000), while the ℓ₂-norm estimator closely identifies the true mapping after finite time, under both Gaussian N(0,100I₅) and non-Gaussian Unif[−8,10]⁵ inputs. In Experiment 2, the ℓ₂-norm estimator outperforms both ℓ₁ and ℓ∞ norm estimators, consistent with Remark 3.4 which shows the other norms introduce an additional √r multiplicative factor in the error bound. In Experiment 3, the estimation error is confirmed to scale as O(ρ^τ): larger ρ yields higher error and larger τ yields lower error, with the error plateauing (not decaying) over time, supporting the constant lower bound Ω(ρ^τ). On the power grid experiment (n generators with nonlinear swing dynamics), the ℓ₂-norm estimator again outperforms least-squares, which is expected since least-squares requires zero-mean disturbances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two parallel lines of system identification research: (1) Kim & Lavaei (2025c) on linear partially-observed systems under adversarial attacks, and (2) Zhang et al. (2025) on parameterized nonlinear identification under sparse or semi-oblivious disturbances. The key departure is the simultaneous relaxation of linearity, zero-mean/i.i.d. disturbance assumptions, and the restriction on adversary information access—allowing fully correlated, nonzero-mean adversarial disturbances that exploit the complete state history at attack times. The finite-memory basis-function reformulation (Volterra/NARMAX-style) is standard in nonlinear system identification, but its combination with a probabilistic adversarial attack model and a sharp optimality proof is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish a tight (matching upper and lower) estimation error bound for nonlinear, partially-observed system identification under fully adversarial disturbances with full information history, providing a clear characterization of when identification is possible (attack probability p < 1/(2τ)). The practical implications for safety-critical cyber-physical systems (power grids, autonomous vehicles) are significant, as the ℓ₂-norm estimator—a simple, widely-used tool—provably achieves optimal performance without requiring specialized robust estimators. Open questions include whether the bound can be tightened under uniformly bounded attack norms, whether linear system instances can achieve the same matching bounds, and how to extend the analysis to nonparametric (infinite-dimensional) identification.
