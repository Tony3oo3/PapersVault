## Problem

Existing token attribution methods for LLMs face two critical failures when applied to modern reasoning models that generate long intermediate chains: (1) an efficiency bottleneck where attributing a multi-token target span of length M within context N requires O(M·N) operations, making long-context attribution take hours; and (2) a faithfulness drop where intermediate reasoning tokens absorb the majority of attribution mass (over 90%), preventing importance from propagating back to the original input tokens that actually dictated the output.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full model context decomposed into three segments (input I, intermediate reasoning T, final output O) along with cached per-layer hidden states and attention maps from a single forward pass. It produces a token-level importance probability distribution over the input tokens I only, obtained by aggregating attribution scores across all layers and heads. The target span can be any contiguous set of generated tokens (e.g., the full reasoning chain or final answer), and the output is specifically the re-normalized importance over I after masking out T and O scores.

## Method

FLASHTRACE introduces two mechanisms built on the ALTI/IFR proximity framework. First, span-wise aggregation exploits the linearity of attention: since the transformed value vector v_j depends only on source token j and not on target position i, summing contributions over a target span S reduces to pre-aggregating scalar attention weights (Σ_{i∈S} α_{i,j}) and computing v_j once per source token, cutting complexity from O(M·N·D) to O(N·D) per hop. Second, recursive attribution treats the importance scores assigned to reasoning tokens in hop k as weighted targets for hop k+1, tracing influence backward through the chain; the final attribution is a weighted sum across hops discounted by the cumulative flow ratio ρ_k (the fraction of mass remaining in reasoning tokens). In practice, a single recursive hop (K=1) suffices to resolve the information absorption problem, keeping total cost comparable to one forward pass per hop.

## Results

FLASHTRACE achieves over 130× speedup over the most efficient baseline (IFR) at a 5k-token target span, completing attribution in under 20 seconds versus over 38 minutes. On RULER Variable Tracking (h2 c3), FLASHTRACE reaches a Recovery Rate of 0.698, far exceeding IFR (0.136) and AttnLRP (0.212). On multi-step reasoning, FLASHTRACE attains the best MAS of 0.446 on MATH and 0.205 on MorehopQA, and the best RISE of 0.128 on MorehopQA, outperforming all baselines. Span-wise aggregation reduces time complexity from O(M·N·D) to O(K·N·(M+D)) while matching the Exhaustive Token-Level Rollout within 10% faithfulness at 93.6% lower runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLASHTRACE outperforms IFR (the strongest baseline) on Recovery Rate across nearly all RULER settings, with the largest gap on Variable Tracking (e.g., 0.698 vs 0.136 on h2 c3) and HotpotQA (0.384 vs 0.268). On faithfulness, FLASHTRACE achieves the best MAS on MATH (0.446 vs IFR's 0.490) and MorehopQA (0.205 vs IFR's 0.228), and the best RISE on MorehopQA (0.128 vs IFR's 0.146). Against AttnLRP, FLASHTRACE wins on both Recovery Rate and faithfulness in every setting tested. Perturbation, REAGENT, and CLP baselines are consistently outperformed on both metrics, with REAGENT showing the weakest Recovery Rate overall. Integrated Gradients (IG) and IG-Attn could not be evaluated for faithfulness due to OOM errors at the required context lengths, though they are the slowest in wall-clock time. The one setting where IFR edges out FLASHTRACE is RISE on HotpotQA (0.140 vs 0.163), though FLASHTRACE still dominates on MAS (0.033 vs 0.074) and Recovery Rate (0.384 vs 0.268) for the same task.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLASHTRACE extends the ALTI/IFR proximity-based attribution framework (Ferrando et al., 2022; Ferrando & Voita, 2024) from single-token targets to multi-token spans, exploiting the linearity of attention to factor out per-source vector transformations and reduce complexity from O(M·N·D) to O(N·D). It departs from prior work by introducing a recursive attribution mechanism that traces importance through intermediate reasoning tokens back to source inputs, addressing a faithfulness gap that all existing single-hop methods (IFR, AttnLRP, IG, perturbation-based) share. The concurrent CAGE (Walker & Ewetz, 2025) also explores multi-hop attribution but operates at sentence-level granularity, whereas FLASHTRACE maintains token-level precision with span-wise aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As reasoning LLMs (o1, DeepSeek-R1) generate thousands of intermediate tokens, the inability of existing attribution methods to trace importance back through reasoning chains to the original input is a practical bottleneck for auditing and debugging high-stakes agent decisions. The 130× speedup and the finding that a single recursive hop resolves most of the information-absorption problem make long-context interpretability feasible in production settings. Open questions include how the method scales to contexts beyond 32k tokens (attention map memory remains O(L·N²)), whether the K=1 sufficiency holds for more deeply nested reasoning (e.g., tool-calling agents with multiple interaction rounds), and whether the proximity-based scores can be made more formally causal rather than correlational.
