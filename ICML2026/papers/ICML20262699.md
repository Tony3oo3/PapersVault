## Problem

Sim-to-real transfer for depth-based robotic manipulation is bottlenecked by the geometric perceptual gap: simulated depth is pristine while real sensors produce complex, heterogeneous noise. Existing methods model this noise as a monolithic stochastic process, conflating physically distinct mechanisms (specular signal loss vs. transparent measurement bias) into a single representation, which causes hallucinated artifacts in clean regions and failure to predict structural voids.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262699/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a 512×512 16-bit simulated depth map as input. It produces a composed realistic depth map D^syn = (1−M)·(D^sim + R), where M is a binary sensing-invalidation mask (pixels where the sensor returns 0/NaN) and R is a continuous measurement residual restricted to valid regions. Depth values are log-scale normalized to [−1,1] (range 0.05–5.0 m) before processing, and the invalidation mask acts as a hard spatial constraint that zeroes out residual generation in hole regions.

## Method

PRISM is a tripartite causal pipeline (Reason → Disentangle → Generate). The Semantic-Physics Reasoner (SPR) uses a frozen MoGe2-Large 3D VFM to extract a global semantic embedding via GAP + MLP, then projects it into multi-scale channel attention vectors that encode material-aware physics priors. The Bimodal Noise Disentangler (BND) is a CNN encoder-decoder that fuses RGB-depth features with SPR priors via Hadamard-product modulation at each decoder stage, outputting a pixel-wise invalidation probability map. The Noise Residual Generator (NRG) is a conditional diffusion model (ControlNet + frozen SD 2.1 U-Net + VAE) that synthesizes measurement residuals, conditioned on log-normalized sim depth and the global semantic embedding injected into the time-embedding, with a mask-aware loss that suppresses gradients in invalidation regions. Training uses Hierarchical Positive-Prioritized Supervision (H-PPS): multi-scale positive-weighted BCE (w_pos=3.0) + Dice Loss + Positive-Preserving Dynamic Hard Mining with a linearly decaying mining ratio γ(t) from 0.25 to 0.1, specifically designed to handle the extreme sparsity (<10% pixels) of invalidation regions.

## Results

On ByteCameraDepth (Realsense D435 in-domain), PRISM achieves the best overall fidelity with MAE 0.076, RMSE 0.085, and δ<1.25 of 0.964, outperforming the strongest generative baseline Stable-Sim2Real (MAE 0.087, RMSE 0.106). For sensing invalidation detection, PRISM reaches IoU 0.952, F1 0.975, and Recall 0.991, substantially exceeding all baselines. In downstream Sim-to-Real manipulation across three policy architectures (DP3, PPT, RISE), PRISM achieves a 92.1% average success rate, a 53.2% absolute improvement over raw simulation (38.9%) and a 3.0% gain over the best baseline (Stable-Sim2Real at 89.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the explicit analytical methods, the gap is largest: GazeboDR (MAE 0.203) and DepthSynth (MAE 0.115) are far behind, while ActiveStereo (MAE 0.094, IoU 0.806) is the strongest physics-based baseline but still trails PRISM by 19% in MAE and 14.6 points in Invalidation IoU. Among implicit data-driven methods, DCL-Depth (MAE 0.098) and Stable-Sim2Real (MAE 0.087) are the closest competitors; PRISM improves MAE by 22.4% and 12.6% respectively, and achieves a decisive 7.6-point IoU advantage over Stable-Sim2Real (0.952 vs. 0.876) on invalidation detection. The advantage is most pronounced on safety-critical Recall (0.991 vs. 0.912 for Stable-Sim2Real), confirming that the physics-grounded disentanglement prevents the hallucination of artifacts in valid regions that plagues monolithic baselines. In downstream manipulation, PRISM's 92.1% average success rate surpasses Stable-Sim2Real (89.1%) by 3.0 points and raw simulation (38.9%) by 53.2 points, with the largest gains on tasks involving specular and transparent objects (e.g., Toyball-to-Drawer: 17.3% vs. 16.3% for Stable-Sim2Real, vs. 4.7% for GazeboDR).

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM departs from both established paradigms of depth noise modeling—explicit analytical simulation (DepthSynth, ActiveStereo) and implicit data-driven translation (DCL-Depth, Stable-Sim2Real)—by introducing a physics-grounded disentanglement that separates sensing invalidation from measurement inaccuracy rather than treating noise as a monolithic stochastic process. Architecturally, it builds on the ControlNet + Stable Diffusion 2.1 conditional generation framework (as in Stable-Sim2Real) but repurposes frozen 3D Visual Foundation Models (MoGe2, DepthAnything) as semantic-physical reasoners to guide where and how noise should appear, a use of VFMs that prior depth-synthesis work has not explored. The H-PPS supervision strategy extends standard hard-example mining with a positive-preserving constraint specifically designed for the extreme sparsity of sensor-failure regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling the physical origin of depth artifacts (specular invalidation vs. transparent inaccuracy) yields measurably more robust sim-to-real transfer than monolithic noise synthesis, with a 92.1% average manipulation success rate across six diverse tasks and three policy architectures. However, the practical impact is tempered by acknowledged limitations: the model is sensor-specific (trained on ByteCameraDepth, tested zero-shot on NYU-Depth-v2), lacks temporal consistency for dynamic scenes, and does not address extrinsic interaction-induced noise. The ~3% absolute gain over the strongest generative baseline (Stable-Sim2Real) across policies, while consistent, suggests the disentanglement framing is more of a principled reorganization than a dramatic performance leap, and the broader question of whether this physics-grounded decomposition generalizes to fundamentally different sensing modalities (e.g., LiDAR vs. ToF) remains open.
