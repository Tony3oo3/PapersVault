## Problem

Existing identifiable virtual face (IVF) generation methods decouple a user's physical identity from a virtual identity for privacy, but they provide no mechanism to verify whether a submitted IVF was legitimately generated by an authorized user or is a fake face produced by an attacker. Additionally, prior GAN-based IVF methods struggle to simultaneously achieve high image fidelity and fine-grained control over pose and expression, which are required for challenge-response face recognition systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262594/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB face image, a user-specific key (one of S=16 keys that selects which virtual identity to use), and 3D facial geometry (FLAME parameters regressed via EMOCA and rendered as a facial normal map) as inputs. It produces a 512×512 virtual face image that preserves the original pose and expression, along with a binary legitimacy verification result obtained by inverting the queried image to recover its diffusion initial noise and checking whether the noise-implied VID embedding is consistent with the image-side identity embedding extracted by a face encoder.

## Method

TIVDiff comprises three modules. (1) Virtual Identity Projection (VIP) is an MLP that maps a PID embedding to a key-conditioned VID embedding, trained with five losses: anonymization (pushing VID away from PID), diversity (separating VIDs across keys), intra-identity consistency, inter-identity separability, and a distribution regularization loss that minimizes KL divergence between the batch VID distribution and a reference Gaussian fitted via PCA on real face embeddings. (2) PoseID-Diff builds on Arc2Face (initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5) and injects the VID through the CLIP text encoder by replacing an <id> token embedding, while a ControlNet branch conditioned on the facial normal map preserves head pose and expression. (3) Identity-Guarded Generative Watermarking (IGGW) establishes a Reversible Distribution-Matched Mapping (RDMM) between the unit-normalized VID and the diffusion initial noise: the VID direction is pseudo-randomized by a per-chunk orthogonal matrix Q and scaled by a chi-distributed radius to match N(0,I), so verification inverts the image to recover noise, applies Q^T to extract per-chunk identity directions, and checks cosine-similarity agreement with the image-side identity embedding—making the watermark resistant to inversion-and-regeneration and face-swap forgery attacks.

## Results

On CelebA, TIVDiff achieves the best identifiability with an average EER of 0.041 and AUC of 0.987, surpassing the original face baseline (EER 0.054) and the second-best method IVFG (EER 0.102) by ~6% in EER. In terms of generation quality, TIVDiff attains a FID of 33.53 on CelebA, substantially outperforming the next-best FAS (68.46) and G2Face (77.50), while also achieving the lowest pose errors (Yaw 3.25°, Pitch 3.03°, Roll 2.29°) and highest expression similarity (0.780). For watermark forgery resistance, IGGW reduces the attack success rate to 1.5% (inversion-and-regeneration) and 18.2% (face-swap), compared to 33–100% for all four generative watermarking baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIVDiff outperforms all six face anonymization/IVF baselines on identifiability, anonymization, diversity, and generation quality. Against the closest IVF competitor IVFG, TIVDiff reduces EER from 0.102 to 0.041 (60% relative improvement) and FID from 186.7 to 33.5, while also achieving much higher diversity (0.905 vs. 0.471 mean mismatch rate). KFAAR and RiDDLE are beaten on identifiability (EER 0.263/0.110 vs. 0.041) and quality (FID 187.6/99.2 vs. 33.5), though KFAAR achieves comparable anonymization PR (0.948 vs. 0.980). DP2, G2Face, and FAS trail significantly on both identifiability (EER 0.315–0.422) and quality. For watermark forgery resistance, IGGW dramatically outperforms all four generative watermarking baselines: Tree-Ring (ASR 33.4%/91.8%), Gaussian Shading (100%/100%), WIND (100%/100%), and PRC (100%/81.4%) all suffer high attack success rates, whereas IGGW limits ASR to 1.5% (I&R) and 18.2% (face-swap), a 31.9% and 63.2% reduction over the best baseline in each setting respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiable virtual face (IVF) generation paradigm introduced by IVFG (Yuan et al., 2022b), which maps physical identities to virtual identities for privacy-preserving face recognition, by replacing the GAN-based generator with a diffusion model (Arc2Face on Stable Diffusion) and adding 3D pose/expression conditioning via FLAME normal maps through ControlNet. The key conceptual departure is introducing trustworthiness as a first-class requirement: prior IVF methods (IVFG, KFAAR, CanFG, FaceAnonyMixer) produce virtual faces whose legitimacy cannot be verified, leaving systems vulnerable to unauthorized synthetic faces. The Identity-Guarded Generative Watermarking (IGGW) scheme addresses this gap by binding the diffusion initial noise to the VID through a reversible orthogonal mapping, rather than using the static-pattern watermarks common in generative watermarking literature, which are vulnerable to forgery attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trustworthiness gap identified here is a genuine deployment blocker for IVF-based face recognition systems: without a verification mechanism, administrators cannot distinguish legitimate virtual faces from attacker-generated fakes, undermining the entire privacy-protection paradigm. The paper's demonstration that a diffusion-based IVF can simultaneously outperform GAN baselines on quality (FID 33.5 vs. 68–187), identifiability (EER 0.041, even below the original-face EER of 0.054), and diversity (0.881 vs. ≤0.287) while adding a forgery-resistant watermark is practically significant for real-world face privacy systems. Open questions include whether the inversion-based verification remains reliable under more aggressive or unknown post-processing, how the shared orthogonal matrix Q scales in multi-tenant deployments, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the CelebA/FFHQ evaluation to in-the-wild, multi-ethnic, and occluded face data.
