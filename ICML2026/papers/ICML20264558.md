## Problem

Existing fair spectral clustering methods (FairSC, sFairSC) rely on eigendecompositions of the graph Laplacian, incurring cubic-time complexity that limits scalability to large graphs. While A-FairSC avoids eigensolvers via a DC-ADMM reformulation, its subproblems remain computationally intensive. The application of Riemannian manifold optimization to fair spectral clustering had not been explored, leaving a gap in scalable, constraint-aware spectral clustering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse graph affinity matrix M ∈ R^{n×n}, sensitive group indicator labels partitioning nodes into h groups, and a target number of clusters k. It produces a binary cluster assignment matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{n×k} obtained by applying k-means to the rows of D^{-1/2}H, where H ∈ R^{n×k} is the final spectral embedding on the Stiefel manifold. The fairness constraint matrix F = D^{-1/2}F̂ (with h−1 columns) is precomputed, and its orthonormal column-space basis U_F ∈ R^{n×(h−1)} is computed once in O(nh²) time before the iterative loop begins.

## Method

R-FairSC reformulates fair spectral clustering as minimizing Tr(H^T L̄ H) over the Stiefel manifold S(n,k) subject to the linear fairness constraint F^T H = 0, encoding orthogonality directly into the manifold geometry. A variable-splitting strategy introduces an auxiliary variable Y coupled to H via the consensus constraint H − Y = 0, decoupling the manifold constraint (on H) from the fairness constraint (on Y). The resulting Riemannian ADMM alternates between: (i) an H-subproblem solved by Riemannian Conjugate Gradient with QR-based retraction on S(n,k), and (ii) a Y-subproblem that reduces to a closed-form Euclidean projection of Z = H + U/γ onto null(F^T) via Y = (I − U_F U_F^T)Z. This decoupling avoids any eigendecomposition of the n×n Laplacian, yielding a per-iteration cost of O(τ_i(|E|k + nk²) + nhk) that scales linearly in graph sparsity. Convergence to a first-order stationary point is guaranteed under standard nonconvex ADMM conditions (Magnusson et al., 2016), with the RCG solver providing local minimizers for the H-subproblem.

## Results

On mSBM graphs (up to 150,000 nodes), R-FairSC achieves zero error rate (matching FairSC, sFairSC, and A-FairSC) while delivering average speedups of 476×, 117×, and 3× over FairSC, sFairSC, and A-FairSC respectively. On the RandLaplace scalability benchmark, R-FairSC achieves the lowest runtime across all settings with 15.2× and 2.2× average speedups over sFairSC and A-FairSC. On real-world datasets, R-FairSC attains up to 70% lower NCut than A-FairSC (Bank, Credit) and up to 240× faster runtime than sFairSC (Credit), while enforcing fairness constraints (‖FᵀH‖_F) more strictly than A-FairSC by an order of magnitude or more.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-FairSC matches the clustering accuracy of all fair baselines (FairSC, sFairSC, A-FairSC) on mSBM (zero error rate) while being dramatically faster: 476× over FairSC, 117× over sFairSC, and 3–5.65× over A-FairSC (the gap widens with graph size). Against A-FairSC specifically, R-FairSC also achieves lower fairness constraint violations (‖FᵀH‖_F) by 1–2 orders of magnitude across all real-world datasets and cluster counts, and up to 70% lower NCut on Bank and Credit. On the RandLaplace scalability test, R-FairSC is 15.2× faster than sFairSC and 2.2× faster than A-FairSC. Standard SC (Shi & Malik, 2000) is included as a non-fair baseline; it fails to recover ground-truth clusters on mSBM (no fairness constraints) and degrades in balance on real-world data, confirming that fairness constraints are necessary for equitable partitions. Overall, R-FairSC wins on every metric tested—speed, fairness constraint satisfaction, and clustering quality—without any clear loss to any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair spectral clustering lineage initiated by FairSC (Kleindessner et al., 2019), which encoded group fairness as linear constraints on the spectral embedding, and made scalable by sFairSC (Wang et al., 2023) via nullspace projection. The closest prior work is A-FairSC (Tonin et al., 2025), which also avoids eigendecomposition by casting the problem as a DC optimization solved with ADMM. The key departure here is formulating the problem as constrained optimization on the Stiefel manifold and using a variable-splitting strategy that decouples the orthogonality constraint (handled by Riemannian conjugate gradient) from the fairness constraint (handled by a closed-form null-space projection), yielding simpler subproblems than the DC framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale fair graph clustering: by replacing O(n³) eigendecomposition with O(nk²) per-iteration Riemannian updates, the method scales to graphs with 150,000+ nodes where prior methods become infeasible. The convergence guarantee (via Magnusson et al., 2016) provides theoretical grounding, though it relies on the RCG subproblem solution being a local minimizer rather than a global one. Open questions include how the method behaves when the number of sensitive groups h grows large relative to n, whether the exact fairness enforcement in the Y-subproblem can be relaxed for further speedup, and whether the Riemannian formulation extends naturally to other constrained spectral problems (e.g., multi-view or temporal fair clustering).
