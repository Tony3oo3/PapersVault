## Problem

Diffusion MLLMs generate tokens in parallel via iterative denoising, yet their reasoning capabilities remain underexplored and standard AR-based reasoning methods (CoT, DDCoT, CCoT) fail to transfer because they assume a stable sequential conditioning chain that does not exist in order-agnostic diffusion decoding. The paper asks how to improve reasoning reliability in dMLLMs in a way that is native to the parallel, multi-step unmasking process rather than forcing sequential dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262125/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on two dMLLMs (LaViDa and MMaDA) that take an RGB image and a text prompt and produce a generated text sequence containing an intermediate rationale followed by a final answer. Generation is controlled by a fixed L/T ratio (e.g., 128/64 or 256/128), where L caps the number of new tokens and T sets the number of denoising iterations; ST-Veto intervenes at each unmasking step within this fixed budget, adding no extra generation passes or training.

## Method

ST-Veto is a training-free, decoding-time veto-and-swap policy that replaces the standard greedy top-k unmasking rule. It computes two per-token signals: (1) a Taylor residual obtained by predicting next-step confidence via a second-order Taylor expansion from a 4-step confidence history, flagging tokens whose actual confidence drops sharply below the predicted trajectory as temporally unstable; and (2) an image-attention ratio averaged over intermediate Transformer layers (L/3, L/2, 2L/3) that measures how much attention a token directs to image tokens, flagging weakly grounded tokens. Using robust statistics (median and MAD) over the masked-position population, tokens in the top-k proposal that are either temporally unstable or visually ungrounded are vetoed and swapped with near-boundary candidates that satisfy both stability and grounding thresholds, ensuring the model commits only spatio-temporally reliable tokens as anchors for subsequent denoising steps.

## Results

On LaViDa (L/T=256/128), ST-Veto achieves 38.25 on MMBench, 48.54 on SQA-IMG, 31.87 on M3CoT, and 41.36 on V*Bench, improving over the base confidence decoder by +6.70, +8.48, +2.32, and +4.19 points respectively. On MMaDA (L/T=256/128), ST-Veto reaches 36.74 (MMBench), 45.31 (SQA-IMG), 27.74 (M3CoT), and 37.17 (V*Bench), with gains of +4.70, +3.61, +2.63, and +5.76 over base. ST-Veto introduces negligible inference overhead (5.73 vs 5.68 samples/s on MMaDA 128/64), while AR-based CoT baselines roughly double generation cost (≈10.9 samples/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-Veto outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark and both dMLLMs. The AR-based CoT methods (DD-CoT, CCoT, SCAFFOLD, ICoT) fail to transfer to diffusion decoding: on LaViDa MMBench 256/128 they score 30.98–31.43, below the base decoder's 31.55, while ST-Veto reaches 38.25 (+6.70 over base). Among the AR baselines, ICoT is the closest to base (e.g., 37.17 vs 35.60 on LaViDa V*Bench 128/64), but ST-Veto still leads by +5.77. The training-free hallucination-mitigation decoders (EAH, OPERA, FarSight; Table 12) show only marginal gains over base (≤1.4 pts), whereas ST-Veto achieves +4.09 to +7.34 on the same settings. Notably, AR-based baselines require two separate inference calls to emulate their sequential pipeline, roughly doubling generation cost (≈10.9 vs 5.7 samples/s), while ST-Veto operates within a single diffusion pass at near-identical throughput to the base decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked discrete diffusion framework for language generation (Sahoo et al., 2024a/b), which provides the order-agnostic unmasking-and-remasking loop that ST-Veto operates within. It departs sharply from the dominant line of multimodal reasoning work—CoT prompting, duty-distinct CoT, compositional CoT, interleaved-modal CoT, and SCAFFOLD—all of which assume a sequential generate-then-condition structure. By demonstrating that these AR-native methods actually degrade performance when applied to dMLLMs, the paper carves out a distinct design space: reasoning control that exploits the global, parallel visibility of all token positions across denoising steps, a property with no AR analogue. The Taylor-residual signal draws on finite-difference numerical analysis, while the image-attention grounding signal connects to the VLM hallucination-mitigation literature (Favero et al., 2024; Kang et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025), but their combination into a discrete veto-and-swap policy at the unmasking decision point is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most important finding is the systematic failure of SOTA AR-based reasoning methods under diffusion decoding, which redirects the community away from adapting sequential prompting strategies and toward diffusion-native mechanisms. ST-Veto itself is a practical, training-free, zero-overhead decoding policy that yields up to 9% accuracy gains across two dMLLMs and four benchmarks, making it immediately deployable. However, the method relies on confidence and attention as imperfect proxies for correctness, is validated only on single-image inputs with current (still nascent) dMLLMs, and its Taylor-based prediction assumes locally smooth confidence trajectories that may break down under non-stationary generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-image or video settings, ultra-long generations where attention dilutes, and whether more principled (e.g., learned) temporal-stability signals could replace the hand-crafted second-order Taylor approximation.
