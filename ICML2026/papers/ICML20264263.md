## Problem

Standard RL algorithms rely on a single fixed discount factor, confining the agent to one planning horizon and failing to balance short-term objectives with long-term strategy in complex sequential tasks. Existing multi-timescale approaches (Gamma-Nets, Laplace, Hyperbolic) estimate values at different γ independently without enforcing the intrinsic mathematical constraint between multi-scale Q-values and the underlying reward sequence, and they aggregate Q-values with static, state-agnostic weights rather than adapting to the current context.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts either RGB images (e.g., 84×84 for DMControl, multi-camera feeds for CARLA) or low-dimensional state vectors, and outputs a continuous action via an unchanged actor network. Internally, the multi-critic ensemble produces a vector of N=6 Q-values (or V-values for on-policy), which the Neural Reward Decoder maps to a T=15-step reward sequence, and the cross-attention module fuses back into a single scalar Q-value (or advantage) for policy updates. The auxiliary decoder and attention modules are training-only, so deployment incurs zero inference overhead beyond the standard actor.

## Method

The method extends any actor-critic algorithm with three coupled components: (1) a multi-γ critic ensemble where each head estimates Q(s,a) under a distinct discount factor from {0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95}; (2) a Neural Reward Decoder f_ψ that learns the ill-posed inverse Vandermonde mapping Q ≈ LR → R, regularized by dual consistency losses—a reward-reconstruction loss against ground-truth rewards (first-step only for off-policy, full T-step for on-policy) and a value-reconstruction loss that projects decoded rewards back through L to enforce cycle consistency; (3) a cross-attention Q-weight predictor that uses the state embedding as query and each critic's latent feature, value, and γ as key/value pairs to produce state-dependent softmax weights, adaptively fusing multi-scale Q-values (or GAE advantages) for the actor update. Off-policy and on-policy variants differ in how the decoder input is constructed: off-policy uses the raw value vector with implicit tail marginalization, while on-policy explicitly subtracts the discounted tail value V_θ(s_{t+T}) to isolate the head value over the decoding window.

## Results

On DMControl 6 common tasks (500K steps), the method achieves an average score of 918.3, outperforming the strongest baseline Resact (915.5) and the primary baseline DrQ-v2 (888.0). On 5 hard DMControl tasks at 1M steps, it reaches 686.6 average, surpassing Dreamer-v3 (674) and Resact (630.2), with particularly large gains on Quadruped Walk (846 vs. 732 for DrQ-v2) and Walker Run (721 vs. 538). On CARLA autonomous driving across 4 weather conditions, the method achieves 343.1 average episodic reward versus 290.1 for ResAct and 223.6 for DrQ-v2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms the primary baseline DrQ-v2 across all benchmarks: on DMControl hard tasks at 1M steps, it improves average score from 564.0 to 686.6 (+122.6), with the largest gain on Quadruped Walk (846 vs. 732). Against the strongest SOTA competitor Resact (AAAI'25), it wins on all 5 hard tasks at 1M steps (686.6 vs. 630.2 average) and on CARLA (343.1 vs. 290.1), while matching or slightly exceeding it on common tasks (918.3 vs. 915.5). It also surpasses the model-based Dreamer-v3 (Nature'25) on hard tasks at 1M steps (686.6 vs. 674 average), despite being a model-free method. Among multi-timescale baselines re-implemented in the DrQ-v2 framework, it outperforms Gamma-Nets, Laplace, and Hyperbolic across all 5 hard tasks (Fig. 5), attributed to its explicit Q↔LR consistency constraint and state-adaptive cross-attention fusion. On CARLA, it beats CycAug (274.1), TACO (265.5), MaDi (232.0), and ResAct (290.1) under all four weather conditions, with the largest margin over DrQ-v2 (343.1 vs. 223.6, +119.5). The method also improves over SAC and PPO baselines in Table 1, achieving 475.2 vs. 206.8 (SAC) and 445.7 vs. 167.7 (PPO) average across 6 DMControl tasks, confirming its plug-and-play generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-timescale RL line initiated by Gamma-Nets, Laplace-based successor representations, and hyperbolic discounting, all of which estimate Q-values under multiple discount factors but aggregate them via static, state-agnostic rules. It draws directly on the 2025 Nature work by Masset et al., which demonstrated that dopaminergic neurons encode reward prediction errors across heterogeneous timescales and that a neural decoder can approximate the ill-posed inverse Laplace transform. The key departure is twofold: (1) a bidirectional consistency loss (reward reconstruction + value reconstruction) that enforces the intrinsic Q ≈ LR manifold constraint across timescales, and (2) a cross-attention Q-weight predictor that replaces fixed aggregation with state-dependent adaptive fusion, addressing a gap the authors identify in all prior multi-γ methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (compatible with SAC, PPO, DrQ-v2) and zero inference overhead make this a practically deployable enhancement for any actor-critic pipeline, with particularly large gains on hard, long-horizon tasks where single-γ methods struggle most. The robustness to the exact choice of γ values (random configurations perform comparably to hand-tuned ones) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the training-time overhead (≈2× FLOPs in the independent variant) can be further reduced via distillation, and whether the consistency mechanism generalizes to distributional or model-based settings beyond the actor-critic frameworks tested here.
