## Problem

Current medical MLLMs rely on a static, global-perception paradigm: they process the entire image at once and express all intermediate reasoning in text, with no mechanism to dynamically re-examine fine-grained visual regions during thinking. This causes them to miss tiny structures, subtle lesion cues, and nuanced pathological details, severely degrading diagnostic accuracy on complex clinical tasks that require iterative, localized visual inspection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a medical image (across 9 modalities including CT, MRI, histopathology, ultrasound, X-ray, fundus, dermoscopy, endoscopy, and OCT) and a free-form text question, Ophiuchus produces an interleaved multimodal reasoning trajectory structured as N-step chains of (thought, tool-invocation, observation) tuples, culminating in either a text answer (multiple-choice or free-form) or a pixel-level segmentation mask. The model can invoke up to 6 external tool calls per query, and tool outputs (masks, zoomed crops) are appended as visual tokens into the ongoing context, making the reasoning chain genuinely multimodal rather than text-only.

## Method

Ophiuchus equips a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone with three external tools (SAM2 for bbox-prompted segmentation, BiomedParse for text-prompted segmentation, and a zoom-in function) and trains it via a three-stage protocol: (1) cold-start SFT on 64k curated samples with GPT-5-generated tool-integrated reasoning trajectories to establish basic tool-selection behavior; (2) self-reflection fine-tuning using a Self-Reflection Sampling (SRS) strategy that identifies cases where the model's tool sequence changes between checkpoints and its answer flips from wrong to correct, then fine-tunes on these self-correction pairs; (3) Agentic Tool Reinforcement Learning (ATRL) using GRPO with a composite reward decomposed into format adherence, answer quality (exact match for VQA, piecewise IoU for segmentation), and a conditional strategic tool-use bonus granted only when the model both answers correctly and invokes at least one tool. The key design insight is that the MLLM learns to act as an agent that autonomously decides when fine-grained visual evidence is needed, where to probe, which tool to call, and how to integrate the returned observation into subsequent reasoning—moving beyond static tool-calling to iterative, reflective visual cognition.

## Results

Ophiuchus-7B achieves a 68.0 average accuracy across 8 medical VQA benchmarks (PathVQA, SLAKE, VQA-RAD, OmniMedVQA, MMMU-H&M, MedXpertQA, In-House-VQA, D_test-VQA), surpassing the best closed-source model (OpenAI-o3, 61.7) by 6.3 points and the best open-source medical reasoning MLLM (Chiron-o1-8B, 57.2) by 10.8 points. On segmentation, Ophiuchus reaches 85.9 DSC on In-domain-Seg and 67.5 DSC on In-House-Seg, exceeding both SAM2 (83.9/65.8) and BiomedParse (84.8/65.1) standalone. The model also leads on report generation (MIMIC-CXR), achieving 87.9 BERTScore for finding generation and 85.7 for impression generation, outperforming GPT-4o (86.2/84.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ophiuchus-7B outperforms every baseline across all 8 VQA benchmarks. Against closed-source SOTA, it surpasses OpenAI-o3 (61.7) by 6.3 and Gemini 2.5 Pro (61.5) by 6.5 in average accuracy, with the largest per-benchmark gains on In-House-VQA (+19.7 over o3) and PathVQA (+6.8 over o3). Among open-source 'think with images' methods (DeepEyes-7B, Mini-o3-7B-v1, PixelReasoner-RL-7B, all ~50.5–50.7 avg), Ophiuchus leads by ~17.3 points, demonstrating that integrating external segmentation tools (SAM2, BiomedParse) alongside zoom-in yields substantially more than local magnification alone. Against medical-specific MLLMs with CoT reasoning (Chiron-o1-8B at 57.2, Lingshu-7B at 56.3), Ophiuchus gains 10.8–11.7 points, and against tool-using medical agents (VILA-M3-40B at 51.9, MMedAgent-7B at 48.1), the margin is 16.1–19.9 points. On segmentation, Ophiuchus (85.9 DSC in-domain, 67.5 in-house) exceeds both SAM2 (83.9/65.8) and BiomedParse (84.8/65.1) standalone, and outperforms reasoning-segmentation MLLMs (MedSeg-R, RCMed) and the multi-agent MMedAgent-7B. The paper further shows that even when all baselines are given the same external tools and fine-tuned on the same 64k dataset (Table 11), Ophiuchus still leads by 9.8 points over the next-best (Chiron-o1-8B at 58.2), confirming the gain stems from the interleaved tool-augmented reasoning paradigm rather than tool access or data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'think with images' line (DeepEyes, Mini-o3, PixelReasoner) by replacing the single zoom-in tool with a richer toolset (SAM2, BiomedParse, zoom-in) and, more importantly, by training the model to *orchestrate* multiple tools with self-reflection rather than merely calling one. It also departs from prior medical tool-augmented agents (MMedAgent, VILA-M3, AURA) that use fixed, disconnected tool invocations, by weaving tool outputs back into an interleaved multimodal chain-of-thought. The RL stage builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeek-R1), adapted with fine-grained, task-specific rewards (format, answer, strategic tool-use) rather than the simplified answer-only rewards used in prior medical RL work (MedR1, GMai-VL-R1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 7B MLLM can surpass both the standalone segmentation tools it calls (Table 10) and closed-source models with tool access (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) on average, suggesting that the *training paradigm* for tool-augmented reasoning matters as much as tool quality. The selective tool-use policy (93.7% necessary, 3.3% unnecessary) and the zero-shot generalization to unseen tools (MedSAM) point toward a practical recipe for building medical AI agents that know when to look closer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric imaging, whether the 64k synthetic dataset (generated by GPT-5/Gemini) introduces distributional biases, and whether the gains persist as base MLLMs improve natively at fine-grained grounding.
