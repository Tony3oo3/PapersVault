## Problem

Existing single-cell trajectory inference methods that handle unbalanced mass (proliferation/apoptosis) treat cell numbers as a continuously varying fluid quantity, overlooking the fundamentally discrete, jump-like nature of birth-death events at single-cell resolution. Moreover, no prior framework simultaneously models stochasticity, unbalanced marginals, and discrete branching dynamics in a simulation-free manner that scales to high-dimensional omics data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265552/cropped_page8_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unnormalized empirical measures (cell populations) at K+1 discrete time points from destructive single-cell sequencing, where total mass may differ across snapshots. It outputs three neural networks (vector field, growth rate, score function) supporting two inference modes: a continuous SDE-based population-level simulation, and a discrete branching inference that simulates individual cell division and death events via a non-homogeneous branching process with state-dependent rate |g_θ(x,t)|. Interpolated distributions at unobserved intermediate time points are also produced.

## Method

USB formulates trajectory inference as the Branching Schrödinger Bridge (BSB) problem and solves it simulation-free via a conditional unbalanced score matching (CUSM) loss. The key construction decomposes the intractable BSB into a tractable conditional path — a Poisson-Brownian bridge that interpolates positions via a Brownian bridge and mass via log-linear interpolation (the infinitesimal-increment limit of a Poisson bridge) — and a static semi-coupling approximated by solving the WFR metric (a second-order expansion of the RUOT growth penalty). Training regresses three quantities simultaneously: the probability-flow ODE drift, the growth rate, and the score function, weighted by the conditional mass m_t(z) to handle unbalanced marginals. At inference, the branching mode simulates a non-homogeneous branching process where each cell's division or death is triggered by a Poisson clock with rate |g_θ(x_t,t)|, enabling discrete single-cell resolution birth-death dynamics without requiring lineage tree priors.

## Results

On three synthetic datasets, USB achieves the best W1 distance on Dyngen (0.131) and Gaussian 1000D (2.136), tying WFR-FM on Simulation 2D (0.019), while also attaining the best RME on Gaussian (0.004) and near-best on Simulation (0.002). On held-out time-point interpolation across four real datasets (EMT, EB, CITE-seq, Mouse), USB achieves the best W1 on EMT (0.298) and CITE-seq (37.087), and is within 0.4 of the best on EB (10.177 vs. 10.075) and Mouse (6.988 vs. 6.586). USB also recovers the ground-truth growth rate with a Pearson correlation of 0.974 on Simulation Gene and achieves a 0.9950 correlation between theoretical and empirical division event counts at single-cell resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USB is compared against 11 baselines spanning deterministic flow matching (MMFM, Metric FM, MIOFlow), stochastic balanced methods (SF²M, BranchSBM), unbalanced deterministic methods (WFR-FM, UOT-FM, VGFM), and unbalanced stochastic methods (TIGON, DeepRUOT, Var-RUOT). On distribution matching (W1), USB ties WFR-FM on Simulation 2D (0.019) and beats it on Dyngen (0.131 vs. 0.135) and Gaussian 1000D (2.136 vs. 2.233); on mass matching (RME), USB is best on Gaussian (0.004 vs. WFR-FM's 0.044) and near-best on Simulation (0.002 vs. 0.001). Against the strongest unbalanced-stochastic baselines, USB substantially outperforms DeepRUOT (W1: 0.019 vs. 0.043 on Simulation; 2.136 vs. 3.785 on Gaussian) and TIGON (0.019 vs. 0.045; 2.136 vs. 2.263), while Var-RUOT is competitive on Simulation RME (0.008) but lags on W1. On real-data held-out interpolation (Table 4), USB matches WFR-FM on EMT (0.298) and beats it on CITE-seq (37.087 vs. 37.221), but is slightly behind on EB (10.177 vs. 10.157) and Mouse (6.988 vs. 6.586). The balanced stochastic baseline SF²M is consistently outperformed by a large margin (e.g., W1 0.224 vs. 0.019 on Simulation; 3.543 vs. 2.136 on Gaussian), confirming the importance of modeling unbalance. Crucially, USB is the only method that simultaneously handles stochasticity, unbalance, simulation-free training, and discrete birth-death simulation (Table 2), a combination no other baseline provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

USB unifies three previously separate research threads: the simulation-free Schrödinger Bridge solver SF²M (Tong et al., 2024b) for stochastic dynamics, the WFR-FM framework (Peng et al., 2026) for unbalanced deterministic flows, and the Branching Schrödinger Bridge (BSB) formulation of Baradat & Lavenant (2021) for discrete birth-death processes. The key conceptual departure is introducing a Poisson-Brownian bridge as the conditional path, which simultaneously encodes Brownian motion in position and log-linear mass growth, and using the RUOT semi-coupling (approximated via WFR) to handle unbalanced marginals. Prior methods handled at most two of the three properties (stochastic, unbalanced, simulation-free) simultaneously, and none modeled discrete jump-like cell division/death at single-cell resolution without requiring lineage-tree priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USB is the first framework to jointly provide stochastic dynamics, unbalanced mass handling, simulation-free training, and discrete single-cell birth-death simulation, filling a gap that the paper explicitly identifies as absent from the literature. The practical impact for single-cell biology is significant: researchers can now simulate realistic proliferation and apoptosis trajectories from snapshot data alone, without longitudinal tracking or lineage trees. A key limitation is the reliance on WFR as a tractable proxy for the intractable BSB/RUOT semi-coupling, which the authors acknowledge may lose fidelity for complex branching mechanisms. The general unbalanced score matching framework is stated to be extensible to other domains involving stochasticity and unbalance, suggesting broader applicability beyond single-cell genomics.
