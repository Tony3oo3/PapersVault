## Problem

TCR-pMHC recognition is a multi-component interface problem (TCR α/β, peptide, MHC) where paired interaction data (<10^5) is orders of magnitude smaller than unpaired component sequences (~10^7 TCRs, ~10^6 pMHCs), and solved structures are scarce (<500). Existing discriminative predictors cannot generate sequences, while autoregressive or structure-based generators fail to capture the joint multi-component conditioning or require templates unavailable for most clinically relevant antigens.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single concatenated sequence of MHC chains + peptide + TCR α + TCR β (700–900 tokens, within ESM-2's 1024 context) with no chain separators; component boundaries are inferred via position-dependent masking. For prediction, it outputs span pseudo-log-likelihood (sPLL) scores: an aggregate score for binding compatibility and per-position interaction scores that decompose MHC-driven anchoring from TCR-driven recognition. For generation, given a fixed pMHC target and a TCR scaffold (fixed V/J genes and framework regions), it produces novel CDR3 α and CDR3 β sequences of the same length as the scaffold.

## Method

DecoderTCR builds on ESM-2 with a two-stage compositional continual pre-training curriculum: Stage 1 applies component-specific informed masking (50% peptide, 20% HLA pocket, 10% other for pMHC; 50% CDR, 10% other for TCR) on abundant unpaired data to learn marginal representations, while Stage 2 applies joint cross-component masking on paired TCR-pMHC sequences to refine interaction dependencies, with implicit experience replay preventing catastrophic forgetting. For generation, the paper introduces Iterative Entropy-Guided Refinement (IEGR), a non-autoregressive decoding algorithm that computes predictive entropy at all masked CDR3 positions simultaneously, resolves the lowest-entropy (highest-confidence) anchor residues first to establish a stable scaffold, then fills high-entropy hypervariable positions conditioned on resolved anchors, and finally performs K rounds of block Gibbs resampling to escape suboptimal configurations. This entropy-ordered schedule naturally respects the biophysical hierarchy where conserved boundary residues constrain hypervariable loop interiors, and the cross-chain interleaving allows CDR3 α and CDR3 β to inform each other's design.

## Results

On zero-shot pMHC binding prediction (16 held-out HLA alleles), DecoderTCR Stage 1 achieves 0.96 ± 0.04 AUROC (Class I) and 0.90 ± 0.02 AUROC (Class II), compared to 0.41–0.45 for ESM-2. On epitope-specific TCR recognition (YLQPRTFLL and GLCTLVAML, both fully excluded from training), DecoderTCR-3B reaches 0.76 and 0.64 AUROC respectively, approaching the strongest supervised baseline NetTCR-2.2 (0.88 and 0.69). For de novo CDR3 design, IEGR decoding yields AF3 ipTM of 0.644 (vs. 0.598 Greedy, 0.612 OneShot) and 34.2% salt-bridge enrichment, while wet-lab validation shows a 3% hit rate in Round 1 improving to 12% in Round 2 via a lab-in-the-loop classifier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecoderTCR decisively outperforms generic ESM-2 (650M/3B) across all tasks: 0.96 vs. 0.41–0.45 AUROC on zero-shot pMHC binding and 0.76 vs. 0.36 on YLQ TCR recognition, confirming that domain-specific compositional pretraining—not model scale—drives performance. On epitope-specific TCR recognition, zero-shot DecoderTCR (0.76 AUROC on YLQ) exceeds four supervised methods (PanPep 0.65, ERGO-II 0.52, pMTnet 0.58, DLpTCR 0.47) but trails the strongest supervised baseline NetTCR-2.2 (0.88) by 0.12 AUROC. On supervised pMHC binding (ESCAPE-seq), DecoderTCR-3B embeddings (0.77 AUROC) match specialized predictors MixMHCpred-3.0, NetMHCpan-4.1, and MHCflurry 2.0 (all ~0.76–0.77), while ESM-2 embeddings lag at 0.62–0.63. For generative design, IEGR outperforms both Greedy and OneShot decoding on AF3 ipTM (0.644 vs. 0.598/0.612) and salt-bridge enrichment (34.2% vs. 17.2%/21.9%), and dramatically surpasses the RFdiffusion+ProteinMPNN structure-based baseline on OLGA log P_gen (−8.52 vs. −18.03, nearly 10 log-units closer to the natural repertoire), despite the latter having access to a solved crystal structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecoderTCR extends the continual pre-training paradigm (Gururangan et al., Ke et al.) to multi-component protein interfaces, using a two-stage curriculum that first learns component representations from abundant marginal data (TCR repertoires, pMHC ligandomes) before refining cross-chain dependencies from sparse paired interactions. For generation, it adapts confidence-based non-autoregressive decoding (MaskGIT, Mask-Predict) and span-based prediction (SpanBERT) to constrained CDR3 redesign, with the entropy-guided ordering motivated by the biophysical hierarchy of conserved anchors versus hypervariable loops. The key departure from prior TCR-pMHC work is the unified multi-component formulation: prior discriminative methods (NetTCR, ERGO-II, pMTnet) model binding but cannot generate, while prior generative methods (ERTransformer, TCR-TRANSLATE) operate on single chains or require structural templates. DecoderTCR is, to the authors' knowledge, the first model to jointly represent the full immune synapse (TCR α/β + peptide + MHC) in a single sequence for both zero-shot prediction and template-free design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit characterization of the prediction-generation gap in TCR design: strong zero-shot discrimination (0.76 AUROC) does not translate to reliable generation (3% experimental hit rate), and a simple classifier on frozen embeddings narrows this gap to 12% via a lab-in-the-loop paradigm. This establishes a concrete iterative strategy for generative immunology where each wet-lab round produces training signal for the next. Key limitations include scaffold-free design remaining unsolved, validation on a single pMHC target, and the fundamental data sparsity (estimates suggest 10⁶–10⁸ epitopes needed for fully generalizable prediction). The compositional pre-training framework and entropy-guided decoding are broadly applicable to any multi-component interface where paired data is scarce but marginal data is abundant.
