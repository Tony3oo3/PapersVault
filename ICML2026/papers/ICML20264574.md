## Problem

Existing MAG benchmarks (MAGB, MM-GRAPH) are limited to 2–3 application domains, rely exclusively on frozen encoders, evaluate only a handful of unimodal or simple multimodal GNNs, and restrict tasks to supervised graph-based objectives. This makes fair comparison of the rapidly growing family of MAG models—including MLLM-enhanced and generative paradigms—impossible under a single protocol. OpenMAG addresses these gaps by providing a unified, modular evaluation standard spanning broader domains, trainable encoders, diverse model families, and both discriminative and generative tasks.

## Input / Output

Each dataset provides a graph whose nodes carry raw text (titles, descriptions, captions) and image/video-frame attributes, with edges defined by domain-specific semantics (co-purchase, co-viewing, threading, shared artist, etc.). The 16 supported encoders project these raw attributes into fixed-size embeddings (768–3584 dimensions) before any model consumes them; the 24 models then produce task-specific outputs ranging from discrete class labels and edge scores to free-form text (evaluated by BLEU-4/CIDEr) and synthesized images (evaluated by CLIP-Score/DINO-Score). A key design choice is that the same node embedding can be routed to any of the 8 downstream tasks without re-encoding, enabling cross-task comparisons under identical feature conditions.

## Method

OpenMAG is a modular benchmark library rather than a single architecture: it standardizes a four-stage pipeline (multimodal encoding → fusion → structure-aware propagation → task projection) and instantiates it with 19 datasets, 16 encoders (frozen extractors like CLIP/DINOv2/Sentence-BERT plus trainable BERT/ViT backbones), and 24 models spanning three paradigms—Graph-enhanced (e.g., DGF, GraphMAE2), Multimodal-enhanced (e.g., MMGCN, LGMRec), and MLLM-enhanced (e.g., GraphGPT-O, InstructG2I, NTSFormer). Evaluation is structured along five axes: Necessity (ablation of modality vs. topology), Data Quality (encoder alignment via retrieval metrics), Effectiveness (cross-task accuracy/generation quality), Robustness (text/image/label/edge noise injection), and Efficiency (theoretical complexity plus empirical time/memory). The benchmark's key contribution is the unified interface that lets any encoder–model–task combination be swapped in and out, producing 14 actionable insights such as the superiority of unified over independent encoders, the dataset-dependent optimal neighbor count for generation, and the dominance of LoRA over Adapter for PEFT-based LLM fine-tuning in MAG settings.

## Results

Across 19 datasets and 8 downstream tasks, LGMRec and DGF consistently dominate graph-based tasks: DGF achieves the best Link Prediction MRR (77.28 on DY, 42.55 on Bili Dance) and Node Clustering NMI (51.29 on Toys, 84.89 on RedditS), while LGMRec leads Node Classification on Grocery (84.27% Acc). For modality-based tasks, a simple MLP surprisingly outperforms all GNN variants on Modality Retrieval (99.46 T2I MRR on Toys), while DGF leads G2Text (6.83 BLEU-4, 44.28 CIDEr on Flickr30k) and LGMRec leads G2Image (68.47 CLIP-Score, 52.73 DINO-Score on SemArt). UniGraph2, despite its generative masked-prediction design, exhibits the worst performance across all graph-based tasks (e.g., 10.34 NMI on Toys clustering), confirming an objective mismatch between reconstruction and discriminative goals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGF (dual graph filtering) is the strongest overall performer, achieving the best results on 8 out of 12 graph-based task-metric pairs, including Link Prediction MRR of 77.28 on DY (vs. 74.47 for DMGC and 70.28 for GCN) and Node Clustering NMI of 84.89 on RedditS (vs. 80.73 for LGMRec). LGMRec is the runner-up, excelling specifically on Node Classification (84.27% Acc on Grocery, 46.26 F1 on Movies) and G2Image generation (68.47 CLIP-Score), but trails DGF on Link Prediction and Clustering. Traditional GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) serve as structural baselines and are consistently outperformed by MAG-specific models, with GCN achieving only 52.84% Acc on Movies versus DGF's 53.89%. DMGC, while competitive on Link Prediction (74.47 MRR on DY), suffers a catastrophic performance collapse under text noise, revealing over-reliance on textual semantics. UniGraph2, the most recent MLLM-enhanced model, performs worst across all graph-based tasks (e.g., 10.34 NMI on Toys clustering vs. 51.29 for DGF), attributed to its generative masked-prediction objective being misaligned with discriminative evaluation. MMGCN and MGAT show moderate performance but are constrained by memory overhead (MMGCN) or lack of structural denoising (MGAT), while a simple MLP surprisingly wins Modality Retrieval (99.46 MRR), indicating that graph aggregation introduces noise for pure alignment tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenMAG positions itself as a direct successor to prior MAG benchmarks (MAGB at KDD'25 and MM-GRAPH at CVPR'25), explicitly addressing their limitations in domain coverage (5–7 datasets vs. 19), encoder flexibility (frozen-only vs. frozen+trainable), model diversity (4–7 vs. 24), and task scope (supervised graph-based only vs. 8 tasks including generative and unsupervised). Rather than proposing a new model, it repurposes and standardizes 24 existing architectures spanning three paradigms—Graph-enhanced, Multimodal-enhanced, and MLLM-enhanced—under a unified pipeline with 16 modality encoders. The paper also distinguishes itself from GraphMLLM (Liu et al., 2025), which it excludes from comparison due to its role-oriented MLLM pipeline and restriction to node classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the MAG learning field proliferates with new architectures (GraphGPT-O, MLaGA, Graph4MM, NTSFormer, etc.), the absence of a standardized evaluation framework has made fair comparison difficult; OpenMAG fills this gap with a modular, open-source library that the community can extend. The 14 derived insights—particularly that MLLM-enhanced models underperform in discriminative settings and that simple MLPs beat complex GNNs in modality retrieval—challenge prevailing assumptions about architectural complexity. Key open questions remain: whether unified generalist MAG models can replace task-specific architectures, how to achieve scalable inference for MLLM-based approaches with quadratic sequence complexity, and how to build robustness against real-world noise without sacrificing accuracy.
