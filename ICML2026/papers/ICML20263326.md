## Problem

In sequential macro placement, the order in which macros are processed has been treated as a static preprocessing step governed by fixed heuristics (area-first, connectivity-first), while the spatial question of 'where to place' has dominated research attention. The paper demonstrates that suboptimal early placement decisions trigger irreversible domino effects that asymptotically degrade solution quality (Ω(l) for chain topologies, Θ(g) for star topologies), yet the ordering dimension remains unexplored because evaluating sequences requires prohibitively expensive full placement cycles and the ordering signal is masked by powerful iterative refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a macro placement instance I=(M, E, G) consisting of l macros with dimensions, area, and connectivity degree, k hyperedge nets, and a 224×224 discrete placement grid, and produces (x, y) coordinates for each macro evaluated by half-perimeter wirelength (HPWL). The LLM generates code-level Python priority functions (static sort keys or dynamic per-step scoring functions) that consume a PlacementContext API exposing macro attributes, shared nets, bounding boxes, and grid parameters. Proxy evaluation runs 50 Monte Carlo greedy placements per candidate strategy in parallel across 8 workers with a 1800-second timeout, computing mean HPWL as the fitness score without invoking full-scale optimization.

## Method

OrderPlace is a three-component framework: (1) a diverse initial population of 9 hand-crafted ordering strategies spanning static (area, degree, lexicographic) and dynamic (spring potential, field potential, entropy, Hamiltonian) types; (2) an LLM-driven evolutionary loop where the LLM (temperature 0.7, Z=4 candidates per generation, 4 iterations) reads top-K evaluated strategies with their source code and generates novel code-level priority functions, validated via syntax checking and functional testing; (3) a wire-mask-guided greedy solver that places each macro at the position minimizing masked HPWL contribution given the current canvas state. The key insight is using the low-uncertainty greedy placer as a 'sensitive probe' that magnifies the ordering signal, combined with a lightweight proxy evaluation (Monte Carlo greedy runs) that filters candidates at a fraction of full-optimization cost. The discovered strategies are predominantly dynamic, phase-adaptive functions that combine gravitational attraction from placed macros, information-theoretic entropy reduction, and net-closure bonuses, with weights that shift as placement progress ρ evolves.

## Results

On the ISPD 2005 benchmark suite, OrderPlace achieves the best average rank of 1.17 across 12 competing methods, securing the lowest HPWL on 7 out of 8 circuits. Compared to the strongest baseline EGPlace, OrderPlace reduces average HPWL by 14.08%, and compared to WireMask-EA by 34.04%. The method also converges significantly faster than EGPlace, with the top evolved strategy (Strategy 1) reaching lower HPWL values much earlier in the optimization trajectory across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrderPlace outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of ISPD 2005 circuits. Against the strongest competitor EGPlace (ICML 2025), OrderPlace achieves significantly lower HPWL on 5 of 6 reported circuits (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p<0.05), with the largest gap on bigblue3 (36.72 vs. 52.41, a 30% reduction); EGPlace edges out OrderPlace only on adaptec1 (5.72 vs. 5.75). Against WireMask-EA (NeurIPS 2023), the paper's direct methodological predecessor, OrderPlace reduces HPWL by 34.04% on average, confirming that optimizing the placement sequence is a critical missing dimension. Among RL-based methods, OrderPlace surpasses EfficientPlace (ICML 2024) by 12–30% and Chipformer (ICML 2023) by 15–40% across circuits. Classical and analytical baselines (SP-SA, NTUPlace3, RePlace, DreamPlace, GraphPlace, DeepPR, MaskPlace) are all dominated by a wide margin, with HPWL gaps exceeding 2× on most circuits. On the larger bigblue2 and bigblue4 circuits (1024 macros), OrderPlace also leads EGPlace by 27% and 2% respectively, demonstrating scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the wire-mask-guided greedy placement solver from WireMask-EA (Shi et al., 2023), repurposing it as a deterministic 'sensitive probe' to isolate the effect of macro ordering from stochastic optimization noise. It departs from the entire lineage of macro placement methods (GraphPlace, MaskPlace, Chipformer, EfficientPlace, EGPlace) that treat placement sequencing as a fixed heuristic prior (area-first, degree-first, netlist order) by reframing it as a first-class optimization variable. The LLM-driven evolutionary strategy discovery follows the automated algorithm design paradigm of EvoHeuristics (Liu et al., 2024b) and AutoS-GNN (Mo et al., 2025), but applies it to a code-level policy space for VLSI-specific ordering functions rather than generic heuristic search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bounds (Ω(l) degradation for chain topologies, Θ(g) for star topologies) provide the first formal evidence that ordering is not a negligible preprocessing detail but an asymptotically critical dimension, which should reshape how sequential placement pipelines are designed. The proxy evaluation mechanism—using a lightweight greedy probe to filter candidate sequences before expensive full optimization—offers a transferable pattern for any sequential decision problem where full evaluation is prohibitive. However, the study is limited to mask-guided greedy solvers on ISPD 2005; the interaction between optimized ordering and stochastic or learning-based placers (RL, gradient-based) remains entirely unexplored, and whether the discovered strategies generalize beyond the 224×224 grid resolution is an open question.
