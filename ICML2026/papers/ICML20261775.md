## Problem

Traditional binary AI-text detection cannot distinguish legitimate editing assistance from wholesale generation, creating fairness risks especially for non-native speakers who rely on AI for polishing. Existing multi-bit watermarking methods apply uniform injection strength across all task types, which compromises semantic fidelity in editing scenarios where alteration is nearly impermissible, and no dataset provides intent annotations for systematic evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261775/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At generation time, the method takes a prompt/context, a binary intent label (Edit or Gen), a secret key κ, and a bias strength δ, and produces watermarked text. At attribution time, the decoder requires only the watermarked text and the secret key—no generation-time side information such as the entropy skip mask or the original prompt. The output is a three-way attribution label (Edit, Gen, or Unknown), where Unknown is returned when the recovered signal falls within an abstention margin around 0.5, ensuring graceful degradation rather than false accusation. Texts shorter than ~150 tokens may lack sufficient watermark capacity for reliable attribution.

## Method

IACW builds on Qu et al.'s pseudo-random segment-assignment multi-bit backbone and extends it along two axes. First, Intent-Adaptive Entropy Gating computes the semantic entropy of the LLM's next-token distribution at each generation step and applies the watermark logit bias only at high-entropy positions (where multiple tokens are equally plausible), skipping low-entropy positions where biasing would produce perceptible artifacts; the entropy threshold is intent-adaptive (τ_edit = 0.5 nats for strict fidelity, τ_gen = 0.3 nats for higher payload density). Second, Confidence-Based Erasure Decoding independently identifies unreliable segments at decode time by computing the vote margin between top-1 and top-2 candidates per segment, flagging low-confidence segments as erasures rather than forced errors, which enables the (15,7) BCH code's erasure-correction capability to recover the payload without requiring the generation-time skip mask. The binary intent is encoded as maximally separated raw payloads (all-zeros for Edit, all-ones for Gen) before BCH protection, maximizing Hamming distance and adversarial tolerance.

## Results

On the IACW-Instruct held-out test set (200 instances), IACW achieves 99.5% intent attribution accuracy on clean text, maintaining 95.0% under 20% token deletion, 69.0% under paraphrasing, and 54.5% under back-translation. In the human-AI mixed workflow, IACW retains 91.0% detectability even when only 10% of content is AI-polished, and 83.0% intent accuracy at 30% AI-polished ratio. On semantic fidelity for editing tasks, IACW's PPL (42.1) approaches the unwatermarked baseline, while removing entropy gating doubles PPL to 89.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qu et al. (the multi-bit watermarking backbone), IACW wins on every robustness metric: 99.5% vs. 93.0% clean intent accuracy, 95.0% vs. 90.5% under 20% token deletion, 69.0% vs. 58.0% under paraphrasing, and 54.5% vs. 36.5% under back-translation. The gap is largest under back-translation (+18 points), where IACW's erasure-aware ECC decoding recovers from clustered segment errors that Qu et al.'s forced-decision decoder cannot handle. Against KGW (zero-bit watermarking), IACW is not directly comparable on intent attribution since KGW carries no payload, but in the human-AI mixed workflow (Table 5), IACW achieves 91–100% detectability at 10–100% AI-polished ratios while KGW drops to 0% below 50% ratio, demonstrating that entropy-gated concentration of the signal in high-entropy positions is more resilient to human editing dilution than KGW's uniform embedding. On semantic fidelity, IACW approaches unwatermarked quality (PPL 42.1, 48.2% human preference vs. no-watermark) while Qu et al. degrades significantly (PPL 89.1 without gating, 38.7% preference), confirming that entropy-guided position selection is essential for editing tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the multi-bit segment-assignment backbone of Qu et al. (2025) and the entropy-based position-skipping heuristics explored by Wang et al. (2025), Lee et al. (2024), Fu et al. (2024), and Pang et al. (2024), but departs in two key ways: it makes the entropy threshold intent-adaptive (stricter for editing, looser for generation) rather than uniform, and it unifies quality-preserving skips with adversarial-attack robustness under a single erasure-decoding framework rather than treating them as separate concerns. The intent-attribution objective itself—binding and recovering an Editing-vs-Generation label from output text alone—is positioned as a new governance framing that goes beyond the presence/absence question addressed by KGW and prior multi-bit schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete governance gap (distinguishing AI-polished from AI-generated content) and contributes a 5,000-instance benchmark (IACW-Instruct) with a reproducible Director-Actor-Judge construction pipeline that the field lacks. However, practical deployment is constrained by the need for model-level logit access (ruling out proprietary APIs), English-only evaluation, and a concerning 54.5% accuracy under back-translation that is barely above chance for binary classification. Paragraph-level attribution in mixed documents and multilingual generalization remain open, and the binary Editing/Generation framing, while justified for robustness, sacrifices fine-grained provenance that some governance contexts may require.
