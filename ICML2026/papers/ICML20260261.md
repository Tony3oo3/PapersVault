## Problem

Standard LRM evaluations reduce reasoning behavior to one-dimensional metrics like final-answer accuracy and token count, which conflate fundamentally different reasoning structures: a focused deductive trace and a diffuse exploratory trace can achieve identical scores. This paper asks how to make the *topology* of a model's reasoning a measurable, comparable object that exposes whether extra tokens reflect productive deduction or verification overhead and meandering.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a free-form textual reasoning trace (an ordered sequence of sentences generated by an LRM solving a 2D grid puzzle) together with the puzzle instance grid, and produces a directed acyclic graph whose nodes are atomic claim occurrences (each verified as correct, incorrect, or unverifiable against an executable puzzle environment) and whose edges encode deductive dependencies or restatement links. From this graph the method outputs a reasoning-flow efficiency score η ∈ [0,1], structural entropy H_str, per-claim validity labels, first-error depth, solution-supporting fraction |V_sol|/|V|, verification overhead |V_ver|/|V_sol|, and standard topology statistics (depth, width, diameter, average path length).

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage extraction pipeline that converts unstructured traces into verifiable reasoning graphs: (i) a two-pass hybrid claim extractor combining deterministic pattern matching with LLM-based completion and screening, (ii) LLM-based rule extraction that attributes a single deduction rule per claim to recover directed edges, and (iii) deterministic verification of each claim against an executable puzzle environment. On the resulting DAG, the model's logical flow is formalized as an absorbing Markov chain where claims are transient states and all leaf nodes feed a single absorbing state; the fundamental matrix N = Σ Q^t yields the total logical mass m(v) through each node. Structural entropy H_str is then computed as the Shannon entropy of the normalized mass distribution, and reasoning-flow efficiency η = (log|V| − H_str) / (log|V| − log|C*|) normalizes this entropy against the minimal claim set C* that specifies the solution, so that η ≈ 1 indicates tightly focused deduction while low η signals diffuse exploration spread across peripheral claims.

## Results

On the 21-puzzle PUZZLES benchmark, GPT-5 achieves the highest accuracy at every difficulty level (83.8% Trivial, 69.5% Human easy, 58.1% Human normal, 5.7% Human hard) while also being the most token-efficient (avg. 12,867 tokens). All models exhibit a steep accuracy collapse as difficulty increases, with the Human hard regime remaining largely unsolved (≤5.7% for all models) despite token budgets growing 3–5× from Trivial to Hard. The proposed reasoning-flow efficiency metric η is essentially uncorrelated with token count (r = −0.05, p = 0.64) but positively correlated with claim accuracy (r = 0.33) and solution-supporting graph fraction (r = 0.55), demonstrating that structural concentration of logical flow captures reasoning quality that verbosity and final-answer accuracy miss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 outperforms all three open-source models (Qwen3 235B, DeepSeek V3.2, Kimi K2) on accuracy at every difficulty level while using fewer tokens, achieving 54.3% average accuracy at 12,867 mean tokens versus 33.8–43.8% at 19,001–36,343 tokens for the open models. Kimi K2 consistently consumes the largest token budgets (up to 61,307 on Human hard) yet does not surpass GPT-5 on any metric, illustrating that raw compute allocation does not translate to better reasoning. DeepSeek V3.2 is competitive at Human normal (44.8%) but collapses to 0% on Human hard, while Qwen3 235B degrades earlier (21.0% at Normal) and also reaches 0% on Hard. The paper's key structural finding is that η separates reasoning quality from verbosity: it correlates with claim accuracy (r = 0.33) and solution-supporting fraction (r = 0.55) but not with token count (r = −0.05), whereas simpler metrics like |V|, width, and token count all correlate negatively with accuracy (r ≈ −0.58 to −0.67) and primarily track puzzle difficulty rather than reasoning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PUZZLES grid-puzzle environment (Estermann et al., 2024) and Simon Tatham's puzzle collection to create a scalable benchmark, but its core departure is from prior LRM evaluation that collapses reasoning into one-dimensional metrics (accuracy, token count). It extends the line of structured trace representations (reasoning graphs, DAG decompositions from Xiong et al. 2025, Minegishi et al. 2025, Zeng et al. 2025) by grounding claims in an executable puzzle environment for deterministic verification, and introduces an absorbing Markov chain formulation to define a flow-efficiency metric η. Unlike prior trace-level studies that catalog failure modes (overthinking, underthinking, diversity collapse), this work provides a single quantitative structural metric that separates focused deduction from diffuse exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical diagnostic tool for reasoning model developers: η can distinguish reasoning quality even when accuracy is saturated and token budgets overlap, and the finding that extra tokens primarily become verification overhead rather than solution-relevant structure has direct implications for test-time compute allocation. The domain-agnostic structural layer (graph construction, Markov chain, η) extends naturally to mathematical reasoning, code generation, and agentic settings where intermediate steps are verifiable. Key limitations include LLM-based extraction variance (mitigated but not eliminated), the need for per-puzzle-family claim/rule schemas, and the fact that the structural analysis is currently demonstrated only on Tents with three open-source models. An open question is whether η could serve as a shaping reward in post-training to incentivize solution-focused reasoning.
