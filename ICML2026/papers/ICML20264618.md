## Problem

Under standard end-to-end IAA training with only overall-score supervision, different image subsets dominated by different aesthetic attributes (e.g., blur vs. saturation) experience opposing gradient directions on shared parameters, causing gradient cancellation and persistent systematic bias. This optimization-level conflict has not been systematically studied in IAA, and existing attribute-aware methods require explicit attribute labels that most benchmarks do not provide.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256 for CNN backbones or 224×224 for ViT backbones) and outputs a scalar aesthetic score normalized to [0,1]. The input image is additionally processed through five attribute-specific transforms (brightness, contrast, blur, hue, saturation) to create six views total, and per-image sensitivity weights are precomputed offline via symmetric perturbation of the trained baseline model (10 forward passes per image, cached as 5 floats). Frozen LLaVA text features serve as semantic anchors but are extracted once offline and never involved in training or inference.

## Method

AGREE is a plug-and-play framework that inserts four mechanisms into any existing IAA backbone without modifying its architecture: (1) attribute-aware decoupling splits the shared backbone output into six independent adapter branches (one overall + five attribute-specific), reducing parameter coupling; (2) multimodal fusion projects frozen LLaVA text anchors into the feature space and concatenates them with visual features via 1×1 convolutions for semantic separation; (3) sensitivity-guided routing uses offline perturbation-based sensitivity scores to compute per-sample normalized weights α_k that gate attribute features before a final 1×1 convolution fusion, so each sample's update is allocated toward its most relevant attribute; (4) error-aware reweighting tracks EMA prediction errors per sample and upweights persistently hard samples in the MSE loss. The theoretical analysis shows that with sparse routing weights and weak cross-branch alignment, gradient updates to different attribute blocks become approximately orthogonal, structurally preventing the cancellation that causes hard-sample stagnation.

## Results

AGREE achieves state-of-the-art SRCC/PLCC on all five IAA benchmarks when integrated with EAT or HKD-IAA. The best configuration (AGREE w/EAT) reaches SRCC 0.789 on AVA, 0.853 on LAPIS, 0.911 on PARA, and 0.488 on TAD66K, while AGREE w/HKD-IAA leads on AADB with SRCC 0.682. Relative improvements over the strongest baseline range from 1.6% (AADB PLCC) to 4.8% (AVA SRCC/PLCC). On hard samples specifically, AGREE yields average SRCC gains of 17.6%–46.8% across datasets, far exceeding overall gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGREE is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on on top of six baselines: TANet, EAT, ELTA, EAMB-Net, HKD-IAA, and AesPrompt. When integrated with EAT, AGREE outperforms the original EAT by +3.7% SRCC on AVA (0.789 vs. 0.752), +5.1% on LAPIS (0.853 vs. 0.802), +2.0% on PARA (0.911 vs. 0.891), +11.5% on AADB (0.669 vs. 0.601), and +2.5% on TAD66K (0.488 vs. 0.476). When integrated with HKD-IAA, AGREE surpasses the original on AADB (0.682 vs. 0.671 SRCC) and LAPIS (0.859 vs. 0.781 SRCC), and also improves on AVA (0.761 vs. 0.753) and TAD66K (0.432 vs. 0.395). Across all six baselines and five datasets, AGREE improves every baseline-dataset combination on SRCC and PLCC, with average SRCC gains of 4.2%–12.6% depending on the dataset. The largest single improvement is +25.4% SRCC (ELTA on LAPIS hard samples), and even the strongest baselines (EAT, HKD-IAA) benefit substantially, confirming that the gains come from the gradient-conflict mitigation mechanism rather than architectural capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the gradient-conflict machinery from multi-task learning (GradNorm, PCGrad, MGDA) to a setting where no explicit tasks exist: under overall-score-only IAA supervision, 'tasks' are implicit attribute-dominant sample subsets whose identities must be inferred. It departs from attribute-aware IAA methods (Jin et al. 2019, Pan et al. 2019) that require auxiliary attribute labels, instead estimating sample-wise attribute sensitivity via perturbation analysis on the trained baseline. The plug-and-play design—attribute-specific adapters, frozen LLaVA text anchors, sensitivity-guided routing, and error-aware reweighting—sits on top of existing end-to-end IAA backbones (ViT, ResNet, Swin, CLIP) without modifying their architectures, distinguishing it from both multi-task IAA approaches and prior single-objective IAA methods that share all parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that cross-model systematic errors in IAA are not isolated outliers but stem from a shared optimization pathology (gradient cancellation across attribute-dominant subsets), and that hard samples are enriched with under-represented attributes (Blur, Hue). This reframing shifts the diagnostic from 'model capacity' to 'update allocation,' and the plug-and-play AGREE module offers a practical remedy with modest overhead (~7.6M params, <10% training time). Open questions include whether the five low-level visual attributes are sufficient, whether perturbation-based sensitivity captures true causal attribute importance, and whether the approach extends to higher-level semantic or compositional aesthetic factors. The approximate-orthogonality assumptions in the theory are empirically supported but not guaranteed for all attribute partitions or domains.
