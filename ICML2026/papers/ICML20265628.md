## Problem

MLLMs adapted for 3D reasoning segmentation suffer from two failure modes: erroneous localization (generating responses semantically unrelated to the 3D scene due to scarce 3D scene-language training data) and boundary ambiguity (coarse object delineation caused by the sparsity and irregular geometry of point clouds). Existing methods either feed raw point clouds or fixed pre-captured RGB views directly to MLLMs, failing to align visual evidence with the specific spatial intent of a language query.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud S ∈ R^{N×6} (6-channel per-point features) and a free-form text instruction as input, and produces a per-point binary segmentation mask M ∈ {0,1}^N along with a natural-language text response. The text response is generated autoregressively by the MLLM and contains a special [SEG] token whose hidden state drives a Transformer decoder to produce the mask, coupling the linguistic reasoning with the geometric prediction in a single forward pass.

## Method

TVDRNet introduces an Active Vision-inspired pipeline with two core modules. The AVPL module uses an MLP to map concatenated text embeddings and mean-pooled point cloud features to camera parameters (azimuth, elevation, focal length), which drive a differentiable renderer to project the point cloud into 8 task-specific 2D views; because rendering is differentiable, segmentation gradients propagate back to the camera parameters, forcing the network to learn a direct text-to-viewpoint mapping. The MGL module concatenates point cloud and multi-view image features, computes a cosine similarity matrix, partitions features into G=6 semantic groups by coherence score, then performs intra-group attention aggregation and inter-group hierarchical attention to yield a unified 3D-aware representation. This representation is projected into a LoRA-tuned LLaVA-v1.5-7B backbone that generates the text response and [SEG] token, which queries a Transformer decoder over the 3D-aware features to produce the final mask. The entire system is trained end-to-end for 100 epochs with AdamW (lr=2e-4, cosine annealing) on 4×NVIDIA L20 GPUs.

## Results

On the Reason3D benchmark, TVDRNet achieves 55.12 Acc@0.25, 42.13 Acc@0.50, and 43.92 mIoU, surpassing the previous best (OpenMaskDINO3D) by 4.11% in mIoU. On Instruct3D, it reaches 49.5 Acc@0.25, 43.4 Acc@0.50, and 37.2 mIoU, outperforming MLLM-For3D by 2.7% in mIoU. On ScanRefer for 3D visual grounding, TVDRNet attains 66.2 Acc@0.25 and 57.9 Acc@0.50 overall, exceeding the top task-specific model UniVLG (65.9/57.5) and the leading LMM-based baseline Reason3D (57.9/41.9) by a 16.0% margin in Acc@0.50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVDRNet outperforms all 15 baselines across both benchmarks. Against the strongest RGB+3D+Text methods, it beats OpenMaskDINO3D by 4.11% mIoU on Reason3D (43.92 vs. 39.81) and MLLM-For3D by 2.7% mIoU on Instruct3D (37.2 vs. 34.5), despite using only 3D+Text input (no external RGB). Among 3D+Text-only methods, the gap is much larger: TVDRNet exceeds Reason3D (Huang et al., 3DV'25) by 12.72% mIoU on Reason3D and 24.77% mIoU on Instruct3D. On ScanRefer, TVDRNet narrowly edges out the best task-specific model UniVLG (66.2 vs. 65.9 Acc@0.25; 57.9 vs. 57.5 Acc@0.50) while substantially outperforming the leading LMM-based baseline Reason3D by 16.0% in Acc@0.50 (57.9 vs. 41.9). Older baselines (OpenMask3D, OpenScene, TGNN, 3D-STMN) are surpassed by 25–40% in mIoU, confirming that the text-guided differentiable rendering paradigm provides a decisive advantage over static point-cloud-only pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TVDRNet carves new ground by repurposing differentiable rendering—previously used for 3D reconstruction, pose estimation, and mesh optimization—as a text-conditioned viewpoint generator for 3D reasoning segmentation. It departs from prior MLLM-based 3D segmentation methods (Reason3D, MLLM-For3D, OpenMaskDINO3D) that either feed raw point clouds or fixed pre-captured RGB views into the language model; instead, TVDRNet learns a direct mapping from text semantics to camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters, inspired by Active Vision theory. The MGL module draws on cross-modal attention and semantic grouping ideas from recent multi-view fusion work, but applies them specifically to fuse rendered multi-view features with sparse point-cloud geometry for mask prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The active-vision framing offers a principled way to address two persistent failure modes in 3D reasoning segmentation—erroneous localization and boundary ambiguity—without requiring dense external RGB captures, which is practical for point-cloud-only deployment settings. However, the approach is validated only on indoor ScanNet-derived benchmarks (Reason3D, Instruct3D, ScanRefer) with a 7B MLLM backbone, leaving open whether text-guided viewpoint optimization generalizes to large-scale outdoor scenes, dynamic environments, or other 3D representations (meshes, NeRFs, Gaussian splats). The reliance on a differentiable renderer over point clouds also limits the visual richness of the generated views compared to photorealistic rendering, which may cap performance on texture-dependent queries.
