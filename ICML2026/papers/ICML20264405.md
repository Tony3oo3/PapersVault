## Problem

Importance-Weighted Neural Processes (IWNPs) suffer from a 'Matthew effect' in the latent space: the log-sum-exp objective causes gradients to collapse onto the single best-performing particle, leaving lower-likelihood particles under-optimized and producing poor tail-risk generation. Naive robust objectives like CVaR mitigate tail risk but degrade average-case performance, creating an unresolved trade-off between worst-case and mean adaptation quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes context observations (x, y pairs) for 1D GP regression, partial image patches for image completion (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, mini-ImageNet), or sparse time-series context points for Sim2Real evaluation. During meta-training, B=16 latent particles are sampled per task and sorted by per-particle negative log-likelihood; during meta-testing, B=32 particles are used. The output is a predictive Gaussian (mean and variance) at target points, from which multi-particle log-likelihoods and CVaR metrics at varying confidence levels are computed to quantify both average and tail-risk generative quality.

## Method

OS-NPs stratify B inference particles into K=4 disjoint quantile bins based on sorted per-particle NLLs, recasting the IWNP objective as a multi-objective problem where each stratum is a separate loss. A regularized worst-case optimization (min over strata of gradient alignment, à la MGDA) determines the merged descent direction, with an L2 penalty on softmax logits preventing weight collapse to a single stratum. To avoid the O(K²d) cost of explicit Gram-matrix construction, the method amortizes the inner loop by approximating GᵀGα via observed one-step loss decay rates (ΔL_k/η), requiring only a single backward pass per iteration. The outer loop then updates encoder-decoder parameters using the weighted sum of stratum-wise gradients, yielding a bi-level pipeline with convergence guarantees under L-Lipschitz gradient and bounded-logit assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 image completion, OS-NPs achieves an average log-likelihood of 7.85 and CVaR₀.₉ of 7.22, outperforming IWNPs (7.78 / 6.30) and TDRO-NPs (7.63 / 3.22). On SVHN, OS-NPs improves CVaR₀.₉ by 59.3% over IWNPs (7.95 vs. 4.99) and by 5.3% over the strongest baseline TDRO-NPs (7.95 vs. 7.55). On 1D synthetic regression with an RBF kernel, OS-NPs improves CVaR₀.₉ by 47% over IWNPs and 16% over GDRO-NPs while maintaining competitive average performance (0.52 vs. 0.56 for IWNPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OS-NPs consistently outperforms IWNPs on all CVaR tail-risk metrics across every dataset while matching or slightly exceeding average performance (e.g., CIFAR-10 CVaR₀.₉: 7.22 vs. 6.30; SVHN CVaR₀.₉: 7.95 vs. 4.99). Against CVaR-NPs, OS-NPs achieves substantially higher average log-likelihood (7.85 vs. 7.19 on CIFAR-10) and avoids the mode-collapse behavior where CVaR-NPs produce identical CVaR values across all risk levels. Compared to GDRO-NPs, OS-NPs wins decisively on SVHN (CVaR₀.₉: 7.95 vs. 5.68) and CIFAR-10 (7.22 vs. 7.21), while GDRO-NPs show flat CVaR curves indicating poor tail differentiation. Against TDRO-NPs, the gap is most dramatic on FMNIST where TDRO-NPs collapse to CVaR₀.₉ = −33.96 while OS-NPs maintain 2.01; on SVHN, OS-NPs still leads by 5.3% in CVaR₀.₉. The ablation study further confirms that the ℓ₂ regularization on the inner-loop logits is critical: removing it (Bin-w/o ℓ₂) drops CIFAR-10 CVaR₀.₉ to 6.31, and simply applying SoTA multi-task methods (Bin-FAMO) fails to balance average and tail performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Importance-Weighted Neural Processes (IWNPs, Foong et al., 2020) as its backbone, diagnosing a gradient concentration pathology (the 'Matthew Effect') in their log-sum-exp objective that causes optimization to collapse onto the single best-performing latent particle. The proposed OS-NPs recast the multi-particle optimization as a stratified multi-objective problem, drawing on MGDA-style worst-case gradient aggregation (Désidéri, 2012) and FAMO's amortized inner-loop solver (Liu et al., 2023a), but introduces an L2-regularized inner problem to prevent the softmax weights from degenerating into a single-stratum winner-take-all. Unlike prior robust meta-learning work that operates at the task level (DRO, adversarial perturbations), this paper targets particle-level generative robustness, a distinct axis of risk specific to latent-variable NPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Matthew Effect diagnosis explains a persistent practical failure mode in IWNPs—tail-risk particles produce low-fidelity generations that can mislead downstream policy optimization in robotics and world-model applications—while the proposed fix adds only ~15% training overhead over vanilla IWNPs. The method is architecture-agnostic (validated on both CNN and MLP backbones) and the stratification parameter K=4 requires minimal tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger particle counts (B > 32), diffusion-based NPs, or transformer NP architectures, and whether the amortized approximation's O(η²) bias becomes problematic in long training regimes. The paper also acknowledges that the regularized inner loop, while theoretically well-posed, is solved approximately, leaving a small optimality gap.
