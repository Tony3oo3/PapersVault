## Problem

Optimal-transport-based partial identification methods become statistically and computationally intractable when both potential outcomes and confounders are high-dimensional, as plug-in Wasserstein estimators deteriorate rapidly with ambient dimension. Existing projection-based dimension-reduction strategies discard residual transport energy on the orthogonal complement, yielding systematically loose causal bounds even when the residual is approximately isotropic and recoverable from one-dimensional projections. The paper addresses how to construct a dimension-adaptive transport primitive that remains a valid lower certificate while recovering the residual component at near-zero statistical cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed triples (Y, X, A) with binary treatment A and a user-specified covariate summary Z = h(X), along with two hyperparameters: the signal subspace dimension r and the number of Monte Carlo slices L. For each stratum z, it constructs conditional empirical measures for treated and control groups, then outputs a certified lower bound on the conditional quadratic Wasserstein distance W²(ν₁|z, ν₀|z) that is guaranteed not to exceed the true transport radius. The bound is designed as a drop-in primitive: it plugs directly into downstream OT-based partial-identification optimization programs (e.g., quadratic dispersion bounds) without requiring parametric assumptions on conditional distributions.

## Method

The core contribution is the Conditioned Subspace-Slicing (CSS) certificate, which decomposes the high-dimensional transport problem into two terms: a projected quadratic Wasserstein distance W²(μ^V, ν^V) on a learned r-dimensional signal subspace V, plus a scaled sliced-Wasserstein correction k·SW²(μ^{V⊥}, ν^{V⊥}) on the (d−r)-dimensional residual, where k = d−r. The signal subspace is recovered via Wasserstein Projection Pursuit (WPP), while the residual energy is estimated by averaging one-dimensional OT costs over L random directions on the unit sphere. The key theoretical insight is that k·SW² is itself a deterministic lower bound on W² on the residual space (via the spherical second-moment identity), and becomes near-tight when the residual Brenier map is directionally isotropic—so the two-term objective strictly improves upon projection-only certificates whenever residual discrepancy is present. Under an Extended Spiked Transport Model (ESTM) with an eigen-gap condition, the CSS optimizer provably aligns with the true signal subspace, and the population certificate gap is bounded by a single residual anisotropy deficit. Finite-sample error bounds scale with the intrinsic signal dimension r rather than the ambient dimension d, with the residual term contributing only a parametric n^{-1/2} rate plus an L^{-1/2} Monte Carlo term.

## Results

On the synthetic Gaussian pushforward (d=30, signal rank r=5, anisotropy α∈{0, 1.0, 1.5}), CSS consistently produces lower certificates closer to the analytic W₂² ground truth than projection-only WPP, with the gap most pronounced at small and moderate subspace dimensions. On the real RHC dataset (27-dimensional outcome vector, stratified by SUPPORT survival-risk score), CSS held-out certificates lie strictly above WPP across all tested subspace dimensions, and both WPP and CSS are well above the full scaled sliced-Wasserstein baseline. Subspace recovery (Figure 2) shows the Frobenius projection error ‖Ω̂−Ω⋆‖_F decreasing consistently with sample size n, reaching small values by n=1000, with only mild secondary effects from residual spectral decay (α).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CSS against two baselines: (1) WPP (Wasserstein Projection Pursuit, Niles-Weed & Rigollet 2022), which captures only the dominant low-dimensional transport signal and discards residual energy, and (2) the full scaled sliced-Wasserstein baseline (d·SW₂², Bonneel et al. 2015), which averages one-dimensional transports over the entire ambient space without learning a signal subspace. CSS wins against WPP on both synthetic and real data: by adding the scaled sliced residual correction on U⊥, CSS recovers transport energy that WPP systematically discards, yielding strictly tighter (higher) lower certificates. The advantage over WPP is largest at small and moderate dimensions where more outcome variation remains in the orthogonal complement. CSS also wins against the full scaled SW baseline on the RHC dataset, demonstrating that learning a task-adaptive signal subspace (via WPP) is essential—averaging over all ambient directions without subspace selection produces substantially weaker certificates. The full SW baseline also remains below the analytic W₂² ground truth in the synthetic experiment, confirming that ambient-space slicing alone is insufficient without the low-dimensional signal term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: the spiked transport model and Wasserstein projection pursuit (Niles-Weed & Rigollet, 2022), which identify low-dimensional subspaces where transport energy concentrates, and sliced Wasserstein distances (Bonneel et al., 2015), which average one-dimensional transport costs over random directions. The key departure from prior work is the coupling of these two principles into a single two-term objective—projected W₂² on a learned signal subspace plus a scaled sliced-Wasserstein correction on the residual—rather than using either in isolation. This is positioned as complementary to recent covariate-assisted PI frameworks (Ji et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025a,b), which address inferential validity, relaxation tightness, and topological consistency, by providing the dimension-adaptive geometric primitive those pipelines require when ambient dimension makes direct conditional transport estimation fragile.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CSS certificate offers a principled 'free lunch' for high-dimensional causal PI: under residual isotropy, one-dimensional projections recover the full residual transport energy at essentially no statistical cost, yielding strictly tighter bounds than projection-only methods without reintroducing the curse of dimensionality. The nonasymptotic guarantees scaling with intrinsic rather than ambient dimension make the approach practical for modern high-dimensional outcome settings. However, the product-structure assumption (independence between signal and residual components) and the isotropy condition on the residual Brenier map are strong; how well these hold for learned embeddings in real causal problems remains an open empirical question. The experimental validation is limited to one synthetic Gaussian model and a single real dataset (RHC), leaving open whether the CSS advantage persists across more heterogeneous data-generating processes and larger-scale applications.
