## Problem

While LoRA has become the default parameter-efficient method for RLVR training, no systematic evaluation has determined whether its rigid low-rank architecture is actually optimal for the distinct optimization dynamics of reinforcement learning with sparse, verifiable rewards. This paper asks which PEFT method is best suited for RLVR and whether the prevailing default to standard LoRA is justified.

## Input / Output

The models consume mathematical problem text and produce structured reasoning traces enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. Training supervision is a binary reward (1/0) computed by symbolic equivalence checking via latex2sympy and math_verify, making the signal extremely sparse compared to token-level SFT. Evaluation spans six math benchmarks (AIME 24/25, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, HMMT) using Avg@k and Pass@k metrics with k=32 for competition-level and k=4 for standard difficulty.

## Method

The paper conducts the first comprehensive benchmark of 12+ PEFT methods (categorized as structural variants, SVD-based initializations, efficiency-oriented, and alternative mechanisms) under RLVR using DAPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B models, targeting all linear modules at rank 32. Three core findings emerge: (1) structural variants like DoRA (46.6%) and AdaLoRA (44.2%) consistently outperform standard LoRA (42.5%) and even full-parameter fine-tuning (44.9%), attributed to architectural inductive biases better aligned with RLVR's optimization landscape; (2) SVD-informed initializations (PiSSA at 0.2%, MiLoRA at 18.0%) suffer catastrophic failure due to spectral misalignment—PiSSA forces updates onto principal components conflicting with RLVR's off-principal regime, while MiLoRA's negligible initialization magnitude causes gradient-driven degeneration back to principal subspace; (3) an expressivity floor exists where extreme compression (VeRA, IA³, Rank-1) creates an information bottleneck that prevents acquisition of complex reasoning behaviors. Extensive ablations across batch sizes, learning rates, ranks, and RLVR algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO) confirm the robustness of these findings across model scales.

## Results

On the 1.5B DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen model, DoRA achieves the highest average accuracy of 46.6% across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, surpassing full-parameter fine-tuning (44.9%) and standard LoRA (42.5%). SVD-based initialization methods collapse catastrophically: PiSSA drops to 0.2% and MiLoRA to 18.0%, while extreme parameter reduction (VeRA at 40.7%, IA³ at 22.3%) reveals an expressivity floor below which RLVR cannot sustain reasoning acquisition. On the 7B model, DoRA and LoRA+ both reach 55.0–55.5% average accuracy, maintaining their lead over standard LoRA (54.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoRA (46.6%) is the standout winner, beating standard LoRA (42.5%) by 4.1 points and even surpassing full-parameter fine-tuning (44.9%) by 1.7 points, with particularly large gains on AIME24 (+5.8) and AIME25 (+5.9). Among structural variants, AdaLoRA (44.2%) and MiSS (43.4%) both exceed LoRA, while LoRA+ (43.9%) and LoRA-FA (43.0%) offer more modest improvements. rsLoRA (42.3%) essentially ties LoRA, confirming that rank-stabilized scaling alone does not change the optimization landscape. SVD-based initializations fail dramatically: PiSSA collapses to 0.2% (near-random) and MiLoRA to 18.0%, both far below the untrained base model (40.5%), attributed to spectral misalignment with RLVR's off-principal update dynamics. Extreme compression methods hit an expressivity floor—VeRA (40.7%) and IA³ (22.3%) fall below LoRA, with IA³ suffering a 20-point drop—while LN Tuning (41.8%) also underperforms LoRA. In the algorithm ablation, DAPO (42.5%) slightly outperforms Dr. GRPO (42.0%) and GRPO (40.5%), but the relative PEFT method rankings remain stable across all three algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA and its many variants) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (GRPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO). Prior work (Wang et al. 2025b, Schulman & Lab 2025) demonstrated that standard LoRA can be competitive with full-parameter RL training, but no systematic comparison of the broader PEFT landscape under RLVR had been conducted. The paper also builds on the off-principal update theory of Zhu et al. (2025), which provides the mechanistic lens for interpreting why SVD-based initialization methods fail. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes 12+ existing PEFT architectures and evaluates them under a unified RLVR protocol, carving new ground as the first such comprehensive benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides practitioners with concrete, actionable guidance for choosing PEFT methods in RLVR pipelines, directly challenging the widespread default to standard LoRA. The identification of an expressivity floor and the mechanistic explanation for SVD-initialization failure (spectral collapse due to vanishing initialization magnitude) open questions about how to design adapters specifically for sparse-reward optimization. Key limitations include evaluation on a single model family (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) and a single domain (mathematical reasoning), leaving open whether the findings generalize to other architectures, modalities, or RLVR tasks. The paper also notes the need for migration to higher-performance frameworks (e.g., VeRL) and longer training horizons to validate asymptotic behavior.
