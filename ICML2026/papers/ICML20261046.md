## Problem

Token mixing layers in sequence models face a fundamental trade-off between decoding speed, memory (cache) size, and the ability to capture long-range dependencies, yet existing architectures (attention, SSMs, RNNs) are typically analyzed in isolation without a shared lens. The order of recurrence—how many past states influence the current output—is an underexplored design axis that directly governs this trade-off, and no principled framework existed for constructing mixers at arbitrary, controlled complexity levels while providing theoretical guarantees on expressivity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a causal sequence of n input vectors x_1,...,x_n ∈ R^d (token embeddings) and produces a sequence of n output vectors y_1,...,y_n ∈ R^d, where each y_i is a weighted linear combination of all past inputs x_1,...,x_i and all past outputs y_1,...,y_{i-1}. The mixing is fully determined by two n×n lower-triangular matrices A (direct input-to-output coefficients) and B (output-to-output recurrence coefficients), whose sparsity patterns—rather than their values—control the per-token decoding cost and cache footprint. Causality is enforced structurally: A is lower-triangular and B is strictly lower-triangular, guaranteeing (I−B) is always invertible.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified factorization Y = (I−B)^(−1) AX that decomposes any causal linear token mixer into a direct input-mixing component (A) and a recurrent propagation component (B), recovering attention (B=0), linear recurrences/SSMs (subdiagonal B), and dense recurrences as special cases. The key design tool is a translation-invariant sparsity pattern parameterized by a strictly increasing function f: a token at position i attends only positions i−f(k), yielding O(f^(−1)(n)) per-token decoding time. Expressivity is analyzed through a communication graph where shortest-path length (governed by integer decompositions of i−j into sums of f-values) and graph congestion (the maximum number of information paths through any single node) serve as theoretical proxies for long-range capacity and memory bottleneck. Concrete choices such as f(k)=2^k (logarithmic time, logarithmic path depth) and f(k)=k²+1 (square-root time, constant path depth via Lagrange's four-square theorem) span a controlled Pareto surface between O(1) and O(n) complexity. A cache-efficient variant recursively restricts the active working set to positions already present in the previous step's cache, reducing cache size to O(f^(−1)(n)) while preserving the induced access geometry, and is shown empirically to maintain or even slightly improve task performance.

## Results

On synthetic tasks, the dense recurrence (infinite-order, O(n)) achieves near-perfect scores across all three benchmarks: 100% Copy, 99.99% Associative Recall, and 99.80% Multi-hop Recall, substantially outperforming standard attention on multi-hop recall (99.80% vs. 39.21%). The sublinear pattern f(k)=k²+1 (O(√n) time) reaches 99.66% on Copy with congestion bounded by 4, while the exponential pattern f(k)=2^k (O(log n) time) achieves 92.63% on Copy. In language modeling on OpenWebText (125M–775M params), the O(n) resolvent mixer matches or slightly surpasses standard full attention, and the O(√n) pattern recovers most of the accuracy gap at a fraction of the per-token cost, tracing a clear speed–accuracy Pareto frontier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard full attention (Vaswani et al., 2017). On synthetic tasks, the dense recurrence (O(n)) matches attention on Copy (100% vs. 100%) and Associative Recall (99.99% vs. 100%) while dramatically outperforming it on Multi-hop Recall (99.80% vs. 39.21%), confirming that recurrence is essential for state-tracking tasks. The O(√n) pattern f(k)=k²+1 nearly matches attention on Copy (99.66% vs. 100%) at a fraction of the per-token cost, but lags on recall tasks (53.61% vs. 100% on Associative Recall). In language modeling on OpenWebText, the O(n) resolvent mixer (dense A, strictly lower-triangular B) consistently sits at or slightly below standard full attention perplexity across 125M, 355M, and 775M parameter scales, while the O(√n) and O(log n) variants form a tight low-perplexity frontier that recovers most of attention's accuracy at substantially lower theoretical cost. Cache-efficient variants perform slightly worse than their non-cache counterparts but the gap remains small, suggesting the sparsification does not significantly degrade expressivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies attention, state-space models, and linear recurrences under a single matrix factorization Y = (I−B)⁻¹AX, building directly on Mamba-2's equivalence between SSMs and structured linear attention (Dao & Gu, 2024), S4's diagonal-structured recurrences (Gu et al., 2022c), and ChaCAL's infinite-order recurrence (Fagnou et al., 2024). Rather than proposing a single new architecture, it treats the sparsity patterns of A and B as a design space, introducing translation-invariant patterns parameterized by a stride function f(k) that systematically interpolate between O(1) recurrences and O(n) attention. The cache-efficient variant, which quantizes the active cache onto periodic lattices, is a genuinely new construction not present in prior SSM or attention literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides principled tools—shortest-path length and graph congestion—for predicting a mixer's expressivity before training, and the resulting Pareto frontier (O(1) → O(log n) → O(√n) → O(n)) gives practitioners a clear menu of complexity-expressivity tradeoffs. However, all empirical validation is on small models (4M–775M parameters) with unoptimized PyTorch kernels, so whether the theoretical gains survive at LLM scale with competitive inference latency remains open. Key future work includes specialized block-triangular CUDA kernels exploiting the periodic cache structure, learned (non-translation-invariant) patterns, and scaling studies at longer contexts.
