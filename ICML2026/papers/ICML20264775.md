## Problem

The Tubal Nuclear Norm (TNN), the standard low-rank regularizer in the t-SVD framework, applies a uniform elementwise penalty to all transformed singular values, treating every frequency slice identically. This fails to capture the empirically observed dual spectral sparsity in real tensor data, where most spectral energy concentrates in a small subset of frequency components while those active components are themselves low-rank. The paper asks how to explicitly model and exploit this two-level structure—inter-frequency sparsity and intra-frequency low-rankness—within a unified tensor regularizer with matching statistical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method operates on third-order tensors of size d₁×d₂×m (with d₁≥d₂) that are partially observed under either i.i.d. Gaussian noise (with sampling ratios of 5%–20%) or signal-dependent Poisson noise with nonuniform sampling masks. In the completion setting, the output is a fully reconstructed tensor evaluated by PSNR and SSIM; in the clustering setting, the output is cluster assignments evaluated by ACC, NMI, and PUR. The spectral transform (DCT, DFT, or adaptive) is applied along the third mode before all singular-value operations, and the final estimate is mapped back to the original domain via the inverse transform.

## Method

The paper introduces the tensor ℓ_p-Schatten-q quasi-norm, defined as (Σᵢ(Σⱼ σⱼ(M(T)_{:,:,i})^q)^{p/q})^{1/p}, where p controls inter-frequency group sparsity and q controls intra-frequency spectral decay, with TNN recovered at (p,q)=(1,1). For optimization, a reweighted ℓ_{1/2} proxy approximates the nonconvex Schatten-q^p penalty per slice, yielding adaptive weights w_{i,k} that couple the overall energy of each frequency slice with individual singular-value magnitudes; each singular value is then updated via a half-thresholding (ℓ_{1/2} proximal) step within a scaled ADMM loop that decouples data fidelity from the dual spectral regularizer. Theoretical analysis establishes matched minimax lower and upper bounds under a Gaussian location model, showing the estimation risk decomposes into a combinatorial frequency-selection cost and a parametric within-slice estimation cost, with distinct regimes depending on whether p>q or p≤q and on the relationship between m and d².

## Results

On noisy tensor completion, the proposed ℓ_p(S_q) regularizer (with p,q ∈ (0,1)) consistently achieves the highest PSNR across all six benchmark datasets and three sampling rates (5%, 10%, 15%). At 10% sampling, it reaches 31.62 dB on Salinas A, 29.02 dB on Indian Pines, and 39.86 dB on Hair, outperforming the strongest baseline TNN-DCT (29.61, 28.40, 38.18 dB respectively) by 1.5–2.0 dB. SSIM values are competitive, with the proposed method matching or slightly exceeding TNN-DCT in most settings. In Poisson tensor completion at 20% non-uniform sampling, Bi-ℓ_p(S_q) also attains the highest PSNR across seven datasets, and in image clustering it achieves the best ACC, NMI, and PUR on all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ℓ_p(S_q) outperforms all 11 baselines in PSNR across every dataset and sampling rate in the noisy tensor completion benchmark. The strongest competitor is TNN-DCT (Lu et al., 2019), which the proposed method exceeds by 1.5–2.0 dB at 10% sampling (e.g., 31.62 vs. 29.61 on Salinas A) and by up to 2.4 dB at 5% sampling (28.72 vs. 26.52). TNN-DFT (Zhang & Aeron, 2017) and the k-Support norm (Wang et al., 2021) trail TNN-DCT by 2–4 dB, while matrix-level baselines NN (Candès & Tao, 2010) and SNN (Liu et al., 2013) are 5–10 dB behind. The non-convex ℓ_{1-2} norm (Tan et al., 2023) and Schatten-1/2 (Kong et al., 2018) are competitive with TNN-DFT but still 2–3 dB below the proposed method. In Poisson tensor completion, Bi-ℓ_p(S_q) surpasses PoissonNN (Cao & Xie, 2015), TMac (Xu et al., 2015), TNN-DFT/DCT (Zhang & Ng, 2021), and BTR-DFT/DCT (Wang et al., 2025) in PSNR at 20% non-uniform sampling. For image clustering, the method achieves the best ACC, NMI, and PUR on FRDUE, FRDUE100, PIE10, and USPS1000, confirming that the dual spectral prior benefits downstream representation learning beyond reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the t-SVD-based tensor recovery framework by introducing a two-level spectral regularizer that separates inter-frequency sparsity from intra-frequency low-rankness, departing from the uniform element-wise penalty of the Tubal Nuclear Norm (TNN). Conceptually, it adapts the double-sparse (ℓ_u(ℓ_q)) structure studied for vectors and matrices (Li et al., 2024) to the tensor spectral domain, where groups correspond to transformed frontal slices and within-group structure is governed by singular values. The proposed ℓ_p(S_q) quasi-norm subsumes TNN (p=q=1), tensor Schatten-p (p=q), and tensor average rank (p→1, q→0) as special cases, positioning it as a unifying generalization rather than an isolated new norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual spectral sparsity framing provides a more principled inductive bias for tensor data where spectral energy is naturally concentrated in a few frequency bands, and the minimax bounds under the Gaussian location model give the first sharp characterization of how estimation difficulty decomposes into frequency-selection cost and within-slice spectral estimation cost. However, the authors themselves note that the source of the statistical gain (element-wise sparsity vs. group-level frequency selection vs. their interaction) remains unresolved, and the practical benefit of explicit frequency weighting over within-slice shrinkage alone appears modest. Open algorithmic questions include sensitivity of the reweighted solver when the p–q gap is large, and whether the nonconvex coupled structure admits more stable or globally optimal solutions.
