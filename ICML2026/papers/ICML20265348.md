## Problem

Parameter-free federated learning methods like PAdaMFed eliminate hyperparameter tuning but double the per-round communication cost relative to FedAvg, and naively adding compression introduces coupled errors that destabilize normalized updates. No existing compressed FL algorithm simultaneously achieves parameter-free operation, robustness to arbitrary data heterogeneity, and independence from the compression ratio in its stepsize configuration.

## Input / Output

Each of N=100 clients holds a local dataset (28×28 grayscale MNIST/Fashion-MNIST or 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10) and receives the global model θ^t (d=21,840 parameters for the CNN) at the start of each round. The client performs K local stochastic gradient steps and uploads a single model-sized vector (the momentum increment or its compressed version), while the server broadcasts only the updated global model θ^{t+1}—halving both uplink and downlink traffic compared to PAdaMFed's two-vector protocol. The final output is a trained image classifier whose test accuracy is reported against communication rounds or total bits transmitted.

## Method

ParFreFL replaces PAdaMFed's accumulated local model differences with a global momentum vector g^t computed from SCAFFOLD-style control variates, so the server never needs local models θ_i^{t,K}, cutting uplink cost in half. Gradient normalization (m/‖m‖) at both local and global levels makes all stepsizes explicit functions of system constants (S, K, T) rather than problem-dependent quantities, while client-specific momentum smooths stochastic noise without requiring server-to-client momentum broadcast. ComParFreFL further compresses communication by defining a single transmitted quantity δ_i^t = m_i^t − c_i^{t−1} that unifies the momentum increment and the error-feedback residual, so only one compressed vector is uploaded per round. The control variate is updated as c_i^t = c_i^{t−1} + C(δ_i^t), keeping the recursion closed under biased compression. Crucially, the stepsize formula β=√(SK)/√T, η=1/(K(SKT)^{1/4}), γ=(SK)^{1/4}/T^{3/4} is independent of the compression ratio q, making the algorithm robust to dynamic network conditions without reconfiguration.

## Results

ParFreFL achieves O(ε^{-2}) communication rounds to reach stationarity E‖∇f(θ)‖ ≤ ε, improving over PAdaMFed's O(ε^{-3}), while transmitting only d parameters per round (half of PAdaMFed's 2d). The convergence rate is O((Δ+L+σ)/(SKT)^{1/4}), demonstrating linear speedup in both participating clients S and local steps K. ComParFreFL further handles biased compression (e.g., Top-k sparsification) with the same parameter-free stepsizes independent of the compression ratio q, and in experiments achieves competitive accuracy with an order-of-magnitude reduction in communication bits compared to full-precision methods on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAdaMFed (Yan et al., 2024), ParFreFL halves per-round communication from 2d to d parameters and improves communication complexity from O(ε^{-3}) to O(ε^{-2}); in experiments (Figure 1), ParFreFL matches PAdaMFed on i.i.d. MNIST/FMNIST and achieves superior accuracy under non-i.i.d. (Dirichlet α=0.1) settings. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), both ParFreFL and ComParFreFL consistently outperform the grid-search-tuned baseline across all four dataset splits, confirming the benefit of parameter-free normalized updates. Against SCAFCOM (Huang et al., 2024a), ComParFreFL reduces downlink from 2d to d parameters and eliminates all problem-specific stepsize dependencies (q, L, σ², Δ); in Figure 3, ComParFreFL-0.05 converges faster than SCAFCOM-0.05 under equivalent total-bit budgets, and in Figures 4–5 it maintains flat accuracy across learning rates 0.05–0.5 while SCAFCOM degrades sharply outside its tuned value. Against LOCAL-SGD-C (Gao et al., 2021) and FED-EF-SGD/ASM (Li & Li, 2023), ComParFreFL is the only method that simultaneously supports arbitrary data heterogeneity, partial participation, and biased compression without requiring the compression ratio q or smoothness constant L in the stepsize, making it the first parameter-free compressed FL algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PAdaMFed (Yan et al., 2024), the first parameter-free federated learning algorithm, by halving its per-round communication cost through two key modifications: using a global momentum vector as the descent direction (eliminating the need to transmit local models) and adopting client-specific momentum (eliminating the need to broadcast globally aggregated momentum). For the compressed variant, it departs from the standard error-feedback design in SCAFCOM (Huang et al., 2024a) and prior compressed FL methods by unifying the momentum increment and compression error residual into a single transmitted quantity, a construction that keeps the control-variate recursion closed under biased compression. The partial-participation handling follows the SCAFFOLD control-variate framework (Karimireddy et al., 2020b), but the specific interplay between gradient normalization, momentum, and compressed control variates is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of parameter-free optimization and communication compression addresses two of the most practical barriers to FL deployment simultaneously, and the compression-ratio independence of ComParFreFL is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such guarantee in the compressed FL literature, enabling dynamic adaptation to network conditions without reconfiguration. However, the experimental validation is limited to relatively small-scale image classification tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18), and the communication complexity carries a q²S factor that may be non-negligible for aggressive compression with many participating clients. Open questions include whether the parameter-free guarantees hold for larger-scale or more complex architectures, whether the theoretical stepsize prescriptions (which depend on total rounds T) are practical in streaming or indefinite-training settings, and how the method performs under more realistic network dynamics (asynchronous participation, heterogeneous compression ratios across clients).
