## Problem

Overparameterized deep networks memorize mislabeled training samples, degrading generalization, yet existing detection methods rely on multi-stage pipelines, tailored loss functions, or architecture-specific modules that disrupt standard training workflows. A key unresolved difficulty is that prediction entropy alone cannot separate mislabeled samples from hard-but-correctly-labeled ones, since both exhibit persistently high entropy. The paper asks whether a simple, plug-and-play statistic derived from training dynamics can reliably distinguish these two groups across diverse medical imaging domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images with their assigned class labels and tracks the model's posterior distribution at every training epoch (150 epochs by default) to build per-sample entropy trajectories. It outputs a single scalar SEI value per sample, which is compared against an adaptive threshold computed as the mean SEI of a small set of artificially relabeled auxiliary-class samples (N/(K+1) instances reassigned to a synthetic prompt). Samples falling below this data-driven cutoff are flagged as mislabeled and removed from the training set.

## Method

The core contribution is the Signed Entropy Integral (SEI), built on two observations: (1) correctly labeled samples show steady entropy decrease during training while mislabeled samples maintain elevated entropy, and (2) the alignment between the model's argmax prediction and the assigned label over time provides a complementary signal that entropy alone lacks. Signed entropy extends Shannon entropy by multiplying by (−1) when the predicted class disagrees with the assigned label, so it is positive for label-consistent uncertainty and negative for label-contradicting uncertainty. SEI then sums signed entropy across all T training epochs, yielding a scalar where easy-clean samples produce large positive values, hard-clean samples produce moderate positive values (early negative contributions are offset by later positive ones), and mislabeled samples produce strongly negative values. The method is implemented with CLIP (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16 vision encoder, Transformer text encoder) trained with a contrastive objective for 150 epochs, and an adaptive threshold is set by the mean SEI of artificially mislabeled auxiliary-class samples, requiring no manual tuning.

## Results

Under confusion-calibrated noise (the more realistic setting), SEI achieves F1 scores of 79.91% (ISIC, η=0.5), 73.04% (DeepDRiD, η=0.5), and 81.85% (PANDA, η=0.5), consistently outperforming all 10 baselines across all three medical datasets and five noise rates. On the real-world CheXpert dataset with natural clinical label noise, SEI reaches 83.59% F1, surpassing the second-best method (AUM, 80.34%) by 3.25%. On CIFAR-100N with human annotation noise, SEI achieves 81.41% F1, outperforming the runner-up SIMIFEAT (79.21%) by 2.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEI outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and noise rate under both symmetric and confusion-calibrated noise. The largest margins appear on PANDA under confusion-calibrated noise, where SEI beats the second-best method (AUM) by up to 11.87% at η=0.1 and 5.33% at η=0.5. On ISIC, the gap over the runner-up (AUM or LEMoN depending on noise rate) ranges from 1.29% to 10.32%, with the most dramatic advantage at η=0.4 (74.98% vs. 64.66% for ReCoV). On DeepDRiD, SEI leads by 3.96–6.37% over AUM, the strongest competitor. On the real-world CheXpert dataset, SEI (83.59%) surpasses AUM (80.34%) and LEMoN (79.42%) by 3.25% and 4.17% respectively. On CIFAR-100N, SEI (81.41%) edges out SIMIFEAT (79.21%) and LEMoN (78.40%), confirming generalizability beyond medical imaging. Notably, training-trajectory-based methods (AUM, GMM) and CLIP-based methods (DEFT, LEMoN) are the closest competitors, while loss-based approaches (INCV, BMM, CORES) and confidence-based methods (CL) trail significantly, especially under confusion-calibrated noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-dynamics line of noisy-label detection (O2U-Net, AUM, CORES) by replacing loss-based or single-epoch prediction statistics with a temporally integrated, sign-augmented entropy measure. Unlike prior methods that rely on multi-stage pipelines, tailored loss functions, or training-free feature-space heuristics (SIMIFEAT, DEFT, LEMoN), SEI is a single scalar computed from standard training outputs, making it architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play. The use of CLIP as the default backbone draws on the contrastive image-text alignment literature, but ablations confirm the core signal (signed entropy dynamics) transfers to plain ResNet-50 and ViT classifiers, positioning the contribution as a general training-dynamics statistic rather than a CLIP-specific trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically attractive solution for medical imaging pipelines where annotation errors are common and manual re-inspection is infeasible, with only ~6.5% additional compute and no architectural changes. Its consistent SOTA performance across three medical datasets (dermoscopy, fundus photography, histopathology) and generalization to natural-image noise (CIFAR-100N) suggest broad applicability. However, the paper is limited to the hard-label setting and explicitly defers uncertain or multi-annotator labels to future work; the adaptive thresholding via synthetic auxiliary-class samples is acknowledged as partly heuristic, and the method assumes a reasonably converged training run rather than early-stopped or undertrained models.
