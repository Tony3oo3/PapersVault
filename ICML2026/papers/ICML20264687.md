## Problem

Existing test-time guidance methods for flow-based and diffusion generative models struggle to navigate multimodal reward landscapes, particularly when high-reward regions reside in low-probability areas of the data distribution, and they fail entirely on non-differentiable rewards. Additionally, prior theoretical analyses rely on impractical L∞ pointwise error bounds that are not controlled by standard L2 training objectives, leaving a gap between training quality and guaranteed guidance performance.

## Input / Output

SPT takes a pretrained deterministic transport map T_θ (from a flow matching or diffusion model trained with an L2 loss), a scalar reward function R(x) (which may be non-differentiable, e.g., a binary event indicator), and a temperature ladder of K chains. It operates entirely in the Gaussian source space z ∈ ℝ^d, requiring only evaluations of R(T_θ(z)) without gradients. The output is a data-space sample x = T_θ(z) drawn from the reward-tilted distribution p̃(x) ∝ p(x)exp(βR(x)), applied to text-to-image generation (Stable Diffusion v1.4/1.5/XL with ImageReward) and dynamical system trajectory sampling (Lorenz '63, FitzHugh-Nagumo).

## Method

SPT performs gradient-free sampling in the source (Gaussian) space by coupling two mechanisms: (1) local exploration via the preconditioned Crank-Nicolson (pCN) operator, which yields dimension-independent Metropolis-Hastings acceptance rates because the Gaussian prior cancels in the acceptance ratio, leaving only the reward improvement; and (2) global exploration via parallel tempering, maintaining K Markov chains at inverse temperatures 0 = β₀ < β₁ < … < β_K = β with periodic replica exchange between adjacent chains to cross low-probability barriers. Step sizes θ_k decrease along the ladder, transitioning from aggressive global jumps at low temperature to fine-grained refinement at the target temperature. Theoretically, the paper establishes a novel end-to-end L2-based error bound (Corollary 4.4) showing the 2-Wasserstein distance between SPT's output and the target tilted distribution is bounded by C·ε + L·C'·ρ^t, where ε is the transport map approximation error controlled by the standard L2 training loss and ρ^t is the geometric mixing rate of the PT chain, replacing the impractical L∞ assumptions of prior work.

## Results

On the ImageReward text-to-image benchmark, SPT achieves the highest ImageReward score across all Stable Diffusion variants: 1.409 on SD 1.4 (52% over FK Steering's 0.927), 1.411 on SD 1.5 (57% over FK Steering's 0.898), and 1.479 on SD XL (14% over FK Steering's 1.298). SPT also outperforms the base model on CLIP diversity (0.346 vs 0.316 on SD 1.4; 0.351 vs 0.286 on SD XL). On dynamical system trajectory sampling with a non-differentiable binary reward, SPT reaches 99% success rate on Lorenz '63 and 81% on FitzHugh-Nagumo, compared to FK Steering's 94% and 35% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPT consistently outperforms FK Steering [40] across every evaluated metric and model variant: ImageReward improves by 52–57% on SD 1.4/1.5 and 14% on SD XL; CLIP diversity nearly doubles (0.346 vs 0.193 on SD 1.4); and dynamical-system success rate jumps from 35% to 81% on the low-probability FitzHugh-Nagumo event. Against the unsteered Stable Diffusion baseline [37], SPT raises ImageReward by 5–7× while also increasing sample diversity (0.346 vs 0.316 on SD 1.4), showing that guidance and diversity are not in tension. Training-based alignment methods (DPO [47], DDPO [6]) and Best-of-K [47] remain far below SPT on ImageReward (e.g., 0.343 and 0.737 vs 1.411 on SD 1.5), confirming that test-time source-space tempering avoids the need for costly retraining. Under a fixed 5,250 NFE budget, SPT's median ImageReward significantly exceeds FK Steering's (Welch's t-test p = 1.3×10⁻¹¹), and the 1D toy experiment shows SPT maintaining W₂ = 0.09 where FK Steering degrades to W₂ = 2.98 as the reward peak sharpens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the source-steering duality principle from Wang et al. [48], which showed that steering a Gaussian source distribution is equivalent to steering the data distribution. It departs from that work by replacing impractical L∞ pointwise error assumptions with an L2-based end-to-end bound that aligns with standard flow matching training objectives. The algorithmic core combines the classical parallel tempering framework [12] with the preconditioned Crank-Nicolson operator [5] as a gradient-free local kernel, distinguishing it from prior PT-for-generative-models approaches (CREPE [19], Markovian Flow Matching [9]) that rely on time-based tempering and gradient-based Langevin dynamics. The method is also compatible with fast deterministic ODE solvers, unlike FK Steering [40] which requires stochastic SDE sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L2 error bound (Corollary 4.4) is the first to rigorously connect standard training loss to test-time guidance quality, closing a gap that left practitioners without theoretical justification for using approximate transport maps in source steering. The ability to handle non-differentiable binary rewards (demonstrated on FitzHugh-Nagumo neuron spiking) addresses a practical limitation of all gradient-based steering methods. Key limitations include the requirement for a Gaussian source distribution, a fixed temperature grid without adaptive scheduling, and the restriction to continuous domains—extending to discrete diffusion or LLMs is identified as future work. The method's dependence on the number of chains K and iterations t_max for convergence also raises practical questions about compute scaling in very high-dimensional settings.
