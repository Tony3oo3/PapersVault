## Problem

While the internal mechanisms of text-only LLMs and large vision-language models have been extensively studied, the internal workings of audio-visual LLMs (AVLLMs) remain largely unexplored. A key open question is where information derived from one modality (audio or video) is encoded within the token representations of the other modality, which is critical for understanding how AVLLMs integrate multimodal signals and for diagnosing failure modes such as object hallucination.

## Input / Output

The causal tracing framework operates on audio-video clips from VGGSound paired with a 20-option multiple-choice text prompt, producing a predicted class label. The hallucination mitigation method (ASD) takes the same audio-video clip and text prompt as input to an AVLLM and produces a text caption, with the intervention applied at each autoregressive decoding step by modifying attention weights. Sink tokens are identified globally across all transformer layers using a frequency-based criterion rather than per-layer, since the authors observe substantial layer-wise variation in sink token identities in AVLLMs.

## Method

The paper introduces a unimodal dominance framework that identifies samples where one modality governs the model's prediction (e.g., audio-dominant: joint prediction matches audio-only but not video-only), then applies causal tracing by corrupting the dominant modality and patching hidden states of non-dominant modality tokens before the self-attention layer to measure recovery via indirect effect metrics. This reveals that cross-modal information is primarily localized in attention sink tokens, and further dissection via a Modality Dominance Score (MDS) shows that a distinct subset—cross-modal sink tokens receiving high attention from the complementary modality—serves as the primary carrier of cross-modal information, while unimodal sink tokens do not. Leveraging this insight, the authors propose Adaptive Sink-Guided Decoding (ASD), a training-free method that at each decoding step amplifies attention to cross-modal sink tokens and suppresses attention to unimodal sink tokens, with an adaptive guidance scale γ_t computed from the ratio of unimodal to cross-modal sink attention and stabilized via momentum-based temporal smoothing.

## Results

On VGGSound-Animal (where AVLLM-specific hallucinations are most prevalent), ASD reduces sentence-level hallucination (C_S) from 48.21% to 36.91% on Qwen2.5-Omni (7B) and from 37.74% to 25.07% on video-SALMONN-o1 (7B), while improving ALOHa from 40.71 to 42.77 and 36.21 to 43.29 respectively. On the more general AudioSet benchmark, ASD achieves the largest relative gains, cutting C_S from 11.39% to 6.57% (42% reduction) and C_I from 14.91% to 9.50% on video-SALMONN-o1 (7B). ASD also maintains competitive F1 scores (e.g., 72.98 on AudioSet/Qwen2.5-Omni vs. 69.73 vanilla), indicating that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of caption richness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASD consistently outperforms both baselines across all three datasets and both models. PAI (attention amplification for all multimodal tokens) actually worsens hallucination in most settings: on VGGSound-Animal/Qwen2.5-Omni it increases C_S from 48.21% to 51.24% and C_I from 37.13% to 38.11%, and on AudioSet/Qwen2.5-Omni it raises C_S from 8.92% to 11.84%. VCD (contrastive decoding with distorted inputs) similarly degrades performance, e.g., increasing C_I from 37.13% to 41.28% on VGGSound-Animal/Qwen2.5-Omni. In contrast, ASD reduces C_S by 23.4% (48.21→36.91) on VGGSound-Animal/Qwen2.5-Omni and by 42.3% (11.39→6.57) on AudioSet/video-SALMONN-o1, while also improving ALOHa by 2.06 and 2.83 points respectively. The gains are largest on VGGSound-Animal, where audio-visual disagreement hallucinations are most prevalent, validating the targeted mechanism of steering attention toward cross-modal sink tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends causal tracing (Meng et al., 2022; Basu et al., 2024) from text-only LLMs and LVLMs to the bidirectional audio-visual setting by introducing a unimodal dominance framework that isolates cross-modal information flow. It builds on the attention sink token literature in LVLMs (Kang et al., 2025; Darcet et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2026) but discovers a previously unreported functional heterogeneity within sink tokens in AVLLMs. For hallucination mitigation, it departs from prior training-free methods (PAI, VCD) that operate on all multimodal tokens uniformly, instead targeting a specific mechanistic subset identified through interpretability analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first mechanistic interpretability study of AVLLMs, revealing that cross-modal information is not uniformly distributed but concentrated in a functionally distinct subset of sink tokens (cross-modal sink tokens). The practical ASD method provides a training-free, plug-and-play hallucination mitigation that consistently outperforms existing baselines, though it shows modest F1 trade-offs. Open questions include whether the cross-modal sink token concept generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., text-audio, text-haptic) and whether the layer-wise dynamics of sink tokens in AVLLMs (which differ substantially from LVLMs) reflect a fundamental architectural property of multi-encoder LLM backbones.
