## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods (e.g., Wanda, SparseGPT) are hand-designed through extensive expert trial-and-error, incurring high labor costs, and suffer from an 'outlier value issue' where uniform sparsity causes severe performance degradation at high pruning ratios because some layers are disproportionately sensitive to weight removal. The paper asks whether LLMs can automatically design their own optimal pruning algorithms without expert knowledge, and identifies the root cause of the outlier value issue as uniform sparsity ignoring per-layer importance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-1/2 7B–70B, Pythia-12B) and 128 calibration text samples (sequence length 2048) as input. A separate search LLM (GPT-4o) is used to generate candidate pruning importance functions; the search is performed once on LLaMA-2-7B at 50% sparsity, and the discovered formula is then frozen and directly transferred to other model families, sparsity levels, and downstream tasks without re-running the search. The output is a pruned sparse LLM (no weight updates) and the discovered importance score function, which in this work takes the form ||W_ij||₁/||W_i,j||₁ · √(||X_j||₁ + ||X_j||₂²).

## Method

AutoPrune has two core components. First, a Graph-driven Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) module uses GPT-4o to automatically discover a pruning importance function through a four-stage reasoning pipeline (Analysis → Hypothesis → Conceptual Formula → Computational Concept), where each stage branches into k=5 candidates at varying temperatures, producing ~625 candidate algorithms in a directed acyclic graph; each candidate is converted to an executable importance function, applied to prune the search model, and ranked by WikiText-2 perplexity, with the best candidate selected. Second, Skew-aware Dynamic Sparsity Allocation (SDSA) addresses the outlier value issue by computing per-layer biased sample skewness of absolute weight magnitudes, range-normalizing across layers, and converting to retention weights via a softmax whose temperature β is modulated by global sparsity (β(S_g) = S_g · ln M / (Δγ̃ + ε)), ensuring uniform allocation at low sparsity and progressively releasing skewness sensitivity at high sparsity. The entire pipeline is training-free with no weight updates, and the discovered pruning rule generalizes across model families and sparsity levels.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity (lower is better), AutoPrune consistently outperforms Wanda and SparseGPT across LLaMA-1 and LLaMA-2 at 50% and 60% unstructured sparsity. At 60% sparsity, the gains are most pronounced: AutoPrune reduces perplexity from Wanda's 8.56 to 7.63 on LLaMA-1 13B (−0.93) and from 7.75 to 7.02 on LLaMA-2 13B (−0.75). On zero-shot tasks at 50% sparsity, AutoPrune achieves 72.29% on BoolQ and 56.68% on RTE for LLaMA-1 7B, surpassing Wanda (71.22%, 55.60%) and SparseGPT (72.05%, 54.15%). The SDSA sparsity allocation further improves all methods, e.g., reducing Wanda's 70%-sparsity perplexity from 85.77 (uniform) to 66.93 on LLaMA-1 7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoPrune outperforms all four baselines across the majority of settings. Against Wanda (the strongest baseline), AutoPrune achieves lower perplexity at every sparsity level tested; the gap widens substantially at 60% sparsity (e.g., 0.93 PPL on LLaMA-1 13B, 0.75 on LLaMA-2 13B) and in structured 2:4/4:8 patterns (0.19 on LLaMA-2 70B at 2:4). Against SparseGPT, AutoPrune wins at 50% and 60% unstructured sparsity on all model sizes, though SparseGPT's weight-update mechanism gives it a slight edge at 70B scale. Against Magnitude, AutoPrune's advantage is overwhelming (e.g., 9.63 vs. 6e2 perplexity at 60% on LLaMA-1 7B). Against OWL (the closest sparsity-allocation competitor), SDSA achieves lower perplexity at every sparsity level from 30% to 80%, with the largest margin at 80% (892.35 vs. 1244.62 on LLaMA-1 7B), confirming that skewness-aware allocation better mitigates the outlier value issue than OWL's outlier-ratio-based mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Wanda (Sun et al., 2024) training-free unstructured pruning framework, adopting its calibration protocol and weight×activation importance paradigm, but departs from the manual design lineage of SparseGPT, Wanda, and Magnitude by proposing that an LLM (GPT-4o) can automatically discover the pruning importance function via a Graph-driven Chain-of-Thought search. The SDSA component extends the layer-wise sparsity allocation line (OWL, ER, ER-plus) by using per-layer weight skewness as the allocation signal rather than activation-based outlier statistics. Conceptually, the work bridges AutoML-for-pruning (AMC, Auto Graph Encoder-Decoder) with LLM-specific compression, though the 'automated' search is a single one-shot GPT-4o call on one model rather than an iterative optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The skewness-based diagnosis of the outlier value issue and the resulting SDSA scheduler are practical, low-cost additions that meaningfully improve high-sparsity pruning (e.g., 66.93 vs. 85.77 PPL at 70% on LLaMA-1-7B), and the plug-and-play transfer of a single discovered metric across model families is appealing for practitioners. However, the headline 'LLMs design their own pruning algorithms' framing is somewhat overstated: the search is a one-shot GPT-4o call, the performance gains over Wanda are modest (0.04–0.93 PPL), and the GCoT component's contribution over linear CoT is small (0.54 MMLU points). Open questions include whether the discovered metric truly generalizes to unseen architectures beyond LLaMA/Pythia, whether iterative or multi-objective search would yield substantially better pruning rules, and whether the approach extends to structured or multimodal settings as the authors suggest.
