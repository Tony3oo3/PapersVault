## Problem

Multi-disease EHR modeling faces a dual challenge: disease prevalence follows a long-tail distribution that starves rare outcomes of data, and latent etiological structure is higher-order and risk-factor-specific (e.g., age groups cardiovascular/metabolic diseases while smoking groups respiratory/oncological ones). Existing methods either model diseases independently (ignoring shared structure and yielding poor rare-disease estimates) or use black-box multitask architectures that entangle all risk factors into a single latent space, obscuring which specific pathways each risk factor drives and providing no principled uncertainty over the learned structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular EHR data: a P-dimensional covariate vector per subject (e.g., 71 risk factors spanning demographics, biomarkers, lifestyle, and socioeconomic indicators in UKB), binary disease outcomes over V conditions (e.g., 66 Phecode-mapped ICD-10 diseases), and at-risk indicators R_{i,v} that restrict modeling to valid subject-disease pairs (excluding prior-baseline diagnoses). Outputs include per-disease risk probabilities, a latent V×E incidence matrix encoding which diseases co-participate in each pathway, a P×E activation matrix indicating which risk factors modulate which pathways, and posterior inclusion probabilities (PIPs) for all structural variables (hyperedge existence, disease membership, feature activation), enabling uncertainty-aware interpretation of the discovered pathway topology.

## Method

BHPI models disease pathways as hyperedges in a latent hypergraph where risk factors act directly on hyperedges rather than on individual diseases, inducing disease-specific effects via β_{j,v} = d_v^{-1} Σ_e H_{v,e} μ_{j,e}. A hierarchical sparsity structure (existence z_e → membership m_{v,e} → feature activation γ_{j,e} → effect μ_{j,e}) with spike-and-slab priors enforces logical constraints, while a repulsion prior penalizes highly overlapping hyperedges for the same risk factor using an overlap coefficient, promoting parsimonious and identifiable pathway discovery. Posterior inference uses a structured variational family that explicitly conditions membership and effect factors on hyperedge existence (preserving the z_e=0 ⇒ m_{v,e}=γ_{j,e}=μ_{j,e}=0 hierarchy that standard mean-field VI would violate), combined with Pólya-Gamma augmentation to render the logistic likelihood conditionally Gaussian. The resulting CAVI algorithm performs repulsion-aware coordinate updates where competing hyperedges for the same risk factor are coupled through expected overlap terms, achieving O(N·E·(P+V)) per-iteration complexity that scales to biobank-scale cohorts.

## Results

On the UK Biobank (N≈277,291; 66 diseases, 71 risk factors), BHPI achieves a mean ΔAUC of +1.2 (p=3×10⁻⁵) over optimally tuned per-disease logistic regression for rare diseases (<2% prevalence), with smaller but positive gains at higher prevalence (+0.2, p=0.02 for ≥5%). Predictive calibration is competitive: BHPI ties for best Brier score (0.041) and achieves ECE of 0.005. On simulated data (V=30 diseases, E=5 hyperedges), BHPI reaches 75.00 AUC (N=2000), outperforming LightGBM (68.50) and multi-label baselines (71.0–71.5), while recovering latent hypergraph structure with >96% AUC on incidence and effect-selector matrices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BHPI is the only method that consistently outperforms optimally tuned per-disease logistic regression across all prevalence strata on UK Biobank, with the advantage concentrated in the rare-disease regime (+1.2 AUC points, p=3×10⁻⁵). Bayesian Factor Regression (BFR), which tests generic low-rank latent sharing, underperforms logistic at every prevalence group (−0.2 to −0.8), indicating that unstructured factor sharing does not capture the risk-factor-specific pathway structure. The clique-expanded pairwise graph derived from the same discovered hyperedges achieves at most +0.1 in any group and is negative at 2–5% and ≥5% prevalence, because clique expansion collapses higher-order group membership into a ~65%-dense pairwise graph that loses the structural selectivity of the hypergraph representation. On simulated data, BHPI also outperforms tree-based (LightGBM: 68.50 vs. 75.00 AUC) and multi-label baselines (Binary Relevance, Classifier Chains, RAkELd: 71.0–71.5 AUC), while matching or slightly exceeding optimally tuned logistic regression (73.86).

## Relation to Prior Work

BHPI sits at the intersection of multi-task learning for EHR (Caruana, 1997; Rajkomar et al., 2018), structured disease-network models (Menche et al., 2015; Blumm et al., 2009), and hypergraph representation learning (Feng et al., 2019; Yadati et al., 2019). It departs from multi-task learning by disentangling risk-factor-specific pathways rather than entangling all features into a shared latent space, from disease networks by conditioning structure on individual risk factors rather than aggregating across all inputs, and from hypergraph neural networks by treating the hypergraph topology as a latent random variable inferred via a fully Bayesian generative model with Pólya-Gamma augmentation rather than assuming it is observed or derived from heuristic similarity metrics. The structured variational inference that preserves the logical hierarchy (existence → membership → effect) is the key technical departure from standard mean-field VI, and the repulsion prior is a novel identifiability mechanism specific to this hypergraph setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BHPI addresses a genuine gap in EHR analytics: providing interpretable, risk-factor-specific disease pathway structure with calibrated uncertainty, rather than black-box predictions. The rare-disease stabilization (+1.2 AUC for <2% prevalence) and the demonstration that clique-expanded pairwise graphs destroy the predictive signal (AUC ≈ 0.50) make a concrete case for higher-order structure in disease modeling. Open questions include whether the linear observation model is sufficient for complex EHR interactions, how the framework scales to longitudinal data and high-dimensional genetic features, and whether the permutation symmetry of latent hyperedges (acknowledged but not fully resolved) limits scientific interpretability. The 74-minute training cost for 277K samples is practical but non-trivial, and the framework's applicability beyond EHR multi-disease settings remains untested.
