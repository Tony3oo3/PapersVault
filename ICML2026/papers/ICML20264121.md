## Problem

Existing audio separation models are either restricted to fixed taxonomies (e.g., four-stem music or two-speaker speech) or support only a single prompting modality, making them inadequate for open-domain mixtures where sound boundaries are ambiguous and context-dependent. The field also lacks unified real-world benchmarks with multimodal prompt coverage and reliable reference-free evaluation metrics that correlate with human perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264121/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an audio mixture encoded into a 25 Hz, 128-channel DAC-VAE latent sequence, conditioned on any subset of three prompt types: free-form text (concise noun/verb phrases via T5-base cross-attention), a binary visual mask over video frames (processed via SAM2 and Perception Encoder), and temporal span intervals (converted to frame-synchronous active/silent tokens). It jointly produces both a target stem and a residual stem in a single pass by denoising a concatenated [x_tgt, x_res] ∈ R^{T×2C} representation, with 16-step midpoint ODE inference and candidate re-ranking.

## Method

SAM AUDIO is a flow-matching generative model built on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operating in DAC-VAE latent space, where the model learns a velocity field transporting Gaussian noise to the joint target-residual distribution. Three prompt modalities are integrated differently: text features enter via cross-attention layers, while video and span features are frame-aligned and combined with the noisy latent through elementwise addition, providing complementary global semantic and local temporal conditioning. An auxiliary REPA alignment loss projects intermediate DiT hidden states to match PANN-AED event-detection embeddings, enforcing event-level semantic and temporal consistency beyond the flow-matching objective. At inference, predicted temporal spans from PE-A-Frame are automatically appended to text prompts, yielding consistent gains across domains without manual annotation. Training data is scaled via a pseudo-labeling pipeline that bootstraps stems from 1M hours of in-the-wild audio using an intermediate checkpoint, filtered by CLAP alignment, aesthetic quality, and ImageBind audio-visual correspondence.

## Results

On SAM AUDIO-BENCH, SAM AUDIO (3B) achieves overall subjective scores (OVR) of 3.59 (General SFX), 4.29 (Speech), 4.15 (Speaker), 4.05 (Music), 4.00 (Instrument in-the-wild), and 4.45 (Instrument professional), consistently outperforming all prior open-source and proprietary baselines. The model attains a ~36% net win rate over SoloAudio on general sound separation, a 17.6% NWR over Demucs on professional instrument separation, and a ~39% NWR improvement over AudioShake on speaker separation. The proposed SAM Audio Judge (SAJ) metric achieves Pearson correlation of 0.883 (speech), 0.815 (music), and 0.815 (sound) with human ratings, substantially exceeding CLAP (0.37–0.49), SDR Estimator (0.18–0.37), and Gemini-2.5-pro (0.35–0.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM AUDIO outperforms all open-source general-purpose text-prompted models (AudioSep, FlowSep, SoloAudio, CLAPSep) by large margins across every domain, with OVR gaps of 0.6–2.4 points. Against specialized non-promptable systems, SAM AUDIO surpasses Demucs (4.45 vs. 4.26 OVR) and Spleeter (4.45 vs. 3.90) on professional instrument separation, and exceeds MossFormer2 (4.15 vs. 2.54), Tiger (4.15 vs. 2.50), and Fast-GeCo (4.15 vs. 2.71) on speaker separation. Among proprietary systems, SAM AUDIO achieves 4.45 OVR vs. AudioShake's 4.28 on professional instruments (~4% relative gain) and 4.15 vs. 3.51 on speaker separation (~39% NWR improvement), while also beating MoisesAI (4.05 vs. 3.90) and LalalAI (4.00 vs. 3.03) on music and in-the-wild instruments respectively. The only marginal case is professional instrument separation where the gap over AudioShake is modest (~0.17 OVR), and a minor degradation is observed when using predicted spans for full-length MUSDB stems. On visual prompting, SAM AUDIO outperforms DAVIS-Flow by 5–48% NWR and CLIPSep by even larger margins across general SFX, speaker, and instrument tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM Audio extends the generative audio separation line (FlowSep, SoloAudio) by replacing single-modality conditioning with a unified multimodal framework built on flow matching over a Diffusion Transformer in DAC-VAE latent space. It departs from prior discriminative masking approaches and from the fragmented landscape of text-only (AudioSep, CLAPSep) or visual-only (ClipSep, DAVIS-Flow) prompted separation by supporting text, visual mask, and temporal span prompts—individually or jointly—within one model. The span prompting mechanism and the REPA-based alignment loss with PANN-AED are novel additions not present in prior separation architectures, while the pseudo-labeling data engine (bootstrapping stems via an intermediate checkpoint plus multi-model filtering) represents a practical departure from purely synthetic mixing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical gap in audio AI: the absence of a single, controllable, open-domain separation system that matches or exceeds specialized tools across speech, music, and general sounds. The introduction of SAM AUDIO-BENCH (real in-the-wild clips with tripartite multimodal annotations) and SAM Audio Judge (reference-free, 0.88 Pearson correlation with human ratings vs. ~0.49 for CLAP or Gemini-2.5-pro) provides the field with evaluation infrastructure that was previously missing. Open questions remain around visual prompting's underperformance relative to text (attributed to noisier video supervision and inherent ambiguity of masks), handling of occluded or off-screen sound sources, and whether the pseudo-labeling pipeline can be further improved to reduce reliance on synthetic mixtures.
