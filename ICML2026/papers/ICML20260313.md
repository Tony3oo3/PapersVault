## Problem

Differential privacy and its variants face persistent challenges: parameters are difficult to interpret and communicate, real-world deployments often use large and potentially vacuous privacy budgets, and no non-vacuous guarantees exist for deterministic mechanisms such as invariant statistics used in official statistical dissemination. The paper addresses the absence of a systematic, semantics-first framework that can generate purpose-driven privacy definitions from explicit, testable assumptions while also recovering existing guarantees as special cases.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a data release mechanism (a Markov kernel mapping datasets to output distributions), a class of Receiver data-priors (probability distributions over the dataset space, which may depend on the data value), a set of proper scoring rules as privacy scores, and privacy parameters (κ for maximum allowable privacy loss and δ for failure probability). It produces a binary membership decision: whether the mechanism belongs to the privacy class defined by the guarantee. The assessment is robust, requiring the tail probability condition to hold uniformly over all datasets in the universe and all priors in the specified class, rather than at a single data point.

## Method

The paper constructs a two-player Stackelberg game between a Sender (data custodian) and a Receiver (adversary), grounded in Bayesian Persuasion but modified with information asymmetry, related utility functions, and worst-case robustness. The key insight is that the privacy value attained under Receiver's Bayes-optimal decision is itself a proper scoring rule, so privacy is measured as the relative change in this score between Receiver's prior and posterior beliefs. A mechanism is (S, Q_x, κ, δ)-PP if, with probability at least 1−δ under the mechanism's randomness, the relative privacy score does not exceed κ, uniformly over all datasets and all priors in the class. This framework recovers probabilistic DP (via the negative log-probability score and two-component discrete priors), Rényi DP and f-divergence privacy (via an expectation-based variant), and uniquely yields non-vacuous guarantees for deterministic mechanisms such as the empirical average and cell suppression.

## Results

The paper establishes that probabilistic differential privacy (ε,δ)-PDP is exactly equivalent to (L, H, ε, δ)-persuasive privacy (Proposition 6), where L is the negative log-probability score and H is the class of two-component neighbouring data-priors. For deterministic mechanisms, the empirical average M(x,·) = δ_x̄ satisfies (I, G_x^r, κ^r, 0)-PP with privacy loss κ^r = r₁ + log r₂ (Proposition 7), and cell suppression with threshold h ≥ r satisfies (L_n^r, C_n^{h,α}, −log α, 0)-PP (Proposition 8). Composition yields additive parameters (κ₁+κ₂, δ₁+δ₂), and the framework recovers Rényi DP via an expectation-based variant (PPE) with g_α(s) = e^{(α−1)s} (Proposition 12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical benchmarks but instead establishes theoretical equivalences and contrasts with existing privacy definitions. Persuasive privacy exactly recovers (ε,δ)-PDP (and hence pure ε-DP) via the negative log-probability score and two-component discrete priors, providing a new game-theoretic semantics for DP that was previously only available post-hoc. Rényi DP and f-divergence privacy are recovered under a minor modification (Assumption 4* replacing tail probability with expectation), showing the framework subsumes multiple DP flavours. Compared to QIF, persuasive privacy is more robust because it uses worst-case tail probabilities over x and Q rather than averaging over a joint distribution; compared to Pufferfish privacy, persuasive privacy conditions on each x individually rather than marginalising over x, and its guarantees for deterministic mechanisms rely on the output being uninformative under the scoring rule rather than on prior uncertainty making the extended mechanism stable. The framework also reveals that PDP's failure of the sender post-processing inequality is not a fundamental privacy weakness but a restriction on proof techniques, since receiver post-processing (the adversarial robustness property) always holds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Bayesian Persuasion (Kamenica & Gentzkow, 2011) by recasting the Sender-Receiver information design game as a two-player Stackelberg game where Sender is also a strategic player, and on the Grünwald-Dawid (2004) result connecting Bayes-optimal decisions to proper scoring rules. It departs from differential privacy's counterfactual-pair formulation by instead taking a worst-case, single-dataset perspective over a class of Receiver priors, and from quantitative information flow by using tail-probability (rather than expectation) conditions for robustness. The framework subsumes probabilistic DP, Rényi DP, and f-divergence privacy as special cases, while also connecting to statistical disclosure control's Sender/Receiver vocabulary and to pufferfish privacy's Bayesian modeling of data uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to assign non-vacuous privacy guarantees to deterministic mechanisms (e.g., the empirical average, cell suppression) is a significant conceptual advance, since DP and most of its variants fundamentally cannot certify such mechanisms—yet they are central to real-world statistical dissemination like the US Census. The semantics-first construction means each assumption (transparency, Bayes rationality, robustness) can be independently tested against application context, addressing a long-standing criticism that DP's parameters are hard to interpret. Open questions include how to systematically select appropriate scoring rules and prior classes for specific data contexts, whether utility-optimal mechanisms can be characterized within a given privacy class, and whether the framework's restrictions (e.g., the failure of sender post-processing) can be relaxed without losing the game-theoretic justification.
