## Problem

Current MLLMs and their 'think-with-image' variants (cropping, zoom-in) reason over isolated objects and ignore the structured spatial and semantic relationships among scene elements, leading to inefficient multi-turn target navigation and weak holistic understanding. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether explicit scene-graph representations can be internalized into MLLMs to enable fine-grained, relation-aware visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query (VQA, grounding, or captioning instruction) as input. Before inference, the image is pre-processed into a hierarchical scene graph whose nodes carry category, attribute descriptions, 2D bounding boxes, and depth ranges, and whose edges encode spatial, interaction, and semantic relations. The model outputs a text answer accompanied by a node-articulated chain-of-thought that explicitly tags each visited entity with <entity>, <bbox>, and <depth> markers, and for grounding tasks additionally emits a bounding-box coordinate list.

## Method

SaGe introduces an automated data engine that converts flat image-text pairs into hierarchical scene graphs via compositional node mining (Qwen2.5-VL-72B detects objects and sub-components at multiple scales), depth-aware node modeling (Depth Anything + SAM for per-entity depth ranges), and edge construction from node priors rather than direct MLLM relation inference. From these graphs, GPT-OSS-120B samples 120K training pairs spanning attribute-centric, spatial, multi-hop navigation, and captioning queries, each augmented with a node-articulated CoT. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on the 120K corpus to internalize graph-aligned reasoning, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a novel node-as-proxy reward scheme. The reward decomposes into a node-grounded reward (an MLLM judge verifies that each visited node's cropped visual region matches its textual description) and a node-relevance reward (an LLM judge assigns a 5-tier semantic relevance score to visited nodes relative to query-anchored entities), jointly encouraging trajectories that are both visually grounded and semantically coherent without requiring ambiguous edge-level supervision.

## Results

SaGe achieves 89.0% accuracy on VStarBench (both 3B and 7B variants), surpassing the proprietary GPT-4o (67.5%) and the much larger InternVL3-78B (76.4%). On HRBench-4K and HRBench-8K, SaGe-7B reaches 76.5% and 73.4% overall, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-32B (73.4% and 70.4%) and all tool-based fine-grained methods. On spatial reasoning, SaGe-7B scores 79.4% on CVBench-2D and 80.5% on CVBench-3D, with a notable +9.0% gain in the 3D Depth sub-metric over Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Across general vision tasks, SaGe-7B attains 62.3% on MMStar, 89.8% on RefCOCO, and 87.2% on ChartQA, consistently exceeding its Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaGe-7B (89.0%) decisively outperforms all open-source fine-grained perception baselines on VStarBench: DeepEyes (85.6%), Thyme (82.2%), LVR (81.7%), Pixel-Reasoner (80.6%), and Vision-R1 (79.6%), with margins of 3.4–9.4 points. It also surpasses the 32B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL (81.2%) and the 78B InternVL3 (76.4%) by 7.8 and 12.6 points respectively, and exceeds the proprietary GPT-4o (67.5%) by a substantial 21.5 points. On HRBench-4K, SaGe-7B leads all compared methods (76.5% vs. Thyme's 77.0% is the closest, but SaGe wins on the FSP sub-metric at 94.0 vs. 91.0). On spatial reasoning (CVBench-2D/3D), SaGe-7B achieves 79.4%/80.5%, beating Qwen2.5-VL-32B on the 2D Relation sub-metric (93.2 vs. 92.0) and the 3D Depth sub-metric (88.8 vs. 84.3), though it trails Qwen2.5-VL-32B on CVBench-3D overall (80.5 vs. 83.1). On general tasks, SaGe-7B edges out Qwen2.5-VL-7B on MMStar (+2.0), RefCOCO (+1.7), and ChartQA (+1.6), while the 3B variant already surpasses GPT-4o on VStarBench (89.0 vs. 67.5) with only 3B parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaGe departs from the dominant 'think-with-image' paradigm (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, LVR, Thyme) that relies on heuristic cropping and zoom-in operations over isolated objects, arguing that such approaches neglect structured relational context among scene elements. Instead, it pre-constructs hierarchical scene graphs (nodes with attributes, bounding boxes, and depth ranges; edges encoding spatial, interaction, and semantic relations) and reasons over them via node-articulated chain-of-thought. The training pipeline combines supervised fine-tuning on 120K graph-sampled data with GRPO-based reinforcement learning (inherited from DeepSeek-R1), introducing a novel node-as-proxy reward scheme that sidesteps the ambiguity of edge-level supervision by treating visited nodes as proxies for valid navigation paths. The base architecture is Qwen2.5-VL, and the scene graph construction leverages Depth Anything, SAM, and GPT-OSS-120B in an automated hybrid annotation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly structuring visual scenes as graphs and training MLLMs to reason over them yields substantial gains in fine-grained perception and spatial reasoning, with a 3B model surpassing GPT-4o on VStarBench and a 7B model outperforming its 72B teacher. The node-as-proxy reward design offers a practical recipe for RL in multimodal settings where verifiable edge-level signals are inherently ambiguous. However, the approach depends on the quality of automated scene graph construction (94.5% node / 92.9% edge accuracy), depth cues are coarse ranges rather than precise estimates, and evaluation is limited to eight benchmarks primarily focused on high-resolution perception. Open questions include scalability to very complex or dynamic scenes, whether the graph construction cost is prohibitive at deployment time, and whether the paradigm generalizes to video or 3D understanding.
