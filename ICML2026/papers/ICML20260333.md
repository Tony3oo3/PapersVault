## Problem

The Bistable Memory Recurrent Unit (BMRU) enables persistent memory and direct mapping to ultra-low power analog circuits (Schmitt triggers), but its full state replacement during updates completely blocks gradient flow, causing poor convergence and high initialization sensitivity. Meanwhile, state-of-the-art parallelizable RNNs (SSMs, gated RNNs) rely on contractive fading-memory dynamics that preclude persistent memory and are typically emulated in digital hardware, making them unsuitable for sub-microwatt analog deployment. The paper asks whether persistent memory can be made a reliable, trainable primitive that matches fading-memory RNNs on complex sequential tasks while retaining hardware co-design properties.

## Input / Output

The recurrent cell receives a sequence of m-dimensional vectors (m=256) produced by a nonlinear encoder that projects task-specific inputs (pixels, audio features, characters, or synthetic scalars) and concatenates sinusoidal positional encodings. The hidden state has dimension d (typically 4–256), which is independent of m; the cell projects internally from m to d and back. For the CMRU, states are quantized to integer multiples of a fixed scale α, while the αCMRU uses continuous states. Outputs are pooled (last or mean) and decoded to class labels, scalar regression targets, or next-character distributions depending on the task.

## Method

The key insight is that the BMRU's update mode (z_t=1) erases the previous state, yielding ∂h_t/∂h_{t-1}=0 and blocking backpropagation through time. The Cumulative Memory Recurrent Unit (CMRU) modifies the update to h_t = z_t ⊙ [S(ĥ_t)⊙α + ε·h_{t-1}] + (1−z_t)⊙h_{t-1}, where ε∈[0,1] retains a fraction of the previous state during updates, creating a temporal skip connection with ∂h_t/∂h_{t-1}=ε. At ε=1 the cell becomes a selective integrator with unit gradient in both retain and update modes; at 0<ε<1 it exhibits event-driven fading (memory decays only during update events, not with elapsed time). The αCMRU variant replaces fixed α with an input-dependent α_t for continuous-state expressivity. The cell admits a selective state-space representation where ε directly sets the eigenvalues of the state transition matrix during updates, unifying persistent and fading memory under one parameterization. Training uses surrogate gradients for the Heaviside and sign functions, and an ε-annealing schedule (1→0) produces models deployable on existing Schmitt trigger hardware at sub-microwatt power.

## Results

On Pathfinder (r=3, d=32), both CMRU variants achieve above 90% accuracy (αCMRU 92.00%, CMRU 90.20%) while LRU and minGRU remain at chance level (~50%). On ListOps at r=6, d=32, CMRU and αCMRU reach ~61% accuracy (near state-of-the-art) versus 39.4% for LRU and 59.7% for minGRU. On sequential MNIST with d=32, increasing ε from 0 to 1 lifts CMRU accuracy from ~30% (original BMRU) to 96%, with dramatically reduced cross-seed variability. On keyword spotting at minimal capacity (r=1, d=4), all architectures exceed 95% on the 11-class digit task, with CMRU within 1–2% of the best baselines while retaining analog-hardware compatibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LRU (Orvieto et al., 2023), the CMRU variants show a decisive advantage on tasks requiring discrete long-range state preservation: on ListOps at r=6, d=32, CMRU reaches 61.39% versus LRU's 39.37%, and on Pathfinder both CMRU variants exceed 90% while LRU sits at chance (50.14%). Conversely, LRU outperforms CMRU on smooth-integration tasks—IMDb (67.03% vs. 65.04% at r=6) and sCIFAR10 (62.34% vs. 58.81% at r=6)—consistent with its fading-memory dynamics being better suited to continuous temporal aggregation. Against minGRU (Feng et al., 2024), the pattern is similar: CMRU/αCMRU dominate on ListOps (61.4% vs. 59.7% at r=6, with much lower variance) and Pathfinder (90–92% vs. 49.3%), while minGRU edges ahead on IMDb and sCIFAR10 by 1–3 points. On the practical keyword-spotting benchmark at minimal capacity (r=1, d=4–16), all four architectures are within 1–2% of each other, with minGRU and LRU marginally ahead but CMRU retaining its unique analog-hardware compatibility at 100 nW. On Shakespeare character-level language modeling, hybrid minGRU+αCMRU achieves the lowest cross-entropy (1.441), slightly below standalone minGRU (1.453) and αCMRU (1.443), while LRU lags at 1.504, confirming that persistent and fading memory are complementary rather than competing primitives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bistable Memory Recurrent Unit (BMRU) by identifying gradient blocking during full-state-replacement updates as the core training bottleneck and proposing a cumulative update formulation (adding ε·h_{t-1}) that restores gradient flow while preserving persistent memory. It positions the resulting CMRU and αCMRU within the broader parallelizable RNN landscape alongside SSMs (Mamba, S4) and parallelizable gated RNNs (minGRU, LRU), showing via a selective state-space representation that the CMRU's hyperparameter ε directly controls eigenvalues of the state transition matrix, unifying persistent and fading memory regimes. The work also connects to hardware co-design literature on Schmitt-trigger-based analog RNNs and to recent theoretical results on negative eigenvalues for state-tracking tasks (Grazzi et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes persistent memory as a reliable, trainable primitive for parallelizable RNNs and demonstrates a clear complementarity: CMRU variants dominate on tasks requiring discrete long-range retention (Pathfinder, ListOps) while fading-memory cells retain an edge on smooth temporal integration, motivating hybrid architectures. The ultra-low power deployment path (sub-microwatt analog CMOS via ε-annealing or native charge-accumulator circuits) addresses a practical gap where digital TinyML still consumes milliwatts. Key open questions include whether the persistent-memory advantage persists at larger model scales, whether ε can be made learnable or automatically optimized, and whether the quantization constraint of the CMRU (vs. the αCMRU) fundamentally limits expressivity on more complex tasks.
