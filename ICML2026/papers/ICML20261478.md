## Problem

While prior work characterized the global optima of the DeepWalk objective or analyzed gradient descent on the computationally expensive softmax variant, the optimization trajectory of the practically used Skip-gram with Negative Sampling (SGNS) implementation remained unexplored. The stochastic SGNS updates have variance roughly n times larger than their expectation, making it unclear whether gradient descent from random initialization still recovers meaningful structure on non-trivial graph classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261478/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a graph's adjacency matrix, random walk parameters (walk length L, context window S), embedding dimension d, and a negative sampling parameter s_n, and produces two d-dimensional vectors per node (node and context embeddings). The analysis requires a small-norm random initialization (radius τ₀² = O(n^{-5/b-3/2})) and runs for M = O(log n) epochs of T = n/(6(1+s_n)²η) iterations each, with learning rate η = O(1/(n² log(1/δ))). The co-occurrence matrix C is derived from the graph's random walk transition matrix, and sampling of node pairs is equivalent to drawing from C/|C|.

## Method

The paper decomposes the SGNS update matrix H_{(i,j)} into a linear part L_{(i,j)} (a scalar multiple of the co-occurrence matrix minus a rank-one correction) and a nonlinear residual E_{(i,j)} involving the sigmoid function. For small-norm embeddings, the nonlinear term is negligible, reducing the update to a stochastic power iteration on the expectation L = E[L_{(i,j)}], which is a weighted sum of random-walk autocovariance matrices. A potential function tracking the fraction of embedding mass orthogonal to the top 2K eigenvectors of L is shown to decrease each epoch via a novel conditional martingale inequality that handles the n× variance gap between stochastic and expected updates. This yields (1-o(1)) alignment with the 2K-dimensional spectral subspace and, for SBMs with p > 1.1q, recovery of the hidden community structure even in one dimension.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical. Theorem 4.1 proves that after M = O(log n) epochs of length T = n/(6(1+s_n)²η), the fraction of embedding mass lying in the top 2K eigenspace of the expected linear update matrix L is at least 1 − C/n^(3/2) with high probability. Theorem 5.1 then shows community recovery: for distinct clusters i, j, the separation between intra-cluster means satisfies |μ_Ωi − μ_Ωj| ≥ 1/(60K²√n)·‖w‖, while the average intra-cluster deviation is at most c‖w‖²/n^(1+ρ). Experiments on SBM graphs (n=1200, K=2,3,4, p=0.6, q=0.1) confirm that 1-dimensional embeddings with s_n=1 recover ground-truth communities, while increasing s_n causes embeddings to collapse onto the uninformative all-ones direction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical benchmark comparisons against other embedding methods. The contribution is positioned against prior theoretical analyses rather than empirical baselines. Compared to Harker & Bhaskara (2024), which analyzed gradient descent on the softmax-based DeepWalk objective, this paper extends the trajectory analysis to the practically important SGNS variant with its high-variance stochastic updates. Compared to Karkada et al. (2026), which derived closed-form dynamics for a quartic Taylor approximation of Word2Vec under symmetric embeddings and vanishing step size, this paper analyzes the true SGNS objective with asymmetric embeddings and finite (though small) learning rates. Compared to Davison et al. (2024) and Barot et al. (2021), which characterized global optima of the DeepWalk objective on SBMs, this paper provides the first analysis of the SGD trajectory itself, showing that random small-norm initialization plus gradient descent actually reaches the informative subspace. The paper also notes that its recovery guarantees hold for all embedding dimensions (including d=1), whereas spectral methods typically require d > K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work analyzing DeepWalk/Node2vec embeddings on Stochastic Block Models from studying global optima (Barot et al. 2021, Davison et al. 2024, Zhang & Tang 2024) and softmax-based gradient descent trajectories (Harker & Bhaskara 2024) to the practically dominant Skip-gram with Negative Sampling (SGNS) variant. The key departure is handling the high-variance stochastic updates inherent to SGNS, where the per-step variance is roughly n times larger than the expected update, requiring new conditional martingale inequalities and a careful linearization of the nonlinear sigmoid-based update. The analysis draws on stochastic PCA techniques (Balsubramani et al. 2013, Jain et al. 2016, Shamir 2015) but must account for additional error terms from the sigmoid nonlinearity and asymmetric embeddings, distinguishing it from Karkada et al. (2026) who analyze a quartic Taylor approximation under symmetric-embedding and vanishing-step-size assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous analysis of the SGNS optimization trajectory on non-trivial graph classes, filling a gap between the well-understood global-optima guarantees and the actual training dynamics practitioners use. The finding that the negative sampling parameter s_n = 1 is critical for community recovery (because the uninformative all-ones eigenvector's eigenvalue vanishes exactly at s_n = 1) provides a concrete, theoretically grounded recommendation for practitioners. Open questions include extending guarantees to larger initializations (where the linear approximation breaks down) and to smaller spectral gaps p − q that would match the recovery thresholds of spectral methods. The result that even one-dimensional embeddings can recover cluster structure under these conditions is notable and suggests the method's expressiveness is more constrained by the optimization dynamics than by embedding dimensionality.
