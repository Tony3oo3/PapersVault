## Problem

Modern sub-quadratic sequence mixers (Mamba, GLA, GDN) rely on linear or affine hidden-state updates, limiting their expressivity relative to softmax attention and preventing them from solving inherently sequential state-tracking tasks at constant depth. Additionally, none of these models maintain explicit belief-state uncertainty, which would provide a principled, uncertainty-driven gating mechanism. The paper asks whether Kalman-filter-style nonlinear, uncertainty-aware updates can be made parallel-trainable while retaining linear-time complexity.

## Input / Output

The KLA layer consumes a D-dimensional token sequence of length T (after a 1D causal convolution and SiLU activation in the Mamba-style block) and produces a D-dimensional output per position via a query-conditioned readout of the posterior mean. Optionally, it also emits the posterior variance (Q²Λ⁻¹) as an uncertainty signal. The internal state is expanded to N×D dimensions (N=16 by default), with each entry running an independent scalar Kalman filter, so the model maintains a full Gaussian belief N(μ_t, Λ_t⁻¹) over the latent state at every timestep rather than a single point estimate.

## Method

KLA reframes sequence mixing as Bayesian filtering in a linear-Gaussian state-space model where tokens are noisy observations of a latent state evolving under an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck prior. The key technical insight is that in information form (precision and natural parameter), the Kalman precision recursion is a Möbius (fractional-linear) transformation representable as a 2×2 matrix, and the mean recursion is affine given the precision path; both compose associatively and are therefore computable via parallel prefix scans in O(T) work and O(log T) depth. This yields uncertainty-driven forget and input gates (precision ratios) that are history-dependent and nonlinear, going beyond the ad-hoc gates of Mamba/GLA/GDN. The architecture follows Mamba's fused-MLP block with parameter-efficient state expansion (outer-product key-value drive), and is trained with cross-entropy on the posterior-mean readout plus an optional negative log marginal likelihood loss to calibrate posterior variances.

## Results

At 180M parameters (10.9B tokens), standalone KLA achieves 42.78% average zero-shot accuracy across eight commonsense benchmarks, outperforming GPT (42.20%), Mamba (42.30%), and GDN (41.93%). The GPT+KLA hybrid (replacing only the final attention layer with KLA) reaches 44.95%, the strongest result at this scale. On the A5 permutation-composition state-tracking task, KLA solves it at constant depth (1–2 blocks) while linear SSM/attention baselines require depth growing with sequence length. On MAD-Lab synthetic tasks, KLA matches or surpasses all deterministic baselines, with the largest gains on noise-corrupted tasks (Noisy Recall, Fuzzy Recall, Selective Copy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KLA outperforms all three standalone sub-quadratic baselines (GPT, Mamba, GDN) on average accuracy at both 45M and 180M scales, with the gap largest at 45M (37.68% vs. 34.78% for GPT, 37.08% for Mamba, 36.25% for GDN) and narrowing at 180M (42.78% vs. 42.20/42.30/41.93%). KLA's per-task strengths include HellaSwag (34.00 at 180M vs. 32.20 for GPT), BoolQ (63.00 at 45M vs. 37.40 for GPT), and Arc-Easy (40.60 at 45M vs. 40.20 for GPT), while it underperforms on LAMBADA (clean copying), consistent with uncertainty-weighted updates favouring robust over verbatim recall. The GPT+KLA hybrid (replacing only the final attention layer) achieves the best overall average at both scales (38.33% at 45M, 44.95% at 180M), beating pure GPT by 2.75 points at 180M. On the A5 state-tracking benchmark, KLA is the only parallel-trainable architecture to solve the task at constant depth (1–2 blocks), whereas Mamba, GDN, GLA, and softmax attention all require depth growing with sequence length. On MAD-Lab synthetic tasks, KLA matches or surpasses Mamba, GDN, GLA, and mLSTM, with the most pronounced gains on selection-under-corruption tasks (Noisy Recall, Fuzzy Recall, Selective Copy), and probabilistic decoding (KLA+) provides further improvements on noise-corrupted tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

KLA reframes sequence modeling as Bayesian filtering, building on the Kalman filter's information-form recursions and the temporal parallelisation framework of Särkkä & García-Fernández (2021), but shows that the precision update is already a Möbius (fractional-linear) map composable via 2×2 matrix multiplication—eliminating the 5-tuple augmentation those earlier methods required. Architecturally it follows Mamba's fused-MLP block, state expansion, and 1D causal convolution scaffolding, while its gating mechanism departs fundamentally from Mamba/GLA/GDN: rather than ad-hoc input-dependent gates, KLA's forget and input gates emerge from Bayes' rule as precision ratios driven by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process-noise parameter. The paper positions KLA as a strict expressivity upgrade over linear/affine SSM and GLA updates (fractional-linear vs. linear) while retaining their O(T) training and O(1) inference complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KLA is the first stacked Bayesian-filtering primitive pretrained at the billion-token scale, and its Möbius structure yields a genuine expressivity advantage: it solves the A5 word problem at constant depth (1–2 layers) where linear SSMs and attention require depth growing with sequence length, placing it beyond TC⁰ while remaining scan-parallelisable. The explicit posterior uncertainty it produces opens avenues for hallucination detection, OOD detection, and epistemic prompt uncertainty that deterministic SSMs cannot offer. Key limitations include propagating only the posterior mean (not full covariance) between stacked blocks, restricting dynamics to diagonal LTI form, and evaluating at relatively small scales (45M/180M); extending to richer dynamics, full-posterior propagation, and larger pretraining budgets are natural next steps.
