## Problem

While low-rank factorization is widely adopted for fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA), no stable recipe exists for pretraining LLMs from scratch using exclusively low-rank factorized weights without auxiliary full-rank guidance. The fundamental obstacle is uncontrolled spectral norm growth in the composite weight update ΔW = ΔAB^T + AΔB^T + ΔAΔB^T, caused by the scaling invariance of the factorization, which triggers exploding activations and training divergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text tokens from the FineWeb corpus (batch size 512, sequence length 2048) and produces next-token predictions via standard cross-entropy loss. All non-embedding weight matrices in the LLaMA-style transformer are factorized as W = AB^T with rank r = 0.25n, yielding models with ~42% fewer parameters than their dense counterparts (e.g., 454M vs. 780M). The output is a compact, inference-efficient model that matches dense-model perplexity and downstream accuracy at equal FLOPs.

## Method

Spectron combines gradient orthogonalization (via 5 Newton-Schulz iterations on momentum, as in Muon) with adaptive spectral renormalization to bound the composite weight update spectral norm. The key mechanism is setting the local constraint radius ρ = η/(||A||_2 + ||B||_2 + 1) at each step, where ||A||_2 and ||B||_2 are estimated via a single power iteration, ensuring ||ΔW||_2 ≤ η regardless of current factor magnitudes. This adaptively shrinks factor updates as the spectral norms of A and B grow, directly preventing the unbounded singular value expansion that destabilizes naive low-rank training. The total computational overhead is sub-1% FLOPs (6k_ns·nm² for orthogonalization plus 2mn for power iteration), a 25× reduction versus the ~25% overhead of self-guided training. The paper further derives compute-optimal scaling laws (N_opt ∝ C^0.479, D_opt ∝ C^0.521) via IsoFLOP analysis across 47M–1.5B parameters, showing low-rank models favor smaller parameter counts trained on proportionally more tokens.

## Results

On Factorized Llama-454M, Spectron achieves 12.11 validation perplexity, outperforming self-guided training (13.70) by 11.6% and naive AdamW (14.57) by 16.9%. Across all three model scales (94M, 297M, 454M), Spectron consistently achieves the best perplexity and downstream accuracy: e.g., on HellaSwag it reaches 40.11% at 454M vs. 34.85% (self-guided) and 34.05% (AdamW). At equal FLOPs, the 454M factorized model matches the 780M dense model's validation loss, yielding a 42% parameter reduction with no performance loss. Compute-optimal scaling laws are derived with exponents N_opt ∝ C^0.479 and D_opt ∝ C^0.521, indicating low-rank models are smaller but more token-intensive than Chinchilla-optimal dense models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectron outperforms naive AdamW across all model scales and metrics, reducing perplexity by 6–17% (e.g., 12.11 vs. 14.57 at 454M) while also improving downstream accuracy by 1–6 points. Against self-guided training (Wei et al., 2024a), the prior state-of-the-art for stable low-rank pretraining, Spectron achieves 6–12% lower perplexity and 1–5 points higher HellaSwag accuracy, while requiring only <1% FLOP overhead versus self-guided's ~25% additional FLOPs for auxiliary dense weights. In Appendix G, Spectron beats LoRO (Mo et al., 2025) by 2.7 perplexity points (21.86 vs. 24.56) and is ~10% faster in wall-clock time on LLaMA-94M. Spectron also outperforms CoLA (Liu et al., 2025b) in perplexity (21.86 vs. 24.21) and wall-clock time (2,013s vs. 2,479s), and is shown to be complementary to CoLA's architectural changes (combined: 19.95 perplexity). When combined with ReLoRA (Lialin et al., 2024) and SST (Zhao et al., 2025), Spectron's spectral normalization provides additional 12.9% and 20.4% perplexity reductions respectively, confirming its optimizer-level contribution is orthogonal to architecture-level low-rank methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of low-rank factorized pretraining (Wei et al. 2024a, Huh et al. 2024) and gradient orthogonalization (Muon, Jordan et al. 2024). All prior methods for stable low-rank pretraining rely on auxiliary full-rank weights—either as dense guidance (self-guided training), frozen backbones (LoRA-style adapters), or hybrid architectures—because they could not resolve the spectral norm instability of the composite factor update. Spectron departs by identifying the instability as uncontrolled growth in the spectral norm of the product matrix ΔW (rather than individual factor norms, as Wei et al. observed) and solving it with a minimal adaptive scaling rule combined with Newton-Schulz orthogonalization, eliminating all full-rank auxiliary components. The compute-optimal scaling analysis extends Chinchilla's IsoFLOP framework to the low-rank regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 42% parameter reduction with matched dense performance at equal compute and sub-1% training overhead is a practically significant efficiency gain for both training memory and inference cost, and the derived scaling laws (N_opt ∝ C^0.479) suggest the benefit grows at larger compute budgets. However, all experiments are capped at 1.5B parameters—far below frontier scale—so whether the stability mechanism and scaling exponents hold at trillion-token regimes remains unverified. The wall-clock gap between dense and low-rank training (unfused matmul kernels) is an acknowledged practical limitation that custom kernels could address. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal architectures, whether communication-efficient distributed strategies can be designed for factorized parameters, and whether the near-Chinchilla exponents persist beyond the 47M–1.5B range studied.
