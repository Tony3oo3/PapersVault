## Problem

Critic-free RLVR methods for large reasoning models (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++) estimate the policy-gradient baseline using per-prompt empirical means, which are unbiased but high-variance when rollouts per prompt are few (typically ≤16). This paper identifies that treating each prompt's value-function estimate independently ignores cross-prompt structure, leaving variance on the table that could be reduced by borrowing strength across prompts in the batch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265725/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each RL step the method consumes a batch of n prompts, each with m generated responses and their scalar verifiable rewards (e.g., binary correctness). It produces a per-response shrinkage baseline b_i^j that is a convex combination of a leave-one-out prompt-level mean and a leave-one-out batch-level mean, weighted by an adaptively estimated coefficient λ̂_i computed from batch reward statistics. The baseline is a direct drop-in replacement for the existing per-prompt mean in any critic-free policy-gradient pipeline, requiring no extra hyperparameters, no additional network, and negligible computation (a few lines of code).

## Method

The core contribution is a James-Stein-inspired shrinkage baseline that provably reduces policy-gradient variance. The key insight is that value functions must be estimated simultaneously across all n prompts in a batch, so the per-prompt mean can be shrunk toward the global batch mean to trade a small amount of bias for a larger variance reduction, strictly lowering the batch-level mean squared error of the baseline. To preserve the unbiasedness of the policy gradient, the method uses a two-level leave-one-out construction: the local component is the prompt-level mean with the current response excluded, and the global component is the batch-level mean with the current prompt excluded, ensuring the baseline is independent of the held-out reward. The optimal shrinkage coefficient λ* = (n−1)/n · v₂/(s₂+v₂) is derived in closed form (Theorem 1) and estimated plug-in from batch statistics, where v₂ captures average per-prompt reward variance and s₂ captures dispersion of true value functions across prompts. The resulting baseline adapts automatically: with few rollouts it leans toward the global mean, with many rollouts it reverts to the per-prompt mean, and as training progresses toward deterministic policies it further reduces shrinkage.

## Results

On GSM8k with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, the JS shrinkage baseline achieves the highest final test accuracy across all rollout budgets: 55.22% (2 gen), 57.33% (4 gen), and 58.93% (8 gen), outperforming all competing baselines. On math reasoning (Qwen2.5-Math models, DAPO17k), JS yields 1.1%–4.3% Pass@1 gains over RLOO. On logic puzzles, JS achieves 4.8%–15.2% Pass@1 improvements, and on RLHF (Llama-3.2-3B), JS wins on all three Arena benchmarks (e.g., Arena-Hard-v2.0: 7.4% vs. 5.6% for RLOO). Gradient variance is reduced by 11.2%–67.1% depending on model and task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The JS shrinkage baseline is compared against five critic-free RLVR methods: RLOO, GRPO, ReMax, REINFORCE++, and DAPO. Against RLOO (the primary baseline), JS consistently wins across every task domain: 1.1%–4.3% on math reasoning, 4.8%–15.2% on logic puzzles, and positive gains on all three RLHF benchmarks (e.g., +32.1% relative on Arena-Hard-v2.0). Against GRPO and RLOO specifically, JS achieves the best accuracy at every rollout count in Table 2 (55.22/57.33/58.93% vs. RLOO's 54.49/56.34/58.31% and GRPO's 53.68/56.24/58.28%). Against REINFORCE++ and ReMax, JS also dominates across all settings, with REINFORCE++ performing best only at 2 rollouts among the non-JS methods. Against DAPO, JS is orthogonal and additive: applying JS on top of DAPO on Countdown yields 65.5% vs. DAPO alone at 59.2% (+6.3%), confirming the shrinkage baseline composes with other algorithmic improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical James-Stein shrinkage estimation and modern critic-free policy gradient methods for LLM post-training (GRPO, RLOO, ReMax, REINFORCE++). While prior RLVR methods estimate baselines either per-prompt (RLOO, GRPO) or globally across the batch (REINFORCE++), this work recognizes that the batch-level value function estimation is a multi-parameter problem amenable to shrinkage, borrowing strength across prompts to reduce overall MSE. The leave-one-out construction to preserve gradient unbiasedness follows the RLOO lineage, while the data-driven shrinkage coefficient (requiring no hyperparameters) departs from both the fixed per-prompt and fixed global baselines used in existing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shrinkage baseline is a zero-hyperparameter, negligible-compute drop-in replacement that provably reduces policy gradient variance, making it immediately adoptable in existing RLVR pipelines for large reasoning models. The adaptive behavior—shrinkage naturally increases when rollouts are scarce and decreases as training progresses toward determinism—suggests the method is robust across the full range of practical RLVR settings. Open questions include whether the variance reduction compounds with other stability techniques (e.g., DAPO's dynamic sampling), whether the theoretical guarantees tighten under non-i.i.d. prompt distributions, and whether the approach extends to dense-reward settings beyond verifiable binary correctness.
