## Problem

Dense LLM scaling couples model capacity to FLOPs linearly, while MoE decouples them but introduces large memory overhead, hardware efficiency challenges, and rapidly saturating sparsity benefits. All existing scaling axes—dense parameters, training data, and expert sparsity—encounter fundamental bottlenecks under data-constrained and compute-limited regimes. This paper asks whether token-indexed parameters can serve as a novel, orthogonal scaling dimension that expands model capacity without increasing backbone FLOPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tokenized text sequences and produces next-token predictions, identical to the backbone. The distinguishing detail is that each token ID serves a dual role: it indexes the standard input embedding table and simultaneously retrieves per-layer modulation vectors from auxiliary V×d embedding tables. These retrieved vectors are applied via element-wise Hadamard products (ReToken) or sparse top-K mixtures (MoRT), so the token ID drives both representation and modulation without altering the input/output interface.

## Method

ReToken augments each Transformer layer with a learnable V×d table; each token retrieves a vector, normalizes it onto a hypersphere, applies a learnable per-dimension scaler, and gates the MLP residual via a Hadamard product (p = 1 + s ⊙ Norm(E[x])). MoRT generalizes this by maintaining a pool of n_e modulator experts per layer and using a lightweight linear router on the RMSNorm'd hidden state to select a top-K sparse mixture, which is injected as an additive residual scaled by 1/√(2N_l) to control variance. The key architectural insight is that element-wise modulation adds O(d) FLOPs per token per layer—negligible compared to the backbone's Θ(d²) attention/FFN—while the retrieval-based design is decoupled from the GEMM critical path, enabling asynchronous prefetch overlap and CPU offloading. Theoretically, the paper shows MoRT rescales the Kaplan constant A by a factor (1+ηγ(ρ))⁻¹, yielding a scale-invariant multiplicative downward shift of the compute-optimal frontier and a 35% compute saving at iso-loss, with token-indexed parameters themselves following a predictable power-law scaling behavior.

## Results

On the 3.2B-A0.5B MoE backbone, MoRT improves the overall average accuracy from 26.75 to 32.34 (+5.59), with standout gains of +7.25 on ARC-C, +7.08 on MMLU, and +6.31 on GSM8K. On the 1.5B-A250M MoE, MoRT lifts the average from 18.87 to 22.78 (+3.91). On the 1.5B Dense-XL backbone, ReToken raises the average from 22.22 to 26.54 (+4.32), with +9.50 on TriviaQA and +4.55 on MMLU. IsoFLOPs analysis confirms a consistent 2.27% loss reduction across five compute budgets, equivalent to a 35% compute saving to reach any target loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is each backbone model against itself augmented with ReToken or MoRT (no external method baselines are included). ReToken consistently outperforms the backbone-only model across all four dense scales (190M–1.5B) and both MoE configurations, with the largest relative gain on the smallest model (Dense-S) and the largest absolute gain on Dense-XL (+4.32 avg). On MoE backbones, MoRT further outperforms ReToken by +1.64 (1.5B-A250M) and +3.10 (3.2B-A0.5B) in average accuracy, confirming that the sparse routing over a pool of modulators adds meaningful capacity beyond the static per-token gate. Gains are broad-based: all 14 downstream tasks improve with MoRT on the 3.2B-A0.5B backbone, with no regressions observed. The IsoFLOPs comparison (vanilla MoE vs. MoE+MoRT at matched FLOPs) shows MoRT achieves a 2.27% lower compute-optimal loss at every budget from 3×10¹⁸ to 3×10²⁰ FLOPs, translating to a 35% compute reduction for iso-performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kaplan scaling law framework by introducing token-indexed parameters as a new, orthogonal capacity dimension, building directly on the compute-optimal frontier formalism of Hoffmann et al. (2022) and the MoE routing machinery (top-K selection, sigmoid gating, auxiliary load-balancing loss) from Fedus et al. (2022). It departs from prior vocabulary-scaling work (Tao et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025) by scaling along the hidden feature dimension d rather than the vocabulary size V, and from large-memory-layer approaches (Product Key Memory, PEER, Ultra-Sparse Memory) by using lightweight element-wise Hadamard modulation instead of key-value retrieval or full expert FFN computation. The key conceptual departure is that substantial model capacity can be injected through per-token, per-dimension gating vectors with negligible FLOPs overhead, rather than through additional matrix multiplications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35% compute saving (scale-invariant across five orders of magnitude of FLOPs budgets) and the demonstrated power-law scaling of token-indexed parameters themselves position this as a potentially widely-adopted, plug-in technique for reducing training cost without architectural redesign. However, the backbone scale remains modest (up to 9.8B parameters), and the authors explicitly leave frontier-scale validation, post-training interaction, and compatibility with quantization/distillation as open questions. Whether the scale-invariant improvement holds at 100B+ parameter scales, and whether the Zipfian-deduplication and CPU-offloading strategies remain effective under production workloads, are the critical open questions for practical adoption.
