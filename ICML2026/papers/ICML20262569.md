## Problem

Non-stationary probabilistic time series forecasting requires capturing time-varying uncertainty, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: parametric methods (e.g., DeepAR) are efficient yet constrained by fixed likelihood families, while diffusion-based generative models (e.g., TMDM, NsDiff) are expressive but rely on rigid or heuristic priors that fail to adapt to dynamic distributional shifts. This paper bridges the gap by injecting a learned parametric prior into a generative push-forward framework, achieving both the efficiency of parametric estimation and the expressiveness of sample-based generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate time series x ∈ R^{H×C} (H=96 lookback steps, C variables) and produces K=100 Monte Carlo samples approximating the conditional predictive distribution over the future horizon y ∈ R^{L×C} (L=192 steps). The output is inherently sample-based rather than parametric, so the ensemble size at inference is freely adjustable to trade off estimation precision against computational cost. The backbone encoder and push-forward mapping are both lightweight MLPs (channel-independent, 2-layer with GeLU), keeping inference to a single forward pass per sample rather than an iterative denoising chain.

## Method

PPM operates in three stages: (1) a temporal encoder f_θ maps the input to sufficient statistics (μ, σ) of a diagonal Gaussian conditional prior p_θ(z|x); (2) K latent samples are drawn via the reparameterization trick and pushed forward through a lightweight 2-layer MLP g_φ to yield forecast samples, defining the predictive density as the push-forward measure q_φ(y|x) = (g_φ)_# p_θ(z|x); (3) training optimizes a hybrid objective combining KDE-based NLL (Gaussian kernel, bandwidth h=0.3, log-sum-exp with a floor ε for numerical stability) and a Mean MSE anchor on the sample mean. The key insight is that the structure-aware parametric prior encodes rich context-dependent information, substantially reducing the transport burden on g_φ compared to starting from generic noise. Theoretically, the paper proves a finite-(K,h) NLL error bound decomposing into smoothing bias O(h²/ε) and finite-K fluctuation O(1/(εh√K)), and establishes W₁-universality of the conditional push-forward family under standard MLP approximation assumptions.

## Results

PPM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all seven datasets on both probabilistic (CRPS, QICE) and point-forecasting (MSE, MAE) metrics. The headline improvements are up to 31.2% CRPS reduction and 44.3% QICE reduction compared to the second-best model (NsDiff), with the largest gains on high-variability datasets like Traffic (CRPS 0.252 vs. 0.367) and Electricity (CRPS 0.206 vs. 0.286). PPM also delivers a 2× to 100× inference speedup over leading diffusion-based baselines while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPM outperforms all seven baselines (DeepAR, TimeGrad, TimeDiff, D3VAE, DiffusionTS, TMDM, NsDiff) on every dataset and every metric (CRPS, QICE, MSE, MAE). The closest competitor is NsDiff, which is second-best on most datasets; PPM reduces CRPS by 31.2% on Traffic (0.252 vs. 0.367) and by 28% on Electricity (0.206 vs. 0.286), while QICE improvements are even more dramatic (e.g., 2.744 vs. 8.366 on Traffic, a 67% reduction). TMDM is competitive on the ETT sub-datasets (e.g., CRPS 0.322 on ETTm2) but degrades severely on high-dimensional, high-variability data like Electricity (CRPS 0.462) and Traffic (CRPS 0.562), where its fixed unit-variance prior fails to capture time-varying uncertainty. Diffusion-based methods (TimeGrad, TimeDiff, D3VAE, DiffusionTS) generally show poor QICE calibration (often >5%) and are 2×–100× slower at inference than PPM. On point-forecasting metrics, PPM also leads: e.g., MSE 0.524 on Traffic vs. 0.640 (NsDiff) and 0.968 (DeepAR), confirming that the hybrid NLL+MSE objective yields both calibrated distributions and accurate trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPM carves a middle path between two established families: parametric probabilistic forecasters (DeepAR, BetterDeepAR) that impose fixed likelihood forms, and iterative deep generative models (TimeGrad, TMDM, NsDiff) that learn flexible distributions via multi-step denoising. Conceptually, it repurposes the push-forward measure from optimal transport—already the unifying lens behind Normalizing Flows and Diffusion models—but replaces the expensive iterative transport with a single learnable mapping from a context-adaptive parametric prior. The hybrid KDE-NLL + Mean-MSE objective and the theoretical consistency/expressiveness analysis are new contributions that ground this single-pass design, while the plug-and-play backbone-agnostic architecture (MLP, iTransformer, ns_Transformer) positions PPM as a drop-in upgrade over existing point forecasters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×–100× inference speedup over diffusion-based competitors, combined with up to 31% CRPS and 44% QICE reductions, makes well-calibrated probabilistic forecasting substantially more accessible in latency-sensitive settings (traffic, weather, finance). The robustness to prior family (Gaussian, Uniform, Laplace, Student-t, Logistic, Gumbel all converge to similar performance) is a practical boon that reduces hyperparameter burden. Open questions remain: the KDE bandwidth h is dataset-sensitive and the current formulation models marginal per-coordinate densities rather than the full joint distribution, limiting capture of cross-time-step and cross-variable dependencies. Future work on adaptive/multi-bandwidth KDE, CRPS-based training objectives, and joint-distribution scoring (energy score, variogram score) are natural next steps.
