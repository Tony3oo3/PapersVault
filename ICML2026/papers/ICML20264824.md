## Problem

Existing inference-time guidance for diffusion models requires repeated score or gradient evaluations of the reward function, introducing bias, high computational overhead, or both, and fundamentally cannot sample exactly from the reward-tilted posterior without solving an intractable backward Kolmogorov equation. The paper asks whether an approximation-free inference-time scaling procedure can be designed that works even with suboptimal guidance and requires no derivative information from the reward.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264824/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SDXL), a guidance potential G, and a reward function r that may be a fully black-box neural network (e.g., DPO-tuned models, CLIP-based scorers) with no accessible gradients. It produces N resampled terminal samples from the reward-tilted posterior q(x) ∝ p_data(x)·r(x), evaluated on 256×256 ImageNet images for inverse problems and standard-resolution text-to-image outputs. The key constraint is that the reward need only be evaluable (not differentiable), and the guidance G only needs its gradient ∇_x G, which is already available from the diffusion model's score network.

## Method

URGE (Unbiased Resampling via Girsanov Estimation) lifts Sequential Monte Carlo from particle space to path space: it simulates N trajectories under the guided SDE, assigns each trajectory a multiplicative importance weight computed via the Girsanov Radon-Nikodym derivative between the base and guided path measures (involving only the Itô integral of ∇_x G against the Brownian increment and the reward differential dr), and periodically resamples trajectories proportional to these weights. No score function, Hessian, Laplacian, or PDE evaluation of the reward is required—only pointwise evaluations of r and the already-computed guidance gradient. The paper proves a marginalized equivalence: the backward conditional expectation of the Girsanov path weight exactly recovers the infinitesimal particle-level reweighting of FK-Corrector/AFDPS, confirming that both schemes produce the same approximation-free terminal law while URGE's path-space formulation enables derivative-free weight construction and greater flexibility in numerical discretization.

## Results

On the 30-dimensional Gaussian Mixture Model, URGE achieves the best MMD (0.06), SWD (0.62), Mean L2 error (3.20), and Covariance Frobenius error (181.31), outperforming all baselines including AFDPS+VCG (MMD 0.08, SWD 0.83). On ImageNet-256 inverse problems, URGE matches AFDPS-ODE on Gaussian Deblurring (PSNR 22.38 vs 22.57) and Box Inpainting (PSNR 23.27 vs 22.75) while requiring no high-order derivatives. On text-to-image generation with SD v1.5 and N=4 particles, URGE achieves CLIP-Score 0.2997, HPS 0.2927, ImageReward 0.9955, and GenEval 0.7800, surpassing FK-Steering (0.2901, 0.2849, 0.8397, 0.7200) and the base model (0.2730, 0.2619, 0.2144, 0.6400) on all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URGE consistently outperforms FK-Steering (Singhal et al., 2025) across all three evaluation settings: on the GMM benchmark it achieves MMD 0.06 vs 0.07 and SWD 0.62 vs 0.85; on text-to-image it gains +0.010 CLIP-Score, +0.008 HPS, and +0.156 ImageReward over FK-Steering with N=4 particles. Against AFDPS (Chen et al., 2025), URGE matches or slightly exceeds performance on inverse problems (e.g., PSNR 22.38 vs 22.43 on Gaussian Deblurring, 23.27 vs 23.13 on Box Inpainting) while being fully derivative-free, and clearly surpasses it on the GMM task (MMD 0.06 vs 0.10, SWD 0.62 vs 1.04). URGE also outperforms AFDPS+VCG (Ren et al., 2025) on the GMM benchmark (MMD 0.06 vs 0.08, SWD 0.62 vs 0.83, Mean 3.20 vs 4.13) without requiring explicit variance control. FK-Corrector (Skreta et al., 2025) is only comparable on inverse problems (PSNR 18.36 vs 22.38 on Gaussian Deblurring, where URGE is substantially better) and is inapplicable to text-to-image generation due to its dependence on reward derivatives, making URGE the only method that works across all three task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

URGE extends the line of SMC-based inference-time scaling for diffusion models initiated by FK-Corrector (Skreta et al., 2025) and AFDPS (Chen et al., 2025), which correct biased guidance by reweighting particles in state space using the infinitesimal generator. The key departure is lifting resampling from particle space to path space via a Girsanov change of measure, replacing derivative-heavy generator-based weights with a simple multiplicative Radon-Nikodym derivative along each trajectory. This path-space formulation is grounded in classical SMC theory (Del Moral et al., 2006; Doucet et al., 2000) and the Feynman-Kac framework, but applied here to the full diffusion path rather than to marginal states. The paper also positions itself against FK-Steering (Singhal et al., 2025) as a discrete-time baseline and against Doob's h-transform approaches as the theoretically exact but intractable target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The derivative-free property is practically significant: many real-world reward models (CLIP-based aesthetic scorers, DPO-tuned diffusion models, human preference networks) are black-box neural networks whose gradients with respect to the sampling trajectory are inaccessible or prohibitively expensive, making FK-Corrector and AFDPS inapplicable. The theoretical equivalence between path-space and particle-space SMC (Theorem 3.3) provides a clean unification and justifies the method's approximation-freeness. Open questions include the computational cost of maintaining N particles at scale, whether higher-order path discretizations (beyond Euler-Maruyama) yield meaningful gains, and whether the approach extends naturally to discrete diffusion or flow-based models where the Girsanov construction is less straightforward.
