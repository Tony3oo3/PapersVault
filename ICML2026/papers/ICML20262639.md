## Problem

Existing RL-based post-training for Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) relies on GRPO, which suffers from two fundamental bottlenecks: sparse sequence-level rewards that provide no token-level credit assignment signal, and prohibitive computational overhead from requiring multiple on-policy rollouts per sample over long video contexts. Meanwhile, supervised fine-tuning avoids sparsity but introduces off-policy distribution mismatch that causes compounding errors in long-horizon temporal reasoning. No prior method combines strict on-policy optimization with dense, step-wise supervision for TVG.

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (sampled at 2 FPS, capped at 768 frames with a maximum of 8,192 video tokens) paired with a natural language query describing an event. It autoregressively generates a sequence of reasoning tokens followed by a final temporal boundary expressed as start and end times in seconds, precise to two decimal places (e.g., '12.54 to 17.83'). During training, only 2,500 samples are selected via the TVDF curriculum from a pool of ~96K instances, and a single on-policy rollout per sample suffices—eliminating the 8-rollout requirement of GRPO.

## Method

Video-OPD performs strictly on-policy optimization: the student samples a trajectory from its current policy, and a fixed frontier teacher (GRPO-trained Qwen3-VL-32B) evaluates each student-generated token without generating any tokens itself. The per-token reward is defined as the negative log-probability gap r_t = −(log π_θ(a_t|s_t) − log π_tea(a_t|s_t)), which is the pointwise reverse KL contribution, yielding dense, step-specific supervision that replaces GRPO's single episode-level scalar. Policy parameters are updated via standard policy-gradient with these dense token-level rewards, requiring only one rollout per sample. On top of this, the TVDF curriculum iteratively validates teacher reliability using ground-truth IoU (excluding unreliable samples) and prioritizes trajectories with the largest aggregated teacher–student reverse KL divergence, steering training toward samples that are both teacher-reliable and maximally informative for the current student.

## Results

On three standard TVG benchmarks (Charades-TimeLens, ActivityNet-TimeLens, QVHighlights-TimeLens), Video-OPD [Qwen3-VL-8B] achieves R@0.7 of 32.4, 35.8, and 50.4 respectively, outperforming GRPO [Qwen3-VL-8B] (27.6, 32.1, 41.5) by 17.4%, 11.5%, and 21.4% on R@0.7. Video-OPD also surpasses off-policy distillation baselines (OP-RKD, OP-FKD) by 4.6–10.3 points on R@0.7 across datasets. The method converges in approximately 20% of GRPO's training time while achieving superior final performance, and generalizes to broader video understanding benchmarks (TempCompass, MVBench, Video-MME) where off-policy distillation causes noticeable degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-OPD consistently outperforms GRPO [Qwen3-VL-8B] on all nine R@threshold metrics across the three TVG benchmarks, with the largest gains on R@0.7 (e.g., 50.4 vs. 41.5 on QVHighlights, +21.4% relative). Against off-policy distillation baselines, Video-OPD surpasses both OP-RKD and OP-FKD on R@0.3 and R@0.7 across all datasets, though OP-FKD edges ahead on R@0.5 for Charades (47.3 vs. 45.8) and QVHighlights (58.9 vs. 60.3). Compared to the prior RL-based TVG method Time-R1-7B, Video-OPD achieves substantially higher scores (e.g., R@0.7 of 50.4 vs. 36.1 on QVHighlights). Among proprietary models, Video-OPD approaches Gemini-2.5-Flash on R@0.3 for ActivityNet (60.5 vs. 66.8) and QVHighlights (73.8 vs. 78.2) but remains below it on R@0.5 and R@0.7 for Charades and ActivityNet; it is well below Gemini-2.5-Pro across all metrics. Among open-source models, Video-OPD achieves the best performance on every benchmark, surpassing Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (base) by 9.3–20.9 points on R@0.7 and all other open-source 7B/8B models by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the on-policy distillation framework introduced by Lu & Lab (2025) to the temporal video grounding (TVG) domain, replacing the sparse sequence-level rewards of GRPO-based post-training (Time-R1, TVG-R1) with dense token-level reverse-KL supervision from a fixed teacher. It departs from prior RL-for-TVG work by eliminating the multi-rollout requirement entirely, and from off-policy distillation by maintaining strict on-policy trajectory sampling. The TVDF curriculum is a novel contribution that repurposes ground-truth temporal annotations as a validation oracle rather than a direct supervision target, a design not present in either the RL or distillation literature for TVG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Video-OPD achieves ~17% average improvement over GRPO while requiring only ~20% of the training compute, making high-quality TVG post-training accessible to resource-constrained groups. The finding that the student consistently surpasses the teacher after three OPD rounds suggests the framework is not capacity-limited by the teacher, opening the door to iterative self-improvement. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other long-horizon video reasoning tasks beyond TVG, how sensitive performance is to teacher quality at scale, and whether the TVDF curriculum's reliance on ground-truth IoU validation limits applicability to settings where annotations are unavailable or noisy.
