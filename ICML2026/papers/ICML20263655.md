## Problem

Existing statistical guarantees for flow-matching generative models scale with the ambient dimension d_x, failing to explain how these models benefit from the low-dimensional structure of real data. While Chen et al. (2023) established intrinsic-dimension rates for score-based diffusion under a linear latent subspace assumption, analogous guarantees for flow matching—and for higher-order flow matching incorporating acceleration terms—remained entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263655/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples {x_i} from a target distribution supported on a d₀-dimensional linear subspace of R^{d_x} (i.e., x = Uh with U ∈ R^{d_x×d₀} having orthonormal columns), along with fresh Gaussian noise X₀ ~ N(0, I_{d_x}) at training time. The transformer operates on the d₀+1 dimensional input (latent coordinate h̄ = U^T x plus time t), not the full ambient space, and outputs a structured velocity field u_θ(x,t) = U[α_t U^T x + β_t ŝ(U^T x, t)] + κ_t(I − UU^T)x whose tangent part is learned and whose normal part is closed-form. The theoretical output is a W₂( P̂_T, P_T ) bound on the distribution recovered by integrating the learned velocity over [t₀, T].

## Method

The key technical contribution is an explicit tangent/normal velocity decomposition (Theorem 3.1): under the linear latent subspace assumption, the optimal flow-matching velocity splits into a d₀-dimensional latent-transport term U[α_t h̄ + β_t ∇_{h̄} log p_t^h(h̄)] and a closed-form orthogonal contraction κ_t(I − UU^T)x. The latent term is derived via a generalized Tweedie's formula applied to the linear Gaussian channel h̄ = μ_t h + σ_t ε, concentrating all statistical complexity on the d₀-dimensional latent score. A transformer approximates only this latent score on R^{d₀}×[t₀,T], while the orthogonal component requires no learning. The decomposition extends verbatim to k-th order velocities (Theorem 3.2) by replacing κ_t with κ_{k,t} = σ_t^{(k)}/σ_t. This yields d₀-dependent rates for velocity approximation (Theorem 4.1), velocity estimation (Theorem 4.2), and W₂ distribution estimation (Theorem 4.3), all free of the ambient dimension d_x.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical rates for flow-matching transformers under the low-dimensional linear latent subspace assumption (d₀ ≪ dₓ). The velocity approximation error (Theorem 4.1) is O(B² N^{-β} (log N)^{d₀ + β/2 + 1}), the velocity estimation error (Theorem 4.2) is O(n^{-1/(16d+15)} (log n)^{max{d₀ + β/2 + 1, (8d+16)/3}}), and the 2-Wasserstein distribution estimation error (Theorem 4.3) is O(n^{-1/(32d+30)} (log n)^{½·max{d₀ + β/2 + 1, (8d+16)/3}}). Critically, all rates depend on the intrinsic dimension d₀ (via the patch dimension d) rather than the ambient dimension dₓ, demonstrating that flow-matching transformers escape the curse of dimensionality. The same rates extend to higher-order (K-th order) flow matching via Theorem 3.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baseline comparisons; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The key theoretical comparison is against existing full-ambient-space flow-matching analyses: Fukumizu et al. (2025) and Su et al. (2025) establish near-minimax-optimal Wasserstein convergence rates whose exponents scale with the ambient dimension dₓ, which deteriorate exponentially as dₓ grows. In contrast, this paper's rates depend only on d₀, providing the first d₀-dependent distribution-estimation guarantee for flow-matching transformers. The paper also positions its velocity decomposition (Theorem 3.1) as the flow-matching analogue of the score decomposition in Chen et al. (2023) for diffusion models, noting two structural advantages: it applies to any affine conditional flow (not just Gaussian noising paths), and the orthogonal component is a closed-form linear function requiring no learnable nonlinearity. Jiao et al. (2024) study latent flow-matching transformers but their encoder reconstruction error still scales with dₓ, a limitation this paper overcomes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the direct flow-matching analogue of Chen et al. (2023), who established a tangent-normal score decomposition for diffusion models under the same linear latent subspace assumption. While prior flow-matching statistical analyses (Benton et al., 2024; Fukumizu et al., 2025; Kunkel & Trabs, 2025) operate entirely in the full ambient space R^{dₓ} and yield rates scaling with dₓ, this work introduces a velocity decomposition that isolates the d₀-dimensional latent transport from a closed-form orthogonal contraction, thereby transferring the intrinsic-dimension rate structure from diffusion to flow matching. The higher-order extension (Theorem 3.2) builds on the K-order flow matching framework of Su et al. (2025), and the transformer approximation machinery draws on the universal approximation results of Hu et al. (2025a) and the density/score bounds of Fu et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing that flow-matching transformers escape the curse of dimensionality under a manifold assumption, closing a gap left by prior full-space analyses whose rates deteriorate exponentially in dₓ. The clean structural finding that the orthogonal velocity component is always a simple linear term (with no learnable nonlinearity) means all statistical complexity concentrates on a d₀-dimensional score, a property that extends uniformly to arbitrary order K. Key limitations include the restriction to linear latent subspaces (rather than general Riemannian manifolds) and the open question of whether increasing the flow-matching order K yields concrete sampling-efficiency or numerical-stability gains beyond the inherited d₀-dependent rates.
