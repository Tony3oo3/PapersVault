## Problem

Existing autoencoder-based latent CoT compression methods use textual CoT as the reconstruction target, forcing latent tokens to preserve surface-level linguistic features (word choice, syntax) rather than reasoning structure. This linguistic inductive bias causes latent representations to memorize how reasoning is expressed instead of how it is organized, leading to structural errors in reconstructed CoTs and limiting logical abstraction and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a question as text input, tokenized by the standard LLM tokenizer. The corresponding CoT is rendered into a 512×512 RGB image with bounding boxes for step segmentation and directed arrows for logical dependencies, then compressed into 8 discrete visual latent tokens via a 1D VQ-VAE (TiTok). At inference, the visual encoder is discarded entirely, so the LLM consumes only the 8 latent tokens plus the question tokens and outputs the final answer (ImgCoT) or a few key textual reasoning steps followed by the answer (L-ImgCoT), reducing token consumption from ~100–240 to 8–140.

## Method

ImgCoT replaces the textual CoT reconstruction target with a visual CoT reconstruction target, fundamentally shifting the inductive bias from linguistic to spatial. Textual CoT is rendered into images with explicit spatial cues (bounding boxes for step boundaries, directed arrows for inter-step dependencies), then compressed by a 1D VQ-VAE (TiTok) into 8 discrete latent tokens via nearest-neighbor codebook lookup with straight-through estimation. The LLM is fine-tuned autoregressively to predict latent tokens (MSE loss) and generate the answer (cross-entropy loss) conditioned on the question and latent tokens. The key insight is that spatial inductive bias encourages latent tokens to encode global reasoning layout (step dependencies, logical flow) rather than word-level details, yielding better reasoning structure preservation and stronger out-of-domain generalization. L-ImgCoT further augments the 8 visual latent tokens with a small set of textual reasoning steps whose average token log-likelihood falls below a corpus-derived threshold γ, selectively retaining domain-specific details the LLM cannot infer implicitly while still achieving ~30% token reduction over full CoT.

## Results

ImgCoT compresses full CoT into just 8 visual latent tokens while matching or exceeding Full-CoT accuracy across all three LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B, Llama3.2-3B) and four benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, GPQA, ProsQA). For example, on Qwen2.5-1.5B, ImgCoT achieves 19.5/38.7/41.8/100.0% on MATH/GSM/GPQA/ProsQA with 8 tokens, versus Full-CoT's 19.4/44.1/40.0/99.6% with 126–239 tokens. L-ImgCoT further outperforms Full-CoT on every benchmark (e.g., 45.2% vs 44.1% on GSM, 43.6% vs 40.0% on GPQA for Qwen2.5-1.5B) while reducing inference cost by ~30%. On out-of-domain generalization (Table 2), spatial inductive bias yields 1.3–3.6% absolute gains over linguistic inductive bias across four held-out math benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImgCoT decisively outperforms all four latent-reasoning baselines (Coconut, ICoT, CODI, CoLaR) across every model and benchmark. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, ImgCoT achieves 19.5% on MATH vs. CoLaR's 4.9%, ICoT's 12.5%, Coconut's 8.8%, and CODI's 4.3%—a 6–15 point margin over the strongest baseline. On GSM8K the gap is even wider: ImgCoT scores 38.7% versus CoLaR's 6.9% and ICoT's 22.3%. Against Full-CoT, ImgCoT matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (e.g., 24.1% vs 23.8% on MATH for Llama3.2-3B) while using only 8 tokens instead of 105–240, a 13–30× token reduction. L-ImgCoT consistently surpasses Full-CoT on all benchmarks (e.g., 45.2% vs 44.1% GSM, 43.6% vs 40.0% GPQA for Qwen2.5-1.5B) at roughly 30% lower token cost, making it the strongest overall method when inference latency is less constrained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the autoencoder-based CoT compression framework of Su et al. (Token Assorted, ICML 2025), which uses VQ-VAE to compress textual CoT into latent tokens that an LLM reasons over. The key conceptual departure is replacing the textual CoT reconstruction target with a visual CoT reconstruction target (rendering reasoning steps into images with spatial layout cues), thereby shifting the inductive bias from linguistic form to spatial structure. It also draws on the image tokenization literature (TiTok, VQ-VAE) as the compression backbone and positions itself against the broader latent CoT reasoning line (Coconut, ICoT, CODI, CoLaR), arguing that all prior text-based approaches share a fundamental limitation of encoding surface-level language rather than reasoning organization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that the *type* of inductive bias in the compression objective matters more than the amount of information retained—reframes how to think about latent reasoning representations and could guide future work beyond CoT compression. Practically, compressing 100+ token CoTs into 8 visual tokens while matching or exceeding full-CoT accuracy is a compelling efficiency win, and the L-ImgCoT hybrid offers a tunable quality-efficiency tradeoff. However, all experiments use small models (0.5B–3B) with low absolute accuracies, the visual latent tokens remain partially uninterpretable, and the rendering pipeline adds engineering complexity; whether the approach scales to frontier-scale LLMs or domains requiring precise symbolic manipulation (e.g., formal logic, code generation) remains an open question.
