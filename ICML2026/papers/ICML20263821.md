## Problem

Differential privacy has been widely applied to explicit-feedback matrix factorization, but implicit feedback (binary clicks, purchases) is far more prevalent in practice and poses a fundamentally harder privacy challenge: its one-class structure requires negative sampling, which creates complex interdependencies among training instances and inflates sensitivity to levels that make standard DP mechanisms prohibitively noisy. No prior work provides a differentially private implicit MF framework for cross-silo recommendation with rigorous pure ε-DP guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary user-item interaction matrices partitioned across K silos (either by user subsets or item subsets), a target privacy budget ε, and a latent dimension d. It outputs private item and user profile matrices (each a d-dimensional embedding per item/user) that are then used to score and rank items for recommendation. Unlike iterative DP-SGD approaches that require (ε,δ)-DP composition over many steps, DPIMF perturbs each per-item objective exactly once, yielding a pure ε-DP guarantee with no composition overhead, and the per-item updates are mutually independent and parallelizable.

## Method

DPIMF applies one-shot objective perturbation to a redesigned implicit MF loss, combining three mechanisms to suppress the noise scale. First, a complementary negative sampling scheme restricts the negative term to the unobserved user set Ũ_i with weight α₀, inducing coefficient cancellation so that the quadratic term decomposes as α₀G_U + (1−α₀)G_{U_i}, where the global aggregate G_U carries zero sensitivity and only the item-local term (scaled by 1−α₀) requires perturbation. Second, spectral truncation eigendecomposes the noise-perturbed quadratic coefficient and clamps non-positive eigenvalues to a small constant, ensuring the privatized objective remains bounded and convex without consuming additional privacy budget (post-processing). Third, a Poisson importance sampler retains each observed interaction with probability 1/w_ui and reweights by w_ui, where the per-user weights are solved via a convex program to achieve a prescribed amplified privacy loss ε′ at minimum expected sample size; this amplification is data-independent and provably tightens the privacy guarantee. At the optimal setting α₀=1, the quadratic and regularization sensitivities vanish entirely, leaving only the linear term to be noised, and the per-item cost reduces to the same O(d³) solve as non-private iALS.

## Results

On ML-10M, DPIMF-IS achieves HR@10 of 0.54 at ε=10 and 0.33 at ε=0.5, consistently outperforming all DP baselines across the full privacy budget range. Against DP-SGD methods, DPIMF-IS leads SGDMF by 0.18 in absolute HR@10 at ε=0.5 (0.33 vs. 0.15) and EFVAE+DP-Adam by 0.22 (0.33 vs. 0.11), with gaps narrowing to 0.06 and 0.11 respectively at ε=10. Against non-iterative DP recommenders, DPIMF-IS yields average HR improvements of 18.3% over DPLCF and 8.3% over LDPICF, with corresponding NDCG gains of 12.1% and 5.4%. The advantage is most pronounced under tight privacy (ε<5), validating the privacy amplification from importance sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPIMF-IS dominates all six baselines across all three datasets (ML-10M, YahooMusic, Amazon) and all tested privacy budgets. Against the non-private GT (iALS) baseline, DPIMF-IS approaches GT performance when ε>5 in the user-profile scenario, confirming that the privacy overhead becomes negligible at moderate budgets. Against DPMF (objective perturbation for explicit feedback), DPIMF consistently outperforms on all datasets, benefiting from the complementary loss design that reduces sensitivity specific to implicit feedback. Against the KNN-based input-perturbation methods DPLCF and LDPICF, DPIMF-IS achieves 18.3% and 8.3% average HR improvements respectively, with the gap widening under tighter privacy (ε<5) due to the privacy amplification from Poisson importance sampling. Against DP-SGD methods SGDMF and EFVAE+DP-Adam, DPIMF-IS leads by 0.18 and 0.22 absolute HR@10 at ε=0.5, with the advantage shrinking to 0.06 and 0.11 at ε=10; this pattern reflects the noise accumulation inherent to per-step gradient perturbation versus DPIMF's one-shot objective perturbation. The paper does not report any setting where DPIMF loses to a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the objective-perturbation DP-MF line (Hua et al. 2015; Berlioz et al. 2015) from explicit feedback to implicit feedback in cross-silo settings, a combination not previously addressed. It departs from DP-SGD-based federated recommenders (Li et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2024) by using a one-shot Laplace perturbation on the loss coefficients rather than per-step gradient noise, enabling pure ε-DP without composition overhead. The complementary negative sampling design is novel, exploiting the algebraic identity G_Ui + α₀·G_{Ũi} = α₀·G_U + (1−α₀)·G_Ui to induce coefficient-level cancellation that directly reduces sensitivity. The Poisson importance sampling for privacy amplification draws on the subsampling literature (Steinke 2025) but is the first application to DP-enabled matrix factorization with a formal per-pair amplification guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Implicit feedback dominates real-world recommender systems, yet prior DP methods were designed for explicit ratings and degrade severely on sparse binary data; DPIMF closes this gap with a principled, theoretically grounded solution. The finding that complementary loss at α₀=1 eliminates quadratic and regularization sensitivities entirely (making spectral truncation vacuous) is a clean structural result that simplifies implementation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to richer interaction signals (dwell time, multi-behavior), heterogeneous trust models beyond honest-but-curious, and scaling to industrial-scale datasets where the O(nd²) Gramian computation may become a bottleneck. The paper also acknowledges that DP guarantees do not eliminate all inference risks and that recommendation accuracy improvements may reinforce existing data biases.
