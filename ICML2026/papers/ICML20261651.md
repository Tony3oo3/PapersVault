## Problem

Prior universal approximation results for Transformers rely on feed-forward sub-layers, deep attention stacks, or strong data/architecture assumptions, leaving it unclear whether the attention mechanism alone is the essential source of expressiveness. This paper asks whether softmax self-attention, equipped only with linear transformations, can by itself approximate any continuous sequence-to-sequence function on a compact domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261651/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a token sequence X ∈ R^{d×n} (d-dimensional tokens of length n) and, for in-context learning, input-output pairs (x_i, y_i) with a shared parameter vector w. It produces an approximated sequence-to-sequence output f(X) ∈ R^{d×n} with L_p or L_∞ error, a per-token truncated linear model output Range_{[a,b]}(w_i^T x_i + t_i) placed at a designated row index via a mapping G, or a gradient-descent update w − η∇L(w). The approximation error decomposes into an interpolation term (b−a)/p and a finite-β softmax term max{|a|,|b|}·ε_0, both driven to zero by increasing the number of anchors p and the inverse temperature β.

## Method

The core technique is an interpolation-based selection scheme: the output range [a,b] is partitioned into p uniform anchors, these anchors are encoded into the value matrix V, and the key-query matrices are designed so that each column of Softmax(K^T Q) approximates a one-hot vector selecting the anchor closest to the target truncated linear value. This turns a single attention head into a piecewise-linear (generalized ReLU) approximator with O(1/n) precision, and H-head attention improves this to O(1/(nH)) by distributing anchors across heads. Stacking two multi-head attention layers (or one layer followed by a softmax) then yields a sequence-to-sequence universal approximator by composing d·n scalar approximators. The same interpolation mechanism extends to in-context learning, where multi-head softmax attention with skip connections simulates one step of gradient descent on any C^1 loss, improving on prior results that required ReLU-based attention or auxiliary FFNs.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: single-head attention approximates n generalized ReLUs (truncated linear models) with O(1/n) precision, and H-head attention improves this to O(1/(nH)) precision (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2). Two-layer multi-head attention suffices for sequence-to-sequence universal approximation of any continuous function on a compact domain (Theorem 3.3). Empirically, proof-of-concept experiments on synthetic data (d=10, n=50, hidden dim=32, 1000 samples, 50 epochs, A100 GPU) confirm that MSE scales as O(1/p) with interpolation points p, O(1/H) with heads H, and linearly with truncation interval |b−a|, matching the theoretical rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no head-to-head benchmark comparisons against other methods; the `compares_to` list is empty. Instead, the paper positions its contribution architecturally against prior universal-approximation results for Transformers: Yun et al. (2020a) and Kajitsuka & Sato (2024) require attention plus feed-forward sub-layers and deep stacks (O(n(1/δ)^{dn}/n!) layers), while this work shows that attention alone (two layers, no FFN) suffices. For in-context gradient descent, Bai et al. (2024) rely on ReLU-based attention and sometimes FFNs, whereas this paper proves standard softmax attention alone can perform ICGD (Theorem 4.1). The empirical section validates the theoretical approximation rates on synthetic tasks rather than comparing against competing architectures on a shared benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of Transformer universal approximation results (Yun et al. 2020a; Kajitsuka & Sato 2024; Takakura & Suzuki 2023; Hu et al. 2025), all of which rely on feed-forward sub-layers or deep attention stacks to achieve universality. The key conceptual departure is showing that the softmax attention mechanism alone, augmented only by linear transformations, suffices as a sequence-to-sequence universal approximator — a claim the authors frame as the first of its kind. The proof technique (interpolation-based anchor selection via near-argmax softmax) builds on the classical universal approximation of ReLU networks (Pinkus 1999; Lu et al. 2017) by showing attention can replicate generalized ReLUs (truncated linear functions), and extends Bai et al. (2024) by replacing their ReLU-attention ICGD construction with standard softmax attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a clean theoretical separation of concerns: it isolates the softmax attention mechanism as the essential expressive component of Transformers, suggesting that the often-criticized FFN blocks are not architecturally necessary for universality. However, the practical impact is tempered by the exponential head complexity H = O(d(1/δ)^{dn}) and the purely theoretical (expressiveness-only) nature of the guarantees — no optimization or generalization results are provided. The in-context learning extension (simulating gradient descent with softmax attention) is a meaningful step toward understanding ICL mechanisms, but the authors explicitly note it only simulates one step of gradient descent rather than establishing full in-context universal approximation. Open questions include whether the interpolation-selection technique can be made computationally tractable, whether the sequence-wise linear map in Theorem 3.1 can be replaced by standard token-wise operations without loss of generality, and whether these expressiveness results have any bearing on optimization dynamics.
