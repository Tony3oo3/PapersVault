## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods rely almost exclusively on global [CLS] token representations and semantic labels, overlooking the part-level visual cues that are critical for distinguishing closely related categories. While ViT patch tokens inherently encode part-level observations, they lack explicit supervisory signals to guide the model toward meaningful part correspondences, and prior part-aware approaches (e.g., SPTNet) do not account for variability in scale, pose, and occlusion across samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images along with a small labeled subset of known categories, and additionally requires offline-constructed part-level correspondence labels (generated in 5–180 minutes via PCA + k-means on frozen DINOv2/DINOv3 patch tokens). At inference time, PartCo adds zero latency: it uses the same backbone features and clustering pipeline as the host baseline. The output is a cluster assignment per unlabeled image, evaluated via Hungarian-matched clustering accuracy on 'All', 'Old', and 'New' categories.

## Method

PartCo constructs part-level correspondence labels in two steps: first, PCA on DINOv2 patch tokens yields an objectness mask that separates foreground from background; second, a further PCA on masked features produces a 3-D fine-grained representation, which is clustered via k-means to assign discrete part labels (1st-order for fine-grained datasets, 2nd-order for generic ones). During training, patch features are pooled by part label, projected through a small MLP head, and supervised by a part-level contrastive loss that pulls together features of the same part type and class while repelling different ones. This loss is added as a plug-and-play term to the existing GCD objective (L_total = L_gcd + L_pc), requiring no architectural modification to the host baseline. The framework is backbone-agnostic, validated with DINOv2, DINOv3, and CLIP, and integrates seamlessly into both parametric (SimGCD, SPTNet, FlipClass) and non-parametric (SelEx) GCD methods.

## Results

On the Semantic Shift Benchmark (SSB) with DINOv2, PartCo-SelEx achieves 85.5% average 'All' ACC, outperforming the SelEx baseline (83.1%) by +2.4% and surpassing most competing GCD methods including NCGCD-SelEx (84.0%), ConGCD-SelEx (82.6%), and PartGCD (75.6%). PartCo-SimGCD improves SimGCD by +9.8% (69.0% → 78.8% average 'All' ACC on SSB), while PartCo-SPTNet and PartCo-FlipClass gain +8.2% and +4.8% respectively. On generic datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100), PartCo-SelEx reaches 94.6% average 'All' ACC, edging out Hyp-SelEx (93.2%) and RLCD (94.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartCo-SelEx (85.5% avg 'All' ACC on SSB) outperforms NCGCD-SelEx (84.0%), ConGCD-SelEx (82.6%), PartGCD (75.6%), DebGCD (74.9%), MOS (75.4%), RLCD (76.9%), and SEAL (76.3%), but falls slightly short of Hyp-SelEx (86.0%) by 0.5 points. PartCo-SimGCD (78.8%) surpasses all non-SelEx-based methods including Hyp-SimGCD (78.8%, tied), PartGCD (75.6%), DebGCD (74.9%), MOS (75.4%), RLCD (76.9%), and APL (72.4%), representing a +9.8% gain over the SimGCD baseline (69.0%). On generic datasets, PartCo-SelEx (94.6% avg 'All') beats Hyp-SelEx (93.2%), RLCD (94.1%), FlipClass (93.9%), and DebGCD (94.1%), while PartCo-SimGCD (92.7%) edges out the SimGCD baseline (92.4%) by a smaller margin. The paper also demonstrates that PartCo is complementary to implicit part learning: combining PartCo with SPTNet yields 82.6% on CUB, exceeding either method alone (81.1% for PartCo-SimGCD, 76.3% for SPTNet). PartCo's gains are most pronounced on fine-grained SSB datasets (+9.8% for SimGCD) compared to generic datasets (+0.3% for SimGCD), consistent with the hypothesis that part-level cues are most discriminative when global appearance is similar across classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartCo positions itself within the GCD literature by departing from the dominant reliance on [CLS] token global representations. It builds directly on SimGCD (parametric self-distillation) and SelEx (non-parametric hierarchical clustering) as host frameworks, and connects to the part-level GCD line (SPTNet's spatial prompts, APL's part queries, PartGCD's GMM-based decomposition). The key conceptual departure is that PartCo pre-computes part-level correspondence labels offline from frozen DINOv2 patch tokens via PCA + k-means, then uses them as a training-only contrastive supervisory signal—avoiding end-to-end part learning and requiring no architectural modification to the host model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartCo's plug-and-play design (no inference cost, ~5% training overhead) makes it immediately applicable to any existing GCD pipeline, and the consistent gains across DINOv2, DINOv3, and CLIP backbones suggest the benefit stems from a general property of ViT patch tokens rather than a specific pretraining recipe. The finding that explicit part-level correspondence alone can match the performance of hyperbolic-embedding methods (HypCD) through a fundamentally different mechanism is a useful conceptual data point. Open questions include whether the PCA-based label construction generalizes to non-natural-image domains (medical, satellite), whether the 1st/2nd order choice can be automated, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D category discovery where part correspondence is more complex.
