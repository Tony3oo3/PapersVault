## Problem

Video Diffusion Transformers (VDiTs) are bottlenecked by the quadratic complexity of dense self-attention, which becomes prohibitive at high resolutions and long durations (e.g., >20,000 tokens for 480p/81-frame video). Existing sparse-attention accelerators (VORTA, SVG) rely on rigid spatial-vs-temporal head classification that discards tokens outside predefined scopes, causing severe information loss on complex hybrid motion where objects shift spatial coordinates across frames. The paper identifies that ~71% of attention heads fall in a 'mixed band' of spatio-temporal interactions that no binary categorization can capture.

## Input / Output

Video-SVD is a plug-and-play acceleration layer applied to existing VDiT backbones (HunyuanVideo at 720p/128 frames, Wan2.1 at 720p/81 frames); it takes the same text prompt as the original model and produces the same generated video, but replaces the internal O(N²d) attention computation with a three-stage basis-composition pipeline. The method requires a one-time offline calibration per checkpoint and resolution bucket (e.g., ~75h CPU for Wan2.1-1.3B) to extract the basis bank, after which inference is training-free. It reduces peak VRAM from O(N²) to O(N·N_tile) via tiled inference, enabling deployment on consumer GPUs (21.8 GB for Wan2.1-1.3B vs. 25.5 GB original).

## Method

Video-SVD decomposes dense attention into three stages: (1) Offline, it learns K=16 checkpoint-adaptive orthogonal bases via streaming Incremental SVD on large-scale QK^T matrices, capturing shared low-dimensional motifs (global context, spatiotemporal grids, motion trajectories) with strict orthogonality; (2) Online, it estimates basis composition weights by projecting a small m×m anchor-sampled sub-matrix (5% uniform sampling) onto the corresponding basis sub-regions, then reconstructs the full attention matrix as a weighted sum of K bases, reducing per-element cost from d=128 multiplications to K=16 scalar additions; (3) Online, a dual-stream residual module (content MLP conditioned on V, weights, and a pooled error descriptor; position MLP conditioned on RoPE frequencies) recovers fine-grained texture and positional information lost in the low-rank approximation. The key distinction from prior sparse methods is that Video-SVD preserves full global context through basis composition rather than pruning tokens, and the learned bases naturally encode hybrid spatio-temporal patterns that rigid spatial/temporal classification cannot represent.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (128 frames), Video-SVD achieves a 1.92× end-to-end speedup (570 s vs. 1095 s) with PSNR 29.5, SSIM 0.917, and LPIPS 0.126, while using only 40.5 GB memory (vs. 47.6 GB original). On Wan2.1-1.3B (81 frames), it reaches 1.75× speedup (268 s) with PSNR 27.5 and 21.8 GB memory; on Wan2.1-14B, it achieves 1.79× speedup (644 s) with PSNR 28.0 and 37.3 GB memory. In all cases, VBench scores remain within 0.1–0.6 points of the full-attention original (e.g., 82.32 vs. 82.35 on HunyuanVideo), confirming near-lossless quality under substantial acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-SVD dominates all four sparse-attention baselines (STA, PAB, VORTA, SVG) on every quality metric across all three model backbones. The gap is most pronounced in PSNR: on HunyuanVideo, Video-SVD scores 29.5 dB versus 22.7 (STA), 23.3 (PAB), 25.9 (VORTA), and 26.4 (SVG) — a 3–7 dB advantage that translates to visibly sharper output. On Wan2.1-1.3B, the PSNR margin is similarly large (27.5 vs. 22.1–25.2), and on Wan2.1-14B it reaches 28.0 vs. 24.8–27.1. In terms of speed, Video-SVD achieves the highest speedup in every setting (1.92×, 1.75×, 1.79×) and the lowest memory footprint (40.5, 21.8, 37.3 GB), whereas PAB actually exceeds the original model's memory (>80 GB on HunyuanVideo, >60 GB on Wan2.1-14B). VBench scores are within 0.1–0.6 points of the full-attention original for Video-SVD, while baselines drop 0.5–1.5 points, confirming that the orthogonal-basis-plus-residual design preserves temporal coherence better than rigid spatial/temporal pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-SVD departs from the dominant sparse-attention paradigm (VORTA, SVG, SVG2, Radial Attention) that hard-assigns each attention head to a rigid 'spatial' or 'temporal' bucket and prunes tokens outside that scope. Instead, it draws on the low-rank approximation lineage (Linformer, Nyströmformer, Linear Attention) but replaces generic static projections with checkpoint-adaptive orthogonal bases learned via streaming Incremental SVD from actual video attention matrices, plus a dual-stream residual module for high-frequency recovery. The key conceptual departure is the observation that ~71% of heads occupy a hybrid spatio-temporal 'grey zone' that neither spatial nor temporal sparsity can capture, motivating basis composition over token pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 1.75–1.92× end-to-end speedup with near-original PSNR (29.5/28.0 dB) and a 15–20% memory reduction that enables consumer-GPU deployment of 14B-parameter video models. The finding that the spatial/temporal dichotomy used by VORTA and SVG is fundamentally misaligned with real motion patterns (71.4% of heads in the mixed band) challenges a working assumption in the sparse-attention-for-video literature. Open questions include whether the one-time per-checkpoint calibration remains practical as video models evolve rapidly, how the method scales to substantially longer sequences (beyond 128 frames), and whether the low-rank smoothing limitation in high-frequency synthetic domains (pixel/voxel art) can be addressed without sacrificing the speedup.
