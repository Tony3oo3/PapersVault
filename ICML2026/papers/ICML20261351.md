## Problem

Cross-domain class-incremental learning requires a model to acquire new classes across shifting domains while retaining prior knowledge in a shared parameter space, but existing methods entangle what to update with how to update, leading to unstable adaptation and severe forgetting. Most prior approaches progressively stack task-specific adapters per domain, which increases model complexity, complicates optimization as the task sequence grows, and can aggravate forgetting through accumulation of domain-specific parameters.

## Input / Output

At each incremental session, the model receives an image-caption pair (RGB image and a class-related text descriptor) from the current domain, processed through frozen EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14 and BLIP2 Q-Former encoders to extract cross-modal semantic features. The output is a class label prediction over the accumulated set of classes seen so far across all domains. The model operates under a strict constraint that data from previous domains is unavailable at later sessions, and class labels are disjoint across domains.

## Method

PMDO disentangles cross-domain adaptation into two coupled mechanisms: a Domain-Aware Knowledge Decoupler (DKD) that determines what to update, and a Stability-Aware Trajectory Regulator (STR) that determines how to update. DKD uses a lightweight MLP to produce per-neuron domain-relevance scores from cross-modal features, fuses them with a persistent learnable soft mask via Top-k selection, and gates gradient flow at the neuron level so only a domain-relevant subset of shared parameters (controlled by a budget ratio ρ) is updated. STR parameterizes LoRA factors (A, B) and constrains their update directions by projecting gradients onto a sample-adaptive mixture of K orthonormal direction subspaces, where a lightweight router assigns soft expert weights based on visual and textual features. The total training objective combines cross-entropy loss with a budget regularizer (preventing mask collapse) and an entropy-based sharpness regularizer (encouraging sparse expert routing), jointly optimizing the incremental model, decoupler, and regulator.

## Results

On the CD-CIL benchmark (Order-I, 9 domains, 1,281 classes), PMDO achieves an average Top-1 accuracy of 87.6%, outperforming the strongest baseline GMM (85.7%) by 1.9 points. Under the more challenging Order-II (random permutation), PMDO reaches 85.3% average accuracy versus GMM's 84.1%. The metric reported is the mean of per-session Top-1 accuracy (Avg = Σ Acc_t / T), and PMDO leads on every individual domain in both task orders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMDO outperforms all eight baselines on the average Top-1 accuracy metric in both task orders. Against the strongest baseline GMM (Cao et al., 2024), PMDO gains +1.9 points on Order-I (87.6 vs. 85.7) and +1.2 points on Order-II (85.3 vs. 84.1). The next-closest competitors are GIFT (Wu et al., 2025) at 82.6% and LEBA (Gu et al., 2026) at 81.3% on Order-I, trailing PMDO by 5.0 and 6.3 points respectively. Among earlier methods, MoE-Adapters (Yu et al., 2024) achieves 77.9%, ZSCL (Zheng et al., 2023) reaches 79.2%, WiSE-FT (Ding et al., 2022) scores 79.1%, iCaRL (Rebuffi et al., 2017) attains 79.3%, and LwF (Li & Hoiem, 2017) records 79.0%—all substantially below PMDO. Notably, PMDO's advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained domains (Aircraft: +2.1 over GMM; DTD: +1.8) and is consistent across all nine domains, suggesting the decoupled update-scope and trajectory-regulation design generalizes across heterogeneous visual categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMDO departs from the dominant adapter-stacking paradigm in cross-domain class-incremental learning (MoE-Adapters, LEBA, GIFT), which progressively adds task-specific components and grows model complexity with each new domain. Instead, it operates within a fixed shared parameter space, borrowing LoRA as the adaptation substrate and repurposing mixture-of-experts routing not to select among separate adapters but to project gradients onto sample-adaptive directional subspaces. The conceptual framing—decoupling 'what to update' (parameter masking inspired by hippocampal selective consolidation) from 'how to update' (subspace-constrained gradient projection)—is a clean reorganization of ideas from continual learning regularization and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but the individual components (soft masks, Top-k gating, LoRA, expert routing) are all established techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of current CD-CIL methods: adapter-stacking approaches accumulate parameters and computational overhead as the domain sequence grows, making long-horizon incremental learning increasingly expensive. PMDO's fixed-parameter design with selective masking and gradient projection offers a more scalable alternative, though the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (GMM) are modest (~1–2 points average across three domain orders). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the BLIP2/EVA-CLIP backbone, how it behaves with more than 9 domains or larger class counts, and whether the expert bank (K=36 orthonormal subspaces) can be learned more efficiently or pruned. The method's reliance on frozen cross-modal encoders for routing also limits its applicability to settings where such encoders are unavailable.
