## Problem

Existing positional encoding methods for Spiking Neural Networks are largely inherited from ANNs and disrupt the spike-driven computation paradigm that gives Spiking Transformers their energy efficiency. Specifically, prior SNN positional encodings either fail to represent relative positional information between tokens or are incompatible with the linear-complexity self-attention mechanism, leaving Spiking Transformers without a principled way to inject order information into sequential tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes tokenized text sequences (max length 128 for short-text tasks, 1024 for long-text tasks) and produces binary spike activations (0/1 vectors) at each time step. Rather than adding positional vectors to input embeddings, SPE encodes position directly into the firing thresholds of neurons in the query and key branches, so positional information is carried implicitly in the temporal spike patterns rather than in continuous-valued representations. The output remains standard spike-driven activations compatible with downstream classification or regression heads.

## Method

SPE introduces the PE-LIF neuron, a variant of the soft-reset LIF neuron whose threshold θ_{i,j} is modulated by a sinusoidal function of token position i and channel index j (adapted from Vaswani's positional encoding formula). PE-LIF neurons replace standard LIF neurons exclusively in the Q and K branches of Spiking Self-Attention, so that position-dependent thresholds shape which spikes fire and when, encoding relative positional information into the spike trains without adding trainable parameters or breaking the O(N) linear attention property. A Membrane Potential Regularization Surrogate Gradient (MPR-SG) is applied during backpropagation to enforce the condition E[û]=E[s], which the authors prove is necessary for the attention score E[q_m^T k_n] to contain a function g(m−n) of relative position. Theoretical analysis further shows g(m−n) exhibits long-term decay via a Riemann-Lebesgue argument, confirming that distant tokens receive diminishing positional association.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (8 tasks), Spikingformer with SPE achieves an average score of 72.6, surpassing the baseline Spikingformer (66.8) by 5.8 points and outperforming all SNN and QNN baselines. The most dramatic gains appear on fine-grained semantic tasks: +8.9 points on CoLA (24.4→33.3) and +24.8 points on STS-B (54.5→79.3). SPE also reduces energy consumption by 27.2% (6.76→4.92 mJ) while introducing zero additional trainable parameters. On six text classification benchmarks, SPE reaches 91.05% average accuracy on long-text tasks (max length 1024), exceeding the strongest SNN baseline Log-PE by 7.43 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPE (Spikingformer w/SPE) outperforms all SNN baselines: it beats Spikingformer without PE by 5.8 points on GLUE average (72.6 vs. 66.8), SpikeBERT by 12.9 points (72.6 vs. 59.7), and LIF-BERT by 17.7 points (72.6 vs. 54.9). Against QNN baselines, SPE surpasses BiT (71.0), BiPFT (70.8), and BiBERT (63.2) on average, though it trails BERT_3L (75.9) and BERT_base (83.2) as expected given the spike-driven constraint. On the six text classification benchmarks (Table 1), SPE outperforms CPG-PE by 3.32 points on short-text average (78.68 vs. 75.36) and by 8.96 points on long-text average (91.05 vs. 82.09), and beats Log-PE by 2.22 and 7.43 points respectively. Notably, SPE achieves these gains with zero additional parameters (109.8M) while CPG-PE requires 0.6M extra, and it reduces energy by 27.2% relative to the Spikingformer baseline. SPE also exceeds BERT_3L on QQP (85.8 vs. 85.2), QNLI (82.9 vs. 85.8 — actually below), and MRPC (83.5 vs. 86.4 — below), but surpasses it on CoLA (33.3 vs. 31.7) and STS-B (79.3 vs. 85.7 — below), showing competitive but not uniformly superior performance to the 3-layer ANN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spikformer/Spikingformer line of work on Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) by addressing a gap the authors identify: positional encoding has been largely unexplored in Spiking Transformers, with prior SNN methods (CPG-PE, Gray-PE, Log-PE) either failing to capture relative positions or breaking the linear-attention property. Conceptually, SPE departs from the ANN tradition of injecting position into attention scores (à la RoPE or Shaw et al.) by instead encoding position into the firing thresholds of LIF neurons, letting spike dynamics propagate positional information. The sinusoidal threshold formula directly adapts Vaswani et al.'s positional encoding, but repurposes it as a neuron-level parameter rather than an input-level addition, and the MPR-SG surrogate gradient is a novel training mechanism with no direct ANN analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPE offers a parameter-free, theoretically grounded way to give Spiking Transformers relative positional awareness while preserving their linear-complexity attention and spike-driven computation—two properties that prior relative-PE methods sacrifice. The simultaneous 27% energy reduction and accuracy gains (up to 24.8 points on STS-B) suggest that better positional inductive bias can make spiking networks both more capable and more efficient, which is important for neuromorphic hardware deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal Spiking Transformers, whether the approximate satisfaction of E[û]=E[s] via MPR-SG holds at scale, and whether the threshold-based encoding can be mapped efficiently onto existing neuromorphic chips like Loihi or Tianjic.
