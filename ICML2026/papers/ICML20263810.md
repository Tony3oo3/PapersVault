## Problem

Unpaired domain translation requires balancing faithfulness (preserving source content) and realism (matching the target distribution), but existing methods force a trade-off: diffusion-based approaches achieve high realism yet lack explicit faithfulness control and require multi-step sampling, while optimal transport methods provide one-step inference and explicit cost regularization but depend on unstable adversarial training that degrades quality under data scarcity. No prior method combines one-step inference, explicit faithfulness control, and diffusion-level realism without adversarial objectives.

## Input / Output

For pixel-space image translation, RDMD takes a single source image (64×64, 128×128, or 256×256 RGB) and directly outputs the translated image in one forward pass (NFE=1), with no random noise input. For latent-space ImageNet translation, it consumes a latent code plus a target class label from a class-conditional LDM and produces a translated latent code. For text detoxification, it maps a toxic sentence to a neutral paraphrase. The generator architecture is shared across the target score, fake score, and generator networks, all initialized from the pre-trained target diffusion model's denoiser at a fixed noise level σ̂.

## Method

RDMD replaces the adversarial distribution-matching loss in OT-based translation with a diffusion-based distribution matching distillation (DMD) objective, while retaining an explicit transport cost regularizer (L2 or LPIPS) weighted by λ. Training proceeds via coordinate descent: the generator G_θ(x) is updated using the score difference between a 'fake' diffusion model (trained by denoising score matching on generator outputs) and the pre-trained target diffusion model, plus the gradient of the transport cost; the fake score is updated by standard denoising on the generator's outputs. The generator is initialized from the target diffusion model's denoiser at a fixed σ̂, yielding meaningful translations from the first iteration and convergence in hundreds of GPU-minutes. Theoretically, the paper proves that as λ→0, the optimal solution of the regularized objective converges in probability to the Monge optimal transport map under quadratic cost, establishing RDMD as a soft-constrained OT solver that avoids adversarial training entirely.

## Results

On 64×64 AFHQv2 Cat↔Wild and 128×128 CelebA Male↔Female, RDMD achieves the best LPIPS (faithfulness) across all four translation directions with only 1 NFE: 0.352 (Wild→Cat), 0.374 (Cat→Wild), 0.236 (Male→Female), and 0.237 (Female→Male), while maintaining competitive FID (17.31, 14.43, 9.30, 6.68 respectively). On 256×256 AFHQv2 Cat→Dog, RDMD achieves the best FID (44.32) among all baselines. In the data-efficiency experiment (5k samples), RDMD achieves FID 20.99 / LPIPS 0.238, substantially outperforming DIOTM (31.34/0.352) and ASBM (43.97/0.349). On text detoxification (ParaDetox), RDMD achieves J-score 0.537 with 1 NFE, approaching the paired Cosmos model (0.554) while requiring <1% of the inference steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDMD decisively outperforms both OT-based baselines (DIOTM and ASBM) on all four image translation tasks in both FID and LPIPS, confirming that replacing adversarial training with diffusion-based distribution matching yields superior realism and faithfulness. Against one-sided diffusion methods (ILVR, SDEdit, EGSDE), RDMD achieves substantially better LPIPS (e.g., 0.352 vs. 0.483–0.529 on Wild→Cat) at the cost of slightly higher FID, and requires only 1 NFE versus 35. Two-sided diffusion methods (DDIB, CycleDiff) achieve lower FID (e.g., 5.39 vs. 9.30 on Male→Female) but at 70–140 NFE and with worse LPIPS (0.327–0.342 vs. 0.236), and they require pre-trained source-domain diffusion models. On the 256×256 Cat→Dog benchmark, RDMD achieves the best FID (44.32) among all GAN-based (CycleGAN 85.9, StarGANv2 54.88, CUT 76.21) and diffusion-based baselines. In the 5k-sample data-efficiency test, RDMD (FID 20.99) dramatically outperforms DIOTM (31.34) and ASBM (43.97), which produce distorted images. On text detoxification, RDMD (J-score 0.537, 1 NFE) approaches the paired Cosmos model (0.554, 200 NFE) while far exceeding unpaired diffusion baselines DDIB (0.244) and CycleDiff (0.326).

## Relation to Prior Work

RDMD sits at the intersection of two established paradigms for unpaired translation: Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), which trains a one-step generator by matching perturbed distributions via a pre-trained diffusion model, and Optimal Transport (OT) methods, which formalize translation as finding the minimum-cost mapping between source and target distributions. The key conceptual departure is replacing the adversarial discriminator used in OT methods (DIOTM, ASBM, NOT) with the diffusion-based distribution matching loss from DMD, while retaining the explicit transport cost regularization that gives OT methods their faithfulness guarantee. The theoretical analysis draws on entropy-transport problem frameworks (Liero et al., 2018) to prove convergence to the Monge map, and the practical setup leverages pre-trained EDM/LDM backbones for strong initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDMD offers a practically attractive combination: one-step inference with explicit, tunable faithfulness control (via λ) and diffusion-level generation quality, without the training instability and data hunger of adversarial OT methods. The demonstrated data efficiency advantage (maintaining quality at 5k samples where DIOTM and ASBM collapse) addresses a real deployment concern. Open questions include whether few-step variants can close the FID gap to multi-step methods like DDIB, whether λ can be varied at inference time without retraining, and whether the asymptotic convergence guarantee can be tightened to finite-λ bounds. The method's generality across pixel space, latent space, and text modalities suggests broad applicability, though the FID ceiling relative to multi-step diffusion baselines remains a limitation for quality-critical applications.
