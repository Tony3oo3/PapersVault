## Problem

Machine unlearning for quantized neural networks (QNNs) is underexplored, and the first dedicated framework (Q-MUL) suffers from two fundamental limitations: it trains models to memorize incorrect labels (conflating forgetting with misremembering and introducing class-specific biases), and it uses scalar gradient reweighting that cannot resolve directional conflicts between forgetting and retain gradients in the constrained discrete parameter space of quantized models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained quantized model (e.g., ResNet-18 with 4-bit weights and activations, or MobileNetV2 with 2-bit weights and full-precision activations), a forgotten dataset D_f, and a retained dataset D_r, and produces an unlearned quantized model whose parameters remain in the discrete quantized space. Unlearning is formulated as a quantization-aware training process where gradients are computed through the Straight-Through Estimator, and the method is evaluated via Forget Accuracy, Retain Accuracy, Test Accuracy, Membership Inference Attack success rate, and Average Gap against a full-retrain gold standard.

## Method

OEU introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Entropy-Guided Unlearning (EGU) replaces the 'learn wrong labels' objective with negative entropy maximization on forgotten samples, driving the output distribution toward the uniform 1/K state (maximum uncertainty) and providing a provably unbiased forgetting direction with zero KL divergence from the ideal forgotten state. Second, Gradient Orthogonal Projection (GOP) projects the forgetting gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the retain gradient at each layer, mathematically guaranteeing under first-order approximation that unlearning updates do not increase the retain loss while preserving the descent direction for forgetting. The full pipeline computes separate forget and retain gradients per iteration, applies layer-wise normalized orthogonal projection (with a tunable strength α), and updates parameters with the combined projected gradient, all within the QAT framework using STE for backpropagation through quantization nodes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 4-bit ResNet-18 under random data forgetting, OEU achieves an Average Gap (AG) of 0.58% at 10% forgetting ratio, 1.31% at 30%, and 1.37% at 50%, consistently outperforming the prior state-of-the-art Q-MUL (1.30%, 1.68%, 1.91% respectively). On the more challenging CIFAR-100, OEU's advantage widens: at 30% forgetting, OEU achieves AG of 1.98% versus Q-MUL's 3.90% (49% reduction), and at 50% forgetting, OEU reaches 3.67% versus Q-MUL's 7.06%. On Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), OEU attains the best AG of 5.03% compared to Q-MUL's 8.17%, with a Forget Accuracy gap of only 1.31% from the Retrain gold standard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OEU outperforms all nine baselines on the primary AG metric across every dataset and forgetting ratio. Against the closest competitor Q-MUL (Tong et al., ICCV 2025), OEU reduces AG by 55% on CIFAR-10 at 10% forgetting (0.58% vs 1.30%) and by 49% on CIFAR-100 at 30% forgetting (1.98% vs 3.90%), with the gap widening to 48% at 50% forgetting on CIFAR-100 (3.67% vs 7.06%). The label-manipulation baselines (Random Labels, ℓ₁-sparse, SalUn) achieve moderate AG on CIFAR-10 (1.31–3.45%) but degrade sharply on CIFAR-100 (6.05–10.53% at 10%), confirming the bias introduced by the 'learning wrong answers' paradigm. Gradient Ascent (Graves/Thudi) and Influence Unlearning (Koh/Izzo) perform poorly on quantized models, with IU reaching catastrophic AG values of 58–70% at higher forgetting ratios due to the constrained discrete parameter space. Fine-Tuning (Golatkar/Warnecke) preserves retain accuracy but fails to forget, showing FA gaps of 6–8% above Retrain. On Tiny-ImageNet, OEU's AG of 5.03% beats Q-MUL (8.17%) and ℓ₁-sparse (9.02%), while on MobileNetV2 (2-bit) OEU maintains the lowest AG across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN at all forgetting ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Q-MUL (Tong et al., ICCV 2025), the first unlearning framework for quantized neural networks, and departs from its two core mechanisms: Similar Labels (a label-manipulation strategy) and Adaptive Gradient Reweighting (scalar magnitude adjustment). Conceptually, it reframes the forgetting objective from the 'learn incorrect answers' paradigm inherited by most approximate unlearning methods (Random Labels, Gradient Ascent, Influence Functions) toward entropy maximization, and replaces scalar reweighting with a geometric operation (orthogonal projection) borrowed from multi-task learning (cf. PCGrad). The QAT/STE machinery from the quantization literature (LSQ+, PACT, DSQ) provides the training substrate, but the paper's contribution is orthogonal to quantization design itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically urgent gap: GDPR-compliant data removal from quantized models deployed on edge devices, where retraining is infeasible and prior methods were designed for full-precision networks. The entropy-guided objective and orthogonal projection together offer a cleaner theoretical story (unbiased forgetting direction + first-order retain preservation guarantee) than the ad-hoc label-shifting and scalar weighting used previously. However, the evaluation is confined to image classification with CNNs (ResNet-18, MobileNetV2) at 2–4 bit, leaving open whether the approach transfers to transformers, generative models, or more aggressive quantization regimes (1-bit, mixed-precision). The moderate but consistent gains over Q-MUL (e.g., ~50% reduction in average gap at 30% forgetting) suggest the method is sound but unlikely to be a drop-in replacement without re-tuning for new architectures.
