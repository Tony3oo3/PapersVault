## Problem

Most multimodal large language models rely on autoregressive backbones with separate output decoders for non-textual modalities, leaving the architectural design space largely unexplored. While mask-based discrete diffusion models have shown strong potential in NLP, no prior work has built a unified any-to-any multimodal system entirely on such a diffusion backbone to jointly model understanding and generation across text, image, and speech.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262518/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single unified token sequence where text, image (MAGVIT-v2 tokens at 16× downsampling, codebook 8192), and speech (GLM-4-Voice tokens at 12.5 Hz, codebook 16384) are interleaved with modality-specific boundary tokens. During inference, the initial fully-masked sequence length is adaptively set (e.g., 3.5× text length for TTS, 0.2× speech length for ASR), and a special [begin-of-speech] token is pre-infilled at the 25% position to guide simultaneous text-and-speech output. Outputs are decoded back to waveforms via the GLM-4-Voice decoder or to pixels via MAGVIT-v2, with parallel decoding over multiple tokens per forward pass.

## Method

Omni-Diffusion extends the Dream-7B discrete diffusion LLM by expanding its vocabulary with 16,384 speech and 8,192 image tokens, then trains a unified mask-token predictor over the joint distribution of all multimodal tokens using cross-entropy loss computed only on masked positions. A three-stage progressive training pipeline first aligns visual tokens with the language model's semantic space, then jointly aligns speech, and finally fine-tunes on a constructed Speech-Driven Visual Interaction (SDVI) dataset requiring simultaneous speech and image processing. Key training innovations include attenuated tail-pad masking (scaling pad-token mask ratio by γ=0.6 to prevent overfitting on padding) and inference-time techniques: a position penalty that suppresses logits at sequence boundaries to prevent repetitive image patterns, special token pre-infilling that anchors speech generation after a text segment, and adaptive token-length initialization that exploits the correlation between speech duration and text length to reduce decoding steps.

## Results

On speech tasks, Omni-Diffusion achieves 6.69 WER on LibriSpeech (ASR) and 2.22 WER on LibriTTS (TTS), outperforming the any-to-any baseline AnyGPT (8.5 WER) and even surpassing dedicated TTS models CosyVoice (2.89) and CosyVoice2 (2.47). On visual understanding, Omni-Diffusion scores 76.4 on POPE, 1176.1 on MME-Perception, 31.1 on MMMU-val, and 34.7 on Seed-2-Plus, matching or exceeding specialized visual LLMs. For text-to-image generation on MSCOCO, it achieves CLIP-T 0.236 and CLIP-I 0.662, competitive with methods relying on external pretrained diffusion models. The model also maintains strong quality with as few as 10 diffusion steps (CLIP-T 0.2325, CLIP-I 0.6524), demonstrating efficient parallel decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest any-to-any competitor AnyGPT (8B, autoregressive), Omni-Diffusion (7B, diffusion) wins decisively on ASR (6.69 vs. 8.5 WER) and POPE (76.4 vs. 67.7), and edges ahead on CLIP-I (0.662 vs. 0.650). Versus NExT-GPT (7B, any-to-any with external diffusion decoders), Omni-Diffusion substantially outperforms on Seed-2-Plus (34.7 vs. 26.2) and CLIP-T (0.236 vs. 0.225), though NExT-GPT retains a slight edge on CLIP-I (0.691 vs. 0.662) thanks to its dedicated diffusion decoder. On TTS, Omni-Diffusion (2.22 WER) surpasses both CosyVoice (2.89) and CosyVoice2 (2.47), the strongest dedicated TTS baselines, while on ASR it trails the speech-specialized GLM-4-Voice (6.69 vs. 2.82 WER). Compared to understanding-only visual LLMs, Omni-Diffusion is within ~2.5 points on POPE (76.4 vs. InstructBLIP's 78.9) and MME-P (1176.1 vs. 1212.8) while simultaneously supporting generation, and it leads on Seed-2-Plus (34.7 vs. Emu's 33.5). Overall, Omni-Diffusion matches or exceeds specialized visual LLMs on understanding while adding generation capabilities, and outperforms prior any-to-any systems on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mask-based discrete diffusion models (MDMs), previously applied to text-only language modeling (Dream-7B, LLaDA), into a unified any-to-any multimodal framework covering text, speech, and images. It departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm in multimodal LLMs (AnyGPT, NExT-GPT, VITA) by directly modeling the joint distribution over all multimodal discrete tokens within a single diffusion backbone, rather than relying on an LLM plus modality-specific output decoders. The approach is also distinguished from Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) methods like NeXT-Omni, which refine the entire sequence simultaneously at each step, whereas MDMs iteratively decode and freeze top-k highest-confidence tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first evidence that mask-based discrete diffusion can serve as a viable backbone for any-to-any multimodal systems, matching or slightly exceeding autoregressive baselines on speech, visual understanding, and generation benchmarks while offering parallel decoding for efficiency. The practical techniques (position penalty, special token pre-infilling, attenuated tail-pad masking) are transferable to other MDM-based systems. Open questions include whether this approach scales beyond 7B parameters, extends to video or additional modalities, and can handle more complex downstream tasks like instruction-based visual editing, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
