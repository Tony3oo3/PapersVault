## Problem

Existing DPO variants for diffusion models equate trajectory likelihoods with final-state marginal probabilities, an oversimplification that misrepresents the reward structure and leads to suboptimal alignment. Segment-wise decompositions have been proposed but rely on heuristic designs without theoretical guarantees, introducing inconsistencies across timesteps and undermining reward propagation stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264286/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via a fine-tuned latent diffusion model (SD-1.5 at 512×512 or SDXL at 1024×1024). During training, the value-distribution function is conditioned on intermediate noisy states x_t obtained by DDIM-perturbing Pick-a-Pic images at random timesteps, while the VRPO stage operates on short L-step segments (L=1 for SD-1.5, L=4 for SDXL) of a 20-step DDIM rollout, using classifier-free guidance at scale 5.0.

## Method

The framework has two stages grounded in stochastic optimal control. In Stage 1, a CDF-based value-distribution function F_φ(z|x_t, t) is trained to model the distribution of terminal rewards Z(x_t,t)=r(x_1) under the reference diffusion process, using a frozen PickScore backbone with a lightweight monotonic CDF head and a composite loss (CRPS + binary cross-entropy + total-variation smoothness). In Stage 2, the long diffusion trajectory is decomposed into short L-step segments; candidate endpoints are ranked by the learned optimal value V*_φ(x_t,t,η)=log∫e^{ηz}dF_φ(z|x_t,t), and the diffusion model is updated via a DPO-style likelihood-ratio loss on these segment-level preferences. The key theoretical result is that the optimal value function admits the closed form V*(x_t,t,η)=log E_{x_1~p_ref(·|x_t,t)}[exp(ηr(x_1))], and under sufficient model capacity, stage-wise VRPO provably converges to the tilted target distribution p*(x_1)∝p_data(x_1)exp(ηr(x_1)), with a unique pathwise optimal score function valid for all t∈[0,1].

## Results

On SDXL, VRPO achieves 23.07 PickScore, 1.24 ImageReward, 7.19 Aesthetic Score, and 0.2867 HPSv2, outperforming the strongest baseline SPO (22.78, 1.05, 6.80, 0.2858) across all four metrics. On SD-1.5, VRPO reaches 21.76 PickScore and 0.67 ImageReward versus SPO's 21.20 and 0.27, a margin of over 0.55 on PickScore. VRPO also requires the least total training time among all baselines (0.5 A6000 hours for value training + 174 A100 hours for diffusion training on SDXL, versus 152 + 124 A100 hours for SPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRPO outperforms all five baselines on every metric for both SDXL and SD-1.5. Against the closest competitor SPO (Liang et al., 2024), VRPO gains +0.28 PickScore, +0.19 ImageReward, and +0.39 Aesthetic Score on SDXL, and +0.56 PickScore, +0.40 ImageReward, and +0.07 Aesthetic on SD-1.5. Against Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), the margin is even larger: +0.74 PickScore and +0.30 ImageReward on SDXL. MAPO (She et al., 2024) and the simple classifier-guidance baseline are substantially weaker, confirming that trajectory-level DPO variants and naive reward injection are inferior to the value-as-return formulation. Notably, VRPO achieves these gains while training only a lightweight prediction head on top of frozen CLIP embeddings, whereas SPO fine-tunes the entire CLIP model, and VRPO also completes training in less wall-clock time than all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-DPO line of work (Wallace et al., 2023; Liang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023) by identifying a fundamental flaw in how prior methods handle the relationship between trajectory likelihoods and terminal-state marginals. It grounds the correction in stochastic optimal control (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025), deriving a closed-form optimal value function that serves as a principled return signal for short trajectory segments, rather than relying on heuristic segment decompositions. The two-stage design—learning a CDF-parameterized value-distribution function, then applying segment-level DPO (VRPO)—departs from both the single-stage Diffusion-DPO and the full-CLIP fine-tuning approach of SPO, instead using a lightweight prediction head on frozen CLIP embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3) that VRPO converges to the optimal tilted distribution p(x)exp(ηr(x)) under sufficient capacity, combined with the pathwise uniqueness of the optimal score (Remark 4), provides a clean foundation that prior diffusion-DPO methods lacked. The practical finding that a lightweight value head on frozen CLIP outperforms SPO's full-CLIP fine-tuning while consuming far less compute is directly useful for practitioners. Open questions include whether the short-segment approximation degrades at very high resolutions or in video diffusion, whether the CDF discretization (50 bins) is sufficient for more complex reward landscapes, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-reward or constrained settings.
