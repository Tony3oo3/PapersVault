## Problem

Standard NMF solvers enforce nonnegativity throughout optimization (interior methods), which can trap them on barren plateaus in the nonconvex landscape, causing slow convergence or convergence to suboptimal stationary points. The paper asks whether starting from the unconstrained SVD optimum and approaching the nonnegative orthant from the exterior can yield both lower reconstruction error and faster convergence than any interior-only strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a nonnegative data matrix X ∈ R^{n×m} (dense or sparse, with optional missing entries for the matrix-completion variant) and a target rank r, and produces nonnegative factor matrices U ∈ R^{n×r} and V ∈ R^{m×r} satisfying KKT optimality conditions for the Frobenius-norm NMF objective. The algorithm is objective-agnostic: it requires only an optimal solution to the corresponding unconstrained low-rank problem as a starting point, so it extends to KL-divergence, Itakura-Saito, and total-variation variants without modification.

## Method

eNMF decomposes NMF into three stages that separate low-rank approximation from nonnegativity enforcement. First, a truncated SVD yields the unconstrained global optimum (U*, V*), and an ADMM-based procedure solves an orthogonal Procrustes problem to find a rotation R that maps (U*R, V*R) to the point on the rotational manifold closest to the positive orthant. Second, if the rotated factors remain infeasible, an exterior penalty method with closed-form row-wise projected block coordinate descent (PBCD) pushes negative entries to zero while minimizing reconstruction error on the nonnegative entries. Third, HALS descends the now-feasible factors to a KKT-satisfying local minimum. The key geometric insight is that the SVD-anchored rotational manifold often intersects or nearly intersects the positive orthant, so the rotation step alone can reach the global NMF optimum on synthetic and some real datasets, and even when it does not, the exterior warm start avoids the slow constrained descent that plagues interior methods.

## Results

Under equal-time budgets, eNMF achieves up to 30% lower reconstruction error than the best competitor (e.g., 7234 vs. 7899 on Face at r=20; 8.97 vs. 9.69 on Verb at r=100). Under equal-error budgets, eNMF reaches the target reconstruction error 2–10× faster than all baselines: on synthetic data it is ~5–6× faster than AO-ADMM and ~16–39× faster than HALS/NeNMF/FPGM/Vavasis, while on real datasets it is ~14–26% faster than the closest competitor. On synthetic benchmarks (SNR 20–100 dB, r=50–500), eNMF attains the unconstrained global minimum (relative error = 1.00) in every setting, whereas competitors range from 1.05 to 1.40. Across 400+ experimental settings, 99% of the time different algorithms converge to equivalent factor matrices (modulo permutation and scaling), with eNMF reaching those minima fastest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eNMF outperforms all nine baselines (HALS, NMF-ADMM, Grad-Mult, ALS, AO-ADMM, A-HALS, NeNMF, FPGM, and Vavasis R1D) on every dataset and rank tested, under both equal-time and equal-error protocols. AO-ADMM is consistently the closest competitor, but eNMF still achieves 14–26% lower runtime on real datasets and 5–6× lower runtime on synthetic datasets. HALS, NeNMF, FPGM, and Vavasis R1D are the slowest, requiring 16–39× longer than eNMF on synthetic data to reach the same reconstruction error. Under equal-time budgets, eNMF's reconstruction error advantage over the best baseline ranges from ~1.4% (Audio, r=10) to ~8.4% (Face, r=20), with Verb showing 5.4–7.4% improvements. Notably, on synthetic data and Audio at higher ranks (r ∈ {40, 80, 100}), eNMF reaches the unconstrained global minimum via a single rotation step, while all interior methods stall on barren plateaus and only asymptotically approach eNMF's error over days of computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'interior' paradigm for NMF (Lee & Seung multiplicative updates, HALS, ADMM-based solvers) that enforces nonnegativity at every iteration, instead exploiting the rotational invariance of the unconstrained SVD solution (Vavasis, 2009) to approach the positive orthant from the exterior. The ADMM-based rotation step generalizes Vavasis's linear-transformation insight by restricting the transformation to a single orthogonal matrix, while the feasibility-attainment and HALS descent stages reuse standard tools. The work also connects to the barren-plateau literature in optimization by showing that interior methods stall in flat regions of the NMF landscape, whereas the exterior warm start bypasses these plateaus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 99% of NMF runs across 400+ experiments converge to geometrically equivalent factors (modulo permutation and scaling) suggests the NMF landscape is far more structured than previously assumed, with most local minima being rotations of one another rather than genuinely distinct solutions. This reframes the practical problem from 'finding the right local minimum' to 'reaching the shared solution geometry fastest,' which the exterior approach does substantially better. Open questions include whether the rotation-based initialization extends to online/streaming NMF, whether the equivalence result holds for divergences beyond the Frobenius norm, and whether the NP-hardness of finding the exact orthogonal transform can be circumvented more efficiently at scale.
