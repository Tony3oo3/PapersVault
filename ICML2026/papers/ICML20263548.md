## Problem

Existing multimodal image fusion methods allocate modality contributions through implicit mechanisms (attention maps, gating activations) that do not explicitly distinguish whether cross-modal differences represent valid complementary evidence or imaging degradation. This causes biased fusion decisions in discrepancy-sensitive regions—such as overexposed areas—where stronger pixel responses are mistaken for more reliable information, leading to information loss and artifact propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263548/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts pairs of images from the same scene captured under different sensing conditions (infrared + visible, multi-exposure, multi-focus, or medical structural + functional), at resolutions ranging from 640×480 to 1024×768, and produces a single fused image at the original resolution. Training is fully unsupervised: no ground-truth fused images are required, and the network is optimized against max-composite references using pixel-intensity and Sobel-gradient fidelity losses. The model is lightweight (0.18M parameters, 1.00 GFLOPs on MSRS), making it suitable for real-time deployment.

## Method

IDEAL reformulates fusion as observation-conditioned probabilistic decision learning, where local modality contribution is an explicit decision variable rather than an implicit activation. A convolutional encoder extracts four-scale features, which are then processed by two complementary modules: the Modal Difference Attention (MDA) module computes cross-modal spatial differences and uses Q/K/V attention to highlight regions requiring contribution decisions while suppressing noise, and the Conditional Decision Gating (CDG) module projects features into the frequency domain via FFT to extract power-spectrum energy, complementary spectral evidence, and spectral-entropy reliability as decision observations. These observations parameterize a symmetric Beta prior over local contribution weights, whose mean yields an interpretable gating policy and whose concentration controls decision uncertainty; when evidence is insufficient, an uncertainty modulation term shrinks the policy toward conservative 50/50 mixing, preventing unreliable cues from dominating. The calibrated weights modulate modality-specific features before concatenation, and a progressive decoder reconstructs the fused image, all trained end-to-end with unsupervised intensity and gradient losses.

## Results

On the MSRS dataset, IDEAL achieves the best EN (7.03), AG (4.43), and SD (50.93) among all compared methods, while using only 0.18M parameters, 1.00 GFLOPs, and 0.37G memory—orders of magnitude lighter than CDDFuse (1.19M params, 220.92 GFLOPs) or EMMA (1.52M params, 16.75 GFLOPs). On M3FD and FMB, IDEAL similarly leads in EN and SD, and achieves the best AG on M3FD (4.65) and second-best on FMB (4.79 vs. GIFNet's 4.93). For downstream object detection on MSRS, IDEAL attains the best mAP@[0.5:0.95] of 67.5, confirming that its decision-aware fusion improves high-IoU localization quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEAL outperforms all eight baselines on EN and SD across MSRS, M3FD, and FMB, with the largest margins over YDTR (e.g., EN 7.03 vs. 5.73 on MSRS) and LRRNet. On AG, IDEAL leads on MSRS (4.43 vs. EMMA's 4.07) and M3FD (4.65 vs. GIFNet's 4.16), but trails GIFNet on FMB (4.79 vs. 4.93). GIFNet achieves the highest SF on M3FD (14.61) and FMB (17.87), and SAGE attains the best SCD on M3FD (1.66 vs. IDEAL's 1.52), suggesting these methods amplify high-frequency responses at the cost of contrast and information preservation. In terms of efficiency, IDEAL is dramatically lighter: 0.18M parameters and 1.00 GFLOPs versus CDDFuse (1.19M, 220.92 GFLOPs), EMMA (1.52M, 16.75 GFLOPs), and GIFNet (0.725M, 91.21 GFLOPs), while still delivering the strongest overall fusion quality. For downstream detection, IDEAL achieves the best mAP@[0.5:0.95] (67.5) and second-best mAP@0.5 (93.0 vs. SAGE's 93.3), with competitive Precision (93.7) and Recall (85.0), confirming that its uncertainty-aware contribution decisions translate into more robust object localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deep multimodal fusion—where contribution allocation is an implicit byproduct of attention maps, gating activations, or foundation-model features—by explicitly reformulating fusion as observation-conditioned probabilistic decision learning. Rather than scaling network capacity (as in CDDFuse, SwinFusion, GIFNet) or injecting semantic priors (as in SAGE, TextFusion), IDEAL constructs explicit decision-relevant observations from cross-modal spatial differences (MDA) and frequency-domain statistics (CDG: power-spectrum energy, complementary spectra, spectral-entropy reliability), then maps them to a symmetric Beta prior over local modality contribution. The theoretical analysis of decision-induced information regret in discrepancy regions provides a principled justification for why explicit, uncertainty-aware decisions outperform implicit deterministic activations, a framing absent from prior fusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean conceptual lens—treating fusion as local decision-making under uncertainty—that could inform future fusion architectures beyond the specific MDA+CDG implementation. The striking efficiency (0.18M parameters, 1.00 GFLOPs) while matching or exceeding much heavier baselines suggests that explicit decision structure can substitute for brute-force model capacity, a potentially useful design principle. However, the decision observations are limited to spatial discrepancy and frequency-domain statistics; the authors acknowledge that semantic cues, task-specific feedback, and temporal consistency remain unexplored, and the Beta-prior mechanism, while interpretable, is a relatively simple probabilistic wrapper that may not scale to more complex multi-modality or video fusion settings.
