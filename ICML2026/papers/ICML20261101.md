## Problem

Diffusion-based world models require many iterative denoising steps, making them too slow for interactive use and long-horizon rollouts. Existing feature caching methods designed for single-modal image/video diffusion transfer poorly to world models because of two world-model-specific obstacles: token heterogeneity from multi-modal coupling (RGB and depth) and spatial variation creates a long-tailed difficulty profile, and non-uniform temporal dynamics mean a small set of hard tokens drives error growth, making uniform skipping either unstable or overly conservative.

## Input / Output

WorldCache operates as a training-free inference-time wrapper around multi-modal diffusion world models (HunyuanVoyager-13B, Aether-5B, LingBot-14B) that take an image, text prompt, and camera trajectory as conditioning and produce coupled RGB video and depth video via spatially concatenated latents (z_t = concat[z_t^r, z_t^d] ∈ R^{c×f×2h×w}). The method works at the model-output (token) level rather than layer-wise, so it introduces negligible memory overhead (~0.02–0.03 GB) compared to layer-wise baselines that exceed 100 GB. For 3D reconstruction tasks, the same cached outputs also yield depth maps and camera poses evaluated against ground truth.

## Method

WorldCache introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Curvature-guided Heterogeneous Token Prediction (CHTP) computes a physics-grounded curvature score κ_i = ‖a‖/(‖v‖²+ε) from the last three FULL backbone outputs, then partitions tokens into Stable (0th-order reuse), Linear (1st-order extrapolation), and Chaotic (cubic-Hermite damped predictor that blends current and historical velocities with a smoothstep schedule) groups, allocating the most conservative prediction to the hardest tokens. Second, Chaotic-prioritized Adaptive Skipping (CAS) constructs a dimensionless drift indicator e_i(t) = κ_i·‖ỹ_{t,i} − ỹ_{t+1,i}‖ for chaotic tokens only, accumulates it over consecutive cached steps, and triggers a FULL backbone evaluation only when the accumulated score exceeds a single threshold η. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that the curvature-weighted deviation is invariant to global feature rescaling, so one threshold generalizes across heterogeneous token scales and timesteps without per-step recalibration.

## Results

On HunyuanVoyager-13B (512×768, 49 frames), WorldCache achieves the best perceptual fidelity among all accelerated methods (PSNR 23.49, SSIM 0.770, LPIPS 0.176) while delivering a 3.65× end-to-end speedup with negligible memory overhead (50.58 GB vs. 50.44 GB baseline). On Aether-5B (480×720, 41 frames), it attains PSNR 31.87, SSIM 0.924, LPIPS 0.066 with a 1.68× speedup. For 3D reconstruction on Aether, WorldCache matches the baseline depth accuracy (Abs Rel 0.341 vs. 0.340) while achieving the largest 2.61× acceleration and the lowest rotation error (RPE rot 0.796) among all accelerated methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldCache outperforms all seven baselines on perceptual fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) on both HunyuanVoyager-13B and Aether-5B. Against the strongest model-wise baseline EasyCache, WorldCache improves PSNR by +1.73 dB (23.49 vs. 21.76) on Voyager and +9.03 dB (31.87 vs. 22.84) on Aether, while also achieving a higher speedup (3.65× vs. 3.58× on Voyager; 1.68× vs. 1.49× on Aether). Layer-wise methods (DuCa, ToCa, TaylorSeer, HiCache) suffer severe quality degradation (PSNR drops of 10–20 dB on Voyager) and require >100 GB memory, making them impractical on a single GPU; TaylorSeer and HiCache are actually slower than the unaccelerated baseline on Voyager (0.88× and 0.96×). TeaCache achieves a competitive 3.38× speedup on Voyager but with substantially lower fidelity (PSNR 16.25 vs. 23.49). HERO, which combines caching with token merging, is the weakest baseline overall, achieving only 0.96× speedup on Voyager with the highest memory overhead (173.42 GB) and degraded quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-caching paradigm for diffusion models (TeaCache, EasyCache, DuCa, ToCa, TaylorSeer, HiCache) from single-modal image/video generation to multi-modal diffusion world models that jointly denoise coupled RGB and depth latents. The key conceptual departure is identifying that prior caching methods implicitly assume homogeneous token dynamics, which breaks down in world models due to (i) modality-induced token heterogeneity (appearance vs. geometry tokens evolve on different timescales) and (ii) non-stationary temporal regimes where a small chaotic-token subset dominates error accumulation. Rather than a new architecture, WorldCache repurposes classical differential-geometry ideas (curvature as a predictability cue, Hermite interpolation for damped prediction) into a token-adaptive caching policy with a dimensionless drift trigger.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.7× training-free speedup with near-lossless quality (98% WorldScore retention, PSNR within ~1dB of baseline) is practically significant for interactive world simulation and long-horizon rollouts, where current 13B-parameter world models are prohibitively slow. The finding that layer-wise caching methods (which store per-block features) actually underperform or even slow down world models compared to simpler model-level caching highlights a mismatch between fine-grained caching granularity and multi-modal token structure. Open questions include whether the curvature metric generalizes to world models with more than two coupled modalities (e.g., adding audio or action channels), whether the percentile-based grouping can be made fully automatic, and whether the approach transfers to autoregressive or non-diffusion world model architectures.
