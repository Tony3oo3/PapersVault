## Problem

One-shot skeleton-based action recognition must classify unseen actions from a single exemplar, but existing methods rely on Euclidean embeddings that cannot capture the tree-like joint→part→body hierarchy of skeleton data, and they lack mechanisms to efficiently exploit the single exemplar at test time by focusing on discriminative body regions. This limits cross-modal alignment with action semantics and generalization to novel categories under extreme data scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 70-frame skeleton sequences (25 joints) from NTU/PKU-MMD datasets, partitioned into three granularities: Full Body (C-dim), Body Joint (C×V), and Body Part (C×4, grouped as head, arms&hands, torso, legs&feet). Text descriptions are generated at matching granularities via LLM prompts and encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. LLM-derived body-part and body-joint importance masks (summing to 1.0) are pre-computed offline and applied during inference. The output is a per-class logit vector combining a skeleton-skeleton cache retrieval score and a skeleton-text hyperbolic distance score, from which the predicted action label is read.

## Method

SkelHCC couples two components in a shared Lorentz hyperbolic space. The EH-HCLIP module projects multi-granularity skeleton and text features onto the hyperboloid via the exponential map and trains them with a weighted multi-granularity hyperbolic contrastive loss (bidirectional InfoNCE using Lorentzian distance) plus a hyperbolic entailment cone loss that enforces partial order between paired skeleton-text embeddings; only a 0.5M-parameter MLP head is trained on a frozen CTR-GCN backbone and frozen CLIP text encoder. At inference, the training-free LMV-Cache stores the single support exemplar's multi-granularity features as cache keys and one-hot labels as values, then computes LLM-mask-weighted similarities at each granularity (full-body dot product, body-joint and body-part Hadamard-masked similarities) and fuses them via exponential affinity and matrix multiplication to yield a cache logit. The final prediction is a residual combination of the cache logit and a hyperbolic skeleton-text distance logit, requiring no backbone updates or online LLM calls.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 120 (the primary benchmark), SkelHCC achieves 71.6% top-1 accuracy under the 100 base-class / 20 novel-class one-shot setting, outperforming the previous SOTA CrossGLG (62.6%) by 9.0% and the best non-multimodal method SkeletonX (69.1%) by 2.5%. Under the more challenging 20 base-class setting, SkelHCC reaches 52.0%, surpassing CrossGLG (45.3%) by 6.7%. On NTU RGB+D 60, SkelHCC attains 84.1% (vs. 75.6% for CrossGLG), and on PKU-MMD II it achieves 40.0% (vs. 38.3% for SkeletonX). Notably, SkelHCC requires only 0.5M trainable parameters with a frozen backbone, compared to 1.6–60.3M parameters with backbone updates in all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkelHCC comprehensively outperforms all 17 baselines across every base-class setting on NTU RGB+D 120. Against the closest non-multimodal competitor SkeletonX (Zhang et al., 2025), SkelHCC gains 3.8% (20-class) to 2.5% (100-class) while using 3× fewer trainable parameters (0.5M vs. 1.6M) and keeping the backbone frozen. Against the SOTA multimodal method CrossGLG (Yan et al., 2024), SkelHCC improves by 6.7% (20-class) and 9.0% (100-class), with the gap widening as base classes decrease—indicating stronger data efficiency. Compared to the large-parameter MotionBERT (Zhu et al., 2023, 60.3M params), SkelHCC surpasses it by 10.6% under 100 base classes with 120× fewer adaptation parameters. On NTU RGB+D 60, SkelHCC leads CrossGLG by 8.5% (84.1% vs. 75.6%) and SkeletonX by 0.9%; on PKU-MMD II it achieves 40.0% vs. 38.3% for SkeletonX. A key differentiator is that all competing methods require backbone updates during one-shot adaptation, whereas SkelHCC freezes the pretrained CTR-GCN backbone and trains only a 0.5M-parameter MLP interface, enabling faster and more stable adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkelHCC sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) hyperbolic vision-language representation learning, extending Desai et al.'s (ICML 2023) hyperbolic image-text CLIP from pixel data to skeleton sequences with an explicit joint→part→body hierarchy; (2) training-free cache-based few-shot adaptation, adapting the Tip-Adapter (Zhang et al., ECCV 2022) key-value retrieval paradigm from images to multi-granularity skeleton features with LLM-derived importance masks; and (3) one-shot skeleton action recognition, departing from prior metric-learning and attention-based methods (APSR, uDTW, CrossGLG) by reframing classification as retrieval in a shared hyperbolic manifold rather than fine-tuning a Euclidean backbone. The key conceptual departure is that the hyperbolic geometry is not merely a representational choice but is explicitly tied to the bio-anatomical hierarchy of the skeleton, with the entailment cone loss (Ganea et al., 2018) enforcing partial-order structure between skeleton and text embeddings at each granularity level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme parameter efficiency: only 0.5M trainable parameters (a single MLP head) are needed to outperform methods requiring 1.7M–60M parameters, and the LLM-derived masks are pre-computed offline so no online LLM inference is required at test time. This makes the framework attractive for deployment in data-scarce settings like healthcare monitoring where annotating novel actions is prohibitively expensive. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to few-shot (k>1) settings, whether the hyperbolic hierarchy generalizes to other structured modalities (e.g., 3D point clouds, depth sequences), and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the frozen skeleton backbone. The paper also acknowledges that the multi-granularity partitioning relies on fixed bio-anatomical priors that may not transfer to non-humanoid or atypical body configurations.
