## Problem

Audio-visual LLMs incur prohibitive computational costs from massive redundant token sequences, and existing unimodal compression methods ignore the synergistic information density between audio and video. More fundamentally, the paper identifies a previously overlooked theoretical bottleneck: aggressive sparse token sampling causes high-frequency RoPE components to violate the Nyquist limit, producing phase-wrapping collisions (positional aliasing) that make distant timestamps indistinguishable and corrupt temporal monotonicity essential for audio-visual synchronization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames (28×28 pixel patches, up to 144 tokens/frame at 1 FPS), audio (25 tokens/second at ~40 ms granularity), and a text instruction, concatenated into a single sequence following the vanilla Qwen2.5-Omni layout. The CS2 module selects a sparse subset (5–20% budget) from the unified audio-visual pool while preserving original positional indices, and the LLM decoder produces a standard text response. A key structural constraint is that selected tokens retain their original positions rather than being re-indexed, which is a prerequisite for Sync-RoPE's aliasing correction to function correctly.

## Method

EchoingPixels couples two co-designed components. The Cross-Modal Semantic Sieve (CS2) is a trainable copy of the first N=4 LLM layers with causal attention replaced by bidirectional attention, enabling global cross-modal context and text pre-fusion before selection; a 2-layer MLP scorer assigns importance to each audio-visual token, and a global Top-K over the unified pool (trained via Straight-Through Estimator) performs extractive selection with dynamic cross-modal budget allocation. The Synchronization-Augmented RoPE (Sync-RoPE) resolves positional aliasing by repartitioning the 64 RoPE frequency channels from the standard [t,h,w] split into [t_high, h, w, t_low], reallocating ultra-low-frequency channels to the temporal dimension so that max(Θ_low) ≪ π/E[T_s], mathematically guaranteeing a bijective, monotonic position-to-phase mapping on the sparse grid. The system is fine-tuned end-to-end on 390k samples for one epoch, with Sync-RoPE applied in both the CS2 encoder and the main decoder.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, EchoingPixels retains 99.0% of the full model's average performance using only 20% of tokens (55.5 avg on audio-visual, 63.5 avg on video-only), and still achieves 95.2% at a 10% budget. On the larger Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, EchoingPixels at 10% preserves 94.1% of the full model's performance. In terms of efficiency, the 20% budget yields a 2.23× forward-latency speedup and 2.26× peak CUDA memory reduction, scaling to 2.96× and 2.61× at 5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoingPixels decisively outperforms all three baselines at comparable or stricter token budgets. Against FastV (attention-score-guided pruning, 20% budget), EchoingPixels at 20% achieves 99.0% relative performance vs. FastV's 84.6% on the 3B model—a 14.4-point gap—and FastV additionally suffers OOM errors on WorldSense and Video-MME with audio for the 7B model. Against PyramidDrop (pyramid-style attention-based dropping, 20% budget), EchoingPixels leads by 10.7 points (99.0% vs. 88.3%) on the 3B model, with PyramidDrop also hitting OOM on the 7B model. Against OmniZip (audio-guided video compression, 20% budget), EchoingPixels leads by 11.1 points (99.0% vs. 87.9%) on the 3B model, and critically, OmniZip cannot handle video-only inputs (N/A on Video-MME w/o audio and MLVU), whereas EchoingPixels supports both audio-visual and video-only evaluation. Even when FastV is fine-tuned on the same 390k training samples (FastV-tuned), it reaches only 91.9% relative performance, still 7.1 points below EchoingPixels, confirming the gains stem from the architectural design (cross-modal selection + Sync-RoPE) rather than training data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video token reduction literature (FastV, PyramidDrop, OmniZip) from unimodal or loosely-coupled compression to a joint audio-visual stream, and grounds the design in a previously unexamined theoretical bottleneck: positional aliasing under sparse sampling. It builds directly on the RoPE/TMRoPE positional encoding framework of Qwen2.5-Omni, reparameterizing the spectral allocation of rotational frequencies as a low-pass filter to satisfy the Nyquist criterion on the reduced grid. The Cross-Modal Semantic Sieve departs from abstractive Q-Former-style compression by performing extractive selection over a unified pool with bidirectional cross-modal attention, and from prior unimodal pruning by enabling dynamic, data-driven budget allocation between modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of positional aliasing as a fundamental failure mode for any aggressive token reduction in RoPE-based models provides a principled explanation for why naive sparsification degrades temporal reasoning, and Sync-RoPE offers a minimal, theoretically-motivated fix that could be adopted by any RoPE-based multimodal model performing token pruning. The 2–3× latency and memory reductions at 5–20% token budgets with 91–99% performance retention make this practically relevant for deploying AV-LLMs in resource-constrained settings. A key limitation is that Sync-RoPE is specific to RoPE's frequency-domain structure and does not extend to learnable absolute embeddings or relative position biases, leaving the aliasing problem for those architectures open. Whether the cross-modal synergy gains generalize beyond the Qwen2.5-Omni family and to longer sequences remains to be validated.
