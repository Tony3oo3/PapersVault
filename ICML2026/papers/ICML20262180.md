## Problem

Drift-free Riemannian diffusion models require evaluating and sampling from the manifold heat kernel (the transition density of Brownian motion) for both forward noising and conditional-score computation, yet this kernel is rarely available in closed form beyond a few highly symmetric manifolds. Existing approximations—short-time asymptotics and spectral expansions—are accurate only in restricted time regimes or demand strong algebraic structure (e.g., compact Lie groups), leaving a practical gap for general manifolds such as SPD(n) or permutation-quotiented point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method takes an explicit manifold specification (Riemannian metric and a global coordinate representation via embedding or quotient) plus optional conditioning variables (e.g., weather predictors for traffic, binary labels for EEG), and produces manifold-valued samples: geolocation points on S², rotation matrices on SO(3), SPD matrices for traffic flow or brain connectivity, or molecular point clouds on R^{k×n}/S_n. A key practical detail is that the pipeline requires a two-stage training process: first a PINN surrogate for the heat kernel is trained (using higher-order autodifferentiation in JAX), and only then is the diffusion denoiser trained via denoising score matching, with forward sampling performed by MCMC (geodesic random walk) initialized from a warped Gaussian.

## Method

The core idea is to treat the heat kernel as the solution of a known PDE (the manifold heat/Fokker-Planck equation) and approximate it with a physics-informed neural network. Given a manifold, the authors (i) embed it in an ambient space to obtain a global coordinate system and derive the Laplace-Beltrami operator via a projection matrix, (ii) replace the singular Dirac-delta initial condition with a principled short-time parametrix (Minakshisundaram-Pleijel) expansion evaluated at a small t₀, and (iii) train a PINN on the log heat kernel to minimize the PDE residual and match the initializer, using modified MLPs with manifold-aware embeddings (Fourier, permutation-invariant, or GNN-based depending on geometry). The trained PINN provides both log-density and score evaluations for the DSM objective, while forward noising uses MCMC with the warped Gaussian as proposal. The paper also proves stability theorems showing that a small initialization error or a small PDE residual propagates to controlled log-density and score errors for all later times, and demonstrates the full pipeline on S², SO(3), SPD(10), and R^{k×n}/S_n.

## Results

On the S² geolocation benchmarks (Volcano, Earthquakes, Floods, Wildfires) and synthetic SO(3), the PINN-based method outperforms RSGM on all five tasks, e.g. achieving 3.56 vs. 3.51 log-likelihood on Volcano and 0.21 vs. 0.18 on SO(3). On SPD(10) NYC taxi traffic, it reduces mean Frobenius distance from 6.15 (SPD-DDPM) to 5.21. On QM9 molecule generation, it improves molecular stability (98.63% vs. 95.20%) and validity (99.60% vs. 98.13%) over EDM(VE), at the cost of lower novelty (41.70% vs. 63.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RSGM (De Bortoli et al., 2022), the PINN-based method wins on all five S²/SO(3) tasks, with the largest gain on Earthquakes (0.24 vs. 0.10 log-likelihood) and consistent improvements of 0.04–0.05 on the remaining datasets. Against SPD-DDPM (Li et al., 2024) on NYC taxi SPD(10) traffic, the method reduces mean Frobenius distance from 6.15 to 5.21 and the 75th percentile from 7.49 to 6.31, indicating tighter generation. Against DiffeoCFM (Collas et al., 2025) on EEG brain connectivity, results are mixed: the method achieves higher α-precision (0.75 vs. 0.62) and Q-F1 (0.69 vs. 0.61) on BNCI2014-002 but lower AUC (0.75 vs. 0.81) and CAS-F1 (0.70 vs. 0.74); on BNCI2015-001 it is roughly competitive with DiffeoCFM on Q-F1 (0.90 vs. 0.90) but trails on AUC (0.71 vs. 0.73). Against EDM(VE) (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) on QM9, the method produces more stable (98.63% vs. 95.20%) and valid (99.60% vs. 98.13%) molecules but substantially lower novelty (41.70% vs. 63.30%) and uniqueness (84.61% vs. 99.12%), reflecting a validity–novelty trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Riemannian score-based generative modeling (De Bortoli et al., 2022) and physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2023). The central bottleneck it addresses—evaluating and sampling the manifold heat kernel—has previously been tackled via short-time asymptotics (Varadhan, Schwinger-DeWitt), spectral expansions on compact Lie groups (Lou et al., 2023), or projection into Euclidean tangent spaces. The key departure is reframing the heat kernel as a PDE solution problem and approximating it with a PINN trained on the coordinate-derived Fokker-Planck equation, a strategy that requires no closed-form kernel or special algebraic structure beyond an explicit manifold specification. The paper also extends the setting to quotient manifolds (permutation-quotiented point clouds), a geometric formulation not previously explored in the Riemannian diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PINN-based heat-kernel surrogate provides a general, geometry-agnostic backend for drift-free Riemannian diffusion, potentially unifying treatment of diverse manifolds (spheres, Lie groups, SPD spaces, quotient spaces) under one PDE-solving paradigm. However, the authors themselves note that Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) bypasses heat-kernel estimation entirely and is simpler to implement when exp/log maps are available, which limits the practical urgency of this approach. Open questions include whether PINN training remains stable and accurate as manifold dimension grows, whether the workflow can be automated to near plug-and-play, and whether the PDE-surrogate idea transfers to other generative frameworks such as Schrödinger bridges or controlled diffusions.
