## Problem

Standard multimodal domain generalization trains separate encoders and a fusion module end-to-end on fused features, which causes each encoder to exploit source-specific cross-modal co-occurrences (statistical relationships arising from recording conditions) rather than learning domain-invariant features. The authors term this failure mode Fusion Overfitting and show that prior MMDG methods addressing norm alignment, representation decomposition, or loss-landscape flattening do not directly counteract it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes any combination of two or three modalities per sample — RGB video clips, audio waveforms, and optical flow — encoded by SlowFast (2304-d), ResNet-18 (512-d), and SlowFast slow-pathway (2048-d) backbones respectively. The regularizer operates on the per-encoder feature embeddings before fusion, and the system outputs a single action class label (8 classes on EPIC-Kitchens, 7 on HAC). Evaluation follows leave-one-domain-out splits across three source domains per dataset.

## Method

MER-DG decomposes the log-determinant entropy of each encoder's feature covariance matrix into two additive terms: a marginal-entropy loss (a hinge loss enforcing a per-dimension variance floor of γ=1) and a spectral-entropy loss (negative log-determinant of the standardized correlation matrix, encouraging decorrelation across dimensions). This decomposition avoids the numerical instability of directly maximizing differential entropy while ensuring all feature dimensions remain active and uncorrelated. The regularizer is applied independently to each encoder's batch of embeddings as an additive term (weighted by λ) to the base classification or contrastive loss, making it architecture-agnostic and compatible with any fusion mechanism. Empirically, this restores cross-domain representational alignment, increases effective rank (RankMe), reduces domain-classification accuracy on frozen features, and recovers standalone encoder performance to near-independent-training levels.

## Results

On the multi-source domain generalization setting (Video+Audio), MER-DG improves the standard Fusion baseline from 59.09% to 62.82% average Top-1 accuracy on EPIC-Kitchens (+3.73%) and from 63.70% to 71.43% on HAC (+7.73%). Against state-of-the-art MMDG methods, MER-DG adds +1.96% over SimMMDG (61.95%→63.91%) and +0.69% over CMRF (63.91%→64.60%) on EPIC-Kitchens, with corresponding gains of +2.70% and +1.34% on HAC. Across all modality combinations (V+A, V+F, A+F, V+A+F) and both multi-source and single-source settings, MER-DG consistently improves every baseline, with the largest gains (up to 7.73%) on the simplest Fusion architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MER-DG is evaluated as a plug-in regularizer on top of four baselines: standard Fusion, Cross-Modal Contrastive (CMC), SimMMDG (Dong et al., 2023), and CMRF (Fan et al., 2024). Against SimMMDG, MER-DG consistently wins on both datasets: +1.96% average on EPIC-Kitchens (63.91% vs 61.95%) and +2.70% on HAC (70.53% vs 67.83%) in the V+A multi-source setting. Against CMRF, the gains are smaller but still positive: +0.69% on EPIC-Kitchens (64.60% vs 63.91%) and +1.34% on HAC (73.25% vs 71.91%). The larger improvements over SimMMDG compared to CMRF are expected, as CMRF already incorporates sharpness-aware minimization and cross-modal training that partially mitigate representation collapse. Against the simpler Fusion and CMC baselines, MER-DG achieves substantially larger gains (3.73–7.73% on Fusion; 0.57–1.78% on CMC), confirming that the method's benefit scales with the severity of Fusion Overfitting in the base architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Log-Determinant entropy estimator from information-maximization and self-supervised learning (Liu et al., 2022; Erdogan, 2022) to address a distinct failure mode in multimodal domain generalization. Prior MMDG methods—RNA-Net (norm alignment), SimMMDG (shared/specific decomposition), CMRF (loss-landscape flattening), and MBCD (collaborative distillation)—target optimization dynamics or representation structure but do not address the specific mechanism the paper identifies: joint fusion training causing each encoder to discard domain-invariant features in favor of source-specific cross-modal co-occurrences. MER-DG is positioned as an orthogonal, additive regularizer that can be layered on top of any of these methods, and the paper validates this by integrating it into four distinct baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Fusion Overfitting as a named, mechanistically explained failure mode—with converging evidence from CKA alignment, spectral rank, domain classification, and standalone accuracy—gives practitioners a diagnostic tool for when multimodal fusion underperforms expectations. The practical finding that a fused video+audio model can underperform a video-only model (59.09% vs. 59.46%) is a useful warning for system designers. Open questions include whether the effect persists with more than three modalities, in higher-dimensional or transformer-based encoders, and whether the entropy decomposition (marginal + spectral) is optimal compared to alternative diversity objectives like Barlow Twins redundancy reduction. The narrow evaluation scope (two small-scale action-recognition datasets, 8 and 7 classes) limits generalizability claims.
