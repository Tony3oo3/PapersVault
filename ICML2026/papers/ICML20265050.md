## Problem

Attention sinks—tokens accumulating disproportionate attention mass—are treated as load-bearing structures in autoregressive LMs, but whether this intuition transfers to diffusion transformers' bidirectional, non-causal attention remains unresolved. Prior reports of sink-like phenomena in diffusion models are fragmented, rely on fixed-position definitions borrowed from AR settings, and use observational rather than causal interventions, leaving open whether high incoming attention mass indicates functional necessity or merely a replaceable routing pattern.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes text prompts (553 GenEval prompts for primary results, 5,000 COCO captions for corroboration) and produces paired generated images under baseline versus intervention conditions using strict seed-matched inference on SD3 (joint attention over image+text tokens) and SDXL (U-Net self/cross-attention). Beyond the generated images, the method outputs per-head, per-timestep attention mass distributions, sink identification statistics, and paired quality metrics (CLIP-T, ImageReward, HPS-v2, LPIPS, FID_shift) with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, enabling causal attribution of any observed differences to the intervention rather than sampling variance.

## Method

The paper defines attention sinks dynamically as the top-k key positions receiving the largest incoming attention mass, computed separately for each attention head and denoising timestep, rather than assuming a fixed position like index-0. Causal necessity is tested via paired, training-free interventions along two independent pathways: a score-path that adds log η to sink logits (η=0 effectively zeros their attention), and a value-path that replaces sink value vectors with zeros, means, or interpolations. Across 553 GenEval prompts on SD3 with SDXL corroboration, the key findings are: (1) dynamic sinks are not at index-0 (<0.2% overlap), peak in middle layers during early denoising, and are concentrated on text-conditioning tokens (>99.9%); (2) removing these sinks does not degrade CLIP-T, ImageReward, or HPS-v2 at k=1, with only a marginal metric-dependent HPS-v2 boundary under stronger interventions (k≥10); and (3) sink suppression induces perceptual shifts ~6× larger than equal-budget random masking, revealing an empirical dissociation between trajectory-level perturbation and semantic alignment robustness.

## Results

Across 553 GenEval prompts on Stable Diffusion 3, dynamically identified attention sinks (top-1 per head, per timestep) are removed with >10⁸× reduction in incoming mass, yet CLIP-T shows no significant degradation (Δ = −0.5×10⁻³, 95% CI [−1.7, +0.7], p = 0.44 for single-layer L12; Δ = +0.9×10⁻³, p = 0.08 for multi-layer L6+12+18). HPS-v2 and ImageReward likewise show no significant change at k=1. Under stronger top-5 removal, CLIP-T remains within the practical equivalence margin (Δ = +1.1×10⁻³) while HPS-v2 exhibits a small negative shift (Δ = −0.0020, p = 0.001). Sink masking induces ~6× larger perceptual drift (LPIPS 0.347) than equal-budget random masking (LPIPS 0.053) at k=1, revealing a sink-specific trajectory perturbation that does not affect alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a causal analysis paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; the key contrast is between sink-specific masking and equal-budget random masking. At k=1, sink masking produces LPIPS of 0.347 versus 0.053 for random masking (ΔΔ = +0.295, p < 0.0001), a ~6× gap that widens to ΔΔ = +0.332 at k=5, confirming the perceptual shift is sink-specific rather than an artifact of removing any token. Cross-architecture validation on SDXL (N=100) confirms the non-necessity finding: both self-attention (ΔCLIP-T = +0.0004, CI includes zero) and cross-attention (ΔCLIP-T = −0.0003, CI includes zero) show no alignment degradation, with cross-attention inducing larger perceptual drift (LPIPS 0.077 vs. 0.045). Under stronger interventions (k ≥ 10), HPS-v2 reveals a sink-specific degradation (ΔΔ = −0.005 at k=10, p = 0.007; ΔΔ = −0.020 at k=50, p < 10⁻⁴) that is dose-dependent, while CLIP-T remains stable across all budgets (k ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20, 50}, all CIs include zero). The paper also validates alignment robustness with BLIP2-VQA (Δ = +0.0001, p = 0.97), a non-CLIP compositional probe, further supporting that the non-necessity finding is not an artifact of the CLIP embedding space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-sink literature from autoregressive LMs (where sinks are fixed-position, e.g., BOS tokens, and are treated as load-bearing for KV caching and entropy stability) to diffusion transformers, where the authors show sinks are dynamic, phase-dependent, and concentrated on text-conditioning tokens rather than index-0. It departs from prior fragmented, observational reports of sink-like phenomena in diffusion models (Arriola et al. 2025; Rulli et al. 2025; Wen et al. 2025) by providing the first systematic causal analysis via paired, training-free interventions along both score and value paths. The work also positions itself relative to attention sparsification methods for DiT (Wang et al. 2024; Yuan et al. 2024; Ren et al. 2025), which make implicit assumptions about preserving high-mass recipients, and to attention-editing methods (Chefer et al. 2023; Hertz et al. 2022) that modify dominant attention for controllability, clarifying that usefulness for optimization does not imply necessity for alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that dynamically identified attention sinks are dispensable for semantic alignment in diffusion transformers provides the causal prerequisite for safe attention sparsification in DiT architectures, directly informing design choices in efficiency-oriented methods that currently lack grounding for which high-mass recipients to preserve. The empirical dissociation—sinks carry structured trajectory-level information (~6× larger perceptual shift than random masking) while remaining alignment-irrelevant—opens a potential avenue for sink-specific generation steering, though the paper does not pursue this. Key limitations include scoping to proxy metrics (CLIP-T, HPS-v2) rather than fine-grained compositional correctness (Gecko, T2ICompBench), absence of realized efficiency gains, and the metric-dependent HPS-v2 boundary at k≥10 that hints at preference-oriented dimensions not captured by alignment proxies. Whether the same dispensability holds under alternative attention mechanisms (linear attention) or in video diffusion remains open.
