## Problem

Recent multi-modal time series models claim strong performance on complex reasoning and QA tasks, yet no prior benchmark tests whether they can perform the foundational skill of describing structural properties of univariate time series. Existing evaluations either use specialized datasets in near-isolation without head-to-head comparisons, or focus on complex reasoning without isolating basic description capabilities, leaving a critical gap in understanding what these models actually understand about time series.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263998/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes univariate time series in three modality-specific formats: comma-separated numeric strings for LLMs, matplotlib-rendered plot images for VLMs, and raw NumPy arrays for TSLMs. The three tasks produce distinct outputs: a binary True/False judgment (recognition), a single letter A–D selecting the correct description from four options (differentiation), and a free-form natural language description capped at 150 tokens (open generation). The unified dataset contains 46,843 time series–description pairs with sequence lengths ranging from 12 to 2048 steps, 90.44% of which are real-world signals.

## Method

BEDTime unifies five heterogeneous public datasets (TRUCE-Stock, TRUCE-Synthetic, SUSHI, TaxoSynth, NICU-HR) into a single benchmark formatted for three tasks across three modalities, with dataset-specific preprocessing to handle multi-annotation series, class/subclass labels for distractor selection, and mortality-based constraints for NICU-HR. Negative examples for recognition and differentiation are constructed via four dissimilarity strategies—Sentence-BERT cosine similarity, DTW distance, Euclidean distance, and LCSS—ensuring robustness to the choice of sampling method. Evaluation combines accuracy for objective tasks, bidirectional NLI entailment (DeBERTa-based) plus GPT-5-as-judge for generation quality, and six-criterion human annotation (coherence, pattern identification, temporal localization, noise filtering, abstraction, linguistic quality). Six robustness perturbations probe generalization: linear interpolation for length, uniform random masking for missingness, amplitude scaling, additive Gaussian noise at nine levels, controlled plot pixelation for VLMs, and chain-of-thought prompting for LLMs.

## Results

On the Recognition task, GPT-5.4-mini-Vision (VLM) achieves the highest accuracy across all five datasets, reaching 97% on SUSHI and 88% on TRUCE-Synthetic, while the best LLM (GPT-5.4-mini) peaks at 89% on SUSHI. On Differentiation, GPT-5.4-mini-Vision reaches 98% on both SUSHI and NICU-HR, compared to 96% for GPT-5.4-mini (LLM) on NICU-HR. For open-ended generation on SUSHI, GPT-5.4-mini-Vision achieves 16.29% bidirectional NLI entailment and 58.00% Gen→GT entailment, roughly 3× the best LLM (GPT-5.4-mini at 5.43% bidirectional). TSLM ChatTime-7B performs below random (10–13% accuracy) on most datasets due to frequent refusals, while ChatTS-14B reaches 78% on SUSHI Recognition but still trails all VLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLMs consistently outperform their LLM counterparts across all datasets and both Recognition and Differentiation tasks: GPT-5.4-mini-Vision beats GPT-5.4-mini (LLM) by 11 points on TRUCE-Stock Recognition (.79 vs .68) and 14 points on SUSHI (.97 vs .89), while Gemini-3.0-Vision edges Gemini-3.0 (LLM) by 8 points on TRUCE-Stock (.73 vs .65). Among TSLMs, ChatTS-14B outperforms comparably-sized LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B) on SUSHI Recognition (.78 vs .51) and TaxoSynth (.77 vs .59), but is still surpassed by all top VLMs. ChatTime-7B performs catastrophically, with below-random accuracy (10–13%) on TRUCE-Stock and TRUCE-Synthetic due to frequent model refusals, making it the weakest model evaluated. On open-ended generation (SUSHI), GPT-5.4-mini-Vision achieves 58% Gen→GT NLI entailment versus 35.2% for GPT-5.4-mini (LLM) and 23.5% for ChatTS-14B, confirming the visual modality's advantage. Human evaluation further shows GPT-4o-Vision scoring 0.891 on Pattern Identification versus 0.361 for GPT-4o-Text and 0.738 for ChatTS-14B, with all models achieving perfect Coherence (1.00). The LLMTime prompting strategy (GPT-4o+LT) provides only marginal gains over vanilla GPT-4o (e.g., .84 vs .74 on SUSHI Recognition), suggesting that specialized prompting cannot close the gap to VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying five previously independent time series datasets (TRUCE-Stock, TRUCE-Synthetic, SUSHI, TaxoSynth, NICU-HR) into a single benchmark structured around three fundamental description tasks—recognition, differentiation, and open-ended generation—rather than the complex reasoning or QA formulations that dominate prior benchmarks like TimeSeriesExam or TRUCE. It departs from the prevailing evaluation paradigm of near-isolated model testing by enabling direct head-to-head comparisons across three modalities (LLMs, VLMs, TSLMs) that prior benchmarks never jointly evaluated. The paper also introduces systematic robustness testing (length, missingness, noise, amplitude, image quality, CoT) that no prior time series-language benchmark included.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEDTime provides the first standardized, extensible framework for evaluating whether multi-modal time series models can perform the foundational description skills that underpin all more complex reasoning tasks, and its findings—that VLMs outperform dedicated TSLMs and that all models are fragile to realistic perturbations—directly challenge the field's assumption that purpose-built time series encoders are necessary for structural understanding. Key open questions include whether the VLM advantage persists on multivariate or domain-specific signals, whether TSLMs can be improved to match VLM performance, and how to build automated evaluation that is less sensitive to numerical inputs than general-purpose NLI models. The benchmark's release of code and data positions it as a likely standard reference for future time series-language model development.
