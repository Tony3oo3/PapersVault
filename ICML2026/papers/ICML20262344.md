## Problem

Latent actions learned from single-view videos for VLA pretraining are not sufficiently action-centric: they encode viewpoint-specific cues (camera motion, appearance shifts) alongside the underlying robot actions, reducing their mutual information with ground-truth actions and limiting their utility as pseudo-labels. Prior LAMs lack an explicit mechanism to disentangle action-driven transitions from viewpoint-driven appearance changes, especially problematic for egocentric human videos with substantial viewpoint variation.

## Input / Output

MVP-LAM takes time-synchronized multi-view video frames (224×224) and extracts DINOv2 feature-space observations at a fixed temporal stride H; the encoder consumes consecutive observation pairs (o_t, o_{t+1}) from each view to produce a discrete latent action token (4 codes from a 16-entry codebook, 128-dim each). For downstream VLA use, the pretrained VLM takes a single RGB image plus a language instruction and outputs the predicted latent action token, which is then decoded via multi-head attention into a continuous 7-D robot action over a horizon of H steps.

## Method

MVP-LAM is a VQ-VAE trained on multi-view videos with a cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective: for two synchronized views, the decoder must reconstruct each view's next-frame feature both from its own latent (self-viewpoint) and from the other view's latent (cross-viewpoint), i.e., D(o_t^v, z_t^{v̄}) ≈ o_{t+1}^v for v ≠ v̄. Because the decoder is not conditioned on the latent's source viewpoint, any viewpoint-specific factor encoded in z_t^v would increase the cross-viewpoint loss, so minimizing L_cross forces the latent to retain only shared, action-relevant transition information. The paper derives a lower bound I(Z;A) ≥ H(Z) − I(Z;V,V′|S,S′) − C, showing that reducing viewpoint information in the latent directly increases action information under a fixed-capacity VQ bottleneck. The architecture follows UniVLA (frozen DINOv2 → spatiotemporal transformer encoder → VQ quantization → decoder), trained on 312k trajectories from OXE multi-view robot data and EgoExo4D human videos. For VLA pretraining, a Prismatic-7B VLM is trained with cross-entropy to predict MVP-LAM tokens from image+language, then finetuned with LoRA and a multi-head attention action decoder to output continuous robot actions.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark, MVP-LAM achieves 60.4% average success rate and 75.0% grasping rate, substantially outperforming UniVLA (39.6% / 65.6%) and LAPA (57.3% / 71.9%). On LIBERO, the VLA pretrained with MVP-LAM reaches 94.1% average success rate, surpassing UniVLA (92.5%) and remaining competitive with the state-of-the-art π₀ (94.2%) while using only single-view observations and ≤60k pretraining trajectories versus π₀'s additional wrist-view and proprioceptive inputs. In terms of action-centricity, MVP-LAM achieves 62% higher mutual information I(Z;A) and 12% lower linear-probe NMSE with ground-truth actions on Bridge V2 compared to the prior LAM (UniVLA), and maintains these advantages under out-of-distribution LIBERO evaluation and viewpoint perturbation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniVLA (the primary LAM baseline sharing the same VLM backbone and finetuning pipeline), MVP-LAM improves SIMPLER average success rate from 39.6% to 60.4% (+20.8 pp) and LIBERO average from 92.5% to 94.1% (+1.6 pp), while also achieving higher mutual information and lower linear-probe NMSE on both in-distribution (Bridge V2) and out-of-distribution (LIBERO) evaluations. Against LAPA, MVP-LAM wins on SIMPLER average success (60.4% vs 57.3%) and LIBERO average (94.1% vs 65.7%), though LAPA edges out on LIBERO-Goal linear probing. Against OpenVLA and Octo (non-latent-action VLA baselines), MVP-LAM achieves substantially higher success rates on both SIMPLER (60.4% vs 36.4% / 17.7–19.8%) and LIBERO (94.1% vs 76.5% / 75.1%). Against the state-of-the-art π₀, MVP-LAM is essentially tied on LIBERO (94.1% vs 94.2%) but wins decisively on SIMPLER (60.4% vs 36.5%), while using only single-view RGB without wrist cameras or proprioception. Against Moto, MVP-LAM achieves higher MI and lower NMSE on Bridge V2, though Moto's larger codebook (K=128, L=8) gives it a slight edge on LIBERO-Goal linear probing in the extended evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVP-LAM is a direct architectural modification of UniVLA (Bu et al., 2025), adopting its VQ-VAE latent action encoder-decoder and VLM pretraining pipeline, but replacing the standard self-reconstruction objective with a cross-viewpoint reconstruction loss trained on time-synchronized multi-view videos. This departs from the entire prior line of latent action models (LAPA, Moto, UniVLA, Genie), which all train on single-view video and are therefore vulnerable to encoding viewpoint-specific cues as spurious action signals. The paper also positions itself against exogenous-noise mitigation methods (LAOM, object-centric slot decomposition, VLM-based distractor filtering) that require additional supervision or external model quality, arguing that the cross-viewpoint objective achieves action-centricity without any of these dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-viewpoint reconstruction provides a simple, supervision-free mechanism to make latent actions more informative about ground-truth robot actions, and the ablation cleanly isolates that the objective (not merely multi-view data) is responsible for the gain. Practically, this translates to a 3× reduction in pretraining data needed for competitive VLA performance on LIBERO and SIMPLER, which is significant given the cost of robot data collection. Key limitations include the requirement for time-synchronized multi-view capture (additional instrumentation), simulation-only VLA evaluation without real-robot experiments, and weaker OOD linear-probing performance on LIBERO-Goal, suggesting that scaling to larger multi-view robot datasets (e.g., DROID, AgiBot) and relaxing synchronization requirements are important next steps.
