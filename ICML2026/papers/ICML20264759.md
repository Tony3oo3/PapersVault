## Problem

Greedy regression trees are fast but can deviate substantially from optimal solutions, while provably optimal methods based on dynamic programming and branch-and-bound are computationally prohibitive for general piecewise linear regression trees, especially with continuous features. The paper addresses the open challenge of scalable algorithms that combine the efficiency of greedy induction with the accuracy of near-optimal search for linear-leaf regression trees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data with a mix of continuous and binary features plus a continuous target, where binary features are used exclusively for splitting and continuous features serve both as split predicates and leaf-regression covariates. It outputs a depth-bounded piecewise linear regression tree in which each leaf holds a ridge-regularized linear model (with an unpenalized intercept), yielding interpretable scalar predictions. The tree's sparsity is controlled by a structural leaf-penalty parameter λ, and the number of candidate thresholds per continuous feature (T) is a tunable hyperparameter that trades off search breadth against runtime.

## Method

CLARITree combines a one-step lookahead split-selection strategy with incremental rank-one Cholesky updates of the regularized Gram matrix. At each node, the algorithm enumerates all candidate feature-threshold splits via a linear sweep, maintaining left and right child Cholesky factors through O(k²) rank-one updates/downdates as samples are transferred, rather than refitting O(k³) regressions from scratch. For each candidate split, the remaining subtree is completed by a greedy CholeskyTree subroutine, and the split minimizing the completed objective is selected before recursively applying CLARITree to the children. This design handles continuous features directly without binarization (saving a factor of T in split-evaluation cost), achieves a total runtime of O(kn log n + d²nk⁴T), and provably dominates the greedy baseline while remaining within ~0.01 R² of the optimal solver at over two orders of magnitude speedup.

## Results

On 16 regression datasets (depth=4, 20 thresholds), CLARITree achieves the best or tied-best test R² on nearly all datasets, with the optimal solver STreeD occasionally edging ahead by ≤0.01 R² on small/medium-scale data (e.g., Airfoil: 0.88 vs 0.89; Auto MPG: 0.84 vs 0.85). On large-scale datasets where STreeD exceeds the 10-minute time limit, CLARITree clearly wins: California Housing R²=0.75 vs STreeD 0.70, Temperature (Max) R²=0.88 vs STreeD 0.82, Seoul Bike R²=0.72 vs STreeD 0.69. CLARITree achieves over two orders of magnitude speedup relative to the optimal solver on most datasets while remaining within ~0.01 R² of optimal, and consistently outperforms greedy baselines GUIDE, PILOT, and M5 by 0.02–0.37 R² depending on dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the optimal dynamic-programming solver STreeD (Van Den Bos et al., 2024), CLARITree is within ≤0.01 R² on small/medium-scale datasets where STreeD completes within 10 minutes (e.g., Airfoil 0.88 vs 0.89, Auto MPG 0.84 vs 0.85), but clearly outperforms it on all five large-scale datasets where STreeD hits the time limit (California Housing 0.75 vs 0.70, Temperature Max 0.88 vs 0.82, Seoul Bike 0.72 vs 0.69), achieving over two orders of magnitude speedup. The restricted variant STreeD-S (univariate leaf models) is consistently 0.04–0.12 R² below CLARITree on most datasets. Against greedy baselines, CLARITree outperforms GUIDE by 0.02–0.37 R² (e.g., Optical Net 0.92 vs 0.80, Airfoil 0.88 vs 0.84), PILOT by 0.03–0.37 R² (e.g., Airfoil 0.88 vs 0.51, Temperature Max 0.88 vs 0.78), and M5 by 0.02–0.34 R² (e.g., Airfoil 0.88 vs 0.54, Temperature Max 0.88 vs 0.72). The only dataset where CLARITree does not achieve the best R² is Servo (n=167), where all methods show high variance and PILOT/M5 marginally edge ahead (0.52 vs 0.51), a difference well within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLARITree extends the SPLIT lookahead framework (Babbar et al., 2025), which was developed for classification trees, to the fundamentally harder setting of piecewise linear regression with continuous features. The key technical departure is the use of rank-one Cholesky updates to the regularized Gram matrix, which replaces the expensive per-node ridge regression refitting that has historically limited optimal and near-optimal regression tree methods (STreeD, Zhang et al.) to constant-leaf or single-feature-linear settings. The greedy completion subroutine (CholeskyTree) is positioned as a principled alternative to M5/GUIDE/PILOT, differing in that split selection is driven by the downstream ridge-regularized objective rather than node means or variance reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CLARITree achieves near-optimal test R² (within ~0.01 of the exact solver) while running 2+ orders of magnitude faster, and reliably completes depth-4 models on large datasets (20k+ samples) where the optimal solver STreeD cannot finish even given 64 hours. This makes high-quality interpretable regression trees accessible for real-world tabular problems where exact search is intractable. Open questions include extending the framework to approximate the full Rashomon set of near-optimal trees, incorporating accelerated depth-two solvers for final split decisions, and whether the lookahead depth can be increased without prohibitive cost.
