## Problem

LLMs generate numbers as discrete token sequences optimized with cross-entropy, which fails to encode numerical proximity and produces brittle regression predictions. Existing regression-aware fine-tuning methods (e.g., RAFT) address this but require full-parameter updates that are computationally expensive and prone to catastrophic forgetting in low-resource biomedical settings, while standard PEFT methods (LoRA, P-Tuning) optimize generic next-token likelihood without any mechanism for continuous-target alignment. SPR-RAFT closes this gap by making regression-awareness explicit under a strict parameter budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous biomedical evidence serialized into a single text sequence (trial protocols with ICD-10 codes and SMILES strings, NHANES lab panels with units and NA markers, sentence pairs, or bare SMILES), prepended with a 32-token learnable soft prompt. The model outputs a [THINK] rationale span followed by a [REG] anchor token and a canonical numeric string under a fixed rendering policy (e.g., 2-decimal fixed-point for TrialBench, 1-decimal on [0,4] for BIOSSES). At inference, two predictive distributions—a probability-weighted candidate-set estimate and a heteroscedastic Gaussian from the regression head—are fused via correlation-aware GLS to yield a final scalar prediction with calibrated variance.

## Method

SPR-RAFT decouples information routing from value extraction within a frozen LLM backbone. A learnable soft prompt (m=32 continuous tokens) conditions the frozen transformer to compress task-relevant evidence into the hidden state at a dedicated [REG] readout token, while a lightweight two-layer MLP head (d→d/4→1) on that hidden state predicts a mean and log-variance. Training jointly optimizes three losses: a Huber loss on the distribution-based branch (which scores K=32 candidate numeric strings via teacher-forced log-likelihood and computes probability-weighted moments in value space), a heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL on the head branch, and an optional cross-entropy on rationale/numeric tokens. At inference, the two branches are fused using a Generalized Least Squares formulation with a learnable correlation coefficient ρ (constrained to (0,1) via sigmoid), which prevents the variance collapse that naive inverse-variance weighting suffers from when both branches share the same frozen backbone. Only ~1.72M parameters (~0.04% of the 4B Qwen3 backbone) are updated, making the adapter a hot-swappable ~3.5 MB (BF16) module per task.

## Results

On TrialBench Duration (Phase I), SPR-RAFT achieves MAE 0.70±0.03, RMSE 1.08±0.06, and R² 0.73, a 25.5% relative MAE reduction over the strongest LLM baseline (RAFT: MAE 0.94). On BIOSSES, SPR-RAFT reaches Spearman ρ = 0.95 and Pearson r = 0.93, matching or exceeding the specialized BERT-based system (0.91/0.92). On molecular property prediction, SPR-RAFT attains RMSE of 0.455 on FreeSolv (47.9% reduction over the best LLM baseline) and 0.664 on Lipophilicity, surpassing graph-based specialists like Graphormer (2.210/0.740). Across all benchmarks, only ~1.6M parameters (~0.04% of the 4B backbone) are trained.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPR-RAFT consistently outperforms RAFT (Lukasik et al., 2025) across all benchmarks: 25.5% MAE reduction on TrialBench Duration (Phase I), +9% AUC on NHANES (0.84 vs 0.75), +6.7% Spearman ρ on BIOSSES (0.95 vs 0.89), and 47.9% RMSE reduction on FreeSolv (0.455 vs 1.881). Against LoRA fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021), SPR-RAFT achieves substantially better results on every task (e.g., NHANES AUC 0.84 vs 0.60; BIOSSES Pearson r 0.93 vs 0.66). On TrialBench, SPR-RAFT surpasses the multimodal deep learning baseline from Chen et al. (2024) on all phases (e.g., Phase III Duration MAE 1.15 vs 1.44). On BIOSSES, SPR-RAFT matches or slightly exceeds the specialized BERT-based system (Sogancioglu et al., 2017) with Pearson r 0.93 vs 0.92. On molecular property prediction, SPR-RAFT outperforms all graph-based models (GIN, Graphormer), zero-shot molecular LLMs (GIMLET), and recent specialist molecular LLMs (Mol-LLM, LlaSMol, InstructMol, BioT5+), achieving the lowest RMSE on ESOL (0.597), FreeSolv (0.455), and Lipophilicity (0.664). On NHANES, SPR-RAFT (AUC 0.84) remains slightly below XGBoost (0.87) and FT-Transformer (0.86), acknowledging that purely tabular settings still favor dedicated tabular models, but it is the best-performing LLM-based method by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPR-RAFT sits at the intersection of two lines of work: regression-aware LLM fine-tuning (RAFT, Lukasik et al. 2025), which aligns token-level probability distributions with continuous regression error via Bayesian decision theory, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (P-Tuning, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning), which freezes the backbone and optimizes a small set of conditioning parameters. The key departure is that RAFT requires full-parameter fine-tuning while standard PEFT methods optimize generic next-token likelihood without any mechanism for numerical proximity. SPR-RAFT bridges this gap by combining a soft prompt (from the P-Tuning lineage) with a dedicated [REG]-anchored regression head and a dual-branch loss (Huber on probability-space moments + heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL on representation-space), plus a correlation-aware GLS fusion that accounts for shared-backbone error correlation between the two branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the extreme parameter efficiency (~0.04% of a 4B backbone, ~1.6 MB adapter) enabling hot-swappable task-specific regressors on a single shared frozen model, which is attractive for resource-constrained biomedical deployment. Open questions include: (i) the distribution-based branch's dependence on a predefined candidate grid, which can bias estimates under heavy-tailed or out-of-distribution targets; (ii) inference latency from scoring K=32 candidates, which grows with context length; and (iii) whether the Gaussian uncertainty assumption remains calibrated under distribution shift. The paper honestly acknowledges that on purely tabular benchmarks (NHANES), dedicated tabular models like XGBoost still outperform, limiting the method's advantage to heterogeneous and unstructured evidence settings.
