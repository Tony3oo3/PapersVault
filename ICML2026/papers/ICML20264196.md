## Problem

Classical extragradient methods for monotone variational inequalities require two gradient evaluations per iteration and achieve only a suboptimal O(1/k) last-iterate rate, while optimistic single-call methods lack acceleration. In the stochastic setting, no existing single-call method provides a last-iterate convergence guarantee for unconstrained monotone Lipschitz VIs without relying on variance reduction or growing batch sizes, leaving a gap between the one-sample online model and provable last-iterate convergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monotone L-Lipschitz operator G: R^d → R^d (or a stochastic oracle Ĝ(x,ξ) satisfying E[‖Ĝ‖²|x] ≤ σ² + κ‖G(x)‖² for κ ≥ 1), an initial point x₀, and a single scalar step-size hyperparameter (η* or γ*). It produces the last iterate x_k with a convergence guarantee on the squared operator norm ‖G(x_k)‖², using exactly one operator evaluation per iteration. The analysis is in the unconstrained R^d setting, measuring progress via the residual norm rather than the gap function, which is the natural stationarity measure when the domain is unbounded.

## Method

GOMA augments the generalized optimistic method (which uses separate step sizes γ_k and η_k for prediction and correction) with a Halpern-type anchoring term β_k(x₀ − x_k) that injects artificial strong monotonicity into the operator, accelerating convergence while vanishing asymptotically. Two parameter schedules are analyzed: a fixed update step η* with exploration scaled by (1−β_k), and a fixed exploration step γ* with update scaled by (1−β_k), both yielding the optimal O(1/k²) deterministic last-iterate rate. For the stochastic setting, a simplified variant sets γ_k = 0, eliminating past-gradient reuse and reducing the update to a single forward step at the anchored interpolation point y_k = β_k x₀ + (1−β_k) x_k. The key stochastic mechanism is that anchoring contracts the deviation from a deterministic reference trajectory by a factor (1−β_k) at each step, keeping accumulated noise at O(1/√N) with a single sample per iteration and no variance reduction, yielding an O(1/√k) last-iterate rate on E‖G(x_k)‖² under state-dependent noise with κ ≥ 1.

## Results

In the deterministic monotone Lipschitz setting, GOMA achieves the optimal O(1/k²) last-iterate rate on the squared operator norm ‖G(x_k)‖², with an explicit bound of 264L²/(k+6)²·‖x₀−x⋆‖² at the largest admissible step size η*=1/(2√3·L). In the stochastic setting with state-dependent noise (κ≥1), the simplified single-call GOMA variant achieves E‖G(x_N)‖² ≤ (1570L²κ‖x₀−x⋆‖² + 8σ²/κ)/√(N+1), i.e., an O(1/√k) rate on the squared residual (equivalently O(1/k^{1/4}) on E‖G(x_k)‖), corresponding to O(1/ε⁴) SFO complexity. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first last-iterate guarantee in squared operator norm for unconstrained stochastic monotone Lipschitz VIs without variance reduction or growing batches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOMA is the only method in Table 1 that simultaneously satisfies all seven desiderata: unbounded domain, single-call, no variance reduction, no growing batch, and tolerance of unbounded state-dependent noise (κ>1). Against DSEG, GOMA matches the O(k^{-1/2}) deterministic rate while being single-call and extending beyond the affine-only stochastic guarantee. Against FEG, GOMA matches the O(k^{-1}) deterministic rate but is single-call and does not require growing batches (FEG's stochastic guarantee needs σ_k=O(1/k) variance decay enforced by increasing minibatches). Against E-Halpern and RAIN++, GOMA trades a faster stochastic rate (O(k^{-1/3}) and Õ(k^{-1/2}) respectively) for the elimination of variance reduction machinery; empirically in the multiplicative-noise experiment (κ>1), E-Halpern and FEG diverge while GOMA converges. In the deterministic quadratic experiment (Figure 1), GOMA and FEG are the only converging methods, with GOMA achieving uniformly smaller residuals than FEG by a roughly constant factor and without oscillations. Against GABP (Abe et al., 2025), the comparison is not directly apples-to-apples since GABP operates on a compact domain with the gap function as progress measure, whereas GOMA works on ℝ^d with the squared operator norm; within their respective regimes GOMA's O(k^{-1/4}) rate on E‖G‖ is faster than GABP's Õ(k^{-1/7}) on E[GAP].

## Relation to Prior Work

GOMA unifies three previously separate threads in variational inequality algorithms: the two-time-scale optimistic update (Mokhtari et al., 2020; Hsieh et al., 2020), Halpern-type anchoring for acceleration (Halpern, 1967; Diakonikolas, 2020; Yoon & Ryu, 2021; Tran-Dinh & Luo, 2021), and the single-query optimistic gradient method (Popov, 1980). In the deterministic setting, GOMA matches the O(1/k²) last-iterate rate already achieved by EAG, FEG, and anchored Popov, but with a simpler pseudo-fixed-step-size schedule. The key departure is in the stochastic setting: by dropping the optimistic extrapolation entirely (γ_k=0) and relying solely on anchoring for stability, GOMA achieves the first last-iterate guarantee for stochastic monotone Lipschitz VIs that requires neither variance reduction nor growing batches, and tolerates unbounded state-dependent noise (κ>1) where prior single-call methods (E-Halpern, RAIN++) assume bounded variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that strong last-iterate convergence is compatible with the one-sample-per-round online model under unbounded noise, which is the natural regime for adversarial training, multi-agent RL, and online learning. The main limitation is the O(1/ε⁴) SFO complexity, which is a factor of ε² away from the O(1/ε²) lower bound established by Chen & Luo (2024) and attained by variance-reduced, growing-batch methods; closing this gap without those mechanisms is explicitly identified as open. Additional open directions include extending the analysis to constrained settings (where the progress measure changes from ‖G‖² to the gap function), negative comonotone operators, and scaling to large-scale RL and adversarial training where stochasticity and gradient efficiency are central concerns.
