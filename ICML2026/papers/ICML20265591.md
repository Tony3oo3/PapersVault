## Problem

Existing replay-based federated continual learning (FCL) methods generate synthetic data using only generic signals such as class labels or normalizing flows, ignoring the spatial relationships across clients and temporal evolution of data distributions that are inherent in FCL settings. This oversight leads to models biased toward specific regional or temporal manifestations of a task, limiting generalization in real-world scenarios like geographically varying disease outbreaks.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a raw data sample (28×28 images, 32×32 images, 64×64 images, 18,000-dim gene expression vectors, or 49-dim ILI time-series) together with the client's row of the spatio-temporal relational graph as a conditioning vector. The generator takes random Gaussian noise, a target class label, and the corresponding graph row to produce synthetic latent representations for replay. The server-side discriminator outputs a predicted relational graph (N×N matrix) rather than a conventional real/fake scalar, and the final model outputs class labels or regression predictions.

## Method

GFedCL constructs a spatio-temporal relational graph on the server by computing pairwise attention scores between clients' classifier updates (spatial attention via GAT) and historical graph patterns over a sliding window (temporal attention), then taking their element-wise product as edge weights. This graph conditions a dual-component adversarial framework: local encoder-generator-predictor triplets on each client, and a global discriminator on the server that is trained to reconstruct the relational graph from noised embeddings rather than classify real vs. fake. The minimax objective forces the encoder and generator to produce structurally invariant representations whose conditional expectation of the graph equals the marginal expectation, achieving first-moment alignment between real and synthetic distributions at equilibrium. Differential privacy is enforced by adding Laplace noise to transmitted encodings and graphs, and theoretical analysis shows this noise does not alter the equilibrium point.

## Results

GFedCL achieves the best accuracy across all five datasets: 77.23% on EMNIST-Letters, 73.67% on CIFAR-100, 77.39% on TinyImageNet, 75.32% on TCGA-BRCA, and 2.41×10⁻² MSE on US-States. The headline result is a 27.95 percentage-point accuracy gain over AFL (49.44%) on TinyImageNet, and an 18.34-point gain over AFL (55.33%) on CIFAR-100. Forgetting scores remain competitive (3.34% on EMNIST-Letters, 4.24% on CIFAR-100), confirming that the spatio-temporal graph-guided replay effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting without sacrificing retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFedCL outperforms all six baselines on every dataset. On EMNIST-Letters, it leads AFL (75.17%) by 2.06 points and C²Prompt (68.47%) by 8.76 points. On CIFAR-100, the gap is dramatic: GFedCL (73.67%) surpasses the next-best C²Prompt (67.93%) by 5.74 points and AFL (55.33%) by 18.34 points. On TinyImageNet, GFedCL (77.39%) beats C²Prompt (72.41%) by 4.98 points and AF-FCL (54.91%) by 22.48 points; the paper highlights a 27.95-point margin over AFL (49.44%). On TCGA-BRCA, GFedCL (75.32%) edges out C²Prompt (72.48%) by 2.84 points and AFL (68.48%) by 6.84 points. On the US-States regression task, GFedCL achieves the lowest MSE (2.41) versus AFL (2.62) and MFCL (2.74). Notably, replay-based baselines (FedCIL, MFCL, AF-FCL) consistently underperform non-replay methods (AFL, C²Prompt), underscoring the paper's argument that naive replay without spatio-temporal structure is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends replay-based federated continual learning (FCL) by introducing spatio-temporal relational graphs as a conditioning signal for adversarial synthetic data generation. Prior replay-based FCL methods (AF-FCL, FedCIL, MFCL) condition generation only on class labels or use generic generative models (normalizing flows), ignoring the spatial relationships across clients and temporal evolution across tasks that are inherent to the federated setting. GFedCL departs from standard GAN design by replacing the conventional real/fake discriminator with one that predicts relational graph structures, and it draws on graph attention networks (GAT) and temporal attention mechanisms to construct these graphs from client model updates. The theoretical analysis builds on BiGAN-style equilibrium arguments but adapts them to the graph-prediction objective and incorporates differential privacy noise into the equilibrium characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in FCL: existing replay methods discard spatial and temporal structure that is readily available in federated settings, and the 27.95% accuracy gain on TinyImageNet (over a weak best baseline of ~49%) demonstrates the practical value of exploiting this information. The theoretical guarantee that DP noise does not alter the equilibrium point is a useful result for privacy-preserving federated generative models more broadly. However, the analysis is limited to first-moment (mean) alignment, the non-convex optimization may not reach the characterized equilibrium in practice, and the evaluation is confined to relatively small datasets (≤200 classes) with a limited set of baselines, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more heterogeneous federated deployments.
