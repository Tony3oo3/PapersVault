## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit two predominant failure modes in contextual reasoning: context neglect, where models rely on pre-trained knowledge instead of information provided in context, and superficial pattern matching, where models exploit shallow correlations rather than genuine underlying patterns. Existing rule-based RL approaches that improve mathematical and scientific reasoning show negligible gains on contextual reasoning tasks, indicating a fundamental gap in how current training paradigms address context-dependent inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of K context examples (image-text pairs, where outputs can be empty) and a query, and must produce a structured reasoning chain following an <analyze>–<solve>–<answer> format. The benchmark comprises 16 tasks across three categories—rule-based, knowledge-based, and preference-based reasoning—each designed so that correct answers cannot be obtained from pre-trained knowledge alone. Visual inputs are capped at 401,408 pixels, and tasks span diverse scenarios including visual pattern recognition, procedural instruction following, anomaly detection, sign language understanding, and personalized recommendation.

## Method

CAR employs a two-stage training strategy on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Stage 1 uses supervised fine-tuning on 388 high-quality samples to establish an 'analyze-then-solve' reasoning paradigm, directly addressing context neglect. Stage 2 applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning with two key innovations: (1) a context-aware reward that replaces the original multimodal context with the model's own generated context analysis and checks whether the model can still solve the problem from that extracted information alone, thereby validating genuine pattern capture; and (2) hierarchical advantage estimation that constructs semantically equivalent context variants (via example permutation or visual augmentation) and computes advantages both within each variant (local) and across all variants (global), encouraging invariance to irrelevant surface variations. The total reward combines format, accuracy, and context-aware components with a correctness-gating mechanism that grants the context-aware reward only when the model answers correctly in both the reasoning and verification steps, preventing reinforcement of superficial patterns.

## Results

CAR-7B achieves 38.14% overall accuracy on ContextReasonV-Bench, a +22.09% improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (16.05%). Gains are particularly strong on knowledge-based reasoning (+28.57%, from 14.05% to 42.62%) and preference-based reasoning (+20.34%, from 22.33% to 42.67%), with rule-based reasoning improving by +11.83% (14.37% → 26.20%). On general vision tasks, CAR-7B also improves over the base model, reaching 49.60% average (vs. 45.58%), with notable gains on MMMU-Pro (+7.72%) and BLINK (+6.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAR-7B (38.14%) substantially outperforms all open-source MLLMs, including the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (32.56%), and decisively beats rule-based RL models Vision-R1-7B (14.85%) and MM-Eukera-7B (16.38%), confirming that standard RL on mathematical reasoning does not transfer to contextual reasoning. Against proprietary models, CAR-7B falls short of Gemini-2.5-Pro-Thinking (63.92%), o4-mini (42.76%), and Claude-4-Sonnet-Thinking (42.11%), and is marginally below GPT-4o (39.39%) and Qwen3-VL-Plus (48.07%). The SFT-only variant (CAR-SFT, 27.68%) already surpasses the base model by +11.63%, but the full two-stage CAR-7B adds another +10.46% over SFT, with the RL stage contributing the largest gains on knowledge-based (+12.24%) and preference-based (+11.29%) tasks. On general vision benchmarks, CAR-7B improves over the base model across all eight tasks (e.g., MMMU-Pro +7.72%, BLINK +6.06%, HallusionBench +3.83%), indicating that contextual reasoning training does not degrade general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of RL-for-multimodal-reasoning (Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, VLM-R1) and multimodal in-context learning (VL-ICL Bench, SMMILE, CAMA), but carves a distinct niche by targeting *contextual reasoning*—the ability to extract and apply latent patterns from provided context—rather than zero-shot reasoning or simple in-context pattern matching. It departs from prior RL-for-reasoning work by demonstrating that rule-based RL on mathematical/scientific tasks does not transfer to contextual reasoning, and from in-context learning work by requiring multi-hop, cross-example pattern induction across three reasoning types (rule-based, knowledge-based, preference-based) where pre-trained knowledge is explicitly insufficient. The context-aware reward (verifying that extracted context alone suffices for correct answers) and hierarchical advantage estimation over semantically equivalent context variants are novel twists on GRPO that have no direct precedent in the cited literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest proprietary model (Gemini-2.5-Pro-Thinking) achieves only 63.92%, and all open-source models fall below 33%, indicating contextual reasoning remains a significant unsolved capability gap. The finding that rule-based RL models show negligible or negative improvement on contextual reasoning challenges the assumption that general reasoning RL transfers broadly, suggesting task-specific training signals are necessary. Open questions include whether the two-stage approach scales to larger models or more diverse context types, whether the 16-task benchmark adequately captures the full spectrum of real-world contextual reasoning (e.g., multi-turn dialogue, long-horizon agent memory), and whether the low absolute performance (38.14%) reflects inherent task difficulty or insufficient training data (only 1.7k samples).
