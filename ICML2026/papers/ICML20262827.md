## Problem

Existing unsupervised camouflaged object detection methods generate pseudo labels through simple post-processing of DINO features, producing masks that are excessively noisy and coarse. These low-quality labels cause the model to learn erroneous information and are fundamentally incapable of delivering the fine-grained boundary segmentation that camouflaged objects require, where foreground and background are visually nearly indistinguishable.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images resized to 476×476 pixels with no pixel-level annotations. In Stage 1, DINOv2-ViT-L14 (frozen) extracts 34×34 spatial feature maps used to build an N×N affinity matrix (N=1156 patches); in Stage 2, the trained network outputs simultaneous pixel-level foreground and background masks at full resolution. The pseudo-label generation is performed offline over the training set (CAMO-Train or COD10K) before any model training begins.

## Method

DualUCOD is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (DESPL) constructs an affinity matrix fusing DINOv2 cosine semantic similarity with a Gaussian color-consistency term (weighted by λ=0.1), derives the symmetric normalized graph Laplacian, and extracts its two leading eigenvectors; an entropy-based criterion selects the more discriminative eigenvector as the primary thresholding cue (via Otsu), while the auxiliary eigenvector refines low-confidence boundary pixels, and multi-augmentation consistency fusion averages masks across T=8 spatially-aligned augmentations. Stage 2 trains a dual-branch network: one branch (BGFBR) refines foreground/background masks by explicitly fusing gradient-magnitude boundary maps from the RGB input with multi-scale features from a Global Perception Module, while the other branch (DBCL) performs contrastive learning between original and photometrically-augmented views using a content-aware soft spatial query and InfoNCE loss to pull same-image representations together and push cross-image representations apart. The total loss combines binary cross-entropy segmentation losses on both original and augmented views plus the contrastive term.

## Results

Under the DINOv2 backbone, DualUCOD achieves the best Fβw on CHAMELEON (0.754), CAMO (0.702), and NC4K (0.745), outperforming EASE by 1.3%, 1.8%, and 1.0% respectively. Under the DINO backbone, DualUCOD leads on CHAMELEON (Fβw=0.643) and CAMO (Fβw=0.629), surpassing UCOD-DPL by 1.8% and 0.8%. On the challenging NC4K dataset with DINOv2, DualUCOD reduces M to 0.052 and achieves Eφ=0.896, outperforming RISE by 0.9% in M and 2.8% in Eφ. Inference time is 0.6 s per image, faster than EASE (1.5 s) but slower than RISE (0.2 s) and SdalsNet (0.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the DINOv2 backbone, DualUCOD outperforms EASE (CVPR'25) on CHAMELEON (Fβw 0.754 vs 0.741), CAMO (0.702 vs 0.684), and NC4K (0.745 vs 0.735), but trails EASE on COD10K (0.635 vs 0.656). Against RISE (ICCV'25), DualUCOD wins on all four datasets in Fβw, with the largest margin on CAMO (0.702 vs 0.610, +9.2%). Under the DINO backbone, DualUCOD surpasses UCOD-DPL (CVPR'25) on CHAMELEON (Fβw 0.643 vs 0.625) and CAMO (0.629 vs 0.621) but falls short on COD10K (0.535 vs 0.577) and NC4K (0.671 vs 0.680). Compared to SdalsNet (AAAI'25) under DINO, DualUCOD improves Fβw by 2.8% on CAMO (0.629 vs 0.601) and by 2.9% on CHAMELEON (0.643 vs 0.612). Against general unsupervised segmentation methods (TokenCut, MaskCut, FOUND, ProMerge), DualUCOD achieves substantially higher Fβw across all datasets, confirming the advantage of task-specific spectral pseudo-labeling over generic clustering approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral segmentation lineage from Shi & Malik's normalized cut formulation and EAGLE's eigen-aggregation approach, adapting both to the specific challenge of camouflaged object detection where foreground-background similarity breaks standard spectral assumptions. Unlike prior UCOD methods (UCOD-DPL, EASE, RISE) that rely on simple DINO feature post-processing or multimodal LLMs, DualUCOD constructs a principled affinity matrix fusing semantic and color cues, then leverages the two leading eigenvectors of the normalized graph Laplacian with entropy-based adaptive selection. The boundary-guided refinement and dual-branch contrastive learning modules are novel architectural additions that go beyond the spectral pseudo-labeling core, positioning the work as a hybrid of classical graph-based segmentation and modern self-supervised representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DESPL strategy is architecture-agnostic (demonstrated by improving SINet by 5.8% in Sα), making it a potentially reusable pseudo-labeling component for any COD model. The theoretical justification in Appendix A.1—showing why exactly two eigenvectors are optimal for binary foreground-background cuts and why V1's magnitude carries discriminative information—provides a clean principled foundation that prior heuristic approaches lacked. However, the method remains limited by DINOv2's spatial resolution for small camouflaged objects, and the 2–4% improvements over strong baselines like UCOD-DPL and EASE, while consistent, do not represent a paradigm shift. Open questions include whether the spectral approach generalizes to multi-object camouflage scenes and whether higher-resolution feature extraction can close the gap on small objects.
