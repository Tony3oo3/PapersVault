## Problem

Existing training-free sparse attention methods for DiT-based video generation suffer from two unresolved limitations: they apply uniform sparsity ratios across all transformer layers, ignoring the pronounced layer-wise heterogeneity in attention pruning tolerance, and they partition queries and keys into blocks independently, ignoring the intrinsic Q-K coupling that determines optimal block alignment. The paper shows that attention sparsity is an intrinsic, input-stable property of each layer, motivating layer-specific and coupling-aware sparsification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263056/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is applied to existing DiT-based video generation models (Wan2.1/2.2 and HunyuanVideo series) without any retraining. For text-to-video, it takes a text prompt; for image-to-video, it takes a reference image plus a text prompt. Output is a generated video at 720p (720×1280) resolution, with 81 frames for Wan-series models and 129 frames for HunyuanVideo-series models, all evaluated on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU.

## Method

SVOO is a two-stage training-free sparse attention framework. In the offline stage, a small calibration set of random inputs is used to measure per-layer, per-head attention density (minimum fraction of entries covering 95% of cumulative attention mass); a Gaussian is fitted to these densities and the upper 95th percentile yields a conservative per-layer sparsity schedule. In the online stage, a bidirectional co-clustering algorithm jointly partitions queries and keys into coupled blocks by alternating between query-aware key clustering and key-aware query clustering, where each token is assigned to the block whose centroid best matches its cross-attention affinity profile to the opposite side. Block-pair selection then uses a threshold-dependent strategy that balances attention recall against the offline sparsity budget, and dense attention is computed only over the top selected block pairs. Clustering results are reused across diffusion steps (recomputed every 20 steps) to amortize overhead, and the entire pipeline is implemented with Triton kernels and FlashInfer dynamic-blocksize sparse attention.

## Results

SVOO achieves up to 1.93× speedup on Wan2.1-1.3B-T2V and 2.17× on HunyuanVideo-13B-T2V while maintaining PSNR of 29.99 dB and 24.88 dB respectively, outperforming all baselines on the speedup metric. On the 1.3B model, SVOO also matches or exceeds the original dense-attention quality (PSNR 29.99 vs. no reference, ImageQual 66.57% vs. 66.58%, AesQual 64.45% vs. 64.47%). Across all seven evaluated models (Wan2.1/2.2 T2V and I2V, HunyuanVideo T2V and I2V), SVOO consistently delivers the highest speedup while ranking first or second on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVOO outperforms all four baselines (SpargeAttn, SVG1, SVG2, Radial) on speedup across every model configuration, achieving 1.61×–2.17× versus 1.38×–1.99× for the runners-up. On the Wan2.1-1.3B-T2V model, SVOO leads SVG2 by 0.20× in speedup (1.93× vs. 1.73×) while also improving ImageQual by 4.74% and AesQual by 4.30%. On the larger Wan2.1-14B and Wan2.2-14B models, SVOO achieves the best PSNR (27.79 and 24.85 dB) and SSIM, surpassing SVG2 by 0.44 and 0.37 dB respectively. On HunyuanVideo-13B-T2V, SVOO delivers the highest speedup (2.17×) with the best ImageQual (67.93%), though SVG1 is slightly faster in raw latency (897s vs. 821s for SVOO) due to a different resolution setting. On the I2V task (Table 2), SVOO similarly dominates: it achieves the best PSNR on Wan2.2-14B-I2V (29.68 dB) and the highest speedup on HunyuanVideo-13B-I2V (2.17×), confirming consistent gains across both text-to-video and image-to-video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVOO sits within the training-free sparse attention family for DiT-based video generation, which itself builds on the observation that attention maps are highly sparse. It departs from prior block-wise methods (SVG, SVG2, SpargeAttention, Radial) along two axes: (1) it replaces uniform cross-layer sparsity with an offline per-layer profiling schedule, and (2) it replaces independent Q/K k-means partitioning with a bidirectional co-clustering algorithm that explicitly enforces Q-K coupling. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2) provides a concentration bound justifying why a single offline calibration suffices, a justification absent from prior empirical-only approaches. Implementation-wise it reuses FlashInfer dynamic-blocksize kernels and Triton, positioning it as a drop-in acceleration layer rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a consistent 1.6–2.2× speedup across seven production video models (Wan2.1/2.2, HunyuanVideo) with near-dense quality (PSNR ≈ 30 dB on the 1.3B model), making it a deployable inference-time optimization without retraining. The layer-wise stability result, backed by both empirical profiling and a theoretical bound, opens the door to pre-computing sparsity schedules for new checkpoints at negligible cost. Open questions include whether the stability property transfers to non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net-based video models), whether the co-clustering overhead remains negligible at 4K or 1000+ frame resolutions, and whether the offline calibration set size can be further reduced without degrading the conservative quantile estimate.
