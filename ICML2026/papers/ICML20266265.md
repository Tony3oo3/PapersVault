## Problem

Autonomous AI scientists can automate literature review, idea generation, and experiments, but generating publication-ready academic illustrations (methodology diagrams and statistical plots) remains a labor-intensive bottleneck. Prior code-based approaches (TikZ, SVG, Python-PPTX) lack the expressiveness for modern AI publications' intricate icons and custom shapes, while image generation models struggle to consistently meet scholarly standards. Additionally, no dedicated benchmark existed for rigorously evaluating automated academic illustration quality.

## Input / Output

The system takes a methodology description (~3,020 words average) and a figure caption (~70 words average) as input, optionally augmented with N reference triplets (context, caption, illustration) retrieved from a 292-sample reference set. For methodology diagrams, the output is a raster image with aspect ratio constrained to [1.5, 2.5] to match landscape academic layouts; for statistical plots, the Visualizer instead produces executable Python Matplotlib code to guarantee numerical precision. The full pipeline costs approximately $2.5 and ~10 minutes per diagram with T=3 refinement iterations.

## Method

PAPERBANANA is a reference-driven agentic framework that orchestrates five specialized agents in two phases. The Linear Planning Phase uses a Retriever (VLM-based generative retrieval over candidate metadata, prioritizing visual structure over topic similarity), a Planner (in-context learning from retrieved triplets to synthesize a detailed textual description), and a Stylist (refines the description using an auto-summarized aesthetic guideline covering color palette, shapes, lines, layout, and typography). The Iterative Refinement Loop pairs a Visualizer (image generation model, or code generation for statistical plots) with a Critic (VLM that inspects the rendered output against the source context and produces a refined description), iterating for T=3 rounds. The key design insight is decoupling content planning from style refinement and grounding both in retrieved reference illustrations, enabling the system to learn logical composition and stylistic norms without any parameter training.

## Results

On PAPERBANANABENCH (292 test cases from NeurIPS 2025), PAPERBANANA with Nano-Banana-Pro achieves an overall score of 60.2, outperforming the Vanilla Nano-Banana-Pro baseline (43.2) by +17.0%. Per-dimension gains over the vanilla baseline are: Faithfulness +2.8% (45.8 vs. 43.0), Conciseness +37.2% (80.7 vs. 43.5), Readability +12.9% (51.4 vs. 38.5), and Aesthetics +6.6% (72.1 vs. 65.5). For statistical plot generation, PAPERBANANA achieves a +4.1% overall improvement over vanilla Gemini-3-Pro, with gains of +1.4%, +5.0%, +3.1%, and +4.0% in Faithfulness, Conciseness, Readability, and Aesthetics respectively. A blind human evaluation on 50 cases confirms a 72.7%/20.7%/6.6% win/tie/loss rate for PAPERBANANA over vanilla Nano-Banana-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAPERBANANA (w/ Nano-Banana-Pro) decisively outperforms both agentic baselines across all four evaluation dimensions. Against Paper2Any, PAPERBANANA achieves 45.8 vs. 6.5 in Faithfulness, 80.7 vs. 44.0 in Conciseness, 51.4 vs. 20.5 in Readability, and 72.1 vs. 40.0 in Aesthetics (Overall: 60.2 vs. 8.5); the paper attributes Paper2Any's weakness to its focus on high-level idea presentation rather than faithful methodological flow. Against AutoFigure (evaluated on a 49-case subset), PAPERBANANA leads in Faithfulness (45.8 vs. 37.0) and dominates in Conciseness (80.7 vs. 10.2), Readability (51.4 vs. 10.0), and Aesthetics (72.1 vs. 14.0), indicating that retrieval-grounded style guidance and iterative critique are critical for polished academic diagrams. Compared to the strongest non-agentic baseline (Vanilla Nano-Banana-Pro, Overall 43.2), PAPERBANANA gains +17.0% overall, with the largest improvement in Conciseness (+37.2%), suggesting the agentic pipeline's primary value lies in producing focused, clutter-free illustrations. Notably, PAPERBANANA still trails the human reference in Faithfulness (45.8 vs. 50.0), with failure analysis pointing to fine-grained connectivity errors (misaligned arrows, incorrect source-target nodes) that current critic models struggle to detect.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAPERBANANA departs from the dominant code-based paradigm for diagram generation (TikZ, SVG, Python-PPTX) by leveraging state-of-the-art image generation models (Nano-Banana-Pro, GPT-Image-1.5) within a multi-agent orchestration, arguing that code-based methods lack expressiveness for the intricate icons and custom shapes in modern AI publications. Conceptually, it repurposes retrieval-augmented in-context learning and iterative self-critique (borrowed from agentic LLM pipelines) into the visual domain, separating content planning (Planner) from aesthetic refinement (Stylist) in a way that prior systems like Paper2Any and the concurrent AutoFigure do not. The introduction of PAPERBANANABENCH, with its paired test/reference sets enabling both retrieval-augmented generation and reference-based evaluation, fills a gap left by existing benchmarks like SridBench, which lack the dual-set structure and focus on recent ML methodology diagrams specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine bottleneck in autonomous AI scientist pipelines—visual communication of discoveries—and provides both a practical framework and a rigorous benchmark that the field lacked. Key open questions remain: the faithfulness gap versus human experts (particularly fine-grained connectivity errors that current VLM critics cannot detect), the raster-only output limiting editability, and the trade-off between the unified style guide's academic compliance and stylistic diversity. The finding that random retrieval performs comparably to semantic retrieval suggests the field may be over-investing in precise content matching when general structural/stylistic exposure is the critical signal, a direction worth further investigation.
