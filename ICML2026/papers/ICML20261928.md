## Problem

Set-valued retrieval objectives (diversity, coverage, complementarity) are non-decomposable and lack the property-aligned supervision needed to train efficient fan-out retrievers. RL can optimize such set-level rewards through interaction, but deploying an RL-tuned LLM at query time incurs prohibitive autoregressive latency and repeated retrieval calls. Conversely, diffusion-based generative retrieval enables single-pass fan-out in embedding space but critically depends on large amounts of objective-aligned training targets that are scarce or ambiguous in non-decomposable retrieval tasks.

## Input / Output

The system takes a broad, open-ended text query (2–8 words describing a theme, style, or scenario) and produces a set of L=12 retrieval-direction embeddings (each 128-dim) in a single non-autoregressive diffusion pass, which are then mapped to concrete database items via nearest-neighbor search over a frozen embedding space (CLIP/Matryoshka for Polyvore fashion items, MuLan for music playlists). The output is inherently order-agnostic: rows of the target tensor are randomly permuted during training to enforce permutation invariance, and the final result is an unordered set of retrieved items rather than a ranked list.

## Method

R4T operates as a three-stage RL-compiled pipeline. First, a Fan-Out Language Model (FOLM) is trained via Soft-GRPO (GRPO augmented with both forward and reverse KL penalties against the sampling policy) using composite set-level rewards that jointly encode groundedness (distance to nearest database embedding), diversity (Vendi Score over retrieved representatives), and alignment (cosine similarity to the original query). Second, the optimized FOLM synthesizes supervision by sampling 128 fan-out trajectories per query at temperature 0.9, constructing coherent target tensors Z_target ∈ R^{L×d} from either content embeddings (OAR) or sub-query embeddings (WSCR). Third, a lightweight 53.9M-parameter diffusion transformer (6 layers, hidden dim 1024, EDM/VE formulation with classifier-free guidance) is trained to model p(Z_target | z_q), enabling all L retrieval directions to be generated in a single 256-step SDE pass at inference. The key insight is using RL purely as a one-time objective transducer—converting complex reward specifications into scalable training data—so that the expensive autoregressive policy is never deployed, and the lightweight diffusion prior inherits the reward-shaped fan-out distribution at a 12–20× latency reduction.

## Results

On the OAR task (Table 1), R4T-FOLM with Gemma3-4B achieves an average LLM-judge score of 49.1 on Polyvore and 58.1 on Music, outperforming the strongest Best-of-N baseline (40.9 and 49.2 respectively) by 8.2 and 8.9 points. On the WSCR task (Table 2), R4T-FOLM (Qwen) reaches 20.9 Recall@5K and 64.6 Hit@5K, surpassing the best LLM baseline (Gemini-2.5-Flash at 15.7/52.1). R4T-Diffusion retains most of the quality (16.5 Recall@5K, 57.5 Hit@5K) while delivering a 12×–20× inference speedup over autoregressive fan-out, reducing latency from ~1.46 s (batch 8) to 0.07 s for the diffusion model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R4T-FOLM consistently outperforms all three LLM baselines (Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemma3-4B, Qwen3-4B) in both their zero-shot and Best-of-N (N=5) configurations across every metric on both datasets. On Polyvore OAR, R4T-FOLM (Gemma) improves average score from 40.9 (Best-of-N Gemma) to 49.1, a gain of 8.2 points, while requiring only a single inference pass rather than 5 independent fan-out executions. On Music OAR, the gap widens to 8.9 points (58.1 vs. 49.2). On WSCR, R4T-FOLM (Qwen) achieves 20.9 Recall@5K versus 15.7 for Gemini-2.5-Flash and 10.1 for zero-shot Qwen3-4B, while R4T-Diffusion (Qwen) retains 16.5 Recall@5K and 57.5 Hit@5K at a fraction of the latency. The No Fan-out baseline is the weakest across all settings, confirming that query expansion is necessary but that zero-shot expansion alone suffers from semantic drift and redundancy. R4T-Diffusion trades a small quality drop (≈2–3 points on diversity/alignment) for a 12×–20× speedup, making it the practical deployment choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

R4T sits at the intersection of two active research threads: RL-optimized retrieval (DeepRetrieval, s3, Zhou et al.) and diffusion-based generative retrieval (EDM, DiT, Prompt-to-slate). Prior RL-for-retrieval work deploys the RL-tuned policy directly at inference, incurring autoregressive latency; prior diffusion retrievers require large amounts of property-aligned supervision that is unavailable for non-decomposable set-valued tasks. R4T's key conceptual departure is repurposing RL as a one-time objective transducer—synthesizing training data for a lightweight diffusion retriever rather than serving as the inference mechanism itself—thereby decoupling reward-driven discovery from efficient deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RL-as-data-generator paradigm offers a general recipe for converting non-decomposable, set-level objectives into scalable supervision without human annotation, potentially extending to planning, design, and creative generation beyond retrieval. Practical impact is significant: the 12–20× inference speedup over autoregressive fan-out makes property-aligned set retrieval viable in production settings. Open questions include the upfront RL training cost for large or frequently changing databases, the difficulty of encoding subjective preferences (creativity, cultural sensitivity) as scalar rewards, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two evaluated domains and specific embedding backbones (CLIP, MuLan).
