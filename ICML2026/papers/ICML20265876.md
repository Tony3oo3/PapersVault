## Problem

Evaluating the agentic web search capabilities of LLMs remains an open challenge because existing benchmarks conflate a model's memorized world knowledge with genuine retrieval ability. Static factual QA datasets (SimpleQA, BrowseComp, TriviaQA) are increasingly answerable offline, while time-sensitive benchmarks (FreshQA, SealQA) use fixed questions whose answers change slowly, allowing strong models to reason or partially recall correct answers without searching. Academic reasoning benchmarks (HLE, GAIA) measure domain knowledge rather than search behavior per se, and deep research benchmarks evaluate long-form reports with subjective criteria rather than concise, verifiable factual answers.

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents a single factual question per trial, derived from a cluster of 5+ news articles covering the same recent event; the question is self-contained and must be answerable without access to the source articles. The agent receives the question and may issue up to 5 search queries (via the Tavily Search API, returning top-10 results with title, URL, and snippet) and up to 5 full-page visits (returning plain-text content). The expected output is a concise factual answer—typically 1–5 words—graded as CORRECT, INCORRECT, or NOT_ATTEMPTED by GPT-4.1 using a SimpleQA-style rubric. The dataset itself is constructed from Wikipedia Current Events Archive summaries and articles from an allowlist of ~100 reputable outlets within a 14-day window around each event date.

## Method

LIVE NEWSBENCH is built by an automated pipeline that (1) collects major global news events from the Wikipedia Current Events Archive, (2) uses GPT-4.1 to rewrite event summaries into search queries submitted to the Brave Search API (restricted to an allowlist of ~100 outlets and a 14-day temporal window), (3) verifies article-to-event relevance with GPT-4.1, and (4) uses GPT-5.1 Thinking to generate Q&A pairs that require synthesizing information across multiple articles in the cluster, followed by self-consistency filtering for answer correctness and a paraphrased-guideline adherence check. A human annotator then validates a 200-sample test subset (rejecting ~15% of auto-verified pairs), with an independent NLP researcher confirming 92% agreement on 100 annotations. For evaluation, the authors implement a custom ReAct-style agent framework exposing three actions—Search, Visit, and Finish&Answer—with deterministic budget enforcement (5 searches, 5 visits) and the Tavily Search API as the retrieval backend, enabling controlled comparison across 19 LLMs and 3 commercial search APIs. The dataset is partitioned chronologically (training: May–Sep 2025, validation: Oct 2025, test: Nov–Dec 2025) and committed to quarterly refreshes, with the human-verified split maintained for at least two years.

## Results

On the LIVENEWSBENCH human-verified test set (n=200, standard budget of 5 searches + 5 visits), accuracy spans a wide range from 11.0% (Llama 3.1 8B) to 90.0% (GPT-5.2 Official Web Search API). Among local agent frameworks, DeepSeek V3.2 Thinking achieves the highest accuracy at 84.5% (3.3±1.3 searches, 2.6±1.4 visits), followed by DeepSeek V3.2 No Thinking (83.0%) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (82.0%). In the no-internet setting, all models drop to 10–21.5% accuracy, confirming that web search is essential for high performance and that the benchmark resists memorization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against two time-sensitive factual QA benchmarks—FreshQA and SealQA-Hard—evaluated in a no-internet (memorization-only) setting. On FreshQA, frontier models achieve 55.3–74.3% accuracy without search (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro at 74.3%, GPT-5.2 at 72.2%), and on SealQA-Hard they reach 21.3–46.5% (Gemini 3 Pro at 46.5%). In stark contrast, on LIVENEWSBENCH the same models score only 10.0–21.5% without internet access (GPT-5.2 at 21.5%, Gemini 3 Pro at 20.5%, DeepSeek V3.2 Thinking at 10.0%), indicating that LIVENEWSBENCH questions are far less answerable from memorized knowledge. This 30–55 percentage-point gap in no-internet accuracy demonstrates that LIVENEWSBENCH more cleanly isolates genuine agentic search capability from internal world knowledge, making it a more discriminative evaluation for web search agents than prior time-sensitive benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIVENEWSBENCH carves out a distinct niche by combining several design properties that no prior benchmark simultaneously satisfies: regularly refreshed questions (not just answers), automated large-scale Q&A generation, multi-hop search requirements, and objective factual evaluation. It draws conceptually on the 'live benchmark' paradigm from LiveBench and LiveCodeBench (contamination-limited, periodically updated evaluations) and on the agentic search evaluation setting established by FreshQA, SealQA, and BrowseComp, but departs from all of them by sourcing questions from post-training-cutoff news events and enforcing multi-source synthesis. The agent framework itself is a straightforward ReAct-style implementation using Tavily Search, positioning the contribution squarely in benchmark design and data curation rather than in novel agent architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark addresses a concrete and growing gap: as agentic search becomes a headline feature of frontier LLMs, the field lacks a contamination-resistant, scalable, and objectively scored evaluation that cleanly isolates search capability from memorized knowledge. The automated pipeline (≈$700 per release) and the resulting ~1K-sample training set are practically important for the RLVR training of search agents, a resource the paper notes is scarce. Key open questions include whether the GPT-5.1-based generation introduces systematic biases that favor certain model families, whether the English-centric, news-event-grounded scope generalizes to other domains, and how the benchmark will hold up as models are explicitly trained on similar news-derived QA data. The finding that leading models plateau well below their extended tool budgets (15 searches/visits) suggests the current bottleneck is multi-hop reasoning quality rather than search volume, a useful diagnostic for future agent design.
