## Problem

Diffusion wavelets and geometric scattering transforms provide an effective alternative to message-passing GNNs by capturing multiscale graph geometry in a single layer, but they were only defined for scalar-valued node signals. When data carries vector-valued features (e.g., wind velocity components, neural population velocities, or tangent-bundle-valued signals), no principled wavelet-based framework existed that could process these features while respecting the geometric structure of the underlying manifold and maintaining rotational equivariance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes geometric graphs whose vertices lie in R^D, equipped with both scalar-valued and vector-valued node features, where the vector features are assumed to take values in the tangent bundle of an unknown low-dimensional manifold. The vector diffusion matrix Q (an nD × nD sparse block matrix) is constructed once as a preprocessing step and cached; the k-NN graph structure and local PCA frames are also precomputed. Outputs include node-level regression/classification predictions, graph-level scalar predictions (e.g., diameter), reconstructed masked vector signals, and low-dimensional embeddings for downstream probing classifiers.

## Method

The core contribution is the vector diffusion matrix Q, whose (i,j) block is P[i,j]·O_{i,j}, where P is the standard scalar diffusion operator and O_{i,j} = U_i U_j^T is a change-of-coordinate matrix built from local PCA frames (left singular vectors of kernel-weighted neighbor displacement matrices). A key structural result (Lemma A.2) shows Q^m[i,j] = P^m[i,j]·O_{i,j}, meaning scalar diffusion governs propagation magnitude while local frames handle directional alignment, which yields rotational equivariance of both the wavelets and scattering coefficients (Theorems 3.2–3.3) and frame bounds analogous to the scalar case (Theorem 3.1). Vector diffusion wavelets are then defined as Ψ̃_j = Q^{2^{j-1}} − Q^{2^j}, and VDW-GNNs incorporate these in scattering modules (alternating wavelet filters and equivariant activations) with hidden feature mixing layers, trained via supervised regression, MSE reconstruction, or supervised contrastive loss depending on the task.

## Results

On the synthetic ellipsoid diameter prediction task (Table 1), VDW-GNN achieves a rotated-test MSE of 0.0037 with only 59,779 parameters, outperforming EGNN (0.0284, 284,036 params) and TFN (0.0828, 29.7M params) by 7.7× and 22× respectively. On the real-world wind field reconstruction task (Table 3), VDW-GNN achieves the best test MSE (2.75) and second-best rotated-test MSE (3.23) with 20,099 parameters and the fastest per-epoch training time (0.0041 s). On the neural activity classification task (Table 4), VDW-GNN reaches 66% mean SVM classifier accuracy, slightly exceeding MARBLE (65%) while using 8.8× fewer parameters and training 6× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDW-GNN decisively outperforms both equivariant baselines on the ellipsoid diameter task: it achieves 7.7× lower rotated-test MSE than EGNN (0.0037 vs. 0.0284) and 22× lower than TFN (0.0037 vs. 0.0828), while using 4.7× fewer parameters than EGNN and 496× fewer than TFN. All non-equivariant baselines (GCN, GAT, GIN, LEGS, and the non-equivariant VDW-GNN ablation) suffer catastrophic degradation on the rotated test set (MSE > 1.4), confirming that rotational equivariance is essential for this task. On the wind field reconstruction task, VDW-GNN matches EGNN's test MSE (2.75 vs. 2.77) with 6.7× fewer parameters and 3× faster per-epoch training, and substantially outperforms DD-TNN (12.76 MSE), the only other method designed for tangent-bundle-valued features. On the neural activity task, VDW-GNN slightly edges out MARBLE (66% vs. 65% accuracy) with 8.8× fewer parameters and 6× faster training, while significantly outperforming CEBRA (57%) and LFADS (35%). The non-equivariant VDW-GNN ablation (MSE 2.92 on rotated test) demonstrates that the equivariant structure, not the wavelet architecture alone, drives the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scalar-valued diffusion wavelet framework (Coifman & Maggioni, 2006) and geometric scattering transforms (Gama et al., 2018; Zou & Lerman, 2019) to vector-valued node features on geometric graphs, using the vector diffusion matrix Q from Singer & Wu (2012) in place of the standard diffusion matrix P. Unlike DD-TNN (Battiloro et al., 2024), which also targets tangent-bundle data but relies on spectral eigendecomposition of the connection Laplacian, VDW-GNNs use powers of Q as a sparse-multiplication proxy for the heat semigroup, avoiding explicit eigendecompositions. The rotational equivariance is inherited from the wavelet construction itself (via local PCA-based coordinate frames) rather than imposed through architectural constraints as in EGNN or TFN, yielding a fundamentally different route to equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a parameter-efficient equivariant GNN (≈60K parameters) that matches or beats EGNN (284K) and TFN (29M) on geometric tasks, with the equivariance built into the wavelet operator rather than the network architecture. Theoretical frame bounds guarantee stable inversion and noise robustness, properties not typically available for equivariant GNNs. Open questions include: whether the vector-valued convergence-to-continuum results (established for the heat-semigroup version by Battiloro et al.) transfer to the Q-power approximation used here; how the method behaves when the manifold hypothesis is violated; and whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (diffusion scales, k-NN parameters) can be addressed via learnable scales as in Tong et al. (2024).
