## Problem

Vision-Language Models suffer from high inference latency because modern visual encoders produce hundreds to thousands of visual tokens that must be attended to at every decoding step. Existing visual token pruning and KV eviction methods rely on attention weights, feature similarity, or entropy-based heuristics to decide which visual tokens to retain, but these local signals only indirectly approximate a token's actual influence on the model's semantic decision, often discarding semantically critical yet visually subtle tokens while retaining visually salient but decision-irrelevant ones.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a standard VLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5 7B or Qwen2.5-VL) that takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and autoregressively generates text. During decoding, SIVS periodically (every T=10 steps by default) re-evaluates which visual KV pairs to retain at a fixed intermediate layer ℓ* (≈0.6L), evicting the rest for the next interval. The method is training-free and introduces no parameter updates; it only modifies which visual KV entries are accessible to the attention mechanism at each step.

## Method

SIVS constructs a prediction-conditioned Semantic Lens at each update step by extracting the Top-K candidate token embeddings from the final layer, performing a reduced QR decomposition to obtain an orthonormal basis Q spanning the model's instantaneous decision subspace. It then computes the hidden-state difference between vision-aware and vision-blind decoding at an intermediate layer ℓ*, projects this difference onto the semantic subspace via Π_sem = QQ^T to isolate decision-relevant visual impact (δ_sem), and decomposes δ_sem into per-token contributions by projecting each visual token's attention-weighted value vector onto the semantic correction direction. A distribution-aware threshold (μ_s + ασ_s) over the resulting token scores determines which visual KV pairs to retain, with α providing a continuous accuracy-compression trade-off. The semi-dynamic design refreshes selection every T steps, exploiting the empirically observed bursty temporal structure of visual semantic demand to avoid noisy re-evaluation during low-impact phases.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B, SIVS achieves 99.95% relative accuracy across seven benchmarks (GQA, MM-Bench, MME, POPE, ScienceQA, TextVQA, MileBench) while compressing 87.66% of visual KV states, the highest compression among all compared methods. SIVS also attains the lowest decoding latency at 18.78 ms per step, a 23.5% speedup over the vanilla model (24.56 ms). On Qwen2.5-VL models scaled from 3B to 72B, SIVS maintains 86–91% visual KV compression with near-lossless performance (96.3–100.2% retention), confirming the method's generality across architectures and scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIVS outperforms all four direct baselines on both accuracy and compression simultaneously. Against the strongest baseline LOOK-M (EMNLP'24), SIVS improves relative accuracy from 99.85% to 99.95% while increasing compression from 80% to 87.66%, and also achieves lower decoding latency (18.78 ms vs. 21.96 ms). FastV (ECCV'24) and SparseVLM (ICML'25) both achieve only ~77.8% compression with substantially lower accuracy (96.46% and 96.84% respectively), with FastV suffering a particularly large drop on GQA (49.6 vs. 61.9 vanilla). VisPruner (ICCV'25) shows the weakest overall accuracy at 95.20% despite matching the same 77.8% compression as FastV and SparseVLM. In a compatibility test with ToMe (ICLR'23) on GQA, SIVS alone (61.7, 90.28% compression) substantially outperforms ToMe alone (58.7, 63.9%), and the combined SIVS+ToMe pipeline (59.8, 84.99%) still exceeds ToMe's accuracy while achieving higher compression, confirming the two methods operate at complementary stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIVS departs from the dominant paradigm in visual KV compression (FastV, SparseVLM, LOOK-M, VisPruner), which selects tokens based on attention magnitudes, feature similarity, or structural heuristics, by reframing the selection criterion as semantic impact on the model's instantaneous output distribution. Conceptually, it draws on the logit-lens and tuned-lens literature (Hewitt & Manning; Belrose et al.) for the observation that mid-to-late layers encode linearly decodable semantic structure, and on cross-modal information-flow analyses (Zhang et al., 2025c) for the layer-selection principle. The QR-decomposition-based Semantic Lens is a new mechanism that repurposes the model's own Top-K prediction geometry as a measurement basis, rather than introducing learned or static importance scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87% visual KV compression at >99% accuracy retention is practically significant for deploying VLMs under memory-bandwidth constraints, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately adoptable. The core conceptual contribution—that attention magnitude is a poor proxy for a token's contribution to the final semantic decision—challenges a widely used assumption in the field and provides a geometrically grounded alternative. Limitations include minor accuracy drops on tasks requiring globally distributed or visually subtle cues (GQA, TextVQA), and the method has not yet been extended to video or hierarchical memory settings, which the authors identify as future directions.
