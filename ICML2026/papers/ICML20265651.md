## Problem

Existing sequential conformal prediction methods for time series, such as SPCI, estimate tail conditional quantiles of nonconformity scores independently at each quantile level, producing volatile interval widths and quantile crossing that leads to non-nested prediction intervals across significance levels. This undermines interpretability and practical deployment when users need coherent uncertainty quantification at multiple confidence levels simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265651/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step, CQACP consumes a d-dimensional feature vector X_t, a window of w recent prediction residuals, a pre-trained black-box point predictor f̂, and a target significance level α. It evaluates a base conditional quantile learner on a fixed grid of quantile levels (e.g., p ∈ {0.01, …, 0.99}) to obtain noisy grid-level quantile estimates, then outputs a smooth, monotone adjusted conditional quantile curve and a one-step-ahead asymmetric prediction interval [f̂(X_t) + Q̂_adj(ρ̂α), f̂(X_t) + Q̂_adj(1−α+ρ̂α)] that is guaranteed to be nested across all α values.

## Method

CQACP treats the conditional quantile function of the nonconformity score as a structured curve rather than a collection of independent estimators. It fits a low-dimensional Cornish-Fisher (Hermite polynomial) approximation via OLS to the grid of noisy base quantile estimates, pooling information across quantile levels to denoise unstable tail estimators. If the OLS solution violates monotonicity, a constrained least squares re-estimation enforces a non-decreasing curve on the Gaussian-quantile scale. A single time-varying asymmetry ratio ρ̂_t, optimized at a reference significance level α₀, is shared across all α to guarantee nested intervals. Theoretically, the CF projection step provably reduces conditional MSE of the quantile estimator (removing variance at rate σ²(1−K/n) at the cost of O(K^{−2s}) approximation bias), and the full procedure achieves asymptotic (1−α) conditional coverage under serial dependence without requiring exchangeability.

## Results

At α=10% (nominal 90% coverage), CQACP achieves empirical coverage of 90.1%, 90.0%, 90.1%, and 90.0% on Realized Volatility, Index Return, Exchange Rates, and Electricity respectively—closest to the target among all sequential conformal baselines. Its average interval widths are 0.9%, 3.4%, 0.4%, and 9.2%, which are less than half the widths of the narrowest ACI-type methods (2.3%, 6.6%, 1.3%, 24.1%) and comparable to or slightly wider than SPCI (0.8%, 3.4%, 0.4%, 9.3%). CQACP also exhibits the lowest standard deviation across five runs (0.1%–0.3%) among the conformal methods, indicating stable calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest sequential conformal baselines, CQACP wins on coverage: it hits 90.0–90.1% on all four datasets, whereas SPCI under-covers (87.9–89.3%) and EnbPI is inconsistent (85.9–91.2%). Interval widths are essentially tied with SPCI (e.g., 0.9% vs. 0.8% on Realized Volatility; 9.2% vs. 9.3% on Electricity), so the coverage gain comes without widening. Against ACI-type methods (ACI, OGD, SF-OGD, PID, ECI), CQACP achieves comparable coverage but with dramatically narrower intervals—less than half the width of the best ACI variant (ECI) on every dataset, and ACI itself produces unbounded (∞) widths. Against probabilistic forecasting models (DeepAR, TFT), CQACP is more reliable: DeepAR under-covers to 85.3% on Index Return and over-covers to 93.0% on Electricity, while TFT shows high variance (5.7% std. on Realized Volatility); CQACP stays within 0.1% of nominal on all datasets. The ablation in Table 3 further shows CQACP is robust to the choice of base quantile learner (QRF, QAR, KNNQR), maintaining ~90% coverage even when SPCI drops to 85.7–87.9% under weaker learners.

## Relation to Prior Work

CQACP extends the sequential conformal prediction line (SPCI, EnbPI) by replacing the per-quantile-level conditional quantile estimation with a structured Cornish-Fisher/Hermite polynomial projection that pools information across the entire quantile grid. This departs from SPCI's design of independently estimating tail quantiles and plugging them into interval endpoints, and from ex-post monotone rearrangement (Chernozhukov et al., 2010) by performing cross-quantile denoising *before* enforcing monotonicity. The CF representation itself is a classical tool (Cornish & Fisher, 1938) recently repurposed for conditional moment estimation in finance (Zhang & Zhu, 2026) and distributional RL (Kuang et al., 2023); here it is brought into the conformal calibration loop as a low-dimensional sieve that stabilizes noisy tail quantiles while preserving asymptotic conditional validity under serial dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantile-crossing and interval-nesting problem is a practical pain point in risk reporting and multi-horizon decision-making, and CQACP addresses it with a clean, model-agnostic mechanism that works with any base quantile learner. The theoretical guarantee that the CF projection strictly reduces grid-level MSE (even with biased base estimators) and the asymptotic conditional coverage proof under serial dependence give the method a solid statistical foundation. Open questions include extending to multi-step joint coverage, multivariate prediction regions, and combining CQACP's nested score-quantile engine with ACI-style online level adaptation for distribution shift. The method's reliance on a central quantile grid (Assumption 1) and the need for cross-validation to select K are practical limitations that may matter in very short-sample or extreme-tail settings.
