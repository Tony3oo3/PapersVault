## Problem

Computing high-precision Nash equilibria in two-player zero-sum games has relied almost exclusively on first-order methods (e.g., PRM+) with sublinear O(1/T) or O(1/√T) convergence, making high-accuracy solutions prohibitively expensive. No direct second-order method existed that could exploit curvature information in the bilinear saddle point problem without resorting to LP reformulations that destroy exploitable structure. This paper introduces the first such direct second-order method and a principled hybrid framework combining it with state-of-the-art first-order game solving.

## Input / Output

The method takes a payoff matrix A ∈ R^{n×m} and an initial strategy profile (x₀, y₀) in the product of probability simplices Δⁿ × Δᵐ. It produces a Nash equilibrium (x*, y*) certified by a duality gap below a user-specified tolerance. Internally, the second-order phase operates in a lifted space R^{n+m} (displacing feasible iterates along the normal cone direction by γF(ẑ)), and the final output is obtained by projecting the lifted fixed point back onto the feasible strategy set S.

## Method

The core construction is a Douglas-Rachford splitting (DRS) residual operator R_DRS^γ = Id − T_DRS^γ, which is shown to be monotone, 1-Lipschitz, piecewise affine, and strongly semi-smooth—properties that make it well-suited for a regularized semi-smooth Newton (SSN) method. The SSN step solves (G_k + μ_k I)Δz_k = −r_k where G_k is a generalized Jacobian element derived from the simplex projection's active set, and μ_k = θ_k‖r_k‖ provides adaptive regularization. A hybrid framework warm-starts the SSN phase using PRM+ iterates: once the duality gap drops below a threshold, a lifting step z = ẑ − γF(ẑ) places the iterate in the lifted space where the residual norm is small enough to guarantee local quadratic convergence (‖R(z_{k+1})‖ ≤ C‖R(z_k)‖²). The convergence analysis replaces the classical BD-regularity assumption with a local error bound (metric subregularity) and a stability condition implied by monotonicity, extending prior results for matrix games to the DRS residual operator.

## Results

On 400×800 random uniform matrix games, PSSN v2 reaches a duality gap of 10⁻⁸ in 4.29 s, 10⁻¹⁰ in 4.31 s, and 10⁻¹² in 4.32 s, compared to 6.42 s, 21.97 s, and >500 s (not reached) for PRM⁺(QA) respectively. On 400×800 random normal games, PSSN v2 achieves 10⁻¹⁰ in 5.08 s versus 19.83 s for PRM⁺(QA), a ~4× speedup, while PRM⁺(LI) cannot reach 10⁻¹⁰ or 10⁻¹² within the 5×10⁵ iteration budget. The key finding is that once the SSN phase engages near equilibrium, additional precision (from 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻¹²) costs less than 0.1 s, confirming the theoretical local quadratic convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PRM⁺ (Farina et al., 2021), the proposed PSSN v2 method is slower at low precision (10⁻² to 10⁻⁶) due to the one-time resolvent inversion and Newton system overhead, but achieves a 1.5× speedup at 10⁻⁸ (4.29 s vs 6.42 s on uniform games) and a 5× speedup at 10⁻¹⁰ (4.31 s vs 21.97 s). At 10⁻¹², PRM⁺(QA) fails to converge within the iteration budget while PSSN v2 finishes in 4.32 s. Against Extragradient (Korpelevich, 1976), the gap is even more pronounced: EG takes 46.5 s to reach 10⁻⁵ on uniform games and fails to reach 10⁻⁶, whereas PSSN v2 reaches 10⁻⁶ in 3.64 s. The paper also reports that OGDA is intermediate between EG and PRM⁺ but still substantially outperformed by PSSN v2 at high precision. Overall, the hybrid method wins decisively in the high-precision regime (≤10⁻⁸) where first-order methods' sublinear convergence becomes a bottleneck, while losing at low precision where the per-iteration cost of solving Newton systems dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper constructs the first direct second-order method for Nash equilibrium computation in two-player zero-sum games by combining three established threads: Douglas-Rachford splitting (Eckstein & Bertsekas, 1992) to build a monotone, piecewise-affine residual operator; semi-smooth Newton methods (Qi & Sun, 1993; Zhou & Toh, 2005) to solve the resulting root-finding problem with local quadratic convergence; and Predictive Regret Matching+ (Farina et al., 2021) as a first-order warm-starting phase. The key departure from prior work is that classical second-order approaches either reformulate the bilinear saddle point problem as a linear program (losing exploitable structure) or are restricted to unconstrained settings (Asl et al., 2024), whereas this method operates directly on the constrained problem via the DRS residual. The convergence analysis extends prior metric-subregularity results for matrix games (Tseng, 1995; Gilpin et al., 2012; Wei et al., 2021) to the DRS residual operator and replaces the restrictive BD-regularity assumption with a local error bound plus monotonicity-based stability, following the framework of Zhou & Toh (2005).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work breaks a roughly two-decade reliance on first-order methods for game solving by demonstrating that curvature information can be directly exploited in the bilinear saddle point setting without LP reformulation, yielding order-of-magnitude speedups (e.g., ~5s vs. ~22s for 400×800 games at 10⁻⁸ duality gap) in the high-precision regime. The hybrid framework is general in principle—any first-order method that reduces the duality gap can warm-start the SSN phase—but the practical impact is currently limited to matrix games; the authors explicitly identify extension to extensive-form games (the CFR+ setting used in poker and Diplomacy) as the critical next step. Open questions include whether the instance-dependent switching threshold can be made adaptive without the tuning difficulties observed in the HPSSN variant, and whether inexact linear system solves can preserve the convergence guarantees at larger scales.
