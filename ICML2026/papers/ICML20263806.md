## Problem

Language-based prompting in text-to-image diffusion models cannot fully capture a user's specific mental image due to the high dimensionality of image space relative to language, limited human expressivity, and imperfections in joint language-image models. This creates a fundamental 'last-mile' gap between the user's target image x* and the best prompt-generated image x^p*. The paper asks whether iterative human preference feedback can close this gap without any model retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263806/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (which produces a starting image x^p* already close to but not matching the user's target) and iteratively presents K=4 candidate images per round for B=50 rounds, from which the user selects N 'best' images (N≥1). The output is a single personalized image x̂* aligned with the user's imagined target. Operationally, MultiBO runs on SDXL with DDIM (50 inference steps, guidance scale 5.0), editing self-attention Q/K/V features during the first 20% of denoising steps at layers 34–64, with the entire pipeline consuming ~9 GB memory and ~20 minutes wall-clock time including human feedback.

## Method

MultiBO combines two key modules: (1) MultiwiseGPR, a likelihood model that generalizes the pairwise Bradley-Terry-Luce probit model to an N-out-of-K multinomial-logit (softmax) regression, enabling richer preference signals per user query and estimated via Laplace approximation; and (2) a Dynamic Balanced Subspace (DBS) acquisition function that computes a single Expected Improvement point, constructs K bridge vectors between the current best and EI point, and adds eigen-weighted perturbations in a dynamically sized subspace (determined by spectral gap of the GPR posterior gradient covariance) to balance exploration and exploitation. Rather than optimizing in the ~16k-dimensional latent space, MultiBO constrains the search to a low-dimensional warping parameter space (affine + thin-plate spline transformations) applied to self-attention Q, K, V features, which control spatial structure, texture, and color. The entire procedure is training-free and inference-time, requiring no backpropagation or task-specific data, and converges in ~32 iterations on average.

## Results

On the personalized image generation task (B=50 iterations, single user), MultiBO achieves CLIP-I2I of 0.9364 and LPIPS of 0.5497, the best target-alignment scores among all non-oracle methods (L2-guided oracle: 0.9811 / 0.1958). In human evaluation with 30 volunteers, MultiBO attains a 70.82% win rate and MOS of 3.58, substantially outperforming the next-best method (DEMON choose-generate: 15.60% WR, MOS 1.66). MultiBO also matches or exceeds dedicated reward-optimization baselines on their own target metrics (e.g., ImageReward 0.8883 vs. DEMON_ImageReward 1.1530, Aesthetic 6.6690 vs. DNO_Aesthetic 7.7674) while avoiding the reward-hacking artifacts that degrade image quality in those baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiBO decisively outperforms all five baselines on target-alignment metrics: CLIP-I2I of 0.9364 vs. DiffusionDPO (0.8365), IterComp (0.8539), DEMON_Aesthetic (0.8109), DNO_Aesthetic (0.6931), and DAS_Aesthetic (0.6849); LPIPS of 0.5497 vs. 0.7535, 0.6988, 0.6785, 0.8487, and 0.8495 respectively. Against the training-based methods (DiffusionDPO, IterComp), MultiBO matches or exceeds their reward-metric scores (e.g., ImageReward 0.8883 vs. 0.6029 for DiffusionDPO) while requiring no training data, using only 50 human queries. Against inference-time methods (DEMON, DNO, DAS), MultiBO achieves comparable scores on their respective target rewards but critically avoids reward-hacking: DNO_Aesthetic and DAS_Aesthetic inflate Aesthetic to ~7.7 yet produce visually degraded images, whereas MultiBO maintains visual integrity. In the direct human-in-the-loop comparison (DEMON choose-generate mode), MultiBO converges closer to x* within 50 iterations while DEMON stalls around iteration 30, and MultiBO's 70.82% human win rate dwarfs DEMON's 15.60%. The ablation MultiBO_Subspace (optimizing in a 100D latent subspace instead of attention space) underperforms on all metrics, confirming that the self-attention warping constraint is essential for efficient convergence under the B=50 budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiBO extends Preferential Bayesian Optimization (Chu & Ghahramani, 2005) from pairwise Bradley-Terry-Luce likelihoods to a multi-choice (N-out-of-K) multinomial-logit likelihood (MultiwiseGPR), and adapts the Dynamic Balanced Subspace acquisition strategy from Bounce (Papenmeier et al., 2023) for high-dimensional BO. For the image-generation side, it builds on DreamMatcher (Nam et al., 2024) by performing optimization in self-attention Q,K,V space, but constrains the search to valid affine+TPS warping transformations rather than free-form feature manipulation. The closest prior work, DEMON (Yeh et al., 2024), also performs training-free inference-time alignment with human feedback, but operates on high-dimensional noise via PF-ODE stochastic optimization and uses only binary (choose/generate) feedback; MultiBO's key departures are the richer multi-choice preference signal and the lower-dimensional attention-warping parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiBO addresses a practical 'last-mile' gap: after language prompting saturates, users still have a specific target image in mind that no prompt can fully capture. The training-free, query-bounded (B=50) framework offers a viable interactive personalization path without requiring large preference datasets or model fine-tuning. Key limitations include inability to recover missing objects from the starting image, weakening preference signal when candidate images are near-equivalent, and the restriction to attention-warping transformations that limits the edit vocabulary. Open questions include whether informed priors from pre-trained reward models can accelerate convergence, and whether the approach generalizes beyond SDXL to newer architectures like FLUX or video diffusion.
