## Problem

Existing deep multi-view graph clustering methods over-rely on static local topology under a homophily assumption, causing over-smoothed representations in heterophilic graphs where neighborhood aggregation introduces structural noise. Additionally, current multi-view fusion strategies employ static or view-level weighting that ignores the fact that a view's informativeness varies significantly at node granularity, yielding consensus embeddings with poor discriminability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264900/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view attributed graph with V adjacency matrices A^v ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and a shared node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D} as input. It produces K cluster assignments for all N nodes by applying K-means to a learned consensus representation H ∈ R^{N×d}. The structural refinement step uses two similarity thresholds (δ_u for adding edges, δ_l for pruning edges) and a learnable per-view frequency balance α^v, while the fusion step uses per-node weights ω_i^v and per-view scores derived from pseudo-label clustering accuracy.

## Method

APGC comprises two synergistic modules. The ABSR module augments node attributes with channel-specific Gaussian noise, encodes them via multi-channel autoencoders into view-specific embeddings Z^v, computes pairwise cosine similarity, and performs bidirectional structural refinement: edges with similarity above δ_u are forced to 1 (adding high-quality connections), edges below δ_l are forced to 0 (pruning heterophilic noise), and intermediate edges retain their original values. The PGMF module then applies dual-channel graph filtering on the refined adjacency—low-pass (I − L̃_r)^l for smooth intra-view semantics and high-pass L̃_r^h for discriminative high-frequency features—combined via learnable α^v, followed by a pseudo-label-guided multi-level fusion that weights each view's contribution at both node-level (learnable ω_i^v) and view-level (score^v computed from clustering accuracy against K-means pseudo-labels). Training jointly minimizes a reconstruction loss, a graph-level alignment contrastive loss weighted by view scores, a node-level contrastive loss using top-M positives and cluster-center negatives, and a self-supervised KL divergence loss toward a sharpened target distribution.

## Results

APGC achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance on 6 homophilic and heterophilic multi-view graph datasets. On homophilic datasets, it reaches 95.3% ACC / 82.1% NMI / 86.4% ARI on ACM and 94.2% ACC / 80.6% NMI / 85.8% ARI on DBLP. On the challenging heterophilic Wisconsin dataset (homophily ratio 0.19), APGC achieves 80.1% ACC, 54.4% NMI, 63.0% ARI, and 67.6% F1, substantially outperforming the next-best method NGCE (73.7% ACC, 46.8% NMI, 46.4% ARI, 47.2% F1). On synthetic ACM with homophily ratio 0.00, APGC maintains 97.7% ACC while traditional methods like MAGCN drop below 40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APGC outperforms all 14 baselines on the vast majority of metrics across six datasets, with only two exceptions: on Texas ACC (0.765 vs. NGCE's 0.776) and Chameleon ARI (0.180 vs. NGCE's 0.193). On homophilic datasets, APGC surpasses the strongest multi-view baseline BMGC by average margins of 5.8% (ACC), 2.7% (NMI), 5.9% (ARI), and 2.9% (F1); on IMDB it beats the suboptimal TRCGC by 1.5%, 3.0%, 5.5%, and 2.8% respectively. On heterophilic datasets, the gains over the strongest competitor NGCE are more pronounced: average improvements of 2.8% (ACC), 3.3% (NMI), 6.7% (ARI), and 10.4% (F1), with Wisconsin showing the largest gap (6.4% ACC, 7.6% NMI, 16.6% ARI, 20.4% F1). Traditional single-view methods (VGAE, DAEGC, AGE) and early multi-view methods (O2MAC, MAGCN) are consistently outperformed by 10–50+ points on heterophilic graphs, confirming that the ABSR structural refinement and PGMF multi-level fusion are critical for handling low-homophily structures. On the large-scale Amazon dataset (Appendix C.2), APGC achieves 80.2% ACC and 57.4% ARI, outperforming BMGC (78.6% ACC, 56.3% ARI) while running at 80 ms/epoch on ACM—faster than VGMGC (780 ms) and NGCE (480 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

APGC synthesizes ideas from several established threads in multi-view graph clustering: autoencoder-based attribute augmentation (O2MAC, MAGCN), heterophily-aware representation learning via high-pass filtering (HGRL, GREET, DSSL), and pseudo-label-guided structural refinement (DuaLGR, TRCGC). Its key departure is the bidirectional structural refinement (ABSR), which uses learned attribute embeddings to simultaneously add semantically consistent edges and prune heterophilic ones, going beyond the one-directional edge removal or decoupling strategies in prior heterophily methods. The pseudo-label guided multi-level fusion (PGMF) extends view-level weighting (BMGC) to node-level adaptive fusion, addressing a granularity gap that prior methods largely ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in DMGC—over-reliance on static topology in heterophilic graphs and coarse multi-view fusion—that limit real-world applicability. The bidirectional refinement mechanism is a clean, interpretable strategy that could transfer to other graph learning tasks beyond clustering. However, scalability remains a concern: all primary experiments use graphs with fewer than 5,000 nodes, and the only large-scale test (Amazon) shows a marginal gap over BMGC. The authors themselves flag extremely sparse graphs and more refined frequency fusion as open directions, and the reliance on K-means pseudo-labels for guiding fusion introduces a chicken-and-egg dependency that could affect convergence in harder regimes.
