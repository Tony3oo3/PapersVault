## Problem

CLIP-style models align whole images with short captions but cannot produce contrastive, retrievable embeddings for arbitrarily shaped pixel regions paired with long compositional text. Region-aware variants (Alpha-CLIP, MaskCLIP) are restricted to bounding boxes and short phrases, while long-text variants (LLM2CLIP, LongCLIP) lack pixel-level visual grounding. No prior framework simultaneously accepts arbitrary mask prompts and document-length text within a unified contrastive objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a full RGB image and an arbitrary-shaped binary mask (no cropping required) plus a text string of any length, producing a single contrastive visual embedding focused on the masked region and a text embedding from LLaMA3-8B. The mask is consumed via a dedicated 1-channel patch embedding layer that is element-wise summed with the image patch embedding before entering the ViT, so the mask acts as a soft spatial gate rather than a hard crop. Setting the mask to all-ones recovers standard global image-text alignment, enabling the same model to serve both region-level and whole-image retrieval.

## Method

PixCLIP modifies the CLIP ViT by adding a parallel mask patch embedding layer (zero-initialized) whose output is summed with the image patch embedding, and replaces the text encoder with LLaMA3-8B fine-tuned via the LLM2Vec adaptor. Training uses a three-branch contrastive objective: (1) Mask-Text Contrastive Alignment performs standard InfoNCE between mask-guided visual features and LLM text features; (2) Fine-Grained Cropping Alignment adds a positive-only contrastive loss between features from a tightly cropped mask region and the original mask-aware features to suppress background leakage; (3) Local-Global Representation Enhancement pools dense global features over the mask region and aligns them with the global embedding via self-supervised contrastive loss, enforcing consistency between local and global semantics. The total loss is L_CL + 0.25·L_FC + 0.25·L_LG, trained on the newly constructed LongGRIT dataset (1.5M mask–long-text pairs) generated by a multi-MLLM pipeline (InternVL2-76B → Qwen2-72B verification → Griffon-G-26B spatial context → DeepSeek-R1-70B merging).

## Results

On the comprehensive retrieval benchmark (Table 4), PixCLIP achieves an average I2T/T2I of 86.7/85.0, outperforming LLM2CLIP (84.1/80.5) and FG-CLIP (86.6/82.7), with particularly strong gains on long-text datasets (DOCCI: 96.7/96.4 vs. LLM2CLIP's 85.5/86.8). On the region-level RefCOCO task, PixCLIP reaches 59.9/69.9 (Val/Test), surpassing the previous SOTA Alpha-CLIP (55.7/61.1) by over 8 points on Test. For pixel-level mask-text retrieval on Ref-SAV, PixCLIP achieves 47.3% M2T Recall@1, nearly doubling Alpha-CLIP's 28.3%. On zero-shot region classification (ImageNet-S), PixCLIP attains 69.57% Top-1 with a visual prompt, exceeding Alpha-CLIP (68.89%) and CLIP (66.48%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixCLIP consistently outperforms all region-aware CLIP variants: on RefCOCO Test it beats Alpha-CLIP (69.9 vs. 61.1), Red Circle (69.9 vs. 58.6), ReCLIP (69.9 vs. 46.1), and CPT (69.9 vs. 36.1); on ImageNet-S zero-shot classification it surpasses Alpha-CLIP (69.57% vs. 68.89%), MaskCLIP (69.57% vs. 67.86%), and MaskedAdaptedCLIP (69.57% vs. 57.86%). Against long-text retrieval specialists, PixCLIP leads FG-CLIP (86.7 vs. 86.6 avg I2T; 85.0 vs. 82.7 avg T2I) and LLM2CLIP (86.7 vs. 84.1 avg I2T; 85.0 vs. 80.5 avg T2I), with the largest margin on DOCCI (96.7 vs. 85.5 I2T). On the pixel-level Ref-SAV mask-text retrieval, PixCLIP nearly doubles Alpha-CLIP's Recall@1 (47.3% vs. 28.3% M2T; 47.9% vs. 27.5% T2M). PixCLIP also outperforms SigLIP2 (86.7 vs. 77.8 avg I2T) and Long-CLIP (86.7 vs. 75.9 avg I2T) on retrieval, and beats DreamLIP on Winoground fine-grained reasoning (35.25 vs. 26.0 text accuracy). The only notable trade-off is a slight drop in Flickr30k I2T (84.2 vs. LLM2CLIP's 88.5), suggesting a minor cost in short-text image-to-text retrieval, though PixCLIP recovers strongly on the T2I direction (87.0 vs. 78.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

PixCLIP sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: region-aware CLIP variants (MaskCLIP, Alpha-CLIP, ReCLIP) that handle local visual regions but are limited to bounding boxes and short captions, and long-text CLIP extensions (LongCLIP, LLM2CLIP) that handle extended descriptions but only at the global image level. It builds directly on LLM2CLIP's LLM-as-text-encoder paradigm (using LLaMA3-8B via LLM2Vec's self-supervised fine-tuning recipe) and CLIP's contrastive training objective, but introduces a parallel mask patch-embedding layer and a three-branch training framework to unify arbitrary-shaped pixel prompts with long compositional texts. The LongGRIT dataset extends GRIT-20M's mask annotations with multi-MLLM-generated long captions, filling a data gap that neither prior region-aware nor long-text CLIP work addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PixCLIP demonstrates that a single CLIP-style model can serve both pixel-level region retrieval and global image-text retrieval without architectural switching, which is practically valuable for applications like region-based visual search and accessibility tools. The LongGRIT dataset (1.5M verified mask-long-text pairs) provides a reusable resource for the community studying fine-grained alignment. Key limitations include the relatively small dataset size (1.5M samples) and the absence of hard-negative mining, both of which the authors acknowledge as barriers to further scaling. An open question is whether the three-branch training recipe generalizes beyond the ViT-B/16 scale or to other modalities (e.g., video regions), and whether the 10% full-image ratio heuristic transfers to different domain distributions.
