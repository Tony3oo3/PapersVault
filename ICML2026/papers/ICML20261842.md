## Problem

Treatments can shift entire outcome distributions in covariate-dependent ways (e.g., altering variance or tail risk for specific subpopulations), yet valid statistical tests for such conditional distributional treatment effects (DTEs) have been lacking. The existing KCD test of Park et al. (2021) is not doubly robust, relies on permutation resampling without type-1 error guarantees, and uses a degenerate U-statistic that precludes analytical critical values. This paper provides the first provably valid, doubly robust kernel-based test for global homogeneity of conditional potential outcome distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational data (X, A, Y) along with user-specified bounded characteristic kernels on the covariate and outcome spaces, a kernel bandwidth (or a finite grid thereof for the aggregated test), and nuisance models for the propensity score and conditional outcome embeddings. It produces a scalar SKCD test statistic (available in both MMD and Wald-type closed forms), a binary rejection decision at a chosen significance level, and—upon rejection—uniform-in-y confidence bands for the SCoDiTE witness function ψ(x,y) that localize where in the covariate-outcome space the distributional effect is significant. The bootstrap for critical values re-weights pre-computed influence function values rather than refitting nuisances, reducing per-replicate cost to O(n²).

## Method

The paper defines the Smoothed Conditional Distributional Treatment Effect (SCoDiTE) as a Hilbert-valued parameter ψ_P in a tensor-product RKHS H_X ⊗ H_Y, reformulating the conditional DTE null as the linear moment condition ψ_P = 0. It derives the efficient influence function (EIF) of ψ_P, proving pathwise differentiability via a reparameterization argument that reduces the SCoDiTE to a difference of counterfactual kernel mean embeddings, and constructs a cross-fitted one-step (AIPW) estimator that is doubly robust and locally asymptotically minimax optimal. The key inferential insight is that under the null the test statistic T_n = n⟨Ω_n(ψ̄_n), ψ̄_n⟩_H converges to a known quadratic form in a Hilbert-valued Gaussian, so critical values are obtained by bootstrapping the empirical mean of the EIF (no nuisance refitting), yielding a permutation-free test with exact type-1 error control and consistency. Closed-form O(n³) expressions are derived for both the MMD statistic (Ω=I) and a Wald-type statistic (Ω = regularized inverse covariance), the latter exploiting the low-rank structure of the empirical covariance to avoid an n²×n² inversion. A max-aggregated variant (SKCDAgg) combines evidence across a fixed grid of bandwidths with joint bootstrap calibration.

## Results

In the MNIST-based simulation (n ∈ {250, 500, 1000, 2000}, α = 0.05), SKCD_MMD and SKCD_Wald achieve approximately 80% power at n = 2000 under outcome model misspecification, while the KCD baseline (Park et al., 2021) plateaus at a substantially lower level. Under propensity misspecification, SKCD_MMD maintains type 1 error close to the nominal 5% level, whereas KCD suffers significant inflation. On the SIPP 401(k) dataset (9,915 households), the SKCD test rejects the global null of equal conditional potential outcome distributions at α = 0.05, and uniform confidence bands localize the effect to specific household profiles (e.g., a 58-year-old with high education shows a significant distributional shift in Net Financial Assets, while a 36-year-old with a large family shows no detectable effect). Nyström approximations yield up to 5.4× runtime gains for the Wald-type statistic while preserving qualitative type 1 error and power behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the KCD test of Park et al. (2021), which uses a degenerate two-sample U-statistic and permutation resampling. SKCD (both MMD and Wald variants) consistently outperforms KCD on power across all valid configurations, with the gap most pronounced under outcome model misspecification where SKCD reaches ~80% power at n = 2000 while KCD plateaus. On type 1 error control, SKCD_MMD is notably more robust: under propensity misspecification it stays near the nominal 5% level, whereas KCD inflates significantly; under outcome misspecification, KCD's type 1 error diverges sharply as n increases while both SKCD variants remain stable. Computationally, SKCD avoids the O(Mn³) cost of KCD's permutation loop (M = 10²–10³) by computing the EIF once and using a bootstrap with O(n²) per-replicate cost, achieving O(n³ + Bn²) worst-case complexity. The double robustness property further distinguishes SKCD: its estimator remains consistent if either the propensity or outcome model is correct, a guarantee KCD's non-doubly-robust estimator lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional distributional treatment effect (DTE) testing framework of Park et al. (2021), which relied on a degenerate U-statistic (KCD) and permutation tests without validity guarantees, by reformulating the null as a linear moment condition on joint potential outcome–covariate distributions (the SCoDiTE). The estimation strategy directly builds on Luedtke & Chung's (2024) one-step estimator for Hilbert-valued parameters, extending it from marginal to conditional DTEs with cross-fitting and double robustness. The bandwidth-aggregated test (SKCDAgg) is inspired by Schrab et al.'s (2023) MMD-aggregated two-sample test, and the Wald-type statistic generalizes the kernelized Hotelling T² of Eric et al. (2007) to the counterfactual setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first kernel-based test for global homogeneity of conditional potential outcome distributions with provable type 1 error control, double robustness, and local asymptotic minimax optimality—addressing a gap where prior methods either lacked validity guarantees (Park et al.) or were limited to best linear projections (Kallus & Oprescu). The permutation-free bootstrap and closed-form MMD/Wald statistics reduce worst-case complexity from O(Mn³) to O(n³ + Bn²), making the test practical for moderate-to-large datasets. Open questions include extending to continuous treatments and instrumental variable settings, data-driven selection of the Wald regularization parameter ε, and formalizing guarantees under Nyström approximation.
