## Problem

Diffusion-based video inpainting achieves strong per-frame reconstruction but suffers from temporal inconsistency that accumulates over long sequences, because the sampling process implicitly generates temporally coupled latent trajectories whose long-horizon stability is never explicitly modeled. Existing approaches either impose hard frame-to-frame constraints that suppress fine structural detail or lack any mechanism to detect and correct drift that builds up gradually along time-indexed denoising trajectories, creating an unresolved trade-off between temporal coherence and generative fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on VAE latents of masked video frames, using bidirectional optical flow (RAFT) estimated on the latent grid for motion-aligned warping, plus a coarse text prompt for scene context. It is inserted as a plug-and-play inference-time control layer into existing frame-wise diffusion pipelines (SDI, BrushNet) without modifying backbone weights or applying post-hoc temporal blending. The output is a sequence of inpainted video frames at the same resolution as the input, with stabilization applied selectively during the denoising loop rather than as a post-processing step.

## Method

The core contribution is a risk-gated, inference-time trajectory stabilization framework with three coupled components. First, a drift-aware risk accumulator measures motion-aligned latent deviation via optical-flow warping of neighboring frames and maintains an exponentially weighted risk state R_i(t) that distinguishes persistent trajectory drift from transient noise. Second, when risk exceeds a threshold, a soft control signal contracts the unstable latent trajectory toward a stability basin defined by two complementary terms: long-range anchor references (sparsely selected structurally reliable frames warped into the current frame) and short-range neighborhood propagation, spatially gated by a confidence mask so that only uncertain/occluded regions receive anchor guidance while reliable regions continue free evolution. Third, a trajectory-consistent transition prior constructs temporally interpolated motion-aligned references between anchor pairs to ensure smooth inter-anchor evolution without abrupt snapping. The entire mechanism operates on intermediate latent states during diffusion sampling, requiring no retraining and adding only modest per-step overhead.

## Results

On DAVIS, the best augmented pipeline (BrushNet+Ours) achieves PSNR 33.70, SSIM 0.9735, LPIPS 0.0248, and VFID 0.112, while CoCoCo+Ours reaches the lowest VFID of 0.111. On HQVI, SDI+Ours attains the best VFID among diffusion-based methods (0.1795) with PSNR 31.85 and LPIPS 0.0325. Across all four diffusion backbones tested, the trajectory stabilization layer consistently improves PSNR by 1.5–2.6 dB, reduces LPIPS by 30–45%, and lowers VFID by 10–20% relative to the unmodified backbone, with the largest gains on long sequences and high-risk segments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is an inference-time plug-in, so the most direct comparison is each backbone against its augmented version: SDI improves from PSNR 30.48→32.21 and LPIPS 0.0546→0.0339 on DAVIS; BrushNet from 31.04→33.70 and 0.0478→0.0248; CoCoCo from 32.51→33.04 and 0.0305→0.0274; DiffuEraser from 31.80→32.48 and 0.0450→0.0396. Against the strongest propagation baseline ProPainter (PSNR 34.19, VFID 0.098 on DAVIS), the best augmented pipeline (BrushNet+Ours, PSNR 33.70, VFID 0.112) remains slightly behind on DAVIS but narrows the gap substantially on HQVI, where ProPainter's VFID degrades to 0.2128 while SDI+Ours achieves 0.1795. Among diffusion-based methods, CoCoCo+Ours attains the best DAVIS VFID (0.111) and SDI+Ours the best HQVI VFID (0.1795), both outperforming RGVI (0.1825), VideoPainter (0.2052), and DiffuEraser (0.2962) on HQVI. On the long-video VPBench-L benchmark, BrushNet+Ours leads all methods with PSNR 23.11, SSIM 0.871, LPIPS 0.085, and VFID 0.17, confirming that the trajectory-stabilization advantage grows with sequence length where drift accumulation is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground within the well-established video inpainting task by reframing temporal inconsistency as accumulated instability along time-indexed denoising trajectories in latent space, rather than as a per-frame reconstruction error or an output-level consistency violation. It departs from propagation-based methods (ProPainter, E2FGVI, RGVI) that transfer content along optical flow and from frame-wise diffusion pipelines (AVID, DiffuEraser, CoCoCo) that impose uniform cross-frame guidance during sampling, arguing that both fail to detect or correct drift that accumulates persistently over long horizons. The method is positioned as a lightweight, plug-and-play inference-time control layer that operates orthogonally to the diffusion backbone, drawing on control-theoretic ideas (risk-gated feedback, energy-descent stabilization) applied to the latent sampling dynamics, without requiring retraining or architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-stability perspective offers a principled lens for understanding and regulating long-horizon drift in any frame-wise generative temporal pipeline, potentially generalizing beyond inpainting to other video editing or completion tasks. Practically, the plug-and-play nature (no retraining, works with SDI, BrushNet, DiffuEraser, CoCoCo) makes it immediately deployable, and the long-video results showing stable SSIM while baselines degrade are encouraging for real-world applications. However, the method still depends on reliable optical flow estimation and operates in a frame-wise setting, leaving open whether the trajectory-stabilization principle can be integrated into end-to-end video diffusion models or whether the fixed risk parameters (γ, τ) generalize across diverse motion regimes without per-scene tuning.
