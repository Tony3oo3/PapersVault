## Problem

Representation similarity metrics (CKA, RSA, SVCCA) are non-identifiable for head-level transfer prediction: tasks can share identical frozen activations yet have orthogonal head updates, rendering activation-only scores uninformative in the 'activation-dark' regime common to shared-vocabulary LLM families. Classical Fisher alignment captures this label-conditioned update geometry but is computationally prohibitive at vocabulary scale (K ≈ 10⁵), where even the error second moment Γₑ costs ~61 GiB per task and naive projections cost O(mK) per sample.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen model checkpoint and a streaming set of (input, label) pairs per task, requiring only a single forward and backward pass per sample with no gradient accumulation or fine-tuning. Tasks must share an output coordinate system (e.g., a common vocabulary) for head-level comparisons to be well-defined; disjoint label subsets are handled via a masked-softmax embedding. The output is a 16 KB float32 task signature (m = 4096 random-feature coordinates) plus a 192 KB per-task streaming state for split-sample estimation—small enough to store alongside a model hash—whose pairwise cosines yield Fisher alignment scores and source-selection rankings over candidate domains.

## Method

The central theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that head Fisher alignment is exactly the cosine between kernel mean embeddings of joint (activation, error) features under a product polynomial kernel, yielding an exact three-factor decomposition into activation geometry, error geometry, and a coupling ratio ρ that measures deviation from separability. FisherSketch estimates this product-kernel cosine in one streaming pass using factored Random Maclaurin features: because the per-example head gradient factorizes as g = a ⊗ e, Rademacher projections onto a and e are computed separately, reducing per-sample cost from O(mKd) to O(m(d+K)). At vocabulary scale (K = 128,256), a structured random Hadamard transform (SRHT) handles the K-dimensional error side in O(K log K) per sample while dense Rademacher projections handle the activation side, with empirical-Bayes shrinkage stabilizing the coupling estimate. The resulting 16 KB signatures support pairwise alignment via simple dot products, enabling training-free source selection across 100+ domains on Llama-3.1-8B with 45.7% top-1 accuracy and 98.44% of oracle normalized transfer, and remaining informative in verbalizer-shift regimes where activation similarity collapses to a constant.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B across 100 domains (K=128,256 vocabulary), FisherSketch (SRHT) achieves 45.7%±5.0 top-1 source selection (≈10.9× random baseline of 4.2%) and 98.44%±0.30 of oracle normalized transfer, with max regret 0.119±0.013. In the verbalizer-shift stress test where activation similarity is constant (S_cov(M_a)≈1), FisherSketch reaches 66.7% top-1 and 95.7% of oracle, while activation-only scores collapse to the 20% random baseline. The method compresses each task from ~61 GiB (full Γ_e) to a 16 KB float32 signature with a 192 KB streaming state, and achieves an 89× speedup over exact O(n²) computation at n=2000 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019), the paper demonstrates a fundamental non-identifiability gap: on ViT-B/16 same-dataset pairs, CKA correlates only 0.43 Pearson / 0.45 Spearman with exact head Fisher, whereas FisherSketch's FA-CKA variant reaches 0.79 / 0.90. In the verbalizer-shift regime (fixed-prefix prompting on BoolQ/RTE/CB), activation-only scores (the CKA-analog) are constant and reduce to the 20% random baseline, while FisherSketch achieves 66.7% top-1. Against LogME (You et al., 2021) and LEEP (Nguyen et al., 2020), the paper reports a small-K controlled sweep (Appendix O.7) where S_cov(Γ_e) correlates 0.977 with transfer gain, matching LogME (0.976) and exceeding LEEP (0.964); however, these label-aware baselines require finite label maps and do not scale to K≈10⁵, whereas FisherSketch operates in a single streaming pass at vocabulary scale. On natural domain shifts (Table 1), the activation-only marginal S_cov(M_a) slightly outperforms FisherSketch on top-1 (46.3% vs. 45.7%) with lower max regret (0.114 vs. 0.119), so FisherSketch is best positioned for the activation-dark regime rather than uniform dominance across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the exact Kronecker gradient structure of affine layers (central to K-FAC optimization methods) not for curvature approximation but for cross-task Fisher alignment, reframing it as a product-kernel cosine between mean embeddings in a joint activation-error space (Theorem 4.2). The sketching mechanism draws on Random Maclaurin features for unbiased squared-inner-product estimation, extended here to the factored Kronecker setting to achieve O(m(d+K)) per-sample cost. Conceptually, the paper departs from representation-similarity metrics (CKA, RSA, SVCCA) by proving they are non-identifiable for head-level transfer (Theorem 3.2), and from finite-label transferability scores (LogME, LEEP) by operating at vocabulary scale (K≈10⁵) via streaming random projections rather than label-conditioned class statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16 KB task signature makes vocabulary-scale Fisher alignment practical for the first time, enabling training-free source selection across LLM families in the activation-dark regime where representation similarity is uninformative. The non-identifiability theorem provides a clean formal limitation of CKA/RSA/SVCCA for head-level transfer, though the authors carefully scope this to head comparisons under shared output spaces. Key limitations include the head-level restriction (full-network extension is diagnostic rather than exact), the shared-vocabulary requirement, and empirical validation on a single model family (Llama-3.1-8B) with a small verbalizer-shift protocol (9 runs). Open questions include whether the coupling term ρ remains informative under population Fisher, how signatures behave across checkpoints, and whether the approach extends to heterogeneous output spaces without an explicit transport map.
