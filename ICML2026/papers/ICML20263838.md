## Problem

Boltzmann Generators for equilibrium molecular sampling have relied almost exclusively on normalizing flows, which face a fundamental tradeoff: discrete-time flows are topologically constrained (cannot model the disjoint, multi-modal supports of molecular energy landscapes) and numerically unstable, while continuous-time flows are more expressive but require expensive ODE solvers for likelihood evaluation, rendering importance sampling computationally prohibitive. This paper asks whether an autoregressive, diffeomorphism-free paradigm can overcome both limitations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes molecular conformations as n×3 Cartesian coordinate matrices, flattened into a single d=n×3 vector and ordered residue-by-residue (backbone atoms followed by sidechain atoms per residue). Each coordinate is discretized into L uniform bins (4096–8192) over a data-standardized interval, and the model predicts a Categorical distribution over bin indices per dimension. At inference, continuous coordinates are recovered by adding uniform noise within the sampled bin, and final equilibrium samples are produced via self-normalized importance sampling against the target energy function. The transferable model ROBIN additionally conditions on the peptide amino-acid sequence for zero-shot generalization to unseen sequences.

## Method

ARBG factorizes the joint molecular density as a product of per-dimension conditionals p_θ(x)=∏_j p_θ(x_j|x_<j), modeled by a transformer that predicts the next coordinate's bin index autoregressively—directly analogous to next-token prediction in LLMs. The key design choice is a uniform bin parameterization: rather than mixture density networks (which suffer mode collapse), the model outputs a Categorical over L bins, yielding an exact log-likelihood in a single forward pass with no Jacobian determinants or ODE integration. This eliminates the topological constraints of flows (no diffeomorphism is required) and unlocks LLM-style inference-time tools, including temperature scaling and an Autoregressive Twisted SMC scheme that injects partial energy evaluations as twist functions to resample and reject physically implausible substructures mid-generation. The paper proves (Proposition 1) that the irreducible KL error of this parameterization equals the weighted sum of within-bin non-uniformity, and demonstrates that 132M-parameter ROBIN achieves over 60% reduction in zero-shot E-W2 error on 8-residue peptides versus the prior SOTA (Prose, a discrete-time NF).

## Results

ARBG achieves the best E-W2 and T-W2 across all four single-peptide benchmarks (AL3, AL4, AL6, Chignolin), with particularly dramatic gains on the 10-residue Chignolin system where it reduces E-W2 from 10.819 (SBG) to 1.723 and T-W2 from 3.778 to 2.632. In the transferable setting, ROBIN (132M parameters) reduces the zero-shot E-W2 on 8-residue peptides from 10.038 (Prose) to 4.251, a >60% improvement, while also improving T-W2 (2.325 vs 2.456) and TICA-W2 (0.943 vs 0.988). ARBG also outperforms the AR-based GIVT baseline on every system, confirming that the uniform bin parameterization and LLM-style training recipe are critical to the gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARBG comprehensively outperforms all flow-based baselines. Against the prior single-system SOTA SBG (Tan et al., 2025a), ARBG reduces Chignolin E-W2 from 10.819 to 1.723 (6.3×) and T-W2 from 3.778 to 2.632; on AL6, E-W2 drops from 1.189 to 0.328. The few-step CNF FALCON (Rehman et al., 2026a) is the closest flow-based competitor on AL3 (E-W2 0.544 vs 0.202) but still loses by 2.7×, and FALCON-A is weaker still. The equivariant CNF ECNF++ (Klein et al., 2023b) and discrete flow RegFlow (Rehman et al., 2026b) are substantially worse, with ECNF++ reaching E-W2 of 10.668 on AL6 versus ARBG's 0.328. Among AR baselines, GIVT (Tschannen et al., 2024) and GMM-PixelCNN++ are competitive on small systems but degrade sharply on Chignolin (E-W2 45.6 and 23.3 respectively vs ARBG's 1.723), confirming that the uniform bin parameterization is critical for scalability. In the transferable setting, ROBIN beats the prior SOTA Prose (Tan et al., 2025b) on all 8-residue metrics (E-W2 4.251 vs 10.038, T-W2 2.325 vs 2.456, TICA-W2 0.943 vs 0.988) and also outperforms TarFlow (Zhai et al., 2025), BioEmu (Lewis et al., 2025), UniSim (Yu et al., 2025), and TimeWarp (Klein et al., 2023a) on 8AA, though Prose retains a slight edge on 4AA E-W2 (0.932 vs 1.168).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant flow-based paradigm in Boltzmann Generation (Noe et al., 2019), where both discrete normalizing flows and continuous normalizing flows have been the near-exclusive architecture choice due to their tractable likelihoods. ARBG instead adopts the autoregressive factorization familiar from LLMs and PixelCNN++ (Salimans et al., 2017), applying it directly to continuous molecular coordinates via a novel uniform binning parameterization that supersedes the more complex Mixture Density Network (Bishop, 1994) formulations. The key conceptual departure is that autoregressive models are not constrained to be diffeomorphisms, thereby avoiding the topological limitations that force flows to perform extreme deformations to model the multi-modal, disconnected structure of Boltzmann distributions. The Autoregressive Twisted SMC inference procedure further extends the LLM toolkit (temperature scaling, sequential intervention) into the molecular domain in a way that is architecturally impossible for flow-based generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARBG provides the first scalable, diffeomorphism-free framework for Boltzmann Generation, resolving a fundamental tradeoff in the field: discrete flows are expressive but topologically constrained, while continuous flows are expressive but computationally prohibitive for likelihood evaluation. The demonstration that LLM-style scaling laws and inference-time interventions (temperature, SMC) transfer to molecular conformation sampling opens a path toward increasingly large and complex molecular systems using well-understood transformer infrastructure. Key limitations remain: the imposed dimension ordering may affect performance on non-peptide molecules, the uniform bin width Δ bounds coordinate precision, and the integration of informative priors (as in TFEP) into the AR framework is unexplored. Whether ARBG's advantages persist at protein-scale systems (hundreds of residues) with sharper energy landscapes remains an open question.
