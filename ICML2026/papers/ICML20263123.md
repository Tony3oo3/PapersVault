## Problem

Selecting the best LLM to deploy typically requires evaluating every model on every test query, which is prohibitively expensive as inference costs and annotation effort grow. While best-arm identification algorithms (e.g., UCB-E, Successive Rejects) can reduce evaluation costs by adaptively discarding weak models, they treat arms as independent and ignore the well-documented correlation between models' responses to the same prompt. Prior attempts to exploit this correlation (e.g., UCB-E-LRF) either lack theoretical guarantees or require a priori knowledge of the correlation strength, leaving a gap for a hyperparameter-free method with provable guarantees that improve with model similarity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes K candidate LLMs, a test set of L queries, and a fixed evaluation budget n (expressed as a percentage of the K×L total model-query pairs). At each step it selects one model and one query to evaluate, observing a score S_ij ∈ [0,1] (deterministic or stochastic, sampled without replacement per model-query pair). It outputs the index of the best-performing model along with a theoretical upper bound on the identification error probability that depends on a new correlation-aware hardness measure H_3 rather than the classical H_2.

## Method

SySRs modifies the classical Successive Rejects algorithm by introducing synchronization: instead of independently sampling test queries for each model, every phase evaluates all remaining models on the same randomly drawn subset of queries, analogous to a paired comparison test. The budget n is split into K−1 phases following the standard SR schedule, and at the end of each phase the model with the lowest empirical mean is eliminated. The key theoretical contribution is a new hardness measure H_3 that depends on the variance of the score difference V_{*,i} = Var(S_{i*J} − S_{(i)J}) rather than the sum of per-arm variances; positive between-model correlation reduces this variance, tightening the error bound P(error) ≤ K(K−1)/2 · exp(−(n−K)/(log K · H_3)). The proof uses a Bernstein-type concentration inequality for paired differences (Lemma B.2) instead of Hoeffding's inequality, yielding a bound that improves from Θ(1/Δ²) to Θ(1/Δ) in the maximally correlated two-arm regime. The algorithm is entirely hyperparameter-free, unlike UCB-E variants that require tuning an exploration parameter a.

## Results

Across 15 standard LLM benchmarks, SySRs achieves 95.1% average identification accuracy at just 12% of the full evaluation budget, outperforming SR (89.7%), the best UCB-E variant (86.8% at a=1.0), and UCB-E-LRF (60.8%). In the worst case, SySRs reliably identifies the best model on all 15 benchmarks using at most 35% of model/query pairs (100% Confidence Budget), compared to 43% for SR, ~50% for the best UCB-E hyperparameter, and 55% for UCB-E-LRF. The correlation-aware hardness H₃ is on average 0.56× the tighter-analysis hardness H₃′ and 0.61× the classical H₂, confirming that both the Bernstein-based analysis and the synchronization mechanism contribute to the gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SySRs outperforms all baselines at every budget level tested. Against SR (the closest competitor), SySRs gains roughly 5–11 percentage points at low budgets (e.g., 95.1% vs. 89.7% at 12%) and maintains a 1.6–3.2 point lead in the 16–30% range, with the gap narrowing only at 35% where both reach near-perfect accuracy. Against UCB-E, SySRs dominates across all three exploration parameters (a=0.1, 1.0, 10.0); even the best UCB-E setting (a=1.0) trails SySRs by 8.3 points at 12% budget and by 1.7 points at 35%. Against UCB-E-LRF from Zhou et al. (2025), SySRs leads by a wide margin (95.1% vs. 60.8% at 12%), and UCB-E-LRF never surpasses 93.2% even at 35% budget. A consistent secondary finding is that synchronization itself helps: SyUS beats US by ~9–11 points, SyUCB-E (a=1.0) beats UCB-E (a=1.0) by ~2–3 points, and SySRs beats SR by ~5–11 points, confirming that evaluating all remaining models on the same test instances is broadly beneficial regardless of the underlying bandit strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Successive Rejects (SR) algorithm from Audibert et al. (2010) by introducing synchronization: all remaining models are evaluated on the same test instances in each phase, turning independent arm sampling into paired comparisons. It builds directly on Zhou et al. (2025), who first framed LLM model selection as a fixed-budget best-arm identification problem and proposed UCB-E-LRF (low-rank factorization) to exploit model correlation, but without theoretical guarantees. The key departure from prior correlated-arm bandit work (Gupta et al., 2021b) is that SySRs is hyperparameter-free and its guarantees improve with correlation without requiring a priori knowledge of the correlation strength.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners selecting among dozens or hundreds of LLMs, SySRs reduces evaluation cost to at most 35% of full model/query pairs while guaranteeing correct identification, which is substantial given surging inference and annotation costs. The theoretical contribution—a hardness measure H₃ that provably tightens with between-model correlation, matched by a minimax lower bound in the two-armed case—gives the first principled account of when and why synchronization helps. Open questions include extending to top-m ranking (preliminary experiments suggest SySRs helps but is not purpose-built for it), designing UCB variants with correlation-aware guarantees, and making low-rank factorization approaches both robust and theoretically grounded.
