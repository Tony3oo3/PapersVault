## Problem

Automated evaluation of vlog editing plans lacks standardized criteria, dedicated benchmarks, and effective reward models, making it difficult to distinguish varied-quality edits. Unlike standard video understanding, vlog editing involves highly subjective, multi-dimensional judgments (creativity, pacing, cinematography, etc.) that current MLLMs handle poorly, as evidenced by their inability to reliably rank editing plans of different quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262975/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a concatenated video of raw clips (separated by 3-second black screens, processed at 1 FPS with max 64 frames at 64×28×28 pixels per frame) and a JSON editing plan specifying cut timestamps, playback speed, voiceover, shot order, title, and subtitle. It outputs six integer scores (1–5) across Creativity, Consistency, Concept Design, Cinematography, Narration, and Pacing, plus per-dimension textual feedback that flags specific issues with timestamp references. Scores serve as reward signals for Best-of-N selection or RL, while feedback drives iterative plan refinement.

## Method

VlogReward is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, trained via SFT (100k samples) followed by GRPO (40k samples). The key technical contribution is an inter-group comparison reward (CR) that augments standard GRPO's intra-group score reward (SR). Standard GRPO computes relative advantages only within rollouts of a single sample, causing 'direction blindness' where the model improves absolute score accuracy but fails to preserve correct quality ordering across different editing plans. CR addresses this by rewarding rollouts whose predicted score sums maintain the correct relative ordering (sign of predicted difference matches sign of ground-truth difference) against other plans of the same raw footage, averaged over all m−1 comparisons. The total reward is r_format × [(1−λ)·r_score + λ·r_comp] with λ=0.25, enabling a tunable trade-off between scoring accuracy and discrimination capability. This yields a generative reward model that outputs interpretable multi-dimensional scores and diagnostic feedback rather than a single scalar.

## Results

On VRMBench, VlogReward achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three metrics: 51.3% average Score Accuracy, 73.5% average Comparison Accuracy, and 67.6% average Best-of-N Accuracy. These results significantly outperform the strongest closed-source baseline Gemini-3-Pro (38.6% / 71.9% / 52.8%) and the strongest open-source baseline Qwen2.5-VL-72B (32.1% / 46.0% / 34.6%). The inter-group comparison reward (CR) provides consistent gains over standard GRPO with score reward only (GRM-GRPO: 50.3% / 69.4% / 65.0%), with the largest improvement on Comparison Accuracy (+4.1 pp) and Best-of-N Accuracy (+2.6 pp). On the UltraFeedback generalization task, SR+CR also improves over SR alone (47.0 vs 46.1 score, 56.4% vs 54.7% comparison, 53.6% vs 51.2% BoN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VlogReward outperforms all 11 external MLLM baselines on every metric. Against the strongest closed-source model Gemini-3-Pro, VlogReward leads by +12.7 pp on Score Accuracy (51.3 vs 38.6), +1.6 pp on Comparison Accuracy (73.5 vs 71.9), and +14.8 pp on Best-of-N Accuracy (67.6 vs 52.8). GPT-5 is the second-strongest closed-source baseline (36.4 / 63.1 / 51.5), trailing VlogReward by 14.9 / 10.4 / 16.1 pp respectively. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B is the best (32.1 / 46.0 / 34.6), still far behind VlogReward by 19.2 / 27.5 / 33.0 pp. Internally, VlogReward (SR+CR) beats GRM-GRPO (SR only) on all three metrics, while GRM-GRPO-Inter-Advantage (computing advantages across groups) actually degrades performance (49.1 / 56.0 / 55.7), confirming that the proposed inter-group comparison reward design is superior to naive inter-group advantage computation. On the UltraFeedback transfer task, SR+CR also consistently beats SR-only GRPO across score, comparison, and BoN metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing an inter-group comparison reward that addresses a 'direction blindness' limitation of standard intra-group relative advantages. In the broader multimodal reward modeling literature, it departs from the dominant pairwise-comparison paradigm (used by R1-Reward, BaseReward, etc.) by formulating a generative reward model that outputs multi-dimensional integer scores plus diagnostic textual feedback across six expert-defined criteria. For vlog editing specifically, prior work has largely decomposed the problem into sub-tasks (highlight detection, shot ordering, summarization) or relied on ad-hoc IoU metrics and human ratings; this paper is the first to propose a unified, standardized evaluation framework with a dedicated large-scale dataset and benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive benchmark (VRMBench) and 100k-scale training dataset for vlog editing evaluation, filling a critical gap that has limited reproducibility in this area. The inter-group comparison reward is a lightweight, generalizable RL technique (validated on UltraFeedback) that could benefit other preference-learning tasks beyond vlogs. Key limitations include the restriction to montage-style vlogs, 1 fps / low-resolution video processing, and inherently subjective ground-truth scores that represent consensus rather than absolute truth. Open questions include generalization to other vlog genres (tutorials, sit-down monologues), incorporation of fine-grained stylistic elements (transitions, color grading, music), and whether the framework scales to longer-form or multi-creator content.
