## Problem

Diffusion models achieve high-fidelity generation but their latent spaces are high-dimensional and not explicitly organized for temporal or condition-dependent structure, so linear interpolations produce implausible intermediate states and existing conditioning methods (ControlNet, InstructPix2Pix) fail to yield consistent traversal directions across time or auxiliary variables. No general recipe existed for aligning pretrained diffusion latents with system dynamics while preserving generative fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB frame sequences (256×256×3) paired with per-frame auxiliary variables (normalized time, TMS coil angle in degrees, 12-dimensional AU intensity vectors, or 2D reach velocity), or for the monkey spiking domain, 182-channel spike data pre-mapped to 16-D S4 latents. Outputs include generated frame sequences for interpolation, n-step-ahead extrapolation, or counterfactual class transfer, plus a low-dimensional embedding trajectory (d=3–8) in C-space and downstream class labels (responder/non-responder, steady/unsteady). The critical architectural constraint is that all editing operators operate in the compact C-space while rendering is deferred to the original 32×32×4 diffusion latent space Z, with a kNN decoder bridging the two.

## Method

ConDA introduces a plug-and-play contrastive geometry layer with three stages: (1) a conditional latent diffusion model (cLDM) is trained and diffusion inversion (DDIM or Rex-RK4) maps each frame to a high-dimensional feature latent z_s ∈ Z; (2) a supervised InfoNCE objective (implemented via CEBRA) learns a compact embedding h_ψ: Z×Y→C where positives are frames sharing the same auxiliary-variable label, organizing C-space geometry to align with dynamics; (3) nonlinear trajectory operators (C²-continuous B-splines, second-order Taylor extrapolation, or KDE-based class-mode interpolation) traverse C-space, and a neighborhood-preserving kNN decoder lifts edited embeddings back to Z for final diffusion sampling. The key design choice is separating editing from rendering: the low-dimensional C-space (d<10) supports interpretable, smooth traversal with simple operators, while the high-dimensional Z-space preserves reconstruction fidelity, and the kNN decoder exploits the contrastive neighborhood structure rather than learning a parametric mapping. This makes the framework solver-agnostic (works with DDIM, Rex-RK4, and IsoDiff latents) and applicable across physical and biological domains without architecture changes.

## Results

In the primary interpolation task (Table 1), ConDA's Spline and TEX-2 methods in C-space achieve near-perfect reconstruction: 35.70 PSNR / 0.00 RMSE on Fluid, 38.58 PSNR / 0.00 RMSE on Ca²⁺, and 38.99 PSNR / 0.00 RMSE on DISFA, compared to 28.16 PSNR / 16.12 RMSE for linear Lerp in raw Z-space. For n-step-ahead prediction (Table 2), Spline in C-space yields the lowest RMSE across all three datasets (1.48 Fluid, 1.09 Ca²⁺, 0.52 DISFA). On classification (Table 3), SVM-RBF trained in C-space achieves F1 = 0.63 for TMS responder/non-responder vs. 0.30 in Z-space, and F1 = 0.85 for steady/unsteady flow vs. 0.52 in Z-space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConDA's Spline/TEX-2 in C-space substantially outperform all baselines: Lerp and Slerp (Preechakul et al., 2022) remain poor in both Z and C spaces (e.g., 28.16 vs. 35.70 PSNR on Fluid), confirming that trajectories are inherently nonlinear. LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) improves markedly when moved from Z to C space (RMSE drops from 4.04 to 1.07 on Fluid) but still trails Spline/TEX-2 by a wide margin. In the ablation (Table 4), ConDA outperforms both PCA and β-VAE (Higgins et al., 2017, β=4) on test-segment prediction across all three image datasets (e.g., 36.26 vs. 35.76 PSNR on DISFA). cLDM with ConDA also substantially outperforms CcGAN (Ding et al., 2021) in both generation and reconstruction quality. Finally, when applied on top of IsoDiff (Hahm et al., 2024) latents with Rex-RK4 inversion, ConDA further improves interpolation fidelity (42.51 PSNR / 0.00 RMSE on DISFA), demonstrating solver- and model-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConDA sits at the intersection of latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022), diffusion inversion via DDIM (Song et al., 2021), and contrastive representation learning (CEBRA, Schneider et al., 2023), adding a supervised contrastive geometry layer that organizes pretrained diffusion latents using auxiliary variables. It departs from conditioning-based editing (ControlNet, InstructPix2Pix) which guides samples without organizing latent geometry, and from GAN latent editing which had interpretable directions but suffered from imperfect inversion. The paper also builds on LDNS (Kapoor et al., 2024) for neural spiking data, showing that ConDA can be applied on top of structured S4 latents to further improve interpretability. The key architectural departure is the explicit separation of editing (in a compact contrastive space C) from rendering (in the original high-dimensional diffusion latent space Z), a principle not present in prior diffusion-based editing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConDA provides a general, plug-and-play recipe for making pretrained diffusion models controllable for spatiotemporal dynamics, validated across five physically and biologically distinct domains (fluid dynamics, calcium imaging, TMS E-fields, facial expressions, monkey motor cortex). The near-zero RMSE achieved by simple nonlinear operators (spline, TEX-2) in the contrastive embedding suggests that diffusion latents encode dynamics-relevant structure that was previously inaccessible through direct manipulation. Key limitations include the lossy many-to-one embedding (mitigated by kNN lifting), emphasis on local rather than global geometry, and the computational cost of DDIM inversion. Open questions include whether learned decoders can replace kNN lifting, whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or less structured dynamics, and whether the contrastive objective can be made unsupervised.
