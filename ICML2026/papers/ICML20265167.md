## Problem

MoE-based methods for multimodal continual instruction tuning (MCIT) suffer from two coupled forms of forgetting: router drift, where previously seen inputs are progressively reassigned to different experts as new tasks arrive, and expert drift, where shared experts are overwritten by new-task updates and lose functionality on earlier tasks. Existing MoE+LoRA approaches for MCIT still exhibit substantial accuracy degradation on early tasks as the sequence grows, and no prior work explicitly diagnoses or addresses both drift mechanisms simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model (LLaVA-v1.5-7B backbone) receives an RGB image and a text instruction, producing a free-form text response. Images are encoded by a frozen CLIP-L/14-336 vision encoder and projected into the language embedding space; only LoRA modules (rank 8) inserted into the LLM's FFN layers are trainable. The TriGap benchmark introduces diverse output formats across 10 tasks—multiple-choice letters, numerical values, single-word phrases, chemical formulas, and capitalized phrases—making format preservation a non-trivial part of the output contract.

## Method

SAME stabilizes MoE-based MCIT through three complementary mechanisms. (1) Spectral-aware routing maintains a running uncentered covariance of router inputs, performs SVD to split the update space into a task-relevant subspace (V_parallel) and a history-preserving null subspace (V_perp), then applies anisotropic scaling (inverse sliding-window singular values) to updates in V_parallel while projecting the residual update onto V_perp so that old-task routing predictions are approximately unchanged. (2) Curvature-aware Riemannian scaling preconditions each LoRA expert's gradient with the damped low-rank pseudo-inverse of the historical input covariance C^{t-1}, downweighting updates along directions heavily used by prior tasks and thereby bounding functional degradation without replay. (3) Adaptive expert activation computes a per-expert score from current-task routing utilization minus a running AGOP-proxy of historical importance, and temporarily freezes experts below a threshold during the current task, reducing redundant backpropagation and protecting historically critical experts from cross-task interference.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark (8 sequential tasks), SAME achieves 66.82% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline HiDe-LLaVA (63.95%) by +2.87% and MoE-LoRA (50.58%) by +16.24%. On UCIT (6 tasks), SAME reaches 67.12% average accuracy, outperforming ModalPrompt (65.52%) and HiDe-LLaVA (64.19%). On the newly proposed TriGap benchmark (10 tasks with heterogeneous domains and non-uniform data scales), SAME attains 46.53% average accuracy, exceeding MoE-LoRA (44.45%) by +2.08%. In terms of retention, SAME achieves the lowest average forgetting (F̄ = −4.23%) compared to MoE-LoRA (−19.04%) and HiDe-LLaVA (−6.38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAME outperforms all eight baselines on average across all three benchmarks. On CoIN, the largest gains are over MoE-LoRA (+16.24%) and LLaVA-CMoE (+7.59%), with SAME excelling particularly on REC (59.87% vs. 23.40%) and GQA (61.70% vs. 48.91%), though LLaVA-CMoE retains an edge on ImageNet (94.50% vs. 90.21%). On UCIT, SAME leads ModalPrompt by +1.60% and HiDe-LLaVA by +2.93%, with the strongest per-task advantage on ImageNet-R (83.83% vs. 81.50%). On the more challenging TriGap benchmark (10 tasks, non-uniform scales), SAME surpasses MoE-LoRA by +2.08% and CL-MoE by +2.42%, with notable gains on DocVQA (43.87% vs. 37.49%) and IconQA (64.03% vs. 52.70%). The forgetting analysis confirms SAME's advantage: average forgetting of −4.23% versus −19.04% for MoE-LoRA, indicating substantially better retention of early-task knowledge throughout the sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE+LoRA paradigm for multimodal continual instruction tuning (established by MoELoRA, CL-MoE, HiDe-LLaVA, and LLaVA-CMoE) by diagnosing two distinct failure modes—router drift and expert drift—that prior methods address only implicitly through generic regularization or dual-momentum schemes. The spectral-aware routing draws on subspace projection ideas from continual learning (GPM-style null-space projection, VPT-CPG), but departs by using anisotropic, curvature-weighted scaling within the signal subspace rather than hard projection. The curvature-aware expert scaling is derived from a Riemannian gradient perspective under a metric induced by historical input covariance, and the adaptive expert activation borrows the AGOP trace (Radhakrishnan et al., 2024) as a lightweight sensitivity proxy, repurposing it for task-level freezing decisions rather than feature selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TriGap benchmark (10 tasks, heterogeneous domains, non-uniform scales, diverse instruction formats) fills a real gap in MCIT evaluation, as existing benchmarks (CoIN, UCIT) use short sequences and balanced data that understate long-horizon drift. The diagnostic protocol separating router drift from expert drift provides a reusable analytical framework for future MoE-based continual learning work. However, the method assumes known task boundaries and operates in a rehearsal-free setting; the authors note that robustness under ambiguous task boundaries and tighter coupling between inference-time routing and drift control remain open. The formatting-induced forgetting analysis (case-sensitivity errors dominating ScienceQA degradation) highlights a practical vulnerability in MLLM continual tuning that is likely underappreciated in the field.
