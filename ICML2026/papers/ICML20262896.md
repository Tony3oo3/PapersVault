## Problem

Task-vector-based model merging, which composes independently fine-tuned checkpoints into a single multi-task model, has been shown to perform well on average accuracy but its impact on subgroup fairness remains unexplored. This paper identifies that merging systematically amplifies performance disparities across subgroups even when individual checkpoints exhibit moderate gaps, and provides the first sensitivity-based theoretical characterization of why and how this amplification occurs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a shared pretrained ViT backbone, independently fine-tuned task-specific checkpoints (one per task), labeled target-task data with subgroup (class) annotations for Stage 1 fine-tuning, and unlabeled data from all tasks for Stage 2 coefficient optimization. It produces a single merged model parameterized as θ_pre + Σ α_t τ̂_t that simultaneously serves all tasks, with the target task's subgroup fairness explicitly controlled while auxiliary tasks retain competitive accuracy.

## Method

FairMerging is a two-stage framework grounded in a Taylor-expansion-derived fairness bound showing that the subgroup fairness gap F(D) ≤ Δ_α·ΔG(D) + ½Δ_α²·ΔΛ(D) + O(Δ_α³), where Δ_α is the merging magnitude and ΔG, ΔΛ are global sensitivity terms. Stage 1 fine-tunes the target model with an objective that penalizes the across-subgroup variance of gradient norms and Hessian eigenvalues (reducing ΔG and ΔΛ) while maintaining accuracy via an L2 proximity term. Stage 2 first applies Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to decorrelate task vectors and rescale auxiliary vectors to unit norm, then optimizes merging coefficients by minimizing an unlabeled entropy objective (inherited from AdaMerging) augmented with a fairness cost J_fair(α) = Δ̃_α·Δ̃G(D) + ½Δ̃_α²·Δ̃Λ(D) that directly penalizes the theoretical bound, with a trade-off weight η controlling the fairness–accuracy Pareto frontier.

## Results

On the primary ViT-B/32 six-task merging setup, FairMerging reduces the average subgroup fairness gap F(D) from 1.49 (best baseline, AdaMerging) to 0.42, a 72% relative reduction, with improvements on every dataset (e.g., UTK-Face: 2.62→0.57; GTSRB: 2.04→0.62). In terms of multi-task accuracy, FairMerging achieves 75.04% average accuracy, trailing only AdaMerging (79.25%) while outperforming MetaGPT (73.87%), Ties-Merging (72.24%), DARE (71.92%), and Task Arithmetic (63.63%). The gains generalize across backbones: on ViT-L/14 the average F(D) drops to 0.33 and on ViT-S/16 to 0.37, while average accuracy remains competitive (78.05% and 77.51% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairMerging decisively outperforms all five merging baselines on the fairness metric F(D) across every target task, reducing the average gap from 1.49 (AdaMerging, the strongest baseline) to 0.42. On multi-task accuracy, FairMerging (75.04% Avg Acc) trails AdaMerging (79.25%) by about 4.2 points but surpasses MetaGPT (73.87%), Ties-Merging (72.24%), DARE (71.92%), and Task Arithmetic (63.63%). At the per-task level, FairMerging achieves the best accuracy on FER-2013 (52.75%) and CINIC10 (92.15%), while AdaMerging leads on UTK-Face (75.84%) and GTSRB (90.48%). The ablation shows that Stage 2 (fairness-aware coefficient optimization) alone achieves an average F(D) of 0.58, already beating all baselines, while Stage 1 (sensitivity control) provides a complementary but smaller reduction when paired with any baseline (e.g., AdaMerging drops from 1.49 to 1.26). The results are consistent across ViT-L/14 and ViT-S/16 backbones, confirming that the fairness gains are not architecture-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of task-vector-based model merging (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, DARE, AdaMerging, MetaGPT) and the growing fairness literature on how post-training transformations disproportionately affect subgroups. It builds directly on the task vector representation from Ilharco et al. (2023) and the entropy-based coefficient optimization from AdaMerging (Yang et al., 2024), but departs from both by introducing a sensitivity-based theoretical lens (second-order Taylor expansion of subgroup losses) that had not been applied to merging before. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to formally characterize subgroup fairness degradation in model merging, bridging a gap between the merging-for-performance literature and the fairness-under-compression/DP/robustness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all five standard merging baselines systematically amplify subgroup disparities—even when individual checkpoints show moderate gaps—raises a practical concern for any deployment pipeline that relies on model merging for multi-task composition. The theoretical bound (Theorem 4.2) provides actionable design levers (merging magnitude, global sensitivity) rather than just an empirical observation. Key limitations include the reliance on labeled target-task data and subgroup annotations for Stage 1, the restriction to class-as-subgroup definitions, and evaluation limited to image classification with ViT backbones. Open questions include whether the fairness amplification persists under severe label scarcity, noisy subgroup labels, or in LLM merging settings where subgroup structure is less natural.
