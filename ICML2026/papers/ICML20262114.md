## Problem

Reward-maximizing RL methods for LLM reasoning (e.g., GRPO) sharpen the output distribution and reduce generation diversity, while GFlowNet-based distribution-matching methods (e.g., FlowRL) preserve diversity but treat the learned partition function solely as a normalizer. The paper identifies that this partition function already encodes per-prompt online accuracy information that has never been exploited, and asks whether this free signal can be leveraged to improve sample efficiency without additional computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question prompt x and produces a generated completion y (a reasoning chain for math or a code solution). The partition function Z_φ(x) is parameterized as a 3-layer MLP on top of frozen, pre-computed last-hidden-layer embeddings of the reference model applied to x, and the resulting accuracy estimate β log Z_φ(x) is clipped to [0,1]. Training generates N=8 rollouts per selected prompt with a maximum generation length of 3072 tokens, and the replay buffer stores only correct (r=1) prompt-output pairs with a fixed capacity of 128.

## Method

PACED-RL reinterprets the GFlowNet partition function as an online accuracy estimator via Proposition 4.1, which shows p_old(x) = β log Z*(x) − β D_KL(π_old‖π_θ); since the KL term is empirically bounded below 4×10⁻³, the practical estimator reduces to p_old(x) ≈ β log Z_φ(x) at zero extra cost. This estimate drives two mechanisms: (1) difficulty-aware adaptive prompt selection, which greedily picks the top-m prompts whose predicted accuracies are closest to a target τ=0.5 at each step, and (2) accuracy-estimation-error-prioritized replay, which prioritizes correct rollouts whose observed accuracy (N_correct/N) diverges most from the predicted accuracy, exploiting the off-policy tolerance of the GFlowNet objective. The training objective is a modified Trajectory Balance loss (Eq. 6) that replaces π_ref with π_old, anchoring the KL regularization at the current policy rather than the base model, so that the partition function directly tracks the evolving policy's accuracy landscape.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-Math-7B model, PACED-RL achieves an average pass@1 of 40.1% across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, improving over GRPO (37.2%) and FlowRL (36.5%) by 29.1% and 40.0% respectively on AIME24. On the 1.5B model, PACED-RL reaches 32.1% average pass@1, outperforming all baselines while using only 1.0× the rollout budget (vs. 2.0–4.0× for DS and LILO). For code generation, PACED-RL reaches GRPO's and FlowRL's best pass@1 on HumanEval+ in 0.49× and 0.42× of their training time, respectively. On pass@k (a diversity proxy), PACED-RL outperforms GRPO and FlowRL by up to 14.2% and 9.1% across k ∈ {2,…,256} on AIME24/25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACED-RL consistently outperforms GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) across all benchmarks and both model scales; the gap is largest on AIME24 (7B: 28.7 vs. 23.9, a 29.1% relative gain) and AIME25 (7B: 13.1 vs. 10.3). Against FlowRL (Zhu et al., 2026), the closest GFlowNet-based baseline, PACED-RL improves AIME24 by 40.0% (7B: 28.7 vs. 24.9) and MATH500 by 4.6 points (80.0 vs. 76.4). Compared to over-sampling-based adaptive selection methods DS (Yu et al., 2025b) and LILO (Foster et al., 2026), PACED-RL matches or exceeds their average performance (7B: 40.1 vs. 39.4 and 40.1) while requiring only 1.0× the rollout budget versus 2.5× and 4.0×, respectively; on the hardest benchmark AIME25 (7B), PACED-RL leads LILO by 11.9% (13.1 vs. 11.7). Against MoPPS (Qu et al., 2026), which also avoids over-sampling, PACED-RL achieves a clear 1.5-point average advantage on the 7B model (40.1 vs. 38.6) and a 1.7-point advantage on the 1.5B model (32.1 vs. 30.4), demonstrating that the partition-function-based accuracy signal is more reliable than Bayesian posterior estimation over per-question histories.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACED-RL extends the GFlowNet-based distribution-matching framework introduced by FlowRL (Zhu et al., 2026) and grounded in the Trajectory Balance objective (Malkin et al., 2022) and GFlowNet foundations (Bengio et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting the learned partition function Z_φ(x)—previously treated solely as a normalization overhead—as a per-prompt online accuracy estimator, thereby unlocking difficulty-aware adaptive prompt selection and estimation-error-prioritized replay at zero additional rollout cost. This positions the work at the intersection of the adaptive curriculum literature (LILO, DS, MoPPS) and prioritized experience replay (PER, GFlowNet replay), but eliminates the auxiliary over-sampling or Bayesian tracking mechanisms those approaches require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that PACED-RL achieves 29–40% pass@1 gains over GRPO and FlowRL on AIME benchmarks while matching their compute budget (1.0× rollouts), offering a drop-in efficiency improvement for GFlowNet-based RLVR pipelines. The KL-divergence approximation (discarding the β·D_KL term) is empirically validated but not theoretically guaranteed, and the method is currently restricted to binary-reward settings with a lightweight MLP partition function on frozen embeddings—both are natural constraints that limit generality. Open directions include extending the partition-function-as-difficulty-signal idea to multi-step reasoning (step-level credit assignment via Sub-Trajectory Balance), asynchronous training for scalability, and inference-time applications such as adaptive self-consistency allocation.
