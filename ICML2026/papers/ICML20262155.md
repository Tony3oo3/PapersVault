## Problem

Deep learning surrogates for 3D PDEs overfit to canonical coordinate systems and fail under rigid transformations, while existing equivariant architectures (EGNN, EMNN) rely on local message passing that cannot efficiently capture the global interactions essential to PDE dynamics. Fourier Neural Operators provide an efficient global receptive field in the spectral domain, but establishing 3D SE(3)-equivariance within them remains impractical due to the prohibitive cost of 3D spectral group convolutions. This paper addresses the open challenge of achieving both global spectral modeling and exact 3D equivariance at practical computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregular 3D surface point clouds (meshes) annotated with rotation-invariant physical features—such as vertex degrees, angle defects, projected inlet velocities, or force magnitudes—deliberately excluding absolute Cartesian coordinates. Internally, the encoder projects these irregular points onto a uniform regular grid for FFT-based processing, and the decoder maps results back to the original point cloud. Outputs include scalar fields (pressure, von Mises stress, turbulent kinetic energy, turbulent viscosity, specific dissipation rate) and vector fields (deflection, wall shear stress), where vector predictions are reconstructed by decomposing the target onto a per-node SE(3)-equivariant local basis and predicting only the invariant scalar projection coefficients.

## Method

EqGINO integrates two equivariant primitives into a three-stage pipeline: an EqGNO encoder that lifts irregular geometry to a regular grid using kernels parameterized solely by relative distances (ensuring SE(3)-invariance), a stack of EqFNO layers performing isotropic spectral convolution, and an EqGNO decoder projecting back to the point cloud. The key mechanism is Orbit-based Weight Sharing in the EqFNO: Fourier modes with the same radial magnitude ‖k‖₂ share a single weight parameter, enforcing the constraint W(Rk)=W(k) and reducing spectral parameter complexity from O(K³) to O(K). Full-FFT (rather than RFFT) is required because RFFT's half-space storage forces anti-linear conjugation operations that break the complex-linearity needed for rotation equivariance. A block-diagonal structure on the spectral weight tensor (grouping channels into G independent subspaces) offsets the doubled FLOPs of Full-FFT, and for vector field prediction a per-node local basis constructed from the global physical vector and relative position converts the equivariant vector regression into a scalar regression problem solvable by the isotropic backbone.

## Results

On the in-distribution (canonical→canonical) setting, EqGINO (G=4, K=40) achieves relative L₂ errors of 0.1958 (AhmedBody Wall Shear Stress), 0.1773 (ShapeNetCar Pressure), and 0.1710/0.3853 (DeepJEB Deflection/Stress), competitive with non-equivariant SOTA (Transolver: 0.1286 WS; GINO: 0.1113 Defl). Under zero-shot discrete rotation (canonical→90° multiples), EqGINO maintains identical performance (e.g., 0.1958 WS, 0.1710 Defl) while non-equivariant baselines degrade 3–15× (GINO: 0.6238 WS, 2.3194 Defl; Transolver: 0.7946 WS, 1.5885 Defl). On continuous SE(3) generalization, EqGINO again leads all baselines (0.2087 WS, 0.1560 Press, 0.1621 Defl) with faster convergence to unseen orientations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EqGINO is competitive with the strongest non-equivariant baselines in-distribution: it trails Transolver on AhmedBody WS (0.1958 vs 0.1286) and ShapeNet Pressure (0.1773 vs 0.1194), and GINO on DeepJEB Deflection (0.1710 vs 0.1113), but matches or beats them on most other metrics. Under zero-shot discrete rotation, EqGINO's performance is essentially unchanged (by design), while every non-equivariant baseline degrades severely—GINO's DeepJEB Deflection error jumps from 0.1113 to 2.3194 (21×), and Transolver's from 0.1615 to 1.5885 (10×). Among equivariant baselines, EqGINO dominates across all settings: it outperforms T-EMNN (the strongest equivariant baseline) by 3.7× on AhmedBody WS (0.1958 vs 0.7295) and 1.9× on DeepJEB Deflection (0.1710 vs 0.3200) in-distribution, with the gap widening dramatically under rotation. The SE(3)-equivariant Transolver* variant, which removes coordinate features but lacks spectral global receptive field, still lags EqGINO by 3.8× on AhmedBody WS (0.7458 vs 0.1958), confirming that equivariance alone is insufficient without global spectral interactions. On continuous SE(3) generalization (Table 2), EqGINO leads all baselines on every metric, with the largest margins over GINO (0.2087 vs 0.2779 WS) and Transolver (0.2087 vs 0.3349 WS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GINO architecture (Li et al., 2023), which unifies GNO-based geometric encoding with FNO-based spectral processing for irregular 3D domains, and fundamentally redesigns both components into standalone equivariant modules (EqGNO and EqFNO). It departs from equivariant GNNs (EGNN, EMNN, T-EMNN) that enforce SE(3)-symmetry through local message passing but lack the global receptive field essential for PDE dynamics, and from the 2D equivariant FNO (G-FNO, Helwig et al., 2023) whose spectral group convolutions become computationally prohibitive in 3D. The key conceptual departure is the Orbit-based Weight Sharing strategy, which enforces spectral isotropy by tying weights to frequency magnitude rather than direction, reducing parameter complexity from O(K³) to O(K) while guaranteeing exact discrete equivariance by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine open problem—scalable 3D equivariant spectral modeling for PDE surrogates—and provides a practical solution with dramatic parameter reduction (~98% vs. GINO) that makes equivariant spectral operators feasible for industrial-scale geometries. The finding that exact discrete equivariance (Octahedral group) serves as a strong structural prior enabling rapid generalization to continuous SE(3) rotations, even with limited augmented data, has broader implications for how symmetry constraints can be partially enforced to bootstrap continuous invariance. Key limitations include the restriction to discrete equivariance due to grid-based FFT, the O(Ndr³) neighbor-search cost in the GNO modules that becomes prohibitive at million-point scale, and the Full-FFT overhead that necessitates the block-diagonal channel grouping trade-off. Future work on strictly continuous equivariance and further computational efficiency for industrial meshes remains open.
