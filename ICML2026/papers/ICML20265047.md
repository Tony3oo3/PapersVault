## Problem

While KL-regularized RLHF has sharp theoretical guarantees (O(log T) regret online, O(ε⁻¹) sample complexity offline), and alternative divergences like forward KL and chi-squared have shown empirical benefits in mitigating over-optimization, no unified theoretical framework existed for online RLHF under a general f-divergence regularized objective. This paper provides the first provable performance bounds for online f-divergence regularized RLHF, covering the entire f-divergence function class rather than treating each divergence individually.

## Input / Output

At each round, the agent receives a context prompt x drawn from distribution ρ, samples two actions (responses) a¹ and a², and observes a binary preference label y ∈ {0,1} indicating which action is preferred under the Bradley-Terry model. The reference policy π₀ (typically the SFT checkpoint) is fixed throughout training. The output is a sequence of policies π_t that converge to the f-divergence regularized optimal policy π_f*, with the learning objective J_f(π) = E[r*(x,a) − η⁻¹D_f(π,π₀|x)] balancing reward maximization against divergence from the reference.

## Method

The paper proposes two distinct sampling algorithms under a unified f-divergence framework. Algorithm 1 (optimism-based) extends the optimism-in-face-of-uncertainty principle by constructing an exploration bonus b_t from the Eluder dimension of the reward class, yielding O(log T) cumulative regret. Algorithm 2 (derivative-based) introduces a novel 'derivative-as-uncertainty' principle: the derivative h' of the inverse function h=(f')⁻¹ measures how sensitively the optimal policy responds to reward estimation errors, and this sensitivity is used directly to construct three sampling policies (π'θ, π+θ, π−θ) that balance exploration without solving explicit uncertainty parameters. The key theoretical insight is that the Hessian of the value function at the optimum is proportional to the negative covariance under the derivative-based sampling distribution, enabling an O(1/T) sub-optimality gap. Both results hold uniformly across the f-divergence family, with the specific choice of f entering only through constants C(f,R_Θ,η) and M(f,R_Θ,η) that measure policy sensitivity to reward perturbations.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees: Algorithm 1 (optimism-based) achieves O(log T) cumulative regret (Theorem 3.3), and Algorithm 2 (derivative-based) achieves an O(1/T) sub-optimality gap (Proposition 4.4), both for general f-divergence regularized RLHF. In experiments on a 25-dimensional linear BT model with 10 actions, Algorithm 2 consistently achieves lower single-step and cumulative regret than Algorithm 1, greedy sampling, and uniform sampling. The experiments further confirm the theoretical prediction that chi-squared mixed KL (f = x log x + (x−1)²) and f = x log x − log x yield smaller sub-optimality gaps than standard reverse KL, consistent with their smaller constants C(f, R_Θ, η) and M(f, R_Θ, η) being less than 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four sampling strategies: the proposed optimism-based exploration (Algorithm 1), the proposed derivative-based exploration (Algorithm 2), greedy sampling (using the estimated reward directly), and vanilla uniform sampling. Algorithm 2 achieves the best convergence in both single-step and cumulative regret across both tested f-divergences (chi-squared mixed KL and f = x log x − log x), outperforming Algorithm 1 which in turn outperforms greedy sampling. Greedy sampling remains competitive due to the intrinsic stochasticity of the optimal policy under f-divergence regularization (Proposition 2.3), a phenomenon also noted in Wu et al. (2025) for KL divergence. Uniform sampling performs worst, as expected. Across divergence choices, chi-squared mixed KL and f = x log x − log x produce tighter sub-optimality gaps than standard reverse KL, empirically validating the theoretical constants C and M being below 1 for these divergences (Section 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online KL-regularized RLHF framework of Zhao et al. (2025a) and Xiong et al. (2023) from the specific reverse-KL regularizer to a general f-divergence family, while simultaneously filling the gap left by Zhao et al. (2025b), who analyzed f-divergence regularized RLHF only in the offline setting. The optimism-based algorithm generalizes the classical optimism-in-face-of-uncertainty principle with an f-divergence-aware bonus, and the derivative-based algorithm generalizes the mixed-sampling idea of Feng et al. (2025) (PILAF) from KL to arbitrary f-divergences. Empirical f-divergence RLHF work (Huang et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2024, Sun et al. 2025) motivates the study but lacks the theoretical guarantees provided here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified theoretical treatment of online RLHF under general f-divergence regularization, yielding O(log T) regret and O(1/T) sub-optimality bounds that recover prior KL-specific results as special cases. The 'derivative-as-uncertainty' interpretation—that f′ naturally encodes policy sensitivity to reward perturbations and can serve as an exploration signal without explicit confidence-set solving—is a conceptual contribution potentially applicable beyond RLHF to any f-divergence-based learning. Key limitations include the single-step (contextual bandit) formulation, the finite reward class assumption, and the requirement that f′ be invertible with 0 outside its domain (excluding total variation and plain chi-squared). Extending to multi-turn RL and relaxing the realizability/finite-class assumptions are natural open directions.
