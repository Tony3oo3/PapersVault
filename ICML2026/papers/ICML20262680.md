## Problem

In online link recommendation on evolving graphs, the platform's exposure decisions reshape which links form and what feedback is later observed (performative prediction), causing fairness metrics computed on logged outcomes to drift after deployment. No existing framework makes exposure-counterfactual fairness (comparing what happens if a candidate is shown versus not shown) identifiable from a single online stream while providing deployment-stable monitoring and control under policy-induced distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262680/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the system receives a source node and a candidate set of up to 201 destinations (1 true positive + 200 sampled negatives) from the evolving graph, scores them with a backbone predictor, and assigns synthetic protected group labels. It outputs a size-K exposure slate with logged per-candidate marginal entry propensities, bandit-style feedback only on exposed candidates, and decision-layer logit offsets (per-slice multicalibration offsets and per-group PI dual biases) that adjust future exposure. Fairness is reported as counterfactual treatment-effect gaps and calibration diagnostics computed from GA-DR pseudo-outcomes on rolling windows, accompanied by Residual-OI certificates with explicit concentration radii.

## Method

COPF is a decision-layer wrapper that (i) enforces an online prequential protocol with stochastic Top-K exploration and propensity logging to make exposure counterfactuals identifiable, (ii) estimates counterfactual quantities via graph-aware self-normalized doubly robust (GA-DR) pseudo-outcomes that remain consistent under temporal mixing and bounded local interference, (iii) audits Residual-OI over slice-indicator auditor families (group × score-bucket × optional structural role) to produce finite-window certificates, and (iv) steers exposure through a PI primal-dual controller that updates per-group logit biases to enforce treatment-effect parity and a minimum-effect guardrail. A noisy transfer theorem (Theorem 4.5) shows that plug-in Residual-OI on estimated GA-DR residuals implies high-probability bounds on true counterfactual group gaps, with the error decomposed into propensity/nuisance estimation terms, a mixing-dependent concentration radius, and the auditor confidence radius. The framework is backbone-agnostic and operates as an orthogonal deployment-time layer rather than modifying the underlying link-prediction model.

## Results

On the synthetic bipartite stream with GraphMixer, Base+COPF improves Deploy-phase NDCG@10 from 0.1417 to 0.1996 and Hits@10 from 0.2952 to 0.4154, while reducing worst-case treatment-effect gap g_gap^TE from 0.0477 to 0.0274 and worst-case calibration gap g_max^Cal from 0.9178 to 0.7067. On TGBL-Wiki with TGN, COPF reduces the PRE worst-case g_gap^TE from 0.0528 to 0.0217 and improves POST NDCG@10 from 0.2219 to 0.2296 with a lower POST mean g_gap^TE (0.0090→0.0067). On TGBL-Review, where treatment-effect gaps are already small, COPF is essentially non-binding (POST NDCG@10: 0.0389→0.0392; POST g_gap^TE: 0.0030→0.0029), confirming it activates only when exposure-induced instability is detected.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPF is evaluated as a decision-layer wrapper against three backbones (EdgeBank, TGN, GraphMixer) and compared to training-time fairness baselines (TGN-Adv adversarial debiasing, TGN-Reweight group reweighting, TGN-Penalty fairness regularization). On the synthetic bipartite stream with injected group bias, Base+COPF dominates Base on both utility and fairness in the Deploy phase: NDCG@10 improves by 41% (0.1417→0.1996) while worst-case g_gap^TE drops by 43% (0.0477→0.0274). On TGBL-Wiki with TGN, COPF reduces the PRE worst-case treatment-effect gap by 59% (0.0528→0.0217) with negligible utility cost, and improves POST NDCG@10 by 3.5% (0.2219→0.2296). The training-time debiasing variants (Adv, Reweight) show similar behavior under the shared OPP pipeline, and adding COPF on top (e.g., Adv+COPF) further reduces worst-case g_gap^TE in PRE (0.0900→0.0342) and POST (0.0765→0.0193), confirming COPF is orthogonal to representation-level debiasing. On TGBL-Review, where gaps are already small, all methods including COPF are essentially non-binding, indicating the controller correctly avoids unnecessary intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPF sits at the intersection of performative prediction (Perdomo et al., 2020), outcome indistinguishability auditing (Dwork et al., 2021, 2025), and doubly robust off-policy evaluation (Dudik et al., 2011; Swaminathan & Joachims, 2015), but departs from each in a specific way: rather than studying performative dynamics in isolation or auditing realized outcomes, it defines fairness on exposure counterfactuals (shown vs. not shown) and makes them identifiable from a single propensity-logged online stream. The Residual-OI auditing mechanism is adapted from the Any-Kernel framework for evolving graphs (Dwork et al., 2025) but applied to GA-DR pseudo-outcome residuals rather than raw predictions, and the multicalibration loop (Hébert-Johnson et al., 2018) is repurposed to calibrate no-exposure residuals. The paper positions itself as an orthogonal deployment-time wrapper that can be layered on top of training-time fairness methods (adversarial debiasing, reweighting) rather than replacing them, and contrasts with post-hoc analyses of logged snapshots (Cao et al., 2025) by targeting deployment-time comparability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap: when a recommender is performative, fairness metrics computed on the realized log can be misleading and drift after policy updates, yet no prior work provides an identifiable, online, certificate-backed mechanism for monitoring and controlling such drift in link recommendation. The transfer theorem (plug-in Residual-OI implies bounds on true counterfactual gaps under mixing and local interference) is a useful modular guarantee that decouples the auditing routine from the estimation layer. Key limitations temper the impact: TGB streams lack real demographic attributes (forcing synthetic placebo splits), the banditized feedback model (Y⁽⁰⁾≡0) stress-tests selection but makes calibration harder to interpret, and the active-auditor convergence analysis under dependence is left open. Future work on real sensitive attributes, stronger interference models, and comparisons to simpler online fairness controllers would strengthen the practical case.
