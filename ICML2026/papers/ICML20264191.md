## Problem

Existing representation similarity metrics (CKA, SVCCA, MutualNN, CKNNA) suffer from three critical limitations: they produce scores whose interpretation shifts with dataset size and dimensionality, they are fragile to even small numbers of outliers, and their exact computation is intractable at scale, forcing reliance on heuristic batched approximations without theoretical error bounds. This paper asks whether a similarity framework can simultaneously be interpretable, robust, and computationally scalable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of N paired embeddings X = {x_i} and Y = {y_i} (generated from the same source) along with a distance function for each space (e.g., Euclidean or negative cosine). It outputs a scalar score in [0,1] with a direct probabilistic reading: the fraction of ordinal relationships (distance orderings) preserved between the two spaces. A score of 0.5 is a universal random-alignment baseline independent of N, dimensionality, or distribution. For large-scale use, the approximation scheme samples triplets or quadruplets uniformly at random, requiring only O(1/ε² · log(1/δ)) evaluations regardless of dataset size N.

## Method

The paper introduces two ordinal similarity metrics: TSI (Triplet Similarity Index), which measures the fraction of triplets (i,j,k) where the anchor-based distance ordering d(x_i,x_j) vs d(x_i,x_k) is preserved in both spaces, and QSI (Quadruplet Similarity Index), which measures agreement on comparisons between disjoint distance pairs. The key insight is that by discarding absolute distances and retaining only their relative order, the metrics inherit invariance to translations, isotropic scaling, and orthogonal transforms while gaining robustness: a formal bound shows the score deviation under partial corruption is at most 1 − c_S, where c_S is the fraction of comparisons untouched. Computationally, the authors exploit a connection to Kendall's Tau to achieve O(N² log N) exact computation, and a Monte Carlo sampling scheme (leveraging the U-statistic structure) provides estimates with Hoeffding-type concentration bounds whose complexity is independent of N. A central theoretical result establishes that TSI = 1 if and only if Mutual Nearest Neighbors alignment holds simultaneously for all k from 1 to N−2, unifying multi-scale neighborhood consistency into a single metric.

## Results

TSI and QSI maintain a stable dissimilarity baseline of ≈0.5 for independent representations across all tested sample sizes (N up to 1000) and dimensionalities (D up to 500), whereas CKA, SVCCA, and PWCCA scores span nearly the full [0,1] range and can even approach 1 for independent sets. Under 2% outlier perturbation, TSI retains ≈94% similarity (matching its theoretical lower bound for |U|=980, N=1000), while CKA, CKNNA, and MutualNN drop to near-zero. For approximation, TSI/QSI achieve monotonic convergence with absolute deviations orders of magnitude smaller than standard batched approximations (which yield 10⁻¹–10⁻² errors at N_B=100). On CLIP multimodal alignment (ImageNet, 50k samples), TSI and QSI correctly track the monotonic increase in alignment from Small (63.3%) → Medium (68.1%) → Large (75.4%) zero-shot accuracy, while CKA exhibits a counter-intuitive score drop between Medium and Large models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019), TSI/QSI win on all four experimental axes: baseline stability (CKA scores vary wildly with N and D, sometimes approaching 1 for independent sets), outlier robustness (CKA drops to near-zero with 2% outliers while TSI holds at ≈94%), approximation quality (CKA's batched HSIC estimator stagnates at high error), and CLIP alignment (CKA's score drops from Medium to Large due to the 512→768 dimensionality change, while TSI/QSI increase monotonically). Against SVCCA and PWCCA (Raghu et al., 2017; Morcos et al., 2018), TSI/QSI are more stable at the dissimilarity baseline and more robust to outliers, though SVCCA/PWCCA show moderate resilience compared to CKA. Against MutualNN and CKNNA (Huh et al., 2024), TSI/QSI are substantially more robust to outliers (MNN/CKNNA collapse to near-zero due to dot-product hubness) and offer provable approximation guarantees, while MNN/CKNNA's local-only nature means they cannot detect global geometric rearrangements (demonstrated in the global failure mode of Section 5.2). The paper also shows TSI is formally equivalent to joint MNN alignment across all scales (Corollary 1), positioning TSI as a multi-scale generalization of the neighborhood consistency that MNN captures at a single k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws its conceptual foundation from Non-metric Multidimensional Scaling (NMDS), which preserves rank order of pairwise distances rather than exact values, and adapts the efficient O(M log M) computation of Kendall's Tau (Knight, 1966) to derive tractable algorithms for the proposed metrics. It positions itself against the dominant family of representation similarity metrics—CKA, SVCCA, PWCCA, MutualNN, and CKNNA—arguing that their reliance on precise nominal values creates interpretability, robustness, and scalability failures. The key departure from prior ordinal work (triplet losses, ordinal embedding) is that TSI and QSI evaluate the *consistency* of ordinal relationships between two existing representation spaces as a similarity score, rather than using externally specified triplet constraints to construct or optimize embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point: existing metrics like CKA lack a stable dissimilarity baseline (shifting with dimensionality and sample size) and are fragile to even 2% outliers, which undermines their use in tracking training dynamics or comparing models of different scales. The 0.5 universal baseline, provable outlier robustness, and O(1)-in-N approximation with Hoeffding-type guarantees make TSI/QSI immediately usable at scale (e.g., 50k ImageNet points) with interpretable scores. Open questions include whether the 1D lower-bound analysis generalizes to higher dimensions, whether scores below 0.5 are practically achievable in realistic settings, and whether the ordinal framework captures all geometric structure relevant to downstream task performance beyond alignment.
