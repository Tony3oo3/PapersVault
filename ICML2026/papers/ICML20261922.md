## Problem

Decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning for networked service systems is vulnerable to Byzantine agents that can exploit the inherently unbounded state space of traffic queues to destabilize both the learning process and the physical queuing dynamics. Existing resilient MARL methods assume bounded state spaces or bounded basis functions, leaving a critical gap for service systems where queue lengths are unbounded and job-server affinity introduces time-varying, continuous state dimensions that amplify adversarial impact.

## Input / Output

Each agent observes the global traffic state (unbounded queue backlogs in R_{≥0}^N) and a time-varying affinity vector f(t) ∈ [0,1]^m capturing job-server compatibility, along with its private local reward signal. The communication graph topology and a known safe baseline policy π^safe are shared across all cooperative agents. The algorithm outputs per-agent routing or scheduling actions drawn from a synchronized mixture of the learned policy and the safe policy, plus consensus-aggregated critic parameters (value and reward estimates) that converge to a bounded neighborhood of the mean-field solution.

## Method

The proposed algorithm combines two complementary defenses within a decentralized actor-critic framework with linear function approximation and eligibility traces. First, a coordinate-wise Weighted Mean-Subsequence-Reduced (W-MSR) aggregation rule trims the H largest and H smallest neighbor estimates per coordinate before weighted averaging, providing Byzantine resilience under (2H+1)-robust graph connectivity. Second, a synchronized stability-constrained policy mixture uses a single shared Bernoulli variable b_t ~ Bernoulli(1/‖s‖) so that all agents simultaneously either execute the learning policy or the safe policy, preserving the linearity of the Foster-Lyapunov drift analysis and guaranteeing geometric ergodicity of the MMDP for any learning policy. The key theoretical result is that cooperative agents' critic parameters converge almost surely to a bounded neighborhood of the mean-field linear system's solution, with neighborhood radius governed by adversarial perturbation magnitude, and actor parameters converge to the limit set of a projected differential inclusion whose diameter vanishes in the absence of attacks.

## Results

The headline metric is average end-to-end service time per task across three service systems. Under Byzantine attack without defense, service time explodes (e.g., 4.46×10⁵ in LLM routing vs. 6.59 nominal), while the proposed resilient algorithm recovers near-nominal performance (6.88 in LLM, 4.13 in MEC, 4.48 in Delivery). Average learned task-server affinity drops sharply under attack (e.g., 0.801→0.472 in LLM) but is partially restored by the defense (0.664), confirming that the algorithm preserves the affinity structure critical for stable routing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three robust aggregation baselines—mean aggregation, coordinate-wise median (Blanchard et al., NeurIPS 2017), and projection-based Krum (Chen et al., POMACS 2017)—in Figure 6. In the MEC scenario, the proposed W-MSR + synchronized policy mixture significantly outperforms all three baselines under Byzantine attacks, demonstrating superior robustness in highly dynamic environments where queue stability and learning are tightly coupled. In the LLM routing scenario, the proposed method achieves performance comparable to the median and Krum baselines, with slightly more conservative behavior attributed to its stronger stabilization effects. In the delivery scenario, all methods exhibit similar performance, suggesting this environment is inherently less sensitive to adversarial perturbations. Overall, the proposed framework is most advantageous in adversarial-sensitive systems with strong learning–queue coupling, while remaining competitive in more stable settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consensus-based decentralized actor-critic framework of Zhang et al. (2018) and the Byzantine-resilient MARL line (Wu et al., 2021; Ye et al., 2024) by removing the standard bounded-state-space assumption that underpins prior resilient MARL guarantees. It draws on W-MSR aggregation from robust consensus (LeBlanc et al., 2013) for the information layer and introduces a synchronized stability-constrained policy mixture as a complementary mechanism for the physical layer, combining ideas from Foster-Lyapunov drift analysis (Meyn & Tweedie, 2012) with the actor-critic linear approximation framework of Konda & Tsitsiklis (1999). The affinity state modeling of job-server correlation is a new structural element not present in prior MARL-for-service-systems work, and the paper explicitly positions itself against the gap where existing resilient methods 'do not directly address the risk of divergence' in unbounded spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—Byzantine agents exploiting unbounded queue state spaces to cause divergence rather than merely slowing convergence—that is directly relevant to deploying MARL in LLM inference, edge computing, and manufacturing. The ablation showing that W-MSR (information-layer protection) and synchronized policy mixture (physical-layer stabilization) each fail independently but succeed together provides a useful architectural principle for designing resilient decentralized learning in queueing systems. Limitations include the requirement of a known safe policy, the (2H+1)-robustness graph assumption, linear function approximation only, and a fixed 20% Byzantine fraction; open questions include adaptive adversaries, stochastic perturbations, and robustness to model/communication uncertainties that the authors flag for future work.
