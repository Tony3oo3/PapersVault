## Problem

Machine learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) trained with standard energy, force, and stress losses only indirectly constrain the curvature of the potential energy surface, leading to systematic errors in second-order force constants that degrade phonon dispersions and derived thermodynamic properties. The paper demonstrates that Hessian error strongly correlates with error across multiple phonon metrics, motivating direct supervision of force constants rather than relying on indirect constraints from lower-order derivatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262189/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on periodic supercells containing hundreds to thousands of atoms (required to avoid self-interaction in finite-displacement phonon calculations), taking atomic positions and species as input. During training, only a single column of the 3N×3N Hessian is computed per structure per step via stochastic sampling of one atom-Cartesian-direction pair, reducing cost from O(N²) to O(N). The model outputs a scalar predicted energy whose analytical first and second derivatives yield forces, stress, and force constants; downstream phonon frequencies, vibrational thermodynamic properties, and thermal conductivity are derived from these.

## Method

PFT introduces a four-term loss that adds a force-constant term L_Φ matching the MLIP's analytical energy Hessian to DFT-computed force constants from finite-displacement phonon calculations. To scale to large supercells, PFT stochastically samples one Hessian column per structure and computes the corresponding loss via a single Hessian-vector product (HVP), implemented as a forward-mode JVP through a reverse-mode gradient (a 'triple-backward' during optimization). A co-training scheme interleaves K steps of standard energy/force/stress training on the upstream dataset (e.g., MPtrj) for every PFT step on phonon data, mitigating catastrophic forgetting with minimal Hessian error cost. The approach is model-agnostic and requires only that the MLIP support automatic differentiation through its energy prediction, demonstrated on both Nequix (JAX) and MACE-MP-0 (PyTorch/FeNNol) architectures.

## Results

On the MDR Phonon benchmark, Nequix MP PFT reduces average MAE across four phonon thermodynamic properties (ω_max, S, F, C_V) by 55% relative to the Nequix MP base model (from 24/32/12/6 to 12/14/5/3), achieving state-of-the-art accuracy among all MPtrj-trained models. For thermal conductivity on Matbench Discovery, PFT improves κ_SRME from 0.446 (Nequix MP) to 0.307, and from 0.267 (Nequix OAM) to 0.198, both setting new records for MPtrj-trained models. On the Matbench Discovery stability task, co-training preserves performance within <1% of the base model, while without co-training F1 drops from 0.751 to 0.301.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFT consistently outperforms all MPtrj-trained baselines on phonon properties: Nequix MP PFT (12/14/5/3) beats eSEN-MP (24/14/4/5), SevenNet-l3i5 (25/25/9/4), and MACE-MP-0 (61/60/24/13) on ω_max, S, and C_V, while matching eSEN-MP on S. The stronger Nequix OAM PFT model (9/9/3/2) further improves all metrics and surpasses the OAM base (17/18/7/4) by ~50% on average. For thermal conductivity, Nequix MP PFT (κ_SRME=0.307) outperforms the next-best MPtrj-trained model eSEN-30M-MP (0.340) and Eqnorm MPtrj (0.408), while Nequix OAM PFT (0.198) is the overall best entry in the compliant leaderboard. Directly fine-tuning on phonon displacement data (without Hessian supervision) catastrophically degrades performance (ω_max MAE jumps from 24 to 182), confirming that explicit Hessian alignment is essential. On Matbench Discovery stability, PFT with co-training maintains near-identical performance to the base model (F1: 0.748 vs. 0.751), whereas without co-training F1 collapses to 0.301, demonstrating the critical role of the co-training strategy in preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Nequix MP foundation model (Koker et al., 2025) and extends the line of work on using analytical Hessians for phonon prediction initiated by Fang et al. (2024), who first demonstrated AD-based phonon computation and Hessian training on small organic molecules. It departs from prior Hessian-based approaches (Amin et al., 2025; Gangan et al., 2025; Burger et al., 2025) by targeting large universal MLIPs fine-tuned on DFT force constants from large periodic supercells, using stochastic Hessian column sampling with Hessian-vector products to achieve linear scaling in system size. The co-training strategy to prevent catastrophic forgetting is a practical addition not present in prior Hessian-training work, motivated by the limited diversity of phonon datasets relative to pretraining corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFT provides a simple, model-agnostic fine-tuning recipe that addresses a known and systematic limitation of universal MLIPs (curvature softening due to equilibrium-biased training data), with a 55% average improvement in phonon thermodynamic properties at a fraction of pretraining cost. The generalization to third-order force constants and thermal conductivity suggests that correcting PES curvature has downstream benefits beyond the directly supervised quantity. Open questions include evaluation on off-equilibrium configurations (where phonon data is absent), whether the ~30 meV/atom energy discrepancy between MDR Phonon and MPtrj datasets confounds results, and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse phonon databases or higher-order derivatives like anharmonic force constants.
