## Problem

Neural networks generalize well on natural data, but it remains unclear which physical properties of natural dynamics are actually responsible for this learnability. While locality and spatiotemporal symmetry are necessary conditions, this paper asks whether they are sufficient, and if not, what additional functional properties distinguish learnable from unlearnable dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260929/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a 16×16 binary grid representing the initial state of a 2D cellular automaton with a 9-cell Moore neighborhood and torus topology. For CNNs the full grid is consumed directly; for MLPs and Transformers, the (2T+1)×(2T+1) causal neighborhood around each target cell is extracted as a flattened vector or token sequence. The output is the predicted binary grid after T timesteps (T ∈ {2,…,7}), and the paper additionally computes junta coefficients W_k (Fourier-Walsh spectral weight binned by interaction degree) as diagnostic features for predicting learnability across rules.

## Method

The paper samples 512 random CA rules (outer-totalistic and general) and trains CNN, MLP, and Transformer architectures—each with T stacked blocks to match the prediction horizon—to forecast T-step evolution. The key finding is a hierarchy of predictive power: locality and symmetry are necessary but insufficient; perturbation sensitivity (average spectral degree) explains only R²≈0.4–0.76 of accuracy variance; while junta coefficients (the full distribution of Fourier-Walsh weight across interaction degrees) achieve R²>0.9 at longer horizons. Junta coefficients are estimated by measuring noise sensitivity NS_δ at 66 geometrically-spaced noise levels and solving a binned linear system via non-negative least squares. The connection to circuit complexity is made explicit via the Linial-Mansour-Nisan theorem: learnable rules concentrate spectral weight at low degrees, corresponding to functions computable by small AC⁰ circuits, suggesting that the inductive bias of neural networks aligns with a low-combinatorial-complexity prior.

## Results

Junta coefficients (spectral weight distribution across Fourier degrees) predict CA learnability with R² = 0.89–0.92 at T ≥ 5 for CNNs, substantially outperforming perturbation sensitivity (PS) alone, which achieves only R² = 0.70–0.76 in the same regime. The combined PS + Junta model provides no improvement over Junta alone, confirming that Junta coefficients subsume sensitivity information while capturing additional spectral structure. Across all three architectures (CNN, MLP, Transformer), the same qualitative pattern holds: spectral concentration at low degrees is the dominant predictor of learnability, with CNNs performing best overall (avg. accuracy 0.98 at T=2, dropping to 0.66 at T=7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three predictive features for learnability: perturbation sensitivity (PS) alone, junta coefficients alone, and a combined model. Junta coefficients consistently and substantially outperform PS across all architectures and time horizons — e.g., at T=7 for CNNs, Junta achieves R²=0.92 vs. PS at R²=0.70, a gap of over 20 percentage points. The combined model (PS + Junta) yields identical R² to Junta alone at every setting, confirming that PS is a linear combination of the junta coefficients and adds no independent information. For the underlying prediction task, the paper uses a logistic regression baseline; CNNs outperform it by 0.34 accuracy at T=2 but the gap shrinks to 0.06 at T=7, while MLPs and Transformers barely exceed the baseline at longer horizons. Among architectures, CNNs dominate (avg. accuracy 0.98 at T=2), followed by Transformers (0.70) and MLPs (0.71), with the gap widening at larger T due to the quadratic attention cost limiting Transformers to T≤5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al. 2019; Yang and Salman 2020; Gorji et al. 2023) from continuous frequency domains to the Boolean hypercube via Walsh-Fourier analysis, and connects it to circuit complexity through the Linial-Mansour-Nisan theorem. It builds on prior work using CAs as model systems for studying neural network learning (Wulff and Hertz 1992; Gilpin 2019; Springer and Kenyon 2021) but departs by systematically testing whether physical constraints (locality, symmetry) are sufficient for learnability and introducing junta coefficients as a diagnostic. The sensitivity-learnability connection (Franco 2006; Novak et al. 2018; Bhattamishra et al. 2023) is shown to be necessary but incomplete, motivating the full spectral distribution as a richer predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, measurable criterion (junta coefficients / spectral weight distribution) that predicts when neural networks can learn dynamical systems, achieving R² > 0.9 across architectures. The negative result—that locality and symmetry, the structural constraints shared between physics and CNNs, are insufficient for learnability—challenges the intuition that physical constraints alone explain the inductive bias. Key limitations include the very coarse Boolean CA abstraction of physics, restriction to short time horizons where static rule properties dominate, and inability to probe emergent long-time dynamics. The central open question is whether the low-degree spectral concentration hypothesis, validated here in a toy setting, actually reflects how real physical dynamics filter out high-complexity interactions.
