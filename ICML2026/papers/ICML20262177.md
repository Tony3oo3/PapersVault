## Problem

Standard value-based and actor-critic RL methods rely on Bellman recursion, which structurally collapses under non-exponential discounting (hyperbolic, survival-based, or time-varying kernels) because exponential discounting is the unique kernel satisfying both multiplicativity and time homogeneity. Existing remedies—extended HJB systems, BSVIEs, or heuristic value pipelines like UGAE—reinstate recursion through state augmentation or relax to equilibrium notions, but both routes introduce nonlocal couplings that become brittle in high dimensions and undermine scalability of global equation-driven solvers.

## Input / Output

PG-DPO requires access to a differentiable dynamics model (drift b and diffusion σ), a two-time discount kernel D(s,t), and a query point (t,x); it does not require a pre-trained value function or critic. The neural policy u_θ(t,x) is not the final output but an intermediate exploration proxy; the actual control is synthesized per-query by the Adjoint-MC projection, which runs M_MC Monte Carlo rollouts from (t,x) under the frozen warm-start policy and solves a small action-space Hamiltonian maximization (a handful of Newton or log-barrier iterations). Optionally, the per-query projected actions can be amortized offline into a lightweight student policy π_φ(t,x) for single-forward-pass deployment.

## Method

PG-DPO is a two-stage, Bellman-free variational framework that replaces value-function decomposition with the Pontryagin Maximum Principle. Stage 1 warm-starts a differentiable policy u_θ by maximizing the Monte Carlo rollout objective via BPTT, optionally randomizing anchor times. Stage 2 (Adjoint-MC projection) interprets BPTT state-gradients as a stochastic adjoint estimator: it averages pathwise costates λ^{(j)}(t,x) = ∂Ĵ^{(j)}/∂x over M_MC rollouts anchored at the decision time t to obtain a stabilized diagonal costate λ̂(t,x), then synthesizes the control by pointwise Hamiltonian maximization û(t,x) ∈ argmax_u H(t,t,x,u,λ̂,Ẑ) solved via warm-started Newton or interior-point iterations. The key theoretical contribution is a rigorous correspondence (Theorem 2.1) showing that adapted BPTT sensitivities coincide with the discretized anchored adjoint up to a predictable Hamiltonian-residual correction and Euler error, and a local control-proximity guarantee (Theorem 2.2) bounding the Stage 2 projected control's distance from the exact diagonal Pontryagin/equilibrium control in terms of warm-up residual, adjoint estimation error, and numerical solver tolerance.

## Results

On the Merton problem with hyperbolic discounting (Case 2), PG-DPO achieves L1 errors of 3.47e-3 (consumption) and 6.36e-8 (investment), reducing errors by 1–5 orders of magnitude over all baselines. On the time-varying hyperbolic benchmark (Case 3), PG-DPO attains L1 errors of 6.35e-3 to 7.39e-3 across three impatience profiles, while the best baseline (DPO) remains at 1.08e-1 to 1.31e-1. On the survival-discounted target control (Case 1), PG-DPO achieves L1 errors of 1.45e-2 to 2.97e-2 with the smallest standard deviation, outperforming PPO (2.18e-1 to 2.51e-1) and PINN (1.11e0 to 1.65e0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-DPO outperforms all four baselines across all three discounting regimes (Cases 1–3) by 1–5 orders of magnitude in L1 error. Against PPO (model-free actor-critic), PG-DPO reduces L1 error from ~0.2–0.5 to ~0.006–0.03, with PPO also exhibiting the largest variance across seeds. Against PINN (global PDE-residual fitting), PG-DPO achieves roughly 10–100× lower error; PINN's global surrogate fails to recover accurate gradients despite larger model capacity. Against Deep BSDE/BSVIE (global backward-equation solvers), PG-DPO is 10–1000× more accurate; these methods are structurally mismatched to non-multiplicative discounting because their backward representations assume Bellman-consistent recursion. The closest competitor is DPO (Stage I, the warm-start policy without Pontryagin projection), which is 3–20× worse than PG-DPO, confirming that the Adjoint-MC projection step is the critical source of accuracy gain rather than the rollout optimization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prior PG-DPO framework (Huh et al., 2025) from time-consistent objectives to general non-exponential discounting by enforcing a decision-time-anchored (diagonal) Pontryagin condition. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant Bellman-recursion paradigm (HJB/BSDE solvers, extended HJB for time-inconsistent control, BSVIEs) by abandoning value-function decomposition entirely and instead coupling the Pontryagin Maximum Principle with Monte Carlo rollouts, interpreting BPTT as a stochastic adjoint estimator. The structural decomposition of discount-kernel properties (multiplicativity × time homogeneity, Figure 1) provides a clean taxonomy that unifies survival discounting, hyperbolic discounting, and time-varying impatience under a single failure analysis, positioning the method against both critic-based RL (PPO) and global equation-driven solvers (PINN, Deep BSDE/BSVIE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, recursion-free alternative for a class of problems (non-exponential discounting) that is central to behavioral economics and survival-based risk modeling but has been handled only through increasingly complex PDE/BSDE extensions or heuristic value pipelines. The rigorous local control-proximity guarantee (Theorem 2.2) and the demonstration that Stage 2 projection enforces near-zero Hamiltonian residual even from a weakly optimal Stage 1 warm-start provide theoretical grounding that goes beyond empirical tuning. Key limitations include the requirement for a differentiable simulator (restricting applicability to model-based or learned-dynamics settings), dependence on BPTT gradient quality, and the need for further variance reduction. Open directions include amortized projection via distillation, extension to non-smooth or data-driven discount kernels, and integration with learned dynamics models.
