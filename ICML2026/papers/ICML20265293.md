## Problem

Existing approaches for functional calcium imaging data remain task-specific, with separate models for behavior decoding and neural dynamics forecasting, making it difficult to establish a unified pretraining-fine-tuning paradigm for large-scale multi-animal recordings. This limits reuse of learned representations and prevents a single backbone from transferring across the diverse objectives common in systems neuroscience.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265293/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes trial-aligned, z-scored calcium traces from multi-animal, multi-session datasets (e.g., 8 mice, 286 sessions, 273,770 neurons at 6 Hz effective sampling rate), which are first smoothed with an exponential moving average and segmented into trials. The NQ tokenizer compresses each neuron's trace into discrete tokens at a reduced temporal resolution (window length L=4), producing a shared vocabulary of K=128 tokens. For forecasting, the model autoregressively generates tokens for the trial remainder and detokenizes them back to continuous traces; for decoding, a task-specific head upsamples latent features to the original temporal resolution and regresses behavioral velocity signals (roll, pitch, yaw).

## Method

CalM is a two-stage self-supervised framework. Stage 1 trains a VQ-VAE-based Neural Quantizer (NQ) that maps single-neuron calcium traces into a shared discrete vocabulary, motivated by the stereotyped fast-rise/slow-decay shape of calcium transients; the NQ is stabilized with Gumbel-Softmax entropy regularization, an orthogonality penalty on the codebook, a dead-code revival strategy, and an auxiliary autoregressive head that encourages temporally predictive tokens. Stage 2 trains a Dual-Axis Transformer (DAT) on the tokenized population matrix, factorizing attention into bidirectional self-attention across neurons within each timestep (neural axis) and causal self-attention across time per neuron (temporal axis), with RoPE for positional encoding and learnable neuron/session embeddings for cross-animal generalization. The DAT is trained with cross-entropy on next-token prediction, augmented by scheduled sampling (replacing input segments with model predictions to reduce exposure bias) and neighborhood replacement (substituting tokens with codebook neighbors to build robustness to quantization errors). Downstream tasks attach lightweight heads: autoregressive rollout with NQ detokenization for forecasting, and a GLU-style nonlinear head with a global neuron-weight bank for behavior decoding, all while keeping the pretrained backbone frozen.

## Results

On single-session neural forecasting, CalM achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.4025±0.0858 on real data and 0.5207±0.0692 on simulated data, outperforming the strongest baseline POCO (0.2901 / 0.4688) by 39% and 11% respectively. On multi-session forecasting (Tseng dataset), CalM reaches 0.3799±0.0978 (held-in) and 0.3383±0.0860 (held-out) correlation, marginally exceeding POCO (0.3668 / 0.3322). For multi-session behavior decoding, CalM with a fine-tuned head outperforms POYO+ by 10.1% average R² on held-in data (up to 20.9% on yaw) and by 7.2% on held-out data, with all differences statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁶).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalM clearly outperforms all generic time-series baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, TCN) on single-session forecasting, achieving 0.4025 correlation versus 0.0822, 0.1918, and 0.1854 respectively on real data. Against the dedicated forecasting baseline POCO, CalM wins by a wide margin on single-session settings (0.4025 vs. 0.2901 real; 0.5207 vs. 0.4688 simulation) and remains competitive on multi-session data (0.3799 vs. 0.3668 held-in, p=7.82×10⁻⁴; 0.3383 vs. 0.3322 held-out, not significant). For behavior decoding, CalM with a nonlinear head surpasses POYO+ on multi-session held-in data by 10.1% average R² (pitch: 0.7873 vs. 0.7227; roll: 0.7540 vs. 0.6751; yaw: 0.7076 vs. 0.5853) and on held-out by 7.2%, with all six comparisons highly significant (p < 10⁻⁶). On single-session decoding, CalM-Nonlinear beats POYO+ on pitch (0.7715 vs. 0.6077) and roll (0.7314 vs. 0.6066) but trails slightly on yaw (0.6493 vs. 0.6711). Classical baselines GLM and RRR are consistently outperformed on decoding (e.g., CalM 0.7715 vs. GLM 0.6248 on pitch R²), confirming that self-supervised pretraining captures richer neurobehavioral structure than linear or reduced-rank models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CalM extends the neural foundation model (NFM) paradigm—previously applied to spiking data via POYO/POYO+ (Azabou et al., 2023, 2024) and Neural Data Transformers (Ye et al., 2021, 2023)—to functional calcium imaging, where prior approaches remained strictly task-specific (decoding-only or forecasting-only). The tokenizer is a direct architectural descendant of VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), adapted with autoregressive regularization and codebook diversity losses to produce temporally predictive discrete tokens. The dual-axis transformer (bidirectional attention across neurons, causal attention across time) departs from generic multi-variate time-series forecasters like iTransformer and PatchTST, which ignore neuron-set heterogeneity, and from POCO's population-conditioned design, by learning a shared vocabulary that enables permutation-invariant multi-animal pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CalM is the first self-supervised pretraining framework that unifies neural forecasting and behavior decoding under a single backbone for calcium imaging, reducing the need for task-specific engineering in large-scale functional recordings. Key limitations include reliance on trial-aligned data (limiting transfer to spontaneous activity), the absence of direct trace-space optimization during forecasting, and a modular rather than end-to-end architecture. Open questions include whether the pretrained representations extend to multimodal settings (behavior, anatomy, stimuli), whether the framework generalizes to non-trial-structured recordings, and whether the dual-axis factorization scales to even larger populations or longer sequences.
