## Problem

Sinha & Vaze (2024) achieved optimal O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) cumulative constraint violation (CCV) for Constrained Online Convex Optimization (COCO), but their algorithm requires prior knowledge of the horizon T, and the CCV bound scales as Õ(√T) for every prefix t rather than Õ(√t). This paper asks whether one can design a COCO algorithm that simultaneously achieves O(√t) regret and Õ(√t) CCV for all t ≥ 1 without resorting to the doubling trick, which is known to cause practical instability through repeated restarts.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of adversarially chosen G-Lipschitz convex cost functions f_t and constraint functions g_t, where g_t is revealed only after the learner commits to action x_t (so per-round constraint satisfaction is generally impossible). It requires only the subgradient norm bound G and the decision-set diameter D as hyperparameters, and produces a sequence of actions x_t ∈ X along with guarantees that hold simultaneously for every prefix t: O(√t) regret against the best fixed feasible action and Õ(√t) CCV. The framework extends to dynamic regret (adapting to unknown path length P_t) and optimistic settings (adapting to cumulative prediction error E_t) without any a priori horizon or problem-quantity knowledge.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the fixed, horizon-dependent Lyapunov function of Sinha & Vaze (2024) with a sequence of time-varying, horizon-oblivious Lyapunov functions Φ_t(x) = e^{λ_t x} − 1, where λ_t decreases as a function of t. Because the shrinking λ_t breaks the monotonicity of Φ_t(Q(t)) that underpinned the prior analysis, the paper introduces a multiplicative correction in the virtual-queue recursion: Q(t) = (λ_{t-1}/λ_t)·Q(t−1) + g̃_t(x_t). Since λ_{t-1}/λ_t ≥ 1, this factor compensates for the shrinking Lyapunov scale and restores the invariant Φ_t(Q(t)) ≥ Φ_{t-1}(Q(t-1)), while Q(t) remains a valid upper bound on the true CCV. The algorithm then constructs surrogate losses by linearly combining the pre-processed cost and constraint with coefficients given by the Lyapunov derivative Φ_t′(Q(t)), and feeds these to an AdaGrad subroutine with adaptive step sizes. The specific choice λ_τ = 1/(4√τ·√(log τ+1)·(log(log τ+1)+1)) ensures ∑λ_τ² < 1/4, which closes the regret decomposition inequality and yields the stated anytime bounds without any restarts.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 2, which establishes that Algorithm 2 achieves Regret_t ≤ 2GD(√t + 1) and CCV_t ≤ 8GD√t·√(log t + 1)·(log(log t + 1) + 1)·log(5t) simultaneously for all t ≥ 1, without requiring knowledge of the horizon. In the dynamic regret setting (Theorem 7), the bounds adapt to the unknown path length P_t, yielding D-Regret_t ≤ 2GD√(1+P_t)(√t+1). In the optimistic setting (Theorem 8), regret scales as O(√E_t(f̃)) and CCV as Õ(√E_t(g̃)), where E_t denotes cumulative prediction error. Empirically, on a constrained online shortest-path problem, the anytime policy achieves lower cumulative regret and CCV than both the fixed-horizon baseline and its doubling-trick adaptation, while avoiding the periodic instability caused by restarts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the fixed-horizon algorithm of Sinha & Vaze (2024, NeurIPS), which achieves O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) CCV but requires prior knowledge of the horizon T. The critical distinction is that Sinha & Vaze's CCV guarantee is Õ(√T) for all intermediate times t (i.e., not time-uniform), whereas this paper's anytime algorithm achieves Õ(√t) CCV for every prefix t ≥ 1, matching the minimax lower bound up to logarithmic factors at every time step. Empirically on the online shortest-path problem (Figure 1), the proposed anytime method outperforms both the fixed-horizon baseline and its standard doubling-trick adaptation in cumulative regret and CCV. The doubling-trick variant exhibits periodic performance degradation at each restart due to discarding accumulated information, a phenomenon consistent with prior observations by Besson & Kaufmann (2018) and Zhang et al. (2024). The fixed-horizon method, while theoretically optimal in a worst-case sense, uses a single conservative trade-off parameter that hinders practical performance, whereas the time-varying Lyapunov parameter in this paper adapts more aggressively in early rounds, yielding a superior performance trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lyapunov-based COCO framework of Sinha & Vaze (2024), which achieves optimal O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) CCV but requires a priori knowledge of the horizon T. The key departure is replacing the fixed, horizon-dependent Lyapunov function with a sequence of time-varying, horizon-oblivious Lyapunov functions, combined with a multiplicative correction in the virtual-queue recursion to restore the monotonicity property that the time-varying setting breaks. The optimistic extension builds on Lekeufack & Jordan (2025) and the base OCO subroutine draws on Rakhlin & Sridharan (2014), while the dynamic regret component adapts the AdaGrad analysis of Orabona (2019) to path-length-dependent learning rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime guarantee removes a significant practical barrier: in real deployment (autonomous driving, network routing, safety-critical bandits), the interaction horizon is rarely known in advance, and the doubling trick's restarts discard information and cause instability. The time-varying Lyapunov technique with multiplicative queue correction is a potentially reusable analytical tool for other online problems where a fixed-horizon analysis needs to be made horizon-oblivious. Open questions include removing the dependence on the ambient diameter D (rather than the smaller feasible-set diameter D*), designing fully parameter-free variants, and obtaining regret-violation trade-offs that allow trading CCV for regret.
