## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained flow or diffusion models to maximize task-specific rewards (e.g., binding affinity, dipole moment) while satisfying domain-specific constraints (e.g., energy bounds, synthesizability) remains an open problem. Existing reward-guided fine-tuning methods regularize toward the pre-trained model via KL divergence but cannot certify hard constraint satisfaction, and naively adding constraints as weighted reward terms is unreliable because the appropriate weighting varies across tasks and training phases, often yielding high-reward samples that violate constraints.

## Input / Output

CFO takes a pre-trained flow model (e.g., FlowMol for 3D molecular coordinates and atom types), a scalar reward function r(x), a scalar constraint function c(x) with an upper bound B, and algorithmic hyperparameters (number of outer iterations K, initial penalty ρ_init, growth rate η, contraction rate τ, multiplier lower bound λ_min). It outputs a fine-tuned velocity field (policy) whose terminal distribution maximizes expected reward while satisfying E[c(x)] ≤ B in expectation. The constraint and reward need not be differentiable if a gradient-free inner solver (e.g., DiffusionNFT) is used, and the fine-tuned model is sampled at the same per-sample cost as the base model with no additional inference-time guidance.

## Method

CFO is an augmented Lagrangian dual algorithm that decomposes the constrained generative optimization problem into a sequence of standard KL-regularized fine-tuning subproblems. At each outer iteration k, it constructs an augmented objective f_k(x) = r(x) − (ρ_k/2)[max(0, c(x) − B − λ_k/ρ_k)]², solves an unconstrained entropy-regularized control subproblem via a pluggable FINETUNINGSOLVER (Adjoint Matching for differentiable objectives, DiffusionNFT for gradient-free ones), then updates the Lagrange multiplier λ via a projected dual step based on the empirical constraint gap G_k, and adaptively increases the penalty ρ by a factor η only when a contraction statistic V_k fails to shrink by the user-defined rate τ. This proximal-point-style dual update automatically tightens the constraint penalty as needed without manual weight selection. The paper proves that under a bounded-approximation-error assumption on the inner solver, CFO's limiting policy minimizes infeasibility (and is feasible if the problem is feasible), and under the stronger assumption that solver error vanishes asymptotically, the limit is a global maximizer of the constrained objective.

## Results

On the energy-constrained dipole moment maximization task (GEOM Drugs, FlowMol), CFO with Adjoint Matching achieves a dipole moment of 8.39±0.10 Debye while satisfying the −80 Ha energy bound (expected energy −82.28±0.41 Ha), matching the unconstrained AM reward (8.37±0.07 D) that violates the constraint (−78.31±0.38 Ha). On the visually interpretable MoG task, CFO improves mean reward from −7.62 to −4.75 while reducing constraint violation from 0.58 to 0.12, whereas unconstrained AM raises reward to −2.93 but increases violation to 2.47. CFO also transfers to the gradient-free DiffusionNFT solver, achieving 8.27±0.19 D at −80.72±0.93 Ha (feasible) versus NFT alone at 8.30±0.07 D but −78.67±0.62 Ha (infeasible).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adjoint Matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025), CFO achieves a statistically indistinguishable dipole moment (8.39 vs. 8.37 D) while shifting expected energy from −78.31 Ha (constraint violation) to −82.28 Ha (feasible), demonstrating that constraint enforcement comes at essentially no reward cost. Against DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al., 2025), CFO with NFT as the inner solver matches reward (8.27 vs. 8.30 D) and satisfies the −80 Ha bound (−80.72 Ha) where unconstrained NFT does not (−78.67 Ha), confirming solver-agnostic transfer. Against DiffOpt (Kong et al., 2024), an inference-time constrained sampler, CFO achieves a per-sample violation rate of 8.5% versus 10–50% for DiffOpt across three hyperparameter regimes, while incurring 44–55× lower per-sample sampling cost since CFO fine-tunes the model once and then samples at base-model speed. On the MoG task, CFO reduces constraint violation from 0.58 to 0.12 (AM: 2.47) and from 0.58 to 0.06 with NFT (NFT alone: 1.76), consistently outperforming both unconstrained baselines on feasibility while retaining most of the reward gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper wraps the classical augmented Lagrangian scheme (Birgin & Martinez, 2014) around existing reward-guided fine-tuning solvers for flow and diffusion models (Adjoint Matching, DiffusionNFT, Flow-GRPO), converting a constrained generative optimization problem into a sequence of unconstrained KL-regularized subproblems. It departs from prior constrained generation work (Khalafi et al., 2024; Kong et al., 2024), which enforces constraints at inference time via guidance or dual training while keeping model weights fixed, by instead internalizing constraints into the fine-tuned weights so that standard sampling suffices at inference. The entropy-regularized control formulation from Uehara et al. (2024) and Domingo-Enrich et al. (2025) provides the inner-solver foundation that CFO orchestrates with adaptive dual updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFO offers a principled, provably convergent alternative to the ad-hoc fixed-weight Lagrangian approach that dominates current practice, eliminating the unreliable manual tuning of constraint penalties. The practical finding that CFO matches unconstrained fine-tuning compute (K=6, N=10 vs. N=60 gradient steps) while satisfying constraints makes it immediately deployable in molecular design pipelines. Key limitations include the drop in molecular validity during optimization (34%→9%) and the reliance on approximate solver assumptions for guarantees; open directions include inference-time constraint handling, protein engineering, and integrating differentiable geometry relaxation to recover validity.
