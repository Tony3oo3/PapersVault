## Problem

Expert-annotated time series datasets, particularly in medical domains, suffer from low inter-annotator agreement due to subjective criteria and inconsistent thresholds, undermining supervised classification. Existing ranking methods either rely on latent scalar scores (BTL) or fixed-dimensional feature representations (RankNet, RankSVM) that discard temporal structure, while the Ideal Point Model has only been studied in Euclidean latent spaces. This paper asks whether IPMs can be adapted to rank time series directly using elastic distances that respect temporal deformations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of time series (univariate or multivariate, potentially of different lengths) together with a binary preference label indicating which series is more severe or abnormal. It outputs a full ranking of all time series ordered by their distance to an estimated ideal time series, plus the ideal itself as an explicit waveform. The ideal can be constrained to a lower-dimensional space (r < T timestamps) to reduce complexity, and the model naturally handles variable-length inputs through DTW/TWED alignment without requiring resampling or fixed-length feature extraction.

## Method

The core contribution is an Ideal Point Model where the latent geometry is induced by an elastic time series distance (DTW or TWED) rather than a Euclidean metric on a fixed-dimensional embedding, so the ideal is itself a time series and preferences are determined by distance-to-ideal. The paper proves new Lipschitz continuity bounds for both DTW and TWED, derives generalization bounds on the excess risk of the empirical minimizer, and establishes almost-sure convergence of the estimated ideal to the true ideal under a logistic noise model. To enable gradient-based optimization, a differentiable soft-TWED is introduced by replacing the min operator in the TWED dynamic program with a softmin, yielding an end-to-end trainable objective optimized with ADAM and a hinge loss. A low-dimension variant restricts the ideal to r < T timestamps, reducing the convergence rate from O(T²/√n) for DTW to O(T·r/√n) while preserving ranking quality.

## Results

On the Basic Motions dataset (UCR archive), the proposed IPM with DTW achieves a Kendall Tau of 0.69 (±0.08) and with TWED 0.70 (±0.10), substantially outperforming the L²-based IPM (0.42 ± 0.26) and most feature-based ranking baselines. On synthetic data with time-warping deformations, TWED maintains a Kendall Tau of 0.78 at warp strength 0.5 and N=500, while L² drops to 0.48, demonstrating the robustness of elastic distances. On semi-synthetic Handwriting data, DTW achieves Kendall Tau 0.72 versus 0.59 for Rocket(2000)+RankSVM. The strongest baseline overall is RankSVM with ROCKET(1000) features at 0.83 Kendall Tau on Basic Motions, though it requires a feature extraction pipeline and lacks the interpretability of the estimated ideal signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DTW/TWED IPM outperforms all RankNet variants: RankNet (0.61), RankNet CNN (0.66), RankNet+ROCKET(200) (0.58), and RankNet+ROCKET(1000) (0.66) on Basic Motions Kendall Tau. Against RankSVM, the method beats RankSVM+ROCKET(200) (0.65) and Catch22+RankSVM (0.66), but is outperformed by the strongest baseline RankSVM+ROCKET(1000) (0.83). The Catch22 feature extractor is consistently weak: Catch22+L² scores only 0.27, and even Catch22+RankSVM (0.66) trails the proposed method. On synthetic data with temporal warping, the advantage is most dramatic: at warp strength 1 and N=500, TWED achieves 0.74 Kendall Tau while L² drops to 0.42, and at warp strength 3 the gap widens to 0.67 vs. 0.23. The key trade-off is that RankSVM+ROCKET(1000) achieves the highest score but operates on extracted features without interpretability, whereas the IPM directly estimates an ideal time series that can be visualized and analyzed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ideal Point Model (Coombs, 1950; Canal et al., 2022) from vector-space embeddings to time series by making the ideal point itself a time series and using elastic distances (DTW, TWED) as the underlying geometry. It builds directly on the soft-DTW framework (Cuturi & Blondel, 2017) to introduce soft-TWED, a differentiable variant enabling gradient-based optimization. The theoretical analysis draws on the noise model and recovery guarantees from Canal et al. (2022), while the empirical evaluation positions the method against standard learning-to-rank baselines (RankNet, RankSVM) and feature-extraction pipelines (Catch22, ROCKET) that operate on fixed-dimensional representations rather than raw waveforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored problem: ranking time series when expert annotations are noisy and subjective, as is common in medical and industrial monitoring. The interpretability of the estimated ideal time series (visualizable, analyzable for temporal patterns) is a genuine advantage over black-box ranking methods. Key limitations include the single-ideal-point assumption (which may not capture datasets with multiple abnormality modes) and the O(NT²d) computational cost for long series, though the low-dimension model partially mitigates this. Open questions include whether the multi-ideal extension sketched in the appendix scales well, and whether the theoretical convergence rates (O(T²/√n) for DTW) are tight in practice.
