## Problem

Deep visual features serve as the primary interface in modern vision systems (edge-cloud inference, multi-task pipelines, distributed training), yet there is no principled, task-aligned tolerance boundary that characterizes how much distortion each feature component can absorb without degrading downstream performance. Existing metrics like MSE are task-agnostic, and prior machine-vision JND work remains confined to the image domain, leaving feature-space quality control to heuristic tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an intermediate feature tensor f ∈ R^{C×H×W} extracted at a standardized split point (backbone final feature map for classification; FPN neck output for detection and instance segmentation) and produces a same-shaped FeatJND perturbation map δ = G_θ(f). For the quantization application, δ is converted to a non-negative per-token tolerance map s = |δ|/(|f|+ε), which is then scaled by a global factor λ to yield a token-wise step-size map Δ = λs shared uniformly across all channels at each spatial location.

## Method

FeatJND is formulated as a chance-constrained optimization that maximizes distortion magnitude M(δ) subject to the probability of task performance drop exceeding a tolerance ε being bounded, then relaxed via a hinge-based soft constraint and Lagrangian dual into a two-term training objective: maximize M(G_θ(f)) − λ_t · E[D_t(f+G_θ(f), f)]. The task discrepancy D_t is task-specific and differentiable—temperature-scaled KL divergence on classifier logits for classification, a sum of KL (RPN/ROI classification) and smooth-L1 (RPN/ROI regression) terms for detection, plus MSE on mask logits for instance segmentation. The estimator G_θ is a lightweight CNN (one 3×3 conv followed by 1×1 residual blocks with BN and ReLU), trained with the downstream task network frozen, using 1×1 kernels because backbone/neck features already aggregate spatial context through downsampling. The key design insight is that the learned δ encodes heterogeneous per-component tolerance: regions with large δ are task-insensitive and can absorb more distortion, enabling structured perturbation that outperforms unstructured Gaussian noise at matched NRMSE and guiding token-wise quantization step-size allocation under a fixed noise budget.

## Results

Across image classification, object detection, and instance segmentation, FeatJND-based distortions consistently preserve higher task performance than unstructured Gaussian perturbations at matched NRMSE levels (Figs. 4–6). In the token-wise quantization application, FeatJND-guided step-size allocation yields substantial gains over random permutation: e.g., on ImageNet classification with Swin-B at σ_std=1.6, FeatJND achieves 81.6% Top-1 vs. 64.6% for random (Δ=17.0 pp), and on COCO detection with PVTv2-b3 at σ_std=2.0, it reaches 46.8% mAP vs. 37.6% for random (Δ=9.24 pp). Both FeatJND and random allocation significantly outperform global uniform step-size quantization under the same noise budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FeatJND against three baselines. (1) **Gaussian noise (unstructured perturbation):** At matched NRMSE, FeatJND-based distortions consistently achieve higher Top-1 accuracy (classification), mAP (detection), and mAP (instance segmentation) across all backbones (ResNet-18/34/50, Swin-T/S/B, PVTv2-b1/b2/b3), confirming that the predicted JND map captures heterogeneous feature tolerance and allocates perturbation to less task-sensitive regions. (2) **Random step-size permutation (quantization):** Under the same quantization noise budget E(Δ²/12), FeatJND-guided allocation outperforms random permutation by 2.3–21.1 pp on classification, 2.9–20.1 pp on detection, and 2.3–17.3 pp on instance segmentation, with gains growing at higher noise levels. (3) **Global uniform step size (quantization):** Both FeatJND and random allocation significantly outperform uniform quantization, indicating that spatially-varying step sizes are essential; FeatJND further improves over random by directing larger steps to task-irrelevant tokens. The paper does not compare against learned quantization or other JND-based compression methods, as this is positioned as the first feature-space JND formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Just Noticeable Difference (JND) paradigm from the image domain to deep feature space. Prior machine-vision JND work (Jin et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021a) defined imperceptibility via task-metric changes but operated on pixel-level distortions, while feature-quality modeling in collaborative inference typically relies on MSE or post-compression accuracy drops tied to specific architectures. FeatJND departs from both by formulating a learnable, per-feature perturbation map at standardized split points (backbone or FPN neck output), and from adversarial attacks by seeking the maximum *safe* distortion rather than the worst-case perturbation that crosses decision boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FeatJND provides a task-aligned tolerance boundary that could serve as a general building block for feature-centric systems (compression, quantization, watermarking), replacing heuristic MSE-based quality control with a principled, learnable threshold. The token-wise quantization application demonstrates immediate practical value, showing that JND-guided step-size allocation outperforms both random permutation and global uniform quantization under matched noise budgets. Key open questions include whether a single FeatJND predictor can transfer across tasks and models, whether structured (e.g., low-rank) noise patterns can be exploited in large-model features, and how the framework scales beyond the three tasks and simple CNN estimator evaluated here.
