## Problem

Reinforcement learning with combinatorial action spaces is challenging because feasible action sets grow exponentially and are governed by hard constraints, making direct policy parameterization intractable. Existing approaches either embed learned value functions into mixed-integer programs (restricting critic architecture) or learn deterministic structured policies (limiting expressiveness and exploration). The paper asks whether the expressiveness of modern flow-based generative policies can be transferred to combinatorial RL while guaranteeing feasibility by design.

## Input / Output

The method takes an MDP state s and a state-dependent feasible set A(s) defined by combinatorial constraints (e.g., subset selection, routing, scheduling), and outputs a feasible binary decision vector a ∈ {0,1}^m. Internally, the policy operates on the unit sphere S^{m-1} of cost directions, where each direction parameterizes a linear objective for a downstream combinatorial optimization solver; the solver's positive scale invariance justifies restricting to the sphere. For graph-structured domains (STI testing), a Graph Isomorphism Network backbone processes the contact network, and actions are further constrained by a frontier rule requiring tested nodes to be adjacent to previously tested ones.

## Method

LSFLOW learns a stochastic policy in a compact spherical latent space via spherical flow matching, where each sampled cost direction c ∈ S^{m-1} specifies a linear objective for a combinatorial optimization solver that maps it to a feasible structured action, guaranteeing feasibility by construction. To avoid repeated solver calls during policy optimization, the critic is trained directly in the latent cost space, amortizing solver cost to one call per environment step. The policy is updated via a weighted spherical flow-matching objective with exponential reweighting by the latent critic, which is shown to implement a KL-regularized policy improvement step. To stabilize learning under the piecewise-constant, discontinuous value landscape induced by the solver, a smoothed Bellman operator applies von Mises-Fisher kernel smoothing on the sphere, yielding C^∞ value functions and provable γ-contraction with a consistent fixed point as the kernel concentrates.

## Results

On the four-task SEQUOIA benchmark (Dynamic Scheduling, Routing, Assignment, Intervention), LSFLOW achieves an average reward of 27.62, outperforming the strongest baseline SRL (22.91) by 20.6% and SEQUOIA (22.21) by 24.3%. LSFLOW also wins on every individual task, with the largest margin on Dynamic Intervention (17.21 vs. SRL's 13.36). In terms of efficiency, LSFLOW trains in 1.29 hours on average—approximately 3.0× faster than SRL (3.89 h) and 7.1× faster than SEQUOIA (9.11 h). On the real-world STI testing task, LSFLOW achieves higher detection efficiency than SRL for chlamydia and gonorrhea, matches it on HIV, and provides stronger early-stage detection on syphilis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSFLOW outperforms all three learned baselines on every one of the four benchmark tasks. Against SEQUOIA (Xu et al., 2025), which embeds a Q-network into a mixed-integer program for greedy action selection, LSFLOW gains 4.85 points on average (27.62 vs. 22.21), with the largest margin on Dynamic Intervention (+6.37) where SEQUOIA's MIP-based approach struggles most; SEQUOIA is also excluded from the STI testing experiment because it requires MLP-based value networks and cannot accommodate the graph neural network backbone needed for that task. Against SRL (Hoppe et al., 2025), which learns a deterministic structured policy via Fenchel-Young losses, LSFLOW improves by 4.71 points on average (27.62 vs. 22.91), with gains of +4.35 on Scheduling, +3.19 on Routing, +7.47 on Assignment, and +3.85 on Intervention; the authors attribute this to SRL's deterministic policy limiting exploration in complex combinatorial landscapes. Against DQN-Sampling (He et al., 2016), which draws 100 candidate feasible actions and selects the highest-valued one, LSFLOW leads by 9.47 points on average. In terms of wall-clock training time, LSFLOW (1.29 h) is roughly 3× faster than SRL (3.89 h) and 7× faster than SEQUOIA (9.11 h), because it avoids differentiating through the solver and trains the critic directly in the latent cost space.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSFLOW sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) flow-matching policies for continuous-action RL, where exponential reweighting has been shown to implement KL-regularized policy improvement (Ma et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2025a), and (2) solver-augmented combinatorial RL, where prior methods either embed value networks into MIPs for greedy selection (SEQUOIA, Xu et al., 2025) or learn deterministic structured policies end-to-end (SRL, Hoppe et al., 2025). The key departure is the observation that the solver's positive scale invariance (Lemma 3.1) makes the latent space naturally spherical, enabling the use of spherical flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) as the policy parameterization. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first flow- or diffusion-based stochastic policy for RL with combinatorial action spaces, combining generative expressiveness with solver-guaranteed feasibility in a way neither prior thread achieves alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a new methodological direction for combinatorial RL by showing that the expressiveness of generative policies (rich multimodal stochastic distributions) can be transferred to discrete, constraint-heavy action spaces without sacrificing feasibility. The smoothed Bellman operator with vMF kernel, together with the contraction and consistency theorems, provides a principled way to handle the discontinuities inherent in solver-based action selection. However, several open questions remain: the smoothing introduces a bias-variance tradeoff with no adaptive schedule, inference still requires a solver call per step (a shared limitation with all solver-based methods), and the authors acknowledge that backpropagating through the flow sampler for direct value maximization destabilized training. The empirical gains (20.6% average over SRL) are solid but evaluated on relatively small-scale benchmarks, and the real-world STI testing application, while motivating, is limited to a single domain with a frontier constraint.
