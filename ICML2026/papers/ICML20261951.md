## Problem

The Lion optimizer, discovered via program search by Google, has shown strong empirical performance in training large models but lacked rigorous convergence guarantees under standard assumptions. Prior analyses required additional conditions such as function coerciveness, large batch sizes, or bias-correction assumptions on stochastic gradients. This paper closes that gap by establishing convergence rates for Lion and its variants under standard smoothness and bounded-noise assumptions, in both centralized and distributed settings.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on stochastic gradient estimates ∇f(x;ξ) that are unbiased with bounded variance σ², along with hyperparameters (momentum coefficients β₁, β₂, learning rate η, weight decay λ, and for the VR variant a STORM coefficient γ). The convergence guarantee is expressed in terms of the ℓ₁-norm of the true gradient, E[‖∇f(x_t)‖₁], which is the natural measure for sign-based methods and introduces a d^{1/2} dimensional factor. In the distributed setting, the input additionally involves n heterogeneous nodes each computing local stochastic gradients, and the output rate depends on the product nT, reflecting linear speedup.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a careful bounding of the update step ‖x_{t+1}−x_t‖² via a recursive argument on ‖x_t‖² that avoids the coerciveness assumption previously required (Lemma 3.5). For the variance-reduced variant (Lion-VR), the m_t momentum estimator is modified with a STORM-style correction term γ(∇f(x_t;ξ_t)−∇f(x_{t−1};ξ_t)), which under the average smoothness assumption accelerates the rate from T^{-1/4} to T^{-1/3}. In the distributed setting, each node maintains local momentum estimators and sends them to a parameter server that aggregates and broadcasts the sign of the sum, achieving linear speedup in n. For communication efficiency, the paper introduces bidirectional 1-bit compression: nodes apply an unbiased sign operator S_G(·) to their local estimators before uploading, and the server applies S_1(·) to the aggregated vector before broadcasting, yielding rates that converge to zero as T→∞ without the error floor of naive double-sign compression.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, Lion-VR achieves 76.47% top-1 accuracy, improving over AdamW (76.03%) by +0.44% and over the original Lion (76.01%) by +0.46%. For ViT-S with RandAug+MixUp augmentations, Lion-VR reaches 79.13%, outperforming Lion (79.01%) by +0.12% and AdamW (78.35%) by +0.78%. In the distributed setting (4 nodes), Dis-Lion-VR achieves 74.12% on ResNet-50, a +0.74% improvement over Dis-Lion (73.38%), while its communication-efficient variant Dis-Lion-VR-CE retains most of the gain at 73.92% with bidirectional 1-bit sign compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Lion-VR against two baselines: the original Lion optimizer (Chen et al., 2023) and AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019). Lion-VR consistently outperforms both across all model and augmentation configurations. On ResNet-50, Lion-VR beats AdamW by +0.44% and Lion by +0.46%; on ViT-S without augmentation, it improves over Lion by +0.33% and AdamW by +0.46%. With RandAug+MixUp, the gap over AdamW widens to +0.78% while the gain over Lion narrows to +0.12%, suggesting Lion already benefits more from strong augmentations. In the distributed setting, Dis-Lion-VR extends these advantages, achieving +0.74% over Dis-Lion on ResNet-50 and +0.85% over Dis-Lion on ViT-S with augmentations, while the communication-efficient variant (Dis-Lion-VR-CE) sacrifices roughly 0.2–0.5% accuracy in exchange for bidirectional 1-bit sign compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lion optimizer (Chen et al., 2023) by providing the first convergence analysis under standard smoothness assumptions, removing the coerciveness requirement imposed by Dong et al. (2024) and the large-batch/Frank-Wolfe-gap framework of Sfyraki & Wang (2025). It incorporates the STORM variance reduction estimator (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019) into Lion's momentum update to improve the rate from T^{-1/4} to T^{-1/3}, and adapts the unbiased sign operation from sign-based distributed optimization (Jin et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2023) to achieve bidirectional 1-bit compression. Unlike prior distributed Lion analyses that require bias correction (Liu et al., 2024) or bounded heterogeneity (Tang & Chang, 2024), the distributed results here hold under standard assumptions with linear speedup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since Lion is a production optimizer used by Google for training large language models and other architectures, rigorous convergence guarantees under standard assumptions fill an important theoretical gap that practitioners have lacked. The linear speedup result and the communication-efficient bidirectional 1-bit variant are directly relevant to scaling Lion to multi-node training with bandwidth constraints. Open questions include whether the d^{1/2} dimensional factor in the ℓ₁-norm convergence rate is truly unavoidable (no matching lower bound exists yet), and whether the bounded-gradient assumption required for the unbiased sign operator can be eliminated via more general compression schemes without sacrificing the rate.
