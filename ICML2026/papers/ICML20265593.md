## Problem

Existing generalized smoothness conditions such as (L₀,L₁)-smoothness and ℓ-smoothness are defined exclusively in terms of the ℓ₂-norm, restricting their applicability to gradient descent in Euclidean space. Mirror descent, a fundamental algorithm for non-Euclidean optimization, has no convergence guarantees under these relaxed smoothness assumptions, leaving a critical gap between theory and the widespread practical use of mirror descent in LLM training, reinforcement learning, and other non-Euclidean settings.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a differentiable objective f on a domain 𝒳, a distance-generating function ψ that is strongly convex with respect to a chosen norm ‖·‖ (defining the Bregman divergence and prox-mapping), and a nondecreasing sub-quadratic link function ℓ governing the Hessian growth. The output is a sequence of iterates with explicit convergence rate bounds (e.g., O(1/T) for average and last iterate, O(1/T²) for accelerated variants) together with a uniform gradient bound ‖∇f(x_t)‖* ≤ G that holds along the entire trajectory. The norm and its dual enter asymmetrically: the Hessian operator norm is measured in the dual norm ‖·‖* while step sizes are in the primal norm ‖·‖, which is the key structural departure from prior Euclidean formulations.

## Method

The paper introduces ℓ*-smoothness, which bounds the Hessian via ‖∇²f(x)h‖* ≤ ℓ(‖∇f(x)‖*)‖h‖ for a general norm and its dual, fully encompassing L-smooth, (L₀,L₁)-smooth, and ℓ-smooth conditions as special cases. The central technical ingredient is a generalized self-bounding property (Lemma 3.4) that bounds the squared dual norm of the gradient by 2ℓ(2‖∇f(x)‖*)(f(x)−f*), thereby converting the hard problem of tracking dual-norm monotonicity into the tractable problem of controlling suboptimality gaps. For accelerated mirror descent, a 'time partition' technique bounds the stability term e_t = ‖y_t − x_{t−1}‖ by splitting the timeline at a threshold τ, using contraction for small t and hyperbolic decay for large t. For stochastic mirror descent, a 'chain-of-events' analysis with inductive union bounds manages both gradient and noise terms under sub-Gaussian noise, yielding a high-probability rate of Õ(√log T / √T). All rates match the state-of-the-art under classic L-smoothness, and the framework extends to non-convex composite optimization via a gradient-mapping criterion.

## Results

Under the proposed ℓ*-smoothness condition, mirror descent achieves O(1/T) convergence for both average-iterate and last-iterate (Theorem 3.5), accelerated mirror descent achieves the optimal O(1/T²) rate (Theorem 3.7), and stochastic mirror descent achieves Õ(1/√T) high-probability convergence (Theorem 4.2). All rates match the state-of-the-art under classic L-smoothness. Empirically, the ℓ*-smoothness assumption is validated on GPT-2 models (124M–770M) pretrained on FineWeb and a 6-layer Transformer on WMT'16, as well as CNNs on CIFAR-10, showing strong agreement between predicted and observed layer-wise curvature bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior convergence guarantees rather than empirical baselines. The ℓ*-smoothness framework fully encompasses the ℓ-smoothness of Li et al. (2023a) and the (L₀,L₁)-smoothness of Zhang et al. (2020b) as special cases (Remark 2.3), while extending to arbitrary norms beyond ℓ₂. All convergence rates match those under classic L-smoothness: O(1/T) for mirror descent, O(1/T²) for accelerated mirror descent, and Õ(1/√T) for stochastic mirror descent. Compared to Li et al. (2023a), the paper avoids dimension-dependent factors (e.g., √n in Proposition 2.8) by using dual norms, and provides a simpler proof for accelerated mirror descent without requiring a smaller step size or auxiliary stabilization sequences. For stochastic optimization, the result matches the state-of-the-art rate of Liu & Zhou (2024) while operating under a strictly more general function class. The composite (non-convex) result in Theorem 5.1 matches the O(1/T) gradient-mapping rate of Ghadimi et al. (2016) and Lan (2020) under standard smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized smoothness framework of Li et al. (2023a) and Zhang et al. (2020b) from the Euclidean ℓ₂ setting to arbitrary norm-dual-norm pairs, introducing ℓ*-smoothness as the first non-Euclidean generalized smoothness model. While prior work established convergence for gradient descent and Nesterov acceleration under ℓ-smoothness, no theory existed for mirror descent and its variants (accelerated MD, optimistic MD, mirror prox) under any generalized smoothness condition. The key technical departure is a generalized self-bounding property (a non-Euclidean Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality) that bounds gradient dual norms via suboptimality gaps, circumventing the failure of direct gradient tracking in non-Euclidean mirror descent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mirror descent is a workhorse algorithm in LLM pretraining, reinforcement learning, network quantization, and diffusion model watermarking, yet lacked convergence guarantees under the relaxed smoothness conditions that empirically hold in modern ML. By matching classic O(1/T), O(1/T²), and Õ(1/√T) rates under ℓ*-smoothness, the paper provides the theoretical justification for using mirror descent on the non-smooth objectives that actually arise in practice. The dimension-dependent tightening of smoothness constants (e.g., √n or 1/n factors over the Euclidean formulation) suggests that choosing geometry-adapted norms can yield meaningful practical speedups. Open questions include whether the sub-quadratic growth assumption on ℓ(·) is truly necessary, whether the results extend to adaptive step-size variants like Adam under non-Euclidean generalized smoothness, and whether the empirical ℓ*-smoothness observed in GPT-2 and CNNs persists at larger scales.
