## Problem

Token-adaptive layer execution reduces FLOPs by selectively bypassing transformer layers, while quantization reduces memory by lowering weight precision, yet naively combining them causes additional accuracy degradation beyond either technique alone. This occurs because token-adaptive models reduce redundancy in two compounding ways: they limit the diversity of execution paths explored during fine-tuning (collapsing into fixed layer subsets) and they lower the number of active parameters at inference, making each remaining parameter more critical and less tolerant to quantization noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces generated text, identical in interface to the underlying LLM (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, LLaMA3.2-3B). At inference, each transformer layer's router outputs a two-class probability via softmax, and a single global threshold θ (searched in (0, 0.5]) determines whether the layer executes or is bypassed, allowing the execution ratio to be tuned post-training without retraining. The model is fine-tuned per downstream task using the benchmark's official training split, and the threshold is calibrated on a small 300-sample set via a two-phase coarse-to-fine grid search.

## Method

QTALE builds on D-LLM's per-layer router architecture and introduces two components to restore quantization robustness. First, a quantization-robust training strategy adds an entropy regularization loss (−λ₂·L_entropy) to the fine-tuning objective, which keeps the gap between execute and bypass router logits small so that Gumbel noise can meaningfully flip routing decisions throughout training, preventing the model from collapsing into a fixed subset of active layers. Second, an inference-time execution ratio adjustment mechanism replaces D-LLM's argmax decision rule with a softmax-plus-threshold rule: a layer executes if its execute-class probability p_{l,0} ≥ θ, where a single global θ shared across all layers controls the overall execution ratio in a training-free manner. Lowering θ below 0.5 increases the execution ratio, reintroducing parameter redundancy to absorb quantization errors, while the two-phase grid search on a calibration set selects the optimal θ per task. The total training loss is L_CE + λ₁·L_rate − λ₂·L_entropy, and the approach is compatible with arbitrary PTQ methods (AWQ, GPTQ, MagR) and can be combined with weight pruning.

## Results

On the CommonsenseQA (CSQA) benchmark suite, QTALE maintains the accuracy gap to quantization-only (Full) models below 0.5% across all tested configurations. For LLaMA2-7B under 3-bit AWQ quantization, QTALE achieves 72.79% CSQA average accuracy versus 72.22% for the quantized full model, while D-LLM drops to 70.57%. Under 4-bit quantization on LLaMA3.1-8B, QTALE reaches 79.17% CSQA average (vs. 79.67% full, 77.68% D-LLM). On MMLU, QTALE at 3-bit achieves 45.11% (LLaMA3.1-8B) and 44.78% (LLaMA2-7B), substantially closing the gap to full models (44.56% and 46.12%) compared to D-LLM (43.49% and 42.84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D-LLM (the primary token-adaptive baseline), QTALE consistently wins under quantization: at 4-bit, QTALE gains +1.86% CSQA average on LLaMA2-7B (79.18 vs. 77.32) and +1.49% on LLaMA3.1-8B (79.17 vs. 77.68); at 3-bit, the advantage widens to +2.22% (72.79 vs. 70.57) and +2.84% (69.65 vs. 66.81) respectively. At 16-bit (no quantization), both methods are comparable, confirming that QTALE's entropy regularization does not sacrifice pre-quantization accuracy. Against SLEB (structured layer-wise pruning, Appendix B.3), QTALE achieves a similar ~1.25× speedup at 20% pruning but with substantially better accuracy: SLEB at 20% + 4-bit yields 63.34% CSQA average versus QTALE's 79.18%, and at 3-bit the gap widens to 43.65% vs. 72.79%. QTALE also outperforms D-LLM under alternative PTQ methods (MagR+GPTQ) and under 50% unstructured sparsity (Wanda), demonstrating that its quantization robustness generalizes beyond AWQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

QTALE directly extends D-LLM (Jiang et al., 2024), which introduced token-adaptive layer execution via per-layer router modules trained with Gumbel-Softmax and a ratio regularization loss. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis showing that D-LLM's Gumbel-Softmax stochasticity collapses during training (decision-flipping ratio drops to zero by epoch 4) because router logit gaps grow beyond the range where Gumbel noise can flip decisions, effectively reducing the model to a fixed layer-pruning pattern. QTALE addresses this with an entropy regularization term on router outputs (analogous in spirit to stochastic depth) and replaces the fixed argmax inference rule with a single global softmax threshold θ that allows training-free execution ratio adjustment. The quantization side leverages standard PTQ methods (AWQ, GPTQ, MagR) without modification, positioning QTALE as a training-side fix that makes token-adaptive models quantization-robust rather than a new quantization algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that QTALE enables simultaneous FLOPs reduction (~50%) and memory reduction (4× at 4-bit) while keeping the accuracy gap to quantization-only models below 0.5% on CommonsenseQA, which is a meaningful step toward deploying LLMs on memory-constrained hardware (e.g., 12 GB consumer GPUs). However, the speedup from token-adaptive execution is modest (~1.28×) and quantization provides no additional latency benefit, so the primary gain is memory rather than throughput. Limitations include the requirement for task-specific fine-tuning (not purely post-training), the small but nonzero calibration overhead for threshold selection, and the fact that the approach has only been validated on 3B–8B models with relatively short-context benchmarks. Open questions include whether the entropy-regularized training generalizes to larger models or longer generation tasks, and whether the threshold mechanism can be learned end-to-end rather than calibrated via grid search.
