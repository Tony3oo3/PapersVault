## Problem

Existing backpropagation-free test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models rely on cache-based designs that store and retrieve test-sample features, introducing two critical limitations: inference latency grows with the number of classes (e.g., ADAPT drops to 14.5% of CLIP's speed on ImageNet-1K), and performance degrades when the cache is sparse at stream onset or contains incorrect pseudo-labeled samples. This paper asks whether TTA can be performed without any external memory, achieving both high efficiency and stable adaptation from the very first test sample.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled RGB images (or point clouds with 1024 points each) one at a time (batch size 1) through a frozen CLIP visual encoder, alongside fixed handcrafted text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [class]') encoded by the CLIP text encoder to produce C class-specific text embeddings. The output is a per-sample classification probability over C classes, computed by summing the original CLIP zero-shot logits with prototype-based logits. No labeled data, augmentation, or backpropagation is required at any point during the stream.

## Method

PTA maintains a set of C class-specific knowledge prototypes (each a d-dimensional vector) that are zero-initialized and updated in real-time via a dynamic Exponential Moving Average: each incoming sample's visual feature is injected into every prototype with an adaptive weight β_j = 1 − exp(−s_j/h), where s_j is the zero-shot confidence for class j and h is a temperature parameter that prevents early high-confidence samples from dominating. To prevent prototypes from drifting out of CLIP's pretrained text-image embedding space, a text-anchored prototype is maintained by interpolating the visual prototypes with the original CLIP text embeddings using a small coefficient w (default 0.01). The final prediction combines the original CLIP zero-shot logits with prototype-based logits (cosine similarity between the visual feature and the text-anchored prototypes), preserving CLIP's foundational capability while adding accumulated domain knowledge. Because all knowledge is stored and retrieved solely within the fixed-size prototype matrix (C×d), no external cache population or similarity-based retrieval is needed, yielding 92% of CLIP's inference speed on ImageNet-1K versus 50% for cache-based TDA.

## Results

On 10 cross-domain image recognition benchmarks, PTA achieves 69.38% average top-1 accuracy with ViT-B/16, improving over the CLIP zero-shot baseline (65.64%) by 3.74% and outperforming all backpropagation-free TTA methods (best prior: ADAPT at 68.74%). On 4 OOD ImageNet variants, PTA reaches 64.45% OOD average accuracy, competitive with the best backprop-free method BoostAdapter (64.99%). In terms of efficiency on ImageNet-1K, PTA runs at 81.8 FPS (92% of CLIP's 89.3 FPS), compared to 44.6 FPS (50%) for TDA and 12.9 FPS (14%) for ADAPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTA (69.38% avg) outperforms all backpropagation-free TTA baselines on cross-domain benchmarks: ADAPT (68.74%, +0.64%), TCA (68.69%, +0.69%), BoostAdapter (68.68%, +0.70%), TDA (67.97%, +1.41%), SCA (67.75%, +1.63%), MTA (65.95%, +3.43%), and BCA (65.90%, +3.48%). Against backpropagation-based methods, PTA matches DPE (69.40%) within 0.02% while requiring no gradient computation, and surpasses TPT (65.10%) by 4.28% and DiffTPT (65.47%) by 3.91%. On OOD generalization, PTA (64.45% OOD avg) slightly trails BoostAdapter (64.99%) but edges out ADAPT (64.41%), SCA (64.30%), and TDA (63.89%). On efficiency, PTA retains 92% of CLIP's inference speed on ImageNet-1K, versus 50% for TDA and 14% for ADAPT. On robust point cloud analysis, PTA consistently outperforms the cache-based Point-Cache across all 4 corrupted benchmarks and 5 corruption severity levels (e.g., +1.43% avg on ModelNet-C at severity 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's contrastive vision-language framework and positions itself within the backpropagation-free test-time adaptation (TTA) lineage for VLMs. It departs from the dominant cache-based paradigm (TDA, ADAPT, SCA, BoostAdapter) that stores and retrieves individual test samples in class-conditioned external memory, replacing this mechanism with a compact set of class-specific knowledge prototypes updated via an adaptive exponential moving average. Conceptually, PTA is closer to prototype learning in classical few-shot classification than to the retrieval-augmented designs of prior TTA work, while retaining CLIP's text embeddings as anchoring regularizers to prevent prototype drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the efficiency gap that has limited deployment of backpropagation-free TTA in large-scale, latency-sensitive settings: PTA retains 92% of CLIP's inference speed on ImageNet-1K versus 50% (TDA) and 14% (ADAPT), while matching or exceeding their accuracy. Open questions remain around non-stationary streams where EMA-based prototypes may retain stale information, and the paper acknowledges that fairness-aware or drift-aware prototype resets are needed for sensitive deployment scenarios. The extension to point cloud analysis (ULIP + PTA) suggests the prototype accumulation principle is modality-agnostic, but the gains there are more modest and the evaluation is limited to four corrupted benchmarks.
