## Problem

Standard training objectives for Boltzmann generators (forward KL, flow matching, score matching) rely solely on sample matching and ignore target energy values that are already computed during dataset construction. The log-variance objective, which directly leverages energy labels, was previously restricted to on-policy variational training requiring repeated energy evaluations and prone to mode collapse. The paper asks whether energy-based regularization can be applied off-policy on fixed, energy-labeled datasets to improve both final performance and data efficiency without additional on-policy samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed dataset of molecular configurations paired with their target energy labels (from unbiased or biased MD simulations) and a target energy function E(x) for computing log importance weights. It produces independent samples from the Boltzmann distribution via a discrete normalizing flow, along with exact likelihood evaluations that yield importance weights w(x) = p̃_X(x)/q_X^θ(x) for unbiased estimation of thermodynamic expectations. A key practical detail is that LDR can be evaluated over arbitrary reference distributions—including biased datasets or importance-sampled subsets—so it can incorporate data that standard objectives cannot directly use.

## Method

The core contribution is off-policy log-dispersion regularization (LDR), which generalizes the log-variance objective to a family of objectives L_LD^θ = E_{r_X}[ρ(f^θ(x) − c^θ)] where f^θ(x) = −log q_X^θ(x) − E(x)/(k_B T) and c^θ is the batch mean. The paper proves that when the reference distribution r_X lacks full support (as with any fixed dataset), log-dispersion alone is not a divergence and leaves the model unconstrained outside the data manifold; it therefore demotes LDR to a shape regularizer combined with a standard data-based objective: L^θ = λ_data · L_data^θ + λ_LD · L_LD^θ. This hybrid preserves the correct optimum (Proposition A.3) while providing an additional training signal that aligns the proposal with the target energy landscape. Two variants are studied—LDR-L2 (p=2, recovering log-variance) and LDR-L1 (p=1, less sensitive to energy outliers)—and the framework is applied across three settings: unbiased equilibrium data, biased simulation data with importance-sampling-based self-refinement, and purely variational training via Constrained Mass Transport, where it reduces required target evaluations by up to 10×.

## Results

On unbiased datasets, LDR consistently improves both NLL and ESS across all benchmarks. For alanine dipeptide with 1×10⁶ samples, LDR-L2 achieves NLL −214.432 and ESS 98.25%, outperforming the unregularized forward KL (NLL −214.378, ESS 87.94%) and matching the performance of unregularized training with 5×10⁶ samples (>5× data efficiency gain). For alanine hexapeptide at 5×10⁶ samples, LDR-L1 improves ESS from 29.60% to 39.59%. In the variational setting, CMT+LDR with 1×10⁷ target evaluations matches or exceeds CMT with 1×10⁸ evaluations, demonstrating ~10× efficiency in target energy evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDR outperforms all five baselines across every tested setting. Against the path-gradient forward KL (Vaitl et al., 2024), LDR-L1 achieves NLL 2.734 vs. 2.812 and ESS 96.98% vs. 77.97% on the GMM task with 500 samples, showing that energy-based regularization provides a stronger signal than density-gradient-based correction in low-data regimes. In the variational setting, CMT+LDR-L2 (NLL −214.429, ESS 97.81% with 1×10⁷ evaluations) substantially outperforms both FAB (NLL −214.412, ESS 94.95% with 2.13×10⁸ evaluations) and TA-BG (NLL −214.419, ESS 95.76% with 1×10⁸ evaluations), achieving better final performance with 10–20× fewer target energy evaluations. Compared to vanilla CMT (NLL −214.358, ESS 85.20% with 1×10⁷ evaluations), CMT+LDR-L2 improves ESS by over 12 percentage points at the same evaluation budget, and on alanine hexapeptide at 1×10⁸ evaluations, CMT+LDR-L2 reaches ESS 31.47% while vanilla CMT suffers mode collapse (ESS 5.92%). For Cartesian-coordinate training (Tan et al., 2025a), LDR-L2 improves ESS from 19.40% to 35.89% and NLL from −219.583 to −219.815, nearly doubling the effective sample size. The only setting where LDR shows a slight disadvantage is alanine hexapeptide at 1×10⁶ samples, where the model is too far from target for the LD objective to be informative, though it still improves ESS from 13.10% to 16.35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the log-variance objective, originally developed for on-policy variational training of diffusion samplers (Richter & Berner, 2023; Richter et al., 2020), as an off-policy shape regularizer for Boltzmann generators. The key conceptual departure is demoting the log-dispersion objective from a standalone divergence to a regularizer combined with standard data-based objectives (forward KL, flow matching), which the authors prove is necessary because fixed datasets lack full support and thus LD alone cannot constrain the model outside the data manifold. The framework is applied on top of existing training pipelines—forward KL for data-based training and Constrained Mass Transport (CMT) for variational training—without requiring additional on-policy energy evaluations, and is positioned as complementary to the recent path-gradient forward KL approach (Vaitl et al., 2024) that uses target density gradients rather than energy values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data efficiency is the central bottleneck for practical Boltzmann generators, and this paper shows that energy labels already computed during dataset construction can be leveraged as a free regularization signal, yielding up to 10× reductions in target evaluations for variational training and >5× data efficiency gains for data-based training. The method's broad applicability across unbiased, biased, and purely variational settings, combined with its simplicity (a single additional loss term with one hyperparameter), makes it easy to integrate into existing pipelines. Open questions include LDR's behavior under increasingly biased reference distributions, extension to diffusion-based Boltzmann generators, and whether the gains persist in transferable multi-system settings where internal coordinates are unavailable.
