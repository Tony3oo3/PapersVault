## Problem

Existing process monitoring methods capture either temporal causal relationships (via Granger-type predictive effects) or cross-environment invariance, but not both simultaneously, causing detection performance to degrade under distribution shifts. No prior spectral framework jointly optimizes for temporal target relevance and robustness to environment-dependent mean shifts within a single principled formulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time-series observations (m variables, N samples), a lag order s to construct time-lagged embeddings z_t ∈ R^{m(s+1)}, K environment labels (in practice, contiguous temporal segments of a single operating mode), and L target variable indices taken one step ahead. It produces three feature subspaces (stable W_s, environment-varying W_v, slow-feature W_sf) and four jointly calibrated monitoring statistics (T²_s, T²_v, T²_sf, SPE) whose thresholds are set on a held-out normal run via a shared marginal-rank OR-alarm procedure targeting a specified false-alarm budget.

## Method

CFL formulates feature extraction as a generalized Rayleigh-quotient problem that simultaneously maximizes lagged cross-covariance with one-step-ahead targets (via the target-relevance matrix A_y, a sum of L rank-1 outer products) and penalizes environment-dependent mean shifts (via the between-environment scatter matrix A_e, analogous to Fisher's between-class scatter). The stable subspace W_s is obtained from the top d_s generalized eigenvectors of A_y w = λ(A_e + εI)w, while a complementary varying subspace W_v from A_e w = λ(Σ_d + εI)w captures the environment-sensitive directions that faults manifest along. A third slow-feature eigenproblem (SFA's Ω_d vs. Σ_d) is added to detect dynamics-dominated faults that leave mean structure nearly unchanged. Theoretical analysis proves that under a three-way subspace decomposition (predictive-invariant, spurious mean-shift, residual), the top eigenvalues diverge as 1/ε while the rest remain bounded, yielding a spectral gap that identifies the mean-invariant predictive subspace, with a Davis-Kahan perturbation bound quantifying finite-sample sensitivity.

## Results

On the Tennessee Eastman Process (18 detectable faults), CFL achieves the highest average fault detection rate of 94.6% at the lowest realized false-alarm rate (1.3%) and the lowest mean detection delay (24 samples) among all 16 methods. The closest competitor, Mahalanobis distance, reaches 93.7% FDR at 2.0% FAR with 26-sample delay; SFA follows at 93.5% FDR. The paper notes the top margin is small (CFL vs. Mahalanobis: +0.90 points, 95% bootstrap CI [−0.13, 2.47]), and CFL is best on 7 faults in a three-way tie with SFA and Mahalanobis. The slowness monitor component is critical, raising Fault 19 (a dynamics-dominated fault) from 45.8% to 90.0% FDR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFL outperforms all 15 baselines on average FDR (94.6%), with the closest competitors being Mahalanobis distance (93.7%), SFA (93.5%), and GMM (92.2%). CFL also achieves the lowest realized FAR (1.3% vs. 2.0% for Mahalanobis and 3.0% for SFA) and the lowest mean detection delay (24 vs. 26 for Mahalanobis/GEV-SFA and 30 for SFA). Among dynamic latent-variable methods, DiCCA (85.2%) and DiPCA (79.2%) trail CFL by roughly 9–15 FDR points, while DiPLS (73.6%) is substantially worse, confirming that temporal predictability alone without invariance is insufficient. Reconstruction-based methods (AE 77.2%, VAE 78.0%, LSTM-AE 81.3%) and density-based methods (LOF 77.4%, IF 55.1%) are all clearly inferior. The largest single-fault gap is on Fault 19 (dynamics-dominated), where CFL's slowness monitor achieves 90.0% FDR versus 26.0% for PCA, 5.9% for DiPLS, and 7.2% for IF, though SFA (91.4%) and Mahalanobis (90.5%) are comparable on this specific fault.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFL unifies two previously separate lines of work: dynamic latent-variable methods (DiPCA, DiCCA, DiPLS) that capture temporal predictability through lagged representations, and causal invariance principles (IRM, ICP, independent causal mechanisms) that seek features stable across environments. The key departure is formulating both objectives—maximizing lagged target covariance and penalizing between-environment mean shifts—as a single generalized Rayleigh-quotient eigenproblem, rather than optimizing them separately. The environment-sensitivity matrix A_e is explicitly identified as the between-class scatter matrix of Fisher discriminant analysis, and the constrained-trace formulation is the classical ratio-trace relaxation, grounding the method in well-studied multivariate statistics. SFA's slow-feature eigenproblem is incorporated as a complementary fourth monitor rather than as the primary feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical characterization (spectral separation, Davis-Kahan perturbation bounds) for when the extracted subspace is mean-invariant and predictive, which is useful for practitioners choosing between temporal and invariance-based feature extraction. However, the empirical gains are modest: the top margin over Mahalanobis (+0.90 FDR points) is not statistically resolved, and the 'causal' framing is explicitly acknowledged as a proxy—mean invariance is neither necessary nor sufficient for causal validity, and the TEP environments are temporal segments rather than true operating-mode changes. Open questions include whether the framework extends to genuine multi-environment data, stronger distributional invariance beyond first moments, and nonlinear feature maps; the single-dataset evaluation limits generalizability claims.
