## Problem

Group-wise data attribution for diffusion models asks a counterfactual question: how would a generated output change if an entire training group (e.g., an artistic style or object class) were absent? The gold-standard answer requires Leave-One-Group-Out (LOGO) retraining of N+1 models from scratch, which becomes computationally prohibitive as the number of groups grows. Existing instance-level attribution methods (influence functions, TRAK, DAS) do not directly target this group-removal counterfactual and fail to capture nonlinear group interactions when scores are simply aggregated.

## Input / Output

The method takes a generated image (32×32 pixel-space for CIFAR-10; 512×512 VAE latent for Stable Diffusion) and, in the conditional setting, a text prompt describing the image's content and style. It produces a scalar ELBO-difference score per group, which is then ranked to identify the most influential training groups. A key practical detail is that the unlearned counterfactual models are precomputed once (one per group), so query-time evaluation requires only forward-pass ELBO computation under each precomputed model without re-running unlearning. ELBO is computed in latent space only, excluding the VAE decoder likelihood term, which is shared and fixed across all models.

## Method

GUDA approximates each LOGO counterfactual model by applying machine unlearning to a single shared full-data model rather than retraining from scratch. The attribution score for group k is the ELBO difference between the full model and the unlearned model θ_{-k}^{ul} on a query sample (Eq. 5). The unlearning objective combines a forget term (setting-specific) and a score-matching preservation term (Eq. 7) that distills the frozen full model's noise predictions on retain-set samples to prevent catastrophic forgetting. For unconditional generation (GUDA-U), the forget loss uses ReTrack-style importance-weighted redirection: noisy forget-set samples are trained to denoise toward K nearest retain-set neighbors weighted by the forward diffusion posterior. For conditional text-to-image (GUDA-C), the forget loss redirects the model's response under a forget prompt toward the frozen full model's response under a CLIP-weighted style-selection anchor that preserves content while swapping style descriptors to a retain-style prompt, resolving the condition-distribution mismatch that breaks a naive ReTrack extension. This yields a ~100× wall-clock speedup over LOGO retraining on CIFAR-10 while matching or exceeding its attribution accuracy on head-focused metrics.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10 classes, 2,048 queries), GUDA achieves 72.7% Top-1 agreement, 0.798 MRR, and 0.677 NDCG@3, outperforming the nearest competitor DAS (71.6% Top-1) while being ~17× faster (2h 02m vs. 35h 24m total). On UnlearnCanvas (16 artistic styles, 320 queries with Stable Diffusion 1.5), GUDA reaches 45.6% Top-1 and 0.734 NDCG@3, substantially exceeding CLIPA (33.8% Top-1) and Wang et al. (4.7% Top-1). GUDA achieves a ~100× wall-clock speedup over the LOGO retraining oracle on CIFAR-10 (2h 02m vs. 207h 47m) by replacing full retraining with lightweight unlearning fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUDA outperforms DAS (Lin et al., 2025) on Top-1 (72.7% vs. 71.6%), MRR (0.798 vs. 0.794), and NDCG@3 (0.677 vs. 0.675) on CIFAR-10, while being ~17× faster in total wall-clock time (2h 02m vs. 35h 24m); DAS edges out GUDA only on Spearman (0.267 vs. 0.265). Against D-TRAK (Zheng et al., 2024), GUDA wins decisively on all head metrics (Top-1: 72.7% vs. 60.9%; NDCG@3: 0.677 vs. 0.639). TRAK (Park et al., 2023) performs near-randomly (11.8% Top-1 vs. 10% chance), confirming that standard influence functions do not transfer well to unconditional generative models. On the text-to-image UnlearnCanvas benchmark, GUDA (45.6% Top-1) substantially outperforms Wang et al. (2024) (4.7% Top-1), whose instance-level unlearning approach fails to capture group-level counterfactual effects, and also beats the CLIPA similarity baseline (33.8% Top-1) by 11.8 points on Top-1.

## Relation to Prior Work

GUDA bridges two previously separate research threads—training-data attribution (influence functions, TRAK, DAS, D-TRAK) and machine unlearning for diffusion models (ESD, ReTrack, Forget-Me-Not)—by reframing group-wise attribution as a counterfactual data-removal query that unlearning can approximate. It builds directly on ReTrack's importance-weighted redirection as the unlearning operator for the unconditional setting and on score-matching distillation (Chen et al., 2025) for the shared preservation term, but departs from both prior attribution and unlearning literature by targeting an explicit LOGO counterfactual estimand (ELBO difference) rather than instance-level influence scores or certified deletion. The conditional extension (GUDA-C) introduces weighted style-selection anchors to resolve a condition-distribution mismatch that makes a naive ReTrack extension inapplicable to text-to-image models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× wall-clock speedup over LOGO retraining makes group-level attribution practically feasible for large diffusion models, with direct implications for copyright assessment, fair compensation for artists, and model debugging. A notable negative result is that instance-level methods (TRAK, D-TRAK, Wang et al.'s unlearning-based approach) degrade to near-random group-level rankings, reinforcing that group effects are non-additive and cannot be recovered by aggregating per-instance scores. Open questions include scaling to hundreds or thousands of groups, handling overlapping group memberships, extending beyond artistic styles to other semantic partitions, and whether the ELBO proxy preserves likelihood ordering in more complex latent-space settings.
