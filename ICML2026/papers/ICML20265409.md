## Problem

Quantization-aware fine-tuning (QAF) methods like QLoRA are widely deployed for resource-constrained LLM adaptation, yet they substantially degrade safety alignment far more than full-precision fine-tuning. The paper identifies that this vulnerability stems from two quantization-specific mechanisms—an initial safety shift and a distorted optimization path—that existing safety-aware fine-tuning defenses do not account for, leaving a gap in safe QAF.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (quantized to 4-bit NormalFloat), a fine-tuning dataset mixed with a proportion p of harmful samples from BeaverTails, and a small safety dataset of 128 harmful-prompt/safe-response pairs from AdvBench used to construct the safety matrix. It outputs a 4-bit quantized model with safety-aware LoRA adapters (rank 32) on Q, K, V, O attention matrices, producing text responses at inference. The safety matrix is a d_out × d_out projection matrix derived from SVD of layer activations, requiring only a single forward pass over the safety dataset.

## Method

ExSQF is a two-stage framework that explicitly integrates safety constraints into the QAF pipeline. In the initialization stage, it constructs a safety matrix C_safe = I − U_S U_S^T (projecting onto the orthogonal complement of the top-r_s safety feature subspace extracted via SVD of harmful-prompt activations), then iteratively initializes LoRA adapters by alternating 4-bit quantization of the residual, SVD-based quantization-error decomposition, and safety-constrained projection (B ← C_safe·B) over T=5 iterations. In the refinement stage, after standard LoRA fine-tuning completes, it re-applies the safety projection only to the output (O) matrix's B adapter (B_final ← C_safe·B_final), correcting the distorted optimization path while minimally constraining task performance. The theoretical analysis proves that quantization error ε adds a strictly non-negative safety risk term ΔR_Q = ½ε^T H_safe ε on top of the LoRA risk, and that the interaction term ε^T H_safe δ_θ distorts the update direction, justifying the two-stage correction.

## Results

On SST5 (n=1000, p=0.1), ExSQF achieves a Harmful Score of 12.6 and Finetune Accuracy of 52.8%, compared to QLoRA's 23.4/54.2% and LoftQ's 16.8/52.4%. Averaged across all poison ratios (p=0.1–0.3), ExSQF attains HS=14.4 and FA=51.9%, reducing Harmful Score by 37.9% over QLoRA (52.3) and 15.3% over LoftQ (29.7) while incurring only a 0.7% FA drop. Across three fine-tuning datasets (SST5, AGNEWS, GSM8K), ExSQF achieves the best average HS of 12.6 and the best average FA of 71.1%, surpassing even full-precision LoRA (HS=17.7, FA=69.9). The method generalizes across four model architectures (Llama-3-8B, Llama-2-7B, Qwen-2-7B, Gemma-2-9B), reducing HS by an average of 17.5% over QLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExSQF outperforms all six baselines on Harmful Score while maintaining competitive Finetune Accuracy. Against the primary 4-bit QAF baseline QLoRA, ExSQF reduces HS by 37.9% (14.4 vs 52.3 avg) with only 0.7% FA loss. Against the strongest QAF baseline LoftQ, ExSQF further reduces HS by 15.3% (14.4 vs 29.7) while slightly improving FA by 0.1%. Against the full-precision safety-aware method AsFT, ExSQF still achieves a 4.4% lower HS (14.4 vs 18.8) at a 0.8% FA cost, demonstrating that explicit safety constraints in the quantized pipeline surpass full-precision safety fine-tuning. QPiSSA is the worst-performing QAF method (HS=70.4), with ExSQF reducing its HS by 79.5%. On GSM8K, ExSQF uniquely surpasses full-precision LoRA in FA (76.2% vs 71.3%), suggesting that safety-constrained quantization initialization better preserves multi-step reasoning capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: quantization-aware fine-tuning (QLoRA, LoftQ, QPiSSA) and harmful fine-tuning defenses (SafeLoRA, AsFT, SaLoRA). It explicitly draws on LoftQ's iterative SVD-based quantization error decomposition for adapter initialization but augments it with a safety matrix projection derived from the orthogonal complement of safety-critical feature subspaces. The paper departs from prior QAF work by identifying safety alignment as a previously overlooked failure mode of quantization, and from prior safety defenses by showing that their assumptions break under quantization (Section 5), thereby motivating a dedicated QAF-safety framework rather than a direct transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since QLoRA is the dominant paradigm for deploying LLMs on consumer hardware, the finding that it silently and substantially degrades safety alignment (8.2-point Harmful Score increase vs. LoRA) has immediate practical implications for anyone deploying quantized models in safety-critical settings. The clean theoretical decomposition into an initial safety shift plus a distorted optimization path provides a principled diagnostic framework that could inform future quantization schemes. Key limitations include evaluation only at 4-bit QAF (no PTQ, no 8-bit or 3-bit), and the safety matrix construction relies on a small set of harmful prompts, raising questions about coverage of the full safety feature space. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to post-training quantization, larger models, and whether the safety subspace rank r_s=32 is sufficient for more complex safety properties.
