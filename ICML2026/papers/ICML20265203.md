## Problem

Existing trustworthy multimodal fusion methods rely on the classifier's own prediction confidence to judge data quality, creating a circular dependency that fails when the model is overconfident yet wrong. This paper identifies this flaw and proposes evaluating reliability through latent-space transport geometry instead of statistical confidence scores, providing an independent signal that can flag corrupted or conflicting inputs even when the classifier is fooled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265203/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes heterogeneous multimodal pairs (RGB+depth, image+text, X-ray+text report), encodes each modality into a shared latent space via pre-trained extractors (ResNet-50/152, BERT/RoBERTa), and produces a fused class label along with per-modality fusion weights and a scalar reliability score per modality. The reliability score is the squared initial velocity of a learned Rectified Flow evaluated at the observed latent feature, making it a one-step geometric measurement rather than a multi-step generative reconstruction. Fusion weights are computed from a competition-interaction mechanism that combines intra-modal transport energy (Boltzmann weighting) with cross-modal transport costs (exponential gating), all decoupled from classifier logits via stop-gradient operations.

## Method

GMF instantiates Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge transport via single-step Rectified Flow, where the squared initial velocity ||v_θ(z,0)||² serves as a learned correction score measuring how far an input deviates from the clean latent manifold. Intra-modal flows transport each feature toward a class-agnostic prior to detect sensor noise and missing modalities, while direction-specific cross-modal flows transport one modality's feature toward another's manifold to detect semantic conflicts. The fusion weights are derived as the unique entropy-regularized minimizer of a geometric energy objective (Theorem 4.4), and a Geometric Barrier Principle (Theorem 4.5) proves that conflicting modalities incur transport costs bounded below by (δ−2ε)², exponentially suppressing their contribution. Training uses separated gradient paths: the geometric loss updates only velocity networks, while the evidential task loss and conflict-aware regularizer update only the classification heads, keeping the reliability signal independent of the decision boundary.

## Results

On NYU Depth V2 (RGB-D), GMF achieves 71.9% clean accuracy and maintains 55.2% under severe Gaussian noise (σ=2.0), outperforming the best baseline UAW-EEF (50.2%) by 5.0 points. On UPMC Food-101 (Image-Text), GMF reaches 93.1% clean and 58.7% at σ=2.0, again leading all baselines. Under semantic conflict on MVSA-Single, GMF achieves a 76.8% Safe Rejection Rate and 89.4 CDR (AUROC), roughly doubling the best baseline DBF (35.2%, 71.2). On PneumoniaMNIST, GMF attains 91.2% accuracy with the lowest ECE (0.068) and highest reliability-to-correctness correlation (r=0.78).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMF outperforms all eight baselines across every corruption condition on both NYU Depth V2 and UPMC Food-101. The gap is most pronounced under severe noise (σ=2.0): GMF leads UAW-EEF by 5.0 points (55.2 vs 50.2) on NYU and by 5.6 points (58.7 vs 53.1) on Food-101, while the optimization-based MLA actually degrades below clean performance (35.4% vs 72.1% on NYU). Among evidential/belief-based methods, PDF (ICML'24) and DBF (AISTATS'25) are the strongest statistical baselines, but both still rely on classifier confidence and lose 7–10 points to GMF under σ=2.0 noise. On the semantic conflict benchmark (MVSA-Single), GMF's 76.8% Safe Rejection Rate and 89.4 CDR more than double DBF (35.2%, 71.2) and UAW-EEF (26.8%, 64.5), confirming that geometric transport cost detects conflicts that belief-mass methods miss. On PneumoniaMNIST, GMF achieves the best accuracy (91.2%), the lowest ECE (0.068 vs 0.095 for DBF), and the highest reliability correlation (r=0.78 vs 0.61), indicating superior risk stratification for clinical triage.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMF departs from the dominant confidence-based paradigm in trustworthy multimodal fusion (QMF, PDF, TMC, DBF, UAW-EEF), which derives fusion weights from the classifier's own output entropy or evidential parameters. Instead, it repurposes Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) and the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge framework (De Bortoli et al., NeurIPS 2021) as a geometric reliability oracle: the squared initial velocity of a lightweight two-layer MLP serves as a transport-cost proxy for data quality, decoupled from the decision boundary. The evidential classification head (Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018) is retained but modulated by these geometry-derived weights rather than by statistical confidence, and the fusion mechanism is shown to be the entropy-regularized minimizer of a geometric energy objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode—circular dependency—in a widely used class of methods, and offers a principled geometric alternative with theoretical guarantees (optimality of weights, exponential suppression of conflicting modalities). The 1-step inference at ~18 ms makes the approach practical for real-time deployment, and the low sensitivity to the reference prior (Table 7–8) reduces tuning burden. Key limitations remain: the entire framework inherits the quality of upstream encoders (Assumption 4.1), the semantic-consistency condition in Theorem 4.5 is idealized relative to the training loss, and the conflict protocol relies on synthetic shuffling rather than naturally occurring contradictions. Open questions include whether the geometric barrier generalizes to more than three modalities or to continuous-valued regression tasks, and whether the lightweight velocity network can be replaced by implicit representations to further reduce parameters.
