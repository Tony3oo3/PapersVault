## Problem

Sampling for discrete flow matching (DFM) typically relies on τ-leaping with an Euler approximation of stepwise cumulative intensities, which incurs significant discretization error under limited function evaluations (NFE). This error is amplified near the terminal time, where the schedule-dependent growth term κ̇_t/(1−κ_t) in standard factorized DFM induces severe stiffness. The paper addresses the underexplored question of how to improve DFM sampling quality when the NFE budget is strictly constrained.

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a trained DFM neural network (which outputs coordinate-wise transition intensities g_t^{d,θ}(s;x)), a source distribution (uniform or absorbing state), and optionally a text prompt for text-to-image generation. It produces a discrete token sequence (for text) or a sequence of image tokens (for text-to-image via the FUDOKI backbone). The method operates on a uniform grid in the reparameterized τ-domain and requires exactly one model evaluation per step, with the only additional state being a cached intensity vector from the previous step. A positivity clamp with a floor ε₀ is applied to extrapolated intensities before Poisson event-count sampling.

## Method

TR-CIE combines two components. First, a schedule-based time reparameterization τ(t) = −ln(1−κ_t) maps physical time to a new variable under which the singular growth factor κ̇_t/(1−κ_t) is exactly absorbed by the Jacobian dt/dτ, leaving the reparameterized intensity equal to the raw network output g_t^{d,θ}(s;x) and eliminating terminal stiffness. Second, a cumulative-intensity extrapolation rule approximates the stepwise integral of the reparameterized intensity via a linear (Adams-Bashforth-style) extrapolation that reuses the cached model output from the previous step (τ_{n−1}, x_{n−1}) as a history term, yielding the update Λ̂_{n,d,s} = h_n[(1+r_n/2)û_{n,d,s} − (r_n/2)û_{n−1,d,s}] with step-ratio coefficient r_n = h_n/h_{n−1}. This requires only one NFE per step, matching the cost of standard Euler τ-leaping. Theoretical analysis establishes O(h³) local error for a two-evaluation reference variant and O(h³) + O(h·d_H(x_n, x_{n−1})) for the practical one-evaluation variant, with the global KL divergence decomposed into integration, freezing, and time-variation error terms.

## Results

On masked DFM text generation (GPT-2 perplexity, lower is better), TR-CIE achieves 252.15 at NFE=8, nearly halving the 495.25 of Euler τ-leaping and outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. At NFE=256, TR-CIE reaches 135.92, still ahead of the best baseline (θ-Trapezoidal at 139.35). On text-to-image generation (GenEval accuracy, higher is better), TR-CIE scores 64.27% at NFE=8 versus 60.67% for Euler τ-leaping, and 69.59% at NFE=32 versus 68.71% for θ-Trapezoidal. The gains are most pronounced in the low-NFE regime (8–16), which is the primary target of the method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR-CIE outperforms all six baselines across every NFE budget on both masked and uniform DFM text generation. The advantage is largest at low NFE: at NFE=8, TR-CIE (252.15) nearly halves the perplexity of Euler τ-leaping (495.25) and substantially beats the high-order solvers θ-Trapezoidal (589.63) and θ-RK2 (591.30), which degrade under the halved step count needed to match the 1-NFE budget. FHS and HO-FHS are the closest competitors at high NFE (144.88 and 141.32 at NFE=256), yet TR-CIE still leads by 9 and 5 perplexity points respectively. On GenEval text-to-image accuracy, TR-CIE wins at NFE=8 (64.27% vs. 61.40% Tweedie, 59.03% θ-Trapezoidal) and NFE=32 (69.59% vs. 68.71% θ-Trapezoidal), though θ-RK2 edges it slightly at NFE=4 (54.32% vs. 53.05%). On the synthetic countdown task, TR-CIE achieves the lowest error rate across all NFE, with the gap over baselines widening as NFE decreases. The ablation confirms both components are complementary: TR alone (376.06) and CIE alone (275.87) at NFE=8 each improve over Euler (439.94), but the full TR-CIE (209.84) achieves the largest gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Poisson τ-leaping framework of Gillespie (2001) for simulating CTMCs and the standard factorized DFM rate parameterization of Gat et al. (2024), where the schedule-dependent growth term κ̇_t/(1−κ_t) induces terminal stiffness. It departs from the higher-order discrete diffusion solvers of Ren et al. (2025b) (θ-RK2, θ-Trapezoidal), which achieve second-order convergence but require two NFE per step, by combining a schedule-based time reparameterization (which exactly cancels the singular growth term) with an Adams-Bashforth-style one-step extrapolation that reuses cached model outputs, maintaining a cost of one NFE per step. The method is thus a structured adaptation of classical numerical ODE ideas (variable-step extrapolation, change of variables) to the specific algebraic structure of factorized DFM intensities, rather than a new generative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TR-CIE offers a drop-in, zero-overhead sampler improvement for the most common DFM implementations, with the largest gains precisely in the low-NFE regime (8–32 steps) where practitioners are most constrained. However, the method is scoped to standard factorized DFM with schedules that produce terminal stiffness; for non-factorized parameterizations or milder schedules the cancellation mechanism may not apply, limiting generality. The extreme sensitivity of the positivity clamp ε₀ (Table 11: perplexity jumps from ~210 to ~2500 at ε₀=10⁻⁶) raises practical robustness concerns, and the authors acknowledge the need for a sharp uniform improvement guarantee over Euler and for human evaluation on image generation. Open questions include whether the history-reuse drift term can be further controlled and whether the approach extends to variable-length or non-sparse coordinate-update settings.
