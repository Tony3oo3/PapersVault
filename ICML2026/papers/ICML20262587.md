## Problem

Canonical ratio clipping in PPO/GRPO imposes fixed bounds on the probability ratio, which forces the feasible upward update margin to scale linearly with the old action probability. This structurally nullifies gradient signals for low-probability, positive-advantage tail actions, suppressing exploration and inducing rapid entropy collapse. Existing heuristic fixes (Clip-Higher, DAC) relax bounds ad hoc without a principled geometric foundation, and can violate simplex constraints at high probabilities.

## Input / Output

The method operates within the GRPO training loop: given a math prompt and a group of G sampled responses from the old policy, it computes per-token probability ratios r_t = π_θ(a_t|s_t)/π_old(a_t|s_t) and group-normalized advantages. For each token, the Band operator takes the old-policy probability p = π_old(a_t|s_t) and a single trust-region radius δ, and produces dynamic clipping bounds [r̲, r̄] that replace the fixed [1−ε, 1+ε]. The output is the updated policy parameters θ, with the per-token surrogate objective using the Band-clipped ratio instead of the canonical clip.

## Method

BandPO introduces the Band operator, which projects f-divergence trust regions (e.g., KL, TV, χ²) into probability-aware scalar clipping intervals. The key reduction (Lemma 1) shows that the optimal candidate distribution Q preserving the complement proportions Q(b)/P(b)=c for all b≠a reduces the high-dimensional convex program to a univariate constraint g_f(p,r) = p·f(r) + (1−p)·f((1−rp)/(1−p)) ≤ δ, where r = Q(a)/P(a). The Band bounds are the endpoints of the feasible interval for r, found via bisection (KL) or closed-form (TV, χ²), with simplex saturation handled explicitly. In the GRPO objective, the canonical clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε) is replaced by Band_{f,δ}(r; a, P), governed solely by the interpretable radius δ. Theoretically, the upper bound diverges as p→0 (unlocking tail exploration) while the lower bound converges to 0, and both bounds remain strictly within simplex limits, resolving the exploration bottleneck without heuristic tuning.

## Results

BandPO (GRPO w/ Band_KL,0.05) achieves the best average mean@32 across all four model scales: 40.17% (1.5B), 22.16% (3B), 50.71% (7B), and 47.13% (8B) on AMC2023/AIME2024/AIME2025. The largest gain over canonical GRPO is +5.34 points in mean@32 on the 3B model (22.16 vs. 16.82), with a +10.46-point gain on AMC2023 alone. For pass@32, BandPO leads on 1.5B (61.25 vs. 56.12), 3B (42.32 vs. 31.94), and 8B (67.06 vs. 63.58), while on 7B it is slightly below GRPO (66.05 vs. 66.76) in a near-saturated regime. Wall-clock overhead is minimal: the Band bound computation adds only ~676 ms per step (~0.4% of iteration time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against canonical GRPO (symmetric ε=0.2), BandPO improves average mean@32 by +3.26, +5.34, +1.84, and +2.41 points on 1.5B, 3B, 7B, and 8B respectively, with the 3B gap driven largely by AMC2023 (55.49 vs. 45.03). Against Clip-Higher (DAPO's asymmetric ε+=0.28, ε−=0.2), BandPO gains +0.45, +2.11, +2.41, and +0.80 points in mean@32, showing that simply widening the upper clip is insufficient. Against DCPO-DAC (dynamic-adaptive clipping), BandPO leads by +1.39, +0.80, +1.14, and +0.57 points in mean@32, confirming that heuristic dynamic bounds without a divergence-geometric foundation still leave performance on the table. On pass@32, BandPO wins on 1.5B (+5.13 over GRPO), 3B (+10.38), and 8B (+3.48), but trails GRPO by 0.71 points on 7B where pass@32 is near-saturated (~66–67%). A heuristic relaxation of Band's high-probability bound (Relaxed Band) consistently degrades mean@32 by 0.6–1.9 points, confirming that the tighter head constraint is a beneficial consequence of the trust-region geometry rather than an artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

BandPO sits at the intersection of two well-studied threads: TRPO's f-divergence trust regions (Schulman et al., 2015) and PPO's ratio clipping as a computationally cheap surrogate (Schulman et al., 2017), both of which have been inherited by GRPO and its LLM-RL descendants. Recent adaptive-clipping variants—DAPO's Clip-Higher (Yu et al., 2025) and DCPO's DAC (Yang et al., 2025)—attempt to fix the exploration bottleneck by heuristically widening or dynamically adjusting the fixed bounds, but lack a unifying geometric derivation. BandPO departs from this heuristic lineage by *deriving* the clipping interval as the exact projection of an f-divergence trust region onto the scalar ratio space, reducing the multi-hyperparameter clipping problem to a single interpretable radius δ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing ad-hoc clipping thresholds with a single trust-region radius, BandPO simplifies hyperparameter tuning and provides a theoretically grounded explanation for why fixed bounds suppress tail-action gradients and drive entropy collapse. The finding that heuristically relaxing Band's tighter high-probability constraint consistently degrades performance (Section 6.3) suggests the geometric constraint is doing useful work beyond merely preventing overshoot. However, evaluation is limited to mathematical reasoning with sparse verifiable rewards on models up to 8B parameters; generalization to code generation, denser process-level rewards, and ultra-large models remains open. The paper also notes that alternative bound-computation metrics (e.g., token-level entropy) could yield further gains.
