## Problem

Widely used visual spatial intelligence benchmarks like VSI-Bench suffer from two systematic validity failures: (1) point-cloud-based 3D annotations drift from what is actually visible in video frames, producing incorrect or ambiguous ground-truth answers, and (2) evaluations assume full-scene observability while modern VLMs operate on sparsely sampled frames (16–64), making many questions unanswerable under the model's actual inputs. These issues distort performance comparisons and obscure whether model failures stem from missing visual evidence or deficient spatial reasoning.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes sparsely sampled video frames (16/32/64/all-frame budgets, hierarchically nested via np.linspace) from 381 indoor scenes across five datasets (ScanNet v2, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, 3RScan, MultiScan) at 640×480 or 360×640 resolution, paired with a text question. Outputs are either numerical answers (object counts, distances in meters, object dimensions in centimeters, room areas in square meters) scored via Mean Relative Accuracy, or multiple-choice answers (relative direction, relative distance, route planning) scored by exact match. Uniquely, ground-truth answers are frame-adaptive—each QA pair is regenerated per sampling budget so that answers remain correct under the specific frames the model receives—and dummy-video variants (query-dropped, first-frame-repeated, all-black) provide controlled hallucination probes where the ground truth is deterministically zero.

## Method

ReVSI rebuilds VSI-Bench through three coupled protocols. First, object labels and 3D bounding boxes are re-annotated by domain experts using a custom web interface across 381 scenes, employing a gravity-aligned oriented bounding box algorithm (Algorithm 1) that projects points to the xy-plane, finds the minimum-area 2D convex hull orientation, and aligns the z-axis to gravity, followed by manual refinement; this expands coverage from 3,185 objects/65 labels to 5,365 objects/504 open-vocabulary labels. Second, all QA pairs are regenerated with explicit bias mitigation: single-count queries are reintroduced for common co-occurring categories, cumulative cross-category counting is added, sub-1m distance questions are removed, near-fixed-size object categories are excluded or subsampled, and room boundaries are manually polygon-annotated from top-down views rather than computed via Alpha Shape on noisy reconstructions. Third, a frame-aware evaluation protocol rasterizes sampled frames using ground-truth camera poses, computes per-object 2D pixel coverage (5% threshold), and constructs frame-adaptive QA sets at each budget; visibility metadata enables dummy-video construction where all frames containing the queried object are removed, decoupling scene context from task-relevant evidence to expose hallucination and prior-driven predictions.

## Results

On ReVSI (64-frame setting), proprietary models consistently outperform open-source models: Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest average score of 60.9, followed by Gemini 3 Flash (57.6) and GPT-5.2 (50.9), while the best open-source models are InternVL3.5-38B (54.1) and Qwen3-VL-32B (53.3). This reverses the VSI-Bench trend, where open-source models appeared substantially stronger (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B scored 61.8 on VSI-Bench vs. 53.3 on ReVSI). Fine-tuned specialized models show dramatically smaller gains on ReVSI than on VSI-Bench (e.g., Cambrian-S-7B drops from 67.5 to 49.1; SpaceR-7B drops from 43.5 to 30.5), and several even underperform their base models. Dummy-video stress tests reveal that all fine-tuned models hallucinate near-zero-count answers (0.0–8.1% accuracy where GT=0), while Qwen3-VL-32B achieves 100% on black videos but InternVL3.5-38B scores only 1.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most striking comparison is between proprietary and open-source models: on VSI-Bench, open-source models like Qwen3-VL-32B (61.8) and InternVL3.5-38B (60.8) appeared to rival or exceed proprietary models, but on ReVSI the ranking inverts, with Gemini 3 Pro (60.9) and Gemini 3 Flash (57.6) clearly ahead of all open-source models (best: InternVL3.5-38B at 54.1). The paper attributes the VSI-Bench discrepancy to severe hallucination in open-source models that goes unpenalized under VSI-Bench's biased data (e.g., 53% of counting answers are '2'). Among fine-tuned specialized models, none show the large gains reported on VSI-Bench: Cambrian-S-7B drops from 67.5 to 49.1, VST-7B-SFT from 65.2 to 46.4, and SpaceR-7B from 43.5 to 30.5, with SpaceR-7B actually underperforming its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 33.9) on ReVSI. VLM3R-7B is the strongest fine-tuned model on ReVSI (50.1 avg) but still trails its VSI-Bench score (60.9) by over 10 points. Dummy-video experiments further differentiate models: Qwen3-VL-32B achieves 100% on black videos (correctly answering 0) while InternVL3.5-38B scores only 1.2%, and all fine-tuned models (Cambrian-S, VST, SpaceR, Spatial-MLLM, VLM3R) score below 8.1% on query-dropped videos, exposing catastrophic overfitting to noisy training supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly rebuilds VSI-Bench (Yang et al., CVPR 2025), the most widely adopted benchmark for evaluating VLM spatial reasoning from video, by identifying two systematic validity failures: annotation-to-video ground-truth drift (noisy 3D reconstructions producing incorrect object labels, sizes, and counts) and scene-observability mismatch (questions unanswerable under the sparse frame budgets modern VLMs actually use). It departs from the broader spatial-reasoning benchmark lineage (ScanQA, SQA3D, SPAR-Bench, EmbSpatial-Bench) by enforcing frame-level consistency between model inputs and ground truth, introducing frame-budgeted QA variants (16/32/64/all), and constructing dummy-video controls that decouple scene context from task-relevant visual evidence. The annotation pipeline draws on five real-scanned 3D datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, 3RScan, MultiScan) but replaces their original annotations with expert-verified, gravity-aligned 3D bounding boxes and manually delineated room polygons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes that conclusions drawn from VSI-Bench—particularly the apparent superiority of open-source models and the large gains from 3D-specific fine-tuning—are artifacts of biased evaluation data rather than genuine capability differences. The dummy-video diagnostics reveal that several fine-tuned spatial models (Cambrian-S, Spatial-MLLM, VLM-3R) achieve near-zero accuracy when queried objects are simply absent, indicating their 'spatial reasoning' is largely memorized scene priors rather than visual grounding. A key open question is scalability: the benchmark relies on costly expert-level 3D annotation across 381 scenes, and the authors explicitly flag the need for automated or semi-automated pipelines to generate training-scale spatial supervision. The frame-budgeted evaluation protocol and visibility metadata are likely to become standard practice for any future video-based spatial benchmark.
