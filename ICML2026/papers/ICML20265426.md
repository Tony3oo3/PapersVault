## Problem

Existing samplers for Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) rely on greedy, local certainty heuristics (e.g., confidence, entropy, margin) to select which masked positions to decode next, ignoring how each decoding decision affects uncertainty across all remaining positions. This myopia causes the sampler to commit to locally certain but globally suboptimal tokens, propagating errors and inflating cumulative uncertainty throughout the generation trajectory. The paper asks whether MDMs' bidirectional architecture can be exploited to efficiently evaluate the downstream information gain of a decoding action, enabling globally informed sampling without the expensive multi-step look-ahead required by autoregressive models.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on pre-trained MDMs (no retraining required). It takes a fully masked sequence of length L over a discrete vocabulary (all positions set to the mask token 0) and iteratively unmaskes positions over T decoding steps to produce a complete sequence. For text tasks, the input is a prompt with masked reasoning/code/writing spans, and the output is the generated text (up to 1024 tokens). For image generation, the input is a text prompt and the output is a 512×512 RGB image decoded over 50 steps with a cosine schedule. The sampler is compatible with both full-attention MDMs (Dream-7B) and semi-autoregressive block-diffusion models (SDAR-8B, TraDo-8B) using KV-cache.

## Method

The Info-Gain Sampler replaces greedy certainty-based position selection with an objective J_IG = IG − C that balances the immediate uncertainty cost C (sum of marginal entropy over the positions being decoded) against the information gain IG (reduction in average state uncertainty over all remaining masked positions after the action is applied). The key architectural insight is that MDMs' bidirectional attention allows computing IG for any candidate action in a single forward pass, bypassing the next-token bottleneck that makes look-ahead prohibitively expensive in autoregressive models. At each step, the sampler generates N diverse candidate actions via a two-stage process (token sampling with temperature τ_token, then position sampling via softmax over certainty scores with temperature τ_pos), evaluates all N candidates in parallel through a batched forward pass, and selects the action maximizing J_IG. Efficient implementations include a shared dual-cache strategy (reusing KV states for tokens outside the active block) and a high-confidence bypass that skips full evaluation when token probabilities exceed a threshold γ, reducing practical overhead to ~24% at N=8.

## Results

On the full-attention MDM (Dream-7B-Instruct, K=1), Info-Gain Sampler achieves 62.0% average accuracy across GSM8K, MATH-500, HumanEval, MBPP, Sudoku, and Countdown, surpassing the best greedy baseline PC-Sampler (59.1%) by 2.9 pp and the concurrent look-ahead method LookUM (58.2%) by 3.8 pp, while reducing cumulative entropy from 78.4 to 48.6. On semi-autoregressive MDMs (SDAR-8B-Chat, K=1), the improvement is more pronounced: 66.2% average accuracy vs. 54.6% for LookUM (+11.6 pp). For text-to-image generation, Info-Gain achieves the best GenEval average (58.2) and ImageNet-512 FID (38.1 vs. 43.3 for Confidence), and attains a 62.8% average win rate in creative writing on AlpacaEval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Info-Gain Sampler outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest greedy baseline PC-Sampler (Huang et al., 2025), it gains +2.9 pp average accuracy at K=1 (62.0 vs. 59.1) and reduces cumulative entropy by 38% (48.6 vs. 78.4). Against the concurrent look-ahead method LookUM (Lee et al., 2025), which also evaluates future states, Info-Gain wins by +3.8 pp on full-attention MDMs and by a larger +11.6 pp on semi-AR SDAR-8B-Chat (66.2 vs. 54.6), with the gap driven primarily by code generation (HumanEval: 59.2 vs. 52.3) where the immediate-cost term mitigates local errors. The simpler greedy baselines—Entropy (Ye et al., 2025), Confidence (Chang et al., 2022), Margin (Kim et al., 2025a), and KLASS (Kim et al., 2025b)—are consistently outperformed by 6–10 pp in average accuracy, with the largest gaps on code tasks (e.g., HumanEval: 59.2 vs. 46.4 for Entropy). Uniform sampling (Nie et al., 2025) serves as a weak lower bound, achieving only 27.2% average accuracy. On image generation, Info-Gain improves FID from 43.3 (Confidence) to 38.1 and IS from 53.3 to 63.0, and on creative writing it achieves a 62.8% average win rate, peaking at 80.3% against Entropy at high temperature (τ=1.5, K=2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDM sampling literature by departing from the dominant greedy certainty-based paradigm (Entropy, Confidence, Margin, KLASS, PC-Sampler) and instead formulating decoding as an information-gain optimization problem. It builds on the MDM framework (Austin et al., 2021a) and the efficient block-wise KV-cache strategy of Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025b) for practical deployment. The key conceptual departure from the concurrent LookUM (Lee et al., 2025) is the explicit decomposition of action quality into immediate cost minus future uncertainty reduction, rather than a single look-ahead certainty score, and the observation that MDMs' bidirectional attention makes this estimation tractable in one forward pass—something that would require expensive tree search in autoregressive models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MDMs gain traction as alternatives to autoregressive generation (Dream, SDAR, TraDo, LLaDA), the quality of the inference-time sampler becomes a critical bottleneck; this training-free method offers a drop-in improvement of 2.9–11.6 percentage points across reasoning, coding, planning, and image generation without retraining. The consistent reduction in cumulative entropy (62–66% of the best greedy baseline) and robustness to high sampling temperatures suggest the information-gain objective captures a more principled decoding signal than local certainty. Open questions include whether the N=8 candidate budget is optimal, whether the action sampler's reliance on local heuristics for candidate generation can be improved, and whether the computational overhead (despite batching and cache reuse) remains acceptable at very long sequence lengths or in latency-sensitive deployment settings.
