## Problem

Global-scale audio geolocation—predicting where a recording was made from its acoustic content—remains largely unexplored, especially for natural and wildlife sounds. While image geolocation leverages salient visual cues like landmarks and architecture, audio lacks such explicit geographic markers, making it unclear whether bioacoustic signals (species vocalizations with well-defined geographic ranges) can serve as reliable location cues at a global scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes audio recordings (average ~20 s) converted to 128-bin mel spectrograms, split into 3-second clips with 1.5 s stride and resized to 224×224×3 RGB images. At test time, all clip-level predictions are averaged to produce a single recording-level location embedding, which is matched against a gallery of precomputed GeoCLIP location embeddings (default: 137K training-set locations) to yield (lat, lon) coordinates. An auxiliary head simultaneously predicts a binary species-presence checklist derived from SINR range maps.

## Method

AG-CLIP builds on GeoCLIP's frozen location encoder and adds a MobileNet-V3 audio backbone (pretrained on iNatSounds species classification) with two 2-layer MLP decoders: one projects audio features into GeoCLIP's location embedding space via a contrastive loss, and the other predicts SINR-derived species checklists via a BCE loss (weighted at 0.01). The key insight is that the auxiliary checklist objective encourages the encoder to attend to background ambient sounds and associate them with species likely in that habitat, rather than only foreground vocalizations. At test time, densely sampled 3 s windows are independently geolocated and their predicted location features are averaged before retrieval against the gallery. The paper also introduces species oracles (upper-bound geolocation from full SINR range checklists) and demonstrates that spatiotemporal aggregation of recordings within H3 hexagons over weeks to years substantially improves accuracy by pooling complementary species information.

## Results

On the iNatSounds test set, AG-CLIP achieves a median geolocation error of 1082 km, with 6.4% accuracy at city scale (25 km), 17.2% at region scale (200 km), 41.0% at country scale (750 km), and 71.2% at continent scale (2500 km). The species oracle upper bound (full checklist) reaches 97.8% region accuracy and 100% continent accuracy, confirming that species information is a strong geolocation signal. Spatiotemporal aggregation across a year within 253 km² neighborhoods further improves region accuracy to 30.4% and continent to 88.2%. On the species-rich XCDC benchmark, AG-CLIP achieves 4.3% region and 71.9% continent accuracy, substantially below the oracle's 25.8% region accuracy, highlighting the difficulty of exploiting multi-species soundscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AG-CLIP substantially outperforms both off-the-shelf soundscape mapping baselines on iNatSounds. Against GeoCLAP (Khanal et al., 2023), AG-CLIP reduces median error from 6856 km to 1082 km and improves region accuracy from 1.3% to 17.2% (13×), country from 7.0% to 41.0% (6×), and continent from 24.9% to 71.2% (3×); the paper attributes GeoCLAP's poor performance to domain shift, as it was trained on urban sounds from SoundingEarth. Against Taxabind (Sastry et al., 2025), AG-CLIP reduces median error from 4944 km to 1082 km and improves region accuracy from 2.2% to 17.2% (8×), country from 11.9% to 41.0% (3.4×), and continent from 35.3% to 71.2% (2×); Taxabind's weaker performance is attributed to its reliance on implicit audio–location pairing through images rather than explicit paired data. AG-CLIP also beats the best non-retrieval baselines: hierarchical classification (16.6% region, 68.7% continent) and annotated species ranges (10.8% region, 72.3% continent), with the species-range method slightly edging out AG-CLIP only at the continent level (72.3% vs 71.2%). On the WABAD in-the-wild dataset, AG-CLIP maintains its lead over both GeoCLAP (15.2% continent) and Taxabind (15.8% continent), achieving 45.0% continent accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image geolocation paradigm (retrieval, classification, regression over geographic grids) to the audio modality, building directly on GeoCLIP's contrastive location-embedding framework and adding an auxiliary SINR species-checklist prediction head. It departs from prior audio geolocation work (Pokorny et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2017), which was limited to distinguishing sounds within or among a few urban regions, by formulating the problem at global scale with a bioacoustic focus. The species-range oracle experiments draw on SINR (Cole et al., 2023) for range map prediction, and the retrieval baselines (GeoCLAP, Taxabind) come from the soundscape-mapping literature, which the paper positions as a complementary but distinct task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic benchmark for global-scale bioacoustic geolocation and quantifies a striking gap: species checklists achieve 97.8% region-level accuracy as an oracle, yet the best end-to-end model reaches only 17.2%, revealing that current architectures cannot exploit the multi-species signal in complex soundscapes. The XCDC dawn-chorus dataset and the finding that species-rich recordings actually degrade performance (due to distribution shift and vocal overlap) identify concrete bottlenecks for future work. Broader applications span conservation monitoring, digital forensics (multimodal geo-forensics on film), and privacy, but the low absolute accuracy and geographic bias in iNatSounds mean the task remains substantially harder than its image counterpart.
