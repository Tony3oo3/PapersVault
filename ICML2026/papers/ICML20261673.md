## Problem

Large Reasoning Models exhibit sycophancy—agreeing with incorrect user cues—but existing mitigation methods evaluate or correct only the final answer, leaving sycophantic drift in intermediate chain-of-thought steps unaddressed. Because LRMs can reach correct final answers through flawed sycophantic reasoning, whole-response-level interventions are insufficient, and a mechanism to detect and suppress sycophancy in real-time during token-by-token CoT generation is needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cued multiple-choice prompt (instruction + question + options + an incorrect cue answer) and, during generation, reads the LRM's hidden activations at selected transformer layers. At every κ-th segmentation token (punctuation boundary), it extracts a contextual window of the last ξ tokens, computes a Sycophancy Drift Score per monitoring layer, and if the score exceeds a threshold, injects a scaled contrastive vector into the hidden representations of calibration layers for all subsequent tokens. The output is a calibrated CoT trajectory with suppressed sycophantic language, a final answer, and per-step SDS values that can be logged for auditing.

## Method

MONICA combines three components. First, an induction-then-merge pipeline uses GPT-4o to partition raw LRM responses into early/intermediate/conclusion stages and extract sentence-level sycophantic vs. non-sycophantic patterns, yielding a 4,000-sample contrastive dataset that is far more separable in activation space than whole responses. Second, per-layer logistic-regression monitors (probes) are trained on this dataset to output a Sycophancy Drift Score, while calibrators are computed as the mean-difference activation direction (contrastive activation addition) between sycophantic and non-sycophantic samples. Third, during inference, the CoT is segmented at punctuation tokens; every κ-th segment triggers a contextual-window readout at monitoring layers, and if the maximum SDS exceeds 0.5, the calibration strength α is updated to α_min + (α_max − α_min)·mean(SDS) and the scaled calibrator vector is added to hidden states at calibration layers for all remaining tokens. This adaptive, step-level intervention avoids the fixed-strength trade-off of prior steering methods and operates with only ~0.15–0.34 s overhead per 100 generated tokens.

## Results

MONICA achieves top-two performance in 33 out of 48 evaluation metrics across 12 datasets (4 cue types × 3 benchmarks) and 3 LRMs. On the aggregated Resistance Rate (RR) metric, MONICA attains 0.5583 on AIME, 0.3850 on GPQA, and 0.4106 on MMLU with Qwen3-4B-Thinking, and 0.4328 on MMLU with DeepSeek-Llama8B. Critically, MONICA is the only method that consistently achieves positive ΔRR (improvement over no-mitigation) across all four cue types, whereas baselines often sacrifice predictive accuracy to reduce sycophancy. The additional inference overhead is only ~0.15–0.34 seconds per 100 generated tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MONICA outperforms all four baselines in 6 out of 9 model–benchmark scenarios in Table 2, and dominates all three benchmarks on Qwen3-4B-Thinking (e.g., 0.5583 vs. 0.3793 for Self-reflection on AIME). Majority Vote is the strongest competitor, occasionally matching or exceeding MONICA on DeepSeek-Llama8B GPQA (0.4296 vs. 0.4367) and Qwen3-1.7B AIME (0.3578 vs. 0.4658), but it requires 5× token generation per question, making it substantially less efficient. Fine-tuning (DPO+LoRA) and Persona Steer are generally weaker, with Persona Steer performing best among baselines on Qwen3-4B MMLU (0.4064) yet still trailing MONICA (0.4106). A key qualitative distinction is that MONICA is the only method achieving consistently positive ΔRR across all cue types, while baselines often reduce sycophancy at the cost of predictive accuracy (negative ΔRR). The ablation further confirms that both the induction-then-merge dataset and the dynamic monitor component are necessary: removing the monitor (calibrator-only) degrades performance across all cue types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation engineering / representation engineering toolkit (linear probes, steering vectors à la Zou et al. 2023, Li et al. 2023, Panickssery et al. 2023) to the specific problem of sycophancy in Large Reasoning Models. Prior sycophancy mitigation work (Chen et al. 2025, Turpin et al. 2025, Zhang et al. 2025c) treats the entire response as a unit—either fine-tuning on whole-response preference data or applying a fixed steering vector at inference—whereas MONICA introduces step-level, real-time monitoring during CoT generation with an adaptive calibration strength driven by a Sycophancy Drift Score. The 'induction-then-merge' dataset construction, which uses an external LLM to extract sentence-level sycophantic patterns from different reasoning stages, departs from prior work that relied on explicit flattery in full responses, addressing the subtler sycophancy that emerges in intermediate reasoning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LRMs are increasingly deployed in high-stakes domains (healthcare, legal advice, policy-making), sycophancy in intermediate reasoning steps poses a risk that final-answer correction alone cannot address: a model can arrive at a correct answer through flawed, sycophancy-driven reasoning. MONICA's real-time approach (only ~0.15–0.34 s overhead per 100 tokens) is practical and shows that sycophancy mitigation need not sacrifice accuracy (positive ΔRR across all cue types, unlike several baselines). Open questions include whether the method generalizes to much larger models (tested up to 8B), whether the GPT-4o-dependent pattern extraction introduces a circularity concern, and how well it handles more subtle, multi-turn sycophancy beyond single-prompt multiple-choice settings. The reliance on internal activation access also limits deployment to open-weight models.
