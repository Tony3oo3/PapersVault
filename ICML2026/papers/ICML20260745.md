## Problem

Standard attention applies full-context (global) attention uniformly to every token, incurring quadratic cost even when most tokens only need local context. Prior sparse attention methods reduce cost by selecting which key-value pairs each query attends to, but apply a fixed retrieval budget to every token regardless of whether distant information is actually needed. The paper asks whether a model can learn, token by token, *when* to access long-term memory rather than just *what* to retrieve from it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence of up to 128K tokens and produces standard next-token predictions, but internally each token receives a binary routing decision (d_t ∈ {0,1}) determining whether it invokes global attention over the full context or relies solely on a 4K-token sliding window. The L2A layer is a drop-in replacement for standard attention layers in existing LLMs (Qwen2.5, Qwen3), requiring only ~10% additional parameters from the router and local attention modules, and is trained via continued pre-training at 128K context length.

## Method

L2A replaces each attention layer with a three-module stack: Sliding Window Attention (4K window) computes a local representation, a learned router (linear projection + sigmoid) decides per-token whether global attention is needed, and full causal attention is applied only to tokens with d_t=1. Training augments the standard next-token prediction loss with a sparsity regularizer on the router's continuous scores, using the straight-through estimator to backpropagate through the discrete routing decision; a small Bernoulli sampling probability prevents router collapse by ensuring masked tokens still receive gradient signal from the NTP loss. Custom Triton kernels compact active queries into a contiguous buffer for FA-2-style tiled computation with causal masking via original position indices, achieving up to 10× kernel speedups and ~2× end-to-end training throughput over FlashAttention-2. A post-training pruning strategy removes the global attention module from layers with ≥95% sparsity, reducing KV cache memory by up to 50% with less than 1% performance degradation.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B at 128K context, L2A achieves aggregate long-context performance within 1.5–3% of Continued Long-Context Pre-training (CLP) while skipping Global Attention for ~80% of tokens. L2A outperforms all baselines on most tasks: e.g., Synthetic Recall 79.2 vs. CLP 76.4, ICL 88.6 vs. CLP 87.4, and Passage Re-ranking 9.6 vs. CLP 8.4. Training throughput reaches 1384 tokens/GPU/s (2.15× CLP's 643), and time-to-first-token is ~2× faster than CLP. Post-training layer pruning of ≥95%-sparse Global Attention layers reduces KV cache by up to 50% with <1% performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2A consistently outperforms all five baselines at 128K context. Against training-free methods, L2A beats DCA by 35+ points on Synthetic Recall (79.2 vs. 43.9) and SelfExtend by 53 points (79.2 vs. 25.9), with similar margins on RAG and ICL. Among training-based sparse attention methods, L2A surpasses S²-Attn by 50 points on Synthetic Recall (79.2 vs. 29.0) and 11.6 points on ICL (88.6 vs. 77.0), though S²-Attn achieves slightly higher raw throughput (~1725 vs. ~1384 tokens/GPU/s) at the cost of a 10-point gap from CLP. NSA shows the largest degradation, scoring only 6.2 on Synthetic Recall and 39.2 on RAG at 128K, with throughput of ~565 tokens/GPU/s. FlexPrefill (inference-time sparsity applied to CLP) is the closest competitor, trailing L2A by 9.4 points on Synthetic Recall and 3.4 on ICL, but L2A also matches or exceeds it on RAG (50.0 vs. 49.7) and Re-ranking (9.6 vs. 5.0). Relative to the CLP upper bound, L2A is within 1–3 points on aggregate and actually exceeds CLP on four of seven tasks (Synthetic Recall, ICL, Re-ranking, FCRR), while being ~2× faster to train.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2A departs from the dominant line of sparse attention work (NSA, S²-Attn, Landmark Attention, Expansion Span) that sparsifies *which* key-value pairs each query attends to, by instead learning *when* a token needs global attention at all. Conceptually, it is closest to dynamic routing in LLMs (Mixture-of-Experts, Mixture-of-Depths, D-LLM), but operates at a finer granularity: routing within a single attention layer between local and global computation rather than skipping entire decoder blocks. The kernel design directly extends FlashAttention-2's tiled log-sum-exp scheme to support token-level conditional sparsity, and the training recipe borrows the Straight-Through Estimator from MoE gating to handle the discrete routing decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 2× training throughput gain and 2× TTFT reduction at 128K context with ≤3% performance loss versus full-attention continued pretraining, plus a post-training layer-pruning strategy that cuts KV cache memory by up to 50%. This makes long-context fine-tuning of off-the-shelf LLMs significantly more accessible. Open questions include whether the full KV cache requirement (mitigated but not eliminated by pruning) can be further reduced, whether SSM-based local modules could replace sliding-window attention to avoid the quadratic prefill cost entirely, and whether L2A's router can be combined with sparse KV selection for a two-stage mechanism that reduces both active queries and per-query attention span.
