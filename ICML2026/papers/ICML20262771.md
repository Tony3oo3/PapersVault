## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for LLMs rely on either prohibitively expensive full retraining or aggressive fine-tuning (e.g., gradient ascent on the forget set), both of which cause catastrophic degradation of semantically related knowledge and general linguistic capabilities. The paper asks whether unlearning can be reframed as a precise, targeted knowledge re-mapping problem—overwriting sensitive inputs to a neutral state—without the collateral damage inherent in optimization-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262771/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained LLM's down-projection weight matrices (W_down) at a small set of identified FFN layers, a forget set of (subject, relation, object) triples, a utility set of ~10^5 key-value pairs sampled from Wikidata to anchor general knowledge, and a target neutral state (typically the <EOS> token's MLP output). It produces modified W_down matrices via a single left-multiplication by a computed projection matrix D*, leaving all other parameters untouched. In the multi-sample variant (ZeroUnlearn-GD), the update is additive and applied iteratively via gradient descent over the same constrained subspace.

## Method

ZeroUnlearn reformulates unlearning as a constrained optimization over a multiplicative parameter update (W̃ = DW) rather than the standard additive perturbation. The key mechanism is a three-term objective: a zero term enforcing orthogonality between updated outputs and original sensitive representations, a forget term redirecting sensitive keys to a neutral target, and a utility term preserving general knowledge. By performing SVD on M_f^T and constraining D to the right null space via the projector P = I − VV^T, the zero term vanishes identically, eliminating the trade-off between erasure and capacity. This yields a closed-form solution D* = P(A+W)W^T(W(B+I)W^T)^{-1} for few-shot settings. For multi-sample unlearning, the framework switches to an additive update (W + D_m) with a null-space constraint on K_0, solved via gradient descent (ZeroUnlearn-GD) to avoid the O(d^6) Kronecker-product inversion of the theoretical closed form.

## Results

On the MCF dataset with Llama-3.1, ZeroUnlearn achieves 0.00% Efficacy (complete erasure) while maintaining PPL of 7.77 (vs. 7.47 base) and Specificity of 16.82%, whereas ROME retains 24.40% Efficacy and MEMIT retains 9.60%. On ZsRE with Llama-3.2, ZeroUnlearn reduces Efficacy to 27.85% (vs. 32.82% base) with PPL of 13.08 (vs. 12.88 base), outperforming AlphaEdit (29.59%) and MEMIT (32.32%). In the multi-sample setting (1,000 samples), ZeroUnlearn-GD achieves 0.00% Efficacy on MCF with Llama-3.2 while keeping PPL at 13.05, compared to MEMIT (13.80% Eff.) and AlphaEdit (1.40% Eff.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroUnlearn decisively outperforms all editing-based baselines on unlearning efficacy: on MCF/Llama-3.1 it achieves 0.00% Eff. vs. AlphaEdit (0.20%), MEMIT (9.60%), and ROME (24.40%, essentially unchanged from base). Among optimization-based baselines, GA achieves near-zero Efficacy but catastrophically destroys the model (PPL >1000, Specificity ≈ 0%), and FT similarly collapses Specificity to 0% while inflating PPL (18.25 on MCF/Llama-3.2). AlphaEdit is the strongest editing baseline, reducing MCF Efficacy to 2.60% on Llama-3.2, but ZeroUnlearn still halves this to 0.40% while maintaining comparable PPL. In the multi-sample setting (Table 2), ZeroUnlearn-GD achieves 0.00% Eff. on MCF/Llama-3.2, outperforming AlphaEdit (1.40%) and MEMIT (13.80%), while preserving PPL within 0.17 of the base model. NPO variants (Appendix H) show only marginal improvement over base (e.g., 35.91% vs. 39.66% on RWKU Level 1), whereas ZeroUnlearn reaches 35.70% with stable PPL, confirming that gradient-based unlearning without structural constraints is insufficient for precise erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroUnlearn reframes machine unlearning as a knowledge editing problem, building directly on the key-value memory formulation and causal tracing layer identification from ROME (Meng et al., 2022a) and the multi-edit batch paradigm from MEMIT (Meng et al., 2022b). Its key departure from both editing and unlearning literature is the multiplicative parameter update (left-multiplying W by a projection matrix D) combined with an explicit orthogonality constraint that forces the updated representation to be orthogonal to the original sensitive embedding, going beyond the additive rank-one or least-squares updates of ROME/MEMIT/AlphaEdit. The gradient-based variant (ZeroUnlearn-GD) extends this to multi-sample settings where the closed-form solution becomes computationally intractable (O(d⁶) inversion), positioning it as a practical alternative to optimization-based unlearning methods like GA that cause catastrophic model collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a computationally efficient (sub-second SVD, ~14 GB memory) and theoretically grounded approach to targeted knowledge removal that preserves general model utility, which is critical for compliance-driven deployment of LLMs. However, the advantage is most pronounced in controlled few-shot editing benchmarks (MCF, ZsRE); on the more realistic RWKU benchmark (Appendix H), gains over AlphaEdit and NPO-full are modest, and the closed-form variant degrades noticeably at 500–1000 samples. Open questions include whether the null-space orthogonality constraint generalizes to non-factual unlearning (e.g., behavioral or stylistic unlearning), how the method interacts with multi-layer knowledge redundancy, and whether the requirement for a large utility set (10⁵ Wikidata samples) limits applicability in data-scarce or proprietary settings.
