## Problem

Indirect reciprocity—helping those who have helped others—is difficult to sustain among decentralized, self-interested LLM agents because classic reputation models assume centralized monitoring and static, game-specific norms, while recent LLM-agent works either seed altruistic agents or grant centralized access to others' interactions. It remains unclear whether and how public gossip can enable self-interested agents to build reputations, coordinate social norms, and sustain cooperation without any trusted authority.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its private observation (current environment state and role assignment as actor or witness), a growing private experience memory of past interactions, and a shared public gossip log containing all previously broadcast messages. The action module outputs a decision (binary cooperate/defect in matrix games, a continuous investment or return amount in the trust game, or a product-quality/purchase-type choice in the market), while the gossip module outputs a free-text message (under 150 words) tagged with exactly one of five hierarchical tones—praising, neutral, mocking, complaint, or criticism—along with an internal reflection that is stored in memory for future rounds.

## Method

ALIGN equips each self-interested LLM agent with two prompt-based modules: an action module that conditions decisions on private experience and the public gossip log, and a gossip module that generates open-ended evaluative messages in one of five hierarchical tones after observing a partner's action. The game-theoretic analysis proves that in infinite-horizon repeated donation games, truthful public gossip sustains cooperation as a subgame-perfect equilibrium whenever the discount factor satisfies γ ≥ c/b, extending the classic image-scoring result to an imperfect-monitoring setting. The five-tone gossip protocol is the key design choice: it conveys not only the observed action but also the witness's personal judgment, enabling the community to align on normative expectations and using negative tones as a cost-free verbal punishment that raises the perceived future cost of defection. Ablations confirm that replacing this open-ended hierarchical gossip with binary reputation signals (with or without a shared convention) substantially reduces cooperation, because binary labels lack the normative context needed for robust indirect reciprocity. No fine-tuning or training is involved; all behavior emerges from strategic reasoning over accumulated gossip and experience, with a reflection step allowing agents to adapt their strategies over time.

## Results

In the infinite-horizon repeated donation game, ALIGN agents achieve dramatically higher cooperation and welfare than non-gossiping baselines. Reasoning models (DeepSeek-V3.1 Reasoner, o4-mini) jump from 0% cooperation and 0 discounted return without gossip to 98–100% cooperation and 15.1–15.4 discounted return with ALIGN. Chat models also improve substantially: GPT-4o Mini goes from 36% cooperation (5.55 discounted return) to 99% (15.23), and Gini coefficients drop from 0.63–0.73 to 0.02–0.08, indicating near-equal welfare distribution. ALIGN also resists malicious entrants: cooperation toward a greedy defector decreases as negative gossip propagates, and most LLMs achieve positive utility gaps over collusive gossipers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is non-gossiping agents with identical action modules (same LLM, same prompts, but no public gossip channel). ALIGN wins decisively across all 8 LLMs: reasoning models that defect universally without gossip (0% cooperation, 0 discounted return) achieve 69–100% cooperation and 10.7–15.4 discounted return with ALIGN. Among chat models, GPT-4o Mini improves from 36% to 99% cooperation, while Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite (the weakest performer) still improves from 8% to 60%. The Gini coefficient drops from 0.63–0.73 (highly unequal) to 0.02–0.08 (near-equal) for most models, showing that gossip not only increases total welfare but also distributes it more evenly. In robustness tests, ALIGN agents reduce cooperation toward a greedy defector as negative gossip accumulates, and most LLMs maintain positive utility gaps over collusive attackers who spread false criticism, confirming that cross-validation of gossip against private experience provides defense against coordinated manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical game-theoretic framework of indirect reciprocity (Nowak & Sigmund's repeated donation game, image scoring, and Ohtsuki & Iwasa's leading eight norms) by introducing a public gossip channel as an imperfect monitoring mechanism and proving that cooperation equilibria persist under this relaxed assumption. It departs from recent LLM-agent reputation work (Ren et al., 2025; Vallinder & Hughes, 2024) by requiring fully decentralized, self-interested agents without seeded altruism or centralized observability, and by replacing binary reputation labels with open-ended hierarchical gossip in five tones. The ALIGN architecture is novel in combining a gossip module and an action module with reflection memory, but its game-theoretic backbone is a direct extension of the grim-trigger analysis in the repeated donation game literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges a gap between theoretical indirect reciprocity models (which assume centralized monitoring or static norms) and practical decentralized LLM-agent ecosystems, showing that linguistic gossip can sustain cooperation without a trusted authority. A key open question it raises is whether these findings generalize beyond the four structured testbeds to open-ended, large-scale multi-agent deployments where gossip channels are noisy, adversarial, and unbounded. The authors also flag ethical concerns about reputation-based ostracism, echo chambers, and malicious defamation in real-world agentic societies, suggesting that responsible design of gossip protocols (transparency, veracity verification) is an active area for future work.
