## Problem

In QoS networks, routers must schedule packets with hard deadlines while packet weights (importance scores) are only revealed after processing, creating a fundamental tension between online scheduling and reward estimation. The paper introduces the K-OPSD problem, where packets belong to K stochastic types, and asks whether α-no-regret algorithms exist in this setting. A key open question is whether the finite-K assumption can break the well-established golden-ratio Φ competitive ratio barrier in 2-bounded instances.

## Input / Output

At each discrete time slot t, the scheduler observes a set of arriving packets (each with arrival time, deadline, and type from [K]) plus the current buffer state, but the actual weight of any packet is only revealed if it is scheduled in that slot. The algorithm outputs a single scheduling decision per slot (which buffered packet to transmit). Performance is measured via α-regret R_{α,T} = E[G_OPT] − α·E[G_ALG], where OPT uses true mean weights, and the goal is sublinear regret (α-no-regret) combined with the tightest possible competitive ratio α.

## Method

The paper develops both deterministic and randomized algorithms by combining bandit confidence bounds with thresholding scheduling rules. For deterministic algorithms, EDF_Φ^L applies UCB optimism to the β-EDF thresholding rule, while ALG^θ introduces a dynamic threshold sequence (solving a system of K+1 linear equations) with an epoch-based restart mechanism that achieves θ_K < Φ competitive ratio in 2-bounded instances; ALG^θ,U extends this to the learning setting by replacing true weights with UCBs. For randomized algorithms, ALG^R2 and ALG^Rs use both UCBs and LCBs to handle ratios of unknown quantities, achieving 5/4-regret and e/(e-1)-regret respectively. All algorithms attain Õ(√KT) regret bounds matching the sleeping-bandit lower bound, and a three-instance construction with signed perturbations provides a matching Ω(√T) lower bound for θ_K-regret.

## Results

All proposed algorithms achieve an α-regret upper bound of Õ(√KT), matching the information-theoretic lower bound for the standard sleeping bandit setting. In the 2-bounded deterministic setting, ALG^θ breaks the long-standing golden-ratio barrier, achieving a competitive ratio θ_K ∈ [√2, Φ) for finite K (e.g., θ_2 = √2 ≈ 1.41, θ_3 = 1.5), with a matching Ω(√T) lower bound proving near-optimality. For randomized algorithms, ALG^R2 achieves the tight 5/4 competitive ratio in 2-bounded instances, and ALG^Rs achieves e/(e−1) ≈ 1.582 for arbitrary s-bounded instances, both with Õ(√KT) regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the sleeping bandit baseline (Kleinberg et al., 2010 / AUER), the paper's 1-bounded K-OPSD results exactly match the Õ(√KT) regret bound, confirming that K-OPSD generalizes sleeping bandits without additional cost. Against the classic Φ-competitive EDF_β algorithm (Kesselman et al., 2001), the paper strictly improves the 2-bounded deterministic competitive ratio from Φ ≈ 1.618 to θ_K < Φ whenever K is finite (e.g., √2 for K=2), while maintaining the same Õ(√KT) regret rate. The Hajek (2001) lower bound construction is adapted to yield a matching Ω(√T) lower bound for θ_K-regret, establishing near-tightness. For randomized algorithms, the paper matches the 5/4 competitive ratio of Chin et al. (2006) in the 2-bounded case and the e/(e−1) ratio for unbounded s, both with Õ(√KT) regret—no prior work combined these competitive ratios with learning guarantees. The Vesely et al. (2022) unbounded deterministic Φ result is not improved in the learning setting (marked N.A. in Table 1), leaving a gap for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online packet scheduling with deadlines (OPSD, studied since Kesselman et al. 2001 and Hajek 2001) and stochastic bandit learning (specifically sleeping bandits, Kleinberg et al. 2010). It introduces the K-OPSD problem, which combines the deadline-constrained scheduling structure of OPSD with the partial-feedback learning structure of sleeping bandits, where packet weights are unknown and must be estimated through UCB/LCB confidence bounds. The deterministic algorithms build directly on the EDF_β thresholding family and the AUER sleeping-bandit algorithm, while the randomized algorithms adapt the potential-function analysis of Bienkowski et al. 2011. The confidence bound construction follows Levy et al. 2024, and the competitive ratio analysis for ALG^θ reuses the system of equations from Hajek's Φ lower-bound construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to rigorously connect bandit learning with online packet scheduling under deadlines, establishing that K-OPSD strictly generalizes sleeping bandits and providing tight α-regret bounds of Õ(√KT) across multiple slackness regimes. The most impactful result is breaking the long-standing Φ ≈ 1.618 competitive ratio barrier for 2-bounded scheduling when the number of packet types K is finite, achieving θ_K ∈ [√2, Φ) — a result that is tight and of independent interest for the scheduling literature. Open questions include whether randomized algorithms can similarly exploit finite K to beat the 5/4 barrier, whether tight Φ-regret can be achieved for general (unbounded) instances, and whether the framework extends to contextual or linear bandit structures. The practical motivation (QoS buffer management, real-time ad auctions) is compelling but the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation.
