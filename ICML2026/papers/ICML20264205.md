## Problem

Standard RL for LLM post-training relies on a single scalar reward per rollout, providing extremely sparse learning signal that is insufficient when the base model cannot yet solve the task. Distillation offers dense supervision but requires costly expert demonstrations that don't scale to frontier models. The paper identifies that naive multi-turn RL with text feedback improves second-turn revision ability but fails to internalize feedback into first-turn competence, leaving a gap between training-time feedback access and test-time single-turn performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a text response as output. During training, after each generation the model receives a natural-language critique from a feedback provider (simulated by Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507), which is appended to form an extended prompt for a second-turn revision. Critically, this feedback is available only during training; at inference time the model must produce a good response on the first turn without any external critique. The feedback is semantically rich text (localizing errors, naming violated constraints, suggesting fixes) rather than a scalar or binary correctness label.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary methods for RL from Text Feedback (RLTF). RLTF-SD (Self Distillation) treats the policy's own feedback-conditioned second-turn generations as implicit teacher demonstrations, distilling them into the single-turn policy via an advantage-weighted regression objective with a first-turn mean-reward baseline; this design avoids the 'gradient-signal collapse' failure mode of second-turn baselines (where updates vanish when the teacher is consistently correct) and removes importance weighting to control variance. RLTF-FM (Feedback Modeling) adds an auxiliary cross-entropy loss that trains the policy to predict the feedback provider's critiques, supplying dense token-level supervision that improves representation conditioning in directions weakly identified by sparse reward alone. Theoretically, the paper shows reward-only RL under base rollouts suffers from rare-event estimation bottlenecks and geometric concentration of the gradient signal, while feedback modeling acts as a representation preconditioner. Empirically, both methods consistently outperform GRPO (single- and multi-turn), Feedback Descent, and Rejection Sampling across reasoning puzzles, competition math, and creative writing benchmarks.

## Results

On reasoning puzzles, RLTF-SD and RLTF-FM achieve single-turn accuracy of 0.802/0.880 on Knights and Knaves, 0.976/0.978 on Binary Matrix, and 0.830/0.905 on Shortest Path, substantially outperforming GRPO single-turn (0.373/0.125/0.385) and GRPO multi-turn (0.352/0.950/0.384). On competition math (MATH500 trained on DeepMath), RLTF-FM reaches 0.636 mean@32 accuracy versus 0.578 for GRPO multi-turn and 0.424 for Feedback Descent. On creative writing (LitBench), RLTF-SD scores 8.80/10 versus 6.83 for GRPO single-turn and 8.25 for Feedback Descent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), both RLTF-SD and RLTF-FM consistently outperform on all nine task configurations. A striking finding is that GRPO multi-turn training yields single-turn performance nearly identical to GRPO single-turn (e.g., 0.352 vs. 0.373 on Knights and Knaves), confirming that naively treating feedback as additional context does not internalize its learning signal. Against Feedback Descent (Lee et al., 2025), which optimizes in text space via pairwise comparisons, RLTF methods win on all reasoning tasks (e.g., 0.880 vs. 0.055 on Knights and Knaves) and most math tasks (0.636 vs. 0.424 on MATH500/DeepMath), though Feedback Descent is competitive on LitBench (8.25 vs. 8.80 for RLTF-SD). Against Rejection Sampling (Scheurer et al., 2023), which SFTs only correct second-turn generations, RLTF-SD outperforms it in ablation experiments (Figure 2), demonstrating the benefit of variance reduction via baselines over pure likelihood maximization on accepted samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes RL from Text Feedback (RLTF) as a distinct problem between sparse scalar-reward RL and dense demonstration-based distillation, building on the self-distillation paradigm (Snell et al., 2022) where a model learns from its own privileged-information-conditioned outputs, and on multi-turn RL interaction protocols (Zhou et al., 2024; Shani et al., 2024). The Self Distillation method repurposes advantage-weighted regression (Peng et al., 2019) for distilling feedback-conditioned rollouts into the single-turn policy, while Feedback Modeling introduces a novel auxiliary objective of predicting the critique itself. The paper explicitly departs from naive multi-turn RL (which improves second-turn but not first-turn performance) and from text-space optimization methods like Feedback Descent (Lee et al., 2025), arguing that parameter-space optimization is necessary to internalize feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As text feedback becomes increasingly abundant through human-AI interaction, automated judges, and tool-mediated workflows, RLTF offers a scalable middle ground that avoids both the information poverty of scalar rewards and the cost of expert demonstrations. The gradient-signal collapse analysis reveals a practical pitfall in standard GRPO-style baselines for multi-turn settings that practitioners are likely to encounter. Open questions include handling noisy or adversarial feedback, extending to truly long-horizon interactions, and understanding the interplay with process reward models; the theory is also limited to a frozen-rollout (batch) regime near the base policy, leaving the full online dynamics uncharacterized.
