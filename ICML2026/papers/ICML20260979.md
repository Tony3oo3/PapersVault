## Problem

Decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning faces a persistent exploration-coordination tension: intrinsic rewards promote exploration under sparse feedback, yet effective cooperation requires agents' behaviors to remain consistent over a limited communication graph. Existing methods couple exploration bonuses and coordination regularizers through fixed coefficients or handcrafted schedules, making performance brittle and prone to either fragmented conventions or premature behavioral collapse. No prior work has directly coupled information-driven exploration with a topology-aware notion of behavioral compatibility through a single constrained objective in fully decentralized MARL.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation o_t^i and one-hop neighbor action distributions (or logits) of size |A| over a possibly time-varying communication graph with edge dropout; the local information state h_t^i is an RNN hidden state summarizing observation, action, and message history. The method outputs decentralized per-agent stochastic policies π_i(·|h_t^i) that produce coordinated team actions, with the consensus constraint computed locally via Jensen-Shannon divergence between each agent's policy and a weighted average of its active neighbors' policies. Communication cost is O(|E_t||A|) per step, and the entire framework operates as a reward-level modification on top of any standard policy-gradient learner.

## Method

IEC casts the exploration-coordination trade-off as a constrained optimization problem: maximize extrinsic return augmented with two exploration signals (ensemble dynamics-prediction disagreement for epistemic uncertainty and count-based state-coverage novelty) subject to a graph-induced policy disagreement budget C(π;G) ≤ δ, where C is a Jensen-Shannon-divergence-based neighbor mismatch that admits a Dirichlet-energy interpretation on the communication graph. The constrained problem is solved via Lagrangian relaxation with a lightweight primal-dual update: the augmented reward r_IEC = r_ext + α_info·r_info + α_nov·r_nov − λ·r_con is fed to a standard PPO/IPPO learner, while the nonnegative multiplier λ is updated by projected ascent λ ← Π_[0,λ_max](λ + η_λ(Ĉ − δ_k)), acting as a dynamic shadow price of consensus. When disagreement exceeds the budget, λ increases and strengthens coordination pressure; when agents remain aligned, λ relaxes and preserves exploration capacity. The framework is learner-agnostic (only reshaping rewards), reduces to fixed-weight reward shaping when the dual update is disabled, and is supported by theoretical results including KKT conditions at fixed points, a Pinsker-type bound linking the constraint to total-variation mismatch, and a proof that fixed-weight scalarization is structurally brittle on phase-dependent tasks.

## Results

Across three benchmarks, IEC consistently outperforms baselines, with the largest gains in high-coordination settings. On GridWorld MultiRoom (deep dependency chains), IEC improves win rate by ~7% over MACE at N=128. On Overcooked Large (spatial bottlenecks), IEC outperforms MACE by over 20% in WR at N=128 and maintains ~87% WR even at N=8. On SMAC 8m (large-scale combat), IEC achieves >87% WR across all configurations while IPPO collapses to ~0% WR, and IEC edges out MACE (87.5% vs. 84.5% at N=8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IEC is compared against three main baselines: MACE (Jiang et al., 2024a), a decentralized novelty-sharing exploration method; IPPO (De Witt et al., 2020), independent PPO without coordination; and MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022), a CTDE baseline. IEC wins decisively over IPPO on all benchmarks, with the gap most dramatic on SMAC 8m where IPPO achieves ~0% WR while IEC exceeds 87%. Against MACE, IEC gains ~7% WR on GridWorld MultiRoom, over 20% on Overcooked Large, and ~3% on SMAC 8m (87.5% vs. 84.5% at N=8), with the advantage growing as coordination demands increase. Against MAPPO (CTDE), IEC matches or exceeds performance while operating fully decentralized, confirming that the spectral consensus constraint compensates for the absence of a centralized critic. The ablation further shows that removing the adaptive dual update (IEC-W/o-adapt) costs 5–10% WR, validating that the primal-dual mechanism—not just the constraint itself—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

IEC builds on the PPO/IPPO policy-gradient framework (Schulman et al., 2017; De Witt et al., 2020) as a learner-agnostic base, and repurposes two well-studied ideas—ensemble-based intrinsic motivation (ICM/VIME lineage) and constrained RL via Lagrangian relaxation (CPO lineage)—into a single coupled objective for decentralized MARL. The key conceptual departure is treating the exploration-coordination tension not as a fixed-weight mixture of heuristics but as a constrained spectral optimization problem, where the consensus constraint is a Jensen-Shannon divergence over the communication graph interpreted as a Dirichlet-energy regularizer, and the dual multiplier adapts via projected ascent on observed violations. This positions IEC between the intrinsic-motivation MARL literature (MAVEN, MACE) and the constrained-optimization literature, bridging them through a topology-aware spectral constraint that neither family had previously combined in a fully decentralized setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primal-dual formulation offers a principled, self-tuning alternative to hand-crafted weight schedules for balancing exploration and coordination, which is a persistent practical pain point in decentralized MARL. The Dirichlet-energy interpretation connects MARL policy consistency to graph signal processing, opening a path to richer spectral tools (e.g., frequency-selective coordination, multi-scale smoothness). Limitations include the Dirichlet-energy approximation requiring neighbor policies to be close in logit space (weaker early in training), the need for one-hop communication even under dropout, and theoretical guarantees limited to fixed-point KKT conditions without explicit convergence rates. Open questions include scalability to very large agent counts with dynamic topologies, extension to competitive or mixed-motive settings, and whether the spectral constraint can be replaced by learned graph structures.
