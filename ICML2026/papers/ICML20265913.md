## Problem

Expressive generative policies (diffusion, flow matching) can represent the multimodal action distributions needed for complex control, but they are fragile in online RL due to intractable likelihoods and gradients that must propagate through long sampling chains under non-stationary data. Unimodal Gaussian policies are stable to optimize but suffer from mode-covering, placing probability mass in low-reward regions. The paper asks whether one can design an online RL framework that retains generative expressiveness while keeping policy optimization tractable and stable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state observations (9–24 dimensional for DMControl tasks, 11–17 for Gym locomotion) and outputs continuous actions (2–21 dimensional) squashed to [−1, 1] via tanh. Structurally, the state is first mapped by a Gaussian encoder to a latent variable whose dimensionality matches the action dimension, and the latent is then decoded to an action by a conditional generative model (diffusion or flow matching) conditioned on the state. During rollouts, the decoder operates deterministically (DDIM τ=0 or ODE integration), while during refinement it is trained with stochastic latent inputs drawn from a fixed N(0, I) prior.

## Method

GORL factorizes the policy as π(a|s) = ∫ π_θ(ε|s) π_φ(a|s,ε) dε, confining all RL gradient updates to the tractable latent encoder π_θ while delegating expressive action synthesis to a likelihood-free generative decoder g_φ(s,ε). Training follows a two-timescale alternating schedule: Phase 1 optimizes the encoder via standard policy gradients (PPO or SAC) with the decoder frozen, and Phase 2 refines the decoder via supervised diffusion/flow-matching loss on fresh rollouts, with latent inputs anchored to a fixed N(0, I) prior to break the self-reconstruction feedback loop. After each decoder update, the encoder is re-initialized to N(0, I) to match the decoder's training support, preventing misalignment with the new transport map. The framework is algorithm-agnostic (compatible with any on/off-policy optimizer and any conditional generative architecture) and is theoretically grounded by an unbiased latent policy gradient lemma and a trust-region-style performance bound under bounded latent divergence.

## Results

On the six-task DMControl benchmark (180M steps, 5 seeds), GORL-Diff achieves the highest final episodic return on all six tasks, with headline results of 874.63 on HopperStand (vs. 286.09 for Gaussian PPO, a >3× margin) and 908.96 on WalkerWalk. On the 21-dimensional humanoid tasks, GORL-Diff reaches 976.10 on HumanoidStand and 326.30 on HumanoidRun, exceeding the strongest non-GORL baseline (Gaussian PPO at 74.97 and 17.39) by over an order of magnitude. GORL-FM is competitive, leading on FingerSpin (903.92) and FishSwim (641.01), while both GORL variants substantially outperform the generative baselines FPO and DPPO, which collapse to near-zero returns on HopperStand and WalkerWalk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GORL decisively outperforms Gaussian PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) on all six DMControl tasks and both humanoid tasks; the gap is most dramatic on HopperStand (874.63 vs. 286.09) and HumanoidStand (976.10 vs. 74.97), where the unimodal policy cannot represent the multimodal action distributions needed for high-reward strategies. Against FPO (McAllister et al., 2025), GORL wins on every task, and FPO exhibits severe late-stage collapse—dropping to 3.94 on HopperStand and 29.00 on WalkerWalk—attributed to misalignment between its flow-matching surrogate and PPO's likelihood-ratio constraint under distribution shift. DPPO (Ren et al., 2024) is also consistently outperformed, with particularly poor results on HopperStand (2.14) and FishSwim (143.52), suggesting that modeling the denoising chain as an MDP does not resolve the fundamental optimization fragility of generative policies in the online setting. In the off-policy compatibility check (Appendix F.2), GORL instantiated with SAC and a diffusion decoder matches or exceeds standard Gaussian SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) on Hopper-v2, Walker2D-v2, and HalfCheetah-v2, confirming the framework is not restricted to on-policy optimizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

GORL builds on the generative modeling foundations of diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020) and conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as policy parameterizations, but departs sharply from prior online generative RL methods (FPO, DPPO) that attempt to backpropagate RL gradients through the generative sampling chain. It also contrasts with DSRL, which freezes a pretrained diffusion backbone and optimizes only a latent steering policy, limiting expressiveness to the fixed generator's support. GORL's key conceptual departure is the structural decoupling of optimization from generation: confining all RL gradient updates to a tractable latent Gaussian policy while training the generative decoder separately via supervised objectives anchored to a fixed prior, using a two-timescale alternating schedule with stage-wise re-initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework resolves a long-standing tension in continuous-control RL—stable optimization versus multimodal expressiveness—with a clean, algorithm-agnostic recipe that works with both on-policy (PPO) and off-policy (SAC) optimizers and both diffusion and flow-matching decoders. The >3× gain on HopperStand and >10× gain on 21-DoF humanoid tasks, supported by ablations isolating the fixed-prior anchoring and stage-wise re-initialization mechanisms, make this a practically impactful contribution for anyone deploying generative policies online. Open questions include adaptive stage triggering (currently fixed a priori), latent dimensionality choices beyond matching action dimension, scaling to visual observations and higher-DoF systems, and the ~1.87× wall-clock overhead from periodic decoder refinement.
