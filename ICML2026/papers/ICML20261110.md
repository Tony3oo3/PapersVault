## Problem

Multimodal learning suffers from imbalanced optimization where strong modalities dominate training and suppress lazy ones, yet existing remedies (gradient modulation, conflict alleviation) operate within monolithic frameworks that couple all modalities under a single optimization gradient and a single temporal scale. This entangled design makes cross-modal interference inevitable and prevents independent exploitation of modality-specific semantics, capping joint learning performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261110/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—audio clips, video frames (3 frames sampled per clip), RGB images, text descriptions (GloVe embeddings + Bi-GRU), and 3D point clouds—each processed by a modality-specific encoder (ResNet18, DGCNN, or CNN). Beyond the final class label, the method also reports unimodal recognition accuracy by feeding a single modality with zero vectors for the rest, enabling evaluation of per-modality semantic exploitation. The fusion module is a 3-layer MLP cascade (CCMS) rather than a single concatenation or attention layer.

## Method

MoNet reformulates multimodal learning under the Nested Learning paradigm into two nested sub-processes. The outer level (DMSM) treats each modality encoder and the joint classifier as independent associative memories optimized by separate momentum-SGD optimizers, decoupling the entangled training objective so that each modality learns task-relevant features without cross-modal gradient interference. The inner level (AMCF) implements a Coordinated Continuum Memory System (CCMS): a 3-layer MLP cascade at distinct update frequencies that receives the outer-level classification error signal, decomposes it via dual EMA filters into swift, consensus, and steady components, and routes each to a different layer—slowing dominant modality signals while accelerating underleveraged ones. The CCMS is optimized with DeepMomentum (a deeper nested optimizer), and the entire system is trained end-to-end under supervised cross-entropy loss without any explicit modality weighting or heuristic balancing.

## Results

MoNet achieves the best fusion recognition accuracy across all eight datasets spanning three tasks. On audio-visual recognition, it reaches 83.11% on CREMA-D (vs. 79.40% for the best baseline DGL), 71.23% on AVE, 93.06% on AVSBench, and 60.90% on VGGSound50. On image-text and 2D-3D tasks, it attains 74.25% on MVSA, 90.42% on FOOD101, 98.47% on 3D MNIST, and 92.50% on ModelNet. Notably, MoNet also improves unimodal accuracy simultaneously (e.g., video accuracy on CREMA-D rises from 72.96% under DGL to 74.33%), demonstrating that decoupled optimization benefits both individual modalities and their fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoNet outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset in fusion accuracy. The largest gains are over gradient-modulation methods: OGM-GE (76.43% on CREMA-D) and AGM (65.68%), where MoNet improves by 6.7 and 17.4 points respectively, indicating that heuristic gradient suppression is insufficient. Among the more recent conflict-alleviation methods, DGL (79.40% on CREMA-D, 91.98% on AVSBench) and ARL (78.87%, 91.94%) are the strongest competitors, but MoNet still leads by 3.7 and 1.1 points on CREMA-D and AVSBench. InfoReg (74.77% on CREMA-D, 69.89% on AVE) and MMPareto (69.53%, 69.73%) trail MoNet by 8.3 and 1.5 points on CREMA-D and AVE. On the 2D-3D and image-text tasks (Table 2), MoNet similarly leads DGL by 0.61 points on ModelNet and InfoReg by 0.20 points on MVSA, with the most margin on FOOD101 (90.42% vs. 89.30% for MLA). A distinguishing property is that MoNet simultaneously improves unimodal accuracy (e.g., video on CREMA-D: 74.33% vs. 72.96% for DGL), whereas baselines like DGL sacrifice fusion performance to boost individual modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Nested Learning (NL) paradigm introduced by Behrouz et al. (NeurIPS 2025), which models learning as hierarchical associative memories operating at different temporal scales, and repurposes its Continuum Memory System (CMS) and Deep Momentum Gradient Descent as core algorithmic components. It departs from the dominant line of work on imbalanced multimodal learning (OGM-GE, AGM, MMCosine, DGL, ARL, InfoReg) that addresses modality imbalance through gradient modulation or conflict alleviation within a monolithic optimization loop. The key conceptual departure is restructuring the entire multimodal training process into nested sub-processes: an outer Decoupled Multimodal Stable Memory (DMSM) that runs independent per-modality optimization streams, and an inner Adaptive Multimodal Coordinated Fusion (AMCF) that performs multi-timescale error-signal decomposition for balanced fusion, rather than attempting to balance a single entangled gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the NL paradigm to multimodal learning, this paper offers a principled architectural alternative to the growing zoo of heuristic gradient-modulation methods, suggesting that the root cause of modality imbalance is the monolithic optimization structure itself rather than the gradient magnitudes. The consistent improvements across eight datasets spanning audio-visual, image-text, and 2D-3D tasks, with simultaneous gains in both unimodal and fusion accuracy, validate the decoupling-and-coordination design. However, the method adds considerable architectural complexity (independent optimizers per module, EMA-based signal decomposition, multi-frequency CMS layers), and the authors acknowledge this is an initial exploration whose broader applicability to more complex multimodal tasks (e.g., generation, retrieval, open-vocabulary) remains untested. Open questions include whether the NL framing generalizes to more than two modalities, to transformer-based backbones, and to settings where modalities are partially missing.
