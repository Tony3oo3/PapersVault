## Problem

Molecular dynamics is inherently serial because each timestep depends on the previous one, and machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) are much slower per force call than classical force fields, making long simulations computationally infeasible. While speculative sampling has been applied to language models and diffusion models, no method existed to accelerate second-order Langevin MD while provably preserving the target model's trajectory distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262208/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LSD takes an initial (q, p) state, a fast draft force field (e.g., Orb-v3 or EMT), a slower target force field (e.g., UMA-S or UMA-M), and Langevin parameters (T, γ, Δt) as input. It outputs a sequence of verified (q_n, p_n) states whose joint distribution is identical to sequential target-model sampling, with the guarantee holding for arbitrary draft models including non-conservative ones. The draft and target are separate force field models evaluated on the same positions, and the only difference between their integrator steps is the force-dependent momentum mean.

## Method

LSD uses a fast draft model to propose ABOBA integrator steps, which are verified in parallel by a pool of target model instances using a reflection-maximal coupling on the momentum Gaussians. The key insight is that the (BOB) momentum sub-step produces a Gaussian whose covariance Σ is force-independent, so draft and target differ only in mean, enabling a closed-form acceptance/rejection test with reflection of rejected samples to the equal-likelihood hyperplane. Theorem 3.1 (pre-/post-processing) proves this momentum coupling extends to the full (ABOBA) step with optimal acceptance rate, since the (A) position update is invertible and force-independent. A pipelined asynchronous protocol replaces the synchronous draft-then-verify loop, and speculative error correction subtracts the most recent verified force error from the draft prediction to reduce rejection rates by up to 75%.

## Results

LSD achieves error-free speedups of 3–9× across draft-target combinations on FCC copper, with the theoretical upper bound speedup ≲ 1/(c + ⟨β⟩) tightly matching measured values (Fig. 5). Speculative error correction (EC) reduces rejection rates by up to 75%, making high-friction (τ = 1 ps) simulations practically viable. For distributional correctness, LSD corrects the 42.8 K excess heating of a non-conservative draft model down to 1.1 K (matching the conservative target's 1.0 K), and reproduces Li self-diffusivities in LGPS within sampling error of the target model. A high-dimensional MMD test confirms LSD trajectories are statistically indistinguishable from target-model trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against Graph Parallelism (GP) from Sriram et al. (2022), a spatial domain-decomposition approach for MLIP-based MD. On FCC copper with UMA-S, LSD outperforms GP by roughly an order of magnitude in throughput for small atom counts (N ≲ a few hundred), where GP is bottlenecked by a fixed ~20 ms compute overhead. At larger atom counts (N > ~1000), GP surpasses LSD because LSD's speedup is fundamentally capped by the rejection rate, which grows with system size per Eq. (12). The paper notes the two methods are complementary and can be combined for concurrent time- and space-parallelism. Additionally, LSD's pipelined (asynchronous) verification is shown to strictly dominate the synchronous speculative sampling protocol (Alg. 1) from Leviathan et al. (2023): in the limit of perfect acceptance, pipelined LSD reaches the full draft-target speedup 1/c, while the synchronous variant is always bounded below 1/c due to idle target-model gaps between draft bursts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts speculative sampling—originally developed for autoregressive LLM decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) and later extended to continuous diffusion models (De Bortoli et al., 2025)—to the fundamentally different setting of second-order Langevin molecular dynamics. The key mathematical tool, reflection-maximal couplings for Gaussian distributions (Bou-Rabee et al., 2020), is repurposed to couple draft and target momentum updates within the ABOBA splitting integrator, while the pre/post-processing theorem (Thm. 3.1) generalizes the coupling to full integrator steps. Unlike hybrid Monte Carlo (Duane et al., 1987), which requires an explicit Hamiltonian for its Metropolis criterion, LSD operates purely on forces and thus admits non-conservative draft models. The pipelined asynchronous verification protocol departs from the synchronous fixed-lookahead scheme of prior speculative sampling work, yielding a simpler speedup bound independent of a lookahead integer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LSD provides the first provably exact (zero relative error) speculative acceleration method for Langevin MD, with demonstrated 3–9× speedups on realistic MLIP-driven systems. The main limitation is that rejection rates grow with atom count (scaling as √N under the paper's assumptions), making LSD most beneficial for systems below ~10³ atoms where it outperforms graph parallelism by an order of magnitude; for larger systems, spatial and temporal parallelism must be combined. Open questions include whether online distillation or inference-time learning of draft models can push the practical atom-count ceiling higher, and how the method interacts with long-range interactions that violate the spatial homogeneity assumption underlying the rejection-rate model. The speculative error correction technique (reducing rejections by up to 75%) is a practical contribution that could transfer to other draft-verify settings.
