## Problem

Conventional visual object trackers localize targets using handcrafted spatial priors (center heatmaps, corner distributions) that provide only surrogate supervision poorly aligned with actual tracking metrics like IoU and AUC. This paper asks whether a tracker can learn where to localize directly from task-oriented feedback, eliminating the need for manually designed spatial supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a template image (128×128) and a search frame (256×256) as input, processed jointly by a one-stream Transformer encoder (HiViT-T/B/L) to produce a search feature map of shape H×W×C. Each spatial position on this map is treated as a candidate action with its own regressed bounding box; at inference, the location with the highest policy score is selected and its corresponding 4-coordinate box is output. During training, clips of T=8 consecutive search frames are sampled to compute sequence-level AUC rewards, though inference remains strictly frame-by-frame.

## Method

RELO formulates target localization as a Markov decision process over the spatial feature map: a regression head predicts a bounding box at every spatial location, a policy head outputs a categorical distribution over locations, and a value head estimates expected reward for variance reduction. The policy is optimized via actor-critic with a composite reward combining frame-level IoU and sequence-level AUC, directly aligning training with evaluation metrics. Training proceeds in two stages: a supervised regression warmup (Giou + L1 loss at the GT location only) that equips the model with basic box-prediction capability, followed by RL policy optimization on 8-frame clips with the regression head frozen. A layer-aligned temporal token propagation mechanism passes temporal tokens from layer l of frame t−1 only to layer l of frame t, preserving the encoder's hierarchical semantic structure while providing cross-frame context at negligible computational cost.

## Results

RELO-L256 achieves 75.1% AUC on LaSOT and 57.5% AUC on LaSOT_ext (without template updates), setting new state-of-the-art among large-size trackers. On short-term benchmarks, it reaches 87.3% AUC on TrackingNet and 81.8% AO on GOT-10k. The base-size variant RELO-B256 also leads its group with 73.3% AUC on LaSOT and 54.2% AUC on LaSOT_ext. The most notable margin is on LaSOT_ext, where RELO-L256 outperforms the next-best method (ARPTrack-L384) by 3.3 AUC points, indicating stronger robustness under cross-category appearance shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RELO-L256 outperforms all large-size baselines across all four main benchmarks. The largest margin is on LaSOT_ext (57.5 vs. 54.2 for ARPTrack-L384 and 54.0 for SUTrack-L224), a 3.3-point gap that highlights the benefit of reward-driven localization under cross-category shift. On LaSOT, RELO-L256 leads ODTrack-L384 (75.1 vs. 74.0) and LoRAT-L224 (75.1 vs. 74.2) by 1.1 and 0.9 AUC points respectively. On TrackingNet and GOT-10k, the margins are smaller but consistent (87.3 vs. 86.6 AUC; 81.8 vs. 81.5 AO). In the base-size group, RELO-B256 beats SUTrack-B224 by 1.1 AUC on LaSOT_ext and 2.6 AO on GOT-10k, while matching or exceeding ARPTrack-B256 on all metrics. On transfer benchmarks (Table 3), RELO-L256 achieves the best TNL2K AUC (63.6) and NFS AUC (71.3), confirming that the gains are not benchmark-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

RELO builds on the one-stream Transformer tracking framework of OSTrack (Ye et al., 2022) and adapts ODTrack's (Zheng et al., 2024) temporal token propagation, but fundamentally departs from the dense-prediction paradigm by replacing handcrafted spatial priors (center heatmaps, corner distributions) with a reinforcement-learning localization policy over the spatial feature map. Unlike prior RL-based trackers that use RL for post-hoc box refinement or auxiliary decisions (template updating, camera control), RELO makes RL the core mechanism for learning *where* to localize, formulating each spatial position as an action in a Markov decision process. The layer-aligned temporal token propagation is a targeted modification of ODTrack's deep-to-shallow strategy, restricting cross-frame information exchange to matched semantic levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By directly optimizing task-oriented rewards (IoU + AUC) rather than surrogate spatial priors, RELO aligns training with evaluation and removes heuristic design choices that have persisted in the field. The notably larger gains on LaSOT_ext versus LaSOT suggest that reward-driven localization is particularly beneficial under distribution shift, where rigid center/corner priors become unreliable. Open questions include whether richer reward formulations can capture occlusion recovery and temporal stability, whether the approach extends to multi-object and multi-camera settings, and whether RL can be extended beyond localization to the full tracking pipeline (template updating, search-region adaptation, re-detection).
