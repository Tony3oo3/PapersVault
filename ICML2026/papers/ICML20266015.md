## Problem

Scaling text-to-video diffusion models to ultra-high-resolution (2K/4K) generation introduces three coupled failures: temporal dynamics collapse into near-static outputs, spatial expansion causes semantic repetitions and inconsistencies, and fine-grained detail synthesis degrades with motion blur and texture loss. Existing cascaded pipelines restrict the high-resolution stage to perceptual detail refinement rather than content completion, and VSR-based approaches only enhance low-level sharpness without generating new semantic or structural content, yielding pseudo-UHR results that appear sharper but lack genuine realism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a photorealistic ultra-high-resolution video at 2K (2160×3840) or 4K resolution, typically 49 frames long. Internally, the pipeline first generates a low-resolution latent at 720×1280, upsamples it in latent space to the target resolution, and then refines it through frequency-specialized denoising stages. The output is evaluated on VBench (250 prompts) and assessed via FIDpatch on 256×256 patches sampled from 8 frames per video.

## Method

LUVE is a three-stage latent-cascaded framework built on Wan2.1-1.3B. Stage 1 (LMG) generates motion-consistent low-resolution video latents using the pretrained flow-matching DiT backbone. Stage 2 (VLU) employs a lightweight ~22M-parameter video latent upsampler (VLUer) that combines a VRT-based encoder, an MLP-based implicit neural representation upsampler for arbitrary-scale continuous upsampling, and a VRT-based decoder, all operating directly in latent space to avoid VAE codec overhead; it is trained with latent L1 loss plus pixel-space L1 and frame-difference losses. Stage 3 (HCR) introduces dual frequency experts: a Low-Frequency Expert (LoRA injected into frozen attention modules with a low-pass filter, trained in the high-noise interval t∈[t_switch,1)) that enhances global semantic coherence, and a High-Frequency Expert (LoRA injected into frozen FFN modules with a high-pass filter, trained in the low-noise interval t∈[0,t_switch]) that synthesizes fine-grained textures. Training data is curated per expert—HPS v3 filtering (threshold 6.5) for the LFE and Unsharp Masking augmentation for the HFE—on the UltraVideo dataset.

## Results

On VBench (250 prompts), LUVE-2K achieves the highest average score of 84.34, surpassing the best baseline UltraWan-4K (83.75) by 0.59 points, with notable gains in image quality (71.15 vs. 71.44 for UltraWan-4K) and aesthetic quality (59.78 vs. 57.69). On the UHR-specific evaluation (Table 2), LUVE-4K achieves the lowest FIDpatch of 39.87 (vs. 48.64 for UltraWan-4K) and the highest Detailness score of 5.40/10. In the VSR comparison (Table 3), LUVE outperforms all four VSR baselines across MUSIQ (58.01), MANIQA (0.410), NIQE (3.16), and DOVER (0.784).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUVE-2K outperforms all T2V baselines on VBench average score, beating UltraWan-4K (83.75) by 0.59 and CineScale-2K (83.63) by 0.71, with the largest margins in aesthetic quality (+2.09 over UltraWan-4K) and background consistency (+0.65 over UltraWan-4K). Against the native-resolution Wan2.1 baselines, LUVE shows dramatic gains in image quality (71.15 vs. 68.28 for 720p; 71.15 vs. 58.26 for 1K) and aesthetic quality (59.78 vs. 56.46 and 49.89 respectively). In the UHR-specific MLLM evaluation, LUVE-4K achieves the best FIDpatch (39.87 vs. 48.64 for UltraWan-4K, a 17.7% reduction) and highest Detailness (5.40 vs. 4.64), while UltraWan-4K retains a slight edge in IQ on VBench (71.44 vs. 71.33). Against VSR methods, LUVE wins on all four metrics: MUSIQ (58.01 vs. 56.54 for FlashVSR), MANIQA (0.410 vs. 0.407 for STAR), NIQE (3.16 vs. 3.20 for FlashVSR), and DOVER (0.784 vs. 0.761 for STAR). In the human study, LUVE receives 63.50% preference for overall video quality, far exceeding STAR (15.67%), UltraWan (12.42%), and CineScale (8.42%), with 95% confidence intervals exceeding 50% on all four dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUVE builds on the Wan2.1 flow-matching video foundation model as its generative backbone and draws on implicit neural representation (VideoINR) and the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) for its latent upsampling module. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant cascaded HR video generation paradigm (FlashVideo, LaVie, Waver, Seedance, Longcat-Video), where the high-resolution stage is restricted to perceptual detail refinement, by repositioning the HR stage as a semantic content completer. It also moves beyond both training-free upscaling (ScaleCrafter, HiDiffusion) and VSR-based pipelines (RealBasicVSR, STAR, FlashVSR), which the authors argue produce pseudo-UHR outputs lacking genuine semantic richness, by performing native UHR synthesis entirely within the latent space using frequency-specialized LoRA experts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: existing T2V models degrade severely at UHR resolutions, and VSR post-processing cannot recover meaningful semantic content, limiting applications in VR, digital humans, and professional media. The PSD-motivated split into low-frequency (semantic coherence) and high-frequency (texture detail) experts, each operating at a distinct denoising phase, offers a principled decomposition that could inform future frequency-aware generative designs. However, computational efficiency remains a significant barrier—91 minutes and ~39 GiB for a 49-frame 4K clip—leaving a substantial gap before large-scale commercial deployment, and the authors explicitly flag efficient UHR generation as the key open problem.
