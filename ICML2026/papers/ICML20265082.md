## Problem

Existing brain encoding and decoding methods treat these as isolated, unimodal tasks that rely heavily on external priors (frozen CLIP, diffusion models, LLMs) to compensate for limited semantic content in neural signals, overlooking the brain's intrinsic nature as a multimodal integration system. No prior framework unifies bidirectional mapping across brain, vision, and language within a single model capable of any-to-any generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265082/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes fMRI single-trial beta-weight voxel signals (~12,700–15,700 voxels per subject from visual cortex ROIs in NSD), natural RGB images from COCO, and text captions (either original COCO annotations or detailed Qwen3-VL-generated descriptions). All inputs are tokenized into a shared discrete Omni space: brain signals via a custom VQ-VAE (codebook 128, compression ratio 128, embedding dim 32), images via a pretrained image tokenizer, and text via subword tokenization. Outputs are synthesized fMRI voxel maps, reconstructed RGB images, or free-form textual captions, generated autoregressively as interleaved token sequences from the same model.

## Method

BrainJanus operates in two stages. First, a Unified Brain Tokenizer (VQ-VAE with codebook size 128, compression ratio 128, embedding dimension 32) is pretrained from scratch in a cross-subject setting to quantize continuous fMRI dynamics into discrete tokens aligned with vision and language tokens in a shared Omni space. Second, a single decoder-only Transformer backbone (initialized from Janus-7B, 30 layers, hidden dim 4096, 32 attention heads) is fine-tuned via LoRA (applied to Q/V projections, scaling 16, dropout 0.2) using next-token prediction over arbitrarily interleaved multimodal token sequences. This unified autoregressive objective enables the same model to perform brain-to-image, brain-to-text, image-to-brain, and text-to-brain generation by simply conditioning on different modality token prefixes. The paper further identifies a critical evaluation vulnerability in semantic-level brain encoding metrics (demonstrated via a trivial 'Padding Hacking' baseline that achieves perfect scores by leaking visual embeddings) and adopts cross-entropy loss exclusively for encoding to enforce genuine neural pattern learning.

## Results

On brain-to-text decoding (GT=Qwen detailed captions), BrainJanus achieves a BERTScore of 38.12 and CLIP score of 96.2%, surpassing the previous best (UMBRAE: BERT 30.91, CLIP 94.7%) by 7.21 and 1.5% respectively. On brain-to-image decoding, BrainJanus—the only autoregressive method compared—reaches 94.4% CLIP semantic similarity and 97.1% AlexNet-5 feature similarity, outperforming all diffusion-based baselines (MindEye2: 93.0% CLIP; UMBRAE: 93.5% CLIP). For brain encoding, BrainJanus achieves 72.8% Inception and 75.3% CLIP (image→fMRI) and 77.0% CLIP, 28.3 BERT (text→fMRI), exceeding both Transformer Encoding and Linear Regression baselines across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainJanus outperforms all brain-to-image baselines on CLIP semantic similarity: 94.4% vs. MindEye2 (93.0%), UMBRAE (93.5%), Ozcelik & VanRullen (90.9%), and Takagi & Nishimoto (82.1%), despite being the only autoregressive (diffusion-free) method in the comparison. On brain-to-text decoding, BrainJanus leads by a wide margin—BERTScore 38.12 vs. UMBRAE (30.91), MindLLM (30.62), and MindEye2 (29.12)—with a 7.2-point gap over the runner-up. For brain encoding, BrainJanus (Inception 72.8%, CLIP 75.3%) beats both the Transformer Encoding baseline (70.4%, 72.4%) and Linear Regression (65.9%, 68.5%) on all semantic metrics, while the Padding Hacking analysis reveals that prior semantic-level evaluation protocols (used by MindSimulator and SynBrain) are vulnerable to trivial information leakage, achieving 100% scores without any biological mapping. The zero-shot result (BrainJanus trained only on brain-to-text, applied to brain-to-image) reaches 77.3% CLIP, demonstrating cross-task generalization that no prior method achieves. Notably, BrainJanus's low-level pixel metrics (PixCorr 0.173, SSIM 0.292) remain below diffusion-based methods (MindEye2: 0.322, 0.431), reflecting the inherent trade-off between autoregressive token generation and pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainJanus draws on two distinct research lineages: (1) brain decoding/encoding methods (MindEye2, UMBRAE, MindLLM for decoding; MindSimulator, SynBrain for encoding) that treat these as separate tasks with task-specific architectures and heavy reliance on external priors like CLIP and Stable Diffusion; and (2) unified multimodal generation models (Janus-Pro, Emu3, Chameleon) that demonstrate discrete tokenization plus autoregressive next-token prediction enables any-to-any cross-modal synthesis. The key departure is transplanting the unified autoregressive paradigm from purely digital modalities (text, image, video) into the biological domain, introducing a from-scratch VQ-VAE brain tokenizer that maps fMRI signals into the same discrete token space as vision and language, thereby eliminating the need for separate aligners and generative backbones per task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified any-to-any formulation enables zero-shot cross-task transfer (e.g., a model trained only on brain-to-text generalizes to brain-to-image), suggesting that shared neural semantics are captured in the token space rather than being task-specific. More critically, the Padding Hacking analysis exposes a fundamental vulnerability in the semantic-level evaluation protocol for brain encoding: a trivial zero-padding baseline achieves perfect reconstruction scores, meaning current metrics cannot distinguish genuine neural synthesis from information leakage. This finding should prompt the field to reconsider how brain encoding quality is validated. Open questions include extending beyond visual cortex to whole-brain activity, handling more diverse neural modalities (EEG, MEG), and addressing the hallucination risk introduced by powerful generative priors.
