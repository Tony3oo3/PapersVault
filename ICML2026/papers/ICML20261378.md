## Problem

Existing Bayesian experimental design methods assume fixed feasibility, cost, and utility during training and cannot adapt to dynamic constraints (transition limits, budget exhaustion, physical movement restrictions) that emerge at deployment. Amortized policies trained on unconstrained trajectories perform sub-optimally when post-hoc constraint masking forces them onto unseen feasible regions, and non-amortized methods are too slow for sequential planning under evolving constraint states.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task model p(y|x,θ), a prior p(θ), a design space X, a path-dependent constraint state z_t (encoding the previous design, remaining budget, or physical system state), and a planning horizon H. At each step it outputs a single design x_t that satisfies the current feasible set X(z_t), executed sequentially over a finite horizon T via a receding-horizon strategy. The constraint state evolves deterministically via a transition function f(z_t, x_t), so the feasible set changes at every step and couples decisions across time.

## Method

COPEx is a semi-amortized planner that combines offline pre-training of a Transformer Neural Process posterior network q_φ (GMM output, trained by NLL) and an RL-trained design policy π_ψ (ALINE) with online multi-step lookahead over a scenario tree of fantasy observations. At each step, the tree alternates between design decisions and branching over m fantasy outcomes sampled from the amortized posterior predictive, enabling fast belief updates and EIG estimation (via an adaptive contrastive surrogate) at every node without retraining. Following the one-shot multi-step tree construction of Jiang et al. (2020b), all design variables across the tree are jointly optimized by fixing base noise ε via reparameterization, reducing the stochastic problem to a deterministic constrained nonlinear program solved with SLSQP. The pre-trained policy warm-starts the tree optimizer, and a hybrid exploration-exploitation initialization (policy + random trees) handles cases where constraints shift the feasible region outside the policy's training distribution.

## Results

On the CES task under global budget constraints, COPEx with a 1-step lookahead (H=1) achieves the highest cumulative EIG of 7.03±0.55 (B=100) and 7.47±0.55 (B=150), substantially outperforming its myopic variant (5.26±0.39) and all baselines. On the location finding task, COPEx consistently outperforms VPCE and ALINE across all transition constraint levels (δ ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}), with the performance gap widening as constraints tighten. On cost-aware active learning, COPEx achieves lower RMSE at every accumulated cost level compared to all four GP baselines, with the non-myopic variant (H=1, H=3) further improving over the myopic version under tight budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPEx (H=1) outperforms VPCE by a factor of ~3.2× in EIG on the CES task (7.03 vs. 2.18 at B=100) while running ~5× faster (19.5s vs. 105.8s per step). Against RL-BOED, COPEx achieves 42% higher EIG (7.03 vs. 4.93) at B=100, with the gap narrowing at B=150 (7.47 vs. 4.98), reflecting that RL-BOED's fixed policy cannot adapt to the global budget constraint. ALINE, the strongest amortized baseline, is outperformed by COPEx by 58% in EIG (7.03 vs. 4.46) at B=100 and 31% at B=150 (7.47 vs. 5.70), confirming that post-hoc constraint masking degrades policy quality. On the location finding task, COPEx beats both VPCE and ALINE across all constraint levels, with the advantage growing as δ decreases from 0.2 to 0.05. On cost-aware active learning, COPEx dominates all four GP baselines (GP-US, GP-VR, GP-EPIG, GP-RS) in RMSE-vs-cost curves, with the non-myopic variants (H=1, H=3) clearly separating from the myopic version under tight budgets (B=2 or 4), while under large budgets (B=4 or 8) the myopic variant performs on par with the non-myopic ones since constraints only bind near the end of the trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPEx adapts the one-shot multi-step tree optimization paradigm from Bayesian optimization (Jiang et al., 2020b) to the sequential Bayesian experimental design setting, where the key challenge is that EIG estimation and posterior updates at each tree node are far more expensive than GP-based utilities. It combines this with amortized inference (Transformer Neural Process posterior network) and a pre-trained amortized design policy (ALINE) for warm-starting, creating a semi-amortized framework. The critical departure from prior amortized BED methods (Foster et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2026) is that those assume fixed feasibility and cost during training, whereas COPEx explicitly handles dynamic, test-time constraints (transition bounds, global budgets, heterogeneous costs) through online receding-horizon planning over a scenario tree.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: existing BED methods cannot adapt to the dynamic constraints (budget exhaustion, movement limits, varying costs) that are ubiquitous in real deployment, and the paper demonstrates that post-hoc masking of infeasible actions in amortized policies leads to significantly suboptimal designs. The finding that deeper planning horizons (H=3) can degrade performance relative to H=1 on the CES task—due to compounding bias in the amortized posterior—highlights a fundamental tension between lookahead depth and surrogate fidelity that remains an open problem. Limitations include exponential tree growth, restriction to continuous design spaces, and dependence on the quality of the amortized posterior, all of which bound the method's scalability to longer horizons or higher-dimensional problems.
