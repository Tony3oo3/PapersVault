## Problem

LLMs inherit culture-default biases from skewed pretraining data, leading to systematic misalignment on culturally sensitive value-based decisions. Existing mitigation methods treat cultural values as independent, unstructured signals and lack demographic grounding, making outputs brittle, inconsistent across regions, and difficult to interpret. The paper addresses the absence of a framework that simultaneously captures structured cross-topic value relationships (via ontology) and grounds reasoning in demographically matched empirical evidence.

## Input / Output

The system takes a query question with discrete response options and a target demographic profile (e.g., country, age, gender, education) as input. At inference, it retrieves ontology triples and K=5 demographically similar WVS respondents via dense embedding search, then produces a final answer selection along with explicit multi-agent reasoning traces. Each persona agent outputs a structured JSON containing its chosen option, a ≥250-word reasoning trace citing specific value summaries and ontology edges, and alignment factors; the judgment agent synthesizes these into a single final prediction with an evidence-first adjudication rationale.

## Method

OG-MAR integrates three components: (1) a fixed 76-class cultural ontology (12 top-classes, 64 sub-classes) whose 150 object-property relations are constructed via Competency Questions answered by LLMs conditioned on culturally diverse WVS value profiles, then consolidated by human experts; (2) a retrieval pipeline that uses a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v2-xxlarge topic classifier to select relevant top-classes, followed by dense retrieval (E5-base) of ontology triples and demographically similar respondents; and (3) a multi-agent inference stage where K=5 Value-Persona Agents simulate culturally grounded reasoning under ontology constraints, and a Final Judgment Agent performs Constrained Meta-Adjudication that prioritizes ontology consistency and evidence grounding over raw vote counts, using demographic relevance only as a tie-breaker. The key architectural insight is that structured ontology triples provide cross-topic value dependencies that unstructured retrieval misses, while multi-persona simulation preserves diverse value perspectives that a single inference pass would collapse.

## Results

Across six regional benchmarks (EVS, GSS, CGSS, ISD, AFRO, LAPOP) and four LLM backbones, OG-MAR achieves average accuracies of 0.6007 (GPT-4o-mini), 0.6308 (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite), 0.5705 (Qwen 2.5), and 0.6317 (EXAONE 3.5), consistently outperforming all five baselines with statistically significant gains (paired t-test, Holm-Bonferroni, p < 0.05). The largest improvements appear on culturally challenging settings: CGSS (China) reaches 0.7017 with Gemini 2.5 and 0.6307 with EXAONE, while ISD (India) reaches 0.7810 with EXAONE. Distribution-level metrics (EMD, Proportion Correlation, TVD) confirm that OG-MAR also improves response-distribution alignment beyond item-level accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OG-MAR outperforms all five prompting/retrieval baselines on average across all four backbones. Against the strongest retrieval baseline, ValuesRAG (Seo et al., 2025), OG-MAR gains +0.005 to +0.070 in average accuracy (e.g., 0.6308 vs. 0.5731 on Gemini 2.5; 0.6317 vs. 0.5613 on EXAONE), with the largest margins on CGSS and ISD where cultural distribution shift is greatest. Against the multi-agent Debate baseline (Ki et al., 2025), OG-MAR improves by +0.022 to +0.074 on average, showing that ontology-guided persona simulation with constrained adjudication is more effective than unconstrained iterative critique. Against Role Assignment (Tao et al., 2024) and Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022), gains are consistent but smaller (+0.028 to +0.097 and +0.023 to +0.092 respectively), confirming that structured value retrieval and multi-persona reasoning add value beyond simple prompting or majority voting. In a post-training comparison, OG-MAR (GPT-4o-mini) outperforms CultureLLM (Li et al., 2024, Qwen 3.5-9B + LoRA) on five of six datasets (e.g., EVS 0.6206 vs. 0.5988; CGSS 0.6509 vs. 0.6599 is a near-tie; ISD 0.6192 vs. 0.6975 is the one loss), while CultureLLM wins only on GSS (0.6069 vs. 0.5480), suggesting post-training is competitive in the model's dominant pretraining region but less effective cross-culturally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads—cultural-bias mitigation in LLMs (role assignment, ValuesRAG, multi-agent debate), ontology engineering (CQ-driven construction, METHONTOLOGY/NeOn lifecycles), and multi-agent reasoning—into a unified architecture. It departs from prior cultural-alignment methods by treating values not as independent unstructured signals but as nodes in a curated ontology with explicit cross-topic relations, and from prior multi-agent frameworks by grounding each persona in demographically retrieved survey profiles constrained by ontology triples. The ontology construction itself repurposes the CQ-by-CQ paradigm from knowledge engineering, adapting it to a fixed 76-class cultural taxonomy with LLM-generated and human-validated object properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OG-MAR offers a principled, interpretable path to cultural alignment that goes beyond prompting heuristics, and its consistent gains across six regions and four backbones (including a post-cutoff ISSP 2023 control) strengthen the case that structured value knowledge, not just more retrieval, drives alignment. However, the framework incurs the highest token cost among compared methods, its gains over the strongest baseline (ValuesRAG) are moderate (roughly +0.01 to +0.05 in average accuracy), and the ablation showing a single-judge variant is competitive suggests the multi-persona simulation layer contributes less than the ontology-grounded retrieval pipeline. Open questions include whether the ontology can be auto-verified at scale, how well the fixed 12-topic taxonomy generalizes to culturally local constructs outside the WVS frame, and whether routing or pruning can reduce the multi-agent overhead without sacrificing alignment quality.
