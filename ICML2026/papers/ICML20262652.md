## Problem

Photometric ambiguities—arising from textureless regions, reflections, insufficient coverage, or dark lighting—make multi-view geometry ill-posed in Gaussian Splatting surface reconstruction, yet existing methods either target limited cases (e.g., reflection-specific ray tracing) or apply uniform external regularizations without identifying where ambiguity actually occurs. The paper identifies two built-in primitive-wise representational ambiguities in 3DGS (edge overblowing and under-determined color blending) and reveals that the Spherical Harmonics coefficients themselves carry an intrinsic signal for detecting these ambiguous regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262652/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled 2× for DTU/TnT, 2× or 4× for Mip-NeRF 360 indoor/outdoor) along with depth priors from foundation models—either monocular (Depth Anything V2) for the AmbiSuR-Mono variant or metric multi-view (Depth Anything 3) for the standard AmbiSuR variant. It outputs a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF and novel view renderings, trained for 30,000 iterations on a single RTX 3090 Ti. The method is implemented atop the PGSR codebase with architecture-agnostic modifications, requiring no changes to the underlying Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline.

## Method

AmbiSuR introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, Gaussian Primitive Truncation clips each Gaussian at 2σ to eliminate the low-opacity tail whose gradient is provably suppressed (Appendix A shows the core-to-edge gradient ratio exceeds 1 beyond r_crit≈1.83σ), preventing overblown geometry. Second, Ray-Color Consistency penalizes the weighted variance of per-primitive colors along each ray, enforcing that blended primitives share similar optical properties and blocking the under-determined color shortcuts that produce redundant over-reconstruction. Third, the SH Ambiguity Indication module uses the L2 norm of high-degree spherical harmonic coefficients as a free-lunch indicator: a dual-end percentile selection (top η_U and bottom η_L) identifies primitives suffering from inconsistent or insufficient photometric supervision, and an Amorphous Local Regularizer applies a depth-derived normal loss only to those primitives with parameter separation (freezing non-target primitives and excluding opacity/scaling), enabling precise, prior-compatible correction without degrading well-constrained regions.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (Chamfer distance, lower is better), AmbiSuR achieves a mean of 0.46, outperforming the strongest baseline GeoSVR (0.47) and all other 3DGS-based methods (PGSR 0.52, MILo 0.68, GOF 0.74). On Tanks and Temples (F1-score, higher is better), AmbiSuR reaches 0.589, surpassing GeoSVR (0.56) and PGSR (0.52). On Mip-NeRF 360, AmbiSuR maintains competitive novel-view-synthesis quality (outdoor PSNR 24.79, indoor PSNR 30.06) while delivering superior surface geometry, with training time of ~0.6h per scene on an RTX 3090 Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AmbiSuR outperforms all 3DGS-based baselines on DTU: it beats PGSR (0.52→0.46), MILo (0.68→0.46), GOF (0.74→0.46), and 2DGS (0.80→0.46) in mean Chamfer distance, with the largest gains on ambiguous scenes like scan 69 (0.50→0.42) and scan 83 (1.08→0.97). Against the strongest voxel-based SOTA GeoSVR (0.47), AmbiSuR achieves a marginal but consistent improvement (0.46) while being faster (0.6h vs 0.8h) and operating on the unstructured 3DGS representation. On Tanks and Temples, AmbiSuR (F1 0.589) surpasses GeoSVR (0.56), PGSR (0.52), and MILo+ (0.49), with particularly large gains on challenging scenes like Courthouse (0.34→0.39) and Meetingroom (0.37→0.45). On Mip-NeRF 360, AmbiSuR's rendering quality (outdoor PSNR 24.79) is competitive with GOF (24.82) and PGSR (24.76), confirming that the geometric improvements do not sacrifice appearance fidelity. Implicit SDF methods (NeuS 0.84, Neuralangelo 0.61) are substantially outperformed but require 12–128+ hours of training versus AmbiSuR's 0.6h.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the PGSR surface reconstruction pipeline (Chen et al., 2024a), positioning itself against two lines of prior work: (1) methods that handle photometric ambiguity through complex ray tracing for reflections (ReflectiveGS, MaterialRefGS) or heavy external regularization (GeoSVR, VCR-GauS), and (2) 3DGS surface methods focused on primitive-surface alignment (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF, MILo). The key conceptual departure is reframing photometric ambiguity as an *intrinsic* property of the Gaussian Splatting representation—identifying two built-in primitive-wise ambiguities (edge overblowing and under-determined blending) rather than treating it solely as an external supervision deficiency. The SH-based ambiguity indicator repurposes an existing component (view-dependent color coefficients) as a diagnostic signal, which no prior 3DGS surface method exploits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in providing architecture-agnostic, low-cost techniques (Gaussian truncation at 2σ, SH-norm-based primitive selection) that can be dropped into existing 3DGS pipelines to improve surface accuracy in ambiguous regions without retraining or complex ray models. The counterintuitive finding that *both* extremely high and extremely low SH coefficients indicate problematic primitives (dual-end indication) opens a new diagnostic axis for 3DGS optimization. Limitations include continued reliance on depth priors (monocular or metric), inability to handle refractive surfaces, and the method being validated primarily on the PGSR backbone. Open questions include whether the SH indicator generalizes to higher-degree SH or alternative appearance models, and how these techniques interact with explicit light transport modeling for complex materials.
