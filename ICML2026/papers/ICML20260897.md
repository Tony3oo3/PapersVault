## Problem

Prior work characterized the sample complexity of robust mean estimation under mean-shift contamination only for Gaussian and Laplace base distributions, leaving open whether consistent estimation is possible for general distributions and what governs the sample complexity. This paper resolves that open question by identifying a Fourier-analytic condition on the base distribution that determines when consistent estimation is achievable and quantifies the exact sample complexity up to polynomial factors.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n i.i.d. samples from the α-mean-shift contaminated distribution D_μ^(α) (where an adversary replaces a fraction α of clean samples with shifted copies of D) together with oracle access to the characteristic function φ_D of the known base distribution. It outputs a mean estimate μ̂ satisfying ||μ̂ − μ|| ≤ ε with probability at least 2/3, requiring the true mean to be bounded (||μ|| ≤ R) and the base distribution to satisfy a frequency-witness condition parameterized by (ε, A, δ). The sample complexity is Õ_α(d/δ²), where δ captures the worst-case Fourier mass of D at frequencies that can distinguish the true mean from any ε-far candidate.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Fourier deconvolution: since the contaminated characteristic function factors as φ_D(ω)·φ_Q(ω), dividing the empirical characteristic function by the known φ_D(ω) yields an estimate of the adversary's shift distribution Q. The algorithm then performs a grid search over a finite cover of candidate means and frequencies, scoring each candidate by the worst-case discrepancy between the estimated φ_Q and the model (1−α)e^{2πiω·μ̂}. The key technical ingredient is the frequency-witness condition: for every error direction v with ||v|| ≥ ε, there must exist a frequency ω where the phase shift v·ω is bounded away from integers (making the wrong candidate detectable) and |φ_D(ω)| ≥ δ (making the deconvolution statistically stable). The matching lower bound constructs two hard-to-distinguish contaminated distributions by designing a smooth periodic window in Fourier space that cancels the mean-shift signal precisely on the lattice bands where φ_D concentrates its L² mass, then applies Plancherel's theorem and a TV-distance-to-L²-characteristic-function inequality to bound the statistical distinguishability.

## Results

The paper establishes that the sample complexity of mean estimation under α-mean-shift contamination is governed by a Fourier witness parameter δ, yielding an upper bound of Õ(d/δ²) samples and a matching (up to polynomial factors) lower bound of 1/δ^{Ω(1)} samples. For the Gaussian, the sample complexity is Õ(d·e^{O((α/ε)²)}) with a matching Ω(e^{Ω((α/ε)²)}) lower bound. For the Laplace distribution, the upper bound is Õ(dα²/ε⁴) with a lower bound of Ω((α/ε)^{1/2}), and for the uniform distribution on [-1,1], the upper bound is Õ(1/ε) with a lower bound of Ω((α/ε)^{1/6}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparison' is between the new upper and lower bounds and prior results restricted to specific distribution families. Prior work by Kotekal & Gao (2025) and Diakonikolas et al. (2025) established matching minimax rates only for the Gaussian and Laplace cases. This paper generalizes those results to arbitrary base distributions satisfying a mild spectral condition (the frequency-witness condition), providing the first qualitative characterization of sample complexity for general D. The upper and lower bounds are qualitatively matching: for the Gaussian they are tight up to polynomial factors in d, and for the Laplace and uniform cases the gap between upper and lower bounds is polynomial in (α/ε). The paper also shows that consistency is impossible for band-limited distributions (e.g., sinc² density), a result not available in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves an open question explicitly posed by Kotekal & Gao (2025), who characterized the sample complexity of mean-shift contamination only for Gaussian and Laplace base distributions and asked for a matching minimax lower bound for a generic base distribution D. It also extends the algorithmic framework of Diakonikolas et al. (2025), who gave the first polynomial-time estimator for the Gaussian/Laplace cases, by replacing distribution-specific Fourier arguments with a distribution-agnostic 'frequency-witness condition' on the characteristic function. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the introduction of the Fourier witness: a single spectral parameter δ that simultaneously governs both the upper bound (via a deconvolution-based algorithm scanning a frequency cover) and the lower bound (via a Plancherel-based indistinguishability argument), unifying what were previously separate distribution-specific analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first qualitative characterization of when consistent mean estimation is possible under mean-shift contamination for arbitrary base distributions, showing that mild spectral conditions on the characteristic function suffice and that band-limited distributions (e.g., sinc²) render the problem fundamentally impossible. The qualitative matching of upper and lower bounds (both polynomial in 1/δ) gives a clean picture of the problem's difficulty, though a quantitative gap remains between the two. For the robust statistics community, this closes a natural gap in the theory; for broader ML, the practical impact is limited since the contamination model is relatively narrow and the algorithms require oracle access to the base distribution's characteristic function. Open questions include closing the quantitative gap, extending to other statistical functionals (covariance, quantiles), and understanding whether the frequency-witness condition can be verified or estimated from data alone.
