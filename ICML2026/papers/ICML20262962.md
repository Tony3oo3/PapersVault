## Problem

Existing LLM agent planning systems rely on fixed, hand-crafted planning structures (linear to-do lists, DAGs, hierarchical trees) that cannot adapt to the structural diversity of open-ended tasks. The paper argues that no single universal planning topology exists—different task domains and even different instances within a domain require different topologies, revision cadences, and navigation strategies—yet no prior work provides a mechanism to autonomously synthesize and dynamically revise task-specific planning architectures.

## Input / Output

The meta-planner Todo-14B receives a ~13k-token composite context comprising the user query, a role-defining system prompt, the abstract BasePlanning interface, tool definitions, and three randomly sampled planning examples from the PlanFactory pool. It outputs not a final answer but an executable Python planning module paired with a YAML configuration file that specifies the planning topology, initialization logic, adaptation triggers, and navigation strategy. This generated planning system is then instantiated in the PlanFactory runtime, where a downstream agent (e.g., GPT-5-mini, DeepSeek V3.2, or Kimi K2) executes it to produce the final answer.

## Method

TodoEvolve comprises two components: PlanFactory, a modular design space that decomposes seven representative planning paradigms into four standardized dimensions (Topology, Initialization, Adaptation, Navigation) via a unified BasePlanning interface, and a two-stage training curriculum for the meta-planner. Stage 1 (SFT) fine-tunes Qwen3-14B on 3,360 execution-verified planning trajectories to establish syntactic and structural competence. Stage 2 (IGPO) applies a DPO-style preference optimization where preference pairs are constructed using a novel Cognitive Impedance metric that penalizes total cost, execution failures, instability, and—critically—the ratio of planning cost to execution cost (a 'bureaucracy penalty' ensuring planning effort does not outweigh execution). Data construction uses a Bootstrap-and-Filter pipeline: evolutionary sampling generates diverse planning candidates, each is executed in PlanFactory, and only trajectories matching ground-truth answers are retained, with impedance gaps determining winner/loser pairs for IGPO.

## Results

On the GAIA benchmark, TodoEvolve + Smolagents with GPT-5-Mini achieves a 72.12% average pass@1 score, a +16.37% absolute improvement over the vanilla Smolagents baseline (55.75%) and +3.03% over Flash-Searcher with the same backbone (69.09%). On xBench-DS, the same configuration reaches 75.0%, and with DeepSeek V3.2 the framework attains 70.91% GAIA average and 53.85% on the most challenging GAIA Level 3 subset. In the head-to-head framework comparison (Table 4), TodoEvolve dominates all seven static planning baselines across WebWalker-QA (70.00% vs. best 63.33%), DeepSearch-QA (42.00% vs. best 30.00%), and GAIA Level 2 (57.14% vs. best 30.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flash-Searcher (Qin et al., 2025), the closest same-backbone competitor, TodoEvolve with GPT-5-Mini improves GAIA average from 69.09% to 72.12% (+3.03 pp) and xBench-DS from 69.0% to 75.0% (+6 pp), while matching its GAIA L3 score of 46.15%. With DeepSeek V3.2, the gap widens to +10.3 pp on GAIA average (70.91% vs. 60.61%). Against OWL (Hu et al., 2025b), TodoEvolve (GPT-5-Mini) gains +11.51 pp on GAIA average (72.12% vs. 60.61%) and +19.21 pp on GAIA L3 (46.15% vs. 26.92%), though OWL's pass@3 setting narrows the L1 gap. Against OAgents (Zhu et al., 2025), TodoEvolve leads by +5.45 pp on GAIA average and +5.81 pp on L2, while tying on L3 at 46.15%. In the direct framework comparison (Table 4, Kimi-K2 backbone), TodoEvolve outperforms all seven static planners—Co-Sight, FlowSearch, Flash-Searcher, AgentOrchestra, OAgents, JoyAgent, and OWL—by substantial margins: +6.67 pp over the best (JoyAgent) on WebWalker-QA, +12 pp over OWL on DeepSearch-QA, and +27.14 pp over JoyAgent on GAIA Level 2, while maintaining comparable or lower API costs than most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-style preference optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing human preference pairs with execution-verified, impedance-ranked plan pairs, and by applying the objective not to an action policy but to a code-generating meta-planner. Conceptually, it departs from the long line of static agentic planning architectures (CoT, ToT, ReAct, Flash-Searcher, OWL, OAgents) by treating the planning topology itself as a generative output rather than a fixed scaffold, unifying seven prior paradigms under the PlanFactory four-dimensional design space (topology, initialization, adaptation, navigation). The broader RL-for-agents literature (Search-R1, RAGEN, ToRL) optimizes action selection within a fixed loop; TodoEvolve instead optimizes the loop's architecture, which is the key conceptual shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 14B meta-planner can generate task-specific planning code that consistently boosts heterogeneous backbones (GPT-5-Mini, DeepSeek V3.2, Kimi K2) by 10–16 points over hand-engineered frameworks suggests that architectural design, not just model scale, is a first-order lever for agent performance. Open questions include whether the four-dimensional PlanFactory abstraction is expressive enough for highly stateful or memory-centric long-horizon agents (acknowledged by the authors), whether the relatively small training corpus (3,360 SFT + 2,000 IGPO samples) generalizes beyond the five evaluated benchmarks, and whether the one-shot planner generation at inference time can match iterative evolutionary search in truly novel domains. The cognitive impedance metric as a multi-objective tie-breaker (correctness → stability → token efficiency) is a practical but modest contribution that could be adopted more broadly in agent training pipelines.
