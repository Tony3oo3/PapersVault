## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for LLM reasoning suffer from sparse binary outcome rewards that arrive only after a full response is generated, leading to either high computational overhead (value-model-based methods like PPO) or high-variance Monte Carlo advantage estimates that cause training instability and advantage collapse (value-model-free methods like GRPO). This paper asks whether the RLVR objective can be reformulated as a supervised learning problem that avoids both the sparsity and variance issues inherent to the RL paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text query (e.g., a math problem) and generates a full reasoning chain ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format. The key distinction from standard RLVR is that the binary outcome reward R(q,o) ∈ {0,1} is consumed as a supervised classification label rather than an RL reward signal, and the score function ψ is computed from per-token log-probability ratios between the policy and a periodically-reset reference policy, aggregated via RLOO leave-one-out averaging over G=8 sampled responses per query.

## Method

PACS recasts RLVR as binary cross-entropy classification: the policy-parameterized score function ψ(q,o;π_θ) = β·log(π_θ(o|q)/π_ref(o|q)) is passed through a sigmoid and trained to predict the ground-truth reward R(q,o) via cross-entropy loss. Gradient analysis (Eq. 4) shows the resulting update decomposes into an actor term (policy gradient weighted by per-sample cross-entropy) and a critic term (reward prediction residual), unifying both roles in a single shared-parameter network without separate value heads or alternating schedules. The RLOO leave-one-out estimator provides unbiased relative advantage scores with lower variance than group-mean baselines, and the sigmoid-bounded residual naturally attenuates updates as predictions converge, implicitly regularizing the policy toward a Gibbs distribution that prevents entropy collapse. A periodic hard-reset of the reference policy to a recent policy snapshot prevents unbounded growth of the log-probability ratio reward proxy over training.

## Results

PACS achieves average pass@1 scores of 62.63% (4B) and 63.32% (8B) across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, yielding gains of +8.26% and +9.57% over the respective Qwen3 base models. On the hardest benchmark BeyondAIME, PACS-8B reaches 64.02% at pass@64, surpassing GRPO (57.64%) and PPO (53.80%) by 6.4 and 10.2 points respectively. Notably, PACS-4B (45.63% on AIME 2025) outperforms the significantly larger Qwen3-14B (36.22%), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. PACS also maintains higher solution diversity (0.0392 vs. 0.0249 on AIME 2025) and superior out-of-domain transfer (45.96 on LiveCodeBench vs. 38.01 for GRPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RL baselines trained on the same base models, PACS consistently outperforms both PPO and GRPO across all four core benchmarks (AMC23, AIME 2024, AIME 2025, BeyondAIME) at every pass@k setting; the gap is largest on BeyondAIME where PACS-8B leads GRPO by 6.4 points at pass@64 (64.02% vs. 57.64%). Against open-source reasoning models, PACS-4B surpasses all 7B-scale competitors (LIMO 52.05%, OpenThinker3-7B 47.52%, Sky-T1-7B 40.97%) and even the larger Qwen3-14B (56.16% avg.) by 6.5 points on average. On out-of-domain tasks, PACS achieves 45.96 on LiveCodeBench and 52.46 on GPQA, outperforming GRPO (38.01 / 48.55) and PPO (35.87 / 48.48), confirming that gains are not domain-specific overfitting. In diversity analysis, PACS-8B yields a pairwise cosine distance of 0.0392 on AIME 2025 versus 0.0249 for GRPO, indicating substantially richer solution exploration. The only comparison where PACS does not lead is against closed-source frontier models (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 86.18% avg.), which operate at a fundamentally different scale and compute budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACS departs from the standard RLVR optimization paradigm (PPO, GRPO, DAPO) by reframing policy learning as a supervised binary classification problem where the verifiable outcome reward serves as the label and the policy model parameterizes a sigmoid score function trained with cross-entropy loss. The score function is instantiated using the RLOO leave-one-out estimator (Kool et al., 2019) applied to log-probability ratios relative to a reference policy, borrowing the group-relative advantage concept from GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) but repurposing it as a supervised target rather than a policy gradient weight. A key theoretical contribution is showing that the resulting gradient decomposes into an actor term (policy improvement weighted by cross-entropy loss) and a critic term (reward prediction residual), implicitly unifying both roles within shared parameters without separate value networks or alternating schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PACS addresses two persistent pain points in RLVR training—sparse-reward-induced gradient collapse (when all group rewards are identical) and entropy collapse toward deterministic policies—through a single supervised objective, which is a practical simplification for practitioners. The demonstrated parameter efficiency (PACS-4B outperforming Qwen3-14B on AIME 2025) and strong out-of-domain transfer to code and science QA suggest the learned reasoning is not merely domain-specific template memorization. Open questions include whether the implicit Gibbs regularization remains sufficient at larger scales or with more complex reward structures, whether the periodic reference-policy reset strategy generalizes beyond math, and whether the supervised framing can be extended to multi-turn or tool-use settings where rewards are not simply binary.
