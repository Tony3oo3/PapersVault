## Problem

Frozen LVLMs produce unstable, hallucinated localizations for rare-disease brain MRI grounding because single-pass inference is highly sensitive to prompt phrasing and minor visual perturbations, and the data scarcity of long-tailed pathologies makes supervised fine-tuning infeasible. Existing adaptation methods require parameter updates and curated labels per disease category, which do not scale to open-world clinical deployment where rare abnormalities are often indistinguishable from healthy tissue and cannot be annotated at scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a brain MRI scan and a natural language instruction (e.g., 'Identify all abnormalities in this scan') via a frozen Qwen2.5-VL model (3B–72B parameters), and outputs a consolidated set of 2D abnormality bounding boxes in JSON format, each paired with a consensus reliability score σ ∈ [0,1]. A 100-sample development set drives prompt optimization, and per test image the pipeline generates M=7 spatial-preserving augmentations (rotations ±3°, scaling ×0.9–1.1, translations ±20 px, horizontal flips) processed in a single vLLM batch alongside the original view.

## Method

DDL decomposes test-time adaptation into two complementary spaces without any parameter updates. In the language space, DAPE iteratively evolves instruction prompts using a meta-LLM (Gemini 3 Flash) that partitions the cumulative instruction history into success and failure tails, then synthesizes new candidates via contrastive refinement until top-k performance stabilizes. In the visual space, V-PUP runs the frozen LVLM on M=7 spatial-preserving perturbations of the input, and RHC consolidates the resulting predictions by inverse-transforming each view's boxes to reference coordinates and solving a bipartite matching problem via the Hungarian algorithm anchored to the original image's detections. Each final bounding box receives a reliability score σ = 0.6·Consensus + 0.4·Consistency, where Consensus is the fraction of views reproducing the detection and Consistency is the mean IoU across matched views. The core insight is that genuine anatomical abnormalities remain spatially invariant under small geometric perturbations while hallucinated detections are visually unstable, enabling perturbation-based verification to separate robust diagnostic signals from stochastic decoding noise.

## Results

On the NOVA rare-disease benchmark (281 pathology types), DDL improves mAP@75 by up to 104.7% over the vanilla baseline (e.g., 7B: 0.036 → 0.075) and by 66–105% across model scales (3B–72B). On the BTD common-pathology benchmark, DDL achieves consistent gains at strict thresholds (e.g., 32B: mAP@75 0.160 → 0.206, +28.7%). DDL also outperforms supervised fine-tuning on NOVA at all IoU thresholds (3B: 0.298 vs. 0.283 mAP@25; 0.066 vs. 0.046 mAP@75), demonstrating that test-time stabilization is more effective than parameter adaptation under extreme data sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDL dominates all prompting-based baselines on the NOVA rare-disease benchmark, with the largest margins at strict localization thresholds: it outperforms Meta-Optimization (Yang et al., 2023) by 35% on mAP@75 (0.066 vs. 0.049) and Gradient-based Optimization (Pryzant et al., 2023) by over 20× (0.066 vs. 0.003). On BTD (common pathologies), DDL is competitive with Meta-Opt (0.403 vs. 0.408 mAP@25) and VISER (Izadi et al., 2025) (0.403 vs. 0.412 mAP@25) but wins at mAP@75 (0.283 vs. 0.269 and 0.257 respectively). Against training-based methods, DDL clearly outperforms SFT on NOVA (0.298 vs. 0.283 mAP@25; 0.066 vs. 0.046 mAP@75) and LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) (0.298 vs. 0.225 mAP@25), confirming that inference-time stabilization surpasses parameter adaptation under data scarcity. However, on BTD, full SFT (Zheng et al., 2024) still edges out DDL at coarse thresholds (0.479 vs. 0.403 mAP@25; 0.308 vs. 0.283 mAP@75), indicating that when sufficient labels exist for common pathologies, supervised training remains competitive. Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022), Visual-Instruction (Wang et al., 2025), and Gradient Optimization (Pryzant et al., 2023) all degrade severely on NOVA, with mAP@75 dropping below 0.005, highlighting that linguistic reasoning alone cannot compensate for perceptual instability on rare pathologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDL carves new ground by repurposing three established ideas—LLM-based prompt optimization (Yang et al., 2023; Pryzant et al., 2023), test-time adaptation (Wang et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2020), and ensemble/self-verification (Kossen et al., 2024)—into a unified inference-time framework for clinical grounding, without any parameter updates. It departs from weight-based TTA by operating entirely in output space: prompt evolution in language space (DAPE) and perturbation-based spatial consensus in visual space (V-PUP + RHC). The use of the Hungarian algorithm for one-to-one multi-view detection consolidation, anchored to a reference prediction, is a novel application of classical assignment to LVLM grounding, distinguishing it from reference-free aggregation (averaging, DBSCAN) used in prior ensemble work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that for rare-disease grounding where annotated data is practically unobtainable, test-time verification can outperform supervised fine-tuning—a finding with direct implications for open-world clinical deployment where retraining per pathology is infeasible. The calibration analysis revealing that consensus reliability scores only become predictive of localization accuracy at 32B+ scale and under distribution shift highlights a previously underexplored interaction between model capacity and inference-time verification. Key limitations include the 8× test-time computation cost (7 augmented views per image), reliance on a proprietary meta-LLM (Gemini) for prompt evolution, and evaluation restricted to brain MRI with a single LVLM family (Qwen2.5-VL). Open questions include amortizing the multi-view verification cost, extending to 3D volumetric data and other modalities, and whether learned uncertainty models can replace the heuristic consensus score.
