## Problem

Role-playing LLMs face a fundamental character-user conflict: preserving role authenticity while meeting user needs. Prior dual-perspective approaches (prompt conditioning, auxiliary modules, multi-agent reflection) realize duality externally, leaving the base attention mechanism to process both perspectives uniformly without explicit factorization. This paper argues that dual-perspective reasoning must be intrinsic to the generation mechanism to enable token-level dynamic arbitration between self-understanding and other-understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a structured input of role description R (character traits, knowledge boundaries, behavioral constraints), user query U (intent, emotional cues, task specifications), and dialogue history D. Role context is preprocessed via spaCy NER and syntactic parsing to extract trait, knowledge, and temporal sub-representations; user intent is parsed by a lightweight BERT classifier (87.3% accuracy). The output is a role-playing text response generated token-by-token, where each token carries an interpretable fusion weight α (self-perspective trust) and β (other-perspective trust) that dynamically balances character voice against user responsiveness at every generation step.

## Method

KSKT extends Qwen3-4B-Thinking with four integrated components operating at every 4th of 36 layers. (1) Dual-Stream Axial Attention factorizes attention into separate self and other streams with independent QKV projections and learned attention biases, fused via token-level simplex-constrained weights (α+β=1) that enforce a strict trade-off rather than signal accumulation. (2) Mutual-Understanding Position Encoding augments RoPE with learned MLP-based relevance signals encoding each position's importance to role context versus user intent. (3) Bipolar Reasoning allocates deliberation budget T∈{2,4,6,8,10} via a two-stage gate (pre-gate triggers System-2 thinking chain ~25% of the time; post-gate fuses fast/slow representations), supervised by heuristic conflict scores from fusion-weight divergence and expert-routing entropy. (4) Self-Awareness MoE routes to four dimension-specific experts (Personality, Knowledge, Emotion, Capability) using character-conditioned queries extracted via cross-attention over role context. Training follows a three-phase curriculum (2+1+1 epochs) that progressively activates auxiliary losses—consistency, understanding, then balance—on 130K mixed samples, with the architectural factorization accounting for ~75% of the gain over a supervision-matched control.

## Results

On CharacterBench, KSKT (4.1B params) achieves 3.92/3.87 (zh/en) average, a 6.2% overall improvement over the Qwen3-4B-Thinking base (3.69/3.63), with the largest gains in Persona (Attribute Consistency: 4.73 vs. 4.35) and Emotion (Empathetic Responsiveness: 3.40 vs. 2.95, +15.3%). On SOTOPIA, KSKT improves the Relationship dimension (explicit self-other coordination) by 19.3% (1.45→1.73) and Goal by 12.4% (7.07→7.95) over the base model. At 4.1B parameters, KSKT surpasses Qwen2-72B-Instruct (3.80/3.68) with ~18× fewer parameters and is competitive with frontier closed-source models (Claude Opus 4: 3.89/3.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KSKT outperforms all evaluated baselines on CharacterBench. Against the Qwen3-4B-Thinking base (3.69/3.63), KSKT gains +0.23/+0.24, with an iso-supervision control (Wider-FT + Proxy Losses) attributing ~75% of the gain to the dual-stream factorization architecture rather than richer supervision. KSKT (4.1B) exceeds Qwen2-72B-Instruct (3.80/3.68) on both languages despite ~18× fewer parameters. Among dedicated role-playing methods, KSKT surpasses Character-R1 (3.89/3.78) by +0.03/+0.09, RAR (3.80/3.68) by +0.12/+0.19, and Neeko (3.50/3.38) by a wide margin, even though those methods are trained on a stronger base (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 3.79 vs. KSKT's 3.69 base). On SOTOPIA, KSKT's largest gain is on Relationship (+19.3%), the dimension requiring explicit self-other coordination, while prompting (3-shot) cannot close the gap: Base+3-shot reaches Overall 3.28 vs. KSKT's 3.30 without prompting, with a persistent Relationship gap (1.58 vs. 1.73).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes axial attention (Ho et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2020) from spatial factorization to perspective factorization, splitting self-understanding (role constraints) and other-understanding (user intent) into separate attention streams with learned fusion. It draws on dual-process cognitive science (Stanovich, Sloman, Evans) and MoE routing (Jacobs et al., 1991; Fedus et al., 2022) to add adaptive deliberation depth and dimension-specific expert specialization. The key departure from prior dual-perspective work (prompt-level conditioning, auxiliary modules, cross-model reflection) is making the duality intrinsic to the attention mechanism itself, enabling token-level arbitration rather than post-hoc reconciliation. The base model is Qwen3-4B-Thinking, and the training curriculum borrows from progressive loss activation strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that targeted architectural factorization can rival 18× parameter scaling for role-playing quality, with a well-controlled attribution analysis showing ~75% of gains are architectural rather than data/supervision-driven. This suggests that for multi-perspective coordination tasks, inductive bias design may matter more than brute-force scale at smaller model sizes. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic training dialogues (limiting external validity), persistent arbitration failures under extreme conflicts (42.5% of failures are architectural), and an inability to handle third-order adversarial reasoning even at maximum deliberation budget. Open questions include whether the inductive bias generalizes to broader alignment settings (helpfulness-honesty trade-offs) and how it scales when re-trained on larger base models.
