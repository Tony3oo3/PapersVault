## Problem

Existing decentralized generative approaches (federated learning, mixture-of-experts) can only model the union of siloed data and fail to generate novel attribute combinations that no single data source has observed. When the factors required for composition are distributed across isolated silos, federated averaging produces incoherent models and independently trained experts occupy incompatible velocity spaces, making standard composition techniques invalid. The paper asks how to recover compositional structure when no single data source contains sufficient information to support composition on its own and raw data cannot be shared.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian latent noise vector x₀ ~ N(0,I) and a discrete attribute vector y = (y₁,…,y_k) specifying a combination of conditions, and produces a sample in the data space via ODE integration of a learned velocity field. Critically, y can specify attribute combinations never observed by any individual client (e.g., a digit with a color pairing absent from all local datasets). For images, outputs are 28×28×3 (Colored MNIST) or 256×256 latent-space chest X-rays; for robotics, outputs are full state-action trajectory sequences of horizon H over a 3D workspace.

## Method

DCFM enforces global conditional independence across data silos through a shared flow-matching velocity space. Stage I trains local experts with marginal-label conditioning (randomly masking attributes to null tokens) and a local CI penalty that penalizes the discrepancy between the joint velocity and the sum of marginal velocity differences. DCFM-A then performs an all-gather of expert parameters and trains each local model with a cross-peer CI penalty: a routing vector assigns potentially different frozen peer experts to each marginal attribute, and the local model is pushed to match the composed velocity from those peers, learning to disentangle attributes across silos. DCFM-B instead distills all local experts into a single monolithic student via synthetic data replay (ODE-integrating frozen teacher paths to generate intermediate states) combined with a global CI penalty on the student's own velocity field, yielding a single model that can sample novel compositions in one forward pass without multi-expert routing.

## Results

On Colored MNIST (10 clients, 50% coverage), DCFM-A achieves FID* of 11.41 (IID) and 9.29 (Non-IID), while DCFM-B achieves 12.24 and 9.15 respectively, substantially outperforming the best baseline (DDM+L at 16.38/17.12). On OGBench robotic planning (2 clients, 75% coverage), DCFM-A reaches 56.33% success rate on novel diagonal movements (IID) and 53.0% (Non-IID), compared to 40.0% and 18.33% for the best baseline (DFD+L). On NIH Chest X-ray 14 (4 clients, 14 binary disease attributes), DCFM achieves comparable FID to baselines but yields a downstream classifier with higher compositional recall (sensitivity) on rare disease co-occurrences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCFM decisively outperforms all three baselines on novel composition metrics across every benchmark. On Colored MNIST, DCFM-A/B reduce FID* by 40–75% relative to the best baseline (DDM+L: 16.38 IID / 17.12 Non-IID vs. DCFM-A: 11.41 / 9.29), with DFD being the worst on novel compositions (FID* = 38.27 Non-IID). On robotic planning, DCFM-A achieves 53.0% success rate on unseen diagonal movements under Non-IID, nearly 3× the best baseline (DFD+L: 18.33%) and ~6× FedFlow+L (9.0%). On known compositions, DFD slightly edges out DCFM on FID^o under Non-IID (7.02 vs. 7.33), but this advantage is negligible compared to the large gap on novel compositions. The key differentiator is that DCFM enforces global conditional independence across experts, whereas FedFlow assumes parameter averaging yields a coherent model, DDM lacks structural alignment between independently trained experts, and DFD's energy-based routing collapses toward observed data regions under non-IID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCFM sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: compositional generative modeling (Liu et al., 2022; Gaudi et al., 2025) and decentralized/federated generative learning (Tun et al., 2023; McAllister et al., 2025; Hahn & Lee, 2025). It builds directly on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as its generative backbone and extends the compositional score factorization from diffusion models to flow velocities (Eq. 5), while adapting the CI penalty from CoInD (Gaudi et al., 2025) to a cross-peer setting. The key departure is that prior compositional methods assume centralized data or compatible expert score spaces, and prior decentralized methods assume parameter averaging or energy-based routing suffices; DCFM explicitly targets the regime where neither assumption holds, enforcing global conditional independence across structurally incompatible local experts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and demonstrates a concrete failure mode: local conditional independence is provably insufficient for global compositional generalization under non-IID data, a gap that prior decentralized generative work overlooked. DCFM-B's distillation into a single student model with constant communication cost (2 units) offers a practical path for privacy-sensitive deployments in healthcare and robotics where data silos are the norm. Open questions include whether the minimum coverage threshold (Definition 3.1) is tight, how the framework scales to high-cardinality attribute spaces (beyond k=14), and whether the diversity loss observed in DCFM-B can be mitigated without sacrificing the global CI guarantee.
