## Problem

Existing video hallucination research targets isolated error types (e.g., action misinterpretation or temporal inconsistency), but in real-world videos, hallucinations emerge as entangled, multi-factor compositional errors where spatial and temporal cues interact within a single query. This paper identifies that even state-of-the-art VLLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL, GPT-5) suffer substantial accuracy drops (5.71%–9.32%) when transitioning from single-type to compositional hallucination queries, yet no systematic benchmark or decoding-time mitigation method addresses this regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (typically 2–10 seconds, from real-world or AI-generated sources) and a textual query in either binary yes/no or multiple-choice format, and produces a calibrated answer by reweighting the VLLM's token-level logit distribution at inference time without updating any backbone parameters. The benchmark (OmniVCHall) pairs 823 videos with 9,027 VQA samples across four sub-tasks (S_YNQA, C_YNQA, S_MCQA, C_MCQA), and MCQA items include adversarial options such as 'All are correct' or 'None of the above' to force explicit cross-modal verification rather than elimination-based shortcuts.

## Method

TriCD is a triple-pathway contrastive decoding framework that computes three logit streams per token: an original baseline, a negative logit from an Adaptive Perturbation Controller (APC) that dynamically selects a subset of eight video perturbation tools (e.g., reverse, shuffle, blur, mirror) via dual-stage cross-attention over the video-query hidden states, and a positive logit from a Saliency-Guided Enhancement (SGE) module that fuses DINOv3 spatial attention with Farneback optical-flow temporal saliency through a learnable gating weight β to reweight vision tokens. The final calibrated logit is q_t = q_t^o + α₁(q_t^p − q_t^o) + α₂(q_t^o − q_t^n), widening the margin between grounded evidence and hallucination pathways. APC and SGE are jointly optimized with REINFORCE using a sparse ±1 reward on final answer accuracy while the VLLM remains frozen, making the approach plug-and-play across backbones. Ablations show APC contributes more to compositional gains than SGE, and random perturbation degrades performance below the uncalibrated backbone, confirming that context-aware tool selection is essential.

## Results

On the OmniVCHall benchmark, TriCD achieves average accuracy improvements of 9.61% (Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B: 0.67→0.73), 12.03% (VideoLLaMA3-7B: 0.60→0.67), 11.48% (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B: 0.45→0.51), 9.24% (InternVL3.5-8B: 0.62→0.68), and 9.76% (VideoChat-Flash-7B: 0.56→0.62) over their respective backbones. The largest gains appear on compositional sub-tasks: C_YNQA improves by up to 9 percentage points and C_MCQA by up to 11 percentage points (e.g., Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B C_MCQA: 0.53→0.64). Cross-benchmark transfer to VideoHallucer and EventHallusion yields consistent gains averaging 11% over the backbone, confirming generalizability beyond the training set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriCD is compared against three contrastive-decoding baselines: MotionCD (Kong et al., 2025), which penalizes logits from motion-corrupted negatives; TCD (Zhang et al., 2024a), which contrasts original frames with temporally distorted sequences; and DINO-HEAL (Li et al., 2025a), which uses DINOv2 saliency to reweight vision tokens. TriCD wins on every backbone and every sub-task, with average gains of 9.24–12.03% versus 1.65–4.47% for the best baseline (TCD). The advantage is most pronounced on compositional tasks: on C_MCQA, TriCD improves Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B from 0.53 to 0.64, while TCD reaches only 0.55 and MotionCD only 0.54. On the cross-benchmark transfer setting (VideoHallucer + EventHallusion), TriCD again dominates, achieving 5.48–20.22% relative gains versus 0.60–4.94% for baselines, with the largest margin on weaker backbones (e.g., VideoChat-Flash-7B: TriCD ↑20.22% vs. TCD ↑2.53%). Ablations confirm that removing the Adaptive Perturbation Controller (APC) drops accuracy more than removing Saliency-Guided Enhancement (SGE), and replacing APC with random perturbation actually degrades performance below the unmodified backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding (CD) paradigm for hallucination mitigation from static, uniform perturbations (TCD, MotionCD) and single-saliency enhancement (DINO-HEAL) to a unified triple-pathway framework that adaptively selects context-aware negative perturbations via a learned controller and fuses spatial (DINOv3) with temporal (Farneback optical flow) saliency for positive evidence anchoring. The key departure is formulating perturbation selection and saliency fusion as a reinforcement learning problem (REINFORCE with sparse accuracy reward), enabling instance-specific calibration without updating VLLM parameters. Conceptually, the paper also shifts the evaluation focus from isolated single-type hallucinations to compositional hallucinations—where multiple interacting evidence types must be jointly verified—drawing on the minimal-sufficiency definition from explanation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18% accuracy gap between the best VLLM (Gemini-3-pro, 0.77) and human evaluators (0.95) on OmniVCHall, combined with the finding that real-world videos are harder than AI-generated ones (contrary to intuition), highlights that dense spatiotemporal entanglement—not scenario novelty—is the primary bottleneck for compositional reasoning. The introduction of camera-based hallucination as a distinct failure mode and the demonstration that random perturbation actually degrades performance below the backbone (0.63 vs. 0.67) underscore the need for learned, context-aware calibration. Open questions include whether the RL-optimized policy generalizes to longer, noisier, or safety-critical video settings beyond the curated 2–10 second clips, and whether the compositional framing transfers to other modalities (e.g., audio-visual).
