## Problem

The HiPPO matrix, widely used to initialize Linear State Space Layers (LSSLs) in models like S4 and S5, has a conjectured asymptotic eigenvalue formula for its normal part that remained unproven, and the resulting SSM solution is only available implicitly through operator exponentials. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating a continuous limit of the HiPPO matrix as an operator on an L² function space, enabling rigorous eigenvalue analysis and an explicit closed-form solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1D time-series signal u(t) and produces a processed sequence y(t) via convolution with an SSM kernel. The kernel is parameterized by a continuous output function c(x) over [0,N] and a time step Δt, and is computed explicitly as e^{−t/2}∫₀^N c(x)√(2x+1)·J₀(2√t·√(x²+x)) dx, where J₀ is the Bessel function of the first kind. In the empirical validation, flattened 28×28 MNIST images (length-784 sequences) are processed to produce class labels.

## Method

The paper introduces the Continuized-HiPPO (CHIP) Operator, defined as the continuous spatial limit of the HiPPO matrix: a separable Volterra integral operator K_{iχ,iχ̄} plus a scalar shift −½I on L²([0,N]), where χ(x)=√(2x+1). The key structural insight is that powers of a separable Volterra integral operator remain Volterra operators with a simple kernel involving (∫φψ)^{n−1}/(n−1)!, which allows the operator exponential exp(tK) to be evaluated in closed form. This yields an explicit SSM convolution kernel expressed as a Bessel-function integral (Theorem 6.1), and separately, the eigenvalues of the skew-symmetric part are computed exactly as (N²+N)/((2k+1)iπ), proving the conjectured asymptotic τₙ^(N) = N²/((2n−1)π) − (2n−1)π/6 + o(1) via a norm-convergence argument between the discrete and continuous operators.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves the conjectured eigenvalue asymptotics for the normal part of the HiPPO matrix, showing τ_n^(N) = N²/((2n−1)π) − (2n−1)π/6 + o(1), and derives an explicit Bessel-function-based solution formula for the LSSL induced by the Continuized-HiPPO (CHIP) operator. As an empirical sanity check on Sequential MNIST (784-length sequences, d_model=64, N=64), the explicit-kernel model achieves 97.85% validation accuracy and 0.0666 validation loss, falling between the S4 model with fixed HiPPO parameters (97.32%, 0.0847) and the S4 model with learnable A and B (98.7%, 0.0404).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed explicit-kernel model (derived from the CHIP operator) against two S4-style baselines on Sequential MNIST: one with fixed HiPPO A and B matrices, and one with learnable DPLR-structured A and B. The explicit-kernel model outperforms the fixed-HiPPO S4 baseline by 0.53 percentage points in accuracy (97.85% vs. 97.32%) and reduces validation loss from 0.0847 to 0.0666, suggesting the Bessel-function kernel captures the HiPPO dynamics more faithfully than the discrete matrix at N=64. However, it trails the fully learnable S4 model by 0.85 percentage points in accuracy (97.85% vs. 98.7%) and 0.026 in validation loss, which is expected since the learnable S4 can adapt A and B to the task while the explicit-kernel model uses a fixed operator structure. The authors frame this experiment as a sanity check rather than a competitive benchmark, emphasizing that the main contribution is the theoretical eigenvalue analysis and explicit solution formula.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HiPPO matrix (Gu et al., 2020) and the Linear State Space Layer framework (Gu et al., 2021), positioning the HiPPO matrix as a trapezoidal-rule discretization of a continuous integral operator on L². The key conceptual departure is treating the state-space dimension N as a spatial variable and taking N→∞ to obtain the Continuized-HiPPO (CHIP) Operator, which then connects the S4/S4D/S5 line of structured state-space models to classical Volterra integral operator theory. The eigenvalue analysis resolves a conjecture from Gu et al. (2022a) that had remained unproven, and the explicit Bessel-function kernel representation draws on the theory of separable Volterra operators (Gustavson & Rosen, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rigorous proof of eigenvalue asymptotics (τₙ^(N) ~ N²/((2n−1)π) − (2n−1)π/6) provides the theoretical foundation that diagonal SSM initializations (S4D, S5) have relied on empirically, and the explicit Bessel-function kernel formula offers a closed-form alternative to computing the SSM impulse response via matrix exponentials. However, the empirical validation is limited to Sequential MNIST with a small model (d_model=64, N=64), and the explicit-kernel model does not outperform the learnable S4 baseline, leaving open whether the closed-form representation yields practical speedups or new architectural insights at scale. The SISO restriction and the single-dataset evaluation are notable limitations for assessing broader applicability.
