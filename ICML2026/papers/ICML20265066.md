## Problem

Whole-slide H&E-to-ST inference must map gigapixel histology images to high-dimensional gene expression vectors at sparse, irregular measurement locations, yet existing approaches either ignore spatial context (per-location predictors), propagate long-range structure slowly through fixed-range coupling, or waste computation on background regions with dense-grid backbones. No prior method simultaneously captures multiscale tissue context, operates directly on the active tissue footprint without padding, and remains robust to slide-specific acquisition variation under a compute budget proportional to the number of measured spots.

## Input / Output

The method takes a co-registered H&E whole-slide image and a set of N sparse 2D ST measurement coordinates as input; patch features are extracted at each coordinate via a frozen visual encoder, and the locations are mapped onto a discrete sampling lattice where only observed sites carry data. The output is a G-dimensional gene expression vector (e.g., 2000 highly variable genes) predicted at each measured location, trained in log-expression space with MSE. Because the tissue footprint is non-rectangular and irregular, the model never materializes a dense H'×W' grid—computation is confined to the N active sites and their local neighborhoods.

## Method

HiST builds a dyadic encoder-decoder hierarchy (analogous to a U-Net) directly on the sparse lattice of measured locations, alternating monoscale refinement blocks with dyadic coarsening/uncoarsening transitions that reduce token count by ~4× per level, making total compute a convergent geometric series dominated by the finest scale. Within each level, a Dual-Path Attention mechanism combines sparse windowed multi-head self-attention (with learnable relative positional bias over lattice offsets) for local geometric correspondence with cross-attention to a single slide calibration token that acts as a low-rank global bottleneck for distributional correction. The coarsening operator aggregates up to four children per 2×2 parent cell with learned per-offset weights, while uncoarsening uses an MLP followed by channel-to-space pixel shuffle to scatter features back to active child sites. A slide calibration token, shared across slides but refreshed per forward pass via self-attention over all tissue tokens, provides a lightweight pathway to absorb slide-level acquisition shifts. Training uses supervised MSE in log-expression space with coordinate-only augmentations (random rotation, shear, mirror, spot dropout) applied to the lattice indices while keeping patch features fixed, enforcing geometric robustness without re-encoding images.

## Results

On the HEST-1k multi-organ benchmark (11 datasets, 4-fold slide-level cross-validation, top 2,000 HVGs), HiST achieves an average R² of 0.34 and PCC of 0.59, improving over the strongest spatially-aware baseline STFlow (R² 0.27, PCC 0.54) by +26% and +9% respectively. HiST also attains the best or tied-best R² and PCC on most individual datasets. In terms of efficiency, HiST runs at 0.07 s/slide inference with 1.38 GB peak reserved memory, compared to STFlow's 0.76 s/slide and 46.37 GB, representing roughly a 10× speedup and 33× memory reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiST outperforms all four baselines on average R² and PCC. Against ST-Net (He et al., 2020), which predicts each location independently from its local patch, HiST gains substantially by integrating multiscale spatial context. Against BLEEP (Xie et al., 2023), a retrieval-based method that searches a reference embedding bank at inference, HiST avoids the additional retrieval cost and achieves better accuracy with a single forward pass. Against UNI (Chen et al., 2024b), a foundation-model-based predictor, HiST matches or exceeds performance while being far more efficient. Against STFlow (Huang et al., 2025), the strongest spatially-aware iterative generative baseline, HiST improves average R² from 0.27 to 0.34 (+26%) and PCC from 0.54 to 0.59 (+9%), while reducing inference time from 0.76 s to 0.07 s per slide and peak reserved memory from 46.37 GB to 1.38 GB. HiST achieves the best average performance across all 11 datasets and attains the best or tied-best result on most individual organs.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiST synthesizes ideas from three distinct lines of work: (i) hierarchical multiscale backbones (U-Net, Swin Transformer) that expand receptive fields through resolution changes, (ii) sparse/irregular-domain processing that avoids computing on background, and (iii) global conditioning tokens (CLS, prefix-tuning) for domain adaptation. It departs from prior H&E-to-ST predictors by rejecting both the i.i.d. per-spot paradigm (ST-Net, BLEEP) and the fixed-range neighborhood coupling paradigm (graph message passing, STFlow's local attention), instead casting the problem as a lattice-indexed sparse field and applying dyadic coarsening/uncoarsening directly on the active tissue footprint. The paper explicitly positions itself as complementary to iterative generative approaches (flow matching, diffusion), arguing that an efficient supervised backbone can already achieve competitive accuracy without multi-step refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× inference speedup and ~30× peak-memory reduction over STFlow while matching or slightly improving accuracy is a significant practical win for whole-slide spatial transcriptomics prediction, where compute and memory are the primary bottlenecks at scale. However, absolute predictive accuracy remains modest (R² ≈ 0.34 on average), and the authors acknowledge that per-gene recovery for low-expression genes is still constrained, limiting near-term clinical utility. Open questions include whether the sparse hierarchical backbone can support super-resolution beyond the training grid, whether uncertainty quantification is feasible given the efficiency headroom, and whether the approach scales to pan-cancer foundation models or can serve as a backbone for iterative generative refinement on whole slides.
