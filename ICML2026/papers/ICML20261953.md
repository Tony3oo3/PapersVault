## Problem

Existing evolutionary model merging methods rely on stochastic, hand-crafted mutation operators that treat the coefficient search space as unstructured, leading to low sample efficiency under realistic constraints of small validation sets and limited evaluation budgets. The paper asks whether the search for optimal merging coefficients can be reformulated as a learnable generative task that captures the geometry of high-performance regions from historical search trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained base model θ_pre, N expert models fine-tuned from it (yielding task vectors τ_i = θ_i − θ_pre), and a small validation set D_val (200 samples). Optimization is restricted to the low-dimensional coefficient vector λ ∈ R^N (N = 8–10 in main experiments), and the output is the optimal λ* and the corresponding merged model θ(λ*) = θ_pre + Σ λ_i τ_i. Notably, the only trainable components are small 5-layer MLP generators operating in the N-dimensional coefficient space; the merged model itself requires no gradient-based training.

## Method

EvoGM replaces stochastic mutation with a dual-generator architecture (G_{−→+} and G_{+→−}, both 5-layer MLPs with tanh output) trained on winner-loser pairs constructed from the search history archive. Training combines a cycle-consistency loss (enforcing reversibility and preventing mode collapse) with an optimization-guided loss that pulls generated candidates toward the winner-set centroid. The forward generator G_{−→+} then serves as a learned evolutionary operator, transforming the entire population toward higher-performing regions each iteration. A distinctive multi-round 'basis shift' mechanism periodically replaces the expert pool with elite merged models and recomputes task vectors, allowing the search to escape local optima by progressively refining the available expert directions rather than searching over a static basis.

## Results

On the multi-task unseen-task setting (10 Qwen2.5-1.5B experts, 8 benchmarks), EvoGM achieves a leading average test score of 0.380, outperforming PSO-Merging (0.372), Model Swarm (0.372), and CMA (0.375), while the base model scores only 0.352 and multi-task fine-tuning drops to 0.330. On the seen-task GLUE benchmark (8 FLAN-T5 experts), EvoGM reaches 82.4 average accuracy, a 1.2-point gain over PSO-Merging (81.2) and a 12% relative improvement over PSO-Merging on STS-B (80.9 vs. 71.9). In the single-task unseen setting, EvoGM attains the highest test accuracy on 5 of 8 benchmarks, including a 15% relative gain over PSO-Merging on NLGraph (0.537 vs. 0.465).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoGM outperforms all merging-based baselines on the multi-task unseen-task average (0.380 vs. CMA 0.375, PSO-Merging 0.372, Model Swarm 0.372), with the largest margin on AbstainQA (0.130 vs. 0.078 for CMA, a 66% relative improvement). On the seen-task GLUE benchmark, EvoGM leads all 11 baselines in average accuracy (82.4 vs. PSO-Merging 81.2, CMA 79.4, TA 78.9), with particularly strong gains on STS-B (80.9 vs. 71.9 for PSO-Merging, +12% relative). In the single-task unseen setting, EvoGM wins on 5 of 8 test benchmarks, including NLGraph (0.537 vs. 0.465 for PSO-Merging) and GSM8K (0.434 vs. 0.354 for PSO-Merging), though it trails Model Swarm on MMLU (0.625 vs. 0.606) and MMLU-Pro (0.224 vs. 0.236). Notably, static merging methods (TA, TIES, DARE, Model Soup) and multi-task fine-tuning all underperform the base model or single best expert on several unseen tasks, confirming that evolutionary search is necessary for effective cross-domain transfer. The multi-round basis shift is shown to be specific to EvoGM: applying the same schedule to PSO-Merging degrades its average from 0.4018 to 0.3425, while EvoGM improves to 0.4214.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoGM sits at the intersection of two research threads: evolutionary model merging (Akiba et al., 2025; Feng et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025), which reframes coefficient search as gradient-free population-based optimization, and generative optimization (Jiang et al., 2026), which learns structured proposal distributions from sparse evaluative feedback. The key departure from prior evolutionary merging methods (CMA-ES, PSO-Merging, Model Swarm) is replacing hand-crafted stochastic mutation/crossover operators with a dual-generator architecture trained via cycle-consistency and winner-loser preference signals. The task-vector formulation from task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) provides the low-dimensional optimization space, but EvoGM's contribution is making the search over that space data-driven and adaptive rather than purely stochastic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free model merging is increasingly important for amortizing fine-tuning costs across tasks, and EvoGM addresses the core bottleneck of sample efficiency under limited validation budgets. The finding that the basis-shift mechanism actively degrades PSO performance (Table 5: 0.4018 → 0.3425) while boosting EvoGM (0.4214) is a useful negative result showing the multi-round expert update is specifically synergistic with learned generative operators, not a generic search improvement. Key limitations remain: the framework requires homogeneous experts from a shared base model, optimizes a single scalar objective (no Pareto front), and the generative model is a small MLP that may not capture complex high-dimensional structure as expert counts grow beyond 20. Extending to heterogeneous architectures and preference-aware multi-objective merging are natural next steps.
