## Problem

Existing LiDAR adversarial attacks—whether point-level perturbations, sensor spoofing, or handcrafted mesh insertions—rarely induce complete object disappearance and are difficult to realize in physical environments. The adversarial potential of text-to-3D generative models for producing semantically plausible, physically deployable 3D objects that evade LiDAR-based 3D object detectors remains entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete verb-object-pose (VOP) prompt triplet sampled from a structured space of 7 verbs × 13 objects × 7 poses (637 combinations), an environment point cloud, and a closed-set pool of real-world physical objects. It produces a 3D adversarial human-object composition rendered as a LiDAR point cloud, the optimized discrete prompt, and a rigid transformation matrix (SE(3)) for aligning the selected physical object to the generated adversarial geometry. Unlike continuous perturbation methods, the output is a semantically interpretable, physically assemblable object combination rather than a modified point cloud.

## Method

OBJVanish first establishes through empirical analysis that multi-object compositions (e.g., a pedestrian holding an umbrella) dominate LiDAR invisibility over single-object attribute changes like topology or intensity modifications. The core optimization operates in the latent space of a frozen text encoder: three learnable logit vectors parameterize categorical distributions over verbs, objects, and poses, relaxed via Gumbel-Softmax to enable gradient-based optimization. The adversarial objective minimizes the detection confidence from a differentiable surrogate LiDAR detector (e.g., PointRCNN) on the generated object's rendered point cloud, with a clamped margin to prevent over-optimization. A Gaussian Splatting-based text-to-3D generator (LGM) synthesizes the 3D human-object composition from the optimized prompt, which is then differentiably rendered into LiDAR point clouds. For physical deployment, the optimized object index is retrieved from a curated pool of real-world objects and aligned to the generated geometry via a rigid transformation, enabling low-cost assembly without custom fabrication.

## Results

OBJVanish achieves a 94.6% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on PointRCNN in the CARLA digital setup, substantially outperforming all five baselines (47.5%–79.3%). Cross-dataset transferability is strong: adversarial objects optimized in CARLA transfer zero-shot to KITTI (91.7% avg. ASR) and nuScenes (94.0% avg. ASR) without re-optimization. In physical deployment with a 128-beam RS-Ruby LiDAR, ASR ranges from 81.6% (PDV) to 90.3% (IA-SSD) across six SOTA detectors, with the highest ASR (96.2%) observed for IA-SSD under 64-beam configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBJVanish outperforms all five baselines by a wide margin on the primary PointRCNN/CARLA benchmark. The closest competitor is the re-implemented Tu et al. (2020) mesh-optimization method at 79.3% ASR, still 15.3 points below OBJVanish; the authors note that Tu et al. and Zheng et al. (2025) optimize 3D object geometry directly, limiting their optimization space compared to OBJVanish's discrete prompt search. Point-cloud perturbation methods (AdvPC at 64.2%, NI-FGSM at 62.9%) require direct manipulation of sensor data, making physical deployment impractical, and trail OBJVanish by roughly 30 points. ScAR (47.5%) is the weakest baseline, as it focuses on scaling adversarial robustness rather than generating evasive objects. Beyond the single-detector comparison, OBJVanish also demonstrates consistent evasion across all six evaluated detectors (PointRCNN, IA-SSD, SAFDNet, VoxelNeXt, HEDNet, PDV) with average ASR of 87.7% in the full prompt-optimization setting, whereas the baselines are only reported on PointRCNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: adversarial object attacks on LiDAR detectors (Tu et al. 2020, Cao et al. 2019b) and text-to-3D generation (LGM, Meshy, Shap-E). It departs from prior adversarial object work by replacing direct mesh/geometry optimization with discrete prompt optimization over a verb-object-pose space, using Gumbel-Softmax relaxation to make the combinatorial search differentiable. The generation backbone (LGM, a Gaussian Splatting-based model) is repurposed from its benign content-creation role into an adversarial pipeline, with differentiable LiDAR rendering closing the loop to a surrogate detector. The empirical detectability study (multi-object compositions vs. single-object attribute changes) is positioned as a prerequisite that motivates the compositional prompt design, distinguishing it from prior work that assumed single-object geometry optimization was sufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practically concerning attack vector: an attacker can assemble off-the-shelf objects (umbrellas, boxes, wheelchairs) in specific poses to make a pedestrian invisible to LiDAR detectors, without any custom fabrication or sensor access. The finding that multi-object compositions are the dominant vulnerability factor (reducing detection from ~90% to ~30-37%) is actionable for detector designers. Key limitations include the stationary-vehicle physical setup, reliance on a single surrogate detector for optimization, and the closed-set object pool that constrains real-world generality. Open questions include whether the attack transfers to multi-sensor (LiDAR + camera) fusion systems, how it scales to moving ego vehicles and denser LiDAR, and whether the stochastic nature of text-to-3D generation undermines reproducibility in adversarial settings.
