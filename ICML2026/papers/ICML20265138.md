## Problem

Existing heterogeneous federated fine-tuning methods (e.g., FlexLoRA, FedHL) rigidly couple the downloaded adapter rank to the local trainable rank, forcing compute-limited clients to discard high-rank global information they could afford to receive. This coupling wastes available bandwidth and, more critically, initializes clients in a heavily truncated subspace that induces compounding gradient drift over local epochs, degrading both convergence stability and cross-client generalization.

## Input / Output

The server receives per-client resource triplets (downlink bandwidth B_i, VRAM budget M_i, step-time budget T_i) and aggregated LoRA updates, then outputs a per-client, per-layer dual-rank configuration {r^tot, r^train} via water-filling. Clients receive full-r^tot LoRA adapters (B ∈ R^{d×r^tot}, A ∈ R^{r^tot×p}) but only upload the updated r^train-rank prefix, so uplink communication scales as O(r^train) while the forward pass exploits the full r^tot subspace. The server reconstructs the global model via residual aggregation that subtracts initialization terms to preserve truncated components losslessly.

## Method

FedHera decouples information reception from gradient optimization through a dual-rank mechanism. On the server side, a layer-wise spectrum-preserving water-filling algorithm (a greedy knapsack variant) allocates r^tot to maximize retained singular-value energy under bandwidth constraints, then allocates r^train ≤ r^train within the downloaded subspace under composite memory/compute costs with adaptive scalarization. On the client side, a prefix-gating mask restricts gradient flow to only the first r^train ranks while the frozen tail (r^tot − r^train) remains active in the forward pass as a stable spectral anchor, implicitly regularizing local updates against the global manifold. An Adaptive Tail Warm-up (ATW) module further modulates the frozen tail's contribution via a gating factor λ that grows from 0 toward 1 based on cosine alignment between the client's local update and the aggregated global update, preventing immature early-round tail directions from destabilizing optimization. The server aggregates via residual updates (subtracting initialization terms) to eliminate systematic bias from heterogeneous rank truncation.

## Results

Under the Skewed resource distribution, FEDHERA achieves the highest final-round accuracy across all six reasoning benchmarks, reaching 47.1% on GSM8K, 46.2% on MetaMathQA, 58.4% on HellaSwag, 42.2% on BoolQ, 58.0% on PIQA, and 51.7% on WinoGrande. On generative tasks (Skewed), FEDHERA attains ROUGE-L of 0.709 on Alpaca and 0.875 on E2E NLG, with the lowest evaluation loss (1.140 and 0.492 respectively). In drift analysis on Qwen3-0.6B/E2E NLG, FEDHERA achieves the lowest absolute drift (2.060) and relative drift (6.641), compared to 2.991/9.647 for FlexLoRA and 4.170/12.223 for FedHeLLo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDHERA consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task and metric. Against FlexLoRA (Bai et al., 2024), FEDHERA gains +1.4 pp on GSM8K (47.1 vs 45.7) and +2.9 pp on WinoGrande (51.7 vs 48.8), while reducing absolute drift from 2.991 to 2.060; the gap is largest on WinoGrande where the coupled rank strategy most severely limits the global basis. Against FedHL (Peng et al., 2025), FEDHERA leads by +0.8 pp on GSM8K and +2.5 pp on WinoGrande, with a marginal drift advantage (2.060 vs 2.072 absolute); on E2E NLG, FEDHERA achieves ROUGE-L 0.875 vs 0.865 and evaluation loss 0.492 vs 0.513. Against FedHeLLo (Zhang et al., 2025), the advantage is most pronounced: +1.0 pp on GSM8K, +2.6 pp on WinoGrande, and a large generative gap (ROUGE-L 0.875 vs 0.730 on E2E NLG; absolute drift 2.060 vs 4.170), reflecting that layer-pruning heterogeneity is less effective than rank decoupling. Against FLoRA (Wang et al., 2024), tested only in federation-scale diagnostics (N=100–500), FEDHERA maintains lower loss (0.579 vs 0.642 at N=100), higher ROUGE-L (0.790 vs 0.773), and lower drift (2.060 vs 2.416 absolute), with the gap widening as N increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDHERA builds directly on the SVD-based heterogeneous federated LoRA line initiated by FlexLoRA (Bai et al., 2024) and extended by FedHL (Peng et al., 2025), both of which aggregate reconstructed weight updates and redistribute adapters via truncated SVD. The key departure is conceptual: all prior methods enforce r^train ≡ r^tot, conflating communication bandwidth with optimizer-state memory, whereas FEDHERA explicitly decouples these into a dual-rank (r^tot, r^train) configuration. The server-side residual aggregation is adopted from FedHL as a stabilization plug-in, while the novel contributions—the spectrum-preserving water-filling allocator, prefix-gated training with a frozen spectral tail, and Adaptive Tail Warm-up—are client-side mechanisms orthogonal to the aggregation choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling insight is practically significant for edge deployments (e.g., Jetson, smartphones) where WiFi 6/5G bandwidth far exceeds VRAM capacity for gradient computation; the 2.27× information gain ratio quantifies how much more global basis a client can leverage per unit of training memory. However, the performance margins over the strongest baseline (FedHL) are modest (e.g., 47.1% vs 46.3% on GSM8K-Skewed), and the evaluation is entirely simulation-based with calibrated rank-equivalent budgets rather than real heterogeneous hardware. Open questions include whether the frozen-tail anchoring generalizes to extreme non-IID settings (the paper's robustness test is preliminary), whether the water-filling allocator's advantage persists at much larger model scales, and whether the theoretical bound (adapted from FlexLoRA) can be tightened to reflect the dual-rank structure more precisely.
