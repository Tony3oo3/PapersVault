## Problem

Most graph neural networks assume undirected edges, and naive adaptations to directed graphs amplify oversmoothing and gradient pathologies that cap model depth. While unitary convolutions (UniGCN) provably prevent representational collapse, they do not incorporate edge directionality or edge features, leaving a gap for directed graphs with rich edge information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a (not necessarily symmetric) directed adjacency matrix, a node feature matrix, and per-edge feature vectors. Edge features are compressed via a trainable map into a single complex scalar per edge, forming an edge-weighted adjacency matrix that feeds the unitary propagator. Internally the network operates in complex space; for real-valued prediction tasks the real component of the final embedding is extracted.

## Method

Dune constructs a skew-Hermitian generator Π(A) = (i/4)(A + A†) + (1/4)(A − A†) from the directed adjacency matrix, guaranteeing that exp(Π(A)t) is unitary for any t > 0 and thus preserving Frobenius norms at every layer. This eliminates both exponential oversmoothing and vanishing/exploding gradients without skip connections or normalization. Edge features are incorporated by building a complex-valued edge-weighted adjacency matrix A_E via a trainable scalar map, which then replaces A in the Hermitian projection. The matrix exponential is evaluated through a low-order Taylor approximation (typically T ≤ 10 terms). In hybrid architectures, Dune serves as a message-passing backbone for graph transformers, where its wave-like propagation implicitly encodes bidirectional walk profiles, reducing the reliance on expensive spectral positional encodings such as Multi-q Magnetic-Laplacian eigendecompositions.

## Results

On 7 directed node-classification benchmarks, Dune achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA accuracy, with the largest gains on OGBN-Arxiv (72.16% vs. 68.76% for NDDGNN, +3.4 pp), SNAP-Patents (75.12% vs. 74.15%), and Questions (80.45% ROC-AUC vs. 77.58%). In hybrid SAT+GNN graph regression on the Open Circuit Benchmark and HLS datasets, Dune as a message-passing backbone with no positional encoding already matches or exceeds other backbones that rely on Magnetic-Laplacian multi-q encodings (e.g., 2.612 RMSE on DSP vs. 2.616 for bidirected-GIN + Maglap-Multi-q). Dune remains trainable beyond 100 layers, whereas non-unitary baselines such as GCN diverge at 64 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest directed MPNN baseline NDDGNN, Dune wins on 5 of 7 datasets (OGBN-Arxiv +3.4 pp, Arxiv-Year +0.8 pp, SNAP-Patents +1.0 pp, Roman-Empire +0.8 pp, Questions +2.9 pp ROC-AUC), with NDDGNN retaining a narrow edge on Cora (88.14 vs. 87.82). Compared to the undirected unitary predecessor UniGCN, Dune's incorporation of directionality yields large gains, most notably on SNAP-Patents (+10.45 pp) and Roman-Empire (+5.37 pp), confirming that directionality is critical. Against directed attention-based models (Dir-GAT, Dir-SAGE), Dune outperforms on all heterophilic and large-scale datasets (e.g., Roman-Empire 92.58 vs. 82.25 for Dir-GAT) while Dir-GAT holds a marginal 0.09 pp advantage on Citeseer. In the hybrid SAT setting (Table 3), Dune as a backbone with no positional encoding already matches or beats bidirected-GIN + Maglap-Multi-q on all five regression targets (e.g., DSP 2.612 vs. 2.616; LUT 2.021 vs. 2.082), indicating that unitary convolutions implicitly encode the geometric information that dedicated PEs are designed to supply. On undirected graph benchmarks (Table 15), Dune also outperforms GINE and GatedGCN, suggesting the unitary formulation is broadly beneficial beyond directed settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dune extends UniGCN (Kiani et al., 2024), which introduced unitary graph convolutions for undirected graphs via the exponential map of the adjacency matrix, to the directed setting by projecting the (generally asymmetric) adjacency matrix into the Lie algebra of the unitary group via a Hermitian projection. This simultaneously resolves the non-unitarity problem for directed graphs and allows edge features to be incorporated through an edge-weighted adjacency matrix. The paper also positions Dune against the directed MPNN literature (Dir-GCN, MagNet, Dir-GAT, NDDGNN) and the directed positional-encoding / graph-transformer literature (Geisler et al., Huang et al.), showing that unitary propagation can substitute for explicit spectral or random-walk encodings in hybrid architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dune provides the first provable no-oversmoothing and bounded-gradient guarantees for directed GNNs, enabling stable training beyond 100 layers where existing directed architectures degrade. The finding that unitary propagation implicitly encodes bidirectional walk profiles and reduces the marginal benefit of positional encodings in hybrid transformer architectures opens a path toward simpler, more scalable directed graph models. Key limitations include the edge-to-scalar compression bottleneck for rich edge features, scalability to SNAP-scale graphs, and the fact that the no-oversmoothing proof assumes unitary weight matrices without nonlinearities; directed link prediction and explicit learned positional encodings via unitary GNNs remain open directions.
