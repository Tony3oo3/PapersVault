## Problem

Large-scale Set Cover instances (10^5–10^9 variables) exceed the reach of exact MIP solvers, and existing neural LNS methods that select neighborhoods via point-estimate predictions fail to capture coordinated variable interactions and degrade under distribution shift when subproblem topology differs from training. The paper asks whether the intermediate denoising dynamics of a discrete diffusion model—rather than its final output—can serve as a transferable structural signal for identifying high-impact repair neighborhoods without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Set Cover instance as a bipartite incidence graph (sets × elements) together with a current feasible incumbent solution, and iteratively produces an improved feasible solution by identifying and repairing localized variable clusters. Crucially, the neural pipeline operates only on bounded 'halo' subproblems (≤ N_max sets) extracted via k-hop expansion around high-replaceability seeds, so GPU memory and inference cost remain constant regardless of global instance size. The intermediate output is a sensitivity-weighted affinity matrix over candidate sets, which is partitioned into K disjoint repair neighborhoods via METIS before sequential steepest-descent re-optimization.

## Method

GLNS is a five-stage pipeline: (i) halo-based decomposition extracts bounded subproblems around expensive, replaceable sets; (ii) a 6-layer bipartite GATv2 GNN predicts which variables likely change between the incumbent and a local optimum, trained with Focal + Tversky loss for high recall; (iii) a discrete Bernoulli diffusion model (GAT denoiser) generates a pairwise cluster-affinity matrix over candidate sets, pretrained for structural validity (symmetry + transitivity) and then refined via reward-weighted regression on solver cost feedback; (iv) the key novelty—trajectory sensitivity—measures cumulative squared changes in predicted affinities across all denoising timesteps, identifying variables whose cluster assignments are unstable and thus structurally coupled or ambiguous; (v) a multiplicative gating term (1 + τ·S̃_i·S̃_j) modulates the converged affinity matrix to upweight edges between jointly unstable variables, and METIS partitions the resulting graph into neighborhoods repaired sequentially by steepest-descent local search. The trajectory signal requires no additional learnable parameters and provides a transferable proxy for structural importance that outperforms final-step predictions, static GNN embeddings, and spectral methods across cost regimes and scales.

## Results

On medium-scale RAIL instances (N≈10³–10⁴), GLNS achieves a primal gap of 3.28% (Unicost) and 2.55% (Varied Costs), outperforming the closest neural LNS baseline Diffusco-LNS (3.65% / 2.88%) and the best constructive solver FAST T2T (5.87% / 5.2%). On the Airline Crew Scheduling benchmark, GLNS reaches 9.1% gap versus 9.96% for Diffusco-LNS and 11.13% for FAST T2T. On large-scale instances (N≥10⁵) under a 60-second budget, GLNS achieves a Primal Integral of 760.59, beating Gurobi with presolve (995.57, 1.31×) and outperforming CP-SAT (2505.00, 3.29×) and SCIP (144041.53, 189×) by wide margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLNS outperforms all 11 baselines across every benchmark and cost regime. Against constructive neural solvers, the gap is substantial: DIFUSCO (Sun & Yang, 2023) and DIFFUCO (Sanokowski et al., 2024) achieve 6.42–7.89% gaps on RAIL versus GLNS's 2.55–3.28%, and FAST T2T (Li et al., 2024) and GNN (Shafi et al., 2023) similarly trail by 2–4 percentage points. Among neural LNS methods, GLNS beats the closest competitor Diffusco-LNS (Feng et al., 2024) by 0.37–0.86 gap points (e.g., 2.55% vs. 2.88% on RAIL Varied, 9.1% vs. 9.96% on Airline), and outperforms GNN-LNS (Song et al., 2020), CL-LNS (Huang et al., 2023), and RL-LNS (Wu et al., 2021) by 0.55–3.46 points. Against heuristic baselines, GLNS improves over Random-LNS and ALNS (Lan et al., 2007) by 0.84–1.63 points on RAIL and 1.66 points on Airline. On large-scale instances (N≥10⁵), GLNS achieves a Primal Integral 1.31× better than Gurobi with presolve, 3.29× better than CP-SAT (Perron & Furnon, 2025), and 189× better than SCIP (Bolusani et al., 2024), confirming its advantage in tight-time-budget regimes where exact solvers struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLNS extends the diffusion-guided LNS paradigm introduced by DIFUSCO-LNS (Feng et al., 2024), which samples neighborhoods from the final denoised output of a discrete diffusion model (Lou et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is treating the denoising trajectory itself as a structural signal: rather than discarding intermediate predictions, GLNS measures prediction instability across timesteps to identify variables involved in coupled or ambiguous repair decisions. This draws on prior observations that intermediate diffusion states encode uncertainty (Graikos et al., 2022) and prediction stability as an importance criterion (Toneva et al., 2018), but adapts them to discrete combinatorial optimization for the first time. The overall pipeline is grounded in the classical LNS destroy-repair framework (Shaw, 1998) and complements discriminative neural LNS approaches (Song et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2021) that the paper shows to be limited by myopic point-estimate neighborhood selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that trajectory-guided neighborhood selection transfers robustly across cost distributions and scales to 10^5+ variable instances without retraining, addressing a core generalization failure mode of neural CO methods. The finding that a simple Random-LNS baseline outperforms several learned neural policies (RL-LNS, GNN-LNS) on highly constrained SCP instances is a useful cautionary result for the neural LNS community, suggesting that learned biases can be counterproductive when the destroy-repair structure already provides strong inductive bias. Open questions include a theoretical characterization of when trajectory instability correlates with structural importance, adaptive halo sizing, and tighter integration with branch-and-bound solvers. The practical impact is strongest in the 10–60 second wall-clock regime for large instances, where GLNS provides reliable high-quality feasible solutions that can serve as warm starts for exact solvers.
