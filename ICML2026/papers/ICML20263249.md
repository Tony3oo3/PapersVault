## Problem

Dense state-transition (decay) matrices in linear attention improve performance over diagonal ones, but constructing them naively costs O(d³) parameters. Existing work relies on rank-1 low-rank factorizations (diagonal + outer product) to keep costs manageable, yet this severely limits the expressive capacity of the off-diagonal interactions. The paper asks how to raise the effective rank of the decay matrix to r with minimal additional parameters and computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard text token embeddings and produces next-token predictions via a transformer-style stack of Head-in-Head linear attention blocks (token mixer) and SwiGLU feed-forward blocks (channel mixer). Within each attention head, the feature dimension d is partitioned into r groups of size d/r (r must divide d), and the output is the sum of r linear-attention sub-outputs, each querying its corresponding key/value partition against the shared memory state. The r×r mask matrix is either a static learnable parameter (r² per head) or generated data-dependently via a linear projection (r²d parameters).

## Method

Head-in-Head introduces an r×r mask matrix M_t that is broadcast to the full d×d state-transition matrix via element-wise multiplication, partitioning the memory state into r blocks and enabling selective enhancement or suppression of inter-block connections. This raises the effective rank of the decay matrix from 1 to r with only r²d additional parameters, compared to rd² required by standard rank-r extensions. To ensure numerical stability, the mask is constructed as M_org·M_org^T (with per-row L2 normalization of M_org), guaranteeing non-negative eigenvalues and keeping the state-transition eigenvalues within [0,1]. For efficient parallel training, the authors derive a chunk-wise WY-representation scheme where the scalar inversion in DeltaNet generalizes to an r×r matrix inversion, maintaining quadratic complexity in sequence length. The design is plug-and-play: it composes with any existing dense-A_t linear attention scheme (DeltaNet, Gated-DeltaNet, RWKV7, LongHorn, MesaNet, KDA, etc.) without architectural changes.

## Results

At 1.3B scale (100B tokens), HH-GDN achieves the best perplexity among all linear-attention baselines: 16.10 on WikiText and 13.05 on Lambada, outperforming Gated-DeltaNet (16.30 / 14.52) and DeltaNet (16.72 / 15.42). On the MAD synthetic benchmark, HH-GDN scores 77.2 average, surpassing Gated-DeltaNet (74.9) and DeltaNet (74.4) while remaining closest to the Transformer upper bound (80.3). On recall-intensive tasks, HH-GDN reaches 37.07 average, improving over Gated-DeltaNet (36.38) and DeltaNet (35.94), though still trailing the Transformer++ baseline (41.04). On LongBench, HH-GDN achieves 17.57 average, the best among all tested models including Transformer++ (17.45) and DeltaNet (17.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HH-GDN consistently outperforms its direct baselines Gated-DeltaNet and DeltaNet across all evaluated metrics: it reduces WikiText PPL from 16.30 to 16.10 and Lambada PPL from 14.52 to 13.05, while improving the common-sense reasoning average from 51.76 to 52.65 and recall average from 36.38 to 37.07. Against older linear-attention models (RetNet, GLA, HGRN2), the gains are larger, with HH-GDN leading by 3.9–4.9 points on the CSR average. On the MAD synthetic benchmark, HH-GDN (77.2) surpasses all linear-attention baselines (65.4–74.9) but remains below the Transformer (80.3), particularly on Fuzzy Recall (41.1 vs. 57.8). On recall-intensive tasks, HH-GDN (37.07) still trails the Transformer++ baseline (41.04) by ~4 points, indicating that the fixed-memory bottleneck is not fully resolved. In the head-to-head comparison with GatedDeltaProduct at matched parameter scale (1.3B and 2B), HH-GDN wins on both perplexity and recall (e.g., 16.10 vs. 16.23 Wiki PPL; 37.07 vs. 35.89 recall at 1.3B), and also on LongBench (17.57 vs. 17.45 for Transformer++). On RWKV7 (Appendix F), HH-RWKV7-r4 improves Lambada PPL from 33.77 to 26.67 and recall from 29.98 to 30.74, confirming generality across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense state-transition matrix design in linear attention (DeltaNet, Gated-DeltaNet, RWKV7) by introducing a block-wise r×r mask that partitions the memory state within a single head, raising the effective rank from 1 to r. The key departure from prior rank-enhancement work (e.g., DeltaProduct, which stacks r state transitions at rd² parameter cost) is a parameterization insight: a small r×r mask broadcast via element-wise multiplication achieves rank-r structure with only r²d additional parameters. The chunk-wise parallel training form is derived from the WY representation, and the mask normalization proof ensures eigenvalue stability analogous to the original DeltaNet constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design is broadly applicable to any dense-decay linear attention variant (GDN, RWKV7, MesaNet, KDA), making it a practical drop-in for the growing linear-attention LLM ecosystem. However, empirical gains are modest (roughly 1–2 points over Gated-DeltaNet at 1.3B scale), data-dependent masks show marginal or even negative effects on recall tasks, and training speed is slower due to r-dimensional matrix operations that cannot leverage Tensor Cores. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at larger scales (7B+), whether larger r values yield diminishing returns, and whether the training-efficiency gap can be closed with optimized kernels.
