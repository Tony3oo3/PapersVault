## Problem

Existing diffusion-based optimization solvers trained in a supervised paradigm suffer from a distributional misalignment: the learned output distribution (approximately Gaussian around the optimum) places exponentially little probability mass on the feasible region as dimensionality grows (≈1/2^d for linear constraints). This makes supervised diffusion solvers produce infeasible solutions with high probability, and the paper proves that multiple sampling cannot rescue this failure. No prior diffusion-based framework effectively enforces hard constraint satisfaction in nonconvex optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem instance vector x (e.g., nodal active/reactive demands for ACOPF, or SMPL joint positions plus root rotation and transform offset for motion retargeting) and outputs a near-optimal feasible solution vector y ∈ R^{d_y}. For problems with equality constraints h(y;x)=0, the diffusion model generates only the basic variables y_b ∈ R^{d_y−m}, and the nonbasic variables y_n are recovered exactly via a Newton solver. At inference, K_e=64 samples are drawn from the trained diffusion model and the best candidate is selected by maximizing the weight function, which jointly scores feasibility and optimality.

## Method

DiOpt trains a conditional DDPM in two phases: a supervised warm-start (first r_s=20% of epochs) that guides the noise-prediction network toward the vicinity of optimal solutions, followed by a bootstrapped self-supervised refinement phase. In the refinement phase, K_t=16 candidate solutions are sampled per problem instance and assigned weights that are positive (exponential of optimality gap) for feasible points and negative (sum of constraint violations) for infeasible points, so the weighted variational loss steers the diffusion model toward the feasible region before optimizing the objective. A periodic reset operation alternates between the full weight and a feasibility-only weight to prevent the distribution from collapsing to a single point (which would degenerate back to supervised behavior). A look-up table stores the historically best candidate per instance to stabilize training against sampling stochasticity, and at inference the best of K_e samples is selected by the same weight function.

## Results

DiOpt achieves the best feasibility-optimality trade-off on 4 out of 5 main tasks. On QPSR, DiOpt reaches 81.87% feasibility with a 2.48% optimality gap (objective −9.53), while RectFlow achieves only 0% feasibility despite a 0.43% gap. On Retargeting, DiOpt attains 100% feasibility with a 0.65% gap, matching RectFlow's feasibility but with a smaller gap (0.65% vs. 1.24%). On ACOPF118, DiOpt achieves 84.33% feasibility with a 2.26% gap, whereas RectFlow and MBD both collapse to 0% feasibility. MBD fails catastrophically across all tasks (e.g., 45672% gap on CQP), and DC3 exhibits numerical instability on CQP (N/A results).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiOpt decisively outperforms RectFlow (supervised diffusion) on feasibility across all tasks: on QPSR, CQP, and ACOPF118, RectFlow achieves 0% feasibility while DiOpt reaches 81.87%, 69.95%, and 84.33% respectively, despite RectFlow having slightly smaller optimality gaps (e.g., 0.43% vs. 2.48% on QPSR). On Retargeting, both DiOpt and RectFlow achieve 100% feasibility, but DiOpt has a smaller gap (0.65% vs. 1.24%). DiOpt also substantially outperforms DC3, which suffers from numerical instability on CQP (N/A) and large gaps on QPSR (33.61%) and Retargeting (30.16%). MBD (Model-Based Diffusion) fails catastrophically on every task, with gaps exceeding 169% on Retargeting and 45,672% on CQP, and near-zero feasibility everywhere. The one exception where DiOpt does not dominate is ACOPF57, where RectFlow achieves a marginally smaller gap (0.19% vs. 0.24%) and DC3 has slightly higher feasibility (94% vs. 93.33%), though DiOpt still matches IPOPT's objective value exactly (3.81). All learning-based methods (DiOpt, RectFlow, DC3, MBD) achieve inference times of ~0.01–0.07 s, orders of magnitude faster than IPOPT (0.42–4.36 s), confirming the real-time advantage of the learned approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiOpt builds on the DDPM generative framework (Ho et al., 2020) as its sampling backbone and adapts the weighted variational loss from QVPO (Ding et al., 2024a), originally designed for diffusion-based reinforcement learning, into a bootstrapped refinement mechanism for optimization. It also draws on Liang & Chen (2024) for the target distribution that places mass on near-optimal feasible points rather than a single optimal point. The key departure from prior diffusion-based solvers (RectFlow, DiffuSolve, MBD) is the identification of a fundamental distributional misalignment under supervised training and the introduction of a two-phase curriculum—supervised warm-start followed by self-supervised bootstrapping with constraint-aware weighting—that avoids the need for large feasible-sample datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to successfully integrate diffusion models into constrained nonconvex optimization with hard inequality constraints, addressing a gap where prior diffusion solvers collapsed to near-zero feasibility in high-dimensional settings. The theoretical bound (feasibility ≈ 1/2^d under supervised training) provides a clear diagnostic for why existing approaches fail and motivates the bootstrapping design. Limitations include training efficiency (self-supervised sampling is costly), reliance on equality-constraint completion via Newton's method, and the fact that feasibility still falls short of 100% on the hardest benchmarks (e.g., 84% on ACOPF118). Open questions include whether the bootstrapping mechanism scales to very high-dimensional problems (d_y > 1000) and whether the reset/weighting schedule can be made adaptive rather than epoch-based.
