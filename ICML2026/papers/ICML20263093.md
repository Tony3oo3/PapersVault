## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods are fragmented: they either decouple motion estimation from geometry, restrict motion to pairwise views or a single reference frame, or sacrifice geometric quality for trajectory modeling. No prior feed-forward framework supports flexible, dense 3D motion querying from any arbitrary source frame to any arbitrary target timestamp within a unified model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (2–18 frames, longer side up to 504 px) and produces a factorized 4D representation: per-frame base geometry (depth map, ray map, camera parameters) plus a time-dependent 3D displacement field. All point maps are viewpoint-invariant in a world coordinate system anchored to the first frame's camera. At inference, the user specifies any source frame index and any target timestamp to retrieve the full 3D point map P_i^{t_i→τ} = P_i^{t_i} + ΔP_i^{t_i→τ}, enabling dense tracking, scene flow, or static reconstruction on demand.

## Method

4RC introduces an encode-once, query-anywhere-and-anytime paradigm built on a ViT-Giant encoder (40 layers, 1536-dim, initialized from DA3) that alternates frame-wise and global self-attention, appending each frame's patch tokens with dedicated camera and time tokens to form a compact spatio-temporal latent. A dual-DPT geometry head decodes per-frame depth, rays, and camera parameters, while a lightweight 4-layer motion decoder uses AdaLN to inject the target-time token and cross-attention to the target frame's spatial tokens to regress the dense 3D displacement field, with DPT multi-scale upsampling for high-resolution output. The key design choice is the minimally factorized output: decomposing each frame into viewpoint-invariant base geometry plus a 3D displacement, which decouples shape learning from temporal correspondence and improves temporal consistency. Training is end-to-end with aleatoric-uncertainty-weighted losses on depth, rays, camera, and motion, augmented by spatial gradient constraints on depth and temporal gradient constraints on displacement (velocity), using AdamW for 50 epochs on 16 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On dense 3D tracking, 4RC achieves APD 85.44 / EPE 1.022 on Kubric and APD 56.63 / EPE 1.611 on Waymo, substantially outperforming the closest concurrent method V-DPM (71.12 / 2.849 and 41.44 / 1.948 respectively), a 36% APD gain on Waymo. On sparse point tracking, 4RC attains APD 85.86 (PO), 88.65 (DR), 87.82 (ADT), and 87.32 (PStudio), surpassing SpatialTrackerV2 on 3 of 4 datasets. For camera pose estimation on TUM-dynamics, 4RC achieves the best ATE (0.010) and RPE_t (0.008) among all methods including specialized 3D reconstruction models like Pi3. On video depth (Bonn), 4RC matches the best δ<1.25 score of 97.4, while on Sintel it trails Pi3 (62.2 vs 67.7) due to smaller training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4RC decisively outperforms all concurrent feed-forward 4D reconstruction methods on dense tracking: on Waymo it gains 36% APD over V-DPM (56.63 vs 41.44) and 25% over TraceAnything (56.63 vs 21.25); on Kubric it leads V-DPM by 14.3 APD points. On sparse point tracking, 4RC beats SpatialTrackerV2 on PO (85.86 vs 73.66), DR (88.65 vs 80.87), and PStudio (87.32 vs 85.63), but loses on ADT (87.82 vs 95.48), where the tracking-specific method retains a clear advantage. Against V-DPM, 4RC wins on PO, DR, and ADT in APD but trails slightly on EPE for PO (0.2498 vs 0.1955) and PStudio (0.1304 vs 0.1165). For camera pose estimation, 4RC achieves the best ATE and RPE_t on TUM-dynamics (0.010, 0.008), surpassing even Pi3 (0.014, 0.009) and VGGT (0.012, 0.010) despite being trained on fewer datasets. On multi-view 3D reconstruction (7-Scenes), 4RC attains the best accuracy (0.034) among 4D methods, far exceeding TraceAnything (0.232) and St4RTrack (0.240), though Pi3 remains slightly better on NRGBD completeness. On video depth, 4RC is competitive with Pi3 and VGGT on Bonn (δ<1.25 = 97.4) but lags on Sintel (62.2 vs 67.7/68.8), attributed to its smaller training set focused on 4D supervision rather than pure reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

4RC extends the feed-forward 3D reconstruction lineage (DUSt3R → VGGT → DA3) to the dynamic 4D setting by introducing a factorized output (base geometry + time-dependent displacement) and a conditional decoder that queries arbitrary source-target frame pairs. It departs from concurrent 4D methods (St4RTrack's pairwise formulation, Any4D's first-frame-only motion, V-DPM's global aggregation) by enabling per-frame, on-demand motion decoding from a single compact latent. The encoder is initialized from DA3 and the streaming variant (S-4RC) builds on STream3R's causal attention, positioning 4RC as a natural 4D extension of the current state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction stack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The encode-once, query-anywhere paradigm offers a practical and flexible recipe for 4D reconstruction that avoids the quadratic cost of predicting all N² motion pairs and supports online/streaming inference via S-4RC, which is relevant for robotics and egocentric perception. Limitations remain for extreme depth, transparent objects, and highly chaotic motion where training data provides insufficient supervision. Open questions include whether incorporating camera or depth priors as additional inputs, and scaling training to cover broader kinematic diversity, can close the remaining gap to specialized 3D reconstruction methods on pure geometry benchmarks.
