## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has become a key technique for unlocking complex reasoning in LLMs, but scaling by increasing training steps (as in ProRL) plateaus after a few thousand steps with clear diminishing returns. The paper asks whether a complementary scaling axis—increasing the number of rollouts per example to hundreds—can break through this saturation and yield continuous performance gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261653/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math, code, STEM, logical puzzles, instruction-following) and generates full reasoning chains with final answers, evaluated via pass@1 with 32k context length and nucleus sampling (top_p=0.95, temperature 0.6). A distinctive operational detail is that each prompt receives N=512 rollouts (vs. 16 in the ProRL baseline) while the number of prompts per RL step drops from 512 to 128, keeping total samples per step comparable. Training uses a 16,384-token context window on 64 H100 GPUs via the veRL framework.

## Method

BroRL is a rollout-allocation recipe that keeps the ProRLv2 objective (clipped PPO with REINFORCE++-style decoupled advantage normalization, dynamic sampling, clip-higher, and periodic reference resets) fixed but reallocates sampling compute from many prompts with few rollouts to fewer prompts with many rollouts (N=512) once small-N step scaling has saturated. The theoretical motivation is a mass-balance decomposition (Theorem 1) showing that the one-step change in correct-token probability mass ΔQ_pos splits into two always-nonnegative sampled terms and a conditional unsampled coupling term S_R(Q_pos·U_neg,2 − Q_neg·U_pos,2) that can be negative; Lemma 2 proves the unsampled second-moment terms shrink as p²(1−p)^N, so larger N suppresses the harmful coupling and drives ΔQ_pos toward nonnegativity. Empirically, BroRL revives a 1.5B model saturated at 3K ProRL steps, achieving continuous gains on math, code, and reasoning benchmarks while also improving efficiency: the larger N raises the dynamic-sampling pass rate from 41% to 62% and nearly doubles generation throughput (72.4 vs. 36.5 samples/s) by shifting GPU inference from a memory-bound to a compute-bound regime.

## Results

Starting from a saturated 3,000-step ProRLv2 checkpoint (Math 61.69, Code 52.00, Reasoning Gym 61.29), BroRL (N=512) achieves Math 63.03, Code 54.20, and Reasoning Gym 63.09 after 191 additional RL steps (173.8 h), while ProRL (N=16) degrades to Math 62.02, Code 52.74, and Reasoning Gym 61.45 after 535 steps (133.8 h). At comparable wall-clock compute (~122–134 h), BroRL already surpasses ProRL's final scores on all three benchmarks. A paired t-test over >10,000 problem instances confirms a statistically significant advantage for BroRL (Δ=0.0033, t=4.84, one-tailed p=6.5×10⁻⁷). BroRL also nearly doubles generation throughput (72.4 vs. 36.5 samples/s) and improves the dynamic-sampling pass rate from 41% to 62%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProRL (Liu et al., 2025a; N=16, 512 prompts/step), BroRL (N=512, 128 prompts/step) wins decisively on all three benchmarks at comparable wall-clock compute: at ~122–134 h, BroRL scores Math 62.85 / Code 53.48 / RG 62.82 versus ProRL's Math 62.02 / Code 52.74 / RG 61.45. Critically, ProRL's Math and Reasoning Gym scores actually *degrade* after an initial small gain (Math 62.08→62.02, RG 62.10→61.45), while BroRL improves monotonically throughout. On the 4B model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct), continued ProRLv2 training after saturation causes a noticeable math drop (71.65→69.58), whereas BroRL (N=256) maintains a stable trajectory (71.64) with continued code and reasoning gains. The paper also reports that BroRL's advantage is statistically significant (paired t-test, p=6.5×10⁻⁷) and that BroRL achieves higher scores with fewer generated tokens (e.g., Math: 63.66 with 15,760 tokens vs. ProRL's 62.02 with 16,506 tokens), indicating superior score-per-token efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR scaling literature initiated by ProRL/ProRLv2, which established training-step count as the primary scaling axis, by identifying per-prompt rollout size N as a complementary and underexplored dimension. The training recipe inherits the ProRLv2 stack (clipped PPO with REINFORCE++-style advantage normalization, DAPO dynamic sampling, Clip-Higher, and truncated importance sampling) but reallocates compute from many prompts × few rollouts to fewer prompts × many rollouts in the post-saturation regime. The theoretical contribution—a mass balance decomposition of the one-step logit update into always-positive sampled terms and a conditionally-signed unsampled coupling term—provides a mechanistic explanation for why small-N updates can degrade performance, connecting to the broader finite-sample 'sampling error' literature in RL. Unlike adaptive-allocation methods (DARS, AR3PO) that decide where to spend rollout budget, BroRL argues for uniformly broadening exploration once step-scaling has plateaued.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the RLVR plateau as a potentially algorithmic artifact (insufficient exploration) rather than a fundamental limit of RL, which could reshape how practitioners allocate compute in late-stage reasoning training. The nearly doubled generation throughput from shifting from memory-bound to compute-bound inference is a practical efficiency win that lowers the barrier to large-N training. However, the empirical gains are modest in absolute terms (~1 point on math over ~100 hours from an already-strong 3K-step checkpoint), the theoretical analysis relies on a one-step linear surrogate, and the results are validated only on 1.5B and 4B models with a specific hardware setup. Open questions include adaptive N allocation based on prompt difficulty, the optimal N-to-batch-size tradeoff under fixed compute, and whether the benefits persist at 70B+ scale or in multi-turn interactive settings.
