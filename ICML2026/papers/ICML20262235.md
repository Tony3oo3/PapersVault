## Problem

E-value-based online FDR control methods (e-LOND, e-LORD, e-SAFFRON) guarantee valid finite-sample FDR under minimal assumptions but suffer from systematic power loss due to the 'overshoot' phenomenon: once an e-value crosses the rejection threshold, the excess evidence is discarded by the standard Markov-inequality bound, treating a marginal rejection identically to a highly confident one. This paper asks whether the wasted overshoot can be rigorously recovered to improve power without sacrificing FDR validity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of e-values e_t (or p-values converted via the Vovk–Wang calibrator e = (1−p+p·log p)/(p·(−log p)²)) arriving one at a time, with no knowledge of the total number of hypotheses or future observations. At each step it outputs a binary rejection decision δ_t and a dynamically updated significance level α_t, with the guarantee that FDR(t) ≤ α for all t. The p-value-to-e-value conversion is used in the real-data experiment (Wildenhain et al. 2016) where only p-values are reported.

## Method

The core mechanism is the elementary inequality I(y≥1) ≤ y − (y−1)_+, which is strictly tighter than the standard Markov bound I(y≥1) ≤ y whenever y > 1. Applying this with y = α_j e_j yields a tighter bound on the rejection indicator that subtracts the overshoot O_j = (α_j e_j − 1)_+ from the cost charged to the wealth budget, effectively refunding excess evidence. This 'plug-in' replacement C_j → (C_j − O_j)_+ uniformly improves any e-value algorithm whose validity proof relies on the Markov indicator bound, producing SCORE-LOND, SCORE-LORD, and SCORE-SAFFRON that strictly dominate their baselines. Under a mild Conditional Positive Quadrant Dependence (CPQD) assumption, a further upgrade (SCORE+) replaces the predictable local denominator R_{j−1}+1 with the global R_t ∨ 1, enabling retroactive wealth updates where each new discovery retroactively reduces the effective cost of all prior tests, releasing additional alpha-wealth for more aggressive future testing.

## Results

On the Wildenhain et al. (2016) chemical-genomics dataset (417,026 interactions, α=0.1), SCORE+-LORD detects 44,210 discoveries versus 25,954 for e-LORD (a 70% increase), and SCORE+-SAFFRON detects 43,624 versus 29,867 for e-SAFFRON (a 46% increase). In synthetic experiments (T=1000, α=0.05), power ratios relative to baselines range from 1.08–1.49 for standard SCORE and 2.25–5.64 for SCORE+, with the largest gains occurring in the dependent autoregressive-exponential setting. All methods maintain valid FDR control (empirical FDR well below the nominal level) across both independent and dependent settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two e-value-based baselines from Zhang et al. (2025): e-LORD and e-SAFFRON. On the real Wildenhain dataset, SCORE-LORD outperforms e-LORD by 11% (28,808 vs. 25,954 discoveries), while SCORE+-LORD nearly doubles the yield at 44,210 (a 70% gain). Similarly, SCORE-SAFFRON exceeds e-SAFFRON by 26% (37,748 vs. 29,867), and SCORE+-SAFFRON achieves a 46% improvement (43,624 vs. 29,867). In synthetic experiments across both independent (Gaussian mixture) and dependent (autoregressive exponential) settings, standard SCORE consistently achieves power ratios of 1.08–1.49 over its baseline, while SCORE+ achieves ratios of 2.25–5.64, with the advantage growing over time as retroactive wealth updates accumulate. The dominance is strict: the paper proves that SCORE thresholds are uniformly at least as large as baseline thresholds, and strictly larger whenever any prior overshoot occurs, guaranteeing that SCORE never rejects fewer hypotheses than the original procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the e-value-based online FDR control framework introduced by Xu & Ramdas (2024) for e-LOND and Zhang et al. (2025) for e-LORD and e-SAFFRON, which themselves are e-value analogues of the classical LOND/LORD/SAFFRON alpha-investing procedures. Conceptually, SCORE is positioned as a universal 'plug-in' refinement: it exploits the elementary inequality I(y≥1) ≤ y − (y−1)₊ to tighten the Markov-derived indicator bound that underlies validity proofs for all these e-value procedures, without altering their structural design. The paper also draws a clear distinction from p-value-based wealth-recovery methods (ADDIS, SUR) and structure-adaptive approaches (SAST, SMART), arguing that SCORE achieves comparable overshoot-refund benefits while operating within the e-value paradigm and guaranteeing finite-sample FDR control without density estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCORE framework provides a clean, universally applicable mechanism that strictly dominates existing e-value-based online testing procedures, with the improvement magnitude growing over time as overshoots accumulate. The retroactive wealth update (SCORE⁺) under the mild CPQD assumption is a notable conceptual advance, enabling more aggressive testing strategies that nearly double discovery counts in the large-scale chemical genomics experiment. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other e-value-based procedures beyond the LOND/LORD/SAFFRON family, whether it applies to offline multiple testing settings, and how the decaying-memory FDR extension (outlined in Appendix C) performs empirically. The CPQD assumption, while mild, remains a theoretical boundary that the stress test in Appendix B.2 shows can be violated in adversarial settings, limiting the scope of SCORE⁺ guarantees.
