## Problem

Training Multimodal Process Reward Models (MPRMs) for step-level visual reasoning supervision typically requires large-scale Monte Carlo-annotated corpora (e.g., 565K rollouts with 3.17M annotated steps), incurring substantial computational cost. This paper asks whether MPRM performance truly scales with the rollout budget or whether existing MC-annotated data contains substantial redundancy, and if so, what principled criterion can identify the most informative subsets that preserve full-data performance at a fraction of the training cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes MC-annotated reasoning rollouts from VisualPRM400K-v1.1, where each rollout pairs a question with associated images and a step-by-step solution; every step carries a per-step MC score (fraction of N=16 sampled continuations reaching the correct answer) and a binarized label (positive if score > 0). BIS is computed purely from these stored MC statistics—no additional model calls, relabeling, or architecture changes are needed. The output is a ranked rollout subset (top-ρ fraction per source) used to train the MPRM, which at inference produces a scalar step-level correctness score (the 'Yes' probability at each <prm> token position) for reranking candidate trajectories.

## Method

The paper introduces the Balanced-Information Score (BIS), a rollout-level criterion that jointly captures label mixture and label reliability: BIS(x) = (p_pos(x)(1−p_pos(x)) + α) · R(x), where p_pos is the fraction of positive-labeled steps and R(x) is the average MC score over positive steps. A teacher-student theoretical framework formalizes why these two factors matter: the mixture term estimates the teacher's per-step uncertainty q*(φ)(1−q*(φ)) (the source of informative gradient signal), while the reliability term inversely tracks the effective label-flip noise η_eff induced by low-MC pseudo-positive labels, which inflates gradient variance and shrinks the true signal. The theory shows the two factors couple multiplicatively in the expected gradient norm, so a rollout is most informative only when it is both mixed and reliable. In practice, rollouts within each of the 38 source datasets are ranked by BIS in descending order and the top ρ fraction is retained; the resulting subset is trained with the standard single-pass fine-tuning protocol. Experiments on InternVL2.5-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B show that BIS-10% matches or surpasses full-data performance, saving 95.5% of training compute.

## Results

On VisualProcessBench, BIS-selected subsets match or surpass full-data MPRM performance at small data fractions. For InternVL2.5-8B, BIS-10% achieves 65.46% overall micro-F1, exceeding Full-Data (65.12%) while using only 10% of rollouts, compared to 62.86% for Random-10% (+2.6 pts). For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the advantage is even larger in low-budget regimes: BIS-5% reaches 64.42% vs. Random-5% at 53.54% (+10.9 pts), and BIS-25% (65.53%) matches Full-Data (65.57%). In Best-of-N reranking (N=16), MPRMs trained on BIS-25% outperform both Random-25% and Full-Data MPRMs across all four benchmarks (e.g., +1.6 pts over Full-Data on MM-K12, +1.5 pts on MathVista).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Random-ρ, which retains a random fraction ρ of rollouts from each source. BIS consistently outperforms Random-ρ at every tested ratio for both backbones, with the largest gaps in low-budget regimes: +10.9 pts at ρ=5% and +5.5 pts at ρ=15% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and +2.6 pts at ρ=10% for InternVL2.5-8B. Against the Full-Data reference (565K rollouts, 1,100 steps), BIS-10% on InternVL2.5-8B (65.46%) actually exceeds Full-Data (65.12%), and BIS-25% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (65.53%) matches Full-Data (65.57%), demonstrating that 90% of the training data is redundant. Among heuristic 25% subsets, Mixed-25% (mixture-only) is the strongest baseline at 64.70% overall, still 0.76 pts below BIS-25% (65.46%), while Reliable-25% (reliability-only) drops to 62.75% (−2.71 pts), confirming that both mixture and reliability are necessary. In Best-of-N reranking, BIS-25% MPRM outperforms both Random-25% and Full-Data MPRM on all four benchmarks (MM-K12, OlympiadBench, MathVerse, MathVista), with gains of +1.6 to +1.5 pts over Full-Data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VisualPRM framework (Wang et al., 2025b) for MPRM architecture, training objective, and the VisualPRM400K-v1.1 MC-annotated corpus, and on the Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024d) methodology for generating per-step MC scores via N sampled continuations. It positions itself in the data-selection line of work for process supervision (alongside DreamPRM, ACTPRM, SCAN), but departs from those methods by requiring no extra model calls, relabeling, or architectural changes—BIS is a purely post-hoc scalar computed from MC statistics already stored in the dataset. The theoretical contribution (a teacher-student logistic framework linking gradient noise, label mixture, and MC reliability) is novel and provides the principled justification for the scoring criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MPRM training is noise-limited rather than data-limited, and that 10% of rollouts can match full-data performance, offers a concrete 90%+ compute reduction for a task that currently requires training on 565K rollouts. The BIS criterion is simple, model-agnostic, and immediately applicable to any MC-annotated corpus, making it a practical recipe for future MPRM training pipelines. Open questions include whether the insight generalizes to other MC-annotated settings (e.g., text-only PRMs, different N values, or non-binary step labels), whether the linear teacher-student abstraction captures the behavior of large transformer MPRMs, and whether BIS can be extended to active or iterative selection settings where the model's evolving uncertainty matters.
