## Problem

Latent Action Models (LAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are typically trained in isolation: LAMs learn visual dynamics from unlabeled video without access to real action trajectories, while VLAs are constrained by frozen LAM pseudo-labels that cannot adapt during policy training. This decoupling leaves LAM representations ungrounded in actual robot actions and prevents VLAs from benefiting from the forward-dynamics priors encoded in LAMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262844/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The LAM component consumes pairs of RGB frames (I_t, I_{t+C} with C=5 for pre-training, C=16 for alignment) and produces a continuous latent action z_t and a quantized codebook token z_t^q (codebook size 128). The VLA component takes a current RGB observation, a natural language instruction, and a robot proprioceptive state (up to 64 dimensions across up to 64 embodiments) and outputs a continuous action chunk of C=16 steps (up to 32 action dimensions) via flow-matching integration. The alignment signal is extracted at the L-2 layer of the 16-layer DiT, specifically the hidden state token corresponding to the final timestep of the action chunk to enforce temporal consistency.

## Method

LARA introduces a bidirectional representation alignment loss that jointly optimizes a LAM and a flow-matching diffusion VLA by maximizing cosine similarity between the LAM's continuous latent action z_t (pre-quantization) and an intermediate DiT feature h_t^θ at layer L-2, projected through a learnable MLP head. The full objective combines the standard flow-matching action loss, the LAM VQ-VAE reconstruction loss, and the alignment loss with small balancing weights (w1=w2=0.01). This creates reciprocal regularization: the LAM's inverse dynamics are grounded by real action trajectories (suppressing spurious visual changes like lighting), while the VLA's intermediate representations are anchored to forward-predictive latent actions (reducing kinematically plausible but functionally incorrect trajectory hallucinations). Training follows a three-stage pipeline: LAM pre-training on unlabeled video, LARA joint pre-training on robot demonstrations with action labels, and LARA joint post-training on target-task data. The framework is plug-and-play, applicable as full pre-training, a post-training enhancement module for existing VLAs (e.g., GR00T-N1.6, π0.5), or a latent action refiner that improves LAM pseudo-label quality for downstream frameworks like LAPA and Moto-GPT.

## Results

Under the OXE-Constrained setting, LARA (full) achieves 88.6% average success on LIBERO and 65.2% on SIMPLER-ENV, outperforming the best OXE-only baselines (OpenVLA at 76.5% LIBERO, Moto-GPT at 61.4% SIMPLER) by ~12% and ~4% respectively. As a post-training enhancement module, GR00T-N1.6-LARA reaches 95.6% LIBERO and 79.9% SIMPLER, surpassing the vanilla GR00T-N1.6 by +0.6% and +1.3%. On the real-world G1-Real(50) benchmark, LARA (full) achieves 74.0% average success (vs. 56.0% for DiT-only), and the LARA-refined LAM yields a +15.7% average improvement over vanilla LAM pseudo-labels on SIMPLER-ENV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under OXE-Constrained pre-training, LARA (full) decisively outperforms all OXE-only baselines: +12.1% over OpenVLA (88.6 vs 76.5) and +32.5% over OpenVLA on SIMPLER (65.2 vs 32.7), +13.5% over Octo on LIBERO, +3.8% over Moto-GPT on SIMPLER (65.2 vs 61.4), and +22.9% over LAPA on LIBERO (88.6 vs 65.7). In the Unconstrained setting, GR00T-N1.6-LARA marginally beats GR00T-N1.6 (+0.6% LIBERO, +1.3% SIMPLER) and π0.5-LARA improves over π0.5 by +0.9% on LIBERO (97.8 vs 96.9). LARA (full) under OXE-constrained still surpasses several large-scale unconstrained models including π0-FAST (+3.1% LIBERO), Magma (+2.9% SIMPLER), and SpatialVLA (+10.5% LIBERO), though it trails villa-X (90.1 vs 88.6 LIBERO) and DreamVLA (92.6 vs 88.6 LIBERO) which benefit from larger pre-training data. On the real-world G1-Real(50) benchmark, LARA (full) achieves 74.0% average success versus 56.0% for the DiT-only baseline (+32.1%), and GR00T-N1.6-LARA improves over GR00T-N1.6 by +5.56% on G1-Real and +3.2% on GR1-Sim-24(30). The LAM refinement experiment shows LARA-LAM outperforms vanilla LAM by +15.7% average on SIMPLER-ENV when used as pseudo-labels in the Moto-GPT pipeline, confirming bidirectional benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

LARA sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) LAM-based VLA pre-training (Moto-GPT, LAPA), which uses latent action tokens as pseudo-labels but trains LAM and VLA in a decoupled, multi-stage pipeline, and (2) representation alignment in diffusion models (REPA, REPA-E), which improves generation quality by aligning intermediate DiT features with frozen pre-trained representations. The key departure is making the alignment target itself updatable: rather than anchoring the VLA to a frozen LAM or a static visual encoder, LARA jointly optimizes both models so that the LAM's latent actions and the VLA's DiT features co-evolve through a bidirectional cosine-similarity loss. Architecturally, it builds on the GR00T-N1 diffusion VLA backbone and the Moto-GPT LAM design, but the training paradigm—end-to-end joint optimization with a lightweight projection head—is the novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional regularization insight is the paper's most valuable conceptual contribution: the LAM is grounded by real action trajectories (suppressing spurious visual changes like lighting shifts), while the VLA is regularized by forward-dynamics priors (reducing kinematically plausible but functionally ineffective action hallucinations). This addresses a real gap in LAM-based VLA pipelines where the LAM is frozen and potentially misaligned with the actual robot's action space. However, the experiments are constrained to OXE subsets due to compute limits, the alignment depth is architecture-dependent (L-2 for GR00T-N1.6 but L for π0.5), and a noticeable performance gap remains versus fully large-scale pre-trained models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to internet-scale video pre-training, whether the bidirectional effect persists with larger codebooks or continuous (non-VQ) latent actions, and whether the plug-and-play post-training mode generalizes to non-diffusion VLA architectures.
