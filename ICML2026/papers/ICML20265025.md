## Problem

Vision-language foundation models (CLIP, SigLIP, BLIP, etc.) are typically compared through aggregate downstream metrics like retrieval accuracy, which fails to reveal how their representations differ structurally on specific sample subsets. While the SPEC framework began studying sample groups organized differently by two embeddings via kernel-difference eigendecomposition, it does not explicitly enforce the asymmetric objective of isolating subsets that are weakly clustered under a specified reference embedding while being strongly clustered under the target. This paper formalizes that asymmetric objective as a well-posed optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes two embedding representations (e.g., CLIP and DINOv2 for unimodal, or BLIP and CLIP for multimodal) evaluated on a shared reference dataset, and converts them into normalized RBF kernel similarity matrices. For multimodal settings, each reference item is a paired image-text sample, and the joint kernel is formed via Hadamard product of per-modality kernels. The output is a sequence of unit-norm discrepancy directions in R^n, each identifying a sample subset strongly grouped under one embedding but suppressed under the other; these directions can be projected via t-SNE for visualization, scored via the generalized Rayleigh quotient for quantification, or used as targeted slices for kernel-based embedding alignment.

## Method

KODA formulates contrastive embedding clustering as a quadratically constrained program: maximize x^T K_A x subject to x^T K_B x ≤ ε and ||x||₂ = 1, where K_A and K_B are the kernel matrices of the target and reference embeddings. Although non-convex, KKT analysis shows optimizers are eigenvectors of K_A − λK_B for some λ ≥ 0, reducing the search to a one-dimensional sweep over λ with a leading-eigenvector computation at each step; multiple discrepancy modes are extracted iteratively with orthogonality constraints. For scalability, a covariance-block reduction (Proposition 4.3) maps the n×n eigenproblem to a (d₁+d₂)×(d₁+d₂) problem when kernels admit explicit feature maps, and for multimodal product kernels whose tensor-product feature dimension is infeasible, joint Random Fourier Features provide a 2r-dimensional approximation with Frobenius-norm concentration and Davis-Kahan eigenspace stability guarantees (Theorem 5.1).

## Results

On the contrastive embedding alignment task, fine-tuning CLIP on KODA-identified slices improves AMI from 0.25 to 0.78 (target DINOv2: 0.83) and from 0.52 to 0.91 (target BLIP: 0.96) on FFHQ and MS-COCO respectively. On cross-modal retrieval over the KODA-selected subset, CLIP's average recall drops by 11.12 points while SigLIP's drops only 3.91 points, confirming that the identified slices capture genuine cross-modal alignment differences. Against the SPEC baseline, KODA's Top-1 discrepancy direction achieves a generalized Rayleigh quotient roughly 20× larger at the tightest constraint (q=0.1), and even the Top-5 average remains clearly above SPEC across all constraint levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole explicit baseline is SPEC (Jalali et al., 2025a), which identifies discrepancy directions via eigendecomposition of the kernel-difference matrix without an explicit weak-clustering constraint. On the generalized Rayleigh quotient xᵀK₁x / xᵀK₂x (measuring how strongly a direction is expressed under one kernel relative to the other), KODA's Top-1 direction is approximately 20× stronger than SPEC's Top-1 at the tightest constraint quantile q=0.1, and KODA's Top-5 average remains above SPEC's Top-1 across all quantiles from 0.1 to 1.0. This gap is attributed to KODA's explicit constraint xᵀK_B x ≤ ε, which forces the discovered direction to be genuinely suppressed under the reference embedding, whereas SPEC's eigendirections may reflect structure present in both embeddings. In the alignment experiments, KODA-identified slices enable CLIP to reach 0.78 AMI against DINOv2 (vs. 0.25 unaligned) and 0.91 AMI against BLIP (vs. 0.52 unaligned), approaching the target model's own self-consistency (0.83 and 0.96 respectively), demonstrating that the discovered subsets are actionable for targeted representation alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SPEC framework (Jalali et al., 2025a), which compares two embeddings by eigendecomposing their kernel-difference matrix on a shared reference set. KODA departs from SPEC by replacing the implicit kernel-difference spectral analysis with an explicit constrained quadratic program that enforces strong clustering under one embedding while suppressing it under the other, yielding interpretable asymmetric discrepancy directions. The scalability machinery draws on classical Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) and covariance-operator eigenvalue reductions, extended here to the multimodal product-kernel setting where tensor-product feature spaces become infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dozens of vision-language foundation models (CLIP, SigLIP, BLIP, OpenCLIP, etc.) coexist with similar aggregate benchmark scores, practitioners lack principled tools to understand *how* their representations structurally differ on specific data subsets. KODA provides interpretable sample slices that can drive targeted alignment (demonstrated via kernel-based fine-tuning) and amplify retrieval gaps, offering a concrete diagnostic beyond aggregate metrics. Key limitations include the requirement for a shared reference dataset, the unsupervised nature of discrepancy discovery, and the absence of formal guarantees linking discovered directions to semantic categories; extending to unmatched datasets and task-conditioned notions of discrepancy are natural next steps.
