## Problem

Pretrained neural networks lose accuracy when inputs undergo unknown geometric transformations (e.g., perspective warps, PDE symmetries), and existing canonicalization or sampling methods are either restricted to specific transformation families, rely on deterministic optimization over rugged energy landscapes, or operate in high-dimensional data space rather than on the transformation group itself. This paper asks whether one can sample an inverse transformation from a general (non-compact, non-Abelian, non-bi-invariant) Lie group using only an energy function defined on the data space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single out-of-distribution sample x̃ = g·x (an image or PDE initial condition), a pretrained energy function (classifier logit confidence or VAE ELBO), and the Lie group structure (e.g., PGL(3,ℝ) for homographies, SL(2,ℝ)⋊H(1,ℝ) for heat equation symmetries). It outputs a sampled group element g from the posterior p(g|x̃), which yields a canonicalized in-distribution sample g⁻¹·x̃ and a final prediction g·f(g⁻¹·x̃). The energy need only be accurate within each orbit of the group action, not across the full data space, and the entire pipeline is training-free.

## Method

TIED models the posterior over inverse transformations as a Boltzmann density on the Lie group and samples from it via a reverse diffusion SDE that stays on-manifold by expressing all updates in the Lie algebra through left-trivialization. The key theoretical contribution is a trivialized target score identity (Prop. 4.2) that expresses the score of the noisy marginal as an expectation of clean energy gradients, generalized to arbitrary Lie groups without requiring a bi-invariant metric—unlike prior Lie-group extensions. Practically, the score is estimated by Monte Carlo over noise samples drawn from the forward kernel k_t (Prop. 4.3), and the trivialized gradient is computed via standard automatic differentiation using the identity ∇_𝔤 f(g) = ∇_v f(g·exp(v))|_{v=0} (Prop. A.1). The reverse SDE is discretized with Euler steps where each update is g ← g·exp(γ²s·Δt + γ·z), guaranteeing on-manifold evolution. The paper further proves the posterior is G-equivariant (Prop. 3.2), ensuring the resulting test-time predictor f̃(x̃) = g·f(g⁻¹·x̃) is equivariant for any pretrained f.

## Results

On MNIST under affine transformations Aff(2,ℝ), TIED with VAE energy achieves 96.95% accuracy (vs. 55.48% for the unmodified ResNet18), and with classifier confidence energy achieves 82.64%. Under homography transformations PGL(3,ℝ), TIED reaches 97.43% (VAE energy) and 89.84% (classifier energy), compared to 87.95% for the unmodified network. On PDE solving, TIED achieves the lowest relative L2 error across all three settings: 0.043 on the 1D heat equation, 0.052 with data augmentation, and 0.178 on the 1D Burgers equation, outperforming the next-best baseline (LieLAC) in every case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIED outperforms all five baselines in every experimental setting. Against the closest competitor LieLAC (trivialized optimization), TIED gains +2.57% accuracy on affine with VAE energy (96.95 vs. 94.38) and +8.85% with classifier energy (82.64 vs. 73.79); on PDE solving, TIED reduces L2 error from 0.066 to 0.043 (heat) and from 0.187 to 0.178 (Burgers). Kinetic Langevin, which uses only the energy gradient without diffusion, underperforms substantially on affine with classifier energy (53.98% vs. TIED's 82.64%), likely due to the rugged energy landscape from the neural network. The iterative search methods ITS and FoCal are competitive on affine with classifier energy (67.18% and 66.97% respectively) but still trail TIED by ~15 points, and ITS does not extend to homography. TIED also surpasses the specialized equivariant networks affConv/homConv (95.08%/95.71%) under both transformation groups when using VAE energy, demonstrating that a training-free test-time approach can match or exceed purpose-built equivariant architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIED sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) score-based diffusion sampling for Boltzmann densities (Song et al., 2020; Akhound-Sadegh et al., 2024; De Bortoli et al., 2024), which is here transplanted onto Lie groups; (2) left-trivialization for optimization and SDEs on manifolds (Lezcano Casado, 2019; Kong & Tao, 2024; Zhu et al., 2025), which provides the on-manifold update mechanism; and (3) test-time canonicalization for robustness (Kaba et al., 2023; Schmidt & Stober, 2024; Shumaylov et al., 2024; Singhal et al., 2025), which motivates the application. The key departure from prior diffusion-on-Lie-groups work (Zhu et al., 2025) is that TIED requires only an energy function rather than clean samples, and its trivialized target score identity (Prop. 4.2) removes the bi-invariant metric assumption that constrained earlier Lie-group extensions of the target score identity (De Bortoli et al., 2024) to groups isomorphic to ℝᵏ × K with K compact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIED offers a training-free, architecture-agnostic recipe for making any pretrained model equivariant to a broad class of Lie groups at test time, with the energy potentially coming from the model's own prediction confidence. The practical impact is significant for foundation models that must handle out-of-distribution geometric perturbations without retraining or specialized equivariant architectures. Limitations include the per-sample diffusion sampling cost (mitigated by N≤10 MC samples and parallelization), the need for a well-behaved energy landscape, and the fact that gains over the strongest gradient-based baseline (LieLAC) are modest in several settings. Open questions include whether consistency distillation or other acceleration techniques can reduce the test-time cost, and whether the approach extends to infinite-dimensional groups or non-diffeomorphic actions.
