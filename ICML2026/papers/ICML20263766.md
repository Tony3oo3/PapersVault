## Problem

Existing VLM editors focus on simple factual corrections (e.g., renaming an object) and do not address reasoning-heavy visual question answering tasks where visual features and domain knowledge must be logically connected. Moreover, no prior editor allows users to supply their own chain of reasoning during editing, which is critical for producing edits that generalize beyond surface-level semantic similarity to samples sharing the same underlying reasoning facts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At editing time, ReasonEdit receives an image, a text question, the correct answer, and a human-provided chain of N_s factual reasoning statements, along with (optionally) user-cropped image patches serving as visual evidence for each statement. At inference, given a new image-text query, the method retrieves K nearest codebook entries and prepends the collected reasoning sentences as context to the VLM prompt, producing a corrected answer without any weight modification. The codebook grows sequentially with each new edit, with a key-merging procedure that caps memory overhead to roughly 80% of unmerged storage.

## Method

ReasonEdit is a retrieval-based, weight-free editor that stores each edit as key-value entries in a discrete codebook: an answer entry keyed by the full image-text embedding, plus one reasoning entry per factual statement keyed by the embedding of a visual-evidence image patch paired with that statement. The key innovation is a topology-balanced dual embedding E_dual that concatenates a mean-pooled vision-layer embedding (selected by maximizing bimodal sample modularity) with a pretrained sentence-transformer text embedding scaled by a weight w chosen to maximize the harmonic mean of vision and language modularities. This selection is grounded in network science: image-text embeddings are treated as nodes in a weighted similarity graph, and Newman's modularity quantifies how well the graph aligns with expected unimodal or bimodal partitions, exposing and correcting the vision-bias or language-bias that single-layer embeddings exhibit. At inference, a KNN search over codebook keys retrieves relevant reasoning facts as prompt context, with a percentile-based rejection threshold to preserve locality on unrelated queries.

## Results

On A-OKVQA (the larger dataset with 5,000–7,000+ errors per VLM), ReasonEdit achieves near-perfect reliability (Acc = 0.97–0.99) and strong generality across all four VLMs. Most notably, ReasonEdit substantially outperforms all baselines on the two new reasoning-enhanced metrics: Rationale Generality (R-Gen = 0.77–0.86) and Chain-of-Error Generality (CoE-Gen = 0.95–0.98), where the best baseline (IKE-COT) reaches only R-Gen ≈ 0.66–0.77 and CoE-Gen ≈ 0.74–0.85. On FVQA, ReasonEdit similarly leads with Acc = 0.96–1.00, R-Gen = 0.81–0.88, and CoE-Gen = 0.92–0.97. In sequential editing over 5,000+ edits, ReasonEdit maintains stable performance while weight-updating editors (FT, MEND, BalancEdit) exhibit catastrophic degradation in accuracy and generality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonEdit outperforms all five baselines (and their COT-augmented variants) on reliability, text generality, image generality, rationale generality, and chain-of-error generality across all four VLMs and both datasets. The closest competitor is IKE-COT, which achieves competitive accuracy (0.96–0.99) and image generality (0.95–0.98) but trails ReasonEdit by 8–15 points on R-Gen and 10–24 points on CoE-Gen, indicating that merely storing reasoning facts without topology-aware retrieval is insufficient for reasoning-level generalization. GRACE-COT achieves the best locality (0.98 on LLaVA A-OKVQA) but suffers from poor CoE-Gen (0.62) and R-Gen (0.61), as its single-layer embedding lacks the balanced multimodal topology needed to retrieve relevant reasoning. Weight-updating editors (FT-COT, MEND-COT, BalancEdit-COT) are consistently the weakest, with MEND-COT barely exceeding the unedited baseline on generality metrics and all three showing catastrophic degradation in sequential editing. The COT variants of all baselines yield only marginal gains over their non-COT counterparts (e.g., IKE-COT vs. IKE: R-Gen 0.76 vs. 0.62 on LLaVA A-OKVQA), confirming that adding reasoning supervision alone cannot replicate the retrieval-based generalization that ReasonEdit achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReasonEdit extends the retrieval-based model editing lineage (GRACE, IKE) by incorporating human chain-of-thought reasoning as structured input during editing, a setup absent from prior VLM editors that focus on label correction or simple entity renaming. It departs from weight-updating editors (MEND, BalancEdit) by avoiding any parameter modification, instead storing reasoning facts in a codebook for selective retrieval. The topology-balanced dual embedding, grounded in Newman modularity from network science, provides a principled alternative to the ad hoc single-layer embedding selection used by prior retrieval-based VLM editors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that merely training editors to generate reasoning (COT-variants) yields only marginal gains in reasoning-level generalization, whereas retrieving and injecting the correct human reasoning facts at inference time is what enables substantial R-Gen and CoE-Gen improvements—a conceptual finding that shifts emphasis from learning reasoning to retrieving it. Open questions include how the codebook scales to thousands of edits, how to handle genuinely incorrect or incomplete human reasoning beyond the noise-injection experiments, and whether the modularity-based embedding selection transfers to architectures without clear vision/language block boundaries. The new evaluation metrics (R-Gen, CoE-Gen) provide a useful framework for assessing whether edits generalize through shared reasoning rather than surface similarity.
