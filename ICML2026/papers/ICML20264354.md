## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of Decentralized SGD characterize topology effects solely through the spectral gap (the second-largest eigenvalue in absolute value of the mixing matrix), predicting that sparse topologies like rings should dramatically slow convergence in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings. However, extensive experimental evidence shows that in the homogeneous case, topology choice has surprisingly little practical impact on training behavior. This paper resolves the theory-practice gap by showing that the full eigenvalue spectrum of the mixing matrix, not just the spectral gap, governs the convergence rate.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a connected network topology specified by a symmetric, doubly stochastic mixing matrix W ∈ [0,1]^{n×n}, along with n local loss functions {f_i} and their stochastic gradient noise parameters (σ, ζ). The output is a convergence rate bound for the average parameter x̄^(r) that depends on the spectral quantity (1/n)Σ_{i=2}^n λ_i²/(1−λ_i²) rather than the coarser (1−p)/p, where p is the spectral gap. This spectral quantity is strictly smaller than (1−p)/p for all non-complete topologies, yielding tighter (less pessimistic) bounds that better match observed training dynamics.

## Method

The key technical insight is that stochastic gradient noise is independent across nodes, so when gossip averaging acts on the noise term, the worst-case operator norm bound (giving max_{i≥2}(λ_i²)) can be replaced by an averaged bound (1/n)Σ_{i=2}^n λ_i² via a trace argument (Lemma 6). This refinement is embedded into a recursive analysis of the consensus error Ξ^(r,k) = (1/n)E‖X^(r)W^k − X̄^(r)‖_F², where the recursion is unrolled over k steps and the geometric series in λ_i²/(1−λ_i²) is summed to yield the final rate. The heterogeneity term (ζ or ζ_★) still depends on the spectral gap p, reflecting a fundamental limitation of Decentralized SGD under data heterogeneity, while the noise term (σ or σ_★) enjoys the improved full-spectrum dependence. The resulting Theorem 1 improves transient iteration counts from O(n⁷) to O(n⁵) for ring topologies and from O(n⁵) to O(n³ log²(n)) for torus topologies in the non-convex homogeneous case.

## Results

The main quantitative result is the improvement in transient iteration (number of rounds R before the stochastic noise term dominates) in the non-convex homogeneous case: Theorem 1 reduces the transient from O(n⁷) to O(n⁵) for ring, O(n⁵) to O(n³ log²(n)) for torus, and O(n³ log²(n)) to O(n³) for hypercube, compared to the prior best bound (Koloskova et al., 2020b). Experimentally, on MNIST with n=200 nodes and logistic/ridge regression, the final loss increases monotonically with (1/n)Σ(λᵢ²/(1−λᵢ²)) even when the spectral gap p is held constant, confirming that the full eigenvalue spectrum governs convergence. On Fashion-MNIST with LeNet (n=25), test accuracy decreases as (1/n)Σ(λᵢ²/(1−λᵢ²)) increases in both homogeneous and heterogeneous (Dirichlet α) settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the convergence rate of Koloskova et al. (2020b) (Proposition 1), which relies solely on the spectral gap p to characterize topology effects. The paper's Theorem 1 strictly improves upon this bound in the homogeneous/near-homogeneous regime by replacing (1−p)/p with (1/n)Σ(λᵢ²/(1−λᵢ²)), yielding a two-power reduction in transient iterations for ring (O(n⁷)→O(n⁵)) and torus (O(n⁵)→O(n³ log²(n))). The paper also contrasts with Neglia et al. (2020), which captured all eigenvalues but required bounded stochastic gradients and failed to improve upon Proposition 1, and Vogels et al. (2022), which introduced the 'effective number of neighbors' but whose rate depends on a hyperparameter γ making direct comparison unclear. The heterogeneity-dependent term (1−p)ζ²/p² remains unchanged, confirming that the spectral gap dependence for heterogeneous data is a fundamental limitation of Decentralized SGD rather than an artifact of the prior analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Decentralized SGD algorithm of Lian et al. (2017) and the convergence analysis framework of Koloskova et al. (2020b), which established the standard spectral-gap-based convergence rates. The key departure is a novel proof technique that exploits the independence of stochastic gradient noise across nodes to replace the spectral-gap-only bound (1-p)/p with the full-spectrum quantity (1/n)Σ(λ_i²/(1-λ_i²)) in the noise term. Prior attempts to capture full eigenvalue effects—Neglia et al. (2020) with bounded-gradient assumptions and Vogels et al. (2022) with a hyperparameter-dependent 'effective number of neighbors'—failed to yield rates provably better than the spectral-gap baseline, making this the first improvement under standard smoothness assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing theory-practice gap: prior analyses predicted that sparse topologies (ring, torus) should dramatically slow convergence in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings, yet experiments consistently showed robustness to topology when data is similar across nodes. The practical implication is that topology design for decentralized learning should target the full eigenvalue spectrum rather than merely maximizing the spectral gap, opening new design directions. However, the heterogeneity term still depends on the spectral gap, so the improvement is specific to near-homogeneous regimes; extending the technique to other decentralized methods (gradient tracking, time-varying topologies) and to strongly heterogeneous settings remains open.
