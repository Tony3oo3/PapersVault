## Problem

Existing modality-missing prompt tuning methods for Multimodal Transformers (MTs) condition prompts solely on the observed modalities, inadvertently constraining the MT's reasoning to the modality-reduced subspace and cutting off access to latent information sources of the missing modalities. The paper identifies this as the Implicit Modality-Reduction (IMR) Bottleneck and asks whether current prompting paradigms can be restructured to restore the full multimodal reasoning scope of pretrained MTs under incomplete inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts incomplete dual- or tri-modal inputs where one or more modalities (RGB image, text, or audio) may be absent; missing text is represented as an empty string and missing images as all-ones pixel tensors. It produces a class label for the downstream task (multi-label movie genre, binary hate-meme detection, 101-way food recognition, or 4-way emotion recognition). The key unusual aspect is that the method requires a one-time offline forward pass over all modality-available training samples through the frozen MT to build the modality-specific information collections before any prompt tuning begins.

## Method

AOEPT shifts the paradigm from adapting MTs to degraded inputs to actively compensating for missing-modality information via a three-stage modal-contextualized prompting pipeline. First, layer-wise token representations from frozen MT forward passes over modality-available training samples are pooled, refined via K-means clustering into N' prototypes, and distilled into lightweight Modal-Contextualized Prompts (MCPs) per layer using cross-attention (or MLP/initialization alternatives), forming global modality-level latent repositories. Second, for each incomplete sample, the MCPs are instantiated into instance-aware prompts by element-wise gating with a sigmoid-MLP projection of the pooled remaining-modality representation, selectively activating the most relevant missing-modality information; an intra-modal latent consistency regularization (InfoNCE-style) further disentangles sample-specific cues. Third, these instance-aware prompts are inserted into the first N encoder layers (newly instantiated per layer) and propagated through the remaining layers, with only the MCPs and classification head optimized. The paper provides an information-theoretic proof (Lemma E.1, Proposition E.2) showing that observed-only prompting yields zero conditional mutual information with the missing modality, while AOEPT's explicit conditioning on the modality repository establishes a valid information-access path, and introduces the NM²I diagnostic metric to empirically quantify the severity of the IMR bottleneck.

## Results

Under the 70% modality-missing setting, AOEPT achieves average F1-Macro of 53.22 on MM-IMDb, average AUC of 69.63 on HateMemes, and average Accuracy of 84.29 on Food101, outperforming the strongest baseline SyP (51.88, 68.11, 83.56 respectively). Under the more challenging 90% missing rate, AOEPT maintains a clear lead with 51.45 F1-M, 68.57 AUC, and 82.06 ACC, while baselines degrade more sharply. AOEPT also introduces the NM²I diagnostic metric, showing that baseline methods yield near-zero mutual information between prompts and missing-modality representations, whereas AOEPT achieves substantially higher NM²I, confirming it breaks the Implicit Modality-Reduction bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOEPT outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and missing scenario at both 70% and 90% missing rates. Against the strongest baseline SyP (ICCV'25), AOEPT gains +1.34 F1-M on MM-IMDb, +1.52 AUC on HateMemes, and +0.73 ACC on Food101 at 70% missing, with margins widening to +1.87, +0.85, and +0.80 at 90% missing. Compared to the original MAPs (CVPR'23), the improvements are larger: +2.86 F1-M, +6.50 AUC, and +3.86 ACC at 70% missing. Notably, AOEPT achieves these gains with fewer learnable parameters and lower per-batch inference cost than retrieval-based (RAGPT) or memory-based (MemPrompt) methods, which incur substantial overhead from instance-wise retrieval or memory mechanisms. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that removing the Modal-Contextualized Prompts (reverting to random initialization) drops performance back to MAPs-level, validating that the modality-level prior distillation is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the prompt-tuning paradigm for modality-missing learning in Multimodal Transformers, building on the lineage from MAPs (CVPR'23) through DCP, MemPrompt, SyP, and RAGPT. Its key conceptual departure is identifying the Implicit Modality-Reduction (IMR) bottleneck: prior methods treat prompts as learnable signals to adapt MTs to degraded input structures, inadvertently confining reasoning to the observed-modality subspace. AOEPT reframes the problem from passive adaptation to active compensation by distilling global modality-wise priors into lightweight Modal-Contextualized Prompts (MCPs) via frozen forward passes and k-means clustering, then instantiating them into instance-aware prompts conditioned on remaining modalities. The information-theoretic analysis (Lemma E.1, Proposition E.2) formalizes why observed-only prompting cannot access missing-modality information, while the NM²I metric provides a diagnostic tool absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the IMR bottleneck and the Modality Information Scaling bottleneck (where baselines plateau or even degrade as training data becomes more modality-complete) offers a useful diagnostic lens for the modality-missing prompt-tuning community. AOEPT's solution is practical: it requires no external retrieval, no reconstruction networks, and introduces minimal learnable parameters, making it deployable in resource-constrained settings. However, the paper acknowledges that NM²I is not monotonic with performance, that the method assumes similar training/test semantic distributions, and that the performance gains over strong baselines (SyP, MemPrompt) are consistent but moderate (1–3 points). Open questions include whether the modal-contextualized prompting paradigm transfers to generative multimodal tasks (e.g., MLLMs) and whether additional constraints on prompts could extract more predictive information from the restored modality space.
