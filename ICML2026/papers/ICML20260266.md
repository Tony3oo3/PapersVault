## Problem

Anisotropy in Transformer representations has been extensively documented but rarely explained through an explicitly geometric lens that accounts for local manifold structure and on-manifold phenomena. In particular, no prior work has derived how frequency-biased sampling attenuates curvature visibility in the embedding space, nor has it connected this geometric attenuation to the gradient dynamics that preferentially amplify tangent directions during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260266/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes transformer model checkpoints sampled uniformly from the first 30% (early) and last 30% (late) of training across ten models (encoder and decoder architectures, 160M–1.7B parameters). From each checkpoint it extracts token embedding rows, intermediate-layer activations, and per-sample backpropagated gradient rows, which are centered around a fitted early-context mean. Outputs are structured as per-layer, per-phase statistics: dimension-normalized gradient energy ratios (tangent vs. matched-rank normal), Monte Carlo null p-values, IsoScore* anisotropy deltas for tangent vs. normal removal, and one-sided sign-test p-values across anchor tokens.

## Method

The theoretical core establishes that under a local C³ manifold hypothesis, a radial sampling density g(t) that concentrates at small ambient radius (as occurs for high-frequency tokens) drives a moment ratio η_g → 0, which suppresses both intrinsic (Ricci) and extrinsic (second fundamental form) curvature contributions in the marginal directional distribution, reducing geometric expressivity. A complementary gradient analysis shows that for small t, tangent covariance scales as O(t²) while normal covariance scales as O(t⁴), so backpropagated gradient energy along normal directions is parametrically suppressed by a factor of O(t) relative to tangent directions, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop amplified through attention and residual connections. Empirically, the paper fits a fixed low-rank tangent proxy Q_T via PCA on pooled early-checkpoint activations (a concept-based mechanistic-interpretability approach used during training rather than post hoc), then tests whether ordinary backpropagated true gradients concentrate disproportionately in Q_T versus deterministic matched-rank normal subspaces, and whether removing Q_T recovers more isotropy (via IsoScore*) than removing an equally sized normal basis, across encoder and decoder models at multiple depths and training phases.

## Results

Across 10 encoder- and decoder-style models, activation-derived tangent subspaces capture dramatically more true-gradient energy per dimension than matched-rank normal controls: energy ratios (E_r) range from ~4× (EuroBERT-210m, last layer, late) to over 4×10⁶ (Pythia-160m, embedding input, early), with Monte Carlo null p-values frequently at the floor of 1/21 ≈ 4.8×10⁻². Removing the tangent proxy recovers substantially more isotropy (ΔIso*_T) than removing a matched normal basis (ΔIso*_N): for example, in the early phase, ΔIso*_T reaches 89.6% for Gaperon-1.5B first-layer output and 104% for OLMo-1B first-layer output, while ΔIso*_N stays near zero or slightly negative. Frequency-induced variance collapse is confirmed in causal decoders with Pearson ρ ≈ −0.5 between log-frequency and mean embedding distance to centroid, versus a much weaker ρ ≈ −0.2 to +0.1 in EuroBERT encoders, consistent with the theory's prediction that milder radial concentration yields a weaker tangent bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between activation-derived tangent subspaces (Q_T) and deterministic matched-rank normal subspaces (Q_N,det) of equal dimension, evaluated against ordinary backpropagated true gradients. Tangent directions consistently outperform the normal baseline on both energy concentration and anisotropy explanation: E_r is typically 10–1000× in the early phase, and ΔIso*_T is positive and large while ΔIso*_N is near zero or slightly negative, with one-sided sign-test p-values often at 6×10⁻⁸. A second axis of comparison is architectural: decoder models (Pythia, SmolLM2, Gaperon, OLMo) show markedly stronger tangent alignment and frequency-induced variance collapse (ρ ≈ −0.5) than encoder models (EuroBERT, ModernCamembert), where the effect is attenuated (ρ ≈ −0.2 to +0.1) and energy ratios are lower, consistent with the theory's prediction that milder radial concentration weakens the tangent-bias mechanism. A third comparison is temporal: the early training phase (first 30% of steps) exhibits the strongest tangent dominance, while the late phase (last 30%) shows reduced but still significant effects, supporting the claim that anisotropy is imprinted early and then carried forward under smaller updates. No external method baselines are compared; the paper's contribution is the geometric mechanism and its empirical validation rather than a competitive benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anisotropy literature (Ethayarajh 2019; Gao et al. 2019; Mu & Viswanath 2018) by grounding the phenomenon in local differential geometry: it derives how frequency-biased radial sampling attenuates curvature visibility via a chord-arc expansion (building on Smolyanov et al. 2007) and how gradient updates preferentially amplify tangent directions over normal ones. It departs from prior post-hoc anisotropy analyses by using concept-based mechanistic interpretability (Fel et al. 2023a,b) during training to extract activation-derived tangent proxies and test them against true backpropagated gradients, rather than only measuring anisotropy on final checkpoints. The stratified-manifold view of token spaces (Robinson et al. 2025, 2026) motivates the local (rather than global) geometric framework, and the frequency-norm relationship (Oyama et al. 2023; Liang et al. 2021) is repurposed as a sampling-law assumption driving the curvature-attenuation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes anisotropy from a training pathology to a natural consequence of the interplay between frequency-biased sampling geometry and tangent-dominant gradient dynamics, suggesting it may function as implicit dimensionality reduction that aids generalization in overparameterized models. A key limitation is the purely local and early-training scope of the analysis: the paper does not explain how loss-driven gradients subsequently reshape and sharpen representations in later training, leaving open how the early tangent imprint interacts with objective-specific directional selection. The weaker effect in encoder models (EuroBERT) compared to decoders, attributed to MLM masking and multilingual balancing, highlights that the mechanism's strength is architecture- and objective-dependent. For practitioners, the practical implication is that anisotropy is largely 'baked in' during the first ~30% of training, making post-hoc isotropy corrections potentially less effective than early-training interventions.
