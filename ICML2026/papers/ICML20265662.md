## Problem

Audio Language Models suffer from attention dilution on long-form recordings, where persistent background patterns dominate the model's focus and cause rare salient events (especially late-occurring ones) to be missed. Existing drift-detection approaches that could gate ALM processing require long-term historical buffering, offline calibration, or domain-specific training, making them impractical for real-time, training-free deployment on non-stationary audio streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265662/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous audio stream segmented into 4-second sliding windows, each encoded by a frozen PANN model into a 527-dimensional AudioSet probability vector. These vectors are mapped to frequency-specific oscillatory drives on a 64×64 spatial lattice (Δt = 0.01 s), and the OWM's global energy is compared against an online adaptive threshold to emit drift timestamps. Detected segments are forwarded to AudioQwen for semantic interpretation, yielding event labels and descriptions; the Time Sent Ratio (fraction of audio forwarded) averages ~0.6, meaning roughly 40% of ALM invocations are eliminated relative to exhaustive processing.

## Method

NAACA's core is OWM, a 2D recurrent pressure–velocity field on a G×G lattice governed by a damped wave equation with spatially varying propagation speed c(x,y) and damping k. Each of the 527 PANN categories is assigned a unique carrier frequency (51–1200 Hz) and a deterministic spatial parcel; the category probability modulates a sinusoidal drive injected at that parcel, producing frequency-selective oscillatory activity across the grid. The wave-speed field is a Bragg-matched striped square wave whose optimality for maximizing modal coupling and spatial frequency differentiation is proven analytically (Theorem 2.4), enabling the system to amplify energy transients from any substantial shift in the encoder probability vector. Salience is detected by monitoring the total system energy E(t) = ½Σ(p² + v_x² + v_y²) against an adaptive threshold T_adapt = μ + 2σ(1 + α·trend) computed over a 20-sample sliding window, with persistence filtering (≥50% of P=3 recent frames) and a cooldown period to suppress false positives. The entire pipeline is training-free: no OWM, encoder, or ALM parameters are updated, and the only online computation is the adaptive threshold, making the system immediately deployable without warm-up or reference corpora.

## Results

On the XD-Violence test set (500 samples, frame-level labels), NAACA achieves 70.60% Average Precision (AP), a 17.10-point absolute improvement over the exhaustive AudioQwen baseline (53.50% AP) while reducing ALM invocations by ~40% (median Time Sent Ratio of 0.597). OWM drift points align with ground-truth salient event frames at 61.1% temporal precision. On the USoW dataset, the median Time Sent Ratio is 0.650, confirming consistent ~35% cost reduction across diverse urban soundscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among audio-only methods, NAACA (70.60% AP) outperforms the exhaustive AudioQwen baseline by 17.10 points, the supervised HL-Net by 10.10 points, and AVadCLIP by 18.09 points, all without any task-specific training. A random 4-second segment selection baseline (60.44% AP) isolates the contribution of shorter inputs (6.94 points) from OWM-based saliency selection (an additional 10.16 points), confirming that the oscillatory gating mechanism is the primary driver of the gain. Against video-only baselines, NAACA trails Holmes-VAU (87.68%), VadCLIP (84.51%), TRACE (83.67%), and S3R (80.26%) by 9.7–17.1 points, a gap the authors attribute to inherent acoustic ambiguity (e.g., Abuse/Fighting confusion at 45%) and the absence of visual cues for high-energy transients. The paper notes that TRACE, despite its zero-shot label, requires training of a temporal encoder and cross-attention fusion layer, making it less directly comparable to NAACA's fully training-free protocol. Overall, NAACA establishes the strongest training-free, audio-only result on XD-Violence while simultaneously reducing computational cost by ~40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAACA carves new ground at the intersection of wave-physics-based signal processing and ALM attention management, departing from both statistical drift detectors (which require long historical windows) and representation-based methods like MCD-DD or DriftLens (which need offline reference distributions). The Oscillatory Working Memory repurposes the damped wave equation on a 2D lattice as a compact online state, drawing on Bragg-resonance physics for optimal sensitivity and on cortical oscillatory dynamics (beta/gamma bands) as a design analogy rather than a direct computational model. Unlike video-language gating approaches (Dispider, Holmes-VAU) that rely on supervised or contrastive detectors, NAACA is entirely training-free, using only a frozen PANN encoder and a frozen AudioQwen ALM with no fine-tuning or domain-specific calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, no-historical-data requirement makes NAACA practically deployable for continuous audio monitoring in resource-constrained or rapidly changing environments where offline calibration is infeasible. However, performance is inherently bounded by the PANN encoder's AudioSet coverage, the hard-gating interface may discard boundary context that soft-attention or KV-cache modulation could preserve, and evaluation is limited to anomaly-detection AP on one primary quantitative dataset. Open questions include whether OWM-gated inputs retain sufficient context for deeper reasoning tasks (audio QA, multi-turn summarization) and whether the physics-based gating generalizes beyond the 527-class PANN space to other encoder architectures or open-vocabulary settings.
