## Problem

Cross-subject emotion recognition suffers from two major limitations in existing approaches: (1) most domain generalization methods are tightly coupled to EEG-specific feature designs, limiting transferability to other modalities, and (2) data augmentation methods assume i.i.d. data, yet emotion datasets exhibit pronounced distributional heterogeneity with multiple distinct sub-distributions of varying scales. This mixed-distribution nature causes holistic modeling to bias toward dominant subsets and degrades generalization to minority distributions and unseen subjects.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a frozen encoder's representation space rather than on raw signals directly. A modality-appropriate encoder (CNN for EEG 3D tensors, MLP for physiological vectors, LSTM for societal behavior time series) is first trained to convergence, then frozen to produce fixed-dimensional representations (512 or 1024-d). The cross-subject protocol enforces zero subject overlap between training and test sets, and the final output is a discrete emotion class label (3 or 4 classes depending on the dataset).

## Method

MixEmo comprises two components. Unseen Distribution Generation (UDG) applies a Gaussian Mixture Model to partition the frozen representation set into M single-distribution prototypes, then synthesizes novel distributions by randomly sampling from each prototype as a 'main' subset and combining it with samples from all other prototypes under the constraint m/n ≥ M−1, guaranteeing each synthesized subset follows a distribution distinct from all originals (proven via Proposition 2.1). Heterogeneous Distribution Collaborative Learning (HDCL) then trains a shared prediction model via an alternating scheme: each subset is split into support and query sets, a proxy model is fine-tuned on the support set, and the shared model is updated using query losses weighted by a softmax of loss magnitudes (Weighted Gradient Update), with Stochastic Weight Averaging applied across iterations to reach flatter optima. The convergence of this composite objective is formally established (Theorem 3.1) under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, yielding an O(1/√T) gradient-norm bound.

## Results

On the cross-subject setting, MixEmo achieves the best accuracy and F1-score across all five datasets. The largest gains appear on SE-F1530 (52.67% Acc, 45.80% F1) and WESAD (70.37% Acc, 67.58% F1), where improvements over the strongest baseline reach up to 22.44% in accuracy and 17.27% in F1. On the EEG-specific comparison (Table 3), MixEmo outperforms all four EEG-tailored methods on DEAP (29.52% Acc vs. best 27.63%) but is slightly behind DMMR on SEED (62.04% vs. 62.41% Acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixEmo outperforms all seven general data augmentation baselines (Mixup, MAML, CTGAN, GT-GAN, PCF-GAN, InfoTS, TimeDP) on every dataset for both accuracy and F1. The closest competitor is CTGAN, which leads on SEED (58.52% Acc) and WESAD (66.59% Acc) among baselines, yet MixEmo still surpasses it by 3.52 and 3.78 percentage points respectively. Against the four EEG-specific cross-subject methods (EEGMatch, DMMR, MoGE, EmT), MixEmo wins on DEAP (29.52% vs. 27.63% best) but trails DMMR slightly on SEED (62.04% vs. 62.41% Acc; 60.32% vs. 61.35% F1). The authors note that the EEG-specific baselines cannot be applied to the physiological or societal-behavior datasets, so MixEmo's modality-agnostic design gives it a practical advantage despite the narrow SEED gap. Overall, MixEmo's largest absolute gains over the best general baseline appear on SE-F1530 (+3.05% Acc, +1.10% F1 over CTGAN) and WESAD (+2.78% Acc, +1.92% F1 over GT-GAN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing cross-subject emotion recognition through the lens of distributional heterogeneity rather than the standard domain generalization or instance-level augmentation paradigms. It repurposes the MAML-style support/query split from task-level meta-learning to distribution-level collaborative learning (HDCL), and introduces a GMM-based partitioning of the embedding space into distribution prototypes that are then recombined to synthesize unseen distributions (UDG). Conceptually, it departs from prior work by operating at the distribution level rather than the instance level (contrasting with Mixup) and by being explicitly modality-agnostic (contrasting with EEG-specific methods like EEGMatch, DMMR, MoGE, and EmT), while drawing on standard tools like GMM, SWA, and weighted gradient updates in a novel compositional arrangement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of distributional heterogeneity across five emotion datasets spanning three modalities provides a useful empirical foundation that challenges the i.i.d. assumption underlying many augmentation strategies. The modality-agnostic design is practically valuable as multimodal emotion recognition grows, and the framework's generality (CNN/MLP/LSTM encoders) makes it adaptable to new modalities without architectural redesign. However, absolute cross-subject performance remains low (e.g., 29.52% on DEAP, 52.67% on SE-F1530), and the authors acknowledge that when intra-class structures are highly compact, the generated distributions only partially cover sparse regions. Open questions include whether the GMM-based partitioning generalizes to non-Gaussian or highly overlapping distributions, and whether the framework can close the substantial gap between cross-subject and non-cross-subject performance.
