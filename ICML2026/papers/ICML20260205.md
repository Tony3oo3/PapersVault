## Problem

When a generator is fine-tuned to reduce the probability of being filtered by a downstream safety classifier, it tends to push outputs toward the classifier's decision boundary, where small modeling errors amplify both over-blocking of benign content and under-blocking of harmful content. Existing safety-alignment methods optimize the generator in isolation rather than the compound system, leaving this boundary-proximity failure mode unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (jailbreak attempts, harmful requests, benign instructions, or long-context queries up to 1,000 words) and produces a text completion of at most 96 tokens. The completion is then scored by a safety classifier (e.g., Llama Guard 2-8B); if the score exceeds a threshold (τ=0.5), the output is replaced with a refusal token ⊥. During training, both the classifier score and a separate reward model score are computed per completion to form the composite reward signal.

## Method

Boundary Guidance is a reinforcement-learning fine-tuning method that augments a utility reward with a margin term based on the safety classifier score: R(x,y) = u(x,y) + λ|t(x,y) − τ|. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 1) shows that under mild monotonicity assumptions, compound-system utility is globally minimized at the classifier threshold and nondecreasing in the margin |t − τ|, so rewarding distance from the boundary is aligned with system-level objectives. Training uses GRPO with LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) on 4-bit quantized policy models (Qwen2.5 0.5B–14B, Gemma-2-9B), with the safety classifier and reward model serving as frozen scoring functions. The key distinction from prior work is that the reward explicitly penalizes borderline outputs even when they are locally optimal under a naive rejection-minimization objective, steering probability mass toward confidently safe or confidently unsafe regions rather than the fragile margin.

## Results

Across four model families (Qwen2.5-0.5B/7B/14B, Gemma-2-9B-it), Boundary Guidance consistently reduces judged harmfulness: from 0.27→0.17 (Qwen2.5-0.5B, Δ=−0.09***), 0.19→0.04 (Qwen2.5-7B, Δ=−0.15***), 0.07→0.04 (Gemma-2-9B, Δ=−0.03***), and 0.14→0.03 (Qwen2.5-14B, Δ=−0.11*). Helpfulness improves significantly for Qwen2.5-0.5B (+0.13***) and Gemma-2-9B (+0.03**), is flat for Qwen2.5-7B (+0.03, n.s.), and shows a small non-significant drop for Qwen2.5-14B (−0.05). Table 2 confirms the mechanism: the percentage of classifier scores outside the [0.1, 0.9] margin interval increases for every model and every prompt type after fine-tuning (e.g., jailbreak prompts for Qwen2.5-0.5B go from 80% to 97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the un-fine-tuned base model in the same compound system (generator + Llama Guard 2 filter). Boundary Guidance achieves a Pareto improvement (both helpfulness and harmfulness improve) for Qwen2.5-0.5B and Gemma-2-9B-it, and a near-Pareto improvement for Qwen2.5-7B (harmfulness drops sharply, helpfulness is flat). The only regression is a non-significant −0.05 helpfulness drop on Qwen2.5-14B. In the guard-only ablation (no reward model), larger models (7B, 14B, Gemma-2) still see significant harmfulness reductions with minimal helpfulness loss, but Qwen2.5-0.5B collapses (helpfulness −0.73, −34%) due to near-universal refusals. The prompt-aware boundary reward (Eq. 2) uniformly hurts: harmfulness increases for 3 of 4 models (e.g., +0.13 for Qwen2.5-14B) while helpfulness drops by 0.22–0.25 across the board, because the model learns to produce more explicit harmful content that the filter still cannot catch. Weaker classifiers (ShieldGemma-2B, Granite-Guardian-125M) reduce harmfulness more than Llama Guard 2 but at a large helpfulness cost (−0.89 and −0.31 respectively), suggesting a minimum classifier capacity threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard RLHF/safety-alignment fine-tuning paradigm (Safe RLHF, C-DPO, SafeDPO) that trains generators to minimize the probability of being filtered, by instead explicitly rewarding outputs that are far from the safety classifier's decision boundary. Theoretically, it draws on selective prediction with a reject option and monotone likelihood ratio conditions to justify why compound-system utility is minimized at the filter threshold. Methodologically, it uses GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a simple margin-shaped reward, positioning itself between inference-time classifier guidance (PPLM, GeDi) and training-time safety alignment, while connecting to the emerging compound-systems literature (Baker et al., 2025; Wichers et al., 2024) that treats generator-filter interactions as a joint optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Boundary Guidance offers a drop-in reward-shaping term that practitioners can add to existing RLHF pipelines to reduce both over-refusal and under-blocking without retraining the safety classifier. The negative result on prompt-aware rewards (which intuitively should help but uniformly hurt performance) and the finding that guard-only rewards suffice for models ≥7B provide practical guidance for deployment. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multiple correlated safety classifiers, how performance degrades when the training-time and deployment-time classifiers differ, and whether the monotone likelihood ratio assumption holds for all harm categories in practice.
