## Problem

ANN-to-SNN conversion achieves competitive performance in classification and discrete control but suffers substantially larger degradation in continuous control, where suitable baselines are largely absent. The paper identifies the root cause: small action approximation errors become positively temporally correlated across consecutive decision steps, inducing cumulative state distribution shift and severe long-horizon performance loss that is absent in single-shot or categorical-output settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained ANN policy (trained with DDPG, TD3, SAC, or DrQ-v2) and converts it to an SNN without any additional training. At inference, the SNN receives either low-dimensional vector states (e.g., 17-dim for HalfCheetah) or 84×84×3 RGB frames from the DeepMind Control Suite, runs for T internal simulation steps (2–64) per decision step, and outputs a continuous action vector. The only added state is the residual membrane potential carried from the previous decision step, making the conversion fully training-free and drop-in compatible with existing pipelines.

## Method

Cross-Step Residual Potential Initialization (CRPI) suppresses temporally correlated conversion errors by carrying over residual membrane potentials across consecutive decision steps. Specifically, after each decision step k, the difference between the final and initial membrane potentials (v_k^l[T] − v_k^l[0]) is computed per layer, clipped to a valid firing range, and added (scaled by a tunable α) to the initial membrane potential of the next step, effectively subtracting the previous step's residual error from the current one. This first-order correction drives the expected cosine similarity of successive residual errors below zero, breaking the positive temporal correlation that causes error amplification. CRPI requires no gradient computation, no architectural modification, and introduces less than 1% additional accumulation operations, making it a lightweight inference-time mechanism that composes with existing conversion techniques such as signed neurons, multi-threshold neurons, and differential coding.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) visual tasks, CRPI-converted SNNs achieve Average Performance Ratios (APR) of 74.19% (IF, T=32) and 91.84% (IF, T=64), substantially exceeding directly trained SNN baselines (LIF: 54.19%, TC-LIF: 61.25%, Spiking-WM: 68.54%). With more expressive neuron models, CRPI pushes APR above 100%: SNM (T=16) reaches 101.41%, MT (T=2) reaches 101.30%, and DC (T=2) reaches 100.43%, matching or surpassing the original ANN policy. On MuJoCo vector tasks, CRPI consistently improves APR across all neuron models, RL algorithms, and simulation lengths, with the largest single gain of +18.00% (MT, T=2, DDPG: 68.74% → 86.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CRPI-converted SNNs against two directly trained SNN approaches on the six DMC visual tasks. TC-LIF (Zhang et al., AAAI 2024), a two-compartment spiking neuron model trained from scratch, achieves an APR of 61.25% at T=8; CRPI with IF neurons at T=64 reaches 91.84% (+30.6 pp), and CRPI with SNM at T=16 reaches 101.41% (+40.2 pp), both substantially outperforming TC-LIF. Spiking-WM (Sun et al., PNAS 2025), which incorporates a learned world model, achieves 68.54% APR at T=8; CRPI with IF T=64 (91.84%) and SNM T=16 (101.41%) again exceed it by 23–33 percentage points. The gap is most pronounced on Cheetah_Run and Quadruped_Walk, where directly trained SNNs lose 25–65% of ANN performance while CRPI-converted SNNs retain 87–97%. These results demonstrate that leveraging a well-trained ANN policy with CRPI's error-decorrelation mechanism is far more effective than training SNNs from scratch, even when the SNN baseline includes auxiliary components like world models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion literature from classification and discrete control (Atari) to continuous control, where prior work is largely absent. The CRPI mechanism builds directly on the rate-coding conversion framework of Bu et al. (2022b) and the membrane potential distribution assumptions of Bu et al. (2022a), repurposing the residual potential concept from a within-step initialization trick into a cross-step error decorrelation mechanism. It departs from prior SNN-for-RL work (which focuses on training spiking policies from scratch via STBP or e-prop) by operating entirely in the gradient-free conversion regime, and from prior ANN-to-SNN conversion in RL (Patel et al., Tan et al.) by identifying a fundamentally different failure mode—temporal error amplification—that is specific to continuous action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes continuous control as a critical and previously underexplored benchmark for ANN-to-SNN conversion, revealing that the dominant failure mode (state distribution shift from temporally correlated errors) is qualitatively different from the approximation-error regime in classification. CRPI's practical value is high: it is gradient-free, adds <1% computational overhead, and is compatible with diverse neuron models and RL algorithms, making it a drop-in improvement for existing conversion pipelines. Open questions include adaptive selection of the correlation parameter α (currently grid-searched), whether the mechanism generalizes to other sequential generation tasks vulnerable to error accumulation (language modeling, diffusion), and whether the positive temporal correlation persists under different environment dynamics or policy architectures.
