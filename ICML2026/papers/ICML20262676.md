## Problem

Best-of-N selection depends on reliable verification to distinguish correct from incorrect reasoning trajectories, but widely used logit-based confidence proxies suffer from calibration collapse—where output confidence becomes decoupled from or even negatively correlated with correctness—while sample-agreement methods like self-consistency are costly and can miss correct minority trajectories. This paper asks whether the model's internal latent dynamics during generation contain a training-free signal that remains informative precisely when output-level confidence fails.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262676/cropped_page2_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N sampled reasoning trajectories (temperature 0.7, top-p=0.9) and extracts residual-stream hidden states from a single chosen transformer layer via a separate batched GPU forward pass decoupled from vLLM generation. It outputs a per-candidate LVE score (Shannon entropy of the normalized velocity distribution over time steps) and selects the minimum-entropy trajectory as the final answer. Scoring requires only 7.1 KB/token of hidden-state transfer versus 608 KB/token for full logits, and the entire pipeline is training-free with no external verifier or logit access.

## Method

Motivated by metastable dwell-and-jump dynamics in cognitive neuroscience, the paper hypothesizes that correct reasoning trajectories concentrate latent representation updates into a few decisive transitions rather than spreading them uniformly across tokens. LVE operationalizes this by computing per-step hidden-state update magnitudes (velocities), normalizing them into a probability distribution over time steps, and taking the Shannon entropy of that distribution—lower entropy indicates temporally concentrated, selective updates characteristic of structured reasoning. The length-normalized variant (LogNorm-LVE) divides by log T to correct for the fact that longer trajectories have a larger entropy capacity, yielding a bounded [0,1] score comparable across candidates of different lengths. Selection is argmin LVE subject to a minimal total-update gate, requiring no training, no external verifier, and no logit access. The key distinction from output-level metrics like token entropy or UID is that LVE probes the temporal organization of internal state changes rather than surface-level prediction probabilities, making it robust to the calibration collapse that undermines logit-based confidence.

## Results

On MATH500 (500 problems), LogNorm-LVE achieves 92.2% Pass@1 at N=10 with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, the highest among all tested methods, and 85.0% with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. At N=3, LVE already reaches 87.4% on MATH500, nearly matching Self-Consistency at N=10 (87.6%) with ~70% fewer samples. Across the four-benchmark average at N=10, LVE/LogNorm-LVE attain 56.5%/56.4% (Qwen) and 52.5%/52.8% (Llama), outperforming all logit-based and entropy-based baselines. Candidate-level ROC on MATH500 confirms the signal: LVE achieves AUC 0.89 (Qwen) and 0.88 (Llama), while Self-Certainty drops to AUC 0.18/0.16 under calibration collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Consistency (majority voting), LogNorm-LVE gains +4.6 pp on MATH500 (92.2% vs. 87.6%, Qwen) and +1.2 pp on Llama (85.0% vs. 83.8%), while matching or slightly exceeding it on AIME25/BRUMO25; the key advantage is sample efficiency, with LVE at N=3 (87.4%) nearly matching Self-Consistency at N=10 (87.6%). Self-Certainty and DeepConf, both logit-confidence-based, suffer severe calibration collapse: Self-Certainty scores 38.1% avg (Qwen) and 30.9% (Llama), below Random (44.4%/41.0%), and its candidate-level AUC drops to 0.18/0.16, meaning the model is more confident when wrong. Low-Token-Entropy (mean next-token entropy) achieves 52.4% avg (Qwen) and 44.1% (Llama), consistently below LVE by 4+ pp. UID-Global and UID-Local, which measure non-uniformity in output surprisal, are the closest competitors (55.1% and 54.0% avg on Qwen), but LogNorm-LVE still edges them out on MATH500 (92.2% vs. 90.0%/90.6%) and GPQA (53.5% vs. 50.5%/52.0%), with the gap widening on Llama where UID-Local reaches only 50.4% avg versus 52.8% for LogNorm-LVE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of Best-of-N selection and cognitive neuroscience by repurposing the metastability concept (dwell-and-jump dynamics in neural populations) as a measurable proxy for LLM reasoning quality. It departs from output-level signals (logit confidence, token entropy, UID-style surprisal non-uniformity) that are vulnerable to calibration collapse, and from aggregated internal statistics (activation sparsity, cross-sample agreement) by instead scoring the *temporal concentration* of hidden-state updates along each individual trajectory. The method is explicitly positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, self-consistency voting and UID metrics, and it requires no trained verifier or external reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a training-free, low-overhead reranking signal that remains informative precisely when logit-based confidence fails (calibration collapse), with the clearest gains on deductive reasoning tasks where structured internal transitions matter. Key limitations constrain broader impact: white-box hidden-state access is required (excluding closed-source models), gains are task-dependent (modest on knowledge-heavy GPQA), and raw LVE carries measurable length correlation that LogNorm-LVE only partially addresses. Open questions include whether the signal persists in larger open-weight models, non-Transformer architectures (e.g., Mamba), and whether the metastability analogy has any mechanistic grounding beyond serving as a useful inductive bias for the entropy-of-velocity score.
