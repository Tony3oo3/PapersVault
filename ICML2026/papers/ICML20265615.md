## Problem

Spectral methods achieve high accuracy for low- and middle-dimensional PDEs but suffer from the curse of dimensionality when d ≫ 10, while physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) scale to high dimensions yet deliver limited accuracy. This paper bridges the gap by asking whether a neural network operating in the spectral domain can match or exceed spectral-method accuracy in high dimensions, and whether it can additionally recover spectral coefficients that are missing or uncomputed due to incomplete information.

## Input / Output

The network receives a multi-index frequency vector k ∈ ℕ₀ᵈ (sampled from a sparse index set such as a hyperbolic cross) and a scalar time t, and outputs a single scalar spectral coefficient û(k,t). The physical-domain solution u(x,t) is recovered a posteriori via the truncated spectral expansion u_N(x,t) = Σ_k û_k(t) φ_k(x). A key design choice is that the network is trained only on a subset of valid coefficients (proportion s), and must generalize to predict the remaining (1−s) masked coefficients, making the output a complete coefficient field even when the training data is incomplete.

## Method

Modified SINNs extend the original SINN architecture (which maps (k,t) → û via an MLP trained with a collocation-based spectral loss) by inserting two multiplicative modules that encode harmonic-analysis priors. First, a coefficient-decay scaler multiplies the network output by e^{−‖α·k‖₁} (for analytic solutions) or 1/∏|k_j|^{n_j} (for finitely smooth solutions), where α and n are learnable parameters that redistribute approximation capacity toward dominant low-frequency modes and suppress high-frequency noise. Second, a basis-embedding module—an MLP z^a(k) whose input is the matrix of 1-D basis evaluations φ_{k_i}(x_j) on a coarse collocation set X_c—learns to approximate the quadrature-based coefficient û_k^{X_c}, thereby injecting the structural information of the spectral basis into the network. The final output is the product û_md(k,t) = z^a(k) · û^θ(k,t) · e^{−‖α·k‖₁}, and a theorem shows that z^a(k) converges to the full-grid quadrature coefficient at the same rate as the underlying quadrature rule, provided the training index set has sufficient rank. Training is supervised: the network is fit to known coefficients (from SGSM or analytical solutions) and simultaneously learns to predict masked coefficients, enabling a hybrid SGSM+SINN strategy where SGSM computes available coefficients and SINN fills in the missing ones.

## Results

On high-dimensional Poisson equations (relative L² error), Modified SINNs achieve 1.61E-4 at d=2, 1.99E-4 at d=10, and 7.75E-3 at d=100, while PINNs (PirateNets) degrade to 5.50E-1 and DRMs (NDRMs) to 5.94E-2 at d=100. On high-dimensional Heat equations, SINNs maintain 3.66E-4 at d=10 versus 2.09E-2 (PINN) and 3.40E-2 (DRM). In the hybrid SGSM+SINN strategy on the Schrödinger equation, incorporating SINNs to recover masked coefficients reduces SGSM error by up to 99.62% (from 4.41E-1 to 1.67E-3 at d=8, s=0.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PINNs (PirateNets), Modified SINNs achieve substantially lower relative L² error at every tested dimension: at d=100 on the Poisson equation, SINN reaches 7.75E-3 versus 5.50E-1 for PINN (~70× improvement), and on the Heat equation 3.69E-1 versus 7.39E-1. Against DRMs (NDRMs), SINNs are consistently superior, with the gap widening in high dimensions (e.g., 7.75E-3 vs. 5.94E-2 at d=100 on Poisson). Against sparse grid spectral methods (SGSM, Shen & Yu 2010/2012), SINNs are less accurate when all coefficients are available (s=1) but outperform SGSM once coefficients become partially unavailable (s<1); the hybrid SGSM+SINN strategy recovers masked coefficients and reduces SGSM error by 82–99.6% across dimensions d=2, 5, 8 on the Schrödinger equation. The ablation (Table 5) confirms that both the coefficient decay scaler and the basis embedding module contribute: removing both degrades error from 5.55E-4 to 6.54E-3 at s=1.0, and from 3.08E-3 to 2.11E-1 at s=0.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Spectral-Informed Neural Network (SINN) framework of Yu et al. (2025), which first embedded spectral collocation into a neural network loss to avoid spatial derivative computation. The key departure is the integration of two harmonic-analysis priors—a learnable coefficient-decay scaler and a basis-embedding MLP—that were absent from the original SINN. Conceptually, the work also bridges the sparse-grid spectral method (SGSM) literature (Shen & Yu, 2010; 2012) and the PINN/DRM line (Raissi et al., 2019; Weinan & Yu, 2018; Wang et al., 2024), positioning SINNs as a middle ground that retains spectral accuracy while gaining the dimensionality scalability of neural networks. The hybrid SGSM+SINN strategy further connects to compressed-sensing and sparse-recovery ideas in spectral methods (Brugiapaglia et al., 2018; Gross & Iwen, 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a neural network can serve as a surrogate for missing spectral coefficients, effectively turning an incomplete spectral representation into a near-complete one—a capability that pure spectral methods lack and that could reduce the data requirements of high-dimensional spectral solvers. However, the authors are candid that all experiments assume sparse coefficient structure (separable or symmetric solutions), and genuinely dense high-dimensional PDEs remain intractable for all current methods including PINNs and SINNs. An important open direction is using machine learning to automatically identify which spectral coefficients are most informative, rather than relying on fixed hyperbolic-cross index sets, and extending the framework to non-separable, non-symmetric solutions where the current accuracy degrades noticeably.
