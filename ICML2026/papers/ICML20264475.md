## Problem

Most video tokenizers for latent diffusion are trained purely for reconstruction, but diffusion models are optimized to denoise heavily corrupted latents, creating a systematic mismatch. The paper shows empirically that better reconstruction fidelity (rFVD) does not necessarily yield better generation quality (gFVD), and over-prioritizing reconstruction can even cause diffusion training to fail. The central question is: what tokenizer training objective produces a latent space well-conditioned for denoising-based video generation?

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 16-frame video clips at 256×256 resolution, partitioned into 2×16×16 spatiotemporal patches yielding 2048 tokens, and produces a 16-channel latent representation. During training, the encoder processes only the unmasked subset of tokens (up to 80% masked) and the latent is further corrupted by interpolative Gaussian noise; at inference for downstream diffusion, encoding is performed normally with no masking and zero noise. The decoder reconstructs the full RGB video from the (potentially corrupted) latent tokens.

## Method

VideoMAETok is a ViT-based asymmetric autoencoder with a lightweight encoder and a heavier decoder (inverting VideoMAE's capacity allocation), using RoPE, RMSNorm, and SwiGLU. Training combines two complementary corruptions: (1) MAE-style random masking with a variable ratio up to 80%, where the encoder processes only visible tokens for efficiency, and (2) interpolative latent noise injection (z′ = (1−τ)z + τ·ε(γ) with τ~U(0,1), γ=3.0) that exposes the decoder to a continuum of corruption magnitudes matching diffusion denoising. The total loss is a weighted sum of pixel MSE, VAE-style KL regularization, perceptual loss, and an adversarial GAN objective. The key insight is that by forcing the decoder to reconstruct clean videos from strongly corrupted latents, the learned latent space becomes robust and well-structured for downstream denoising, yielding substantially better gFVD than reconstruction-oriented VAE baselines despite worse rFVD.

## Results

On Kinetics-600, VideoMAETok (B-L) paired with LightningDiT-XL achieves a gFVD of 42.16, and on UCF-101 it reaches 49.93, both state-of-the-art among compared tokenizers. The smaller S-B variant still achieves gFVD of 52.43 (Kinetics-600) and 61.89 (UCF-101) with the same generator. In terms of reconstruction, VideoMAETok (B-L) reports rFVD of 10.01 on Kinetics-600 and 21.78 on UCF-101, which is higher than Cosmos-L (3.28 / 5.55) but the paper argues rFVD is a poor proxy for generation quality. The largest variant requires only 0.78 TFLOPs, roughly 7.5× less compute than Cosmos-L (5.82 TFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMAETok (B-L) with LightningDiT-XL achieves the best gFVD on both benchmarks, substantially outperforming all baselines: on Kinetics-600 it reaches 42.16 vs. VFRTok-L (124.78), Cosmos-M (125.02), and OmniTokenizer (242.54); on UCF-101 it reaches 49.93 vs. VFRTok-L (71.34), Cosmos-L (75.11), and OmniTokenizer (88.89). The gap is most dramatic against LTX (358.48 / 345.82) and Cosmos-S (210.21 / 191.49), where VideoMAETok is roughly 8× better on gFVD. On reconstruction (rFVD), VideoMAETok trails Cosmos-L (10.01 vs. 3.28 on K600) and VFRTok-L (10.01 vs. 4.64), but the paper argues this trade-off is expected and desirable since over-optimizing reconstruction harms downstream generation. In the text-to-video setting (Table 4), VideoMAETok also outperforms SD-VAE (Rombach et al., 2022) on VBench total score (78.82 vs. 74.56) using the same MMDiT generator trained on OpenVid-1M. Overall, VideoMAETok wins on the generation metric (gFVD) across every comparison while accepting a moderate reconstruction penalty, and it does so at lower compute cost than Cosmos-L and OmniTokenizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMAETok inverts the capacity allocation of VideoMAE (Tong et al., 2022), which uses a heavy encoder for discriminative tasks, instead adopting a lightweight encoder and heavier decoder suited to faithful video reconstruction and generation. It combines MAE-style high-ratio token masking with interpolative latent noise corruption, the latter adapted from DeTok (Yang et al., 2025) and MAETok (Chen et al., 2025) in the image domain. The key conceptual departure from prior video tokenizers (Cosmos, VFRTok, OmniTokenizer) is training the autoencoder as a corruption-inversion model rather than a clean-input reconstructor, explicitly aligning the tokenizer's training difficulty with the denoising objective of downstream diffusion generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that reconstruction fidelity (rFVD) is a poor and potentially actively harmful proxy for generation quality (gFVD) in video tokenizers, extending a tension previously observed in images to the video setting. The practical recipe—simply adding masking and latent noise to standard VAE training—yields large gFVD gains with off-the-shelf generators, making it an easy-to-adopt baseline. Open questions include how to better balance reconstruction and generation objectives (decoder fine-tuning only partially closes the gap), whether semantic distillation can further improve latent structure, and how the approach scales to longer temporal windows and text-conditioned generation at scale.
