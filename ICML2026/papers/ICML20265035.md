## Problem

Existing LLMs reason strictly autoregressively, and prior attempts at parallel reasoning either rely on hand-crafted decomposition rules, depend on supervised distillation from stronger teacher models (imposing an 'intelligence ceiling'), or silently fall back to sequential decoding. No framework enables a model to self-evolve genuine, 100% parallel reasoning capability without external supervision while maintaining stable large-scale RL training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265035/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text reasoning problem (math competition, logic puzzle) and produces a structured parallel reasoning trajectory following a Map-Process-Reduce schema: a <guideline> block containing multiple <plan> entries, followed by independent <step> blocks that execute in parallel (with no cross-attention between them via a custom attention mask), and a <takeaway> block for aggregation. Multiple such blocks can chain sequentially, and the final output includes a boxed answer. The parallel structure is enforced architecturally through parallel positional encoding and attention masking, not merely as a text format.

## Method

NPR employs a three-stage self-distilled progressive training paradigm. Stage 1 (NPR-ZERO) applies DAPO RL with a format-aware reward to a seed model, inducing the parallel tag structure without any external teacher. Stage 2 (NPR-BETA) performs rejection sampling on NPR-ZERO outputs (filtering for correctness and format compliance), then trains with parallel SFT using a custom parallel attention mask and positional encoding that isolate sibling <step> blocks from each other. Stage 3 (NPR) introduces PAPO (Parallel-Aware Policy Optimization), a modified PPO objective that replaces group-level with batch-level advantage normalization, removes clip-masking on special structural tokens to preserve their gradients, and eliminates importance sampling in favor of a strict on-policy stop-gradient ratio for stability. A re-engineered NPR Engine (built on SGLang) fixes KV-cache double-free, branch-aware token budgeting, and illegal parallel schema states to enable stable large-scale parallel RL rollouts.

## Results

On eight reasoning benchmarks, NPR (Parallel RL on Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) achieves an average score of 65.0, outperforming the base model (63.7), Multiverse-4B (50.1), and Multiverse-32B (52.5). On the Non-Thinking Qwen3-4B backbone, NPR reaches 64.4 average versus 39.9 for the base model, with the largest single-benchmark gain on AIME25 (19.1 → 53.8, +34.7 points). NPR also delivers up to 4.6× inference speedup over autoregressive decoding (AIME25: 2979.8 TPS vs. 646.8 TPS) and achieves 100% genuine parallel execution across all test cases, compared to 30%+ autoregressive fallback observed in Multiverse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (the direct base model, AVG 63.7), NPR (Parallel RL) achieves 65.0, a +1.3 point average gain with the largest improvements on AIME24 (+3.3) and HMMT25 (−0.2, slight regression) and ZebraLogic (+1.5). Against the Non-Thinking Qwen3-4B (AVG 39.9), NPR delivers a dramatic +24.5 point average improvement, with AIME25 jumping from 19.1 to 53.8. Against Multiverse-4B (AVG 50.1, trained on teacher-distilled data s1.1-8k), NPR (AVG 65.0) wins by +14.9 points on average, with the self-distilled orz-8k data alone (NPR-BETA, AVG 59.0) already surpassing MV-4B by +8.9 points. Against the larger Multiverse-32B (AVG 52.5), the 4B NPR model outperforms by +12.5 points, demonstrating that self-distilled parallel RL on a smaller model surpasses teacher-supervised parallel SFT on a much larger one. In inference efficiency (Table 3), NPR achieves 4.6× speedup on AIME25 versus 2.4× for Multiverse, and in parallel trigger rate (Table 4), NPR maintains 100% genuine parallelism across all eight benchmarks while MV-32B drops to 45.8% on ZebraLogic.

## Relation to Prior Work

NPR builds directly on the Multiverse parallel attention mask and positional encoding design (Yang et al., 2025b) and the DAPO RL framework (Yu et al., 2025), but departs from both in critical ways: it replaces teacher-distilled training data with self-distilled trajectories from the model's own generations, and it introduces PAPO—a parallel-aware policy optimization that removes importance sampling, uses batch-level advantage normalization (inspired by Lite-PPO), and preserves gradients on structural special tokens. Unlike prior parallel reasoning methods (APR, Parallel-R1, ParaThinker) that either operate on toy tasks or bootstrap from supervised data, NPR achieves end-to-end native parallel RL without external supervision, and its NPR Engine addresses practical stability issues (KV-cache double-free, token budget underestimation) that prior SGLang-based parallel engines left unresolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that self-distilled parallel trajectories outperform teacher-generated ones by ~10 points on average challenges the prevailing assumption in distillation-based approaches that stronger teachers are necessary for capability transfer, suggesting a path toward truly self-evolving reasoning architectures. The 100% genuine parallel trigger rate (vs. 30%+ autoregressive fallback in baselines) and up to 4.6× inference speedup while improving accuracy make this practically relevant for deployment. However, all experiments are confined to 4B-parameter Qwen3 models on math and logic benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, generalizes to open-ended or multi-modal reasoning, and whether the progressive three-stage curriculum is necessary or an artifact of the small model size.
