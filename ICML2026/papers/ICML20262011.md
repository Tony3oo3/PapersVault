## Problem

Jia et al. (2023) established an entropic characterization of minimax rates for Gaussian mixture estimation under Hellinger distance but left open whether TV(f_π, f_η) ≍ H(f_π, f_η) holds for Gaussian location mixtures. The standard inequalities H² ≤ TV ≤ √2·H are too loose to yield optimal rates for robust density estimation under Huber contamination, where the data-generating process is misspecified in total variation. This paper resolves the open question by proving the sharp relation H ≤ TV^{1-o(1)} with an explicit 1/log log(1/TV) correction and constructing examples showing the correction is necessary.

## Input / Output

The method takes two probability measures π and η supported on the d-dimensional cube [-M,M]^d and produces a quantitative upper bound on the Hellinger (or χ²) distance between the induced Gaussian mixture densities f_π and f_η in terms of their TV distance. The bound has the form H ≤ (C₀ ∨ TV^{-α(TV)})·TV where α(t) = (2+δ)/log(log(1/t)∨e), with C₀ depending polynomially on M²d. The sharpness construction produces explicit sequences of (n+1)-point discrete mixing distributions supported on Chebyshev nodes in [-M,M] that achieve the lower bound H ≥ TV^{1-0.33/log log(1/TV)}.

## Method

The proof expands the ratio g = (f_π - f_η)/φ_d in the orthonormal Hermite polynomial basis, where coefficients are moment differences Δ_k = ∫θ^k d(π-η)(θ). The key step is bounding ‖g‖_{L²(φ_d)} in terms of ‖g‖_{L¹(φ_d)} = 2·TV by decomposing g into a finite-degree polynomial q ∈ Π_n^d and a tail r, then applying a newly derived multivariate Nikolskii-type inequality (via the Christoffel-Darboux kernel and Mehler's formula) and a restricted-range inequality to control ‖q‖_{L¹} from below in terms of ‖q‖_{L²}. The tail is controlled by compact support via Stirling's formula. The optimal truncation n ≈ 2log(1/t)/log log(1/t) yields the log-log exponent. For sharpness, the construction matches lower-order moments so that the L² projection is exactly proportional to x^n (using Chebyshev nodes and Vandermonde inversion), while higher-order moments are recursively bounded via the Chebyshev polynomial recurrence, producing a hyper-exponential decay rate exp(-n log n/2) in both L¹ and L² norms.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.1 / Corollary 2.4) establishes that for Gaussian location mixtures with mixing distributions supported on [-M,M]^d, the Hellinger distance is bounded by H(f_π, f_η) ≤ (C₀ ∨ TV^{-α(TV)}) · TV, where α(t) = (2+δ)/log(log(1/t) ∨ e) and C₀ depends only on δ, M, d. The sharpness construction (Theorem 3.1) shows the exponent gap 1 - α(TV) is necessary: there exist sequences of mixing distributions for which H ≥ TV^{1 - 0.33/log log(1/TV)}, disproving the conjectured equivalence TV ≍ H. As a direct application, the minimax rate for robust density estimation under Huber contamination ε is ε^{1 - Θ(1)/log log(1/ε)} in squared Hellinger distance, which is shown to be optimal (Theorems 4.5–4.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against the general f-divergence inequality H²(p,q) ≤ TV(p,q) ≤ √2·H(p,q) (equation 1), which gives only H ≤ TV^{1/2} for arbitrary distributions. For Gaussian location mixtures, the new result improves this to H ≤ TV^{1-o(1)} with o(1) = Θ(1)/log log(1/TV), a dramatic tightening as TV → 0. The paper explicitly resolves an open question from Jia et al. (2023), who proved H² ≍ KL for bounded-support Gaussian mixtures and asked whether TV ≍ H also holds. The sharpness construction (using Chebyshev-node mixing distributions with matched lower-order moments) demonstrates that TV ≍ H is false: the gap, while sub-polynomial, is unavoidable. In the robust estimation application, the Yatracos estimator achieves the minimax rate ε^{2(1-Θ(1)/log log(1/ε))} in squared Hellinger distance, matching the lower bound up to the constant in the log-log correction (0.33 in the lower bound vs. (2+δ) in the upper bound).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves an explicit open problem posed by Jia et al. (2023), who established an entropic characterization of minimax rates for Gaussian mixture estimation under Hellinger distance but left the analogous TV characterization unresolved. The proof strategy draws on classical approximation theory—specifically Nikolskii-type inequalities (Nevai & Totik, 1987) and restricted-range inequalities (Lubinsky, 2007)—extended here to the multivariate setting via Christoffel-Darboux kernels and Mehler's formula. The sharpness construction repurposes Chebyshev nodes and Vandermonde matrix inversion to build mixing distributions whose Hermite expansions concentrate on a single high-degree monomial, a technique rooted in classical polynomial approximation. For applications, the paper leverages the Yatracos (1985) minimum-distance estimator framework for robust density estimation under Huber contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first sharp (up to a 1-o(1) exponent) inequality between TV and Hellinger distances for Gaussian mixtures, enabling an entropic characterization of minimax TV estimation rates and optimal robust density estimation rates under Huber contamination with a direct implication for empirical Bayes regret bounds with outliers. The negative result—that TV and Hellinger are genuinely not comparable (the log-log gap is necessary)—challenges the intuition that these distances should be equivalent for smooth parametric families. Open questions include extending to sub-Gaussian or heteroscedastic mixing distributions, establishing the sharp TV–L² relation, and resolving the optimal sample-size dependence in the robust setting, which remains open even in the clean (ε=0) one-dimensional case.
