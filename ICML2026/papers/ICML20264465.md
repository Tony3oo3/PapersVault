## Problem

Existing LLM decoding and uncertainty estimation methods operate exclusively in language space, ignoring the task-specific structure (discrete labels, sets, graphs, numeric values) that underlies many practical outputs. This limits both generation quality—since optimal responses may not exist among sampled generations—and uncertainty estimation, which fails to capture task-relevant error. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling LLM outputs in a task-dependent latent structure can yield better responses and more informative uncertainty estimates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and M Monte-Carlo sampled LLM responses (M=20 in experiments), which are mapped via a task-specific encoding function g_T (e.g., DeBERTa clustering for classes, GPT-4.1-mini for set extraction, KGGen for graphs, EmbeddingGemma for semantic embeddings) into a latent space L. It produces a synthesized Bayes-optimal latent response ℓ_Bayes that need not correspond to any individual sample, along with a scalar Bayes risk R_Bayes quantifying uncertainty. The latent space and dissimilarity d_T are task-dependent: 0-1 loss on [K] for classification, Hamming distance on {0,1}^K for multi-answer QA, cosine distance on the unit sphere for translation, structural Hamming distance on graphs for summarization, and KL divergence on the probability simplex for ambiguous ground-truth.

## Method

The core contribution is a decision-theoretic framework that performs Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding directly in a task-dependent latent space rather than in raw language. The encoding g_T induces a push-forward distribution p(ℓ|x) over latent responses, and a task-specific dissimilarity d_T defines the Bayes risk R(ℓ̃) = E_ℓ[d_T(ℓ̃, ℓ)]. The Bayes-optimal action ℓ_Bayes = argmin R(ℓ̃) admits closed-form solutions for all studied structures (e.g., majority vote per element for sets, normalized mean embedding for semantic spaces, mode for classes), enabling O(M) computation versus O(M²) for language-space MBR. Crucially, ℓ_Bayes is synthesized by aggregating across the full output distribution rather than selected from samples, allowing the model to abstain (e.g., output the empty set) when uncertainty is high. The associated Bayes risk R_Bayes is proven to be a lower bound on the true epistemic risk under the unambiguous ground-truth assumption, providing a principled, task-aligned uncertainty measure that outperforms semantic entropy and other variability-based estimators.

## Results

On multi-answer QA (MAQA, set-structured), the Bayes-optimal response reduces Hamming distance by 18–24% over beam search (e.g., 0.729 vs. 0.892 for Gemma-3-4B; 0.525 vs. 0.581 for Qwen3-30B-A3B) and improves F1 by 0.5–3.3 points. On machine translation (WMT19), it lowers cosine distance from 0.131 to 0.124 (Gemma-3-4B) and from 0.113 to 0.109 (Qwen3-30B-A3B). On the probability-simplex MAQA variant, KL divergence drops by 23–33% (e.g., 7.940 vs. 10.330 for Gemma-3-4B). For uncertainty quantification, the Bayes risk achieves the best PRR on 4 of 5 latent structures (e.g., 0.819 on MAQA sets, 0.754 on MAQA simplex for Gemma-3-4B), outperforming semantic entropy, KLE, SAR, and COCOA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Bayes-optimal latent response consistently outperforms beam search, self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023), contrastive search (Su et al., 2022), autoregressive sampling, greedy decoding, and the MAP of the latent belief across all four model families and all structured tasks. The most notable margin is over ℓ_Sample (MBR in raw language space), which requires O(M²) pairwise comparisons: on MAQA Hamming, Bayes(ours) improves by 0.109 (Gemma-3-4B) to 0.026 (Qwen3-30B-A3B), and on the KL-divergence simplex task by 2.57–3.21 points. On single-label classification (TriviaQA), all methods including Bayes(ours) coincide at the same exact-match score, as expected under 0-1 loss. For uncertainty quantification, the Bayes risk (R_Bayes) outperforms semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023), kernel language entropy (Nikitin et al., 2024), SAR (Duan et al., 2024), COCOA (Vashurin et al., 2025b), and p(True) (Kadavath et al., 2022) on 4 of 5 latent structures in PRR; the only structure where it is merely competitive is classification [K], where existing estimators were explicitly designed. The gap is largest on set-structured MAQA (PRR 0.819 vs. 0.776 for SE on Gemma-3-4B) and the probability simplex (PRR 0.754 vs. 0.703 for SE), confirming that task-aware structural distance d_T is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding from the raw language space to a task-dependent latent structure, departing from prior MBR work (Bertsch et al., 2023; Eikema et al., 2025; Lukasik et al., 2024) that selects among sampled generations using learned or heuristic similarity metrics. For uncertainty quantification, it builds on the sample-diversity paradigm (semantic entropy, KLE, SAR) but replaces distance-agnostic semantic variability with a task-specific structural dissimilarity, yielding Bayes risk as a principled uncertainty estimate. The connection to 1-Wasserstein distance under a Dirac ground-truth assumption provides a formal justification that prior UQ methods for LLMs lack. The framework also subsumes self-consistency and majority voting as special cases of latent-space MBR under 0-1 or Hamming loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a general, closed-form recipe for synthesizing Bayes-optimal responses (centroids, majority-vote sets, normalized mean embeddings) that outperform any single sampled generation, with O(M) complexity versus O(M²) for language-space MBR. The task-aware uncertainty estimate consistently tracks output quality better than semantic-entropy-based methods on structured tasks, providing a practical signal for selective prediction or abstention. Key limitations include the need for a reliable mapping g_T from text to latent structure, the computational cost of Monte Carlo sampling, and the infeasibility of inverse mapping for some tasks. Open questions include extending the framework to tasks without a natural latent metric, reducing sample requirements further, and integrating the approach into iterative or multi-turn settings.
