## Problem

Existing genomic foundation models treat DNA as raw nucleotide strings, overlooking the regulatory context and variant information essential for interpreting genetic variation. This forces models to implicitly learn genomic structure from sequence alone, making them computationally inefficient and poor at generalizing to rare variants and cross-context tasks like eQTL or splicing prediction. The paper asks whether explicitly encoding biological annotations and variants into the tokenization scheme can yield more accurate and efficient genomic representations than scaling alone.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6,144-nucleotide DNA sequences where SNVs/insertions are replaced by special variant tokens (e.g., checked-A) and GENCODE annotation boundaries (CDS, exon, transcript start/end) are inserted as dedicated tokens, all relative to the HG38 reference. For variant effect prediction, embeddings are extracted by running the decoder on both the forward sequence ending at the variant and the reverse-complement sequence starting at the variant, averaging each strand's embedding, and concatenating the reference and mutated vectors into a 2d-dimensional representation fed to a linear probe. For generative tasks (promoter design), the model takes a gene sequence followed by a task-identifier token and autoregressively produces a promoter sequence.

## Method

BioToken is a modular tokenization framework that augments the standard ACGT vocabulary with special variant tokens for SNVs/insertions and six annotation tokens marking CDS, transcript, and exon boundaries, shifting the learning objective from positional memorization to functional representation learning. BioFM is a 265M-parameter decoder-only transformer derived from Mistral (23 layers, grouped-query attention, sliding-window attention disabled) with RoPE frequency scaled from 10⁴ to 10⁵ to better capture long-range genomic dependencies. It is pretrained via next-token prediction on ~333B tokens from 50 individuals across all five 1000 Genomes superpopulations, with 50% reverse-complement augmentation applied strand-specifically to annotations. Downstream evaluation uses linear probing on variant embeddings (reference vs. mutated concatenation) across an 11-fold chromosome-based split, and a separate instruction-tuning stage on EPDNew enables biologically plausible promoter generation. Ablations confirm that both variant tokens and annotation tokens independently contribute to performance, with annotations particularly important for pathogenicity and variant tokens for rare-variant and expression tasks.

## Results

On the newly introduced Variant Benchmark (8 tasks, 11-fold CV), BioFM (265M params) achieves an overall AUROC of 0.780, outperforming the second-best model NT-500M-v2-MS (AUROC 0.727) despite having roughly half the parameters and ~100× less pretraining compute than Evo2-7B. On expression prediction, BioFM with LoRA fine-tuning reaches median AUROC 0.812 versus Enformer's 0.782 at 98K context (p=0.002), and on sQTL prediction it achieves median AUROC 0.676 versus Splice Transformer's 0.629. On the Genomic Long-Range Benchmark (GLRB), BioFM attains AUROC 0.793, surpassing models that required extensive fine-tuning and longer contexts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioFM (265M) outperforms NT-500M-v2-MS (500M) on overall Variant Benchmark AUROC (0.780 vs 0.727) and on specific tasks including Common vs Rare (0.746 vs 0.69) and Expression (0.786 vs 0.74). Against Evo2-7B (~100× more compute), BioFM wins on 5 of 8 Variant Benchmark tasks and on the Common & Rare classification, though Evo2-7B retains an edge on coding and noncoding pathogenicity in zero-shot settings. On expression prediction, BioFM matches Enformer at 12K context and significantly outperforms it after LoRA fine-tuning (0.812 vs 0.782 AUROC, p=0.002); on eQTL, BioFM (0.697) beats Enformer (0.674). For splicing, BioFM (0.676) substantially outperforms Splice Transformer (0.629) and Borzoi (0.63). BioFM also beats Borzoi on eQTL (0.699 vs 0.689). However, BioFM does not surpass CADD on noncoding pathogenicity (Figure 2b), and on the GUE benchmark it ranks second overall, trailing DNABERTv2 on epigenetic/TF-binding tasks and NT-500M-v2-MS on promoter tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mistral 7B decoder-only transformer architecture (retaining grouped-query attention, disabling sliding-window attention, and extending RoPE) but departs fundamentally from prior genomic foundation models (Nucleotide Transformer, Evo2, HyenaDNA, Caduceus) that treat DNA as raw nucleotide strings. The key conceptual departure is BioToken, which explicitly encodes SNVs, insertions, and GENCODE annotations (CDS, exon, transcript boundaries) as special tokens during pretraining, rather than relying on the model to implicitly learn genomic structure from sequence statistics. This differs from Evo's task-specific conditioning tokens and Saberi et al.'s structural tokenization by embedding regulatory context directly into the foundational representation. The paper also introduces a Variant Benchmark that extends beyond existing evaluations (BEND, GLRB) to cover a broader spectrum of variant-mediated molecular processes including meQTL and common-vs-rare classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that a 265M-parameter model with explicit biological inductive biases can match or exceed 7B+ models trained on ~100× more compute—challenges the prevailing scaling paradigm in genomic foundation modeling and could substantially lower the barrier to entry for genomic research. The modular BioToken framework is extensible to additional annotation types (epigenetic marks, chromatin accessibility, RNA structure) and potentially to protein language models, suggesting a path toward unified multi-modal biological representations. Key open questions include whether the advantage persists at biobank-scale training cohorts (the model was trained on only 50 individuals), whether annotation quality limitations restrict applicability to non-model organisms, and whether the annotation tokens introduce subtle evaluation biases that distance-matched controls only partially address.
