## Problem

Isabelle-based neural theorem proving remains limited because existing methods train on large, noisy, and semantically imbalanced raw proof corpora, while general-purpose data selection techniques fail to capture the structural and logical dependencies specific to formal proofs. The paper identifies that prover performance is constrained more by training data quality than model capacity, and that Isabelle's specialized proof structure demands domain-specific selection criteria rather than surface-level textual filtering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266253/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives only the formal Isabelle lemma statement (no informal proof or sketch) as the primary input; during inference, each subsequent generation round additionally conditions on accumulated verifier error messages from prior failed attempts. The output is a complete Isabelle proof script, verified by the PISA environment running Isabelle 2025. Inference uses nucleus sampling (top-p=0.95) with dynamic temperature scheduling over up to 64 attempts per theorem, with early stopping upon successful verification.

## Method

The framework has two components. At training time, a PSR-guided pipeline scores ~200k Isabelle theorem-proof pairs along three dimensions—proof complexity (log-transformed proof/theorem length plus structural marker count), semantic coverage (TF-IDF vectors clustered via K-Means into 50 groups), and reasoning diversity (Shannon entropy of tactic-label distributions per cluster)—then performs multi-stage sampling with quantile-based complexity pruning, entropy-weighted cluster quotas, and explicit suppression of one-shot template proofs, yielding a 4k high-quality subset for LoRA fine-tuning of DeepSeek-Math-7B-Base. At inference time, Dynamic Feedback-Based Prompt Optimization conditions each generation round on the original problem plus a distilled history of prior failed attempts and their Isabelle error messages, while linearly increasing sampling temperature across rounds to shift from exploitation to exploration, and applying early stopping to redirect the fixed 64-attempt budget toward unsolved theorems.

## Results

On miniF2F-test (Isabelle), the full framework achieves 90.6% Pass@64, substantially surpassing all prior Isabelle-based NTP methods. Fine-tuning solely on the PSR-selected 4k high-quality dataset (without dynamic feedback) already reaches 84.8% Pass@64, outperforming fine-tuning on the full ~200k raw corpus (51.2%). On miniF2F-valid, the full framework achieves 86.5% Pass@64, confirming strong generalization beyond the test split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework (90.6% Pass@64 on miniF2F-test) outperforms all six baselines by a wide margin. The closest competitor, ProofAug (61.9%), uses the same base model (deepseek-math-7b-base), making the 28.7-point gap particularly striking and attributable to the data-centric strategy rather than model capacity. Subgoal-XL (56.1%) and Lyra (51.2%) trail further behind, while DSP (39.3%) and POETRY (42.2%) represent earlier, less effective approaches. Notably, even the high-quality data SFT component alone (84.8%) exceeds every baseline, indicating that the PSR-guided data selection is the primary driver of improvement. The advantage is consistent on miniF2F-valid (86.5% vs. 58.6% for ProofAug), confirming the gains are not test-set-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes data-centric selection ideas from the NLP literature (MoDS, ALPAGASUS, LIMA) and adapts them to the formal theorem proving domain, where prior NTP work (DSP, Subgoal-XL, LEGO-Prover, ProofAug) has focused almost exclusively on model architecture, search strategies, or training-time optimization. The key conceptual departure is treating training data quality as the primary lever for Isabelle NTP, formalized through the PSR criterion (proof complexity, semantic coverage, reasoning diversity) that exploits Isabelle-specific structural properties rather than surface-level textual signals. At inference time, the paper reframes verifier feedback as a dynamic data signal for prompt conditioning, distinguishing it from prior self-correction or verification-driven reward approaches by operating purely at the prompt level without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~4k PSR-curated samples dramatically outperform ~200k raw proof pairs (84.8% vs. 51.2% Pass@64) challenges the prevailing assumption in NTP that scaling training data is the primary path to improvement, and provides a practical, low-cost recipe for data-efficient fine-tuning in Isabelle. The released 4k dataset and LoRA adapter lower the barrier for future work on formal theorem proving. Open questions include whether the PSR criterion generalizes to other ITPs (Lean, Coq), whether the gains extend to project-level verification tasks beyond miniF2F, and how the semantic structure of Isabelle proofs interacts with model behavior during generation.
