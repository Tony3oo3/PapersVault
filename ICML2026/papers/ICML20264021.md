## Problem

Kernel matrix operations (matrix-vector products, spectral norm estimation, kernel sums) naively require Ω(n²d) time, and SETH-based lower bounds rule out subquadratic exact computation. The best prior subquadratic algorithms (Backurs et al., 2021) suffer from high polynomial dependence on the target error ε (e.g., 1/ε^7.7 for spectral norm), leaving a significant gap between what is achievable and what is information-theoretically necessary. This paper closes part of that gap by reducing the ε-dependence across all studied tasks while also establishing conditional hardness lower bounds.

## Input / Output

All algorithms take a dataset X = {x₁,…,xₙ} ⊂ ℝ^d and a precision parameter ε, but critically access the kernel matrix K only through KDE datastructure queries rather than reading individual entries, making them modular with respect to the underlying KDE subroutine. The non-negative matrix-vector product requires an entry-wise non-negative unit vector y and returns z ≈ Ky with per-coordinate (1+ε) multiplicative and ε/√n additive error. The spectral norm algorithm outputs both a scalar λ and a witnessing unit vector u satisfying uᵀKu ≥ (1−ε)λ₁(K), while the kernel sum algorithm samples only Θ(√n/ε²) points from X to estimate 1ᵀK1 up to a 1+ε factor.

## Method

The paper's core technical contributions are threefold. First, a faster non-negative matrix-vector product algorithm replaces the O(1/ε) geometric bucketing of prior work with O(log(n/ε)) power-of-2 buckets and an adaptive choice of KDE additive error μ_t per bucket, reducing the ε-dependence from 1/ε^(3+3p_g) to 1/ε^(2+p_g). Second, a new analysis of noisy power iteration tracks the projection ⟨v₁, z_t⟩ onto the top eigenvector and the norm ‖Kz_t‖ rather than mass above/below a spectral threshold, proving that δ = O(ε) precision per matrix-vector product suffices (versus δ = O(ε²) previously), yielding a 1/ε^4.5 improvement in spectral norm estimation. Third, the kernel sum algorithm uses a three-step strategy: random principal submatrix sampling, heavy/light row filtering via KDE queries, and a balanced square submatrix resampling that equalizes the number of KDE queries to the datastructure size, achieving n^((1+p_g)/2) dependence instead of the prior n^((2+5p_g)/(4+2p_g)).

## Results

The paper improves the polynomial dependence on ε for four fundamental kernel matrix problems. For the top eigenvalue (spectral norm) of a Gaussian kernel matrix, the ε-dependence drops from ~1/ε^7.692 to ~1/ε^3.173 (a factor of ~1/ε^4.519). For non-negative matrix-vector products, the ε-dependence improves from ~1/ε^3.346 to ~1/ε^2.173. For the kernel sum 1ᵀK1, the n-dependence improves from n^0.659 to n^0.587. All algorithms access the kernel matrix only through KDE queries, running in time linear in d and sub-quadratic in n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline compared is Backurs et al. (ICML 2021), which provides the prior best theoretical algorithms for all four problems studied. The paper wins on every problem: for the top eigenvalue, the ε-exponent drops from 7.692 to 3.173 (a ~4.5× reduction in the power of 1/ε), achieved by showing that δ = O(ε) precision in noisy power iteration suffices (vs. δ = O(ε²) previously) combined with a faster matrix-vector product subroutine. For non-negative matrix-vector products, the ε-exponent drops from 3.346 to 2.173 by replacing the prior geometric bucketing (O(1/ε) buckets) with a coarser power-of-2 partition (O(log n) buckets) plus adaptive KDE additive error parameters. For the kernel sum, the n-exponent improves from 0.659 to 0.587 by sampling a balanced square submatrix of light rows/columns rather than only subsampling rows, which is the regime where KDE queries are most efficient. The paper also provides SETH-based lower bounds showing that these improvements cannot be extended to mixed-sign vectors or asymmetric kernel matrices, where nearly quadratic time is required.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of subquadratic kernel matrix algorithms initiated by Backurs et al. (ICML 2021), which first showed how to leverage KDE data structures (with p_g=0.173+o(1) from Charikar et al., FOCS 2020) to approximate matrix-vector products, top eigenvalues, and kernel sums without explicitly constructing the n×n matrix. The key conceptual departure is a new analysis of noisy power iteration showing that δ=O(ε) precision per matrix-vector product suffices (and is necessary) for a 1−ε relative eigenvalue guarantee, versus the δ=O(ε²) previously believed required. The paper also introduces an adaptive bucketing strategy for weighted KDE queries that eliminates a 1/ε factor in the matrix-vector product, and a three-step sampling algorithm for the kernel sum that achieves a better n-dependence than prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reductions in ε-dependence (e.g., from ~1/ε^7.7 to ~1/ε^3.2 for spectral norm) are substantial for the theoretical landscape of kernel matrix approximation, and the SETH-based lower bounds for asymmetric kernel matrices delineate a clear boundary for what KDE-based approaches can achieve. Key open questions remain: whether general mixed-sign matrix-vector products are conditionally quadratic-hard (the paper gives evidence via an intermediate problem), the complexity of computing λ₂(K), and whether the iteration count for noisy power iteration can be improved beyond Ω(log n/ε). The practical impact is currently limited since the algorithms are theoretical and the KDE data structures themselves have significant constants, though the empirical validation in Appendix G confirms the δ=O(ε) scaling is correct in practice.
