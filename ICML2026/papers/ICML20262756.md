## Problem

Vision-language models are increasingly deployed in morally sensitive domains like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis, yet existing moral alignment benchmarks are either text-only or rely on AI-generated images that introduce distribution shift. There is a lack of high-quality, real-image-based, and morally diverse multimodal benchmarks that can systematically assess whether VLMs adhere to widely shared human moral values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262756/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample is a real-world image-text pair (2,481 total) where the moral violation is annotated as either text-centric (violation conveyed primarily through text, image field may be empty) or image-centric (violation conveyed primarily through the image, text field is empty). The model must produce either a binary moral judgment (morally wrong / not morally wrong) or identify the specific violated moral topic(s) from a 13-topic taxonomy, with both single-label (hit rate) and multi-label (F1) scoring for norm attribution.

## Method

The paper introduces a taxonomy of 13 fine-grained moral topics organized into three domains (personal, interpersonal, societal) grounded in Turiel's Domain Theory, providing a principled psychological foundation for moral categorization. Building on this taxonomy, the authors curate 2,481 expert-verified real-world image-text pairs scraped from open-source websites, explicitly filtering out AI-generated content, with each sample annotated for both the violated moral topic(s) and the modality (text-centric vs. image-centric) carrying the moral signal. A majority-vote annotation protocol with 83% agreement threshold and senior-expert adjudication ensures label quality. Evaluation comprises two complementary tasks—Moral Judgment (binary classification) and Moral Norm Attribution (single-norm hit rate and multi-norm F1 over 13 topics)—applied to 19 open- and closed-source VLMs using greedy decoding at temperature 0, enabling systematic analysis across model scale, family, modality sensitivity, and topic-level moral reasoning patterns.

## Results

On MORALISE, proprietary VLMs achieve 88.28% average accuracy on Moral Judgment, 66.60% hit rate on Single-Norm Attribution, and 52.90% F1 on Multi-Norm Attribution, while open-source models score 83.55%, 42.63%, and 31.93% respectively. The best overall model, GPT-o4-mini, reaches 89.04% judgment accuracy and 72.77% single-norm hit rate; the strongest open-source model, Gemma3-27B, achieves 89.55% judgment and 67.29% single-norm attribution. Performance drops sharply from binary moral judgment to fine-grained norm attribution (proprietary models lose ~22 points from judgment to single-norm, and a further ~14 points to multi-norm F1), revealing that identifying *which* norm is violated remains substantially harder than detecting *whether* a violation exists.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MORALISE is compared against four prior moral benchmarks in Table 1. Unlike MoralBench (Ji et al., 2024) and ETHICS (Hendrycks et al., 2020), which are text-only and cover only 6 topics, MORALISE is multimodal (image + text) and spans 13 fine-grained moral topics grounded in Turiel's Domain Theory. Compared to VIVA (Hu et al., 2024), which uses real images but lacks multi-class topic labels and modality-centric violation annotations, MORALISE adds both. Relative to M3oralBench (Yan et al., 2024), which relies entirely on AI-generated images, MORALISE uses expert-verified real-world photos, covering 13 topics (vs. 6) and evaluating 19 models (vs. 10). Among the 19 evaluated VLMs, proprietary GPT models lead in norm attribution (GPT-o4-mini: 72.77% single-norm hit, 56.04% multi-norm F1), but open-source Gemma3-27B is competitive and even surpasses GPT-4o on multi-norm F1 (57.08% vs. 56.52%), while smaller models like LLaVA-7B (65.33% judgment, 15.81% single-norm) and Qwen2.5-VL-3B (85.83% judgment, 12.09% single-norm) reveal persistent gaps in fine-grained moral reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the moral reasoning evaluation literature from text-only settings (ETHICS, MoralBench) to the multimodal vision-language domain, grounding its taxonomy in Turiel's Domain Theory rather than ad-hoc topic lists. It departs from the closest prior multimodal effort, M3oralBench, by replacing AI-generated images with expert-verified real-world photographs and introducing modality-centric annotations that isolate whether the moral signal resides in the image or the text. The 13-topic taxonomy spanning personal, interpersonal, and societal domains is more granular than the 6-topic schemes in prior benchmarks, and the multi-label norm attribution task goes beyond the binary or single-label formulations used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed in morally sensitive domains (autonomous driving, medical diagnosis, education), a principled benchmark for their moral alignment is a practical necessity that this paper addresses. The consistent finding that visual moral reasoning lags well behind text-based reasoning across all 19 models identifies a specific, actionable weakness for VLM developers. The scaling plateau beyond ~10B parameters suggests that targeted moral alignment training, rather than further scaling, is the likely path forward. Open questions include whether the 13-topic taxonomy generalizes across cultures, whether the modality gap can be closed with better visual pretraining, and how to incorporate moral alignment signals into VLM training pipelines.
