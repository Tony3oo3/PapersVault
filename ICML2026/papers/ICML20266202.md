## Problem

While autoregressive Transformers trained on context-free grammars can accurately predict next tokens, it remains unclear what hierarchical parsing information is encoded in their hidden states and how that information is organized across layers. Prior work either probes only the final layer, uses full-derivation-tree targets unavailable during generation, or shows correct generation without characterizing the internal algorithmic computation. This paper addresses the gap by characterizing prefix-conditioned parsing representations layer-by-layer and providing a matching theoretical construction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a token prefix x_{1:t} drawn from a depth-bounded PCFG (depth 4, terminal alphabet {1,2,3}, with sequences up to 2^48 in length) and produces a next-token probability distribution over the terminal vocabulary. For probing analyses, the same forward pass yields hidden states h_t^{(ℓ)} at every position t and every layer ℓ, which are then decoded by trained MLP probes into multi-hot vectors representing Earley chart item types or CYK completed constituents. The probing targets are defined solely from the observed prefix (not the full derivation tree), making them appropriate for the autoregressive setting.

## Method

The paper establishes a local-to-global parsing mechanism through both empirical probing and a theoretical construction. Empirically, 5-layer GPT-style Transformers trained on 50 random depth-4 PCFGs are probed at every layer and position: span-restricted CYK probing reveals that shorter-span constituents are recovered in earlier layers while longer-span constituents require deeper layers, and Earley-state probing shows that prefix chart information peaks at layer 4 before the final layer reorganizes it for prediction. Theoretically, for balanced full-binary PCFGs of depth K, the paper constructs a (K+1)-layer Transformer where the first K layers implement CYK-style inside dynamic programming in parallel at dyadic boundaries (positions t ≡ 0 mod 2^k), and the final layer aggregates maximal completed constituents via attention to perform an Earley-style prefix rollout that bypasses the sequential COMPLETE operation entirely. Causal intervention experiments confirm the construction: masking the theory-predicted dyadic-boundary attention edge at each layer produces the largest accuracy drop at exactly that layer's predicted offset, producing a diagonal pattern matching the hierarchical span structure.

## Results

Trained 5-layer Transformers on depth-4 PCFGs achieve >99% grammar-satisfaction accuracy on 20,000 sampled sequences, with an average position-wise KL divergence of 0.0881 to the exact Earley-induced next-token distribution (vs. 0.5862 for a uniform random-guess baseline, a 6.7× reduction). Layer-wise probing reveals that Earley-state accuracy peaks at 93.6% (Layer 4, MLP probe) and CYK completed-constituent accuracy peaks at 92.6% (Layer 4), both increasing monotonically from Layer 1 before dropping at the final layer. A causal intervention on the balanced full-binary grammar confirms the theoretical construction: masking the theory-predicted dyadic-boundary attention offset produces the largest accuracy drop in exactly the predicted layer (e.g., Layer 1 at offset 1 drops to 0.6596 vs. 0.9995 baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper without direct method-vs-method benchmarking; the 'baselines' serve diagnostic rather than competitive roles. The uniform random-guess baseline (KL = 0.5862) is outperformed by the trained Transformer (KL = 0.0881) by a factor of ~6.7×, confirming the model learns the grammar rather than memorizing. Linear probes recover the same qualitative layer-wise trend as MLP probes but with consistently lower accuracy (e.g., 0.8802 vs. 0.9264 for CYK at Layer 4), supporting the choice of nonlinear probes for multi-label structured targets. The theoretical construction is validated against the trained model via causal intervention: masking the predicted dyadic-boundary attention edge in Layer 1 at offset 1 drops generation accuracy from 0.9995 to 0.6596, while masking the same offset in other layers has negligible effect, producing the exact diagonal pattern the theory predicts. Attention mass inside the theory-predicted local window reaches 67%, 81%, 92%, and 100% for Layers 1–4, quantitatively confirming the local-to-global aggregation mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probing-based analysis of Allen-Zhu & Li (2023), who showed that Transformers trained on layered PCFGs encode inside-outside-style information, by probing every layer at every prefix position with prefix-conditioned targets (Earley chart states and CYK completed constituents) rather than only the final layer with full-derivation-tree labels. The theoretical construction draws directly on the inside algorithm (Lari & Young, 1990) for the bottom-up span aggregation and on the Earley algorithm (Earley, 1970) for the final-layer prefix rollout, but combines them in a causal autoregressive setting that prior constructive results (Yao et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2023; Garnier-Brun et al., 2024) did not address, as those worked in recognition or masked-language-modeling settings where the full string is visible. The paper also departs from recognition-focused CFL studies (Hahn, 2020; Weiss et al., 2021; Delétang et al., 2022) by requiring the model to maintain a running prefix summary sufficient for generation at every position, not just to accept or reject a complete string.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local-to-global mechanism—CYK-style parallel bottom-up aggregation in early layers followed by Earley-style prefix-conditioned rollout in the final layer—provides a concrete, testable mechanistic hypothesis for how Transformers handle hierarchical structure during autoregressive generation, going beyond the existence results of prior work. The O(log L)-depth construction for O(2^K)-length sequences is a clean theoretical anchor, and the causal intervention (masking dyadic-boundary attention edges) provides stronger-than-probing evidence that the trained model actually uses the predicted information flow. Key limitations include the theoretical construction being restricted to balanced full-binary PCFGs, the empirical setting using small depth-4 grammars and 5-layer models, and the open question of whether this mechanism generalizes to natural-language hierarchies or to the much deeper, wider Transformers used in practice. Extending the construction to general bounded-depth PCFGs and studying training dynamics that produce the local-to-global organization are natural next steps.
