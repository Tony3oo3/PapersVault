## Problem

While causal effect estimation has received substantial attention, directly learning rankings of treatment effects from observational data has remained largely unexplored. Existing approaches either first estimate CATEs (solving a harder problem than necessary for ranking) or use non-orthogonal ranking objectives that are sensitive to nuisance estimation errors, lacking the robustness guarantees that Neyman-orthogonal methods provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational data (X, T, Y) with tabular covariates, a binary treatment indicator, and a continuous outcome, under standard causal identification assumptions (consistency, positivity, unconfoundedness). It outputs a real-valued scoring function g: X → R whose pointwise evaluation at inference time directly ranks individuals by their treatment effects—no pairwise comparisons are needed at test time. The smoothness parameter κ controls a bias-variance trade-off: smaller κ approaches pure ranking (ordering only), while larger κ recovers more of the CATE magnitude structure.

## Method

Rank-Learner is a two-stage, model-agnostic learner. In the first stage, nuisance functions (response surfaces μ₁, μ₀ and propensity score e) are estimated via cross-fitting with arbitrary ML models. In the second stage, a scoring function is trained by minimizing a novel Neyman-orthogonal pairwise ranking loss: binary cross-entropy between pairwise preference probabilities σ(g(X)−g(X′)) and pseudo labels that augment smooth soft targets σ((τ(X)−τ(X′))/κ) with an orthogonal correction term. This correction is the product of an uncertainty-dependent weight (peaking when the pairwise ordering is ambiguous) and the difference of doubly robust scores between the two individuals, making the loss first-order insensitive to nuisance estimation errors. The key insight is that standard CATE estimation via MSE is unnecessarily difficult for ranking, and the orthogonal pairwise loss recovers the correct treatment effect ordering while remaining robust to first-order nuisance errors—established formally via Theorem 5.1 (orthogonality) and Theorem 5.2 (correct population minimizers).

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (AUTOC, higher is better), Rank-Learner achieves 1.34±0.01 at n=1,000, outperforming the DR-learner (1.33±0.02), T-learner (1.32±0.02), and plug-in ranker (1.31±0.02), approaching the oracle value of 1.40. On the semi-synthetic benchmarks (n=1,000), Rank-Learner leads on all three datasets: MOVIELENS (1.35 vs. 1.34 for DR-learner), MIMIC-III (1.18 vs. 1.17), and CPS (0.95 vs. 0.92). On the real-world CRITEO uplift benchmark (AUUC ×10³, 1M test samples), Rank-Learner achieves 5.90±0.40, substantially outperforming the DR-learner (5.17±1.13) and T-learner (5.08±1.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rank-Learner consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and training size. Against the T-learner (Künzel et al., 2019), the advantage is most pronounced in small-sample regimes: at n=100 on the synthetic benchmark, Rank-Learner achieves 1.00 AUTOC versus 0.88 for the T-learner, a gap of 0.12. Against the DR-learner (Kennedy, 2023), which is itself Neyman-orthogonal but targets CATE magnitudes rather than ranking, Rank-Learner leads by 0.01–0.02 AUTOC at n=1,000 on the synthetic benchmark and by 0.01–0.08 on the semi-synthetic datasets (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.92 on CPS). Against the non-orthogonal plug-in ranker (following Vanderschueren et al., 2024), the orthogonal correction yields the largest relative gains in low-data settings (e.g., 1.00 vs. 0.69 at n=100) and a clear margin on the real-world CRITEO benchmark (5.90 vs. 5.04 AUUC ×10³ at 1M test samples). The consistent ordering across all settings—Rank-Learner > DR-learner > T-learner ≈ Plug-in ranker—supports both the benefit of directly targeting ranking over CATE estimation and the value of Neyman-orthogonalization for the ranking objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of orthogonal statistical learning (Kennedy, 2023; Chernozhukov et al., 2018) and learning-to-rank (Burges et al., 2005), applying influence-function-based orthogonalization to a pairwise ranking objective for treatment effects. It builds directly on the soft ranking loss of Vanderschueren et al. (2024) as the base loss to be orthogonalized, and embeds the doubly robust score from the DR-learner (Kennedy, 2023) into the resulting pseudo labels. The key departure is from both standard CATE estimators (which recover effect magnitudes unnecessarily) and prior ranking methods (Kamran et al., 2024; Vanderschueren et al., 2024) that lack Neyman-orthogonality, making them sensitive to nuisance estimation error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Many real-world allocation problems (ICU triage, ad targeting, policy design) only require the relative ordering of treatment effects, not their exact magnitudes, yet practitioners have been forced to solve the harder CATE estimation problem. Rank-Learner provides the first model-agnostic, Neyman-orthogonal two-stage learner for this task, with the practical advantage that orthogonality yields robustness to nuisance misspecification—critical in observational settings where propensity scores and outcome models are imperfectly estimated. Open questions include extension to multi-treatment and continuous-treatment settings, threshold-based decisions where magnitudes near a boundary matter, and whether the smoothness parameter κ can be selected adaptively without a validation set.
