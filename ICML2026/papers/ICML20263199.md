## Problem

Flow maps enable high-quality one-step image generation but lack an explicit sampling trajectory, making it impossible to incorporate external constraints for conditional generation or solve inverse problems. Unlike iterative diffusion models that can be guided at each denoising step, a flow map's output is fixed once the initial noise is chosen, creating a 'guidance gap' that has limited flow maps to unconditional settings. The paper asks whether conditional sampling can be achieved by learning the right initial noise rather than steering a trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of SD-VAE on ImageNet 256×256 images. It takes a degraded observation y (e.g., a masked, blurred, or noisy latent vector) together with an inverse problem class label c identifying the forward operator, and produces conditional samples from the posterior p(x|y) in a single forward pass (1 NFE) or a small number of multi-step evaluations. For reward alignment, the observation is replaced by a context c (e.g., a text prompt), and the output is a sample from the reward-tilted distribution p(x|c) ∝ p_data(x) exp(βR(x,c)).

## Method

VFM reformulates the inverse problem in noise space: given observation y, it learns a Gaussian noise adapter q_φ(z|y,c) = N(μ_φ(y,c), diag(σ_φ²(y,c))) that approximates the noise-space posterior p(z|y), then maps z to data via a mean-flow parameterized flow map f_θ(z) = z − u_θ(z,0,1) in one step. The key technical contribution is a variational training objective derived by taking the KL divergence between two factorizations of the joint p(x,y,z), yielding three terms: a mean flow loss (imposing ODE structural constraints on f_θ), an observation loss (matching y to A(f_θ(z))), and a KL regularization on the adapter. Crucially, the flow map and adapter are trained jointly, with the mean flow loss evaluated on (x,z) pairs where z is drawn from the adapter, creating an implicit coupling that lets f_θ reshape the noise-to-data mapping to compensate for the adapter's simple Gaussian assumption. A theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) shows that under a linear-Gaussian setting, separate training almost surely fails to recover the posterior mean, while joint training recovers it exactly. For reward alignment, the observation loss is replaced by a reward maximization term, enabling fine-tuning of a pretrained flow map to sample from reward-tilted distributions in under 0.5 epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 box inpainting, VFM achieves FID 41.76, MMD 0.051, and CRPS 0.455/0.329 (single/10-sample average), substantially outperforming all guidance-based baselines (FID 62–83, MMD 0.13–0.18). On Gaussian deblurring, VFM achieves FID 44.12 and MMD 0.044, again the best distributional metrics. VFM requires only 1 NFE (~0.015 s) versus 250×2 NFEs (~7–47 s) for guidance methods, yielding roughly two orders of magnitude speedup. On PSNR/SSIM, guidance methods score higher for a single sample, but VFM's 10-sample average narrows or exceeds the gap (e.g., PSNR 23.92 vs. 23.41 for FlowDPS on deblurring).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFM decisively outperforms all seven guidance-based baselines (Latent DPS, Latent DAPS, PSLD, MPGD, FlowChef, FlowDPS, LFlow) on distributional metrics: FID is 41.76 vs. 62–83 for inpainting and 44.12 vs. 83–114 for deblurring; MMD is 0.051/0.044 vs. 0.13–0.24; CRPS is 0.455/0.329 vs. 0.47–0.72. The frozen-θ baseline (1 NFE, no joint training) is equally fast but performs far worse (FID 136/173), confirming that joint training of the flow map and noise adapter is essential. On pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM), guidance methods score higher for a single VFM draw, but averaging 10 VFM samples closes or reverses the gap (e.g., PSNR 23.92 vs. 23.41 for FlowDPS on deblurring). On LPIPS, VFM is competitive even without averaging (0.354 inpainting, 0.392 deblurring). For unconditional generation, VFM-B/2 reaches FID 10.64 at 2 steps, competitive with mean-flow baselines (MF-B/2: 6.17, DMF-B/2: 5.63) despite being trained for only 50 additional epochs versus ~240 for the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

VFM sits at the intersection of two lines of work: flow maps (Boffi et al., 2025; Geng et al., 2025) for one-step generation, and amortized variational inference (Kingma & Welling, 2013) for posterior approximation. The key conceptual departure from prior conditional generation methods (DPS, DAPS, PSLD, FlowDPS) is reframing conditioning from 'guiding a sampling trajectory' to 'learning the proper initial noise'—a shift that is only possible because flow maps provide a deterministic noise-to-data mapping with no intermediate states to steer. The joint training of the flow map and a simple Gaussian noise adapter, with the mean flow loss serving as the data-fitting term, distinguishes VFM from frozen-generator approaches (Venkatraman et al., 2025a) that instead use more complex neural SDE adapters while keeping the generator fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VFM achieves roughly two orders of magnitude speedup over iterative guidance methods (0.03s vs. ~44s for DAPS) while matching or exceeding them on distributional metrics (FID, MMD, CRPS) for image inverse problems, making fast posterior sampling practical for uncertainty quantification and real-time restoration. The theoretical result that joint training recovers the exact posterior mean while separate training almost surely fails (Proposition 3.1) provides a principled justification for the co-adaptation of generator and adapter. Key limitations include the training-based nature (unlike inference-time DPS), the Gaussian adapter assumption that may be insufficient for highly multimodal posteriors, and the need to modify the backbone flow map, which can slightly degrade unconditional quality. Extending to more expressive noise models (normalizing flows, energy transformers) and to video/temporal settings are natural next steps.
