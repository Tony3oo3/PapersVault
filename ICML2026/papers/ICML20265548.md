## Problem

Dense Transformer-based world models (e.g., DINO-WM) apply uniform self-attention to all image patches, wasting computation on static backgrounds and requiring ~120 s per MPC decision cycle on Push-T, which is prohibitive for real-time robotic control. Naive sparsification (predicting only dynamic patches and copying static ones) reduces open-loop error but causes closed-loop MPC success to plummet because it ignores context-driven background feature adjustments that pre-trained encoders like DINOv2 inherently encode, creating a rugged, non-smooth cost landscape for the planner.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a current RGB frame encoded by a frozen DINOv2-ViT-S/14 into N×D patch tokens, a scalar action vector, and a pool of historical frame features. It outputs a complete next-frame latent feature map (predicted foreground tokens concatenated with corrected background tokens) in the DINOv2 feature space. During planning, the MPC cost is computed only over a sparse task-relevant mask (regions differing between current and goal images), and the final output is a single action step extracted from a CEM-optimized Gaussian over H-step action sequences.

## Method

DDP-WM decomposes scene dynamics into sparse primary dynamics and low-rank context-driven background updates, processed by four sequential stages: (1) a single cross-attention layer fuses historical frame features into the current state; (2) a lightweight 6-layer ViT localizes dynamic regions at 2×2 sub-patch resolution, producing a sparse binary mask; (3) a 6-layer ViT primary predictor operates exclusively on the masked dynamic tokens to predict next-frame foreground features; (4) a Low-Rank Correction Module (LRM) applies one asymmetric cross-attention layer where background tokens query the newly predicted foreground tokens, enforcing a unidirectional causal flow that models background updates as a low-rank consequence of primary dynamics. The LRM is the key architectural insight: it contributes almost no open-loop accuracy gain but is essential for producing a smooth, funnel-shaped MPC cost landscape that enables reliable CEM convergence. Training is stepwise (localization → primary predictor → LRM) with MSE losses on foreground and background regions respectively, and a Sparse MPC Cost Mask further restricts planning cost evaluation to task-relevant pixels.

## Results

On the five simulated benchmarks, DDP-WM achieves 100% success rate on PointMaze, 98% on Push-T, 98% on Wall, 0.31 Chamfer Distance on Rope, and 0.24 on Granular, matching or surpassing the SOTA dense model DINO-WM on all tasks. The headline efficiency gain is a ~9× reduction in FLOPs (23G→2.5G) and a 7.5× speedup in MPC decision time (120s→16s) on Push-T. On the real-world DROID benchmark, DDP-WM achieves an Action Score of 47.90±0.62 with a 4.26× speedup, surpassing both JEPA-WMs (46.5±0.4) and V-JEPA 2-AC (42.9±2.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA baseline DINO-WM, DDP-WM wins on all five tasks: Push-T success rate improves from 90% to 98% (statistically significant, p≈0.013 over 13 seeds), PointMaze from 98% to 100%, Wall from 96% to 98%, Rope CD from 0.41 to 0.31, and Granular CD from 0.26 to 0.24, while simultaneously achieving a 9.2× FLOPs reduction and 7.5× MPC latency speedup on Push-T. Compared to IRIS, DDP-WM dominates by large margins on every task (e.g., Push-T: 98% vs 32%; Wall: 98% vs 4%). Against DreamerV3, DDP-WM wins on Push-T (98% vs 30%), Rope (0.31 vs 2.49), and Granular (0.24 vs 1.05), ties on PointMaze (both 100%), but trails slightly on Wall (98% vs 100%). Versus Sparse Imagination, DDP-WM wins on Push-T (98% vs 78.3%) and Wall (98% vs 95%), ties on PointMaze, and is the only method evaluated on the harder Rope and Granular tasks. On the real-world DROID benchmark, DDP-WM outperforms both JEPA-WMs and V-JEPA 2-AC in Action Score while being 4.26× faster than the dense JEPA-WMs baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDP-WM builds directly on the DINO-WM paradigm (frozen DINOv2 observation model + learnable ViT transition model in pre-trained feature space + CEM-based MPC), but departs fundamentally from its dense self-attention predictor by introducing a structured, physics-motivated sparsity: a dynamic localization network identifies sparse primary-dynamics regions, a powerful predictor handles only those tokens, and a single-layer cross-attention Low-Rank Correction Module (LRM) updates background features at negligible cost. This contrasts with general token-sparsification methods (DynamicViT, TokenLearner) that rely on learned importance scores, and with object-centric world models (Slot Attention, FOCUS) that require explicit object segmentation; DDP-WM instead operates at the patch level in a data-driven way that naturally extends to deformable and multi-body systems. The LRM's unidirectional causal cross-attention design is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior sparse or dense world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~9× reduction in MPC decision time (120 s → 16 s on Push-T) while simultaneously improving success rate (90% → 98%) addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for real-time robotic control, making high-fidelity world-model planning practically viable. The conceptual insight that closed-loop planning success depends on optimization-landscape smoothness rather than raw prediction accuracy — demonstrated by the open-loop/closed-loop paradox where naive sparse prediction matches full-model accuracy yet fails in planning — is broadly applicable to any model-based control setting. Limitations include the fixed third-person camera assumption and graceful degradation to dense prediction under drastic illumination changes; extending to egocentric settings and verifying the low-rank background-update assumption across more diverse physical regimes remain open.
