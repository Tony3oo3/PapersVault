## Problem

GRPO-style RL fine-tuning for LLMs relies on heuristic importance-ratio clipping to approximate trust-region constraints, but this mechanism fails to regulate samples whose ratios exceed the clipping threshold, causing gradient masking and training instability under policy staleness. Additionally, the uniform 'one-size-fits-all' deviation tolerance across queries leads to entropy collapse: easy prompts become deterministic while complex reasoning tasks are deprived of exploration budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (query) and generates N=8 rollout trajectories per query from the reference policy, with maximum response lengths of 1,024 tokens (1.5B model) or 3,072 tokens (7B model). A scalar reward is assigned to each full trajectory, and the method computes a per-query dual variable λ_q from the reward distribution of those N rollouts. The output is an updated autoregressive policy that assigns sequence-level probabilities to generated responses, with the trust-region constraint enforced independently per query rather than globally.

## Method

QUATRO formulates RL fine-tuning as a query-conditioned trust-region problem that explicitly constrains the KL divergence between the updated and reference policies per query, then derives the exact optimal policy via Lagrangian duality. The resulting objective (Eq. 14) contains a log-ratio stabilizer term that symmetrically corrects the current policy toward the trust-region optimum without any importance-ratio clipping. The per-query dual variable λ_q is obtained by minimizing a scalar log-partition function over the N sampled rewards, automatically calibrating update sharpness: high-variance reward distributions yield larger λ_q (more conservative updates), while concentrated distributions permit sharper updates. The advantage A_q^i is computed from the dual variables (λ_q, μ_q) rather than group-wise mean/std normalization, and the method optionally adds a KL penalty to a pretrained reference model, yielding a geometric interpolation between the trust-region optimal policy and the pretrained model. Removing the log-ratio term and substituting group-normalized advantages reduces the objective exactly to GSPO, isolating the log-ratio stabilizer and dual-derived advantage as the key differentiators.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, QUATRO (δ=0.001) achieves 68.68 average Pass@256 across six math benchmarks, outperforming GRPO (64.36), GSPO (60.85), and CISPO (64.32). On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, QUATRO (δ=0.001) reaches 73.41 Pass@256 versus GSPO's 65.70, a gap of 7.7 points. The performance advantage grows monotonically with k: at k=1 the gap is modest (e.g., 32.63 vs 31.66 for δ=0.1 vs GRPO), but by k=256 it widens substantially, indicating QUATRO preserves more diverse correct reasoning paths. Final policy entropy is 0.23 for QUATRO (δ=0.001) versus 0.05–0.07 for all baselines, confirming sustained exploration throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUATRO outperforms all six GRPO-style baselines (GRPO, GPG, GMPO, GSPO, DAPO, CISPO) across every k value and both model scales. The most striking gap is against GSPO at high k: on the 1.5B model, QUATRO (δ=0.001) achieves 68.68 Pass@256 versus GSPO's 60.85 (a 7.8-point margin), and on the 7B model the gap is 73.41 vs 65.70 (7.7 points). Against GRPO, the advantage is 4.3 points at k=256 (1.5B) and grows further at intermediate k values. At k=1, QUATRO with δ=0.1 leads all baselines (32.63 vs 32.17 for GMPO), while tighter δ settings (0.01, 0.001) trade a small amount of Pass@1 for substantially larger gains at k≥32, reflecting a deliberate exploration–exploitation trade-off controlled by the trust-region radius. The UCC@k metric confirms the mechanism: at k=256 on MATH500, QUATRO recalls 97.20 unique correct solutions versus 73.25 for GPG and 16.17 for GRPO, demonstrating that the gains stem from preserving diverse reasoning paths rather than repeated sampling of a single solution.

## Relation to Prior Work

QUATRO extends the trust-region policy optimization lineage from TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015) through GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by replacing heuristic importance-ratio clipping with an exact per-query KL-constrained formulation solved via Lagrangian duality. The key conceptual departure is making the trust-region constraint query-conditioned rather than global, so that the dual variable λ_q automatically calibrates update magnitude to each prompt's reward variance. Remarkably, the resulting objective is structurally close to GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025a): removing the log-ratio stabilizer and substituting group-normalized advantages recovers GSPO exactly, positioning QUATRO as a principled refinement rather than a wholesale redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in robustness: QUATRO tolerates 10× learning-rate increases and 5× inner-loop rollout reuse without the reward collapse or entropy collapse that plague clipping-based baselines, which is directly relevant to scaling RL fine-tuning pipelines where aggressive hyperparameters are common. The introduced UCC@k metric fills a genuine gap in evaluation by distinguishing diverse correct solutions from repeated sampling of a single answer. Open questions include adaptive selection of the trust-region radius δ (currently a manual hyperparameter), whether the approach transfers to settings with continuous or fine-grained rewards, and whether the per-query dual optimization remains well-conditioned as model scale and sequence length grow substantially.
