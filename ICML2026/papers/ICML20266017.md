## Problem

Existing GUI grounding benchmarks (ScreenSpot, WinSpot, ScreenSpot-Pro) predominantly feature single-window or sanitized screenshots, creating a distribution shift from real desktop environments characterized by multi-window stacking, occlusion, and visual clutter. This gap means no prior benchmark provides parametric, factor-level control over complexity dimensions (occlusion ratio, layout density, semantic similarity), making it impossible to systematically isolate which interference factors most degrade model robustness.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a 2560×1440 synthesized desktop screenshot (composed from real window captures on a Windows 11 wallpaper) and a natural language instruction as input, and the model autoregressively generates normalized spatial coordinates (e.g., in [0,1000] range) as plain text tokens to locate the target element. Evaluation uses Click Accuracy—whether the predicted point falls within the ground-truth bounding box—rather than IoU or distance thresholds, directly reflecting whether the agent would successfully trigger the target widget.

## Method

The core contribution is a parameterized multi-window synthesis framework that generates test scenes by compositing real window screenshots onto a 2560×1440 canvas while controlling three orthogonal difficulty axes: layout density (2–15 windows), target visibility ratio (30%–100%), and semantic similarity of distractors (1–5 levels). Spatial placement uses Category-Position Priors derived from HCI window-management studies (e.g., communication apps in periphery, browsers central), and distractor selection is guided by cosine similarity of text embeddings to inject 'hard negative' semantically identical elements. Occlusion is enforced by iteratively positioning distractor windows until the target's visible ratio satisfies a constraint, verified post-render with pixel-level masks. The metadata foundation comprises 585 screenshots and 1,356 instruction-target pairs across 9 application domains, annotated via a two-stage pipeline (OmniParser for bounding-box detection, QwenVL2.5-72B for precise functional descriptions). Two complementary evaluation protocols—single-factor controlled analysis and a five-level difficulty ladder (L1–L5)—enable both isolated factor attribution and holistic stress testing of five MLLM baselines.

## Results

On the WinDeskGround benchmark (L3 difficulty), InfiGUI-G1-7B achieves the highest overall Click Accuracy at 44.89%, followed by UGround-7B (42.61%), UI-TARS-1.5-7B (40.16%), OS-Atlas-7B (28.13%), and SeeClick (4.48%). In the idealized single-window setting, top-tier models (UGround, UI-TARS, InfiGUI) all exceed 80% accuracy, but performance degrades sharply under multi-window complexity: at the extreme L5 level (10–15 windows, 30–50% visibility), all models collapse to 7–11% accuracy. Occlusion is identified as the most critical bottleneck, with accuracy dropping from >75% at full visibility to below 20% when target visibility falls to 30–50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiGUI-G1-7B outperforms UGround-7B by 2.28 points overall at L3 (44.89% vs. 42.61%) and by a wider margin in the challenging Advanced Tools category (28.57% vs. 14.29%), suggesting its adaptive exploration policy better handles complex professional software. UGround remains competitive in Communication (52.75% vs. 38.99%) and Media (55.08% vs. 53.39%), indicating complementary strengths across architectures. SeeClick is decisively outperformed by all other models across every domain, with overall accuracy of only 4.48% at L3, confirming that its fixed low-resolution architecture cannot isolate targets in cluttered multi-window environments. At the extreme L5 stress test, UI-TARS and InfiGUI maintain residual accuracy of ~10–11% while UGround drops to 7.08%, showing that reinforcement learning and adaptive policies provide slightly better survivability under compounding occlusion and semantic interference. All models share a critical vulnerability to occlusion: accuracy collapses from >75% at full visibility to below 20% when target visibility drops to 30–50%, a gap no current architecture overcomes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the GUI grounding literature (SeeClick, UGround, UI-TARS, InfiGUI) but departs from prior benchmarks (ScreenSpot, WinSpot, ScreenSpot-Pro) by treating multi-window complexity as a parametrically controllable evaluation axis rather than a natural data distribution. Rather than collecting and annotating complex desktops directly, it repurposes high-fidelity single-window metadata and synthesizes multi-window scenes through a parameterized pipeline drawing on HCI spatial priors and text-embedding-based semantic similarity injection. The annotation pipeline itself builds on OmniParser for coarse element detection and QwenVL2.5-72B for refined textual descriptions, but the core contribution is the synthesis framework and the resulting controlled evaluation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a concrete robustness gap: all five evaluated models collapse from >75% to <20% accuracy when target visibility drops to 30–50%, revealing a shared reliance on complete visual features that no current architecture overcomes. The parametric design enables factor-level dissection (occlusion vs. clutter vs. semantic confusion) that prior naturally-distributed benchmarks cannot support, making it a useful diagnostic tool for future model development. Key limitations include static single-step evaluation (no temporal dynamics or multi-step window manipulation), synthetic composition that may not fully capture real interaction constraints, and the conservative layering assumption in the semantic-interference protocol that likely underestimates real-world ambiguity. Open questions include how WinDeskGround scores correlate with downstream real-environment benchmarks and whether hybrid approaches (visual + accessibility tree) can close the occlusion gap.
