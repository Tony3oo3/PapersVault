## Problem

Self-supervised object-centric representations have been restricted to 2D RGB inputs, while existing 3D object-centric methods operate on colorless point clouds, rely on neural rendering, or produce dense representations unsuitable for downstream policy learning. No prior work demonstrates self-supervised decomposition of colored 3D observations (RGB-D or RGB voxels) into compact, interpretable, and controllable per-object latents that directly improve robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265805/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts three 3D modalities at 64³ resolution: RGB-D images (4×H×W), binary occupancy voxel grids (1×D×H×W), or colored RGB voxel grids (3×D×H×W), where point clouds are first voxelized into regular grids via an axis-aligned bounding box. It outputs M foreground latent particles—each a low-dimensional vector encoding 3D keypoint position, bounding-box scale, transparency, and appearance features—plus a single background latent, per-particle segmentation masks, and a full-resolution scene reconstruction. The particle tokens are also consumed downstream by an entity-centric diffusion policy for manipulation.

## Method

3D-DLP extends the Deep Latent Particles (DLP) VAE framework to 3D by replacing 2D CNNs with 3D CNNs, bilinear spatial transformers with trilinear STNs, and 2D position/scale attributes with 3D vectors, while eliminating the composition-order variable since volumetric rendering resolves occlusions naturally. Two key methodological contributions make this work on sparse, colored voxel scenes: (1) an appearance-aware K-means prior that clusters occupied voxels in a joint CIELAB-color + 3D-position space with lightness weighting, replacing the spatial-softmax keypoint prior that fails on discontinuous voxel grids; and (2) a chroma loss that penalizes chrominance error separately from luminance on occupied voxels, preventing the gray-collapse failure mode of plain MSE. The decoder renders each particle as a canonical cubic RGBA patch placed via 3D STN, then composites all particles with a background volume using either noisy-OR (occupancy) or normalized alpha-mixture (RGB) blending. Trained end-to-end via ELBO with a sparsity regularizer on particle transparency, the model requires no annotations; downstream, the learned particle tokens are plugged into an adapted EC-Diffuser policy with proprioceptive and CLIP language conditioning for imitation learning.

## Results

On RGB voxel reconstruction (Table 1), 3D-DLP-VC achieves 24.41±0.26 Masked PSNR and 0.910 IoU on MimicGen, substantially outperforming the deterministic AE (11.39 PSNR, 0.582 IoU) and VAE (4.35 PSNR, 0.244 IoU) baselines. On 12 MimicGen manipulation tasks, 3D-DLP + EC-Diffuser achieves a 48.1% mean success rate, compared to 30.8% (2D-DLP single-view), 34.1% (2D-DLP multi-view), and 47.3% (EquiDiff voxel-only). On 10 RLBench tasks, 3D-DLP wins 9 of 10 among matched-compute methods and 7 of 10 against the published PerACT numbers, with margins of 19–48 absolute points on the largest wins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2D-DLP (single-view and multi-view), 3D-DLP achieves a clear advantage on multi-object stacking and assembly tasks (e.g., Stack: 94.6% vs. 70.0%/78.0%; Stack Three: 70.0% vs. 18.0%/14.7%) but loses on tasks where a single object dominates the view (e.g., Coffee: 36.0% vs. 72.7%/82.0%), likely because the 2D projection preserves fine appearance detail for that specific object. Against EquiDiff (SE(3)-equivariant diffusion on raw 64³ voxels), 3D-DLP edges out the dense-voxel baseline on mean success rate (48.1% vs. 47.3%) and wins 6 of 12 tasks, with the largest gaps on Stack Three (+57.3 pts) and Mug Cleanup (+30.0 pts), while EquiDiff retains an advantage on Kitchen (+8.3 pts) and Coffee Prep. (+34.0 pts). On RLBench, 3D-DLP wins 9 of 10 tasks among the three matched-compute methods (losing only Close Jar to 2D-DLP single-view, where the small axis-aligned target favors per-view 2D tokens), and surpasses the published PerACT numbers on 7 of 10 tasks, with PerACT's three wins (Close Jar, Push Buttons, Stack Blocks) attributable to its dense-voxel inductive bias for small/precise targets and its keypose-classification head for discrete multi-step actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Latent Particles (DLP) framework from 2D RGB images to 3D observations (RGB-D, occupancy voxels, and RGB voxels), introducing two key methodological components to handle the sparsity and color of volumetric data: an appearance-aware K-means keypoint prior (replacing the spatial-softmax prior that fails on sparse voxel grids) and a chroma reconstruction loss to prevent gray collapse. It deliberately departs from neural-rendering-based 3D object-centric approaches (uOCF, DynaVol-S, GrabS) by using a direct 3D reconstruction objective, and from supervised methods like GROOT by being fully self-supervised. For downstream control, it plugs the learned 3D particle tokens into an adapted EC-Diffuser policy, positioning the work as a bridge between self-supervised scene decomposition and imitation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first practical pipeline from self-supervised 3D object-centric decomposition to improved robotic manipulation, showing that compact 3D particle representations outperform both 2D particle baselines and dense voxel-only policies on multi-object tasks. Key limitations include the memory overhead of voxelization (64³ grids), reliance on static backgrounds and recurring object types, and the absence of eye-in-hand observations. Open questions include extending 3D-DLP to dynamics and world modeling, scaling to highly diverse real-world scenes with novel objects, and learning directly from raw point clouds to avoid voxelization costs.
