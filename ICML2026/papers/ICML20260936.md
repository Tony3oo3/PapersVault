## Problem

Federated PEFT of LLMs is vulnerable to backdoor poisoning, yet existing defenses (PEFTGuard, AlignIns, Krum, FLAME) all require server access to per-client real-valued adapter updates—precisely what secure aggregation and privacy-preserving FL protocols hide. No unsupervised, value-free vetting mechanism exists that can identify poisoned clients before aggregation while remaining compatible with secure aggregation constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260936/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client transmits only a Top-K set of salient coordinate IDs (integer indices into a fixed, globally-shared Q/V LoRA parameter space of dimension M, with K ≪ M) derived from the absolute magnitudes of its local adapter update; no real-valued weights or gradients leave the client. The server outputs a vetted cohort (benign clients cleared for aggregation) and a set of flagged suspicious clusters, which determines participation in the subsequent secure-aggregation phase. The sketch budget K is set once via a coverage+stability rule (cumulative salience coverage ≥ τ and resample overlap ≥ ρ₀) and held fixed across rounds.

## Method

INDEXGUARD exploits the insight that shared backdoor triggers induce a common salience imprint in self-attention Q/V LoRA coordinates: poisoned clients concentrate their Top-K update magnitudes on a trigger-specific subset, yielding high within-adversary and low cross-group index overlap even under non-IID data. In Phase A, each client computes |Δθ| over the monitored Q/V adapter tensors, extracts Top-K indices, and sends only those indices; the server computes pairwise Index-Overlap Similarity (IOS = |S_i ∩ S_j|/K), clusters clients via hierarchical clustering on 1-IOS, and flags non-majority clusters whose cohesion (mean intra-cluster IOS) exceeds a median+γ threshold as suspicious. In Phase B, only the vetted cohort participates in standard secure aggregation of their real-valued adapter updates. The paper provides theoretical guarantees—alignment (overlap lower-bounds shared salience mass), stability (Top-K reproducibility under sub-Gaussian noise with margin Δ), scale-robustness (invariance to bounded diagonal rescaling), and a separation theorem showing adversarial cohesion strictly exceeds benign cohesion when the trigger-imprint fraction K_T/K dominates benign heterogeneity ρ_B—making the approach compatible with secure aggregation, optional HE, adapter-level DP, and ZK-SNARK-based protected reporting.

## Results

INDEXGUARD achieves near-ceiling client-vetting performance across all evaluated settings: AUC of 1.000 on instruction-following toxic backdoor corpora (toxic-backdoors-alpaca/hard) and AUC of 0.998 on standard classification (AG News, SST-2), with detection accuracy of 99.39% (SC) and 100% (IF). The only non-perfect results occur on stealthier triggers (RIPPLES rare-word and StyleBkd), where AUC drops to 0.982–0.996 and accuracy to ~98%. End-to-end mitigation suppresses ASR from ~1.0 to near-zero while preserving clean accuracy (e.g., 0.921→0.895 on SST-2, 0.690→0.651 on AG News).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INDEXGUARD essentially ties PEFTGuard (the strongest centralized PEFT-specific detector) on instruction-following tasks (both 100% Acc / 1.000 AUC) and marginally leads on standard classification (99.39% vs. 99% Acc; 0.998 vs. 1.000 AUC), while being unsupervised and secure-aggregation compatible—properties PEFTGuard lacks. Against the next-best centralized baselines, INDEXGUARD's advantage is decisive: MNTD (S&P'21) reaches only 88% Acc / 0.937 AUC on SC and collapses to 51% / 0.510 on IF; PICCOLO (S&P'22) achieves 76% / 0.890 on SC and is not applicable to IF; AttenTD (NAACL'22) and T-Miner (USENIX'21) perform at or near chance level (50% Acc, AUC 0.500–0.606). Critically, all five baselines require access to per-client real-valued updates or a trained model, making them incompatible with the secure-aggregation setting that INDEXGUARD targets; INDEXGUARD is the only method in the comparison that is simultaneously unsupervised, value-free, and secure-aggregation compatible.

## Relation to Prior Work

INDEXGUARD departs from two established lines of work: centralized PEFT backdoor detectors (PEFTGuard, AttenTD, PICCOLO) that require access to real-valued adapter weights and/or supervised labeled corpora, and federated backdoor defenses (Krum, FLAME, FreqFed, AlignIns) that assume the server can inspect per-client update values. It repurposes the well-studied phenomenon of salience concentration in self-attention (Michel et al., Voita et al.) into a privacy-preserving vetting signal, showing that the *support* of Top-K salient coordinates—rather than their magnitudes—carries sufficient discriminative structure to separate poisoned from benign clients. The theoretical analysis draws on order-statistics concentration and sub-Gaussian tail bounds for Top-K stability, connecting to robust statistics literature but applied to a new index-only setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a concrete and previously unaddressed gap: no prior method can detect backdoored clients in federated PEFT while remaining compatible with secure aggregation (i.e., without the server seeing per-client real-valued updates). For privacy-sensitive cross-silo deployments (healthcare, enterprise), this enables always-on integrity vetting without relaxing cryptographic protections. Key limitations include the requirement for a shared PEFT coordinate space across clients, reduced efficacy when malicious clients form the majority, and partial vulnerability to adaptive attackers who mix benign-looking indices into their reported support. Open questions include extending to fully adaptive multi-trigger adversaries, tighter integration of ZK-verified reporting into the FL protocol, and combining index-only vetting with robust aggregation as complementary layers.
