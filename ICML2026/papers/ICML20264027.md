## Problem

Prior activation geometry work established that transformers encode belief-state geometry in their residual stream for single latent generators, but left open what structure emerges when the data-generating process is composed of multiple parts. This paper asks whether transformers learn to factor their world into conditionally independent components, and whether this factoring is merely a sufficient solution or a genuine inductive bias that persists even when it sacrifices predictive fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264027/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives integer tokens (vocabulary size 433) that encode the Cartesian product of subtokens from five latent factors (three 3-state Mess3 HMMs and two 3-dimensional Bloch Walk GHMMs), with no explicit signal of the underlying factorization. The primary analytical output is the geometric structure of residual-stream activations: effective dimensionality via PCA and cumulative explained variance, pairwise orthogonality of factor subspaces via a vary-one protocol and normalized subspace overlap metrics, and the arrangement of context embeddings within each recovered subspace compared against ground-truth predictive vectors.

## Method

The paper formalizes two competing representational hypotheses using generalized hidden Markov models: a joint representation in the tensor product of factor spaces (dimension ∏d_n − 1) and a factored representation in orthogonal subspaces via the direct sum (dimension Σ(d_n − 1)), proving that conditionally independent dynamics keep predictive vectors on the product-state manifold where a lossless linear map to the factored form exists. GPT-2-style decoder-only transformers (4 layers, d_model=120) are trained via standard next-token cross-entropy on sequences from three progressively harder regimes: fully independent factors, conditionally independent dependency chains, and indecomposable generators with a noisy channel that breaks conditional independence. Analysis uses linear regression to recover factor geometries, PCA/CEV to track dimensionality over training, a vary-one protocol to identify and compare factor subspaces, and subspace overlap metrics to quantify orthogonality. The same pipeline is applied to vanilla RNNs and LSTMs to test architectural generality.

## Results

For five independent factors (three Mess3 + two Bloch Walk, each with 3D latent space), the joint representation requires 242 dimensions while the factored representation requires only 10. Trained transformers converge to ~11 effective dimensions (95% cumulative explained variance), matching the factored prediction and ruling out the joint alternative, even with d_model up to 480. Orthogonality analysis shows factor subspaces become approximately orthogonal over training (sum of individual factor dimensions drops to 14, close to the union dimension of 12). In the noisy-channel regime (ε up to 0.5), transformers first collapse to the 10-dimensional factored representation within hundreds of gradient steps regardless of ε, only later expanding dimensionality under sustained loss pressure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it tests two competing geometric hypotheses (joint tensor-product vs. factored direct-sum representation) against the same trained model. The factored hypothesis is strongly supported: activations concentrate in ~10 dimensions rather than 242, organize into approximately orthogonal per-factor subspaces, and persist even when the data generator violates conditional independence (noisy channel, ε > 0). The joint representation, while always lossless, is never the preferred solution—the model dwells in the factored regime and departs only under sustained gradient pressure. Across architectures, vanilla RNNs at d_model=32 closely replicate the transformer's factored CEV curves, while LSTMs show the same phenomenon at smaller d_model sizes, suggesting the inductive bias toward factoring is not specific to the transformer architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation geometry program of Shai et al. (2024), which established that transformers trained on single GHMM generators recover belief-state geometry in their residual stream, to the multi-part setting where the data-generating process decomposes into conditionally independent factors. It builds on the GHMM and predictive vector formalism from Upper (1997) and Riechers & Crutchfield (2018), and connects to Trager et al. (2023), who showed linear factorization of multimodal embeddings under conditional independence, by extending that structural result into the autoregressive next-token setting and deriving competing geometric predictions (joint vs. factored). The paper departs from prior work on engineered factored representations (RL-based factored MDPs, dedicated architectures like NICE) by showing that standard next-token pretraining alone induces factored structure without any architectural or training modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that transformers exhibit an inductive bias toward factored representations—even when the factored solution is provably lossy and the model has sufficient capacity for the joint alternative—provides a principled explanation for why models decompose the world into parts and suggests that interpretable low-dimensional orthogonal subspaces may persist in larger models trained on complex data. This opens a concrete path for interpretability: rather than needing to understand the full network, one could discover and trace information flow within separable factor subspaces. Key open questions include whether the bias survives when model capacity is insufficient even for the factored representation, how it scales to real-world data with unknown factor structure, and whether the specific architectural mechanisms (embedding layer, attention, MLP) responsible for the bias can be identified.
