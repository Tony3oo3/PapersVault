## Problem

Preference optimization methods (DPO, SimPO) used to align LLMs for recommendation are vulnerable to reward hacking: models inflate training reward margins on sampled positive-negative pairs without genuinely improving global ranking quality. The paper identifies the root cause as ε-insensitive regions—structural flat zones in pairwise preference models where gradient updates on sampled pairs exert negligible influence on the vast pool of unsampled negatives, a problem that is acute in recommendation due to the one-class nature of implicit feedback data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's chronological interaction sequence (item titles as text) and a candidate set of 20 randomly selected non-interacted items, constructs a text-based prompt, and outputs a ranked item list grounded by the LLM's output probability distribution. Items are represented purely by their textual titles (no IDs or embeddings), and the model is fine-tuned with LoRA (32×8) on LLaMA-2-7B, making the approach applicable to the fine-tuning stage where a small candidate set must be ranked rather than generating items from scratch.

## Method

SIRIUS introduces a pseudo-negative sample—a virtual item not in the observed item set—whose reward is deliberately constructed to act as a universal anchor that guarantees non-vanishing gradient propagation to unsampled pairs. Under DPO, the pseudo-negative reward is input-dependent, set to −β log π_ref(y_u^i|x_u) + β log Z(x_u) to dominate the bounded subset of problematic unsampled negatives; under SimPO, a fixed constant r=0 suffices since the reference-free reward is globally bounded above. The augmented training objective couples the positive with both a sampled negative and the pseudo-negative, shrinking the measure of the ε-insensitive region so that misordered unsampled pairs receive effective corrective gradients. Theoretically, the paper proves that for any pairwise preference model with bounded rewards, such pseudo-negatives always exist, and under the Bradley-Terry model derives the exact area of the ε-insensitive region (up to 63.8% of the preference space for ε=0.99), confirming the structural prevalence of the problem.

## Results

On the three benchmarks, SimPO w/o LN + SIRIUS achieves HitRatio@1 of 0.7315 (LastFM), 0.7857 (Goodreads), and 0.8550 (Steam), setting new state-of-the-art results. DPO + SIRIUS reaches 0.6593, 0.6744, and 0.7757 respectively, surpassing the stronger S-DPO variant (which uses three sampled negatives) on LastFM and Steam. The method also maintains high ValidRatio (≥0.998) across all datasets, confirming that predictions remain within the candidate set. Relative improvements over the best baseline (SimPO w/o LN) range from 9.6% (LastFM) to 12.1% (Goodreads) to 6.9% (Steam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIRIUS, when applied to SimPO w/o LN, achieves the best HitRatio@1 on all three datasets (0.7315/0.7857/0.8550), outperforming the strongest preference-optimization baseline S-DPO (0.6493/0.6744/0.7712) by 12.6%, 16.4%, and 11.0% respectively. Notably, SIRIUS with a single pseudo-negative surpasses S-DPO which uses three sampled negatives, demonstrating that the virtual anchor is more effective than simply increasing the number of real negatives. Against vanilla DPO (0.6393/0.6462/0.7333), DPO + SIRIUS improves by 3.1%, 4.4%, and 6.0%. The method also substantially outperforms LLM-based recommenders: LLaRA (0.4750/0.5292/0.4927) and TALLRec (0.4180/0.4983/0.4637) trail by 54% and 74% on LastFM respectively. Traditional sequential models (GRU4Rec, Caser, SASRec) achieve the lowest HitRatio@1 (0.24–0.43), confirming the advantage of LLM-based preference optimization with SIRIUS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DPO/SimPO preference optimization lineage for LLM alignment, extending it to the recommendation setting where the one-class nature of implicit feedback creates a structural asymmetry between sampled and unsampled negatives. It departs from prior reward-hacking analyses in RLHF (which focus on reward model imperfections) by identifying a gradient-propagation failure intrinsic to all pairwise preference models under the Bradley-Terry framework, formalized as ε-insensitive regions. The proposed SIRIUS method is conceptually distinct from S-DPO's multi-negative sampling: rather than drawing more real negatives, it introduces a single virtual pseudo-negative anchor that guarantees non-vanishing gradients for unsampled pairs, a mechanism not present in any prior preference optimization variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ε-insensitive region framework provides the first structural explanation for why DPO-based recommenders exhibit reward hacking, shifting the diagnosis from 'noisy reward signals' to 'gradient vanishing for unsampled items'—a distinction that guides targeted fixes rather than ad-hoc regularization. The practical impact is that a single pseudo-negative outperforms S-DPO's multiple real negatives at lower computational cost, offering a drop-in improvement for existing LLM4Rec pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal item representations, whether the DPO-specific input-dependent anchor (which only provides a local guarantee) can be made globally bounded, and whether the theory generalizes beyond Bradley-Terry to other preference models with different sensitivity profiles.
