## Problem

Self-distillation, where a model repeatedly trains on pseudo-labels from its own previous iterate, is known to improve generalization in early iterations yet can degrade performance through model collapse in later ones. Despite this well-documented tension, no precise statistical characterization existed for the iterative mechanism: what exactly causes the U-shaped risk curve, and at what point does denoising give way to signal erosion? This paper provides the first rigorous decomposition of the generalization error dynamics for iterative self-distillation in the overparameterized regime.

## Input / Output

The procedure starts from a single noisy training set (X₀, Y₀) with Gaussian features drawn from a possibly anisotropic covariance Σ, and at each subsequent iteration draws a fresh independent feature matrix X_t of the same dimensionality to generate noiseless pseudo-labels Y_t = X_t β̂_{t-1}. The output is the full sequence of ridgeless (or ridge) estimators {β̂₀, …, β̂_K} together with a deterministic risk recursion that predicts the test error at every iteration, and an iterated GCV criterion that selects the optimal stopping time t* without requiring a held-out validation set. The critical design choice is that each iteration uses an independent feature matrix rather than reusing the original design, which is what makes the projection non-idempotent and the dynamics non-trivial when p > n.

## Method

In the proportional asymptotic regime (p, n → ∞ with p/n → ρ > 1), the paper derives deterministic recursions for the prediction risk of each iterate by characterizing the ridgeless estimator through a Gaussian sequence model and proving concentration around deterministic equivalents. The key mechanism is that each ridgeless update β̂_t = (X_t^T X_t)^+ X_t^T X_t β̂_{t-1} applies an orthogonal projection onto the row space of X_t, which acts as an iteration-dependent spectral filter: eigendirections with large covariance eigenvalues (carrying true signal) are retained with survival factor s/(s+τ) ≈ 1, while isotropic noise-like directions are attenuated at rate (1+τ)^{-1} per step. This produces a clean decomposition of risk into a systematic error term (signal forgetting, increasing in t) and a stochastic error term (denoising, decreasing in t), whose rate mismatch under anisotropic covariances generates the U-shaped curve. The paper further proves uniform consistency of an iterated GCV estimator that depends only on the initial dataset and the cumulative projection operator, enabling fully data-driven early stopping.

## Results

The paper's headline result is a deterministic recursion showing that iterative self-distillation in overparameterized linear regression produces a U-shaped test-risk curve: stochastic error decays at rate (1+τ)^{-t} while systematic (signal-forgetting) error grows at the slower rate governed by (s/(s+τ))^{t+1}. In the spiked covariance model (s=25, ρ=2), early iterations reduce generalization error substantially before signal forgetting dominates (Figure 1b). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50, moderate label noise (η=0.4) yields a clear U-shape: test error drops from 47.7% to 43.6% at t=2, then rises to 45.0% by t=4, while high noise (η=0.8) produces monotonic improvement. The iterated GCV (iGCV) estimator tracks the empirical U-shaped risk curve and identifies the optimal stopping point without a validation set (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is optimally tuned ridge regression (minimum over λ). In the spiked covariance model, Figure 3b shows that as spike strength s increases, iterative self-distillation's minimum risk over t falls below the best achievable ridge risk, because self-distillation acts as a direction-adaptive spectral filter that preserves the dominant signal direction while contracting the isotropic subspace, whereas ridge applies uniform shrinkage. In the isotropic limit (s=1), the two methods behave similarly and the U-shape disappears, confirming that the advantage requires anisotropy. The iGCV stopping rule is validated against the empirical test risk (Figure 4), showing it captures the bottom of the U-curve across aspect ratios and iteration counts. The paper also contrasts with Garg et al. (2025), who study model-collapse prevention under reused covariates with real-label mixing; in that setting the projection is idempotent and the denoising-forgetting trade-off does not arise, whereas the fresh-covariate setting studied here produces the non-trivial spectral filtering dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional asymptotic framework for ridgeless regression (Han & Xu, 2026; Hastie et al., 2022) to the multi-step iterative self-distillation setting, building on the single-step distributional characterization of Ildiz et al. (2025) for knowledge distillation and the uniform GCV consistency results of Patil et al. (2021). It deliberately departs from Garg et al. (2025), who study model-collapse prevention under reused covariates with real-label mixing (where the projection is idempotent), by analyzing pure iterative self-distillation with fresh independent covariates and no real-label anchoring, where each step applies a new data-dependent projection. The paper also positions itself within the broader self-training and weak-to-strong generalization literature (Burns et al., 2024; Shumailov et al., 2024), providing the first precise statistical characterization of the iterative teacher-student transfer mechanism in the overparameterized regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous decomposition of self-distillation risk into a monotonically increasing signal-forgetting term and a monotonically decreasing denoising term, explaining the empirically observed U-shaped risk curve and yielding a principled early-stopping criterion (iterated GCV) that requires no validation set. The spectral-filter interpretation—where eigendirections carrying strong signal are retained while weakly aligned or noise-like directions are suppressed—offers a mechanistic explanation for why self-distillation can initially outperform ridge regression in anisotropic settings. Key open questions include extending the theory to nonlinear models (where representation learning may stabilize or amplify drift), to reused or partially renewed covariates, and to filtered/reweighted pseudo-labels, all of which are common in practical self-training pipelines but remain analytically uncharacterized.
