## Problem

Existing few-shot transfer learning methods—gradient-based meta-learning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning—search continuous hypothesis spaces of neural network weights, making all known generalization bounds vacuous in the low-shot regime (n ≤ 16). No prior approach provides non-vacuous, certifiable accuracy guarantees for adapting large foundation models to new tasks with only a handful of examples, despite such guarantees being essential for high-stakes deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small support set (1–16 labeled examples per class for 5-way vision tasks, or per-user data for LLM personalization), a frozen pre-trained foundation model (CLIP or Flan-T5), and a pre-trained weight-space diffusion model over upstream task adapters. It outputs a single selected PEFT adapter (LoRA-XS with 2,592 parameters for LLMs, or a 2-token CoOp prompt with 1,024 parameters for vision), a PAC-Bayes risk certificate computed on that adapter, and task predictions. Unusually, the 'learning' step is gradient-free discrete selection rather than continuous optimization, and the certificate is always evaluated on the finally deployed adapter regardless of which search heuristic was used.

## Method

STEEL (Sample ThEn Evaluate Learner) replaces continuous gradient-based adaptation with discrete selection from a finite hypothesis space. In the upstream phase, PEFT adapters are trained independently on N source tasks, and a diffusion model is then trained over these adapter parameters to learn the distribution of task solutions (the 'solution manifold'). In the downstream phase, |Θ| adapters are sampled from the diffusion model, each is scored by its empirical risk on the support set, and the best-scoring adapter is selected—no gradient steps are taken on downstream data. The key theoretical contribution is that this finite hypothesis space admits a PAC-Bayes bound with complexity term C·√(log|Θ|/(2n)), which remains non-vacuous even at n=8–16, whereas continuous-parameter methods yield vacuous bounds. The diffusion model provides two advantages over a raw model zoo: compactness (one generative model vs. N stored adapters) and interpolation (generating novel adapters between upstream solutions). Two search strategies are supported—exhaustive evaluation (optimal but O(|Θ|)) and hierarchical clustering-based search (O(log|Θ|))—and the certificate remains valid under either, with suboptimal selection only loosening but never invalidating the bound.

## Results

On 16-shot 5-way vision benchmarks, STEEL achieves 97.5–100% non-vacuous certificate ratios across CUBirds, Describable Textures, FGVC-Aircraft, and Flowers-101, with median bounds of 0.30–0.61, while SGD and BBPT yield 0% non-vacuous episodes (all bounds >2.5). On the LaMP LLM personalization benchmark, STEEL attains 65.12% (LaMP-2), 15.48% (LaMP-3), and 99.16% (LaMP-5) non-vacuous episodes with median bounds of 0.80, 0.93, and 0.81 respectively, versus 0% for SGD and MeZO. Empirical accuracy is competitive: STEEL reaches 88.40% on CUBirds (vs. 90.32% SGD) and 63.74% on LaMP-2 (vs. 63.25% SGD), paying a small accuracy cost for dramatically stronger certificates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEEL decisively outperforms all gradient-based and zeroth-order baselines (SGD, MeZO, BBPT) on certifiability: these methods achieve 0% non-vacuous episodes across every benchmark, while STEEL reaches 65–100% non-vacuous. Against LoRA-Hub (Huang et al., 2024), STEEL wins on non-vacuous ratio (e.g., 97.5% vs. 35% on FGVC-Aircrafts) and median bound (0.36 vs. 0.52 on CUBirds), though LoRA-Hub edges out STEEL on raw accuracy for CUBirds (89.45% vs. 88.40%). Meta-PB (Zakerinia et al., 2024) achieves some non-vacuous episodes (97.5% on CUBirds) but with substantially looser bounds (median 0.55 vs. 0.36) and fails entirely on FGVC-Aircrafts (0% non-vacuous). In the head-to-head OOD comparison under CUB-C corruption (Appendix F.3), MAML (Finn et al., 2017) provides no certificate at any severity level (median bound 2.6–2.7) and its accuracy advantage over STEEL is ≤1 pp and reverses at severe corruption. Zero-shot CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), when certified via a Langford-style bound, yields looser median bounds than STEEL on 3 of 4 vision datasets (e.g., 0.43 vs. 0.40 on Flowers, 0.71 vs. 0.61 on FGVC-Aircrafts), confirming that STEEL's learned hypothesis space provides both tighter guarantees and stronger empirical performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEEL sits at the intersection of three research threads: weight-space generative modeling (NNDiffusion, ProtoDiff, MetaDiff), which learns distributions over neural network parameters but provides no generalization guarantees; PAC-Bayes certification for deep models (Dziugaite & Roy, Lotfi et al.), which yields non-vacuous bounds only in the large-data regime; and meta-learning / model-zoo transfer (MAML, LoRA-Hub), which adapts to new tasks but searches continuous hypothesis spaces that render bounds vacuous at low shot counts. The key departure is a paradigm shift from continuous gradient-based adaptation to discrete selection over a finite hypothesis space generated by a diffusion model, making classical finite-hypothesis PAC-Bayes bounds (Eq. 3) applicable for the first time in few-shot deep learning with large foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical demonstration of non-vacuous generalization certificates for few-shot learning with large LLM and vision architectures, providing a principled deploy/no-deploy signal that gradient-based methods structurally cannot offer. The accuracy cost is modest (1–2 pp on most benchmarks), but the certifiability gap is dramatic (0% vs. 65–100% non-vacuous episodes). Open questions include tightening the certificate via covering numbers or diffusion-likelihood priors, closing the remaining accuracy gap through stronger weight-space architectures or mixture hypothesis classes, and extending to cross-domain transfer where the upstream-downstream distribution shift is more severe. The framework also raises privacy concerns (diffusion model memorization of user-specific adapters) that need addressing before real-world deployment.
