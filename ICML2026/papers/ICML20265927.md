## Problem

Planning-driven model-based RL methods (TD-MPC2, BOOM, BMPC) rely on zero-order optimizers like MPPI that treat the differentiable world model as a black box, discarding the reliable return gradients inherent in physics-governed dynamics. This causes slow convergence and oscillatory updates on sharp, rugged return landscapes, fundamentally limiting achievable performance in high-dimensional continuous control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional state vectors (e.g., 67-dim humanoid, 223-dim quadruped dog, 151-dim Unitree H1hand) and outputs a single continuous action (24, 38, or 61 dimensions respectively) executed in a receding-horizon manner. Internally, observations are encoded into a 512-dim latent space where a 3-step planning horizon is rolled out; the score network S(z,a) provides gradient estimates in this latent space, replacing expensive BPTT unrolling during the 5-iteration Langevin refinement loop.

## Method

The paper proposes LaRO, which frames online planning as sampling from a Boltzmann-form planning policy π*(a|s) ∝ exp(J_plan/α) and applies Langevin dynamics (gradient ascent + Gaussian noise) to refine the immediate action a_t while subsequent actions follow the learned policy. To avoid costly BPTT during planning, a score-augmented world model jointly learns dynamics f, reward R, value Q, and an amortized score network S(z,a) that distills the analytical return gradient ∇_a J_plan via a score-matching loss, enabling 1.79 ms per Langevin step versus 12.72 ms for BPTT. Maximum Look-Ahead Planning (MLAP) runs MPPI and LaRO in parallel and executes whichever candidate achieves the higher look-ahead return, synergizing global sampling coverage with gradient-guided local precision. Two theoretical results are provided: a Wasserstein-2 convergence bound under bounded score error (Theorem 4.1), and a proof that model-based return gradients achieve strictly lower worst-case error than direct value-function gradients (Theorem 4.2), justifying the model-based Langevin approach over model-free alternatives.

## Results

On the 7-task DMC-Hard suite, BOOM-L achieves an average Total Average Return (TAR) of 847.2, surpassing the prior SOTA BOOM (802.7) by +5.5% and BMPC (704.4) by +20.3%. On the more challenging 7-task Humanoid Bench, BOOM-L reaches an average TAR of 773.3, exceeding BOOM (634.2) by +21.9% and DreamerV3 at 10M steps (564.3) by +37.0%. The largest single-task gain is on H1hand-run, where BOOM-L scores 823.0 versus BOOM's 329.3 (+149.9%), demonstrating the critical advantage of gradient-guided planning on rugged landscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOOM-L outperforms all five baselines across all 14 tasks. Against the direct predecessor BOOM (MPPI-only planner), BOOM-L improves DMC-Hard average TAR by +5.5% (847.2 vs. 802.7) and H-Bench average by +21.9% (773.3 vs. 634.2), with the largest gap on H1hand-run (+149.9%, 823.0 vs. 329.3) where MPPI's zero-order sampling gets trapped in local optima. Against BMPC, BOOM-L leads by +20.3% on DMC-Hard and +75.9% on H-Bench. Against TD-MPC2, the margins are even larger: +68.1% on DMC-Hard and +303.5% on H-Bench. Model-free SAC and imagination-driven DreamerV3 are far behind, with DreamerV3 at 10M steps still trailing BOOM-L by 37.0% on H-Bench and 69.0% on DMC-Hard, confirming that planning-driven methods with gradient-informed refinement substantially outperform both paradigms on high-dimensional locomotion within 1M steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the planning-driven MBRL lineage (TD-MPC → TD-MPC2 → BMPC → BOOM) by replacing the MPPI-only zero-order planner with a hybrid that incorporates first-order gradient information. Architecturally, it adopts the TD-MPC2 latent-space formulation but augments it with an amortized score network that distills BPTT return gradients into a single forward pass. Conceptually, it departs from prior model-free Langevin RL work (Psenka et al., 2024; Ishfaq et al., 2025) by computing gradients through an H-step world-model rollout rather than through the value function, and from pure gradient-based planners by retaining stochastic noise for multimodal exploration. The MLAP framework is a simple return-based selection mechanism that runs MPPI and LaRO in parallel, positioning the contribution as a targeted fix to MPPI's known weakness on sharp landscapes rather than a new planning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: planning-driven MBRL discards reliable gradients from differentiable, physics-governed world models, and the resulting performance ceiling is especially visible on high-dimensional locomotion tasks. The +149.9% improvement on H1hand-run and consistent gains across 35 tasks demonstrate that this gap is practically significant. Open questions include how the score network behaves under model misspecification in sparsely visited state regions (the authors acknowledge this limitation), whether epistemic uncertainty can be integrated to gate gradient trust, and whether adaptive triggering (AHP) can close the remaining efficiency gap without sacrificing peak performance. The score-augmented world model is a reusable component that could benefit other differentiable-planning frameworks beyond BOOM.
