## Problem

Existing second-order bilevel optimization methods (AID, ITD) achieve iteration complexities identical to first-order methods (O(ε^{-2}) or O(ε^{-1.75})), making the practical advantage of accessing second-order oracles unclear. This paper asks whether a second-order method can achieve a strictly improved iteration complexity that demonstrates the benefit of Hessian information in bilevel optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes gradient and Hessian oracles of both the upper-level function f(x,y) and lower-level function g(x,y), along with an initial point x₀. It produces an (ε, O(√ε))-second-order stationary point of the hyper-objective φ(x) = f(x, y*(x)). A key design choice is that the Hessian of the proxy function L_λ*(x) is constructed from second-order information of f and g only (no third-order derivatives of g are needed), and the auxiliary variables y and w are obtained via accelerated gradient descent on strongly convex subproblems.

## Method

FSBA applies cubic-regularized Newton (CRN) iterations to the penalty-based proxy L_λ*(x) rather than φ(x) directly, avoiding the need for third-order derivatives of g. At each iteration, it estimates ∇L_λ*(x) and ∇²L_λ*(x) by introducing auxiliary variables y ≈ y_λ*(x) and w ≈ y*(x) solved via AGD, then constructs the hypergradient and hyperHessian from second-order oracles of f and g. This yields Õ(ε^{-1.5}) oracle complexity, matching the nonconvex lower bound. To reduce the per-iteration cost of Hessian computation, LFSBA reuses the approximate Hessian at snapshot points for m consecutive iterations, improving computational complexity by a factor of √d/κ^{0.25} over FSBA. An inexact variant (IFSBA) replaces explicit Hessian inverses with Chebyshev polynomial approximations and solves the cubic subproblem via gradient-type methods, requiring only Hessian-vector products.

## Results

The FSBA method achieves an iteration complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^3 \epsilon^{-1.5})$ for finding an $(\epsilon, \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\epsilon}))$-SOSP of the hyper-objective, improving upon the $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{3.75}\epsilon^{-1.75})$ rate of the best existing first-order method (RAF2BA). The lazy variant LFSBA reduces the computational complexity by a factor of $d^{0.5}/\kappa^{0.25}$ over FSBA by reusing the Hessian every $m = \Theta(1 + d/\sqrt{\kappa})$ iterations. In experiments on data hypercleaning, hyperparameter tuning, and few-shot meta-learning, FSBA/LFSBA/IFSBA consistently converge faster than all first-order baselines (ITD, AID, F²BA) and second-order baselines (PZOBO, qNBO) within the same running time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ITD (Ji et al., 2021) and AID with conjugate gradient (Maclaurin et al., 2015), FSBA and LFSBA converge significantly faster in both data hypercleaning (breast-cancer, australian) and hyperparameter tuning (20 Newsgroups) experiments, reaching lower objective values within the same number of iterations or running time. Compared to the near-optimal fully first-order F²BA (Chen et al., 2025b), which achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$ complexity, FSBA's $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-1.5})$ rate translates to visibly faster convergence in all three experimental settings. In few-shot meta-learning on miniImageNet and FC100, IFSBA achieves higher test accuracy than both PZOBO (Sow et al., 2022b) and qNBO (Fang et al., 2025) within the same running time, confirming the practical benefit of the second-order oracle. The LMCN variant also improves upon the MCN method (Luo et al., 2022) for nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax problems by a factor of $d^{0.5}/\kappa^{0.25}$ in computational complexity via the lazy Hessian strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the penalty-based reformulation of bilevel optimization (Kwon et al., 2023b; Chen et al., 2025b) and the cubic-regularized Newton (CRN) framework for nonconvex optimization (Nesterov & Polyak, 2006; Luo et al., 2022). Prior second-order bilevel methods (AID, ITD) only exploited Hessian-vector products to estimate the hypergradient and then applied first-order descent, yielding iteration complexities matching first-order methods (O(ε^{-2}) or O(ε^{-1.75})). The key departure here is using the full hyperHessian—computed via the penalty reformulation to avoid third-order derivatives of g—to perform genuine CRN steps, combined with the lazy Hessian mechanism (Doikov et al., 2023) to amortize second-order oracle cost. The LMCN extension also generalizes the MCN method (Luo et al., 2022) for nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax problems with improved computational complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that second-order oracles provide a genuine iteration-complexity acceleration in bilevel optimization, resolving an open question raised by the near-equivalence of first- and second-order methods in prior work. The O(ε^{-1.5}) rate matches the Carmon et al. (2020) lower bound for nonconvex optimization, confirming near-optimality. The lazy Hessian variant (LFSBA) reduces computational cost by a factor of √d/κ^{0.25}, making second-order bilevel methods more practical for high-dimensional problems. Open questions include extending to stochastic and distributed settings, removing the strong convexity requirement on the lower level, and accelerating the inner solvers with higher-order methods to reduce κ-dependence.
