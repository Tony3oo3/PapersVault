## Problem

Despite the widespread use of actor-critic (AC) methods in reinforcement learning, it remained theoretically unclear whether the critic-as-baseline mechanism provides weak variance reduction (a constant factor) or strong variance reduction (variance vanishing at the optimum, enabling linear convergence). This paper resolves the question by proving that entropy-regularized AC with an exact critic achieves strong variance reduction, and characterizes how the critic's estimation error governs convergence when the critic is learned online.

## Input / Output

The method operates on finite, discounted MDPs with tabular state space S and action space A, where the agent observes a state s and outputs a softmax action distribution π(a|s) parameterized by logits θ ∈ R^{|S||A|}. The critic is a tabular Q-function updated via temporal-difference (TD) errors sampled from the occupancy-weighted state-action distribution, while the actor receives a single stochastic gradient sample per outer iteration. A projection operator T_τ constrains the policy to maintain π(a|s) ≥ τ_λ for all (s,a), preventing the policy from becoming too deterministic and ensuring uniform exploration.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a variance decomposition showing that when the critic equals the true regularized Q-function, the stochastic actor gradient's variance is upper-bounded by a constant times the squared norm of the deterministic gradient, so the variance vanishes exactly at the optimum (strong variance reduction). This yields O(log(1/ε)) sample complexity, matching deterministic policy gradient. When the critic is inexact, the actor's own sampling variance is fully absorbed into the descent term, and convergence is governed solely by the critic's bias (from insufficient TD iterations H) and variance (from stochastic TD noise). The algorithm alternates H critic TD updates per single actor gradient step, with a carefully designed projection operator that is a true L1-projection onto the set of policies with minimum probability τ_λ, ensuring the Lojasiewicz constant remains uniformly bounded. The resulting sample complexity is O~(1/ε) for the inexact-critic case, improving over prior O~(1/ε^3) and O~(1/ε^5) bounds for AC.

## Results

The paper establishes that entropy-regularized Actor-Critic (Ent-AC) with an exact critic achieves O(log(1/ε)) sample complexity, matching deterministic policy gradient and proving AC is a strong variance-reduction method (variance vanishes at the optimum). With an inexact critic learned online, Ent-AC achieves Õ(1/ε) sample complexity, requiring H = Õ(log(1/ε)) critic TD updates per actor step. Empirically, increasing the number of critic updates H from 8 to 64 monotonically closes the gap to the exact-critic oracle across all Gridworld (2×2 to 4×4) and Synthetic (|S| up to 16) environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ent-AC dominates all four baselines on sample complexity in the entropy-regularized setting. Against the closest entropy-regularized competitor, Cayci et al. (2024), Ent-AC improves the rate from Õ(1/ε⁵) to Õ(1/ε) — a four-order-of-magnitude gap — while also being the first to establish strong variance reduction. Against the unregularized baselines (Olshevsky & Gharesifard 2023 at Õ(1/ε²), Kumar et al. 2024 and Gaur et al. 2024 at Õ(1/ε³)), Ent-AC achieves a strictly better rate and additionally provides the strong variance-reduction guarantee that none of these methods establish. The key structural difference is that Ent-AC's actor sampling variance is fully absorbed into the descent term, so the residual error is governed entirely by the critic's bias and variance rather than by the actor's own stochasticity. Empirically, the paper confirms that the critic's accuracy (controlled by H) is the dominant factor: at H=8 the learned policy shows a significant gap to the exact-critic oracle, while at H=64 the gap is nearly closed across all eight environment configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of actor-critic methods (Konda & Tsitsiklis 1999; Kumar et al. 2024; Gaur et al. 2024) by leveraging the Lojasiewicz inequality framework from entropy-regularized policy gradient theory (Mei et al. 2020b; Xiao 2022) and the projection-operator technique from Zhang et al. 2021a and Labbi et al. 2026b. The key departure is showing that the critic-as-baseline mechanism in AC constitutes a *strong* variance-reduction method (analogous to SVRG/SAGA in optimization), rather than merely a multiplicative variance reduction as assumed in prior AC analyses. The authors also modify the projection operator from prior policy-gradient work to be less aggressive in policy shifts, which is critical for maintaining critic warm-starts across actor updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that AC with an exact critic achieves O(log(1/ε)) sample complexity—matching deterministic gradient methods—is the first proof that AC enjoys strong variance reduction, fundamentally changing the theoretical understanding of why the critic works. The more practical O(1/ε) rate with an inexact critic (versus O(1/ε³) in prior work) provides a clear prescription: invest computational budget in the critic. However, the analysis is restricted to finite-state tabular MDPs with entropy regularization and softmax policies, leaving open whether these properties extend to function approximation, continuous action spaces, or the unregularized setting. The empirical validation on small Gridworld and synthetic MDPs confirms the monotonic benefit of more critic updates but does not test scalability to deep RL regimes.
