## Problem

Existing medical foundation models are trained and evaluated on hospital-grade data and target professional clinical settings, leaving home-based health management largely unexplored. Home care introduces three distinct challenges: heterogeneous, lower-quality data from consumer-grade devices without standardized large-scale datasets; the need for adaptation to highly variable individual health baselines rather than population-level models; and a broad spectrum of tasks (from daily monitoring to chronic disease risk) lacking a unified evaluation benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262850/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes heterogeneous multimodal inputs unified into a shared semantic space: RGB images (food photos, skin lesions, oral cavity, retinal fundus), physiological signals converted to images (Lead-I ECG segments of 10 s, triaxial sleep accelerometry of 30 s), and free-text symptom descriptions. Outputs span 11 tasks in both open-QA (free-form medical advice, drug recommendations, diagnosis text) and closed-QA (multiple-choice classification for disease risk, sleep quality, etc.) formats, with the drug recommendation task restricted to open-QA due to its inherently multi-label nature.

## Method

DIYHealthGPT builds on Phi-3-mini (3.8B) with a CLIP-L/14 vision encoder projected via a two-layer MLP into the language embedding space. The core novelty is H²LoRA, a PEFT mechanism that combines (i) a Shared Low-Rank Expert Mixture—where a shared low-rank matrix A^t anchors K expert matrices B_k^t routed by a task-embedding-conditioned MoE gate for cross-task knowledge sharing with task-level specialization—and (ii) Hyper LoRA Adaptation, where dedicated hypernetworks H_A and H_B generate instance-dependent offsets ΔA^t and ΔB_k^t conditioned on the unified input embedding Z, enabling per-patient personalization beyond coarse task-level adaptation. A global soft-MoE inter-task router then fuses outputs from all N task-specific H²LoRA blocks. Training follows a four-stage pipeline: cross-modal alignment (PubMedVision + LLaVA-558k), medical domain adaptation (10% sampled DIYHealth-900K), task-specific expert training with hard-MoE, and cross-task knowledge transfer with the soft-MoE router.

## Results

On DIYHealthBench (11 home care tasks), DIYHealthGPT-3.8B achieves an average ACC of 86.80 and MCC of 82.36 under closed-QA, and an average F1-Bio of 87.34 and ROUGE-L of 52.11 under open-QA. This represents a 22.7% average ACC improvement over the best general-domain baseline (InternVL3-8B, 68.79 ACC) and a 16.7% F1-Bio improvement over the best medical-domain baseline (Lingshu-7B, 73.77 F1-Bio). Notably, DIYHealthGPT matches or exceeds InternVL3-8B on the MAG task (59.73 ACC) while dominating on all other tasks, with particularly large gains on Heart (83.10 vs. 37.15 ACC) and Sleep (51.90 vs. 21.54 ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIYHealthGPT-3.8B outperforms all 21 baselines on average across both closed-QA and open-QA settings. Against the strongest general-domain model InternVL3-8B, it gains +18.01 ACC (86.80 vs. 68.79) and +17.34 F1-Bio (87.34 vs. 70.00); against the strongest medical-domain model Lingshu-7B, it gains +16.06 ACC and +13.57 F1-Bio. The margin is most dramatic on Heart (83.10 vs. 48.94 ACC over MedGemma) and Sleep (51.90 vs. 46.73 ACC over Med-R1), while on MAG the model ties InternVL3-8B at 59.73 ACC, reflecting that this task relies more on broad medical knowledge than home-care-specific signals. Compared to same-size medical models (HealthGPT-3.8B, Med-R1-2B), DIYHealthGPT leads by 18.3 and 19.0 ACC points respectively, and the fine-tuning ablation (Table 4) confirms that gains are not solely attributable to the dataset: even after fine-tuning Gemma 3-4B and LLaVA-Med v1.5-7B on the same DIYHealth-900K data, DIYHealthGPT still leads by 5.84 ACC and 1.06 F1-Bio. The H²LoRA ablation (Table 3) shows that removing either the Expert Mixture or Hyper LoRA component degrades performance by 1.3–1.4 ACC, confirming both mechanisms contribute complementary gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two recent PEFT lines—MoELoRA's expert-mixture routing and HyperLoRA's hypernetwork-generated adapters—into a unified H²LoRA mechanism that adds an inter-task soft-MoE fusion layer on top. Architecturally, it extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by addressing the tension between cross-task knowledge sharing and instance-level personalization that neither MoELoRA nor HyperLoRA resolves alone. On the application side, it departs from the clinical-centric medical foundation model lineage (LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo, HealthGPT, Lingshu) by explicitly targeting home-based DIY care with consumer-grade inputs, self-reported data, and personalized health baselines, a setting the authors argue has been 'largely unexplored to date.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIYHealthBench is the first unified benchmark for home care AI, filling a genuine gap: prior medical LVLMs are evaluated on hospital-grade imaging and expert-annotated data, leaving no standardized way to assess models on the heterogeneous, lower-fidelity signals users actually produce at home. The H²LoRA mechanism offers a practical recipe for multi-task + personalized PEFT that could transfer beyond health. Key open questions include whether the largely LLM-synthesized QA pairs in DIYHealth-900K truly capture the noise and variability of real home-collected data, whether the personalization mechanism generalizes beyond the 11 curated tasks, and whether the 3.8B model's advantage over larger baselines persists on genuinely out-of-distribution home inputs rather than the tailored benchmark.
