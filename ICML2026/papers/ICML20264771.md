## Problem

Agentic RL for web search is prohibitively expensive due to long horizons and costly environment interactions, and group-based optimization (GRPO) discards entire rollout groups whenever reward variance is zero, wasting all trajectories from those groups. Existing experience-based agent methods treat repositories as static artifacts or decouple experience evolution from the RL loop, leaving context-space and parameter-space optimization as disjoint stages rather than co-evolving signals.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a user query and sequentially observes web environment states (search results, crawled page content) within a ReAct-style loop, selecting from four actions: SEARCH, CRAWL, RETRIEVE, and ANSWER. Beyond the final answer, the system produces an evolving experience repository of structured entries (title, description, content, dual semantic embeddings, binary success label) that persists across training iterations. The RETRIEVE action allows the agent to autonomously fetch top-k relevant experiences as in-context strategic guidance, with retrieval triggered emergently at roughly 4–5% of steps rather than being forced at every turn.

## Method

EVOLVING ROLLOUTS augments standard GRPO parameter-space optimization with a complementary context-space channel that evolves a structured experience repository in lockstep with the policy. At each training step, rollout groups are compressed into step-level intent summaries and passed through a frozen LLM extractor that performs contrastive analysis across parallel attempts, distilling transferable strategies from all-success groups, anti-patterns from all-failure groups, and comparative lessons from mixed groups—ensuring zero-variance groups that GRPO would discard still contribute learning signal. New experiences are consolidated into the repository via embedding-similarity merging (threshold θ ∈ [0.85, 0.95]) to prevent unbounded growth while retaining policy-aligned, on-distribution knowledge. The pipeline proceeds in three stages: cold-start experience construction from open-source trajectory datasets, experience-conditioned SFT on self-generated trajectories, and GRPO with simultaneous policy and repository updates, where the agent's retrieval decisions are learned end-to-end without auxiliary objectives.

## Results

On Pass@1, EVOLVING ROLLOUTS with Qwen3-8B achieves 45.6% on GAIA, 44.0% on xBench-DeepSearch, and 12.0% on HLE, yielding a 33.9% average that surpasses the much larger Qwen3-30B-A3B reference model (32.2% average). The 4B variant reaches 44.7% on GAIA and 13.0% on HLE, matching the 30B reference on both benchmarks. Across all three experience-augmented baselines (Training-Free GRPO, ReasoningBank, ExpSeek) under the same Qwen3-8B backbone, EVOLVING ROLLOUTS leads on both GAIA and xBench by 8.7–16.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three experience-augmented web-agent baselines under the same Qwen3-8B backbone, EVOLVING ROLLOUTS (8B) wins decisively: it outperforms ExpSeek by 8.7 points on GAIA (45.6 vs. 36.89) and 6.8 points on xBench (44.0 vs. 37.20), ReasoningBank by 13.6 and 16.0 points respectively, and Training-Free GRPO by 16.3 and 18.0 points. Against the larger Qwen3-30B-A3B reference model, the 8B EVOLVING ROLLOUTS agent surpasses it on average (33.9 vs. 32.2), on GAIA (45.6 vs. 44.7), and on xBench (44.0 vs. 39.0), while trailing slightly on HLE (12.0 vs. 13.0). The 4B variant matches the 30B reference exactly on GAIA (44.7) and HLE (13.0) but falls short on xBench (36.0 vs. 39.0). Notably, the 30B model also serves as the fixed experience extractor, so the 8B policy exceeding it demonstrates that co-evolution of policy and experience yields gains beyond what the extractor alone can provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by adding a complementary context-space optimization channel that co-evolves a structured experience repository alongside parameter updates, addressing the well-known zero-variance gradient vanishing problem in group-based RL. It departs from prior experience-driven agent evolution work (ReasoningBank, FLEX, AgentKB, MUSE) by tightly coupling experience extraction and reuse to the RL training loop rather than treating the repository as a static artifact or a test-time mechanism. The contrastive extraction design—distilling both success strategies and failure anti-patterns from parallel rollout groups—builds on the broader trend of treating failures as reusable learning signals, but is the first to make this process an integral part of the policy optimization iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in web-agent RL where zero-variance rollout groups (all-success or all-failure) waste expensive environment interactions, and the co-evolution mechanism recovers signal from these otherwise discarded trajectories. The finding that a smaller policy (8B) can match or exceed a much larger reference model (30B-A3B) through experience co-evolution suggests a path toward more sample-efficient agent training, though the evidence is limited to three benchmarks and two model scales. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond web search to other long-horizon agentic domains, how the experience repository quality and retrieval precision scale with training duration, and whether the modest retrieval rate (~4-5%) represents an optimal operating point or a limitation of the current GRPO-driven trigger mechanism.
