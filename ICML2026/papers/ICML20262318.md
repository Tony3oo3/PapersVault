## Problem

LVLMs exhibit persistent language bias, producing answers from textual priors rather than visual evidence, yet prior mitigation strategies (contrastive decoding, attention boosting, data curation) lack a quantitative measure of how much each individual training sample or token actually depends on the image. This paper asks whether visual dependency can be directly measured per sample and per token, and whether this signal can guide selective training to concentrate optimization on genuinely visual evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262318/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multimodal instruction-tuning triplets (image, question, answer) and an aligned LVLM from the pre-training stage (adapter trained; vision encoder and LLM frozen) as input. VIG is computed via two forward passes per sample—one with the original image and one with a Gaussian-blurred image to simulate visual absence—yielding a scalar score per sample and a per-token loss-difference vector. The output is a selectively trained LVLM in which only high-VIG samples (top p%) and high-VIG tokens (above threshold τ_p) contribute to the loss, reducing active supervision tokens by 34–79% depending on model scale.

## Method

VIG is defined as the log-ratio of perplexities on the same answer with and without visual conditioning, equivalently the difference in cross-entropy loss, which decomposes into a sum of per-token loss differences, making it simultaneously a sample-level and token-level measure of visual contribution. For selective training, all multimodal samples are ranked by VIG and the top p% (p=70 in main experiments) are retained; within these samples, only tokens whose individual VIG exceeds the same threshold τ_p contribute to the gradient update, while unselected tokens are masked from the loss rather than removed from the input. The approach requires no architectural modification or inference-time overhead—VIG scoring is a one-time, forward-only, fully parallelizable procedure—and reusing a single threshold at both sample and token levels avoids introducing extra hyperparameters. Empirically, VIG aligns with known benchmark-level modality dependencies (positive for COCO, negative for GQA/SQA) and highlights visually grounded tokens (colors, spatial relations, attributes) over function words, while preserving long-tail concept coverage in the retained data.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B, VIG-guided selective training (using only 38.45M active tokens, a 34% reduction) improves over the vanilla baseline on every benchmark: LLaVA-W 59.02→61.22, MMVet 28.62→32.71, MMBench 65.46→66.33, CHAIR C_S 52.93→47.00, and MMHal Hall. 71.25→62.78. The effect is even more pronounced at scale: LLaVA-1.5 13B trained on only 12.14M tokens (79% reduction) still improves across all benchmarks, and Open-Qwen2VL 2B (41% token reduction) consistently gains on both vision understanding and hallucination metrics. VIG training also preserves text-only capabilities, with GSM8K, MMLU, HellaSwag, and TruthfulQA scores remaining within ±0.5 of the vanilla model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIG training is the only method that improves over the vanilla LLaVA-1.5 7B baseline on all eight reported metrics simultaneously, whereas every baseline exhibits trade-offs. Against VCD (contrastive decoding), VIG wins on LLaVA-W (+0.67), MMVet (+5.70), MMBench (+1.99), CHAIR C_S (−2.81), and MMHal Hall. (−13.23), but trails on DocVQA (22.51 vs 22.98) and POPE F1 (85.93 vs 86.53). Against PAI (attention boosting), VIG dominates on vision understanding (LLaVA-W +3.68, MMVet +4.72, DocVQA +0.53) and MMHal Hall. (−9.52), while PAI achieves a lower CHAIR C_S (35.46 vs 47.00). Against VAR (attention redistribution), VIG wins on MMVet (+1.73), CHAIR C_S (−3.09), and MMHal Score (+0.09), but VAR edges ahead on DocVQA (23.11 vs 22.51) and POPE F1 (86.10 vs 85.93). Against LACING (dual-attention training), VIG wins on LLaVA-W (+0.13), DocVQA (+1.06), POPE F1 (+0.35), and MMHal Hall. (−1.76), while LACING leads on MMVet (34.15 vs 32.71) and CHAIR C_S (30.85 vs 47.00). Notably, VIG training is orthogonal to all four baselines: combining VIG with VCD, PAI, VAR, or LACING yields additive gains, with VIG+LACING achieving the best MMVet (37.01) and MMBench (67.89) scores overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the selective modeling principle from Lin et al. (2024), which showed that not all tokens are equally useful for LLM pretraining, to the multimodal instruction-tuning setting by introducing Visual Information Gain (VIG) as a principled, perplexity-based criterion for sample- and token-level selection. It departs from prior language-bias mitigation work (contrastive decoding, attention boosting, dual-attention architectures) by operating purely at the data level with a model-agnostic metric, and borrows the Gaussian-blur visual-absence simulation from Xing et al. (2025) to compute the text-only perplexity baseline. Unlike data-curation efforts that filter by model-generated quality scores or heuristic rules, VIG provides a direct, decomposable measure of how much each token's prediction uncertainty is reduced by the image, grounding the selection in information-theoretic terms (cross-entropy reduction / KL divergence).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIG offers a practical, one-time scoring procedure that can be reused across training runs and model variants, making it a potentially widely adoptable tool for improving LVLM data efficiency without architectural changes or inference overhead. The finding that training on 34–79% fewer tokens consistently improves both vision understanding and hallucination metrics across four model families is practically significant for practitioners seeking to reduce training cost. Key limitations include the forward-pass overhead of VIG computation (though parallelizable and one-time), and the empirical validation is concentrated on the LLaVA-1.5 and ShareGPT4V families with a fixed p=70 ratio; generalization to substantially different architectures (e.g., native multimodal pretraining) and to domains beyond standard VQA/captioning remains an open question.
