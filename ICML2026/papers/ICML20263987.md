## Problem

Equilibrium Propagation (EP) is restricted to conservative systems whose dynamics derive from a scalar energy function, enforcing symmetric interactions and excluding feedforward architectures, biological circuits, and driven physical systems with non-reciprocal forces. The earlier Vector Field (VF) generalization to non-conservative dynamics computes an incorrect gradient that degrades with increasing Jacobian asymmetry, collapsing to chance-level performance for strongly asymmetric or purely feedforward networks. This paper closes that gap by deriving two algorithms that recover the exact cost gradient for arbitrary differentiable non-conservative dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes grayscale or RGB images (MNIST/Fashion-MNIST at 28×28, CIFAR-10 at 32×32) min-max normalized to [−1,1], with one-hot class targets encoded in {−1,1}. Inference proceeds by relaxing the dynamical system to a stationary state given the input projection; learning then contrasts two nudged stationary states (with ±β cost perturbation) to produce a local parameter update. The output is both a class prediction from the stationary state and the optimized weight matrices (input-to-hidden J^in and recurrent J^dyn), with the key distinction that J^dyn need not be symmetric.

## Method

Asymmetric EP (AsymEP) preserves the original inference dynamics but augments the nudged phase with a spatially local corrective force −2A_J(x̄⁰,θ)(x−x̄⁰), where A_J is the antisymmetric part of the Jacobian at the free equilibrium. This correction makes the augmented system's Jacobian equal to the transpose of the original Jacobian, so the contrastive displacement between ±β nudged states yields the exact post-synaptic gradient term. Dyadic EP instead doubles the state space to (z, z′) and defines a bilinear energy H(z,z′) = −(z−z′)ᵀF((z+z′)/2, θ) whose saddle-point dynamics decouple into the original forward dynamics (mean m) and a difference variable d that relaxes to the error signal. Both algorithms are proven equivalent to first order, match the computational cost of standard EP, and are validated on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 where they outperform EP and VF, with Dyadic EP matching backpropagation accuracy (90.69% vs 90.66%) on a deep CNN.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a deep feedforward CNN, Dyadic EP achieves 90.69±0.14% test accuracy, statistically indistinguishable from backpropagation (90.66±0.25%, p=0.75), while AsymEP reaches 89.74±0.14%. On Fashion-MNIST with a two-hidden-layer network, AsymEP with asymmetric connectivity achieves 86.78±0.14% (CH dynamics), surpassing the backpropagation baseline of 87.37±0.29% within one standard deviation and outperforming VF by 1.6–8.5 percentage points depending on the dynamics and connectivity. On MNIST with a single hidden layer, AsymEP reaches 94.9±0.2% at high structural asymmetry (r_str=0.875) where VF collapses to chance level (8±4%), and in a purely feedforward architecture AsymEP achieves 92.7±0.5% versus VF's 64.3±2.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard EP (Scellier & Bengio, 2017), AsymEP consistently achieves higher accuracy on MNIST (Figure 1) and, remarkably, with asymmetric connectivity on Fashion-MNIST (86.78%) surpasses EP on symmetric networks (84.30%) despite training only forward weights, suggesting that relaxing symmetry constraints improves expressivity. Against the Vector Field algorithm (Scellier et al., 2018), AsymEP wins decisively across every experimental setting: on CIFAR-10, VF collapses entirely to chance level (10%) while AsymEP and Dyadic EP match backpropagation (~90%); on Fashion-MNIST, AsymEP outperforms VF by 1.6% (CH asymmetric), 8.3% (CH feedforward), 5.5% (PC asymmetric), and 7.7% (standard feedforward); on MNIST with fixed structural asymmetry, VF accuracy degrades monotonically with r_str and reaches chance (~5–8%) at r_str=1, while AsymEP maintains 93–95% accuracy throughout. In the purely feedforward MNIST experiment, VF can only train the output layer (reducing to an Extreme Learning Machine at 64.3%), whereas AsymEP's antisymmetric Jacobian correction generates effective backward connections enabling full-network training at 92.7%. Dyadic EP, the paper's variational formulation, matches backpropagation on CIFAR-10 with no statistically significant difference (p=0.75), confirming that the doubled-state-space approach recovers exact gradients in the infinitesimal-nudging limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Equilibrium Propagation (EP) from conservative (energy-derivable) systems to arbitrary non-conservative dynamics with non-reciprocal interactions. It builds directly on the Vector Field (VF) algorithm (Scellier et al., 2018), which was the first attempt to generalize EP but provably computes the wrong gradient outside the conservative regime; the paper identifies the antisymmetric Jacobian as the source of error and corrects it. The Dyadic EP formulation draws on the Bateman-Galley formalism for mapping dissipative systems onto conservative ones by doubling degrees of freedom, and constitutes an independent first-principles generalization of Dyadic Learning (Høier et al., 2023, 2024) from Hopfield networks to arbitrary force fields. The two proposed algorithms—AsymEP (local antisymmetric-Jacobian correction) and Dyadic EP (doubled-space variational energy)—are shown to be mathematically equivalent, with AsymEP being the first-order projection of Dyadic EP onto the original state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work removes the fundamental symmetry constraint that has limited EP to conservative systems, opening the algorithm to feedforward architectures, biological circuits, and a broad class of non-equilibrium physical systems (nonlinear optics, active matter, exciton-polariton condensates). The complete collapse of VF to chance level on CIFAR-10 (10%) while AsymEP and Dyadic EP match backpropagation (≈90%) underscores that prior 'generalizations' of EP were not merely suboptimal but fundamentally incorrect for non-conservative dynamics. Key open questions include: how to physically implement the local antisymmetric-Jacobian correction at the synapse level without explicit digital transposition, whether the temporal non-locality (requiring storage of the free equilibrium) can be eliminated in hardware, and whether the framework extends to stochastic or time-varying non-conservative dynamics.
