## Problem

Existing prompt-based domain incremental learning methods face a fundamental trade-off: methods like CP-Prompt achieve high accuracy on known domains but generalize poorly to unseen ones, while MoP-CLIP prioritizes unknown-domain inference at the cost of known-domain accuracy. The root cause is inter-domain overlap in feature space, which makes distance-based domain selection unreliable, and prompt overfitting that increases cross-domain confusion. No prior method simultaneously optimizes performance across both known and unknown domains without data replay.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with domain-specific visual and textual prompts. At inference, the model must be agnostic to the test sample's domain origin: it computes prediction probabilities from each of S domain-specific prompt models and fuses them via hybrid energy-distance weights to produce a final class label over U categories. The textual modality enters only through category label prompts (lowercase English strings) prepended to the CLIP text encoder, not as free-form input.

## Method

HEDP introduces two coupled mechanisms inspired by Helmholtz free energy. During training, an energy regularization loss (L_reg) constrains each domain's prompt to produce a compact, well-separated energy distribution: a boundary loss penalizes in-domain energies exceeding a threshold Θ, and a midline loss aligns the batch mean energy toward a shared reference Δ, ensuring lower energy reliably indicates higher domain-prompt alignment. At inference, a hybrid weighting scheme fuses two complementary signals per domain: an energy factor (EF) measuring the gap between each prompt model's Helmholtz energy and the minimum across all domains, and a distance factor (DF) measuring cosine distance from the sample's frozen-backbone feature to K-means cluster centers of each domain. These are combined as F(x) = EF(x)/α + DF(x)/β, softmax-normalized into domain weights, and applied to aggregate domain-specific prediction probabilities. The key insight is that the distance factor provides stable geometric prior from the frozen CLIP space while the energy factor provides prompt-conditioned statistical discriminability, and their gradients lie in approximately orthogonal subspaces, making the hybrid robust to domain shifts that would fool either signal alone.

## Results

On CDDB-Hard (no replay), HEDP achieves 93.72% Known AA with only −0.08% Average Forgetting, and 83.74% Unknown AA, surpassing CP-Prompt (93.65% / 80.4%) and MoP-CLIP (88.56% / 81.98%). On DomainNet, HEDP reaches 74.19% Known(all) AA and 67.09% Unknown AA, outperforming CP-Prompt (73.15% / 57.57%) by 1.04% and 9.52% respectively. On CORe50 (indoor→outdoor), HEDP attains 94.37% Unknown AA, a 2.57% gain over the strongest baseline ESN (91.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEDP outperforms all 16 baselines across all three benchmarks in both known and unknown domain settings. Against the strongest prompt-based competitor CP-Prompt, HEDP gains +0.07% Known AA and +3.34% Unknown AA on CDDB-Hard, +1.04% Known AA and +9.52% Unknown AA on DomainNet, and +3.70% Unknown AA on CORe50. MoP-CLIP, which prioritizes unseen-domain inference, is surpassed by HEDP on both known (93.72 vs. 88.56 on CDDB-Hard) and unknown (83.74 vs. 81.98) metrics, confirming HEDP's dual optimization. Non-prompt methods (EWC, LwF, LUCIR, iCaRL) show severe forgetting (AF as low as −42.62%) and substantially lower unknown-domain accuracy, while prompt-based methods without energy regularization (L2P, ESN) lag by 20–30% on unknown domains. The ablation (Scheme 6 vs. 1–5) confirms that both the energy regularization loss and the hybrid distance factor are necessary: removing either causes 1–8% drops, with the distance factor contributing most to unknown-domain generalization (+1.22% on CDDB-Hard Unknown).

## Relation to Prior Work

HEDP builds directly on CP-Prompt's prefix-one-tuning mechanism for domain-specific prompt optimization with a frozen CLIP backbone, while drawing conceptual inspiration from Helmholtz free energy in energy-based models (LeCun et al., 2006; Liu et al., 2020). It departs from prior prompt-based DIL methods like MoP-CLIP and ESN by introducing a training-time energy regularization loss (boundary + midline) that was absent in earlier prompt-tuning approaches, and by fusing energy-based and distance-based cues at inference rather than relying on a single selection criterion. The paper positions itself as resolving the known/unknown domain trade-off that plagues existing methods: CP-Prompt excels on known domains but underperforms on unseen ones, while MoP-CLIP does the reverse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEDP demonstrates that a physics-inspired energy regularization can make domain-specific prompt models produce reliably comparable energy scores across domains, turning energy into a practical domain-similarity metric without requiring out-of-domain data during training. The dual improvement on both known and unknown domains (e.g., 93.72% known AA and 83.74% unknown AA on CDDB-Hard) is practically valuable for deployment scenarios where the model must handle both familiar and novel distributions. However, the linear inference cost in the number of domains (O(S) forward passes) remains a scalability bottleneck for long incremental sequences, and the paper acknowledges that dynamic prompt selection and adaptive hyperparameter calibration are needed to address this. The moderate 2–3% gains over strong baselines like CP-Prompt and MoP-CLIP, while consistent, do not yet constitute a paradigm shift in how DIL is approached.
