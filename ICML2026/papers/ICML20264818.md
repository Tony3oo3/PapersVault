## Problem

Standard end-to-end training constrains only the terminal output of deep networks, leaving the intermediate latent trajectory weakly regulated. Existing deep supervision methods apply uniform hard labels at every depth, which can force premature categorical confidence in shallow layers before features have matured. The paper asks whether the shape of the latent inference trajectory matters for discriminative modeling and, if so, what depth-dependent supervision schedule is appropriate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264818/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR) to 224×224 (ImageNet, Food-101, iNat19) and produces a single class label at inference. During training, lightweight auxiliary classification heads (normalization + linear layer) are attached to every intermediate layer, but all auxiliary heads are discarded at test time, so inference uses only the terminal classifier with no additional FLOPs or memory overhead.

## Method

The paper formalizes the forward pass of residual architectures as a Conditional Discriminative Flow (CDF), where each residual block is a first-order Euler step of an input-conditioned ODE. Using Tuned Lens probes, it discovers that semantic evidence E(t) accumulates along a consistent exponential schedule E(t) ≈ ae^{βt}+c (R²>0.97) across model scales, training paradigms, and datasets. Deep Trajectory Supervision (DTS) exploits this by attaching auxiliary heads to intermediate layers and assigning each a depth-dependent soft target: the target logit vector is τ_t = G_max·α(t)·e_y with α(t) = (e^{βt}−1)/(e^{β}−1), so early layers receive low-confidence targets and confidence ramps up exponentially toward the terminal layers. The training objective sums layer-wise cross-entropy losses against these scheduled targets plus the standard terminal classification loss, and the gradient analysis shows the auxiliary signal acts as a local semantic regulator that corrects class-specific drift while preventing premature saturation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with the DeiT-III recipe, DTS improves Top-1 accuracy from 80.4% to 81.2% (DeiT-III-S) and from 72.7% to 75.2% (DeiT-III-T). On CIFAR-100, DTS achieves 57.0% Top-1 versus 54.8% for terminal-only training (+2.2 pp), and on iNat19 it reaches 49.1% versus 46.4% (+2.7 pp). Gains are consistent across all six evaluated datasets and both ViT and CNN backbones, with the largest improvements on fine-grained and medium-scale benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Deep Supervision (Lee et al., 2015), DTS consistently wins: on CIFAR-100 it reaches 57.0% vs. 55.9% for DS (+1.1 pp), and on CIFAR-100+RandAug it achieves 64.1% vs. 62.9% where DS actually underperforms the baseline (−0.6 pp). Against Aligned Training (Jiang et al., 2024), DTS leads on every reported setting, with particularly large margins on ImageNet-1K (81.2% vs. 77.2% for DeiT-III-S; 82.3% vs. 79.7% for ConvNeXt-T) and Food-101 (54.8% vs. 51.4% for ViT-S). The fixed-margin + depth-weighted control (FixMgn+DW) narrows the gap substantially (e.g., 83.0% vs. 83.9% on CIFAR-10 ViT-S), confirming that the exponential target trajectory shape—not merely the presence of auxiliary heads or depth-dependent loss weights—is the primary source of DTS's gains. On ResNet backbones the advantage over both DS and Aligned Training is most pronounced on Tiny-ImageNet (60.8% vs. 57.5%/57.9% for RN18) and iNat19 (61.8% vs. 60.1%/61.8% for RN18).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical deep supervision (Lee et al., 2015) by replacing uniform hard-label targets at intermediate layers with a depth-dependent soft-target schedule, motivated by an empirical observation from Tuned Lens probing (Belrose et al., 2023) that semantic evidence accumulates exponentially across depth. The theoretical framing borrows from Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) to formalize the forward pass as a Conditional Discriminative Flow, and from Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for the broader principle that regulating trajectory shape is a useful inductive bias. The key conceptual departure from prior intermediate-supervision work (including aligned training, Jiang et al. 2024) is that DTS specifies *how* target confidence should evolve with depth rather than merely *that* intermediate layers should be supervised, grounding the schedule in a measured kinematic regularity rather than an ad hoc choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTS offers a simple, low-overhead training modification (auxiliary heads removed at inference, +2–4% per-step cost) that consistently improves convergence speed and terminal accuracy across ViTs, ResNets, and ConvNeXts on six datasets, suggesting that the geometry of the inference trajectory is a previously underexploited degree of freedom. The robustness of the exponential evidence pattern across model scales, training paradigms (supervised, contrastive, self-supervised), and task difficulties makes it a potentially reusable empirical prior for any architecture with residual structure. Open questions include whether the exponential schedule is truly optimal or merely a good fit, whether the CDF interpretation extends to non-residual or very deep architectures, and whether the scalar evidence target can be generalized to regulate richer aspects of the high-dimensional latent flow.
