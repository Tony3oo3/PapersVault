## Problem

Current video object segmentation methods rely on explicit appearance memory banks for temporal tracking, which fail under large viewpoint changes and long-term occlusions. Existing 3D-aware tracking approaches follow a multi-stage 'Tracking-by-Mapping' paradigm that projects 2D results into 3D via off-the-shelf estimators, suffering from error propagation between disconnected modules. The paper asks whether geometry can serve as a latent, end-to-end foundation for identity reasoning rather than a post-hoc verification tool.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266235/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a sequence of N unposed RGB images (no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required) at 512×512 resolution, conditioned on either a visual prompt (point or box on any single frame) or a text prompt (natural-language referring expression). It jointly produces per-frame binary instance masks and a comprehensive geometry set per frame: camera pose in SE(3), a pixel-aligned 3D point map in local camera coordinates, and a per-pixel confidence map. The architecture is permutation-equivariant, so input frames need not be in temporal order.

## Method

G²TAM builds on Pi3's permutation-equivariant transformer with a DINOv2 backbone and introduces a cross-modal spatial encoder that performs early fusion of vision tokens with prompt tokens (visual coordinates via Fourier positional encoding, or CLIP text embeddings) into a unified geometric-semantic representation. Alternating intra-view self-attention and global cross-view attention blocks process the fused sequences, after which two lightweight decoders predict geometry (inherited from Pi3) and instance masks (adapted from SAM) simultaneously. The key insight is that spatially-aligned geometric features serve as implicit persistent memory for identity reasoning, eliminating explicit temporal memory banks. Training is multi-task: a composite loss combines segmentation (BCE + Dice) and geometry (point reconstruction, normal, confidence, and camera pose) objectives with weights λ_seg=2.0 and λ_geo=1.0, initialized from Pi3 and fine-tuned on a large-scale aggregation of VOS, reconstruction, and joint datasets.

## Results

On the new InsTrack benchmark for Promptable Instance Spatial Tracking (PIST), G²TAM achieves 74.3 S-mIoU and 80.1 S-SR overall, substantially outperforming SAM2 (47.6 S-mIoU) by +26.7 points on the visual prompt subset and ReferDINO (41.7 S-mIoU) by +30.6 points on the text prompt subset. On 3D visual grounding, G²TAM reaches 45.7% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, surpassing the prior zero-shot SOTA VLM-Grounder (32.8%) by 12.9 points. For semi-supervised VOS, G²TAM edges out SAM2 across all benchmarks (e.g., MOSE 77.8 vs. 75.2 J&F), and for RVOS it improves over ReferDINO by +2.9 J&F on Ref-YTVOS and +2.8 on Ref-DAVIS17.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary InsTrack PIST benchmark, G²TAM dominates all baselines by large margins: it outperforms SAM2 (47.6 S-mIoU) by +28.2 points on visual prompts and ReferDINO (41.7 S-mIoU) by +30.6 points on text prompts, while also matching or exceeding the reconstruction quality of dedicated geometry models Pi3 (Abs.Rel 2.54) and VGGT (2.67). On 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer), G²TAM achieves 45.7% Acc@0.5 versus VLM-Grounder's 32.8%, a 12.9-point gap, and also surpasses the point-cloud-based 3D-VisTA (46.2% Acc@0.5) in a zero-shot setting without requiring 3D inputs. For semi-supervised VOS, G²TAM consistently beats SAM2 by 1–2.6 J&F points across MOSE, DAVIS, SA-V, and YTVOS, with the largest gain on MOSE (77.8 vs. 75.2), indicating stronger robustness under heavy occlusion. On RVOS, G²TAM improves over ReferDINO by +2.9 J&F on Ref-YTVOS (72.2 vs. 69.3) and +2.8 on Ref-DAVIS17 (71.7 vs. 68.9), and also leads on the motion-expression MeViS benchmark. The only area where G²TAM does not clearly win is inference speed: it runs at 21.6 FPS vs. SAM2's 30.2 FPS on a single A100, a ~1.4× overhead attributed to cross-view attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

G²TAM builds directly on Pi3's permutation-equivariant cross-view attention architecture for geometry reconstruction, SAM/SAM2's prompt encoding and mask decoder design, and DINOv2 as the visual backbone. Its key conceptual departure is from the 'Tracking-by-Mapping' paradigm (LMK, Bhalgat et al.) that projects 2D tracking results into 3D via off-the-shelf pose/depth estimators, and from conventional VOS methods (SAM2, Cutie) that rely on explicit temporal memory banks. Instead, G²TAM unifies appearance and geometry within a single latent, spatially-aligned feature space, treating geometry as implicit memory for identity reasoning rather than as a post-hoc verification signal. The paper also introduces the InsTrack dataset and the Promptable Instance Spatial Tracking (PIST) task, which had no prior formulation in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of InsTrack and PIST provides the community with a dedicated benchmark for evaluating spatial (rather than purely temporal) consistency in promptable tracking, filling a gap where existing VOS benchmarks only assess frame-to-frame continuity. The finding that joint reconstruction-segmentation training yields a 4.0% segmentation improvement and that geometry-aligned implicit memory outperforms explicit memory banks on several benchmarks suggests a promising direction for reducing reliance on hand-crafted temporal propagation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor/long-sequence scenarios, whether the ~30% inference speed penalty over SAM2 can be mitigated (cross-view attention dominates runtime), and whether the geometry-as-implicit-memory paradigm generalizes to dynamic scenes with significant object motion rather than camera motion.
