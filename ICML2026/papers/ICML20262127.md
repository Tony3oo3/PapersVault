## Problem

Existing machine learning Hamiltonian (MLH) models are evaluated primarily by reconstruction metrics (Hamiltonian MAE, orbital energies), leaving it unclear how accurately they perform when energies and forces are computed directly from predicted Hamiltonians. A recent study reported that even strong Hamiltonian predictors yield far less accurate energy predictions than MLIP approaches, raising the question of whether MLHs can ever reach the energy-force accuracy required for practical atomistic simulations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262127/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes molecular geometry (atomic numbers and 3D positions), the overlap matrix S, a scalar flow time t, and an intermediate Hamiltonian state H_t, and outputs a predicted Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian matrix in an atom-centered orbital basis. Crucially, the Hamiltonian is not itself the final output: it is fed into the Roothaan-Hall eigenproblem to obtain orbital coefficients, from which a density matrix is assembled and the full KS energy functional (kinetic, Hartree, XC, nuclear repulsion) is evaluated via PySCF, with forces taken as analytic gradients of that energy. This means one QHFlow2 energy evaluation corresponds to a single Fock build rather than a full SCF loop, but still incurs O(N³) Coulomb-integral cost via density fitting.

## Method

QHFlow2 trains via conditional flow matching on the residual between a cheap MinAO initialization and the converged SCF Hamiltonian, using a rotationally invariant tensor-expansion prior for the noise distribution. The architecture combines an SO(2)-equivariant eSEN message-passing backbone (efficient local-frame edge updates) with a novel two-stage pair update: an SO(2)-equivariant pair initialization followed by an SO(3) tensor-product refinement via Clebsch-Gordan coupling of node irreps, which improves off-diagonal Hamiltonian accuracy and reduces sensitivity to cutoff and radial-basis hyperparameters. The final Hamiltonian is constructed by mapping irrep-valued node and pair features to orbital-pair matrix blocks through a learnable SO(3)-equivariant tensor expansion, with Hermiticity enforced by symmetrization. This design achieves 40–50% lower Hamiltonian error than prior state-of-the-art models while using roughly half the parameters and 2.8× faster inference.

## Results

On the MD17/rMD17 benchmark, QHFlow2-m (43M params) achieves Hamiltonian MAE of 1.68–2.63 μHa across six molecular systems, reducing energy MAE to 0.003–0.084 meV (up to 20× lower than NequIP) while reaching NequIP-level force accuracy (0.338–1.350 meV/Å) for the first time among Hamiltonian predictors. On QH9-stable-id, QHFlow2-xl (990M) achieves Hamiltonian MAE of 4.6 μHa, total energy MAE of 0.21 meV, and force MAE of 1.2 meV/Å, reducing energy error by up to 20× relative to MACE. QHFlow2 also achieves 40–50% lower Hamiltonian error than the prior state of the art (QHFlow) with roughly half the parameters and 2.8× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QHFlow2 outperforms all prior Hamiltonian predictors (SPHNet, QHNet, QHFlow, QHNetV2, WANet) on every reported metric across both MD and QH9 benchmarks, with the largest gains on off-diagonal Hamiltonian accuracy and downstream energy. Against MLIP baselines, QHFlow2-m achieves up to 20× lower energy MAE than NequIP on MD17/rMD17 while matching NequIP's force accuracy (the first Hamiltonian model to do so); on QH9, it reduces energy error by up to 20× relative to MACE and improves upon EquiformerV2. Compared to the closest prior Hamiltonian model QHFlow (Kim et al., 2025), QHFlow2-m cuts Hamiltonian MAE by ~40–50% (e.g., 2.63 vs. 5.60 μHa on ethanol) using fewer parameters (43M vs. 30M) and 2.8× faster inference. Against HELM (Kaniselvan et al., 2025), which shares the eSEN backbone, QHFlow2 achieves substantially lower energy errors on MD17 due to its two-stage pair update and direct DFT energy evaluation. QHFlow2 also surpasses eSEN (Fu et al., 2025) on rMD17, confirming that the SO(2) backbone combined with the SO(3) tensor-product refinement yields consistent gains over the backbone alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

QHFlow2 extends the MLH lineage (QHNet → QHFlow → SPHNet/WANet/QHNetV2/HELM) by replacing the SO(3)-equivariant backbone with the more scalable SO(2) edgewise-nodewise scheme from eSEN, and by introducing a two-stage pair update that combines SO(2) initialization with an SO(3) tensor-product refinement for off-diagonal Hamiltonian blocks. The training paradigm follows the equivariant conditional flow matching framework established in QHFlow (Kim et al., 2025), with a residual parameterization over a MinAO initialization. The key conceptual departure from prior MLH work is the evaluation protocol: rather than reporting only Hamiltonian reconstruction or SCF-acceleration metrics, this paper computes total energies and analytic forces directly from predicted Hamiltonians via a unified PySCF pipeline, enabling controlled comparison against MLIP baselines (NequIP, MACE, EquiformerV2) on the same splits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that Hamiltonian prediction can serve as a viable single-framework alternative to MLIPs for atomistic simulation, providing not only competitive forces but substantially lower energy errors and access to electronic-structure objects (orbitals, densities) in one model. Open questions include extension to open-shell and spin-polarized systems, transferability across DFT functionals and basis sets, scaling to OMol25-scale datasets where Hamiltonian label generation remains expensive, and whether one-shot analytic forces from non-self-consistent densities can be made strictly conservative without full SCF. The rMD17 benchmark with recomputed, self-consistent Hamiltonians is a practical resource for the community.
