## Problem

In LLM-driven neural architecture search, free-form code edits suffer from functional entanglement: a single revision can simultaneously modify multiple interacting architectural factors (e.g., operator parameterization and forward-pass wiring), causing unpredictable performance shifts and frequent executability violations. Existing LLM-driven NAS methods treat each iteration as a holistic rewrite without a mechanism to isolate which functional factor to intervene on, making it difficult to attribute improvements or compose gains across multi-round evolution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial architecture as executable Python code annotated with two disjoint, tag-delimited regions (OPERATOR for module definitions, ACTION for forward-pass composition), along with a task specification and a fixed evaluation budget B. It produces a complete updated program that must preserve boundary tags, keep the non-selected region byte-identical to the parent, and pass a three-stage feasibility pipeline (syntax parse, interface invariance, forward-only tensor-shape/masking check) before entering full training. Non-executable proposals are rejected pre-evaluation and do not count toward the budget, so the output is always a valid, evaluable architecture scored by OOD accuracy and MACs.

## Method

SPARK factorizes each evolution step into three sequential LLM calls: an Architecture Scope Router (ASR) deterministically selects one of two functional factors (OPERATOR or ACTION) to edit, a Refinement Compass (RC) converts recent search signals (stagnation, feasibility failure rates) into a factor-conditioned directive, and a Scoped Architecture Refiner (SAR) generates a single-shot code patch restricted to the selected factor's tagged region. Factor-scoped tokenization enforces locality via fixed boundary tags and a line-based diff check that rejects any proposal whose edits leak outside the chosen region or touch frozen scaffolding. The search backbone is an island-based MAP-Elites archive (OpenEvolve) that samples parents and inspirations, discretizes cells by OOD accuracy and MACs, and migrates elites across islands. The key design principle is separating *where* to intervene (discrete factor selection) from *how* to refine (factor-conditioned patch generation), which reduces cross-factor interference, raises the cumulative valid-proposal rate from ~0.25–0.3 to ~0.5–0.6, and yields a 28.1× sample-efficient speedup on CLRS-DFS over EvoPrompting.

## Results

On the representative 10-task CLRS subset, SPARK achieves 83.92% mean OOD accuracy with comparable model-level MACs (453K avg vs. 450K for the CLRS reference), outperforming all baselines. On the full 30-task CLRS suite, SPARK reaches 83.91% mean OOD accuracy vs. 80.89% (EvoPrompting) and 75.98% (CLRS reference). On the primary DFS task, SPARK attains 83.74% OOD accuracy using only 57 evaluated candidates, yielding a 28.1× sample-efficient speedup over EvoPrompting's 1600-evaluation budget. Gains are most pronounced on graph-reasoning tasks (DFS: 46.78→83.74; SCC: 43.43→77.34; TS: 87.27→99.95) and in low-reference regimes (QS: 0.47→10.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARK outperforms all five baselines on the 10-task CLRS mean OOD accuracy: +12.7 pp over the CLRS reference (83.92 vs. 71.22), +15.6 pp over OpenEvolve (68.30), +9.5 pp over EvoPrompting (74.42), +6.3 pp over FunSearch (77.61), and +4.5 pp over EoH (79.43). On the primary DFS task, SPARK's 83.74% surpasses EvoPrompting (68.14%), FunSearch (74.50%), and EoH (77.27%) by 15.6, 9.2, and 6.5 pp respectively, while OpenEvolve degrades below the CLRS reference (32.54 vs. 46.78). On the full 30-task suite, SPARK improves over the CLRS reference on 21/30 tasks and over EvoPrompting on 19/30 tasks, though it underperforms on a few tasks (BFS, Floyd-Warshall, KMP) where the reference or EvoPrompting already saturates. Model-level MACs remain within ~3K of the CLRS reference across tasks, confirming gains stem from search dynamics rather than capacity scaling. Beyond CLRS, SPARK also improves over OpenEvolve on MNIST-1D (95.99 vs. 70.25), CartPole-v1 (489.00 vs. 377.55), 20News (59.13 vs. 57.10), and BCW (97.04 vs. 96.90), while matching it on Mice Protein Expression (61.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLM-driven NAS methods (EvoPrompting, OpenEvolve, FunSearch, EoH) that treat each search iteration as a single holistic code rewrite, by identifying 'functional entanglement'—where a free-form edit inadvertently couples operator-level and action-level decisions—as a systematic reliability bottleneck. SPARK departs from this paradigm by decomposing each revision into an explicit scope-selection step (ASR) followed by a scope-conditioned refinement step (RC+SAR), drawing on structure-aware code editing and MAP-Elites archive-based search. The factor-scoped tokenization and feasibility pipeline are novel contributions that make the intervention target explicit, whereas prior LLM-driven NAS methods leave functional roles implicit in the code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that factor-isolated editing yields a 28.1× evaluation-efficiency speedup and substantial OOD accuracy gains without increasing model compute suggests that the bottleneck in LLM-driven NAS is not architectural knowledge but the controllability of how that knowledge is applied. Open questions include whether the two-factor (OPERATOR/ACTION) decomposition generalizes to architectures with more complex or automatically discoverable functional roles, and whether the transfer failures on tasks like BFS and Floyd-Warshall indicate fundamental limits of single-task evolution. The additional LLM-call overhead for routing and directive generation also raises practical cost considerations for large-scale deployment.
