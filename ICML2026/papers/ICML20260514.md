## Problem

Multi-modal prompt learning for CLIP-style VLMs can overfit to environment-specific spurious correlations (background, texture, style) that degrade sharply under distribution shift. Existing causal prompting methods still optimize deterministic prompts, which act as high-capacity shortcut channels that transmit spurious information even when invariance is encouraged. The paper addresses the missing explicit information-capacity constraint on the prompt pathway combined with intervention-driven training signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260514/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class-label text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with stochastic prompt tokens injected into both vision and text transformer branches. At inference, the deterministic posterior mean of the latent prompt is used (no sampling or interventions), producing a class label via cosine similarity in the shared embedding space. The framework is plug-and-play: it drops into existing MaPLe (shallow layers, M=2) or MMRL (deeper layers, M=5) prompt placements with only ~4–7M additional trainable parameters.

## Method

Do-Prompt introduces a compress-and-intervene framework with two synergistic components. First, a Variational Prompt Bottleneck models each layer's prompt as a stochastic latent variable u_i inferred from shallow frozen-layer context via a diagonal Gaussian posterior, regularized by a KL divergence against a standard normal prior to explicitly cap the information the prompt channel can carry. Second, the latent prompt is split by index into a task-stable core (u_core) and an environment-related (u_env) subspace; do-style perturbations (zero-masking, batch swapping, or Gaussian resampling) are applied exclusively to u_env, and a consistency loss (KL between original and intervened predictions) penalizes reliance on environment-specific prompt content. The full training objective is L_cls + β·L_KL + λ·L_do, and the method reduces to standard MaPLe/MMRL when σ→0 and λ=0, confirming backward compatibility.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization benchmark (16-shot, 11 datasets), Do-Prompt improves the average Harmonic Mean (HM) by +1.75% when added to MaPLe (78.37→80.12) and by +1.97% when added to MMRL (80.59→82.56). On cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet→10 targets), average gains reach +1.56% (MaPLe) and +1.77% (MMRL), with a standout +9.20% on EuroSAT. On domain generalization (ImageNet→ImageNet-V2/Sketch/A/R), MMRL+Do-Prompt achieves up to +1.90% on ImageNet-Sketch and ImageNet-A, the most challenging distribution shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Do-Prompt (on MMRL) achieves 82.56% average HM, outperforming all seven baselines: CLIP (71.70%), CoOp (70.83%), CoCoOp (75.83%), APP (72.61%), PromptSRC (78.61%), MaPLe (78.37%), and MMRL (80.59%). The most notable gains appear on datasets with strong domain or texture bias: EuroSAT HM improves from 85.50% (MMRL) to 91.28% (+5.78%), DTD from 73.99% to 75.94% (+1.95%), and FGVCAircraft from 40.95% to 43.60% (+2.65%). On cross-dataset transfer, Do-Prompt beats MaPLe by +1.56% and MMRL by +1.77% average, with the largest single-dataset gain of +9.20% on EuroSAT. On domain generalization, MMRL+Do-Prompt surpasses MMRL on all four ImageNet variants (e.g., ImageNet-Sketch: 47.80→49.70, ImageNet-A: 48.90→50.80). Do-Prompt also outperforms the text-only prompt methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, APP) by a wide margin (5–12% HM), confirming that the multi-modal prompt pathway combined with the variational bottleneck and interventions is essential for robust generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

Do-Prompt sits at the intersection of two lines of work: variational information bottleneck (VIB) regularization for representation learning (Alemi et al., 2017; Du et al., 2020) and causal-invariance-inspired robustness methods (IRM, GroupDRO, REx). It applies both specifically to the prompt pathway in multi-modal prompt tuning (MaPLe, MMRL), where prior causal prompting efforts (Gao & Dong, 2025) still optimize deterministic prompts without explicitly constraining information capacity. The key departure is treating prompts as stochastic latent variables with a KL-regularized bottleneck and then performing do-style perturbations on a designated environment sub-branch, rather than relying solely on invariance objectives over deterministic parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly recognized failure mode—prompts memorizing environment-specific shortcuts—that undermines the transferability of otherwise efficient prompt-tuned VLMs. The plug-and-play design with negligible inference overhead makes it directly deployable on top of existing MaPLe/MMRL pipelines. However, the core-environment split is an index-based heuristic rather than a formal causal disentanglement, and experiments are confined to CLIP ViT-B/16 in a 16-shot regime; extending to larger backbones, non-CLIP architectures, and providing stronger semantic validation of the two prompt branches remain open.
