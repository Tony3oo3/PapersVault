## Problem

Adversarial imitation learning (AIL) achieves high-quality imitation but demands extensive online environment interaction. While pretraining policies via behavioral cloning before AIL fine-tuning is a natural strategy, empirical studies consistently show it provides minimal benefits, and the theoretical reason for this failure has remained unexplained. This paper identifies the missing piece: random reward initialization leaves a persistent reward error that dominates the imitation gap, and no prior work provides a theoretical guarantee for any pretraining strategy in AIL.

## Input / Output

The method takes N expert demonstration trajectories (state-action sequences of horizon H) as offline input, plus online environment rollouts during the AIL fine-tuning stage. It produces a policy mapping states to action distributions. The theoretical analysis operates in tabular episodic MDPs with bounded rewards in [0,1], while the empirical implementation uses Gaussian-parameterized policies for continuous control on DMControl tasks. A notable design choice is that the reward function is initialized directly from the BC policy's log-probabilities rather than learned separately, meaning a single supervised training procedure yields both the policy and reward initializations.

## Method

CoPT-AIL is a two-stage algorithm. In Stage 1 (Policy-Reward Co-Pretraining), a BC policy π^BC is trained on expert demonstrations via maximum likelihood, and the reward is simultaneously initialized as r_h^1(s,a) = log(π_h^BC(a|s)). The key theoretical insight is that the log-probability of a soft-optimal expert policy is exactly a potential-based shaping reward of the true reward (derived from the soft Bellman equation), so learning the BC policy implicitly learns an accurate shaping reward without needing to recover the true reward itself. In Stage 2 (Online AIL Fine-Tuning), the co-pretrained policy and reward are refined through standard alternating KL-regularized policy optimization and reward minimax updates. The paper proves that this co-pretraining reduces the reward error to Õ(C|S||A|H²/(KN)), yielding a strictly improved imitation gap bound over standard AIL without pretraining whenever N ≻ C√(|S||A|H²)/K², establishing the first theoretical guarantee for pretraining benefits in AIL.

## Results

The paper evaluates CoPT-AIL on 8 DMControl continuous-control tasks (Walker Stand/Run/Walk, Cartpole Swingup, Hopper Hop/Stand, Finger Spin, Cheetah Run) using learning curves over online environment interactions rather than a single final-score table. CoPT-AIL consistently matches or exceeds the convergence rates of prior SOTA AIL methods (FILTER, PPIL, HyPE) across all 8 tasks, and achieves near-expert performance with significantly fewer online interactions on Cartpole Swingup, Hopper Hop, Hopper Stand, and Finger Spin. In the ablation study, policy pretraining alone (πPT-AIL) yields convergence rates similar to standard AIL and exhibits instability on Cartpole Swingup and Finger Spin, whereas the full CoPT-AIL shows faster and more stable convergence, with particularly pronounced improvements on Walker Stand and Walker Walk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPT-AIL is compared against six baselines: BC, IQLearn, PPIL, FILTER, HyPE, and OLLIE. Against the prior SOTA AIL methods (FILTER, PPIL, HyPE), CoPT-AIL consistently matches or exceeds their convergence rates across all 8 DMControl tasks, reaching near-expert returns with fewer online interactions on four tasks (Cartpole Swingup, Hopper Hop, Hopper Stand, Finger Spin). Against OLLIE, the most directly comparable method that also incorporates reward pretraining, CoPT-AIL achieves faster and more stable convergence, particularly on Walker Stand and Walker Walk. Against BC, CoPT-AIL substantially outperforms it, as BC suffers from compounding errors in long-horizon rollouts. The ablation against πPT-AIL (policy pretraining alone) confirms that the reward pretraining component is essential: without it, convergence is comparable to vanilla AIL and unstable on several tasks, validating the paper's theoretical claim that reward error is the dominant bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial imitation learning (AIL) literature by providing the first theoretical analysis of pretraining within AIL. It builds on the GAIL framework (Ho & Ermon, 2016) and the online AIL convergence analysis of Shani et al. (2021), but departs from prior work that only analyzes AIL from random initialization. The key conceptual departure is from prior reward-pretraining approaches (Watson et al., 2023; OLLIE/Yue et al., 2024), which learn the reward separately; here, the authors show via reward-shaping theory (Ng et al., 1999) that the BC policy's log-probability is itself a valid shaping reward of the true reward, enabling joint policy-reward pretraining from a single BC procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal guarantee that pretraining can provably accelerate AIL, filling a gap where prior empirical observations of minimal benefit from policy pretraining lacked theoretical explanation. The reward-shaping connection is practically appealing since it eliminates a separate IRL step, but the theory is confined to tabular MDPs, and extending it to function approximation (the regime relevant to deep IL) remains open. Broader implications include parameterizing rewards with policy models and applying the framework to foundation-model-based robot learning, though these are left as future work.
