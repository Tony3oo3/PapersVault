## Problem

Clustering-based features in machine learning require choosing a single resolution parameter (k, γ, or a cut threshold) that is global, fixed, and ad hoc, yet real data exhibits intrinsic multi-scale structure where different samples benefit from different granularities. While recent theory shows that sweeping the resolution parameter yields only a finite set of structurally stable partitions (configurations), no prior work formulates learning to adaptively weight these configurations per sample for downstream prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen embedding vector x ∈ R^d from any pretrained model (CLIP, BERT, GIN, DimeNet++, FT-Transformer, TabPFN) and produces a fixed-dimensionality augmented feature vector [x; z(x)] where z(x) ∈ R^{d_c} (d_c=32 in all experiments) is the energy-aware mixed configuration representation. Configuration extraction is a one-time offline preprocessing step on the kNN graph of the embedding space; at inference the selector adds only a small MLP forward pass. The default protocol is batch-transductive (test batch ≥ 50 samples for stable graph construction), with an inductive kNN-lookup fallback for small-batch or streaming deployment that retains ≥75% of the gains.

## Method

MixConfig has two components: (1) configuration extraction, which builds a kNN graph over the embedding and runs the Parallel-DT algorithm to sweep the resolution parameter γ, identifying all finite stable plateau partitions Ω = {ω_1,…,ω_m} along with their energy statistics (Hamiltonian H_i, attraction h_a, repulsion h_r, plateau width Δγ_i); and (2) an energy-aware selector that computes per-sample compatibility scores by jointly encoding sample context (MLP of x), per-configuration cluster assignment embeddings (learnable embedding tables), and the energy statistics, then applies softmax to yield mixing weights on the (m−1)-simplex. The mixed representation z(x) = Σ w_i(x)·c_i is concatenated with x and passed to any downstream predictor. For non-differentiable predictors (XGBoost, RF), a two-stage procedure trains the selector on a differentiable surrogate before freezing it. The key design insight is that energy statistics—particularly plateau width and attraction/repulsion balance—serve as an explicit inductive bias telling the selector which resolutions are structurally stable, rather than treating all configurations as interchangeable features.

## Results

MixConfig achieves consistent improvements across all eight benchmarks spanning tabular, vision, molecular, and text domains. Headline gains include +1.6 pp Top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100 (75.8% vs. 74.2% Base), +1.9 pp on ImageNet-1K (78.2% vs. 76.3%), +1.5 pp AUC on OGBG-MolHIV (81.9% vs. 80.4%), and +1.0 pp accuracy on SST-2 (95.5% vs. 94.5%). On tabular OpenML-CC18, MixConfig improves Binary AUC by +1.2 pp (90.7% vs. 89.5%) and Multi-class accuracy by +1.1 pp (79.3% vs. 78.2%). All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05, paired t-test, 5 seeds) with Cohen's d > 0.8 across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixConfig outperforms all three clustering-based baselines on every benchmark. Against DeepCluster (single-resolution k-means pseudolabels), MixConfig wins on 7 of 8 benchmarks; DeepCluster is inconsistent, helping only on Binary AUC (+0.5 pp) and AG News (+0.4 pp) while actively hurting on Multi-class (−0.3 pp), CIFAR-100 (−0.2 pp), and MolHIV (−0.3 pp) because a single global resolution introduces noise when the optimal scale is sample-dependent. Against HDBSCAN (hierarchical density-based features), MixConfig achieves larger and more consistent gains: +1.2 pp on CIFAR-100, +1.4 pp on ImageNet-1K, +1.0 pp on MolHIV, and +0.7 pp on SST-2, reflecting HDBSCAN's lack of principled stability criteria for selecting meaningful partitions. Against CSPA (classical cluster-ensemble consensus, evaluated in the ablation on BBBP and CIFAR-100), MixConfig achieves +4.1 pp on BBBP (90.3% vs. 86.2%) and +1.5 pp on CIFAR-100 (75.8% vs. 74.3%), confirming that collapsing multiple resolutions into a single discrete consensus discards the multi-resolution information that adaptive per-sample weighting exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Parallel-DT algorithm (Pitsianis et al., 2023) and the BlueRed geometric characterization (Liu et al., 2021), which establish that resolution-based clustering yields a finite set of structurally stable partitions (configurations). It departs from prior clustering-as-features work (DeepCluster, HDBSCAN, cluster ensembles) by replacing a single fixed resolution or a discrete consensus with learned per-sample continuous weights over all stable configurations, using Hamiltonian energy statistics (plateau width, attraction/repulsion balance) as an explicit inductive bias in the selector. The authors explicitly distinguish their approach from Mixture-of-Experts (weighting precomputed structural representations vs. routing to trainable subnetworks) and from Matryoshka-style multi-resolution embeddings (downstream adaptive weighting vs. joint training of nested representations).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (frozen embedding → configuration extraction → energy-aware selector → any predictor) makes this broadly applicable across tabular, vision, molecular, and text domains without architectural changes, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot modify their base models. The most impactful finding is the sample-efficiency result: MixConfig trained on 10% of labels matches the base model trained on 50%, suggesting configurations act as effective unsupervised structural priors in data-scarce regimes relevant to drug discovery and medical diagnosis. Key limitations include the non-differentiable configuration extraction (preventing joint optimization with the embedding), the requirement of ≥50 samples for stable kNN graphs, and modest absolute gains (1–2 pp) on already-strong baselines. Open directions include streaming/incremental configuration updates, joint architecture search, and cluster-level (rather than partition-level) stability tracking.
