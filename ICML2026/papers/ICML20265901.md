## Problem

The internal mechanisms by which LVLMs translate visual stimuli into coherent emotional narratives remain unexplored, primarily because existing causal analysis tools are ill-suited for emotion: visual counterfactuals are scarce, and emotional expression is diffuse across long-form generation rather than anchored to a single token, rendering standard Next-Token-Prediction metrics ineffective. This paper bridges the gap by introducing a steering-vector-based causal attribution framework tailored for descriptive emotional reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265901/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (a single frame extracted from video in benchmark datasets) paired with a text query prompt containing a task instruction and a neutral event description, and produces an open-ended emotional narrative. For mechanistic analysis, the input is a contrastive pair: the same neutral text paired with either an emotional image or its pixel-level neutralized counterfactual (generated via Google Nano Banana), isolating the visual affect variable. Evaluation uses an LLM-as-Judge to extract emotional keywords from the narrative and maps them onto standardized emotion wheels via a hierarchical function, computing hit-rate or F-score metrics rather than discrete token probabilities.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage I extracts generalized emotional steering vectors by differencing residual-stream activations at the last input token between emotional and neutral counterfactual inputs, then averaging over pairs where the model successfully expressed the target emotion. Stage II performs hierarchical circuit discovery: critical layers are identified by injecting steering vectors and measuring hit-rate change; attention heads are localized via a Latent Restoration Metric that measures cosine similarity of attention outputs to the steering direction (replacing logit-difference for open-ended tasks); and upstream MLP neurons are traced via Attribution Patching (a first-order Taylor approximation backpropagating gradients from the Key projection). The discovered mechanism reveals a functional decoupling: middle layers (l8–l18) aggregate emotional intent through sentiment-specific attention heads (with cohesive clusters among negative sentiments), while deep layers (l19–l35) execute narrative generation through emotion-general heads. Guided by this, VEENA is a training-free inference-time intervention with two synergistic components: Visual Emotion Enhancement (VEE) applies element-wise scaling masks to pre-softmax attention scores on critical heads—amplifying V→Q flow during prefill and V→L flow during decoding—and Emotional Neuron Augmentation (ENA) sparsely scales the intermediate activations of identified emotion-salient MLP neurons, together improving average MER-UniBench performance by +6.7% without additional latency.

## Results

On the comprehensive MER-UniBench (9 datasets spanning Basic Emotion Recognition, Sentiment Analysis, and Fine-grained Emotion Recognition), VEENA raises the average performance from 58.1% to 64.8% (+6.7%) on Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, with the largest gain of +9.2% on the most challenging OV-MERD+ benchmark. The intervention operates on only ~3% of parameters (Top-10 attention heads and Top-30 neurons) with negligible latency overhead (~14.7–15.0 ms/token). General vision-language capabilities are preserved: POPE accuracy improves by +0.24%, CHAIR hallucination scores decrease, and MME perception score increases by +13.4 points, confirming the orthogonality of the discovered emotional circuits to object perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEENA is compared against two recent SOTA training-free inference-time methods: VISTA (Li et al., 2025b) and PAI (Liu et al., 2024b). VEENA decisively outperforms both across all three MER tasks. Against VISTA, VEENA gains +13.09 on MER2023 Hit Rate (66.03 vs. 52.94), +1.13 on CMU-MOSI WAF (54.97 vs. 53.84), and +8.85 on OV-MERD+ F_s (49.67 vs. 40.82); notably, VISTA actually degrades MER2023 below the unmodified baseline (52.94 vs. 53.92). Against PAI, VEENA still leads by +6.42 on MER2023 (66.03 vs. 59.61), +0.60 on CMU-MOSI (54.97 vs. 54.37), and +3.35 on OV-MERD+ (49.67 vs. 46.32). The authors attribute this consistent superiority to VEENA being explicitly grounded in the discovered 'Adapt-Aggregate-Execute' emotion mechanism, enabling precise, interpretable intervention on identified circuits rather than the generalized attention or latent-space adjustments employed by the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mechanistic interpretability from text-only LLMs (where emotion circuits have been localized via lexical-substitution counterfactuals) to LVLMs, where the absence of clean visual counterfactuals and the diffusive nature of emotional expression make standard NTP-based causal analysis inapplicable. It repurposes activation steering (Liu et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2025b) to extract generalized emotional directions, adapts activation patching (Golovanevsky et al., 2024) into a Latent Restoration Metric operating in hidden space rather than token-probability space, and borrows attribution patching (Nikankin et al., 2025) for recursive neuron tracing. The key conceptual departure from prior LVLM interpretability work (Jiang et al., 2025; Neo et al., 2025), which has focused exclusively on object perception and hallucination, is the shift to abstract affective semantics and the construction of semantically controlled visual counterfactuals via image-editing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of how LVLMs process emotion, yielding a concrete 'Adapt-Aggregate-Execute' model with a testable functional decoupling (sentiment-specific aggregation heads vs. emotion-general execution heads) that could guide future architecture design or targeted fine-tuning. VEENA offers a practical, training-free lever for reducing emotional hallucinations in deployed LVLMs without retraining, which is valuable given the ethical risks of affective misalignment. However, the analysis is confined to 4B–8B models, static images, and four basic emotions, leaving open whether the discovered circuits persist at larger scale, extend to video/audio modalities, or generalize to mixed and subtle affective states like sarcasm.
