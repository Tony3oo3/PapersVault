## Problem

Existing multi-modal clustering methods that rely on single-granularity, sample-level representations struggle to balance global cross-modal consensus with fine-grained intra-modal discriminability: fine-grained features are prone to local heterogeneity and redundant perturbations, while coarse-grained representations lose local structural information. Information-bottleneck-based MMC approaches inherit this limitation because they operate solely on individual samples without any mechanism for adaptive granularity control or structural quality-aware weighting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multi-modal feature matrices (n × d^i per modality, where modalities can be images, text, or video) along with the number of clusters K and four hyperparameters (α for structural constraint strength, β for compression-preservation trade-off, τ for purity threshold, and n_min for minimum GB size). Beyond the final K-cluster assignment, the method also produces an intermediate granular-ball partition whose count is data-adaptive (ranging from 22 to 578 across datasets), with each GB characterized by its multi-modal mean center that serves as the unit for all subsequent information-theoretic computations.

## Method

SGB-IB replaces individual samples with granular-balls (GBs) as the fundamental unit of the information bottleneck. GBs are generated by recursively binary-splitting the dataset using K-means, where splitting terminates when a GB's multi-modal purity (average mutual information between features and K-means pseudo-labels across all modalities) exceeds threshold τ or its size drops below n_min. A structure-aware weight S_G is then computed from each GB's similarity ratio (SR), defined as the ratio of its average cosine similarity to its own cluster versus other clusters, which modulates the information preservation term to prioritize high-quality, cross-modal-consistent GBs. The IB objective is optimized at the GB level via iterative extract-merge operations, where each GB is temporarily removed from its cluster and reassigned to the cluster minimizing a merger cost computed with Jensen-Shannon divergences over GB centers, ensuring monotonic improvement of the objective.

## Results

SGB-IB achieves the best or near-best clustering accuracy across all five benchmark datasets. Headline results include 55.71% ACC / 45.31% NMI on SOCCER, 53.56% ACC / 34.49% NMI on Reuters, 90.95% ACC / 75.26% NMI on BBC, 65.23% ACC / 71.83% NMI on WVU, and 53.94% ACC / 69.86% NMI on COIL100. The largest margin over the strongest baseline is on BBC, where SGB-IB surpasses FPMVS-CAG (79.50% ACC) by over 11 percentage points. On COIL100, SGB-IB leads on ACC but trails SMVSC by 0.27 points on NMI (69.86 vs. 70.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGB-IB outperforms all 15 baselines on ACC across every dataset, with the most dramatic gains on BBC (90.95% vs. 79.50% for FPMVS-CAG, +11.45 pp) and WVU (65.23% vs. 62.52% for INMKC, +2.71 pp). On NMI, SGB-IB leads on SOCCER (45.31 vs. 45.12 for AmIB), Reuters (34.49 vs. 30.32 for SMVSC), BBC (75.26 vs. 72.75 for AmIB), and WVU (71.83 vs. 65.06 for ALPC), but is narrowly surpassed by SMVSC on COIL100 (69.86 vs. 70.13). Among the dedicated multi-modal clustering methods, the closest competitors are FIMVC-VIA on SOCCER (50.81% ACC) and INMKC on Reuters (51.42% ACC), both of which SGB-IB exceeds by 4–5 percentage points. The single-modal IB baseline (Tishby et al., 1999) and its all-modal variant (AmIB) are consistently outperformed, confirming that the GB-level structural awareness and structure-aware weighting provide meaningful gains over vanilla information-bottleneck clustering. Overall, SGB-IB achieves the best or second-best result on 9 out of 10 metric-dataset combinations, with the sole exception being COIL100 NMI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the Information Bottleneck (IB) framework for representation learning (Tishby et al., 1999; Lou et al., 2013) and the Granular-Ball (GB) computing paradigm for coarse-grained data partitioning (Xia et al., 2019, 2025). Prior IB-based clustering methods operate exclusively at the individual-sample level, while existing GB-based clustering methods rely on geometric criteria (distance, purity) without information-theoretic grounding. SGB-IB departs from both by elevating the IB optimization unit from samples to GBs and driving GB generation via mutual-information-based purity rather than heuristic distance thresholds, creating a closed loop where structural quality directly modulates the information-theoretic objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in multi-modal clustering—fixed single-granularity representations that cannot simultaneously preserve fine-grained intra-modal detail and coarse-grained cross-modal consensus—by introducing data-adaptive granularity through recursive GB splitting. However, the method still depends on K-means pseudo-labels for initialization and does not handle missing or misaligned modalities, which are common in real-world multi-modal settings. The core GB-IB coupling is described as task-agnostic, suggesting potential transfer to other structured representation learning problems, but this remains unvalidated. Open questions include scalability beyond the tested datasets (largest: 7,200 samples), sensitivity to the number of modalities, and whether the information-theoretic purity criterion generalizes to non-convex or high-dimensional feature spaces.
