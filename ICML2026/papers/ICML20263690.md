## Problem

Stochastic optimization with Markovian noise and zero-order (gradient-free) oracles has not been studied before. First-order methods under Markovian noise incur a multiplicative penalty in mixing time τ, and zero-order methods under i.i.d. noise incur a multiplicative penalty in dimension d, leading to the natural hypothesis that combining both would yield a dτ multiplicative penalty. This paper shows the hypothesis is false: the correct scaling is additive (d+τ), meaning that when τ ≤ d, Markovian noise imposes no additional cost over i.i.d. noise for gradient-free methods.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only zero-order function evaluations F(x, Z) where the noise state Z follows a uniformly geometrically ergodic Markov chain with mixing time τ, in either one-point (each query draws a fresh noise state) or two-point (two queries share the same noise state) feedback mode. The problem is μ-strongly convex and L-smooth (or G-Lipschitz non-smooth) in d dimensions. The output is an ε-approximate minimizer x* satisfying E‖x^N − x*‖² ≤ ε, with oracle complexity scaling as (d+τ) rather than dτ, and the paper provides matching information-theoretic lower bounds confirming optimality up to logarithmic factors.

## Method

The core technique is a randomized batching scheme that assigns each Markov noise sample to a random direction e ~ RS₂^d for the finite-difference gradient estimate, combined with a Multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator using a geometrically distributed batch size. The key insight is that random directions decouple the variance into two terms: one independent of τ (controlled by the direction randomness) and one independent of d (controlled by the Markovian batching), yielding the additive (d+τ) scaling. The MLMC estimator uses a truncated log-geometric batch size to reduce bias by a factor of BM while only reducing variance by B, and the overall algorithm wraps this estimator inside Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent with carefully tuned momentum parameters. The paper also extends the analysis to non-smooth problems via ℓ₂-ball smoothing and proves adversarial robustness to bounded additive oracle errors.

## Results

The paper establishes that zero-order accelerated SGD under Markovian noise achieves oracle complexity O((d+τ)σ₂²/(με)) for two-point feedback and O(d(d+τ)Lσ₁²/(μ³ε²)) for one-point feedback in the smooth strongly convex setting. The headline finding is that when the mixing time τ is less than the problem dimension d, the convergence rate is identical to the i.i.d. case — the τ dependence vanishes entirely. This contrasts with first-order methods, which always pay a multiplicative τ factor. The results are confirmed optimal via matching information-theoretic lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against first-order Markovian methods (Beznosikov et al., 2024), which achieve O(τσ₂²/(με)) for smooth strongly convex problems. The proposed zero-order two-point method achieves O((d+τ)σ₂²/(με)), which is strictly better than the first-order Markovian bound whenever τ < d (since d+τ < dτ for τ > 1). Against the i.i.d. zero-order baselines (Hazan & Kale, 2014 for two-point; Akhavan et al., 2020 and Gasnikov et al., 2017 for one-point), the paper's Markovian results reduce to the known i.i.d. rates when τ = 1, confirming consistency. For non-smooth problems, the paper extends the i.i.d. bounds of Shamir & Zhang (2013) and Duchi et al. (2012) to the Markovian setting with the same additive (d+τ) scaling. The lower bounds (Theorem 2) match the upper bounds up to log factors, confirming that no algorithm can improve the (d+τ) scaling, and that the gap between the paper's results and the first-order Markovian lower bound of Beznosikov et al. (2024) is inherent to the zero-order oracle model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: zero-order optimization (ZOO) under i.i.d. noise (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017; Dvurechensky et al., 2021; Gasnikov et al., 2017) and first-order stochastic optimization under Markovian noise (Duchi et al., 2012; Beznosikov et al., 2024; Solodkin et al., 2024). It is, to the authors' knowledge, the first work to study the combined setting of zero-order oracles with Markovian stochasticity. The key technical tool is a randomized batching scheme inspired by Multilevel Monte Carlo (Glynn & Rhee, 2014; Dorfman & Levy, 2022), adapted here to exploit the structure of finite-difference gradient estimators in a way that first-order methods cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that complexity scales as (d+τ) rather than the naively expected d·τ—means that for problems where the mixing time is smaller than the dimension (a common regime in high-dimensional RL and bandit settings), the Markovian structure imposes essentially no additional cost over i.i.d. noise. This provides a principled argument for preferring zero-oracle methods over gradient-based ones when interacting with correlated stochastic environments. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex or PL-structured objectives, relaxing the uniform boundedness assumptions on noise, and designing algorithms that adapt to unknown mixing time without requiring it as a hyperparameter.
