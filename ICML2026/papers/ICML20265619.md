## Problem

Deploying custom gesture recognition on resource-constrained smart wearables requires simultaneously minimizing model parameters, reducing training data to a handful of samples per new gesture, and maintaining high accuracy with strong generalization. Existing approaches like Apple's Delta-Encoder suffer from large pre-trained feature extractors, linear memory growth with stored delta vectors, and poor generalization to novel gestures because augmentation remains tied to the pre-training data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265619/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw 100 Hz triaxial linear acceleration and angular velocity signals from a wrist-worn IMU (e.g., ST LSM6DSOW 6-axis sensor on a commercial smartwatch) and outputs a discrete gesture class label. For few-shot adaptation to a new custom gesture, the data augmentation module requires only ≤5 real sample embeddings as guidance, while the inference-time feature extractor is constrained to ~32k parameters and ~260 KiB RAM to fit on-device deployment budgets.

## Method

BiCrossNet pairs a dual-branch single-modality extractor (CNN for local spatial patterns + SRU for temporal dependencies, each processing acceleration or angular velocity separately) with a symmetric cross-attention module that enables bidirectional feature interaction between the two modalities, yielding ~32k parameters with higher discriminative power than larger baselines. For data augmentation, a Delta Generator uses a diffusion process with Laplacian noise (sharp peak, heavy tails matching fine-grained motion differences) and L1 loss to synthesize delta vectors directly from random noise, eliminating the need to store pre-computed vectors or the full pre-training dataset. An Embedding Generator further decouples augmentation from a separate decoder by learning, end-to-end, to produce realistic sample embeddings from random noise conditioned on a few real-sample embeddings of the new class, requiring only the single generator model at inference time.

## Results

On the public UMAHand dataset (7 gestures), BiCrossNet with the Embedding Generator achieves 98.13% F1-score and 98.16% accuracy using only 32,642 parameters. On the self-developed GRHand dataset (9 custom gestures), the same configuration reaches 96.13% F1-score and 96.30% accuracy. Inference latency is 9.16 ms with 260.18 KiB RAM on an Intel Xeon Gold 6342 CPU, representing 17.3% lower latency and 27.6% less memory than the Apple baseline. Without any data augmentation, F1 drops to 54.56% (UMAHand) and 54.51% (GRHand), underscoring the critical role of the proposed generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AppleDE (Xu et al., CHI 2022), BiCrossNet with the Embedding Generator improves F1-score by 11.7% on UMAHand (98.13% vs. 88.31%) and by 8.85% on GRHand (96.13% vs. 87.45%), while reducing feature-extractor parameters by 27.8% (32,642 vs. 56,752). Against Improved TCNN (Kim et al., ICCAS 2024), the comparison is more nuanced: on UMAHand with the Embedding Generator, BiCrossNet (98.13% F1) marginally edges out TCNN (98.08% F1) with 27.8% fewer parameters, but on GRHand the gap widens dramatically to 96.13% vs. 77.99% F1, indicating that TCNN's architecture struggles to extract useful features from the augmented embeddings on transient, application-specific gestures. When paired with the Delta Generator, BiCrossNet also outperforms TCNN on GRHand (90.99% vs. 93.13% F1 for TCNN-D-G, a slight TCNN win) but leads on UMAHand (97.14% vs. 97.36%, near-tie). Overall, BiCrossNet achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all configurations while maintaining the smallest model footprint, and its advantage is most pronounced on the harder, real-world GRHand gestures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Apple's Delta-Encoder framework (Xu et al., CHI 2022) for IMU-based custom gesture recognition on wrist-worn devices, replacing its encoder-decoder architecture with a diffusion-based Delta Generator that uses Laplacian noise and L1 loss, and further introducing an Embedding Generator that eliminates the separate decoder entirely. It also departs from the Improved TCNN feature extractor (Kim et al., ICCAS 2024) by proposing a lighter dual-branch CNN+SRU architecture with symmetric cross-attention, reducing parameters from 45k/56k to 32k. The conceptual lineage is clear: the paper targets two specific limitations of the Apple baseline—storage overhead from pre-computed delta vectors and poor generalization to novel gestures—while keeping the overall few-shot customization workflow intact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling on-device deployment of custom gesture recognition on resource-constrained smartwatches (260 KB RAM, 9.16 ms latency, 32k parameters), which is a real engineering bottleneck for commercial products. The GRHand dataset (40+ hours, 180 participants, commercial smartwatch hardware) fills a gap by providing realistic unconstrained data for transient interactive gestures rather than continuous activities. Open questions include whether the cross-attention design generalizes to multi-axis or multi-sensor configurations, whether the diffusion-based augmentation scales to larger gesture vocabularies, and whether the approach transfers to other body-worn IMU placements beyond the wrist.
