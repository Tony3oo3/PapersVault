## Problem

In 3D IC floorplanning, multiple hardware design rules (boundary alignment, grouping, inter-die alignment, pre-placement, non-overlap, outline, and shape constraints) must be satisfied simultaneously, yet existing analytical, heuristic, and RL-based methods can only address a narrow subset of these rules. Violations of unhandled rules require labor-intensive manual post-processing by expert engineers, and simply adding penalty terms to reward functions proves insufficient for enforcing complex hard constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-channel stacked tensor of design rule matrices (adjacent block mask, adjacent terminal mask, alignment mask, position mask, wire mask, canvas image) processed by a CNN, alongside a netlist graph (node feature matrix and adjacency matrix) encoded by a Transformer. The output is a hybrid action per timestep: a discrete (x, y) coordinate sampled from a masked W×H probability distribution for the current block, and a continuous aspect ratio sampled from a Gaussian for the next block (since the next state requires the shape to recompute all rule masks). The availability mask M is applied to the position logits via additive masking (M−1)·10⁸, hard-filtering infeasible placements before sampling.

## Method

RulePlanner frames 3D floorplanning as an episodic MDP solved by an actor-critic agent trained with Hybrid PPO over a discrete-continuous action space. The core novelty is a unified constraint-handling mechanism with three components per rule: (1) a spatial matrix representation (e.g., adjacent terminal mask encoding block-terminal distance, adjacent block mask encoding adjacency length) that serves as both input feature and constraint source; (2) a binary availability mask M = T̄ ⊙ B̄ ⊙ Ā ⊙ P that prunes the action space by zeroing out positions violating any rule; and (3) quantitative violation metrics (block-terminal distance, block-block adjacency length, alignment score) incorporated into a dense, self-adaptively normalized reward. The encoder combines a CNN over stacked rule matrices with a two-layer Transformer over the netlist graph (using the adjacency matrix as attention mask and sinusoidal encoding for placement order), feeding a decoder that produces the position probability map and an MLP that outputs Gaussian parameters for aspect ratio. The framework is modularly extensible: new design rules are incorporated by defining their corresponding matrix, binary mask threshold, and reward metric without modifying the core architecture.

## Results

On Task 1 (boundary constraint), RulePlanner achieves a block-terminal distance of 0.000 across all eight circuits (ami33, ami49, n10–n300), indicating perfect terminal alignment, while the best baseline (Analytical) averages 0.169 and the next-best (FlexPlanner) averages 0.306. On Task 2 (grouping constraint), RulePlanner attains a block-block adjacency length of 0.223 on average, surpassing the best baseline WireMask-BBO (0.082) by 2.7×. Zero-shot transfer from n100 to other circuits preserves over 92% of training performance on adjacency length and alignment score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RulePlanner dominates all seven baselines on the two primary constraint metrics. On block-terminal distance (Task 1), it achieves 0.000 on every circuit, whereas the closest competitor Analytical (Huang et al., 2023) averages 0.169 and FlexPlanner (Zhong et al., 2024) averages 0.306; heuristic methods (B*-3D-SA, WireMask-BBO) and other RL baselines (GraphPlace, DeepPlace, MaskPlace) all exceed 0.35. On block-block adjacency length (Task 2), RulePlanner's 0.223 average is 2.7× the best baseline WireMask-BBO (0.082) and 3.1× MaskPlace (0.070); Analytical and B*-3D-SA are near zero (0.003 and 0.042), indicating they essentially ignore grouping constraints. On HPWL, WireMask-BBO and Analytical achieve slightly lower values on larger circuits (n30–n300), but the paper argues this reflects a deliberate trade-off: RulePlanner prioritizes full constraint satisfaction over marginal wirelength gains, and its HPWL remains competitive (within ~5% on most circuits). The zero-shot transfer experiment further shows RulePlanner generalizes to unseen circuits without fine-tuning, maintaining >92% of training performance, a capability none of the baselines demonstrate.

## Relation to Prior Work

RulePlanner extends the RL-based 3D floorplanning line initiated by FlexPlanner (Zhong et al., 2024), which introduced alignment masks and multi-modality representations for inter-die constraints, and MaskPlace (Lai et al., 2022), which pioneered the use of matrix masks to constrain the action space. The key conceptual departure is unifying seven heterogeneous design rules (boundary, grouping, alignment, non-overlap, outline, shape, pre-placement) under a single availability-mask mechanism, whereas each prior method was tailored to one or two specific constraints. The novel adjacent terminal mask and adjacent block mask are new representations that encode boundary and grouping constraints as spatial matrices, complementing the existing alignment and position masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: current industrial 3D floorplanning workflows require expert engineers to manually fix residual constraint violations, a labor-intensive bottleneck that this framework aims to eliminate. The modular three-part extensibility pattern (rule matrix → action-space constraint → reward metric) provides a clean recipe for incorporating future design rules without architectural redesign. Key limitations include the absence of thermal optimization, evaluation only on MCNC/GSRC benchmarks (which are larger than typical industrial circuits per the authors' own note), and the acknowledged trade-off where HPWL is sometimes slightly worse than greedy baselines that ignore design rules. Open questions include whether the framework scales to the hundreds of constraints in real SoC designs and how to integrate power/thermal maps into the state representation.
