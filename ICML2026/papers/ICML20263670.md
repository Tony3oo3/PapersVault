## Problem

Standard DPO tuning selects checkpoints by maximizing the preference margin, but this proxy can point in the opposite direction from actual capability. The paper asks whether, and under what conditions, the optimized DPO margin decouples from reasoning and other probe-level capabilities, and whether training path (not just terminal β) produces persistent, capability-specific degradation that standard aggregate evaluation would miss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each experiment starts from a fresh 7B base checkpoint (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-2-7B, or Qwen-1.5-7B) and trains with LoRA under a fixed DPO recipe, varying only β across a 13-point logarithmic grid (0.0005–0.100) with denser resolution near β≈0.006–0.015. Outputs are length-normalized log-probability margins on fixed capability probes (14 logic, 3 arithmetic, 4 format, 3 sycophancy, 3 negation), the final DPO preference margin, and a training-roughness score; the path-dependence experiments additionally produce per-seed paired differences between matched-duration training paths. Two LoRA configurations are used (r=16/α=32 for canonical sweeps, r=8/α=16 for path-dependence and 1000-step runs), and all runs use lr=5×10⁻⁵, AdamW, effective batch 8.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a controlled β-sweep protocol under a frozen-configuration principle (each β point retrains from the same base checkpoint with identical hyperparameters), applied across three 7B architectures to map the capability landscape as a function of alignment pressure. Three diagnostic tools distinguish it from prior DPO analyses: (1) a matched-duration path-dependence test (Path B: 200 steps at β=0.02 then 200 at β=0.01, versus Path C: 400 steps at β=0.01) that isolates β-history effects from training duration via paired t-tests over 5 seeds; (2) an expanded 14-probe logic suite spanning three difficulty levels that decomposes the canonical 3-probe aggregate into direct-inference and fallacy-detection clusters, revealing stable sign opposition (13/14 probes sign-stable across 3 seeds × 3 β values) rather than a reasoning transition; and (3) a multi-seed variance scan (5 seeds) that localizes a seed-sensitive region near β≈10⁻² in Mistral-7B. The key empirical finding is that the DPO preference margin can strongly anticorrelate with logic-probe capability (r=−0.91 for LLaMA-2-7B), and that transient high-β exposure induces persistent arithmetic and format degradation (d_z=3.73, p=0.002) that does not recover when β is reduced, while logic and sycophancy are unaffected.

## Results

Across a 13-point β sweep on three 7B architectures, the DPO preference margin strongly anticorrelates with logic-probe capability in LLaMA-2-7B (Pearson r = −0.91, p < 10⁻⁴, n = 13), meaning the optimized proxy and the measured target point in opposite directions. Under matched-duration controls (400 steps), transient exposure to β = 0.02 causes persistent arithmetic degradation (d_z = 3.73, p = 0.002) and format degradation (d_z = 3.38, p = 0.003) relative to constant-β training, while logic and sycophancy show no significant effect (p > 0.3). An expanded 14-probe logic suite reveals that 13 of 14 probes maintain stable sign across 3 seeds × 3 β values, with direct-inference probes consistently negative and fallacy-detection probes consistently positive after DPO. Mistral-7B exhibits 27–2259× higher seed variance than LLaMA-2-7B near β ≈ 0.006, marking a seed-sensitive region absent in the other architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic empirical study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the 'baselines' are alternative training paths and evaluation choices. The key comparison is Path B (anneal: 200 steps at β = 0.02, then 200 at β = 0.01) versus Path C (400 steps at constant β = 0.01): Path C outperforms Path B on arithmetic (+1.87 margin, 5/5 seeds consistent) and format (+1.08, 5/5 consistent), showing that transient high-β exposure causes persistent, capability-specific degradation that does not recover. Path A (200 steps, constant) is included to show that the A-vs-B comparison alone confounds β-history with training duration, motivating the matched-duration C control. Across architectures, Mistral-7B shows a 'plastic' response with elevated seed variance (27–2259× higher than LLaMA-2-7B at β = 0.006), LLaMA-2-7B shows 'selective' rigidity (logic frozen across a 20× learning-rate range, spread only 0.125), and Qwen-1.5-7B shows smooth trade-offs without sharp discontinuities. The 3-probe logic aggregate is shown to be an aggregation artifact: the expanded 14-probe suite is positive at every β tested, whereas the 3-probe aggregate crosses zero at isolated points due to stable sign opposition between probe clusters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its training objective, treating the regularization parameter β as a systematic control variable to map capability landscapes across three 7B architectures. It connects to the reward over-optimization literature (Gao et al., 2023) by documenting a concrete Goodhart's-law instance where the optimized proxy (preference margin) anticorrelates with measured capability, and to the loss-of-plasticity literature (Dohare et al., 2024) by showing persistent capability degradation after transient high-β exposure. The probe-cluster sign-structure finding extends the metric-artifact analysis of Schaeffer et al. (2023) from scale-based emergence to fixed-scale alignment tuning, showing that aggregate crossings can reflect internal probe opposition rather than genuine transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is direct: the standard DPO tuning pipeline (select checkpoint by highest preference margin) can preferentially select capability-impaired models, and annealing schedules that pass through high-β regions can induce persistent arithmetic and format degradation that does not recover. Key limitations include the 7B scale and LoRA-only setting, the weak external validation (ProntoQA at chance for both base models), and the use of two different LoRA configurations across experiment families. Open questions include whether the β≈0.01 sensitive region and the probe-cluster structure persist at frontier scale, whether full fine-tuning reproduces the path-dependence effects, and what mechanistic account explains why discrimination-type probes improve while direct-deduction probes degrade under the same alignment pressure.
