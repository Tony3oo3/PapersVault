## Problem

Backpropagation is inapplicable to gradient-agnostic objectives (e.g., the hard 0-1 loss, truncated losses) and black-box systems where gradients vanish or are inaccessible. While likelihood ratio (score-function) estimators provide unbiased gradients for such objectives, their variance grows prohibitively with model depth and parameter dimensionality when noise is injected at the input or hidden-neuron level, rendering them impractical for modern deep architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mini-batch (X, Y), current parameters θ, a loss function L(z, y) that may be entirely non-differentiable (treated as a black box), a noise scale σ, and a repetition count n. It produces updated parameters θ′ via a single VJP through the backbone. Critically, the n-fold stochastic expansion applies only to the lightweight loss evaluation at the output level (dimension d, e.g., number of classes), not to the backbone forward pass, so the expensive network computation is performed exactly once per iteration regardless of n.

## Method

OVLR injects isotropic Gaussian noise at the model output (z = μ(θ) + σε) and applies the likelihood-ratio trick to obtain an unbiased gradient estimator of the form E[L(z,y)·ε/σ]·∇_θ μ(θ). Two variance-reduction mechanisms are combined: (1) output-level repetition, which computes the deterministic backbone output μ(θ) once and reuses it across n perturbed loss evaluations, and (2) antithetic sampling, which pairs each noise draw ε with −ε to cancel dominant odd-order noise terms. The resulting scalar/vector gradient signal v is propagated back through the entire network via a single vector-Jacobian product (equivalent to Z.backward(v) in PyTorch), requiring no architectural changes and no gradient from the loss function. This design confines the n-fold computational overhead to the cheap loss evaluations while keeping backbone forward and backward costs constant, yielding near-BP training throughput with orders-of-magnitude variance reduction.

## Results

On standard differentiable tasks, OVLR matches backpropagation within 1% top-1 accuracy across ResNet-18, DenseNet-121, and ViT-B/16 on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, with per-epoch training time within 0.5% of BP (e.g., 62.10% vs 63.10% for ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100; 88.17% vs 86.79% for ViT-B/16 where OVLR actually wins). On the gradient-agnostic Hard 0-1 loss where BP produces zero gradients, OVLR achieves 61.74% top-1 on CIFAR-10, and with a Mixed Loss (α=0.5) reaches 87.92%, competitive with cross-entropy training. Under 60% symmetric label noise, OVLR attains 73.0% peak accuracy versus 66.3% for standard CE (+6.7%). On true black-box IOH benchmarks, OVLR scores 0.9917 normalized (86.7% success) on discrete PBO and 0.2389 on continuous BBOB, outperforming REINFORCE (0.7040, 0% success) and CEM (0.1922).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard backpropagation (Rumelhart et al., 1986), OVLR stays within 1% top-1 accuracy and adds <0.5% per-epoch time across all tested architectures, and even surpasses BP by 1.38% on ViT-B/16/CIFAR-100. Compared to vanilla parameter-level REINFORCE (Williams, 1992), OVLR achieves 96.80% vs 82.70% accuracy on MNIST while being 14× faster (30.02s vs 428.74s) and using 73× less memory (65.55MB vs 4835MB). Against GLR-style input-level repetition (Peng et al., 2025), OVLR is 2.6× faster and uses 1.6× less memory at higher accuracy (96.80% vs 94.60%). On the Hard 0-1 loss, OVLR (62.04% CIFAR-10, 225.1s) ties PPO (62.12%, 373.2s) in accuracy but is 40% faster, and dramatically outperforms GRPO (45.26%) and zeroth-order methods (23.92%); STE-style and surrogate baselines achieve higher accuracy (67.66%, 68.54%) but optimize a CE proxy rather than the 0-1 objective directly. On black-box IOH benchmarks, OVLR outperforms CEM (Rubinstein, 1999) on continuous BBOB (0.2389 vs 0.1922) and Gaussian-REINFORCE (0.2389 vs 0.0431), while on discrete PBO it leads (1+1)-EA (0.9917 vs 0.9688) and REINFORCE (0.9917 vs 0.7040).

## Relation to Prior Work

OVLR extends the line of likelihood ratio (score function) methods for neural network training initiated by REINFORCE (Williams, 1992) and advanced by the push-out method (Peng et al., 2022), ULR (Jiang et al., 2024), and FLOPS (Ren et al., 2025a). The critical departure is in *where* stochasticity is injected: prior LR-based training methods perturb inputs or hidden neurons, causing gradient variance to explode with model depth and parameter count. OVLR instead confines all perturbation and antithetic sampling to the low-dimensional output space, decoupling the stochastic estimation from the backbone and enabling a single deterministic forward pass followed by lightweight loss evaluations. This design choice, combined with classical antithetic sampling (Hammersley & Morton, 1956) adapted to the output level, is what makes the method scalable to modern architectures like ViT and Mamba.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OVLR provides the first practical, scalable pathway to directly optimize gradient-agnostic objectives (hard 0-1 loss, truncated losses) in deep networks without resorting to biased surrogates or prohibitively expensive parameter-space sampling. The demonstrated robustness gains under extreme label noise (60%) and outliers (20–40%) suggest practical value for real-world data pipelines where clean supervision is unavailable. Key limitations remain: the theoretical guarantees require bounded losses, very high-dimensional outputs (C=5000) demand larger repetition budgets, and the method's utility is inherently scoped to settings where BP fails or is unreliable. Open questions include extending the framework to unbounded-loss regimes (e.g., RL with unbounded rewards) and whether output-level perturbation can be combined with other variance reduction techniques (control variates, importance weighting) for further gains.
