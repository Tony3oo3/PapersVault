## Problem

Debate-based scalable oversight protocols require two equally-capable AI provers with one guaranteed truthful, an assumption that may not hold in practice. Classical interactive proofs (IP = PSPACE) do not relativize, meaning they cannot handle computations with oracle access (e.g., human judgment or a database), which is essential for AI safety. This paper asks whether relativizing single-prover interactive proofs are possible without debate, under structured assumptions on the computation or oracle.

## Input / Output

The protocol takes an input string x of length n, a succinct description of an oracle Boolean circuit C with depth D, size S, and ℓ oracle gates organized into d adaptivity levels, and oracle access O: {0,1}* → {0,1}. The verifier produces an accept/reject decision while making only O(1/ε) oracle queries (or just 1 query in the low-degree setting) and running in near-linear time, whereas the prover runs in polynomial time. The circuit's oracle gates may be adaptive (queries depend on prior answers), organized into d levels where level-i queries depend only on levels 1 through i-1.

## Method

For robust oracle circuits, the protocol recursively applies interactive proofs of proximity (IPPs) across d adaptivity levels, using unique-decoding checksums (Reed-Solomon syndromes) to 'fix' the implicit input at each level so that each IPP's soundness guarantee applies to a well-defined string; a doubly-efficient interactive proof (DEIP) at the base level verifies the initial queries are computed correctly from x. For the low-degree oracle setting, the prover commits to two low-degree polynomials encoding queries and answers via a lattice-based polynomial commitment scheme, then uses Kilian's succinct argument of knowledge to prove the circuit outputs 1 under those polynomials; the verifier makes a single oracle query at a random field point and applies Schwartz-Zippel to detect incorrect encodings. The robust proof system achieves statistical soundness with O(1/ε) oracle queries, while the argument systems achieve computational soundness with O(1) rounds and O(1/ε) or 1 oracle queries respectively.

## Results

The paper presents four relativizing doubly-efficient single-prover protocols for oracle-aided computation. In the robust setting, the statistical-soundness proof system (Theorem C.5) achieves verifier time Õ(σd(εℓD+1/ε+n)), query complexity Õ(σd/ε), and round complexity Õ(dD), where d is the adaptivity level. The computational-soundness argument system (Theorem C.10) improves this to verifier time Õ(1/ε+n), O(1/ε) queries, and O(1) rounds. For low-degree polynomial oracles, the argument system (Theorem D.4) achieves verifier time Õ(n), only 1 oracle query, and Õ(1) rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the relevant comparisons are against prior models and impossibility results. The paper's central contribution is showing that single-prover relativizing interactive proofs exist under structured assumptions, in contrast to the debate model (Irving et al. 2018; Brown-Cohen et al. 2024) which requires two competing provers with equal ability and a truthfulness assumption. The impossibility result of Barbara, Chiesa, and Guan (2025) shows no relativizing interactive proof exists for all oracle-aided computations; this paper circumvents that barrier by restricting to robust computations or low-degree oracles. Compared to the debate approach, the single-prover protocols eliminate the need for two equally-capable AI models and the assumption that one is truthful, at the cost of requiring either robustness of the computation or algebraic structure in the oracle. The computational-soundness argument systems (Theorems C.10, D.4) achieve substantially better parameters than the statistical-soundness proof systems (Theorems C.1, C.5), trading unbounded-prover security for O(1) rounds and near-optimal query complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the debate model (Irving et al. 2018; Brown-Cohen et al. 2024), which was introduced specifically to recover relativization for interactive proofs in the AI safety setting, by showing that a single prover suffices under structural assumptions on the computation or oracle. It builds on the doubly-efficient interactive proof framework of Goldwasser, Kalai, and Rothblum (2015) and the interactive proof of proximity constructions of Rothblum and Rothblum (2020), extending both to the oracle-aided computation setting. For the argument systems, it repurposes Kilian's (1992) succinct argument of knowledge and Cini et al.'s (2024) lattice-based polynomial commitments as core protocol components, adapting them to handle oracle queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the two-prover debate assumption—requiring equally capable, properly incentivized AI models where one is truthful—is not a fundamental requirement for relativizing verification, opening a path to scalable oversight protocols with a single AI model. However, the results require structural assumptions (ε-robustness of the computation or low-degree algebraic structure of the oracle) that may not hold for arbitrary AI tasks, and Barbara et al. (2025) prove no relativizing IP exists for all computations, so the scope is inherently limited. Key open questions include formalizing when natural tasks satisfy robustness, experimental validation on realistic oversight tasks, and whether protocols can be designed for oracles beyond low-degree polynomials (e.g., general 'learnable' oracles).
