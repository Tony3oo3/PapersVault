## Problem

Existing graphon estimation and graph learning methods assume a single underlying generative model per dataset or per class, but real-world graph datasets are mixtures of multiple populations. This single-graphon assumption produces semantically inconsistent augmentations in mixup (where graphs within a class may arise from distinct generative processes) and introduces false negatives in contrastive learning (where graphs sharing the same generative model are incorrectly treated as negatives). The paper asks whether one can explicitly recover and exploit the mixture of underlying graphons to improve both supervised and unsupervised graph learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of graphs as adjacency matrices (optionally with node features and class labels for supervised tasks) and produces three outputs: (i) a set of K estimated graphons {Ŵ₁,…,Ŵ_K} with an assignment function π mapping each graph to its generating component, (ii) for GMAM, augmented graphs sampled from interpolated graphons with soft labels, and (iii) for MGCL, 32-dimensional graph-level embeddings. A key implementation detail is that graphon estimation via SIGL jointly recovers latent node positions η ∈ [0,1] for each node, which are required to query edge probabilities from the estimated graphon during augmentation. The clustering operates on 9-dimensional empirical motif density vectors (connected motifs up to 4 nodes), and the number of clusters defaults to K = log T.

## Method

The framework proceeds in three stages: (1) compute empirical motif density vectors for each graph, (2) cluster graphs via k-means in motif space, and (3) estimate a graphon per cluster using SIGL (an implicit neural representation trained to approximate the graphon function W:[0,1]²→[0,1]). A novel theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) bounds the difference in empirical motif densities between two graphs by the cut distance of their underlying graphons plus a sampling error term that scales as O(√k) in motif size—tighter than the classical O(k) bound—justifying motif-based clustering. For supervised learning (GMAM), the graphon mixture is estimated per class, and augmentation interpolates between graphons from different classes (W_λ = λŴ_{i,π(G_a)} + (1−λ)Ŵ_{j,π(G_b)}) before sampling new graphs, preserving finer semantic structure than single-graphon mixup. For unsupervised learning (MGCL), augmentation resamples a fraction of edges according to the cluster graphon's edge probabilities at the estimated latent positions, and the InfoNCE loss is modified to restrict negatives to graphs from different clusters, eliminating false negatives from the same generative model.

## Results

In supervised graph classification (GMAM), the method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 6 out of 7 TUDataset benchmarks, with headline results of 74.45% on IMDB-BINARY, 92.25% on REDDIT-BINARY, and 80.25% on COLLAB, consistently outperforming the single-graphon G-Mixup (e.g., 74.45% vs. 72.40% on IMDB-BINARY) and the closest competitor MomentMixup (e.g., 56.46% vs. 56.09% on REDDIT-MULTI-5K). In unsupervised graph contrastive learning (MGCL), the method achieves the lowest average rank (A.R.) of 1.42 across seven datasets, ranking first on NCI1 (79.18%), PROTEINS (75.54%), DD (79.07%), and MUTAG (90.46%), and second on the remaining three. The synthetic clustering experiment confirms that motif-based clustering (MBC) achieves 80.0% accuracy in the varying-size setting, closely approaching the theory-based upper bound of 81.4% and substantially outperforming GNN-based embeddings (GCN: 58.6%, GIN: 25.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMAM outperforms all 9 baselines on 6 of 7 datasets, with its only loss being a narrow 0.30-point gap to MomentMixup on AIDS (98.20% vs. 98.50%). Against the most direct competitor G-Mixup (single graphon per class), GMAM improves by 2.05 points on IMDB-BINARY (74.45% vs. 72.40%) and 2.13 points on REDDIT-BINARY (92.25% vs. 90.20%), confirming that modeling multiple graphons per class yields meaningfully better augmentations. In the unsupervised MGCL setting, the method achieves the best average rank (1.42) versus DRGCL (3.57) and simGRACE (4.14), winning outright on NCI1 (79.18% vs. 79.10% for simGRACE), PROTEINS (75.54% vs. 75.30%), DD (79.07% vs. 78.90% for RGCL), and MUTAG (90.46% vs. 89.50% for DRGCL). MGCL trails only on IMDB-BINARY where InfoGraph leads (76.20%), and is second on COLLAB and RDT-BINARY. In the synthetic clustering experiment, moment-based clustering (MBC) at 80.0% accuracy nearly matches the theory-based upper bound (81.4%) while GNN-based embeddings (GCN 58.6%, GIN 25.7%, Graph2Vec 28.6%) and spectral methods (22.9%) fall far short, validating motif densities as a superior structural signature for identifying generative models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graphon-based graph learning literature from single-graphon models to explicit mixtures of graphons, using motif moment vectors as the clustering mechanism to identify mixture components. It builds directly on SIGL (Azizpour et al., 2025) for graphon estimation and moment matching (Ramezanpour et al., 2025) for scalable graphon learning, but departs from both by rejecting the single-graphon assumption that underlies prior work. For downstream tasks, it generalizes G-Mixup (Han et al., 2022) from one graphon per class to a mixture per class, and extends GraphCL (You et al., 2020) by replacing random augmentations with graphon-conditioned resampling and restricting InfoNCE negatives to cross-cluster graphs only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded way to handle heterogeneity in graph datasets—a common but under-addressed issue in practice—without requiring labels or task-specific supervision for the mixture estimation step. The tighter concentration bound (O(√k) vs. O(k) in the classical result) is a clean theoretical contribution that strengthens the statistical justification for motif-based graph clustering. Key limitations include the dense-graphon assumption (sparse graphs are out of scope), the sensitivity of moment vectors to small graph sizes (high sampling variance), and the heuristic choice of K = log T; extending the framework to sparse graphon processes and adaptive cluster selection remain open.
