## Problem

Many scientific inference problems are underdetermined, admitting multiple mechanistically distinct hypotheses consistent with the same observations, yet current LLM benchmarks reward only single-answer correctness and leave untested whether models can systematically explore and cover the full admissible hypothesis set. This paper asks: can LLMs, treated as samplers, recover the complete set of valid hypotheses rather than collapsing onto a small subset of preferred explanations?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260545/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance provides domain-specific observations (single-node intervention response vectors for causal graphs, binary top-down M×M projections for 3D voxel reconstruction, or phenotype input-output pairs for Boolean programs) along with task parameters (node count, grid dimensions, operator set/depth) that control the size of the enumerated admissible set |H_O|. The LLM generates N independent hypothesis samples (typically N = |H_O|) under a fixed prompt, and the output is a triple of rates—Validity (VR), Uniqueness (NR), and Recovery (RR)—computed by deterministic validators and task-specific canonicalizers against the exactly enumerated ground-truth set, with no LLM-as-judge involved.

## Method

HypoSpace frames LLM evaluation as set-valued inference: for each instance the complete valid hypothesis set H_O is pre-enumerated, and the model's N samples are scored on three complementary indicators—VR (fraction of proposals consistent with observations), NR (order-respecting non-redundancy via task-specific canonicalizers), and RR (coverage of H_O by valid-and-unique proposals). Three structured domains (causal DAG inference, gravity-constrained 3D voxel reconstruction, Boolean genetic interaction) provide natural difficulty knobs that scale |H_O| while keeping validation deterministic. A theoretical head-tail analysis shows that peaked model-induced distributions make expected coverage grow at most as K + N(1−α), explaining why high VR coexists with low RR under realistic budgets. As a training-free intervention, complexity-stratified decoding iterates over structural-complexity levels and allocates the sampling budget proportionally to the ground-truth distribution at each level, partially mitigating the simplicity bias that causes models to neglect complex valid hypotheses.

## Results

Across three structured domains (causal inference, 3D voxel reconstruction, Boolean genetic interaction), frontier reasoning models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Grok-4) maintain near-100% Validity Rate (VR) even at the hardest difficulty, but their Recovery Rate (RR) drops sharply as the admissible hypothesis set |H_O| grows. On the most discriminative Boolean task (hard setting), GPT-5 achieves 48.0% RR and Gemini-2.5-Pro 47.4%, while non-reasoning models (GPT-4o: 14.1%, LLaMA-3.3-70B: 11.0%) collapse further. On larger-scale extensions (|H_O|=160 causal, |H_O|=125 3D), all three top models retain 100% VR but lose 20–42 percentage points in NR/RR relative to their hardest original settings, confirming a systematic coverage failure under peaked generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 7 LLMs (5 reasoning, 2 non-reasoning) as hypothesis samplers. GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro are the strongest overall, achieving 100% VR/NR/RR on easy settings and leading on hard settings (e.g., 48.0% vs. 47.4% RR on Boolean Hard), though both still lose >50% of the admissible set. Grok-4 matches or exceeds them on Causal (99.8% RR at 6 nodes) and 3D (99.8% RR at tp=3) but trails on Boolean (36.5% RR Hard). DeepSeek-R1 is competitive on Causal and 3D (79.5% and 85.2% RR at hardest) but degrades more on Boolean (36.0% RR). Claude-Opus-4 shows the steepest reasoning-model decline, dropping to 51.1% RR on Causal Hard and 23.8% on Boolean Hard. Non-reasoning models (GPT-4o, LLaMA-3.3-70B) are consistently 3–10× worse on RR at hard difficulties (e.g., 6.7% vs. 99.2% on Causal 6-node), confirming that explicit reasoning mitigates but does not eliminate mode collapse. On the real-world yeast genetic interaction instance, GPT-5 and Grok-4 achieve 100% RR while Claude-Opus-4, LLaMA, and GPT-4o score 0% VR, illustrating that validity and coverage failures can be fully decoupled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both scientific-discovery benchmarks (LLM-SR, Gravity-Bench, Auto-Bench) that assume a single ground-truth answer per input, and creativity-aware evaluations (HypoBench, IdeaBench, CreativEval, LiveIdeaBench) that rely on LLM-as-judge or human raters for novelty scoring. It repurposes the classical creativity triad (appropriateness, originality, fluency) into a set-valued inference framework where the admissible hypothesis set is exactly enumerated and validated by deterministic checks, eliminating subjective judgment. The theoretical grounding draws on the underdetermination literature (Van Fraassen, Stanford) and connects to novelty search (Lehman & Stanley) in the observation that peaked generators systematically under-explore, but the paper's contribution is the operationalization of this insight as a measurable diagnostic rather than a search algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first quantitative, model-agnostic measurement of hypothesis-space coverage for LLMs, revealing that even frontier reasoning models exhibit a structural mode collapse that persists across tasks, scales, and decoding strategies—a limitation invisible to standard accuracy metrics. The clean theoretical analysis (peaked distributions imply sublinear coverage) gives a principled explanation and points toward distribution-reshaping interventions rather than brute-force sampling. Key open questions include whether the coverage failure reflects a fundamental property of autoregressive likelihood training or is an artifact of the structured/synthetic task design, and whether the Boolean canonicalizer's limited algebraic scope underestimates true model diversity. The paper explicitly disclaims real-world scientific discovery evaluation, so generalization to open-ended domains remains untested.
