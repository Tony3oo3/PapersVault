## Problem

Non-deterministic robotic policies (diffusion-based and autoregressive VLMs) suffer from significant instability under single-shot inference, succeeding or failing under identical conditions. Existing inference-time sampling approaches for robotics either incur prohibitive latency by repeatedly querying the policy or generate candidates from naive Gaussian distributions that ignore inter-dimensional action correlations, and lack interpretable verifiers for low-level actions. This paper addresses both gaps with a plug-and-play framework that improves policy performance without any further fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input RGB images from a third-view and wrist camera, a text task instruction, and a small set (N=4) of initial action chunks produced by a frozen policy model. It outputs a single selected action chunk (10×7 for CALVIN, 50×8 for real-world, encoding joint angles/end-effector pose and gripper state) computed as a score-weighted average of candidates exceeding a progress threshold, along with per-candidate task-progress scores. The VLM backbone is invoked only once per decision step, with all candidates evaluated in parallel through a lightweight action head, keeping additional verification latency negligible.

## Method

TapSampling comprises two components. First, an Action-VAE with transformer encoder/decoder is trained to reconstruct action chunks via a compressed Gaussian posterior; at inference, N policy actions are encoded into N latent Gaussians, mixed into a single posterior, and M latent samples are drawn and decoded into diverse action candidates at ~5× lower latency than repeated policy sampling. Second, a task-progress verifier built on VLA-Adapter (Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone) is trained to predict task-progress change Δp for a query action via L1 regression, with training data constructed automatically from expert trajectories: forward action sequences yield positive progress labels (+k/t) and reversed sequences yield negative labels (−k/t), requiring no manual annotation. At inference, candidates scoring below a threshold are discarded and the remaining actions are combined via score-weighted averaging, yielding interpretable selection where higher scores correspond to faster task completion.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, TapSampling improves the average success length of Diffusion Policy from 2.41 to 2.58 (+0.17), OpenVLA from 3.30 to 3.51 (+0.21), and VPP from 4.39 to 4.46 (+0.07), all without further policy fine-tuning. On LIBERO-Long, the method raises π0.5's average success rate from 96.8% to 98.0% (+1.2%), outperforming TACO (96.6%). In real-world Franka experiments, TapSampling lifts π0's average success rate from 78.3% to 83.3% (+5.0%), with the largest gain on the Stack task (unseen: 75.0% → 85.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TapSampling is a plug-and-play framework, so its primary comparison is against the unmodified base policies: it consistently improves Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA, VPP (CALVIN), π0.5 (LIBERO-Long), and π0 (real-world) without any policy retraining. On LIBERO-Long, TapSampling (98.0% avg) outperforms TACO (Yang et al., 2025a; 96.6% avg), the closest competing inference-time sampling method, by 1.4 points. Against RoboMonkey (Kwok et al., 2025), TapSampling achieves an approximately 12× speedup in verification latency when evaluating 16 candidate actions, thanks to a single VLM forward pass shared across all candidates. In the sampling-strategy ablation (Table 4), Learned Posterior Sampling (the paper's default) achieves 4.46 avg length at 0.488 s latency, versus Policy Sampling's 4.50 at 2.638 s (~5× slower) and Gaussian Sampling's 4.43 at 0.465 s, confirming a favorable efficiency–quality trade-off. The SOTA references in Table 1 (DreamVLA at 4.44, Seer at 4.28) are not directly comparable since TapSampling is applied to different base policies, but VPP + TapSampling (4.46) slightly exceeds DreamVLA's 4.44.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time sampling paradigm—well established for LLMs (self-consistency, verifier-guided selection) and diffusion models (noise optimization, sample search)—to the robotic manipulation domain, where off-the-shelf evaluators for low-level actions are unavailable. It builds directly on VLA-Adapter as the verifier backbone and departs from prior robotic inference-time sampling methods (RoboMonkey's Gaussian sampling + preference-learning verifier, TACO's state-count optimization) by introducing an Action-VAE that captures intra-chunk correlations for efficient candidate generation, and a task-progress-prediction verifier trained on the intrinsic sequential structure of expert trajectories without additional data synthesis. The framework is explicitly policy-agnostic, positioning itself as a complementary axis to the dominant scaling-of-data-and-model-size approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TapSampling offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism to improve deployment reliability of existing generalist policies without retraining, which is valuable given that real-world deployment cannot rely on oracle resets or repeated full-trajectory attempts. The task-progress verifier's interpretable scores (e.g., 'this action would impede progress by 10%') provide a diagnostic tool absent in prior black-box reward or preference-based verifiers. However, the method remains bounded by the underlying policy's capability, the linear-progress assumption may not hold for tasks with long preparation phases, and gains are modest when the base policy is near saturation (e.g., +0.1% on CALVIN Task 1). Open questions include whether the verifier generalizes to truly novel task categories beyond the training distribution and whether the framework extends to multi-robot or dexterous manipulation settings.
