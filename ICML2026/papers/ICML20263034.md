## Problem

Antibody language models capture epistatic interactions but treat sequences as i.i.d. samples from a stationary distribution, ignoring the temporal dynamics of affinity maturation. Classical phylogenetic CTMC models explicitly represent evolutionary dynamics but assume site independence, missing epistatic effects. No existing framework simultaneously models the continuous-time evolutionary process and captures complex intra- and inter-chain epistasis in antibody sequences.

## Input / Output

COSINE takes a parent antibody sequence (heavy and/or light chain, joined by a chain-break token) and a branch length t (expected mutations per site) as input, and outputs per-site 20×20 rate matrices conditioned on the full sequence context. From these, it produces a factorized transition probability p(y|x,t), a selection score via log-likelihood ratio against a frozen Thrifty SHM model, and sampled descendant sequences via Gillespie or Guided Gillespie. In guided mode, an antigen-specific predictor (mean and variance of binding affinity) steers the sampling process, with the Taylor-approximated guidance requiring only a single gradient computation per step.

## Method

COSINE parameterizes a continuous-time Markov chain with a neural network (ESM2 150M backbone with a softplus output head) that maps the full parent sequence to L site-specific rate matrices, relaxing the classical independent-sites assumption by conditioning rates on sequence context. The paper proves (Proposition 4.1) that this factorized transition likelihood is a first-order approximation of the sequential point mutation process, with L1 error bounded by O(λ²t²), and that Gillespie sampling from the learned instantaneous rates provably recovers the true transition distribution (Lemma 4.2). Training uses supervised MLE on ~2M parent-child transitions from ~120K clonal families, with the ESM2 initialization providing transfer learning. For fitness inference, the selection score is derived as a log-likelihood ratio between COSINE and Thrifty SHM, requiring no manual clamping. For design, Guided Gillespie applies classifier guidance via a Taylor-approximated form (TAG) that linearizes the predictor's mean around the current sequence, achieving a 500× speedup over exact oracle guidance while maintaining comparable fitness improvements.

## Results

On the FLAb2 VEP benchmark (Spearman correlation), COSINE achieves 0.613 on Koenig Heavy Expression, 0.464 on Adams Expression, and 0.456–0.536 across four binding assays, outperforming all baselines on 6 of 7 datasets (losing by only 0.005 to ProGen2-Small on Koenig Light Expression). In constrained CDR optimization (5 mutations, ≤5 oracle calls), COSINE attains the highest mean ΔBind (0.198) and max ΔBind (0.395) among budget-constrained methods, matching the unbounded greedy upper bound on max affinity (0.395 vs 0.466) while using only 5 oracle calls vs ~2756. Guided Gillespie sampling from naive antibodies shifts predicted binding affinity distributions to overlap with known CoV-AbDab binders at γ=5, with a 500× speedup from the Taylor-approximated guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSINE outperforms DASM (the closest evolutionary-modeling baseline) on all seven VEP datasets, with the largest margin on Adams Expression (0.464 vs 0.270, +0.194) where the wildtype is a mouse antibody. Against the antibody-specialized language models AbLang-2 and PRISM, COSINE wins decisively on every dataset (e.g., 0.613 vs 0.096 on Koenig-H Expression). General protein LMs ESM2-150M/650M and ProGen2-Small/Medium are all surpassed on binding assays by 0.10–0.35 Spearman, and on expression assays COSINE leads by 0.10–0.58. The sole exception is Koenig Light Expression, where ProGen2-Small edges COSINE by 0.005 (0.513 vs 0.508). In the constrained CDR optimization task (Table 2), COSINE matches the unbounded greedy upper bound on max ΔBind (0.395 vs 0.466) while using only 5 oracle calls per sequence versus ~2756 for greedy, and outperforms the PoE baselines (AbLang-1-PoE: 0.167, ESM2-PoE: 0.149) and the genetic algorithm (0.196) on mean ΔBind. Against the classical WAG substitution model, COSINE achieves lower per-token NLL at every branch length, with the gap widening from 0.005 at t<0.01 to 0.160 at t≥0.15.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSINE bridges two previously separate paradigms: classical phylogenetic CTMCs (WAG, LG), which model temporal evolutionary dynamics but assume site independence, and deep protein language models (ESM2, AbLang, ProGen2), which capture epistasis but treat sequences as i.i.d. draws from a stationary distribution. It builds directly on the mutation-selection decomposition of Halpern & Bruno (1998) and the DASM/Thrifty framework (Matsen et al., 2026; Sung et al., 2025), but replaces DASM's manually clamped selection factor with a log-likelihood ratio that is mathematically consistent by construction. The Guided Gillespie sampling procedure adapts the classifier-guidance formalism of Nisonoff et al. (2025) from discrete diffusion/flow models to the rate matrix of a sequential point mutation CTMC, a setting not previously addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling the temporal process of affinity maturation rather than just the marginal sequence distribution, COSINE provides a principled mechanism to disentangle selection from neutral somatic hypermutation, yielding more accurate fitness estimates and enabling directed antibody design via guided sampling. Key limitations include the first-order approximation (error quadratic in branch length), the exclusion of indels, and the antibody-specific focus, which restricts generalization to broader protein evolution. Open questions include whether the approximation error becomes problematic for longer evolutionary timescales, how the framework extends to indel-aware models, and whether the inter-chain epistasis captured by the categorical Jacobian can be leveraged for more accurate paired-chain design.
