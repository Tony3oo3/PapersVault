## Problem

In omniprediction with long-term constraints, prior work (Bechavod et al., 2025) provided regret guarantees only against a fixed benchmark action feasible at every round, which can be the empty set in adversarial settings, rendering guarantees vacuous. Moreover, the prior algorithm's regret bound scaled linearly in the number of specified subsequences, making it impossible to derive dynamic regret bounds (which require guarantees over Ω(T²) contiguous intervals). This paper addresses both gaps by providing the first algorithm achieving simultaneous dynamic swap regret and vanishing constraint violation for all downstream agents.

## Input / Output

The forecaster observes a feature vector x_t each round and outputs a prediction p_t ∈ [0,1]^d (sampled from a distribution over the outcome space). Downstream agents consume p_t in a purely stateless manner: they evaluate their utility and constraint functions at p_t, discard actions predicted to violate any constraint, and select the remaining action maximizing predicted utility. The predictions must be conditionally unbiased across an arbitrary collection of subsequences S, where each subsequence is defined by an indicator function h_S(t, x_t) that can depend on both the round index and the feature.

## Method

The core algorithm, DECISION-INFEASIBILITY-CALIBRATION, instantiates the UNBIASED-PREDICTION framework (Noarov et al., 2025) with a union of two event families: decision events (rounds where a specific agent plays a specific action) and infeasibility events (rounds where a specific action is predicted to violate a specific constraint). At each round, the algorithm solves a minimax game to produce a prediction distribution that simultaneously controls conditional bias across all these events, yielding both decision calibration and infeasibility calibration guarantees. The key technical advance is that the dependence on the number of subsequences |S| is only logarithmic (via the minimax game's event weighting), compared to the linear dependence in prior work. This logarithmic scaling is what enables deriving dynamic regret bounds by summing per-interval adaptive regret over the Δ+1 intervals induced by a dynamic benchmark's change points, yielding sublinear regret whenever Δ(φ) = o(T) without requiring the algorithm to know Δ in advance.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical guarantee (Corollary 3.14) shows that the DECISION-INFEASIBILITY-CALIBRATION algorithm achieves constrained swap dynamic regret bounded by Õ(L_U |A| T^{1/4}(Δ(φ̄)+1)^{3/4} + L_U √(|A|T(Δ(φ̄)+1)) + J(L_C+L_C²)|A|√(T(Δ(φ̄)+1))), which is sublinear whenever Δ(φ̄) = o(T). For the static case (Δ=0), this reduces to Õ(√T) regret and Õ(√T) cumulative constraint violation. Critically, the dependence on the number of subsequences |S| is only logarithmic (hidden in Õ), in contrast to the linear dependence in prior work, enabling non-trivial dynamic regret bounds over all Ω(T²) contiguous intervals. For the zero-margin benchmark (λ=0), the bounds degrade to Õ(T^{2/3}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Bechavod et al. (2025), the direct predecessor that introduced online omniprediction with long-term constraints. The key quantitative difference is the dependence on the number of subsequences |S|: Bechavod et al.'s bounds scale linearly in |S|, while this paper's bounds scale only logarithmically (hidden in Õ notation). This distinction is decisive for dynamic regret: since dynamic regret requires guaranteeing low regret over all Ω(T²) contiguous intervals, the linear dependence of Bechavod et al. yields trivial (linear-in-T) bounds, whereas the logarithmic dependence here yields the non-trivial Õ(√T) dynamic regret guarantee. The illustrative experiment in Appendix G confirms this theoretically predicted gap empirically: as the number of subsequences grows, the elimination-based method of Bechavod et al. accumulates substantially larger cumulative constraint violation, while the proposed stateless constrained-best-response method keeps CCV small and maintains low constrained external regret. Additionally, this paper's downstream agents are entirely stateless (no need to track which actions have violated constraints), whereas Bechavod et al. require agents to maintain an elimination set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct follow-up to Bechavod et al. (2025), which introduced omniprediction with long-term constraints and gave static regret guarantees with a linear dependence on the number of subsequences. The key technical departure is achieving only logarithmic dependence on the number of subsequences via a new decision-plus-infeasibility calibration framework built on the UNBIASED-PREDICTION algorithm of Noarov et al. (2025), which is what makes dynamic regret bounds (requiring ~T² contiguous intervals) non-trivial. The paper also extends the dynamic regret literature (Chen et al., Vaze, Lekefack & Jordan) from single-agent online convex optimization to the multi-agent omniprediction setting, and introduces dynamic swap regret as a strictly stronger benchmark than the previously studied dynamic external regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to provide simultaneous dynamic regret and vanishing constraint violation guarantees for multiple downstream decision makers in a prediction-as-a-service setting, which is practically relevant for scenarios like energy management where different consumers face different constraints and time-varying feasibility. The stateless decision rule (constrained best response to predictions) is a significant practical simplification over the elimination-based stateful algorithm of Bechavod et al. (2025). Limitations include the restriction to discrete action spaces and linear/affine utility functions, and the zero-margin (standard) benchmark yields weaker T^{2/3} rates rather than the T^{1/2} rates achieved with positive margins. Open questions include extending to continuous action spaces, non-linear utilities, and whether the T^{2/3} zero-margin rate can be improved.
