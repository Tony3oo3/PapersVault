## Problem

Geometry formalization in Lean has been fragmented from the broader MATHLIB ecosystem: existing efforts (LeanEuclid, LeanGeo) rely on custom axiom systems (System E) with SMT-based automation, creating a 'logical island' incompatible with standard MATHLIB and increasing the trusted computing base. These datasets are also far too small (<1,100 problems) to support the large-scale training that enabled breakthroughs in algebra and number theory. Native MATHLIB autoformalization of geometry introduces additional challenges—implicit diagrammatic assumptions (topological configuration, non-degeneracy) must be made explicit as compilation guards rather than deferred to external solvers.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes text-only natural language geometry problem statements (no diagrams) and produces syntactically valid Lean 4 theorem statements grounded in native MATHLIB, specifically using `Plane := EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2)`. Each output theorem explicitly encodes non-degeneracy hypotheses (e.g., `AffineIndependent ℝ ![A, B, C]`), topological ordering constraints (e.g., `Sbtw ℝ D A B` for strict betweenness), and positivity conditions (e.g., `r > 0` for circle radii) that are implicit in informal statements. The formalizations are verified by the Lean 4 compiler (v4.17.0-rc1) and are immediately usable by general-purpose MATHLIB-trained provers without any geometry-specific retraining.

## Method

EUCLEAN is a four-stage LLM-based pipeline (using DeepSeek-V3) that progressively transforms informal geometry into explicit MATHLIB formalizations: (1) Constraint Explication identifies and injects implicit topological and non-degeneracy conditions; (2) Configuration Anchoring via 'Prove-First' reasoning generates an informal proof sketch to resolve spatial ambiguities and commit to a specific geometric configuration; (3) Formalization Mapping retrieves canonical MATHLIB primitives and associates required preconditions through a Geometry-MATHLIB Alignment mechanism; (4) Iterative Repair feeds Lean compiler error messages back to the model for syntax and type correction. The key design choice is grounding geometry in analytic MATHLIB (EuclideanSpace over ℝ²) rather than synthetic axiom systems, which eliminates custom axioms and SMT dependencies, enabling direct knowledge transfer from general-purpose provers like Goedel v2 (13.6% base proof success without geometry-specific training) and supporting large-scale dataset construction (177,597 problems in Numina-Geometry).

## Results

On human evaluation of 180 OMNI-Geometry problems (difficulty ≥ 4.0), EUCLEAN achieves 48.89% TOP1 and 73.33% TOP5 semantic accuracy, with performance strongest in the 4.0–6.0 difficulty band (50.94% TOP1, 77.36% TOP5) and declining to 20.00% TOP1 / 60.00% TOP5 at difficulty 8.0+. For downstream neural theorem proving, the base Goedel v2 model (no geometry-specific training) already achieves 13.6% Pass@1 on Numina-Geometry, and SFT on the dataset improves this to 15.1%, confirming MATHLIB-native transferability. The pipeline retains 98.5% of OMNI-Geometry and 96.6% of Numina-Geometry problems after compiler-guided iterative repair.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EUCLEAN is compared against three prior geometry-in-Lean efforts: LeanEuclid (173 problems, System E axioms, SMT-dependent), LeanGeo (~200 problems, extended System E, SMT-dependent), and MATP-BENCH (1,056 multimodal problems, mixed prover/library). EUCLEAN dominates on scale by over two orders of magnitude (178,365 vs. <1,100 problems), matching the scale of LeanWorkbook (57K–83K) that enabled breakthroughs in algebra. Critically, EUCLEAN is the only approach that uses pure MATHLIB without custom axioms or external SMT solvers, meaning its formalizations are immediately compatible with general-purpose neural provers (Goedel v2, DeepSeek-Prover-V2) without retraining on a separate axiom system. The downstream experiment confirms this advantage: Goedel v2 achieves 13.6% Pass@1 on Numina-Geometry out-of-the-box, a result impossible with System E–based formalizations that require dedicated solvers. No head-to-head accuracy comparison on a shared benchmark is provided, as the baselines operate in incompatible formal systems; the comparison is structural (ecosystem compatibility, scale, verification guarantees) rather than a single-metric race.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant geometry-in-Lean approach (LeanEuclid, LeanGeo) that relies on System E's custom axiomatic framework and external SMT solvers, instead formalizing geometry natively in MATHLIB using EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2) with explicit non-degeneracy guards. Conceptually, it repurposes the LLM-based autoformalization pipeline (constraint explication, retrieval-based mapping, iterative repair) that has proven effective for algebra in LeanWorkbook, but adapts it to the stricter requirements of MATHLIB's analytic geometry where implicit diagrammatic assumptions become rigid compilation barriers. The 'Prove-First' configuration anchoring strategy is a novel methodological addition not present in prior autoformalization work, designed to resolve topological ambiguities before formal mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding geometry in standard MATHLIB rather than isolated axiom systems, this work reduces the trusted computing base and enables a single neural theorem prover to handle geometry alongside algebra and number theory without domain-specific retraining—demonstrated by Goedel v2's immediate 13.6% success rate without geometry-specific training. However, the modest downstream gain (13.6%→15.1%) and 48.89% TOP1 formalization accuracy reveal that the bottleneck is not formalization format but the scarcity of high-quality geometry proof traces in existing corpora. Open questions include whether frontier provers (Aristotle proved 25/100 sampled problems) can generate sufficient proof traces to close this gap, and whether the text-only approach (no diagram parsing) limits coverage to a mathematically rigorous subset of geometry problems.
