## Problem

Existing noise-robust cross-modal hashing methods rely on either hard noise separation (discarding mislabeled instances) or label smoothing (relaxing targets into soft distributions), both of which fail under high noise rates: separation becomes brittle when clean and noisy confidence distributions overlap, while smoothing blurs discriminative boundaries. The paper asks how to extract reliable discriminative supervision from noisy labels without sacrificing data utilization or class separability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-extracted features: 4096-d CNN activations (fc7) for images and either sparse BoW vectors or 300-d Doc2Vec embeddings for text, paired with potentially noisy one-hot category labels. A 3-layer MLP encoder per modality maps these into L-bit binary hash codes (16–128 bits) in a shared Hamming space, where retrieval is performed via bitwise XOR. The key constraint is that the continuous relaxation (tanh) is used during training while sign() produces the final binary codes at inference.

## Method

RFCMH introduces fuzzy set theory into cross-modal hashing via a two-stage (warm-up → fine-tuning) training strategy. The Fuzzy Admissibility Refinement (FAR) module computes per-category possibility scores via a sigmoid head and a label-aware necessity measure that quantifies semantic exclusivity against competing categories; their weighted combination yields an admissibility score that is first aligned with annotations (warm-up) and then used to derive reliability-weighted margin constraints that suppress noisy categories (fine-tuning). The Dual-Granularity Structural Alignment (DGSA) module enforces intra-instance cross-modal consistency via a q-contrastive surrogate (whose gradient grows strictly slower than 1/p, stabilizing optimization of near-binary representations) and inter-instance separation via a margin-violating-negative LSE loss. Together, FAR provides noise-robust soft supervision while DGSA preserves well-structured embedding geometry in the shared Hamming space.

## Results

On INRIA-Websearch (I2T, 128-bit), RFCMH achieves mAP of 55.2, 54.7, 54.9, and 52.0 at noise rates 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, and 0.8 respectively, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline RSHNL (52.4, 51.6, 49.0, 42.9) by 2.8–9.1 points. On XMediaNet (I2T, 128-bit), the advantage is most pronounced: RFCMH reaches 54.0 mAP at 0.8 noise versus 38.9 for RSHNL and 19.5 for NRCH. On XMedia (I2T, 128-bit), RFCMH maintains 90.2 mAP at 0.8 noise while RSHNL drops to 84.7 and NRCH to 82.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFCMH outperforms all 12 baselines across every noise rate, code length, and dataset. The closest competitor is RSHNL (self-paced hashing), which RFCMH surpasses by 2.8–9.1 mAP points on INRIA-Websearch and by 5.1–15.1 points on XMediaNet at high noise. Noise-robust baselines using hard sample separation (NRCH, NACD) remain relatively stable but plateau well below RFCMH (e.g., NRCH: 40.5 vs. RFCMH: 52.0 at 0.8 noise on INRIA-Websearch). General supervised baselines (HMAH, WASH, DECH) degrade catastrophically at 0.6–0.8 noise, with DECH collapsing to 3.0 mAP on INRIA-Websearch. On XMediaNet (200 classes), the gap is most extreme: RFCMH achieves 54.0 mAP at 0.8 noise while NRCH manages only 19.5 and NACD only 7.6, illustrating that hard separation strategies fail when semantic boundaries are ambiguous across many categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for noise-robust cross-modal hashing—hard noise separation (e.g., NRCH, RSHNL) and uniform label smoothing (e.g., CMMQ, DHaPH)—by introducing fuzzy set theory's possibility-necessity duality as a principled middle ground. Rather than discarding uncertain samples or uniformly softening all labels, RFCMH assigns category-wise admissibility scores that explicitly encode semantic exclusivity, drawing on Zadeh's fuzzy logic framework repurposed for supervised hashing. The Dual-Granularity Structural Alignment module further extends contrastive hashing by replacing the standard InfoNCE log-loss with a q-contrastive surrogate specifically designed to stabilize optimization on near-binary tanh representations, a practical gap not addressed by prior noise-robust CMH methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-flat performance curve across noise rates (e.g., ~90 mAP on XMedia from 0.2 to 0.8 noise) is practically significant for real-world retrieval systems where annotation quality is uncontrollable. The q-contrastive stabilization for near-binary representations is a transferable technique applicable beyond this specific framework. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that it does not handle cross-modal noisy correspondence (mismatched image-text pairs), which in practice co-occurs with label noise, leaving a joint uncertainty modeling problem open. The fuzzy set framing, while conceptually clean, reduces to a weighted combination of two scalar scores, so the theoretical depth is modest relative to the empirical gains.
