## Problem

Existing tool-augmented multimodal reasoning methods (e.g., DeepEyes, OpenThinkIMG) focus on teaching models *how* to invoke tools but ignore *whether* tool invocation is necessary. Blindly encouraging tool usage inflates training and inference costs by ~20% and introduces redundant or misleading visual cues that cause hallucinated responses, particularly on simple queries where global understanding suffices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263177/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question paired with an RGB image (ranging from 2K to 8K resolution for perception tasks) and first emits a mode-selection token (<tool_on> or <tool_off>) before generating its reasoning trajectory. In tool-on mode, it outputs a bounding-box crop request via a structured tool-call, receives the zoomed image as an observation, and continues reasoning; in tool-off mode, it produces a pure text chain-of-thought. Final outputs are a text answer (for QA, hallucination, and reasoning tasks) or a bounding box in JSON format (for grounding tasks), all wrapped in prescribed thinking and answer tags.

## Method

AutoTool introduces an explicit dual-mode reasoning paradigm where two special tokens (<tool_on>, <tool_off>) gate whether the model invokes a zoom-in tool or reasons purely in text space. Training uses GRPO reinforcement learning (no SFT cold-start) with Mode-Specific Policy Optimization (MSPO): the tool-on mode rewards correct tool invocations paired with correct answers but penalizes (R_tool = −0.5) tool invocations that yield wrong answers, while the tool-off mode rewards only tool-free correct answers. To counter the foundation model's inherent bias toward the easier tool-off mode, Adaptive Mode Balancing (AMB) dynamically adjusts the tool-reward coefficient based on the observed frequency of each mode in the current batch, enforcing roughly 50/50 exploration in early-to-mid training. In the final ~20 training steps, the AMB constraint is removed so the model freely selects its preferred mode per query. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL-7B trained for 80 GRPO iterations on 8 H200 GPUs with a Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct reward model for semantic answer verification.

## Results

AutoTool (7B) achieves 90.1% overall accuracy on V*, a 21.0-point gain over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (69.1%), and 76.9% / 74.0% overall on HRbench-4K / HRbench-8K. On hallucination detection (POPE), it reaches 88.9% overall accuracy, surpassing DeepEyes (86.0%) and the base model (87.2%). In terms of efficiency, AutoTool reduces training time by 20.3% (35.8h vs. 44.9h) and inference time by up to 44.9% (on POPE-Random) compared to DeepEyes, while maintaining or improving accuracy across all perception, grounding, and reasoning benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoTool (7B) outperforms the closest same-size competitor DeepEyes (7B) on every perception metric: V* Overall 90.1 vs. 87.4 (+2.7), HRbench-4K Overall 76.9 vs. 74.9 (+2.0), and HRbench-8K Overall 74.0 vs. 71.5 (+2.5), while also reducing training time by 20.3% and inference time by up to 44.9%. Against much larger general MLLMs, AutoTool surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-32B (79.1) and InternVL3-38B (78.0) on V* Overall by 11.0 and 12.1 points respectively, and outperforms GPT-4o (67.5) by 22.6 points, though it trails OpenAI o3 (95.7) by 5.6 points. On grounding (RefCOCO/ReasonSeg), AutoTool leads both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and DeepEyes across all splits (e.g., RefCOCO test 88.5 vs. 86.0 vs. 84.7). On hallucination (POPE Overall), AutoTool achieves 88.9%, beating DeepEyes (86.0%) and the base model (87.2%), and on reasoning benchmarks (MathVista 72.8, MathVerse 45.9) it consistently edges out DeepEyes and the base model. The only baseline that surpasses AutoTool on any reported metric is o3 on V* Overall, and ZoomEye on V* Attribute (93.9 vs. 91.3), though AutoTool leads ZoomEye on V* Spatial (88.2 vs. 85.5) and both HRbench splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based tool-augmented multimodal reasoning line initiated by DeepEyes and OpenThinkIMG, which train MLLMs to invoke visual tools (e.g., zoom-in) via GRPO but treat tool usage as universally necessary. AutoTool departs from this by introducing explicit dual-mode reasoning (<tool_on>/<tool_off>) with mode-specific reward shaping (MSPO) and adaptive mode balancing (AMB), shifting the question from 'how to invoke tools correctly' to 'whether to invoke tools at all.' The zoom-in tool mechanism itself derives from V* guided visual search, and the RL optimization follows the GRPO framework from DeepSeekMath, but the adaptive mode-selection objective and the penalty for tool-then-incorrect-answer trajectories are novel contributions not present in prior tool-use RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption in MCoT literature that more tool invocations always improve reasoning, showing concretely that redundant or misdirected tool calls increase hallucination and computational cost. The practical impact is significant: a 7B model achieves SOTA perception accuracy while cutting training time by ~20% and inference time by up to 45% compared to always-invoke baselines, making tool-augmented reasoning more deployable. Open questions include whether the dual-mode framework generalizes to multi-step agentic tool use beyond a single zoom-in call (partially addressed via DeepEyesV2), whether the 72B reward model dependency can be reduced without quality loss, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D modalities where tool necessity is even less obvious.
