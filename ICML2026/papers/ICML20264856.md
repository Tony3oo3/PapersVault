## Problem

RL-trained multimodal search agents suffer from premature interaction collapse caused by two issues: the terminal reward appended to the last token prevents the GRPO advantage from distinguishing trajectories that share the same scalar reward but differ in CoT length, and excessively redundant tool-response context causes the agent to prematurely terminate multi-turn reasoning. As training progresses, diverse trajectories shrink while repetitive ones proliferate, causing advantage signals to lose discriminative power and the model to converge toward suboptimal two-turn tool interactions.

## Input / Output

The agent takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a concise text answer (capped at 30 words) wrapped in structured XML tags, preceded by a multi-turn interaction trace that alternates between think, tool-call, observe, and summary blocks. During training, the agent interacts with local text-search and image-search tool services (top-3 results per query) that are decoupled from the training loop and built from sliced web-page content and cached images, while evaluation uses online search services. A refining agent compresses raw tool outputs by roughly 82% before they re-enter the context window.

## Method

The core contribution is a co-optimized RL training framework built on GRPO with Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the backbone. First, Structural Proximity-weighted Advantage Injection (SPAI) constructs a G×T reward-position matrix across the entire batch, normalizes it, then applies a TOPSIS-inspired closeness score F_i that measures each trajectory's Euclidean distance to virtual positive and negative ideal solutions; this score asymmetrically modulates the original advantage, amplifying negative signals for the bottom N% of low-reward samples to accelerate exploration. Second, a Bidirectionally Guided Adaptive Smoothing (BGAS) reward uses two Gaussian kernels with dynamically switched parameters (mu, sigma) based on answer correctness: a low-mean, wide-variance regime penalizes unnecessary tool calls when the answer is correct, while a high-mean, narrow-variance regime encourages deeper search when it is incorrect. The final reward is a weighted sum (0.7 accuracy + 0.2 format + 0.1 tool efficiency), and a refining agent performs real-time compression of redundant tool contexts. Training uses the newly constructed BridgeVQA dataset (3,602 multi-hop QA pairs with at least 3 reasoning steps) alongside FVQA-train, with 8 rollouts per problem, batch size 128, and up to 14 interaction rounds over 2 epochs.

## Results

On the primary search-agent benchmark suite, DR-MMSearchAgent achieves an average accuracy of 57.6% across five knowledge-intensive VQA benchmarks, with headline results of 66.8% on FVQA-test, 60.0% on InfoSeek, 63.2% on SimpleVQA, 37.0% on LiveVQA, and 61.0% on MMSearch. On non-search visual reasoning tasks (Table 2), the SPAI mechanism alone yields 61.6% average across MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, ChartQA, and HallusionBench, outperforming all open-source 7B baselines. The method also converges in 160 training steps versus 200 for vanilla GRPO and 225 for DAPO under identical configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest search-agent baseline MMSearch-R1, DR-MMSearchAgent wins on all five benchmarks, with the largest gap on FVQA-test (+8.4 pp, 66.8 vs 58.4) and LiveVQA (+8.7 pp, 37.0 vs 28.3). Versus DeepEyesV2 (which combines SFT + RL), the proposed RL-only method surpasses it on FVQA-test (+6.2 pp), InfoSeek (+8.9 pp), and SimpleVQA (+3.8 pp), but trails by 2.7 pp on MMSearch (61.0 vs 63.7). Compared to DeepMMSearch-R1 and WebWatcher, gains are substantial on the overlapping benchmarks (e.g., +12.5 pp on InfoSeek over DeepMMSearch-R1). On non-search visual tasks (Table 2), SPAI-equipped Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms ThinkLite-VL-7B by 2.0 pp average, VL-Rethinker-7B by 0.5 pp on MathVista, and GPT-4o by 10.4 pp on MathVista, though it marginally trails GPT-4o on MathVerse (50.6 vs 50.8). Against vanilla GRPO as an RL algorithm, the full framework improves FVQA-test accuracy from 57.7% to 66.8% while reducing training steps from 200 to 160.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMSearch-R1 paradigm of training multimodal search agents via GRPO-based reinforcement learning, identifying a specific failure mode—exploration collapse caused by terminal-only reward placement and context redundancy—that prior work overlooked. The SPAI mechanism draws a creative but non-obvious connection to TOPSIS (a multi-criteria decision-making method from operations research), repurposing its ideal-solution distance framework to inject structural proximity signals into advantage estimation across an entire batch of rollouts. The BGAS reward and the BridgeVQA dataset (3,602 multi-hop QA pairs) represent the paper's additional departures from MMSearch-R1's two-turn FVQA training setup, pushing toward genuinely multi-step (≥3 hop) reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that terminal reward placement erodes advantage diversity over training is a practically important insight for anyone training agentic RL models, and the SPAI mechanism offers a generalizable fix (validated on non-search math/chart tasks). The demonstration that a free local tool service matches or exceeds Google-based pipelines at a fraction of the cost has direct deployment implications. Open questions include whether the TOPSIS-inspired global batch comparison scales to much larger rollout groups or longer-horizon tasks, and whether the fixed Gaussian inductive biases in BGAS remain optimal as model capability grows. The 8.4% FVQA gain, while solid, is measured against a single primary baseline (MMSearch-R1), and the LiveVQA gap (37.0 vs. 28.3) suggests temporal-reasoning tasks remain substantially unsolved.
