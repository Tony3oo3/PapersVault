## Problem

Activations account for ~89% of total memory in large-batch LLM training, yet existing compression methods fail to exploit their spectral structure: principal-subspace methods (e.g., GaLore) introduce systematic bias that prevents convergence, while random-subspace methods (e.g., RSO) are unbiased but suffer high variance that slows training. No prior method simultaneously achieves unbiasedness and minimum variance for activation compression.

## Input / Output

During the forward pass, PRAC consumes the full activation matrix X ∈ R^{b×s×n} and produces two low-rank projections XQ₁ ∈ R^{b×s×r₁} and k·XQ₂ ∈ R^{b×s×r₂} along with the projection bases Q₁, Q₂, which are cached for the backward pass. During backpropagation, the stored components are combined to reconstruct X̃ = XQ₁Q₁ᵀ + k·XQ₂Q₂ᵀ, yielding an unbiased gradient estimator. Projection matrices are shared across layers that receive the same input (e.g., Q/K/V projections) and updated only every T₁ or T₂ steps, making their memory footprint negligible relative to the batch-dependent activations.

## Method

PRAC decomposes each activation into a principal component (top-r₁ right singular vectors obtained via SVD, capturing dominant spectral energy) and a random component (r₂ orthonormal vectors sampled from the orthogonal complement of Q₁ via QR of (I−Q₁Q₁ᵀ)S, approximating the long tail). A scaling factor k = (n−r₁)/r₂ is applied to the random component to guarantee unbiased reconstruction. Under the Activation Degenerate Condition (top s singular values dominate; tail energy bounded by q), the paper proves PRAC achieves the minimum variance among all unbiased linear estimators, matching a derived lower bound. In practice, a dynamic update schedule refreshes Q₁ and Q₂ at fixed intervals (e.g., every 500 steps) to amortize SVD/QR costs, and a layer-wise policy allocates higher ranks to linear layers (MLP, attention projections) and lower ranks to non-linear layers (LayerNorm, GeLU), achieving up to 36% total memory reduction with negligible performance loss.

## Results

On LLaMA-1B pre-training (batch 128, seq 256), PRAC achieves a validation PPL of 15.41 with 60.48 GB peak memory, a 36% total memory reduction versus the baseline (which OOMs on a single A800). Across all five model scales (LLaMA 130M–1B, GPT-2 124M–355M), PRAC reduces activation memory by 20–36% while matching or exceeding baseline PPL. On the GLUE fine-tuning benchmark (RoBERTa-Base), PRAC at rank 4 achieves an average score of 86.38 with 38% less activation memory than full fine-tuning (86.28 at 0.42 GB). Training efficiency on 4×A800 GPUs shows PRAC enables a 50% larger batch (64→96), cutting total training time by 25% (156 h → 117 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRAC consistently outperforms both GaLore (principal-only gradient compression) and RSO (random subspace optimization) across all five pre-training configurations. Against GaLore, PRAC achieves lower PPL (e.g., 18.92 vs. 19.65 on LLaMA-350M) while reducing memory far more aggressively (−30% vs. −2%); GaLore's principal-only projection introduces systematic bias that causes late-stage stagnation. Against RSO, PRAC matches or beats PPL (15.41 vs. 15.72 on LLaMA-1B) with substantially greater memory savings (−36% vs. −16%), because RSO's purely random subspace yields high gradient variance that slows convergence. On GLUE fine-tuning, PRAC (rank 4) surpasses both RSO (AVG 86.10) and full fine-tuning (86.28) at 86.38, while using 38% less activation memory than full fine-tuning. Compared to Gradient Checkpointing at matched memory (~32 GB on LLaMA-130M), PRAC reaches every target validation loss 15–20% faster and incurs only ~13% per-step overhead versus GC's ~30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRAC sits at the intersection of two existing subspace-based activation compression lines: principal-subspace methods (GaLore, which projects onto top singular vectors but introduces systematic bias) and random-subspace methods (RSO, which is unbiased but suffers from high variance due to ignoring activation spectral structure). The paper's key conceptual departure is proving that a hybrid projection—principal subspace from SVD plus a random subspace from the orthogonal complement, with a specific scaling factor k=(n-r₁)/r₂—achieves the minimum variance among all unbiased estimators under a formalized 'Activation Degenerate Condition.' This bridges the convergence-theory requirement (unbiased, low-variance gradient estimates) to a concrete, implementable compression design, going beyond prior work that treated bias and variance as separate, unaddressed trade-offs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since activations account for ~89% of peak memory in large-batch LLM training, a method that reduces them by 36% with negligible performance loss and minimal compute overhead has direct practical impact on training scalability and hardware accessibility. The theoretical optimality guarantee (minimum variance unbiased estimator) provides a principled design criterion rather than heuristic tuning, and the method's orthogonality to optimizer-level compression (GaLore, Adam-mini, Muon) means savings compound. Open questions remain: Flash Attention and other tightly-coupled modules are excluded, nonlinear compression schemes are unexplored, and whether the degenerate condition holds robustly across all architectures and training regimes at much larger scales (10B+) is untested.
