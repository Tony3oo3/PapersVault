## Problem

Existing GNN watermarking methods rely on backdoor-based data manipulation, which introduces ownership ambiguity (attackers can claim misclassified samples as their watermark) and vulnerability to data poisoning attacks. No prior method embeds watermarks in GNN explanations while preserving black-box verification, avoiding data modification, and addressing graph-specific structural complexities like multi-hop message passing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260962/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F}, a trained GNN f (black-box), T candidate subgraphs (each of size n_sub = ⌈s·|V^tr|⌉ nodes) presented by a purported owner, and a private M-dimensional watermark vector w ∈ {−1,+1}^M. It produces F-dimensional feature attribution vectors via Gaussian-kernel ridge regression, binarizes them, computes a Matching Index (MI) across the T explanations, and outputs an ownership verdict (True/False) based on whether the MI's z-score exceeds a significance threshold α_v, along with the MI p-value.

## Method

The core mechanism trains the GNN with a dual-objective loss: standard cross-entropy classification loss plus a hinge-like watermark loss that aligns the feature attribution vectors (explanations) of T privately selected subgraphs with a predefined ±1 watermark pattern w at M chosen feature indices. Explanations are computed via a closed-form ridge regression over Gaussian kernel matrices (inspired by GraphLIME but non-iterative), yielding F-dimensional attribution vectors in a single step. At verification time, the T candidate explanations are binarized via sign(), their Matching Index is computed, and a z-test against an empirically estimated natural MI distribution determines ownership. The watermark length M is analytically derived from binomial distribution estimates to guarantee the target MI exceeds the natural baseline at significance α_tgt = 1e-5, and the paper proves that locating the watermarked subgraphs is NP-hard even with full knowledge of the method.

## Results

Across four datasets (Photo, PubMed, CS, Reddit2) and four GNN architectures (GCN, SGC, SAGE, Graph Transformer), the method achieves MI p-values below 0.001 for all T ≥ 3, confirming statistically significant watermark uniqueness. Watermark alignment ranges from 73.8% (CS, GCN) to 97.7% (Photo, SAGE), while test accuracy remains within ~1–5% of non-watermarked baselines (e.g., Photo SAGE: 90.8% vs. 88.2%; PubMed SAGE: 86.0% vs. 81.2%). The watermark is robust to structured pruning (MI p-value only rises after classification accuracy degrades) and fine-tuning (near-zero p-value sustained for ≥25 epochs at 0.1× learning rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the backdoor-based GNN watermarking of Xu et al. (2023), the proposed method achieves consistent 5/5 verification passes across all seeds and datasets, whereas Xu et al. fails on seed 3 for both CORA and PubMed due to an undetectably small trigger value. After fine-tuning, both methods retain their watermarks, but the proposed method shows far lower variance in verification (z-scores of 3.56–7.83) compared to Xu et al.'s backdoor accuracy (std up to 0.353). Critically, the paper demonstrates that Xu et al.'s trigger is detectable via K-Means clustering on PCA-reduced features and can be surgically removed via targeted fine-tuning, dropping backdoor accuracy from 0.87–1.00 to 0.14–0.24, while the proposed method's watermark is embedded in explanation structure and has no detectable data artifact. Against Shao et al. (2024), the prior explanation-based DNN watermarking method, the proposed GNN-adapted version achieves a p-value of 4.00×10⁻¹⁰ on PubMed/GCN versus Shao et al.'s 0.8879 (which fails the significance test entirely), while maintaining comparable test accuracy (0.795 vs. 0.806).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends explanation-based watermarking from DNNs (Shao et al., 2024) to GNNs, replacing the backdoor-based paradigm that dominates GNN watermarking (Xu et al., 2023). It borrows the Gaussian kernel explanation mechanism from GraphLIME (Huang et al., 2023) but replaces iterative optimization with a closed-form ridge regression solution, and introduces a dual-objective training loss that aligns subgraph explanations with a binary watermark vector. The key conceptual departure from prior GNN watermarking is embedding ownership evidence in the model's explanation space rather than in manipulated training data, thereby eliminating the ownership ambiguity and data-poisoning vulnerabilities inherent to backdoor triggers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important gap: GNNs are deployed at scale by major companies (Amazon, Google, Meta, Alibaba), yet no prior method offered a statistically unambiguous, data-manipulation-free watermark for graph models. The NP-hardness proof for watermark location and the demonstrated robustness to pruning/fine-tuning provide strong guarantees, but the knowledge distillation defense remains somewhat ad hoc (injecting a linear perturbation into soft labels), and the method's scalability to very large graphs (e.g., Reddit2 with 232K nodes) is only lightly explored. Open questions include whether the explanation-based approach generalizes cleanly to graph-level tasks beyond the preliminary MUTAG result, and whether the ridge-regression explanation is sufficiently faithful to the GNN's true decision boundary for adversarial settings.
