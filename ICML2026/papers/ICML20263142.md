## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based data augmentation methods for RL (e.g., SynthER) generate transitions that, even when matching the original dataset in size, lack sufficient informative content for effective policy learning. The core gap is the absence of controllability in the synthesis process: there is no mechanism to prioritize generating high-value transitions that most benefit the agent's training, forcing practitioners to rely on substantially larger synthetic datasets to achieve adequate coverage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a transition dataset D of (s, a, r, s') tuples and a learned hashing-based state representation h(·) that maps F-dimensional states to K-bit binary codes (K ≤ F). For vision-based environments (VD4RL), 84×84×3 image observations are first encoded into a latent space via a pretrained CNN before being treated as state vectors. The output is a set of synthetic transitions (s, a, r, s') generated by the EM-based CDM, which can either entirely replace or upsample the original dataset for downstream RL training.

## Method

EMCES builds a controllable diffusion model (CDM) whose condition y(x) = [h(s), r + γe(h(s'))] combines a compact hashing-based state encoding with an EM-estimated optimal discounted return, providing a non-parametric value signal without neural Q-networks. During synthesis, an EM-prioritized condition sampling strategy computes an EMTD-error δ = r + γe(h(s')) − e(h(s)) for each transition and applies a softmax(βδ) distribution over conditions, steering generation toward transitions with the largest value-improvement potential while preserving diversity. The hashing-based state representation uses IsoHash to learn K projection functions and quantize them into binary codes, reducing storage by ~8000× and time by ~25.5× compared to random projection while improving EM update efficiency by grouping similar states. Training proceeds in three stages: learn hash codes from D, populate the EM lookup table Q^S with historical optimal discounted returns, then train the CDM denoiser on (D, C) pairs using the standard EDM L2 objective.

## Results

On D4RL proprioceptive environments, EMCES achieves total normalized scores of 731.4 (TD3+BC), 763.7 (IQL), and 837.0 (EDAC), surpassing SynthER (683.5, 680.0, 791.4) and matching or exceeding original-data baselines (708.5, 754.6, 837.7). On VD4RL vision-based environments, EMCES reaches an average episode reward of 487.82 with BC and 518.00 with DrQ+BC, outperforming both original data (440.08, 414.50) and SynthER (441.84, 472.38). In online RL, SAC (EMCES) consistently outperforms SAC (SynthER) and SAC (PGR) across all six DMC and Gym environments over 200K/100K steps. The hashing-based state representation reduces EM storage cost by ~8000× and time cost by ~25.5× relative to random projection without degrading performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SynthER (the primary transition-level DM baseline), EMCES wins on the majority of D4RL tasks: TD3+BC sum improves from 683.5 to 731.4, IQL from 680.0 to 763.7, and EDAC from 791.4 to 837.0; on VD4RL, average reward rises from 441.84 to 487.82 (BC) and 472.38 to 518.00 (DrQ+BC). Against RTDiff (a trajectory-level diffusion method), EMCES achieves substantially higher sums on D4RL (e.g., TD3+BC 731.4 vs. 696.2; IQL 763.7 vs. 649.3; EDAC 837.0 vs. 721.5), confirming that transition-level controllable synthesis outperforms trajectory-level approaches. In online RL, SAC (EMCES) consistently outperforms both SAC (SynthER) and SAC (PGR) across all six environments (quadruped-walk, reacher-hard, cheetah-run, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Hopper), with the gap most pronounced in early training where sample efficiency matters most. Compared to REDQ (a sample-efficient SAC variant with UTD=20), SAC (EMCES) matches or exceeds performance in most environments while using a lower UTD ratio, indicating that the quality of synthetic data compensates for fewer gradient updates. EMCES also matches or slightly exceeds the original-data baseline in aggregate (e.g., IQL 763.7 vs. 754.6; EDAC 837.0 vs. 837.7), demonstrating that the synthetic data is at least as informative as the original dataset at equal size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the controllable diffusion model (CDM) framework of Ho & Salimans (2021) and the EDM training/sampling procedure of Karras et al. (2022), repurposing them for transition-level experience synthesis in RL. It departs from SynthER (Lu et al., 2023b), which generates uncontrolled transitions, and from PGR (Wang et al., 2025), which guides synthesis via curiosity or value but only in online settings, by introducing episodic memory (EM) as the control signal for the CDM. The hashing-based state representation draws on IsoHash (Kong & Li, 2012) and the state-based EM lookup table design of Ma & Li (2023), but is the first to embed EM into a CDM and use EM-derived TD errors to prioritize which conditions to sample during generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMCES addresses the persistent data-scarcity bottleneck in RL by showing that *which* transitions are synthesized matters as much as *how many*, and that a non-parametric EM mechanism can provide stable, training-free value estimates to guide a diffusion model toward high-utility data. The ~8000× storage and ~25.5× time reduction from the hashing-based state representation makes EM-based conditioning practically deployable at scale. Open questions include whether the EMTD-error prioritization generalizes to sparse-reward or multi-agent settings, how the method behaves when the underlying dataset is highly non-stationary, and whether the periodic retraining requirement in online RL limits its applicability to very long-horizon or safety-critical deployments.
