## Problem

Learned optimizers have been extensively explored for first-order methods, but learned second-order optimization remains underexplored. Classical SR1 quasi-Newton updates do not guarantee positive semi-definite preconditioners, risking non-descent directions, while naive PSD fixes (e.g., symmetric outer products) break the secant constraint. The paper addresses this by introducing a learned SR1 variant that is PSD by construction and secant-consistent through meta-training, without requiring explicit projection at inference.

## Input / Output

In the general optimization setting, the optimizer receives the current iterate, step, descent direction, gradient, and gradient difference (concatenated into an N×5 tensor) and produces a rank-one direction vector and coordinate-wise learning-rate vector at each inner step. For HMR, the pipeline takes a single RGB image with OpenPose 2D keypoints and outputs SMPL shape (β) and pose (θ) parameters after a fixed number of inner iterations. All learnable modules operate element-wise across the N coordinates, making the optimizer dimension-agnostic and transferable across problem sizes without re-training.

## Method

L-SR1 integrates three lightweight MLP modules (Input Encoder, Vector Generator, Learning Rate Generator) into the classical SR1 framework, all operating element-wise to ensure dimension invariance. The core innovation is the Projection-Guided Secant Mechanism (PGSM): the preconditioner is parameterized as B₀ + Σ vᵢvᵢᵀ (PSD by construction via symmetric outer products), and a secant-violation penalty ‖pₖ − B̃ₖqₖ‖² is added to the meta-training loss, motivated by least-squares projection onto the PSD cone but never solved explicitly at inference. A limited-memory buffer of capacity L stores the most recent learned vectors, and coordinate-wise learning rates are parameterized as γ₁·exp(γ₂·α̃ₖ) for numerical stability. Meta-training unrolls K inner optimization steps and backpropagates a combined meta-loss (objective values + secant penalty) through the computation graph using AdamW, requiring no task-specific fine-tuning or annotated data at test time.

## Results

On the 3DPW benchmark, L-SR1 (opt-horizon) achieves 51.58 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming LGD (55.90 mm) by 4.3 mm while using a smaller model (10.4M vs. 17.4M parameters) and, at the 4-step horizon, running in 364 ms versus LGD's 664 ms. L-SR1 with PGSM also achieves the highest overall performance profile across a 30-problem analytic benchmark suite (quadratics, Rosenbrock, Rastrigin at dimensions 50–1000), surpassing L-BFGS, Adam, and AdaHessian. On quadratic functions, L-SR1 with PGSM shows steadily increasing cosine similarity to the Newton direction and lower AUC than the variant without the secant penalty, confirming the benefit of PGSM across dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among optimization-based HMR methods, L-SR1 (opt-horizon, 51.58 mm) outperforms LGD (55.90 mm) by 4.3 mm PA-MPJPE, SPIN (59.20 mm) by 7.6 mm, and SMPLify (106.80 mm) by over 55 mm, all while being trained exclusively on AMASS. At the 4-step horizon, L-SR1 (51.74 mm, 364 ms) beats LGD (55.90 mm, 664 ms) in both accuracy and wall-clock time, and uses 40% fewer parameters (10.4M vs. 17.4M). The PGSM ablation shows that the secant penalty contributes ~1.2 mm improvement (51.58 vs. 52.80 mm) over the no-projection variant. Against regression-based methods (TRACE 50.8, WHAM 35.9, PromptHMR 36.6), L-SR1 is competitive with TRACE but trails WHAM and PromptHMR; however, those methods use 3DPW in training and are substantially larger. On the analytic performance-profile benchmark (30 problems, dimensions 50–1000), L-SR1 with PGSM achieves the highest overall profile, outperforming L-BFGS, Adam, and AdaHessian across the full suite of quadratic, Rosenbrock, and Rastrigin objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-SR1 extends the classical Symmetric Rank-One (SR1) quasi-Newton update by replacing its deterministic rank-one correction with a learned vector generator, embedding the update within the model-based deep learning paradigm (Shlezinger et al., 2023). It departs from prior learned second-order optimizers (Gärtner et al., 2023; Liao et al., 2023) that either use PSD outer products v·vᵀ without secant enforcement or learn full preconditioning matrices, by introducing PGSM: a PSD-by-construction rank-one parameterization paired with a meta-training secant-violation penalty motivated by least-squares projection onto the PSD cone, without solving an explicit projection at inference. The HMR evaluation builds directly on the learned-gradient-descent pipeline of Song et al. (2020), swapping their first-order inner loop for the L-SR1 second-order update while retaining the same self-supervised training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L-SR1 offers a compact (10.4M params), data-efficient learned second-order optimizer that outperforms both classical (L-BFGS) and learned (LGD) baselines on HMR without task-specific fine-tuning, and generalizes across problem dimensions without re-training—addressing a real gap where learned second-order methods have received far less attention than first-order ones. The data-efficiency result (10% of AMASS still beating full-data LGD) and the dimension-transfer property (N=2 training → N=10 testing) suggest the approach could reduce annotation and compute costs in iterative optimization pipelines more broadly. Open questions include whether the meta-training distribution (random quadratics, AMASS) limits transfer to markedly different objectives, whether the additional hyperparameters (buffer size, unroll depth, λ_sec) scale gracefully, and whether the per-iteration cost premium over first-order methods remains acceptable as problem dimensionality grows further.
