## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion methods cannot generate complete, semantically coherent images confined to arbitrary-shaped canvas regions (e.g., product packaging, logo design). Prior approaches either require explicit per-object layouts, are restricted to rectangular bounding boxes, or rely on discrete/discontinuous spatial constraints that fail on irregular geometries while degrading semantic fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and a binary mask (H×W) defining the target canvas region as input, and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image whose salient content is spatially confined within the mask. The mask is pre-processed with a 10-pixel prohibited border to prevent spurious edge minima. Generation uses 50 DDIM steps on a pre-trained latent diffusion model (e.g., SDXL) without any fine-tuning, and the same mask interface supports both content-filling and content-suppression tasks by simply inverting the mask polarity.

## Method

AnyCanvas introduces a Mask-to-Potential Field paradigm that converts a binary mask into a continuously differentiable energy field via a Signed Distance Function, where the interior forms an attractive potential well (Φ < 0) and the exterior a repulsive barrier (Φ > 0). During each denoising step, the negative gradient of this field at the cross-attention centroid determines a displacement direction, while a global potential offset (current Φ minus the mask minimum) controls displacement magnitude through an exponential schedule, decoupling motion from raw gradient instability. An adaptive isotropic scaling factor, computed from the potential at the predicted post-translation centroid, contracts the attention map to conform to irregular contours. These translation and scaling operations are composed into an affine transformation applied selectively only to text tokens whose attention maps exhibit a high conflict ratio with the mask, preserving semantic fidelity for well-aligned tokens. The entire mechanism is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification to the base diffusion model.

## Results

AnyCanvas achieves the best ICQS (harmonic mean of CLIP Score and Canvas-IoU) across all three benchmarks: 32.68 on PTP, 35.67 on DiffusionDB, and 33.49 on PartiPrompts. Compared to the SDXL baseline, AnyCanvas gains 5.68%, 2.93%, and 4.07% relative improvement in Canvas-IoU on PTP, DDB, and P2 respectively, while retaining 99.45% of the base model's CLIP score. The method also generalizes across architectures, yielding 4.70% ICQS improvement on SD2.0, 4.38% on SDXL, and 4.14% on the DiT-based PixArt-α.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyCanvas outperforms all four main baselines on the comprehensive ICQS metric across all three datasets. Against SDXL, it improves ICQS by 1.37, 1.19, and 1.47 points on PTP, DDB, and P2 respectively, driven by spatial adherence gains while preserving semantic quality. FLUX.1 achieves the highest C-IoU on DDB (45.52) and P2 (41.32) but suffers a significant CLIP score drop (29.28 and 31.50 vs. ~32.1–32.2 for AnyCanvas), resulting in lower ICQS; AnyCanvas wins the trade-off on all three datasets. TextCenGen, designed for rectangular bounding boxes, underperforms AnyCanvas on ICQS by 0.97–2.05 points, confirming its unsuitability for irregular shapes. MultiDiffusion trails AnyCanvas on every metric across all datasets, with the largest ICQS gap of 3.13 points on PTP. In the appendix comparison, FreeControl achieves very high C-IoU (55–62) but at a severe CLIP cost (24–27), yielding lower ICQS than AnyCanvas; bounding-box methods LVD and BoxDiff also fall short on irregular shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the cross-attention mechanisms of pre-trained latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) for spatial control, extending the line of training-free attention-modulation methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, TextCenGen) that were previously restricted to rectangular bounding boxes. It departs from optimization-based approaches (MultiDiffusion, FreeControl, BoxDiff) that enforce discrete or discontinuous spatial constraints, instead introducing a continuous, differentiable potential field derived from the Signed Distance Function of a binary mask. The key conceptual departure is framing spatial guidance as a physics-inspired energy minimization problem, where the negative gradient of the potential field drives an affine transformation (translation + scaling) of cross-attention maps at each denoising step, rather than directly optimizing latents or applying hard mask constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyCanvas addresses a practical gap in T2I generation for applications like product packaging and logo design where content must conform to irregular, non-rectangular contours — a setting where existing methods either fail or produce stitching artifacts. The training-free, plug-and-play design means it can be applied to new base models (demonstrated across SD1.4–SDXL and PixArt-α) without retraining, lowering adoption barriers. However, the method struggles with highly fragmented masks (e.g., checkerboard patterns) where the potential field becomes discontinuous, and the paper acknowledges that extending the mechanism to modern MMDiT joint-attention architectures remains an open question. The moderate but consistent improvements (4–5% ICQS gains) suggest the potential-field framing is a useful inductive bias, though the absolute spatial adherence (C-IoU ~36%) still leaves substantial room for improvement.
