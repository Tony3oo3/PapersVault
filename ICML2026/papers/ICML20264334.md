## Problem

Existing transformer expressivity results are highly sensitive to modeling choices such as attention type, model width, and parameter uniformity, yielding diverse and difficult-to-reconcile characterizations. Only a limited set of padded transformer idealizations has been studied, leaving open how robustly the equivalences to natural circuit classes (AC^d, TC^d) hold under changes to these architectural parameters. This paper asks which choices actually matter and which do not for polynomially padded transformers.

## Input / Output

The model processes an input string w of length N over alphabet Σ, appended with P(N) polynomially many dedicated padding (pause) symbols, and operates under fixed-point arithmetic with precision b(N) bits per scalar. The output is a binary language-membership decision extracted from the contextual representation of a designated position. A critical constraint is the volume V(N) = D(N)·b(N) = Ω(log N), which is the minimum needed to encode uncompressed positional information distinguishing N input positions; below this threshold, expressivity becomes difficult to characterize with natural circuit classes.

## Method

The paper establishes exact equivalences between padded transformer families and uniform circuit classes by proving matching upper and lower bounds across a grid of depth, precision, width, and uniformity regimes. The key unifying step (Lem. 3.1) shows that any L-uniform AHAT can be exactly simulated by a logarithmic-precision L-uniform SMAT via temperature-scaled softmax, collapsing the two attention frameworks. For constant depth, a novel constant-width lower bound uses unit-length 2D positional encodings of the form (√(1/n_tgt), √(1−1/n_tgt)) to focus attention on individual circuit gates without logarithmic width, showing precision compensates for width. For looped transformers (Θ(log^d N) iterations), the upper bound exploits uniformity collapse—parameter reuse in the loop yields FO-uniform circuits rather than L-uniform ones—while the lower bound reduces circuit evaluation to iterative reachability and gate-by-gate propagation. The headline result is that, given sufficient volume, expressivity depends only on whether precision is constant (yielding AC^d) or growing (yielding TC^d), and is invariant to attention type, width up to polynomial, and the distinction between fully uniform and L-uniform constructions.

## Results

The paper establishes exact equivalences between polynomially padded transformer families and circuit complexity classes. For constant-depth transformers with sufficient volume (V(N) = Ω(log N)): L-uniform LPT_{c,D}^0 = L-uniform AC⁰ and L-uniform LPT_{l,D}^0 = L-uniform TC⁰, regardless of width D (constant, log, or polynomial). For Θ(log^d N)-looped transformers: L-uniform LPT_{c,l}^d = FO-uniform AC^d and L-uniform LPT_{l,c}^d = FO-uniform TC^d. A key new result is that constant-width log-precision transformers already achieve L-uniform TC⁰ (Lem. 4.1), showing precision compensates for width, and that growing width or precision beyond logarithmic provides no additional expressivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between prior theoretical characterizations and the new unified results. London & Kanade (2025) characterized L-uniform padded SMATs with log-width (★ cells), but left AHATs and constant-width SMATs open; this paper closes those gaps by proving SMATs can simulate AHATs (Lem. 3.1) and that constant-width log-precision transformers already reach TC⁰. Merrill & Sabharwal (2025a) established equivalences for fully-uniform growing-precision AHATs († cells) but did not address L-uniformity or constant precision; this paper extends their lower bounds to L-uniform transformers and proves the matching constant-precision AC^d lower bound via a circuit-evaluation reduction (Thm. 5.1). Li et al. (2024b) showed padded constant-precision SMATs are in AC⁰ but only for growing width; this paper tightens the upper bound to polynomial width and adds the AHAT side. The net effect is that all previously scattered partial results (marked † and ★) are subsumed into a single robust characterization: once volume is sufficient, expressivity depends only on whether precision is constant (AC^d) or growing (TC^d), independent of attention type, width, or uniformity (beyond L).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of work connecting padded transformers to circuit complexity classes, building on London & Kanade's (2025) L-uniform SMAT characterizations, Merrill & Sabharwal's (2025a) fully-uniform AHAT results, and Li et al.'s (2024b) padded SMAT equivalences. The key technical novelty is a simulation lemma showing that logarithmic-precision SMATs can exactly replicate AHATs (Lem. 3.1), which bridges the two attention frameworks that prior work treated separately. By establishing matching upper and lower bounds across the full grid of attention type, width, precision, depth, and uniformity, the paper closes gaps left by prior results that each covered only specific corners of this space (e.g., constant-width or fully-uniform settings).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that padded transformer expressivity is robust to attention type, width, and uniformity, with the AC/TC divide determined solely by numeric precision—simplifies theoretical analysis and increases confidence that these characterizations describe real-world models. The 'uniformity collapse' for looped transformers (L-uniform → FO-uniform) and the result that precision can compensate for width are clean conceptual contributions. Key open questions include characterizing insufficient-volume (o(log N)) transformers, which likely require sub-AC^0 classes like PFO^2[<], quantifying minimal padding needed for specific problems, and understanding how PE complexity independently affects expressivity.
