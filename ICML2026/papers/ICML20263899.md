## Problem

Existing adversarial scenario generation for autonomous driving either ignores vehicle-road physical limits (producing visually extreme but physically unsolvable crashes) or enforces physical feasibility in isolation (collapsing onto a narrow set of extreme boundary-pushing maneuvers). Neither approach targets the most informative regime: scenarios that remain physically solvable in principle yet still cause the deployed autonomy stack to fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263899/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scenario policy consumes a deterministic vector state concatenating privileged CARLA simulator signals (ego position/velocity/yaw, surrounding agent states, route waypoints, and map features) and outputs a diagonal-Gaussian distribution over the joint action of all non-ego agents. The ego vehicle is driven by a fixed surrogate policy (SAC from ChatScene) and is absorbed into the environment dynamics, so the generator only controls adversarial traffic. Generated scenarios are sampled as top-k rollouts per route and replayed under multiple heterogeneous ego controllers (SAC, PPO, TD3, AIM-BEV, TransFuser, BehaviorAgent) for evaluation.

## Method

ScenePilot formulates adversarial scenario generation as a constrained multi-objective MDP with two per-step reward signals: an RSS-derived physical-feasibility score σ (computed from axis-wise braking-limit distances and orthogonal-axis reachable displacement) and an online-learned AV-risk predictor Φ (a lightweight two-layer MLP trained with potential-based reward shaping and weighted BCE). The key mechanism is step-level feasibility-aware shielding: a binary mask m_t switches the policy gradient between risk-optimization (A_t^φ) and feasibility-recovery (A_t^σ) at each timestep whenever a feasibility violation is present or imminent, preserving sensitivity to rare near-boundary events that batch-mean penalties would dilute. A Gaussian-shaped ε-sweep over 8 predefined constraint levels cyclically traverses the physically feasible yet policy-infeasible boundary band during training, with checkpoint resumption at each threshold to stabilize optimization. The actor is a small ~55K-parameter MLP trained with PPO (GAE λ=0.95, clip 0.25) against a two-headed vector-valued critic.

## Results

On SafeBench (8 base traffic scenarios, averaged over SAC/PPO/TD3 ego controllers), ScenePilot achieves the highest mean Collision Rate (CR) of 0.893 and the lowest mean Overall Score (OS) of 0.476, outperforming the strongest recent baselines ChatScene (CR 0.831, OS 0.482) and SCENGE (CR 0.820, OS 0.499). Under heterogeneous AV stacks (Autopilot, AIM-BEV, TransFuser, BehaviorAgent, TD3/SAC/PPO) on Scenario 6, ScenePilot increases CR from 0.380 (KING) to 0.490 and reduces OS from 0.766 to 0.708. Adversarial fine-tuning on ScenePilot-generated scenes reduces the ego's held-out CR from 0.854 (pre-trained) to 0.072, the lowest among all compared generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScenePilot outperforms all seven SafeBench baselines on both CR and OS across the eight base scenarios. Against the strongest recent generators, ScenePilot improves CR by +6.2 pp over ChatScene (0.893 vs. 0.831) and +7.3 pp over SCENGE (0.893 vs. 0.820), while also achieving the lowest OS (0.476 vs. 0.482 and 0.499 respectively). The older SafeBench baselines (LC, AS, CS, AT) are substantially weaker, with CR ranging from 0.584 to 0.676 and OS from 0.573 to 0.620, indicating that unconstrained or rule-based generation produces far less safety-critical interactions. SCSG (CR 0.734, OS 0.544) is intermediate, outperforming the original SafeBench baselines but trailing ScenePilot by ~16 pp in CR. Under heterogeneous AV stacks (Table 2), ScenePilot also beats KING (CR 0.490 vs. 0.380; OS 0.708 vs. 0.766) on Scenario 6, confirming that the generated failures transfer beyond the standard RL-controller setting to end-to-end planners and rule-based agents. In the fine-tuning experiment (Table 4), ScenePilot yields the best post-fine-tuning robustness (CR 0.072, OS 0.898) compared to ChatScene (CR 0.134, OS 0.869), suggesting its boundary-band scenarios provide stronger learning signals for ego-policy improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScenePilot sits at the intersection of physics-constrained adversarial generation (AdvSim, ACERO) and policy-feasibility-guided generation (FREA), but departs from both by explicitly targeting the 'boundary band' where scenarios remain physically solvable yet still break the deployed stack. It builds on the RSS safety model (Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2017) by deriving a tighter physics-limit variant that removes conservative reaction-time assumptions, and adapts constrained multi-objective RL (CMORL-IPO) by replacing batch-level interior-point scalarization with step-level feasibility-aware shielding. The online-learned AV-risk critic draws on potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) and safe-RL advantage intervention (Wagener et al., 2021), but is trained as a lightweight policy-free value network rather than a full safety module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The boundary-band framing provides a principled organizing principle for scenario generation that prior work lacked: it separates 'physically impossible' failures from 'genuinely hard but solvable' ones, making generated stress tests more diagnostically useful for improving deployed stacks. The empirical validation across heterogeneous AV stacks (RL controllers, end-to-end planners, rule-based agents) and the downstream fine-tuning results suggest the approach has practical utility beyond a single benchmark. Key limitations include reliance on fixed SafeBench routes (limiting global trajectory diversity), surrogate-driven top-k selection that may favor failure modes salient to one controller, and evaluation confined to CARLA; extending to open-world route diversity and controller-agnostic selection remain open.
