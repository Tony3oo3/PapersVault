## Problem

Identifying content and style from unpaired multi-domain data requires additional structure beyond distribution matching, but prior identifiability guarantees rely on restrictive assumptions—statistical independence between content and style or sparse Jacobian influence—that fail in practice (e.g., illumination depends on object geometry). This paper asks whether content and style can be provably identified without either assumption, and answers affirmatively by replacing distributional independence with a geometric condition on the generator's local behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes unpaired RGB images from N labeled domains (32×32 MNIST digits with label-dependent color styles; 256×256 AFHQ animal faces across dog/cat/wild domains; 1024×1024 CelebA-HQ male/female faces) and produces disentangled content and style latent codes, counterfactual images (fixed style, varying content), and cross-domain translations (source content + target style). A key design choice is that content and style inputs are statistically dependent by construction—sharing a common noise block r_C1—so the framework accommodates realistic content-style correlations that prior independent-latent methods cannot model.

## Method

The core contribution is Content-Style Differential Independence (CSDI): a structural constraint requiring that the range spaces of the content Jacobian J_c ĝ and style Jacobian J_s^(n) ĝ are orthogonal at every point, so infinitesimal content and style perturbations move the data manifold in perpendicular directions. The CSDI-GAN architecture uses a GAN backbone with separate invertible content encoder e_C and per-domain style encoders e_S^(n), a shared generator ĝ, and per-domain discriminators; content and style are made dependent by sharing a noise sub-vector. Training minimizes a composite loss of GAN distribution matching, invertibility regularization on the latent encoders, and a normalized Jacobian-subspace orthogonality term L_orth. To make L_orth tractable for high-dimensional images, the paper employs Hutchinson-style random-probe vector-Jacobian products (K ≪ d probes per sample) to estimate the cross-term ‖J_s^T J_c‖_F² and the normalizing Frobenius norms without ever materializing the full d×d_C or d×d_S Jacobians, reducing Jacobian-related memory by a factor of (d_C + d_S). The paper proves that under CSDI plus distribution matching and a domain-variability condition, both content and style are identifiable up to invertible transformations, and provides a robustness bound showing that approximate orthogonality (principal angle ≥ π/2 − ξ) only introduces bounded content leakage into the learned style.

## Results

On the generation task, CSDI-GAN achieves the best FID on both AFHQ (4.4) and CelebA-HQ (4.3), outperforming the strongest content-style baselines B.I. GAN (5.2 / 4.6) and I-StyleGAN (5.6 / 4.8), as well as general-purpose GANs StyleGAN2-ADA (6.5 / 5.0) and Transitional-cGAN (6.7 / 6.4). It also attains the highest style-diversity LPIPS scores (0.4452 on AFHQ, 0.3392 on CelebA-HQ). On the translation task, CSDI-GAN achieves the best FID on both AFHQ (7.1) and CelebA-HQ (12.9), substantially outperforming B.I. GAN (10.5 / 24.6), I-StyleGAN (17.6 / 19.7), and StarGANv2 (15.0 / 14.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSDI-GAN outperforms all five baselines on FID for both generation and translation tasks across both datasets. Against the closest content-style competitor B.I. GAN (Shrestha & Fu, 2025), CSDI-GAN reduces FID by 0.8 on AFHQ generation (4.4 vs 5.2) and by 3.4 on CelebA-HQ translation (12.9 vs 24.6), while also improving LPIPS style diversity by 0.046–0.076 points. Against I-StyleGAN (Xie et al., 2023), the FID gap is even larger on translation (7.1 vs 17.6 on AFHQ; 12.9 vs 19.7 on CelebA-HQ), and LPIPS is consistently higher. Against general-purpose GANs (StyleGAN2-ADA, Transitional-cGAN), CSDI-GAN achieves 1.1–2.3 lower FID on generation while additionally providing disentangled content-style controls they lack. On CelebA-HQ translation LPIPS, CSDI-GAN (0.3105) is essentially on par with StarGANv2 (0.3148), the only metric where it does not clearly lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the content-style identifiability framework of Shrestha & Fu (2025) and Xie et al. (2023), which require statistical independence (component-wise or block-wise) between content and style, or Jacobian sparsity (Yan et al., 2023), by replacing these distributional/structural assumptions with a purely geometric one: orthogonality of the content and style Jacobian subspaces (differential independence). The identifiability proof builds on the content-identifiability argument from Shrestha & Fu (2025) and the invertibility guarantee from Zimmermann et al. (2021), while the orthogonality regularizer draws on Hutchinson trace estimation and VJP-based Jacobian probing techniques from StyleGAN2 (Karras et al., 2020b) and Wei et al. (2021). The key conceptual departure is that CSDI permits statistically dependent content and style (e.g., illumination depending on object geometry), which prior identifiability results explicitly excluded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a less restrictive and arguably more realistic identifiability condition for content-style disentanglement from unpaired multi-domain data, expanding the class of problems where provable identification is possible. The scalable Hutchinson-based implementation makes the orthogonality constraint practical for high-resolution image generation, a setting where prior VAE-based identifiability methods struggle. Key limitations include reliance on GAN training (instability, mode collapse) and the assumption that the orthogonality condition holds approximately; extending CSDI to diffusion and flow-matching models is identified as an important open direction. The robustness theorem (Theorem 3.6) quantifying how inexact orthogonality degrades style identification is a useful theoretical tool but the practical gap between exact and approximate CSDI remains to be explored more broadly.
