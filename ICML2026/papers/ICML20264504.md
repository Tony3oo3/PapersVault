## Problem

All-in-One Image Restoration (AiOIR) must handle heterogeneous degradations (rain, blur, haze, snow, low-light) with fundamentally conflicting optimization objectives within a single model. Existing approaches either discretize the continuous degradation space via prompt-based or MoE control mechanisms—making them sensitive to imprecise conditioning—or rely on fixed diffusion schedules that cannot adapt to spatially varying degradation severity. The paper identifies that standard diffusion bridges additionally suffer from a drift singularity at the terminal boundary when imposing strict Dirac constraints, which is exacerbated by the stochastic nature of heterogeneous degradations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image in one inference step. A lightweight frozen auxiliary network ψ(·) first computes a pixel-wise uncertainty map u from the degraded input (via absolute residual between the input and a preliminary restoration estimate), which then spatially modulates all transport coefficients element-wise. Training uses 256×256 crops with L1 loss; evaluation is conducted at 512×512 resolution across five degradation tasks, with the full pipeline (including the uncertainty estimator) requiring only ~58 ms on a single GPU for the large variant.

## Method

UDBM reformulates AiOIR as a stochastic transport problem governed by a relaxed diffusion bridge, where the strict Dirac terminal constraint is replaced by a Gaussian N(x_lq, σ_u²) with pixel-wise variance proportional to the uncertainty map, provably resolving the drift singularity (Theorem 3.3). The transport dynamics are decomposed into a noise schedule and a path schedule, both modulated by u: the noise schedule combines a shared bridge term (t(1−t) profile scaled by η_bridge(u)) that aligns heterogeneous degradation manifolds into a common high-entropy latent space, with a terminal relaxation term (t² scaled by η_relax(u)) that enforces the relaxed boundary condition. The path schedule implements an uncertainty-adaptive time reparameterization α_t(u) = t/(t^π(u)+(1−t)^π(u)) where π(u) interpolates between classical OT (π=1, constant speed) and entropy-regularized OT (π=0.5, viscous deceleration in high-uncertainty regions), derived from the backward Viscous HJB equation of the Schrödinger bridge formulation. A modified UNet backbone predicts the clean image estimate at each step, and a DDIM-style deterministic reverse update enables high-fidelity single-step inference by jointly regularizing terminal dynamics and constraining the flow to a straight geodesic.

## Results

On the All-in-One Image Restoration (AiOIR) benchmark spanning five degradation types (rain, low-light, snow, haze, blur), UDBM-L achieves the best average PSNR of 32.61 dB and SSIM of 0.933, outperforming the previous best AiO method HOGformer (31.57 dB / 0.930) by over 1 dB. UDBM-L also requires approximately 2× fewer FLOPs (46.85G vs. 91.77G) and achieves a 6× inference speedup (58.49 ms vs. 366.37 ms) over HOGformer, all within a single inference step. Even the medium variant UDBM-M (31.60 dB) surpasses HOGformer while using only 11.98 GFLOPs, and the lightweight UDBM-S delivers competitive results at just 5.14 GFLOPs and 5.71M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDBM-L achieves the highest average PSNR (32.61 dB) and SSIM (0.933) among all AiO methods, surpassing the closest competitor HOGformer (31.57 dB / 0.930) by 1.04 dB, with the largest margin on Haze (39.88 vs. 36.60 dB) and Low-light (26.55 vs. 25.57 dB). Against diffusion-based AiO baselines, UDBM-L outperforms DiffUIR (30.18 dB) by 2.43 dB, AdaIR (29.15 dB) by 3.46 dB, and BioIR (29.32 dB) by 3.29 dB, while also being substantially faster (58.49 ms vs. 348.88 ms for DiffUIR). Among task-specific methods, UDBM-L's average (32.61 dB) exceeds the best single-task baselines like Restormer (33.96 dB on Rain only) and MIRNet-v2 (33.89 dB on Rain only) when averaged across all five tasks, demonstrating that a unified model can match or exceed task-specific performance. The medium variant UDBM-M (31.60 dB) marginally beats HOGformer (31.57 dB) while using only 13% of its FLOPs (11.98G vs. 91.77G), and the small variant UDBM-S (30.81 dB) remains competitive with DiffUIR (30.18 dB) at 5% of its computational cost. On real-world and unseen composite degradation benchmarks (CDD11), UDBM-L leads all baselines in LIQE scores (2.898 average vs. 2.697 for DiffUIR), with particularly strong gains on double degradations like L+R (2.161 vs. 1.817 for DiffUIR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion bridge lineage (BBDM, I²SB, DDBM, IR-SDE) by introducing a relaxed terminal constraint that replaces the Dirac delta with a pixel-wise Gaussian, theoretically resolving the drift singularity that prior works patched with heuristics. It departs from the dominant control-modulation paradigm (prompt-learning, MoE routing) and fixed-schedule approaches (DiffUIR) by using pixel-wise uncertainty as a unified spatial signal to adaptively modulate both noise and path schedules. The path schedule is derived from the entropy-regularized optimal transport / Schrödinger bridge framework (Léonard, 2013), while the uncertainty estimation borrows the residual-based proxy from Zhang et al. (2025). Appendix C provides a useful unification showing that DDBM, I²SB, ResShift, RDDM, and DiffUIR are all special cases of the proposed general formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-step inference achieving SOTA across five heterogeneous restoration tasks with 6× speedup over DiffUIR and HOGformer makes all-in-one restoration practically deployable in resource-constrained settings (autonomous driving, mobile imaging). The theoretical resolution of drift singularity via the relaxed bridge fills a genuine gap that prior methods addressed only heuristically. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is reliance on the auxiliary uncertainty-estimation network, which may become unreliable under extreme out-of-distribution domain shifts, potentially bottlenecking restoration quality. Open questions include whether the uncertainty-adaptive scheduling generalizes to video restoration or 3D medical imaging, and whether more robust uncertainty estimators can eliminate the auxiliary network dependency.
