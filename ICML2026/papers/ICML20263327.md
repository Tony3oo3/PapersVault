## Problem

BEV vectorized mapping in complex traffic scenes suffers from target misclassification, spatial localization drift, and ambiguous semantic segmentation, yet uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods remain unexplored in Transformer-based BEV vectorization. Existing UQ approaches like MC-Dropout and Deep Ensembles require multiple forward passes, making them computationally prohibitive for real-time autonomous driving. This paper asks how to embed lightweight, single-pass uncertainty awareness into the full BEV vectorization pipeline to improve both accuracy and reliability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view surround-view RGB camera images (6 cameras on nuScenes, 7 on Argoverse 2) and produces vectorized BEV map elements—lane centerlines, road boundaries, and pedestrian crossings—represented as sets of instance points with topological connectivity. Uniquely, it also outputs per-element epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty scores derived from T stochastic MC-Dropout forward passes at inference, enabling downstream planning modules to assess prediction reliability. The model operates at ~14 FPS on a single GPU, with the uncertainty modules adding only 0.8 ms (1.12%) to total inference latency.

## Method

MapUQ builds on the MapTRv2 Transformer-based BEV architecture (ResNet-50 backbone) and inserts three uncertainty-aware modules at distinct pipeline stages. The Multi-task Semantic Uncertainty Head (MSUH) performs T MC-Dropout forward passes on BEV features to compute epistemic (variance across samples) and aleatoric (entropy of mean distribution) uncertainty, which then dynamically weight the segmentation loss and enforce KL-divergence consistency across stochastic samples. The Progressive ROI Scale Adapter (PRSA) computes an L2 geometric error between predicted and ground-truth points (or prediction dispersion at inference) to generate an adaptive scaling factor that modulates the Deformable Attention receptive field in subsequent decoder layers, preventing polyline fragmentation in high-error regions. The Negative Sample Classifier (NSC) applies MC-Dropout to the classification head, mines the top-r% highest-epistemic-uncertainty predictions as hard negatives, and applies elevated cross-entropy weighting to them while using a contrastive margin to separate high- and low-confidence feature distributions. Training combines standard supervised losses with uncertainty regularization, MC consistency (KL divergence), and contrastive constraints, all balanced by learned weighting coefficients.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, MapUQ (ResNet-50, 110 epochs) achieves a mean AP of 69.7%, improving 1.0% over the MapTRv2 baseline (68.7%). Under the shorter 24-epoch training regime, the gap widens to 1.5% (63.0% vs. 61.5%), demonstrating faster convergence. On Argoverse 2, MapUQ reaches 68.2% mAP (2D) and 65.8% mAP (3D), outperforming MapTRv2 by 0.8% and 1.1% respectively. In uncertainty calibration, MapUQ reduces ECE from 13.1 to 9.3 and NLL from 2.89 to 1.96, while raising AUROC to 91.8% and cutting the false positive rate from 15.7% to 8.4%, all at a negligible 0.2 FPS cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MapUQ outperforms all five compared baselines on the nuScenes validation set. Against the direct baseline MapTRv2 (R50, 110 epochs), MapUQ gains +1.0% mean AP (69.7 vs. 68.7) and +1.5% under 24-epoch training (63.0 vs. 61.5), with the largest per-category improvement on pedestrian crossings (+1.9%). Compared to MapTR (58.7% mean AP), the gain is +11.0%, and against BeMapNet (59.8%), +9.9%. On Argoverse 2, MapUQ leads MapTRv2 by +0.8% (2D) and +1.1% (3D), with the 3D advantage attributed to PRSA's mitigation of terrain-induced projection errors. In uncertainty calibration (Table 4), MapUQ substantially outperforms MapTRv2 (ECE 9.3 vs. 13.1; NLL 1.96 vs. 2.89; AUROC 91.8% vs. 87.6%) and also surpasses PivotNet and MapTR on all five uncertainty metrics, while maintaining comparable inference speed (13.9 FPS vs. 14.1 FPS for MapTRv2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MapTRv2 Transformer-based BEV vectorized mapping framework by integrating uncertainty quantification (UQ) at three distinct pipeline stages: feature-level (MSUH via MC-Dropout), decoding-level (PRSA via error-driven deformable attention scaling), and output-level (NSC via uncertainty-guided hard negative mining). While MC-Dropout and Deep Ensembles have been applied to 2D classification and segmentation, their systematic integration into a multi-stage Transformer-based vectorized mapping pipeline is novel. The work also draws on distance-based deterministic UQ (Mahalanobis distance) as a lightweight complement to stochastic sampling, positioning itself between computationally expensive Bayesian methods and purely deterministic baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that UQ can be embedded into real-time BEV mapping with negligible overhead (0.8 ms for the uncertainty head), making it practically deployable. The most impactful result is the 46% reduction in false positive rate (15.7% → 8.4%) and ECE improvement (13.1 → 9.3), which directly translates to safer autonomous driving by giving the system a 'knowing when it might fail' capability. However, the AP gains remain modest (1.0–1.5%), and the reliance on MC-Dropout with T forward passes at inference raises questions about whether the computational savings truly hold at scale or under stricter latency budgets. Open questions include whether the three-module decomposition generalizes to other vectorized perception tasks (e.g., 3D object detection) and whether true Bayesian or conformal approaches could yield further calibration improvements without the T-pass cost.
