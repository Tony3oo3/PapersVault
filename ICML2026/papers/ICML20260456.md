## Problem

Generating samples from a conditional distribution Y|X that is never fully observed remains a fundamental challenge in statistics and machine learning. Existing generative approaches either rely on adversarial min-max optimization (GANs), which is unstable and prone to mode collapse, or iterative denoising (diffusion models), which is computationally expensive at test time. Moreover, no prior work provides sharp non-asymptotic theoretical guarantees for MMD-based conditional sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning variable X (e.g., a low-resolution image, a noisy image, or a partial image) and an auxiliary noise vector η drawn from a simple reference distribution (Gaussian or uniform) as inputs, and produces a conditional sample from Y|X in a single forward pass of the generator. In image tasks, inputs and outputs are grayscale (MNIST 28×28, FashionMNIST 28×28) or RGB (STL-10 3×96×96, CelebHQ 3×64×64) images, with the generator architecture (CNN, ResNet-style MLP, or MDN) tailored to each task. Unlike diffusion models requiring dozens of iterative denoising steps, CGMMD produces each sample with one evaluation of the trained network.

## Method

CGMMD reformulates conditional sampling as a generalized nonparametric regression problem via the noise outsourcing lemma: a neural network g(η, x) is trained so that g(η, x) ~ P_{Y|X=x}. The training objective is the Expected Conditional MMD (ECMMD) between the true and generated conditional distributions, estimated empirically using a k-nearest-neighbor graph constructed on the conditioning variable X within each mini-batch. This yields a direct, adversary-free minimization problem (no discriminator, no min-max game) that is amenable to standard gradient descent. The nearest-neighbor construction provides a consistent estimator of the conditional MMD discrepancy, and the paper establishes non-asymptotic finite-sample error bounds and convergence of the learned conditional distribution to the true one, adapting to the intrinsic dimensionality of X. After training, sampling is one-shot: draw η from the reference distribution and evaluate the trained generator.

## Results

On the MNIST denoising task (σ=0.9), CGMMD achieves a PSNR of 8.922 and SSIM of 0.718, with a per-image inference time of 5.6×10⁻⁴ s. This is roughly 96× faster than the full classifier-free diffusion model (5.42×10⁻² s per image) and ~1.6× faster than progressive distilled diffusion (9.2×10⁻⁴ s), while the diffusion model attains substantially higher quality (PSNR 13.326, SSIM 0.861). The paper explicitly frames its contribution as a speed–quality trade-off and theoretical guarantee rather than a state-of-the-art quality claim, and qualitative experiments on helix, circle, and two-moons benchmarks show CGMMD preserving conditional structure (curvature, bimodality) more faithfully than GAN-based baselines at elevated noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN-based GCDS (Zhou et al., 2023) and Wasserstein-based WGAN (Song et al., 2025), CGMMD wins qualitatively on the helix and circle benchmarks: at noise levels σ=0.4 and 0.6, CGMMD preserves the helix curvature and circular shape, while both GCDS and WGAN produce visibly degraded or elliptical reconstructions; WGAN additionally suffers training instability without L1 regularization. Against the full diffusion model (Ho & Salimans, 2021), CGMMD loses on both PSNR (8.922 vs 13.326) and SSIM (0.718 vs 0.861) but is ~96× faster per image, making it suitable for latency-sensitive applications. Against distilled diffusion (Meng et al., 2023), CGMMD trades a small PSNR gap (8.922 vs 10.658) for a meaningful SSIM advantage (0.718 vs 0.508) and ~1.6× lower per-image latency. Against conditional normalizing flows (Winkler et al., 2019), CGMMD retains the circular conditional structure in low-probability regions (X=4) where the flow fails, though both perform comparably in high-probability regions. Against SNPE from the sbi toolkit (Tejero-Cantero et al., 2020) on the two-moons SBI benchmark, SNPE captures both the global bimodality and the local crescent shape, whereas CGMMD preserves bimodality but does not fully recover the local crescent curvature—a limitation the authors note is consistent with prior GAN-based observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGMMD sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the noise-outsourcing reformulation of conditional sampling as a regression problem (Zhou et al., 2023; Song et al., 2025), the MMD-GAN lineage of direct (non-adversarial) distribution matching (Li et al., 2015; Dziugaite et al., 2015; Binkowski et al., 2018), and the nearest-neighbor-based ECMMD estimator from Chatterjee et al. (2024). The key departure is replacing the adversarial min-max or Wasserstein objectives used in prior conditional generators with a direct ECMMD minimization estimated via k-NN graphs, and—critically—providing the first non-asymptotic finite-sample guarantees for any MMD-based conditional sampler. The one-shot sampling paradigm itself is inherited from GAN-style generators, but the paper is the first to pair it with a principled kernel-based loss and matching theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main impact is theoretical: by establishing convergence of the learned conditional distribution to the true one (in MMD and in characteristic-function sense) and a uniform concentration result for nearest-neighbor functionals, the paper gives a rigorous foundation that was missing from the MMD-based conditional generation literature. Practically, one-shot sampling offers a clear speed advantage over iterative diffusion models (Table 1 shows ~100× faster per-image inference), though at a quality cost (lower PSNR/SSIM). Open questions include whether the network-size-grows-with-n assumption can be relaxed to fixed architectures, whether the framework extends to flow-based or diffusion-based conditional samplers trained with MMD objectives, and whether the nearest-neighbor estimator scales reliably to very high-dimensional conditioning variables without learned embeddings.
