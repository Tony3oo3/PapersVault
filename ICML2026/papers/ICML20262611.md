## Problem

Prevailing video-based predictive models for robot planning operate in 2D pixel space, inherently lacking the 3D geometric understanding needed for precise spatial reasoning, multi-view coherence, and physical consistency. This limitation is especially acute in fine-grained manipulation tasks where accurate 3D cues are essential, and independently generated multi-view rollouts drift out of alignment over time. The paper asks whether a model can be built that inherently simulates the dynamic 3D structure of a scene rather than projecting it onto 2D surfaces.

## Input / Output

The model consumes calibrated multi-view RGB-D images (4–40 cameras at 512×512) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and a free-form text instruction. The 3D scene is encoded into a sparse voxel latent of ~8000 active voxels in a 64×64×64 grid, each carrying an 8-dimensional feature vector, rather than a dense grid or unstructured 1D code. Outputs are autoregressively rolled-out future 3D latents that can be decoded into point clouds, 3D Gaussian Splatting renderings from arbitrary viewpoints, or—via a goal-conditioned inverse dynamics module—short-horizon robot joint position sequences.

## Method

The core architecture is a two-stage conditional flow-matching generator operating in a structured sparse-voxel 3D latent space (inspired by SLAT/TRELLIS). A Single Dynamics Model (SD) first predicts the coarse voxel positions of the next state on a 16×16×16 grid using a 24-block transformer denoiser with cross-attention on CLIP text embeddings and the current 3D latent; a Latent Generator (LG) then fills in the 8-dim feature vectors on the full 64×64×64 grid, also via flow matching with the same conditioning. Encoding uses DINOv2 patch embeddings unprojected into the voxel grid followed by a pre-trained latent encoder; decoding maps each voxel feature to a set of 3D Gaussians whose centers are constrained near the active voxel. For planning, a goal-conditioned inverse dynamics module—either a learned diffusion policy over point-cloud pairs or a learning-free FPFH+RANSAC+ICP gripper registration pipeline—translates each predicted latent subgoal into executable joint-position action chunks, enabling both open-loop and closed-loop replanning.

## Results

On ManiSkill3 manipulation tasks (StackCube, ToolPull, PegInsertionSide), the structured 4D latent predictive model achieves 61.3% average success rate, outperforming video-based planners UniPi (29.3%) and TesserAct (24.7%) by over 30 points, and surpassing imitation learning baselines DP (55.7%) and DP3 (49.3%). On RLBench tasks (CloseBox, SweepToDustpan, WaterPlants), the method reaches 75.3% average success rate versus 61.7% for TesserAct and 55.0% for UniPi. For 4D generation quality, the method achieves a Chamfer Distance of 5.95 (vs. 42.79–44.07 for baselines) and cross-view cPSNR of 27.42 (vs. 16.67–17.91), demonstrating substantially better 3D consistency. Zero-shot generalization to visual perturbations (lighting, noise, background color, viewpoint shifts) maintains 78–85% success rate, while DP drops to 1–43% and DP3 to 45–49%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines spanning video-generative planners (UniPi, TesserAct, Wan-2.1, OpenSora) and imitation learning policies (Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy). On ManiSkill3 planning, the proposed method (61.3% avg) decisively outperforms both video-based planners—UniPi (29.3%) and TesserAct (24.7%)—by 32 and 37 points respectively, and also surpasses the stronger imitation learning baselines DP (55.7%) and DP3 (49.3%). On RLBench, the method (75.3%) beats TesserAct (61.7%) and UniPi (55.0%) by 13.6 and 20.3 points. For 4D generation quality, the structured 3D latent representation yields a 7× reduction in Chamfer Distance (5.95 vs. ~43) and a 60% improvement in cross-view cPSNR (27.42 vs. ~17) over all video-based baselines. Under zero-shot visual perturbations, the method maintains 78–85% success rate while DP collapses to 1–43% and DP3 to 45–49%, highlighting the robustness advantage of explicit 3D structure over pixel-space or 2.5D representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-generative planning paradigm (UniPi, TesserAct) by replacing 2D pixel-space video prediction with dynamics learned in a structured 3D latent space. It repurposes the SLAT/TRELLIS encoder-decoder (Xiang et al., 2025) as a frozen scene representation and applies conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) to model temporal transitions over sparse voxel features, decoupling coarse geometry prediction (Single Dynamics Model) from fine appearance synthesis (Latent Generator). Unlike prior 3D dynamics methods that rely on object-centric factorizations or predefined primitives, the model learns a holistic scene-level latent that can be decoded into diverse 3D formats (point clouds, 3D Gaussians) without task-specific structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that explicit 3D structure in the predictive model yields dramatically better multi-view consistency (Chamfer Distance ~6 vs ~43 for video baselines) and robust zero-shot generalization to lighting, noise, and viewpoint shifts, addressing a core weakness of 2D video planners for fine-grained manipulation. Key limitations include the requirement for calibrated multi-view RGB-D inputs, validation restricted to tabletop tasks, and sensitivity to fine geometric precision in contact-rich scenarios like peg insertion. Open questions include whether the approach extends to monocular or sparse-sensing settings, scales to more diverse robot embodiments, and whether the structured latent can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on a frozen pre-trained encoder.
