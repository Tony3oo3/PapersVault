## Problem

Terminal embeddings, the key technique behind dimension-free coresets for k-means and k-median clustering, are inherently non-linear and thus cannot preserve linear structures such as line-segments. Since time-series data is naturally modeled as polygonal curves (collections of straight-line segments), no terminal embeddings existed for this setting, leaving dimension dependence in all prior coreset constructions for clustering under the Fréchet distance. The paper asks whether the terminal embedding framework can be extended to affine lines and polygonal curves in high-dimensional Euclidean space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set P of n polygonal curves in R^d, each of complexity at most m (i.e., m+1 vertices connected by straight-line segments), and must preserve Fréchet distances to arbitrary query curves of complexity at most ℓ. The output is a piecewise-linear mapping f: R^d → R^t with target dimension t = O(ℓε^{-4} log(nm)) that guarantees (1±ε) distortion for all point-to-point distances between input curve segments and any point on any query curve's affine line. As a downstream application, this yields an ε-coreset of size Õ(kε^{-2-z}ℓ²log²m) for (k,ℓ,z)-clustering, which is dimension-free and optimal in k and ε for constant ℓ and m.

## Method

The construction combines a subspace-preserving random projection S (a JL-type matrix) with 2ℓ ancillary coordinates. For input curves, the mapping is simply the linear projection Sa. For query curves, a data-dependent orthogonal projection Π onto the span of O(ℓε^{-2}) input line-segments is first computed via a greedy covering procedure (Lemma 3.1) that ensures the cross-term in the squared-distance decomposition is multiplicatively controlled; the query point b is then mapped to SΠb in the main coordinates and (I−Π)b in the ancillary coordinates. This makes the embedding piecewise-linear rather than fully non-linear, avoiding the semidefinite programs or nearest-neighbor structures required by prior point-based terminal embeddings. For the coreset, the terminal embedding is integrated into an improved chaining framework (refining Cohen-Addad et al., 2021) where the enumeration over candidate centers handles long line-segments by reducing to the case where center segments are parallel to input segments, yielding a sequence of increasingly fine nets whose metric entropy is bounded via generic chaining.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 1.3: the first dimension-free ε-coreset of size Õ(kε^{-2-z}ℓ²log²m) for (k,ℓ,z)-clustering of polygonal curves under the Fréchet distance, removing the ambient dimension d from all prior bounds. This is optimal for constant ℓ and m, matching the Ω(kε^{-2-z}) lower bounds for point clustering. Experimentally, on GHG California data (d=2921, n=16 curves, m=327 time steps), terminal embeddings (TE) achieve Fréchet distance approximation ratios very close to plain JL at target dimensions t∈{5,10,20}, while both substantially outperform PCA, with TE uniquely preserving distances to arbitrary query curves in the full ambient space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior coreset constructions (Braverman et al. FOCS'22, Cohen-Addad et al. FOCS'23, Conradi et al. SoCG'24, Cohen-Addad et al. SODA'25), the paper's final result removes the ambient dimension d entirely and improves the k-dependence from k³ or k² down to k, matching the best prior work's linear k. Compared to the strongest prior bound (Cohen-Addad et al. SODA'25: Õ(k·ε^{-1-z}·d·ℓ·log m)), the new result trades the d·ℓ·log m factor for ℓ²·log²m and worsens ε from ε^{-1-z} to ε^{-2-z}, but achieves dimension-freeness. In the empirical comparison (Figure 1), TE performs very similarly to plain JL at the same target dimension, with only a small constant-factor degradation in approximation ratio due to the orthogonal projection step; both TE and JL substantially outperform PCA across all tested dimensions (t=5, 10, 20). The key qualitative advantage of TE over JL is that TE preserves Fréchet distance to arbitrary query curves in the full ambient space, whereas JL only guarantees pairwise preservation among input curves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the terminal embedding framework of Elkin et al. (2017) from finite point sets to affine line-segments and polygonal curves, overcoming the fundamental obstacle that terminal embeddings are inherently non-linear and thus cannot preserve linear structures. The construction is built on subspace-preserving JL sketches (Johnson & Lindenstrauss, 1984) combined with a novel piecewise-linear projection that avoids the semidefinite programs and nearest-neighbor data structures required by prior point-based terminal embeddings. For the coreset application, the paper refines the chaining framework of Cohen-Addad et al. (2021) to directly incorporate the terminal embedding, improving the ε-dependence from ε^{-5-z} to the optimal ε^{-2-z}.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dimension-free coreset for clustering time-series under the Fréchet distance, removing the ambient dimension d from the coreset size bound and achieving optimality in k and ε for constant curve complexity. The piecewise-linear construction is notably simpler than prior terminal embeddings, relying only on oblivious random projections and data-dependent orthogonal projections. Open questions include whether the ε^{-4} target dimension for the embedding itself can be tightened, whether the framework extends to other elastic distances (e.g., DTW), and whether the theoretical guarantees translate to practical speedups at scale — the experimental validation here is limited to a single small-scale dataset.
