## Problem

Test-time training (TTT) adapts pretrained models per-prompt via parameter updates, yet its performance is notoriously unstable and sensitive to hyperparameters such as the number of update steps and the chosen update subspace, with no principled guidance for when, how far, or in which directions to adapt. Prior theoretical work characterizes when TTT can improve in-context learning in specific regimes but leaves the choice of optimization hyperparameters unexplained. This paper fills that gap by casting TTT as implicit Bayesian inference in the kernel regime, unifying the three design questions under a single decision-theoretic framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained model (e.g., distilgpt2), a labeled prompt of n input-output pairs, a query input, and a restricted update subspace (e.g., attention Value matrices at selected heads), operating in a linearized NTK regime. It produces a scalar query prediction, a per-prompt update horizon T selected via prompt evidence, and a query-aware update subspace specifying which Transformer blocks or heads to adapt. Leave-one-out residuals are used to avoid label leakage, and the prompt geometry K = ΦΦᵀ is both prompt-dependent and mechanism-dependent, fundamentally distinguishing TTT from classical early stopping with a fixed Gram matrix.

## Method

The core insight is that few-step TTT in the kernel regime is exactly equivalent to Bayesian inference under a prompt-induced Gaussian prior, where the update horizon T controls prior strength (eigenvalues) and the update subspace A controls prior shape (eigenvectors). Under a Gaussian process benchmark coupling prompt and query, the paper derives a conditional Bayes-gap identity (Thm 5.2) showing query improvement requires both spectral filter match to the prompt's signal-to-noise ratio and eigen-alignment with the prompt-query coupling vector, explaining why fixed steps or subspaces fail under distribution shift. For step selection, choosing T via prompt evidence (marginal likelihood) admits a PAC-Bayes non-overfitting guarantee (Thm 7.2), so tuning T on the prompt does not overfit the residuals. For subspace selection, the Bayes-optimal rank-k update subspace is characterized as the top-k eigenspace of a query-weighted information-capture matrix (Thm 8.2), yielding a practical query-aware scoring rule for Transformer block/head selection. The Transformer specialization reveals that the prompt geometry for value-matrix updates is an attention-sandwiched token-similarity matrix, making the coupling between attention structure and update effectiveness explicit.

## Results

On a digit-shift task with distilgpt2 (2,000 tasks, n=10 prompt pairs), evidence-based per-prompt T selection (MLE-σ) achieves the lowest query MSE of 0.0719±0.0029, outperforming fixed-T TTT (T=8, MSE 0.0734±0.0030) and ICL with no update (MSE 0.0747±0.0031). The selected T_MAP varies substantially across prompts (mean T≈11.2 for MLE-σ, ≈9.8 for fixed-σ), confirming that a single globally optimal horizon does not exist. In the block-selection experiment, QUERY-AWARE head selection consistently outperforms TRACE-TOPK and random selection on query MSE, while TRACE-TOPK improves prompt-fit MSE without transferring to query gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four methods: ICL (no parameter update), TTT with a fixed horizon T=8, and two evidence-based per-prompt T selection variants (MLE-σ and fixed-σ). Evidence-based TTT (MLE-σ) achieves the lowest MSE (0.0719), beating fixed-T TTT by 0.0015 MSE (95% bootstrap CI [0.000605, 0.003079], p=0.00250) and ICL by 0.0028 MSE. Fixed-T TTT (T=8) itself improves over ICL with a 95% CI of [0.000648, 0.001796] and paired permutation p=5.0×10⁻⁵. In the second experiment (block/head selection under a fixed budget), QUERY-AWARE selection consistently outperforms both TRACE-TOPK (which prioritizes dominant prompt features) and random selection on query MSE, while TRACE-TOPK improves prompt-fit MSE but fails to generalize to the query—confirming the paper's theoretical prediction that effective adaptation requires query-aligned, not merely prompt-fitting, update directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TTT framework of Sun et al. (2020) and the emerging theoretical literature on when TTT improves in-context learning (Gozeten et al., 2025; Kuwataka & Suzuki, 2025), but departs from those ICL-centered analyses by reframing TTT as implicit Bayesian inference in the kernel/NTK regime. The spectral-filter machinery is inherited from classical early-stopping theory (Yao et al., 2007; Bauer et al., 2007), and the PAC-Bayes guarantee for step selection draws on Catoni (2007) and Alquier et al. (2024), while the evidence-based hyperparameter selection follows MacKay (1992). The key conceptual departure is treating the update horizon T and the update subspace A as hyperparameters of a prompt-induced Gaussian prior, which prior TTT theory had not formalized, enabling Bayes-gap identities, non-overfitting guarantees, and a Bayes-optimal subspace characterization that are absent from the existing ICL-focused analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to give principled, decision-theoretic guidance for the three practical TTT questions (when to adapt, how far, and in which directions), with a PAC-Bayes guarantee that evidence-based step selection does not overfit the prompt and a concrete scoring rule for selecting Transformer attention blocks/heads. However, the analysis is restricted to a local linearized (NTK) regime with few steps and a Gaussian benchmark, and the empirical validation is limited to a small distilgpt2 model on a synthetic digit-shift task plus a small SST-2 check, leaving open whether the spectral-filter and eigen-alignment principles transfer to large-scale, fully nonlinear TTT. Natural extensions include trajectory-level analysis where the kernel evolves during adaptation, non-squared and self-supervised losses, and scalable approximations to query-aware subspace selection.
