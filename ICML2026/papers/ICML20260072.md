## Problem

Standard split conformal prediction produces symmetric intervals that under- or over-cover in the tails when the noise is skewed or the predictive model is biased, while fully asymmetric methods (CQR, DCP) can be overly wide when the noise is actually symmetric. No existing approach provides a data-driven mechanism to smoothly transition between symmetric and asymmetric prediction bands, leaving practitioners to commit a priori to one or the other.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained point predictor m̂_n(X) (any off-the-shelf model, e.g., Gaussian Process or cubic spline), a pre-training set D_n for learning the band shapes, and a calibration set D_m for the final conformal quantile. It outputs asymmetric prediction intervals of the form [m̂_n(X) − f̂_low(X) − q̂_α, m̂_n(X) + f̂_up(X) + q̂_α], where f̂_low and f̂_up are non-negative kernel sum-of-squares functions and q̂_α is the adjusted conformal quantile. The degree of asymmetry is controlled by a penalty parameter λ_pen selected automatically via a Kruskal-Wallis test over bootstrapped HSIC values.

## Method

The paper defines an asymmetric score S(X,Y) = max(m̂_n(X) − f_low(X) − Y, Y − m̂_n(X) − f_up(X)) where f_low and f_up are kSoS functions parameterized by PSD operators on (potentially different) RKHSs. The learning problem minimizes mean interval width subject to 100% coverage constraints on the pre-training set, regularized by nuclear and Frobenius norms, and augmented with a novel symmetric penalty that either penalizes the operator difference ‖A_low − A_up‖ or the pointwise squared difference on training points. Representer theorems reduce the infinite-dimensional problem to finite-dimensional SDPs, and dual formulations (with O(n²) or O(n) unknowns depending on penalty type) enable scaling to n ≈ 10,000 via low-rank kernel approximations. Hyperparameters are tuned by maximizing HSIC between centered residuals and interval width (for kernel lengthscales) and by a Kruskal-Wallis significance test over λ_pen values (for the symmetry level), with warm-start strategies reducing computational cost by ~65%.

## Results

On 13 real-world regression datasets, penalized kSoS achieves the smallest mean width on 7 of 13 datasets (e.g., Bike: 162.47 vs. CQR 217.98, Miami: 26.9e4 vs. CQR 31.4e4) and is always first or close second on worst-set coverage (WSC^c). On synthetic asymmetric-noise datasets, penalized kSoS achieves the lowest absolute coverage gap (ACG^c) among all methods while maintaining competitive mean width. At scale (n=10,000, low-rank approximation), penalized kSoS attains WSC^c of 0.91 vs. CQR's 0.89 on Kin8nm with mean width 1.12 vs. 1.24, and solves in ~10 s versus >100 s for the full-rank dual at n=1,000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQR (conformalized quantile regression), penalized kSoS achieves substantially smaller mean widths on most datasets—e.g., 162.47 vs. 217.98 on Bike (−25%), 26.9e4 vs. 31.4e4 on Miami (−14%), and 0.39 vs. 0.61 on Kin8nm at n=1000 (−36%)—while also attaining higher WSC^c (0.91 vs. 0.89 on Kin8nm, 0.91 vs. 0.88 on Dataset 4). Against heteroscedastic GP, penalized kSoS trades a small mean-width increase (e.g., 21.74 vs. 21.01 on Concrete) for markedly better adaptivity (WSC^c closer to the 1−α/2 target), and wins outright on mean width for Bike, Miami, Energy, and Sulfur. Against homoscedastic GP, penalized kSoS is competitive on mean width (within 1–5% on most datasets) but consistently achieves superior WSC^c, indicating better local coverage. On symmetric-noise synthetic data (Dataset 1), penalized kSoS slightly outperforms the symmetric kSoS of Allain et al. (2026) in ACG^c, attributed to a small-sample counterbalancing effect and compensation for predictive-model bias; the data-driven penalty tuning automatically selects near-symmetric bands when the noise is truly symmetric (confirmed by Kruskal-Wallis test), while shifting toward asymmetric bands when sample size is small or the base model is biased.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetric kernel sum-of-squares (kSoS) prediction band framework of Allain et al. (2026) to handle asymmetric noise, drawing on the CQR score structure of Romano et al. (2019) but replacing quantile regression estimates with kSoS functions anchored at a point predictor. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a symmetric penalty that creates a continuous spectrum between fully asymmetric and fully symmetric bands, with a data-driven tuning strategy (HSIC + Kruskal-Wallis) to select the appropriate level. The theoretical machinery generalizes the multi-operator representer theorem of Marteau-Ferey et al. (2020) to coupled problems with cross-operator penalties, yielding new SDP and dual formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in conformal prediction: asymmetric noise is common in real applications yet has been largely overlooked, and existing asymmetric methods (CQR, DCP) struggle in small-sample or high-dimensional regimes. The finding that even with truly symmetric noise, controlled asymmetry can yield tighter and more adaptive bands—due to small-sample artifacts and predictive model bias—offers a principled argument against enforcing symmetry a priori. Limitations include a dimensionality ceiling (~15 features), scalability to n~1000 without low-rank approximation (n~10000 with it), and the lack of theoretical analysis on how kernel approximation affects coverage guarantees. Open questions include extending the framework to structured data (time series, graphs) and establishing distribution-free guarantees under low-rank kernel approximation.
