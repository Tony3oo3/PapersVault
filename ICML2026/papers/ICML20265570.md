## Problem

Residual binarization stacks multiple binary (±1) layers to achieve 2-bit LLM quantization with matmul-free inference, but standard QAT causes parallel paths to learn redundant features through a shared global gradient—a failure mode the authors term inter-path adaptation. This breaks down the intended error-compensation hierarchy, severely limiting model expressiveness. Prior work relies on heuristic constraints like path freezing that restrict the solution space rather than resolving the root cause.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained full-precision LLM (Llama2/3, Gemma3, Qwen3) and a 200M-token calibration set (WikiText-2 + C4) as input. It produces a 2-bit model where each linear layer is represented by k binary core matrices (±1) and per-channel scaling vectors (g_i, h_i); the shared full-precision anchor W_FP is discarded after training. At inference, the forward pass uses only additions, subtractions, and element-wise scaling—no matrix multiplications—enabling parallel execution of all binary paths.

## Method

RaBiT maintains a single shared full-precision weight W_FP from which binary paths are sequentially derived on-the-fly during training: B_1 = sign(W_FP), R_1 = W_FP − g_1⊙B_1⊙h_1, B_2 = sign(R_1), so the second path structurally corrects the first path's residual error. Gradients update W_FP via a Straight-Through Estimator on the effective weight, while per-path scales {g_i, h_i} receive standard chain-rule gradients, halving optimizer memory versus independent latent weights. A function-aware initialization (Iterative Residual SVID with I/O Channel Importance-Scaled Preconditioning) prioritizes preserving model functionality over weight approximation, reducing initial KL loss by 81%. Training combines KL divergence and MSE knowledge-distillation losses from the full-precision teacher, and the coupled derivation algorithmically enforces negative inter-path correlation, which the paper proves is necessary for both MSE and KL objectives.

## Results

On Llama2-7B at 2-bit, RaBiT achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 5.78 and a zero-shot QA average of 61.51%, outperforming the best VQ method QTIP (5.86 PPL, 58.97% QA) and all binary/residual baselines (MBOK: 6.99 PPL, 53.63% QA; DBF: 6.10 PPL, 58.42% QA). On Llama3-8B, RaBiT reaches 7.34 PPL and 64.13% QA, while BitStack catastrophically degrades to 2750 PPL. At the system level, 2-bit RaBiT delivers a 4.49× end-to-end decoding speedup (291.88 tok/s vs. 64.96 tok/s for FP16) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaBiT decisively outperforms all hardware-efficient binary/residual baselines: on Llama2-7B it improves Wiki2 PPL by 1.21 over MBOK (5.78 vs. 6.99), 0.32 over DBF (5.78 vs. 6.10), and 1.45 over DB-LLM (5.78 vs. 7.23), with QA Avg. gains of 7.9, 3.1, and 6.4 points respectively. Against the strongest VQ competitor QTIP, RaBiT wins on Llama2-7B (5.78 vs. 5.86 PPL; 61.51% vs. 58.97% QA) and Llama3-8B (7.34 vs. 7.52 PPL; 64.13% vs. 63.88% QA), though QTIP edges ahead slightly on Llama2-13B PPL (5.11 vs. 5.15). RaBiT also surpasses QuIP# and AQLM on Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B across both PPL and QA metrics. On challenging reasoning benchmarks (BBH, GPQA, MMLU-Pro, IFEval), RaBiT averages 27.14 on Llama2-13B versus QTIP's 25.38, confirming superior functional preservation. In inference, RaBiT achieves 4.49× end-to-end speedup over FP16 and 1.68× over QTIP (291.88 vs. 171.74 tok/s), while BitStack suffers catastrophic failure on Llama3-8B (2750 PPL) due to instability in its independent-path design.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaBiT builds on the dual-scale binarization architecture (W = g ⊙ B ⊙ h) from DBF (Boza & Macko, 2025) and the SVID rank-1 decomposition from OneBit (Xu et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from prior residual binarization training by replacing independent latent weights with a single shared full-precision anchor from which binary paths are sequentially derived on-the-fly. This contrasts with heuristic workarounds like path freezing (MBOK, DB-LLM) that constrain the solution space, and with VQ methods (QTIP, QuIP#, AQLM) that achieve accuracy at the cost of hardware-unfriendly lookup tables or rotations. The paper's key conceptual contribution is identifying 'inter-path adaptation'—a specific manifestation of feature co-adaptation in residual binary architectures—and resolving it structurally rather than heuristically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RaBiT redefines the 2-bit accuracy-efficiency frontier by rivaling hardware-intensive VQ methods (e.g., QTIP) while delivering a 4.49× inference speedup via matmul-free operations, making high-quality 2-bit LLM deployment practical on consumer hardware. The identification of inter-path adaptation as the core failure mode provides a clear diagnostic framework for future residual quantization work. Open questions include the impact of extreme compression on safety alignment, whether the coupled mechanism generalizes beyond k=2 paths, and the observed but unexplained suppression of early-layer MSE sensitivity, which may point to a deeper robustness property of residual coupling.
