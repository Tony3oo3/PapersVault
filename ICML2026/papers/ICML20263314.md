## Problem

Upcycling pre-trained dense models into sparse Mixture-of-Experts by replicating FFN weights across all experts creates weight homogeneity that prevents the router from differentiating experts, resulting in low-confidence, undifferentiated routing probabilities. Standard MoE regularizers (load-balancing loss, z-loss) stabilize training but do not directly induce specialization from identically initialized experts, leaving a gap in principled, fine-grained control over router output distributions during upcycled MoE training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263314/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within the LLaVA vision-language framework, taking RGB images (encoded by CLIP-Large) and text prompts as input and producing text responses. DPSL is computed at the token level across the full batch (effective sample size S×T, exceeding 73,000 tokens), shaping the categorical routing probability vectors (one per MoE layer, per token) over N experts. The loss is active only during the warm-up training stage, with the derivative of the CDF (the PDF) already available from the forward pass, incurring no additional backward-pass cost.

## Method

Dirichlet-Prior Shaping Loss (DPSL) regularizes the router by matching the empirical CDF of per-expert routing probabilities (across a batch) to the theoretical Beta CDF derived from a target Dirichlet prior, using the Cramér-von Mises criterion. Symmetric concentration parameters (e.g., α=1) promote balanced, confident expert utilization, while asymmetric priors (e.g., adding a scalar bias to specific experts) encode inductive biases for modality- or task-aware specialization without hard constraints. The loss is applied per MoE layer during warm-up with a fixed λ=0.01, and the optimal concentration α can be selected via a small calibration subset (~3 hours of training). Applied to upcycled VLM MoEs (Qwen2-1.5B, Phi3-mini 3.8B, Llama3.2-1B) in both standard (2in4) and granular (8in16) configurations, DPSL consistently outperforms all upcycling and regularization baselines across six vision-language benchmarks, while also improving unsupervised clustering accuracy by 4.6–14.9% when added to SwAV or SeCu.

## Results

DPSL consistently achieves the highest average score across all five model–configuration combinations in Table 2. On Qwen2-1.5B (2in4), DPSL reaches 38.17 average (vs. 36.60 dense, +1.57); on Phi3-mini 3.8B (2in4), it reaches 40.18 (vs. 38.18 dense, +2.00); and on Llama3.2-1B (2in4), it reaches 35.92 (vs. 34.19 dense, +1.73). ## Baselines & Comparisons

DPSL outperforms all six MoE upcycling/regularization baselines (Sparse Upcycling, Drop-Upcycling, Load-balancing, Z-loss, DeepSeek balancing, and the dense baseline) on the average score across all five model–configuration settings in Table 2, with margins of +0.2 to +1.6 points over the strongest baseline (which varies by setting: DeepSeek balancing on Qwen 8in16 and Llama 8in16, Drop-Upcycling on Qwen 2in4, Z-loss on Phi3 2in4). Against Branch-Train-MiX (BTX) in the task-specialization comparison (Table 3), symmetric DPSL (35.92 avg) beats BTX (35.31 avg) by 0.61 points, and DPSL with a task prior (35.35) narrowly edges BTX by 0.04. In the modality-specialization comparison, DPSL with a modality prior (36.18 avg) substantially outperforms manual hard-assignment routing (34.65 avg) by 1.53 points. In the unsupervised clustering experiment (Table 1), DPSL improves SwAV by 4.6–14.9% and SeCu by 5.9–12.6% across three synthetic 2D regimes, with the largest gain (14.9%) on non-overlapping imbalanced clusters. Notably, DPSL also achieves the lowest expert activation similarity (0.39 vs. 0.57 for load-balancing and 0.59 for z-loss), confirming superior expert differentiation, while maintaining competitive load-balancing (CoV) without an explicit balancing term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Batch Shaping Loss (Bejnordi et al., 2020), a Cramér-von Mises-based distribution-matching technique originally designed for channel-gated networks, into a Dirichlet-prior framework applicable to any module outputting categorical probabilities. It is positioned specifically to address the expert-specialization failure mode in sparse upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2023), where naively replicated FFN weights produce homogeneous routing that standard MoE regularizers (load-balancing, z-loss, DeepSeek balancing) cannot fix because they target load uniformity or numerical stability rather than the shape of the routing distribution itself. The granular expert partitioning follows He et al. (2024), and the method is contrasted with initialization-based diversity (Drop-Upcycling) and pre-specialization (Branch-Train-MiX), which the paper argues are either too slow to converge or too rigid for MoE routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPSL offers a lightweight, interpretable knob (the Dirichlet concentration vector) for controlling expert behavior during upcycled MoE training, with negligible computational overhead (<2% end-to-end) and a simple symmetric default that works across Qwen2, Phi3, and Llama3.2 backbones. The demonstration on unsupervised clustering suggests the technique is genuinely general beyond MoEs, though the clustering gains are on small synthetic 2D tasks. Key limitations include single-seed evaluation for most backbone/configuration combinations, application only during the warm-up stage, and modest absolute gains (≈1–2 points average) that may narrow at larger scale. Open questions include whether DPSL helps when training MoEs from scratch, how it interacts with shared-expert architectures at scale, and whether the modality-aware prior strategy generalizes beyond the specific VLM data splits tested.
