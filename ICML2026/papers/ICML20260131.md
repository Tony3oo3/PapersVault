## Problem

Finetuning pretrained multimodal models like CLIP improves in-distribution accuracy but degrades out-of-distribution robustness through catastrophic forgetting, and the robust-finetuning literature lacks a theoretical account of where and why forgetting occurs. A critical but overlooked limitation is that standard EMA teachers, widely used in dynamic self-distillation for robust finetuning, provably collapse onto the student as training converges, causing the regularizing signal to vanish precisely when OOD robustness is most fragile.

## Input / Output

TRACER finetunes the image encoder of a CLIP-style vision-language model on paired RGB image–text data (e.g., ImageNet-1K at 224×224) while keeping the text encoder frozen at its pretrained state. The student model produces class labels and calibrated confidence scores; the WMA teacher is an internal model whose parameters are a kernel-weighted average of the student's trajectory and which is never used at inference. Training runs for 10 epochs with AdamW (lr 1e-5, weight decay 0.01) and batch sizes of 224–512 depending on backbone.

## Method

TRACER reformulates the linearized multimodal contrastive loss as a matrix least-squares problem via a contrastive target matrix Y_FT, yielding closed-form solutions that expose a geometric decomposition: finetuning mixes knowledge within the task subspace while preserving orthogonal pretrained directions. The key mechanism is a Weighted Moving Average (WMA) teacher that replaces the standard EMA teacher: instead of exponentially decaying toward the current student (and collapsing), the WMA teacher averages the entire student trajectory with a Beta(0.5,0.5) kernel on normalized time, maintaining a persistent teacher–student gap and a continuous restoring force over finite training horizons. The total training objective combines symmetric InfoNCE with a multi-perspective self-distillation loss (feature distillation, contrastive relational distillation, interactive contrastive learning, and cross-knowledge distillation) guided by this WMA teacher, achieving bias-free convergence to the task-optimal solution in the task subspace while preserving orthogonal pretrained knowledge.

## Results

On CLIP ViT-B/16 finetuned on ImageNet-1K, TRACER achieves 82.76% in-distribution accuracy and 65.46% average OOD accuracy across five distribution-shift benchmarks (IN-V2, IN-R, IN-A, IN-S, ObjectNet), the best OOD average among all compared methods. The largest gains appear on the hardest shifts: IN-A (54.72% vs. 51.57% for CaRot) and IN-R (79.30% vs. 77.74% for CaRot). TRACER also attains the lowest average OOD ECE (0.073) on ViT-B/16, indicating superior calibration under shift. Across three backbones (RN50, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14), TRACER consistently achieves the best or near-best OOD accuracy while remaining competitive on ID accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACER achieves the highest average OOD accuracy (65.46%) among all 11 compared methods on ViT-B/16, outperforming the strongest baseline CaRot by +1.43 points and Model Stock by +3.06 points. The margin is largest on the most challenging shifts: TRACER leads CaRot by +3.15 on IN-A (54.72 vs. 51.57) and +1.56 on IN-R (79.30 vs. 77.74), precisely the renditions and adversarial examples where the WMA teacher's persistent regularization is most beneficial. On in-distribution ImageNet accuracy, TRACER (82.76%) is slightly behind Model Stock (84.07%) and CaRot (83.16%), a small trade-off for substantially better OOD robustness. Compared to simpler baselines, the gains are substantial: TRACER improves over Direct FT by +8.38, L2-SP by +8.12, and Static SD by +5.91 on average OOD accuracy, confirming that trajectory-regularized dynamic distillation is far more effective than fixed-anchor or weight-space regularization. On the toy MNIST experiment, Dynamic Distillation (TRACER's mechanism) reduces catastrophic forgetting from 37.9% (Direct FT) to just 0.1%, while retaining near-perfect accuracy on the new task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the linearized contrastive learning theory of Nakada et al. (2023) and Ji et al. (2023) to derive a unified geometric decomposition of finetuning strategies, and extends the self-distillation framework of Furlanello et al. (2018) by replacing the static anchor with a dynamic Weighted Moving Average (WMA) teacher that generalizes the EMA/mean-teacher mechanism of Tarvainen & Valpola (2017). It departs from prior robust finetuning work (WiSE-FT, CaRot, Lipsum-FT) by identifying a specific, overlooked failure mode—EMA teacher collapse—and showing that trajectory-weighted averaging with a Beta(0.5,0.5) kernel restores a persistent regularizing force, yielding bias-free convergence in the task subspace while preserving orthogonal pretrained knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of EMA collapse as a fundamental limitation in robust finetuning provides a concrete explanation for why many dynamic-teacher methods underperform on OOD benchmarks, and the WMA fix is simple enough to adopt in existing pipelines. The geometric decomposition (task subspace vs. orthogonal complement) gives practitioners a principled lens for choosing between L2-SP, static SD, and dynamic SD. Limitations include the linearized encoder assumption, evaluation restricted to CLIP-style vision-language backbones on standard ImageNet shift suites, and no experiments on broader modalities or multimodal LLM settings; extending the trajectory-regularization idea to non-linear regimes, parameter-efficient methods (LoRA), and continual learning remains open.
