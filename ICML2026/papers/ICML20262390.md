## Problem

Large language models underperform in specialized cybersecurity domains due to a lack of high-quality, domain-specific training data and models, while commercial frontier models are constrained by safety guardrails, data-residency compliance, and cost for enterprise security operations. This paper addresses the gap by introducing CyberPal 2.0, a family of cybersecurity-expert small language models (4B–20B parameters) trained on a novel expert-driven data enrichment pipeline that produces structured, evidence-grounded reasoning traces for security tasks.

## Input / Output

The models consume cybersecurity instruction prompts spanning 105 task types (vulnerability-to-CWE mapping, ATT&CK technique identification, detection/mitigation generation, CVSS reassessment under deployment constraints, and unstructured threat-report classification) and produce structured chain-of-thought reasoning traces that follow expert-defined task-specific formats specifying exact intermediate reasoning steps. A key design choice is adaptive reasoning depth: long-form CoT examples are paired with 'step-by-step' requests for complex tasks, while shorter instructions are paired with concise fast-response requests, enabling the same model to handle both reasoning-intensive and lightweight queries. Evaluation uses zero-shot CoT prompting with a regex parser to extract final answers, and the 8k-token context window constrains retrieval to at most four search results per instruction to avoid lost-in-the-middle degradation.

## Method

The core contribution is SecKnowledge 2.0, a three-stage data reformatting and enrichment pipeline that transforms the original SecKnowledge instruction dataset into structured, evidence-grounded supervision. First, an expert-in-the-loop system semi-automatically generates and iteratively refines task-specific reasoning formats for all 105 tasks, where experts execute the full pipeline on representative inputs and edit formats based on LLM-as-a-Judge feedback on readability and factuality. Second, a multi-step LLM-guided search pipeline (query brainstorming → query filtering → retrieval from vector DB or web → parsing) retrieves external evidence to ground each reasoning step, reducing hallucination during reformatting. Third, the backbone LLM (gpt-oss-120b, Medium reasoning) rewrites original answers into format-conformant, evidence-supported reasoning traces, verified by a second LLM-as-a-Judge pass. Models are fine-tuned from base checkpoints (Qwen3-4B/8B/14B-base, gpt-oss-20B) rather than post-trained ones, using incremental training (original SecKnowledge followed by SecKnowledge 2.0), partial prompt loss retention, and a 25% mix of original short-response examples to teach adaptive reasoning depth.

## Results

Across nine cybersecurity benchmarks, CyberPal 2.0 models (4B–20B) achieve 7–14% average gains over their corresponding post-trained baselines, with the largest improvements on CTIBench-RCM (+16–31%) and CTIBench-MCQ (+8–12%). The 20B model scores 87.40% on CTIBench-RCM (root-cause mapping), ranking first overall and surpassing GPT-4o, o1, o3-mini, and Sec-Gemini v1, while the 4B model ranks second. On CTIBench-MCQ (core CTI knowledge), the 20B and 14B models score 75.71% and 75.51% respectively, ranking second and third behind only Sec-Gemini v1. On real-world tasks, CyberPal 2.0 improves threat-report-to-TTP mapping by 2.75–8.75 points and CVE reassessment (normalized MAD) by 0.009–0.096 over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against frontier closed models, CyberPal 2.0-20B outperforms GPT-4o, o1, and o3-mini on CTIBench-RCM (87.40%) and ranks first overall, while on CTIBench-MCQ it ranks second behind Sec-Gemini v1 (75.71% vs. Sec-Gemini v1's higher score); the 4B model still ranks second on RCM. Against open-source cybersecurity models (DeepHat-V1-7B, Lily-Cybersecurity-7B, Primus-merged/reasoning, Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct), CyberPal 2.0-8B achieves 80.37% average, outperforming all by 15.6–27.0 percentage points. Against larger general-purpose open-source models from diverse architectures (Phi-4, Llama 4 Scout 17B, Mixtral 8x22B, Mistral Small 3.2 24B, DeepSeek-V3 685B), CyberPal 2.0-14B achieves the highest average (81.76%), surpassing DeepSeek-V3 (75.23%) by 6.5 points despite having ~49× fewer parameters. The paper also reports that CyberPal 2.0-14B narrows the gap to GPT-4o and LLaMA-4-Maverick on CyberSOCEval Threat Intelligence Reasoning to roughly 4% on average, while the 4B model improves Jaccard score from 10.12 to 51.85 over its baseline on the same task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Levi et al.'s (AAAI 2025) SecKnowledge dataset and CyberPal.AI framework by introducing SecKnowledge 2.0, a reformatting and enrichment pipeline that adds expert-in-the-loop task-specific format definition and multi-step LLM-guided document grounding on top of the original template-based instruction set. Methodologically, it builds on the Reformatted Alignment (ReAlign) pipeline of Fan et al. (2024) as its baseline, departing from it by replacing generic CoT formats with domain-expert-defined reasoning structures and by incorporating retrieval-based grounding to reduce hallucination during answer rewriting. Unlike prior cybersecurity LLM efforts (e.g., Yu et al.'s Primus, Weerawardhena et al.'s Foundation-Sec) that emphasize security-focused pretraining or distillation, this work takes a data-first supervised fine-tuning route, arguing that expert-driven, document-grounded instruction quality is the critical lever for reliable security reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: enterprises constrained by data residency, compliance, and cost requirements now have open-source 4B–20B models that match or surpass frontier closed models (GPT-4o, o1, Sec-Gemini v1) on core threat-intelligence and root-cause-mapping benchmarks, enabling on-premises deployment of security-operations LLMs. Two findings carry broader methodological weight: (1) fine-tuning from a base model yields a 2.7× larger gain than from a post-trained checkpoint under the same recipe, suggesting that high-quality domain data can override the benefits of prior alignment; and (2) the reformatting backbone LLM (120B vs. 400B) has negligible effect, implying pipeline design matters more than teacher scale. Open questions include whether the 8k context window limits real-world applicability (e.g., full malware sandbox reports require 128k tokens), how to mitigate the observed ~6.5% drop in secure code generation pass rates, and whether the expert-in-the-loop format pipeline generalizes to other specialized domains beyond cybersecurity.
