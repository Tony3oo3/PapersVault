## Problem

Stochastic smoothing provides a general mechanism to differentiate non-differentiable black-box functions by perturbing inputs with a probability distribution, but prior gradient estimators required the smoothing density to be differentiable with full support, restricting the usable distribution family. Additionally, existing estimators suffered from high variance and were primarily developed for convex optimizers rather than arbitrary non-differentiable functions. This paper removes the differentiability and full-support assumptions and systematically addresses variance reduction for the resulting gradient estimators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-differentiable black-box function f: R^n → R^m (callable many times at low cost), an input point x, and a smoothing distribution specified by its type (Gaussian, Logistic, Gumbel, Cauchy, Laplace, or Triangular) and scale (scalar γ or invertible matrix L for anisotropic smoothing). It produces an unbiased Monte Carlo estimate of the Jacobian ∇_x f_ε(x) ∈ R^{m×n} and, optionally, the derivative ∂f_ε(x)/∂L ∈ R^{m×n×n} with respect to the scale matrix. The estimator requires s function evaluations of f at perturbed points x + L·ε_i, and the number of samples s is a free hyperparameter that trades off gradient variance against computational cost.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution generalizes stochastic smoothing to any absolutely continuous density (not necessarily differentiable or full-support), proving that f_ε(x) = E_ε[f(x+ε)] remains differentiable and deriving the score-function gradient estimator ∇_x f_ε(x) = E_ε[f(x+ε)·∇_ε(−log μ(ε))] with an indicator for the zero-measure set where the density gradient is undefined. This enables smoothing with Laplace, Triangular, and Wigner Semicircle distributions, which were previously excluded. For variance reduction, the paper develops three orthogonal strategies: (1) covariates (subtracting f(x) or a leave-one-out estimate of f_ε(x) from the integrand), (2) antithetic sampling (pairing ε with −ε for symmetric distributions), and (3) randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) with Cartesian or Latin-hypercube stratification, which reduces variance from O(1/√s) to O(1/s^{1+2/n}). The framework further supports non-diagonal covariance matrices via a Cholesky-based scale matrix L, differentiation with respect to the scale parameter γ, and a k-sample median extension for cases where the expectation is undefined (e.g., Cauchy smoothing of unbounded functions). Empirically, the best configuration is Cartesian RQMC with the LOO covariate, applied to differentiable sorting/ranking, shortest-paths, rendering, and cryo-ET simulation.

## Results

On the 4-digit MNIST sorting benchmark (n=5), generalized stochastic smoothing (GSS) with the Cauchy distribution and 32,768 samples achieves 85.1% exact-match accuracy, marginally surpassing the state-of-the-art monotonic DSN (85.0%). With only 1,024 samples, GSS (Laplace, best cv) already reaches 84.3%, competitive with SOTA. On the Warcraft shortest-path benchmark, GSS with 10,000 samples achieves 96.6% exact-match accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA of 95.8% (Petersen et al., 2021b) and the Fenchel-Young loss baseline of 80.6% (Berthet et al., 2020). Across all four experimental domains, Cartesian RQMC with the leave-one-out (LOO) covariate consistently yields the lowest gradient variance, while antithetic sampling performs poorly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSS outperforms all sorting baselines except the two monotonic DSN variants. Specifically, at 32k samples, Cauchy GSS (85.1%) edges out Error-opt. DSN (85.0%) and Cauchy DSN (84.9%) without requiring a manually designed sorting algorithm; at 1k samples, Laplace GSS (84.3%) already matches or exceeds OT Sort (81.1%), NeuralSort (71.3%), SoftSort (70.7%), and Logistic DSN (77.2%). On the Warcraft shortest-path task, GSS with 10,000 samples (96.6%) surpasses the previous SOTA of algorithmic supervision (95.8%, Petersen et al., 2021b) and dramatically outperforms the Fenchel-Young perturbed optimizer (80.6%, Berthet et al., 2020), which plateaus regardless of sample count. The key advantage of GSS over perturbed-optimizer methods is that it treats the algorithm as a black box and scales gracefully with the number of function evaluations, whereas FY-based methods do not improve beyond a certain sample budget. For differentiable rendering (Utah teapot pose estimation) and cryo-ET parameter optimization, GSS with logistic/Laplace distributions and RQMC+LOO variance reduction consistently outperforms vanilla Monte Carlo, though direct numerical baselines are not tabulated for these two domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes stochastic smoothing from the restricted setting of perturbed convex optimizers (Berthet et al., 2020) and differentiable-density-with-full-support assumptions (Abernethy et al., 2016) to arbitrary non-differentiable black-box functions with any absolutely continuous density, including non-differentiable ones like Laplace and Triangular. It extends the framework from scalar to vector-valued outputs, from isotropic to anisotropic (non-diagonal covariance) smoothing, and introduces a k-sample median extension for cases where the expectation is undefined. The variance reduction analysis from three orthogonal perspectives—covariates, antithetic sampling, and (randomized) quasi-Monte Carlo—repurposes techniques from numerical integration and RL (REINFORCE) into a unified gradient estimation framework for differentiable algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unified toolkit for differentiating non-differentiable algorithms (sorting, shortest paths, rendering, cryo-ET simulators) without requiring custom continuous relaxations, which is valuable for the growing ecosystem of differentiable algorithmic supervision. Key limitations include that zeroth-order estimators are only competitive when first-order gradients are unavailable, the optimal smoothing distribution is problem-dependent with no universal choice, and QMC/RQMC variance reduction is inapplicable in single-sample or online settings. Open questions include whether the framework extends to discrete or combinatorial inputs, whether the algorithm-vs-loss smoothing distinction generalizes beyond the tested domains, and whether the dramatic RQMC variance reductions (up to 2585×) persist in higher-dimensional or adversarial function landscapes.
