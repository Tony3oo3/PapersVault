## Problem

Existing graph OOD detection methods primarily exploit topological structure similarities to identify distributional patterns, overlooking the semantic structure preserved in contextual affinity neighborhoods. The paper argues that samples from the same distribution share contextual affinity—similar relational patterns with a set of reference samples—even when their topological structures differ substantially, and this semantic signal is critical for drawing a discriminative ID/OOD boundary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes batches of attributed undirected graphs (node features, adjacency matrices) as input, requiring only in-distribution graphs during training with no OOD labels. Within each batch, a contextual affinity graph is constructed from pairwise dot-product similarities, and a meta-GNN refines these relations. At test time, each graph is scored by its compression ratio—an online silhouette-like quantity comparing its distance to its assigned cluster center versus the nearest other cluster center—and a fixed threshold η converts this score into a binary ID/OOD label.

## Method

CLINIC operates in three stages. First, it builds a contextual affinity graph per batch using reciprocal k-NN with soft weighting, then applies a meta-GNN with weighted message passing to capture high-order affinity, yielding a contextually affinitive neighborhood for each graph. Second, twin concordance learning is applied: a perturbation-aware loss enforces KL-divergence consistency between the similarity distributions of an original graph and its augmented view over their shared affinitive neighbors, while a contextual-aware loss (SCAN-style soft clustering with entropy regularization) pushes a graph and its affinitive neighbors toward the same cluster assignment. Third, a decision boundary compression loss—inspired by the silhouette score—minimizes the ratio (d^N − d^I)/max(d^I, d^N) to pull ID samples toward cluster centers, and this compression ratio directly serves as the OOD detection score at inference. The total loss is L_ins + β·L_con + γ·L_r, trained end-to-end with a GNN encoder under a fully unsupervised, self-supervised paradigm.

## Results

CLINIC achieves state-of-the-art AUC across 10 graph OOD detection dataset pairs, with average improvements of 1.16% over HGOE and 2.65% over GOOD-D. The strongest results include 99.42% AUC on AIDS+DHFR, 96.32% on BZR+COX2, and 93.11% on Esol+MUV. In the supplementary anomaly detection experiments (14 TU datasets), CLINIC attains the best average rank (1.6) among all 11 compared methods, with notable gains on DD (81.91%), IMDB-B (73.14%), and REDIT-B (89.92%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLINIC outperforms the strongest end-to-end baseline GOOD-D on all 10 dataset pairs, with the largest margins on Tox21+SIDER (+4.19%), ENZYMES+PROTEIN (+3.02%), and ClinTox+LIPO (+2.13%). Against HGOE, CLINIC wins on 9 of 10 pairs, losing only on FreeSolv+ToxCast (82.00 vs. 82.89). Compared to CGOD (WWW 2025), CLINIC leads on 8 of 10 pairs, with the most significant advantage on ClinTox+LIPO (+6.18%) and Tox21+SIDER (+1.59%). Two-step methods (PK/WL kernels with iF/OCSVM/LOF, and InfoGraph/GraphCL with iF/MD) are consistently weaker, typically scoring 10–40 AUC points below the end-to-end approaches. In the supplementary anomaly detection setting (14 TU datasets), CLINIC achieves the best average rank (1.6) versus HGOE (2.0) and GLocalKD (4.1), confirming generalizability beyond the paired OOD protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLINIC extends the graph OOD detection line of work (GOOD-D, HGOE, CGOD, GLocalKD) by shifting focus from topological structure to semantic structure preserved in contextual affinity neighborhoods, a concept borrowed from the contextually affinitive neighborhood refinery framework (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2023). It repurposes concordance learning—previously applied in semi-supervised graph classification (TGNN, HEAL)—as the first method to use it for OOD detection, enforcing consistency between similarity distributions across augmented views within the affinitive space. The meta-GNN message-passing over a batch-level affinity graph and the silhouette-inspired decision boundary compression are novel architectural choices that distinguish CLINIC from prior contrastive or generative OOD approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior graph OOD methods treat all non-matching graphs as equally negative, ignoring that semantically related graphs from the same distribution should be treated differently. The theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.1–3.2) providing a formal link between the compression ratio and OOD separability are a useful addition to a task that has largely lacked such analysis. However, the method's reliance on batch-level k-NN context introduces sensitivity to hyperparameters (k₁, k₂, C, k) that vary across datasets, and the anomaly detection results (Table 3) show CLINIC underperforming HGOE on several datasets, suggesting the contextual affinity mechanism may not generalize uniformly. Open questions include scalability to very large graph batches and whether the approach transfers to node- or edge-level OOD detection.
