## Problem

Most computer vision systems encode the visual world at uniform resolution, making high-resolution, large-field-of-view processing computationally prohibitive—especially for transformers where attention cost scales quadratically with sequence length. While the human visual system solves this via foveation (variable resolution peaking at the fovea), prior computational foveation approaches (log-polar, warped Cartesian) introduce anisotropic sampling and warped receptive fields, and no general-purpose, biologically-plausible foveated interface exists that can be adopted across diverse architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 256×256 ambient resolution) along with one or more fixation coordinates, and samples a constrained pixel budget (e.g., ~4096 pixels) using a foveated pattern whose density depends only on eccentricity from the fixation. The sampled points are reformatted onto a uniformly dense, curved 2D sensor manifold (a V1-like surface) rather than a flat rectangular image. Output is a class label; during inference, multiple fixations produce independent logits that are averaged before the final prediction, and the intermediate activations live on the sensor manifold with locally isotropic receptive fields.

## Method

FOVI defines a foveated sensor using the Rovamo & Virsu cortical magnification function M(r)=1/(r+a), where the parameter a controls foveation strength (smaller a = stronger foveation, a→∞ = uniform sampling). The key processing mechanism is kNN-convolution: receptive fields are k-nearest-neighborhoods on the sensor manifold, and a reference kernel learned in a standard Cartesian frame is mapped into each neighborhood via a polar-to-Cartesian coordinate transformation, enabling weight sharing while preserving orientation alignment across visual space. Two architectures are demonstrated: (1) FOVI-CNNs, an AlexNet-like network with multiple kNN-convolution layers trained end-to-end, and (2) FOVI-ViTs, where kNN-convolution replaces the standard patch embedding in pre-trained DINOv3 ViTs, with LoRA applied to the patch embedding and first half of transformer layers to adapt the frozen backbone to foveated inputs. This yields competitive ImageNet accuracy (84% top-1 for ViT-H+) using only ~1/16 of the pixels and ~1/3 of the GFLOPS of full-resolution baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, FOVI-ViT-H+@64 (a=2.79) achieves 84.4% top-1 accuracy using only 3,976 pixels (1/16 of the 50,176-pixel full-resolution baseline) and 58.43 GFLOPS (≈1/3 of the 172.39 GFLOPS baseline), reaching >96% of the ViT-H+ uniform @224 accuracy (87.1%) in a single fixation. The smaller FOVI-ViT-S+@64 variant achieves 70.0% top-1 at 1 fixation and 73.5% at 3 fixations, outperforming the pixel-matched uniform @64 baseline (69.3% / 72.6%) and the log-polar @64 baseline (64.3% / 69.4%). In the FOVI-CNN experiments on ImageNet-1K with a 64×64 pixel budget sampled from 256×256 inputs, an inverted-U-shaped performance curve over the foveation parameter a peaks at a=0.5, with intermediate foveation outperforming the pixel-matched uniform model (a=500).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-resolution DINOv3 baselines, FOVI-ViT-H+@64 achieves 84.4% top-1 with only 1/16 the pixels and ~1/3 the GFLOPS of ViT-H+ uniform @224 (87.1%), and FOVI-ViT-S+@64 reaches 70.0% versus 79.4% for ViT-S+ uniform @224 (88.2% of baseline accuracy). Against the pixel-matched uniform @64 baseline, FOVI-ViT-S+@64 wins by 0.7 points at 1 fixation (70.0% vs. 69.3%) and by 0.9 points at 3 fixations (73.5% vs. 72.6%), with comparable GFLOPS and latency. Against the log-polar @64 baseline, FOVI-ViT-S+@64 wins decisively by 5.7 points at 1 fixation (70.0% vs. 64.3%) and by 4.1 points at 3 fixations (73.5% vs. 69.4%), which the authors attribute to log-polar's anisotropic sampling causing inconsistent filter orientation and a stronger distribution shift from pre-training. Against the weak-foveation control (a=60.94, near-uniform sampling on the same kNN-convolution manifold), FOVI-ViT-S+@64 (a=2.79) wins by 0.7 points at 1 fixation and 1.0 points at 3 fixations, confirming that moderate foveation provides a measurable advantage over near-uniform sampling under the same pixel budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Rovamo & Virsu (1984) cortical magnification function to define a locally isotropic foveated sensor manifold, and on DINOv3 (Siméoni et al., 2025) as the pre-trained foundation model to be adapted. It departs from prior foveated sensing approaches—log-polar grids and warped Cartesian re-projections—which the authors show produce anisotropic sampling and warped receptive fields, and from Killick et al. (2023), which uses fixed Gaussian-derivative filters rather than learned kernels. The key methodological departure is the introduction of kNN-convolution with a kernel mapping technique that enables standard convolutional weight-sharing over the non-uniform sensor manifold, making the interface compatible with both CNNs and ViTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOVI provides a general-purpose, biologically-grounded foveated sensor interface that can be dropped into diverse architectures, with the practical result that a DINOv3 ViT-H+ variant achieves 84% ImageNet top-1 using only ~4K pixels and roughly one-third the GFLOPS of the full-resolution baseline. The paper is honest about limitations: kNN-convolution introduces computational overhead relative to standard 2D convolution, evaluations are restricted to low-resolution image classification (no detection or segmentation), and the kNN operation has not been fully optimized. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution video, active vision with learned fixation policies, and whether the isotropy advantage over log-polar persists in more complex tasks.
