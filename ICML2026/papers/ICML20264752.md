## Problem

LLMs struggle with unseen, non-linguistic tasks (symbolic, spatial, long-horizon) not merely due to pretraining distribution mismatch, but because the exploration phase—learning environment dynamics through trial-and-error—is computationally prohibitive for parameter-heavy models operating in a high-dimensional token space. This paper identifies exploration inefficiency as the primary bottleneck and asks whether it can be decoupled from the exploitation (reasoning) phase that LLMs are well-suited for.

## Input / Output

The LLM agent receives symbolic environment states (e.g., 4×4 Sudoku grids, 2×2 Rubik's Cube face color arrays, 4×4 2048 tile grids) serialized as text, augmented with task descriptions and rules, and produces one discrete action per turn (a move direction, a number placement, or a face rotation) wrapped in a multi-turn dialogue with explicit thinking blocks. The lightweight scouts consume only the raw symbolic state vector without any language context, and their trajectories are deterministically converted into the LLM's dialogue format via a Textualizer that maps states, actions, and rewards into user/assistant turns with blank thinking placeholders.

## Method

SCOUT (Sub-Scale Collaboration On Unseen Tasks) decouples exploration from exploitation across three stages. First, lightweight scouts (~10⁻⁵ B parameters, MLPs or CNNs) are trained with DQN or PPO on the raw symbolic MDP to rapidly master environment dynamics and generate 4k expert trajectories per task. Second, a Textualizer converts these trajectories into multi-turn dialogue format, and the LLM is warm-started via SFT on this distilled data with thinking blocks left blank, internalizing the task 'physics' without expensive self-exploration. Third, multi-turn PPO (trajectory-level optimization with KL regularization) is applied to the SFT checkpoint, encouraging the model to generate meaningful reasoning in thinking blocks and activating latent world knowledge that the scout's limited capacity could not express. This allows the LLM to surpass the scout's performance (e.g., 0.86 vs. 0.83 average for Qwen2.5-3B) while reducing GPU cost by ~60% compared to direct PPO.

## Results

SCOUT enables Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct to achieve an average success rate of 0.86 across 6 unseen symbolic/spatial tasks (Bandit, 2048, FrozenLake, Sokoban, Rubik's Cube, Sudoku), substantially outperforming the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.60) and all other baselines. The full SCOUT pipeline (Exploration + Distillation + Evolving) yields a +0.68 improvement over the base model (0.18 → 0.86), with the Evolving Stage alone contributing an additional +0.13 over Distillation-only (0.73 → 0.86). SCOUT also reduces GPU cost by ~60% (9.6 vs 24.0 GPU-hours on Rubik's Cube Rotation3) by offloading exploration to CPU-based scouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCOUT (Qwen2.5-3B-It, avg 0.86) decisively outperforms all seven baselines. Against the closest proprietary competitor Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.60), SCOUT gains +0.26, with the largest gaps on Rubik's Cube (0.90 vs 0.16 on Rotation3) and 2048 (1024 vs 256 max tile). Versus the RL-based baselines on the same 3B backbone—Multi-turn PPO/RAGEN (0.38), SPA (0.37), and State Estimation RL (0.28)—SCOUT achieves roughly 2× the average score, confirming that the exploration bottleneck, not reasoning capacity, is the primary limitation. Notably, SCOUT also surpasses its own scout teacher (Scout-DQN, avg 0.83) by +0.03, demonstrating that the LLM's latent world knowledge, once activated via multi-turn RL, can exceed the scout's limited capacity. On individual tasks, proprietary models like GPT-OSS-120B match or exceed SCOUT on Sudoku (1.00 vs 0.97) and Sokoban-Box1 (0.95 vs 0.97), but SCOUT dominates on spatial (Rubik's Cube) and long-horizon (2048) tasks where all proprietary models score near zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RAGEN (Wang et al., 2025d) for its multi-turn PPO training infrastructure and on SPA/State Estimation RL (Chen et al., 2025), which first identified the out-of-distribution performance gap for LLM agents on symbolic tasks. However, SCOUT departs fundamentally from both: rather than trying to improve the LLM's own exploration (as RAGEN does) or its state estimation (as SPA does), it argues that exploration should be entirely delegated to lightweight non-linguistic neural networks (MLPs/CNNs) that operate at ~10^5× lower parameter cost. This positions the work at the intersection of large-small model collaboration and classical deep RL, but with a key distinction: the scouts carry no pretrained linguistic knowledge and learn dynamics purely from trial-and-error, making them true 'physics learners' rather than compressed language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 3B open-source model outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.86 vs. 0.60 average) while saving ~60% GPU hours offers a compelling recipe for resource-constrained labs to build competent agentic systems. The framework's generalization to vision-based POMDPs and multi-task sequential learning without catastrophic forgetting suggests broad applicability. However, limitations remain: experiments are capped at 3B parameters, only PPO is used for the Evolving Stage (GRPO and other algorithms are untested), and the task suite, while diverse, consists of relatively low-dimensional symbolic environments. Open questions include whether the scout-LLM gap widens or narrows at larger scales, whether the approach transfers to truly open-ended or continuous-action domains, and how sensitive performance is to scout quality in more complex settings.
