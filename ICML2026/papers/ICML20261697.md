## Problem

In Federated Graph Learning, clients hold subgraphs with substantially different homophily levels, causing their local gradients to point in conflicting directions during global aggregation and degrading the shared model. Existing remedies rely on multi-channel architectures that multiply client-side memory and communication costs, and most lack formal convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether inter-client optimization conflicts can be resolved at the server level without altering the client-side model architecture.

## Input / Output

Each of M clients receives a local subgraph (node feature matrix X_i ∈ R^{N_i×D} and adjacency A_i) partitioned from a global graph via Louvain community detection, along with a small labeled node subset V_{i,lab} for semi-supervised node classification. The method outputs K group-specific GNN models {w̃_k}; each client is assigned to exactly one group by a one-time, label-free homophily score (the high-frequency area of its graph signal) and receives only its group's updated parameters. Clients maintain a standard single-model ACM-GNN (or GCN) architecture throughout, so the per-client memory and communication footprint matches vanilla FedAvg.

## Method

FedGCM introduces two server-side mechanisms. First, homophily-driven grouping computes each client's high-frequency area S_i = (1/D)Σ_d [X_i]_{:d}^T L_i [X_i]_{:d} / [X_i]_{:d}^T [X_i]_{:d} as a label-free proxy inversely correlated with local homophily, then bins clients into K groups by normalizing S_i to [0, K−1]. Second, RPGrad performs inter-group gradient surgery: for group k's average gradient g̅_k^t, each other group j's gradient is either passed through (if cos(g̅_k, g̅_j) ≥ 0) or orthogonally projected to remove its conflicting component; the resulting rectified gradients are combined with softmax-weighted cosine-similarity coefficients and a trade-off hyperparameter η, yielding a refined direction d̃_k^t that provably maintains positive alignment with g̅_k^t. The server updates each group model along −d̃_k^t and broadcasts group-specific parameters, while clients perform standard local SGD. A convergence theorem under convexity, bounded-gradient, and bounded-deviation assumptions shows the best-iterate optimization gap shrinks to a δR-neighborhood of the optimum with diminishing step sizes.

## Results

FedGCM achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy across all nine benchmarks (four homophilous, five heterophilous). On heterophilous graphs, the gains are most pronounced: 68.6% on Chameleon (+4.3 pp over the best baseline AdaFGL at 64.3%) and 51.2% on Squirrel (+2.5 pp over FedIIH at 48.7%). On homophilous graphs, FedGCM leads by smaller but consistent margins, e.g., 86.1% on Cora, 74.6% on CiteSeer, 88.5% on PubMed, and 96.1% on Physics. The method also matches or exceeds baselines on Minesweeper (85.4%), Amazon-ratings (53.4%), and Penn94 (76.6%), while maintaining client-side computational and communication overhead identical to standard FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGCM outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset. The largest margins appear on strongly heterophilous graphs: +4.3 pp over AdaFGL on Chameleon (68.6 vs 64.3) and +2.5 pp over FedIIH on Squirrel (51.2 vs 48.7), confirming that the gradient-surgery mechanism is most beneficial when homophily heterogeneity is severe. Among the dedicated homophily-heterogeneity methods, AdaFGL is the strongest competitor on heterophilous sets (e.g., 75.5 on Penn94, 52.5 on Amazon-ratings), while FedTAD leads on Minesweeper (84.8) and Amazon-ratings (53.0); FedGCM surpasses both by 0.4–1.1 pp in these cases. On homophilous graphs, the gaps are tighter (0.3–1.6 pp), with FedGSP closest on Cora (85.7) and FedHERO on Physics (95.8). FedGSP suffers an out-of-memory failure on Penn94, and FedGSP/FedHERO also OoM at 50/100-client scale, whereas FedGCM remains stable. Overall, FedGCM's advantage is consistent across all nine datasets and scales to 50 and 100 clients without degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedGCM sits at the intersection of federated graph learning and gradient surgery for multi-task optimization. It departs from the dominant multi-channel paradigm (FedSPA, FedHERO, FedGSP, AdaFGL) that addresses homophily heterogeneity by maintaining parallel model branches on clients, instead shifting conflict mitigation entirely to the server via a residual-projection gradient correction (RPGrad) inspired by PCGrad. The homophily-driven grouping strategy repurposes the spectral high-frequency area as a label-free proxy for graph homophily, a technique borrowed from spectral graph signal analysis (Tang et al., 2022; Tan et al., 2025), to avoid exhaustive pairwise conflict detection. Unlike standard clustered federated learning, FedGCM preserves explicit cross-group interaction through RPGrad rather than training isolated cluster-specific models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling conflict mitigation from client-side resource costs: FedGCM achieves SOTA node classification across nine homophilous and heterophilous benchmarks while maintaining client-side memory and computation identical to vanilla FedAvg, making it suitable for bandwidth- and memory-constrained edge deployments. The convergence guarantee under a convex surrogate, though standard in form, is notably absent from competing multi-channel FGL methods and provides formal grounding for the gradient surgery step. Open questions include whether the static, one-time homophily grouping adapts to non-stationary data distributions, whether the convex surrogate assumption is too restrictive for deep GNNs, and how to make the collaboration strength η adaptive rather than manually tuned. The paper also acknowledges that RPGrad is not a dedicated privacy or security defense, leaving differential-privacy or secure-aggregation integration as important future work.
