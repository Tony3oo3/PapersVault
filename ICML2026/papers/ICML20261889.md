## Problem

Vision-tabular multi-modal learning, critical in healthcare and industrial analytics, lacks a unified benchmark that jointly covers both discriminative prediction and generative reasoning across diverse domains. Existing benchmarks are either confined to narrow clinical subfields, render tables as images (collapsing the tabular modality into vision), or embed images in table cells without systematically probing structure-aware understanding and constrained numerical reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261889/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For discriminative prediction, each sample pairs a 224×224 RGB image with a single-row tabular feature vector (mixing categorical and numerical columns) and produces a discrete class label or continuous regression target. For generative reasoning, the model receives an image plus either a full table (serialized in Markdown, not rendered as an image) or a relational database schema, along with a natural-language question, and must produce a short canonical textual answer; in the database setting (EHRXQA), the model must first generate an executable SQL query to retrieve relevant records before reasoning. The DVM-Car QA dataset pairs a front-view car image with a table of 10–50 candidate vehicles (15 fixed attributes each), requiring the model to localize the correct row via a visual alignment key before performing cell retrieval, constrained counting, or conditional mean computation.

## Method

VT-Bench aggregates 14 datasets across 9 domains (756K+ samples) spanning both discriminative prediction (classification and regression) and generative reasoning (table-form and database-form QA), and evaluates 23 models including unimodal baselines, specialized vision-tabular fusion models (late-fusion and early-interaction), general-purpose VLMs, and tool-augmented methods. The benchmark introduces two modality-level diagnostic metrics: Modality Contribution Ratio (MCR), which ablates one modality by replacing its embedding with the training-set mean and measures the normalized performance drop to quantify per-modality reliance and detect negative transfer, and Modality Informativeness Ratio (MIR), which uses the best unimodal error as a dataset-level prior for relative modality informativeness. Four novel datasets are constructed to fill evaluation gaps: three large-scale medical prediction tasks (Pneumonia, Length of Stay, Respiratory Rate) built from MIMIC-IV/MIMIC-CXR with patient-level splits, and DVM-Car QA, a fine-grained vision-tabular reasoning benchmark with four progressively harder tasks (row localization, cell retrieval, constrained counting, conditional mean) plus an identification diagnostic. A unified Python API supports standardized evaluation, optional modality diagnostics, and extensibility to custom models and datasets.

## Results

In discriminative prediction, multi-modal fusion models consistently underperform the strongest unimodal baselines: the average performance gap (Δ) to the best unimodal model is negative for every multi-modal method (e.g., Concat −0.026 on classification, DAFT −0.056, CHARMS −0.126), while unimodal TabPFN-v2 (rank 5.3) and LightGBM (rank 6.1) remain highly competitive. In generative reasoning, even the best proprietary model (Gemini-3-Flash-Preview) achieves only 72.3% average accuracy on DVM-Car QA, 70.9% on MMQA, and 27.5% on EHRXQA, with open-source models trailing further (e.g., GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking: 51.7% DVM-Car QA, 56.2% MMQA). MCR diagnostics confirm that negative transfer is prevalent: removing one modality improves performance in a majority of model–dataset pairs, indicating that current fusion strategies fail to reliably integrate heterogeneous visual and tabular signals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central finding is that no multi-modal fusion model consistently beats the best unimodal baseline: TabPFN-v2 (rank 5.3) and LightGBM (rank 6.1) outperform all early-interaction models (CHARMS 8.1, MMCL 8.9, TIP 8.5, DAFT 7.7) and both VLMs (Qwen3-VL 7.5, Table-LLaVA 9.1) in average rank. Among multi-modal methods, late fusion (Concat 5.4, MAX 6.5) is markedly more robust than early-interaction fusion, with Concat achieving the smallest negative classification gap (−0.026) and even a positive regression gap (−0.557), while CHARMS and MMCL show the largest degradation (−0.126, −0.127). In generative reasoning, Gemini-3-Flash-Preview leads all models (72.3% DVM-Car QA, 70.9% MMQA, 27.5% EHRXQA), followed by GPT-4.1 (56.1%, 63.6%, 26.4%); among open-source models, GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking is strongest (51.7%, 56.2%, 7.3%), while Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking collapses to 0% on EHRXQA. Tool-augmented methods (StructGPT, Thyme) do not close the gap to proprietary VLMs and in some cases underperform open-source VLMs (e.g., Thyme 36.8% vs. GLM 51.7% on DVM-Car QA). MUL is excluded from MCR analysis due to catastrophic numerical instability in regression (RMSE > 21 million on Respiratory Rate), highlighting that even simple multiplicative fusion can fail catastrophically on unconstrained outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate lines of vision-tabular research—discriminative prediction (RadFusion, DAFT, CHARMS, MMCL, TIP) and generative table reasoning (TableVQA-Bench, MMTabQA, MMT-Bench, MMQA, EHRXQA)—into a single benchmark that treats tabular data as structured input rather than rendering it as an image. It departs from prior table-QA benchmarks that embed images in table cells or render tables visually, instead evaluating true cross-modal evidence integration between a natural image and a structured table/database. The introduction of modality-level diagnostic metrics (MCR and MIR) for dissecting fusion behavior is also novel relative to prior work that reports only aggregate accuracy or AUC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VT-Bench is likely to become the standard reference for vision-tabular multi-modal learning given its scale (756K samples, 14 datasets, 9 domains, 23 models) and its coverage of both discriminative and generative paradigms under a unified API. The finding that multi-modal fusion frequently causes negative transfer—where combining image and tabular inputs degrades performance below the best unimodal baseline—challenges a core assumption in the field and points toward the need for fundamentally different fusion strategies that respect modality heterogeneity. Open questions include how to learn discriminative representations for structurally distinct modalities within a shared space, how to give VLMs reliable table-structure grounding without rendering tables as images, and whether neuro-symbolic or tool-augmented pipelines can close the gap on constrained numerical reasoning and clinical SQL generation.
