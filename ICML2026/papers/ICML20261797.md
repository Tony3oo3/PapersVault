## Problem

Fine-tuning MLLMs on downstream tasks causes catastrophic forgetting of pretrained generalization, and existing mitigation methods fail under deeper fine-tuning regimes. Post-merging approaches (ModelTailor, Grafting, DARE) collapse when updates extend beyond the final few decoder layers, while the leading sparse method SPIDER incurs 3× memory overhead and still underperforms on early layers. The paper identifies that earlier decoder layers are critical for multimodal understanding yet remain largely unexplored in forgetting-mitigation research.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B or NVILA-Lite-2B) and a small downstream dataset (10k samples from COCO-Caption, Flickr30k, ImageNet-R, or IconQA). Uniquely, the importance scoring phase is entirely data-free: it generates N=64 synthetic prompts by sampling random tokens from the model's own vocabulary and propagating them through the network, requiring no access to pretraining or task-specific data. The output is a static binary mask (one bit per parameter) computed once before fine-tuning, which freezes the top (1−ρ)% most important parameters and allows gradient updates only on the remaining ρ% (default ρ=0.1), yielding a sparse fine-tuned model with O(|P|) memory identical to standard fine-tuning.

## Method

Model-Dowser derives a per-parameter importance score S_ij = ‖J_i‖₂ · |W_ij| · |h_j| from a first-order Taylor analysis (Theorem 3.1) showing that the L2 output shift from perturbing weight W_ij factorizes into output sensitivity (Jacobian column norm), connection strength (weight magnitude), and input activity (activation magnitude). To compute this data-free, the method uses the Hutchinson trace estimator with R=8 Rademacher vectors to stochastically estimate Jacobian column norms via squared scalar projections, avoiding explicit Jacobian construction, and averages over N=64 synthetic prompts as a Monte Carlo variance-reduction step. A binary mask is then generated by ranking all parameters within each layer by score and freezing the top (1−ρ) percentile. Unlike SPIDER, which maintains accumulated gradient histories and a soft mask matrix during training (O(3|P|) memory), Model-Dowser's mask is computed once in a single forward-backward pass and remains static throughout fine-tuning, keeping memory at O(|P|) and enabling straightforward scaling to multi-billion-parameter models.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B fine-tuned on the last 20 layers (ρ=0.1), Model-Dowser achieves H-scores of 79.9 on COCO-Caption and 69.7 on ImageNet-R, outperforming the strongest baseline SPIDER (75.2 and 62.8) by 4.7 and 6.9 points respectively. On NVILA-Lite-2B, the gains are even larger: H-scores of 85.7 (COCO-Caption) and 71.2 (ImageNet-R) versus SPIDER's 78.3 and 60.5. Model-Dowser also maintains the same O(|P|) memory complexity as standard fine-tuning, compared to O(3|P|) for SPIDER and O(2|P|) for Grafting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Model-Dowser outperforms all five baselines on both architectures and both primary downstream tasks. Against the strongest sparse-fine-tuning baseline SPIDER (Huang et al., 2025), Dowser gains +4.7 H-score on COCO-Caption and +6.9 on ImageNet-R for LLaVA-1.5-7B, and +7.4 and +10.7 for NVILA-Lite-2B, while also using 3× less memory (O(|P|) vs O(3|P|)). Post-merging methods (Grafting, DARE, Tailor) all degrade sharply when fine-tuning extends beyond the last ~16 layers; at 20 layers on LLaVA, DARE's ImageNet-R H-score drops to 42.7 (vs 69.7 for Dowser), and Grafting reaches only 63.8. The MLLM-specific post-merging method ModelTailor (Zhu et al., 2024) achieves 75.8 H-score on COCO-Caption but still trails Dowser by 4.1 points and falls further behind on ImageNet-R (55.7 vs 69.7). Against random parameter selection (Xu & Zhang, 2024), Dowser's advantage grows with update ratio: at ρ=0.5 on ImageNet-R (NVILA), Dowser scores 62.7 H-score versus 55.0 for random (p=0.004), confirming that principled importance scoring is essential as more parameters are updated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sparse fine-tuning for catastrophic forgetting mitigation and parameter importance estimation. It departs from post-merging methods (ModelTailor, Grafting, DARE) by showing they fundamentally fail under deeper fine-tuning, and from the closest sparse-fine-tuning competitor SPIDER by replacing its dynamic, gradient-history-based masking with a static, data-free importance score computed once via Hutchinson-estimated Jacobian sensitivity, weight magnitude, and input activation. The theoretical grounding (first-order Taylor approximation linking parameter perturbation to output shift) connects to the Optimal Brain Surgeon lineage but makes it tractable for billion-parameter MLLMs through stochastic estimation and synthetic probing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: Model-Dowser achieves the same O(|P|) memory footprint as standard fine-tuning while outperforming SPIDER's O(3|P|) overhead, and requires no access to pretraining data—making it immediately deployable for proprietary MLLMs. The finding that post-merging methods collapse under deeper fine-tuning (beyond the last 4–16 layers) reframes how the community should think about fine-tuning depth in MLLMs. Open questions include whether the static mask remains valid as the model's sensitivity landscape shifts during extended training, how the method scales to 70B+ models, and whether the importance score needs periodic recomputation for multi-task sequential adaptation.
