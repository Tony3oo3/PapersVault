## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods require labeled data from multiple training environments to estimate invariance properties, limiting their applicability when labels are scarce or expensive. This paper asks whether unlabeled multi-environment data can provide useful information about distributional shifts for robust prediction, and shows that this is possible under an anti-causal structure where the outcome Y causes the predictors X, since environment perturbations to X are independent of Y and can be characterized from the marginal distribution of X alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263925/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled (X, Y) pairs from a small subset of environments (e.g., 3–5 out of 6 in the Light Tunnel, or 20–127 out of 128 subjects in VitalDB) plus unlabeled X-only data from all training environments. X is a d-dimensional covariate vector (6 sensor readings in the Light Tunnel; 140-dimensional pretrained CNN embeddings of arterial pressure waveforms in VitalDB), and Y is a scalar outcome (RGB brightness or stroke volume variation). The output is a linear coefficient vector β; the regularization matrix H is computed exclusively from the unlabeled data, so the number of labeled environments can be much smaller than the number of environments contributing to the robustness estimate.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits the anti-causal SCM structure X = f₀(Y, U, ε_X) + ε_e, where the environment perturbation ε_e is independent of Y. Two regularization terms are added to the standard squared-error objective: MIR penalizes β^⊤ Var(K) β, where K is the matrix of environment-specific covariate means (capturing mean shifts in ε_e), and VIR penalizes β^⊤ (1/p)Σ(G_X^{e_i} − Ḡ_X)² β (capturing covariance shifts). Both terms are estimated solely from unlabeled data, and the paper proves that the resulting estimators are optimal for worst-case risk over a class of environments whose perturbation second moments deviate from the training average by at most γ times the observed variation. For nonlinear or high-dimensional settings, the approach is applied to a linear head on top of a fixed pretrained representation φ(X), with the regularization computed in the representation space. The regularization strength γ is selected via nested leave-one-environment-out cross-validation, and when the anti-causal assumption is violated, cross-validation naturally selects γ ≈ 0, recovering the unregularized estimator.

## Results

On the Light Tunnel dataset (6 environments, 6 sensor predictors), MIR achieves lower or comparable RMSE to all baselines across all outcome-intervention pairs under leave-one-environment-out evaluation, with the largest gains when only 3 of 5 training environments are labeled. On VitalDB (128 subjects, 140-dim APW embeddings), VIR achieves lower nMSE across all CVaR quantiles compared to Pooled-Ridge, GroupDRO, and DRIG, with the improvement most pronounced on the worst-performing subjects (highest quantiles). VIR maintains a median per-subject Spearman correlation of ~0.8, comparable to Pooled-Ridge, confirming that robustness gains do not come at the cost of tracking accuracy. The gap between VIR and VIR-Oracle (oracle γ selection) remains significant, indicating room for improved hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIR outperforms AnchorReg (Rothenhausler et al., 2021) on the Light Tunnel dataset across all labeled-environment counts, with the advantage growing as fewer environments carry labels. On VitalDB, AnchorReg is inapplicable because subject-centering removes mean shifts (its regularization term vanishes), so the paper substitutes DRIG (Shen et al., 2026); VIR beats DRIG on nMSE at every CVaR quantile, with the largest margin on the hardest subjects. Both MIR and VIR consistently outperform GroupDRO (Sagawa et al., 2020) on both datasets, particularly in the low-label regime where GroupDRO's convex-combination constraint limits extrapolation. Pooled-Ridge (standard ridge regression) is the weakest baseline on both datasets, showing substantially higher RMSE/nMSE on unseen environments, especially when only a few labeled environments are available. The paper also shows that when the anti-causal assumption is violated (reverse prediction on Light Tunnel), MIR's cross-validation selects γ≈0 and the method collapses to Pooled-Ridge, confirming that the regularization is data-driven and does not harm performance under assumption violations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the invariant-prediction and invariance-regularization literature (Peters et al. 2016; Arjovsky et al. 2019; Rothenhäusler et al. 2021; Shen et al. 2026) by showing that, under an anti-causal structural causal model, the regularization matrix can be estimated entirely from unlabeled multi-environment data. It builds directly on Schölkopf et al. (2012), who discussed anti-causal learning in a single-domain semi-supervised setting, and generalizes that idea to multi-environment domain generalization. The key departure from prior invariance-regularization methods is that the penalty term depends only on the marginal distribution of X across environments, removing the requirement for labeled data from every environment. The theoretical guarantees are closely related to those in Rothenhäusler et al. (2021) and Shen et al. (2026) but are derived under the anti-causal assumption, which is what makes the unlabeled-data-only estimation valid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in healthcare and other high-stakes domains, labeled data from multiple environments are scarce while unlabeled data are abundant, and the anti-causal structure (outcome causes predictors) is natural in settings like physiological monitoring and speech recognition. The worst-case optimality guarantees and the graceful degradation under assumption violations (MIR collapses to Pooled-Ridge when the anti-causal direction is reversed) provide both theoretical assurance and practical safety. Open questions include the significant gap between VIR and VIR-Oracle (hyperparameter tuning remains unsolved), extension to nonparametric or tree-based methods, and accounting for higher-moment shifts in nonlinear settings. The anti-causal assumption, while natural in several domains, limits the method's applicability to settings where the outcome is not a descendant of the predictors.
