## Problem

In model-based RL with policy-planner bootstrapping, two complementary policy improvement mechanisms—exploitative Q-maximization and lookahead planner alignment—are theoretically proven to converge to the same optimum, yet in practice their gradients frequently point in divergent or opposing directions. This gradient inconsistency, exacerbated by approximation errors and non-stationary data, causes destructive interference in policy updates, destabilizing the bootstrapping loop and leading to suboptimal convergence or training collapse.

## Input / Output

The method operates on high-dimensional state vectors (e.g., 67-dim humanoid, 223-dim dog, 151-dim H1hand) and outputs continuous action vectors of corresponding dimensionality (24, 38, 61 dims respectively). It is a drop-in gradient-level module that requires no structural changes to the planner, world model, or loss definitions; it only intercepts the two policy gradients (g_maxQ and g_minKL) before the parameter update, computing a harmonized direction h via a one-dimensional line search over α ∈ [0,2] with negligible overhead (~8% wall-clock increase).

## Method

HDPI formalizes the policy-planner bootstrapping loop as a Dual Policy Improvement (DPI) framework and proves both mechanisms share a common optimum. At each training step, it computes the exploitative gradient g_maxQ (from Q-maximization) and the lookahead gradient g_minKL (from KL-divergence alignment with an MPPI planner), then forms a nominal direction ĝ = g_maxQ + λg_minKL. The key mechanism is a trust-region constrained minimax problem that seeks an update direction h within a ball of radius ρ‖ĝ‖ centered at ĝ, maximizing the worst-case inner product with both original gradients to ensure directional consistency. Via Lagrangian duality, this high-dimensional problem reduces to a closed-form solution h = ĝ + ρ‖ĝ‖/‖d(α*)‖ · d(α*), where α* is found by a simple 1D line search over [0,2], making the harmonization computationally negligible relative to backpropagation and planning.

## Results

On the 7-task DeepMind Control Suite, BOOM-H (HDPI integrated into BOOM) achieves a mean total average return (TAR) of 852.6, a 6.2% improvement over BOOM (803.1) and a 23.1% gain over BMPC (692.8). On the 7-task Humanoid-Bench, BOOM-H attains a leading average TAR of 746.1, marking a 16.8% improvement over BOOM and a dramatic 70.4% increase over BMPC. In the high-velocity H1-Run task, BOOM-H scores 752.1, outperforming the second-best baseline (BOOM, 330.3) by a 127.7% margin. BOOM-H also exhibits the lowest standard deviation across nearly all tasks, confirming superior training stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOOM-H (HDPI) achieves the best average TAR on both benchmarks, outperforming all 10 baselines. Against the closest competitor BOOM (the un-harmonized dual-policy baseline), HDPI yields a 6.2% gain on DMC (852.6 vs 803.1) and a 16.8% gain on H-Bench (746.1 vs 639.0), with the largest per-task margin on H1-Run (752.1 vs 330.3, +127.7%). Against BMPC, which relies solely on planner imitation, BOOM-H improves by 23.1% on DMC and 70.4% on H-Bench, confirming that the dual-gradient harmonization is critical beyond single-pathway learning. Planning-driven TD-MPC2 (491.3 DMC / 187.9 H-Bench) and imagination-driven DreamerV3 (8.4 / 77.0) are substantially outperformed, while model-free SAC (29.7 / 67.3) and DSAC-T (347.2 / 121.9) struggle with the high-dimensional locomotion tasks. Against the four diffusion-RL baselines (QVPO, QSM, DACER, DPMD), BOOM-H dominates by a wide margin—e.g., 852.6 vs 116.9 (DACER) on DMC and 746.1 vs 124.3 (DACER) on H-Bench—demonstrating that gradient-level harmonization in a planning-driven framework far exceeds the expressivity gains of diffusion policies on these continuous-control benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the policy-planner bootstrapping paradigm established by BOOM (Zhan et al., 2025a) and the planning-driven MBRL lineage of TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023) and BMPC (Wang et al., 2025), formalizing the implicit dual-update loop as a unified Dual Policy Improvement (DPI) framework. Its key conceptual departure is proving that the Q-maximization gradient and the KL-divergence planner-alignment gradient, though empirically inconsistent, are theoretically equivalent improvement operators converging to the same optimum. The proposed HDPI harmonization scheme is explicitly distinguished from multi-task gradient surgery (PCGrad, GradNorm) by arguing that the two objectives are not genuinely conflicting but rather misaligned due to approximation error, so the goal is synchronization toward a shared optimum rather than Pareto trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HDPI provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in stabilization module (with only ~8% overhead) for the increasingly popular policy-planner bootstrapping paradigm, which has set new records on high-dimensional locomotion but lacked rigorous convergence guarantees. The theoretical unification (Theorem 3.3) clarifies why the bootstrapping loop works and under what conditions it can fail, offering a principled diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include whether the harmonization extends beyond the specific two-gradient setting (e.g., to multi-planner or multi-horizon configurations), whether the theoretical guarantees hold under the approximation errors that motivate the method in the first place, and whether the approach transfers to imagination-driven MBRL (Dreamer-style) where the dual-gradient structure is less explicit.
