## Problem

In reward-agnostic exploration, the minimax-optimal sample complexity of Li et al. (2024b) is only achievable for restrictively small accuracy parameter ε due to a high-degree polynomial low-order term, limiting practical applicability. Additionally, the minimax lower bound for reward-free exploration in time-inhomogeneous MDPs remained an open conjecture, leaving the optimality of existing upper bounds unconfirmed. This paper addresses both gaps by reducing the low-order term and proving a tight lower bound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263873/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent is given the MDP parameters (|S|, |A|, H), an accuracy ε, and confidence δ, then interacts with an unknown environment for K episodes without ever observing rewards. In the reward-agnostic setting, the agent additionally knows a predefined reward class of size poly(|S|,|A|,H). After all K episodes conclude, the true reward function is revealed, and the agent must output an ε-optimal policy without further environment access. The sample complexity K is the key quantity: O(|S||A|H³ℓ₀³/ε² + |S|²|A|H⁴ℓ₀³/ε) for RAE and O(|S|²|A|H³ℓ₀³/ε² + |S|²|A|H⁴ℓ₀³/ε) for RFE.

## Method

The algorithm is a three-stage meta-procedure. Stage 1 (exploration policy creation) replaces the multiple independent no-regret subroutines of prior work with a single run of an online MDP algorithm (UC-O-REPS, adapted from Rosenberg & Mansour 2019) over a sequence of auxiliary rewards λ_h^t(s,a) = 1/(c + N_h^t(s,a)) that decrease as state-action pairs are visited. This reward sequence implicitly approximates the gradient of the negative-entropy objective over occupancy measures, so the averaged policy from the online algorithm provides near-uniform coverage over all significant (s,a,h) triples without requiring Frank-Wolfe iterations or log-function perturbations. Stage 2 samples N trajectories from this exploration policy and uses two-fold subsampling (Li et al., 2024a) to break temporal correlations, yielding independent transition estimates. Stage 3 applies pessimistic model-based offline RL with Bernstein-style confidence penalties to compute an ε-optimal policy from the estimated dynamics and revealed reward. The key sample-efficiency gain comes from reusing data across the entire online learning process rather than re-estimating transitions at each no-regret iteration.

## Results

For reward-agnostic exploration (RAE), the paper achieves a sample complexity of Õ(|S||A|H³/ε² + |S|²|A|H⁴/ε), matching the optimal high-order term of Li et al. (2024b) while dramatically reducing the low-order term from H⁶|A|⁴|S|⁴/ε to H⁴|A||S|²/ε. For reward-free exploration (RFE), the paper establishes a tight lower bound of Ω(|S|²|A|H³/ε²) for time-inhomogeneous MDPs, resolving the conjecture of Li et al. (2024b) and confirming the optimality of prior upper bounds (e.g., Ménard et al., 2021). The RFE upper bound of Õ(|S|²|A|H³/ε² + |S|²|A|H⁴/ε) matches the best known high-order term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. (2024b), the main competitor for RAE, this paper matches the optimal high-order term H³|A||S|/ε² but reduces the low-order term from H⁶|A|⁴|S|⁴/ε to H⁴|A||S|²/ε, making the bound optimal for a much wider range of ε (not just very small ε). Against Ménard et al. (2021) for RFE, this paper matches the same high-order term H³|A||S|²/ε² and additionally provides the first tight time-inhomogeneous lower bound Ω(|S|²|A|H³/ε²), proving that Ménard et al.'s upper bound is minimax-optimal up to logarithmic factors. Against Jin et al. (2020b), the paper improves the RFE high-order term from H⁵ to H³ and the low-order term from H⁷|A||S|⁴/ε to H⁴|A||S|²/ε. Against Kaufmann et al. (2021), the H³ dependence on horizon improves upon their H⁴. Against Zhang et al. (2020) for RAE, the high-order term improves from H⁵|S||A|/ε² to H³|S||A|/ε². The RAE upper bound also matches the BPI lower bound of Domingues et al. (2021), confirming minimax optimality in the high-order regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reward-free exploration line (Jin et al. 2020b; Ménard et al. 2021) and the reward-agnostic exploration line (Zhang et al. 2020; Li et al. 2024b), but departs from prior algorithms by replacing multiple independent no-regret subroutines (as in Li et al. 2024b's Frank-Wolfe-style approach) with a single run of an online MDP algorithm (UC-O-REPS, adapted from Rosenberg & Mansour 2019) over a carefully designed sequence of auxiliary rewards. The dynamics estimation step leverages the two-fold subsampling framework of Li et al. (2024a) to handle temporal correlations in trajectory data, and the policy estimation step follows the pessimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty approach of Li et al. (2024b). The lower bound construction extends the multi-state binary-tree scheme of Jin et al. (2020b) to the time-inhomogeneous setting by introducing time-dependent transitions and rewards that force the agent to exit the initial state at a specific step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight lower bound for time-inhomogeneous reward-free exploration resolves a standing conjecture from Li et al. (2024b) and confirms the minimax optimality of prior upper bounds (e.g., Ménard et al. 2021) up to logarithmic factors, completing the sample complexity characterization for this setting. The algorithmic contribution makes reward-agnostic exploration practical in moderate-accuracy regimes where Li et al. (2024b)'s high-degree low-order term (H^6|A|^4|S|^4/ε) would dominate, reducing it to H^4|A||S|^2/ε. However, the work is entirely theoretical on tabular MDPs, and the authors explicitly leave non-tabular generalization and empirical validation as future work; a gap remains between the upper and lower bounds in low-accuracy regimes.
