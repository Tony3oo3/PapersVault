## Problem

Training-time backdoor defenses must simultaneously mitigate backdoors, preserve natural accuracy comparable to clean training, avoid requiring a clean reference dataset, and not inflate training time. Prior defenses (ABL, DBD, CBD, V&B, Harvey) fail on at least one of these criteria, with several being bypassed by strong attacks like Low Frequency or Adaptive Blend while others compromise natural accuracy by 5–10%.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single poisoned training dataset (RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR-10/GTSRB, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, or text for SST-2/SST-5) with no access to clean reference data or the training process controlled by the adversary. It outputs a backdoor-free model trained from scratch on a selected coreset that is intentionally smaller than the full clean subset (selection ratio ~55% on CIFAR-10), rather than attempting to recover every clean sample. The coreset's poisoning rate is driven to near 0%, and the final model is a standard supervised classifier (ResNet, VGG, MobileNetV2, DenseNet, or BERT).

## Method

ABCS reframes backdoor defense as a coreset selection problem, exploiting the observation that poisonous samples converge faster (lower entropy) and are rarer than benign samples, so informativeness-based selection naturally favors the primary task. The selection criterion is Cumulative Entropy (CENT), which min-max-normalizes per-sample Shannon entropy at each of T_se epochs and averages across epochs to capture both predictive uncertainty and temporal consistency. The procedure has three phases: (1) a warm-up phase that auto-determines the coreset size via a threshold computed over correctly-predicted samples; (2) a selection phase where each epoch trains on the full poisoned set, then unlearns high-entropy samples using label smoothing (ε=0.9) plus L2 weight regularization to widen the uncertainty gap between benign and poisonous samples, and accumulates the updated normalized entropy; (3) a final from-scratch training on the top-CENT coreset. This yields a defense whose total runtime is comparable to naive training (×0.97 on CIFAR-10) while achieving DER > 95% across eight diverse attacks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 8 attacks, 5% poisoning rate), ABCS achieves an average natural accuracy of 94.38% (vs. 94.41% for no-defense training) while reducing the average Attack Success Rate to 1.84% and achieving a Defense Effectiveness Rate of 98.21%. In the worst case (A-Blend attack), ABCS still limits ASR to 5.71% while retaining 94.06% accuracy. On Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-34), ABCS achieves a DER above 97% across all attacks. Training time is comparable to naive training (×0.97 on CIFAR-10, ×1.25 on Tiny-ImageNet), unlike DBD which requires ×12–×39 overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABCS dominates all five baselines on both ASR and DER while matching or exceeding their natural accuracy. Against the strongest prior method Harvey (ASR 15.32%, DER 91.18%), ABCS reduces ASR by 8.3× (1.84% vs. 15.32%) and improves DER by 7 points. The gap is most dramatic against LF and A-Blend attacks: ABL fails catastrophically (ASR 57.34% and 96.03% respectively), V&B also fails (ASR 39.63% and 75.42%), while ABCS limits ASR to 3.04% and 5.71%. On clean (unpoisoned) datasets, ABCS actually outperforms naive training (94.77% vs. 94.64% on CIFAR-10), whereas ABL drops to 83.42% and CBD to 92.90%. In the worst-case attack scenario, ABCS maintains DER above 95% while all baselines fall below 62%, confirming consistent robustness across the full attack spectrum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes training-time backdoor defense as a coreset selection problem, departing from the dominant 'dataset splitting' paradigm (ABL, DBD, CBD, V&B, Harvey) that aims to precisely separate all benign from all poisoned samples. The Cumulative Entropy (CENT) criterion builds directly on uncertainty-based selection (Coleman et al., ICLR 2020) and the multi-epoch accumulation principle from Forgetting (Toneva et al., ICLR 2019) and EL2N (Paul et al., NeurIPS 2021), but applies min-max normalization per epoch and combines it with a label-smoothing-based unlearning step to sharpen the benign/poisoned separability. The key conceptual departure is that one need not recover all clean samples—selecting an informative subset (~55% of data) suffices, which inherently reduces the risk of including poisoned samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ABCS provides a simple, training-time defense that consistently mitigates eight diverse backdoor attacks (including stealthy ones like WaNet and adaptive A-Blend) with DER above 95% while preserving natural accuracy comparable to clean training, and at comparable or lower computational cost. Its adaptability to clean datasets (no accuracy drop when no poisoning is present) addresses a practical gap in prior defenses. Open questions include the lack of theoretical grounding for why CENT separates benign from poisoned samples, the suboptimal performance against the most sophisticated adaptive attacks (A-Blend ASR ~5.7%), and whether the coreset size can be further reduced without sacrificing natural accuracy.
