## Problem

One-step and few-step flow-based generation requires learning long-range flow maps that satisfy trajectory consistency (the semigroup property), but consistency alone provides no data supervision for these long-range maps. Existing solutions either compose short-range dynamics progressively (causing error accumulation) or derive objectives from the continuous consistency equation via Jacobian-vector products (JVPs), which incur heavy memory overhead, cause numerical instability under mixed precision, and are incompatible with sparse computation primitives like PVCNN.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise as the base input and optionally a class label (for conditional image generation via classifier-free guidance), 64 surface points (for SDF reconstruction), or 25% observed pixel coordinates (for functional image generation). Outputs are 256×256 images (in 32×32×4 VAE latent space or directly in pixel space via JiT), signed distance field functions, 3D point clouds, or functional images. A fixed local step size Δt=10⁻³ is used for the Euler linearization, and the method supports both u-prediction (velocity) and x₁-prediction (terminal state) parameterizations, with the latter being essential for high-dimensional pixel-space and SDF tasks.

## Method

EMF applies a local Euler linearization to the semigroup consistency equation φ_{t→r} = φ_{s→r}∘φ_{t→s} by setting s = t + Δt with a small fixed step, yielding the surrogate relation u_{t→r}(x_t) ≈ u_{t→t}(x_t) + (r−t−Δt)·(u_{t+Δt→r}(x_{t+Δt}) − u_{t→r}(x_t))/Δt. The instantaneous term u_{t→t} is then replaced with the conditional velocity u_t(x|x₁) = (x₁−x)/(1−t), injecting direct data supervision into the long-range objective without any derivative computation. All long-range terms are wrapped in stop-gradient operators, making the training entirely JVP-free and compatible with FlashAttention and sparse libraries. Theorem 4.3 proves the surrogate loss approximates the true consistency loss up to O(Δt) under mild regularity assumptions, and a fraction of training samples with r=t reduces the loss to standard Flow Matching, anchoring the instantaneous velocity. The x₁-prediction variant reparameterizes the mean field as x̃_{t→r} = (1−t)u_{t→r} + x and applies the same linearization, with a 1/(1−t)² time weight for stable optimization in high-dimensional settings.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation, EMF achieves a 1-step FID-50k of 7.2, substantially outperforming MeanFlow (11.1), ShortCut (40.3), and all multi-step baselines (e.g., Reflow 44.8, FM 324.8). On CelebA-HQ-256 unconditional generation, EMF reaches 1-step FID-50k of 10.8 vs. MeanFlow's 12.4 and ShortCut's 20.5. EMF also reduces peak GPU memory by ~43% (57.9 GB vs. 101.9 GB on ImageNet) and training time by ~50% (198.8 ms vs. 400.9 ms per iteration) compared to MeanFlow. On SDF generation (ShapeNet), EMF achieves Chamfer distance 0.046 and F-Score 0.674 in one step, beating MeanFlow (0.060 / 0.584) and multi-step Functional Diffusion (0.101 / 0.707 at 64 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMF outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary 1-step generation metrics. Against the closest competitor MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025a), EMF improves 1-step FID from 11.1 to 7.2 on ImageNet and from 12.4 to 10.8 on CelebA-HQ, while also halving training time and reducing peak memory by ~43%. ShortCut (Frans et al., 2025) and SplitMeanFlow (Guo et al., 2025) are significantly weaker at 1-step (FID 40.3 and 15.7 respectively on CelebA-HQ), as their progressive-extension strategy accumulates error over long horizons. AlphaFlow (Zhang et al., 2025b) achieves FID 11.6 on CelebA-HQ, still 0.7 points worse than EMF. On pixel-space generation (Table 6), EMF with x1/u prediction reaches 1-step FID 30.6, beating MeanFlow's 39.2 and JiT's 440.1. On SDF generation (Table 7), EMF achieves Chamfer 0.046 in one step, outperforming MeanFlow (0.060), Functional Diffusion at 64 steps (0.101), and 3DS2VS at 18 steps (0.144). The Flow Map Matching baselines (Boffi et al., 2025, LSD variant) achieve FID 12.3 on CelebA-HQ with 52.2 GB peak memory, worse than EMF on both quality and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMF is built directly on MeanFlow's trajectory-consistency framework and mean-velocity parameterization, inheriting its semigroup-based formulation for long-range flow maps. The key departure is replacing MeanFlow's JVP-based continuous consistency equation with a local Euler linearization over a short interval [t, t+Δt], which converts the long-range consistency constraint into a form amenable to direct data supervision via Flow Matching's conditional velocities. The x₁-prediction variant and its time-weighted loss are borrowed from Li & He (2025), addressing a known failure mode of u-prediction in high-dimensional pixel-space generation. Unlike SplitMeanFlow (also JVP-free), EMF supports independent training from scratch with direct long-range supervision rather than distillation from a pre-trained teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JVP-free formulation removes a practical bottleneck that prevented MeanFlow from using FlashAttention and from operating in sparse computation libraries (PVCNN, TorchSparse), broadening the applicability of one-step flow-based generation to 3D point clouds, SDFs, and functional representations. The ~50% reduction in training memory and time, combined with improved optimization stability (MeanFlow exhibits loss escalation and collapse under mixed precision), makes one-step generation more accessible at scale. Open questions include whether the local linearization remains accurate for rapidly varying dynamics or very large time intervals, and whether the approach scales to larger models and video generation, where the authors note their compute budget was limiting.
