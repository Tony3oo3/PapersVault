## Problem

Existing message passing neural networks for molecular dynamics simulation face a fundamental scalability-accuracy tradeoff: increasing MPNN depth causes over-squashing and over-smoothing, enlarging the radius cutoff creates prohibitively dense computational graphs, and coarse-grained sparsification (fragmentation, backbone-only modeling) sacrifices accuracy on heterogeneous protein-ligand complexes. No prior method can efficiently model dense, full-atomic interatomic interactions for large-scale systems (up to 40,000 atoms) while maintaining atomic-level prediction accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a molecular system at time t as a point cloud of N atomic coordinates (N×3) and time-invariant atomic features such as element types or charges (N×h), with system sizes ranging from 42 atoms (MD22) to 40,798 atoms (MISATO protein-ligand complexes). It produces either future coordinates at t+1 (S2S task) or per-atom forces and total potential energy (S2EF task, where forces are derived as negative gradients of the predicted energy). For various-scale systems, the dilated distance ranking mechanism enforces a fixed number of neighbors K per layer regardless of total atom count, enabling uniform parallel message aggregation across heterogeneous complex sizes.

## Method

DKMP* (Dilated K-star Message Passing) stacks L one-layer MPNNs, each performing message passing over a distinct K-star graph whose edges connect atom pairs at progressively larger distance shells, forming a contiguous partition of the complete edge set sorted by Euclidean distance under four structural constraints (mutual-exclusion, monotonicity, non-empty, completeness). This design is grounded in the physical fact that pairwise interatomic potentials (Lennard-Jones, van der Waals) are distance-dependent, enabling direct atom-atom interaction at each shell rather than iterative neighbor-mediated propagation, which the authors prove theoretically alleviates over-squashing. Two instantiations are provided: DKMP^C partitions a fixed radius cutoff into L concentric shells for single-scale systems (MD22, Chignolin), while DKMP^R uses distance-ranked K-nearest neighbors with a dilated graph attention module (scalar cross-attention + vector self-attention, inspired by AlphaFold3) for various-scale protein-ligand complexes (MISATO), omitting strict equivariance constraints for computational efficiency.

## Results

On the MISATO dataset (protein-ligand complexes, avg. ~4,880 atoms), DKMP^R achieves N-MSE of 0.88 (train) and 0.92 (test), a ~5.5× improvement over the next-best method EGNO (4.89/5.76) and ~879× over LSRM (794.12/809.15). On Chignolin (166 atoms, S2EF), DKMP^C attains the best Energy MAE of 0.291 kcal/mol and Force MAE of 0.126 kcal/(mol·Å), outperforming the prior SOTA (Neural P³M: 0.454/0.261) by 35.9% and 51.7% respectively. On AdK (3,341 atoms full-atom), DKMP^R achieves F-MSE of 2.715, beating EGNO (2.866) and EGHN (2.882). On the unfiltered MISATO test set (avg. 7,151 atoms, up to 40,798 atoms), all baselines fail with OOM errors while DKMP^R maintains N-MSE of 1.39.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKMP^R dominates all baselines on the MISATO protein-ligand benchmark: it reduces N-MSE by ~5.5× over EGNO (0.92 vs. 5.76 test), ~350× over ESTAG (0.92 vs. 308.69), and ~879× over LSRM (0.92 vs. 809.15), while also achieving the best JS-TIC (0.20 vs. 0.31 for EGNO). On Chignolin S2EF, DKMP^C achieves the lowest Energy MAE (0.291) and Force MAE (0.126), beating Neural P³M (0.454/0.261), LSRM (0.669/0.187), and EquiformerV2 (0.898/0.195), while also being the fastest (0.016 s vs. 0.148 s for Neural P³M). On AdK full-atom (3,341 atoms), DKMP^R achieves F-MSE 2.715, outperforming EGNO (2.866) and EGHN (2.882); notably, EGNN, MPNN, and RF baselines cannot run at all due to OOM. On MD22, DKMP^C achieves the best Energy MAE on 6 of 8 molecules and best Force MAE on 6 of 8, with the two exceptions being small molecules (42 and 56 atoms) where it ranks second. All ML baselines (MACE, GemNet-OC, Allegro, NequIP, Equiformer v1/v2, FreeCG) fail with OOM on the unfiltered MISATO test set (up to 40,798 atoms), while DKMP^R completes inference in 18.43 s for the largest system (1YKP) with N-MSE 0.74.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant message passing neural network (MPNN) framework for molecular dynamics by introducing a dilated K-star graph construction that partitions the complete interatomic edge set into distance-ordered, mutually exclusive subsets processed by sequential one-layer MPNNs. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the graph rewiring literature (addressing over-squashing) and physics-informed force field design (distance-dependent pairwise potentials), departing from prior large-scale MD approaches that rely on coarse-graining (backbone-only, fragmentation) or simply enlarging radius cutoffs. The dilating distance ranking variant draws on AlphaFold3's architectural principles for large-scale protein modeling, while the dilating radius cutoff variant follows the vector-scalar message passing conventions established by ViSNet and EquiformerV2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve atomic-level accuracy in full-atom protein-ligand dynamics simulation at scales up to 40,000 atoms, where all prior ML-based baselines fail with out-of-memory errors. The dilated K-star framework provides a clean, physically motivated solution to the over-squashing problem that generalizes beyond MD to any GNN task requiring long-range interactions without dense graphs. Key open questions remain: long-horizon simulation stability (F-MSE still degrades significantly), the trade-off of relaxing strict equivariance in DKMP^R for efficiency, and whether integrating Ewald summation with the dilated framework can further improve long-range interaction modeling.
