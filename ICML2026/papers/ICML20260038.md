## Problem

Training LLMs on heterogeneous multi-domain data requires minibatch selection that balances convergence speed with cross-domain coverage, but existing approaches either select samples independently per domain (ignoring cross-domain redundancy) or rely on computationally expensive proxy models to learn continuous domain weights. The closest prior method, GREATS, uses a binary selection rule whose objective is non-monotone and lacks submodularity guarantees, preventing provable approximation bounds. This paper asks whether a single, jointly-optimized selection objective can enforce per-domain budgets while reducing gradient conflicts across domains without any auxiliary proxy.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the method receives a mini-batch partitioned into C domains, a small validation set (2 randomly sampled anchor points per step), per-domain cardinality budgets {κ_c^t} summing to a total budget κ, and current model parameters θ_t. Per-example gradients are extracted from the last-layer LoRA parameters, Adam-normalized, and compressed via randomized FFT (FJLT) projection to reduce GPU memory traffic. The output is a selected subset Ŝ_t of size κ respecting all per-domain matroid constraints, which is then used for a single SGD/Adam parameter update; the procedure repeats over T training steps.

## Method

PARTITIONSEL formulates cross-domain minibatch selection as maximizing a validation-guided gradient-matching utility U_t(w) = ⟨w, μ_θt⟩ − (η_t/2)⟨w, K_θt w⟩ over non-negative continuous weights w, subject to a partition-matroid constraint encoding per-domain budgets. The key insight is that introducing non-negative weights (rather than the binary indicators used by GREATS) makes the resulting set function monotone and γ-weakly submodular, yielding a (1 + C̃₁/c_{2κ})^{−2} approximation guarantee for the OMP algorithm. The OMP subroutine iteratively adds one sample per round by selecting from a maximal independent set in the contracted matroid that maximizes aggregate positive gradient mass, then refits weights via accelerated projected gradient ascent (APGA) on the current support. This cross-domain coupling through a single utility penalizes redundant or conflicting gradients across domains, inducing an implicit discrete data mixture at each step without any proxy model or continuous weight optimization.

## Results

On METAMATHQA fine-tuning (QWEN2.5-3B, 50% subset fraction), PARTITIONSEL achieves an average score of 0.6363 across eight math-reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baselines ID (0.6263) and GREATS (0.6240). The largest individual gains are on SAT (+3.63% over ID) and SimulEq (+2.49% over IWD). In the direct comparison against the continuous mixture method DoReMi (Table 3), PARTITIONSEL improves average downstream accuracy from 61.34 to 65.99 (+4.65), with the largest margin on MMLU-Math (+7.49). On MOL-INSTRUCTIONS (molecule generation), PARTITIONSEL achieves the best average BLEU (0.66 at 25% selection) and lowest edit-distance (29.71) across all four sub-tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARTITIONSEL outperforms all four external baselines on average. Against GREATS (Wang et al., 2024), PARTITIONSEL gains +1.23% in average score (0.6363 vs 0.6240), with the largest margins on SAT (+8.18%) and SimulEq (+4.67%), though GREATS retains an edge on GSM8K (+2.27%) and MMLU-Math (+0.10%). Against COLM (Nguyen et al., 2025), the average gap is +2.44%, driven by consistent improvements on SimulEq, AQuA, and SAT. Against GradNorm (Katharopoulos & Fleuret, 2018), PARTITIONSEL leads by +2.58% on average. In the head-to-head comparison with DoReMi (Xie et al., 2023) on the same METAMATHQA training setup, PARTITIONSEL surpasses DoReMi on all three reported benchmarks—MMLU-Math (60.57 vs 53.08), AQuA (61.02 vs 58.66), and SAT (76.36 vs 72.27)—without requiring a proxy model, suggesting that discrete per-batch selection can match or exceed continuous mixture optimization. The internal baselines ID and IWD (independent per-domain selection) confirm that cross-domain coupling is the key driver: PARTITIONSEL beats ID by +1.00% and IWD by +1.38% on average, and reduces the number of conflicting gradient pairs within each batch (Figure 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

PARTITIONSEL extends the GREATS gradient-matching selection rule (Wang et al., 2024) by introducing non-negative continuous weights and a partition-matroid constraint that couples per-domain selections through a single validation-guided utility, rather than selecting each domain independently. The theoretical machinery draws on the RSC/RSM-to-weak-submodularity connection (Elenberg et al., 2018) and the OMP approximation guarantee for structured-sparse objectives (Manupriya et al., 2024a), applied here for the first time to domain-aware LLM minibatch selection. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a discrete, sample-level bridge to continuous data-mixture methods like DoReMi (Xie et al., 2023a) and RegMix (Liu et al., 2025), which require expensive proxy models, by inducing an implicit domain mixture purely through constrained subset selection at each training step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, proxy-free alternative to continuous domain-reweighting methods, which is practically attractive given the computational and faithfulness concerns of training auxiliary models alongside LLMs. The empirical gains over strong baselines (GREATS, CoLM, DoReMi) are consistent but modest (roughly 1–2 points on average benchmarks), and the gradient-conflict reduction provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why cross-domain coupling helps. Open questions include whether the implicit mixture captured by per-step discrete selection can match the expressiveness of continuous reweighting in highly imbalanced or very large-domain settings, and whether the partition-matroid formulation scales efficiently when the number of domains grows substantially.
