## Problem

Standard reinforcement learning objectives such as GRPO assign credit only to the final latent state before token emission, implicitly treating each token as a single-step decision. This creates a fundamental credit-assignment mismatch with Looped Language Models, which perform multiple internal refinement iterations before producing each token, and explains why prior RLVR post-training attempts on LoopLMs have failed to yield meaningful gains over supervised fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., a math word problem) and produces a free-form text response ending with a boxed final answer, identical in format to conventional LLM outputs. The distinguishing detail is internal: before each output token is emitted, the LoopLM executes T_max shared-weight transformer loop iterations, and RLTT exploits the next-token distribution produced at every intermediate loop (not just the terminal one) to shape the training gradient. At inference time, only the terminal-loop distribution is used for sampling, so the output format is unchanged.

## Method

RLTT modifies the REINFORCE-style policy gradient by replacing the single terminal-loop log-probability log P_θ^(T_max)(y_j | x, y_<j) with a weighted sum Σ_t ω_t log P_θ^(t)(y_j | x, y_<j) across all T_max latent loops, distributing the outcome reward across the entire latent thought trajectory rather than bottlenecking it at the final state. Loop weights ω_t are deterministic and can be set uniformly, progressively (favoring later loops), or via the model's learned exit-probability head; a KL-divergence term against a frozen reference policy preserves general language ability. The method is a direct drop-in replacement for GRPO with negligible additional compute (per-loop logits are already produced during the forward pass), requiring only a linear-in-T_max weighted sum and extra memory to retain per-loop log-probabilities. A theoretical analysis under three mild assumptions (monotone uncertainty decrease across loops, diminishing returns of extra tokens, approximately linear total uncertainty cost) proves that trajectory-level credit assignment induces a weakly shorter optimal decoding length than terminal-only credit, explaining RLTT's empirical token efficiency and robustness under constrained inference budgets.

## Results

On the 2.6B scale, Ouro+RLTT achieves 51.2% mean math accuracy (MATH-500: 86.0, AIME24: 33.3, AIME26: 26.7, BeyondAIME: 16.0, GSM8K: 94.0) and 71.8% mean non-math accuracy (ARC-C: 94.4, MMLU-ST: 89.6, GPQA: 38.4, MBPP: 64.6), outperforming Ouro+GRPO by +8.7% on math and +6.6% on non-math. On the 1.4B scale, Ouro+RLTT reaches 46.0% math and 64.8% non-math, surpassing Qwen3-4B+GRPO (31.7% math, 58.7% non-math) despite having fewer parameters. RLTT also reduces training time by 10% (49.05 hrs vs. 54.42 hrs) due to shorter generated responses, and achieves statistically significant gains (p<0.05) on 8/9 benchmarks at 2.6B and 7/9 at 1.4B under sampling-based evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Ouro SFT baseline (Zhu et al., 2025), RLTT yields large gains: on the 2.6B scale, math average improves from 38.8% to 51.2% (+12.4%) and non-math from 64.5% to 71.8% (+7.3%), while on 1.4B the math average rises from 39.6% to 46.0% (+6.4%). Against GRPO (the standard RLVR baseline from DeepSeek-R1, Guo et al., 2025), RLTT wins on every benchmark at both scales, with the largest margins on the hardest tasks: +16.6% on AIME24, +10.0% on AIME26, and +10.0% on BeyondAIME at 2.6B, and +18.7% on GPQA (nearly doubling from 19.7% to 38.4%). Against the non-looped Qwen3+GRPO baselines, Ouro-1.4B+RLTT (46.0% math, 64.8% non-math) substantially outperforms both Qwen3-1.7B+GRPO (27.2%, 51.1%) and Qwen3-4B+GRPO (31.7%, 58.7%), and Ouro-2.6B+RLTT (51.2%, 71.8%) further extends the lead. Against DeepSeekR1-7B, Ouro-2.6B+RLTT achieves higher math accuracy (51.2% vs. 37.2%) and non-math accuracy (71.8% vs. 54.4%) despite using a smaller parameter budget, though at the cost of higher per-token inference FLOPs (2.63× vs. Qwen3-4B).

## Relation to Prior Work

RLTT is a direct modification of the GRPO policy gradient (from DeepSeek-R1) tailored to the Ouro LoopLM architecture, where the key departure is replacing the single terminal-loop log-probability with a weighted sum over all latent loop distributions. It contrasts with LSRL, which also assigns process-level credit to recurrent-depth LLMs but requires decoding intermediate states and calling an external grader (GPT-4.1 nano), introducing substantial overhead. The paper positions itself as resolving a specific failure mode identified by the Ouro authors—standard RLVR cannot improve LoopLMs—by diagnosing the root cause as a credit-assignment horizon mismatch rather than an inherent limitation of RL for latent reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLTT provides a drop-in, verifier-free replacement for GRPO in LoopLMs that resolves the previously unexplained RL failure in this architecture family, with a clean theoretical justification (provably shorter optimal decoding length) and strong empirical transfer to non-mathematical reasoning. Key limitations include dependence on a single LoopLM architecture (Ouro), fixed loop depth that sacrifices adaptive halting, and increased memory footprint that reduces per-GPU token packing. Open questions include whether trajectory-level credit assignment generalizes to other latent-reasoning architectures (Huginn, Coconut), whether it can be integrated with adaptive early-exit mechanisms, and whether the approach extends to non-mathematical training data.
