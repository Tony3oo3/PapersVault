## Problem

Test-time scaling via long chain-of-thought decoding causes linear KV-cache growth, making attention the dominant latency bottleneck. Existing query-aware sparse decoding methods (e.g., Quest) introduce a sequential dependency—KV selection must wait for the current query embedding—and rely on coarse page/block-level granularity, which simultaneously limits serving throughput under high concurrency and degrades reasoning accuracy on TTS benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During decoding, the Inference Rank forwards packed query, key, and value embeddings (for a configurable number of consecutive transformer layers) to the Cache Rank via P2P communication. The Cache Rank consumes a sliding window of W=16 recent query embeddings plus the full KV cache to produce a token-level top-k selection (e.g., 2k tokens from a 32k context), which is returned to the Inference Rank for the attention core. The system operates at token-level granularity rather than the page-level (size 16) used by Quest, and the entire selection pipeline is asynchronous with respect to the forward pass.

## Method

AsyncSpade introduces a dual-rank asynchronous architecture that decouples KV-cache management from the autoregressive decoding loop. A lightweight temporal-regressive module predicts the next-token query state by solving a softmax-normalized ridge regression over a sliding window of recent queries, then applying the solved weights to a single-token-shifted window; an assembled-regression variant averages predictions across all window sizes 1..W via average pooling for robustness. The predicted query drives token-level top-k KV selection on a dedicated Cache Rank GPU, while the Inference Rank continues forward computation in parallel. Consecutive transformer layers are packed into communication units to amortize P2P transfer overhead, and the selection, top-k, and KV reorganization on the Cache Rank are fully overlapped with the forward pass, achieving theoretically optimal TPOT when the inter-rank communication and cache-operation latencies fall below the per-step inference latency.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B with 32k decoding context, AsyncSpade achieves over 20% TPOT reduction compared to Quest and at least 50% TPOT reduction compared to full attention, while matching or surpassing Pass@1 accuracy on AIME24, AIME25, GPQA-Diamond, and MATH-500. In the Needle-in-a-Haystack retrieval test (1/8 sparsity), AsyncSpade scores 0.93 at 10k context and 0.97 at 20k context, closely matching full attention (0.99 and 0.98) while substantially outperforming trivial query-prediction baselines (e.g., Distance-1 at 0.74 and 0.77). The cache management latency is consistently lower than inference time across all tested configurations (e.g., 5.16 ms vs 6.30 ms for Qwen3-32B on A100), confirming full overlap is achievable within practical bandwidth limits (107–230 GB/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Quest (page-level, page size 16), AsyncSpade wins on both efficiency and accuracy: it achieves over 20% lower TPOT by fully overlapping KV selection with forward computation, and it matches or exceeds Quest's Pass@1 on all four TTS benchmarks while consuming fewer FLOPs (token-level selection vs. page-level). Against TOVA (token-level but sequential), AsyncSpade wins decisively on deployability: TOVA's sequential GPU-inefficient top-k selection incurs overhead that negates sparsity latency gains at long generation lengths, whereas AsyncSpade's asynchronous disaggregated design makes token-level granularity practical. Against full attention, AsyncSpade achieves at least 50% TPOT reduction on both Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B while preserving accuracy within 1–2 points on reasoning benchmarks and within 0.01–0.06 on NIAH retrieval, confirming that the predicted-query approximation introduces negligible quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of query-aware sparse attention (Quest, MoBA, SparQ) and systems-level disaggregated serving. It builds on the temporal locality observation from ShadowKV and the query-aware criticality estimation from Quest, but departs fundamentally by decoupling KV selection from the autoregressive decoding loop via an asynchronous two-rank architecture (Inference Rank + Cache Rank). Where prior query-aware methods are constrained by sequential dependency and page/block-level granularity due to co-located KV cache access, AsyncSpade repurposes a lightweight ridge-regression predictor over a sliding query window to enable token-level selection that runs fully in parallel with forward computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As test-time scaling becomes the dominant paradigm for LLM reasoning (o1, R1, QwQ, Qwen3), the linear KV-cache growth during long CoT decoding is a first-order deployment bottleneck; a 50% TPOT reduction while matching accuracy is practically significant for serving cost. Key limitations include the requirement for a dedicated Cache Rank GPU (doubling hardware), reliance on pipeline parallelism only, and non-trivial inter-GPU bandwidth requirements (~107–230 GB/s) that constrain deployment to high-end interconnects. Open questions include whether the approach extends to MoE models with heterogeneous expert routing, whether the Cache Rank can be offloaded to cheaper or CPU-based hardware, and how the method behaves during the prefill phase or with speculative decoding.
