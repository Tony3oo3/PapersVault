## Problem

Existing Open-Ended Deep Research agents either accumulate evidence linearly (suffering from lost-in-the-middle degradation) or rely on outline-centric planning that treats sparse section headers as a proxy for the agent's epistemic state. In the latter case, the LLM must implicitly infer knowledge gaps from the outline alone, providing weak supervision for identifying missing relations and triggering targeted cross-section exploration, while shared knowledge is duplicated or scattered across sections, hindering contradiction detection and global updates.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single user research query and iteratively produces a structured, citation-grounded long-form report. Internally, it maintains two co-evolving artifacts throughout the research loop: an Outline Graph (a hierarchical tree of sections with attached evidence-ID citations) and a Knowledge Graph (a global edge-grounded graph of entities, concepts, and relations). The final report is written section-by-section, where each section retrieves only the evidence units referenced by its citation set from a global evidence bank, ensuring traceability without re-reading the full corpus.

## Method

DualGraph separates what the agent knows (KG) from how it writes (OG) and uses their joint topology to drive exploration. The KG stores atomic knowledge units—core entities, concept nodes, and directed relational edges each grounded by evidence IDs—and is updated each iteration via LLM-based extraction, semantic merging, embedding-based clustering, and Leiden community detection. At each round, a KG-driven gap discovery module generates two types of search chains: Enrich chains targeting existing edges with weak evidence support (ranked by a composite score combining core-entity importance and cross-community bridging), and Explore chains targeting missing relations identified via three complementary signals—semantic similarity between core entities and concepts, SBM-based cross-community link probability/entropy, and structural-hole cues. These chains, combined with the OG's structural context for sections still lacking citations, are fed to an LLM to produce the next round of targeted search queries, forming a closed-loop co-evolution that terminates via a multi-dimensional outline quality evaluator.

## Results

On DeepResearch Bench, DualGraph with GPT-5 achieves the best overall RACE score of 53.08, surpassing all open-source baselines and matching Gemini-2.5-Pro-DeepResearch (52.54). On DeepResearch Bench II, DualGraph (GPT-4.1) reaches a Total score of 41.48, outperforming WebWeaver (36.57) and WebShaper (27.88). On DeepResearchGym, DualGraph (GPT-4.1) attains a 94.31 average quality score, the best among open-source systems, and on DeepConsult it achieves a 64.42% win rate with a 6.42 average score over the OpenAI DeepResearch baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualGraph (GPT-5) outperforms all open-source baselines on DeepResearch Bench RACE by a wide margin: +7.88 over WebWeaver (45.20), +10.16 over LangChain-DR (42.92), +12.13 over Qwen-Agent (40.95), +12.46 over Tongyi-DR (40.62), and +12.80 over WebShaper (40.28). It also surpasses OpenAI DeepResearch (46.68) by 6.40 points and is within 0.54 of Gemini-2.5-Pro-DeepResearch (52.54). On DeepResearchGym, DualGraph (GPT-4.1) leads all open-source systems (94.31 vs. WebWeaver's 90.84) but trails Gemini-2.5-Pro-DR (96.02) by 1.71 points. On DeepConsult, DualGraph (GPT-4.1) achieves a 64.42% win rate and 6.42 average score, beating WebWeaver (50.77% win, 5.65 score) and all other open-source baselines, though Gemini-2.5-Pro-DR remains slightly ahead (61.27% win, 6.70 score). On DeepResearch Bench II, DualGraph (GPT-4.1) scores 41.48 Total, exceeding WebWeaver (36.57) and WebShaper (27.88) but falling short of Gemini-2.5-Pro-DR (45.89). The KG component consistently adds 1.5–2.0 RACE points and 13+ effective citations over the outline-only variant across both GPT-4.1 and GPT-5 backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant OEDR paradigms—linear search-then-generate pipelines (LangChain, gpt-researcher) and outline-centric iterative refinement (STORM, WebWeaver, DeepScholar)—by introducing a dual-graph memory that explicitly separates the agent's epistemic state (Knowledge Graph) from its report structure (Outline Graph). While graph-based retrieval systems (GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG) use graphs as passive indices over fixed corpora, DualGraph constructs its KG incrementally from web evidence and uses topological signals (Leiden communities, SBM cross-community probabilities, structural holes) as an active gap-detection mechanism to generate targeted search queries. The co-evolution loop, where KG-driven search chains and OG structural context jointly produce the next round of queries, is the key architectural departure from prior outline-centric methods that rely solely on the outline to infer what is missing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of 'what the agent knows' from 'how it writes' offers a principled architectural template for long-horizon research agents, and the finding that KG-driven exploration improves effective citations by ~13–14 points while converging in fewer iterations (3.23 vs. 3.88) validates that explicit knowledge-state tracking outperforms implicit outline-based gap inference. However, the additional compute overhead (~38% more tokens than the w/o-KG variant), the dependence on LLM extraction quality for KG construction, and the text-only evaluation scope limit immediate generalizability. Open questions include whether the dual-graph principle extends to multimodal research, whether the SBM/structural-hole signals remain informative at much larger KG scales, and whether the modest citation-accuracy trade-off persists under joint-evaluation protocols as adopted in DeepResearch Bench II.
