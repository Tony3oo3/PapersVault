## Problem

Test-time compute (TTC) strategies like majority voting are well-established for LLMs but their transfer to vision-language models remains underexplored. This paper asks whether and when TTC actually helps in visual reasoning, identifies prediction diversity as the fundamental bottleneck limiting voting gains, and shows that standard majority voting fails in heterogeneous multi-model ensembles because it treats all models equally, allowing weaker models to dilute stronger ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a multiple-choice text question (2–9 answer options) as input. It queries one or more VLMs with U=16 stochastic decoding samples each, extracting a predictive probability distribution over the K answer options per model (via regex-based answer extraction from generated text). The output is a single selected answer option, chosen by the model with the lowest normalized predictive entropy across the ensemble. The method is inference-only and requires no training or model weight modification.

## Method

The paper proposes Entropy-based Test-Time Compute (ETTC), which replaces frequency-based vote counting with confidence-based selection: each model's predictive distribution over K options is aggregated across 16 stochastic samples, its normalized entropy is computed, and the prediction from the least-uncertain model is selected. In the single-model setting, ETTC reduces exactly to majority voting, but in multi-model ensembles it diverges by leveraging confidence disparities to prioritize stronger models over weaker ones. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that voting improvement is monotonically decreasing in prediction dependency (measured via NMI and correctness correlation), and (Theorem 2) that ETTC outperforms majority voting under a mild entropy-accuracy monotonicity assumption, with the gap widening as correlated errors among weaker models increase. Empirically, ETTC consistently surpasses both voting and the best individual model across seven VLMs and six benchmarks, enabling smaller models (e.g., 3B) to safely enhance larger ones (e.g., 72B) without the dilution risk inherent to voting.

## Results

In the cross-family ensemble (LLaMA-11B, Pixtral-12B, Gemma-12B, Qwen-7B), ETTC achieves 66.56% average accuracy across six visual QA benchmarks, outperforming majority voting (63.75%) by +2.81% and the best individual model Qwen-7B (61.30%) by +5.26%. In the same-family Qwen ensemble (3B–72B), ETTC reaches 71.68% average, beating voting (68.84%) by +2.84% and the strongest single model Qwen-72B (69.90%) by +1.78%. Feature-based Best-of-N methods (pivot words, CoT length, composite features) provide no consistent gain over vanilla CoT, and majority voting under Direct Answer prompting yields negligible improvement (<1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is majority voting (self-consistency), which aggregates N=16 stochastic samples and selects the most frequent answer. ETTC outperforms voting on 5 of 6 datasets in the cross-family ensemble (average +2.81%) and on all 6 datasets in the same-family Qwen ensemble (average +2.84%), with the largest gaps on mathematically challenging benchmarks (MathVista: +7.60%, MathVision: +3.52% in cross-family; MMMU: +6.71% in same-family). The one exception is ScienceQA in the cross-family setting, where voting edges out ETTC by 0.24%. Critically, ETTC surpasses the best individual model in both ensemble configurations (66.56% vs. 61.30% for Qwen-7B; 71.68% vs. 69.90% for Qwen-72B), whereas voting frequently underperforms the strongest model due to dilution by weaker members. In the extreme 3B+72B ablation on MathVista, voting drops to 73.15% (below the 72B standalone at 80.58%), while ETTC reaches 84.81%, demonstrating that confidence-based selection prevents weak-model noise from degrading strong-model performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time compute (TTC) literature from LLMs to vision-language models, building directly on self-consistency/majority voting (Wang et al., 2023) and Best-of-N feature-based selection methods developed for text reasoning. It departs from prior TTC work by providing a formal dependency-based analysis (via NMI and correctness correlation) that explains *why* voting gains are limited in VLMs, and by proposing Entropy-based TTC (ETTC) as a principled generalization of majority voting that exploits inter-model confidence disparities in heterogeneous ensembles. The theoretical framework draws on classic ensemble diversity theory (Krogh & Vedelsby, 1994; Tumer & Ghosh, 1996) but applies it to modern generative VLMs with stochastic decoding rather than trained classifier ensembles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a simple, training-free aggregation strategy (ETTC) that consistently outperforms majority voting across diverse VLM ensembles and even text-only reasoning, with the practical implication that smaller, cheaper models can safely augment larger ones without dilution. Key limitations include restriction to multiple-choice tasks, dependence on access to calibrated output distributions, and the assumption that entropy correlates with correctness (which can fail for miscalibrated models). Open questions include whether ETTC generalizes to open-ended generation, whether the entropy-accuracy monotonicity holds under distribution shift, and whether the supervised calibration variant can be made fully unsupervised or require less annotation.
