## Problem

Existing deep learning models for diabetic retinopathy (DR) diagnosis suffer from three interrelated limitations: they rely on single-source, single-view inputs that miss complementary lesion morphology and clinical context; their reasoning is opaque and cannot be mapped to clinically understandable logic; and they lack uncertainty quantification or traceable verification of outputs. No prior multi-view DR framework simultaneously addresses all three gaps with a theoretically grounded fusion strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three input streams per patient: multi-view fundus images (4 views on MFIDDR, 2 on DRTiD, resized to 256×256), DR lesion segmentation masks produced by a pretrained HACDR-Net, and a shared set of GPT-5-generated clinical text descriptions for six lesion concepts (EX, HE, MA, SE, VH, VO) that serve as a fixed semantic anchor across all samples. Outputs include a 5-class DR grade (0–4), per-view predictions for the six lesion concepts, and dual uncertainty scores (concept-level ψ_con and grading-level ψ_gr) that enable provenance-based verification and dynamic view weighting.

## Method

ProConMV builds a Hilbert-RWKV backbone that serializes image patches along a 2D Hilbert curve to preserve spatial locality, then applies bidirectional WKV attention in both vertical and horizontal directions for linear-complexity feature extraction. A Visual-Text RWKV (VT-RWKV) module fuses per-view visual concept embeddings with textual concept embeddings by using text as key/value in a Bi-WKV attention while visual features provide a sigmoid gating signal, aligning concepts with clinical knowledge. The reasoning pipeline follows a concept bottleneck: each view's enhanced concept representation is decoded into six lesion concept predictions, which are then mapped to a DR grade. For multi-view fusion, the paper derives via Rademacher complexity that dynamically weighting views inversely to their Dirichlet-based concept and grading uncertainties (quantified under Subjective Logic) provably reduces the generalization error upper bound relative to static equal-weight fusion. A test-time intervention mechanism allows ophthalmologists to correct concept predictions per view, which propagates through the reasoning chain and updates the fused decision.

## Results

On MFIDDR (four views), ProConMV achieves 86.75% accuracy, 76.05% Kappa, and 86.35% F1 for DR grading, outperforming the best non-interpretable method SMVDR (84.01% Acc, 71.36% Kappa) by 2.74% in accuracy and 4.69% in Kappa. On DRTiD (two views), it reaches 76.77% accuracy and 64.47% Kappa, surpassing the strongest baseline by 2.25% and 3.09% respectively. For lesion concept classification, ProConMV attains 72.26% AUPR and 68.43% F1 on MFIDDR (vs. 68.12% AUPR for the best baseline PCBM), while maintaining a competitive inference time of 8.77 ms thanks to the linear-complexity Hilbert-RWKV backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProConMV outperforms all 12 baselines on both datasets for DR grading. Against the strongest non-interpretable method SMVDR (Mamba backbone), it gains +2.74% accuracy and +4.69% Kappa on MFIDDR, and +2.25% accuracy and +3.09% Kappa on DRTiD. Among interpretable methods, the closest competitor is CEM (MV) (84.12% Acc, 70.83% Kappa on MFIDDR), which ProConMV surpasses by +2.63% accuracy and +5.22% Kappa; on DRTiD, PCBM (MV) leads interpretable baselines at 74.73% accuracy, still trailing ProConMV by 2.04%. For lesion concept classification (Table 2), ProConMV achieves the best AUPR on both datasets (72.26% on MFIDDR vs. 68.12% for PCBM; 55.86% on DRTiD vs. 52.59% for PCBM) and the lowest ranking loss (1.45 vs. 1.91 on MFIDDR). Notably, ProConMV's inference time of 8.77 ms is competitive with the fastest baselines (ETMC at 6.61 ms, Multi-Task at 8.24 ms) while using only 6.70M parameters—far fewer than ResNet-50 (25.26M) or ViT-B (86.61M) backbones used by most competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RWKV-based linear attention for vision (Vision-RWKV), which it extends with a Hilbert-curve scanning mechanism to preserve spatial locality lost in strip serialization; (2) Concept Bottleneck Models (CBM), which it generalizes from single-view to multi-view settings and augments with GPT-5-generated clinical text aligned via a novel VT-RWKV cross-modal module; and (3) evidential deep learning (Dirichlet/Subjective Logic), which it deploys at both the concept and grading levels to drive dynamic multi-view fusion. The key departure from prior multi-view DR work (MVCINN, SMVDR, CVSRA-ViT) is the explicit integration of interpretable concept reasoning with a provable generalization bound derived via Rademacher complexity, and from prior CBM multi-view work (MVCBM, SSMVCBM) is the addition of textual semantic grounding and dual-uncertainty verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine credibility gap in clinical AI: existing DR grading models are opaque, single-source, and unverifiable, which is problematic in safety-critical screening. The dual-uncertainty fusion with a clean generalization bound provides a principled (rather than heuristic) justification for dynamic view weighting, and the test-time intervention mechanism (4.81% accuracy gain when all concept errors are corrected) offers a concrete human-in-the-loop pathway. However, the method still requires per-concept annotations for supervised training, limiting deployment in settings without expert labels; the authors acknowledge this and point toward unsupervised concept learning as future work. Broader open questions include whether the Hilbert-RWKV locality advantage generalizes beyond fundus imaging to other fine-grained medical tasks, and whether the theoretical bound tightens meaningfully at larger sample sizes.
