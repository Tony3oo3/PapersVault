## Problem

Existing cache-then-forecast diffusion acceleration methods (TaylorSeer, HiCache) rely on hand-crafted polynomial bases that impose rigid assumptions on feature dynamics, causing overshoot artifacts, Runge oscillation at long horizons, and 'polynomial flicker' in video generation. Moreover, anchor scheduling in these methods uses ad-hoc fixed intervals or heuristic thresholds, lacking a unified information-theoretic foundation for allocating compute to the most uncertain timesteps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev at 1024×1024, HunyuanVideo, SDXL, or DiT-XL/2 at 256×256), a text prompt, and a small one-time calibration set (~5 full trajectories, ~85 s on H800) to estimate layer-wise scalar temporal kernels. It produces generated images or videos at the same resolution and quality as full inference but with 3.5×–6.3× FLOPs reduction. The calibrated kernel transfers zero-shot across LoRA adapters and moderate CFG changes without re-calibration, enabling plug-and-play deployment in multi-tenant pipelines.

## Method

EigenCache models each cached layer's feature trajectory as a time-indexed stochastic process with a scalar temporal kernel estimated empirically from calibration trajectories via cosine-normalized inner products of compressed features. For forecasting at skipped timesteps, it replaces polynomial extrapolation with regularized Kriging (the GP posterior mean), which is provably the minimum-variance linear predictor under the scalar-kernel model and coincides with the MMSE estimator under joint Gaussianity. For anchor selection, it maximizes a log-determinant information-gain objective over denoising timesteps—a monotone submodular function admitting a (1−1/e)-approximate greedy solution—dynamically allocating compute to high-entropy steps. The resulting posterior variance proxy serves as a per-step uncertainty certificate that controls the speed-quality trade-off in both offline budgeted and online adaptive scheduling modes.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (50 steps, 1024²), EigenCache achieves a 5.59× FLOPs speedup with 0.9985 ImageReward, 29.015 PSNR, and 0.3910 LPIPS in the moderate regime (N=7 anchors), and a 6.32× FLOPs speedup with 0.9120 ImageReward and 28.850 PSNR in the aggressive regime (N=9). On HunyuanVideo at 3.5× acceleration, EigenCache attains a VBench flickering score of 0.972 and smoothness of 0.980, compared to 0.955/0.945 for HiCache and 0.915/0.920 for TaylorSeer. On SDXL, EigenCache achieves 2.30× speedup with FID 17.82, outperforming DeepCache (FID 18.45) and TaylorSeer (FID 18.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EigenCache outperforms the closest competitor HiCache (Hermite-polynomial forecasting) across all reported fidelity metrics in both regimes: at N=7 it gains +0.078 dB PSNR and −0.0072 LPIPS, and at N=9 the gap widens to +0.203 dB PSNR and −0.0224 LPIPS, confirming that covariance-adaptive Kriging suppresses polynomial overshoot at longer prediction horizons. Against TaylorSeer (Taylor-polynomial forecasting), EigenCache leads by +0.381 dB PSNR and −0.1178 LPIPS in the aggressive regime, where derivative-estimation errors compound most severely. Reuse-based baselines (FORA, TeaCache, ToCa, ClusCa) trail substantially on ImageReward (0.55–0.95 vs. 0.91–1.00) and perceptual fidelity, reflecting their inability to correct features during skipped steps. On video (HunyuanVideo, 3.5×), EigenCache's VBench flickering score of 0.972 exceeds HiCache (0.955) and TaylorSeer (0.915), directly validating the 'polynomial flicker' hypothesis. On SDXL, EigenCache beats DeepCache (structure-skipping) by 0.63 FID and TaylorSeer by 0.23 FID at a comparable 2.30× speedup, and on DiT-XL/2 it maintains FID 2.21 under OUSAC's perturbed guidance trajectories versus ToCa's 2.95 under standard scheduling.

## Relation to Prior Work

EigenCache extends the cache-then-forecast paradigm established by TaylorSeer (Taylor polynomial extrapolation) and HiCache (Hermite polynomial upgrade) by replacing their fixed analytic bases with an empirically estimated layer-wise temporal kernel and Kriging-style covariance-adaptive prediction. The scheduling component draws directly on classical Gaussian-process optimal experimental design (Krause et al., 2008), specifically the log-determinant information-gain objective and its submodular structure, which the authors adapt to the reverse-time causal constraint of diffusion sampling. The paper explicitly positions itself as instantiating rather than re-deriving these classical tools, distinguishing itself from concurrent fixed-basis approaches like Spectrum (Chebyshev + ridge regression) and frequency-decomposition methods like FreqCa.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first theoretically grounded (BLUP/MMSE-optimal, submodular-guaranteed) alternative to heuristic polynomial caching, offering a principled mechanism for compute allocation that gracefully degrades under aggressive skip intervals rather than exhibiting polynomial overshoot. The zero-shot LoRA transfer result (gap < 0.002 ImageReward) is practically significant for multi-tenant production pipelines, eliminating per-adapter recalibration. However, the scalar-kernel approximation restricts optimality to a narrow predictor class, end-to-end gains over HiCache remain modest (e.g., 0.9985 vs. 0.9979 ImageReward at N=7), and the approach requires a one-time calibration pass per backbone checkpoint. Open questions include whether phase-wise nonstationary kernels or per-head granularities yield meaningful gains at acceptable cost, and whether the covariance structure remains stable under substantially different noise schedules or base checkpoints.
