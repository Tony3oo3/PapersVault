## Problem

Naively applying Sparse Mixture-of-Experts to heterogeneous federated fine-tuning introduces two critical discordances: expert utilization imbalance (high-resource clients over-specialize experts while low-resource clients' experts remain under-trained) and non-differentiability of Top-K routing (non-activated experts receive zero gradients). The paper's convergence analysis proves these discordances create an irreducible error floor in the global objective that scales inversely with client computational budgets, explaining why resource-constrained clients systematically underperform. Existing heterogeneous LoRA-rank methods fail to address this because dense FFN computation dominates, yielding only ~5% computation reduction.

## Input / Output

Each client receives text inputs and a computational budget β_c ∈ [0,1] that determines its expert activation count K_c = ⌊K_max · β_c⌋ (with K_max = 8). Clients upload LoRA parameter updates and per-expert activation counts to the server, which aggregates them and computes global utilization statistics. The final output is a set of shared LoRA adapter parameters for all SMoE experts and the router, enabling each client to perform inference with its budget-specific number of activated experts, yielding text answers at inference time.

## Method

UB-SMoE introduces two complementary mechanisms forming a self-reinforcing cycle. Dynamic Modulated Routing (DMR) maintains a learnable modulation vector φ per SMoE layer, applied only to the top-N_p=2 candidate experts by raw affinity scores; the server updates φ each round using a tanh-bounded rule that penalizes over-utilized experts and boosts under-utilized ones based on global utilization statistics. Universal Pseudo-Gradient (PG) computes a global pseudo-gradient from inter-round parameter evolution at the server, which clients inject into non-activated experts during local training, scaled by ρ_c = √(K̄/K_c) to amplify the signal for resource-constrained clients. The convergence analysis (Theorem 4.1) under L-smoothness, PL condition, and bounded bias assumptions shows the optimality gap decomposes into a vanishing optimization error O(G²(L+L_eff²)/(μ'√(ΓT))) plus a persistent bias error B_SMoE = 2‖B(Θ*)‖²/μ', which DMR and PG jointly reduce by restoring gradient coverage and balancing expert utilization.

## Results

On the Commonsense-15K benchmark (8 datasets) with OLMoE-1B-7B, UB-SMoE achieves an average accuracy of 0.4267, outperforming the strongest sparsity baseline A³SMoE (0.3861) by 10.5% and the strongest LoRA-rank baseline FlexLoRA (0.3303) by 29.1%. On the dense OLMo-1B backbone, UB-SMoE reaches 0.5636 average accuracy, surpassing FlexLoRA by 63.2% and FLoRIST by 248.6%. At the most constrained budget (β₁), UB-SMoE achieves 0.3936 while heterogeneous LoRA-rank methods collapse to 0.0079–0.0456, demonstrating an 8.7× improvement over the best LoRA-rank method. On TeleQuAD under non-IID data, UB-SMoE attains the best BERTScore F1 at every budget level (47.66 at β₁ to 68.29 at β₄).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UB-SMoE wins on 7 out of 8 Commonsense-15K datasets (losing only on HellaSwag to A³SMoE by 1.9 points) and achieves the best average across all budgets on both OLMoE-1B-7B and OLMo-1B backbones. Against heterogeneous LoRA-rank methods, the gap is dramatic: at β₁, UB-SMoE (0.3936) outperforms FlexLoRA (0.0456) by 8.7×, HetLoRA (0.0079) by ~50×, FLoRA (0.0094) by ~42×, and FLoRIST (0.0112) by ~35×, confirming that LoRA-rank adaptation provides negligible computational savings for low-resource clients. Among sparsity-based methods, UB-SMoE beats A³SMoE (0.3861) by 10.5% and SMoE-LLB (0.3791) by 12.6% on average, with the largest margins at extreme budgets (β₁: +8.2% over A³SMoE; β₄: +53.7% over A³SMoE). On TeleQuAD under non-IID data, UB-SMoE dominates at every budget (e.g., 68.29 vs. 68.23 for SMoE-LLB at β₄, and 47.66 vs. 44.56 for SMoE-LLB at β₁), while under IID data it is competitive at low budgets and best at high budgets. In the homogeneous setting (Table 4), UB-SMoE (0.4320 average) outperforms FedIT (0.4165), FedEx-LoRA (0.3784), FFA-LoRA (0.3382), and FedSB (0.2217), showing the method is not limited to heterogeneous scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends A³SMoE (Tran et al., 2025), which first introduced heterogeneous sparsity (client-specific expert activation K_c) into federated SMoE fine-tuning, by identifying and resolving two optimization discordances that A³SMoE leaves unaddressed: expert utilization imbalance and non-differentiable Top-K routing. It also positions itself against the heterogeneous LoRA-rank line (HetLoRA, FlexLoRA, FLoRA, FLoRIST), arguing that these methods are architecturally mismatched with the dominant FFN computation bottleneck and thus provide only marginal (~5%) savings. The convergence analysis draws on the biased-SGD literature (Ajalloeian & Stich, 2020; Hu et al., 2021) to formalize how sparse activation introduces an irreducible error floor, while the DMR and PG mechanisms are novel contributions specific to the federated SMoE setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that heterogeneous LoRA-rank methods are fundamentally limited to ~5% computation reduction reframes the resource-adaptive federated fine-tuning problem, suggesting that conditional computation (SMoE) is the more natural mechanism for true resource adaptation. The convergence theorem showing an irreducible bias error floor scaling inversely with client budgets provides a clear theoretical justification for why low-resource clients systematically underperform. However, experiments are limited to 1B-parameter models and commonsense reasoning / telecom QA tasks; whether the PG mechanism's cross-round gradient reconstruction remains effective at 70B+ scale, with more diverse data distributions, or under differential privacy constraints remains open. The added communication overhead of uploading utilization statistics and the dependency on a previous round's pseudo-gradient also raise practical deployment questions.
