## Problem

Existing post-training data selection methods for reasoning rely on coarse proxy metrics—length, entropy, overall loss, or manual curation—that fail to capture the causal criticality of individual reasoning steps within a complex chain-of-thought trace. This limits the efficiency of transferring multi-step reasoning skills via supervised fine-tuning, as the most information-dense logical pivots and self-correction moments are not explicitly identified or prioritized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of reasoning trajectories (e.g., the 59K-full s1 dataset with CoT responses distilled from Gemini or DeepSeek-R1) and an off-the-shelf base LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) as input. It produces per-token AIR scores (loss divergence between the base model and a mechanically weakened reference model), which are aggregated into step-level scores (mean divergence over tokens in each reasoning step) and sample-level scores (relative loss divergence normalized by base-model loss). These scores drive two outputs: a selected subset of V=1000 high-value samples for standard SFT, or per-step multiplicative weights (with a normalization constraint ensuring the weighted sum equals total token count) for weighted SFT.

## Method

AIR is a training-free, unsupervised framework grounded in mechanistic interpretability of retrieval attention heads. It first identifies reasoning-critical heads via a token-level recall rate: a head is deemed a retrieval head if, at decoding step t, the generated token w_t appears in the source context (C1) and the head assigns maximal attention to the position of w_t (C2). The top δ% of heads by retrieval score (≈5% empirically) are then masked in a copy of the base model by replacing their attention distributions with a uniform 1/L, yielding a weakened reference model θ_ref that retains all weights but loses its retrieval capability. The AIR score for each token is the cross-entropy loss gap Δℓ(x_t) = ℓ(θ_ref, x_t) − ℓ(θ_base, x_t), which is positive when the base model's prediction depends heavily on the retrieval mechanism. At the step level, scores are averaged over tokens within each step, and the top P% of steps receive an amplified weight α (default 2) in the SFT objective; at the sample level, the relative loss divergence ranks samples for global selection. This mechanism-driven approach requires no labels, no additional training, and no external reference model, distinguishing it from heuristic or influence-function-based baselines.

## Results

On the s1K-1.1 setting (DeepSeek-R1 trajectories), AIR-Step achieves 70.32% average accuracy across AIME 2024, AIME 2025, MATH500, and GPQA Diamond, outperforming the manually curated s1K-1.1 baseline (65.43%) by nearly 5 points and the supervised RHO-step method (68.36%). At the sample level, AIR-Sample reaches 67.14% average, surpassing s1K-1.1 (65.43%) and all heuristic/metric baselines (Random 60.02%, Length 52.50%, IFD 48.91%, Entropy 49.89%, RHO-sample 45.18%). Notably, models trained on only 1K AIR-selected examples outperform R1-distill-Qwen-32B (66.08%), which was trained on 800K examples distilled from DeepSeek-R1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the manually curated s1K-1.1 baseline (65.43% avg), AIR-Sample wins on all four benchmarks (67.14% avg) without any human involvement, and AIR-Step extends this to 70.32% avg. AIR-Step outperforms the supervised RHO-step method (68.36%) by ~2 points and the Entropy step-weighting (66.15%) by over 4 points, despite being entirely unsupervised and training-free. At the sample level, AIR-Sample (54.78% in the Gemini setting) approaches but does not fully match the manually curated s1K (56.48%), while decisively beating all automated baselines: Length (52.50%), Entropy (49.89%), IFD (48.91%), and RHO-sample (45.18%). In the appendix, AIR-Step also outperforms the supervised process reward model ReasonFlux-PRM (66.70/95.60/65.66 vs. 60.00/94.60/61.11 on AIME2024/MATH500/GPQA). Finally, with only 1K training examples, AIR-Step surpasses R1-distill-Qwen-32B (66.08% avg, trained on 800K examples) and approaches R1-distill-Llama-70B (68.28%), demonstrating substantial data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mechanistic interpretability findings on retrieval heads (Wu et al., 2025; Olsson et al., 2022) from model analysis and inference acceleration into the data selection domain. It departs from existing post-training selection methods—manual curation (s1K, LIMO), surface-level heuristics (length, entropy), and influence-based scoring (RHO, IFD, Mirror Influence)—by grounding selection in the causal role of specific attention heads rather than proxy metrics. The approach is conceptually closest to AttentionInfluence (Hua et al., 2025) for pretraining, but repurposes the retrieval-head mechanism for fine-grained, step-level reasoning data curation in the post-training setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIR offers a training-free, unsupervised alternative to labor-intensive manual curation that achieves performance comparable to s1K and outperforms s1K-1.1, suggesting a scalable path to high-quality reasoning distillation. The step-level weighted SFT formulation is a practical contribution that could be adopted broadly. However, the method is validated primarily on Qwen models with a single dataset family (s1), and the assumption that ~5% retrieval heads generalize across architectures and scales remains an open question. Whether the mechanistic grounding provides advantages over simpler influence-based methods at larger scale or in non-Transformer settings is untested.
