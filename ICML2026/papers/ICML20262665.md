## Problem

RL post-training alignment for LLMs is effective but costly and unstable, requiring expensive reward modeling, large-scale preference data, and retraining whenever the reward design changes. Although the KL-regularized RL objective admits a closed-form optimal policy, existing approaches approximate it via iterative gradient-based optimization rather than exploiting its structure directly. This paper asks whether one can sample directly from the optimal RL distribution at inference time, eliminating all training overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262665/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query y, a frozen reference LLM (Qwen3-1.7B/8B for ARMs or LLaDA-8B-Instruct for DLMs), and a training-free self-consistency reward function, and produces a single generated text response sampled from the optimal RL policy. Generation proceeds as a backward Markov chain in a unified MLM framework: for ARMs this is standard left-to-right decoding, while for DLMs it involves flexible non-sequential unmasking of K tokens per step. The reward is computed via majority-vote self-consistency over K sampled completions per candidate, requiring no ground-truth labels at inference.

## Method

ETS derives that the optimal backward transition kernel in the MLM framework factorizes into the reference model's transition kernel multiplied by an energy term E(y, x_s) = E_{p_ref}[exp(r(y,x_0)/λ)], which measures how likely a partially-generated state is to lead to high-reward completions. At each of I guidance steps, the algorithm samples M candidate continuations from the reference model, estimates their energies via K Monte Carlo completions, and selects one via self-normalizing importance sampling. To reduce latency, ETS-IS replaces the expensive reference-model completions with samples from a lightweight proposal (a small Qwen3 model for ARMs, or Fast-dLLM with KV caching and parallel decoding for DLMs), corrected by an importance-sampling weight that preserves unbiasedness. The paper proves a total-variation convergence bound of Õ(I/√M + I/√K), showing the sampling error vanishes as candidates and completions grow, and demonstrates that ETS matches or surpasses post-trained GRPO policies across math, coding, and science benchmarks without any parameter updates.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, ETS achieves 69.34% average accuracy across MATH500, GSM8K, GPQA, and HumanEval, surpassing the GRPO RL baseline (65.98%) and Best-of-N (66.31%) while using only 7.35× inference latency. On Qwen3-1.7B, ETS reaches 54.76% average, outperforming GRPO (48.08%) by 6.7 points. The accelerated variant ETS-IS retains 66.45% average on Qwen3-8B at 5.26× latency, and on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, ETS achieves 51.01% average versus 46.40% for the VRPO RL baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETS consistently outperforms all TTS baselines on average accuracy: on Qwen3-8B it beats Best-of-N by 3.0 points (69.34 vs 66.31) and Beam Search by 6.1 points (69.34 vs 63.25), while Power Sampling achieves comparable per-task scores (e.g., 70.8 on MATH500) but at 41.8× latency versus ETS's 7.35×. Against the RL-trained GRPO baseline, ETS gains 3.4 points on Qwen3-8B (69.34 vs 65.98) and 6.7 points on Qwen3-1.7B (54.76 vs 48.08) without any parameter updates. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, ETS surpasses the VRPO RL baseline by 4.6 points (51.01 vs 46.40) and Best-of-N by 2.1 points (51.01 vs 48.90). The accelerated ETS-IS variant trades roughly 3 points of accuracy for a 28% latency reduction on Qwen3-8B (5.26× vs 7.35×), making it competitive with Best-of-N in speed while still exceeding it in accuracy (66.45 vs 66.31).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified MLM framework (Austin et al., 2021) that subsumes both autoregressive and diffusion language models, and on the closed-form solution of the KL-regularized RL objective (Rafailov et al., 2024). It departs from the dominant training-based RL alignment paradigm (PPO, DPO, GRPO) by showing that the optimal policy can be sampled from directly at inference time via an energy-reweighted backward transition kernel, eliminating the need for any parameter updates. Conceptually, it is positioned as a principled form of Test-Time Scaling analogous to MCTS, but unlike prior diffusion-only RL sampling methods (Dang et al., 2025; Uehara et al., 2024) or Metropolis-Hastings-based approaches (Karan & Du, 2025; Faria et al., 2024), ETS operates in discrete space, covers both ARMs and DLMs, and provides explicit total-variation convergence guarantees. The acceleration via importance sampling with a lightweight proposal model (Qwen3 small models for ARMs, Fast-dLLM for DLMs) is a practical contribution that distinguishes it from prior TTS methods that naively scale candidate counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical implication is that RL post-training alignment can be replaced by inference-time computation, removing the need for costly reward modeling, large-scale preference data, and unstable training dynamics. However, the method still requires 5–7× the base inference latency and depends on a verifiable reward signal (proxied by self-consistency), limiting its applicability to tasks with extractable answers (math, code, science QA) rather than open-ended generation. Open questions include whether the linear error accumulation in guidance steps I can be tightened, whether the self-consistency proxy reward generalizes beyond closed-form-answer tasks, and whether the importance-sampling acceleration remains effective when the proposal model diverges substantially from the reference. The work establishes a clear theoretical bridge between RL alignment objectives and inference-time sampling, but the practical compute overhead means it is best viewed as a complement to, rather than a full replacement for, trained RL policies in latency-sensitive deployments.
