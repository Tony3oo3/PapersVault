## Problem

Existing model growth methods for LLMs typically operate on early-stage checkpoints, failing to leverage the substantial sunk cost embedded in well-converged models, and the interaction between model growth and Mixture-of-Experts architectures has not been systematically studied. This paper asks: what is the optimal strategy for growing a fully converged MoE model to maximize the return on its prior computational investment?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264625/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a well-converged MoE checkpoint (trained beyond the Chinchilla-optimal FLOPs threshold, where a characteristic monotonic layer-wise weight norm profile has emerged) and an additional compute budget, then produces a larger MoE model through sequential depth and/or width expansion followed by continued pre-training on the same text corpus. The growth is applied at a chosen training stage, with the expanded model then trained for a fixed number of additional FLOPs; the paper validates the approach at scales from 3B to 70B parameters over up to 1T tokens, using a GPT-4o tokenizer with 4096-token sequences.

## Method

The core contribution is two orthogonal growth strategies for MoE models. For depth growth, the paper proposes interpositional layer copying (duplicating each layer in place) over the standard stacking approach, motivated by the observation that well-converged models develop a monotonic layer-wise weight norm profile that stacking disrupts (creating a ~10× norm discontinuity) but interposition preserves. For width growth, the paper doubles both total experts (E→2E) and activated experts (k→2k), initializing new experts as noisy copies of originals with minimal Gaussian perturbation (α=0.01) to break symmetry and encourage functional specialization without destabilizing converged weights. The two operations are empirically orthogonal—order-independent, operating on structurally disjoint parameter regions, and redirecting optimization into near-orthogonal subspaces (|cos| < 0.04). A key scaling principle emerges: final performance positively correlates with the amount of sunk FLOPs invested before growth, and under a fixed total budget the grown model matches or slightly exceeds a from-scratch baseline, yielding a 10.6% average accuracy gain over scratch training under identical extra compute.

## Results

At the 17B→70B scale (1T tokens), the orthogonal growth framework achieves an average downstream accuracy of 64.17, surpassing the from-scratch 70B baseline by 6.18 points (10.6% relative gain) under the same extra FLOPs budget, and outperforming the 17B baseline by 2.96 points (4.0% relative gain) under matched total FLOPs. For the 3B→6B depth growth comparison, interpositional layer copying consistently yields lower training loss and higher average accuracy than stacking across all growth timings beyond ~1× Chinchilla-optimal FLOPs. Width growth with minimal Gaussian noise (α=0.01) provides approximately 1% downstream accuracy gain over exact expert replication, while random initialization of new experts performs significantly worse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the stacking baseline (Du et al., NeurIPS 2024; Wu et al., ACL 2024), the proposed interpositional depth growth consistently achieves lower training loss and higher downstream accuracy for well-converged models (beyond ~1× Chinchilla-optimal FLOPs). The advantage is structural: stacking introduces a ~10× norm discontinuity at the layer boundary (e.g., layer 19→20), forcing the model to spend training budget repairing the disruption, while interposition preserves the monotonic weight-norm profile. Against from-scratch training (the 0k baseline in Table 4), growing from a converged checkpoint with the same extra FLOPs yields up to 11.6 points higher average accuracy (47.88 vs 36.29 at 88k start). Under a fixed total FLOPs budget (Table 5), growth from earlier checkpoints (8k–16k start) achieves 47.66–48.53 average accuracy versus 45.03 for scratch training, confirming that the pre-trained smaller model serves as a highly effective initialization. The paper also shows that the two growth dimensions (depth and width) are empirically orthogonal: order-independence is verified at 12B scale with weight cosine similarity >0.82 throughout, and Adam first-moment cosine similarity between pre- and post-growth directions remains |cos| < 0.04.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model growth paradigm (Shen et al., 2022; Du et al., 2024; Wu et al./LLaMA Pro, 2024) from early-stage checkpoints to well-converged MoE models, a setting the authors argue prior work has not systematically addressed. It departs from the widely-adopted stacking baseline by proposing interpositional layer copying, justified through a weight-norm continuity argument grounded in neural ODE theory (Marion et al., 2024). For width growth, it contrasts with MoE upcycling from dense checkpoints (Komatsuzaki et al., 2023; Nakamura et al., 2025), which requires 50%+ noise for expert divergence, by showing that minimal noise (α=0.01) suffices when expanding an already-trained MoE with pretrained routers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.6% accuracy gain over from-scratch training at 70B/1T tokens provides a concrete, scalable recipe for recycling sunk compute in LLM development pipelines, with practical implications for organizations that routinely produce intermediate checkpoints. Open questions include whether the Chinchilla-optimal FLOPs boundary generalizes across architectures and scales, whether the interpositional advantage persists for non-MoE (dense) transformers, and how to optimally schedule learning rates when growing from late-annealing checkpoints. The framework's reliance on a single model family (their own GQA+MoE decoder) limits immediate transferability, though the weight-norm analysis is architecture-agnostic in principle.
