## Problem

VLA models inherit spatial understanding from 2D visual backbones, making them brittle under small objects, viewpoint changes, and occlusions. Existing 3D-augmented VLAs either inject implicit spatial priors (e.g., VGGT, depth-pretrained encoders) that lack precise metric alignment for fine-grained manipulation, or treat point clouds independently from 2D inputs. Moreover, 3D VLA training is bottlenecked by scarce 3D data and a domain gap across depth sources (simulator, sensor, model-estimated) that differ in noise, scale, and geometric bias.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single-view RGB image (256×256), a corresponding depth map (256×256×1 float32, invalid pixels set to 0), a language instruction, and proprioceptive state. The depth is lifted into a point cloud via pinhole intrinsics, then 3D-compressed from ~30k–60k points to ~3k–8k points via 1 cm voxel grid sampling. The VLM autoregressively emits bounding-box tokens and grasp-pose tokens, after which a conditional flow-matching action expert produces continuous end-effector action chunks of length 4 (pose deltas + gripper command).

## Method

ANY3D-VLA lifts RGBD into a camera-frame point cloud, compresses it with 3D grid sampling, and encodes it with a frozen Concerto point-cloud encoder (last 4 spconv layers fine-tuned) to obtain per-point 3D features. These features are aligned back to 2D ViT patches via back-projection and scatter-mean aggregation (with a learnable empty token for patches lacking points), then fused with DINOv2+SigLIP 2D patch tokens through a gated residual: h_fused = h_2D + σ(g)·LayerNorm(MLP([h_2D; h_3D])), where the scalar gate g is initialized so σ(g)≈0.1, preserving the pretrained 2D representation. The key training innovation is hybrid point-cloud training: each trajectory is randomly assigned a depth source (30% simulator/sensor, 30% UniDepthV2, 20% Depth Anything 3, 20% MapAnything), forcing the 3D encoder and fusion layers to learn source-invariant geometric patterns. The model is jointly trained on GRIT (bounding-box supervision) and a large-scale synthetic RGBD manipulation dataset (grasp-pose + flow-matching action supervision) without any explicit depth or point-cloud reconstruction loss.

## Results

In zero-shot real-world evaluation across four challenging scenarios (standard, scale/shape, viewpoint, and appearance-deprived), ANY3D-VLA with hybrid point-cloud training and DA3 inference achieves 62.5% overall success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline SpatialVLA (33.3%) by 29.2 percentage points. In post-training on new tasks (flower arrangement and condiment cup placement), the best configuration reaches 93.3% and 86.7% success rates respectively. On the LIBERO benchmark, ANY3D-VLA achieves 68.5% average success rate (vs. 54.6% for GraspVLA and 68.2% for SpatialVLA), and on CALVIN it achieves an average completion length of 2.68 (vs. 1.97 for GraspVLA and 2.67 for SpatialVLA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANY3D-VLA decisively outperforms all three baselines on real-world tasks. Against SpatialVLA (the strongest 3D VLA baseline), ANY3D-VLA gains 29.2 points in zero-shot evaluation (62.5% vs. 33.3%) and 80.0 points on post-training Task 1 (93.3% vs. 13.3%), indicating that explicit point-cloud-2D fusion far surpasses SpatialVLA's depth-map-based spatial representation. Against GraspVLA (2D VLA), ANY3D-VLA improves post-training Task 1 by 60.0 points (93.3% vs. 33.3%) and Task 2 by 33.4 points (86.7% vs. 53.3%). Against π₀.₅, ANY3D-VLA wins on real-world post-training (93.3% vs. 33.3% on Task 1) but loses on the public LIBERO and CALVIN benchmarks (68.5% vs. 82.9% on LIBERO; 2.68 vs. 3.22 avg. length on CALVIN), which the authors attribute to π₀.₅'s richer pre-training corpus and environment mismatch rather than a methodological limitation. On LIBERO, ANY3D-VLA is nearly tied with SpatialVLA (68.5% vs. 68.2%), and on CALVIN it marginally exceeds SpatialVLA (2.68 vs. 2.67 avg. length), confirming that the 3D point-cloud branch provides consistent but modest gains over depth-map-based 3D VLAs in standardized simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANY3D-VLA builds on the GraspVLA VLM + flow-matching action expert architecture and the π₀ progressive action generation paradigm, adding a 3D point-cloud branch that uses Concerto as a pre-trained point encoder and Sonata-style grid compression for efficient 3D representation. It departs from prior 3D VLA work (PointVLA, 3DS-VLA, OG-VLA) by explicitly fusing native 3D point-cloud features with 2D patch tokens via gated residual fusion rather than treating point clouds as an independent input stream or injecting them through a non-parametric tokenizer. The hybrid point-cloud training strategy—mixing simulator, sensor, and model-estimated depth sources during training—is a novel contribution not present in prior 3D VLA methods, which typically rely on a single depth source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in 3D VLA deployment: the scarcity of large-scale 3D training data and the domain gap between simulator, sensor, and model-estimated depth. The hybrid training strategy and the finding that model-estimated point clouds (Depth Anything 3) can match or exceed commodity sensor depth (RealSense) reduce reliance on expensive depth hardware, making 3D-enhanced VLA more accessible. Open questions include whether stronger 3D backbones or more refined source-scheduling strategies yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to multi-arm, long-horizon, or non-tabletop settings. The plug-in nature of the 3D branch (demonstrated by improving π₀.₅ on LIBERO/CALVIN) suggests broad applicability across VLA architectures.
