## Problem

While prior work (Dutting et al., 2014; Feldman et al., 2016) characterized the limiting revenue of no-swap regret dynamics in first-price auctions as T→∞, no finite-round convergence rates were known. Extending their arguments to approximate correlated equilibria yields only quasi-polynomial dependence on the discretization parameter k, making the implied number of rounds astronomically large. The paper asks whether a polynomial-in-k revenue guarantee is achievable for any ε-approximate correlated equilibrium.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a discrete first-price auction instance with n bidders, private valuations v_i ∈ [0,1], and a discretization parameter k defining the bid set B = {0, 1/k, …, 1}, together with a joint distribution μ over bidding profiles that satisfies the ε-approximate correlated equilibrium condition. The output is a lower bound on expected revenue of the form v_2 − Θ(1/k) − Θ(ε·k²), which translates via the no-swap regret convergence theorem into an explicit round count T = Õ(k⁵/ε²) for the Blum–Mansour algorithm to achieve revenue within Θ(ε) of v_2.

## Method

The paper introduces a dual-fitting technique (borrowed from approximation algorithms) to the auction convergence-rate setting. It formulates a linear program whose optimal value Z_LP* is a lower bound on the revenue of any ε-approximate correlated equilibrium, with constraints encoding the no-deviation condition for every bidder and every pair of bids. The key step is constructing an explicit feasible assignment to the dual variables: λ is set to zero for all bidders i ≥ 3, while for bidders 1 and 2 it equals 1 precisely when the alternative bid s'_i lies in the interval [s_i + 1/k, v_2 − 2/k]. A case analysis over four classes of bidding profiles (who wins, whether bids tie) verifies dual feasibility and yields objective value v_2 − 3/k − Θ(ε·k²). By weak duality, this immediately gives the revenue lower bound, improving the previous quasi-polynomial k^{O(log k)} dependence to polynomial k².

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.6) establishes that the expected revenue of any ε-approximate correlated equilibrium in a discrete first-price auction is at least v₂ − 3/k − Θ(ε·k²), improving the previous quasi-polynomial dependence ε·k^poly(log k) to a polynomial one. Combined with the optimal Blum–Mansour no-swap regret algorithm (O(√(kT))), this implies that after T = O(k⁵·log(n/ε)/ε²) rounds, the time-averaged revenue reaches v₂ − Θ(1/k) − Θ(ε). The upper bound (Theorem 2.8) shows the ε-dependence is tight: there exists an ε-approximate CE with revenue at most 1 − Θ(1/k) − Θ(ε) for two bidders with v₁ = v₂ = 1. Experimentally, with k = 10 and 3 bidders, the time-average revenue surpasses v₂ − 1/k well before the theoretical O(k⁷) bound, and in the Bayesian i.i.d. setting (n ∈ {2,3,4,5}, k = 10) revenue converges to E[v₂] = 1 − (n+1)/2⁻ⁿ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing no-regret algorithms empirically; instead, its primary comparison is theoretical. The key improvement is over the extension of Feldman et al. (2016) and Dutting et al. (2014), which yield a quasi-polynomial bound of ε·k^poly(log k) on the revenue gap and consequently require T = k^O(log k) rounds for convergence. The new dual-fitting approach reduces this to a polynomial Θ(ε·k²) gap and T = Õ(k⁵/ε²) rounds — an exponential improvement in the dependence on k. The upper bound in Theorem 2.8 (revenue ≤ 1 − Θ(1/k) − Θ(ε)) suggests the true ε-dependence may be linear rather than quadratic, leaving a gap between the lower and upper bounds that the authors conjecture is resolvable. Experimentally, the Blum–Mansour algorithm converges to the predicted revenue levels significantly faster than the theoretical O(k⁷) bound for k = 10, and the time-average bidding distribution in the Bayesian setting closely matches the Bayes Mixed Nash Equilibrium CDF F(x) = 1/(1−x) − 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the characterization of exact correlated equilibria in first-price auctions by Feldman et al. (2016) and Dutting et al. (2014), extending their arguments from exact to approximate correlated equilibria in the discrete bidding setting. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a dual-fitting technique from approximation algorithms (Williamson & Shmoys, 2011) into auction theory, which yields a polynomial ε·k² dependence on the approximation parameter rather than the quasi-polynomial k^O(log k) that follows from naively extending prior arguments. The revenue guarantees are then translated into finite-time convergence rates for no-swap regret dynamics using the Blum & Mansour (2007) algorithm and the folk theorem linking no-swap regret to correlated equilibrium (Foster & Vohra, 1997; Hart & Mas-Colell, 2000).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result providing polynomial convergence rates on revenue for no-swap regret bidders in first-price auctions, which is practically relevant since ad auctions occur every millisecond and auctioneers need to know how many rounds are required before revenue stabilizes. A notable open gap remains: the lower bound is Θ(εk²) while the upper bound (Theorem 2.8) is Θ(ε), and the authors conjecture the tighter Θ(ε) bound is correct based on experiments. Extending the dual-fitting approach to the Bayesian setting (where valuations are drawn from distributions) is identified as a key future direction, as is closing the gap between the upper and lower revenue bounds.
