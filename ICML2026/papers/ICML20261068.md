## Problem

LLMs performing Programming-by-Example (PBE) rely primarily on input-to-output mapping without fine-grained process supervision, causing them to produce programs that either partially satisfy examples or completely deviate from the intended logic. Existing prompting strategies (CoT, ToT, WPS) and SFT approaches lack the ability to identify and correct reasoning deviations at intermediate steps during synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of input-output example pairs (no natural language description) and produces a synthesized program that must execute correctly on all provided examples. During training, the LLM generates intermediate natural-language reasoning steps organized in a tree structure; the PRM scores each reasoning state, and the final program is extracted only at terminal nodes. The external instruction mechanism injects targeted natural-language descriptions to repair dead-end reasoning nodes during dataset construction.

## Method

PRM-PBE constructs a process-supervised dataset via a feedback-guided reasoning tree where each node is a natural-language reasoning step, with an external instruction mechanism that repairs logically failed nodes by augmenting their prompts with targeted descriptions to mitigate positive-sample sparsity. A Process Reward Model is then trained with a pairwise ranking loss (Bradley-Terry objective) using node values defined as the fraction of successor paths leading to correct solutions, enabling the PRM to assign higher scores to more reliable intermediate reasoning steps. Finally, a three-stage curriculum learning strategy—progressing from syntax/runtime errors, to logically inconsistent programs, to partial-match programs—schedules training difficulty while PPO optimizes the policy under PRM-guided dense step-level rewards with GAE advantage estimation.

## Results

On five PBE benchmarks (Lists, Playgol, SyGuS, MBPP, PROSE), PRM-PBE with DeepSeek-Coder-V2 achieves an average Pass@1 of 56.61%, surpassing the strongest prompting baseline (WPS with Claude-3.5-Sonnet at 47.88%) by 8.73 percentage points. The method also improves over the SFT baseline (42.76%) by 13.85 points on the same backbone. Gains are consistent across all four open-source backbones tested (Qwen2.5-Coder: 55.06%, Llama-3: 54.18%, Qwen-3: 54.36%), with per-dataset improvements ranging from +4.90 to +8.81 points over SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRM-PBE outperforms all four compared baselines across every benchmark and backbone. Against the SFT baseline (Li & Ellis, 2024), PRM-PBE gains 13.85 points on average with DeepSeek-Coder-V2 (56.61% vs. 42.76%), with the largest per-dataset improvement on MBPP (+8.81 pts). Against the strongest prompting strategy, WPS (Verbruggen et al., 2025), PRM-PBE leads by 8.73 points on average (56.61% vs. 47.88% for Claude-3.5-Sonnet+WPS), and even the 7.6B Qwen2.5-Coder under PRM-PBE (55.06%) exceeds the best proprietary model result. Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022) and Tree-of-Thought (Yao et al., 2023) prompting strategies achieve 42–46% average Pass@1 on proprietary models, trailing PRM-PBE by roughly 10–14 points. The paper further shows PRM-PBE covers 97% of tasks solved by the SFT baseline while additionally solving tasks that all prompting-based baselines fail on, indicating the process reward signal addresses a distinct failure mode (logical deviation and partial matching) rather than merely improving execution reliability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the process reward model (PRM) paradigm—well established in mathematical reasoning (PRM800K, Math-Shepherd) and code generation (CodePRM, process-supervised RL for code)—into the Programming-by-Example domain, where prior LLM-based methods (SFT, CoT, ToT, WPS) rely on outcome-level supervision or inference-time search without fine-grained step-level feedback. The reasoning tree construction with external instruction guidance is a novel data-construction mechanism specific to PBE's inductive setting, addressing the sparsity of positive samples that plagues process supervision when intermediate steps are non-executable natural-language reasoning. The three-stage curriculum organized by failure pattern (syntax errors → logical deviations → partial matches) is a practical scheduling strategy not seen in prior PRM-for-code work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.73% average improvement over the strongest prompting baseline (WPS) across five diverse benchmarks and four base models demonstrates that process-level supervision is a meaningful axis of improvement for PBE beyond what inference-time search can achieve. However, the framework's reliance on reference-program-derived natural language descriptions (D) during training data construction limits its applicability to settings where such oracle information is available, and the authors acknowledge this as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the external instruction mechanism can be replaced by self-generated or execution-derived signals, whether the approach extends to more complex programming paradigms (e.g., graphics, concurrent systems), and whether the compute overhead of reasoning tree construction plus PRM training plus PPO is justified relative to simpler alternatives at scale.
