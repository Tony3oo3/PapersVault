## Problem

Time series foundation model (TSFM) pre-training corpora (e.g., LOTSA) consist almost entirely of regularly sampled, single-variate series without uncertainty annotations, yet astronomical light curves from ground-based surveys like ZTF and Rubin are inherently irregularly sampled, multi-variate, and heteroskedastic. No standardized public benchmark existed to rigorously evaluate whether general-purpose TSFMs can capture the periodic structure in such challenging data, or whether they outperform the long-standing domain baseline of hand-crafted feature extraction (Lomb-Scargle periods, variability statistics).

## Input / Output

Each input is a pair of irregularly sampled g-band and r-band magnitude time series (modified Julian date vs. magnitude) from ZTF DR23, with per-observation uncertainties and gaps spanning multiple orders of magnitude in sampling interval. The TSFM produces a fixed-length embedding (256-dim for Chronos, Astromer, Time-MoE; 384-dim for Moirai) by mean-pooling token representations, which is then fed to task-specific heads: K-means/Ward clustering into 7 clusters, four supervised classifiers (k-NN, logistic, RF, MLP) for 7-class variable star identification, or a multi-class isolation forest for OOD anomaly scoring. The benchmark spans ~40,000 stars split 7:1:2 into train/val/test, with an additional 'anom' split of OOD sources.

## Method

StarEmbed is a benchmark rather than a single new model: it curates ~40,000 expert-labeled ZTF light curves from the CSPVS catalog across seven periodic variable star classes and evaluates six embedding families (Chronos, Chronos-Bolt, Moirai, Moirai-2, Time-MoE, Astromer-1/2) plus hand-crafted features and random embeddings on clustering, classification, and OOD detection. A key architectural insight is that Chronos's point-wise tokenization preserves fine-grained temporal structure under irregular sampling, whereas Moirai's patch-wise tokenization conflates observations separated by very different time intervals, which the paper demonstrates via attention-score visualization and a failure analysis showing TSFMs receive a 'warped' view of the light curve. The paper further proposes a modified RoPE that replaces sequential patch indices with true observation timestamps, showing a 12× improvement in R² on synthetic sinusoidal forecasting, and demonstrates that end-to-end classification fine-tuning (with LoRA or full FT) outperforms forecasting-objective fine-tuning for downstream accuracy.

## Results

On the StarEmbed benchmark (~40k ZTF light curves, 7 classes), hand-crafted features (HC) achieve the best supervised classification with RF (F1 = 0.804±0.003, Accuracy = 0.920±0.001), while Chronos is the best TSFM (k-NN F1 = 0.672, RF F1 = 0.638±0.002). For unsupervised clustering, Chronos leads among TSFMs with ARI(K-Means) = 0.1596, exceeding HC features (0.1197), though HC achieves the highest NMI (0.2700 vs. 0.2374). For OOD source detection, Chronos-Bolt dramatically outperforms all other methods, achieving 51.9% purity at the top-10% threshold versus 25.9% for HC features and 11.6% for random selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hand-crafted features (FATS + light_curve, 138-dim) remain the SOTA baseline for supervised classification (RF F1 = 0.804) and achieve the highest clustering NMI (0.270), but are outperformed by Chronos on pairwise clustering consistency (ARI 0.1596 vs. 0.1197) and dramatically outperformed by Chronos-Bolt on OOD detection (top-10% purity 0.519 vs. 0.259). Among TSFMs, Chronos consistently outperforms Moirai, Moirai-2, and Time-MoE across all three tasks; for example, Chronos achieves RF F1 = 0.638 versus 0.557 (Moirai) and 0.598 (Time-MoE). The domain-specific Astromer models perform worst among all learned embeddings: Astromer-1's embeddings collapse to near-random (F1 ≈ 0.12, matching the random baseline), and Astromer-2, while better (RF F1 = 0.580), is still outperformed by every general-purpose TSFM. Chronos-Bolt, which uses patch-based multi-step generation rather than autoregressive decoding, is the clear winner for OOD isolation (top-1% purity 0.569 vs. 0.213 for HC features), suggesting its tighter inlier manifold better separates rare morphologies. The classification-specific Mantis models (MantisV2, Mantis+) perform on par with Moirai (RF F1 ≈ 0.585–0.588) but below Chronos, which the authors attribute to their patch-based tokenization being less suited to irregularly sampled light curves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed literatures: general-purpose time series foundation models (Chronos, Moirai, Time-MoE) trained on regularly-sampled corpora like LOTSA, and the long-standing astrophysics pipeline for variable star classification built on hand-crafted features (FATS, light_curve) and domain-specific transformers (Astromer). It departs from both by treating astronomical light curves—characterized by irregular temporal sampling, multi-variate structure, and heteroskedastic uncertainties—as a rigorous out-of-domain stress test for TSFMs, a setting explicitly absent from their pre-training data. The modified-RoPE experiment extends the positional-encoding literature by demonstrating that incorporating true observation timestamps (rather than sequential indices) is necessary for TSFMs to capture periodicity in irregularly-sampled series, connecting to concurrent work by Zivanovic et al. (2026) on continuous-time RoPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StarEmbed provides the first public, expert-labeled benchmark (~40k ZTF light curves, seven classes) for evaluating TSFMs on data with the irregular sampling and multi-variate structure that will define the Rubin Observatory era (10–100× data scale-up from 2026). The finding that Chronos-Bolt achieves ~5× OOD detection purity over hand-crafted features while still trailing them on classification highlights a concrete gap: current TSFMs capture coarse morphology but not the fine periodic structure that domain features exploit. The modified-RoPE result identifies a specific, actionable architectural fix for irregular sampling, but the paper also shows that even with fine-tuning, TSFMs do not yet match the simple 138-dim hand-crafted baseline, leaving open whether a TSFM natively pre-trained on irregular, multi-variate, uncertainty-annotated data could close this gap at scale.
