## Problem

Existing LoRA-based unlearning methods in diffusion models apply static, global weight modifications that affect every forward pass regardless of input, causing overly broad forgetting of semantically adjacent concepts. Moreover, each target concept requires a separate training run with its own hyperparameter tuning, making multi-concept erasure (e.g., 100 celebrities) a practical scalability bottleneck. The paper addresses both the rigidity of static adapters and the per-concept training cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, encodes it via CLIP into a 768-dimensional embedding, and feeds this into a small MLP hypernetwork that outputs rank-1-to-9 LoRA weight matrices for the diffusion model's cross-attention (SD) or value-projection/output-transformation (Flux) layers. At inference, a single forward pass of the hypernetwork at the trajectory endpoint s=S produces the final LoRA weights, which are then applied during the 50-step denoising loop. For Stable Diffusion, the weights modify only the conditional CFG branch while the unconditional branch stays frozen; for Flux's flow-matching architecture, they are applied directly to all model parameters. The same hypernetwork handles all concepts: target concepts receive suppressive weights while non-target concepts receive near-zero weights, acting as a built-in semantic switch.

## Method

UnHype replaces static per-concept LoRA adapters with a single hypernetwork H_φ(c, s) that dynamically generates LoRA weights conditioned on the CLIP embedding c and a continuous unlearning step s. The key training insight, borrowed from Hypernet Fields, is gradient-matching: rather than requiring pre-computed target LoRA weights (which would defeat the purpose), the hypernetwork is supervised to match its own trajectory gradient Δθ_pred = H_φ(c,s+1) − H_φ(c,s) against the analytical gradient of a task loss Δθ_task = −η∇_{θ_s} L_task, eliminating the need for any ground-truth adapter. The task loss, adapted from UnGuide, steers the denoising prediction away from the target concept toward a semantically appropriate mapping concept (e.g., 'cat' → 'forest'), while a retention loss penalizes any deviation from zero weights for non-target concepts. This formulation enables a single 300-step training run to amortize over arbitrarily many concepts, and the continuous CLIP-conditioned input allows zero-shot generalization to unseen synonyms at inference time.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 object erasure benchmark (Table 1), UnHype achieves the best composite score H_o = 93.85% (average across airplane, ship, bird), with efficacy Acc_e = 6.61%, specificity Acc_s = 96.20%, and generality Acc_g = 7.67%. On nudity erasure (I2P, Table 2), UnHype reduces NudeNet detections to 8 total (98.9% reduction from the 743 baseline) while improving both FID (13.45) and CLIP (31.43) over the unmodified SD v1.4. On the 100-celebrity removal task (Table 4), UnHype achieves 0.46% residual recognition (Acc_e) and 86.35% specificity (Acc_s), yielding H_o = 92.48%, and on adversarial robustness (Table 5) it records 0.00% ASR under UnlearnDiffAtk and 1.05% under Ring-A-Bell.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnHype outperforms all 15 baselines on the primary object-erasure composite score H_o (93.85%), with the closest competitor being MACE (91.68%) and ESD-u (86.96%); methods like FMN (3.74%) and AC (4.11%) fail to suppress the target concept at all. On nudity erasure (I2P), UnHype achieves 8 total NudeNet detections, beating STEREO (9), SAeUron (18), AdvUnlearn (28), and MACE (111), while also improving FID and CLIP over the unmodified baseline. On the 100-celebrity task, UnHype (H_o = 92.48%) surpasses TRCE (89.85%) and MACE (88.54%), and on adversarial robustness it achieves 0.00% ASR under UnlearnDiffAtk (vs. 3.40% for AdvUnlearn, 4.21% for STEREO, 30.68% for RACE) and 1.05% under Ring-A-Bell (vs. 0.00% for AdvUnlearn, 2.10% for STEREO). On the Imagenette 10-class simultaneous removal, UnHype reaches 0.03 total ResNet-50 accuracy with CLIP 30.61, outperforming SP (0.08, CLIP 26.43), RECE (0.05, CLIP 29.27), and UCE (0.09, CLIP 29.45), while ESD-u achieves zero accuracy only by collapsing generation quality (CLIP 22.52). On ImageNet-Confuse5 fine-grained disentanglement, UnHype attains the highest Overall Acc (87.0%) and Preserve Acc (83.3%), whereas aggressive erasers UCE and RECE collapse Preserve Acc to ~5.5%, and ScaPre (84.3%) is the only other method that preserves neighbors well but with lower UQ (63.75 vs. 68.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

UnHype sits at the intersection of three research threads: LoRA-based concept erasure in diffusion models (MACE, UnGuide), the hypernetwork paradigm for parameter generation (Ha et al., 2016; HyperDreamBooth), and the Hypernet Fields gradient-matching principle (Hedlin et al., 2025) for training weight-generators without precomputed targets. The key departure from prior LoRA unlearning methods is the shift from static, per-concept adapters to a single CLIP-conditioned hypernetwork that amortizes LoRA generation across arbitrary concepts, eliminating the N-separate-runs bottleneck. The task loss is adapted from UnGuide's guided regression formulation, while the removal loss directly instantiates the Hypernet Fields trajectory-supervision mechanism in a new domain (unlearning rather than personalization or recontextualization).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the scalability story: a single 300-step training run handles 100 celebrity erasures simultaneously, whereas per-concept methods require 100 independent runs with separate hyperparameter tuning. The 0% attack success rate under UnlearnDiffAtk and the simultaneous improvement in FID/CLIP over the unmodified baseline suggest that CLIP-embedding conditioning provides a natural robustness mechanism that token-level methods lack. Limitations include keyword-driven over-suppression (innocuous uses of 'nude' in cosmetics or medical contexts) and the reliance on a fixed set of mapping concepts whose quality directly bounds erasure precision. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of concepts, how it handles compositional or negation-based prompts, and whether the gradient-matching training objective generalizes to non-LoRA adapter architectures.
