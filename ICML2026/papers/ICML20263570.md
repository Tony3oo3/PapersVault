## Problem

VLMs exhibit catastrophic failure in visual counting beyond their training distribution, yet the precise locus of this failure within the cognitive pipeline—perception, quantity comprehension, or symbolic articulation—remains unidentified. This paper asks whether the model is blind to objects, lacks a sense of magnitude, or simply cannot project a valid internal quantity onto the correct output token, and provides mechanistic evidence to isolate the bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263570/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are synthetic Go board images (19×19 grid for the toy model, 6×6 for Qwen3-VL) where black stones are targets and white stones serve as distractors (±30 or ±5 in count), with stone sizes calibrated to the model's patch resolution (14×14 or 32×32 pixels) to enforce a clean object-to-token mapping. Textual inputs are character sequences of controlled length. Outputs include greedy-decoded count predictions, binary True/False judgments for comparative counting, and diagnostic metrics (Vision Gap |N_H − N_G| and Language Gap |N_P − N_H|) derived from linear probes on frozen encoder outputs. The synthetic model uses individual-digit tokenization so multi-digit numbers are decomposed into single-character tokens, forcing place-value learning rather than atomic number lookup.

## Method

The paper constructs a controlled synthetic laboratory (2-layer ViT encoder + 2-layer GPT-2 decoder trained from scratch) with a decoupled curriculum: the language decoder is pretrained on textual counting up to N=99, while visual alignment is restricted to N≤49, creating a deliberate visual extrapolation regime (50–99) where labels exist but visual-textual pairings do not. A linear-probing diagnostic extracts a 'hidden number' N_H from frozen visual encoder outputs, enabling decomposition of total error into a Vision Gap (perceptual) and a Language Gap (decoding). Three targeted experiments then rule out each hypothesized bottleneck in sequence: (1) probing shows the Vision Gap remains ~0 even at N=120, rejecting perceptual failure; (2) a comparative counting task (matching an image of 75 stones to a text of 75 characters) achieves >70% accuracy in the extrapolation regime, rejecting magnitude-awareness loss; (3) error topology reveals mode collapse to attractor tokens (49, 5, 6, EOS) rather than Gaussian noise, and attention-head pruning shows 73.58% disjoint critical circuits between visual and textual counting, confirming the fractured magnitude hypothesis. All findings are validated on Qwen3-VL 32B-Instruct, where the same three-stage diagnostic path reproduces with only minor perceptual noise in the real-world encoder.

## Results

On the synthetic Go board task, the Toy VLM achieves 100% exact-match accuracy on textual counting up to N=99, yet visual counting collapses to 0% immediately beyond the training boundary (N>49). Linear probing reveals the Vision Gap |N_H − N_G| remains effectively zero across all regimes (0–120), while the Language Gap |N_P − N_H| explodes at N=50, isolating the failure to symbolic mapping. In the comparative counting task, the model maintains >70% accuracy in the visual extrapolation regime (50–99) despite 0% explicit enumeration. On Qwen3-VL 32B, visual counting flatlines near random guessing for N>8 while textual counting stays robust; the Vision Gap remains <0.1 and 73.58% of critical attention heads are non-overlapping between visual and textual counting circuits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic/analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against competing baselines. Instead, it validates its findings across two model scales: a from-scratch synthetic Toy VLM (2-layer ViT + 2-layer GPT-2) and the state-of-the-art Qwen3-VL 32B-Instruct. The core internal comparison is between three competing failure hypotheses (perceptual blindness, magnitude dissipation, symbolic mapping failure), with the first two rejected via linear probing and comparative reasoning, leaving symbolic mapping as the sole bottleneck. The synthetic-to-real transfer is the key validation: both models exhibit the same 'baseline paradox' (strong text counting, collapsed visual counting) and the same error topology (diffuse stochastic visual errors vs. tightly clustered systematic textual errors), confirming the fractured magnitude phenomenon is structural rather than an artifact of scale or training data. The paper notes that other open-source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, earlier Qwen iterations) were excluded because they lacked sufficient baseline counting accuracy (N<10) to meaningfully test extrapolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the systematic generalization literature (Lake & Baroni, 2018; Press et al., 2022; Anil et al., 2022) from NLP length generalization to the multimodal setting, and builds on the growing body of work diagnosing VLM counting failures (Paiss et al., 2023; Hsieh et al., 2023; Rahmanzadehgervi et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). However, it departs from prior work by shifting from performance benchmarking to mechanistic diagnosis: rather than measuring *how badly* VLMs fail at counting, it decomposes the task into three cognitive stages and uses linear probing, causal interventions (activation steering), and attention-head circuit analysis to pinpoint *where* the failure occurs. The 'fractured magnitude hypothesis'—that visual and textual quantities occupy disjoint statistical manifolds with no shared number space—is a new conceptual framing not present in prior probing or counting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the counting bottleneck is strictly in symbolic mapping (not perception or magnitude awareness) redirects the field's attention from improving visual encoders to enforcing unified cross-modal magnitude representations, with the implication that data scaling alone is insufficient. The practical consequence is significant: models that 'see' 120 objects correctly and can compare them to 120 characters still cannot output '120,' meaning the fix must be architectural (inductive priors for isomorphic number spaces) rather than empirical. Key limitations include the simplified Go-board setting (no occlusion, no semantic filtering), validation on a single foundation model (Qwen3-VL-32B), and the absence of a proposed solution—leaving open how to actually enforce unified magnitude representations in practice.
