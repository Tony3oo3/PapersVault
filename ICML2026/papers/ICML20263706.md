## Problem

Temporal answer grounding in instructional video (TAGV) requires locating a short answer segment within a long untrimmed video, but existing methods struggle with the extreme duration ratio (up to 48.9× on TutorialVQA) and sensitivity to irrelevant content. LVLM-based approaches that process entire videos suffer from information loss and reasoning bias when the target segment is very short, while VLP-based methods lack deeper relational reasoning and are vulnerable to textual redundancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263706/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (sampled at 2 FPS, frames resized to 448×448), a natural language query, and the video's subtitle track as input, and outputs a temporal interval [t_s, t_e] in seconds marking the answer segment. A key design detail is that the model can emit a rejection token [0.0, 0.0] to veto a candidate segment, triggering evaluation of the next candidate in a sequential first-valid loop. Auxiliary LLM-generated inputs (per-candidate subtitle summaries and a query-level pre-answer) are injected to enrich the reasoning context beyond raw frames.

## Method

CACR combines a coarse-to-fine pipeline: first, a Visual-Language Pre-training based Candidate Selection (VBCS) module, instantiated with MutualSL's dual visual-textual predictors, generates K=5 high-quality candidate segments from the full video, exploiting the empirical finding that cumulative maximum IoU increases monotonically with K. Second, a GRPO-optimized reasoning module built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct performs sequential hypothesis-verification over candidates without any SFT cold-start, using a composite reward that blends temporal IoU alignment (weight 0.2), a rejection reward (weight 0.8) that incentivizes discarding non-overlapping candidates, and a format reward. A differentiated prompting strategy feeds the policy model raw candidate features plus subtitle summaries, while the KL-regularized reference model receives a semantically enhanced input with an LLM-generated pre-answer, steering the policy toward intent-aligned predictions. At inference, candidates are evaluated in VBCS confidence order; the first valid timestamp is returned immediately, while rejections advance to the next candidate.

## Results

CACR achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on all six instructional video benchmarks: 72.11 on VehicleVQA, 59.65 on MedVidQA, 45.37 on CMIVQA, 43.65 on TutorialVQA, 41.06 on COIN, and 40.19 on CrossTask. The most dramatic gains appear on TutorialVQA (extreme duration-to-span ratio of 48.9), where CACR's 43.65 mIoU nearly doubles the best LVLM baseline (VTG-R1 at 19.38), and on CMIVQA (heavy subtitle noise), where it surpasses the strongest VLP method MutualSL by 5.95 points (45.37 vs. 39.42). The performance gap between these two hardest datasets is only 1.72 pp, demonstrating robustness across difficulty regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CACR outperforms the strongest VLP baseline MutualSL on all five reported datasets, with the largest margins on VehicleVQA (+6.37 pp, 72.11 vs. 65.74), CMIVQA (+5.95 pp, 45.37 vs. 39.42), and COIN (+5.73 pp, 41.06 vs. 35.33), while the gap on TutorialVQA is narrow (+0.17 pp, 43.65 vs. 43.48). Against LVLM-based methods, CACR's advantage is most pronounced on TutorialVQA, where it achieves 43.65 mIoU versus 18.44 (TimeZero), 19.38 (VTG-R1), and 15.24 (TimeScope)—more than doubling their scores on this extreme-ratio dataset. On CMIVQA, CACR (45.37) also substantially exceeds TimeZero (29.22), VTG-R1 (25.93), and TimeScope (31.61), confirming robustness to subtitle noise. Among LLM-only baselines, SubGPT reaches 58.00 mIoU on MedVidQA but collapses to 0.00 on TutorialVQA, whereas CACR maintains 43.65 there. Ask2Loc, the strongest LVLM competitor on MedVidQA (43.22) and VehicleVQA (55.28), is surpassed by CACR by 16.43 and 16.83 pp respectively, though it does not report TutorialVQA or COIN results.

## Relation to Prior Work

CACR sits at the intersection of two research threads: VLP-based temporal grounding (MutualSL, VTPSL) and RL-enhanced LVLM post-training (GRPO from the DeepSeek-R1 lineage). It departs from prior VLP methods by replacing end-to-end feature matching with a two-stage pipeline—coarse candidate selection via MutualSL followed by fine-grained LVLM reasoning—and from prior RL-based video grounding methods (TimeZero, Time-R1, VTG-R1) by narrowing the processing scope to a small candidate set rather than uniformly sampling the entire video. The rejection reward mechanism and the differentiated prompting strategy (policy model receives raw inputs while the reference model receives a semantically enriched pre-answer) are design choices not present in earlier GRPO applications to video tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for handling the extreme duration-to-answer ratio (up to 48.9×) that plagues instructional video grounding, and the consistent SOTA across six diverse benchmarks (medical, automotive, software, daily-life) suggests the candidate-aware sparse verification paradigm generalizes well. However, the method's reliance on subtitle quality and a fixed VLP backbone (MutualSL) limits its applicability to subtitle-free or noisy-subtitle settings, and the 'causal reasoning' framing is acknowledged by the authors to be a loose metaphor rather than formal causal inference. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-step compositional queries, whether the VBCS stage can be replaced by a learned module, and whether the heavy rejection weighting (α=0.8) remains optimal when candidate quality degrades.
