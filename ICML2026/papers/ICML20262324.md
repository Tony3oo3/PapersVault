## Problem

Existing robust MARL methods primarily defend against value-oriented attacks that minimize Q-value estimates, leaving a critical gap when the interaction structure itself is corrupted—i.e., when agents cannot observe each other or their coordinated actions are disrupted. This paper identifies interaction-level failure as a distinct and underexplored threat mode in cooperative MARL, where coordination collapses abruptly and prior robustness guarantees do not transfer.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation vector (partial environment view within a fixed sight range) and its own local trajectory as input, and outputs a discrete action (movement, attack, ability, or no-op). During adversarial training, the observation input is selectively zero-masked on L dimensions identified as most informative about the other group's actions, and the sampled action is stochastically replaced with an MI-minimizing alternative before execution. The masking budget L scales with the size of the opposing group, and the attack probability follows an adaptive schedule that increases as the policy's success rate exceeds a threshold.

## Method

IBAL partitions agents into two groups and quantifies cross-group influence via conditional mutual information, decomposed into an observation-level term (how sensitive G1's observations are to G2's actions) and an action-level term (how tightly G1's and G2's actions are coupled). The observation attacker uses CLUB to estimate dimension-wise MI and zero-masks the L highest-scoring dimensions, while the action attacker selects actions that minimize the action-level MI estimated via a KL-divergence-based reconstruction model. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows the joint-adversarial Dec-POMDP is value-equivalent to an induced Dec-POMDP with perturbed transition dynamics, enabling standard CTDE training (QMIX or MAPPO) on the induced process. The framework randomly resamples group partitions each episode and applies an adaptive attack-probability schedule, exposing the policy to a spectrum of interaction failures and yielding robustness that generalizes across diverse adversarial attacks and non-parametric perturbations such as agent absence.

## Results

Under adversarial attacks on SMAC, IBAL achieves mean win rates of 80.9% (3m) and 94.8% (3s_vs_3z), outperforming the strongest baseline WALL (64.2% and 83.5% respectively). Under the proposed interaction-breaking attack specifically, IBAL retains 78.3% (3m) and 91.7% (3s_vs_3z) win rates, while most baselines collapse to near 0% (e.g., WALL: 0.0%, ROMANCE: 1.0% on 3m). Under non-parametric perturbations (Dis-1, HP-15), IBAL achieves 96.7% mean on 3s_vs_3z and 88.4% on 8m, exceeding the best baselines (ROMANCE at 76.1% and WALL at 48.1%) by 20–40 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBAL outperforms all six baselines (Vanilla QMIX, FGSM, ATLA, ERNIE, ROMANCE, WALL) across every SMAC scenario and attack type. The most dramatic gap appears under the proposed interaction-breaking attack: on 3m, IBAL achieves 78.3% while WALL, ROMANCE, and FGSM all drop to 0–1%, and on 3s_vs_3z IBAL reaches 91.7% versus ROMANCE's 39.2% and WALL's 26.2%. Under non-parametric perturbations (Dis-1, HP-15), IBAL leads by 19–40 points over the best baseline (e.g., 96.7% vs. ROMANCE's 76.1% on 3s_vs_3z; 88.4% vs. WALL's 48.1% on 8m). FGSM and WALL perform competitively against their own targeted attacks (96.3% and 83.3% respectively on 3m) but are highly vulnerable to interaction-breaking, confirming that value-minimizing objectives do not generalize to coordination-disruption threats. ERNIE, which uses adversarial regularization rather than explicit attacks, shows the weakest robustness overall (51.9% mean on 3m), suggesting that regularization alone is insufficient for interaction-level failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CTDE paradigm (QMIX) and information-theoretic tools (CLUB for MI estimation, Jaques et al.'s model-based influence estimation) to propose a fundamentally different adversarial objective for robust MARL. While prior robust MARL methods (EGA, Wolfpack, FGSM, ATLA, ERNIE, ROMANCE, WALL) target value minimization or inject noise, IBAL instead quantifies cross-group influence via conditional mutual information and designs attacks that explicitly break the information flow enabling coordination. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.2) provides a clean equivalence between the joint-adversarial Dec-POMDP and an induced Dec-POMDP with perturbed dynamics, enabling standard CTDE training under the attack without modifying the learning algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight that interaction-breaking (disrupting information flow between agent groups) is a more fundamental and generalizable failure mode than value minimization shifts how the community should think about robustness in cooperative MARL. The finding that policies trained against MI-based interaction breaking generalize well to diverse unseen attacks, while methods trained against their own specific attacks collapse under interaction breaking, suggests that targeting the structural dependencies of coordination is more effective than targeting learned value estimates. Limitations include additional hyperparameters (group size K, masking budget L) and MI estimation overhead, and open questions include scalability to larger agent populations, continuous action spaces, and non-cooperative or mixed-motive settings.
