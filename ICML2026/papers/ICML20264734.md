## Problem

Existing inference-time answer selection methods (majority voting, static confidence weighting) treat all reasoning traces equally or aggregate confidence over the full trace, ignoring how model certainty evolves along the reasoning chain. This paper asks whether the trajectory of confidence from the beginning to the end of a trace provides additional discriminative signal for selecting correct answers in Best-of-N sampling, and why such a signal would exist in RL-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264734/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a question prompt, the method generates L=512 reasoning traces and extracts per-token top-K=20 logprobs at every decoding step (requiring open-source serving stacks like vLLM). Each trace is partitioned into N=10 position-normalized bins to enable comparison across traces of varying length, and the final output is a single selected answer determined by a weighted voting score that combines answer count, mean trace confidence, and the head-to-tail confidence difference. The method is training-free and operates entirely on existing model outputs.

## Method

The paper discovers that correct reasoning traces exhibit systematically positive confidence gain (tail confidence exceeds head confidence) while incorrect traces show attenuated or declining confidence, a pattern validated across four LLM architectures. It formalizes this as Confidence Dynamic Gain (CDG), defined as the difference between mean confidence in the last P% of position-normalized bins and the first P%, and incorporates it into a trace-level score s_ℓ = C̄_ℓ + β·ΔC_ℓ. The final answer is selected via R(a) = |T_a|^α · μ_a(s_ℓ), where the count-dampening exponent α<1 prevents majority voting from overwhelming the confidence signal. Theoretically, the paper shows that GRPO's uniform advantage assignment combined with answer convergence (correct traces concentrate on a single ground-truth token at the tail while reasoning paths remain diverse at the head) produces asymmetric logit updates that create the observed head-to-tail confidence gap. The method generalizes both majority voting (α=1, β=0) and DeepConf (α=1, β=0) as special cases.

## Results

CDG achieves the highest overall average accuracy of 74.6% across four models (DeepSeek-R1-8B, Gemma-3-27B, gpt-oss-20B, QwQ-32B) and four mathematical benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, HMMT 2025, BRUMO 2025), outperforming majority voting (69.2%), DeepConf-Mean (69.8%), and DeepConf-Tail (72.9%). The gains are most pronounced on harder benchmarks: on AIME 2025, CDG reaches 93.3% with DeepSeek-R1-8B versus 83.3% for majority voting and 83.3% for DeepConf-Tail. On GPQA-Diamond (a non-mathematical benchmark), CDG also leads with 72.2% versus 70.7% for DeepConf-Tail and 68.2% for majority voting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDG consistently outperforms both baselines across all four models and all four mathematical benchmarks. Against majority voting (Self-Consistency, Wang et al. 2022), CDG achieves a +5.4% overall average improvement (74.6% vs. 69.2%), with the largest gap on DeepSeek-R1-8B (+6.6%) and Gemma-3-27B (+6.7%). Against DeepConf (Fu et al. 2025), CDG improves over DeepConf-Mean by +4.8% and over DeepConf-Tail by +1.7% on the overall average; the advantage over DeepConf-Tail is most visible on QwQ-32B (+1.7%) and DeepSeek-R1-8B (+2.5%). Statistical significance tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p < 10⁻¹⁰ for all three baselines) confirm these gains are not due to chance. On the non-mathematical GPQA-Diamond benchmark, CDG (72.2%) also beats DeepConf-Tail (70.7%) and majority voting (68.2%), suggesting the confidence-dynamics signal generalizes beyond math.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Best-of-N answer selection literature by introducing a dynamic confidence signal (head-to-tail confidence gain) that goes beyond the static confidence measures used by DeepConf (Fu et al., 2025) and self-certainty scoring (Kang et al., 2025b). While prior work aggregates confidence over entire traces or fixed tail windows, CDG explicitly models how confidence *evolves* along the reasoning trajectory via position-normalized binning. The theoretical analysis connects the observed confidence dynamics to GRPO's asymmetric advantage assignment, providing a training-mechanism explanation that prior confidence-based voting methods lacked. The voting formulation in Eq. (8) cleanly generalizes both majority voting and DeepConf as special cases, positioning CDG as a strict superset of existing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that correct and incorrect reasoning traces exhibit systematically different confidence *trajectories* (not just levels) offers a new, training-free discriminative signal for LLM answer selection that is consistent across four diverse architectures. The GRPO-based theoretical explanation suggests the pattern should generalize to any advantage-weighted RL with verifiable rewards, opening a path for understanding how training dynamics shape inference-time behavior. Key limitations include the requirement for top-K logprobs at every decoding step (not always available via closed-source APIs), model-specific β tuning, and evaluation primarily on competition mathematics with only preliminary GPQA-Diamond results. Open questions include whether the pattern persists for models trained with entropy-regularized RL (e.g., Qwen-3) or non-mathematical reasoning tasks, and whether the signal degrades as models become more capable.
