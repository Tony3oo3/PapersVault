## Problem

Monitoring deployed ML models for harmful distribution shifts typically relies on supervised risk monitoring (SRM), which requires labeled test data at every time step—a costly and often impractical requirement in production. Unsupervised alternatives avoid labels but demand unverifiable calibration assumptions on the deployed model and need large sample sizes for reliable detection. The paper addresses the gap of how to monitor running model risk with only a small number of labels per step while preserving assumption-free, finite-sample type-I error guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each discrete time step t, the monitor receives a small labeled batch D_t of n_t input-label pairs and a larger unlabeled batch of N_t inputs (e.g., n_t=1, N_t=15 in experiments), along with a pre-deployed model f(·) and an auxiliary predictor f_p(·) that generates synthetic labels for unlabeled inputs. The loss function ℓ is bounded in [0,1], and the PPI-based risk estimates can take values in [−η_t, η_t+1], requiring an affine normalization to [0,1] before applying the CM-EB confidence sequence. The output is a sequential binary alarm decision Φ_t that is anytime-valid (can be checked at any t without inflating the type-I error), together with the lower confidence bound L_t^PP on the running risk and the upper bound U_0^PP on the nominal source risk.

## Method

PPRM integrates prediction-powered inference (PPI) into the sequential SRM testing framework. At each step, the auxiliary predictor f_p(·) generates synthetic labels for unlabeled inputs, and a PPI correction term—computed from the small labeled batch—removes the systematic bias, yielding an unbiased risk estimate R̂_t^PP = R̂_t^U + R̂_t^rect. The CM-EB confidence sequence (Lemma 2.1) is then applied to these PPI estimates to produce an anytime-valid lower bound L_t^PP on the running risk, while a betting-based method (Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2024) gives an upper bound U_0^PP on the source risk; an alarm fires when L_t^PP > U_0^PP + ε_tol. The hyperparameter η_t controlling reliance on unlabeled data is adapted online via a sliding-window plug-in estimator that minimizes the variance of the PPI estimate (Lemma 3.3), ensuring the sequence {η_t} remains predictable and the type-I error guarantee P(∃t: Φ_t=1 | H_0) ≤ δ_S + δ_T holds without assumptions on f_p(·) quality.

## Results

On the CIFAR10-C image classification task (binary loss, external ResNet-1201 predictor), PPRM raises an alarm at approximately t=760, compared to SRM at t≈800, while URM fails to trigger an alarm within the entire monitoring horizon. On the LLM QA task (Qwen2-VL-2B deployed model, prompt shift at t=200), PPRM alarms at t≈500 versus SRM at t≈530, with the true risk exceeding the threshold at t=280. The adaptive hyperparameter η_t further reduces average time to alarm across all predictors, e.g., from 519.08 to 498.31 for Qwen2-VL-7B and from 460.69 to 449.88 for GPT-4.1. The false-alarm rate remains below the target δ_T=0.15 across all time steps, validating the theoretical type-I error guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPRM consistently outperforms SRM (Podkopaev & Ramdas, 2022) across all three experimental domains. On CIFAR10-C with binary loss, PPRM detects the harmful shift ~40 time steps earlier than SRM (t≈760 vs. t≈800); with squared loss and self-synthetic labels, the gap is ~30 steps (t≈760 vs. t≈790). On the LLM QA task, PPRM alarms ~30 steps earlier than SRM (t≈500 vs. t≈530). Against URM (Schirmer et al., 2025), PPRM has a decisive advantage: URM fails to raise an alarm within the entire monitoring window on the image classification task, while PPRM detects the shift well within the horizon. The adaptive η_t variant further narrows the gap to SRM, with the largest gains observed for weaker predictors (e.g., Q2.5-VL3B: 527.80→508.83) and diminishing returns for stronger ones (GPT-4.1: 460.69→449.88), confirming that adaptive reliance on unlabeled data is most beneficial when the auxiliary predictor is less accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends supervised risk monitoring (SRM, Podkopaev & Ramdas 2022), which detects harmful distribution shifts via time-uniform confidence sequences on labeled data, to the semi-supervised setting by integrating prediction-powered inference (PPI, Angelopoulos et al. 2023). It departs from purely unsupervised monitoring (Amoukou et al. 2024; Schirmer et al. 2025) by requiring a small number of labeled samples per step, thereby avoiding the strong calibration assumptions those methods need. The CM-EB confidence sequence framework (Howard et al. 2021) is reused to obtain anytime-valid lower bounds on the PPI-based risk estimates, and the optimal hyperparameter derivation follows PPI++ (Angelopoulos et al. 2023b). Unlike prediction-powered e-value approaches (Csillag et al. 2025) that target instantaneous risk violations, PPRM targets persistent degradation of the running risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in bridging the gap between expensive fully-supervised monitoring and assumption-heavy unsupervised monitoring: PPRM achieves tighter confidence bounds (and thus earlier alarms) with only a handful of labels per step, while maintaining assumption-free type-I error guarantees. The adaptive hyperparameter mechanism is particularly valuable when the auxiliary predictor is weak, preventing the method from degrading below SRM performance. Open questions include integration with test-time adaptation, handling truly intermittent label streams (partially addressed in Appendix D.4), and extension to non-classification tasks such as regression or generative model monitoring. The requirement that n_t > 0 at every step remains a practical constraint in settings where labels arrive sporadically.
