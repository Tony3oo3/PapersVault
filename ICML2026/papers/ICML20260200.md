## Problem

Transformer-based tabular foundation models (TFMs) dominate small-to-medium tabular predictive benchmarks, yet their internal inference mechanisms remain largely unexplored. It is unclear how predictions emerge across depth, whether layers perform distinct or overlapping computations, and whether the full model depth is actually necessary for effective inference. This paper presents the first large-scale mechanistic study of layerwise dynamics in six state-of-the-art tabular in-context learning models to answer these questions.

## Input / Output

The models consume a labeled tabular support set (training rows with class labels) and an unlabeled query set (test rows), operating over sets of examples rather than sequences (row-invariant). Inputs are constrained to ≤10,000 samples and ≤100 features for binary classification, with extensions to multiclass (≤10 classes) and regression. The output is a class label with prediction probabilities; the tabular logit lens additionally produces per-layer intermediate predictions and entropy values, revealing that confident predictions form in early layers while the original decoder's balanced accuracy lags behind its ROC-AUC until late layers perform calibration.

## Method

The paper designs six complementary mechanistic experiments—embedding similarity (CKA and cosine), separation gap tracking, probing classifiers, a novel 'tabular logit lens' (per-layer pre-trained decoders adapted from the tuned lens concept), layer ablation (skipping, repeating, swapping), and self-repair analysis—applied across six TFMs (TabPFN v1/v2/2.5, TabICL, LimiX-2M/16M). These experiments reveal that TFMs form blocks of layers with highly similar representations, that early layers perform critical latent mapping while middle and late layers engage in iterative refinement with overlapping computations, and that self-repair occurs after middle/late layer ablations but not after the first layer. Guided by these findings, the authors pre-train a looped single-layer nanoTabPFN that reuses one transformer block six times during both training and inference, matching the six-layer baseline's performance while using only 20% of its parameters, demonstrating that depth in TFMs primarily facilitates iterative computation rather than learning fundamentally distinct per-layer transformations.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is a mechanistic interpretability study across six state-of-the-art TFMs (TabPFN v1/v2/2.5, TabICL, LimiX-2M/16M), revealing that inference unfolds in four stages (Latent Mapping → Feature Engineering & Labeling → Prediction Ensembling → Prediction Calibration) with substantial depthwise redundancy in middle and late layers. The key quantitative proof-of-concept shows that nanoTabPFN_looped (a single transformer block repeated 6×) achieves performance almost identical to the full 6-layer nanoTabPFN_6l while using only 20% of the parameters (750K vs. 3.7M), confirming that depth in TFMs primarily facilitates iterative computation rather than learning distinct per-layer transformations. A single non-looped layer (nanoTabPFN_1l) is clearly insufficient, and a performance gap remains versus the larger TabPFN(2.5) (10.7M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares nanoTabPFN_looped against two reference TFMs: TabPFN(v1) (Hollmann et al., 2023, ICLR) and TabPFN(2.5) (Grinsztajn et al., 2025). Against TabPFN(v1), nanoTabPFN_looped is substantially smaller (750K vs. 25.8M parameters) and does not match its performance, as expected given the large capacity difference. Against TabPFN(2.5) (10.7M params, 24 layers), the paper explicitly notes that 'there remains a performance gap compared to state-of-the-art models such as TabPFN(2.5),' confirming that the looped single-layer approach is a proof-of-concept at small scale rather than a competitive replacement. The critical internal comparison is nanoTabPFN_looped vs. nanoTabPFN_6l: the looped model matches the 6-layer model's performance almost exactly while using only 20% of the parameters, demonstrating that the iterative (looping) mechanism—not raw parameter count—drives the performance gain. The single-layer non-looped model (nanoTabPFN_1l) loses to both, confirming that one layer without recurrence is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the mechanistic interpretability toolkit developed for LLMs—logit lens, tuned lens, probing classifiers, layer ablation, and self-repair analysis—to the fundamentally different setting of tabular foundation models, which are encoder-only, row-invariant, and trained on synthetic prior-data-fitted tasks rather than next-token prediction. It builds directly on the looped/universal transformer idea (Dehghani et al., 2019) and the nanoTabPFN codebase to test whether iterative reuse of a single block can replace depth. The key departure from LLM interpretability work is the recognition that TFMs' fixed task (classification/regression via ICL) enables new probes like the separation gap and that their smaller scale permits large-scale layerwise studies infeasible for LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding of substantial depthwise redundancy across six state-of-the-art TFMs, combined with the proof-of-concept that a looped single-layer model matches a six-layer model at 20% of the parameters, opens a concrete path toward more parameter-efficient tabular foundation models. However, the looped-architecture result is demonstrated only at the nanoTabPFN scale with 10K training steps, and the authors explicitly note it does not yet extrapolate to larger models like TabPFN(2.5). Open questions include when effective depth becomes genuinely necessary (e.g., for more complex tabular tasks), whether the redundancy persists under ensembling, and whether the observed inference stages hold at the neuronal or circuit level.
