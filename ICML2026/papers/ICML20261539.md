## Problem

Existing Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) are designed for unimodal node features, yet many real-world graphs are multimodal with text and image attributes per node. The only prior attempt at a Multimodal GFM (UniGraph2) averages CLIP-encoded features before graph propagation, collapsing modality-specific information and preventing adaptive cross-modal alignment. The core challenge is to synergistically encode graph structure with heterogeneous multimodal features while respecting the distribution gaps between modalities.

## Input / Output

CAME takes a multimodal graph G=(V,E) where each node carries a raw text description and an image; both are encoded by frozen CLIP into shared d-dimensional embeddings (d=1024). For text-only datasets (Arxiv, Wiki-CS, KGs), the text embedding is duplicated as a placeholder for the missing image modality so all methods receive uniform input. Training operates on PPR-sampled subgraphs (top-128 neighbors per seed node) to bound computation on large graphs. The output is a fused node embedding z_i^(f) ∈ R^d, alongside the unimodal views z_i^(t) and z_i^(v), which are used for downstream node classification, edge classification, link prediction, and few-shot prototype matching via linear probing.

## Method

CAME first runs two independent GAT encoders (one per modality) over the same topology to produce structure-aware unimodal embeddings, avoiding the information dilution caused by early fusion. A dimension-wise sigmoid gate, computed from the concatenation of both unimodal embeddings, then performs fine-grained per-coordinate fusion; the paper proves via an MSE-optimal analysis (Proposition 4.1) that the optimal linear fusion is generally dimension-dependent, justifying this design over scalar gating. A modality-aware Mixture-of-Experts module with three expert groups (text, image, fusion; K=6 experts each) applies two-level routing: intra-group top-k=2 sparse selection for modality-specific expert composition, and an inter-group softmax router that adaptively balances the three views per node. Training uses a tri-view InfoNCE contrastive loss over all three pairs (t,v), (t,f), (v,f) with weights (0.5, 0.1, 0.5), which the paper shows via a rank-based proposition (Proposition 4.3) keeps the fused embedding simultaneously retrievable from both unimodal spaces, preventing unimodal dominance.

## Results

CAME achieves the best performance across all 10 datasets and three generalization settings (in-distribution, in-domain, out-of-domain). On in-distribution node classification, CAME reaches 81.84% on Products, 10.42% MRR on Goodreads-LP, and 28.88% MRR on Amazon-cloth. On out-of-domain transfer, CAME attains 71.66% on Arxiv, 78.57% on Wiki-CS, 94.59% on FB15K-237, and 81.97% on WN18RR. In the few-shot setting (1/3/5-shot), CAME also leads on all datasets, with particularly large gains on multimodal benchmarks like Goodreads-NC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAME outperforms all 9 baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the closest prior MGFm, UniGraph2, CAME gains +1.02 on Products, +1.66 on Goodreads-LP, +4.63 on Amazon-cloth, +8.16 on Amazon-sports, +3.43 on Goodreads-NC, +0.30 on Ele-fashion, +1.12 on Arxiv, +1.50 on Wiki-CS, +2.29 on FB15K-237, and +6.77 on WN18RR. Among graph foundation models, SAMGPT is the strongest non-multimodal baseline (e.g., 78.53 on Products, 79.02 on Goodreads-NC), but CAME surpasses it by 3.31 and 4.19 respectively. Classical self-supervised methods (BGRL, DGI, GRACE, GraphMAE2) trail significantly, with the best among them (GRACE at 73.44 on Products) still 8.4 points below CAME. CLIP-only feature baselines (CLIP-text at 64.83 on Products) confirm that raw multimodal encoding without graph-aware fusion is insufficient. CAME also generalizes to text-only graphs (Arxiv, Wiki-CS), outperforming all baselines despite being designed for multimodal inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAME positions itself as one of the first systematic efforts in Multimodal Graph Foundation Models (MGFMs), extending the GFM paradigm (GCOPE, FUG, SAMGPT) from unimodal node attributes to graphs with text and image features. It departs from the only prior MGFm attempt, UniGraph2, by rejecting coarse averaging of CLIP features before graph propagation in favor of modality-specific GNN encoders, a dimension-wise gated fusion (theoretically grounded via an MSE-optimal linear fusion analysis), and a two-level modality-aware Mixture-of-Experts. The cross-modal contrastive loss generalizes standard InfoNCE from two augmented views to a tri-view (text, vision, fusion) alignment objective, drawing on the self-supervised graph learning literature (DGI, GRACE, BGRL) while repurposing modalities as natural alternative views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that modality-specific structural propagation and fine-grained gated fusion substantially outperform early-fusion baselines across in-domain and out-of-domain settings, CAME provides a practical recipe for building multimodal GFMs that generalize beyond their pretraining corpus. The modality-aware MoE with sparse top-k routing offers a scalable mechanism for handling distribution shifts across domains without inflating per-node computation. Open questions include whether the framework extends naturally to more than two modalities (e.g., audio, 3D), whether the CLIP dependency limits ceiling performance, and whether the PPR-sampling approximation degrades on graphs with highly heterogeneous degree distributions. The paper's reliance on frozen CLIP encoders also leaves open whether end-to-end multimodal pretraining could yield further gains.
