## Problem

Contrastive fine-tuning of LM-based dense retrievers on standard query-document pairs (e.g., MS MARCO) causes an early-peak overfitting phenomenon that suppresses latent reasoning capabilities, degrading performance on reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmarks like BRIGHT. Prior work addresses this by synthesizing reasoning-oriented supervision with LMs, but this pipeline is costly, hard to validate, and introduces potential bias. The paper asks whether the reasoning capacity already latent in LM backbones can be elicited through training alone, without any synthetic data.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes query-positive-negative batches (one query, one positive, and 6–15 negatives per batch) from MS MARCO, with queries truncated to 32 tokens and passages to 196 tokens. At inference, the LM and in-batch attention mechanism are entirely discarded; only the dual-encoder retriever is used to compute dot-product similarity scores between query and document embeddings, adding zero parameters, latency, or memory over a standard contrastive retriever. The final output is a ranked document list scored by cosine similarity of L2-normalized EOS-pooled embeddings.

## Method

ElicitR augments standard InfoNCE contrastive learning with a generative regularization loss that co-trains a small 135M LM alongside the retriever. The LM performs next-token prediction on each text in a query-positive-negative batch, but generation is conditioned not only on its own prefix also on the other in-batch texts via a retriever-weighted in-batch attention mechanism: cross-text attention weights α_ij are computed as a softmax over retriever similarity scores, so the retriever controls how much each text attends to others. This in-batch attention is implemented as a parallel attention stream inside every transformer block of the LM, where each sequence maintains two hidden states (standard causal self-attention and in-batch attention), and the cross-sequence contribution is added at every layer before LayerNorm and FFN. Gradients from the NTP objective flow back through α_ij to update the retriever, encouraging it to assign high similarity to text pairs where one contains information useful for predicting tokens in the other—capturing fine-grained dependencies beyond binary relevance. The framework is first initialized on raw text (Wikipedia + code corpora) using the same NTP objective (Revela-style), then fine-tuned on MS MARCO with the combined loss L = L_InfoNCE + λ·L_genreg, using LoRA (rank 256) on both the retriever and the reference LM.

## Results

On BRIGHT (12 reasoning-intensive retrieval tasks), ElicitR-3B achieves 23.1 average nDCG@10, the best score among all open-source models and surpassing 7B+ models (GritLM: 20.9, E5: 17.9) and proprietary APIs (Google: 19.5, Voyage: 17.7). Across all four retriever scales (0.1B–3B), ElicitR improves over the contrastive-only RepLLaMA baseline by 16–29% relative (e.g., 15.7 vs. 12.2 at 0.1B; 21.4 vs. 17.7 at 1B). On BEIR (13 general-domain tasks), ElicitR matches RepLLaMA within ±0.5 nDCG@10, confirming no degradation in general retrieval. ElicitR-3B ranks top-1 on 7 of 12 BRIGHT tasks and outperforms GPT-4-based reranking baselines (22.6) as a single-stage retriever.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ElicitR-3B (23.1 avg nDCG@10) outperforms all sparse and compact dense baselines by wide margins: BM25 (14.8), SBERT (14.9), BGE (13.7), and Instructor-XL (18.9). It also surpasses all 7B+ open-source models—GritLM (20.9), SFR (18.3), and E5 (17.9)—despite using 2.6× fewer parameters, as well as all four proprietary APIs (Google 19.5, OpenAI 17.8, Voyage 17.7, Cohere 16.6). Against its direct contrastive-only counterpart RepLLaMA, ElicitR wins at every scale with 16–29% relative gains, with the largest improvements on reasoning-heavy tasks (e.g., +106% on Robotics at 0.1B, +53% on Biology at 1B). The one comparison where ElicitR trails is ReasonIR-8B (24.4 vs. 23.1 avg), which uses 2.6× more parameters, 3.5× more training pairs (including ~345K LM-synthesized), and synthesis seeded from BRIGHT's own corpus; however, ElicitR-3B wins on 5 of 12 tasks (Bio., Earth., Econ., Rob., Sus.) while ReasonIR retains advantages on math/coding tasks (Pony, AoPS, TheoQ., TheoT.). On BEIR, ElicitR matches RepLLaMA within ±0.5 nDCG@10 across all four backbones, confirming no general-domain degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ElicitR repurposes the Revela in-batch attention mechanism (Cai et al., 2025), which was originally a self-supervised pretraining objective trained solely on raw text, into an auxiliary generative regularizer applied during supervised contrastive fine-tuning on query-positive-negative batches. This positions the paper as an orthogonal alternative to the dominant paradigm of using LMs to synthesize reasoning-intensive query-document pairs (ReasonIR, RaDeR, DIVER), arguing instead that reasoning capacity is already latent in LM-based retrievers but gets suppressed by contrastive overfitting. The method also builds on the broader LM-backbone-for-retrieval line (RepLLaMA, E5, GritLM) and the LM-guided retriever learning tradition (REPLUG, Atlas), but differs by using NTP gradients through retriever-weighted cross-attention rather than perplexity alignment or cross-attention distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of contrastive overfitting as a specific mechanism degrading reasoning-intensive retrieval (the early-peak phenomenon on BRIGHT absent on BEIR) provides a useful diagnostic for the field, and the proposed fix is practically attractive: no synthetic data pipeline, a 135M auxiliary LM, and a drop-in loss term. Limitations include consistent drops on symbolic tasks (Pony, AoPS) attributable to the Wikipedia initialization lacking formal text, a ~10 GB GPU memory overhead, and a 1.3-point average gap to the 8B ReasonIR baseline that uses 3.5× more training pairs. Open questions include whether domain-specific initialization can close the symbolic-task gap, whether the approach transfers to multilingual or multimodal retrieval, and whether the 135M regularizer size is optimal or whether larger auxiliary LMs yield further gains.
