## Problem

Deep RL agents suffer from persistent instability, representation collapse, and neuron dormancy driven by the inherent non-stationarity of bootstrapped learning targets and evolving data distributions. Prior work has addressed these pathologies through algorithmic modifications, auxiliary objectives, or architectural heuristics, but the role of representation geometry—specifically how the statistical structure of learned embeddings interacts with drifting targets—has received comparatively limited attention. This paper asks what properties representations should satisfy to remain stable under continuously evolving objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RL inputs (pixel frames for Atari, state vectors for Isaac Gym) and produces actions (discrete or continuous) as in the base algorithm. The SIGReg regularizer operates on the penultimate-layer embedding h_η(s) ∈ R^d, projecting it onto K random unit directions and matching each univariate projection to a zero-mean Gaussian via a characteristic-function-based loss. This auxiliary loss is added to the standard RL objective without modifying the policy or value heads, and the random projection directions are resampled across iterations to enforce isotropy in expectation.

## Method

The paper provides a Lyapunov stability analysis showing that, for a linear readout tracking a time-varying TD target, isotropic Gaussian embeddings make the zero tracking-error equilibrium stable: isotropy equalizes contraction across all directions (minimizing the condition number of the embedding covariance), while Gaussianity minimizes the variance of the drift term by eliminating higher-order moment contributions (via Isserlis' theorem). To enforce this structure in practice, the authors adopt Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), which projects embeddings onto a small set of random directions and applies a univariate distribution-matching loss to each projection using a characteristic-function-based objective, yielding a fully differentiable regularizer that simultaneously controls isotropy and tail behavior. Applied as a lightweight auxiliary loss on top of PQN and PPO, SIGReg consistently improves training stability, preserves representation rank, reduces neuron dormancy, and yields substantial AUC gains across Atari-10, full Atari (51/57 games improved), and Isaac Gym continuous control, without introducing architectural complexity or second-order optimization.

## Results

On the full Atari suite (57 games) with PQN, isotropic Gaussian regularization (SIGReg) improves 51/57 games (89.5%), with a mean AUC improvement of 889% and a median improvement of 138% over the RAdam baseline. On the Atari-10 subset, SIGReg improves 90% of games with a 305% average AUC gain under PQN and 70% of games with a 7.9% average gain under PPO. In the non-stationary CIFAR-10 setting, SIGReg reduces representation loss by 78–85%, increases feature rank by 51–76%, and cuts neuron dormancy by 89–98% across all tested optimizers (Adam, RAdam, Kron).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VICReg (covariance whitening), SIGReg substantially outperforms on PQN (305% vs. 144% average AUC improvement) and PPO (7.9% vs. 4.4%), confirming that isotropy alone is insufficient and that Gaussian tail control provides additional stability. Compared to the Kronecker-factored optimizer (Kron) from Castanyer et al. (2026), SIGReg with a first-order optimizer narrows the performance gap significantly without the extra memory and computation of curvature estimation; on CIFAR-10, Kron+SIGReg reaches 72.7% AUC versus 62.6% for Kron alone. Against simplicial embeddings (Obando-Ceron et al., 2026) and weight orthogonalization, SIGReg achieves the strongest overall gains, improving 90% of Atari-10 games with the highest average improvement (Section 4.5, Table 10). Heavier-tailed isotropic alternatives (Laplacian, Logistic) improve a similar fraction of games but yield 25–32% smaller average AUC gains than the Gaussian target, validating the theoretical argument that fast tail decay is critical for controlling drift variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) from the self-supervised learning setting (LeJEPA, Balestriero & LeCun 2025) to deep RL, providing a Lyapunov stability analysis that formalizes why isotropic Gaussian embeddings stabilize linear tracking under non-stationary targets. It positions itself against the dominant line of work addressing deep RL instability through algorithmic or architectural interventions (target networks, auxiliary prediction heads, Kronecker-factored optimization, multi-skip residuals), arguing instead that representation geometry is the root cause and should be shaped directly. The paper also connects to the broader literature on representation collapse, neuron dormancy, and plasticity loss in deep RL, showing that these pathologies are predictable consequences of unconstrained anisotropic geometry under drifting supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that a lightweight first-order regularizer (SIGReg) can substantially narrow the performance gap to expensive Kronecker-factored optimization—without additional memory or compute—makes this a potentially widely adoptable technique for stabilizing deep RL at scale. The observation that Kronecker-factored optimization and multi-skip architectures implicitly induce isotropic Gaussian representations suggests that part of their stability benefit operates through representation geometry rather than curvature estimation alone, reframing how we understand these methods. Key limitations include the assumption of linear readouts and fixed covariance in the theory, the absence of task-specific structure enforcement, and the open question of whether these results extend to fully coupled off-policy actor-critic algorithms and continual RL settings.
