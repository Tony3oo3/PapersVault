## Problem

Existing real-centric deepfake detection methods model the authentic distribution using continuous latent manifolds (VAEs, GMMs, Diffusion Models), which inadvertently create a convex 'safe zone' where interpolated forgery artifacts receive higher likelihood than genuine samples. This continuity enables smooth interpolation of fake features between real anchors, causing ambiguous detection boundaries that the paper identifies as a fundamental, overlooked limitation of the real-centric paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes face images (video frames) at standard resolution, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder into 256 patch tokens of 1024 dimensions each. During training, 8 frames per video are uniformly sampled; during inference, 32 frames are processed and their probabilities averaged to yield a video-level real/fake classification score. The intermediate representation is a spatial residual matrix of normal (off-manifold) components per patch, which is modulated by a sample-specific weight vector from a hypernetwork before final binary classification.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage 1 discretizes the CLIP latent space via a learnable VQ codebook (K=1024) trained with a commitment loss on real samples and a margin-based dispersion loss on fake samples, imposing a strict information bottleneck bounded by log₂K that forces the encoder to discard spurious details enabling artifact interpolation. Stage 2 introduces a masked ViT (75% masking ratio, 2 transformer blocks) to restore spatial dependencies among quantized tokens, followed by an adaptive tangent space projection that learns a 16-dimensional orthonormal basis per code vector to decompose reconstruction residuals into tangent (legitimate variation) and normal (anomaly) components. A hypernetwork maps the [CLS] token to sample-specific weights that fuse global semantic context with local geometric residuals for the final classification decision, balancing discrete rigidity against continuous generalizability.

## Results

Trained on FaceForensics++ (c23), the proposed method achieves a video-level AUC of 0.929 on average across six cross-dataset benchmarks (CDF-v2, FSh, DFD, DFDC, DFDCP, WDF), outperforming the strongest baseline Effort (0.898) by 3.1 points. On the cross-method evaluation (UniFace, BleFace, FaceDan, FSGAN, InSwap, SimSwap), it reaches 0.954 average AUC versus 0.930 for Effort and 0.931 for Forensics Adapter. On the GenImage AIGC benchmark, the method attains 92.3% mean accuracy, surpassing Effort (91.1%) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 23 compared baselines on both cross-dataset and cross-method evaluations. Against the strongest real-centric baseline Effort (ICML'25), it gains +3.1 points in average cross-dataset AUC (0.929 vs. 0.898) and +2.4 points in cross-method AUC (0.954 vs. 0.930), with the largest per-dataset gains on FSh (+5.0) and WDF (+7.0). Compared to Forensics Adapter (CVPR'25), the advantage is +4.1 points on cross-dataset average and +2.3 on cross-method average. Against earlier frequency- and identity-based methods (F3Net, SRM, IID, UCF), the improvement exceeds 10 points in average AUC. On the GenImage AIGC benchmark, the method leads Effort by 1.2% mAcc (92.3% vs. 91.1%) and outperforms UnivFD by 12.8%, though it trails FatFormer (88.9%) on the overall mAcc while surpassing it on SDv1.5, Wukong, VQDM, GLIDE, and SDv1.4 subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Effort (Yan et al., 2025a), which uses parameter-efficient fine-tuning of CLIP ViT-L/14 for real-centric deepfake detection via continuous subspace learning (SVD). The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental flaw in all continuous real-centric approaches (VAEs, GMMs, SVD, Diffusion Models): the inherent convexity of continuous manifolds creates a 'safe zone' where interpolated forgeries receive higher likelihood than real samples. The paper introduces a vector-quantized codebook into the CLIP feature space to impose a discrete information bottleneck, departing from both continuous real-centric methods and prior discrete approaches (SeeAble's fixed prototypes, D3QE's autoregressive statistics) by learning an adaptive codebook with a margin-based dispersion loss for fake samples, then reintroducing local continuity via an adaptive tangent space projection mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'convexity failure' of continuous priors provides a principled explanation for why real-centric deepfake detectors struggle with interpolation-based forgeries (face-swapping, reenactment), and the discrete codebook offers a concrete remedy with only ~14% inference overhead. The tangent space projection is a practical solution to the generalization cost of pure discretization, allowing legitimate intra-class variation (illumination, pose) while rejecting off-manifold artifacts. A stated limitation is the fixed-capacity codebook (K=1024), which may underrepresent extreme poses or severe occlusions; the authors suggest dynamic codebook expansion and multi-scale discretization as future directions. The framework's transfer to general AIGC detection (GenImage, 92.3% mAcc) hints at broader applicability beyond facial forensics.
