## Problem

Streaming PCA algorithms such as Oja's method require full d-dimensional samples, but many physical systems (phased-array radar, mmWave communications, neural recording) can only acquire a handful of scalar measurements per sample. Prior analyses of compressed subspace tracking assumed noiseless data (λ₂ = 0), leaving the noisy setting without convergence guarantees or information-theoretic lower bounds. This paper establishes the first matching upper and lower bounds for adaptive compressed PCA with noisy observations, proving that the d² dimension factor is the fundamental, unavoidable cost of observing only two measurements per iteration.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the algorithm receives a Gaussian sample v_t ~ N(0, Σ) but observes only two scalar projections: one along the current eigenvector estimate u_t and one along a random unit vector b_t drawn from u_t's orthogonal complement. The output is a unit-norm estimate u_T after T iterations, with expected sine-squared error E[1 − (ūᵀu_T)²] decaying as O(λ₁λ₂d²/(Δ²T)). The measurements are adaptive — the 2×d measurement matrix A_t is chosen based on the current iterate u_t — which is essential for achieving the optimal d² rate rather than the d³ rate suffered by non-adaptive schemes.

## Method

The algorithm is a compressed variant of Oja's rank-one update: the full-dimensional observation is replaced by its projection onto span{u_t, b_t}, giving û_{t+1} = u_t + η_t(u_t u_tᵀ + b_t b_tᵀ)v_t v_tᵀ u_t followed by normalization. The convergence proof decomposes the per-iteration change in squared cosine alignment into a self-term (signal exploitation along u_t) and a cross-term (exploration via b_t), bounding each through Cauchy-Schwarz, Isserlis's theorem for fourth moments, and Jensen's inequality to derive the stochastic recurrence x_{t+1} ≤ x_t(1 − 2η̂(1−x_t)) + Sη̂² with S = 3λ₁λ₂d²/Δ² + 15λ₁d/Δ. Analysis proceeds in two phases: a warmup phase with constant step size η̂ = 1/(2S) achieving geometric growth of alignment from 1/d to 0.5 in O(S log d) steps, and a local convergence phase with decaying step size η_t = 2(d−1)/(Δ(4S + t − t₀)) yielding the O(1/t) rate. The matching lower bound applies Assouad's lemma over 2^{d−1} covariance hypotheses, where the key insight is a measurement energy budget argument: two unit-norm measurements per iteration supply total energy 2t spread across d−1 perturbation coordinates, limiting per-coordinate KL divergence and yielding Ω(λ₁λ₂d²/(Δ²t)).

## Results

The paper establishes a matching minimax rate of Θ(λ₁λ₂d²/(Δ²t)) for adaptive compressed PCA with two measurements per iteration, via an upper bound (Theorem 4.1) and a novel Assouad-based lower bound (Theorem 4.2). Empirically, the dimension scaling exponent is 2.16 on d ∈ {16,…,1024}, closely matching the theoretical d² prediction, and the adaptive strategy outperforms non-adaptive random measurements by 4.2× at d=16 growing to 14× at d=64. The tracking extension predicts a steady-state error of x* = V + √(VS) at optimal step size η̂ = √(V/S), validated across four orders of magnitude in drift velocity V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other named algorithms but instead compares three fundamental regimes: fully-observed Oja (rate Θ(λ₁λ₂d/(Δ²t))), adaptive compressed Oja (Θ(λ₁λ₂d²/(Δ²t))), and non-adaptive compressed Oja (Ω(λ₂²d³/(Δ²t))), separating them across three powers of d. Empirically, the adaptive strategy (measuring along the current estimate plus a random orthogonal direction) consistently outperforms non-adaptive random measurements, with the gap widening from 4.2× at d=16 to 14× at d=64, confirming the theoretical d vs d² separation. The empirical convergence curve sits between the upper and lower bounds to within a constant factor of 10,368 (12·864), validating the order-optimality of the rate. Compared to prior adaptive sensing analyses (Ongie et al., 2017; Zhang & Balzano, 2016b), which assumed noiseless data (λ₂=0), this work is the first to handle noisy observations with nonzero trailing eigenvalues, providing the first global convergence guarantee in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adaptive GROUSE framework (Ongie et al., 2017) and the Oja–GROUSE equivalence (Balzano, 2022) from the noiseless regime (λ₂=0) to the noisy setting where trailing eigenvalues are nonzero, providing the first convergence guarantee for adaptive compressed subspace tracking beyond the noiseless case. It also departs from prior compressed sensing analyses (Zhang & Balzano, 2016a; Gonen et al., 2016) by establishing the first information-theoretic lower bound for adaptive compressed eigenvector estimation via Assouad's lemma, and by proving a non-adaptive lower bound that quantifies the cost of fixing measurements before observing data. The stochastic recurrence analysis builds on the self-term/cross-term decomposition used in fully-observed Oja's algorithm convergence proofs (Li et al., 2018; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2017) but must handle the additional adaptive coupling between measurement matrices and iterates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the d² dimension penalty in adaptive compressed PCA is a fundamental information-theoretic limit (matching upper and lower bounds), not an algorithmic artifact, and cleanly separates three regimes—fully-observed (d¹), adaptive-compressed (d²), and non-adaptive-compressed (d³)—across three powers of d. This has direct implications for hardware-constrained sensing systems (mmWave radar, neural interfaces, spectrum sensing) where the number of RF chains or channels is fixed. Open questions include extending to rank-k subspaces (where orthonormalization couples k stochastic recurrences), sub-Gaussian data, and sparse PCA under compression; the current analysis is limited to rank-1 and Gaussian assumptions.
