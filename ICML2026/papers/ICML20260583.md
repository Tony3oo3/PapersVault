## Problem

Existing uncertainty-based hallucination detectors for MLLMs fail in two ways: (1) when the visual module lags behind the language module, models repeatedly produce semantically similar but incorrect answers, yielding falsely low uncertainty and missed detections; and (2) semantic entropy metrics, which rely on discrete clustering of sampled responses, become highly unstable when the sample size K is small. The paper asks whether a prompting strategy can break false consistency and whether a geometric, clustering-free metric can provide more stable uncertainty estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260583/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a task instruction (free-form question or multiple-choice prompt) as input and operates entirely in a black-box setting with no access to model internals or external knowledge bases. It produces a binary hallucination flag (1 = reliable, 0 = hallucinated) by comparing an averaged vMF-based trustworthiness score against a fixed threshold θ = 0.48. Internally, the method requires 2K+1 = 21 forward passes (K = 10) per sample: one for the reference answer, K for direct sampling, one for object recognition, and K for object-bridged sampling, with responses embedded via nli-roberta-large.

## Method

DOUBT combines two components. First, Object-level Understanding and Bridging (OUB) is a two-step prompting scheme: the model is first asked to list up to 3 salient objects in the image, then prompted to re-answer the original question conditioned on that object list, producing a second response set (s_bri) alongside the directly sampled set (s_ori). This decoupling exposes inconsistencies hidden in direct sampling by forcing the model to ground its reasoning in explicit visual entities. Second, instead of semantic entropy, the method normalizes all response embeddings onto a unit hypersphere and computes the mean resultant length R̄ = (1/K)‖Σx̂ᵢ‖, a statistic from the von Mises-Fisher distribution that measures directional concentration without discrete clustering. The final trustworthiness score is the average of R̄ over both response sets; a higher score indicates greater internal consistency and lower hallucination risk. The approach is model-agnostic, training-free, and plug-and-play, requiring only prompting and a fixed embedding model.

## Results

DOUBT achieves the best average detection accuracy across all four benchmarks: 64.50% on LLaVABench, 75.23% on MMVet, 60.22% on MMMU, and 76.07% on ScienceQA, evaluated over ten MLLMs spanning 1B–72B parameters. The largest margins appear on MMVet (+0.96 pp over EigenScore) and MMMU (+2.54 pp over VL-Uncertainty), while on LLaVABench the gap over the second-best method (EigenScore, 63.83%) is 0.67 pp. Performance is notably strong on smaller models, e.g., InternVL2-1B reaches 73.33% on LLaVABench and 78.44% on MMVet, suggesting OUB is most beneficial when the visual module is weakest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOUBT outperforms all six baselines in average detection accuracy on every benchmark. The closest competitor is EigenScore (Chen et al., 2024), which trails by 0.67 pp on LLaVABench (63.83% vs. 64.50%) and 0.96 pp on MMVet (74.27% vs. 75.23%), but DOUBT pulls ahead more decisively on MMMU (60.22% vs. 56.84%) and ScienceQA (76.07% vs. 69.25%). VL-Uncertainty (Zhang et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline on ScienceQA (75.25%) and MMMU (57.68%), yet still falls short of DOUBT by 0.82 pp and 2.54 pp respectively. Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., 2024) and KLE (Nikitin et al., 2024) occupy the mid-range (53–65% avg), while SNNE (Nguyen et al., 2025) and GAVIE (Liu et al., 2024) are consistently the weakest, with GAVIE averaging only 30–44% across benchmarks. Notably, DOUBT's advantage is largest on smaller models (e.g., +8.33 pp over EigenScore on InternVL2-1B for LLaVABench), confirming that OUB is most impactful when the visual module is weakest and direct sampling produces falsely consistent hallucinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sampling-based hallucination detection pipeline introduced by Farquhar et al. (2024), which estimates uncertainty by clustering multiple model responses and computing semantic entropy. DOUBT departs from this lineage in two ways: it replaces semantic entropy with a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) mean resultant length metric that operates directly on normalized embedding directions (avoiding discrete clustering), and it introduces an Object-level Understanding and Bridging (OUB) prompting scheme that decouples object recognition from relational reasoning to break the 'false consistency' failure mode where weak visual modules produce repeatedly similar but incorrect answers. The method is positioned squarely in the black-box, model-agnostic camp, contrasting with white-box internal-signal methods and external-knowledge verification approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The false-consistency problem—where MLLMs confidently repeat incorrect answers due to the visual-language modality gap—is a practical bottleneck for deploying hallucination detectors in safety-critical settings, and DOUBT's OUB mechanism offers a simple prompting-based remedy that requires no architectural changes. The vMF-based trustworthiness metric provides a theoretically grounded, clustering-free alternative to semantic entropy that is more stable in the low-sample regime (K=10) typical of practical deployment. However, the method incurs 2K+1 forward passes (21 with K=10), roughly 2–3× the cost of leading baselines, and its reliance on the model's own object recognition means severe misidentification can propagate; whether the threshold (0.48) generalizes across domains and whether the approach extends to video or multi-image inputs remain open questions.
