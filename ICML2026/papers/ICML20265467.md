## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) are hindered in practical deployment by high computational costs, and while ternary weight quantization (W1.58) is well-established, activation quantization remains the principal bottleneck for ultra-low-bit settings. Prior work has only achieved 4-bit activations for DiTs, and the complex, layer-varying activation distributions make straightforward quantization fail at lower precisions. This paper identifies three specific gaps: the absence of systematic QAT exploration at ultra-low activation bit-widths, the lack of a unified quantizer for diverse DiT activation distributions, and the existence of specific layers that act as bit-width bottlenecks under uniform quantization.

## Input / Output

The quantized DiT-XL/2 model accepts a class label (ImageNet-1K, 256×256) or a text prompt (FLUX-Schnell, MJHQ) and produces a 256×256 RGB image via DDPM sampling (250 steps, cfg=1.5 or 4.0). All weights are fixed at ternary precision (W1.58) while activations are quantized to an average of 2–4 bits per layer; attention scores and adaLN layers are held at 8-bit and 4-bit respectively due to their high sensitivity. The method also extends to fast-generation settings (α-Flow at NFE=1/2, FLUX-Schnell at 4 steps) where the quantized model is evaluated per function evaluation.

## Method

RobuQ combines three key components. First, the RobustQuantizer applies a normalized Hadamard transform to activations, which the paper proves (via Berry-Esseen CLT and off-diagonal covariance decay) converts arbitrary per-token distributions into approximately i.i.d. Gaussians with shared variance σ_t²; a precomputed Lloyd-Max optimal quantizer then operates on the normalized Gaussian, making the scheme distribution-agnostic and supporting both uniform and non-uniform variants. Second, an SVD-initialized low-rank FP branch (rank 16) compensates for quantization error in the ternary weight matrix, with the residual W_res = WH − AB being ternarized. Third, AMPN is the first activation-only mixed-precision pipeline for DiTs: it profiles per-layer sensitivity via short QAT runs (1,000 steps), then solves a discretized dynamic-programming allocation that assigns each layer an activation bit-width from {1,2,3,4} under a target average budget, enabling stable W1.58A2 generation where uniform quantization collapses.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256 (cfg=4.0, 50 steps), RobuQ at W1.58A2 achieves FID 11.06, IS 273.58, and Precision 0.8751, surpassing BitNetv2 at the higher W1.58A4 precision (FID 11.69, IS 196.78) and BinaryDM at W1.58A4 (FID 17.08, IS 115.52). At W1.58A4, RobuQ reaches FID 12.64 and IS 349.22, closely matching the FP baseline (FID 19.11, IS 478.35) while delivering a 17.3× theoretical FLOPs reduction. On FFHQ (unconditional), RobuQ at W1.58A2 achieves FID 38.13 versus 59.50 for the baseline at the same precision. Under 8-GPU QAT scaling (FID-50k, cfg=1.5), RobuQ reaches FID 4.55 at W1.58A4 and 8.47 at W1.58A2, compared to FP FID 3.71.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobuQ decisively outperforms both BinaryDM and BitNetv2 across all tested bit-widths. At W1.58A4 (cfg=4.0), RobuQ achieves FID 12.64 and IS 349.22, improving over BitNetv2 (FID 11.69, IS 196.78) in IS by 77% and over BinaryDM (FID 17.08, IS 115.52) in both FID and IS. The most striking result is at W1.58A2, where RobuQ (FID 11.06) actually beats BitNetv2 at the higher W1.58A4 precision (FID 11.69), demonstrating that the RobustQuantizer and AMPN pipeline effectively compensate for the reduced activation precision. In the W4A4 PTQ setting (Table 4), RobustQuantizer achieves FID 16.13 with per-channel granularity and no calibration data, matching SVDQuant's group-64 result (FID 16.35) while dramatically outperforming QueST (FID 84.20) and SVDQuant at channel granularity (FID 282.19). On FFHQ unconditional generation, RobuQ at W1.58A2 (FID 38.13) improves over the baseline at the same precision (FID 59.50) and over BitNetv2 at W1.58A4 (FID 66.55).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends BitNetv2's W1.58A4 ternary-weight framework (originally designed for LLMs) to Diffusion Transformers, incorporating SVDQuant's low-rank compensation branch and BiMaCoSR's matrix compression ideas into a unified QAT pipeline. It departs from prior DiT quantization work by identifying activation quantization—rather than weight quantization—as the principal bottleneck, and by providing a theoretical argument (via the Generalized CLT) that the Hadamard transform regularizes per-token activation distributions into approximately Gaussian form, enabling a distribution-agnostic quantizer. The activation-only mixed-precision network (AMPN) is novel in that prior mixed-precision methods for diffusion models allocate bits across both weights and activations, whereas here weights are fixed at ternary and only activation bit-widths are optimized via dynamic programming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RobuQ establishes the first stable image generation at W1.58A2 (2-bit average activations) on ImageNet-1K, a regime where prior baselines completely collapse (FID 120 vs. 30), and achieves 15× checkpoint compression and 3.5× measured speedup in deployment. The 'worst-case quantizer' philosophy—designing for the Gaussian distribution because it maximizes entropy and thus quantization difficulty—offers a clean, hardware-friendly principle that could generalize beyond DiTs. Key open questions include whether dedicated mixed-bit (A2/A3) kernels can close the gap between weighted-FLOPs reduction and wall-clock speed, whether the approach scales to larger backbones (e.g., FLUX-scale models) without the current 7.7% Hadamard overhead becoming negligible, and whether the 8-GPU FID-50k results (4.55 at A4, 8.47 at A2) can approach FP quality (3.71) with further training.
