## Problem

Existing multimodal federated learning methods treat permanently missing modalities as statistical deficiencies to be imputed or compensated, conflating structural voids with distributional shifts. When clients have fixed, heterogeneous modality configurations (e.g., one client has audio+video, another has image+text), standard FL, PFL, and MMFL approaches fail because they assume a shared input space or enforce alignment on dimensionally incompatible parameter spaces, producing irreducible optimality gaps and negative transfer. The paper asks how clients on structurally distinct observation domains can collaborate without collapsing into an identity-erasing consensus.

## Input / Output

Each client observes a fixed subset of modalities (audio, video, RGB images, or text) determined by its hardware/sensor configuration, with the absent modalities being permanently unavailable (static absence, not transient dropout). Clients use heterogeneous backbones (MobileNetV2, MobileBERT, or ViLT) tailored to their modality set, and the method extracts a structural representation z_k from local parameters for cross-client coupling. The output is a personalized class prediction per client, evaluated on a private local test set to measure alignment with each client's own distribution rather than a global benchmark.

## Method

GeoEvo reconceptualizes PMFL as an Identity-Aware Potential Game where each client optimizes on its own Riemannian submanifold (constrained by its modality configuration) while coupled through a shared exact potential that counts each pairwise structural alignment once. The server compresses O(K²) pairwise couplings into O(K) per-client Fréchet prototype messages computed on a common structure space, preserving identity-induced geometry rather than enforcing Euclidean averaging. On each client, a Fisher-preconditioned retracted descent step adapts to the block-sparse curvature induced by the client's active modalities, while a manifold-lifted evolutionary search—combining Natural Evolution Strategies for stochastic basin escape and Particle Swarm Optimization anchored to the broadcast prototype—explores beyond local basins in the lower-dimensional structure space. A monotone selection rule over all candidates (current, descent, NES, PSO) guarantees per-step surrogate descent and yields an O(1/√T) stationarity rate under bounded inter-round drift.

## Results

On CREMA-D (audiovisual emotion recognition), GeoEvo achieves 56.06% accuracy at 30% modality missing rate, 57.56% at 50%, and 54.67% at 70%, outperforming all 13 baselines across every missing-rate regime. Notably, at the 70% missing rate GeoEvo (54.67%) numerically surpasses the centralized Oracle (54.10%), indicating that identity-aware geometry-aware adaptation can exceed full-modality consensus under severe structural incompleteness. The paper also reports consistent top-tier performance on CrisisMMD (image-text) and UPMC Food-101 (image-text, large-scale), where GeoEvo degrades far more gracefully than FL, PFL, and MMFL baselines as missing rates increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoEvo outperforms all 13 baselines across every missing-rate setting on CREMA-D, with the margin widening as modality incompleteness increases. At the 70% missing rate, GeoEvo (54.67%) leads the strongest baseline PEPSY (48.90%) by 5.77 pp and even exceeds the centralized Oracle (54.10%) by 0.57 pp. Among FL baselines, SCAFFOLD (47.14%) and FedMoSWA (48.56%) trail GeoEvo by 7.5 and 6.1 pp respectively at 70%. PFL methods (Ditto 43.14%, pFedMoAP 47.50%, HB-PFL 46.95%) suffer the largest degradation under high missing rates, consistent with the paper's argument that Euclidean proximal coupling injects unidentifiable directions. Compensation-oriented MMFL methods (FedMultimodal 44.82%, M³Fed 47.42%, FedMEMA 49.35%) also fall well short, with FedMultimodal showing the steepest drop from 30% to 70% (−4.06 pp vs. GeoEvo's −1.39 pp). The vanilla PMFL baseline (47.54% at 70%) confirms that the identity-aware game formulation alone, without the evolutionary search and Fréchet prototype mechanism, is insufficient to close the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the compensation-centric paradigm in multimodal federated learning (MMFL), where missing modalities are treated as deficiencies to be repaired via imputation or robust training (Feng et al., 2023; Nguyen et al., 2026; Phung et al., 2025). Instead, it reframes modality incompleteness as an intrinsic structural identity that constrains each client to a distinct Riemannian submanifold, drawing on potential game theory (Monderer & Shapley, 1996) to formalize cross-client collaboration as descent on a shared exact potential. The optimization machinery combines Riemannian retraction-based descent (Absil et al., 2008) with Fisher-preconditioned natural gradients, while the nonlocal exploration component repurposes Natural Evolution Strategies (Wierstra et al., 2014) and Particle Swarm Optimization (Kennedy & Eberhart, 1995) in a manifold-lifted setting. The Fréchet prototype compression reduces the O(K²) pairwise coupling to O(K) per-round messages, a communication-efficiency gain not present in prior PFL or MMFL methods that rely on Euclidean consensus or per-pair synchronization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural-identity reframing offers a principled alternative to the dominant imputation/robustness paradigm for handling permanent modality absence, with the potential to guide future work on heterogeneous federated systems where sensor configurations are fixed by hardware or policy. The O(1/√T) surrogate-stationarity guarantee under bounded inter-round drift and the O(K) communication complexity provide theoretical grounding that prior MMFL methods largely lack. However, the empirical evaluation is confined to three relatively small benchmarks (CREMA-D, CrisisMMD, UPMC Food-101), and the method's complexity—requiring Fisher matrix computation, structural extraction maps, lift operators, and three candidate proposals per local step—raises practical scalability questions. Open questions include whether the surrogate approximation error δ_k^t remains controlled at larger K, whether the framework extends to asynchronous or streaming client populations, and whether the evolutionary search components provide meaningful gains over simpler stochastic exploration at scale.
