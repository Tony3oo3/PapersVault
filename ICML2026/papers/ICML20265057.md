## Problem

Flow-matching text-to-image models follow deterministic ODE trajectories that collapse to a few high-probability modes, producing an 'illusion of variety' where different seeds yield nearly identical outputs. Existing diversity-enhancement methods either require costly retraining or degrade image fidelity, leaving a gap for a training-free, inference-time approach that boosts semantic diversity without sacrificing quality or prompt alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed text prompt and a set of m random noise seeds as input, operating on a frozen Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium backbone at 512×512 resolution with 30 Euler sampling steps. It produces a set of m images that are more semantically diverse than the baseline while maintaining comparable FID, CLIP score, and BRISQUE quality. The key constraint is that all m trajectories share the same conditioning and are guided jointly at each step, making this a set-level rather than per-sample intervention.

## Method

OSCAR transforms the standard flow-matching ODE into a controlled SDE by adding two components, both projected orthogonal to the base velocity field: (1) a deterministic control signal that maximizes the log-determinant volume of predicted endpoint features in a semantic space (e.g., CLIP), computed via a finite-difference endpoint extrapolation that requires no extra NFE, and (2) a time-scheduled stochastic perturbation that injects quality-preserving uncertainty. The gradient of the volume objective is pulled back to latent space via two reverse-mode VJPs, and a redundancy-aware reweighting mechanism using leverage scores from the feature Gram matrix adaptively allocates more guidance to underrepresented samples. The orthogonal projection (controlled by λ≈0.95) ensures the diversity signal acts only as a 'lateral push' without opposing the model's forward quality-seeking dynamics, and theoretical analysis shows this design monotonically increases a volume surrogate while preserving the marginal distribution along the alignment coordinate.

## Results

On the COCO class-conditional benchmark (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium backbone, 30 NFE), OSCAR achieves the best diversity-quality trade-off across all CFG scales. At CFG=5.0, it attains a Vendi Score Inception of 4.34 (vs. 3.86 for the base FM-SD3.5 and 4.27 for the closest competitor APG) while simultaneously achieving the lowest BRISQUE (21.72 vs. 23.40 base) and best FID (164.75 vs. 165.51 base). On the compositional T2I-CompBench prompts, OSCAR extends its lead with Vendi Score Inception of 3.96 and KID of 25.15, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin on both diversity and distributional fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSCAR dominates all five baselines on the primary diversity metric (Vendi Score Inception) at every CFG scale, with the largest gap over DPP (4.34 vs. 3.58 at CFG=5.0) and a clear margin over the nearest competitor APG (4.34 vs. 4.27). On perceptual quality (BRISQUE), OSCAR achieves 21.72 at CFG=5.0, substantially outperforming PG (40.34) and APG (27.97), which both suffer visible artifact degradation when pushing diversity. Against the base FM-SD3.5 model, OSCAR improves Vendi Score Inception by +0.48 while simultaneously reducing BRISQUE by 1.68 points and FID by 0.76, confirming that the diversity gain comes without any quality sacrifice. CADS is competitive on 1-MS-SSIM (83.82 vs. 83.91) but trails OSCAR on both Vendi Score Inception (3.88 vs. 4.34) and FID (166.13 vs. 164.75). On the harder compositional T2I-CompBench prompts, OSCAR extends its advantage with a KID of 25.15 (vs. 28.50–30.34 for baselines) and Vendi Score Inception of 3.96 (vs. 3.34–3.45), indicating the orthogonal control principle generalizes beyond simple single-object prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSCAR builds directly on DiverseFlow's idea of operating a diversity objective in a semantic feature space over predicted trajectory endpoints, but replaces its O(K³) DPP-inspired kernel with an O(K²) log-determinant volume energy and, more critically, introduces a unifying geometric constraint: both the deterministic control signal and the stochastic perturbation are projected orthogonal to the base flow velocity. This departs from Particle Guidance (which applies repulsion directly in latent space, conflicting with the quality-generating flow) and from CADS (which anneals the conditioning signal on a schedule). The orthogonal projection idea is related in form to APG's decomposition of classifier-free guidance, but OSCAR repurposes it as a fidelity safeguard for a diversity-inducing control rather than for reducing oversaturation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, training-free mechanism that resolves the long-standing diversity–quality trade-off in flow-matching inference, with theoretical guarantees (monotonic volume increase, marginal distribution preservation) that explain *why* quality is preserved. The geometric principle—diversity signals must be lateral, not opposing, the quality-seeking flow—is potentially transferable to other controlled sampling settings and could serve as a structured exploration prior for RL post-training of flow models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, how it interacts with preference-aligned fine-tuning that further narrows the mode space, and whether the feature-space volume objective remains effective when the semantic encoder itself is biased toward common modes.
