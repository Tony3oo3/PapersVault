## Problem

Existing RL fine-tuning methods for diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) are direct adaptations of autoregressive GRPO-style algorithms that are backward-only, require expensive per-token log-probability estimation, and over-focus on reward mode maximization, leading to mode collapse and reward hacking. No principled RL framework has been developed that exploits dLLMs' unique forward noising process and random-order generation to match the full reward-tilted optimal distribution rather than just its dominant mode.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (reasoning query) and produces a text response (reasoning answer) via a masked diffusion LLM using random-order autoregressive (ROAR) sampling. During training, rollouts are generated at a fixed sequence length of 256 and stored in a replay buffer; the WDCE loss then operates on these clean samples by randomly masking positions and performing forward-only denoising passes. Evaluation is conducted zero-shot at generation lengths of 128, 256, and 512 tokens across four reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, Countdown, Sudoku).

## Method

DMPO reframes dLLM RL fine-tuning as a distribution-matching problem: instead of maximizing expected reward (reverse KL, mode-seeking), it minimizes forward KL (cross-entropy) between the policy's sequence distribution and the reward-tilted optimal distribution p*(o|q) ∝ p_ref(o|q)·exp(r/α). The key implementation is the Weighted Denoising Cross-Entropy (WDCE) loss, which uses importance sampling from a stop-gradient reference policy p_v to approximate the intractable expectation under p*, combined with the standard denoising cross-entropy surrogate for the sequence log-probability. This yields a forward-only, off-policy objective that discards rollout trajectories after sampling and trains via cheap random masking, enabling replay-buffer reuse and compatibility with fast dLLM inference. To address the failure of all-positive importance weights under small batch sizes, DMPO introduces weight baseline subtraction (group, individual, or model-based) that injects negative gradients for low-reward samples, and additionally proposes a Weighted Direct Discriminative Optimization (WDDO) variant with built-in negative gradients via a GAN-like formulation.

## Results

On LLaDA-Instruct (8B) at generation length 256, DMPO-LLaDA achieves 82.41% on GSM8K, 38.20% on MATH500, 80.86% on Countdown, and 63.80% on Sudoku, consistently outperforming all RL baselines. The most dramatic gains appear on planning tasks: +39.63 pp over the strongest non-DMPO RL baseline (cGRPO) on Sudoku and +13.67 pp on Countdown. On Dream-Instruct (7B), DMPO-Dream achieves 84.03% GSM8K, 47.40% MATH500, 54.43% Countdown, and 41.04% Sudoku, surpassing d1-Dream by up to +24.97 pp on Countdown. Overall, DMPO delivers up to 67.97 pp improvement over the base model (Countdown, length 512) without any supervised fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMPO-LLaDA outperforms all six LLaDA-based baselines across all four benchmarks at generation length 256. Against the strongest RL baseline GDPO-LLaDA, DMPO gains +13.67 pp on Countdown (80.86 vs. 67.19) and +39.63 pp on Sudoku (63.80 vs. 24.17), while matching or slightly exceeding it on GSM8K (+1.21 pp) and MATH500 (+0.20 pp). Compared to cGRPO-LLaDA, which is competitive on Countdown (42.58) and Sudoku (24.17), DMPO achieves substantially larger margins of +38.28 pp and +39.63 pp respectively. Against d1-LLaDA (the SFT + diffu-GRPO pipeline), DMPO leads by +0.78 pp on GSM8K, +1.60 pp on MATH500, +54.30 pp on Countdown, and +52.76 pp on Sudoku. On the Dream backbone, DMPO-Dream beats d1-Dream on every task, with the largest gap on Countdown (+24.97 pp: 54.43 vs. 29.46) and Sudoku (+19.26 pp: 41.04 vs. 21.78). Notably, DMPO achieves these gains without any SFT, whereas d1 incorporates SFT as part of its pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMPO builds directly on the stochastic optimal control (SOC) framework for masked diffusion models established by MDNS (Zhu et al., 2025e) and DRAKES (Wang et al., 2025a), adapting their weighted denoising cross-entropy loss and CTMC path-measure Radon-Nikodym derivative machinery to the dLLM policy-learning setting. It departs fundamentally from the dominant GRPO-style lineage (d1, cGRPO, wd1, GDPO) by replacing the backward-only, on-policy, per-token log-likelihood estimation with a forward-only, off-policy, sequence-level importance-sampled objective that exploits the forward noising process unique to diffusion LLMs. The shift from reverse-KL (mode-seeking) reward maximization to forward-KL (mass-covering) distribution matching is the core conceptual departure, motivated by the observation that dLLMs' random-order generation naturally supports diverse response exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMPO provides the first principled RL framework designed natively for dLLMs rather than adapted from AR-LLMs, and its off-policy, forward-only nature yields substantial practical efficiency gains (up to 8× per rollout, 31% of the training budget of d1) that compound with fast dLLM inference techniques. The finding that highly stale rollouts (24× reuse) remain stable due to the p*/p_old importance-weight form (independent of the current policy) challenges the prevailing assumption that on-policy methods are necessary for LLM RL. However, evaluation is limited to two model families (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) and four elementary reasoning benchmarks, leaving open whether the distribution-matching advantage persists on harder tasks, larger models, or non-reasoning domains. The paper also leaves the design of more effective weight-baseline techniques and the extension to other generative model families as open directions.
