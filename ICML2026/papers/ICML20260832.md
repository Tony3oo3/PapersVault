## Problem

Locally constrained decoding (LCD) enforces structural constraints on LLM outputs by masking invalid next tokens, but this myopic strategy distorts the model's conditional distribution and does not guarantee constraint satisfaction within a fixed token budget. While sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) sampling can correct for proposal bias, its convergence rate is bottlenecked by the quality of the proposal and potential distributions, and prior work relies on the weak binary LCD signal. The paper addresses the challenge of constructing tractable, informative proposals and potentials for SMC sampling from the constrained LLM distribution p_lm(x|C).

## Input / Output

The method takes a user prompt, a constraint encoded as a finite automaton (NFA or DFA) built from a JSON schema, SQL grammar, or keyword specification, and a base autoregressive LLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or GPT2-Large). It produces a token sequence of fixed budget n that provably satisfies the constraint, sampled from the conditional distribution p_lm(x_{1:n}|C). The constraint automaton is tensorized into binary matrices of size S×E, E×S, and E×V (states, edges, vocabulary), and an optional distilled HMM with H hidden states provides probabilistic guidance; all operations are GPU-native tensor computations.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary decoding distributions used as SMC proposals and potentials. First, globally constrained decoding (GCD) tensorizes finite automata into three binary matrices (T_out, T_in, B_f) and runs forward/backward reachability passes to mask tokens that cannot be extended to a valid completion within the remaining token budget, guaranteeing constraint satisfaction at termination. Second, probabilistic GCD (P-GCD) distills a hidden Markov model to approximate the LM distribution, then exploits the fact that the tensorized FA induces an HMM with the same circuit structure as the LM HMM; circuit multiplication via Kronecker products yields a product HMM whose parameters live in the Cartesian product of the two hidden state spaces, capturing both hard constraint logic and probabilistic information about future valid completions. Tensor operations (Kronecker-vec identity) reduce per-step transition cost from O((HE)²) to O(H²E + HE²), and backward messages are precomputed to enable on-the-fly normalization of next-token probabilities. P-GCD serves as both a proposal (interpolating between LM and HMM-constrained next-token distributions via a weight w) and a potential (weighting particles by LM prefix probability times HMM look-ahead constraint-satisfaction estimate), and experiments on xLAM, CommonGen, and Spider show faster SMC convergence with significantly fewer particles compared to LCD-based baselines.

## Results

On Spider (text-to-SQL), P-GCD achieves 69.1% execution accuracy at k=8 particles, outperforming LCD (67.2%) and GCD (69.0%). On xLAM (function calling, JSON format), P-GCD with H=16384 reaches 71.6% accuracy at k=16 particles versus 69.7% for LCD, and GCD/P-GCD guarantee 100% constraint satisfaction at all particle budgets compared to LCD's 95.26% at k=4. In terms of efficiency, GCD incurs only a 1.10× latency overhead over unconstrained generation (1.86 ms/token), while P-GCD ranges from 3.11× (H=1024) to 10.2× (H=16384) depending on HMM size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LCD + SMC (Loula et al., 2025), which uses locally constrained decoding as the proposal within the same SMC framework. P-GCD consistently outperforms LCD across all three benchmarks: on Spider, P-GCD gains +1.9 points at k=8 (69.1% vs. 67.2%); on xLAM JSON, P-GCD (H=16384) gains +1.9 points at k=16 (71.6% vs. 69.7%); and on CommonGen, P-GCD as both proposal and potential yields substantially higher BLEU-4 at matched particle counts. GCD also beats LCD on Spider (+1.8 at k=8) and xLAM, and critically guarantees 100% constraint satisfaction at every particle budget, whereas LCD drops to 95.26% (JSON, k=4) and 98.12% (Python-like, k=4). The advantage of P-GCD over GCD is most pronounced in the low-particle regime (k=1–4), where the HMM's probabilistic look-ahead provides the greatest correction; the gap narrows as k increases. The trade-off is computational: GCD adds only 1.10× latency overhead, while P-GCD costs 3.11×–10.2× depending on HMM hidden size, though HMM distillation is a one-time offline procedure (~2 GPU hours for data + 0.2–3 GPU hours for training).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMC-based constrained generation framework of Loula et al. (2025) and Lipkin et al. (2025), which used LCD as a weak proposal, by replacing it with globally constrained decoding (GCD) and probabilistic GCD (P-GCD) proposals/potentials. The probabilistic component builds on GeLaTo (Zhang et al., 2023) and Ctrl-G (Zhang et al., 2024), which distilled HMMs to approximate LM distributions for keyword and DFA constraints respectively, but departs from Ctrl-G by supporting NFAs via tensorized automata and circuit multiplication (Loconte et al., 2025) rather than operating on DFAs alone. The tensorized representation of finite automata for GPU-native inference is, to the authors' knowledge, novel; prior constrained decoding frameworks (Outlines, XGrammar) are optimized for CPU execution and perform only local masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guarantee of constraint satisfaction within a fixed token budget addresses a real failure mode of deployed LCD systems (e.g., unclosed JSON braces), and the NFA support is practically critical since the paper demonstrates DFA construction hitting OOM for real-world SQL grammars at token level. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond regular languages to context-free grammars (which XGrammar handles), how HMM distillation cost scales with model size, and whether the 3–10× latency overhead of P-GCD is acceptable in latency-sensitive production settings. The one-time offline distillation cost (~2 GPU-hours for sampling + 0.2–3 GPU-hours for training) is modest but non-trivial for very large models.
