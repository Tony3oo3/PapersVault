## Problem

Genomic foundation models are evaluated on disjoint benchmarks with incompatible protocols and task-specific reporting, making principled cross-model comparison infeasible. The same model can appear as a breakthrough in one paper and underperforming in another, not because of contradictory evidence but because no shared evaluation framework exists to adjudicate claims of superiority or generality across the rapidly growing ecosystem of 40+ architectures, tokenization schemes, and pretraining corpora.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes frozen weights from 40 genomic foundation models (2M–7B parameters, spanning Transformer encoders/decoders, Mamba, Hyena, CNN-Transformer hybrids, and StripedHyena) and DNA sequences from 100 classification tasks. For each model-task pair, frozen embeddings are extracted and fed into a logistic regression probe evaluated in 1-shot, 10-shot, and full-data regimes; tasks exceeding 10⁵ sequences are subsampled and results are averaged over five fixed random seeds. The output is a complete 40×100 MCC performance matrix from which per-category rankings, scaling correlations, controlled pairwise comparisons, and few-shot degradation profiles are derived.

## Method

GENEB employs a unified linear-probing protocol in which frozen sequence representations from each model serve as features for logistic regression (max_iter=1000), isolating representation quality from task-specific fine-tuning and enabling apples-to-apples comparison across architectures and training regimes. The 100 tasks are aggregated from nine existing benchmarks (NT, NT-rev, GUE, GB, PGB, iPro-WAEL, deep4mc, iDNA-ABF, iDHS-EL) and organized into 13 functional categories spanning regulatory elements, epigenomic modifications, splicing, and evolutionary sequence analysis. To attribute performance differences to individual design choices, the paper constructs controlled comparison pairs matched on architecture, tokenization, and pretraining corpus (within ±2× parameter size), isolating the effect of a single variable at a time. The resulting analysis examines scale-performance Spearman correlations per category, architecture gaps under matched conditions, tokenization interactions, pretraining corpus transfer (human vs. multi-species vs. microbial), and few-shot robustness, revealing that aggregate leaderboards are unstable, scale yields only modest and inconsistent gains, and architectural and pretraining alignment frequently outweigh parameter count.

## Results

GENEB evaluates 40 genomic foundation models on 100 DNA classification tasks across 13 functional categories using a unified linear-probing protocol (logistic regression, MCC metric, 5 seeds). The aggregate scale-performance Spearman correlation is ρ=0.573 (p<0.001), rising to ρ=0.694 after excluding the prokaryotic-only outlier Evo-1-131K, yet 25 instances exist where a model ≥5× smaller outperforms a larger counterpart in macro-MCC. Few-shot performance degrades sharply: mean macro-MCC drops from 0.488 (full-data) to 0.253 (10-shot) to 0.106 (1-shot), with 28 of 100 tasks remaining below 0.35 MCC even at full data. Per-category scaling is significant in 11/13 categories (ρ ranging 0.346–0.587), but two categories (species classification, chromatin accessibility) show no significant scaling, and the hardest tasks (4mC methylation, plant lncRNA) remain unsolved regardless of model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under controlled matched conditions (same pretraining corpus, tokenization, and size tier), Transformer models consistently outperform the evaluated state-space model: GENOMEOCEAN-500M (Transformer-decoder) exceeds ECCDNAMAMBA (Mamba) by +0.131 macro-MCC (0.550 vs. 0.419), and OMNI-DNA-1B shows a comparable +0.149 gap; within Transformers, encoders exceed decoders across all six matched pairs (strongest: GENA-LM-LARGE-T2T over OMNINA-220M by +0.127). The notable exception is chromatin accessibility, where ECCDNAMAMBA reverses the gap and beats GENOMEOCEAN-500M by +0.124 (0.599 vs. 0.475). At the compact end, MUTBERT (86M, Transformer-encoder, single-nucleotide) exceeds ECCDNAMAMBA (537M, Mamba) by +0.110 macro-MCC despite a 6.2× size disadvantage, and is the top sub-100M model in 8 of 13 categories. On pretraining corpus effects, multi-species pretraining yields a modest +0.018 macro-MCC over human-only on average, but a consistent +0.076 on chromatin accessibility (6/6 pairs) and +0.065 on splice sites, while virus/phage tasks slightly favor human-only (Δ=−0.025); microbial-focused pretraining (DNABERT-S) loses +0.084 to eukaryotic multi-species models under matched architecture. Few-shot evaluation reranks the best model in 8 of 13 categories—for example, MUTBERT becomes the 10-shot leader on coding/non-coding (0.748 vs. 0.694 for GENERATOR-EUKARYOTE-3B) and histone modifications (0.300), while SPACE overtakes OMNI-DNA-1B on mouse enhancers (0.379 vs. 0.253) and LUCAONE leads splice sites (0.298 vs. 0.242 for NT-2.5B-MS).

## Relation to Prior Work

GENEB does not build on a specific prior method but addresses a structural gap in genomic ML evaluation: existing benchmarks (NT tasks, GUE/GUE+, Genomic Benchmarks, BEND, DNALongBench) evaluate disjoint model subsets under incompatible protocols, and comparative studies (Wang et al. 2025b, OmniGenBench) cover only ~10 models. Conceptually, it repurposes the MTEB paradigm from NLP—unified probing-based evaluation across a large model set and diverse task categories—into the genomic domain, extending it with controlled comparisons that isolate architecture, tokenization, and pretraining-corpus effects. The paper positions itself as a diagnostic reference framework rather than a single-task leaderboard, explicitly filling the void left by fragmented, paper-specific evaluation practices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GENEB provides the first unified 40-model × 100-task performance matrix for genomic foundation models, enabling category-aware model selection that aggregate leaderboards obscure. Key open questions remain: whether linear-probing rankings survive full task-specific fine-tuning, how long-range regulatory tasks (>10 kb) would reshape the landscape for models like HyenaDNA and Caduceus, and how to extend coverage to prokaryotic and viral genomics where the current eukaryotic skew creates systematic bias. The finding that scale provides only modest, inconsistent gains while architecture and pretraining alignment frequently dominate has direct implications for resource allocation in future model development.
