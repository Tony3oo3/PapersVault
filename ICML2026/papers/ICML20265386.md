## Problem

Single-policy RL agents for LLM-based tasks suffer from simplicity bias: frequent, easy sub-tasks dominate gradient updates and monopolize shared parameters, starving complex multi-step behaviors of learning signal. While Mixture-of-Experts could in principle allocate dedicated capacity per behavior, standard token-level routing fragments temporally coherent action sequences across experts, destroying the phase-level specialization that sequential decision-making requires. No prior work routes experts at the behavioral-phase granularity for agentic RL.

## Input / Output

At each environment step the router consumes three signals: the current observation o_t and task goal g (fused via cross-attention over frozen base-model hidden states) and a 5-step action-observation history encoded by a 3-layer LSTM (hidden dim 256). The router outputs a discrete expert index k* ∈ {1,…,K} (K=4); the selected LoRA expert then autoregressively generates the next action a_t from the shared frozen backbone. The method is algorithm-agnostic: the same router-expert interface plugs into GiGPO, PPO, GRPO, or RLOO by swapping only the advantage estimator and policy objective.

## Method

PA-MoE replaces the monolithic policy with K LoRA adapters (rank 32 on Q/V projections) over a frozen base LLM, selected by a lightweight phase router that integrates goal-conditioned observation encoding and temporal history to predict an expert at the environment-step level. A switching penalty (λ_s=0.05) with a straight-through surrogate enforces temporal consistency so contiguous trajectory segments are handled by the same expert, reducing switches from ~45 (token-level) to ~8 per episode while preserving genuine phase transitions. Training jointly optimizes the router via REINFORCE and the selected expert via the base algorithm's clipped surrogate, augmented by a diversity loss (KL margin between expert policies) and a balance loss (uniform activation frequency). Temperature annealing (τ: 2.0→0.5 over 3000 steps) transitions the router from exploratory to decisive assignments. The key architectural insight is that phase-level granularity—neither token-level (too fine, fragments coherent actions) nor trajectory-level (too coarse, prevents within-episode adaptation)—is the correct routing scale for sequential decision-making, and that learned semantic boundaries outperform frequency-matched random or fixed-interval switching by 1.6–3.2 points.

## Results

On ALFWorld, PA-MoE+GiGPO with Qwen2.5-1.5B achieves 93.8% overall success rate, improving over the GiGPO baseline (86.1%) by +7.7 percentage points, with the largest gains on manipulation-heavy tasks (Heat: 100% vs. 91.3%, Cool: 89.5% vs. 71.7%). On WebShop, PA-MoE+GiGPO reaches 82.3% success and 91.0 score, outperforming GiGPO (67.4%, 83.5) by +14.9 points. Notably, the 1.5B PA-MoE model outperforms the 7B GiGPO baseline (93.8% vs. 90.2%), and with the 7B backbone PA-MoE+GiGPO reaches 95.3% with 100% success on five of six ALFWorld task categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PA-MoE consistently improves every RL algorithm tested: GiGPO gains +7.7% on ALFWorld and +14.9% on WebShop; RLOO gains +4.5% (69.7→74.2); GRPO gains +1.4% overall but see a dramatic +33.8-point jump on the hardest subtask (look-at-object: 53.7→87.5); PPO gains +1.8%. Against gradient-surgery baselines (PCGrad +1.2%, GradNorm +0.7%, CAGrad +1.8%), PA-MoE's +7.7% improvement is 4–11× larger, confirming that persistent parameter isolation outperforms per-step gradient corrections. On parameter efficiency, the 1.5B PA-MoE model (93.8%) surpasses the 7B GiGPO baseline (90.2%) by +3.6 points, and the 7B PA-MoE+GiGPO (95.3%) matches GEPO (94.9%) with substantially lower variance (±0.8 vs. ±3.8). The largest per-task gains concentrate on low-frequency manipulation tasks (Heat +8.7%, Cool +17.8%), directly reflecting the capacity rebalancing that addresses simplicity bias, while high-frequency navigation tasks (Pick) show only modest gains (+1.1–2.7%) since baselines already allocate sufficient capacity there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts architecture (Shazeer et al., 2017) for sequential decision-making by shifting routing granularity from the token level (as in Switch Transformer, Mixtral) to the environment-step level with temporal consistency constraints. It departs from hierarchical RL (options, Option-Critic) by not modifying the action space or introducing temporal abstractions—instead applying temporal structure solely to parameter routing. Against gradient-surgery methods (PCGrad, CAGrad, GradNorm) that resolve conflicts at each update step, PA-MoE provides persistent architectural parameter isolation across training. The method is algorithm-agnostic, integrating with any policy-gradient method (GiGPO, GRPO, PPO, RLOO) while using LoRA adapters for parameter-efficient expert specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that architectural capacity allocation, not raw parameter count, is the bottleneck for heterogeneous agentic tasks: a 1.5B PA-MoE model outperforms a 7B single-policy baseline, and the capacity-matched ablation (Appendix K) shows routing contributes +4.0 points versus +1.5 from capacity scaling alone. The emergent expert specialization (Explore/Manipulate/Navigate/Recover) without explicit phase supervision, validated by 87% step-level overlap with human annotations, suggests phase-aware routing captures genuine behavioral structure. Open questions include extension to continuous control, cross-domain phase transfer, and whether the fragmentation phenomenon identified here also limits standard token-level MoE in language modeling (preliminary evidence in Appendix T). The 0.3% inference overhead makes the approach practically deployable, but K selection still requires task-specific tuning.
