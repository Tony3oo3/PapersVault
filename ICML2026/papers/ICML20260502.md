## Problem

Existing structure-based drug design (SBDD) methods condition molecule generation on rigid, empty binding pockets, implicitly assuming a single static conformation that suppresses intrinsic protein flexibility and fails to capture conformational adaptations upon ligand binding. This paper asks whether low-resolution electron density (ED) derived from the 'filler' (ligand + proximal solvent) can serve as a continuous, physically grounded conditioning representation that naturally encodes ensemble-averaged spatial distributions and conformational variability, enabling generation of ligands compatible with dynamic binding sites.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of 199 pharmacophore-labeled 3D points sampled from a low-resolution electron density map (d_min = 3.5 Å) of the filler region (ligand + solvent within 4.5 Å of the ligand), with each point annotated by its nearest-atom pharmacophore type (HBD, HBA, HBD/HBA, Other) and sorted by spatial coordinates. Two ED sources are supported: calculated ED (CalED) via truncated inverse FFT from atomic coordinates for pre-training, and experimental ED (ExpED) from cryo-EM/X-ray reconstructions for fine-tuning and inference. The output is a full 3D molecule with conformation, autoregressively generated as a sequence of FSMILES tokens, discretized 3D coordinates (σ = 0.1 Å grid), and relative geometric features (bond length, bond angle, dihedral angle), with coordinates recovered during inference by sampling from a constrained spherical surface defined by the predicted local geometry.

## Method

EDMolGPT is a decoder-only autoregressive Transformer (24 layers, 1024 hidden dim, 16 heads, GPT-2 architecture) that concatenates the ED point cloud tokens and the molecule token sequence, predicting each molecular token (FSMILES symbol, discretized x/y/z coordinate, bond length, angle, dihedral) via linear classification heads with cross-entropy loss. The key architectural insight is conditioning on filler-derived ED point clouds rather than rigid pocket geometry, with coordinate embeddings shared between point cloud and molecule tokens since they inhabit the same spatial space. The paper introduces an improved FSMILES fragmentation that prevents over-splitting of small ring-linking fragments, and a two-stage training strategy: pre-training on ~2M curated molecules with CalED, then fine-tuning on PDBbind ExpED to adapt to experimental noise and flexibility. During inference, instead of sampling absolute coordinates from the full grid, the model uses predicted relative geometric features to constrain the next atom position to a narrow spherical patch (with tolerance intervals δ_l ≈ 0.1 Å, δ_θ ≈ 10°, δ_φ ≈ 10°), ensuring geometric consistency while reducing the search space.

## Results

On the DUD-E benchmark (101 targets, CalED setting), EDMolGPT achieves the highest Bioactive Molecule Recovery rate of 41% (ECFP4_TS > 0.5), the best Min-in-place Glide score of −6.92, and the highest Min.<Re. ratio of 37%, indicating that its generated conformations are already near-optimal binding poses. Conformational stability (median strain energy 69 kcal/mol) is competitive with Lingo3DMol (40) and Pocket2Mol (126) without any post-hoc force-field refinement. Drug-likeness metrics (QED 0.57, SAS 3.79, MW 385) are balanced and closer to the Reference ligands (MW 438) than ED2Mol (MW 234) or ECloudGen (MW 213).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDMolGPT outperforms all six baselines on the headline Bioactive Molecule Recovery metric (41% vs. 33% for Lingo3DMol and ECloudGen†, 17% for MolCRAFT, 8% for Pocket2Mol, 3% for TargetDiff and ED2Mol, 6% for ECloudGen), indicating it generates compounds structurally closest to known actives. On binding affinity, EDMolGPT achieves the best Min-in-place Glide score (−6.92) and the highest Min.<Re. ratio (37%), meaning its generated conformations require less re-docking to reach optimal poses compared to all baselines (next best: MolCRAFT at 20.1%). On conformational stability, EDMolGPT (median strain 69 kcal/mol) is competitive with Lingo3DMol (40) and Pocket2Mol (126) without any post-hoc refinement, while ED2Mol's superior strain values (7/24/57) come at the cost of explicit force-field polishing. On drug-likeness, EDMolGPT trades slightly lower QED (0.57 vs. 0.73 for ED2Mol/ECloudGen†) for a much larger molecular weight (385 vs. 234/213), producing more complex, reference-like scaffolds rather than small, trivially drug-like fragments. Overall, EDMolGPT wins on the metrics most relevant to practical drug discovery (recovery, binding pose quality) while remaining competitive on stability and drug-likeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lingo3DMol autoregressive 3D molecule generation framework (Feng et al., 2024) by replacing the rigid pocket geometry conditioning signal with low-resolution electron density (ED) point clouds derived from the filler (ligand + solvent) of holo complexes. It departs from both pocket-based SBDD methods (Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, MolCRAFT) that assume a static binding site, and from prior ED-guided generation (Wang et al. 2022, ED2Mol, ECloudGen) that condition on pocket ED rather than filler ED. The two-stage training strategy—large-scale pre-training on computed ED (CalED) followed by fine-tuning on experimental cryo-EM/X-ray ED (ExpED)—is a novel integration that unifies scalable computational data with experimentally grounded signals, and the decoder-only GPT-2 architecture distinguishes it from the encoder-decoder or diffusion-based designs used by competing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By conditioning on filler ED rather than rigid pocket geometry, EDMolGPT addresses a fundamental limitation of current SBDD: the inability to capture conformational flexibility, which is most pronounced in highly flexible binding regions where pocket ED is often poorly resolved. The demonstration that generated molecules can occupy sterically clashing regions under rigid-pocket assumptions yet retain confirmed bioactivity provides concrete evidence that the ED representation encodes ensemble-averaged dynamics. However, the ExpED evaluation is limited to 92 structures due to the scarcity of experimental density maps, and the modest docking scores under ExpED (-5.4 vs. -6.92 for CalED) suggest the approach's full potential awaits broader experimental data. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to targets without any known binder (requiring MD-derived CalED) and whether the ~1.5 s/molecule generation speed is competitive enough for large-scale virtual screening workflows.
