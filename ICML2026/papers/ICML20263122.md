## Problem

Vision Transformers exhibit systematic failures in spatial reasoning tasks such as mental rotation, but prior explanations attribute these to data scale or optimization artifacts. This paper argues the limitation is intrinsic to the architecture: by formalizing spatial understanding as a Group Homomorphism Problem, it shows that for non-solvable transformation groups (e.g., SO(3)), maintaining structure-preserving embeddings requires solving the Word Problem, which is NC1-complete, while constant-depth ViTs are strictly bounded by TC0. Under the standard TC0 ⊊ NC1 conjecture, this establishes a fundamental complexity barrier that no amount of data or parameter scaling within a fixed-depth architecture can overcome.

## Input / Output

The benchmark renders asymmetric 3D objects (from the Stanford 3D Scanning Repository) as 224×224 grayscale images under fixed lighting, with a fixed set of 6 group generators per complexity level (2D translations, SE(2) rigid motions, or 3D rotations by ±30°). The frozen backbone produces a CLS-token embedding, and six independent linear probes (one per generator) are trained exclusively on atomic transitions (N=1) then recursively composed during testing over sequences of length N∈{2,…,20} extracted from the same generative random walks, ensuring no out-of-distribution confounds. The output is a Ratio Loss (1−R²) normalized against a Global Mean Baseline, where values approaching 1.0 indicate complete regression to variance noise and loss of structural tracking.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a theoretical-empirical framework bridging Group Theory and Circuit Complexity to explain ViT spatial reasoning failures. Theoretically, it formalizes spatial understanding as a Homomorphic Spatial Embedding, proves via reduction that satisfying this embedding requires solving the Word Problem (Lemma 4.3), and combines this NC1 lower bound with the TC0 upper bound of constant-depth ViTs (Lemma 4.2) to establish the Non-Solvable Barrier (Theorem 4.5). Empirically, the LSA benchmark constructs three synthetic datasets spanning the solvability hierarchy (Abelian → Solvable Non-Abelian → Non-Solvable), using recursive linear probing where six per-generator linear maps are trained on N=1 transitions and then recursively applied to predict latent states at increasing compositional depths. Orthogonal Procrustes Analysis (OPA) serves as a deterministic control, confirming that non-solvable generators fundamentally lack valid linear operators in the latent space (negative OPA R²), ruling out optimization artifacts as the cause of recursive collapse.

## Results

On the LSA benchmark, all architectures exhibit a universal complexity hierarchy: Level 3 (Non-Solvable, SO(3)) incurs 5.73×–17.72× higher absolute prediction error than Level 1 (Abelian, Z²) at N=20. The Ratio Loss (1-R²) for Level 3 reaches 0.88 (ViT-Base), 0.75 (DINOv2-Base), and 0.84 (ResNet-50) at N=20, approaching the trivial mean-predictor baseline of 1.0. Scaling model depth within each family (e.g., ViT-Base→Huge, ResNet-50→152) yields negligible improvements (e.g., ViT Level 3 at N=20: 0.88→0.86), confirming the barrier is architectural rather than capacity-driven. OPA verification shows non-solvable 3D rotations yield negative R² (e.g., Rot Y: OPA R² ≈ −0.03), confirming the latent space fundamentally lacks a valid linear operator for these transformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external spatial-reasoning methods; instead, it contrasts three architecture families (ViT, DINOv2, ResNet) at multiple scales to isolate intrinsic architectural limits from objective-induced biases. All three families exhibit the same qualitative degradation pattern on non-solvable tasks, with DINOv2 (self-supervised) achieving the lowest Level 3 Ratio Loss (0.75 at N=20 for Base) compared to ViT (0.88) and ResNet (0.84), suggesting self-supervision provides a modest but insufficient advantage. Within each family, scaling from Base to the largest variant (ViT-Huge, DINOv2-Giant, ResNet-152) reduces Level 3 Ratio Loss by at most 0.03–0.07 at N=20, confirming that additional depth or parameters cannot bridge the TC⁰-to-NC¹ gap. ResNet-152 (152 layers) performs nearly identically to ResNet-50 (50 layers) on Level 3 (0.83 vs. 0.84 at N=20), directly demonstrating that physical layer count does not translate to logical circuit depth. The OPA control further shows that even at the atomic level (N=1), non-solvable rotations fail to admit a valid orthogonal linear operator in any backbone's latent space, ruling out optimization as the cause of recursive collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: circuit complexity bounds for Transformers (Merrill & Sabharwal 2023; Chiang 2025), which established that constant-depth ViTs are confined to TC⁰, and Barrington's Theorem (1986), which places the Word Problem for non-solvable groups at NC¹-completeness. Prior work on spatial reasoning failures in ViTs and VLMs (Stogiannidis et al. 2025; Khemlani et al. 2025; Kong et al. 2025) attributed these deficits to data scale, attention misalignment, or optimization artifacts, but did not invoke architectural expressivity limits. The paper's key departure is formalizing spatial understanding as a Group Homomorphism Problem, thereby translating the empirical observation of spatial failures into a provable complexity-theoretic barrier, and introducing the LSA benchmark as an algebraic-structure-driven evaluation paradigm rather than a task-driven one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a principled, architecture-level explanation for why ViTs systematically fail at compositional spatial reasoning, shifting the blame from data or training to an intrinsic circuit-depth limitation. Practically, it argues against the common strategy of simply scaling depth or parameters to fix spatial reasoning, a claim supported by the near-identical degradation curves across ViT-Base through ViT-Huge and ResNet-50 through ResNet-152. Key open questions include whether the TC⁰ ⊊ NC¹ separation conjecture can be replaced by unconditional lower bounds, whether recurrent or hybrid architectures can resolve the barrier without inheriting RNN-style error compounding, and whether the synthetic LSA protocol generalizes to natural-image spatial reasoning. The reliance on a conditional conjecture and the narrow synthetic evaluation (linear probes on rendered 3D objects) limit the strength of the empirical claim.
