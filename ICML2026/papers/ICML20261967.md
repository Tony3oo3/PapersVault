## Problem

Advancing LLM reasoning requires large quantities of high-difficulty, verifiable training problems, yet existing synthesis paradigms face a fundamental trade-off: rigid structural templates ensure logical validity but constrain problem complexity, while relaxing constraints to increase difficulty produces inconsistent or unsolvable instances. This paper argues that problem synthesis should be reframed as compositional logic engineering rather than monolithic text generation, enabling autonomous exploration of novel reasoning compositions while maintaining logical soundness and precise difficulty calibration.

## Input / Output

The proposer agent consumes a formalized skill library where each atomic skill is a structured tuple encoding reasoning intent, construction method, difficulty effect (1–10 scale), and tool-use hint, along with a target domain specification and curriculum parameters. At each POMDP decision step, the agent observes the active skill subset, full dialogue history including prior tool outputs, and a stage indicator tracking progress through drafting, checking, and refining phases. The output consists of logically valid, difficulty-calibrated problem–answer pairs (10,000–11,000 trajectories) filtered by a three-model verifier ensemble before being fed to downstream solvers trained via GRPO; intermediate agent traces are excluded from solver training to avoid format confounding.

## Method

The framework models problem synthesis as a POMDP where a specialized 4B agent dynamically composes atomic reasoning skills through an iterative Draft-Check-Refine loop, employing internal reflection (τ^think) for logical auditing, sandboxed code execution (τ^exec) for verification, and dynamic skill pruning (τ^edit) to remove misaligned skills mid-process. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) skill acquisition via teacher-guided extraction with rejection sampling, (2) agentic SFT on expert trajectories exhibiting complex tool-use and reflection behaviors, and (3) Multi-Granularity Policy Optimization (MGPO), a novel RL algorithm that fuses trajectory-level terminal rewards with stage-level process rewards through group-standardized advantages, applies an asymmetric hyperbolic secant gate for training stability, and exploits a zero-sum weighting property to cancel the intractable partition function. A curriculum mechanism samples skill categories inversely proportional to their EMA-tracked proficiency, forcing exploration of underperforming reasoning compositions. The layered reward function gates all difficulty bonuses behind a binary validity check, ensuring invalid problems receive zero reward while solvable instances earn difficulty-scaled bonuses based on an external prober's Pass@k estimate.

## Results

On contest mathematics (Mean@64), a 4B solver trained on 10,000 agent-synthesized trajectories achieves 38.3% overall accuracy (AIME24: 53.6, AIME25: 51.2, HMMT: 36.5, AMO-Bench: 11.8), a +4.1-point absolute gain over the zero-shot baseline (34.2%). Scaling to a 30B solver trained on 11,000 mixed trajectories yields 91.6% on AIME 2025 and 81.5% overall across math and coding benchmarks (+5.9 points), establishing state-of-the-art for its scale. Cross-domain transfer to science and general reasoning (Mean@1) yields +5.3 points overall, with +7.3 on SuperGPQA and +6.3 on GPQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Agentic Proposing outperforms all 14 baselines on the 4B contest-math benchmark, achieving 38.3% overall versus the best synthetic baseline PromptCoT 2.0 (36.0%, +1.8) and the best human-curated baseline DeepMath (35.9%, +1.7). Notably, template-driven methods (MetaMath, WizardMath) and frontier-model-generated problems (GPT-5.2-High, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude-4.5-Opus) all *degrade* performance below the zero-shot baseline by 1.2–3.4 points, demonstrating that raw model scale without structured skill composition is insufficient for high-quality synthesis. On the 30B solver (Table 2), Agentic Proposing achieves 91.6% on AIME 2025 and 81.5% overall, surpassing PromptCoT 2.0 (80.8%) by +0.7 points and OpenMathReasoning (74.1%) by +7.4 points. The 4B proposer also generalizes to unseen domains: on CL-Bench it reaches 13.9 (vs. 10.7 zero-shot, approaching Qwen3-Max's 14.2), and on BrowseComp it improves from <30 to 39.7 (vs. DeepResearch's 43.4). In the ablation (Table 5), the specialized 4B Agentic-Proposer outperforms GPT-5.2-High equipped with the same Skill Library and Agentic Workflow by +1.9 points, confirming that domain-specific RL training on agentic trajectories enables a small model to surpass a frontier LLM in problem synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent-based data synthesis line (R-Zero, SocraticZero, DataEnvGym) by reframing problem generation as a POMDP with a latent solvability state, rather than a single-pass or self-play loop. It builds directly on the emergent compositionality principle from Yuan et al. (2025), formalizing atomic reasoning skills as composable modules that a specialized agent orchestrates. The key departure from prior corpus-based methods (MathSmith, DESIGNER, ScaleQuest) is the replacement of fixed structural templates with dynamic skill selection, pruning (τ^edit), and an iterative Draft-Check-Refine loop with tool-assisted verification. The MGPO algorithm introduces multi-granularity (trajectory + stage-level) advantage estimation, going beyond the trajectory-level rewards used in standard GRPO-based proposers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that 10–11k high-precision synthetic trajectories from a 4B proposer can substitute for massive human-curated or frontier-model-generated datasets, reducing production cost to ~2% of comparable pipelines while achieving superior downstream performance. The finding that frontier-scale generators (GPT-5.2, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude-4.5-Opus) consistently degrade solver performance challenges the assumption that generator scale drives synthesis quality, pointing instead to structured compositional guidance and iterative verification as the critical factors. Open questions include extending the skill library beyond math/science to open-ended or multi-modal domains, reducing the three-stage pipeline complexity, and adapting the verifier ensemble to tasks without clear ground-truth answers.
