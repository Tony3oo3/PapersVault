## Problem

Large transformer-based robot navigation policies (e.g., SPOC, PoliFormer, Flare) achieve impressive zero-shot generalization but are purely data-driven and lack any formal guarantee of safe behavior. While Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications can express rich safety constraints (avoidance, geofencing, no-return, temporal ordering), no existing method enforces them at inference time on these pre-trained policies without costly retraining. The core technical challenge is that, unlike LLM constrained decoding where constraints live in token space, robot safety constraints are defined over physical state variables, requiring forward dynamics simulation to check candidate actions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the decoding-layer logits of a pre-trained transformer navigation policy (over a discrete set of low-level action primitives such as forward, strafe, rotate), an STL safety specification over spatial predicates, and an approximate unicycle dynamics model to predict successor states. It produces a modified action probability distribution at each timestep: HCD sets violating actions' logits to −∞ (zero probability), while RCD adds a robustness-scaled offset to all logits, preserving non-zero probability for every action. The final output is a sequence of discrete actions that provably satisfy the STL specification under the assumed dynamics, with runtime overhead of ~10⁻⁵ s per timestep via the STLCG++ library.

## Method

SafeDec is a training-free, policy-agnostic constrained decoding framework that integrates STL safety specifications into the autoregressive action-generation loop. The key insight is that specification checking must be performed in state space (not token space) by simulating each candidate action forward through an approximate dynamics model and evaluating the resulting trajectory against the STL formula. Two decoding strategies are proposed: Hard Constrained Decoding (HCD) masks out any action whose predicted successor state violates the specification by setting its logit to −∞ before softmax, guaranteeing perfect STL satisfaction; Robustness Constrained Decoding (RCD) instead computes the quantitative STL robustness score for each candidate action, converts it to a weight via exponential scaling (exp(α·r)), and adds a scaled offset (β·w) to the original logits, softly biasing the distribution toward safer actions while retaining a fallback probability for all actions. RCD's soft approach trades a small reduction in guaranteed satisfaction for substantially better task success and graceful degradation under dynamics model errors, and is shown to significantly outperform both HCD and Simplex-style filtering on the safety–performance Pareto frontier.

## Results

Across three SOTA navigation policies (SPOC, Flare, PoliFormer) and four STL safety specifications on AI2-THOR, HCD achieves 100% STL satisfaction in all 12 model–specification conditions, while RCD achieves 83–96% STL satisfaction. RCD preserves task success much more closely to the unconstrained baseline, with success-rate drops of only 0.5–6.5 percentage points, compared to 5–12 point drops for HCD and 6–14 point drops for Simplex-style filtering. On the SafetyChores benchmark, combining HCD with the SafeVLA ISA policy reduces the composite safety cost by an order of magnitude (0.205 → 0.015) while retaining 82% task success. Statistical tests (one-sided two-proportion z-tests, n=200) confirm RCD significantly improves STL satisfaction over the unconstrained policy in all 9 conditions (p<0.05) without significantly reducing task success in any condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Simplex-style filtering baseline (Sha, 2001), HCD matches its 100% STL satisfaction in every condition while achieving 0.5–3 percentage points higher task success, because HCD still conditions on the base model's logits rather than defaulting to a fixed safe action. RCD trades some STL satisfaction (83–96% vs. 100%) for substantially better task success, with drops of only 0.5–6.5 points versus 6–14 points for filtering. Against the SafeVLA ISA safe-RL baseline (Zhang et al., 2025) on SafetyChores, SafeDec is complementary: ISA+HCD reduces the composite safety cost from 0.205 to 0.015 (a 13× reduction) at 82% success, while ISA+RCD reduces cost to 0.060 while preserving 86% success—nearly matching vanilla ISA's 86.5%. For the FLARE policy, HCD reduces safety cost from 0.192 to 0.115 with a 2-point success gain, whereas RCD reduces cost to 0.155 but drops success by 3 points, indicating the HCD variant is more effective for this particular policy. Overall, SafeDec wins on safety enforcement (STL satisfaction or safety cost) in all comparisons, with RCD winning on the safety–task-success trade-off and HCD winning on absolute safety guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeDec extends the NLP constrained-decoding paradigm (masking or reweighting logits to enforce syntactic constraints like regex or grammar) to the physical-robotics domain, where constraints are STL formulas over continuous state variables rather than token sequences. The hard-masking strategy (HCD) follows the standard logit-masking approach from structured-output generation (Welleck et al., 2024; Park et al., 2024), while the soft reweighting strategy (RCD) is directly inspired by DExperts (Liu et al., 2021), which reweighted next-token distributions using utility scores from an auxiliary model. The paper positions itself against both post-hoc filtering (Simplex architecture, Sha 2001) and training-time safety methods (SafeVLA, CBFs, safe RL), arguing that inference-time constrained decoding is the missing middle ground that preserves the base policy's learned behavior while guaranteeing specification satisfaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SafeDec provides a practical, training-free mechanism to enforce arbitrary STL safety specifications on large pre-trained navigation policies at inference time, which is critical for deploying such models in safety-critical physical environments where regulatory constraints cannot be baked into training data. The key open questions are the dependence on an approximate dynamics model (here a unicycle model) and the requirement for state-space specifications authored by roboticists; the authors point to learned world models and LLM-generated specifications as natural next steps. Additionally, the framework is limited to safety (not liveness) STL operators, and whether it extends to multi-agent or high-dimensional continuous-action settings remains unexplored.
