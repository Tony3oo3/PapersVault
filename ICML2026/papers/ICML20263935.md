## Problem

Existing methods that enforce normalization equivariance (NE) in neural denoisers require constraining every internal layer to NE-compatible operations (affine-constrained convolutions, sorting nonlinearities, affine residuals), which adds runtime cost and is incompatible with standard transformer components like softmax attention and LayerNorm. The paper asks whether these internal-layer constraints are actually necessary, or whether NE can be enforced purely at the input-output boundary without modifying the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy image (grayscale or 3-channel RGB) of arbitrary spatial size and produces a denoised image of the same size. During training, inputs are random patches (64×64 or 70×70) corrupted with AWGN at a fixed noise level; at test time, full images are processed. The wrapper computes global instance statistics (mean and standard deviation) pooled jointly over all channels and all pixels—matching the global affine group action y→ay+b1—rather than per-channel statistics, and the backbone is queried only on the normalized input lying on the unit-std, zero-mean manifold.

## Method

The paper proves a characterization theorem: a function f is normalization-equivariant if and only if it factors as f(y) = std(y)·g(T_NE(y)) + μ(y)·1 for some g defined on the normalized manifold, meaning every NE map is exactly a normalize–process–denormalize composition. Using this, the authors construct WNE, a parameter-free wrapper that computes global μ(y) and std(y), normalizes the input to T_NE(y), applies an unmodified backbone g_θ (DnCNN, SwinIR, Restormer, etc.), and denormalizes the output with the stored statistics. The backbone is never modified—attention, LayerNorm, and BatchNorm remain intact—and the wrapper adds no measurable GPU overhead (1.00× forward, 0.99× backward). Training uses standard raw-space MSE with no equivariance-specific loss terms. A normalized-coordinate analysis further shows that NE denoising reduces to a regression in which a single difficulty scalar Δ=‖ỹ−x̃‖₂ largely determines the error, and substantial train-test overlap in Δ across noise levels explains the observed mismatch robustness.

## Results

At matched noise (σ_test = σ_train), WNE is competitive with the unwrapped baseline: on DnCNN it achieves 34.77/30.43/27.22 dB on Set12 (vs. 34.92/30.50/27.17 for Baseline) and 33.80/29.15/26.20 dB on BSD68, within 0.15 dB of the baseline at all three noise levels. The headline result is under noise-level mismatch: WNE closely tracks the robustness trend of the architectural NE reference (NE-arch) on DnCNN and stabilizes SwinIR's output PSNR across a wide σ_test range, while the unwrapped baselines degrade sharply. On GPU, WNE adds no measurable overhead (0.99× backward, 1.00× inference), whereas the architectural NE baseline is 1.60×/1.69× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard DnCNN baseline (Zhang et al., 2017), WNE is within 0.15 dB at matched noise and matches or slightly exceeds it at σ=50 (27.22 vs. 27.17 dB on Set12); the decisive advantage appears under mismatch, where WNE's output PSNR degrades gracefully while the baseline flattens. Against the SwinIR baseline (Liang et al., 2021), WNE costs ~0.1–0.14 dB at matched noise but stabilizes performance across the full σ_test sweep, whereas the unwrapped SwinIR collapses once σ_test ≠ σ_train. Against the architectural equivariant references from Herbreteau et al. (2023), WNE closely matches NE-arch's mismatch curve on DnCNN and improves over SE-arch in the high-to-low noise regime, while running at 0.99× the baseline speed versus NE-arch's 1.60× overhead. On the Restormer backbone (Zamir et al., 2022), WNE actually slightly outperforms the baseline at matched noise (35.05 vs. 34.79 dB on Set12 at σ=10) and stabilizes mismatch behavior, confirming the wrapper generalizes across transformer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the normalization-equivariance (NE) and scale-equivariance (SE) priors introduced for blind denoising by Mohan et al. (2020) and Herbreteau et al. (2023), which enforce equivariance by constraining every internal layer (affine-constrained convolutions, sorting nonlinearities, affine residuals). The key departure is a characterization theorem showing that every NE map factors as normalize–process–denormalize, making internal-layer constraints unnecessary: a parameter-free wrapper around an arbitrary backbone (including transformers with softmax attention and LayerNorm) exactly parameterizes the NE function class. The normalize–process–denormalize template has precedent in RevIN for time-series forecasting and in canonicalization-based equivariant maps, but this paper proves it is the complete and unique form of NE for the global affine action, not merely an empirical heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wrapper is a practical drop-in that adds no measurable GPU overhead while restoring mismatch robustness to transformer denoisers (SwinIR, Restormer) that were previously excluded from NE architectures, and the normalized-coordinate analysis (Δ-driven difficulty) gives a mechanistic explanation for why NE generalizes across noise levels and why the asymmetry (low-to-high vs. high-to-low) arises. Open questions include whether exact NE is required for preconditioned plug-and-play methods (Hong et al., 2024) and noisy-only diffusion learning (Levac et al., 2025), and whether the Δ-overlap mechanism extends to non-Gaussian or spatially-correlated noise beyond the AWGN setting studied here. The SIDD real-noise results and Noise2Noise sampling experiment suggest broad applicability, but the core evaluation remains on synthetic AWGN with a single training noise level.
