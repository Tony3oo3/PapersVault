## Problem

Existing multi-agent communication graph generation methods treat the task as a black-box combinatorial search, either training on low-energy graphs without access to their exact energy values or deriving only scalar energy feedback without gradient information. This prevents the system from navigating the underlying energy landscape, causing it to become trapped in local optima and produce redundant, high-cost communication topologies that degrade task utility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task query (e.g., a math word problem or code specification) and a pool of N agents with role descriptions as input. The query is encoded via a dual-path encoder into a logic embedding (topological constraints) and a content embedding (node context), both 384-dimensional, projected to 128-dimensional hidden states. The output is a directed acyclic graph specified by a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and continuous node embeddings V ∈ R^{N×d}, which together define the information-flow topology for multi-agent execution. The DAG constraint ensures deterministic, executable communication sequences.

## Method

MAGE formalizes communication graph generation as energy minimization under a Boltzmann distribution, exploiting the mathematical duality between the generative score (∇log p) and the negative energy gradient (−∇E). A Joint Graph Transformer simultaneously models discrete edge topologies and continuous node embeddings, producing a decomposed joint score (node score + edge score) trained via weighted MSE with classifier-free guidance. During sampling, a two-stage energy-guided mechanism operates: the diffusion backbone acts as an implicit energy optimizer steering the trajectory onto the valid-graph manifold, while a separate GAN-based energy function (with dual heads regressing collaboration cost and task utility) injects explicit first-order gradients at each reverse step. To propagate gradients through the discrete adjacency, Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation yields a continuous approximation, and Tweedie's formula estimates the clean graph before a single gradient-descent refinement step drives the trajectory into deeper energy valleys.

## Results

MAGE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six benchmarks, with an average accuracy of 89.90%, surpassing the previous best (GTD at 87.79%) by 2.11 points. The largest gains appear on MATH (72.22% vs. 68.44% for GTD, +3.78) and MBPP (84.57% vs. 82.50% for GTD, +2.07). MAGE also attains the lowest token cost ($1.43 on GSM8K) among all compared methods while achieving the highest accuracy (94.35%), demonstrating a favorable efficiency-accuracy trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGE outperforms all 11 baselines on every benchmark. Against single-agent baselines, MAGE exceeds CoT (82.27% avg) and SC-CoT (82.78% avg) by 7.6 and 7.1 points respectively. Among human-designed multi-agent frameworks, MAGE surpasses DyLAN (84.62%), LLM-Blender (84.06%), and LLM-Debate (82.65%) by margins of 5.3–7.3 points. Among automated frameworks, the closest competitors are GTD (87.79%) and ARG-Designer (87.65%); MAGE leads GTD by 2.11 points on average, with the largest gap on MATH (+3.78) and MBPP (+2.07). Notably, ADAS (80.19%) underperforms even the vanilla single-agent baseline, while AFLOW (85.42%) and MaAS (86.93%) trail MAGE by 4.5 and 3.0 points respectively. MAGE also achieves the lowest token cost ($1.43 on GSM8K) among all compared automated methods, confirming that its energy-guided sampling prunes redundant communication without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGE extends the graph diffusion paradigm for multi-agent communication topology generation (GTD, Jiang et al. 2025) by introducing an explicit energy-landscape formulation grounded in the score-energy duality (∇log p ∝ −∇E). While prior automated MAS frameworks (GPTSwarm, MaAS, ARG-Designer, AFLOW) treat graph generation as black-box search or zeroth-order optimization relying on scalar rewards, MAGE reframes the problem as continuous energy descent via a two-stage mechanism: an implicit diffusion backbone (built on the SDE framework of Song et al. 2021) plus explicit first-order energy gradients injected during reverse sampling. The Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation for differentiable discrete adjacency updates and the Joint Graph Transformer for heterogeneous (discrete topology + continuous node features) score estimation are the key architectural departures from prior graph diffusion work, which typically targets molecular or static-structure generation rather than task-adaptive agent workflows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The energy-landscape formalization provides a principled, differentiable route to jointly optimizing task utility and coordination cost in MAS, moving beyond the zeroth-order paradigms that dominate the field. Practically, MAGE achieves the best accuracy while consuming the fewest tokens among compared methods, suggesting that energy-guided pruning of redundant edges is a viable path toward scalable multi-agent deployment. However, the framework is validated only on 50 training instances per dataset with a single backbone (GPT-4o-mini), and the energy function is a learned surrogate whose fidelity to true execution cost/utility remains an open question. Generalization to larger agent pools, non-reasoning tasks, and rapidly evolving LLM backbones (where the LlaMA-4 MBPP degradation hints at context-window fragility) are important open directions.
