## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) inherently face an accuracy-parallelism trade-off: pushing for higher parallel decoding degrades generation quality, while preserving accuracy constrains how many tokens can be generated per forward pass. Existing acceleration methods address only one side of this trade-off, and standard dLLM training with random masking provides no guidance on which tokens can be safely decoded early, while multi-block decoding approaches suffer quality loss from incomplete or erroneous cross-block context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (optionally with few-shot examples, up to ~1000 tokens for long-context settings) and produces a complete generated text sequence via iterative token unmasking. During training, the input is a prompt-response pair where the response is partially masked according to the teacher's pseudo-trajectory within a sliding window of size 16–32; during inference, the model operates on blocks of 32 tokens, progressively unmasking tokens across multiple blocks in parallel until an EOS token triggers early stopping.

## Method

d3LLM combines a novel training recipe with an inference strategy to push the accuracy-parallelism frontier. At training time, pseudo-trajectory distillation records the teacher dLLM's token-unmasking order (constrained to one token per step) and uses it to construct noisy training sequences that teach the student which tokens to decode first, paired with curriculum learning that progressively increases mask ratio (0→0.8) and window size (16→32). At inference time, entropy-based multi-block decoding simultaneously unmask tokens across the current and future blocks by prioritizing low-entropy predictions, governed by a five-state block lifecycle (Inactive → Activated → Fully-Activated → Completed but Stabilizing → Completed). A KV-cache refresh mechanism periodically recomputes cached key-value states during the stabilizing phase to prevent error propagation across blocks, and an early-stopping rule halts decoding upon EOS detection. The framework is applied via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 256) to LLaDA, Dream, and Dream-Coder, achieving up to 10× speedup over vanilla dLLMs and 3.6–5× over AR baselines with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

d3LLM-LLaDA achieves the highest AUP (Accuracy Under Parallelism) score on all five LLaDA-based benchmarks, reaching 637.7 on GSM8K-CoT (vs. 358.1 for dParallel, the second-best), 107.6 on MATH, 88.4 on MBPP, 96.6 on HumanEval, and 441.1 on Long-GSM8K. In terms of raw throughput, d3LLM-LLaDA achieves 9.11 TPF with 73.1% accuracy on GSM8K-CoT, translating to 288.9 TPS on H100 (5.0× speedup over Qwen-2.5-7B-it) and 183.3 TPS on A100 (3.6× speedup), while delivering a 10.3× speedup over vanilla LLaDA. d3LLM-Dream similarly achieves the best AUP on 4 of 5 Dream-based tasks (e.g., 391.3 on GSM8K-CoT vs. 245.7 for dParallel), and d3LLM-Coder achieves 2.88 TPF with 79.7% accuracy on HumanEval, an 8× speedup over vanilla Dream-Coder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

d3LLM-LLaDA decisively outperforms all four LLaDA-based baselines (vanilla LLaDA, Fast-dLLM, D2F, dParallel) on every benchmark, with the largest margin over dParallel (e.g., AUP 637.7 vs. 358.1 on GSM8K-CoT, a 78% improvement) and over Fast-dLLM/D2F (roughly 3× higher AUP). On Dream-based models, d3LLM-Dream wins 4 of 5 tasks against Fast-dLLM, Fast-dLLM-v2, and dParallel, but loses on MATH where Fast-dLLM-v2 achieves higher accuracy (48.7% vs. 38.2%) due to being fine-tuned with an additional 1B tokens from LLaMA-Nemotron, whereas d3LLM-Dream uses only 65M additional tokens. Against the distillation baseline SDTT (Self-Distillation Through Time), d3LLM-LLaDA more than doubles the AUP score (637.7 vs. 309.7/310.3) while also achieving nearly 2× higher TPF (9.11 vs. 4.53/4.29). Compared to the speculative decoding method EAGLE-3, d3LLM loses on most tasks (e.g., AUP 637.7 vs. 319.0 on GSM8K-CoT, 107.6 vs. 142.1 on MATH), which the authors attribute to EAGLE-3's verification step preventing accuracy degradation and its unconstrained FLOPs budget; nonetheless, d3LLM substantially narrows the gap between diffusion-based and speculative-decoding approaches. On coding tasks, d3LLM-Coder-7B achieves 2.88 TPF with 79.7% accuracy on HumanEval, surpassing vanilla Dream-Coder-7B (1.00 TPF, 82.9%) in AUP (208.4 vs. 82.9) while remaining competitive with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (86.6% accuracy at 1.00 TPF).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundation dLLMs LLaDA and Dream (and Dream-Coder for code) and the certainty-forcing distillation paradigm introduced by dParallel, but departs from prior dLLM acceleration work (Fast-dLLM, D2F, dParallel) that targets either efficiency or accuracy in isolation. The key conceptual departure is pseudo-trajectory distillation: rather than relying on random masking or ground-truth-only supervision, the student learns the teacher's own token-unmasking order as intermediate guidance, combined with curriculum learning on noise level and window size. On the inference side, it extends single-block diffusion decoding to entropy-based multi-block decoding with a KV-cache refresh mechanism, addressing the quality degradation that plagues prior multi-block approaches like D2F.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AUP metric addresses a real gap in dLLM evaluation by jointly capturing the accuracy-parallelism trade-off that single metrics (TPS or accuracy alone) obscure, and its robustness to weighting-function and threshold choices makes it a practical standard. The 10× speedup over vanilla dLLMs and 3.6–5× over AR baselines with negligible accuracy loss is practically significant for deployment, especially with the SGLang integration reaching 1310 TPS on B200. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to stronger foundation dLLMs (LLaDA 2.0, ReFusion), whether combining with speculative decoding closes the remaining gap to EAGLE-3, and whether the AUP metric can be made differentiable to serve as a training objective.
