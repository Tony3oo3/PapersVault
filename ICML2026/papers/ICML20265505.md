## Problem

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis predicts that independently trained models converge toward shared latent structure, but this has been validated primarily for vision and language. Whether time series—which encode semantics implicitly through temporal variation rather than explicitly through spatial geometry or symbolic tokens—participate in such convergence remains unexplored, and the conditions under which cross-modal alignment succeeds or fails for this modality are unclear.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265505/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes aligned triplets of univariate numeric time series (variable length, up to 5000 timesteps), rendered line-plot images resized to 384×384 pixels, and natural language captions (up to 512 tokens). All three modalities are encoded by frozen pretrained backbones, and only lightweight projection heads are trained to produce a shared 1024-dimensional embedding space. Evaluation produces per-pair alignment scores (cosine margin, Procrustes disparity, CKA, mutual kNN overlap) and bidirectional retrieval metrics (R@1/5/10) on held-out test sets.

## Method

The paper repurposes the CLIP contrastive paradigm as a controlled probe of trimodal representational compatibility: 34 configurations of frozen pretrained encoders (spanning 9 text, 9 vision, and 8 time series models from 60M to 27B parameters) are paired with trainable projection heads, trained with an equally weighted symmetric InfoNCE loss over all three modality pairs (TS-IMG, TS-TXT, IMG-TXT) at fixed hyperparameters. The key empirical findings are that alignment is asymmetric (TS-IMG consistently outperforms TS-TXT across all scales), information density in text improves alignment only up to a saturation threshold, and image embeddings geometrically mediate between time series and text (73.4% of triplets place the image embedding closer to both endpoints than the endpoints are to each other). The study further isolates the effects of indirect textual supervision (ECG clinical reports vs. direct captions), language shift (English vs. German), and visual annotation richness, showing that semantic explicitness and representational format matching—not merely model scale—govern cross-modal convergence.

## Results

Independently pretrained time series, vision, and language encoders exhibit near-orthogonal geometry (mean angular deviation ≈ 90°) in the absence of explicit coupling. After contrastive alignment, TS-IMG alignment consistently outperforms TS-TXT across all 34 encoder configurations, with the gap persisting from 86M to 18B parameter scales. Doubling text information density (ID 417→870) yields negligible changes (Δ ≤ 0.01) in all alignment metrics, confirming saturation. Images act as effective intermediaries: 73.4% of TS-IMG-TXT triplets place the image embedding geometrically closer to both the time series and text endpoints than those endpoints are to each other, and triangle-chain bridging error drops from 0.90 (DINOv2) to 0.34 (SigLIP2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are internal. Across 34 encoder configurations spanning 86M–18B parameters, TS-IMG alignment is consistently stronger than TS-TXT at every scale, with TS-TXT showing the steepest positive scaling trend but remaining the weakest pair in absolute terms. VL-pretrained configurations (e.g., SigLIP, EVA-CLIP) achieve strong IMG-TXT alignment even at small scales, whereas independently pretrained trimodal encoders require substantially more capacity to reach comparable image-text coherence. On MIMIC (indirect clinical text, ID ≈ 149), TS-TXT and IMG-TXT alignment are markedly weaker than on CaTS (direct descriptive captions, ID ≈ 417), while TS-IMG alignment is often stronger on MIMIC due to longer, more structured ECG signals. Shifting from English (MIMIC) to German (PTB-XL) reports degrades all text-involving pairs, with a controlled translation experiment (MIMIC-EN → MIMIC-DE → PTB-XL-GE) confirming progressive alignment loss. Introducing the image modality into a bimodal TS-TXT setup produces a consistent upward shift in alignment, whereas adding a third modality to the already-strong TS-IMG pair often degrades performance, indicating trimodality helps only weakly coupled pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the CLIP-style contrastive alignment framework (Radford et al., 2021; Oord et al., 2018) as a controlled diagnostic tool for studying representational geometry across time series, vision, and language, extending the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024) from its well-studied vision-language domain to a trimodal setting that includes time series. It departs from prior time series-text alignment work (MedCLIP, TimesCLIP) by treating alignment itself as the object of study rather than a means to downstream prediction, and introduces 'semantic explicitness' and information density as governing variables for cross-modal convergence. The information density analysis draws on the uniform information density hypothesis from linguistics (Jaeger & Levy, 2006), adapting surprisal-based metrics to quantify how textual semantic richness shapes alignment quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic empirical characterization of trimodal alignment involving time series, revealing that convergence is fundamentally asymmetric (TS-IMG >> TS-TXT) and governed by how explicitly each modality exposes temporal semantics rather than by model scale alone. The information density saturation finding—that doubling caption richness yields no further alignment gain—challenges the intuitive assumption that more semantic content always helps, and has direct practical implications for annotation budgets in multimodal time series systems. Key limitations include restriction to univariate series, frozen encoders with projection heads (excluding end-to-end fine-tuning), and reliance on synthetic or domain-specific captions; extending to multivariate signals, sensor-based time series, and connecting alignment quality to downstream task performance remain important open questions.
