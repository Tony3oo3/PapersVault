## Problem

Prior work demonstrated that adding external visual structures (shape annotations, grid lines) improves LVLM reasoning, but the internal mechanism behind these gains remained unexplained. This paper asks why external cues help: specifically, how do partition-inducing symbols and lines alter the model's internal representations to improve cross-modal alignment and reduce hallucinations?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261835/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image augmented with partition-inducing external cues (e.g., four symbols {&, #, @, $} and three horizontal lines creating four rows) paired with a text prompt that explicitly references the same symbols, producing structured outputs such as per-partition scene descriptions ('Row &: purple moon, purple star...'), discriminative VQA answers ('red circle' for a queried row), or free-form captions. On synthetic datasets, each image contains 10–20 unique objects drawn from 35 shape-color combinations (7 shapes × 5 colors), each confined to a 28×28 pixel patch; on MS-COCO, grid-based partitions with thin white margins replace simple horizontal lines to handle natural scene complexity. The approach requires no fine-tuning and applies at inference time to both open- and closed-source models.

## Method

The paper introduces Grounding IDs: latent, abstract vectors that LVLMs spontaneously generate when aligned external cues partition both the visual and textual modalities, binding objects within a partition to their corresponding textual descriptions. The core evidence comes from causal mediation experiments (activation swapping between two contexts) showing that object-symbol associations are carried by these IDs rather than local spatial features—after swapping object patch activations, the model follows the transferred binding (0.98 swap accuracy) rather than the locally present object (0.02 standard accuracy). Layerwise logit-lens analysis localizes Grounding ID emergence to layers 20–27, while attention analysis reveals partition-specific diagonal dominance and reduced modality gap in structured inputs. A disjoint-symbol control experiment (querying with a symbol absent from the host image) confirms the IDs are lexical-style identifiers predictable from the symbol itself rather than context-dependent. The practical upshot is a model-agnostic, zero-overhead input augmentation that reduces CHAIR hallucination scores by 10–15 points and improves counting/visual-search accuracy by 5–12 points over baselines.

## Results

On 500 MS-COCO samples, the Structured (Grounding IDs) method reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_s) from 51.60 to 41.00 on LLaVA-1.5 and from 32.40 to 27.20 on Qwen2.5-VL, while also lowering instance-level hallucination (CHAIR_i) from 13.20 to 12.04 and 7.97 to 5.36 respectively. The approach extends to closed-source models, cutting CHAIR_s from 29.20 to 23.20 on GPT-4o and from 44.20 to 37.40 on Gemini-2.5-Pro. On visual reasoning, Grounding IDs raise counting accuracy from 29.67% to 53.00% and visual search accuracy from 30.00% to 52.25% on Qwen2.5-VL (7B), outperforming the VISER baseline (43.33% and 40.00% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Structured (Grounding IDs) method outperforms all three hallucination-mitigation baselines on both CHAIR_s and CHAIR_i across both open-source models. Against OPERA, it reduces CHAIR_s by 7.0 points on LLaVA-1.5 (41.00 vs. 48.00) and by 2.4 points on Qwen2.5-VL (27.20 vs. 29.60), while also cutting inference time from 20.91 s to 3.94 s (LLaVA-1.5) and 23.50 s to 6.04 s (Qwen2.5-VL). Against VCD, the gains are even larger: CHAIR_s drops by 13.4 points on LLaVA-1.5 (41.00 vs. 54.40) and by 6.6 points on Qwen2.5-VL (27.20 vs. 33.80), with VCD actually worsening hallucination relative to its own baseline on LLaVA-1.5. Against SPARC, Structured wins by 14.2 points on LLaVA-1.5 CHAIR_s (41.00 vs. 55.20) and by 6.4 points on Qwen2.5-VL (27.20 vs. 33.60). On visual reasoning, Grounding IDs surpasses the VISER baseline by 9.67 points on counting (53.00 vs. 43.33) and by 12.25 points on visual search (52.25 vs. 40.00) for Qwen2.5-VL 7B, with similar margins on the 3B model and GPT-4o.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Binding ID literature (Feng & Steinhardt, 2023; Saravanan et al., 2025) from examining pre-existing entity-attribute identifiers in standard settings to showing that *external visual cues* can *induce* new, partition-specific identifiers (Grounding IDs) that propagate across modalities. It builds directly on the external-scaffolding line of work (Rudman et al., 2025; VISER/Izadi et al., 2025), which demonstrated that adding grid lines or shape annotations improves LVLM reasoning, but departs by providing a mechanistic causal explanation for *why* these cues work rather than merely showing that they do. The methodological toolkit draws on causal mediation analysis, activation patching, and logit-lens probing from the mechanistic interpretability community, applied here to the multimodal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is significant: a near-zero-overhead input modification (adding symbols and partition lines) outperforms or matches specialized hallucination-mitigation methods (VCD, OPERA, SPARC) across open- and closed-source models, making it immediately deployable without architectural changes. The mechanistic finding that Grounding IDs are abstract, symbol-predictable, and transferable via activation patching (0.98 swap accuracy) provides a concrete target for future interventions and RL fine-tuning strategies. Open questions include whether the effect generalizes beyond row-based partitions to arbitrary spatial layouts, how to integrate cues during training rather than inference, and why discriminative tasks (POPE) show reduced recall under structuring, suggesting the mechanism is most beneficial for sustained multi-object grounding.
