## Problem

The VLA design space remains fragmented: many groups propose architectures with different training protocols and evaluation setups, making it difficult to determine which design choices genuinely improve performance. This paper addresses that gap by systematically dissecting the VLA pipeline under a unified framework and evaluation protocol, distilling 12 key findings into a practical recipe for building strong VLA models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (a third-person view plus a wrist-mounted view), a natural-language instruction, and a proprioceptive state vector (8-step history) that is injected into the VLM rather than the policy head. It outputs a continuous action chunk of 8 timesteps, predicted via flow matching and regularized by a frequency-domain MSE loss computed after a discrete cosine transform, with higher weights assigned to low-frequency components to suppress trajectory jitter.

## Method

Starting from a minimal RT-2-style baseline (LLaMA-3.2-3B + SigLIP2, token-reuse action classification), the paper runs 500+ ablations across three axes: foundational components, perception, and action modeling, to identify which choices matter. The resulting VLANeXt replaces the token-reuse head with a 12-layer transformer policy module conditioned by 16 learnable meta queries, connects the VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B) to the policy via a soft layer-wise strategy that inserts learnable query buffers between modules, and predicts action chunks with flow matching. On the perception side, it uses multi-view (third-person + wrist) inputs and conditions proprioception in the VLM via a linear projector, while deliberately omitting temporal frame history. A lightweight frequency-domain auxiliary loss (DCT-weighted MSE) is added to regularize action prediction, and an auxiliary world-modeling objective (predicting future image tokens) is explored but excluded from the final recipe due to roughly 3x training cost.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, VLANeXt achieves a 97.4% average success rate across the four suites (Spatial 99.0%, Object 99.2%, Goal 96.6%, Long 94.8%), setting a new state of the art. On LIBERO-plus, which tests robustness under 7 types of unseen perturbations, VLANeXt reaches 83.9% total success rate, a 14.3-point improvement over the previous best method OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%). In real-world experiments on four manipulation tasks (single-arm and bimanual), VLANeXt outperforms both OpenVLA-OFT and π0 across all tasks, e.g., 14/20 vs. 10/20 on single-arm table cleaning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLANeXt (97.4% avg) edges out the closest competitor OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) by 0.3 points on LIBERO, with the largest margin on the Spatial suite (99.0 vs. 97.6). The gap widens dramatically on LIBERO-plus, where VLANeXt's 83.9% total success rate surpasses OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%) by 14.3 points, and outperforms π0-Fast (61.6%), π0 (53.6%), UniVLA (42.9%), NORA (39.0%), WorldVLA (25.0%), and OpenVLA (15.6%) by even larger margins. Among direct policy learning baselines, VLANeXt exceeds Diffusion Policy (72.4%), Octo (75.1%), and MDT (76.4%) by 21+ points. In real-world deployment, VLANeXt wins all four tasks against both OpenVLA-OFT and π0, with the largest advantage on single-arm table cleaning (14/20 vs. 10/20 for π0 and 7/20 for OpenVLA-OFT). The only suite where VLANeXt does not lead is Goal on LIBERO (96.6% vs. π0.5's 98.0% and OpenVLA-OFT's 97.9%), though the difference is marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study of the VLA design space rather than a single novel architecture. It builds directly on the RT-2/OpenVLA pipeline (token-reuse, binning-based action classification) as its baseline, incorporates MetaQuery-style multi-query policy modules, and adopts flow matching as the action learning objective. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'soft connection' (learnable query buffer with layer-wise cross-attention) between the VLM and policy, which sits between the fully decoupled MetaQuery design and the tightly coupled π-series architecture. The frequency-domain auxiliary loss is borrowed from time-series forecasting literature (FEDformer, FRED-F) and repurposed as a regularization for action prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe (12 distilled findings from 500+ experiments) for building strong VLA models, which is valuable given the fragmented and inconsistent evaluation landscape of prior VLA work. The finding that principled design choices (soft coupling, VLM-side proprioception, multi-view inputs, frequency-domain loss) can make a 2.5B model outperform a 7B model on robustness benchmarks challenges the assumption that scale is the primary driver of VLA performance. Limitations include evaluation primarily on the LIBERO simulation suite, limited real-world experiments (4 tasks, 50 episodes each), and the recipe's generalizability to more diverse embodiments and longer-horizon tasks remains untested. The released unified codebase lowers the barrier for future systematic VLA research.
