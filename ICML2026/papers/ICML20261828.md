## Problem

Diffusion transformers are trained at limited resolutions (e.g., 1024×1024) because self-attention's quadratic cost makes ultra-high-resolution training infeasible, yet applying them at much higher resolutions causes severe degradation due to positional encoding mismatch. Existing position extrapolation methods (PI, NTK-aware, YaRN) apply a fixed frequency scaling throughout all diffusion steps, ignoring the fact that the diffusion process resolves low-frequency structure early and high-frequency detail late, so a static allocation of representable frequency modes is suboptimal at every stage of sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion transformer (e.g., FLUX.1-Krea-dev trained at 1024×1024) along with a text prompt or class label, and produces images at resolutions far exceeding the training resolution—up to 4096×4096 (16M+ pixels) or beyond—without any retraining, fine-tuning, or additional sampling steps. It also generalizes to non-square aspect ratios (e.g., 4096×1365 panoramas), class-conditional generation on ImageNet (320², 384²), and high-resolution video (1280×720), all using the same time-dependent positional encoding modification at inference time.

## Method

DYPE is grounded in a Fourier-space analysis showing that during reverse diffusion, low-frequency components converge to their final values early in sampling while high-frequency components continue evolving throughout the denoising trajectory. Exploiting this spectral progression, DYPE introduces a time-dependent scaling factor κ(t) = λ_s · t^λ_t that modulates the positional encoding at each diffusion step: early steps (t≈1) apply strong extrapolation scaling to cover the full spectrum of the larger context, while later steps (t≈0) attenuate the scaling toward 1, restoring the model's native training conditions and freeing representable modes for the still-evolving high frequencies. Three variants are derived by injecting κ(t) into existing static methods: DY-PI exponentiates the position-scaling factor, DY-NTK multiplies the NTK-aware frequency exponent, and DY-YaRN dynamically shifts the ramp thresholds α and β that partition the spectrum into low/mid/high bands. The approach is entirely training-free, adds no sampling overhead, and is validated on FLUX, FiTv2, Qwen-Image, and Wan2.1 across text-to-image, class-conditional, video, and editing tasks.

## Results

At the paper's headline resolution of 4096×4096 (16M pixels) on FLUX, DY-YaRN achieves CLIP-Score 26.94, ImageReward 0.15, and Aesthetics 5.17 on DrawBench, and CLIP-Score 29.28, ImageReward 1.09, Aesthetics 6.67, and FID 186.00 on Aesthetic-4K — substantially outperforming the unmodified FLUX baseline (CLIP 16.43, IR −1.97, FID 195.68) and static YaRN (CLIP 25.71, IR −0.34, FID 192.19). In human evaluation, DY-YaRN is preferred over YaRN in 87–90% of pairwise comparisons across text alignment, structure, and detail. On ImageNet class-conditional generation with FiTv2-XL/2, DY-YaRN achieves FID 21.75 at 384², improving over YaRN (22.63) and the base model (38.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DY-YaRN consistently outperforms all positional-extrapolation baselines (FLUX, NTK-aware, TASR, YaRN, DY-NTK) across every resolution tested (2048×3072 through 4096×4096), with the margin widening at higher resolutions — at 4096², DY-YaRN improves CLIP-Score by +1.23 over YaRN and +10.51 over raw FLUX on DrawBench. Against general high-resolution baselines in Table 3, DY-YaRN achieves the best FID (142.74) and patch-FID (96.34) at 2048², and the best patch-FID (78.33) and patch-IS (10.13) at 4096², surpassing U-Net methods (DemoFusion, FreCaS, DiffuseHigh, FreeScale), progressive pipelines (I-Max, UltraPixel, HiFlow), and FLUX+BSRGAN. On ImageNet with FiTv2-XL/2, DY-YaRN achieves the best FID (21.75) and sFID (33.92) at 384², beating YaRN (22.63 / 36.09) and the base model (38.90 / 49.51). In the resolution-scaling study, DY-YaRN remains stable up to 6144² while YaRN degrades at 4096² and FLUX at 3072². The method also generalizes to Qwen-Image (best CLIP 28.85, FID 197.38 on Aesthetic-4K) and Wan2.1 video generation (best VBench scores across all six categories at 1280×720), confirming architecture-agnostic effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYPE extends the line of LLM-derived positional extrapolation methods (PI, NTK-aware, YaRN) that have been adapted to vision transformers for moderate resolution increases. The key departure is introducing a time-dependent scheduler κ(t) that dynamically attenuates the extrapolation strength as diffusion progresses, motivated by a Fourier-space analysis showing that low-frequency modes converge early while high-frequency modes evolve throughout sampling. This contrasts with the closest prior work, TASR (Zhuo et al., 2024), which interpolates in the opposite direction (from PI toward NTK-aware), effectively suppressing high frequencies when they are most needed. The method is training-free and requires no architectural changes, operating purely by modifying RoPE frequency parameters at each denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DYPE provides a simple, drop-in, training-free mechanism that enables pre-trained diffusion transformers to generate images at resolutions far exceeding their training range (up to 16M+ pixels with FLUX) without additional sampling cost, addressing a major practical bottleneck in ultra-high-resolution synthesis. The spectral progression insight is general and validated across multiple architectures (FLUX, FiTv2, Qwen-Image, Wan2.1 video), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include whether combining DYPE with light fine-tuning can push resolution limits further, whether the approach extends to 3D generation, and whether the fixed scheduler form κ(t) = λ_s·t^λ_t is optimal or whether learned schedules could yield additional gains. The method's simplicity (a single time-dependent multiplier on existing extrapolation parameters) makes it immediately deployable in production pipelines.
