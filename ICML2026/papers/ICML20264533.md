## Problem

Naive INT4 quantization of LLMs severely degrades performance due to large-magnitude outliers in activations, weights, and KV cache, creating a fundamental range-precision trade-off. Prior function-preserving transforms (FPTs) that reshape distributions to be more quantization-friendly are either non-mergeable (incurring inference overhead) or insufficiently expressive to handle the hardest quantization settings. The paper asks: can we design transforms that are simultaneously expressive enough to reduce quantization error and mergeable into existing weights so they add zero inference cost?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM in FP16 (e.g., Llama-2 7B/13B, Llama-3 8B, Llama-3.2 3B, Qwen-2.5 32B) and a small calibration set (Wikitext-2, 64 sequences for range initialization, 1024 training steps for end-to-end optimization). It produces a static INT4 quantized model where all transform parameters are merged into the linear layer weights, requiring no custom kernels or additional operations at inference time. The quantized model outputs text tokens with perplexity and zero-shot reasoning accuracy close to the FP16 original, achieving up to 3.9× prefill speedup.

## Method

FPTQuant introduces three novel FPTs that exploit equivariances and independencies inherent to transformer operations: (1) a pre-RoPE transform T_k of scaled 2×2 rotation matrices applied to queries and keys, proven to commute with RoPE and thus mergeable into W_q and W_k; (2) a per-head invertible value transform T_v that is mergeable into W_v and W_o because the batched matmul over attention probabilities is independent of the head dimension; and (3) a pseudodynamic per-token residual scaling S_n computed recursively from existing RMSNorms, which normalizes the residual and reduces intra-block outliers at virtually no cost. These are combined with a mergeable residual rotation (from SpinQuant), a Hadamard transform before the down projection (from QuaRot), and a per-channel scaler (from OSTQuant). Training proceeds in two stages: a cheap local L_p optimization of each transform's merged weights, followed by end-to-end student-teacher training using Jensen-Shannon Divergence loss to match the quantized student's outputs to the FP16 teacher, with learnable quantization grids. The key design principle is that each transform is placed where it commutes with existing operations, making it mergeable and eliminating inference overhead while retaining enough degrees of freedom to be optimized via gradient descent.

## Results

On static INT4 quantization (W4A8KV8), FPTQuant achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 5.85 on Llama-2 7B, 10.65 on Llama-3.2 3B-it, 6.27 on Llama-3 8B, and 5.12 on Qwen-2.5 32B, with 0-shot common-sense reasoning accuracy of 65.96%, 64.00%, 72.72%, and 77.51% respectively. For the harder W4A4KV4 setting, FPTQuant achieves 11.71 PPL on Llama-3.2 3B-it and 9.74 on Llama-3 8B. In terms of speed, FPTQuant delivers a SOTA prefill speedup of up to 3.9× over FP16 and is 15–29% faster than FlatQuant while remaining within 5–6% of the INT4 upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPTQuant consistently outperforms QuaRot and SpinQuant across nearly all models and bit-widths; for example, on Llama-3 8B at W4A8KV8, FPTQuant achieves 6.27 Wiki PPL vs. 6.54 (SpinQuant) and 7.04 (QuaRot), and on Qwen-2.5 32B it reaches 77.51% 0-shot accuracy vs. 75.51% (SpinQuant) and 76.24% (QuaRot). Against OSTQuant, FPTQuant wins decisively—OSTQuant suffers from instability and is not strictly function-preserving, yielding 6.49 PPL on L2-7B vs. FPTQuant's 5.85. The closest competitor is FlatQuant, which achieves slightly better accuracy in the hardest W4A4KV4 setting (e.g., 11.38 vs. 11.71 PPL on Llama-3.2 3B-it, and 61.00% vs. 59.46% 0-shot), but FPTQuant is 15–29% faster at inference and faster to train. In the dynamic quantization setting (Table 4), FPTQuant is on par with or better than all baselines except FlatQuant, while remaining up to 29% faster. SmoothQuant, included only in the dynamic quantization comparison, performs far worse (e.g., 83.1 PPL on Llama-2 7B vs. FPTQuant's 5.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

FPTQuant extends the function-preserving transform (FPT) paradigm for LLM quantization initiated by SmoothQuant (per-channel scaling), QuaRot (Hadamard transforms), SpinQuant (learnable mergeable rotations), and FlatQuant (Kronecker-product transforms). Its key conceptual departure is a systematic exploitation of equivariances and independencies in transformer architecture—specifically RoPE's block-diagonal rotation structure, per-head value independence, and RMSNorm's commutation with per-token scalers—to design transforms that are simultaneously expressive and mergeable into existing weights. The student-teacher (JSD) training framework is adopted from LLM-QAT, and the per-channel up/down scaling follows OSTQuant, but the three novel transforms (pre-RoPE, multihead value, pseudo-dynamic residual scaling) are new constructions grounded in specific commutation proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FPTQuant's practical significance lies in enabling static INT4 quantization with up to 3.9× speedup over FP16 without any custom kernels or non-mergeable operations, making it directly deployable on existing hardware stacks (TensorRT, Qualcomm AI Engine) that lack dynamic quantization support. The pseudo-dynamic residual scaling—normalizing the residual stream at virtually zero cost by reusing existing RMSNorm computations—is a simple but effective insight that reduces intra-block outliers by ~76%. Open questions include whether the gains transfer to MoE architectures, whether the expressivity-cost trade-off can be further narrowed for the hardest W4A4KV4 settings (where FlatQuant still leads), and whether these transforms help emerging microscaling FP4 formats.
