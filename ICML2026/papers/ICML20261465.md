## Problem

Standard multi-view fusion methods (attention aggregation, balanced optimal transport) force every observed view to fully contribute to the fused representation, making the output fragile when views are missing or corrupted by occlusion, sensor noise, or tracking errors. The paper addresses the lack of a principled, differentiable mechanism that can selectively downweight unreliable view evidence without hand-tuned thresholds or reconstruction objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261465/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes V views (RGB video, skeleton sequence, infrared frames) each encoded by a pretrained backbone (Video Swin-B or CTR-GCN) into token sequences, then pooled via learned-query cross-attention into J=16 latent atoms per view. A binary mask indicates which views are present; missing views are simply skipped. The output is a probability vector b_i ∈ Δ_K over K=16 shared learned prototypes (a discrete measure on a fixed support), which is passed to a two-layer MLP classification head producing C action-class logits. The fused representation always has fixed dimension K regardless of how many views are observed.

## Method

The core module computes a prototype-anchored unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) barycenter: each modality's J atoms form a discrete measure, and fusion finds the barycenter on K shared prototypes by minimizing the sum of KL-relaxed entropic UOT costs over observed modalities. The key mechanism is the generalized KL marginal penalty (controlled by τ), which lets the solver leave a fraction of view mass unmatched when transport cost is geometrically high, yielding a differentiable trimming effect without explicit thresholds. The solver is unrolled for T=20 generalized Sinkhorn iterations, making the entire fusion module end-to-end differentiable under standard backpropagation. A theoretical proposition shows that discarding any subset of atom mass incurs a KL penalty bounded by 2τ times the total dropped mass, independent of transport distances, formalizing why the method resists distant outliers. Modality importance weights λ are tied to the transported mass from the UOT solution, so views with more trimmed mass automatically receive less influence in the barycenter.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60 (X-Sub), UOT-Fusion achieves 96.0% Top-1 accuracy at 0% missing and 78.3% at 80% nominal missing, outperforming the strongest 2025 baseline RCMCL by +7.5 points at 80% missing (78.3% vs. 70.8%). On NTU RGB+D 120 (X-Set), the method reaches 94.1% at 0% and 74.6% at 80% missing, a +8.3-point margin over RCMCL (66.3%). Under missing-rate shift (trained at 20%, tested up to 80%), UOT-Fusion degrades by only 15.1 points (94.9%→79.8%) versus 31.2 points for Late Fusion and 23.5 points for MLA. The method adds only ~11% inference overhead (18.2 ms vs. 16.4 ms backbones alone) with 0.8M extra parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOT-Fusion is compared against 11 baselines spanning multi-modal fusion (Late Fusion, Channel Exchange), missing-view learning (DCP, CPSPAN, UIMC, CalMRL), and recent robust multi-modal methods (RCMCL, MV-GMN, MLA, DMRNet). At 0% missing, RCMCL marginally outperforms UOT-Fusion (96.2% vs. 96.0% on NTU 60 X-Sub; 94.3% vs. 94.1% on NTU 120 X-Set), consistent with the paper's positioning that UOT targets robustness rather than clean-regime gains. At 20% missing, UOT-Fusion already leads all baselines (93.7% vs. 93.1% RCMCL on NTU 60). The gap widens sharply with missingness: at 80% missing, UOT-Fusion achieves 78.3% on NTU 60 X-Sub, beating RCMCL by +7.5 points, MLA by +9.4, DMRNet by +10.1, and Late Fusion by +20.0. On NTU 120 X-Set at 80% missing, the margin over RCMCL is +8.3 points (74.6% vs. 66.3%). Under missing-rate shift (trained at 20%, tested at 80%), UOT-Fusion drops only 15.1 points versus 22.3–31.2 points for all baselines, confirming that the UOT trimming mechanism generalizes to unseen missingness levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes KL-relaxed unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat et al., 2018; Liero et al., 2018; Séjourné et al., 2019) and Wasserstein barycenter computation (Cuturi & Doucet, 2014; Claici et al., 2018) as a fusion mechanism for multi-view action recognition, departing from the dominant attention-based or reconstruction-based aggregation paradigm. The Perceiver-style learned-query pooling (Jaegle et al., 2021) is used to compress each modality into a small set of atoms, and the key architectural departure is anchoring the barycenter on a fixed shared prototype support so that the fused representation has fixed dimension and the transport problem remains small. The paper also introduces a mass-dependent modality weighting tied to the UOT solution itself, which distinguishes it from prior missing-view methods (DCP, CPSPAN, UIMC) that rely on separate uncertainty estimation or contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bounded-cost trimming property (Proposition 4.1) gives a clean, interpretable guarantee that outlier mass is penalized linearly in its total weight regardless of transport distance, which is a useful theoretical anchor for robustness claims that prior attention-based fusion methods lack. The practical impact is a plug-in fusion module with ~11% overhead that substantially narrows the accuracy gap under severe missingness (7–8 points at 80% missing) and missing-rate shift (15 vs. 31 point degradation). Key open questions include whether the geometric trimming mechanism generalizes beyond action recognition to other multimodal tasks, whether the fixed prototype support can be made adaptive to handle long-tail or out-of-distribution patterns, and whether the approach can be combined with other robustness techniques (e.g., adversarial training) to address the acknowledged failure mode where all views drift consistently.
