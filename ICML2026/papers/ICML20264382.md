## Problem

Generalized Zero-Shot Learning suffers from severe seen-class bias because training only covers seen categories while the test set mixes seen and unseen classes, causing unseen instances to be misclassified as seen. Existing mitigation strategies operate at the representation level—improving visual-semantic alignment or synthesizing unseen features—without explicitly modeling the label incompleteness of the mixed test space, and transductive methods typically require a pre-separated unseen-class pool that is impractical in deployment.

## Input / Output

PUFE consumes semantic embeddings (normalized vectors in R^d) produced by any pretrained embedding-based GZSL backbone, treating the backbone purely as a frozen feature generator. The seen-class training set provides positive-labeled samples, while the naturally mixed test set (containing both seen and unseen instances with no class-membership labels) serves as the unlabeled pool. The method outputs calibrated semantic prototypes for all categories, binary seen/unseen posterior scores for each test instance, and a final class label prediction refined by a confidence-guided similarity modulation.

## Method

PUFE recasts GZSL inference as a Positive-Unlabeled learning problem in semantic space, where seen categories are positives and the mixed test set is unlabeled. A dual-head network with asymmetric MLP capacities jointly estimates the instance-dependent labeling propensity f1(s) and the seen-class posterior f2(s) via maximum likelihood estimation, augmented by a coordination regularization that enforces consistency between the two heads and a discriminative geometry regularization that maximizes the posterior margin between seen and unseen instances while compacting the seen-class distribution. High-confidence pseudo-instances selected by thresholding the learned posterior are then used to compute per-class pseudo-centroids, which are fused with the original prototypes via an adaptive cosine-similarity-weighted interpolation to pull prototypes toward their true semantic neighborhoods. The entire pipeline is a lightweight postprocessing module requiring no backbone retraining, operating entirely at test time on the naturally mixed unlabeled set.

## Results

PUFE delivers consistent harmonic-mean (H) gains across all seven backbone architectures on CUB, SUN, and AWA2, with the largest improvements reaching +11.2 points (PSVMA on AWA2, H=86.6%) and +10.3 points (PSVMA on CUB, H=84.1%, a new state-of-the-art). ZeroMamba+PUFE achieves the highest reported H on AWA2 at 86.9%, and TransZero+PUFE also gains +10.4 on AWA2 (H=80.6%). The sole negative case is PSVMA on SUN (ΔH=−0.5%), attributed to the backbone's built-in prototype alignment leaving minimal calibration headroom in SUN's sparse attribute space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUFE outperforms all seven backbone baselines (TransZero, MSDN, IFMVZSL, PSVMA, ZSLViT, ZeroMamba, TPR) on CUB and AWA2, with H gains ranging from +3.8 to +11.2 points; on SUN the gains are smaller (+1.1 to +3.6) except for PSVMA which drops by 0.5 points. In the head-to-head plug-in comparison (Table 2), PUFE beats STHS, CCC, and PC on CUB and AWA2 for both TransZero and PSVMA backbones (e.g., PSVMA+PUFE reaches H=84.1 on CUB vs. 82.9 for STHS and 77.4 for CCC; H=86.6 on AWA2 vs. 75.6 for STHS and 84.9 for CCC). On SUN, however, STHS (H=54.2 with PSVMA) and PC (H=56.8 with PSVMA) surpass PUFE (H=51.8), as SUN's sparse, overlapping attribute space limits the calibration headroom that PUFE's prototype-shift mechanism can exploit. Overall, PUFE attains the best H in 12 of 14 backbone-dataset pairs and in 4 of 6 plug-in comparison groups, confirming its robustness as a universal post-processing module across Transformer, state-space, and VLM-based GZSL architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PUFE builds directly on the instance-dependent PU learning framework of Gong et al. (2021, LBE), which decouples labeling propensity from class posterior via MLE without requiring prior class proportions. It departs from two lines of GZSL work: (i) representation-level methods (embedding-based, generative) that refine features without explicitly modeling label incompleteness, and (ii) transductive GZSL methods that presuppose a pre-separated unseen-class pool. The key conceptual departure is recasting GZSL inference as a PU problem over the naturally mixed test set—treating seen categories as positives and the unlabeled mixture as the PU pool—thereby converting bias mitigation into a structured test-time adaptation task that requires no backbone retraining or prior class separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUFE offers a theoretically grounded (generalization bound + calibration guarantee), architecture-agnostic post-processing module that consistently improves seven diverse GZSL backbones by up to 11.2 points in harmonic mean, suggesting that explicit modeling of label incompleteness at test time is a more principled route to bias mitigation than representation-level fixes or cluster-structure assumptions. The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for practitioners to adopt bias-aware inference without retraining. Open questions include whether the calibration guarantee condition (ε_e < ε_s) holds under more extreme domain shifts, how the method behaves under streaming/online test distributions, and whether integration with large-scale self-supervised encoders (e.g., DINOv2) preserves the PU classifier's discriminative quality at higher semantic dimensions.
