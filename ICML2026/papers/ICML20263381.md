## Problem

Supervised crystal property prediction models are data-hungry, yet labeled crystal property data are scarce and unevenly distributed across properties. Existing diffusion-based pretraining methods (CrysDiff, DPF) operate directly in the high-dimensional heterogeneous feature space—jointly modeling discrete atom types, continuous lattice parameters, and periodic fractional coordinates—which demands complex multi-modality architectures and many diffusion steps, yielding representations constrained by this non-smooth input space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263381/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D crystal structure defined by an atom type matrix A (N×k one-hot), a fractional coordinate matrix X (N×3), and a lattice matrix L (3×3), and produces a scalar property value (e.g., formation energy in eV/atom, bandgap in eV, bulk/shear modulus in GPa). Pretraining consumes 380,740 unlabeled crystal structures from GNoME, while finetuning uses property-labeled subsets from JARVIS-DFT (55,722 structures, 9 properties) or Materials Project (69,239 structures, 4 properties). The encoder must preserve rotational, translational, and periodic invariance, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, accepting any symmetry-preserving crystal GNN or transformer as the encoder.

## Method

CrysLDNet performs two-stage latent pretraining followed by lightweight finetuning. In Stage 1, a symmetry-preserving encoder (PDDFormer) maps crystal structures into a compact latent space, and a decoder reconstructs atom types, coordinates, and lattice via cross-entropy and L2 losses with KL regularization. In Stage 2, flow matching (linear interpolation between a zero-centered Gaussian and the clean latent) is applied in this smoother latent space using a DiT denoiser, with the encoder and denoiser trained jointly to progressively enrich the latent representations. The key insight is that performing diffusion in a VAE-compressed latent space avoids the heterogeneous multi-modality problem of feature-space diffusion, enabling a single unified continuous denoising objective. For downstream prediction, the pretrained encoder is paired with a symmetric readout and a small MLP, finetuned end-to-end with MSE loss on limited labeled data.

## Results

CrysLDNet achieves the lowest MAE across all 13 evaluated properties on both JARVIS-DFT and Materials Project, outperforming the strongest supervised baseline (PDDFormer) by 4.26% on average on JARVIS and 4.90% on MP. Against the strongest pretrain-finetune baseline (DPF), CrysLDNet improves by 16.76% on JARVIS and 19.34% on MP. In low-data regimes (20–40% of training data), CrysLDNet(Matformer) surpasses even the stronger PDDFormer supervised model, with average improvements of 12.83% and 22.49% at 40% data. On the OQMD-EXP experimental dataset, CrysLDNet achieves the lowest MAE (0.068) in the 80% experimental finetuning setting, demonstrating effective DFT error mitigation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrysLDNet outperforms all 17 baselines across every property on both JARVIS-DFT and Materials Project. Against the strongest supervised model PDDFormer, CrysLDNet achieves average MAE reductions of 4.26% (JARVIS) and 4.90% (MP), with the largest gains on data-scarce properties like SLME (4.300→4.120) and Spillage (0.358→0.340). Against the strongest pretrain-finetune baseline DPF, improvements are substantially larger: 16.76% on JARVIS and 19.34% on MP, attributed to operating in a smoother latent space rather than the heterogeneous feature space. CrysDiff, the other diffusion-based pretraining method, is outperformed on all JARVIS properties (e.g., Bandgap MBJ: 0.287→0.238, a 17.1% reduction) but has no reported MP results. Among older pretrain-finetune methods (CrysXPP, Crystal Twins, CrysGNN), CrysLDNet achieves 2–3× lower MAE, e.g., on JARVIS Formation Energy (0.062/0.042/0.056 vs. 0.026). The backbone-agnostic ablation (Table 2) confirms that even with the weaker Matformer encoder, CrysLDNet still improves over vanilla Matformer by 7.53% (JARVIS) and 7.87% (MP), isolating the pretraining strategy as the primary source of gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pretrain-finetune paradigm for crystal property prediction by moving the diffusion process from the heterogeneous high-dimensional feature space (discrete atom types, periodic coordinates, continuous lattice) into a smooth VAE latent space, directly analogous to how Stable Diffusion operates in image generation. It builds on flow matching formulations (Lipman et al., 2022; Joshi et al., 2025) for the denoising objective and departs from prior diffusion-based crystal pretraining methods (CrysDiff, DPF) that jointly model all structural components with separate diffusion mechanisms (D3PM for atom types, score-based for coordinates, DDPM for lattice). The backbone-agnostic design, where the VAE encoder can be swapped between Matformer and PDDFormer without modifying the latent diffusion pipeline, is a practical departure from prior methods that tightly couple the pretraining objective to a specific architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrysLDNet addresses the core data-scarcity bottleneck in crystal property prediction, with the largest gains (12–22%) appearing in low-data regimes where supervised models struggle most. The backbone-agnostic design makes the framework future-proof: as stronger crystal encoders emerge, they can be plugged in without redesigning the pretraining pipeline, and the paper demonstrates a 10–12% improvement simply by upgrading from Matformer to PDDFormer. Limitations include dependence on symmetry-preserving encoders, pretraining on a fixed 380K GNoME corpus, and the fact that the framework has not yet been extended to non-periodic or surface systems. An open question is whether the latent diffusion pretraining strategy transfers to other atomistic domains (molecules, proteins) where similar heterogeneous feature spaces exist.
