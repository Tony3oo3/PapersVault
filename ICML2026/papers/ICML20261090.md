## Problem

Existing bias-mitigation strategies for LLMs either require expensive retraining, rely on external classifiers that inherit their own blind spots, or apply global representation-space projections that cannot adapt to prompt-specific semantics. Critically, none provide per-step statistical guarantees at decode time specifying which tokens can be safely emitted while controlling failure risk, and fairness is typically enforced only at an aggregate level rather than as local counterfactual parity at each decoding step.

## Input / Output

COFT takes a text prompt containing sensitive spans (identified via NER or user specification) and a frozen causal LM as input, and produces debiased chain-of-thought reasoning text as output. At each decoding step, the method runs two parallel forward passes on the same generated prefix—one on the factual prompt and one on a length-preserving masked version where sensitive spans are replaced by sentinel tokens—so the two branches remain token-index aligned. The output at each step is a certified candidate token set (with distribution-free marginal coverage at a user-chosen risk level α) from which the next token is sampled using the fused factual distribution.

## Method

COFT is a training-free, three-stage decoding pipeline. Stage I constructs a masked counterfactual prompt by replacing sensitive spans with length-preserving sentinel tokens, maintaining positional alignment between branches. Stage II applies counterfactual logit fusion: a convex interpolation in logit space, ẑ_t = (1−λ)z_t^F + λz_t^CF, which by the softmax identity yields a normalized geometric mixture π̂_t(v) ∝ (π_t^F(v))^(1−λ)(π_t^CF(v))^λ, monotonically contracting KL divergence toward the masked view as λ increases. Stage III imposes a dual-branch split-conformal filter using the nonconformity score s_t(v) = 1 − min{π̂_t(v), π_t^CF(v)}, calibrated offline to a per-position threshold τ_t, admitting only tokens simultaneously probable under both branches. The key theoretical result is that under exchangeability, this dual-branch score provides distribution-free marginal coverage P[v*_t ∈ C_t under both p and p̃] ≥ 1−α, while the geometric-mixture structure of fusion ensures the total-variation gap between the two restricted conditionals is bounded and shrinks with λ. The entire procedure requires no gradients, no auxiliary classifiers, and no weight access, adding only one cached forward pass (≤11% overhead).

## Results

On LLaMA-2-13B, COFT achieves an average rank of 1.0 across all six bias benchmarks (SS=0.26, CP Acc=63.5, BBQ=0.14, BOLD=0.079, Utrecht DP=0.118, COMPAS=0.119), reducing bias by 30–55% (median 38%) relative to vanilla decoding and 20–40% relative to the strongest inference-time baseline (DT-CD). On Mistral-7B-Instruct, COFT similarly attains rank 1.0 (SS=0.24, CP Acc=64.7, BBQ=0.12, BOLD=0.076, Utrecht DP=0.112, COMPAS=0.113). Task utility is preserved within ±0.2 points of vanilla (e.g., GSM8K 47.5 vs. 47.9 on LLaMA-2-13B), and the computational overhead is 10.2% with ≤0.8 GB additional memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COFT outperforms all four primary inference-time baselines on every bias metric for both representative models. Against SDD (Schick et al., 2021), COFT reduces SS bias from 0.36 to 0.26 (−28%) and BBQ from 0.22 to 0.14 (−36%) on LLaMA-2-13B, while SDD also degrades utility (GSM8K 47.1 vs. COFT's 47.5). Against DExperts (Liu et al., 2021), COFT achieves a 21% lower SS score (0.26 vs. 0.33) and 21% lower BOLD toxicity (0.079 vs. 0.099), with DExperts incurring a larger utility drop (GSM8K 46.8). Against DT-CD, the closest conformal-based baseline without counterfactual reasoning, COFT still gains 16% on SS (0.26 vs. 0.31) and 26% on BBQ (0.14 vs. 0.19), confirming that the dual-branch counterfactual mechanism adds value beyond single-branch conformal filtering. The two train-time baselines (CDA from Zhao et al., 2018 and Adversarial LM-Head from Elazar & Goldberg, 2018) achieve intermediate bias scores (SS 0.31 and 0.30 respectively on LLaMA-2-13B) but require weight updates and are outside COFT's frozen-weights threat model; COFT matches or beats them while remaining training-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

COFT sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: inference-time debiasing (DExperts, GeDi, SDD) and conformal prediction for LLMs (Zhao et al., 2023; Cherian et al., 2024). Prior debiasing methods lack distribution-free statistical guarantees, while prior conformal decoding work (e.g., DT-CD) does not enforce counterfactual invariance. The paper's core contribution is unifying counterfactual masking (from the fairness literature, Kusner et al. 2017; Chiappa 2019) with a lightweight logit-space fusion and a dual-branch split-conformal calibration into a single training-free decoding pipeline, drawing on covariate-shift corrections (Tibshirani et al., 2019) for robustness under distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COFT offers a practical, auditable path to deploying frozen LLMs in sensitive domains with quantified per-step risk bounds, without retraining or auxiliary classifiers. Key limitations include reliance on exchangeability (violated if calibration and test-time decoding policies diverge), dependence on accurate sensitive-span detection (NER or user-specified), and stepwise rather than end-to-end sequence-level guarantees. Open questions include whether conditional (input-conditional) coverage can be achieved, how the method handles implicit or pervasive bias cues that resist localized masking, and whether the union-bound composition across steps remains tight for long CoT chains.
