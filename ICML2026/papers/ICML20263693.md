## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks manipulate dense, redundant intermediate features, causing overfitting to surrogate models and poor black-box transferability. While recent SVD-based attacks exploit low-rank subspaces, they are restricted to single-layer optimization and discard fine-grained, layer-dependent discriminative information. The paper addresses the gap of jointly modeling complementary global semantic (low-rank) and localized discriminative (sparse) structures across hierarchical network depths to produce more transferable perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (224×224, ImageNet) and its ground-truth class label, and produces an adversarial example with an ℓ∞-bounded perturbation (ε=16). Internally, it extracts intermediate feature maps at three selected depths (shallow, middle, deep) of a surrogate CNN or ViT, reshapes them into channel×spatial matrices, and decomposes each into low-rank and sparse components. The final output is a single perturbed image optimized via momentum gradient descent over 10 iterations, evaluated for transferability across 24+ target architectures.

## Method

LRS-Attack decomposes each intermediate feature matrix H_l into a low-rank component L_l (capturing global semantic directions) and a sparse component S_l (capturing localized discriminative patterns) via alternating optimization: hard-thresholding for the sparse update and a Warm-started Alternating Low-rank Approximation (WALA) solver for the low-rank update, which reuses previous-iteration factors with QR-projected gradient steps to avoid expensive truncated SVD. A channel-wise rescaling preconditioner (D_l) balances feature magnitudes before decomposition to prevent degenerate solutions. A Hierarchical Mixture of Robust Experts assigns depth-dependent strategies—sparse-dominant for shallow layers, balanced low-rank/sparse for middle layers, and low-rank-dominant for deep layers—each producing separate logits that are aggregated with equal weights alongside the original dense logits. The resulting fused loss is optimized via MI-FGSM, and the depth-aware scheduling (decreasing sparsity k, increasing rank r with depth) aligns the decomposition with the natural evolution of DNN representations from local textures to global semantics.

## Results

On the ImageNet benchmark, LRS-Attack consistently improves black-box transferability over both the no-decomposition baseline (W/O) and the single-layer SVD baseline across all tested surrogate models and attack frameworks. With Inc-v3 as surrogate and MI-FGSM, LRS achieves 61.8% average ASR on CNN targets (vs. 51.7% W/O and 55.5% SVD), and 24.3% on ViT targets (vs. 21.0% and 21.3%). The largest gains appear with DenseNet-121 surrogate: SI-NI-FGSM+LRS reaches 91.5% AvgCNN and 60.4% AvgViT, surpassing W/O (88.4%/56.4%) and SVD (88.8%/53.1%). Under defense settings, LRS also leads, e.g., 48.5% AvgDef with Inc-v3+SI-NI-FGSM vs. 45.5% (W/O) and 46.8% (SVD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRS-Attack is compared against two baselines: (1) W/O, the original attack without any feature decomposition, and (2) SVD, the single-layer low-rank decomposition from Weng et al. (2024). LRS wins over both baselines in every configuration tested across all 21 surrogate-attack combinations (3 CNN surrogates × 7 gradient attacks + 3 ViT surrogates × 4 ViT attacks). The improvement over W/O ranges from ~2% (e.g., Vgg-19+PI-FGSM: 71.1% vs. 69.3% AvgCNN) to ~7% (e.g., DenseNet-121+MI-FGSM: 83.5% vs. 76.3% AvgCNN). Over SVD, gains are typically 1–4% on AvgCNN and 2–4% on AvgViT, with the largest ViT gain being DeiT-B+MIM (86.6% vs. 81.2% AvgViT). Notably, SVD occasionally underperforms W/O (e.g., Vgg-19+DI-FGSM: 63.3% vs. 64.6%), whereas LRS never degrades below W/O, indicating that the sparse component compensates for information lost in pure low-rank truncation. Under defense settings (HGD, JPEG, R&P, NIPS_r3), LRS maintains its lead, e.g., 49.0% AvgDef with Inc-v3+VMI-FGSM vs. 44.4% (W/O) and 47.9% (SVD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based adversarial attack of Weng et al. (2024), which imposed a single-layer low-rank constraint on intermediate features, by (i) adding a sparse component via Robust PCA-style decomposition (Wright et al., 2009; Zhou & Tao, 2011), (ii) generalizing from one layer to a hierarchical mixture of depth-dependent experts, and (iii) replacing exact truncated SVD with a warm-started alternating low-rank approximation (WALA) built on QR-projected subspace iteration (Jain et al., 2013). The channel-wise rescaling preconditioner is borrowed from LLM pruning (Sun et al., 2024) and repurposed here to stabilize the decomposition under iterative adversarial updates. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior feature-level attacks (FDA, FIA, NAA) that operate on dense, redundant activations by explicitly separating global semantic structure (low-rank) from localized discriminative patterns (sparse) at each depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured decomposition offers a principled way to reduce surrogate-model overfitting in black-box attacks, and the consistent 2–6% ASR gains across 20+ target architectures and four defense methods make it a practical drop-in enhancement for existing FGSM-family pipelines. The CKA analysis revealing that LRS preserves *more* cross-model feature similarity than pure SVD while still attacking more effectively is a useful diagnostic: it suggests the sparse component carries model-specific vulnerability while the low-rank component carries transferable semantics. Open questions include whether the fixed S-M-L expert assignment is optimal across all architectures, whether the approach extends to non-ImageNet domains or generative models, and whether the WALA approximation quality degrades under very tight iteration budgets (T < 10).
