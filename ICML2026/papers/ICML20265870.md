## Problem

Whether multi-modal LLMs systematically favor one modality over another when processing co-occurring visual and textual evidence remains insufficiently studied. Prior evaluations either isolate modalities (unimodal-only inputs) or rely on Shapley-value attribution, neither of which reflects how models arbitrate between modalities in realistic multi-modal settings where information naturally co-occurs. No controlled benchmark free of confounding factors (internal knowledge, varying comprehension ability) exists, and no training-free method can explicitly steer this preference.

## Input / Output

The MC² benchmark presents each model with an image, a deliberately conflicting textual context, and a binary-choice question, where the image and text independently support different answers. Before conflict evaluation, all models must achieve >95% accuracy on each modality in isolation, and position bias is eliminated by requiring consistent answers under swapped choice order. The evaluation output is a Vision Ratio (S_vision / (S_vision + S_text)) quantifying preference; the steering method outputs a per-layer direction vector in the model's latent space that, when injected during inference, shifts the model's responses toward the targeted modality without any parameter updates.

## Method

The proposed training-free method has two stages. In probing, paired inputs with vision-preference and text-preference instructions are fed through the model, and the average difference of last-token hidden states across N samples at each layer yields a modality preference direction vector u_ℓ^t; the target layer (e.g., 20–23 for Qwen2.5VL-7B) is selected where this direction shows high absolute magnitude and low variance, confirmed by a mechanistic logit-lens analysis showing modality preference first emerges at these layers. In steering, the direction is scaled by a principled weight w = (1/N) Σ ||x_i,ℓ^t|| / ||u_ℓ^t|| that aligns the probed direction's mean norm with the original hidden-state distribution, avoiding the exhaustive grid search used in prior activation-steering work. During inference, the scaled vector is added uniformly to all tokens (both vision and text) at the selected layer at every decoding step; steering toward vision simply reverses the sign. Unlike ICV's token-wise steering, this unified application prevents out-of-distribution distortion between modality representations, and the method requires no fine-tuning, additional data, or labels.

## Results

On the MC² benchmark, the proposed representation-engineering steering method achieves S_text of 63.6% on Qwen2.5VL-7B and 42.8% on InternVL3-8B (steering toward text), and S_vision of 48.1% on Qwen2VL-7B and 57.1% on OneVision-7B (steering toward vision), consistently outperforming all training-free baselines. On downstream tasks, the method reaches 62.3% on MathVista, 76.6% on TallyQA, and 53.2% on VSR (accuracy), and 10.2/19.89 BLEU on AmbigCaps (En→Tr / Tr→En), surpassing both CoT and InstDesign. On the PhD visual understanding benchmark, steering Qwen2VL-7B toward vision improves the average score from 6.0/27.6 (MLLM baseline) to 18.4/36.1 (PhD-icc/PhD-iac), a gain of over 6 percentage points over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four training-free baselines: zero-shot MLLM, instruction design (InstDesign), chain-of-thought (CoT), and four-shot in-context learning (FewShot). The proposed method wins on all four MC² steering settings, with the largest margin over FewShot on Qwen2VL-7B vision steering (48.1 vs. 17.2, a 30.9-point gap) and over CoT on InternVL3-8B text steering (42.8 vs. 36.7). On downstream reasoning tasks (Table 2), the method beats InstDesign by 3.3 points on MathVista (62.3 vs. 59.0), 2.2 on TallyQA (76.6 vs. 74.4), and 1.9 on VSR (53.2 vs. 51.3), and gains 1.33 BLEU over the strongest baseline on AmbigCaps. The paper also contrasts with ICV (In-Context Vectors, Liu et al. 2023b), showing that ICV's token-wise steering causes significant degradation on PhD (13.6/30.1 vs. the proposed method's 18.4/36.1 for Qwen2VL-7B), attributed to ICV applying disparate steering vectors to vision and text tokens that distort their relative distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023) from steering abstract properties like sentiment or truthfulness to the specific case of modality preference in MLLMs, adapting the probe-then-steer framework (compute a direction from paired activations, add a scaled vector to hidden states) to control whether a model favors visual or textual evidence. It departs from prior modality-preference studies that either isolate modalities (unimodal comparison, Shapley-value attribution) or examine data-level causes of bias, by introducing a controlled conflict-pair evaluation (MC²) where both modalities co-occur and confounds like internal knowledge and comprehension ability are explicitly neutralized. The benchmark construction draws on TDIUC/MSCOCO imagery and LLM-generated conflicting textual contexts, while the steering method is directly inspired by activation-addition and in-context vector approaches but applies a unified (non-token-wise) vector to avoid distorting the vision-text representation distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MC² benchmark provides the first controlled, confound-free protocol for quantifying modality preference across MLLMs, and the finding that Vision Ratio correlates with visual understanding at ρ=0.964 makes it a lightweight diagnostic for model evaluation. The training-free steering method offers a practical, fine-tuning-free lever for adjusting modality reliance, which is relevant for reducing hallucination (steer toward vision) or improving translation grounding (steer toward text). Open questions remain: the evaluation covers only image+text and 8 task types, the 'optimal' Vision Ratio is task-dependent rather than universally 0.5, and whether the steering generalizes to audio/video modalities or to models with fundamentally different architectures (e.g., non-decoder-only) is untested.
