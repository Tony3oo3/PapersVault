## Problem

Conformal prediction sets can be translated into optimal risk-averse decision policies, but existing results (Kiyani et al., 2025b) only provide marginal safety guarantees—safety on average across the population. In safety-critical settings like medical treatment, a high-risk action may appear acceptable because its risks are averaged out by safer decisions elsewhere. This paper introduces action-conditional safety guarantees that ensure each specific action the decision maker takes leads to high utility with probability at least 1−α, conditioned on that action being selected.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained probabilistic model f (e.g., Inception-v3 fine-tuned on chest X-rays, or a rating predictor for recommender systems), a finite discrete action space A, a utility function u: A×Y→R, n calibration samples, and a miscoverage level α. It outputs a prediction set C(x) constructed to satisfy action-conditional coverage (i.e., P(Y∈C(X) | max-min action = a) ≥ 1−α for every a), from which the action a(x) = argmax_a min_{y∈C(x)} u(a,y) and utility certificate ν(x) are derived. The key structural constraint is that the conditioning event in the coverage guarantee is endogenous to the prediction set itself, creating a feedback loop absent in standard conformal calibration.

## Method

The paper has two complementary contributions. At the population level, it reparameterizes the prediction-set optimization via a pointwise coverage function t(x) and proves strong duality: the optimal solution is characterized by a |A|-dimensional threshold vector λ, with each coordinate λ_a controlling the coverage level for action a. At the finite-sample level, it introduces AC-RAC, a calibration algorithm that defines action-specific nonconformity scores λ_a*(x,y) (the minimum λ_a needed for y to enter the prediction set when action a is selected) and calibrates λ by minimizing a pinball-loss objective over n+1 exchangeable points. A key structural result (Lemma 4.1) shows that, conditioned on the induced action, set membership reduces to the scalar test λ_a ≥ λ_a*(x,Y), enabling efficient projected subgradient optimization. The resulting prediction sets achieve distribution-free action-conditional coverage with error decaying at rate |A|/(n·P(action=a)), and the max-min policy applied to these sets satisfies the original action-conditional risk-averse objective.

## Results

On the COVID-19 Radiography Database (medical diagnosis) at α=0.05, AC-RAC is the only method achieving valid action-conditional coverage across all four actions, while RAC, score-1, and score-2 systematically over- or under-cover. The cost of this stronger guarantee is modest: AC-RAC increases mean prediction-set size relative to RAC by only ~3.7% (3.373 vs. 3.252) and has comparable FDR (0.699 vs. 0.682). On the recommender-system task, AC-RAC again uniquely achieves calibrated action-conditional coverage while maintaining worst-case utility comparable to RAC and substantially reducing the rate of critical recommendation errors. Across both tasks, AC-RAC achieves higher worst-case (max-min) utility than score-1 and score-2, and slightly lower utility than RAC, reflecting the expected trade-off between marginal and action-conditional safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAC (Kiyani et al., 2025b), the state-of-the-art marginal-safety method, AC-RAC wins decisively on action-conditional miscoverage (the only method with valid per-action coverage) and critical error rate, at the cost of a small utility gap (slightly lower max-min utility, as RAC is optimized for the weaker marginal constraint). Against score-1 (Sadinle et al., 2019, inverse-probability conformal) and score-2 (Romano et al., 2020, cumulative-tail-mass conformal), AC-RAC dominates on both action-conditional coverage and worst-case utility; these baselines miscalibrate coverage across actions and yield lower conservative utility. Against Calibrated Best-Response (Noarov et al., 2023), which selects actions by maximizing expected utility under a decision-calibrated forecast, AC-RAC provides distribution-free action-conditional safety guarantees that CBR entirely lacks, and reduces critical (catastrophic) decision errors by a significant margin. Notably, in the medical task, baselines RAC, score-1, score-2, and CBR never select the Quarantine action, making their action-conditional error undefined for it, whereas AC-RAC selects Quarantine on 2.72% of test instances and maintains valid coverage for this rare action.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Kiyani et al. (2025b), which established a two-way equivalence between conformal prediction sets and risk-averse decision policies under marginal safety guarantees, by replacing the marginal constraint with an action-conditional one. The finite-sample calibration algorithm builds on the debiasing methodology of Gibbs et al. (2025) for conditional conformal prediction, but introduces a novel action-specific nonconformity score to handle the endogenous conditioning event (the selected action depends on the prediction set itself). Conceptually, the work positions itself as a risk-averse analogue of decision calibration (Foster & Vohra, 1997; Zhao et al., 2021; Noarov et al., 2023), which provides calibrated forecasts for risk-neutral agents, whereas here prediction sets serve as surrogates for quantile-based safety guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-sample, distribution-free action-conditional coverage guarantee in conformal prediction, addressing a gap where marginal guarantees can be dangerously misleading in safety-critical settings (e.g., a high-risk medical action appearing safe only because its risk is averaged out by safer decisions elsewhere). The key limitation is the restriction to finite discrete action spaces, and the one-directional correspondence (AC-CPO → AC-DPO) means the conformal surrogate can be conservative relative to the true optimum. Open questions include extending to continuous or combinatorial action spaces, replacing quantile-based VaR with richer risk measures like CVaR, and parameterizing the action-dependent threshold λ(a) with flexible function classes while preserving finite-sample validity.
