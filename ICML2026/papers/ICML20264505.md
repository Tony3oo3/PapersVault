## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods inject prior information uniformly into diffusion models, ignoring the hierarchical functional divergence of UNet layers. Degradation priors alone fail to ensure semantic plausibility under complex degradations, while semantic priors injected into shallow layers disrupt spatial structures, causing blurring and geometric distortions. The paper addresses how to exploit heterogeneous priors in a layer-aware manner that simultaneously preserves local geometric integrity and global content consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image as input and additionally extracts auxiliary structural cues (depth maps via Depth-Anything, segmentation maps via SegFormer, and DoG edge features) from the same input. It produces a restored high-quality RGB image by solving a reverse stochastic differential equation (IR-SDE) over 100 denoising steps with noise level σ=50. The degradation classifier used during training is discarded at inference, so only the continuous degradation features and the three prior streams condition the restoration.

## Method

TPGDiff coordinates three complementary priors across the UNet backbone in a layer-aware fashion. Semantic priors are obtained via a CLIP-based teacher-student distillation scheme (teacher encodes GT, student encodes LQ with cosine-similarity loss) and are injected into deep UNet layers through cross-attention to constrain global content. Structural priors aggregate depth, segmentation, and DoG tokens via a Structural Token Aggregator (learnable latent tokens with cross-attention) and are injected into shallow layers through a lightweight FiLM-style adapter that predicts channel-wise scale and shift parameters. Degradation priors are extracted by a frozen CLIP visual encoder and modulated into the diffusion time embedding via a learnable temporal adapter, enabling stage-adaptive denoising control across all timesteps. The key design principle is matching each prior type to the layer depth where its representational granularity is most relevant, rather than applying all priors uniformly.

## Results

On the multi-task all-in-one benchmark (Table 4), TPGDiff achieves the best average PSNR of 30.60 dB and SSIM of 0.908 across five degradation types (low-light, deblurring, deraining, dehazing, denoising), outperforming the next-best method VLU-Net (30.11 dB / 0.905). On single-task benchmarks (Table 1), TPGDiff leads on 8 out of 9 degradation types in PSNR, with notable margins such as +1.97 dB on LOL-v2 (24.77 vs. 22.16 for MPerceiver) and +2.00 dB on RESIDE dehazing (32.87 vs. 30.87 for AdaIR/DiffRes). On unknown-degradation generalization (POLED/TOLED), TPGDiff achieves the best SSIM (0.511/0.899) and LPIPS (0.644/0.145), confirming strong cross-domain transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPGDiff outperforms all 33 compared baselines on the majority of metrics across 9 single-task benchmarks, 5 multi-task benchmarks, and 2 unknown-degradation benchmarks. The largest margins appear on challenging degradations: +5.02 dB PSNR over FounDIR-V2 on WeatherBench desnowing (28.17 vs. 23.15), +2.00 dB over AdaIR/DiffRes on RESIDE dehazing (32.87 vs. 30.87), and +1.97 dB over MPerceiver on LOL-v2 low-light (24.77 vs. 22.16). The closest competitors are VLU-Net and PoolNet on the multi-task setting, where TPGDiff leads by 0.49 dB average PSNR and 0.003 SSIM. TPGDiff's only notable losses are on RDNet SSIM (0.910 vs. MaskDCPT's 0.942) and LOL-v2 FID (46.02 vs. MPerceiver's 45.90), both marginal. On unknown-degradation POLED/TOLED, TPGDiff achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS, surpassing DiffUIR (the strongest prior method) by 0.087 SSIM on POLED and 0.012 SSIM on TOLED, while DiffUIR retains a slight PSNR edge on POLED (15.62 vs. 15.64 — essentially tied).

## Relation to Prior Work

TPGDiff builds directly on the IR-SDE diffusion framework (Luo et al., 2023b) as its restoration backbone and extends the all-in-one image restoration paradigm established by AirNet, PromptIR, and DA-CLIP. Its key conceptual departure is the rejection of uniform prior injection: rather than feeding all conditioning signals into the same layers (as in DiffRes, MPerceiver, or DA-CLIP), it assigns semantic priors to deep UNet layers via cross-attention, structural priors to shallow layers via a lightweight adapter, and degradation priors to the time embedding across all timesteps. The structural prior pipeline draws on Perceiver-style token aggregation and off-the-shelf depth/segmentation models, while the semantic prior uses a CLIP-based teacher-student distillation scheme to obtain degradation-invariant content representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-aware prior coordination strategy offers a principled design guideline for future diffusion-based restoration work: matching the granularity of a prior (global semantics vs. local geometry vs. global corruption level) to the representational role of the corresponding network layer. A practical limitation is the dependence on multiple pretrained auxiliary models (CLIP, Depth-Anything, SegFormer) at inference, which adds computational overhead and potential failure modes if those models are out-of-distribution. Open questions include whether the hierarchical injection strategy transfers to non-UNet diffusion backbones (e.g., DiT), whether the three structural cues remain necessary at larger model scale, and how the method behaves under truly unknown compound degradations beyond the POLED/TOLED benchmarks.
