## Problem

Standard LLM-based evolutionary search for scientific hypotheses prioritizes optimization over exploration, causing strong selection pressure that collapses diversity and leads to premature convergence. In real discovery settings, validation is noisy and expensive, so scientists need a set of diverse, high-quality alternative hypotheses rather than a single best one. The paper asks how to efficiently produce such diverse candidate sets under a fixed validation budget without sacrificing quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial candidate population (e.g., 120 molecules from ZINC-250K or a seed program buffer), a black-box oracle scoring function, and a task specification, and operates under a fixed evaluation budget (10,000 oracle calls for molecular discovery, 1,000 for equation and algorithm discovery). It outputs a set of diverse, high-quality hypotheses—molecules, symbolic equations, or optimization algorithms—along with quality metrics (Top-10 AUC, BestAcc, docking scores) and diversity metrics (Tanimoto/CodeBERT pairwise distances, unique scaffold counts). The LLM serves as the evolutionary operator, generating offspring from parent pairs via mutation/crossover prompts, while the oracle provides scores used for selection and swap acceptance.

## Method

EvoDiverse reformulates LLM-based evolutionary search as approximate sampling from a Boltzmann distribution with an evolving power factor, then applies parallel tempering to maintain diversity. Two concurrently evolving pools operate at different inverse temperatures (e.g., β₁=0.8 cold/exploitative, β₂=0.2 hot/exploratory), with stochastic Boltzmann-weighted selection replacing deterministic top-k to tune selection pressure per pool. A Metropolis-Hastings swap operator, adapted from classical PT, exchanges candidates between pools with an acceptance ratio that preserves the product stationary distribution, preventing incompatible candidates from disrupting convergence. An adaptive powering factor ξ is tuned online to keep the swap acceptance rate in a target range (30–50%), aligning convergence speeds across temperatures. Pool-specific LLM prompts further differentiate exploration (novel scaffolds) from exploitation (refining known motifs), and the framework generalizes to multiple temperature levels and non-reversible PT schemes.

## Results

On molecular discovery (JNK3 and GSK3β), EvoDiverse achieves Top-10 AUC of 0.63±0.05 and 0.76±0.02 respectively, outperforming the MOLLEO baseline (0.58 and 0.70) while also improving Top-10 Avg scores (0.74 vs 0.66 on JNK3; 0.82 vs 0.82 on GSK3β). On equation discovery across four scientific domains (LLM-SRBench), EvoDiverse consistently achieves the highest diversity (e.g., 0.305 on Physics vs 0.294 Tempering) and best BestAcc0.1 (e.g., 0.408 on Physics vs 0.301 Tempering, 0.212 on Biology vs 0.104 Tempering). On the circle packing algorithm discovery task (n=26), EvoDiverse achieves the best sum of radii (2.5461) and highest diversity (0.78) simultaneously, surpassing EA (2.4986, 0.61), Island (2.4247, 0.48), and Ensemble (2.4105, 0.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MOLLEO (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), the primary single-pool LLM-evolutionary baseline, EvoDiverse wins on both JNK3 (Top-10 AUC 0.63 vs 0.58, Top-10 Avg 0.74 vs 0.66) and GSK3β (AUC 0.76 vs 0.70), with the AUC gain on JNK3 being statistically significant (p=0.011). Against the LLM-SR Ensemble baseline (Shojaee et al., ICLR 2025) on equation discovery, EvoDiverse dominates on both quality and diversity across all four domains—for example, on Biology it doubles BestAcc0.1 (0.212 vs 0.104) while also improving diversity (0.290 vs 0.255). Against GraphGA (Jensen, 2019), the adapted EvoDiverse-GraphGA variant achieves higher Top-10 AUC on GSK3β (0.67 vs 0.59) and JNK3 (0.43 vs 0.35), confirming the PT framework transfers beyond LLM-based operators. Against PySR (Cranmer, 2023), the state-of-the-art non-LLM symbolic regression baseline, the paper reports comparison in the appendix; EvoDiverse's LLM-based approach is competitive on quality while offering substantially higher hypothesis diversity, which PySR does not explicitly optimize for.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two well-developed but previously disconnected lines of work: LLM-augmented evolutionary algorithms for scientific discovery (MOLLEO, LLM-SR, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve) and parallel tempering (replica exchange) from statistical physics. The key conceptual departure is reframing hypothesis search as approximate sampling from a Boltzmann distribution with an evolving power factor, rather than pure optimization, and then importing the Metropolis-Hastings swap operator from PT to provide principled, detailed-balance-preserving communication between temperature levels. This distinguishes EvoDiverse from island-model EAs (which use heuristic migration that can disrupt convergence) and from pure sampling approaches (which are computationally infeasible with LLMs due to the combinatorial hypothesis space and limited validation budget).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underappreciated bottleneck in LLM-based scientific discovery: diversity collapse under selection pressure, which limits the set of viable candidates available for expensive downstream validation. The PT swap mechanism offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc multi-population strategies, and its consistent gains across molecular, equation, and algorithm discovery suggest broad applicability. Key limitations include the budget-dependent trade-off (more pools = fewer evaluations per pool), task-specific hyperparameter tuning (temperature gap, swap frequency, energy scaling), and the approximate nature of the PT procedure since LLM populations do not follow known stationary distributions. Open questions include automating the energy/ladder choices, extending the framework to tree-search-based discovery, and whether fine-tuning LLMs can further improve intrinsic sample diversity.
