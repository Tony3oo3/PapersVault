## Problem

Flow matching models do not learn strong semantic representations on their own, and the dominant remedy—aligning features with a frozen external encoder (e.g., DINOv2 via REPA)—exhibits unexpected scaling behavior where stronger encoders degrade generation, fails to generalize across modalities (video, audio), and creates a bottleneck that limits the benefits of scaling the generative model. The paper asks whether representation learning can be integrated directly into the flow matching objective without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of modality-specific autoencoders (SD-VAE, FLUX.2 AE, Wan2.2 AE, Songbloom AE), receiving text prompts, class labels, or conditioning images and producing 256×256 images (256 tokens), 45-frame 192p videos (~3k tokens), 10-second audio clips (250 tokens), or 6-step robot action vectors. A key structural detail is that each token in the sequence is conditioned on its own per-token noise timestep (a vector τ ∈ ℝ^N rather than a scalar), enabling heterogeneous corruption across the input.

## Method

Self-Flow introduces Dual-Timestep Scheduling (DTS), which samples two timesteps t and s from the noise distribution and assigns the higher one to a random subset of tokens (mask ratio R_M ≤ 0.5) while the lower one applies to the rest, creating an information asymmetry that preserves the marginal per-token noise distribution. A student network f_θ processes the heterogeneously-noised input x_τ, while an EMA teacher f_θ′ processes the cleaner input x_{τ_min} (all tokens at min(t,s)). The training objective combines the standard rectified-flow velocity loss on x_τ with a cosine-similarity representation loss that forces the student's early-layer features to match the teacher's deeper-layer features, compelling the model to use cleaner tokens to infer noisier ones and thereby learn global semantic structure. No external encoder is required; the teacher is simply an EMA copy of the student, and the entire framework is modality-agnostic, supporting joint multi-modal training with per-modality loss weightings.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, Self-Flow achieves FID 5.70 at 4M steps, outperforming REPA (5.89) without any external encoder and SiT-XL/2 (8.30 at 7M steps). On text-to-image generation, Self-Flow reaches FID 3.61 and FD-DINO 167.98, beating all external and internal alignment baselines. For video generation, Self-Flow achieves FVD 47.81 and framewise FID 8.92, a ~2-point FVD margin over the next best method. On audio, Self-Flow attains the best FAD across all CLAP variants (145.645 CLAP, 0.1634 CLAP-M, 0.1001 CLAP-A).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Flow outperforms REPA (DINOv2) on every T2I metric: FID 3.61 vs 3.92, FD-DINO 167.98 vs 173.35, and CLIP 30.88 vs 30.67, despite REPA explicitly aligning with DINOv2 features. Against the strongest internal-alignment baseline SRA, Self-Flow wins on FID (3.61 vs 3.70), video FVD (47.81 vs 49.75), and audio CLAP (145.645 vs 147.215). External alignment with domain-specific encoders (SigLIP 2 for T2I, V-JEPA 2 and Depth Anything 3 for video, MERT for audio) consistently underperforms or even degrades generation relative to vanilla flow matching, whereas Self-Flow improves across all modalities. On ImageNet, Self-Flow (FID 5.70) beats REPA (5.89) at the same 4M-step budget, and in scaling experiments the 625M Self-Flow model surpasses the 1B REPA model. For video, external alignment is actively harmful: DINOv2 (FVD 49.59), Depth Anything 3 (51.52), and V-JEPA 2 (53.55) all trail vanilla flow matching (50.95) or Self-Flow (47.81).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the rectified flow matching framework (Liu et al., 2022) and the representation alignment paradigm introduced by REPA (Yu et al., 2024), which showed that aligning flow model features with a frozen external encoder (e.g., DINOv2) accelerates convergence and improves generation quality. It departs from both external-alignment methods (REPA, SigLIP 2, V-JEPA 2) and internal-alignment methods (SRA, LayerSync) by introducing Dual-Timestep Scheduling (DTS), which creates information asymmetry within the flow matching objective itself, paired with an EMA teacher for self-supervised feature prediction. The key conceptual departure is eliminating the external encoder entirely: the paper demonstrates that the denoising objective alone provides insufficient incentive to learn semantic representations, and that heterogeneous per-token noise levels can drive this learning natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the now-common practice of bolting external pretrained encoders onto flow/diffusion models, showing that such alignment exhibits inverse scaling (stronger encoders degrade quality) and fails to generalize across modalities (video, audio). The practical impact is significant: DTS is a drop-in modification to existing flow matching training that requires no external models, no architectural changes beyond per-token timestep conditioning, and yields consistent gains across image, video, audio, and joint video-action prediction. Open questions include whether the approach extends to end-to-end autoencoder training (as in REPA-E), how the masking ratio and noise scheduler interact at much larger scales, and whether the learned representations transfer to downstream tasks like planning and world modeling beyond the robotic manipulation experiments shown here.
