## Problem

Bokeh rendering and monocular depth estimation are optically linked through lens imaging geometry, yet existing pipelines treat them in isolation: bokeh renderers depend on noisy depth maps that propagate errors into blur artifacts, while feed-forward depth models struggle in textureless or distant regions where defocus blur could supply independent geometric cues. No prior work exploits this reciprocity by using synthetic defocus as a supervision-free signal to improve depth estimation without requiring ground-truth depth at inference.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single sharp RGB image (512×512 at training resolution) and a thin-lens-derived bokeh strength scalar K (computed from focal length, f-number, and focus distance via a circle-of-confusion formula) as input. Stage-1 outputs a calibrated bokeh stack of N+1 frames (one sharp reference plus N defocused frames at increasing K values, e.g. K∈[10,30]) generated entirely without any depth map. Stage-2 consumes this stack alongside the original sharp frame and produces a full-resolution metric depth map in physical units through an unchanged DPT decoder and camera-aware scale head.

## Method

BokehDepth is a two-stage framework that decouples bokeh synthesis from depth prediction. Stage-1 augments FLUX.1-Kontext (an MMDiT-based rectified-flow transformer) with a lightweight bokeh cross-attention adapter: the scalar K is mapped through an MLP to a conditioning vector, then LoRA projections produce dedicated keys and values for a second attention branch whose output is added to the unified MMDiT attention, enabling depth-free, lens-consistent bokeh generation trained on a hybrid corpus of real EXIF-tagged photos, BokehMe synthetic renders, and DPDD/BLB pairs all unified under the same K parameterization. Stage-2 inserts a Divided Space Focus Attention (DSFA) module into the ViT encoder of UniDepthV2 or Depth Anything V2: Step-1 performs per-frame spatial attention with a K-conditioned token prepended to each frame's patch tokens, and Step-2 performs cross-frame focus attention at aligned spatial locations modulated by FiLM conditioning from the K tokens, letting each pixel directly compare how blur changes along the calibrated defocus axis. Only the reference frame's refined tokens are retained and fed to the original DPT decoder, making DSFA a plug-and-play encoder insertion that leaves the decoder and loss functions unchanged. A theoretical proposition formalizes that regressing bokeh radius on K across the stack yields an unbiased, consistent estimator of per-pixel inverse-depth offset, grounding the defocus cue in thin-lens physics.

## Results

On zero-shot cross-domain metric depth estimation (Table 2), BokehDepth with UniDepthV2 backbone (UDv2+Ours) achieves the best δ₁ on 5 of 6 benchmarks, reaching 0.978 on IBims-1, 0.963 on ETH3D, and 0.895 on HAMMER, while also delivering the lowest AbsRel on IBims-1 (0.039) and ETH3D (0.074). On the in-domain NYUv2 benchmark (Table 3), BokehDepth attains AbsRel=0.039 and RMS=0.043, substantially outperforming UniDepthV2 (0.047/0.180) and Depth Anything V2 (0.056/0.206). Against dedicated Depth-from-Defocus methods (Table 4), BokehDepth achieves the highest δ₁ (0.978) and lowest RMS (0.043) despite generating its bokeh stack from a single sharp image without any ground-truth depth, whereas all competing DfD methods rely on rendered RGB-D pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For bokeh rendering (Table 1), BokehDepth achieves the best PSNR (25.91) and DISTS (0.076) on real EBB! Val200, outperforming GenFocus (25.48/0.081) and DiffCamera (25.07/0.102), while on synthetic SYNTHEBOKEH300 it attains the best perceptual scores (LPIPS 0.139, DISTS 0.073) though DrBokeh leads on PSNR (29.70 vs. 29.12) due to direct depth access. For zero-shot depth estimation (Table 2), UDv2+Ours dominates all six benchmarks, improving δ₁ by 0.25 over UDv2 on HAMMER (0.895 vs. 0.645) and by 0.33 on Sintel (0.671 vs. 0.344), while DAv2+Ours also consistently beats its base model and all other baselines (ZoeDepth, Metric3Dv2, DepthPro). On in-domain NYUv2 (Table 3), BokehDepth reduces AbsRel from 0.047 (UniDepthV2) to 0.039 and RMS from 0.180 to 0.043, a 4× improvement in RMS. Against DfD methods (Table 4), BokehDepth achieves δ₁=0.978 and RMS=0.043, beating the best DfD baseline DFF-DFV (δ₁=0.967, RMS=0.232) by a wide margin despite operating under a strictly harder setting (no ground-truth depth for stack generation). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that the physically calibrated Stage-1 bokeh stack is essential: replacing it with raw FLUX.1-Kontext output collapses δ₁ to 0.608, far below even the no-bokeh baseline (0.914).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: depth-map-based bokeh rendering (BokehMe, DrBokeh) and discriminative monocular metric depth estimation (Depth Anything V2, UniDepthV2). It departs from both by inverting the conventional dependency: rather than using depth to drive bokeh synthesis, it synthesizes calibrated bokeh stacks without any depth input (building on FLUX.1-Kontext and the Bokeh Diffusion control mechanism of Fortes et al., 2025) and then feeds those stacks back into a depth encoder via a new Divided Space Focus Attention module. The theoretical grounding in Proposition 4.1 connects to classical depth-from-defocus (Pentland, Subbarao) but reframes the problem as a per-pixel linear regression over a calibrated bokeh-strength axis, avoiding the fragile global optimization of traditional DfD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of bokeh synthesis from depth prediction breaks a circular dependency that has limited both fields: bokeh quality was bounded by depth accuracy, and depth models lacked a physically grounded geometric cue for textureless or distant regions. The plug-and-play DSFA module makes the approach broadly applicable to any ViT-based depth backbone without decoder modifications. Key limitations include the high Stage-1 latency (~49 s per image with 30 diffusion steps), though the authors note this is offline and cacheable, and that one-step distillation could bring it closer to real-time. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video (temporal consistency of bokeh stacks), dynamic scenes with motion, and whether the physical calibration requirement generalizes to non-thin-lens optics.
