## Problem

Inference-time scaling via parallel sampling (FULL PARALLEL) allocates an identical compute budget of M sequences to every prompt, regardless of difficulty. Easy prompts waste most of this budget on near-identical redundant generations, while hard prompts may need more exploration than M allows. The paper asks whether online model uncertainty signals can guide adaptive, per-prompt allocation of the sampling budget to reduce wasted computation and improve performance simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263965/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and (optionally) a target label for the full EAGER variant used in RLVR pipelines. During autoregressive generation, it consumes the model's top-K (K=20) token probability distributions at each step to compute entropy. Output is a set of reasoning sequences structured as a branching tree (rather than M independent sequences), where the number of active sequences per prompt is dynamic and typically well below M for easy problems. The only tunable hyperparameter is the entropy threshold θ, calibrated on 10 held-out examples per model.

## Method

EAGER monitors top-K token entropy H_t^(K) at every generation step and branches the current sequence into two continuations (taking the top-2 most likely tokens) only when entropy exceeds a threshold θ, reusing the shared prefix across branches to avoid redundant token generation. This EAGER-init stage produces a generation tree with at most M leaves, often far fewer for confident prompts. The saved budget b = min(M·|D| − M_actual, 2M) is then reallocated: in the label-free EAGER-adapt variant, extra sequences go to saturating prompts that hit the M cap; in the full EAGER variant (with target labels), budget is directed to prompts with Pass@k=0, with the entropy threshold lowered by 20% for underutilizing prompts to encourage earlier branching. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no reward model or warm-up stage, and shifts the Pareto frontier by achieving up to +37% Pass@k with 65% fewer tokens versus FULL PARALLEL sampling.

## Results

On AIME 2025, full EAGER (with target labels) achieves up to +37% Pass@k improvement over FULL PARALLEL sampling (e.g., SmolLM3B: 0.73 vs 0.53; GPT-Oss 20B: 0.93 vs 0.90) while generating up to 65% fewer tokens. In the label-free test-time setting, EAGER-adapt still yields +12% Pass@k and 64% fewer tokens compared to FULL PARALLEL. EAGER-init alone reduces token usage to less than half of the FULL PARALLEL baseline while maintaining competitive or superior Pass Rate across all four models (3B–20B) and three task domains (math, science, code).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary FULL PARALLEL sampling baseline, EAGER consistently wins on Pass Rate across all models and benchmarks, and full EAGER (with labels) achieves the highest Pass@k in nearly every setting (e.g., SmolLM3B AIME 2025: 0.73 vs 0.53; Qwen3-4B GPQA: 0.81 vs 0.75). EAGER-init trades a small Pass@k drop for dramatic token savings (typically <50% of FULL PARALLEL tokens) while matching or exceeding Pass Rate. In the head-to-head comparison on AIME 2025 (Table 5, Appendix C), EAGER-adapt (the strongest label-free variant) outperforms ESC on Pass@k for SmolLM3B (0.63 vs 0.53) and GPT-Oss-20B (0.95 vs 0.90), ties DeepConf on Pass@k for SmolLM3B (0.63) and Qwen3-4B (0.80), but trails both by 3 points on DeepSeek-8B (0.77 vs 0.80). However, EAGER-adapt uses substantially fewer tokens than both ESC and DeepConf across all models (e.g., 12×10⁵ vs 11×10⁵ for ESC and 15×10⁵ for DeepConf on Qwen3-4B) and has the lowest wall-clock time among all methods except FULL PARALLEL. ESC, despite reducing tokens relative to FULL PARALLEL, suffers from severely restricted parallelization, yielding wall-clock times 2–5× longer than EAGER variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGER sits within the inference-time scaling paradigm (Snell et al., 2025; Welleck et al., 2024) that replaces single greedy decoding with parallel sampled sequences, building on chain-of-thought and self-consistency foundations. It departs from prior adaptive-generation methods in a specific way: REBASE (Wu et al., 2025) uses a reward model to decide where to branch, DeepConf (Fu et al., 2025) uses confidence to truncate low-quality sequences, and ESC (Li et al., 2024) halts early when trajectories converge. EAGER instead uses token-level top-K entropy as a *branching* signal (rather than a truncation or reward signal), requiring no external model, no warm-up stage, and no training. The connection to Wang et al. (2025), who showed high-entropy tokens drive RL learning, provides empirical motivation but EAGER repurposes the same signal for inference-time compute allocation rather than training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone running inference-time scaling: up to 65% fewer tokens with equal or better Pass@k, and the method is training-free with a single model-dependent hyperparameter that transfers across domains. However, the full EAGER variant's strongest gains (+37% Pass@k) depend on target labels, limiting direct applicability to open-ended or non-verifiable reasoning. Key open questions include whether entropy-guided branching generalizes to substantially larger models (tested only up to 20B), agentic multi-step settings, and whether alternative uncertainty measures (e.g., KL divergence) could further improve the signal. The label-free EAGER-adapt variant, while more broadly applicable, yields more modest gains (+12% Pass@k, 64% fewer tokens).
