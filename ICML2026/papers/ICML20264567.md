## Problem

Multi-turn LLM agents degrade in performance as conversation rounds increase, even when context remains within model capacity. Prior work attributed this to context length or information overload, but the paper identifies a distinct mechanism—conversational inertia—where models exhibit strong diagonal attention to their own previous responses, erroneously treating them as few-shot demonstrations to imitate rather than adapting to new environmental feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations, system prompts, and accumulated conversation context (user inputs and assistant responses across turns) to produce agent actions. For Context Preference Learning, the unusual input is a pair of actions generated from the same state under different context lengths (long vs. short), where the short-context action is designated 'chosen' and the long-context action 'rejected'—no environment reward or expert label is required. At inference, the clip context strategy periodically trims the conversation to L most recent rounds when context reaches threshold H, producing actions with reduced inertial bias while retaining enough history for state identification.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. First, Context Preference Learning (CPL) exploits the observation that for identical states, actions generated with longer contexts exhibit stronger diagonal attention (inertia) than those with shorter contexts. It constructs preference pairs by generating a long-context action and a short-context action at the same state, then trains with DPO using the short-context action as preferred, fine-tuning only 0.4% of parameters via LoRA (rank 16, alpha 16, β=0.01). This requires no environment rewards or expert demonstrations. Second, Clip Context is an inference-time strategy that periodically clears the conversation to L most recent rounds when context reaches H rounds, creating periodic breaks that reset accumulated inertial patterns. Unlike sliding-window approaches, Clip Context is KV-cache compatible because all contexts within a clearing cycle share the same prefix, yielding approximately W× prefill speedup. Attention analysis confirms CPL selectively reduces diagonal attention by over 10% (more than the reduction in overall assistant attention), providing mechanistic evidence that the model learns to suppress positional imitation rather than uniformly downweighting history.

## Results

On the eight-environment agent benchmark, Clip context with Context Preference Learning (CPL) achieves 72.5% average success rate on Qwen3-8B (+7.6 over Base+Window at 64.9%), 59.8% on Llama3.1-8B (+1.2), and 71.1% on GPT-4o-mini (+5.1). Clip context alone outperforms Window context by approximately 4% across all three models, while Long context degrades performance substantially (e.g., 54.4% vs. 64.9% for Qwen3-8B). CPL reduces diagonal attention by 11% on Qwen3-8B and by 14.5% under Long context, and preserves general capabilities (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Redux) within 0.2–0.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four context management strategies (Window, Long, Summarization, Clip) with and without CPL fine-tuning across three models. Clip context consistently outperforms Window context by ~4% average across all models (e.g., 68.9 vs. 64.9 for Qwen3-8B; 71.1 vs. 66.0 for GPT-4o-mini), and substantially outperforms Long context (54.4% for Qwen3-8B), confirming that longer context amplifies conversational inertia rather than helping. Clip matches or slightly exceeds Summarization (68.9 vs. 68.8 for Qwen3-8B Base), and the authors attribute summarization's gains primarily to inertia reduction from context clearing rather than the summary content itself. CPL adds further gains on top of Clip: +3.6 points for Qwen3-8B (72.5 vs. 68.9), +0.6 for Llama3.1-8B, and is most effective under Long context where it reduces diagonal attention by 14.5% versus 11% for shorter contexts. On the BrowseComp deep-research benchmark, Clip-12to6 achieves 29.3±1.5, outperforming Window-6 (25.0±1.7) and matching Sum-12to6 (28.1±1.6) while maintaining higher proactive-answer accuracy (61.0 vs. 63.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for preference calibration but repurposes it in a novel way: instead of requiring environment rewards or expert demonstrations, it constructs preference pairs from the model's own long-context vs. short-context outputs at identical states. Mechanistically, it connects to induction head literature (Olsson et al., 2022; D'Angelo et al., 2025) to explain why diagonal attention to previous assistant tokens produces imitation bias. It departs from prior context management work (sliding window, summarization, retrieval) by identifying conversational inertia as a distinct failure mode rather than treating multi-turn degradation as purely a context-length or information-overload problem, and positions clip context as orthogonal to and combinable with existing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that summarization methods' gains come primarily from context clearing (inertia reduction) rather than the generated summary content challenges a common assumption in the field and suggests practitioners can use simpler, KV-cache-compatible clip strategies instead of more expensive summarization pipelines. The information-inertia tradeoff remains an open challenge: clip context can fail on tasks with strong long-range dependencies where critical early information falls outside the clipped window, and the paper acknowledges this is unsolved across all context management approaches. A promising direction identified is combining inertia-aware adaptive dropping with information-based retention, and extending the analysis to RAG-enhanced agents where multi-round queries still accumulate conversational inertia.
