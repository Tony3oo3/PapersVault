## Problem

Pre-Layer Normalization in deep Transformers suffers from a 'curse of depth' where residual connections drive unbounded growth in hidden-state magnitude and variance across layers, destabilizing training and pushing block Jacobians toward identity. Existing remedies either add normalization overhead (Peri-LN, LNS) or replace normalization with a learnable-scalar tanh (DyT) that still permits depth-wise residual accumulation. No prior method simultaneously provides explicit probabilistic control over depth-wise activation growth and reduces the repeated statistical-computation cost of Pre-LN.

## Input / Output

At each Pre-LN site, BHyT consumes a token hidden-state vector x ∈ R^d and produces a bounded activation γ ⊙ tanh(αx) whose pre-tanh argument is kept within [−λ, λ] with probability at least 1−κ⁻². Unusually, the second Pre-LN site within a block does not recompute the input variance; instead it reuses the first site's exact variance and adds a model-level attention-output variance estimate (1/(Td)‖W_V W_O‖_F² · λ²/κ²) that depends only on fixed projection weights and sequence length, not on the current activations.

## Method

BHyT replaces RMSNorm at Pre-LN sites with a bounded tanh transformation whose input is scaled by α = λ/(κs_x), derived from Chebyshev's inequality to guarantee the pre-tanh argument lies in [−λ, λ] with probability ≥ 1−κ⁻² for any finite-variance distribution. For efficiency, the module computes the per-instance feature variance exactly once per Transformer block (at the attention-side Pre-LN site) and approximates the MLP-side variance by adding a closed-form attention-output variance term that depends only on the Frobenius norm of W_V W_O, sequence length T, and the BHyT hyperparameters—eliminating the second reduction operation. A finite-depth variance-propagation theorem shows that when λ/κ < 1/√L, the per-layer variance amplification under BHyT is strictly smaller than under LNS for every layer up to depth L, providing a theoretical guarantee that depth-wise variance growth is suppressed. Empirically, BHyT achieves ~1.6% faster pretraining throughput and ~1.77% higher generation throughput than RMSNorm on Llama-1B while attaining the lowest pretraining loss and highest 3-shot downstream accuracy across 374M, 1B, and 3B scales.

## Results

On Llama-1B pretrained on 20B C4 tokens, BHyT achieves the lowest pretraining eval loss (2.802) and perplexity (16.47), outperforming RMSNorm (2.877 / 17.75) by 0.075 in loss and 1.28 in PPL. BHyT attains the highest average 3-shot downstream accuracy of 43.42%, exceeding the second-best LNS (42.85%) by 0.57 points and RMSNorm (41.94%) by 1.48 points. In generation throughput, BHyT reaches 1,199.9 tokens/s, surpassing RMSNorm by 1.6% and Peri-LN by 18.0%, while DyT remains fastest at 1,352.0 tokens/s. Training throughput is 83.0K tokens/s, 1.63% faster than RMSNorm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BHyT outperforms RMSNorm on every reported metric: lower pretraining loss (2.828 vs 2.876), lower PPL (16.47 vs 17.75), higher average downstream accuracy (43.42% vs 41.94%), and higher generation throughput (1,199.9 vs 1,181.1 tokens/s, +1.6%). Against the stability-oriented Peri-LN, BHyT achieves lower loss, lower PPL, higher accuracy (43.42% vs 42.31%), and substantially higher throughput (1,199.9 vs 984.4 tokens/s, +18.0%), since Peri-LN doubles normalization operations. Versus LNS, BHyT wins on loss, PPL, and average accuracy (43.42% vs 42.85%) while also being faster in generation (1,199.9 vs 1,150.7 tokens/s, +4.1%). The one comparison where BHyT does not lead is against DyT: DyT achieves higher generation throughput (1,352.0 vs 1,199.9 tokens/s, +12.7%) because it eliminates all statistical computation, but BHyT surpasses DyT on pretraining loss (2.828 vs 2.891), PPL (16.47 vs 18.07), and average downstream accuracy (43.42% vs 42.71%), reflecting BHyT's superior depth-wise stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

BHyT sits at the intersection of two lines of work: normalization-free Transformers (DyT, which replaces RMSNorm with a learnable-scalar tanh) and stability-oriented normalization variants (Peri-LN, LNS) that address the 'curse of depth' identified by Sun et al. (2026). The key conceptual departure from DyT is replacing its learned global scalar with a data-driven, Chebyshev-based input bound that explicitly controls the pre-tanh range per token, while the variance approximation mechanism reduces the per-block statistical overhead that plagues RMSNorm and its stability-enhanced variants. The paper thus carves out a middle ground: bounded nonlinearity for efficiency, data-dependent scaling for stability, and a single exact variance computation per block for practicality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BHyT offers a theoretically grounded, drop-in replacement for RMSNorm that simultaneously suppresses depth-wise activation growth and reduces normalization overhead, which is practically relevant as LLMs continue to scale in depth. However, the empirical gains are modest (≈1.6% training throughput, ≈1.8% generation throughput over RMSNorm; 0.3–0.6 points downstream accuracy), and the theoretical guarantee (λ/κ < 1/√L) becomes increasingly restrictive for very deep networks. Open questions include whether the near-linear tanh regime assumption and the uniform-attention approximation in Theorem 3.4 hold at much larger scales, whether the variance approximation degrades under aggressive learning-rate schedules, and whether the approach extends to architectures with grouped-query attention or mixture-of-experts routing where the structural constants C_Attn and C_MLP are less well-defined.
