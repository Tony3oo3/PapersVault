## Problem

Existing bias evaluation and mitigation for text-to-image diffusion models rely on curated prompt datasets that explore only a limited fraction of the prompt space, leaving unanticipated, contextually-triggered biases undetected even in debiased models. Gradient-based prompt optimization can discover high-bias regions but produces unreadable, non-natural text unsuitable for practical auditing. The paper addresses the need for a method that automatically discovers interpretable, natural-sounding prompts that maximize demographic bias exposure in TTI models.

## Input / Output

The method requires grey-box access to a TTI diffusion model (intermediate UNet or DiT activations at a mid-layer), an autoregressive LLM (Mistral-7B) as the language prior, and pre-trained linear attribute classifiers on DM activations. It produces single-sentence, attribute-neutral text prompts (no explicit gender/race/age mentions) initialized with a fixed prefix template (e.g., 'A photo of a person working as a'). Each discovered prompt is validated by generating 10 images and classifying them with the attribute classifier, with attribute-revealing prompts (those using pronouns or gendered names) filtered out before evaluation.

## Method

BGPS formulates biased prompt discovery as maximizing a joint objective J(a,s) = log P(s) + λ·log(1/K Σ P(A=a | x_T, ε, s)), where the first term is the LLM's token-level log-probability (ensuring naturalness) and the second is the log of the average attribute classifier probability over K stochastic diffusion generations. At each beam search step, B×E candidate token continuations are scored by encoding the partial prompt through the DM's text encoder, running T' diffusion steps on K random noise vectors, extracting mid-block activations, and passing them through the attribute classifier. The key insight is adapting the gradient-free Visually-Guided Decoding framework by replacing CLIP with attribute classifiers trained on DM internal representations, which steers the LLM's decoding toward prompt-space regions that amplify the target demographic attribute while the LLM's vocabulary constraint guarantees interpretable, natural-sounding output. A stochastic expansion factor E' and first-token sampling from the full LLM distribution provide prompt diversity, and the λ parameter controls the bias-strength versus text-naturalness tradeoff.

## Results

On SD 1.5 (Base), BGPS(λ=100) achieves 91% male frequency and 66% female frequency, substantially outperforming LLM(biased) (85% male, 46% female) and PEZ (80% male, 57% female) while maintaining far lower perplexity (129 vs. 1387 for PEZ, ~17× lower). On the fine-tuned debiased model (FT), BGPS(λ=100) still reaches 76% male frequency, exceeding LLM(biased) (73%) and PEZ (78% but with 94% attribute-revealing prompts). On the DiffLens debiased model (DL), BGPS(λ=100) achieves 67% male frequency, dramatically outperforming LLM(biased) (46%) and matching PEZ (84%) while producing interpretable text. For race biasing, BGPS(λ=100) reaches 82% White and 27% Black frequency on Base, compared to 76% and 5% for LLM(biased) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PEZ (Wen et al., 2023), BGPS achieves comparable or higher bias frequency (91% vs. 80% male on Base) while producing prompts with 17–26× lower perplexity (129 vs. 1387) and only 17% attribute-revealing prompts versus PEZ's 94%, making BGPS prompts interpretable and practically usable for auditing. Against the Human-curated baseline from Shen et al. (2024), BGPS substantially increases bias exposure (91% vs. 53% male on Base) by discovering subtle contextual modifiers, though at a modest perplexity cost (129 vs. 96). The key advantage of BGPS over both baselines is its ability to target specific underrepresented groups: while LLM(biased) struggles to increase Black or female frequencies (5% Black, 46% female on Base), BGPS(λ=100) reaches 27% Black and 66% female, revealing hidden associations that neither gradient-based optimization nor stereotype-informed LLM prompting can systematically discover.

## Relation to Prior Work

BGPS adapts the gradient-free beam search decoding framework of Visually-Guided Decoding (Kim et al., ICLR 2025), originally designed for CLIP-based image-to-prompt inversion, by substituting CLIP similarity with attribute classifiers trained on diffusion model activations (from DiffLens, Shi et al., CVPR 2025). This substitution transforms an inversion technique into a bias discovery tool while preserving the LLM's search space for interpretability. The paper positions itself against both curated-prompt bias evaluation benchmarks (TIBET, HEIM, FaintBench, OpenBias) and gradient-based prompt optimization (PEZ), arguing that the former under-explores the prompt space and the latter produces unreadable text. Conceptually, it bridges the bias mitigation literature (which provides the classifier infrastructure) and the prompt optimization literature (which provides the search mechanism) into a unified auditing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that state-of-the-art debiased models (LoRA fine-tuned and DiffLens) retain substantial residual biases—up to 76% male skew—when tested with contextually-triggered prompts, challenging the assumption that debiasing on curated datasets is sufficient. The discovery of systematic linguistic associations (technology/focus terms → male, arts/crafts/emotion terms → female) provides actionable insight for future debiasing strategies that must address semantic associations rather than just explicit demographic terms. Limitations include reliance on grey-box access to intermediate activations, dependence on externally trained classifiers that may carry their own biases, and limited validation on DiT architectures (Flux, SD 3.5) where results are weaker. Open questions include how to optimally integrate BGPS-discovered prompts into debiasing training loops (a preliminary experiment suggests promise) and whether the approach generalizes to more fine-grained or intersectional attributes.
