## Problem

Deep neural networks lose the ability to learn new tasks in continual learning without reinitialization, yet the underlying mechanism remains unclear despite multiple proposed explanations (dormant neurons, parameter norm growth, overfitting). This paper identifies Hessian spectral collapse—the degeneration of the Hessian eigenspectrum so that most curvature directions vanish—as the unifying mechanism that precedes and drives plasticity loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263682/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential task data (28×28 MNIST digits with pixel permutations, ImageNet class pairs, CIFAR-100 incremental classes, or Slippery Ant environment observations) along with the current neural network parameters at each task boundary. It produces updated model parameters trained under a composite objective that augments the standard task loss with an L2 penalty and a differentiable effective-rank penalty computed from per-layer activation covariance matrices, amortized over U optimization steps to control SVD cost.

## Method

The paper first analyzes a linearized ReLU network under the NTK framework, showing that gradient descent contracts residual components at rates set by Gram matrix eigenvalues and deriving an explicit ε-rank threshold (ε_{K,γ} ≈ −log γ / ηK) that separates fast from slow eigenspaces. It proves the loss-weighted Gram matrix G_LW = C^{1/2} G^{(0)} C^{1/2} is spectrally equivalent to the Generalized Gauss-Newton matrix H_GGN = J^T C J, bridging function-space NTK dynamics to parameter-space curvature. Using the KFAC approximation, the rank of H_GGN factors into the rank of the input activation covariance and the backpropagated gradient covariance, identifying effective feature rank as the controllable bottleneck. The resulting L2-ER regularizer minimizes task loss + λ‖θ‖² − β·erank(Σ_l E[a_n^{[l−1]} a_n^{[l−1]T]}), where the L2 term lifts the Hessian floor and the effective-rank penalty explicitly preserves the dimensionality of the non-collapsed curvature subspace, preventing spectral collapse across tasks.

## Results

The paper evaluates L2-ER against six baselines across four continual learning environments (Permuted MNIST, Continual ImageNet, Incremental CIFAR, and Slippery Ant), reporting classification accuracy and online returns respectively. L2-ER is the only method that maintains plasticity across all four environments, while every other baseline fails on at least one. On Permuted MNIST, L2-ER provides an early performance boost before plasticity loss would typically occur. Results are averaged over 5 seeds (10 seeds for Continual ImageNet due to higher variance), with 95% confidence intervals reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2-ER outperforms all three primary baselines by being the only method to maintain plasticity across all four environments simultaneously. CBP (Dohare et al., 2024) succeeds on Permuted MNIST, Continual ImageNet, and Slippery Ant but fails on Incremental CIFAR, where increasing class counts overwhelm the method. LayerNorm-L2 (Lyle et al., 2024) performs well on Continual ImageNet, Incremental CIFAR, and Slippery Ant but fails on Permuted MNIST. Spectral Regularization (Lewandowski et al., 2024a) succeeds on Permuted MNIST, Continual ImageNet, and Slippery Ant but also fails on Incremental CIFAR, and requires roughly twice the training time of L2-ER. Additionally, vanilla BP and ER (effective rank alone) exhibit plasticity loss across all environments, while L2 regularization alone fails specifically on Continual ImageNet. The paper also includes a RESET baseline for Incremental CIFAR, noting that algorithms outperforming RESET are considered to maintain plasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously disparate explanations of plasticity loss—dormant neurons, effective feature rank reduction, parameter norm growth, and Hessian ill-conditioning—under a single mechanism it calls Hessian spectral collapse. The theoretical analysis builds on NTK linearization (Jacot et al., 2018) and the linearized ReLU network model (Dwaraknath et al., 2023), while the practical regularizer is derived from KFAC approximations of the GGN matrix (Martens, 2016; Dangel et al., 2025). A key technical bridge is the spectral equivalence between the loss-weighted Gram matrix in function space and the GGN matrix in parameter space, connecting NTK dynamics to curvature. The paper positions itself against prior second-order diagnostics (Lewandowski et al., 2024a,b) by showing they all target the same eigenspectral bulk rather than distinct pathologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification under spectral collapse provides a single diagnostic (epsilon-rank of the Hessian at task initialization) that predicts when a network will fail to learn a new task, and motivates a simple two-term regularizer (L2 + effective-rank penalty) that is cheaper than full Hessian estimation. However, the causal claim remains empirical rather than proven, the effective-rank penalty introduces nontrivial SVD cost that may limit scalability, and the authors explicitly note the absence of language-model results and the need for more specialized curvature approximations for attention layers. Open questions include whether the spectral-collapse mechanism holds under the non-stationary policy/value dynamics of RL and whether the NTK linearization assumptions are sufficient for deep, non-linear networks in practice.
