## Problem

Prevailing VLA models predict actions as discrete waypoints bound to a fixed sampling rate, which is structurally misaligned with the continuous nature of physical motion. This discretization prevents querying at arbitrary control frequencies, lacks analytically consistent higher-order derivatives (velocity, jerk), and introduces quantization artifacts that make stable impedance control infeasible, confining robots to stiff position control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262968/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB observations (a static scene view plus a wrist-mounted view), proprioceptive state, and a natural language instruction. Rather than outputting a fixed-length waypoint sequence, the MLLM regresses a compact set of SIREN modulation coefficients (γ for frequency scaling, β for phase shifting) that parameterize a continuous action function A(τ) over a normalized domain τ ∈ [−1, 1]. This function can be queried at any temporal resolution K without retraining, and its analytical derivatives yield noise-free velocity and jerk signals for impedance control at deployment.

## Method

NIAF reformulates action decoding as a hypernetwork problem: a pre-trained MLLM (Florence-2 or Qwen3-VL) receives learnable query embeddings, attends to the multimodal context, and outputs modulation vectors that reconfigure the shared meta-parameters of a SIREN decoder via a grouped scheme (G weight tokens per layer control frequency, one bias token controls phase). The SIREN's sinusoidal activations guarantee C^∞ smoothness by construction, and its isomorphic derivative structure (d/dx sin(x) = cos(x)) enables recursive analytical computation of velocity and jerk without finite differencing. Training combines position regression with explicit velocity supervision (using ground-truth velocity from FOC drivers) and jerk regularization, where position and velocity losses constrain the same SIREN-parameterized function from complementary measurements, acting as a cross-signal regularizer. At inference, the model outputs both the continuous position reference Φ(τ) and its analytical velocity (2/T)∇_τΦ(τ), feeding directly into an impedance control law that enables compliant, jitter-free execution.

## Results

On CALVIN, NIAF (0.77B, no robot pretraining) achieves average chain lengths of 4.66 (ABCD→D) and 4.47 (ABC→D), outperforming all baselines including the 9B UniVLA (4.63/4.41) and 1B FLOWER (4.62/4.44). On LIBERO, NIAF attains a 97.9% average success rate with 100% on LIBERO-Object and 95.5% on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, surpassing FLOWER (95.7%), OpenVLA-OFT (95.5%), and π₀ (94.2%). On the Qwen3-VL backbone, NIAF reaches 97.7% average across four suites, leading in three of four.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIAF (0.77B, no robot pretraining) outperforms all ten baselines across both simulation benchmarks. On CALVIN ABCD→D, NIAF achieves 4.66 average chain length, beating UniVLA (9B, 4.63), FLOWER (1B, 4.62), and BEAST (0.77B, 4.61); on ABC→D it reaches 4.47 vs. FLOWER's 4.44 and BEAST's 4.42. On LIBERO, NIAF's 97.9% average surpasses FLOWER (95.7%), OpenVLA-OFT (95.5%), and π₀ (94.2%), with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (95.5% vs. 93.5% for FLOWER and 85.2% for π₀). Against BEAST, NIAF gains +5.4 points on LIBERO average and +2.1 on LIBERO-Long, which the authors attribute to B-spline's low-pass filtering nature over-smoothing critical micro-adjustments. On the Qwen3-VL backbone (Table 3), NIAF (97.7%) beats Qwen3-VL-OFT (96.6%), Qwen3-VL-GR00T (96.5%), and Qwen3-VL-FAST (95.4%). In real-world experiments, π₀.5-NIAF outperforms π₀.5-BEAST and π₀.5-OFT on Shape Insertion, while all action-head replacements underperform vanilla π₀.5 on Towel Folding due to partial forgetting of pretrained priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

NIAF sits at the intersection of three research threads: implicit neural representations (SIREN's sinusoidal activations providing C∞ smoothness), hypernetworks (a primary network generating target-network weights), and VLA action chunking (predicting fixed-horizon trajectories). It departs from the dominant discrete-waypoint paradigm (RT-2, ACT, Diffusion Policy) and compressed-token approaches (BEAST's B-spline control points, FAST's DCT coefficients) by reframing action decoding as parameter regression over a continuous function. The MLLM-as-hierarchical-spectral-modulator design directly extends the Trans-INR and foundation-model-as-hypernetwork lineage, but uniquely maps multimodal semantic context to frequency/phase spectra of a shared SIREN motion prior rather than to arbitrary weight vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in bridging policy-side generation and execution-side compliant control: analytical velocity and jerk signals from the SIREN enable stable impedance control that discrete baselines cannot support, as demonstrated in real-world contact-rich tasks. Key limitations include the dependence on FOC-driver velocity feedback for physics-informed supervision (limiting applicability to low-cost platforms), the fact that NIAF does not improve the backbone's high-level reasoning or zero-shot generalization, and reduced benefit for short-horizon fixed-rate tasks. An open question is whether the continuous-function formulation extends naturally to multi-robot or whole-body control settings where action dimensionality and coupling structure are more complex.
