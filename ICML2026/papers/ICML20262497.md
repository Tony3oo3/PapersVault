## Problem

LLM reasoning improvement is fundamentally constrained by reliance on massive human-curated datasets for SFT and RL, a paradigm showing signs of diminishing scalability. Existing data-free self-play methods for reasoning rely on adversarial dynamics where one role generates challenges to stump another, making them prone to instability and collapse into nonsensical or unlearnable tasks. The paper asks whether a cooperative, non-adversarial multi-agent framework can achieve stable, effective data-free RL for mathematical reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262497/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes two pretrained LLMs (a Coach and a Player) as the only inputs; no external training data is used during the co-evolution stage. The Coach generates mathematical task instructions as text, and the Player produces n independent solution attempts per task, from which majority voting yields a pseudo-label used as a verifiable reward signal. The trained output is an improved Player model evaluated on six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AMC, AIME 2024/2025, Minerva, MATH-500, Olympiad-Bench), with a fixed held-out validation set (AMC by default) providing the environment feedback that drives Coach updates.

## Method

CPMobius introduces a cooperative Coach-Player loop where the Coach acts as an adaptive curriculum designer and the Player as a reasoning solver, both optimized end-to-end without external data. Each round: (1) the Coach proposes m tasks, filtered online to a pedagogically optimal difficulty zone (0.2 ≤ majority-vote accuracy ≤ 0.8); (2) the Player generates n solutions per task, receives binary rewards via majority-voted pseudo-labels, and is updated with GRPO; (3) the updated Player is evaluated on a fixed validation set, yielding an accuracy gap Δ_t; (4) the Coach is updated via REINFORCE with a multiplicative reward R_i^Coach = R_i^Player · Δ_t, reinforcing only tasks that were both solvable by the Player and coincided with measurable global learning progress. This cooperative reward design—rewarding the Coach for the Player's improvement rather than for difficulty—sidesteps the instability of adversarial self-play while maintaining an adaptive, self-adjusting curriculum that progressively increases in complexity as the Player's capabilities grow.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, CPMobius achieves an overall average of 40.7 and OOD average of 38.4, improving +4.9 and +5.4 points over the base model respectively. Across all four base models (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B, OctoThinker-3B-Hybrid-Zero, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct), CPMobius consistently attains the highest overall and OOD average scores among all methods tested. The largest single-benchmark gain is on Minerva, where Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B improves from 16.3 to 28.0 (+71.8% relative) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct from 34.6 to 44.9 (+29.8% relative).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPMobius outperforms both baselines on overall and OOD average across all four base models. Against RENT (entropy minimization), CPMobius leads by +1.5 on overall average and +0.8 on OOD average for Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (40.7 vs. 39.2; 38.4 vs. 37.6), and by +1.7 on overall average for Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (28.8 vs. 27.1). Against R-Zero (adversarial self-play), the margin is larger: CPMobius exceeds R-Zero by +4.2 on OOD average for Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (38.4 vs. 34.2) and by +3.1 on overall average for OctoThinker-3B-Hybrid-Zero (23.6 vs. 20.5). Notably, R-Zero failed entirely on OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B (the Challenger role could not be trained), whereas CPMobius still achieves a +2.6 point improvement over that already strong SFT baseline. The most striking individual-benchmark win is on Minerva, where CPMobius gains +8.7 over RENT and +5.6 over R-Zero on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (28.0 vs. 19.0/19.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

CPMobius builds on GRPO for Player optimization and RLVR with self-consistency (majority voting) for pseudo-label generation, but its core departure is from the adversarial self-play paradigm used by R-Zero and Absolute Zero. Where prior data-free RL methods cast the task-generator and solver as competitors (one trying to stump the other), CPMobius reframes them as cooperative partners: the Coach is rewarded for the Player's measurable learning progress rather than for difficulty, and the Player is rewarded for solving the Coach's tasks. This cooperative reward structure, combined with a difficulty-filtered curriculum (0.2–0.8 accuracy band), positions CPMobius as a multi-agent collaboration approach to curriculum design rather than a zero-sum game.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free RL setting addresses a real scalability bottleneck: as models approach or exceed human-level reasoning, sourcing high-quality verifiable training data becomes increasingly expensive. CPMobius demonstrates that a cooperative Coach-Player loop can push already-RL-optimized models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct) beyond their apparent ceiling without external data, and it succeeds where adversarial R-Zero failed on OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B. However, the method still requires a one-time Coach warm-up (4K PRIME examples) and a fixed held-out validation set (AMC) to compute the progress signal Δ_t, raising open questions about whether the paradigm generalizes to domains lacking a clean verifiable benchmark or can operate without any initialization data. Long-term stability of the co-evolution loop and extension beyond mathematical reasoning remain unexplored.
