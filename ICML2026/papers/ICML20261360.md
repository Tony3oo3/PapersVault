## Problem

Time series modeling is split between generation models that produce high-fidelity numerical sequences but lack explicit structural understanding, and understanding models that provide human-readable interpretations but cannot generate precise numerical outputs due to text-native tokenization disrupting numerical continuity. No unified framework exists that internalizes both capabilities, and existing vision-based time series approaches rely on superficial pixel-level pattern matching rather than genuine temporal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261360/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series (up to 21 variables, lengths up to 2592 steps) that is first converted into an 896×896 TS-image via periodicity-based folding: each variable occupies a horizontal color band, and the temporal axis is rearranged into a periodic grid (f rows × C columns) so that intra-period structure maps to vertical locality and inter-period evolution to horizontal progression. A task-specific masking scheme designates which regions are observed context versus targets to be completed. For understanding tasks, the output is a textual answer preceded by a CoT reasoning chain; for generation tasks (forecasting, imputation), the output is a completed TS-image that is deterministically decoded back to numerical values via the inverse I2TS pipeline.

## Method

TIMEOMNI-VL builds on the Bagel-7B unified multimodal backbone (hybrid autoregressive + diffusion) and introduces two key components. First, Bi-TSI provides fidelity-preserving bidirectional time series↔image mapping: Robust Fidelity Normalization (RFN) combines MAD-based and Std-based scaling with tanh saturation to handle both extreme outliers and step-like signals without signal washout or noise amplification, while encoding capacity constraints (H/N ≥ f, W ≥ L/f) and 896×896 resolution prevent implicit downsampling. Second, the paper formulates understanding-guided generation by constructing a generation CoT from six understanding QAs (variable counting, y-range localization, cycle bounding boxes, mean comparison, anomaly detection, trend analysis) that are derived from the same generation instances, creating a calibrated reasoning chain that conditions the diffusion denoiser. Training jointly optimizes a next-token prediction loss over the CoT and answer text plus a diffusion denoising loss over the target TS-image, with the CoT serving as an explicit control signal that yields an average 8.2% nMASE improvement over generation without understanding context.

## Results

On GIFT-Eval forecasting (nMASE, lower is better), TIMEOMNI-VL achieves 0.878 / 0.816 / 0.784 on short/medium/long horizons, outperforming all text-output LLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash: 1.295/1.201/1.279) and time-series-based models (ChatTime: 0.983/1.439/4.164), while remaining competitive with the best image-based method VisionTS++ (0.915/0.682/0.690). On imputation, TIMEOMNI-VL reaches state-of-the-art nMASE of 0.713–0.927 across masking ratios of 10%–50%, beating even simple statistical baselines (Linear: 0.905–0.968). On the six TS-image understanding tasks, post-training lifts the base Bagel model from near-zero to near-perfect scores (QA1–QA4 approach 1.0), and on TSR-Suite reasoning, TIMEOMNI-VL achieves top-2 accuracy on three of four tasks without any reinforcement learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMEOMNI-VL decisively outperforms all text-output LLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash, Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B) and time-series reasoning models (Time-R1, TimeOmni-1) on forecasting, which largely fail at long horizons due to counting limitations (nMASE > 1, i.e., worse than the NAIVE baseline). Against the closest image-based competitor VisionTS++, TIMEOMNI-VL wins on short-term forecasting (0.878 vs. 0.915) and long-term (0.784 vs. 0.690 is actually VisionTS++ winning on med/long), but the controlled comparison in Table 9 (same 5k training budget) shows TIMEOMNI-VL achieving a lower average nMASE (0.826 vs. 0.900). On imputation, TIMEOMNI-VL (0.713–0.927) beats all baselines including Moment-large (1.220–2.100), statistical baselines (Linear: 0.905–0.968), and VisionTS++ (0.729–0.908). On understanding tasks, TIMEOMNI-VL surpasses both the untuned Bagel backbone (0–0.502) and proprietary VLMs like Gemini-2.5-Flash (0–0.640) across all six QA tasks. On TSR-Suite reasoning, TIMEOMNI-VL (84.0/61.3/163.79/61.4) is competitive with the dedicated reasoning model TimeOmni-1 (87.7/64.0/145.53/58.9), trailing slightly on Tasks 1–2 but leading on Task 4, all without reinforcement learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VisionTS/VisionTS++ line of work, which reframes time series forecasting and imputation as image inpainting, by embedding the TS-image pipeline inside a unified multimodal model (Bagel) that jointly performs understanding and generation. It departs from prior vision-based TS methods (which rely on superficial pixel-level pattern matching) and text-centric understanding models (TSLMs, TSRMs, which fragment numbers into tokens and cannot produce high-fidelity sequences) by introducing Bi-TSI fidelity-preserving converters and a generation CoT derived from understanding QAs, making temporal semantics an explicit conditioning signal for the diffusion-based generation module. The conceptual bridge to vision UMMs—where understanding guides generation—is directly borrowed from the image domain (Zhang et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025) and transplanted to time series for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that time series understanding and generation can be internalized as native capabilities of a single UMM, opening a path where improvements in general-purpose multimodal backbones automatically benefit temporal tasks. However, the framework is currently task-specific (forecasting and imputation only), relies on a single backbone (Bagel-7B), and the TS-image representation introduces residual information loss from spatial interpolation and finite resolution. Open questions include whether the understanding-guided generation paradigm generalizes to other temporal tasks (anomaly detection, classification, irregular series), whether it scales to larger UMM backbones, and whether the 896×896 resolution requirement is practical for deployment. The TSUMM-SUITE benchmark and the Bi-TSI converter are useful community resources regardless of the specific backbone.
