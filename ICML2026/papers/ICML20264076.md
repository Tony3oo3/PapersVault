## Problem

Offline RL suffers from value overestimation when the learned policy queries the Q-function on out-of-distribution actions, and existing remedies either explicitly regularize the policy or rely on heuristic asymmetric losses (e.g., IQL's expectile regression) without a principled derivation. The paper asks whether in-sample learning can be obtained by directly constraining the Bellman evaluation error of the critic itself, yielding a unified framework that simultaneously derives both the value objective and the optimal policy extraction rule.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples collected by a behavior policy, with no online environment interaction. It outputs a standard Gaussian policy π_θ(a|s) parameterized by a 2-layer (MuJoCo) or 4-layer (AntMaze) MLP, avoiding the multi-step sampling overhead of diffusion-based actors. The value function is learned via a one-sided regression on the lagging target critic, and the policy is updated using a composite gradient that combines in-sample weighted behavior cloning with an orthogonal policy-gradient component.

## Method

REG formulates offline RL as a two-level robust optimization: maximize policy return subject to (i) the worst-case Bellman evaluation gap over an ambiguity set of data-consistent critics being bounded by ε₁, and (ii) each critic in the ambiguity set achieving low Bellman loss on the offline distribution within ε₂. Applying convex duality to this problem yields a tractable saddle-point objective whose value-function term is a one-sided regression max(0, Q−V)², providing a principled derivation of IQL's asymmetric update as an approximate dual solution. For policy extraction, the paper introduces Orthogonal Policy Gradient (OPG): the final update takes the full conservative weighted-behavior-cloning gradient g_wbc and adds only the component of the on-policy gradient g_pg that is orthogonal to it (g_pg − proj_{g_wbc}(g_pg)), scaled by λ. This geometric decomposition creates an emergent safety mechanism—when the two gradients align, the update is a regularized improvement step; when they conflict, the update automatically reverts toward the data-supported direction, preventing exploitation of OOD extrapolation errors.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, REG achieves a Locomotion-Average score of 87.8±0.9 and an AntMaze-Average of 77.5±2.3 (normalized scores, averaged over 5 seeds). Among Gaussian-policy methods, REG matches or near-matches the best baseline (ReBRAC: 88.4 Locomotion, 76.8 AntMaze) and substantially outperforms IQL (77.8/57.2), IVR (83.3/71.8), and AlignIQL (69.1/75.3). Against diffusion-policy baselines, REG is competitive with Diffusion-QL (87.9/69.6) on Locomotion while using a simpler Gaussian architecture, though it trails IDQL (81.9/79.2) on AntMaze.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among Gaussian-policy baselines, REG (87.8 Locomotion / 77.5 AntMaze) is within 0.6 points of ReBRAC (88.4 / 76.8) on Locomotion and leads ReBRAC by 0.7 points on AntMaze, making it the top or near-top Gaussian method overall. REG substantially outperforms IQL (77.8 / 57.2), IVR (83.3 / 71.8), EQL (83.7 / 70.2), and AlignIQL (69.1 / 75.3) on both task suites, with the largest gaps on AntMaze where IQL lags by 20+ points. Against diffusion-policy methods, REG is essentially tied with Diffusion-QL on Locomotion (87.8 vs 87.9) and ahead on AntMaze (77.5 vs 69.6), but trails IDQL on AntMaze (77.5 vs 79.2) while matching it on Locomotion (87.8 vs 81.9). The paper highlights that REG achieves this with a standard Gaussian policy and a single-step Bellman target, avoiding the multi-step sampling overhead of diffusion-based actors. On medium and medium-replay datasets—where policy improvement over suboptimal data is most needed—REG's OPG update yields the largest gains over conservative baselines, e.g., 91.9 on hopper-medium vs IQL's 66.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

REG positions itself as a principled bridge between implicit value learning (IQL, IDQL) and explicit conservative methods (CQL, TD3+BC, DICE). It builds on the convex-duality machinery shared with DICE and IVR but departs by constraining the Bellman evaluation error of the critic itself rather than occupancy measures or policy divergence. The key conceptual departure is showing that IQL's heuristic expectile regression emerges as an approximate dual solution to a distributionally-robust optimization over an ambiguity set of data-consistent critics, and the novel Orthogonal Policy Gradient (OPG) update geometrically decomposes the policy gradient into a safe WBC component and an orthogonal mode-seeking component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical derivation provides a long-sought justification for why IQL-style asymmetric losses work, reframing them as solutions to a robust evaluation problem rather than ad hoc heuristics. The OPG mechanism offers a practical, interpretable safety brake for policy improvement that is broadly applicable beyond this specific value-learning objective. However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental limitation: the practical algorithm still bootstraps from single-step Bellman targets, so error compounds over long horizons and is not reduced by more data. Open questions include whether the evaluation-gap regularization extends to multi-step or model-based settings, and whether the framework scales to vision-language-action models where offline data is plentiful but interaction is expensive.
