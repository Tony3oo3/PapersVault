## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks either evaluate static competence or short-to-medium-horizon non-adversarial tasks, leaving a critical gap in measuring whether LLMs can sustain goal-directed behavior across many rounds of observation, action, and feedback under partial observability, adaptive opponents, and delayed consequences. In particular, no prior benchmark simultaneously probes robustness to rule perturbations, competitive strength against peer agents, and faithful grounding of human tactical directives over long horizons with process-level diagnostics beyond terminal win/loss.

## Input / Output

At each decision round (every 47–64 seconds, yielding 28–38 rounds per 30-minute match), the LLM receives a structured JSON observation containing resources, power, buildings, combat units, harvesters, and visible enemies, and must output a budgeted list of unit-level commands (build, move, attack, attack_move) in a normalized JSON schema. A deterministic executor enforces action legality and translates intents into low-level OpenRA engine commands, so the LLM never issues raw APM-level inputs. Beyond the terminal win/draw/loss outcome, the benchmark logs four process-level metrics (kill-loss ratio, mineral income rate, expenditure ratio, scouting coverage), Davidson-model Elo ratings from pairwise cross-play, and three geometric tactical scores (encirclement quality, front-back formation quality, visibility coverage) computed from logged unit positions and map states.

## Method

The benchmark is organized into three complementary tracks evaluated under a shared hierarchical interface that decouples high-level strategic reasoning from low-level execution: Track A pits each LLM against four style-distinct rule-based opponents (Turtle, Normal, Rush, Naval) to attribute wins/losses to specific capability dimensions via process metrics; Track B runs full round-robin cross-play among six frontier LLMs and fits a Davidson extension of the Bradley-Terry model (with a global tie-propensity parameter) to produce opponent-agnostic Elo-style ratings and a pairwise win-rate matrix; Track C injects standardized human tactical directives (encirclement, front-back assault, reconnaissance) against a passive auto-firing opponent and scores realized multi-unit behavior with log-computable geometric metrics—participation-weighted angular coverage for encirclement, engagement-weighted discordant-pair penalties for radial role ordering, and union-of-visible-cells for exploration. All models are evaluated zero-shot via official APIs with default decoding on a single canonical map, with no cross-match memory, and the deterministic executor introduces no additional decision logic, ensuring that measured differences reflect strategic reasoning rather than micro-operation speed.

## Results

Across six frontier LLMs evaluated on a 30-minute Red Alert RTS benchmark, Claude-Sonnet-4.5 achieves the highest overall win rate (0.70) against rule-based opponents and the top Elo rating (2136.02) in LLM-vs-LLM cross-play, while GPT-4o is the only agent that remains completely loss-free in Track A (0.45 W / 0.55 D / 0.00 L) and leads front-back assault coordination (0.504). DeepSeek-V3.2 never secures a win in Track A (0.00 W / 0.75 D / 0.25 L) and ranks last in cross-play Elo (1036.53). Track C reveals pronounced capability fragmentation: Claude leads encirclement (0.647) and visibility coverage (0.833) but drops to fifth in front-back assault (0.456), while Kimi reverses this pattern, leading front-back (0.492) yet trailing in encirclement (0.495).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude-Sonnet-4.5 dominates the competitive tracks, achieving the highest Track A win rate (0.70 mean across four rule-based opponents) and a 506-point Elo lead over second-place Gemini in cross-play, with near-perfect win rates against Gemini, Kimi, Qwen, and DeepSeek. GPT-4o is the most balanced agent: it is the only model with zero losses in Track A, sustains the highest mineral throughput (4691.6 MC mean), and leads front-back assault coordination (0.504), yet falls to third in cross-play Elo (1602.90) behind Claude and Gemini. Gemini-2.5 Flash holds a clear second tier in cross-play (1630.38) and leads visibility coverage (0.801) alongside Claude, but records the weakest encirclement score (0.386) and is draw-dominant in Track A (0.85 draw rate). Kimi-K2.5-FW shows the sharpest cross-track reversal: it suffers the most losses in Track A (0.40 mean loss rate, including 0.80 vs. Rush AI) yet climbs to fourth in cross-play Elo (1548.37) and leads front-back assault (0.492), suggesting heterogeneous-unit coordination emerges more effectively against adaptive opponents. DeepSeek-V3.2 and Qwen-3-Max form the bottom tier in both competitive tracks, with DeepSeek never winning in Track A and Qwen being overwhelmingly draw-dominant (0.90 draw rate); their Elo separation (1160 vs. 1037) is the least statistically certain, with a 95% CI on the difference that crosses zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-agent evaluation line (AgentBench, WebArena, OSWorld) by moving from non-adversarial, short-to-medium-horizon web/software tasks to long-horizon adversarial RTS competition in OpenRA Red Alert, built on the OpenCodeAlert hackathon environment. It departs from prior interactive benchmarks in two key ways: (1) a hierarchical interface where the LLM outputs budgeted macro/tactical intents executed by a deterministic low-level executor, decoupling strategic reasoning from APM; and (2) a three-track protocol (vs. rule-based AI, vs. LLM via Davidson/Elo, and human command following) with process-level diagnostics (KLR, economy, scouting) that go beyond terminal win/loss. The Davidson extension of Bradley-Terry with ties for LLM cross-play ratings follows the Elo-style comparison tradition used in LLM-as-judge work (Zheng et al., 2023) but applies it to competitive game outcomes rather than preference judgments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that model rankings reverse substantially across tracks (e.g., Claude dominates LLM-vs-LLM cross-play but ranks 5th in heterogeneous front-back coordination; GPT-4o leads Track A macro throughput yet falls to mid-tier in adaptive cross-play)—challenges the assumption that a single aggregate score captures an LLM's interactive decision-making competence. This motivates multi-axis, instrumented evaluation as a prerequisite for deploying LLMs in long-horizon adversarial control loops. Key limitations include the single-map, single-game scope, the absence of cross-game adaptation or persistent memory, and the controlled (pre-triggered, non-dynamic) nature of human instructions in Track C, all of which constrain generalizability. Open questions include whether the observed capability fragmentation persists under richer maps, longer horizons, and genuinely dynamic human-AI interaction, and whether process-level metrics like KLR and scouting coverage transfer to other adversarial domains.
