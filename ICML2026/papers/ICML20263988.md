## Problem

Learning effective value heuristics for combinatorial search is hampered by two issues: random-walk training data provides only noisy step-index labels that upper-bound true optimal depth, and heuristic quality can only be assessed by embedding the model into a specific search algorithm, which is computationally expensive and masks heuristic differences at large search budgets. This paper addresses both by modeling depth uncertainty along trajectories and introducing a search-free local-ranking diagnostic.

## Input / Output

The model takes a discrete puzzle state (encoded via the CayleyPy backbone with residual structure) and produces two outputs: a scalar depth regression used directly for node ranking and search, and a discrete depth classification over {0, …, d_max} that supplies distributional emissions for HMM refinement. Training data consists of randomly generated trajectories from the solved cube, where exact optimal depths are available only up to a reference depth d_ref (e.g., 6 for HTM, 7 for QTM); deeper positions receive pseudo-labels from the anchored HMM. Evaluation uses CubeLocalRank, which tests whether the model correctly ranks the best candidate among 18 one-move or 261 two-step local neighbors, alongside downstream beam search on the DeepCubeA benchmark.

## Method

The framework treats optimal depth as a latent trajectory-level variable and refines noisy supervision through four stages. First, a depth-dynamics prior is estimated from exact-depth states: a categorical depth-change distribution q_Δ(d) over {−1, 0, +1} induces a Markov transition matrix A and step-conditioned priors π_t, with the effective depth range (d_max, s_max) selected from tail behavior of the propagated prior. Second, an anchored HMM performs forward-backward inference along each trajectory, combining sparse exact anchors (from a BFS store), emissions from an EMA teacher network (temperature-scaled classification + Gaussian regression likelihood), and step-feasibility masks to yield smoothed posterior marginals γ_t(d) and decoded pseudo-labels. Third, a dual-head student network (shared encoder with scalar regression and discrete classification heads) is trained on these refined labels using a composite loss: MSE and cross-entropy on pseudo-labels, a neighbor-consistency penalty on adjacent trajectory predictions, KL terms matching predicted distributions to step and global priors, and a feasibility penalty. Fourth, the student updates the teacher via EMA, closing a refinement-training loop. The key distinction from prior work is that depth is inferred jointly across the trajectory via HMM smoothing rather than treated as independent per-state regression targets, and heuristic quality is evaluated search-free via CubeLocalRank local ranking accuracy.

## Results

On the DeepCubeA benchmark under QTM at beam 2^18, the proposed method achieves 80.5% optimality (52B training) and 77.8% (10B), outperforming CayleyPy (75.4%) and EfficientCube (69.8%). Under Batch A* (matching DeepCubeA's protocol), the 4M-parameter model reaches 85.4% optimality versus DeepCubeA's 60.3% with 14.7M parameters. On the search-free CubeLocalRank benchmark (HTM, level-1), the method improves Aggregate Rank@1 from 0.40 (CayleyPy-style baseline) to 0.43. On larger puzzles (Santa Claus 2023), the 5×5×5 model solves all 19 instances by beam 2^16, whereas CayleyPy reaches only 68.4% at beam 2^18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CayleyPy (Chervov et al., 2025), the proposed method wins on both HTM and QTM at beam 2^18: 68.1% vs. 62.3% (HTM, 52B) and 80.5% vs. 75.4% (QTM, 52B), with shorter average solution lengths. At the very large beam 2^24 under QTM, the single model (97.7%) closely matches CayleyPy's single model (97.3%) and approaches its 26-model ensemble (98.4%), indicating the gap narrows as search saturates. Against DeepCubeA (Agostinelli et al., 2019), the method achieves a substantial 25.1-point optimality gain (85.4% vs. 60.3%) under Batch A* while using 3.7× fewer parameters (4M vs. 14.7M). Against EfficientCube (Takano, 2023), the method leads by 10.7 points at beam 2^18 under QTM (80.5% vs. 69.8%). On the search-free CubeLocalRank metric, the method improves Aggregate Rank@1 by 3 percentage points over the CayleyPy-style baseline (0.43 vs. 0.40), confirming that the gains reflect intrinsic heuristic quality rather than search-algorithm-specific effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CayleyPy neural backbone (Chervov et al., 2025) but departs fundamentally from its training signal: rather than using random-walk step indices as noisy depth proxies or relying on iterative bootstrapping (DeepCubeA) or abandoning value learning for move prediction (EfficientCube), it models optimal depth as a latent trajectory-level variable refined via an anchored HMM with forward-backward inference. The depth-dynamics prior and EMA teacher-student loop are novel components that replace the ad-hoc label handling in prior work. The paper also introduces CubeLocalRank, a search-free local-ranking benchmark that has no direct analogue in the prior Rubik's Cube literature, where heuristic quality was assessed only through downstream search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The search-free CubeLocalRank protocol addresses a real practical bottleneck: downstream beam search is expensive and saturates at large beam widths, making it hard to isolate heuristic quality from search configuration. The HMM-based label refinement provides a principled, well-motivated solution to the step-vs-depth mismatch that plagues all random-walk training data. Limitations include the dependence on a shallow exact-depth store (d_ref ≤ 7–8 for memory reasons), the assumption that depth-dynamics extrapolate smoothly beyond the labeled region, and the requirement for solver-labeled neighborhoods to construct CubeLocalRank. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to domains without known optimal solvers or with highly irregular state-space geometry, and whether training directly against ranking objectives could further improve heuristic quality.
