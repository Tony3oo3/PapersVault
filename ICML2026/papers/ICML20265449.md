## Problem

Existing D-OCO algorithms with compressed communication (Tu et al., 2022) suffer from prohibitively high quadratic or quartic dependence on the inverse compression ratio ω⁻¹ and superlinear dependence on the number of learners n, making them impractical when aggressive compression (ω≪1) is needed. Moreover, no lower bounds characterizing the dependence on ω had been established for this problem, leaving the optimality of existing regret bounds unknown.

## Input / Output

Each learner i receives its local convex loss function f_{t,i} and the compressed messages from its neighbors in the graph G, with communication governed by a doubly stochastic matrix P (spectral gap ρ) and an ω-contractive compressor C. The algorithm outputs a single decision x_i(b) per block of L rounds (rather than per round), and the theoretical output is regret bounds that scale as O(ω^{-1/2}ρ^{-1}n√lnn√T) for convex and O(ω^{-1}ρ^{-2}nlnnlnT) for strongly convex losses, along with matching lower bounds up to ρ and polylog factors.

## Method

Top-DOGD introduces a two-level blocking update framework that divides T rounds into blocks of size L = L₁ + L₂, updating the decision only once per block. In the first sub-block (L₁ rounds), an online compressed gossip strategy performs multi-step Choco-gossip to exponentially decay consensus and compression errors. In the second sub-block (L₂ rounds), a projection error compensation scheme recursively applies the compressor to the projection residual over L₂ = ⌈ln(8n)/ω⌉ rounds, ensuring the compression error on the projection term is O(1/n) and thereby decoupling it from the main compression error. The blocking structure amortizes the multiple internal communication rounds into exactly one communication per round, preserving the single-communication-per-round constraint of D-OCO. This design reduces the ω-dependence from ω⁻²/ω⁻⁴ to ω^{-1/2}/ω⁻¹ and the n-dependence from superlinear to linear, and the paper further establishes the first lower bounds Ω(ω^{-1/2}ρ^{-1/4}n√T) and Ω(ω^{-1}ρ^{-1/2}nlnT) via a randomized gossip compressor on a 1-connected cycle graph.

## Results

For convex loss functions, Top-DOGD achieves a regret bound of Õ(ω^{-1/2}ρ^{-1}n√ln n·√T), improving upon the previous O(max{ω^{-2}ρ^{-4}n^{1/2}, ω^{-4}ρ^{-8}}n√T) from Tu et al. (2022). For strongly convex losses, the bound is Õ(ω^{-1}ρ^{-2}n ln n·ln T), versus the prior O(max{ω^{-2}ρ^{-4}n^{1/2}, ω^{-4}ρ^{-8}}n ln T). The paper also establishes the first lower bounds for this problem: Ω(ω^{-1/2}ρ^{-1/4}n√T) (convex) and Ω(ω^{-1}ρ^{-1/2}n ln T) (strongly convex), which match the upper bounds up to the spectral gap ρ and polylogarithmic factors in n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DC-DOGD from Tu et al. (2022), which integrates Choco-gossip with D-OGD for compressed communication. Top-DOGD strictly dominates DC-DOGD across all four settings (convex/strongly convex × full/bandit feedback). The key improvements are: (i) the dependence on the compression ratio ω drops from quadratic (ω^{-2}) or quartic (ω^{-4}) to square-root (ω^{-1/2}) or linear (ω^{-1}), which is critical when ω ≪ 1; (ii) the spectral gap dependence improves from ρ^{-4} to ρ^{-1}; and (iii) the network size dependence becomes linear in n rather than superlinear (n^{3/2} in the worst case). The newly established lower bounds confirm that the ω and T dependencies are near-optimal, with the remaining gap lying only in the ρ exponent (ρ^{-1} upper vs. ρ^{-1/4} lower for convex). In the bandit feedback setting (Table 2), Top-DOBD-1 and Top-DOBD-2 similarly improve all three parameters (ω, ρ, n) over Tu et al.'s bandit extensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tu et al. (2022)'s DC-DOGD, which first combined Choco-gossip (Koloskova et al., 2019) with decentralized online gradient descent to handle compressed communication in D-OCO. The key architectural departure is the two-level blocking update framework: the first level applies multi-step Choco-gossip within a block to accelerate consensus convergence (motivated by Wan et al., 2024a's blocking mechanism), while the second level uses a repeated compressor (Huang et al., 2022) to transmit projection-error residuals with exponentially decaying error. This combination decouples the projection error from the compression error, which was the source of the superlinear n-dependence in prior work. The paper also extends the analysis to bandit feedback via classical one-point and two-point gradient estimators (Flaxman et al., 2005; Agarwal et al., 2010; Shamir, 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first lower bounds for D-OCO with compressed communication, showing that the ω^{-1/2} and ω^{-1} dependence in the upper bounds is essentially tight (up to ρ and polylog factors), which anchors the problem's theoretical landscape. The dramatic reduction in ω-dependence (from ω^{-2}/ω^{-4} to ω^{-1/2}/ω^{-1}) is practically significant for settings where aggressive compression (ω ≪ 1) is necessary. However, a gap remains in the spectral gap ρ (ρ^{-1} upper vs. ρ^{-1/4} lower for convex losses), which the authors attribute to the Choco-gossip analysis and identify as the main open direction. Additional open questions include communication-dependent lower bounds and extending the framework to delayed feedback scenarios.
