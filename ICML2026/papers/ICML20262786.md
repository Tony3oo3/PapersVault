## Problem

Adaptive gradient methods like AdaGrad and Adam are widely used in practice and empirically robust to heavy-tailed gradient noise, yet no prior work provides a convergence guarantee for AdaGrad under heavy-tailed noise without algorithmic modifications, nonstandard assumptions, or prior knowledge of the tail index p. The only existing result (Chezhegov et al., 2025) applies to a delayed variant rarely used in practice and still requires gradient clipping plus the value of p as input, leaving the theoretical explanation for AdaGrad's empirical success under heavy tails open.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a differentiable (possibly non-convex) objective f: R^d → R, an initial point x_1, a scalar learning rate γ > 0, and a stochastic gradient oracle whose noise ξ_t = g_t − ∇f(x_t) satisfies a coordinate-wise finite p-th moment condition E[|ξ_{t,i}|^p] ≤ σ_i^p for p ∈ (1,2]. The output is a high-probability or in-expectation upper bound on the average gradient norm (ℓ1 for AdaGrad, ℓ2 for AdaGrad-Norm) after T iterations, with the rate automatically adapting to both the tail index p and the noise level ‖σ‖ without either being specified as a hyperparameter. The analysis also requires coordinate-wise L-smoothness (a vector L ∈ R^d_{>0}) rather than the standard scalar smoothness constant.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a generalized proxy stepsize w_t = v_{t-1} + (∇f(x_t))^2 + c^2, where c ∈ R^d_{≥0} is a free parameter set to c_i = σ_i T^{1/2−1/p} / D_{T,i}^{1/2−1/p} with D_{T,i} depending on log(1 + (σ_i T^{1/p} + E[√u_{T,i}])/λ). This choice, which degenerates to c = σ when p = 2, is critical: naively setting c = σ (as in prior work) yields only Õ(1/T^{(2p−3)/(2p)}), strictly worse than the achieved Õ(1/T^{(3p−4)/(4p)}) rate for p ∈ (4/3, 2]. The per-iteration progress inequality (Lemma A.2) is derived by decomposing the difference between the proxy and actual stepsizes, bounding the cross-term via Hölder's inequality with exponent p, and applying AM-GM to absorb the c-dependent term. For AdaGrad-Norm, a fundamentally different proof strategy exploits the scalar stepsize structure and a bounded-objective assumption to telescope the sum of (f(x_t) − f_⋆)/γ_t, yielding the faster O(1/T^{(p−1)/(2p)}) rate for all p ∈ (1,2] without polylogarithmic factors. The paper also establishes the first algorithm-dependent lower bound for AdaGrad in d = 1, showing the minimax rate is unattainable without prior knowledge of Δ and L.

## Results

The paper establishes the first provable convergence rate for AdaGrad under heavy-tailed noise in non-convex optimization: Õ(1/T^((3p−4)/(4p))) for tail index p ∈ (4/3, 2], without requiring prior knowledge of p or any algorithmic modifications. For AdaGrad-Norm under an additional bounded-objective assumption, the rate improves to O(1/T^((p−1)/(2p))), which is never vacuous for any p ∈ (1, 2] and matches the best-known rate of NSGD(M) without prior information. The paper also provides the first algorithm-dependent lower bound for AdaGrad, showing the minimax rate Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ^(p/(p−1))/ε^((3p−2)/(p−1))) is generally unattainable by AdaGrad without prior knowledge of problem parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical results are positioned against several existing bounds. The minimax rate Θ(1/T^((p−1)/(3p−2))) for heavy-tailed non-convex optimization (Zhang et al., 2020b; Liu & Zhou, 2025) is achievable only by Clipped SGD or NSGD(M) with prior knowledge of the tail index p; AdaGrad's rate Õ(1/T^((3p−4)/(4p))) is strictly slower but requires no such prior information. AdaGrad-Norm under bounded objectives achieves O(1/T^((p−1)/(2p))), exactly matching the parameter-free rate of NSGD(M) (Hübler et al., 2025; Liu & Zhou, 2025), suggesting adaptive gradient methods can be as effective as normalization-based approaches in this regime. The algorithm-dependent lower bound (Theorem 3.3) strictly improves upon the existing one-dimensional lower bound for AdaGrad at p=2 from Jiang et al. (2025) by explicitly capturing dependence on the input learning rate γ. In the boundary case p=2, Theorem 3.1 recovers the well-known Õ(1/T^(1/4)) rate, consistent with prior results (Wang et al., 2023b; Jiang et al., 2025), while the noiseless case (σ=0) yields the optimal Õ(1/T^(1/2)) rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of AdaGrad (Ward et al., 2019) from the classical finite-variance setting to heavy-tailed noise, generalizing the proxy stepsize technique by introducing a free parameter c that is tuned to the tail index p. It builds on the lower-bound framework of Jiang et al. (2025) and Hübler et al. (2025) to establish the first algorithm-dependent lower bound for AdaGrad under heavy tails, and positions itself as a direct response to the limitations of Chezhegov et al. (2025), whose results require delayed updates, gradient clipping, or prior knowledge of the tail index. The work also connects to the normalization-based heavy-tailed analysis of NSGD(M) (Hübler et al., 2025; Liu & Zhou, 2025), showing that AdaGrad-Norm matches the best known rate for that method when objectives are bounded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical evidence that adaptive gradient methods (the family behind Adam, AdamW, RMSProp) can converge under heavy-tailed noise without any algorithmic modifications, partially explaining their empirical success in training attention-based models where heavy tails are prevalent. The lower bound reveals a fundamental limitation: AdaGrad cannot attain the minimax rate for heavy-tailed non-convex optimization, suggesting that the adaptivity mechanism has inherent costs. Key open questions include whether Adam and AdamW (which are more widely used than AdaGrad) enjoy similar guarantees, whether the upper and lower bounds are tight, and whether the vacuous regime p ∈ (1, 4/3] can be closed with improved techniques.
