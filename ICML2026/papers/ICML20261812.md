## Problem

Density and score estimation from i.i.d. samples is bifurcated: classical KDE generalizes across distributions but suffers from the curse of dimensionality, while neural score-matching models achieve high precision but are transductive, requiring full retraining for every new target distribution. No existing method provides a single, distribution-agnostic, plug-in estimator that simultaneously outputs both density and score at arbitrary query points without per-distribution retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261812/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two sets of points: a context set X ∈ R^{n×d} of i.i.d. samples from an unknown density, and a query set Y ∈ R^{n_y×d} at which to evaluate. It produces, for each query y_i, a scalar log-density log f_X(y_i) and a d-dimensional score vector ∇_y log f_X(y_i). The cross-attention design means queries are decoupled from the training sample size n (trained at n=2048 but evaluated at arbitrary n_y), and the squared-norm lift [z, ‖z‖²] is applied to every token before entering the Transformer, making the architecture a constructive generalization of Gaussian KDE rather than a black-box regressor.

## Method

DiScoFormer is a ~800K-parameter, 4-layer, 8-head Transformer encoder without positional encodings, made permutation-equivariant by construction and affine-equivariant via a whitening layer that centers the data and applies the inverse matrix square root of the regularized scatter matrix S = X_c^T X_c + εI, reducing any affine transform to a residual O(d) rotation handled by training augmentation on randomly oriented GMMs. Cross-attention between context (samples) and query streams enables evaluation at arbitrary points; a theoretical result (Proposition 3.5) proves that a single residual cross-attention block with a squared-norm lift exactly represents the KDE score and log-density, with the per-query log-normalizer supplying the density scale. Training is supervised on GMMs with 1–10 modes using a convex combination of MSE losses on log-density and score, and a joint architecture with shared backbone and two output heads enables a label-free consistency loss (score = gradient of predicted log-density) for test-time training on out-of-distribution inputs.

## Results

In the high-dimensional regime (d=100, n=2048), DiScoFormer achieves a score MSE of 0.167 and log-density MSE of 20.8, compared to 1.155/967 for Scott KDE and 1.090/781 for Oracle-bandwidth KDE — a 6.5× reduction in score MSE and 37.5× reduction in log-density MSE. In lower dimensions (d=1 and d=10), the Transformer also consistently outperforms KDE across all sample sizes, with the gap widening as dimension increases. On out-of-distribution targets (Laplace, Student-t), the model remains competitive or superior to KDE, and test-time training with 4–8 steps further closes the gap (e.g., Student-t score MSE drops from 0.1119 to 0.0897 at n=512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiScoFormer outperforms Scott KDE and Oracle-bandwidth KDE on both score and log-density MSE across all tested dimensions (d=1 through d=100), with the advantage growing sharply in high dimensions (6.5× and 37.5× in d=100). Against SD-KDE (Epstein et al., 2025), the paper shows that plugging DiScoFormer's learned score into the SD-KDE pipeline yields visibly less biased density estimates than Emp-SD-KDE (which uses Scott KDE as its score oracle), particularly in 1D bimodal GMMs and at larger sample sizes. Compared to sliced score matching (Song et al., 2019), DiScoFormer requires no per-distribution retraining and avoids the over/under-fitting sensitivity that plagues score matching at different training step counts (10, 100, 1000 steps). In the homogeneous Landau equation simulation, DiScoFormer as a pre-trained score oracle matches the quality of Score-Based Transport Modeling (Ilin et al., 2025) while eliminating the expensive per-step score-matching retraining, and clearly outperforms the KDE-based particle solver.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges classical nonparametric statistics (KDE, Parzen windows) and modern Transformer architectures by proving that self-attention with a squared-norm lift exactly recovers normalized Gaussian KDE weights, scores, and log-densities (Propositions 3.3–3.5). It departs from score-matching methods (Hyvärinen 2005; Song & Ermon 2019), which learn a fixed function for a single distribution, by framing estimation as a sequence-to-operator task where one pretrained model generalizes across distributions and sample sizes. The architecture inherits permutation equivariance from the Transformer and achieves approximate affine equivariance via a whitening layer, extending the symmetry framework of DeepSets and Set Transformers to the richer group of affine transformations. The theoretical connection builds on the attention-as-kernel-smoothing lineage (Katharopoulos et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2023) but makes it fully constructive by showing a single residual block with a specific lift represents the exact KDE score and density at arbitrary query points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in score oracle addresses a practical bottleneck in score-based PDE solvers (Fokker-Planck, Landau) and information-theoretic estimators that previously required per-distribution retraining or suffered from KDE's curse of dimensionality; the 6.5× score MSE reduction in d=100 demonstrates that the curse is substantially mitigated. Key limitations include training exclusively on GMMs (generalization to far-from-GMM targets is bounded but not guaranteed), only approximate rotation equivariance via data augmentation, and the absence of asymptotic consistency guarantees that KDE enjoys with well-chosen bandwidths. Open questions include whether the O(1/N) stability condition in Theorem B.2 can be proven for the trained architecture, whether the GMM training family can be replaced by a more expressive surrogate, and whether the test-time training consistency loss can be made provably convergent.
