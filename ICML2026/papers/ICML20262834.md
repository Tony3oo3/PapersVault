## Problem

Video Diffusion Transformers (vDiTs) suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity that severely limits practical deployment for long-sequence video generation. Existing sparse attention methods either impose rigid static patterns (e.g., fixed vertical grids, global exponential decay) or rely on computationally expensive sampling to achieve dynamic sparsity, both of which fail to capture the continuously evolving, probabilistic mixture of structural patterns in vDiT attention maps. This mismatch between static masks and fluid attention dynamics degrades generation quality while failing to deliver meaningful speedups.

## Input / Output

MOD-DiT is a plug-and-play, training-free inference-time acceleration layer applied to existing video diffusion transformers (HunyuanVideo-13B at 117 frames 768×1280, Wan2.1-14B at 69 frames 768×1280, CogVideoX-v1.5-2B at 89 frames 640×512). It takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a generated video as output, with no changes to the model architecture or training pipeline. The method operates entirely within the attention computation, replacing full O(N²) attention with a dynamically masked sparse variant that achieves 80–83% sparsity while preserving generation quality.

## Method

MOD-DiT reveals that vDiT attention sparsity maps decompose into a dynamic mixture of three structural bases—block-diagonal (intra-frame spatial coherence), parallel-to-main-diagonal (inter-frame temporal continuity), and vertical (global cross-token dependencies)—and models this mixture via a linear approximation solved by a hardware-optimized CUDA least-squares kernel (100× faster than torch.lstsq). In the mid-to-late denoising regime, the intensities of the vertical and parallel-diagonal patterns exhibit stable piecewise linearity, enabling a lightweight linear predictor that extrapolates pattern coefficients over short intervals (Δt steps) without any sampling. An iterative temporal fusion strategy reconstructs a proxy dense attention map from the current masked computation and historical reconstructions, periodically re-anchoring predictions to prevent drift. The final mask is generated by Top-K selection over predicted pattern intensities combined with conditional block-diagonal preservation via static thresholding, applied at three levels of granularity: per attention head, per denoising step, and per block. The framework requires m=12 full-attention warm-up steps before switching to sparse inference, and the entire overhead accounts for only 1–2% of full-attention latency.

## Results

MOD-DiT achieves 2.05× speedup on HunyuanVideo-13B (3405s vs. 6978s full attention) and 1.75× on Wan2.1-14B (1929s vs. 3375s) while maintaining the highest sparsity among all methods (83.23% and 81.37% respectively). On HunyuanVideo, it attains PSNR 27.73, SSIM 0.879, LPIPS 0.119, Subject Consistency 0.9398, and Imaging Quality 0.6587, outperforming all sparse baselines on the speed-quality trade-off. On Wan2.1, it reaches PSNR 22.75, SSIM 0.821, LPIPS 0.152, S.C. 0.9427, and I.Q. 0.6674, again leading in both acceleration and generation fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOD-DiT outperforms all five baselines on both speedup and sparsity across both models. Against SVG (Sparse VideoGen), the gap is most dramatic on Subject Consistency: MOD-DiT scores 0.9398 vs. 0.8301 on HunyuanVideo and 0.9427 vs. 0.7986 on Wan2.1, while also achieving 18–25% higher speedup. Against Radial Attention, MOD-DiT delivers comparable or slightly better quality (e.g., PSNR 27.73 vs. 26.72 on Hunyuan; S.C. 0.9427 vs. 0.9152 on Wan2.1) while being 9–12% faster (3405s vs. 3731s; 1929s vs. 2021s). Against MInference, MOD-DiT achieves substantially higher fidelity (PSNR 27.73 vs. 18.21, SSIM 0.879 vs. 0.638 on Hunyuan) with 35% lower latency. Against SpargeAttention, MOD-DiT wins on both speed (2.05× vs. 1.70× on Hunyuan) and quality (PSNR 27.73 vs. 25.43, LPIPS 0.119 vs. 0.195). Against the latest SVG-2 iteration (Table 6), MOD-DiT still leads on Subject Consistency (0.9398 vs. 0.9325), Motion Smoothness (0.9687 vs. 0.9636), and Imaging Quality (0.6587 vs. 0.6431) with marginally lower latency (1044s vs. 1056s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant sparse attention paradigm for video diffusion transformers, which relies on either static geometric patterns (Radial Attention, Sparse-vDiT) or online sampling to determine dynamic masks (SVG, MInference, SpargeAttention). MOD-DiT instead posits that attention sparsity maps are well-approximated by a linear combination of three structured basis patterns (block-diagonal, parallel-to-main-diagonal, vertical) whose intensities evolve piecewise-linearly across denoising steps, enabling a sampling-free linear prediction of masks. The hardware acceleration builds on FlashAttention-2 for warm-up and SageAttention for sparse execution, while the theoretical framing of three orthogonal interaction subspaces is a novel contribution not present in prior sparse attention work for vDiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2.05× speedup at 83% sparsity with near-lossless quality on HunyuanVideo, achieved in a fully training-free and sampling-free manner, makes this a highly practical plug-and-play acceleration for production video generation pipelines. The three-pattern decomposition provides a principled, interpretable understanding of vDiT attention structure that could inform future architectural design or further compression. Open questions include whether the piecewise-linearity assumption holds for all model scales and architectures, how the warm-up overhead affects short-sequence or few-step distilled models, and whether the approach composes cleanly with emerging quantization and feature-caching techniques.
