## Problem

Text-to-music diffusion models produce repetitive outputs even with varied noise and prompts, and existing inference-time diversity methods like CADS— which perturbs the full conditioning embedding—sharply degrade text alignment and audio fidelity as perturbation strength increases. Moreover, in standard audio-only VAE latent spaces, local neighborhoods do not align with text-implied genre structure, so even successful exploration drifts away from the requested genre, making genre-consistent diversity unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265558/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a tag-based text prompt (tokenized to a fixed-length sequence of max 128 tokens, zero-padded for shorter prompts) and an initial Gaussian noise sample, and produces instrumental music audio clips of at least 30 seconds (64-channel, 1024-step latent at 44.1 kHz). Generation uses 100 DPM-Solver++ steps with classifier-free guidance weight 7.0. PADS is applied purely at sampling time by masking noise injection to padding positions, while TAL requires a retrained VAE and diffusion model but no architectural change to the DiT backbone.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. PADS is an inference-time strategy that generalizes CADS via a binary mask: annealed Gaussian noise is injected only into the padding-indexed (low-salience) positions of the text-conditioning sequence while all non-padding semantic embeddings remain fixed, so the Langevin-like stochastic term in the ODE update is restricted to directions with minimal first-order effect on prompt semantics. TAL is a text-aware latent space built by training a Mixture-of-Experts multimodal VAE with a shared-private factorization: a private audio encoder captures fine-grained acoustics (w_m), a shared text encoder captures genre-level semantics (z_p), and a Latent Alignment loss pulls the audio- and text-derived shared-latent means together. The diffusion model is then trained to denoise in the concatenated TAL representation [w_m; z_p], so sampling naturally stays within genre-faithful neighborhoods. Together, TAL aligns the geometry of the sampling space with text semantics, and PADS provides controlled exploration within that aligned space, yielding 15.4% higher diversity and 71.6% higher within-genre diversity at matched text alignment compared to full-conditioning CADS.

## Results

At matched CLAP-f ≈ 0.32 on SongDescriber, the unified PADS-TAL pipeline achieves Vendi 26.075 (vs. 22.591 for Audio+CADS, a 15.4% improvement) and mean IPR_CLAP-f Recall 0.407 (vs. 0.237, a 71.6% improvement in within-genre diversity). It also attains the best fidelity: KL_passt 0.626 and FD_openl3 135.542, both lower than all other configurations. On MelBench, TAL-based models achieve higher per-genre IPR Recall than SAO for most genres, with especially large gains for acoustically distinctive genres like Metal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CADS (Sadat et al., 2024), the primary perturbation baseline, PADS-TAL dominates on every metric at matched alignment: Vendi 26.075 vs. 22.591 (+15.4%), mRecall 0.407 vs. 0.237 (+71.6%), KL_passt 0.626 vs. 0.669, and FD_openl3 135.542 vs. 147.730. On ACE-Step (Gong et al., 2025), PADS preserves text alignment far better than CADS (CLAP-f 0.2318 vs. 0.1981) while achieving comparable Vendi (23.34 vs. 23.41) and substantially lower KL_passt (0.7918 vs. 0.9240). On MelodyFlow (Le Lan et al., 2024), CADS fails to increase diversity (outputs become noise-like), whereas PADS yields a stable Vendi gain from 18.22 to 21.58. Against sampling-time diversity methods (OSCAR, Particle Guidance, c-VSG, SPARKE, LIG) adapted to the T2M setting, PADS-TAL achieves the best diversity–alignment trade-off in the practically usable regime, maintaining stronger CLAP-f while delivering higher Vendi and IBS. Human evaluation (136 participants) confirms these trends: PADS-TAL scores 3.79/3.71/3.78 on Quality/Diversity/Alignment, outperforming SAO+CADS (3.36/3.56/2.87) on all three axes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CADS (Sadat et al., 2024), a T2I inference-time diversity method that injects annealed Gaussian noise into the full conditioning embedding, by restricting perturbations to a low-salience padding-indexed subspace (PADS), formalized as a mask-controlled generalization where CADS is the M=1 special case. For the representation side, it departs from the standard audio-only VAE in latent diffusion pipelines (as in Stable Audio Open) by introducing a text-aware latent space (TAL) built on a Mixture-of-Experts multimodal VAE (Shi et al., 2019; Sutter et al., 2020; Palumbo et al., 2023) with a novel Latent Alignment loss that pulls audio- and text-derived shared latents closer. The key conceptual departure from prior diversity methods is the observation that in T2M, global attributes like genre are established in early denoising steps, making full-conditioning perturbation particularly destructive compared to T2I.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: T2M diffusion models produce repetitive outputs, and the most natural fix (CADS-style conditioning noise) degrades text alignment and fidelity in music more severely than in images. The unified PADS+TAL pipeline offers a drop-in inference-time strategy (PADS) plus a retraining recipe (TAL) that together yield a 15.4% diversity gain at matched alignment and a 71.6% within-genre diversity improvement, though the latter is relative to a very low baseline (0.237→0.407 mRecall). A key open question acknowledged by the authors is whether the shared latent in the MoE-mVAE is fully exploited by the decoders, and whether the approach generalizes beyond instrumental music to vocal or longer-form generation. The CLAP-f evaluation protocol, while minor, addresses a real reproducibility concern in music evaluation.
