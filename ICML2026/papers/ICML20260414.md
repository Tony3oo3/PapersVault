## Problem

In Multi-Instance Partial-Label (MIPL) learning, each bag carries a candidate label set with one true label and multiple false positives, creating coupled ambiguity in both instance selection and label identification. Existing methods adapt MIL and PLL mechanisms independently, failing to address how these two forms of ambiguity interact, which makes attention-based aggregation easily distracted by irrelevant instances and susceptible to false-positive labels.

## Input / Output

Each input is a bag of variable-size instances (grayscale 28×28 images for MNIST/FMNIST, 38- or 30-dimensional tabular vectors for Birdsong/SIVAL, or 9- to 128-dimensional histopathology patch features for CRC-MIPL) paired with a candidate label set of size r+1 (one true label plus r uniformly sampled false positives). The method outputs a bag-level class prediction over q classes, along with per-instance attention scores that enable key-instance localization evaluation via Top-1 hit rate and average rank metrics.

## Method

AGOPMIPL computes the Average Gradient Outer Product of the classifier's Jacobian across all bags to obtain a metric matrix that rescales the feature space, amplifying directions where the classifier is most sensitive (dominated by cross-bag-coherent true-label signal) while attenuating bag-specific false-positive noise. A dual-branch attention module then computes scores on both the original and AGOP-transformed instance/prototype features, combining them with a learned temperature to aggregate the bag representation. Class-specific prototypes are constructed via label-aware k-means clustering to provide semantic reference points for attention. A progressive disambiguation schedule (warmup-decay) shifts the training target from the noisy candidate-label vector toward the model's own masked predictions within each epoch, and the metric matrix is updated via exponential moving average after each epoch. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) guarantees that under spectral-bounded false-positive noise, the AGOP transform amplifies the true-label subspace relative to orthogonal noise directions by a factor of √((λ_s + σ₋²)/σ₊²).

## Results

On the four MIPL benchmarks, AGOPMIPL achieves the highest accuracy on all SIVAL-MIPL settings (75.9% at r=1, 73.4% at r=2, 70.5% at r=3) and on most MNIST-MIPL and FMNIST-MIPL settings, with a perfect 100% on MNIST-MIPL at r=2. On the real-world CRC-MIPL dataset, AGOPMIPL outperforms all baselines on all four bag constructions, with the largest gain of 25.9% relative improvement on C-KMeansSeg (72.2% vs. 57.3% for the best baseline). Under heavy candidate-set noise (r=5) on 25-class SIVAL-MIPL, AGOPMIPL retains 54.4% accuracy versus 44.9% for FastMIPL and 40.9% for ELIMIPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGOPMIPL consistently outperforms all five MIPL baselines on SIVAL-MIPL across all noise levels, with margins of 5.1–15.0 points over the strongest competitor (FastMIPL). On MNIST-MIPL and FMNIST-MIPL, AGOPMIPL is best or tied at r=1 and r=2, and clearly leads at r=3 (e.g., 98.6% vs. 97.5% for FastMIPL and 78.3% for ProMIPL on MNIST). FastMIPL is the closest competitor, retaining the edge on Birdsong-MIPL at r=2,3 (77.0%/76.7% vs. 76.9%/74.2%) and on FMNIST-MIPL at r=3 (75.8% vs. 75.2%). ProMIPL, ELIMIPL, DEMIPL, and MIPLGP are uniformly outperformed by AGOPMIPL on every benchmark setting. On the real-world CRC-MIPL dataset, AGOPMIPL achieves 53.4%/61.0%/72.2%/58.9% on C-Row/C-SBN/C-KMeans/C-SIFT, surpassing the best baseline (FastMIPL) by 4.7/3.7/14.9/6.3 points respectively, with the 25.9% relative gain on C-KMeansSeg being the most striking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AGOP-based feature learning framework (Radhakrishnan et al., 2024), previously validated in fully supervised settings, into the weakly supervised MIPL domain where supervision is both coarse (bag-level) and ambiguous (candidate label sets). It departs from existing MIPL methods (DEMIPL, ELIMIPL, FastMIPL, ProMIPL, MIPLGP) that inherit MIL attention and PLL disambiguation mechanisms independently, by introducing AGOP as a pre-attention metric transformation that reshapes the feature geometry before bag aggregation. The dual-branch attention module builds on the permutation-invariant pooling paradigm of Ilse et al. (2018), while the progressive label disambiguation strategy adapts curriculum-learning principles to the candidate-set setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25.9% accuracy gain on CRC-MIPL-KMeansSeg and consistent improvements across four benchmarks demonstrate that explicitly modeling the interaction between instance-level and label-level ambiguity—rather than treating them separately—yields substantial practical benefits, particularly on real-world histopathology data. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.4) provides a principled justification for why cross-bag averaging of Jacobian outer products amplifies true-label directions while attenuating bag-specific noise, offering a design principle potentially transferable to other weakly supervised settings. Open questions include whether the linear-classifier assumption (which makes the Jacobian input-independent and trivially computable) can be relaxed for deeper non-linear heads, whether the spectral-boundedness assumption on false-positive sensitivity holds in adversarial or highly structured noise regimes, and whether the approach scales to very large bag sizes typical of whole-slide imaging.
