## Problem

Long chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs suffers from quadratic attention cost, hard context-window limits, and lost-in-the-middle degradation. Existing iterative reasoning methods that periodically summarize intermediate thoughts rely on supervised fine-tuning or fixed heuristics, failing to optimize the three coupled decisions of when to compress, what to preserve, and how to resume—sequential choices whose long-horizon consequences demand trajectory-level optimization that SFT cannot provide.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text query (e.g., a math problem) and produces a multi-iteration reasoning trajectory where each iteration consists of a bounded reasoning segment (≤η=6k tokens) followed by a compressed summary (≤γ=1k tokens), except the final iteration which emits a conclusion. The model autonomously decides iteration boundaries and termination, with a hard cap of φ=5 iterations. Unlike vanilla reasoning that emits a single continuous chain-of-thought, the output is a sequence of (reasoning, summary) pairs connected through the summary channel, with each iteration conditioned only on the original query and the most recent summary rather than the full history.

## Method

InftyThink+ is a two-stage training framework: a cold-start SFT stage that transforms vanilla reasoning data into iterative (query, reasoning, summary) triples to teach the model the InftyThink format, followed by a trajectory-level RL stage using GRPO. The RL stage rolls out complete multi-iteration trajectories per query, assigns a single scalar reward (multiplicative task correctness × quadratic-decay efficiency penalty) shared across all iterations, and computes group-relative advantages at the trajectory level so that early summaries receive gradient signal for their contribution to final success. Loss masking excludes history/summary tokens from the policy gradient, and IcePop token-level gradient masking handles inference-training engine mismatches. The key design insight is separating format acquisition from strategy optimization, allowing the model to learn adaptive summarization timing, content selection, and continuation behavior through end-to-end trajectory feedback rather than imitation.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, InftyThink+ with task-only RL achieves 50.94% on AIME24 (vs. 38.75% for vanilla long-CoT RL, a +12.19 point gain) and 53.96% average accuracy across MATH500, AIME24, AIME25, and GPQA_Diamond (vs. 47.31% for vanilla RL). Adding the efficiency reward (T+E) reduces average latency from 100.21s to 48.37s (−52%) while retaining 50.58% average accuracy. On Qwen3-4B-Base, InftyThink+ with task-only RL reaches 58.71% average accuracy vs. 57.13% for vanilla RL, with a 31.68s latency reduction when the efficiency reward is included.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla long-CoT RL baseline (DeepSeek-R1-style single-pass reasoning), InftyThink+ with task-only RL wins decisively on accuracy: +12.19 points on AIME24 (50.94% vs. 38.75%), +4.79 on AIME25, and +7.69 on GPQA_Diamond, while also reducing average latency from 149.44s to 100.21s. The efficiency-reward variant (T+E) further cuts latency to 48.37s (−68% vs. vanilla RL) at a modest accuracy cost (−3.38 avg points). Against the Markovian Thinker (Aghajohari et al., 2025), which applies RL to fixed-size reasoning chunks, InftyThink+ provides a detailed comparison in Appendix Q; the key distinction is that InftyThink+ uses model-controlled adaptive iteration boundaries rather than rigid fixed-size segments, yielding better accuracy on harder benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25) where the natural reasoning structure does not align with fixed chunk boundaries. Ablations in Table 2 confirm that adaptive timing (learned via RL) outperforms both fixed (5k-token) and random interruption strategies by 2.5–3.0 points on AIME24, validating that the learned policy for when to summarize is a critical source of the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends InftyThink (Yan et al., 2025), which introduced model-controlled iterative reasoning with explicit summarization but relied solely on supervised fine-tuning to teach the format. The key departure is replacing SFT-only training with a two-stage pipeline (SFT cold-start followed by trajectory-level GRPO), enabling the model to learn strategic decisions—when to summarize, what to preserve, how to continue—rather than merely imitating a fixed format. The RL formulation adapts GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and token-level loss averaging (DAPO, Yu et al., 2025b) to the single-trajectory, multi-inference structure of iterative reasoning, with shared advantages across all iterations within a trajectory. It also contrasts with fixed-boundary approaches like the Markovian Thinker (Aghajohari et al., 2025) by allowing the model to autonomously determine iteration boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in reasoning models: the quadratic cost and hard context-window ceiling that make very long chains-of-thought prohibitively expensive and ultimately infeasible. The demonstrated 30–40% inference latency reduction alongside accuracy gains (21% on AIME24) makes iterative reasoning a viable efficiency strategy for production deployment, and the 18–40% speedup in RL training has direct implications for the cost of training future reasoning models. Key limitations include the assumption that reasoning decomposes into relatively independent stages (less clear for highly entangled tasks), the reliance on unstructured natural-language summaries that lack explicit information-priority control, and the dependence on task-specific cold-start data. Open questions include generalization beyond math/science to code and agentic tasks, and whether latent or hybrid summary representations could further tighten the compression–fidelity trade-off.
