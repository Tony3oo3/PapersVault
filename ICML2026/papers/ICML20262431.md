## Problem

Extending Transformer-based autoregressive models to 3D molecule generation faces two unresolved challenges: (1) tokenizing molecules into a canonical 1D sequence that is invariant to both SE(3) transformations and atom index permutations, and (2) designing an architecture that can jointly model hybrid tokens coupling discrete atom types with continuous 3D coordinates. Prior autoregressive approaches either rely on spatial discretization that discards fine-grained geometry or lack a principled mechanism for geometry-aware attention over atom-level tokens.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes no conditioning input and sequentially generates a variable-length sequence of atom-based tokens, each comprising a discrete atomic number and a continuous 3D coordinate vector (x, y, z). For class-conditional generation, a Molecule Class ID—derived from a binary string encoding the presence/absence of predefined SMARTS-matched functional groups—conditions the generation via classifier-free guidance. The output is a full 3D molecular conformation represented as a point cloud M = (t, C) with n atoms, where bond types are inferred from pairwise distances and atom identities during evaluation.

## Method

InertialAR introduces three coupled components. First, generation-oriented canonical tokenization aligns each molecule to a canonical inertial frame (center-of-mass shift → inertia tensor diagonalization → eigenvector axis assignment → fourth-node sign disambiguation) for SE(3) invariance, then reorders atoms via RDKit canonical SMILES serialization for permutation invariance, yielding a unique token sequence per molecule. Second, GeoPE injects geometric awareness into Transformer attention via two branches: ManhattanPE (a 3D rotational positional encoding making attention scores depend only on relative positions c_j − c_i) applied to type-embedding projections, and EuclideanPE (a Nyström low-rank approximation of the RBF kernel over pairwise distances) added as a separate feature branch, so the final attention score simultaneously encodes axis-wise relative geometry and pairwise Euclidean distance. Third, a hierarchical autoregressive decoder first predicts the next atom type via cross-entropy, then predicts its 3D coordinates via a Diffusion Loss denoising objective conditioned on both the autoregressive context and the sampled type, with block-diagonal projection matrices preserving the independence of the two prediction heads.

## Results

On QM9, InertialAR achieves 97.3% Validity, 92.5% Valid&Unique, 99.2% Atom Stability, and 94.5% Molecule Stability, setting new state-of-the-art on Valid, AtomSta, and MolSta. On GEOM-Drugs, it attains 98.0% Validity and 87.0% Atom Stability (best AtomSta among all methods). On the large-scale B3LYP-1M benchmark, InertialAR reaches 99.3% Validity and 24.7% Molecule Stability, dramatically outperforming EDM (92.9% Valid, 0.8% MolSta) and Geo2Seq (73.3% Valid, 0.0% MolSta). For class-conditional generation on QM9, InertialAR with classifier-free guidance achieves an average hit rate of 83.3%, far exceeding EDM (~26%) and Geo2Seq (~42%), while also leading on all validity and stability metrics across all five functional-group classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InertialAR outperforms all GNN-based diffusion baselines (EDM, EDM-Bridge, GDM, GeoLDM, UniGEM, MiDi) on QM9 Molecule Stability (94.5% vs. best 89.8% for UniGEM) and Validity (97.3% vs. best 95.0% for UniGEM). Against the closest Transformer-based AR competitor Geo2Seq, InertialAR wins on QM9 Validity (97.3 vs. 97.1), AtomSta (99.2 vs. 98.9), MolSta (94.5 vs. 93.2), and GEOM-Drugs AtomSta (87.0 vs. 82.5), though Geo2Seq edges ahead on GEOM-Drugs Validity (96.1 vs. 98.0 is actually InertialAR winning; GeoLDM leads at 99.3). On B3LYP-1M, InertialAR dominates both EDM and Geo2Seq across all four metrics, with the most dramatic gap in MolSta (24.7% vs. 0.8% and 0.0%). For class-conditional generation, InertialAR's average hit rate of 83.3% roughly doubles Geo2Seq's 42.2% and triples EDM's 25.7%, while also achieving near-perfect validity (99%+) and atom stability (99%+) across all five functional-group classes, where both baselines fall below 90% on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

InertialAR departs from the dominant SE(3)-equivariant architecture paradigm (EDM, E-NFs, G-SchNet) by instead canonicalizing molecules into a fixed inertial frame, allowing a standard Transformer backbone to operate on SE(3)-invariant inputs without equivariant constraints. The coordinate prediction block directly adopts the Diffusion Loss framework from Li et al. (2024a) for continuous-valued token generation in autoregressive settings, while the ManhattanPE component generalizes the 2D rotational positional encoding of Su (2021) to 3D atom-based tokens. The paper positions itself against both voxel-based and text-sequence tokenization approaches, arguing that atom-level tokenization with inertial-frame canonicalization preserves fine-grained geometry and atom-level granularity that spatial discretization discards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple canonicalization strategy (inertial frame + RDKit reordering) combined with a standard Transformer can match or exceed purpose-built equivariant diffusion models, challenging the assumption that explicit SE(3)-equivariant architectures are necessary for 3D molecular generation. The strong scalability on B3LYP (86M molecules, 50+ atom types) and the high hit rates (~83%) in class-conditional generation suggest the framework could serve as a practical foundation for targeted molecular design. Open questions include whether the inertial-frame approach generalizes to highly symmetric or near-degenerate molecules at scale, whether the Nyström-based EuclideanPE remains efficient for very large molecules (hundreds of atoms), and how the method extends to protein or periodic material domains where the atom-level tokenization assumption may break down.
