## Problem

LLM pre-training universally exhibits a two-phase loss trajectory—precipitous initial decay followed by prolonged asymptotic improvement—but the mechanistic factors dictating the onset of the slow-descent phase remain fundamentally unexplained. Existing analyses focus on restricted tasks (in-context learning, linear regression) or final-state properties (rank collapse), leaving the temporal spectral dynamics that govern the regime shift unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V) at each training step, computes their SVD, and tracks the trace-normalized singular distribution Σ̂_t = Σ_t / tr(Σ_t) via cosine similarity to the final trained state and via step-to-step Frobenius variation ΔΣ_t = ‖Σ̂_{t+1} − Σ̂_t‖_F. The primary outputs are (i) an empirical phase-transition timestamp where SD variation drops below a metastable floor (~10⁻⁴), and (ii) a theoretical stability bound ε ∝ η/‖W‖ that certifies when the singular distribution enters its constrained regime, linking spectral dynamics to a polynomial upper bound on per-step loss decrease.

## Method

The paper identifies the Stability of Singular Distribution (SoSD): the trace-normalized singular value spectrum stabilizes orders of magnitude earlier than the parameter matrices themselves, and this onset tightly synchronizes with the validation loss entering its slow-descent plateau across GPT-2 and LLaMA architectures. Theoretically, under monotonic norm growth (Assumption 4.1) and gradient boundedness (Assumption 4.3), Theorem 4.5 proves that the SD variation is bounded by ε ∝ η/‖W‖, so the inevitable growth of weight norms during training forces the system into SoSD at a finite threshold time T*. Theorem 4.9 then establishes the two-phase coupling: before T*, loss decrease is lower-bounded by O(1) (fast descent); after T*, loss decrease is upper-bounded by O(ε^p) for p > 2 (slow descent), making spectral stability a kinetic bottleneck on further optimization. The framework is then applied to interpret pre-training strategies: learning rate schedules tighten ε by reducing η, weight decay relaxes ε by suppressing ‖W‖ growth (permitting larger spectral updates), and the Muon optimizer achieves lower loss while maintaining SoSD at a lower variation scale (~10⁻⁴ vs. ~10⁻³ for AdamW).

## Results

The paper identifies the Stability of Singular Distribution (SoSD) phenomenon across GPT-2 (124M, 355M) and LLaMA (0.5B, 2B) models, showing that the trace-normalized singular value spectrum stabilizes significantly earlier than the parameter matrices themselves. Theoretically, the stability bound is characterized as ε ∝ η/‖W‖, with Phase I loss decrease lower-bounded by O(1) (specifically 3D²η/(2(1+√d))) and Phase II loss decrease upper-bounded by O(ε^p) for p > 2. Empirically, the SD Variation peaks at ~10⁻² during fast descent and settles to a metastable floor of ~10⁻⁴ upon entering SoSD. Muon achieves approximately one order of magnitude lower SoSD variation (~10⁻⁴) compared to Adam (~10⁻³) while simultaneously reaching lower validation loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a theoretical and empirical analysis rather than a method proposal, so it does not compare against external baseline methods. Instead, it provides internal comparisons to validate the SoSD framework. Muon outperforms Adam on GPT-2 Small under WSD scheduling, achieving consistently lower validation loss and approximately 10× lower SoSD variation (10⁻⁴ vs. 10⁻³), confirming SoSD is optimizer-invariant but scale-dependent. Weight Decay on LLaMA 0.5B maintains higher SoSD levels than the no-WD baseline yet achieves lower final loss beyond 60k steps, demonstrating that higher SoSD does not imply worse performance—rather, WD relaxes the ε ∝ η/‖W‖ constraint by suppressing norm growth. Across learning rate schedules (Step Decay, WSD, Cosine Decay), reducing η tightens the SoSD bound and triggers renewed loss descent, with continuous annealing (WSD, Cosine) providing smoother spectral relaxation than discrete step reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-flow analysis of Chen & Luo (2025), which identified a two-stage condensation-to-rank-collapse transition in linearized attention, by shifting the lens to the temporal dynamics of the singular value distribution in standard (non-linearized) attention. It departs from the broader implicit-regularization literature (rank collapse, max-margin solutions) by identifying an early-onset kinetic bottleneck—Stability of Singular Distribution (SoSD)—rather than a final structural state, and from ICL-specific dynamics analyses by targeting the macroscopic two-phase loss trajectory in general pre-training. The theoretical framework builds on standard tools (Mirsky's inequality, von Neumann trace inequality, softmax Jacobian bounds) but applies them to a novel spectral-stability question that prior work had not formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, spectral explanation for the ubiquitous 'fast-then-slow' two-phase loss trajectory that has been empirically observed but theoretically unexplained in LLM pre-training. The derived stability bound ε ∝ η/||W|| offers a concrete, interpretable quantity that unifies the role of learning rate schedules, weight decay, and optimizer choice as modulators of the SoSD constraint. Key limitations include the theory being restricted to a 1-layer single-head Transformer with full-batch GD and small initialization, and empirical validation limited to models up to 2B parameters; whether SoSD persists and scales identically at 70B+ parameter regimes remains an open question. The margin conditions (Assumption 4.7) required for the slow-descent bound are somewhat strong and their empirical verifiability in deep, multi-head architectures is unclear.
