## Problem

It remains unclear whether microscopy foundation models acquire high-level, biologically meaningful representations or merely exploit low-level shortcuts such as batch effects, architectural inductive biases, and pixel-level correlations. The paper demonstrates that on several widely used benchmarks, intentionally simple baselines (untrained networks, pixel statistics, structure-only tissue views) perform comparably to state-of-the-art pretrained models, revealing that common evaluation metrics often mask a lack of relevant high-level abstractions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264333/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The study consumes multi-channel Cell Painting fluorescence images (5–6 channels), H&E-stained tissue patches at 224×224 pixels (20× magnification), cell-centroid point sets derived from CellViT segmentation masks, and natural RGB images (ImageNet-1k) as inputs. All models produce frozen embeddings that are aggregated to gene-level or compound-level profiles for retrieval tasks, fed into a linear ridge regression head for 50-gene expression prediction (scored by Pearson correlation), or used for cancer subtyping classification. A key design choice is that untrained (randomly initialized) variants of every architecture are run through the identical embedding pipeline, and structure-only inputs discard all pixel appearance, retaining only spatial cell positions rendered as binary 5-nearest-neighbor graph images or processed as point clouds by GCN/EGNN encoders.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary baseline families to calibrate what information is sufficient for benchmark performance: (1) pixel-level statistics (channel-wise mean, standard deviation, skewness) that ignore spatial layout entirely; (2) untrained deep encoders (randomly initialized ResNets, ViTs, and a single-layer CNN) that isolate architectural inductive biases from learned biological content; and (3) disentangled tissue structure representations that retain cell-centroid spatial organization while removing all staining, morphology, and texture, formalized as binary cell-graph images or point clouds processed by GCN and EGNN. These baselines are evaluated alongside a broad set of pretrained foundation models (OpenPhenom, H-Optimus-1, DINOv2/3, GigaPath, Virchow, CONCH, UNI, etc.) on curated benchmarks spanning cell culture (RxRx3-core gene-gene retrieval, JUMP-CP compound retrieval, JUMP-MoA mechanism-of-action retrieval) and tissue (HEST-1k spatial transcriptomics gene expression prediction, BRACS/BACH cancer subtyping), with ImageNet-1k kNN probing as a natural-image reference. Diagnostic analyses include layer-wise performance curves across network depth, PCA effective-dimensionality collapse detection, and pairwise Spearman correlation of gene-gene similarity rankings to determine whether pretrained and untrained models exploit the same relational signal.

## Results

On RxRx3-core (gene-gene retrieval, Recall@5%), untrained ViTs and a minimal SingleConv baseline perform comparably to pretrained foundation models like OpenPhenom, while pixel-statistics features recover a substantial fraction of the signal; only the proprietary MAE-L/8 and MAE-G/8 embeddings achieve a clear 1.5× improvement in averaged top-5% recall over all other models. On HEST-1k-1NN (gene expression prediction, gene-wise PCC), the in-domain H-Optimus-1 reaches 0.68±0.03 on SKCM and 0.49±0.02 on PRAD, while structure-only cell-graph models (pretrained on natural images) are competitive with OOD vision encoders on PRAD (0.38 vs. 0.41) and COAD (0.31 vs. 0.30). On JUMP-CP (compound retrieval, mAP), pretrained and in-domain models consistently outperform untrained baselines, but several compounds show near-random retrieval across all models, and untrained baselines already capture easily detectable phenotypic changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central finding is that on RxRx3-core, untrained ViTs, SingleConv, and pixel-statistics baselines match or approach pretrained foundation models (OpenPhenom, DINOv2/v3), indicating the benchmark does not reliably distinguish learned biological representations from architectural inductive biases; gene-gene similarity rankings from pretrained and untrained ViTs are highly correlated (Spearman), confirming they exploit the same relational signal. On HEST-1k-1NN, in-domain histology models (H-Optimus-1, GigaPath, Virchow, UNI2, CONCH, Kaiko, Hibou, Phikon, CTransPath) consistently outperform OOD ImageNet-pretrained ResNets/ViTs, with H-Optimus-1 achieving the best PCC (0.68 on SKCM); however, structure-only GCN/EGNN models pretrained on natural images rival OOD image encoders on PRAD and COAD, and a simple cell-count baseline is already strong (0.37 on PRAD, 0.40 on SKCM). On JUMP-CP, in-domain OpenPhenom improves with depth while OOD models saturate, and the proprietary MAE-L/8 and MAE-G/8 embeddings achieve 1.5× higher RxRx3-core recall than any publicly available model, though their layer-wise behavior cannot be inspected. ResNet-based representations (both pretrained and untrained) consistently underperform ViTs on RxRx3-core, with PCA analysis showing representational collapse into low-dimensional subspaces that coincides with weak biological recall. Overall, the paper demonstrates that current microscopy benchmarks often fail to separate genuine high-level biological abstractions from low-level shortcuts, with the clearest evidence of learned representations emerging only on in-domain, modality-aligned tasks (JUMP-CP, HEST-1k-1NN subsets like CCRCC and SKCM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the 'simple baselines expose benchmark shortcuts' paradigm established in transcriptomics (Luecken et al., 2022; Bendidi et al., 2024; Ahlmann-Eltze et al., 2025) into microscopy imaging, where analogous concerns about batch effects and confounders have been recognized but not systematically demonstrated through controlled baseline comparisons. It repurposes the untrained-network literature (Ulyanov et al., 2018; Ramanujan et al., 2020; Zhong & Andreas, 2024) as a diagnostic tool for representation quality, and introduces structure-only cell-graph representations (building on Wang et al., 2023 and Ögut et al., 2026) as a new sub-modality baseline for tissue benchmarks. Rather than proposing a new architecture or training method, the paper carves new ground by systematically probing existing microscopy benchmarks (RxRx3-core, JUMP-CP, HEST-1k) with intentionally weak models to calibrate what scores actually certify.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that untrained ViTs and a single-convolution baseline match or approach pretrained foundation models on RxRx3-core challenges the field's working assumption that high benchmark scores reflect learned biological abstractions, and provides concrete diagnostics (pixel statistics, untrained encoders, cell count, structure-only graphs) that should become standard in microscopy evaluation. Open questions include whether the competitive performance of structure-only models on PRAD/COAD reflects genuine spatial-organization signal or is confounded by local cell count, and whether the positive results for MAE-L/8 and MAE-G/8 (which remain private) generalize. The paper's scope is limited to prediction alignment rather than mechanistic interpretability, and the RxRx3-core finding does not uniformly extend to all benchmarks (JUMP-CP and HEST-1k show clearer separation), so the practical takeaway is that benchmark scores require contextualization rather than blanket dismissal.
