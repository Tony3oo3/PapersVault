## Problem

Most video reasoning models generate only textual rationales without indicating when and where key visual evidence appears, making their reasoning unverifiable. Extending o3-style 'thinking with images' to video is non-trivial because it requires coherent joint localization across both time and space in dynamic scenes with motion, occlusions, and camera changes. This gap is compounded by the absence of training data providing synchronized spatio-temporal supervision and the difficulty of training a model to precisely localize objects in time and space simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 uniformly sampled video frames (each at ≤128×28×28 resolution) with annotated key frames inserted additionally, where each frame is prepended with its absolute timestamp, alongside a text question. It produces a single-pass structured reasoning trace that explicitly embeds spatio-temporal evidence using the format `<obj>name</obj><box>[x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]</box>at<t>timestamp</t>s`, followed by a final answer (MCQ letter or free-form text). No external tools, cropping operations, or multi-round inference are required; all grounding is emitted natively within one forward pass.

## Method

Open-o3-Video uses a two-stage training recipe on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: a cold-start SFT phase on STGR-CoT-30k to acquire structured grounded output formats, followed by reinforcement learning with GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) on STGR-RL-36k. The key technical contribution is a composite reward that couples temporal and spatial grounding through two complementary mechanisms: adaptive temporal proximity, which anneals the Gaussian width σ from 4 to 1 to provide dense early gradients and progressively enforce precise timestamp alignment, and temporal gating, which computes spatial IoU rewards only when predicted timestamps fall within a 3-second threshold of ground truth, preventing irrelevant objects from being rewarded. GSPO is chosen over GRPO because it defines importance ratios and clipping at the sequence level rather than token level, stabilizing optimization over long reasoning traces that include timestamps and bounding boxes. The training data (STGR) integrates temporal-only, spatial-only, and 5.9k newly annotated spatio-temporal samples produced via a Gemini 2.5 Pro annotation pipeline with bounding-box filtering and self-consistency checking.

## Results

On the V-STAR benchmark (designed specifically for spatio-temporal video reasoning), Open-o3-Video-7B achieves 61.0% QA accuracy, 24.5/24.0% temporal IoU (Chain1/Chain2), 25.4/6.0% spatial IoU (Chain1/Chain2), yielding 33.7% mAM and 46.6% mLGM — surpassing GPT-4o (26.8/38.2), Gemini-3-Pro (29.9/41.2), and improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by +14.4% mAM and +24.2% mLGM. Across general video understanding benchmarks, the model gains +1.2% on VideoMME (63.6), +1.4% on WorldSense (37.5), +1.1% on VideoMMMU (52.3), +10.1% on LongVideo-Reason-eval (69.4), and +4.5% mIoU on TVG-Bench (20.8) over Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with an average improvement of +3.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On V-STAR, Open-o3-Video decisively outperforms all proprietary models: GPT-4o (mAM 26.8, mLGM 38.2), Gemini-2-Flash (26.9/35.6), and Gemini-3-Pro (29.9/41.2), with the largest gap on spatial grounding (Where Chain1: 25.4 vs. 7.2 for Gemini-3-Pro). Against open-source video LLMs (Video-LLaMA3, LLaVA-Video, VideoChat2, Oryx, InternVL-2.5), the gains are substantial across all dimensions, e.g., +12.0 mAM over Video-LLaMA3 and +16.1 over InternVL-2.5-8B. On general QA benchmarks (VideoMME, WorldSense, VideoMMMU), Open-o3-Video still trails GPT-4o (71.9/42.6/61.2 vs. 63.6/37.5/52.3) but surpasses all open-source baselines including VideoRFT-7B and VideoR1-7B, with the most notable margin on long-video reasoning (LRR: 69.4 vs. 69.4 for VideoRFT, 68.9 for VideoR1) and temporal grounding (TVG-Bench mIoU: 20.8 vs. 14.3/9.6). The task-specialized baselines TRACE (temporal) and Sa2VA (spatial) each excel in one dimension but collapse in the other (e.g., Sa2VA scores 37.5 vIoU on Where Chain2 but 0.0 tIoU on When), whereas Open-o3-Video maintains balanced performance across both. On zero-shot Charades-STA, Open-o3-Video (R1@0.3 = 64.7) approaches the dedicated temporal grounding model TimeSuite (69.9) without any Charades-STA training data, and on HCSTVG-v2 it more than doubles the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline in m_vIoU (23.7 vs. 13.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'Thinking with Images' paradigm (OpenAI-o3, DeepEyes, TreeBench) from static images to video, and unifies previously separate lines of temporal grounding (Time-R1, TVG-R1) and spatial grounding (SpaceR) into a single reasoning trace. Unlike concurrent agent-based approaches (VITAL, Conan, LongVT) that rely on external tool calls for cropping or zooming, Open-o3-Video performs all spatio-temporal localization natively in a single forward pass. The training recipe departs from standard GRPO by adopting GSPO (sequence-level clipping) and introduces two novel reward mechanisms—adaptive temporal proximity and temporal gating—that address the chicken-and-egg problem where spatial IoU rewards are unreliable until temporal predictions are accurate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making video reasoning traces explicitly grounded in timestamps and bounding boxes, the paper provides a verifiable, auditable form of video understanding that was previously limited to opaque text rationales. The STGR datasets and the adaptive-reward training recipe are directly reusable for any video MLLM seeking grounded reasoning, and the confidence-aware test-time scaling offers a practical inference-time improvement without additional model calls. Key open questions remain: performance degrades on longer videos and small objects, audio/speech evidence is entirely absent, and whether the single-pass grounding paradigm can scale to multi-step compositional reasoning beyond the current one-shot localization remains untested.
