## Problem

Prior brain-computer interface research relies on specialized, single-task models (e.g., image reconstruction only, or text decoding only), which curtails versatility and overlooks potential synergistic effects among related neural tasks. No existing framework unifies multiple encoding and decoding directions across brain, image, and text modalities within a single architecture, making it impossible to study how shared neural representations enable positive transfer between tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263004/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes fMRI signals from the visual cortex (~13,000 voxels registered to MNI152 standard space), RGB stimulus images, and text (captions or questions) as inputs, all discretized into token sequences via modality-specific tokenizers (VQ-VAE for images, CLIP tokenizer for text, and a custom Brain Tokenizer for fMRI). Outputs include predicted fMRI voxel patterns, reconstructed images, generated text at multiple granularities (concise ~10-word, detailed ~30-word, or reasoning answers up to 77 tokens), and joint image+text reconstructions. The seven tasks are distinguished solely by which modalities serve as conditioning inputs versus masked targets to be generated.

## Method

The framework has two core components: (1) a Brain Tokenizer that discretizes continuous fMRI into 64 tokens of dimension 16 via a VQ-VAE backbone, trained with a composite loss combining reconstruction, coarse-grained CLIP contrastive alignment, fine-grained cross-attention token prediction, and perceptual alignment through a frozen fMRI-to-CLIP predictor; and (2) a tri-modal MM-DiT backbone (initialized from Muddit) extended with a dedicated brain branch, where all seven tasks are unified under a single continuous-time masked token prediction objective with a cosine schedule. The key architectural choice is discrete diffusion over autoregressive modeling, whose permutation-invariant masking avoids the sequential bias that would confound cross-task synergy analysis. Training follows a progressive curriculum: Stage 1 freezes the Muddit backbone and trains only new brain parameters on joint encoding/decoding, then expands to all six single/bi-modal tasks; Stage 2 fine-tunes the full backbone with DoRA adapters on a curated Brain Question Answering dataset to unlock reasoning capabilities.

## Results

On the joint image-text decoding task (B→I&T), Mind-Omni (442M params, 1 model, 7 tasks) achieves 72.5% AlexNet(2), 84.9% AlexNet(5), 78.8% Inception, and 79.8% CLIP similarity, establishing a new SOTA among unified multi-task frameworks. For language decoding, the B→I&T setting yields BLEU-1 of 29.12, ROUGE of 30.54, CIDEr of 12.26, and BERT score of 87.73, while the BQA reasoning task reaches CIDEr of 223.98 and SPICE of 43.28. On neural encoding (I&T→B), the model achieves gPCC of 0.160, gRSA of 0.408, and semantic-level PCC of 0.754, outperforming all multi-task baselines across both voxel and semantic spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unified multi-task baselines MoPoE (564M) and BraVL (564M), Mind-Omni achieves substantial gains on all reconstruction metrics—for example, B→I&T AlexNet(5) improves from 56.2%/59.1% to 84.9%, and Inception from 59.3%/61.7% to 78.8%. Compared to OneLLM (7B, 4 models), Mind-Omni uses only 442M parameters in a single model yet surpasses it on joint decoding (B→I&T: 84.9% vs 76.1% AlexNet(5), 79.8% vs 77.2% CLIP) and on language decoding (B→I&T: BLEU-1 29.12 vs 18.41, CIDEr 12.26 vs 9.41, BERT 87.73 vs 86.18). On the reasoning (BQA) task, Mind-Omni outperforms UMBRAE (146M + Vicuna-13B) on CIDEr (223.98 vs 156.81), SPICE (43.28 vs 33.70), and CLIP (70.65 vs 69.57) without relying on any external LLM. For neural encoding, Mind-Omni (I&T→B) beats both MoPoE and BraVL on all six metrics (e.g., gPCC 0.160 vs 0.085/0.083; semantic RSA 0.654 vs 0.382/0.417). The model trails the specialized single-task SOTA MindEye2 on pure B→I reconstruction and MindSimulator on voxel-level encoding PCC, but remains competitive at the semantic level and uniquely covers all seven tasks in one architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'feature mapper + frozen generative model' paradigm in neural encoding/decoding (MindEye, MindSimulator, UMBRAE, etc.) by replacing it with a single unified discrete diffusion backbone that directly fuses brain, image, and text tokens. It extends Muddit's bi-modal discrete diffusion framework to tri-modal by adding a brain branch to the MM-DiT, and introduces a novel Brain Tokenizer (VQ-VAE with coarse-grained CLIP contrastive, fine-grained cross-attention, and perceptual alignment losses) to bridge the modality gap between continuous fMRI signals and discrete visual/textual tokens. The choice of discrete diffusion over autoregressive unified models (Emu, Seed-X) is motivated by the need for permutation invariance to study cross-task synergies without sequential-ordering bias, and MNI152 standard-space registration addresses cross-subject anatomical variability without subject-specific modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first framework to unify seven neural encoding/decoding tasks in a single model, Mind-Omni provides a computational testbed for studying inter-task and inter-modality synergies in neural representation, with the notable finding that joint image-text decoding outperforms single-task counterparts on both outputs. The 442M-parameter model's competitiveness with 7B specialized models (OneLLM) on reasoning tasks suggests that unified multi-task learning can be more parameter-efficient than task-specific scaling in the data-constrained fMRI regime. Key limitations include restriction to fMRI (visual cortex only), the NSD dataset (8 subjects), and the relatively small model scale; open questions include whether the observed synergies generalize to other neural modalities (EEG, MEG), larger subject pools, and whether the discrete diffusion architecture scales favorably compared to autoregressive alternatives as data grows.
