## Problem

Value factorization methods in cooperative MARL assume static inter-agent coordination structures, which limits generalization when task semantics shift across maps, unit compositions, or reward regimes. Prior trajectory-abstraction approaches (LAGMA, TRAMA) learn discrete task classes via VQ-VAE but remain agnostic to how agents should dynamically restructure their coordination within each abstracted regime, leaving a gap between semantic task recognition and structural coordination adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263630/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation vector (e.g., position, velocity, and binary flags in SUMO; unit states in SMAC) plus a coordination-aware signal derived from Max-Sum message passing on the sparse graph. The VQ-VAE encoder consumes trajectory segments (sequences of observations) to produce discrete codes from a 256-entry codebook, which are then clustered via K-means into n_cl trajectory classes. Outputs include discrete agent actions, a top-r sparse adjacency matrix updated every K_g=5 environment steps, and a frozen trajectory-class label that conditions the policy; the goal predictor is explicitly excluded from RL gradient flow to prevent task-prediction interference with control learning.

## Method

TACTIC couples three modules under a CTDE framework: (i) a class-conditional VQ-VAE with coverage and entropy regularization that quantizes trajectory histories into discrete task-semantic codes, clustered by K-means with centroid-reuse initialization for label stability; (ii) a dynamic sparse coordination graph where edge importance ζ_ij^(k) is estimated as the action-marginal variance of pairwise payoff functions within each trajectory class k, with top-r pruning and task-conditioned low-rank payoff factorization (rank-R decomposition conditioned on the task embedding) reducing parameterization from O(|A|²) to O(R|A|); and (iii) a pretrained, frozen goal predictor that supplies discrete trajectory-class labels to condition local QMIX-based policies without backpropagating task-prediction gradients. Max-Sum message passing (T=5 rounds) on the sparse graph produces coordination-aware signals that augment agent observations, while a temporal smoothing loss regularizes adjacency changes across timesteps. Training separates representation learning (VQ-VAE + classifier losses) from RL control (TD loss + smoothing), with the graph module updated periodically from replay-buffer mini-batches assigned to each cluster.

## Results

On SMACv1, TACTIC achieves the highest mean episode return across all four evaluated maps: 14.50±0.75 (5m_vs_6m), 18.16±0.10 (8m_vs_9m), 19.22±2.37 (MMM2), and 14.67±1.21 (27m_vs_30m), with corresponding win rates of 0.46, 0.78, 0.89, and 0.46. On SMACv2, TACTIC attains the highest average win rate and the most stable learning curve among all baselines, particularly in sparse-reward scenarios like 10zerg and 10terran. On the SUMO non-signalized intersection task, TACTIC achieves the highest mean return, highest completion rate, and lowest return variance, while also reducing fuel consumption (19,008 mg vs. 21,813 mg) and CO₂ emissions (59,596 mg vs. 60,984 mg) relative to the Random baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACTIC outperforms its closest competitor TRAMA on all four SMACv1 maps, with the largest margin on 5m_vs_6m (14.50 vs. 13.70 reward; 0.46 vs. 0.37 win rate) and a consistent 0.01–0.04 win-rate advantage on the harder maps. Against the strongest graph-based baseline DMCG, TACTIC leads on every map (e.g., 19.22 vs. 17.30 reward on MMM2; 14.67 vs. 14.20 on 27m_vs_30m), and on SMACv2 it achieves the highest and most stable win-rate curve, while DMCG and TRAMA show moderate but less consistent performance. Value-decomposition baselines (QMIX, QTRAN, QPLEX) and memory-based methods (EMC, LAGMA) are substantially outperformed, with QPLEX trailing by 2.7–8.7 reward points across maps. On the SurComb multi-task benchmarks, TACTIC dominates standard SurComb3/4 over TRAMA, but on the mirrored reSurComb variants a simple Static-G ablation slightly surpasses full TACTIC (2.72 vs. 2.50 on reSurComb3), revealing a semantic-alignment boundary where the dynamic graph's conditioning signal degrades. On SUMO, TACTIC achieves the best mean return, completion rate, and lowest return variance among all compared methods (TRAMA, DMCG, QMIX, EMC, IPPO, Random), confirming its advantage in deployment-oriented traffic coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACTIC sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of MARL research: trajectory-level abstraction via VQ-VAE (TRAMA, LAGMA) and dynamic coordination-graph construction (DMCG, DICG, LTSCG). Prior trajectory-abstraction methods condition policies on discrete task classes but leave inter-agent coordination structure fixed, while graph-based methods adapt topology to context without explicitly grounding it in trajectory-level task semantics. TACTIC bridges this gap by mapping VQ-VAE trajectory codes into variance-based edge pruning and task-conditioned low-rank payoff factorization, all within a QMIX value-factorization backbone. The frozen goal predictor design departs from TRAMA by removing task-prediction gradients from the RL loop to prevent interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism for coupling latent task semantics with structural coordination adaptation, addressing a recognized gap in multi-task MARL where agents must generalize across shifting unit compositions and sparse rewards. A notable boundary finding is that on semantically mirrored tasks (reSurComb), a simple static graph can outperform the full dynamic TACTIC, revealing that the method's effectiveness depends on stable alignment between trajectory embeddings and coordination structure. Open questions include whether partial supervision or adaptive clustering can extend the framework to highly overlapping task distributions, and whether the moderate training overhead (4.2h vs 3.4h for TRAMA) is justified in deployment settings where per-episode static graphs suffice.
