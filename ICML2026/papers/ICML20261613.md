## Problem

AlphaZero's MCTS-UCB is inherently sequential: each simulation must wait for a neural network inference before deciding the next action to explore, creating a severe latency bottleneck especially in the single-root (intra-tree) setting. Existing parallelization strategies like virtual loss are ad-hoc and suffer from strength saturation. The paper asks whether a principled, from-first-principles search algorithm can be designed that is naturally parallelizable across a single tree.

## Input / Output

Given a game state, a simulation budget N, an exploration constant C, and a neural network providing prior policies π₀ and values v₀, RMCTS returns a posterior policy (action probability distribution) and an estimated value at the root. The simulation budget is distributed across child actions via a randomized proportional allocation (ASSIGN-SIMS) that guarantees each action receives either ⌊π₀(a)·N⌋ or ⌈π₀(a)·N⌉ simulations. The efficient implementation processes all nodes at the same tree depth as a single GPU batch, reducing the number of sequential inference calls from O(N) to O(log N).

## Method

RMCTS replaces the adaptive UCB-based tree expansion of MCTS-UCB with a breadth-first, prior-guided tree generation: at each node, the simulation budget is split among child actions proportionally to the prior policy, and all nodes at the same depth are evaluated in one batched GPU inference. The key recursive step is that Q-values are computed bottom-up from leaves to root, where each node's value is the expected reward under the optimized posterior policy (from Grill et al., 2020) — the unique policy maximizing expected Q minus a KL-divergence penalty from the prior, solvable in closed form via Newton's method. This local, non-adaptive design means the tree structure is fixed before any value propagation occurs, enabling full intra-tree batching. The paper proves that with a full-support prior, RMCTS converges to minimax play as N→∞, and empirically shows 40×+ speedups over MCTS-UCB in the single-root setting while matching or exceeding its playing quality when given 2× the simulation budget.

## Results

For a single root state, RMCTS achieves 10×–48× speedup over MCTS-UCB (e.g., 8.9 ms vs. 390 ms at N=1024 in Othello), and 2.2×–13.7× speedup in the batch-of-64 training setting. In head-to-head Othello games, RMCTS with N=256 simulations loses to MCTS-UCB (N=256) with a mean score of −4.0, but doubling RMCTS to N=512 flips the result to a mean score of +7.2 while still running 13× faster (178 ms vs. 2.3 s per game). In round-robin training across Othello, Dots-and-Boxes, and Connect-4, RMCTS-trained networks reach the same playing strength as MCTS-UCB-trained networks in roughly one-third the wall-clock training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is AlphaZero's MCTS-UCB (Silver et al., 2018), using the same neural network for priors in all comparisons. At equal simulation budgets, RMCTS is at a small quality disadvantage (mean score −4.0 in Othello at N=256), attributed to its non-adaptive, prior-guided tree expansion being shallower than MCTS-UCB's adaptive deep tactical lines. However, doubling RMCTS's simulation budget (N=512 vs. 256) more than compensates: RMCTS wins with a mean score of +7.2 while still completing each game in 178 ms versus MCTS-UCB's 2.3 s—a 13× speedup. In the training regime (64 simultaneous root states), RMCTS with N=128 matches MCTS-UCB with N=64 in playing strength while processing roughly 3× more training data per unit wall-clock time, as confirmed by round-robin tournaments in Othello, Dots-and-Boxes, and Connect-4 where RMCTS networks trained for 1 hour outperform MCTS-UCB networks trained for 3 hours. The speedup stems from RMCTS's breadth-first, intra-tree batching of GPU inferences (all nodes at a given depth form one batch), eliminating the sequential selection-inference-backpropagation bottleneck inherent to MCTS-UCB.

## Relation to Prior Work

RMCTS builds directly on Grill et al. (2020), who showed that MCTS-UCB's posterior policy can be reinterpreted as a regularized policy optimization problem, and extends their optimized posterior policy from the root node to every node in the search tree. It departs from AlphaZero's MCTS-UCB (Silver et al., 2018) by replacing the adaptive, sequential UCB-guided tree expansion with a non-adaptive, prior-guided breadth-first expansion that enables intra-tree GPU batching. The paper positions itself against both virtual-loss parallelization (Chaslot et al., 2008) and inter-tree batching libraries like mctx, arguing that RMCTS addresses the intra-tree latency bottleneck that those approaches cannot. It also contrasts with Gumbel MuZero (Danihelka et al., 2022), framing the two as targeting complementary efficiency axes: hardware efficiency (RMCTS) versus sample efficiency (Gumbel MuZero).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40×+ intra-tree speedup and ~3× training speedup make MCTS-based game AI substantially more accessible, particularly for real-time single-game applications where GPU latency dominates. However, the evidence is limited to three small games (Connect-4, Dots-and-Boxes, Othello) with modest branching factors; whether the non-adaptive breadth-first strategy maintains its quality parity at the scale of Go, Chess, or Shogi remains an open and critical question. The paper also acknowledges that RMCTS is slightly weaker than MCTS-UCB at equal simulation budgets due to its shallower, less adaptive tree, and that an adaptive multi-re-exploration variant is needed to close this gap. A direct head-to-head comparison with Gumbel MuZero's sequential halving, and a hybrid combining both approaches, are identified as important next steps.
