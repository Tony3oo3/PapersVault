## Problem

Collecting high-quality GUI interaction trajectories from real websites is expensive and fundamentally unverifiable: the agent only observes rendered UI feedback (screenshots) while the true internal state remains hidden, forcing reliance on external verifiers (human annotators or LLM judges) that are both inconsistent and costly. This 'verifier bottleneck' limits the scalability of training data for Web GUI agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265044/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a web theme name (e.g., 'Facebook', 'Gmail') and a reference website name as a style anchor, and produces 29 runnable Vue-based synthetic websites, 11,663 verified GUI trajectories (averaging 21.9 steps each) with step-level thinking traces, and FSM specifications. Each trajectory pairs grounded atomic GUI operations (click, type, hover with CSS-selector-based coordinates) with aligned internal state sequences, and the final training set of ~16K steps combines navigation trajectories with grounding examples in a 2:8 ratio for GRPO.

## Method

AutoWebWorld models each web environment as a Finite State Machine where the internal state is a (page_id, signature) pair and transitions are deterministic functions with explicit preconditions and effect rules, making action validity and goal attainment programmatically checkable. A four-stage pipeline generates the environment: (1) a GPT-5.1 multi-agent loop (proposer–validator–improver) produces the FSM specification; (2) Gemini3-Pro translates the FSM into a runnable Vue front-end with a shared CSS-selector namespace; (3) BFS over the FSM state graph enumerates shortest goal-reaching action sequences; (4) Playwright replays each candidate trajectory on the generated site, discarding any that fail execution. Training uses GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL with a composite reward combining action-type accuracy, coordinate grounding (bounding-box containment), and format compliance, requiring only 16K steps to achieve state-of-the-art 7B performance on WebVoyager.

## Results

On WebVoyager (15-step limit), Ours-7B achieves 27.48% overall success rate, the best among all open-source models and surpassing the strongest frontier model Claude-4.0-Sonnet (26.11%) and GPT-5.1 (18.96%). The 3B variant reaches 15.09%, outperforming several 7B+ baselines including UI-Venus-7B (9.73%) and TongUI-7B (9.83%) with only 16K GRPO training steps. On grounding benchmarks, Ours-7B improves ScreenSpot-V2 from 84.83% to 86.16% and ScreenSpot-Pro from 23.2% to 27.5% over the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone. A clear scaling law is observed: WebVoyager success rises monotonically from 3.92% to 27.42% as training data grows from 8 to 16,253 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours-7B (27.48%) outperforms all open-source baselines on WebVoyager, with the closest competitor being UI-TARS-1.5-7B (26.51%, a +0.97% margin) and OpenCUA-7B (16.74%, a +10.74% margin). Against frontier closed-source models, Ours-7B surpasses Claude-4.0-Sonnet (26.11%) by 1.37% and GPT-5.1 (18.96%) by 8.52%, while Gemini3-Pro (5.46%) is far behind. The 3B model (15.09%) notably exceeds UI-Venus-7B (9.73%), TongUI-7B (9.83%), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (11.73%) despite using only 16K GRPO steps versus their 1M SFT or 350K RL samples. In the matched-budget comparison (Table 4), AutoWebWorld's intrinsically verified trajectories (25.99%) beat Explorer's externally verified real-world trajectories (17.56%) by 8.43% at the same ~10K training steps, while costing $0.04 vs. $0.12 per trajectory. On the AutoWebWorld benchmark itself (Table 7), both UI-TARS-1.5-7B (20.00%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (16.00%) score lower than on WebVoyager, confirming the synthesized environments are not trivially easier than real-world sites.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of synthetic web environment work (WebArena, BrowserGym, WebShop) by repurposing them from evaluation benchmarks into scalable training-data generation pipelines, and departs from real-world trajectory collection methods (Explorer, AgentTrek, Fara, Mind2Web) by replacing external verifiers with intrinsic FSM-based verification. The multi-agent FSM generation loop builds on the Aorchestra orchestration framework (Ruan et al., 2026), while the BFS-over-state-graph trajectory enumeration is a direct application of classical search to a newly made-explicit transition structure. The key conceptual departure from prior synthetic environments is the explicit modeling of all states, preconditions, and deterministic effects, which makes both action validity and goal attainment programmatically checkable without any external judge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The verifier bottleneck—where correctness of GUI trajectories must be assessed by humans or LLM judges—is a major practical barrier to scaling GUI agent training, and AutoWebWorld's intrinsic verification at $0.04/trajectory (vs. $0.15–$1.00 for real-world pipelines) directly addresses this. The demonstrated scaling law (monotonic improvement from 8 to 16K samples on WebVoyager and Online-Mind2Web) suggests synthetic data can be a reliable growth axis for foundational GUI models, not just a stopgap. However, the paper's own limitations are significant: environments are bounded by LLM generator priors (missing edge cases and uncommon interaction patterns), a semantic-GUI alignment gap causes some FSM-valid paths to be discarded, and task diversity is capped by FSM topology—so the approach may struggle with truly open-ended or highly dynamic web interactions that resist finite-state abstraction.
