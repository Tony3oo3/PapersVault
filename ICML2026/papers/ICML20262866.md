## Problem

Explicit Chain-of-Thought VLA methods that generate linguistic reasoning traces or future image predictions before acting suffer from two fundamental limitations: autoregressive generation introduces non-negligible inference latency that undermines real-time closed-loop control and temporal consistency, and confining reasoning to linguistic space imposes a representational bottleneck that cannot faithfully capture fine-grained physical and robotic dynamics. This paper asks whether a VLA model can reason about future physical states efficiently and in a modality that is not limited to language.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 384×384 RGB observations (one or more camera views), a natural language instruction, and synchronized robot proprioceptive states; during training it additionally ingests 1024-point point clouds encoded by Uni3D (training-only, not used at inference). The output is a continuous SE(3) end-effector action sequence generated via flow matching: 7-DoF (3 position + 3 Euler rotation + 1 gripper) for single-arm, 14-DoF for dual-arm, 20-DoF for mobile manipulation, and 26-DoF for dexterous hand configurations. The latent CoT itself is a compact sequence of 3H tokens (H=4 by default, yielding 12 tokens) that autoregressively encodes future visual, geometric, and proprioceptive states without ever decoding to pixels or text.

## Method

LaST₀ repurposes the Janus-Pro (DeepSeek-LLM 1.5B) decoder-only backbone into a Mixture-of-Transformers dual-system architecture where two experts share a unified self-attention context but have task-specific FFN, attention projections, and LayerNorm parameters. The slow reasoning expert operates at a low frequency (every κ steps, e.g., κ=4) and autoregressively predicts a compact latent CoT sequence—interleaving per-step visual latents (SigLIP), geometric latents (Uni3D), and proprioceptive latents (action tokenizer)—trained with a cosine-similarity regression loss rather than discrete token likelihoods. The fast acting expert runs at the native control frequency and generates actions via flow matching, conditioning on high-frequency observations and the most recently updated latent CoT through the shared attention mechanism. During SFT, the action expert is trained with randomly mixed fast-slow operating ratios (1:1, 1:2, 1:4), enabling adaptive frequency selection at deployment; this mixed-ratio training improves robustness without degrading performance. The key architectural insight is that compressing multimodal future states into a handful of continuous latent tokens per timestep avoids both the pixel-level decoding cost of explicit image prediction and the linguistic bottleneck of text CoT, while the asynchronous dual-system design decouples deliberative reasoning from responsive control within a single model.

## Results

On the RLBench benchmark (10 tasks), LaST₀ achieves a mean success rate of 82% (±0.03), surpassing the strongest baseline HybridVLA (74%) by 8% and π₀.₅ (65%) by 17%, while operating at 15.4 Hz inference speed—14× faster than explicit CoT-VLA (1.1 Hz). On LIBERO, LaST₀ reaches a SOTA mean success rate of 98.1%, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and π₀.₅ (96.9%), with particularly strong results on the long-horizon LIBERO-Long suite (95.6% vs. 94.5% and 92.4%). On 10 real-world tasks spanning single-arm, dual-arm, mobile, and dexterous manipulation, LaST₀ achieves a 72% mean success rate on the Franka platform, substantially exceeding π₀.₅ (59%), CoT-VLA (50%), and SpatialVLA (41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaST₀ outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluation setting. On RLBench, it leads HybridVLA (74%) by 8%, π₀.₅ (65%) by 17%, and CoT-VLA (66%) by 16%, while also being the fastest method at 15.4 Hz—14× faster than CoT-VLA (1.1 Hz) and slightly ahead of π₀.₅ (13.8 Hz). On LIBERO, LaST₀ (98.1%) edges out OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and π₀.₅ (96.9%), with the largest margin over CoT-VLA (81.1%), highlighting the advantage of compact latent reasoning over explicit visual CoT. On real-world Franka tasks, LaST₀ (72%) surpasses π₀.₅ (59%) by 13%, CoT-VLA (50%) by 22%, and SpatialVLA (41%) by 31%; on the long-horizon 'place egg' sub-task, the gap widens from 0.66→0.47→0.33 (LaST₀) versus 0.47→0.20→0.07 (π₀.₅) across three consecutive executions. The paper notes that CoT-VLA was reimplemented on the same Janus-Pro backbone to ensure a fair comparison, and that LaST₀'s speed advantage stems from its asynchronous fast-slow MoT design with KV-cache-based latent retrieval rather than autoregressive text/image decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent CoT paradigm from general VLMs (Monet, LCDrive, ThinkAct) to robotic manipulation, replacing explicit linguistic or visual chain-of-thought traces (CoT-VLA, CogACT, HybridVLA) with a compact continuous latent space that jointly encodes future 2D visual dynamics, 3D point-cloud geometry (via Uni3D), and proprioceptive states. Architecturally, it departs from single-backbone VLA models (π0, π0.5, OpenVLA) by introducing a Mixture-of-Transformers dual-system design that decouples low-frequency latent reasoning from high-frequency flow-matching action generation within a shared attention context. The asynchronous frequency coordination and KV-cache-based inference mechanism are novel engineering contributions that enable real-time deployment, building on the fast-slow system concept but implementing it through parameter-separated experts rather than separate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14× inference speedup over explicit CoT VLA methods while simultaneously improving success rates (13–14% over SOTA on real-world tasks) addresses a critical practical bottleneck for closed-loop robotic manipulation, making 'reason-before-act' feasible at control frequencies. The finding that a single latent token per modality suffices for effective reasoning suggests that physical dynamics can be compressed far more aggressively than pixel-level or token-level representations, potentially enabling even more efficient future systems. Key limitations include reliance on VGGT-generated synthetic point clouds during pretraining (due to data scarcity), limited coverage of complex mobile and dexterous manipulation in training data, and the absence of reinforcement learning post-training, which the authors identify as crucial for scaling to longer-horizon tasks with physical constraint enforcement.
