## Problem

Deep time series forecasting models suffer from three compounding failures: periodic patterns attenuate in deep layers (signal dilution), attention mechanisms entangle long-term trends with high-frequency noise, and the prevalent channel-independent paradigm severs intrinsic cross-variable dependencies. No existing framework jointly addresses all three issues with explicit physical inductive bias, leaving models brittle under distribution shift and unable to capture dynamic system coordination in multivariate settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate historical observations X ∈ R^{B×L×C} with a fixed lookback window L=720 (or dataset-specific lengths for high-dimensional benchmarks like Meter at L=1008 and Mobility at L=35), and produces future predictions Ŷ ∈ R^{B×O×C} across horizons O ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Inputs are instance-normalized, patchified (P=16, stride S=8), and modulated by periodic gating before entering the encoder; outputs are generated by a channel-shared linear projection over flattened temporal-embedding features, then inverse-normalized using preserved per-channel statistics. The RLC module additionally extracts channel-wise mean/std descriptors from the latent space as an auxiliary statistical readout aligned to ground-truth future statistics.

## Method

PESD-TSF explicitly decomposes temporal dynamics into three physical dimensions—trend, perturbation, and cross-variable collaboration—through a constrained three-stage pipeline. First, a Multiplicative Periodic Gating mechanism converts discrete calendar features into continuous embeddings and applies sigmoid-based amplitude modulation to preserve periodic structure across depth. Second, a cascaded encoder performs (i) IntAttention with smoothing-convolution detrending (detrended Q/K, full V) for denoised trend extraction, (ii) PatchSampling via dual-branch 1D-convolution and max-pooling to compress temporal resolution by half, and (iii) Cross-Scale Collaborative Attention (CSCA) that applies self-attention over the variable dimension on temporally pooled representations to reconstruct global inter-variable topology without expensive dynamic graphs. Third, a Regularized Latent Component (RLC) module projects channel-wise statistical descriptors into a compact latent space with a column-orthogonality constraint (‖W_rlc^T W_rlc − I‖²_F) and a Pearson-correlation alignment term to future statistics, enforcing disentangled, physically consistent representations. The three-stage order is critical: reordering or bypassing stages degrades performance, confirming that temporal stabilization must precede multi-scale compression, which must precede cross-variable reconstruction.

## Results

On the classic L=720 benchmarks (Table 1), PESD-TSF ranks 1st in 35 out of 38 MSE/MAE metrics across seven datasets (ETTm1/2, ETTh1/2, Weather, Traffic, Electricity). Averaged over O∈{96,192,336,720}, it achieves 0.343/0.370 (MSE/MAE) on ETTm1, 0.244/0.304 on ETTm2, 0.354/0.242 on Traffic, and 0.150/0.244 on Electricity. Compared to DLinear, PESD-TSF reduces MSE and MAE by 14.96% and 14.87% respectively; against lightweight SOTA models (FITS, SparseTSF) it achieves 10.23%–11.59% error reduction. On short-term PeMS forecasting (L=96, O=12), PESD-TSF attains state-of-the-art MAE, MAPE, and RMSE on PeMS03, 07, and 08, and leads in MAPE on PeMS04 (11.62 vs. 12.53 for TimeMixer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PESD-TSF outperforms all 17 baselines on the majority of metrics (35/38 on the classic benchmarks). The closest competitor is TimeBridge (ICML 2025), which narrowly edges PESD-TSF on ETTh1 MAE (0.415 vs. 0.421) and Electricity MSE (0.149 vs. 0.150), while iTransformer (ICLR 2024) wins on ETTh2 MSE (0.328 vs. 0.339). Against the lightweight SOTA models SparseTSF (TPAMI 2025) and FITS (2023), PESD-TSF achieves 10.23%–11.59% error reduction across datasets. Compared to DLinear (AAAI 2023), the gains are most pronounced: 14.96% MSE and 14.87% MAE reduction on average. On short-term PeMS forecasting, PESD-TSF dominates TimeMixer (ICLR 2024) on PeMS03 (MAE 14.48 vs. 14.63), PeMS07 (MAE 19.60 vs. 20.57), and PeMS08 (MAE 14.25 vs. 15.22), but on PeMS04 it trails TimeMixer slightly on MAE (19.41 vs. 19.21) and RMSE (31.73 vs. 30.92) while winning decisively on MAPE (11.62 vs. 12.53). PatchTST (ICLR 2023) and TimeMixer are consistently outperformed by 3–8% in MSE across all long-term datasets, confirming that explicit structured decomposition yields gains beyond what channel-independent attention or multi-scale mixing alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

PESD-TSF synthesizes three established research threads—multi-scale decomposition (Autoformer, TimeMixer), temporal prior encoding (N-BEATS, TFT), and cross-variable attention (iTransformer, Crossformer)—into a unified structured pipeline, but departs from the dominant channel-independent paradigm by explicitly modeling inter-variable collaboration through CSCA. Unlike prior decomposition methods that treat trend and seasonality in isolation, it introduces a Regularized Latent Component with orthogonality constraints to jointly disentangle trend, perturbation, and cross-variable dimensions. The multiplicative periodic gating mechanism replaces standard additive positional encoding with a continuous-time amplitude modulation, a design choice not previously explored in the LTSF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized limitation of the channel-independent paradigm (PatchTST, DLinear) that severs intrinsic cross-variable dependencies, and demonstrates that structured cross-variable modeling can be done efficiently (CSCA operates on time-aggregated representations rather than full sequences). However, the physical interpretability claims are somewhat overstated: the orthogonality constraint applies to a small auxiliary projection head rather than the main forecasting path, and the topology matching rate (20.92%) indicates only partial recovery of physical structure. Open questions include whether the structured decomposition generalizes to highly non-stationary or regime-shifting data, and whether the three-stage ordering (denoise → compress → collaborate) remains optimal under different data characteristics.
