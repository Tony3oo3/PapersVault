## Problem

State-of-the-art segmentation models (SAM, SAM2, and efficient variants) encode the entire image before focusing on a prompted object, wasting computation on irrelevant regions and failing to segment very small objects in high-resolution scenes. No existing prompted segmentation method achieves scale invariance across objects spanning from 0.0001% to 25% of the image area while remaining parameter-efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a 2D Gaussian prompt (center μ and full covariance Σ) that encodes the target object's location, scale, and orientation; a bounding-box prompt can alternatively be converted into an axis-aligned Gaussian. The Gaussian is consumed in two ways: it drives off-grid, multi-resolution patch sampling from the input image (patches centered at continuous sub-pixel coordinates via bilinear interpolation) and it restricts mask prediction to a 5-sigma bounding box around the object center. The output is a binary segmentation mask for the single prompted object, predicted sparsely during training (2048 pixels across seven L1-distance groups from the mask boundary) and densely within the 5-sigma region at inference.

## Method

FLIP (Fovea-Like Input Patching) replaces the full-image encoder with a biologically-inspired, object-centric patching mechanism: K patch sizes (1×1 through 16×16) are sampled from the 2D Gaussian prompt with a total budget N, allocating high-resolution patches to the object center and coarser patches to peripheral regions, with a spatial hash grid enforcing a maximum overlap threshold τ. Resolution-specific embedding MLPs map each patch to a common token dimension, and a ViT encoder with LaPE-style per-layer positional embeddings (computed relative to the Gaussian's rotation and scale) processes the token sequence. A Pixel-Predictor then attends from pixel-coordinate queries to the encoded patch tokens to produce mask logits. The model is trained end-to-end on SA-1B with a sparse binary cross-entropy loss over adaptively sampled boundary pixels, and the entire pipeline is scale-invariant because sampling depends only on μ and Σ, not on absolute image dimensions. This yields FLIP variants from 0.51M to 96.6M parameters that outperform SAM2-L (224.45M) by 7.4 percentage points in mean IoU while running ~2× faster.

## Results

FLIP-Large achieves 83.26% mean IoU across six benchmarks (Hypersim, KITTI-360, OpenImages, COCO, LVIS, ObjaScale) with only 96.6M parameters and 44.13 ms inference time, outperforming SAM2-L (75.87% IoU, 224.45M params, 88.64 ms) by 7.39 percentage points while using 2.3× fewer parameters and running ~2× faster. Even FLIP-Tiny (0.51M parameters, 9.68 ms) reaches 79.90% mean IoU, surpassing all SAM variants including SAM2-L. On the novel ObjaScale scale-invariance stress test, FLIP variants achieve 88.6–90.7% IoU versus 67.7–73.8% for SAM variants, demonstrating superior handling of objects spanning 0.0001% to 25% of image area.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLIP outperforms all six SAM-family baselines (SAM-B/L/H, SAM2-T/S/B+/L, EfficientSAM-T/S, MobileSAM, FastSAM-s/x) on every one of the six evaluation datasets. The most striking comparison is FLIP-Tiny (0.51M params) vs. SAM2-L (224.45M params): FLIP-Tiny achieves 79.90% mean IoU vs. 75.87%, a 4.03-point gain with 440× fewer parameters and ~9× faster inference (9.68 ms vs. 88.64 ms). FLIP-Large (96.6M) beats SAM-H (641.1M) by 7.85 points (83.26% vs. 75.41%) while using 6.6× fewer parameters. On the ObjaScale scale-invariance benchmark, FLIP's advantage is most pronounced: FLIP variants reach 88.6–90.7% IoU while SAM-H drops to 73.76% and SAM2-L to 69.54%, with FastSAM-x failing entirely below 0.1% relative object area. The only other foveated method evaluated, STT (Schmidt & Newcombe, 2025), maintains reasonable performance on small objects but remains over 15 percentage points behind FLIP on ObjaScale. A notable limitation is prompt robustness: under 20% bounding-box noise, FLIP_bb variants degrade sharply (e.g., FLIP_bb-Large drops from 79.53% to 26.34%) while full-image SAM encoders degrade more gracefully (SAM2-L: 75.78% → 44.66%), revealing that FLIP's local sampling makes it more sensitive to prompt inaccuracies.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLIP departs from the dominant SAM/SAM2 paradigm of encoding the entire image before focusing on a prompted object, instead directly sampling multi-resolution patches around the target using a 2D Gaussian prior inspired by biological foveal vision. Architecturally, it builds on LaPE-style layer-wise positional embedding injection for its ViT encoder and borrows the sparse pixel-query attention pattern from SAM's mask decoder, but replaces the full-image encoder with an off-grid, scale-invariant patch sampler. The closest prior work is STT (Schmidt & Newcombe, 2025), which also uses foveated tokenization, but FLIP generalizes from fixed point-centered sampling to object-conditioned, multi-resolution, off-grid patch sampling driven by a full 2D Gaussian (encoding scale and orientation). FLIP is positioned as complementary to unsupervised object-centric models (Slot Attention, SAVI++, Loci): it assumes an object-level prompt rather than discovering objects, making it a prompted foundation model for efficient single-object segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 440× parameter reduction (FLIP-Tiny at 0.51M vs. SAM2-L at 224M) with superior mean IoU across six benchmarks, combined with ~2× faster inference, makes high-quality prompted segmentation viable on resource-constrained and real-time platforms. The ObjaScale diagnostic exposes a concrete failure mode of full-image encoders on small objects in high-resolution scenes, a gap that matters for autonomous driving, medical imaging, and surveillance. However, the prompt-noise ablation reveals a significant practical limitation: FLIPbb degrades far more sharply than full-image encoders under even moderate box perturbations (20% noise drops IoU from ~79% to ~26%), because the same Gaussian controls both patch sampling and mask querying. Open questions include how to robustly derive 2D Gaussian prompts from standard detector outputs or tracking filters, whether uncertainty-aware prompt construction can close the noise-robustness gap, and whether the foveated encoder integrates cleanly into slot-based or temporal tracking architectures as the authors speculate.
