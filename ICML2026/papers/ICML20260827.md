## Problem

Sequential visual localization in indoor environments typically relies on large image databases or dense 3D point clouds, creating prohibitive storage overhead for memory-constrained robots and AR devices. While 3D scene graphs offer a compact, semantically rich alternative, prior work (SceneGraphLoc) used them only for single-image place recognition, leaving sequential pose refinement over image sequences unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260827/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image sequence (subdivided into 16×9 or 32×24 patches depending on dataset), a pre-built 3D scene graph containing coarse object meshes and pre-computed semantic embeddings, ego-motion estimates from a SLAM system (e.g., DROID-SLAM), and camera intrinsics; depth maps are optional. It outputs a 4-DoF camera pose (x, y, z, yaw) per frame, assuming gravity direction is known, with the pose refined sequentially as new frames arrive. The scene graph requires only object-level meshes sufficient for visibility/occlusion checks, not dense geometry.

## Method

SG2Loc formulates sequential localization as a particle filter over 4-DoF poses, where each particle projects coarse scene-graph object meshes into the query image via raycasting to assign object identities to image patches. Particle weights are computed by comparing per-patch semantic features (from the pre-trained SceneGraphLoc encoder) against scene-graph object embeddings using cosine similarity, augmented by photometric (SSIM) and depth (L1) scores for disambiguation. A coarse-to-fine strategy runs three optimization rounds at strides 20, 8, and 4, progressively narrowing the search region around the MLE pose, with adaptive KLD-based resampling controlling particle count. Final pose refinement uses RoMa v2 feature matching and RANSAC-based PnP from PoseLib. The approach requires no per-scene training and stores roughly an order of magnitude less data than image-database or mesh-based baselines.

## Results

On 3RScan, SG2Loc achieves the best joint recall (R@0.25 m, 2°) across all sequence lengths: 0.34 (5 frames), 0.39 (10 frames), and 0.44 (25 frames), with median position errors of 0.15 m, 0.11 m, and 0.07 m respectively. On ScanNet, SG2Loc is competitive, reaching 0.49 joint recall at 5 frames (vs. 0.53–0.54 for HLoc/MeshLoc) and 0.60 at 25 frames (matching ACE's 0.59). Storage is dramatically reduced: 9.8 MB per scene on 3RScan and 28.2 MB on ScanNet, compared to 57.6 MB (HLoc) and 701.3 MB (MeshLoc) on 3RScan, and 2,291 MB (HLoc) and 10,591 MB (MeshLoc) on ScanNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3RScan, SG2Loc decisively outperforms all baselines on joint recall: 0.34 vs. 0.29 (HLoc/MeshLoc) at 5 frames, 0.39 vs. 0.14 at 10 frames, and 0.44 vs. 0.08 at 25 frames, with median position errors roughly 2× lower than HLoc (0.07 m vs. 0.08 m at 25 frames, 0.15 m vs. 0.19 m at 5 frames). On ScanNet, SG2Loc is slightly behind HLoc and MeshLoc for shorter sequences (0.49 vs. 0.53–0.54 joint recall at 5 frames) but matches or exceeds ACE at 25 frames (0.60 vs. 0.59). Loc-NeRF performs very poorly on both datasets (joint recall ≤ 0.08), while ACE is strong on ScanNet but weak on 3RScan (0.11 joint recall at 5 frames). The key differentiator is storage: SG2Loc requires ~6× less than HLoc and ~72× less than MeshLoc on 3RScan, and ~81× less than HLoc and ~375× less than MeshLoc on ScanNet, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SceneGraphLoc (Miao et al., 2024), which performed single-image cross-modal place recognition over a database of 3D scene graphs, into a full sequential pose-estimation pipeline by wrapping the scene graph in a particle filter. It departs from dense-representation localizers (HLoc, MeshLoc, Loc-NeRF, ACE) that require large image databases, per-scene depth maps, or trained coordinate-regression networks, replacing them with compact object-level embeddings and coarse meshes for raycasting. The particle-filter formulation is analogous to Loc-NeRF's Monte Carlo localization but operates on a semantically structured, low-storage scene graph rather than a NeRF or Gaussian Splat, and the coarse-to-fine multi-round optimization with adaptive KLD resampling is a practical engineering contribution layered on top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× storage reduction over HLoc and ~100× over MeshLoc makes sequential visual localization viable on memory-constrained robots and AR headsets where dense maps are impractical, and the keyframing experiment (1.13 m mean position error vs. 16.55 m for HLoc in an online setting) demonstrates practical deployability. Open questions include whether the 2.9 s per-frame integration cost can be reduced to real-time rates, how the method scales to truly large multi-room or outdoor environments, and whether the reliance on pre-built scene graphs (via SceneGraphFusion or manual annotation) limits applicability to settings where such graphs are unavailable. The failure mode on uninformative, near-static view sequences also highlights a fundamental information-theoretic limit that no representation change alone can overcome.
