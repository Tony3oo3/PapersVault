## Problem

One-step generation (via MeanFlow/Consistency Models) and latent-free pixel-space generation (via JiT) have each advanced independently, but merging them into a single end-to-end network is nontrivial: the model must simultaneously learn multi-time-point trajectory modeling and perform implicit manifold compression without a pre-trained tokenizer. Directly predicting velocity fields in high-dimensional pixel space fails catastrophically, leaving a key gap in one-step latent-free generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single sample of Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a raw RGB pixel image in one forward pass (1-NFE) at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolution. The Vision Transformer uses aggressively large patches (16×16 up to 64×64) with a fixed sequence length of 16², so the per-patch dimensionality scales with resolution (768 to 12288) while compute stays roughly constant. The network output is the image itself ('what-you-see-is-what-you-get'), which enables direct application of perceptual losses during training.

## Method

Pixel MeanFlow (pMF) decouples the prediction space from the loss space: the network directly outputs a denoised-image-like quantity x(z_t, r, t) = z_t − t·u(z_t, r, t), hypothesized to lie on a low-dimensional data manifold, while the training loss is defined in velocity space via the MeanFlow identity. Concretely, the ViT outputs x, which is converted to average velocity u = (z_t − x)/t, then to the compound target V = u + (t−r)·JVP_sg, and the loss regresses V against the instantaneous velocity v = ε − x. Time sampling is performed over the full (r,t) plane (0 ≤ r ≤ t), and the Muon optimizer with perceptual loss (LPIPS + ConvNeXt-V2) applied to the x output further improves quality. This x-prediction formulation is critical: at 256×256 with patch dimension 768, u-prediction yields 164.89 FID while x-prediction achieves 9.56 FID (baseline) and 2.22 FID (full model).

## Results

pMF achieves 2.22 FID and 268.8 IS on ImageNet 256×256 with a single function evaluation (1-NFE) in raw pixel space, using the pMF-H/16 model (956M params, 271 Gflops). At 512×512, pMF-H/32 reaches 2.48 FID and 284.9 IS with comparable computational cost to its 256×256 counterpart (272 Gflops). These results make pMF the strongest one-step, latent-free diffusion/flow method, substantially outperforming the only prior method in this category (EPG, 8.82 FID) and matching or exceeding leading GANs (StyleGAN-XL: 2.30 FID at 256×256) with 5.8× lower FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among one-step, latent-free methods, pMF-H/16 (2.22 FID) dramatically outperforms EPG-L/16 (8.82 FID), the only other method in this exact category, by over 6 FID points. Against one-step latent-space methods, pMF-H/16 (2.22 FID) trails iMF-XL/2 (1.72 FID) by 0.5 FID but operates without a VAE decoder, whose overhead (310 Gflops at 256×256) exceeds pMF's entire generator cost. Compared to multi-step pixel-space methods, pMF narrows the gap substantially: it is within 0.1 FID of VDM++ (2.12) and within 0.4 FID of JiT-G/16 (1.82), while requiring only 1 NFE versus 100–256 NFEs. Against GANs, pMF-H/16 matches StyleGAN-XL (2.30 FID) with 5.8× fewer FLOPs (271 vs. 1574 Gflops) and outperforms BigGAN-deep (6.95 FID) and GigaGAN (3.45 FID). At 512×512, pMF-H/32 (2.48 FID) beats both one-step latent methods (MeanFlow-RAE: 3.23, sCT-XXL: 4.29) and nearly matches StyleGAN-XL (2.41) at comparable compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: one-step generation via MeanFlow/iMF (which learns an average velocity field u with a v-space loss) and pixel-space generation via JiT (which parameterizes the denoised image x directly). The key conceptual contribution is introducing a transformation x→u→v that lets the network output x (hypothesized to lie on a low-dimensional manifold) while training with MeanFlow's v-loss, effectively bridging the prediction-space and loss-space gap. The paper positions itself as distinct from Consistency Models (which fix r=0), CTM (which integrates ODEs during training), and Flow Map Matching (which predicts displacement), arguing that the full (r,t)-plane sampling with x-prediction is necessary for the high-dimensional pixel regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

pMF achieves 2.22 FID at 256×256 and 2.48 FID at 512×512 with a single forward pass and no latent tokenizer, filling a key gap in the generative modeling landscape and demonstrating that end-to-end noise-to-pixel mapping is competitive with multi-step latent-based methods. The finding that x-prediction is not merely preferable but essential (u-prediction yields 164.89 FID vs. 9.56 at 256×256) provides a clear design principle for future pixel-space generative models. Open questions include whether adversarial losses (used in latent-based methods) could further close the remaining gap to multi-step methods, whether the manifold hypothesis generalizes beyond ImageNet-class-conditional generation, and whether the approach extends to text-to-image or video generation.
