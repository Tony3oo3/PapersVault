## Problem

Finding non-Hirsch monomial ideals (linearly-presented ideals with diameter exceeding their generator degree) for Kalai's algebraic Hirsch conjecture is an extremely sparse-reward search problem: the probability of a random ideal satisfying both linearity and large-diameter conditions decays rapidly, causing standard RL algorithms like PPO and SAC to fail entirely beyond the smallest instances. The paper asks whether hierarchical RL with mathematically-motivated constraints can exploit the problem's structure to succeed where flat RL and greedy search cannot.

## Input / Output

The agent operates on a fixed degree d and variable count n, starting from a single-node graph and toggling inclusion bits over the C(n,d) possible square-free monomial generators. States are represented as decorated subgraphs of G_max(d,n) with binary-encoded node features (n-bit sequences per generator) and augmented irreducible edges indicating algebraic obstructions to linearity. The output is a mask selecting a subset of generators that form a non-Hirsch ideal, verified by checking that all higher-level syzygy edges are reducible (i.e., the ideal is linearly presented) and that the graph diameter exceeds d.

## Method

The key insight is that successful trajectories consistently pass through 'spines'—path graphs satisfying the diameter constraint—motivating a Chained Constrained Options framework with two sequential temporal abstractions: a learned spine-construction policy π_S and a fixed A*-based linearization policy π_L. Intra-option hard constraints (implemented as action masking) enforce monotonic diameter growth during spine construction and diameter preservation during linearization, pruning invalid actions while preserving reachability of all solutions. The policy and Q-function are parameterized by an equivariant GNN using Syzygy-aware message passing (SConv), which applies cross-attention over binary-encoded node features to capture the bitwise AND/OR operations inherent in syzygy reduction, combined with GAT layers in a GPS transformer. The spine policy is trained via PPO with a sparse goal-hitting reward (1 only upon reaching a non-Hirsch ideal), while the linearization policy is fixed as heuristic-guided A* search using the count of irreducible edges, reducing the learning problem to the bottleneck of discovering supporting spines.

## Results

The Constrained Options HRL framework achieves an effective success rate of 10.8% at d=4, 8.74% at d=5, 6.78% at d=6, and 3.16% at d=7 for constructing non-Hirsch ideals. This represents a ~1,400× improvement over the best standard RL baseline (SAC+HER at 7.78×10⁻⁵ for d=4) and a ~5× improvement over best-first search at d=4 (2.03×10⁻²). Critically, the HRL method is the only approach that successfully scales to d=6 and d=7, where all RL baselines (PPO, SAC, SAC+HuRL, SAC+HER, SAC+Buffer) fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Constrained Options HRL framework against five RL baselines and one classical search method. All standard RL algorithms (PPO, SAC) fail to produce any solutions beyond d=4, with success rates below 10⁻⁵ even at d=4. Adding heuristic-guided reward shaping (SAC+HuRL) or hindsight experience replay (SAC+HER) provides only marginal gains at d=4 (7.78×10⁻⁵ for HER) and still fails completely at d≥5. The Hopper population-buffer approach (SAC+Buffer) extends reach to d=5 but at a negligible 2.27×10⁻⁶ rate, and fails at d≥6. Best-first search is the strongest non-RL baseline, achieving 2.03×10⁻² at d=4 and 3.9×10⁻⁴ at d=7, but the HRL method outperforms it by roughly 5× at d=4 and over 80× at d=7. The HRL framework wins decisively on every degree tested, with the largest relative gains at higher degrees where all other methods collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the options framework (Bacon et al., 2017) by introducing intra-option policy constraints derived from domain-specific mathematical structure, creating a 'Chained Constrained Options' HRL architecture. It builds on PPO for policy optimization within options and draws on the broader HRL literature (Levy et al., 2019; Vezhnevets et al., 2017) to address the well-known instability of multi-level policy learning, using algebraic constraints as a stabilizing mechanism. The graph neural network policy introduces a novel Syzygy-aware message-passing layer (SConv) that encodes the binary operations required by the syzygy reduction algorithm, combined with a GPS transformer backbone. The work also positions itself within the growing line of RL-for-mathematics papers (Fawzi et al., 2022; Swirszcz et al., 2025) but targets a fundamentally harder sparse-reward regime where those prior methods fail entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first successful application of HRL to a problem in commutative algebra, demonstrating that learned temporal abstractions are not merely beneficial but essential for extremely sparse-reward mathematical search where standard RL fails completely beyond the smallest instances. The general paradigm—observe standard RL failure modes to identify bottleneck states, then design constrained temporal abstractions around them—offers a transferable recipe for 'needle-in-a-haystack' agentic search in mathematics. Key limitations include the need for domain-specific insight to identify the spine structure, the fixed heuristic-based linearization step that has not been learned end-to-end, and evaluation only up to degree d=7. Open questions include whether bottleneck identification can be automated without expert knowledge, whether the approach scales to higher degrees, and whether the constrained-options paradigm generalizes to other open problems in algebraic combinatorics.
