## Problem

Prior work presents a tension: some studies show LLM hidden states already encode future reasoning before CoT unfolds, while theoretical and empirical results establish that CoT is indispensable for compositional tasks. The specific gap is characterizing the *latent planning horizon*—how far ahead in the reasoning trajectory internal states actually encode information—across diverse task domains rather than in single-domain settings, and understanding what practical signals can be extracted from this horizon.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-token hidden states from intermediate Transformer layers (e.g., layer 48 of 64 for Qwen3-32B, layer 21 of 28 for the 7B In-Domain LLM) along full CoT trajectories, with the LM head kept frozen throughout. Probing targets three teleological dimensions: next-m tokens (via an offset embedding matrix), total reasoning length (via a single regression head), and final answer (restricted to a fixed 20-token answer space: A–F, YES, NO, even, odd, 0–9). Downstream, the latent signals yield uncertainty AUROC scores and binary CoT-bypass decisions triggered when early-position normalized entropy falls below a threshold.

## Method

Tele-Lens is a per-layer bottleneck low-rank adapter (rank r=256) with GE LU nonlinearity that transforms each hidden state before passing through the frozen LM head, enabling whole-vocabulary prediction from any intermediate layer; an optional offset embedding matrix injects the target prediction position for subsequent-token probing. The adapter is trained for ~5K steps with early stopping across 12 tasks spanning explicit compositional (Parity, Cycle, Subsum), implicit compositional (GSM8K, MATH, AIME, MuSR, Zebra), and knowledge/semantic (CSQA, MMLU, QuALITY, GPQA) domains, using two backbones: off-the-shelf Qwen3-32B and an In-Domain LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct fine-tuned via GRPO) as an upper bound on planning capacity. The key empirical finding is a myopic planning horizon: precise final-answer signals emerge only 1–2 steps before CoT completion on compositional tasks, while early coarse signals on semantic tasks reflect pattern-matching gist inferior to even direct answering without CoT. Leveraging this, the paper proposes a Wooden Barrel Principle for uncertainty estimation—selecting top-k pivot positions by Tele-Lens entropy or general metrics outperforms full-path aggregation by up to 6% AUROC—and a CoT bypass strategy using early-position entropy thresholds that achieves 16.2% thinking reduction on Qwen3-32B with only 0.03% accuracy degradation.

## Results

On uncertainty estimation (AUROC), Tele-Lens with top-5 pivot selection achieves 0.69 average across GSM8K, Zebra, MMLU, and GPQA, outperforming the best baseline (Self-Certainty at 0.60) by 9% absolute. With general metrics on Qwen3-32B, selecting top-100 high-entropy positions yields 3–6% absolute AUROC improvement over full-path aggregation (e.g., Perplexity: 0.72→0.78, Entropy: 0.72→0.75). For CoT bypass, Qwen3-32B with a normalized-entropy threshold of 0.1 achieves 16.2% thinking reduction on CSQA and 12.4% on MMLU with only 0.03% overall accuracy degradation. The In-Domain LLM (7B, GRPO-trained) reaches 98.3% on Parity and 97.2% on Cycle with CoT lengths ~1K characters vs. ~10K+ for Qwen3-32B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Tele-Lens top-k pivot selection against three standard uncertainty metrics—Perplexity (NLL), Entropy, and Self-Certainty (Kang et al., 2025)—aggregated over the full CoT path. Tele-Lens Top-5 dominates all three baselines on every task: it achieves 0.87 vs. 0.76 (Self-Certainty) on GSM8K, 0.77 vs. 0.67 on Zebra, and 0.73 vs. 0.53 on MMLU, yielding a 9% average AUROC gain over the strongest baseline. The improvement is most pronounced on compositional and logical reasoning tasks (GSM8K, Zebra, MMLU) but smaller on GPQA (0.56 vs. 0.51), suggesting the Wooden Barrel principle is less effective when the answer space is highly uncertain. When extended to general metrics without a dedicated prober (Table 3, Qwen3-32B), the same top-100 pivot strategy consistently improves each metric over its full-path counterpart (e.g., Perplexity 0.72→0.78, Entropy 0.72→0.75, Self-Certainty 0.65→0.70), confirming that the benefit is not an artifact of the Tele-Lens adapter but a property of sparse pivot selection itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Logit Lens probing paradigm (nostalgebraist, 2021; Belrose et al., 2023) with a low-rank adapter (Houlsby et al., 2019) to systematically characterize how far ahead LLM hidden states encode future reasoning, across 12 diverse tasks. It directly addresses a tension in prior work: studies like Pal et al. (2023) and Dong et al. (2025) show early hidden states encode subsequent generations, while theoretical analyses (Merrill & Sabharwal, 2023; Li et al., 2024) argue CoT is indispensable for compositional reasoning. The paper's 'myopic planning horizon' framing unifies these by showing early signals are coarse pattern-matching gists rather than precise multi-step plans, and it departs from prior single-domain probing by spanning explicit compositional, implicit compositional, and knowledge/semantic tasks simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The myopic planning horizon provides a principled explanation for why CoT remains necessary despite apparent early-state predictability, with direct implications for adaptive thinking systems (e.g., GPT-1, Claude Code routing) that rely on internal complexity sensing. The Wooden Barrel Principle and top-k pivot strategy offer a simple, general recipe for improving CoT uncertainty calibration (up to 6% AUROC gain) without task-specific training, and the CoT bypass result (16.2% thinking reduction at 0.03% accuracy cost) demonstrates practical efficiency gains. Key limitations include the CoT bypass being restricted to fixed answer spaces (proof-of-concept), the In-Domain LLM being one generation behind Qwen3, and the Wooden Barrel Principle remaining a heuristic without formal theoretical grounding. Open questions include whether richer multi-source signal integration (Tele-Lens + general metrics) can further improve calibration, and whether the myopic horizon characterization holds for larger or differently-architected models.
