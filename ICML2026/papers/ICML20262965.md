## Problem

Existing RTL quality estimation methods either rely on hand-crafted features from shallow graph representations (DFGs, ASTs) or token-based LLM embeddings that encode structural semantics only implicitly. The control data flow graph (CDFG) view exposes both control and data dependencies explicitly, yet no prior work has leveraged it with structure-aware self-supervised learning to learn representations sensitive to the structural properties that drive area and delay.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262965/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes Verilog RTL source code, which is compiled to RTLIL via Yosys and parsed into a CDFG (directed graph with 32 node types carrying type and bit-width attributes, plus directed edges for data/control dependencies). Output is a pair of scalar estimates—post-synthesis area and delay—computed by aggregating node embeddings via joint mean/max pooling into a graph-level vector, then passing through a 3-layer MLP. Target values are log-transformed to handle large magnitude variance, and predictions are evaluated with MAE, MAPE, R², and RRSE.

## Method

StructRTL combines an 8-layer GIN with an 8-layer Transformer encoder (4 heads), where global positional embeddings derived from the smallest 16 Laplacian eigenvectors preserve graph connectivity when nodes are flattened into a sequence. Two tailored self-supervised pretraining tasks operate on post-GNN context-aware embeddings rather than raw features: structure-aware masked node modeling (mask 20% of embeddings, predict node types as 32-class classification using stratified masking and class-balanced focal loss to handle severe class imbalance) and edge prediction (binary classification on 20% sampled positive/negative edge pairs). This design preserves computational integrity of CDFGs, where masking raw operators would introduce ambiguity. A knowledge distillation stage trains a frozen 20-layer GIN PM-netlist predictor first, then aligns the CDFG predictor's final-layer activations with it via a weighted cosine + MSE loss, transferring gate-level insights into the RTL-stage model.

## Results

On the 13,200-design dataset, StructRTL achieves R² = 0.7463 for area and R² = 0.7630 for delay prediction without knowledge distillation, outperforming the best LLM-based baseline (CodeV-QW-7B) by +0.11 and +0.24 in R² respectively. With knowledge distillation from post-mapping netlists, StructRTL further improves to R² = 0.8676 (area) and R² = 0.8872 (delay), approaching the PM-based teacher (R² = 0.9334 / 0.9484). On 102 industrial modules, StructRTL achieves 82.35% area-ranking and 88.23% delay-ranking accuracy, with an inference latency of 0.096 s per design versus 13.97 s for full synthesis (145× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructRTL outperforms all three baseline families across both area and delay prediction. Against the LLM-based VeriDistill/CodeV baselines (Moravej et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025), StructRTL gains +0.11 R² on area (0.7463 vs. 0.6353 for CodeV-QW-7B) and a much larger +0.24 R² on delay (0.7630 vs. 0.5277), with the gap widening to +0.12 and +0.12 R² respectively when knowledge distillation is added. Against the hand-crafted-feature Graph-XGBoost baseline (Sengupta et al., 2022), StructRTL improves R² by +0.35 on area and +0.43 on delay, confirming that learned structural representations substantially surpass manually designed CDFG statistics. Against the end-to-end Graph-GNN baseline (no pretraining), StructRTL gains +0.16 R² on area and +0.10 R² on delay, isolating the contribution of the two self-supervised pretraining tasks. The advantage is most pronounced on delay prediction, which the authors attribute to CDFGs explicitly encoding critical-path structure that token-based LLM embeddings capture only implicitly.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructRTL adapts the generative graph self-supervised learning paradigm from GraphMAE (masked node reconstruction) and MaskGAE (edge connectivity recovery) to the specific challenge of CDFGs, where naive masking of computational nodes introduces semantic ambiguity. It departs from the dominant token-based LLM approach (VeriDistill/CodeV) by operating on explicit structural representations (CDFGs) rather than raw Verilog sequences, and from earlier hand-crafted feature methods (Sengupta et al., Lopera et al.) by learning end-to-end structure-informed embeddings. The knowledge distillation component, inspired by VeriDistill's cross-stage supervision idea, is repurposed to transfer PM-netlist insights into the CDFG predictor rather than into an LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 145× inference speedup over full synthesis makes near-instantaneous quality feedback practical for iterative RTL design cycles, and the consistent large margin over LLM-based baselines—especially for delay prediction—validates the hypothesis that explicit structural representations capture what token sequences encode only implicitly. Key open questions remain: cross-technology-node generalization (the model must be recalibrated per PDK), the performance gap on sequential circuits where inter-cycle dependencies complicate timing, and whether the preliminary register-boundary partitioning strategy scales to designs with 100K+ CDFG nodes. The work also highlights a broader tension in EDA: the gap between what is measurable (post-synthesis metrics) and what is available at design time (RTL), which KD partially bridges but does not eliminate.
