## Problem

Recovering the parameters of multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) processes from steady-state snapshot data—rather than expensive time-series trajectories—is critical for large-scale gene perturbation experiments. Prior identifiability results either relied solely on observational covariances (which cannot resolve two-cycles or unknown diffusion) or required interventions on nearly all coordinates. This paper asks whether a single intervention per strongly connected component (SCC) of the drift graph is sufficient for full parameter recovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the observational steady-state mean μ and covariance Σ, plus interventional means and covariances for a set of coordinates (one per SCC suffices), where each intervention is a hard knockout that zeros all off-diagonal entries in one row of the drift matrix while preserving its diagonal. The output is the drift matrix Λ, input vector b, and diagonal diffusion power matrix D, identifiable only up to a single positive global scaling factor c (a consequence of the homogeneity of the moment equations). The method also recovers the SCC decomposition and a topological ordering of the condensation DAG from mean-shift patterns alone, without requiring the spectral nondegeneracy assumptions needed for parameter identification.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution shows that stacking the steady-state mean and Lyapunov (covariance) equations for both observational and interventional settings yields a linear system whose null space is generically one-dimensional (the global scaling), provided the drift graph is a single SCC and certain spectral/rank nondegeneracy conditions hold. For the multi-SCC case, the paper introduces a recursive algorithm: first, mean shifts under single-node interventions reveal the SCC partition and a topological order (via Theorem 3.3, which characterizes the response set as the downstream region of the intervened SCC); then, for each SCC in topological order, the algorithm conditions on previously resolved upstream components using Gaussian conditional moments to isolate the target SCC's marginal dynamics, reducing the problem to a sequence of single-SCC linear systems. Practically, a regularized least-squares estimator jointly minimizes squared residuals of the mean and covariance equations across all observational and interventional data, with an ℓ₁ penalty on off-diagonal drift entries to promote sparsity and a scale-fixing normalization (e.g., tr(D)=1) to remove the global scaling ambiguity.

## Results

On the three Perturb-seq datasets (Co-Culture, Control, IFN-γ), the full method (Mean+Covariance) achieves DES of 0.43/0.33/0.47 and PDS of 0.67/0.63/0.70, matching or exceeding the oracle interventional-mean baseline (DES 0.52/0.42/0.36, PDS 0.57/0.67/0.70) despite not having access to held-out interventional data. On synthetic data with n=10, relative parameter errors for Λ, D, and b decrease monotonically with the number of interventions; with 10 interventions and non-diagonal diffusion, relative errors reach 0.065 (Λ), 0.072 (D), and 0.066 (b). SCC recovery via mean shifts achieves ARI of 0.881 (hard) and 0.837 (soft) at 10,000 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its full method (Mean+Covariance) against three baselines on real Perturb-seq data. Against the observational-mean baseline, the full method improves PDS by 0.10–0.03 across all three datasets (e.g., 0.67 vs. 0.57 on Co-Culture) and DES by up to 0.12 (0.47 vs. 0.35 on IFN-γ). Against the covariance-only variant (analogous to Dettling et al. 2024 and Rohbeck et al. 2024), the full method substantially outperforms on PDS (0.63–0.70 vs. 0.43 uniformly), confirming that mean terms are essential for perturbation discrimination. On DES, the covariance-only variant is slightly higher on Co-Culture (0.51 vs. 0.43) and Control (0.46 vs. 0.33), but the full method wins on IFN-γ (0.47 vs. 0.42). Notably, the full method matches or exceeds the oracle interventional-mean baseline on PDS for all three datasets (0.67 vs. 0.57, 0.63 vs. 0.67, 0.70 vs. 0.70) and on DES for IFN-γ (0.47 vs. 0.36), despite not having access to held-out interventional measurements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graphical continuous Lyapunov model framework (Varando & Hansen, 2020; Dettling et al., 2023, 2024), which recovers drift structure from observational steady-state covariances alone, by incorporating interventional data to resolve non-identifiability. It departs from prior intervention-aware SDE work (Bicycle, Rohbeck et al., 2024; Lorch et al., 2024) by providing explicit parameter-recovery identifiability guarantees under a much weaker intervention budget—one per strongly connected component rather than interventions on nearly all coordinates. The intervention model (hard row-zeroing) follows the causal SDE literature (Hansen & Sokol, 2014; Boeken & Mooij, 2024), and the moment-matching estimation strategy builds on standard Lyapunov-equation-based approaches, but the recursive SCC-decomposition algorithm and the proof that a single global scaling is the only ambiguity are new contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result provides the first identifiability guarantee showing that minimal interventional data (one per SCC) suffices to recover full OU process parameters from steady-state snapshots, which is directly relevant to large-scale CRISPR Perturb-seq experiments where time-series trajectories are infeasible. The global scaling ambiguity is shown to be harmless for predicting stationary moments under unseen interventions, making the result practically actionable. Key limitations include the diagonal-diffusion assumption, unproven genericity of spectral/rank nondegeneracy conditions, and the requirement that the SCC condensation DAG be connected with a single root. Open questions include formal genericity proofs, extending to non-diagonal or structured diffusion, and whether the framework generalizes to nonlinear SDEs or mean-shift interventions.
