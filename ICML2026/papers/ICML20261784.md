## Problem

Conjugacy-based dynamical systems learning assumes the source vector field is already smoothly conjugate to the target; when this topological compatibility is violated, the conjugacy problem becomes ill-posed. Naively adding a learnable correction to the source introduces a fundamental identifiability failure: diffeomorphic reparameterizations and intrinsic topology-changing perturbations become entangled, yielding non-unique solutions that can collapse to trivial trajectory matching. The paper asks how to learn a minimal, identifiable correction that cleanly separates these two types of variation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained source NODE (fit on abundant, noise-free source trajectories) and extremely sparse target observations (as few as a single noisy trajectory in the observation space, related to the true target by an unknown diffeomorphism). It produces a learned conjugacy diffeomorphism Φ_θ (a Real NVP normalizing flow), a low-rank context modulation (W, ξ) that adjusts the source vector field, and the resulting target dynamics model. A key structural output is the identifiable decomposition of the source-target discrepancy into a diffeomorphic component (absorbed by Φ_θ) and an intrinsic, topology-changing component (captured by the modulation), which enables recovery of canonical structures such as normal forms, symmetries, and bifurcation diagrams.

## Method

The core contribution is an adversarial orthogonality regularizer that enforces the low-rank modulation space P_W to be orthogonal to worst-case orbit-tangent directions, defined as Lie brackets [f_0, v_γ^(k)] induced by layerwise infinitesimal generators of a separate adversarial normalizing flow. Training solves a min-max objective: the main model minimizes a flow-level conjugacy loss plus sparsity penalties over (θ, W, ξ), while an adversary maximizes the trace-ratio alignment between P_W and the realizable orbit-tangent subspace over flow parameters (γ, α). For affine coupling layers, the infinitesimal generators admit closed-form expressions, making the Lie-bracket computation efficient via Jacobian-vector products. The theoretical justification draws on versal unfolding theory: under a finite-codimension assumption, a first-order orthogonal complement to the orbit-tangent space provides a transversal slice sufficient to locally recover smooth conjugacy to any nearby target, and the direct-sum condition P_W ∩ T_{f_0}O_{f_0} = {0} guarantees unique decomposition of the discrepancy into identifiable intrinsic and diffeomorphic parts.

## Results

On the Hopf bifurcation benchmark (Table 1), CM-AO achieves the best invariant-set reconstruction in normal-form coordinates with d_H = 0.081, W_2 = 0.071, and MMD = 0.109, substantially outperforming the next-best method (Augmentation: d_H = 0.107, W_2 = 0.100, MMD = 0.175) and the baseline conjugacy (d_H = 0.317, W_2 = 0.316, MMD = 0.757). In observation space, CM-AO achieves d_H = 0.064 and W_2 = 0.121, competitive with CM (d_H = 0.064, W_2 = 0.131). The largest performance gap appears in normal-form evaluation, indicating that the adversarial orthogonality constraint specifically recovers the canonical (SO(2)-symmetric) structure rather than merely fitting observed trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CM-AO outperforms all five baselines on the primary normal-form invariant-set metrics (d_H, W_2, MMD) in the Hopf benchmark, with the largest margin over the baseline conjugacy method (Friedman et al. / Sagodi & Park), which fails to correct the topological mismatch and yields d_H = 0.317 versus CM-AO's 0.081. Against the closest competitor, Augmentation (Yin et al.), CM-AO improves normal-form d_H by 24% (0.081 vs. 0.107) and MMD by 38% (0.109 vs. 0.175), confirming that the orthogonality constraint isolates the minimal topology-changing correction. Against Context Modulation (Kirchmeyer et al.), CM-AO wins on all normal-form metrics (d_H: 0.081 vs. 0.142; W_2: 0.071 vs. 0.119; MMD: 0.109 vs. 0.177) while matching CM on observed-space d_H (both 0.064) and slightly trailing on observed MMD (0.182 vs. 0.173). Direct Tuning and L²-SP occupy an intermediate tier, improving over the baseline but remaining far from CM-AO, suggesting that unconstrained or weakly-regularized source adjustments fail to separate diffeomorphic from intrinsic variation. On the FitzHugh-Nagumo extrapolation setting (ω=2), CM-AO again leads with d_H = 8.941 and W_2 = 2.806, outperforming CM (d_H = 10.38, W_2 = 7.849) and Augmentation (d_H = 9.725, W_2 = 6.114), with the gap widening relative to the interpolation case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conjugacy-based dynamical systems learning (Friedman et al. 2025; Sagodi & Park 2025), which learns a diffeomorphism pushing a source vector field onto target observations, by relaxing the assumption that the source is topologically compatible with the target. The corrective mechanism draws on context-informed low-rank weight modulation from the NODE literature (Kirchmeyer et al. 2022; Nzoyem et al. 2025), while the identifiability constraint is grounded in versal unfolding theory (Arnold 2012) and operationalized via a GAN-style min-max adversarial scheme (Goodfellow et al. 2020; Yu et al. 2019). The key conceptual departure is the orbit-tangent orthogonality principle: rather than letting the correction absorb arbitrary diffeomorphic variation, the modulation space is enforced to be transverse to the Lie-bracket-generated tangent space of the conjugacy orbit, yielding a minimal unfolding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental identifiability gap in conjugacy learning: without the orthogonality constraint, the correction and the diffeomorphism are entangled, and the learned model can degenerate to plain trajectory matching, forfeiting the topological guarantees that motivate conjugacy-based approaches in the first place. The recovery of hidden symmetries (SO(2), Z2) and correct bifurcation diagrams from symmetry-broken, noisy observations demonstrates that the framework recovers canonical structure rather than merely fitting data. Limitations include testing on low-dimensional benchmarks (2D Hopf/Duffing) plus one 64D reaction-diffusion system, the empirical (finite-batch) enforcement of orthogonality, and the reliance on a model-induced surrogate for the infinite-dimensional orbit-tangent space. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional and nonlocal topological corrections, and whether the adversarial training overhead is justified in regimes where the source-target mismatch is small.
