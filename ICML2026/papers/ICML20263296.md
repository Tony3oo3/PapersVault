## Problem

Early-stage battery degradation signals are extremely weak and entangled with stochastic measurement noise, making them difficult to distinguish from random fluctuations. Existing denoising, decomposition, and diffusion-based approaches assume a relatively separable signal-noise boundary that breaks down at the early degradation stage, where aggressive suppression risks discarding the very cues needed for lifetime prediction. The paper asks whether a discrepancy-aware comparison paradigm—inspired by the binaural effect in biological hearing—can expose genuine weak degradation trends without explicitly removing noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the first 100 cycles of multivariate cycling data (voltage, current, capacity), with each cycle resampled to 300 points (150 charge + 150 discharge) and normalized per the BatteryLife protocol, forming a tensor U ∈ R^{100×300×3}. The output is a scalar predicted lifetime in cycle count (the index at which SOH first drops below 80%, or 90% for CALB), produced by a linear projection of the accumulated bilateral discrepancy energy. The model is trained end-to-end with MSE loss and evaluated under both in-domain and leave-one-dataset-out zero-shot settings.

## Method

DITING consists of three stages: (1) a hierarchical embedding that projects each cycle into a D-dimensional latent vector with rotary, sinusoidal, and learnable positional encodings along cycle-order, within-cycle, and sensor-type dimensions; (2) a health prototype built via Sinkhorn optimal transport over the first K=8 cycles, where the geometric medoid is identified by minimum aggregate transport cost and the prototype is a temperature-weighted fusion of cycle embeddings, yielding degradation residuals R = X − H; (3) a tri-coupled degradation manifestation mechanism modeled as three coupled second-order damped harmonic oscillators, where the left and right branches are driven by anti-phase sinusoidal forces (A·sin(ωt) and A·sin(ωt+π)), the middle branch receives the degradation residuals, and coupling terms κ(x_i − x_M) link all branches through the central state. A symmetrized nonlinear response N(x) = φ(x) − φ(−x) enforces strict central odd symmetry, ensuring zero-mean noise cannot be rectified into a persistent signed drift. The bilateral discrepancy energy E = Σ_t ‖x_L^(t) + x_R^(t)‖₂ accumulates the stable asymmetry induced by directionally consistent degradation while stochastic fluctuations cancel under the mirrored geometry, and E is mapped to lifetime via a linear head.

## Results

On the BatteryLife benchmark (8 datasets, first 100 cycles), DITING achieves the best MAPE on 5 of 8 datasets: CALCE (0.187), SNL (0.152), HNEI (0.035, tied), XJTU (0.049), and MICH (0.102), with the largest margin on CALCE (0.187 vs. 0.221 for CPMLP) and SNL (0.152 vs. 0.174 for CPTransformer). On the 15%-ACC metric, DITING leads on SNL (0.671) and HUST (0.760). In the zero-shot cross-dataset setting, DITING remains competitive, achieving the best MAPE on HNEI (0.137) and XJTU (0.175) among the compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DITING outperforms all seven baselines on CALCE (0.187 vs. best baseline 0.221 for CPMLP), SNL (0.152 vs. 0.174 for CPTransformer), XJTU (0.049 vs. 0.055 for CPMLP), and MICH (0.102 vs. 0.119 for CPMLP), with the largest relative gains on CALCE (~15% lower MAPE) and SNL (~13% lower). On HNEI, DITING ties LiPM at 0.035. However, DITING trails CPGRU on HUST (0.117 vs. 0.098) and MATR (0.125 vs. 0.097), and trails CPMLP on CALB (0.169 vs. 0.148); the authors attribute these losses to the large protocol variation in HUST and MATR favoring recurrent inductive bias. On the 15%-ACC metric, DITING leads on SNL (0.671 vs. 0.598 for DiffBatt) and HUST (0.760 vs. 0.687 for CPGRU), but is slightly behind CPMLP on XJTU (0.911 vs. 0.929) and LiPM on CALB (0.625 vs. 0.684). In the zero-shot cross-dataset setting, DITING achieves the best MAPE on HNEI (0.137) and XJTU (0.175) but trails CPMLP on CALCE (0.642 vs. 0.695) and SNL (0.537 vs. 0.706), indicating that while the discrepancy-aware representation transfers reasonably, it is not uniformly superior under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

DITING departs from the dominant denoising-and-decomposition paradigm in battery lifetime prediction by reframing the problem as a bilateral discrepancy comparison, drawing on the binaural effect from auditory neuroscience. It builds on Sinkhorn optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) for constructing a robust health prototype via Wasserstein medoid fusion, and on coupled second-order oscillator dynamics (Wiggins, 2003) to implement the tri-coupled degradation manifestation mechanism. Unlike prior methods that suppress noise through filtering, wavelet decomposition, or diffusion-based iterative refinement, DITING preserves weak degradation cues by exploiting the directional consistency of aging versus the zero-mean randomness of noise, using anti-phase driving and symmetrized nonlinear responses to cancel stochastic perturbations while accumulating genuine degradation as a stable left-right discrepancy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled alternative to aggressive denoising for early-stage battery prognostics, where weak degradation signals are intrinsically entangled with measurement noise and standard suppression risks destroying the very cues needed for prediction. The cross-domain validation on C-MAPSS and the noise-injection experiments suggest the discrepancy-aware mechanism has broader applicability to weak-signal time-series tasks beyond batteries. However, DITING does not uniformly dominate all datasets (HUST and MATR favor recurrent inductive bias), and zero-shot cross-dataset performance remains weaker than in-domain results, indicating that the health prototype and coupling dynamics still carry dataset-specific assumptions. Open questions include whether the coupled-oscillator formulation scales to longer horizons or more complex multi-degradation-mechanism scenarios, and whether the theoretical guarantees (Theorem 4.1) hold under non-Gaussian or correlated noise distributions.
