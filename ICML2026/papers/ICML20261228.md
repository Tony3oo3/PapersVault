## Problem

Standard Softmax attention in LLMs suffers from numerical instability due to the exponential function and from 'attention smoothing'—the progressive flattening of attention peaks as inference sequence length grows—which cripples length extrapolation and is compounded by the attention sink phenomenon. Existing Softmax-free alternatives (ReLU, Sigmoid) fail to outperform standard Softmax, and positional interpolation methods are post-hoc fixes that do not address the root cause of attention smoothing.

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in replacement for the attention layer within a standard GPT-2 Transformer (124M params, 12 layers, 768 hidden dim, RoPE), consuming the same Q, K, V matrices and producing a sparse L×L attention distribution that multiplies V for the output. Training uses 1024-token sequences from FineWeb-10B (~10B tokens), and the mechanism is evaluated zero-shot at 2K–16K tokens. In the symbolic regression setting, the model additionally takes simulated orbital trajectory sequences (mass, position, distance) and outputs gravitational force magnitudes, which are then fed to PySR for equation discovery.

## Method

LSSAR replaces Softmax attention with a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (LSSA) l2-normalises each row of Q and K (cosine-similarity style), scales the dot-product scores by a dynamic per-row factor log(d)·log(N) where N encodes the number of attended tokens in each row, applies Softplus as the non-linear positivity transform, masks causally, and l1-normalises each row—yielding a numerically stable, length-aware attention distribution. Stage 2 (Sharpening) multiplies the normalised scores by the token-count matrix N, subtracts an offset O (ones, with zeros in the first three rows), applies ReLU^p to hard-threshold and power-amplify surviving scores, and l1-normalises again. The key design insight is that the first l1-normalisation establishes global gradient coupling (preventing the dead-neuron problem of direct ReLU attention) before the second stage introduces sparsity, and the power parameter p acts as an inverse-temperature knob that sharpens peaks without the exponential gradient decay that plagues Softmax under strong sharpening.

## Results

On the GPT-2-124M model trained at 1K sequence length, LSSAR (p=15) maintains a nearly constant validation loss of 3.19–3.32 across 1K to 8K inference lengths, while standard Softmax degrades from 3.19 to 6.28 over the same range. On the passkey retrieval task, LSSAR achieves 86% accuracy at the 1K training length and retains nonzero accuracy (20%) at 4K tokens, whereas Softmax collapses to 0% beyond 1.5K. On downstream zero-shot benchmarks, LSSAR outperforms Softmax on 5 of 7 tasks, with a ~4× improvement on SummScreen (ROUGE-1: 6.31 vs. 1.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SSMax (Nakanishi, 2025), LSSAR (p=15) achieves substantially lower validation loss at 4K (3.23 vs. 5.46) and 8K (3.32 vs. 6.44), while SSMax is marginally better at 1K (3.17 vs. 3.19). Against the Sigmoid attention family (Ramapuram et al., 2024), LSSAR dominates at all lengths beyond 1K: the best Sigmoid variant (b=−log N, l₁-norm) reaches 6.18 at 8K versus LSSAR's 3.32, and the unmodified Sigmoid collapses to 14.50. Against the three ReLU-based baselines (Wortsman et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023), LSSAR wins at 4K and 8K across the board (e.g., 3.32 vs. 10.35, 6.46, and 10.73 at 8K). Notably, applying the same re-weighting (p=15) to Softmax *harms* performance (5.39 at 1K), confirming that the Softplus-based normalisation stage is essential for the sharpening mechanism to work. On passkey retrieval, LSSAR (p=15) achieves 86% at 1K and 20% at 4K, while Softmax drops to 0% beyond 1.5K, demonstrating a qualitative rather than incremental advantage in length extrapolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the log-L scaling insight of Chiang & Cholak (2022) and the entropy-invariance perspective of Su (2021), extending them to a dynamic per-row log-N factor, and on the observation from multiple Softmax-free attention studies (Sigmoid, ReLU variants) that simply replacing the exponential is insufficient. Its key conceptual departure is the decomposition of Softmax into a non-linear positivity transformation plus l1-normalisation, with empirical evidence that the l1-normalisation (not positivity) is the load-bearing component, freeing the design to substitute Softplus for the exponential. The two-stage architecture (LSSA normalisation followed by Shift-ReLU power re-weighting) is novel relative to both prior Softmax-free methods and projection-based sparsifiers like Sparsemax/Entmax, which the paper distinguishes on computational and gradient-flow grounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length extrapolation and the attention-sink phenomenon are among the most practically limiting issues for deploying LLMs on long documents, and a drop-in attention replacement that addresses both without changing the O(L²) paradigm or requiring positional-encoding hacks would be broadly useful. However, all evidence is confined to 45M–355M parameter models trained on ~10B tokens at 1024 context, the current PyTorch implementation is substantially slower and more memory-hungry than standard attention, and no fused kernel exists yet. The symbolic-regression recovery of an inverse-square force law is a compelling but single-scale demonstration; whether the inductive-bias claim generalises to larger, deeper models or to other physical domains remains open. Kernel-level optimisation and validation at production scale are the critical next steps before this can be considered a practical replacement.
