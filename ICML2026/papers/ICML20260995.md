## Problem

Standard text-to-image pipelines apply alignment post-hoc via a single reward model (RLHF or reward fine-tuning), which discards low-quality training data, collapses diversity, and locks reward trade-offs at training time. Each successive stage (pretraining → finetuning → RLHF) further contracts the learned distribution, leaving users with no inference-time control over quality dimensions. The paper asks whether multiple complementary rewards can be integrated directly into pretraining to eliminate these losses.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a text prompt and two binned reward target vectors (positive s⁺ and negative s⁻), each an N-dimensional vector over 7 reward dimensions (aesthetics, HPSv2, ImageReward, PickScore, VQAScore, JINA CLIP, SciScore) with B discrete bins per dimension. The model is a 0.36B-parameter flow-matching transformer operating at 256×256 resolution, producing a single RGB image per forward pass. The reward vectors are the key controllable input: users can set per-dimension targets to navigate trade-offs (e.g., lowering the aesthetic target to 0.625 while keeping others at 1.0 to maximize compositional accuracy).

## Method

MIRO augments a 16M-image pretraining set (CC12M + LAION Aesthetics 6+) by scoring every image with 7 reward models and binning each score into B quantile bins, producing a per-sample reward vector ŝ. During training, the binned reward vector is encoded via sinusoidal embeddings, projected to the text-token dimension, and concatenated with the text token sequence so the flow-matching network v_θ(x_t, c, ŝ) learns p(x|c,s) over the full reward spectrum without filtering. At inference, a multi-reward extension of classifier-free guidance computes v̂ = (1+ω)v(x_t,c,s⁺) − ωv(x_t,c,s⁻), which the paper proves (Theorem 2.1) corresponds to sampling from a reward-tilted distribution p_ω(x|c) ∝ p(x|c,s⁺)·[p(s⁺|x,c)/p(s⁻|x,c)]^ω, steering generation toward the Pareto frontier of all rewards simultaneously. This single-stage supervised training eliminates separate RLHF or reward fine-tuning phases, converges up to 19× faster than the unconditioned baseline, and enables per-reward controllability at inference without retraining.

## Results

On GenEval, MIRO with synthetic captions (0.36B params, 4.16 TFLOPs) achieves an overall score of 68, surpassing FLUX-dev (12B, 1540 TFLOPs) at 67 with 370× less inference compute. The custom-target variant MIRO† reaches 75 overall, with particularly strong gains on Position (58 vs. FLUX-dev's 20) and Color Attribution (58 vs. 47). On PartiPrompts, MIRO with 128-sample inference scaling (532 TFLOPs) achieves state-of-the-art scores of 6.81 (Aesthetic), 1.61 (ImageReward), 0.32 (HPSv2), and 0.229 (PickAScore), all exceeding FLUX-dev while using 3× less inference compute. MIRO also converges up to 19× faster than its baseline on AestheticScore during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRO (0.36B) outperforms FLUX-dev (12B) on GenEval overall (68 vs. 67) and by large margins on Position (46 vs. 20, +130%) and Color Attribution (52 vs. 47, +11%), while using 370× less inference compute (4.16 vs. 1540 TFLOPs). On PartiPrompts without scaling, FLUX-dev retains a slight edge on Aesthetic (6.56 vs. 6.28) and ImageReward (1.19 vs. 1.11), but MIRO with 128-sample inference scaling reverses all four metrics (Aesthetic 6.81, ImageReward 1.61, HPSv2 0.32, PickAScore 0.229) while still using 3× less compute than FLUX. Against SD3-medium (2B), MIRO wins on GenEval overall (68 vs. 62) and Position (46 vs. 34) but trails on Aesthetic (6.28 vs. 6.18 is a win, but ImageReward 1.11 vs. 1.15 is a loss). Compared to Sana-1.6B, MIRO leads on GenEval (68 vs. 66) and Position (46 vs. 18) but Sana edges ahead on Aesthetic (6.36 vs. 6.28) and ImageReward (1.23 vs. 1.11). The single-reward CAD-like models (0.36B) all underperform MIRO on GenEval overall (33–58 vs. 57–68), with the Aesthetic-only model collapsing to 33, confirming that multi-reward conditioning prevents the reward hacking seen in single-objective optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRO extends coherence-aware training (Dufour et al., 2024), which conditions a diffusion model on a single text-image alignment score, to a multi-reward setting where seven complementary quality dimensions (aesthetics, human preference, VQA, CLIP, scientific accuracy) are jointly conditioned during pretraining. The generative backbone is flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) with a transformer architecture, and the inference mechanism is a multi-reward extension of classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022). Conceptually, MIRO applies the 'upside-down RL' paradigm—conditioning on desired returns rather than optimizing them via policy gradients—to T2I alignment, positioning itself as a supervised pretraining alternative to RL-based fine-tuning (DDPO, DPOK, DanceGRPO) and gradient-based reward backpropagation (DRaFT, AlignProp).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By integrating multi-reward alignment directly into pretraining, MIRO eliminates the separate RLHF/DPO stage that dominates modern T2I pipelines, simplifying training and enabling user-controllable reward trade-offs at inference without retraining. The practical efficiency gains are substantial: a 0.36B model with 4.16 TFLOPs inference matches or exceeds FLUX-dev (12B, 1540 TFLOPs) on GenEval and PartiPrompts, and multi-reward conditioning accelerates convergence up to 19× over unconditional pretraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 1B+ parameter models and billion-image datasets, how it interacts with other alignment techniques (e.g., DPO on top of MIRO), and whether the seven-reward suite generalizes to new quality dimensions without retraining. The paper's 256px resolution and 16M-image scale leave the method's behavior at production scale untested.
