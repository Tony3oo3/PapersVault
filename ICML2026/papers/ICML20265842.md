## Problem

Existing medical time series classifiers struggle to simultaneously capture multi-scale local morphology, model long-range temporal dependencies, and handle nonstationarities such as baseline drift, while also failing to adaptively learn latent cross-channel interactions without relying on predefined anatomical graphs. Standard state space models, though efficient, lack the inductive biases needed for these domain-specific challenges and process channels independently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multichannel physiological recordings (T×C) segmented into 1-second windows of 250–256 timesteps, covering EEG (16–33 channels) and ECG (12–15 channels) signals. It outputs a K-class probability distribution over disease categories (e.g., Alzheimer's vs. healthy, myocardial infarction vs. normal) via global mean pooling followed by a linear softmax head. Evaluation is conducted under both subject-dependent (sample-level split) and subject-independent (subject-level split) protocols, with the latter being the primary clinical generalization metric.

## Method

MedMamba stacks L encoder layers, each containing a Tri-Branch Differential State Space Encoder (TDSSE) and a Spatial Graph Mamba (SGM) module, preceded by a Multi-Scale Convolutional Embedding (MCE) with depthwise 1D convolutions at kernel sizes {3,5,7}. TDSSE processes the input in three parallel views—raw bidirectional Mamba, first-order temporal difference (suppressing slow drift), and learnable complex FFT spectral modulation—then fuses them via two-level gated selection (local element-wise gates plus global softmax view weights). SGM learns a per-sample directed adjacency matrix through asymmetric bilinear projections of temporally-pooled channel embeddings, regularized by L1 sparsity and a trace-exponential acyclicity penalty, then performs spatial fusion by combining graph diffusion along the normalized transition matrix with a channel-wise bidirectional SSM pathway. The full model is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss plus the graph structure regularizers, achieving linear complexity in sequence length.

## Results

Under the subject-independent (SI) protocol on five medical datasets, MedMamba achieves 20 top-1 and 3 top-2 results out of 30 metric-dataset entries, and ranks first in F1 score across all five datasets. Headline accuracies include 57.56% on ADFTD (3-class EEG), 84.48% on APAVA (2-class EEG), 89.36% on PTB (2-class ECG), 73.86% on PTB-XL (5-class ECG), and 92.13% on TDBRAIN (2-class EEG). The largest margins over the strongest baseline (MedGNN) appear on ADFTD (+1.44% Acc) and PTB (+4.83% Acc), while on PTB-XL the gap is minimal (−0.01% Acc). Ablations confirm that removing the tri-branch encoder causes the largest single-component drop (up to 5.5% F1 on PTB), followed by the spatial graph module (up to 4.8% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedMamba outperforms all nine baselines on F1 score across all five datasets, with the largest gaps over Transformer-based methods (iTransformer, MedFormer, PatchTST) and the LLM-based GPT4TS. Against the strongest graph-based competitor MedGNN, MedMamba wins on 4 of 5 datasets by clear margins (e.g., +4.83% Acc on PTB, +1.44% Acc on ADFTD) but is essentially tied on PTB-XL (73.86 vs 73.87 Acc) and slightly trails on AUROC for PTB (90.13 vs 93.31) and TDBRAIN (95.62 vs 96.74). KEMed, the other MedTS-specific baseline, is consistently outperformed by 3–12 points in accuracy depending on the dataset. FourierGNN and iTransformer are the weakest baselines, trailing MedMamba by 15–25 points in accuracy on most datasets, highlighting the difficulty of pure spectral or attention-based approaches on nonstationary multichannel medical signals. The robustness experiments further show MedMamba degrades more gracefully than MedGNN under injected baseline drift and missing channels, reinforcing the advantage of the tri-branch encoder and adaptive graph learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedMamba builds directly on the selective state space model lineage (S4 → S5 → Mamba) as its temporal backbone, but departs from standard SSMs by injecting three domain-specific inductive biases: multi-scale convolutional embeddings for local morphology, a tri-branch differential/frequency encoder for nonstationarity, and a sample-conditioned directed acyclic graph module for cross-channel interactions. It positions itself against Transformer-based medical time series methods (MedFormer, iTransformer, PatchTST) that suffer quadratic complexity, and against graph-based methods (MedGNN, TodyNet, FourierGNN) that rely on predefined or fully-connected adjacency structures. The key conceptual departure is coupling the linear-complexity SSM with an adaptive, sparsity- and acyclicity-regularized graph learned per sample, rather than using a fixed anatomical topology or a dense learned graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in clinical deployment: robustness to baseline drift and cross-subject generalization under subject-independent evaluation, which is the protocol that actually mirrors real-world use. The tri-branch design (raw/differential/frequency) and the DAG-regularized adaptive graph are modular components that could be transplanted into other SSM-based architectures for nonstationary multivariate signals. Open questions include whether the DAG constraint provides genuine interpretability benefits beyond regularization, how the method scales to very high channel counts (e.g., 256-channel EEG), and whether the gains persist on modalities beyond EEG/ECG (e.g., fMRI, wearable biosensors). The evaluation is limited to five datasets and two signal types, so broader generalizability remains untested.
