## Problem

Flow matching editors based on MMDiT architectures (e.g., FLUX.1 Kontext) rely on globally conditioned velocity fields and joint attention over all text, latent, and context tokens, which causes semantic entanglement and attribute leakage when multiple spatially disjoint edits must be applied simultaneously. Existing multi-instance editing methods either operate within discrete diffusion denoising steps, require costly multi-turn inference, or fail to maintain coherence as the number of concurrent instructions grows, leaving a gap for efficient single-pass multi-instance editing in the flow matching regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image, N instance-specific text editing prompts (e.g., 'Change SRC to TGT' for infographics or descriptive edits for natural images), and N bounding boxes localizing each edit region, and produces a single edited image in one ODE integration pass. Bounding boxes may be imprecise, overlapping, or nested, and the method handles up to 285 concurrent edits per image on the InfoEdit benchmark. For infographics, text regions are typically small (average area 0.62% of the image), requiring the model to modify high-frequency localized details while preserving the surrounding layout and typography.

## Method

The core contribution is Instance-Disentangled Attention (IDAttn), which partitions the joint attention matrix in MMDiT blocks via additive binary masks (M ∈ {0, −∞}) that enforce structured conditional dependencies: instance-specific prompt, latent, and context tokens can only attend to each other in the disentanglement regime (M^dis), while global and background tokens retain broader access. Two masking regimes are scheduled across layers—M^har (harmonization) in early and late layers to preserve global coherence, and M^dis (disentanglement) in middle layers to enforce instance isolation—motivated by the observation that middle Transformer layers encode semantic bindings. A complementary efficient multi-prompt encoding strategy encodes each instance prompt independently with T5 and concatenates variable-length embeddings, avoiding the linear cost of padding to fixed length while preventing cross-instance semantic mixing in the text encoder. The entire mechanism operates at inference time on FLUX.1 Kontext without architectural retraining, with an optional LoRA (rank 32) fine-tuning step on the Crello Edit paired subset for domain-specific typography preservation.

## Results

On the novel InfoEdit benchmark (up to 285 edits per image), the proposed method achieves a background MAE of 3.41 and MSE of 15.33, compared to 30.20 and 64.26 for the end-to-end FLUX baseline, while also improving CER from 0.77 to 0.64 and Attempt Rate from 39.94% to 52.61%. With optional LoRA fine-tuning (Ours+ft), CER drops further to 0.56 and AR% rises to 80.90. On LoMOE-Bench (2–7 edits), the method achieves the best HPS (0.574), SSIM_B (0.919), and LPIPS_B (0.099) among all FLUX-based variants, while remaining competitive with diffusion-based methods like LoMOE and LayerEdit on CLIP-based scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the end-to-end FLUX baseline, the proposed method dramatically reduces background artifacts on InfoEdit (MAE_B 3.41 vs. 30.20; MSE_B 15.33 vs. 64.26) while also improving text rendering accuracy (CER 0.64 vs. 0.77) and FID (2.41 vs. 4.36), indicating that FLUX simply ignores most editing instructions in single-pass mode. Compared to the multi-turn FLUX μT variant, the proposed method avoids the severe quality degradation that accumulates over sequential edits (FID 2.41 vs. 65.73 on InfoEdit) while maintaining competitive AR%. Against the stitching baselines (FLUX st., Calligrapher st.), the method achieves substantially lower background error (MAE_B 3.41 vs. 29.46 and 39.09) without the per-box inpainting pipeline, though stitching methods report higher AR% by design since each box is edited independently. On LoMOE-Bench, the method trades a small AR% penalty (89.06% vs. 98.96% for LoMOE and 100% for LayerEdit) for significantly better perceptual quality (HPS 0.574 vs. 0.546 and 0.186) and background preservation (SSIM_B 0.919 vs. 0.834 and 0.864), while LoMOE retains a slight edge on Target CLIP (26.00 vs. 25.60) and Structural Distance (0.067 vs. 0.073).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of inference-time attention manipulation for controlled generation (Prompt-to-Prompt, Attend-and-Excite, BoxDiff) from diffusion U-Nets to the flow matching / MMDiT architecture used by FLUX.1 Kontext. While prior multi-instance editing methods (LoMOE, LayerEdit, MDE-Edit) operate within discrete denoising steps of diffusion models, this work identifies that the globally conditioned velocity field and joint attention in MMDiT are the root cause of attribute leakage in flow-based editors, and addresses it architecturally via a two-regime attention mask (disentanglement in middle layers, harmonization in early/late layers). The efficient multi-prompt encoding strategy departs from both single-prompt masking and fixed-length multi-prompt padding by encoding variable-length sub-prompts independently, keeping sequence length proportional to semantic content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-pass formulation offers a clear computational advantage over multi-turn or stitching pipelines as the number of concurrent edits grows, and the method requires no retraining (only optional LoRA fine-tuning), making it broadly applicable to any MMDiT-based flow model. The infographic editing benchmark (up to 285 edits per image, with small text regions averaging 0.62% of image area) fills a genuine gap, as existing multi-instance benchmarks top out at 2–7 edits on natural images. A key open question is end-to-end localization: the method still requires externally provided bounding boxes, and the authors note that integrating promptable detection/OCR with the editing pipeline is a natural but unresolved direction. The finding that FLUX end-to-end ignores 60% of editing instructions on InfoEdit underscores how poorly current SOTA flow editors scale with edit count.
