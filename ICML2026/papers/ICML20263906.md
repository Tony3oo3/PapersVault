## Problem

Standard epistemic uncertainty measures in neural networks equate uncertainty with predictive variability, which vanishes once the function is identified. However, in overparametrized models the Fisher information is singular, so the posterior never collapses to a point mass in parameter space even as n→∞, and this persistent parameter uncertainty is invisible to function-space measures. The paper asks how to characterize and quantify this residual uncertainty arising from permutation symmetries and redundant representations in overparametrized architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular data (x, y) with x ∈ R^p generated from a one-hidden-layer ReLU network with M* true neurons, and fits a wider model with M ≥ M* neurons under L2 regularization (equivalently, an isotropic Gaussian prior with variance (2λ)^{-1}). The output is the full posterior over weights, which in the overparametrized case is supported on a union of splitting manifolds (products of simplices) rather than isolated points, with splitting coefficients following Dirichlet(α,…,α) distributions where α=(p+1)/2. A key structural detail is that the posterior on each manifold is defined as a tube-induced limit (conditioning on shrinking ε-tubes around zero-Lebesgue-measure manifolds), and epistemic uncertainty is quantified both as predictive variance (which vanishes) and as trace of weight covariance (which persists indefinitely).

## Method

The paper analyzes one-hidden-layer ReLU networks f_w(x) = w_2^T φ(W_1 x) under L2 regularization, which breaks rescaling symmetry and leaves only permutation invariance. For correctly specified models (M=M*), the posterior contracts to a uniform mixture of Dirac measures over all M! permutations of the true weights, yielding zero predictive variance but nonzero weight covariance. For overparametrized models (M>M*), the core contribution is characterizing the splitting manifold structure: each true neuron's contribution is redistributed across a group of redundant neurons via coefficients c_m on a simplex, parameterized by surjective assignment maps ζ:[M]→[M*], and the induced posterior on each manifold follows a Dirichlet(α,…,α) law with α=(p+1)/2, derived from the polar Jacobian of the Gaussian prior restricted to the manifold. The paper further proves that single-chain MCMC samplers satisfying permutation equivariance and Lipschitz continuity remain confined to a single permutation chamber almost surely, and validates all theoretical predictions (Dirichlet marginals, moment scaling, chamber confinement) empirically using SGLD and NUTS with deep-ensemble initialization.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: for overparametrized 2-layer ReLU networks (M > M*), the posterior splitting coefficients follow a symmetric Dirichlet distribution Dir(α,…,α) with α=(p+1)/2, and individual neuron weights have posterior mean Θ(M^{-1/2}) and covariance Θ(M^{-1}) under balanced allocation. Empirically, Bayesian deep ensembles (BDEs) outperform MAP-based deep ensembles (DEs) on RMSE for small sample sizes (e.g., 0.220 vs. 0.281 at n=64, M=5), with both converging to ~0.204 RMSE at n=2048. The trace of the posterior weight covariance remains non-zero (due to permutation symmetry) even as predictive variance vanishes, and empirical splitting-coefficient marginals closely match the theoretical Beta(α,(k−1)α) distribution across all tested (n,M) combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The main empirical comparison is between MAP-based deep ensembles (DEs) and posterior-sampling Bayesian deep ensembles (BDEs). BDEs consistently achieve lower RMSE and higher LPPD for small sample sizes (n ≤ 256), where data-related epistemic uncertainty is still significant — e.g., at n=64, M=5, BDEs reduce RMSE from 0.281 to 0.220 and improve LPPD from −629 to +191. For large n (≥1024) and moderate widths (M ≤ 20), both methods converge to nearly identical performance (~0.203–0.205 RMSE), confirming that the additional uncertainty captured by BDEs is purely data-driven and vanishes asymptotically. In the overparametrized regime (M=40) with small n, DEs can slightly outperform BDEs (0.258 vs. 0.299 RMSE at n=64), likely because posterior sampling is harder in high-dimensional non-identifiable spaces. The paper also validates its theoretical predictions against empirical posterior samples: splitting coefficients match the predicted Dirichlet/Beta marginals (Figure 5), and first/second moments of scalar projections align with the closed-form expressions from Theorem 1 (Figure 6). MCMC chains (both NUTS and SGLD) remain confined to a single permutation chamber with expected switch rate below 1, corroborating the theoretical confinement result (Corollary 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the parameter identifiability theory for ReLU networks (Bona-Pellissier et al., 2023) and the splitting-manifold characterization of overparametrized representations (Kobialka et al., 2026), combining them with topological invariance results for MCMC dynamics (Yang et al., 2025) to derive the exact posterior geometry of overparametrized two-layer ReLU networks. It departs from the standard information-theoretic uncertainty decomposition—which is by construction blind to non-identifiable parameter variation—by proposing a variance-based (trace-of-covariance) measure that captures the residual parameter uncertainty induced by permutation symmetries and continuous splitting manifolds. The paper explicitly positions non-identifiability of excessive weights in overparametrized models as an overlooked source of epistemic uncertainty, distinct from the well-studied permutation and rescaling symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the standard Bernstein-von Mises intuition that epistemic uncertainty vanishes in the infinite-data limit, showing that in overparametrized networks the posterior concentrates on non-identifiability manifolds (Dirichlet-distributed splitting coefficients on simplices) rather than point masses, with persistent parameter-space uncertainty even when the function is fully identified. Practical implications include the finding that single-chain MCMC samplers (including NUTS) remain confined to a single permutation chamber, which affects convergence diagnostics and downstream tasks like continual learning that operate on weights. Key limitations include restriction to two-layer ReLU networks with Gaussian priors; open questions involve deeper architectures, non-Gaussian priors, and the interplay between non-identifiability uncertainty and stochastic optimization dynamics.
