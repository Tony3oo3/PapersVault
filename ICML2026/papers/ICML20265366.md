## Problem

Existing KV-cache eviction policies for long-context LLM inference rely on hand-crafted heuristics (local attention windows, key norms, corpus-level query statistics) that fail to capture the input-dependent distribution of token importance, and they permanently discard evicted tokens, causing irreversible information loss that degrades holistic long-context reasoning. IndexMem addresses both gaps by learning a data-driven importance scorer and preserving evicted information in a compact, online-updated latent state.

## Input / Output

The method consumes backbone hidden states X ∈ R^{L×d_model} and pre-RoPE multi-head query states Q ∈ R^{H×L×d_head} at each Transformer layer, producing three outputs: a compressed KV cache retaining the top-(1−r) fraction of tokens under a fixed budget B_max, a per-layer latent memory state (M ∈ R^{d_mem×d_model}, b ∈ R^{d_mem}) updated online at each eviction step, and an augmented attention output o = o_attn + g(q)·m(q) where the gated residual m(q) compensates for evicted-token contributions. The indexer uses an MQA-style design with H_index = H/4 heads and d_index = d_head/8, while the memory module is shared across all attention heads within a layer and uses d_mem = d_model/8.

## Method

IndexMem combines two components. First, a lightweight MQA-style indexer computes per-token importance via gated query-key similarity (with RMSNorm on both q and k, a learnable per-head gate α, and max aggregation over a query set), trained by streaming KL distillation from the frozen backbone's attention logits—avoiding O(L²) materialization by maintaining O(L) running-max vectors in FlashAttention-style chunks. This enables pre-eviction: the indexer predicts which KV entries to retain before the prefill pass, so only selected KV states are computed and cached. Second, a latent memory module with slow weights (a projection Linear_θ and a gate g, trained by gradient) and fast weights (state matrix M and stabilizer b, updated online via outer-product accumulation with exponential decay λ) compresses evicted tokens into a fixed-size state. Rather than injecting latent tokens into softmax (which causes sink collapse), the memory readout m(q) = Linear_θ(q)ᵀM / (Linear_θ(q)⊙2)ᵀb is added as a gated residual to the attention output, providing a safe fallback (g(q)=0) while compensating for irreversibly lost information. Training is two-stage: first the indexer alone via KL distillation, then jointly with the memory module via an MSE objective matching the full attention output to the compressed-cache output plus memory residual.

## Results

On RULER-16K at 90% KV eviction, IndexMem achieves 56.0 on Qwen3-8B, 52.9 on Mistral-7B, and 56.0 on Llama3.1-8B, outperforming the strongest heuristic baselines (SnapKV, PyramidKV) by 25–35 points. On LongBench (all-task average), IndexMem leads at every compression ratio, peaking at 55.26 at 50% compression versus 42.35 (PyramidKV) and 41.44 (SnapKV). Under decoding-time compression on AIME25 and Math500, IndexMem achieves the best accuracy at every KV budget (512–2048 tokens), with the gap most pronounced at the tightest budget of 512 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IndexMem consistently outperforms SnapKV and PyramidKV across all three backbones, with the gap widening sharply at aggressive compression: on Mistral-7B RULER-16K at 90% eviction, IndexMem scores 52.9 versus 21.3 (SnapKV) and 18.0 (PyramidKV), a 32–35 point margin. Against Expected Attention, IndexMem dominates on Mistral-7B (52.9 vs. 15.4 at 90% on RULER-16K) and Llama3.1-8B (56.0 vs. 25.5), though the two are nearly tied at mild compression (≤25%). TOVA is the closest competitor: it slightly edges IndexMem on Llama3.1-8B RULER-16K at 90% (59.2 vs. 56.0) but is clearly beaten on Qwen3-8B (56.0 vs. 52.4) and Mistral-7B (52.9 vs. 47.7) at the same setting, and IndexMem leads TOVA at all moderate compression levels (≤50%) across models. KeyDiff is competitive at low compression on Qwen3-8B but degrades faster under aggressive eviction (37.9 vs. 46.2 at 90% on RULER-4K). On LongBench all-task averages (Table 6), IndexMem leads every baseline at every compression ratio, with the largest gap at 90% (36.51 vs. 32.56 for TOVA, the next-best). In the appendix, IndexMem also outperforms AdaKV (76.1 vs. 22.8 at 4K-90% on Mistral-7B) and xKV/Locret on information-dense LongBench tasks at 75% compression, though Locret is stronger on the single-span retrieval task passage_retrieval_en.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the lightning indexer concept from DeepSeek Sparse Attention (Liu et al., 2025), repurposing a lightweight MQA-style scoring module to predict KV-token importance for eviction rather than for sparse attention routing. It departs from the dominant training-free eviction paradigm (SnapKV, KeyDiff, TOVA, Expected Attention, PyramidKV) by introducing a learnable indexer trained via streaming KL distillation from backbone attention, and further extends the fast-weight memory lineage (Titans, PKM, TTT-E2E) by coupling an online-updated latent state as a residual compensation for evicted tokens. The key conceptual departure is treating eviction and forgetting as a joint problem: prior methods either select tokens heuristically or offload them to CPU, but none combine learned importance estimation with an in-model, fixed-size memory that recovers lost attention contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is the dominant bottleneck for long-context inference, and this paper offers a practical, low-overhead solution (≈20M extra parameters) that works as a frozen-backbone add-on across Qwen, Mistral, and Llama families. The finding that the latent memory module is orthogonal to the eviction policy (it improves SnapKV as well) suggests it could be adopted as a drop-in component in existing inference stacks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and longer contexts, whether end-to-end training of eviction-and-memory into the backbone (rather than as a frozen add-on) yields further gains, and how the method behaves under long chain-of-thought generation where the KV cache grows during decoding rather than just prefill.
