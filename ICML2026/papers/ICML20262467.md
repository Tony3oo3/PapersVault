## Problem

High-resolution image perception in MLLMs faces a fundamental trade-off: visual expert-assisted search is efficient but creates irreversible blind spots when the expert fails to localize tiny or occluded objects, while scan-based search guarantees coverage but wastes computation on information-sparse backgrounds and fractures object semantics through rigid grid partitioning. No existing method bridges the efficiency of expert guidance with the robustness of exhaustive search while preserving semantic integrity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262467/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a high-resolution RGB image (evaluated up to 8K resolution, with discussion of 16K+) and a natural-language text query, and produces a free-form text response. It operates entirely at inference time as a plug-and-play module on top of any pre-trained MLLM (tested with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-OV-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, and models up to Qwen3-VL-32B), requiring no retraining or architectural modification. The adaptive search depth is conditioned on the number of target objects in the query (D=2 for single-object, D=3 for multi-object).

## Method

CVSearch implements a training-free cognitive Assess-then-Search workflow that hierarchically escalates from global assessment to expert-assisted search (SAM 3) to scene-aware scanning, treating expert failure as a signal to activate scanning rather than a dead-end. Within the scanning phase, Semantic Guided Adaptive Patching (SGAP) repurposes the visual expert's dense features (even when localization fails) to over-segment via SLIC superpixels and cluster into semantically coherent regions using agglomerative clustering constrained by a region adjacency graph, with the optimal number of clusters k* determined by minimizing a cost function balancing spatial overlap and silhouette score. A Visual Complexity score (based on feature dispersion via cosine similarity) prunes background nodes from the resulting adaptive image tree. Dynamic Bottom-Up Search then traverses the tree from leaf to root, ranking nodes by a priority combining visual complexity, target existence confidence, and aggregated child scores, with an adaptive sufficiency threshold that decays from τ_q to a lower bound to permit valid but less confident predictions on harder samples.

## Results

CVSearch achieves state-of-the-art results across all high-resolution benchmarks when integrated with open-source MLLMs. With InternVL2.5-8B, it reaches 77.6 overall on HR-Bench 8K and 77.0 on HR-Bench 4K, surpassing the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B (71.6 and 74.8 respectively) and closed-source GPT-4o (55.5 on HR-8K). On V* Bench, LLaVA-OV-7B + CVSearch scores 91.6 overall (95.7 Attribute, 85.5 Spatial), outperforming all prior training-free and RL-based methods. On general benchmarks, CVSearch improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +4.4 on MME-RealWorld-Lite and +3.7 on TreeBench, with a notable +11.5 gain on FineRS-4K OVQA for LLaVA-OV-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVSearch consistently outperforms all visual search baselines across every benchmark. Against expert-assisted methods, it surpasses SEAL (75.4) and DyFo (81.2) by 16.2 and 10.4 points respectively on V* Bench with LLaVA-OV-7B. Against scan-based methods, it beats ZoomEye (90.6) and RAP (79.6) on V* Bench while also achieving substantially higher throughput (3.77 vs 1.29 samples/min on HR-4K, ~3× faster). On HR-Bench 8K, InternVL2.5-8B + CVSearch (77.6) exceeds the 38B InternVL3 (67.0) and the 32B Qwen2.5-VL (71.6) by 10.6 and 6.0 points, demonstrating that the search mechanism compensates for model scale. Compared to RL-based methods (DeepEyes 90.1, Pixel-Reasoner 80.6 on V*), CVSearch achieves higher accuracy (91.6) without any training or curated reasoning trajectories. On general benchmarks, CVSearch improves all three baselines, with the largest gain on FineRS-4K OVQA (+11.5 for LLaVA-OV-7B), confirming robustness on tiny-object perception in real-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CVSearch sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: visual search paradigms for MLLMs (expert-assisted methods like SEAL and DyFo, and scan-based methods like ZoomEye and RAP) and cognitive theories of human visual search (Wolfe's selective/non-selective pathway framework). It departs from prior work by unifying these two paradigms through a failure-aware switching mechanism, and by repurposing SAM 3's dense visual features—previously used only for region proposals—as the semantic prior for adaptive image patching (SGAP). The bottom-up search strategy also inverts the top-down tree traversal used by ZoomEye and RAP, and the entire framework is training-free, contrasting with RL-based zooming policies (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner) that require curated trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, model-agnostic plug-in, CVSearch offers a practical path to HR perception without retraining or architectural modification, and its ~3× throughput advantage over scan-based baselines while matching or exceeding their accuracy makes it attractive for deployment. However, the iterative search still incurs meaningful latency over single-pass inference, and the framework's dependence on SAM 3 as the sole visual expert leaves open how well it generalizes to other segmentation or detection backbones. For ultra-high-resolution imagery (16K+), the authors note that deeper tree levels become necessary, and whether the SGAP pruning mechanism scales gracefully to that regime remains untested.
