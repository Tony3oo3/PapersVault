## Problem

SAE-based image editing methods like SAEdit rely on heuristic feature selection (e.g., PCA over top-k activated features) and manual steering-strength tuning, which leads to suboptimal trade-offs among attribute alignment, content consistency, and image quality. There is no principled, automatic procedure for identifying which sparse SAE features to steer and how strongly to apply them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262941/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base text prompt and a target attribute as input, and produces a single edited RGB image from a frozen diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev or SD 3.5 Large). A set of 50–150 contrastive prompt pairs (with/without the target attribute) is used offline to learn one sparse steering vector per attribute, which is then reused at inference across arbitrary base prompts. At inference, the steering vector is added to token-level SAE latents at each denoising step via a step-dependent exponential schedule, and the steered embeddings condition the frozen DiT backbone.

## Method

SRLS exploits the affine structure of the SAE decoder (D(s) = W_d s + b_d) to reduce the steering-vector discovery problem to a convex Lasso objective that matches the mean reconstruction-space difference across contrastive pairs while enforcing sparsity. A λ-path search via ISTA automatically identifies a compact feature support of size k_min to k_max, and a support-restricted ridge refit then debiases the Lasso-shrunk coefficients on that fixed support. For the remaining inference-time hyperparameter (steering strength γ), SRLS replaces manual tuning with a fixed-budget Bayesian optimization procedure using a Gaussian-process surrogate and expected-improvement acquisition over a one-dimensional black-box reward (harmonic mean of LLM-judged alignment, consistency, and quality). The result is a training-free pipeline that selects both which SAE features to steer and how strongly, without per-prompt manual intervention.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev with all-token steering and Bayesian-optimization strength selection, SRLS achieves a harmonic mean (HM) of 4.24 (out of 5) on LLM-based evaluation, surpassing SAEdit (4.18) and the All-features baseline (3.93). SRLS uses only 307 SAE features on average versus 7,859 for SAEdit and 65,536 for All, while achieving the highest Pass(all) rate of 57.8% (requiring Consistency ≥ 4, Quality ≥ 4, and Alignment ≥ 4 simultaneously). On objective metrics, SRLS consistently outperforms SAEdit and All on CLIP alignment and TOPIQ/MUSIQ quality across the full strength range, with comparable or better LPIPS/DISTS consistency at moderate strengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAEdit (the closest SAE-based competitor), SRLS wins on every reported metric: HM 4.24 vs 4.18, Alignment 4.04 vs 3.96, Consistency 4.33 vs 4.29, Pass(all) 57.8% vs 53.5%, and Pass(edit) 69.3% vs 66.5%, while using roughly 25× fewer features (307 vs 7,859). Against the All-features baseline, SRLS dominates decisively (HM 4.24 vs 3.93, Alignment 4.04 vs 3.51, Pass(all) 57.8% vs 40.8%), confirming that sparse feature selection is the primary driver of the improvement. SEGA and Pix2Pix-Zero are included as qualitative baselines (Figure 2) but are not scored in the main quantitative tables; visually, SRLS preserves structure and injects the target attribute more faithfully than both. On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large (Appendix E), SRLS maintains its lead on alignment metrics over SAEdit while remaining competitive on quality and consistency, confirming cross-architecture generality. The ablation (Table 4) attributes most of the gain to Lasso sparse support discovery (HM 3.08 → 3.71), with ridge refitting adding a further +0.06.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAEdit (Kamenetsky et al., 2025), which introduced SAE-based token-level steering for training-free image editing but relied on heuristic PCA-based feature selection and manual steering-strength tuning. SRLS departs from this by recasting feature selection as a convex Lasso sparse-recovery problem that exploits the affine structure of SAE decoders, and by replacing manual strength tuning with fixed-budget Bayesian optimization. It also situates itself within the broader training-free editing literature (SDEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, SEGA, Pix2Pix-Zero) and the SAE interpretability line (Concept Steerers), positioning sparse optimization as a principled alternative to the ad-hoc selection strategies used in prior SAE-steering methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By automating both feature selection and strength tuning, SRLS removes the two most labor-intensive and error-prone steps in SAE-based editing, making the approach more reproducible and practical for arbitrary attributes without per-concept manual tuning. The finding that ~300 features suffice (versus thousands for SAEdit or all 65k features) suggests that SAE-based editing can be both compact and interpretable. Open questions remain around the dependence on SAE expressive capacity for poorly captured attributes, the limitation of a single steering direction for complex or multi-faceted concepts, and whether more expressive autoencoder architectures or multi-directional steering could further improve quality and stability.
