## Problem

Standard KL divergence in language model distillation is dominated by the teacher's top-K highest-probability tokens, suppressing the informative tail of the output distribution. This mode dominance is exacerbated in pretraining distillation on generic corpora, where the teacher's most probable token frequently differs from the ground-truth next token (39–46% mismatch rate), creating conflicting signals that further suppress tail learning. The paper asks whether a divergence that explicitly decouples and amplifies the tail contribution can improve distilled student quality without increasing computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full vocabulary-level probability distributions of both teacher and student at each token position, along with a text corpus (Regmix for pretraining, OpenWebMath for domain-specific continual learning, or ORCAMEL instruction-response pairs for supervised distillation). It requires no student-side generation, no teacher decoding, and no data-selection pass, so its computational profile matches vanilla KD exactly. The output is a distilled student LM (causal decoder) trained from scratch or via continued pretraining, with a single tunable hyperparameter β (typically 2) and a top-K cutoff (typically 5–10).

## Method

TAD splits the standard KL divergence into two components: D_KL1 over the teacher's top-K probabilities (plus a (K+1)th residual-mass term) and D_KL2 over the normalized tail probabilities (each tail probability divided by the total tail mass α_K^T). The tail term is scaled by a hyperparameter β and normalized by the sequence-mean tail mass, yielding the loss L_DIV = D_KL1 + [β / mean(α_K^T)] · α_K^T · D_KL2, which reduces to exact KL when β=1. Gradient analysis shows that for tail logits, TAD adds a corrective term proportional to (β(X)−1) that is active precisely when the student's top-K mass exceeds the teacher's, pushing probability mass into the tail until the two match; the fixed point remains p^S = p^T, identical to vanilla KD, but the learning trajectory allocates tail mass earlier. The method is rank-anchored (Top-K vs. tail) rather than label-anchored as in DKD, preserving the label-free nature of KL divergence and making it suitable for unlabeled pretraining and generative downstream tasks.

## Results

On the primary pretraining distillation benchmark (Qwen1.5-1.8B → 1.2B student, 2B tokens from Regmix), TAD (K=10, β=2) achieves 47.8 average accuracy across 8 few-shot tasks, outperforming MiniPLM (46.6) and Vanilla KD (45.6). For the larger-model distillation (Phi2-2.8B → 1.1B), TAD (K=5) reaches 50.3 average vs. Vanilla KD's 49.1 and MiniPLM's 48.8, while also achieving the lowest Full-ECE calibration error (1.29 vs. 1.45 and 1.62). In supervised mathematical distillation, TAD (K=1) on Llama2-7B achieves 56.0 GSM8K and 54.0 average across 8 math benchmarks, surpassing Llemma-7B (49.4 avg) at roughly 9× lower compute cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAD consistently outperforms Vanilla KD (forward KL) across all three teacher–student pairs in Table 4, with average gains of +1.2 to +1.6 points and the lowest Full-ECE calibration error in every setting. Against MiniPLM, TAD wins on harder reasoning benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA) while MiniPLM edges ahead on easier tasks (ARC-Easy, StoryCloze); in the symmetric relative-change metric, TAD's advantage over MiniPLM widens to +1.0 to +2.2 points. Reverse KL (RKL) is slightly better than forward KL but remains inferior to TAD and produces the worst calibration (F-ECE 1.77–1.99). Seq-KD and MiniLLM, which require student or teacher sequence generation, underperform even the no-distillation CLM baseline (e.g., 43.8 and 42.3 avg vs. 45.0 for CLM on the 1.2B student) and are too expensive to scale beyond ~1M tokens. In supervised mathematical distillation (Table 7), TAD (K=1) on Llama2-7B achieves 54.0 average across 8 math tasks, beating Llemma-7B (49.4), Orca2-7B (45.1), and WizardMath-7B (41.9) at roughly 9× lower GPU-hour cost, while for TinyLlama-1.1B TAD (42.5 avg) slightly edges out Rho-1 (41.5 avg) at 2.5× lower cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAD extends the standard KL-divergence distillation loss of Hinton et al. (2014) by decoupling the top-K teacher probabilities from the tail, drawing conceptual inspiration from Decoupled Knowledge Distillation (DKD; Zhao et al., 2022) but replacing DKD's label-anchored split with a rank-anchored, label-free Top-K vs. tail decomposition suited to unlabeled pretraining corpora. It also extends the pretraining-distillation-from-scratch paradigm of DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) to causal decoder models, addressing the additional challenge that the teacher's argmax token frequently disagrees with the ground-truth next token on generic substitute corpora. Unlike on-policy methods (MiniLLM, Seq-KD) that require student generation or teacher decoding, and unlike data-selection methods (MiniPLM) that add forward-pass overhead, TAD retains the computational profile of vanilla KL distillation while amplifying the informative tail of the teacher distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that competitive small language models for mathematical reasoning can be produced within a one-week, single-H100 budget, drastically lowering the barrier for academic labs and resource-constrained settings. The finding that a simple rank-based decoupling of KL divergence outperforms more elaborate data-selection (MiniPLM) and on-policy (MiniLLM) approaches at comparable or lower FLOPs suggests that the field may be over-investing in complex distillation pipelines when the core issue is mode dominance in the divergence itself. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-teacher distillation, whether the optimal K and β transfer across domains and model scales, and whether the 39–46% teacher-mode/ground-truth mismatch rate that motivates the method can be further exploited. A limitation is that all students are ~1B active parameters; scaling to larger students or to non-mathematical domains remains untested.
