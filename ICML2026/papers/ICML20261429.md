## Problem

Existing VAR-based super-resolution methods (e.g., VARSR) are limited to a single fixed upsampling factor because standard Residual Quantization does not guarantee that intermediate-scale residuals decode to valid images. Additionally, these methods require either very large transformer backbones (~1B parameters) or large-scale annotated datasets with positive/negative samples to achieve competitive quality, making them impractical for flexible multi-scale deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 128×128 low-resolution RGB image as input, which is bilinearly upsampled to 512×512 and encoded by the RQVAE encoder to produce 1024 conditioning tokens. A single forward pass of the 310M-parameter transformer produces three super-resolved outputs simultaneously at 128×128 (×1, denoising), 256×256 (×2), and 512×512 (×4), with each intermediate scale being a semantically valid image rather than a partial reconstruction.

## Method

The paper introduces Hierarchical Image Tokenization (HIT), which sequentially tokenizes downsampled versions of the image at each target scale, enforcing that higher scales reuse tokens from lower scales so that any prefix of the token sequence decodes to a valid image at the corresponding resolution. The RQVAE vocabulary and scale-specific decoders are finetuned on OpenImages to support this multi-scale quantization. A second contribution is a simplified DPO regularization loss that, using only the (LR, HR) training pair, penalizes the transformer when its output logits favor bilinearly-upsampled LR tokens over true HR tokens, eliminating the need for external reward models or annotated negative samples. The full model is a 310M-parameter GPT-2-style transformer (16 blocks) initialized from VAR-d16, trained with equal-weighted cross-entropy and DPO losses on standard SR datasets (DIV2K, DIV8K, Flickr2K, OST, FFHQ) with Real-ESRGAN degradations, achieving state-of-the-art results without external data or large backbones.

## Results

On the standard ×4 ISR benchmark (128→512), H-VAR (310M params) achieves the best LPIPS across all three test sets: 0.317 on DIV2K-Val, 0.256 on RealSR, and 0.259 on DRealSR. It also attains the best FID on DRealSR (145.1) and the best PSNR on DRealSR (28.73 dB) and RealSR (25.55 dB). Notably, H-VAR matches or exceeds the much larger VARSR (1B params) on perceptual metrics while running 3.7× faster (0.25 s vs 0.93 s inference), and it is the only method that produces semantically valid outputs at intermediate scales (×1, ×2) alongside the target ×4 scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest AR-based competitor VARSR (1B params, trained on proprietary large-scale data), H-VAR (310M) wins decisively on LPIPS across all three datasets (0.317 vs 0.326 on DIV2K-Val; 0.256 vs 0.350 on RealSR; 0.259 vs 0.354 on DRealSR) and on FID for DRealSR (145.1 vs 155.9), while VARSR retains a slight edge on PSNR for DIV2K-Val (23.91 vs 23.84) and MUSIQ for DIV2K-Val and RealSR. The fair same-size comparison VARSR-d16 (retrained with identical 310M backbone and standard data) is dominated by H-VAR on every metric and dataset, with the largest gaps in LPIPS (e.g., 0.256 vs 0.413 on RealSR) and FID (130.4 vs 231.1 on RealSR), confirming that the hierarchical tokenization acts as a strong inductive bias rather than merely a multi-scale feature. Among diffusion-based methods, H-VAR outperforms StableSR (919M) on LPIPS, PSNR, and MUSIQ across all sets, and beats ResShift (173M) on LPIPS and FID, though ResShift retains a PSNR advantage on RealSR (26.31 vs 25.55) and a marginal MUSIQ edge on DIV2K-Val (70.02 vs 69.40). Against GAN-based baselines (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR), H-VAR achieves substantially better LPIPS (0.256–0.317 vs 0.261–0.391) and FID (130–145 vs 132–156 on real datasets), while GAN methods retain a small PSNR advantage on RealSR and DRealSR. Overall, H-VAR delivers the best perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID) at a fraction of the parameter count and inference time of the strongest AR and diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) next-scale prediction paradigm from image generation to Image Super-Resolution, building directly on VAR (Tian et al., 2024) and its RQVAE tokenization, as well as the first VAR-based ISR attempt VARSR (Qu et al., 2025). It departs from prior VAR-based ISR in two key ways: (1) replacing the standard RQVAE with a Hierarchical Image Tokenization that guarantees semantically valid intermediate-scale decodings, and (2) introducing a DPO-based regularization term adapted from LLM alignment to AR image generation, eliminating the need for external reward models or large annotated datasets. The RQVAE is initialized from Switti (Voronov et al., 2025) and finetuned with a scale-specific decoder, while the transformer follows the GPT-2-style architecture used by VAR and VARSR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that hierarchical tokenization acts as a strong inductive bias—enabling a 310M-parameter model to match or exceed a 1B-parameter VARSR trained on proprietary data—is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment of AR-based ISR. Multi-scale output from a single forward pass (×1, ×2, ×4) opens the door to adaptive resolution selection at inference time, which is useful for applications like progressive streaming or variable-quality rendering. Open questions include whether the inductive bias advantage persists at higher resolutions (e.g., 1024×1024), whether the DPO regularization transfers to other AR vision tasks beyond ISR, and how the scale-partition tradeoff (fewer residual steps per scale) affects quality as the number of target scales grows.
