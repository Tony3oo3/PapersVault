## Problem

When Thinking LLMs are used as pairwise judges, single-sample judgments are noisy and standard aggregation rules (majority vote, soft self-consistency, generative self-aggregation) are statistically suboptimal, particularly when a tie option is allowed. These methods discard evidential strength from the full vote distribution (e.g., treating a 5-to-4 split identically to a 9-to-0 consensus) and fail to adapt to the specific LLM's tie propensity or prompt-induced shifts in voting behavior, leading to unreliable evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a response pair (A, B) and n independent discrete votes in {-1, 0, +1} drawn from a Thinking LLM (with temperature T=0.5 to preserve reasoning-path diversity), summarized as three counts (c⁺, c⁰, c⁻). A small calibration set (~5% of the dataset, typically 50–100 labeled examples) is used to fit model parameters offline. The output is a calibrated three-way preference label (A≻B, Tie, B≻A) along with calibrated class probabilities, with the decision rule explicitly aligned to minimize MAE rather than standard accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage distribution-calibrated aggregation built on a Bradley-Terry-Davidson (BTD) multinomial logit model with three parameters (β, η₀, γ) that jointly capture margin sensitivity, baseline tie propensity, and tie-count sensitivity. Two smoothed features are extracted from the vote counts: a log-odds margin s and a tie-evidence feature t, which are linked linearly to a latent margin u and tie logit η. In the first stage, parameters are fitted on a calibration set by minimizing the Discrete Ranked Probability Score (DRPS), a strictly proper scoring rule for ordinal outcomes that is differentiable and Fisher-consistent, solved via L-BFGS-B. In the second stage, at inference time, the calibrated probabilities are fed into an MAE Bayes-optimal decision rule that penalizes complete preference reversals (magnitude-2 errors) more heavily than tie disagreements (magnitude-1 errors), yielding a decision boundary that differs from simple majority voting. This decoupling of the smooth DRPS fitting objective from the nonsmooth MAE decision rule is the key design choice that makes the approach both principled and practically optimizable.

## Results

Using gemini-2.5-flash as the judge, the distribution-calibrated BTD method achieves the lowest MAE across all 8 tasks at both n=4 and n=12 samples. At n=12, headline MAE values include 0.497 on WMT ZH→EN (vs. 0.527 for Self-Consistency), 0.285 on RB2-Math (vs. 0.384 for SC), and 0.081 on RB2-Ties (vs. 0.158 for SC). Pairwise accuracy at n=12 reaches 0.607 on WMT ZH→EN, 0.723 on RB2-Math, and 0.918 on RB2-Ties, consistently exceeding all baselines. In a leave-one-out comparison against 8 individual human raters on WMT ZH→EN, the calibrated LLM judge surpasses 7 out of 8 humans at n=12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The BTD method outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and sample count. Self-Consistency (majority vote, Wang et al. 2023) is the strongest baseline but still trails by 0.03–0.20 MAE depending on the task; notably, SC degrades or stays flat as n increases from 4 to 12 on most RB2 tasks (e.g., RB2-Factuality worsens from 0.615 to 0.647), while BTD consistently improves. Soft Self-Consistency (Wang et al. 2024) and Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency (Taubenfeld et al. 2025) perform comparably to or slightly worse than plain SC, with the largest gaps on RB2-Ties (0.177 and 0.165 vs. 0.081 for BTD at n=12). Universal Self-Consistency (Chen et al. 2023) and Generative Self-Aggregation (Li et al. 2025) are the weakest baselines, with USC showing the largest degradation at higher n (e.g., 0.765 MAE on WMT EN→DE at n=12 vs. 0.588 for BTD). The advantage is most pronounced on tie-heavy tasks (RB2-Ties, RB2-Factuality) where the BTD model's explicit tie-propensity parameter corrects the systematic under-prediction of ties by vote-counting methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation paradigm and the inference-time compute (ITC) literature, where multiple reasoning samples are generated per item and must be aggregated. It departs from the dominant aggregation strategies—majority voting (Self-Consistency), confidence-weighted variants (Soft-SC, CI-SC), and LLM-based synthesis (GSA, USC)—by reframing aggregation as a probabilistic calibration problem. The core model is a three-parameter Bradley-Terry-Davidson (Davidson, 1970) multinomial logit fitted via the Discrete Ranked Probability Score (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007), a strictly proper ordinal scoring rule, followed by an MAE-aligned Bayes decision rule. This connects classical paired-comparison statistics and proper scoring rules to a problem (noisy LLM judge aggregation) that prior work has treated with heuristic vote-counting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge becomes the default scalable evaluation protocol, the aggregation step is a critical but underexplored bottleneck; this paper provides a lightweight, principled solution requiring only ~50–100 labeled calibration examples. The finding that allowing a tie option substantially reduces positional bias (e.g., 14.5% → 3.1% for gemini-2.5-flash) has immediate practical implications for evaluation design. Open questions include when calibration transfers across tasks (the Factuality task is a clear failure case due to extreme LLM tie-aversion), how to extend to multi-judge or k-point Likert settings, and whether the approach generalizes beyond pairwise preference to other ordinal evaluation tasks. The method's dependence on a small labeled set and its sensitivity to the LLM's inherent tie propensity remain practical constraints.
