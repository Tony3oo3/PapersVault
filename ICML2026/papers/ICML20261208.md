## Problem

Current instruction-based image editing methods excel at modifying static attributes but fundamentally fail at Human-Object Interaction (HOI) editing, which requires verb-driven pairwise editing that preserves entity identity while synthesizing contextually logical interactions. No dedicated benchmark exists to evaluate this task across its full cognitive complexity, and standard global metrics (e.g., CLIPscore) cannot simultaneously assess dynamic interaction validity and entangled human-object pair preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB source image and a natural-language HOI instruction (e.g., 'make the person ride the skateboard') as input. The I2V backbone (Wan 2.2, 1280×720, 5-second clip) generates a video, from which a two-stage semantic frame selector extracts a single key frame as the final edited image. The HOI-Edit benchmark provides 705 samples across three cognitive levels, each annotated with paired bounding boxes (subject, object, auxiliary context) and grounding-based Q&A pairs for evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces three components: (1) HOI-Edit, a benchmark with three progressive cognitive levels—Foundational Edits (L1), Context Spatial Understanding (L2), and Causal/Physical Reasoning (L3)—assessing interaction transformation, spatial grounding, and causal-chain simulation; (2) HOI-Eval, a grounded metric that propagates annotated bounding boxes from the original to the edited image via SAM 2 tracking, then uses a VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to perform pair-wise identity verification and context-aware interaction Q&A, with an I+Q&A penalty that zeros the interaction score if spatial/logical/physical constraints are violated; (3) SCPE, an agentic self-correcting framework built on four specialized agents (Generator, Analyzer, Reflector, Curator) that iteratively refine prompts using a Dynamic Playbook—a structured knowledge base mapping diagnosed failure patterns (e.g., proximity bias, physics violations) to validated prompting strategies. The key insight is that I2V models provide a temporal 'replay of the failure process,' exposing *why* errors occur (e.g., a hand steering toward the wrong target), enabling in-process correction that static image models cannot support. SCPE operates gradient-free and backbone-agnostic, requiring only one iteration to surpass commercial SOTA (Nano Banana) on interaction scores.

## Results

On the HOI-Edit benchmark, Wan 2.2 I2V + SCPE achieves the highest Interaction scores across all three cognitive levels: L1 (0.842), L2 (0.791), and L3 (0.805), surpassing the commercial SOTA Nano Banana (0.727, 0.704, 0.740) by 11.5, 8.7, and 6.5 points respectively. On the constraint-aware I+Q&A metric (which zeros out interaction if spatial/causal reasoning fails), SCPE leads at L2 (0.695 vs. 0.596) and L3 (0.653 vs. 0.578). Identity preservation (H/O) scores are slightly lower than Nano Banana's (e.g., L1 H: 0.926 vs. 0.954), attributed to I2V's spatial reconfigurations versus static models' pixel-level preservation. SCPE also improves over its own base Wan 2.2 I2V by 15.2 points on L1 Interaction (0.842 vs. 0.691).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCPE decisively outperforms all open-source static editors (Flux.1 Kontext, ByteMorph, Step1X-Edit, ChronoEdit, Bagel) on Interaction scores by 15–40 points across levels, with the largest gaps at L3 where causal reasoning is required (e.g., 0.805 vs. 0.205 for Flux.1 Kontext). Against the commercial SOTA Nano Banana, SCPE wins on Interaction at every level (L1: 0.842 vs. 0.727; L2: 0.791 vs. 0.704; L3: 0.805 vs. 0.740) and on I+Q&A (L2: 0.695 vs. 0.596; L3: 0.653 vs. 0.578), but trails slightly on identity (H/O), which the authors attribute to I2V's spatial reconfigurations versus static pixel preservation. Compared to its own base model Wan 2.2 I2V, SCPE yields substantial gains: +15.2 on L1 I, +12.9 on L2 I, +12.3 on L3 I, and +14.3 on L3 I+Q&A. On the ByteMorph benchmark, Wan I2V + SCPE also surpasses the native ByteMorph model (fine-tuned on 6M data) in both Human Motion (GPT Score 95.12 vs. 93.80) and Interaction (93.97 vs. 89.86). When paired with the faster TurboDiffusion backbone, SCPE still achieves 0.754 Interaction in 1.4 minutes, outperforming Nano Banana's 0.723 overall Interaction while being ~20× faster than ChronoEdit's 20-minute inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of instruction-based image editing (FLUX.1 Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit, Nano Banana) that treats editing as static pixel repainting, by reframing Human-Object Interaction editing as a dynamic process requiring temporal modeling. It repurposes Image-to-Video generation (Wan 2.2 I2V) as an editing backbone—a non-obvious choice—exploiting the temporal dimension not for video output but for its diagnosability: failed generations expose *why* errors occur via a 'replay of the failure process.' The agentic SCPE framework draws on the broader trend of LLM-driven self-correction and playbook-based experience transfer, but is the first to apply this loop to video-generation-based image editing, and the HOI-Edit benchmark extends flat editing benchmarks (I2EBench, ImgEdit, ByteMorph) into a three-level cognitive hierarchy that explicitly separates foundational interaction, spatial grounding, and causal/physical reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark or metric was designed to evaluate whether an edited image correctly depicts a specific human-object interaction while preserving both entities, and the proposed HOI-Eval (region-sensitive, pair-wise, VLM-based) addresses this with validated reliability (98.5% on HICO-DET, strong human correlation). The practical takeaway—that a gradient-free, backbone-agnostic prompt-refinement loop can push an open-source I2V model past a commercial SOTA editor on interaction quality in a single iteration—is actionable for practitioners. Key limitations temper the impact: the benchmark is small (705 samples), I2V inference latency remains high (30 min vs. ~20 s for static editors), and background preservation still lags behind dedicated image editors, leaving open whether camera-control or LoRA-based stabilization can close that gap without sacrificing interaction quality.
