## Problem

DiLoCo reduces communication frequency for distributed LLM training, but its outer synchronization remains a blocking global barrier that stalls computation, is dominated by the slowest link, and aborts entirely on a single link failure. The paper asks whether exact global synchronization can be relaxed to approximate synchronization via mixing/gossip, reducing blocking communication while preserving the consensus needed for stable optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261564/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained Llama 3 model (160M–1.5B parameters), training text batches from FineWeb or WikiText, and a chosen communication topology (global all-reduce or pairwise random-matching gossip) for each of two mixing slots. Workers run on consumer-grade bandwidth (50 Mbps–1 Gbps) with single-GPU nodes (40 GB A100), sequence length 1024, and global batch sizes of 512 or 1024. The output is the parameter-averaged model after R outer rounds, with the key design choice being which mixing operator fills Mix1 (non-blocking) and Mix2 (blocking), yielding four main configurations from vanilla DiLoCo to fully non-blocking GlobalM1.

## Method

The paper factorizes DiLoCo's outer synchronization into two mixing operations: Mix1, a non-blocking parameter mixing step that overlaps with the H inner optimization steps without introducing temporal staleness (workers compute gradients from their latest local update z_{m,r}, not a stale global state), and Mix2, a blocking outer-gradient mixing step that tightens worker agreement for stability. The key insight is that the 'average-before-optimization' gossip formulation allows Mix1's communication to be fully hidden under local computation, since z_{m,r} and x_{m,r} have the same number of outer update steps—introducing starting-point noise but never temporal lag. A theoretical bound shows consensus error scales as F = 1 + ρ₂/(1−ρ₁), where ρ₁ and ρ₂ are the contraction factors of Mix1 and Mix2, revealing that Mix1 is critical for boundedness while Mix2 tightens the bound. The paper further introduces a Jensen-Shannon distance metric on per-token output distributions as a functional disagreement measure that tracks optimization instability far better than L2 parameter distance, and uses it to identify which parameter blocks (token embeddings, LM head, early transformer layers) most need Mix2 communication, enabling a subset-Mix2 variant that recovers most stability at reduced communication cost.

## Results

On a 1.5B Llama 3 model trained on FineWeb (M=8, H=100, GBS=512), GlobalM1LocalM2 achieves validation perplexity of 18.80 at 10B tokens and 15.60 at 30B tokens, closely matching DiLoCo (18.65/15.52) while improving compute utilization from 53% to 66% at 200Mbps. GlobalM1 (no blocking communication) reaches 100% compute utilization at 200Mbps with a modest perplexity cost (19.42/15.91). The subset-Mix2 variant (GlobalM1LocalM2-subset) further boosts utilization to 82% at 200Mbps while keeping perplexity at 19.15, demonstrating a tunable trade-off between compute utilization and optimization stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiLoCo (the primary baseline), GlobalM1LocalM2 nearly matches perplexity (18.80 vs. 18.65 at 10B tokens) while improving compute utilization at 200Mbps from 53% to 66%; GlobalM1 trades a small perplexity gap (19.42 vs. 18.65) for 100% utilization at 200Mbps, translating to ~16 weeks vs. ~29 weeks wall-clock to reach 1T tokens. Against SyncDP, all DiLoCo-based methods sacrifice 1–2 points of perplexity but gain 36–100× compute utilization, making them practical in low-bandwidth settings where SyncDP is essentially idle. Against NoLoCo (Kolehmainen et al., 2025), the paper's LocalM1M2 configuration (a close structural match) achieves 19.37 PPL at 10B tokens with H=100, while NoLoCo's paper-HP configuration (H=50, twice the communication) only reaches 22.65, and its DiLoCo-HP configuration diverges after ~5k steps. Against Streaming DiLoCo's overlap mechanism, the paper's methods achieve meaningful utilization gains (36%→56–100%) without introducing temporal staleness, whereas Streaming DiLoCo's 5-step overlap yields negligible CU improvement (36%→36% at 100Mbps) and diverges under both tested hyperparameter settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiLoCo's low-communication distributed training by replacing its exact global all-reduce synchronization with approximate synchronization via gossip-based mixing, factorized into a non-blocking Mix1 (overlapping computation without temporal staleness) and a blocking Mix2 (tightening consensus for stability). The closest prior work is NoLoCo, which shares a similar factorized structure but does not study varying Mix1/Mix2 configurations or explicitly optimize for reduced blocking communication. The paper also draws on gossip-based optimization theory (Koloskova et al., 2020; Lian et al., 2017) and consensus-distance analysis (Kong et al., 2021; Khaled et al., 2025), while distinguishing itself from staleness-based overlap methods like Streaming DiLoCo by avoiding temporal lag entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners targeting consumer-grade or geographically distributed training (50 Mbps–1 Gbps links), the paper provides a concrete, tunable trade-off between compute utilization and optimization stability, with GlobalM1 reaching 100% utilization at 200 Mbps versus DiLoCo's 53% while maintaining comparable perplexity. The JS-distance metric as a functional disagreement diagnostic is a practically useful tool for monitoring instability and guiding where blocking communication is actually needed (e.g., the subset-Mix2 variant targeting only 43% of parameters). Open questions include automatically selecting Mix2 based on real-time JS-distance signals, extending the convexity-based theory to non-convex settings, and achieving full fault tolerance under correlated delays.
