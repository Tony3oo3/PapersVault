## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs can induce unexpected, harmful, or deliberately concealed behaviors that are difficult to detect through standard auditing. While LLMs possess some latent self-awareness of their learned behaviors, they produce unreliable self-reports when asked directly, and prior approaches to training models to describe their behaviors (e.g., Goel et al., 2025) failed to generalize beyond narrow, in-distribution behaviors. This paper asks whether a single lightweight adapter can be trained to make many different fine-tuned models reliably verbalize their learned behaviors, including those never seen during training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fine-tuned LLM (a base model M with a merged behavior adapter Δ_i) and a set of generic first-person investigation prompts (e.g., 'Are there any unusual characteristics you display only for certain types of prompts?'), and produces free-text natural-language self-reports in which the model describes its own learned behaviors. At test time, the same single LoRA adapter is applied to entirely new fine-tuned models, and a summarization scaffold can aggregate multiple noisy per-question predictions into a ranked list of the five most likely behaviors. The output is structured as first-person behavioral descriptions rather than classification labels or activation vectors.

## Method

The core contribution is training a single LoRA adapter (the introspection adapter, IA) jointly across N fine-tuned models M_i, each implanted with a known behavior b_i, so that applying the IA to M_i causes it to verbalize b_i when asked. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning minimizing cross-entropy on (prompt, behavior-description) pairs sampled across all M_i, and (2) a DPO refinement stage on held-out models where an LLM judge scores generated responses on a 1–10 accuracy scale and preference pairs are constructed to upweight accurate reports over hallucinated ones. The training distribution spans eight behavior categories (backdoors, quirks, harmful/benign roleplay, heuristic following, sandbagging, obscured malign, rare quirks) with category-specific controls to prevent the adapter from exploiting incidental training-data cues. Three factors drive generalization beyond prior work: scaling to larger base models (Llama-3.3-70B), a substantially more diverse behavior distribution, and the DPO stage. Notably, even a rank-1 single-layer LoRA achieves non-trivial verbalization, suggesting the IA elicits a latent introspective capability already present in the model's representations rather than teaching a fundamentally new one.

## Results

On AuditBench (56 models with hidden concerning behaviors), the IA-equipped investigator agent achieves a 59% success rate, outperforming the next-best scaffolded black-box method (53%) and the best white-box method, activation oracles (44%). The IA produces correct behavioral descriptions on 50 of 56 models (89% raw verbalization rate). On covert malicious finetuning (cipher) attacks, the IA combined with a summarization scaffold achieves 57.8% success, correctly identifying the anti-refusal behavior in 7 of 9 cipher variants. On the RM Sycophant, the IA surfaces 16 of 52 implanted behaviors over 100 prompts, compared to only 2 admitted by the unmodified model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against all prior auditing techniques on AuditBench (Sheshadri et al., 2026), where the IA-equipped agent achieves 59% success rate versus 53% for the best scaffolded black-box method and 44% for activation oracles (Karvonen et al., 2025), the strongest white-box baseline. The IA wins on both comparisons by 6 and 15 percentage points respectively, and uniquely achieves non-zero verbalization on 50/56 models (89%), indicating broad coverage across all 14 behavior types and 4 training configurations. On the cipher attack evaluation, no prior method was tested, but the IA's 57.8% scaffold success (7/9 models) demonstrates detection of a fundamentally different attack class (encrypted anti-refusal) that was never present in training. On UKAISI sandbagging, where ten prior detection methods all failed, the IA provides a statistically elevated signal (33.0% vs 15.8% baseline FPR), though it does not cleanly verbalize the specific sandbagging behavior due to format mismatch with training. Overall, the IA outperforms all prior auditing tools on the standard benchmark and provides the first detection signal on two previously undetectable attack classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Diff Interpretation Tuning (DIT; Goel et al., 2025), which trained a LoRA adapter to make a finetuned model describe a single narrow behavior change. The authors scale DIT to Llama-3.3-70B, expand the training distribution from a handful of behaviors to eight diverse categories (backdoors, sandbagging, harmful roleplay, heuristics, etc.), and introduce a DPO refinement stage to suppress hallucinated self-reports. The broader lineage includes work on behavioral self-awareness (Betley et al., 2025a), privileged-access introspection (Binder et al., 2024), and activation-based explanation methods (Karvonen et al., 2025; Pan et al., 2024), but the paper positions itself as the first to achieve broad out-of-distribution generalization in verbalized behavior reporting via a lightweight adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AI safety auditing: a single trained adapter can rapidly screen many finetuned variants for hidden behaviors, outperforming all prior white-box and black-box auditing tools on AuditBench and detecting covert cipher-mediated finetuning attacks that were previously undetectable from training data alone. Key limitations include a high false-positive rate (especially on the base model), unclear guidance on optimal training distribution composition, and the absence of a mechanistic explanation for why the adapter generalizes across differently-trained models. Open questions include whether IAs achieve genuine introspection in the privileged-access sense, how to reduce hallucination without sacrificing recall, and whether the OOD generalization plateau observed at Qwen-14B scale persists at 70B+ parameters.
