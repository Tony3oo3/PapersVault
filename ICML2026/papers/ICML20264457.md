## Problem

Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL) via automatic relevance determination provides a principled Bayesian mechanism for feature sparsity, but its reliance on a single homoscedastic noise variance renders it sensitive to outliers, heavy tails, and varying dispersion. Existing robustification approaches either break Gaussian conjugacy, impose additional structural assumptions, or fail to integrate naturally into the SBL evidence-maximization framework. The paper asks whether model and data sparsity can be achieved simultaneously within a single, conjugate Bayesian objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (which may be a nonlinear mapping Φ(X), e.g. random Fourier features or an RBF kernel basis) and a target vector y ∈ R^n, and jointly learns weight precisions γ ∈ R^d and per-sample noise variances λ ∈ R^n. Outputs include the sparse posterior weight vector θ, per-sample relevance scores (1/λ_i) identifying noisy observations, predictive mean and variance at test points, and threshold-free effective support sizes (ESS) quantifying the fraction of active weights and samples. In the neural network experiment, fixed DINO-2 features (d=384) with log-transformed crowd-count labels serve as input, and the learned λ ranking drives iterative data pruning.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the scalar noise variance λ in standard SBL with per-sample variances λ_i, so that the marginal likelihood p(y|γ,λ) = N(0, Λ + XΓ⁻¹Xᵀ) is optimized jointly over both weight precisions γ and sample precisions λ. This preserves Gaussian conjugacy and yields a closed-form objective whose log-determinant and quadratic terms decompose into interpretable complexity and fit components. A key result is that the identical closed-form update λ_i^{t+1} = r_i² + x_iᵀΣ_θx_i (residual squared plus model uncertainty) emerges from EM, MacKay's fixed-point iterations, and ℓ₂-IRLS, requiring no new algorithmic machinery. The update is shown to approximate a leverage-aware leave-one-out residual (PRESS), connecting data ARD to classical influence-function diagnostics, and in the RVM/kernel setting it downweights corrupted basis functions that would otherwise distort the induced GP kernel despite basis pruning. A dual objective in weight space (d×d rather than n×n) provides numerical stability when d ≪ n.

## Results

On three UCI tabular regression benchmarks (Energy, Carbon, Protein) with 10% outlier contamination, the proposed joint ARD via EM achieves RMSE of 1.89±0.14, 0.015±0.004, and 525.8±116.3 respectively, representing relative reductions of 32%, 70%, and 45% over their homoscedastic SBL counterparts. The method simultaneously achieves meaningful feature sparsity (ESS(θ) ≈ 12–29%) and data sparsity (ESS(y) ≈ 90–92%, closely matching the injected 10% contamination rate). On the Boston kernel regression (RVM) task, joint ARD via ℓ₂-IRLS consistently achieves the lowest RMSE across contamination levels, and on ShanghaiTech crowd counting with DINO-2 features, learned noise variances effectively identify corrupted high-count labels, stabilizing MAE under progressive data removal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed joint ARD methods (EM, MacKay, ℓ₂-IRLS) consistently outperform both robust and sparse baselines across all three tabular datasets. Against the Student-t robust regression baseline (Geweke, 1993), joint ARD via EM achieves lower RMSE on Energy (1.89 vs. 2.23), Carbon (0.015 vs. 0.028), and Protein (525.8 vs. 1060.3), while also providing feature sparsity (ESS(θ) ≈ 12–29%) that Student-t lacks entirely (ESS(θ)=100). Against Huber regression (Huber, 1964), the advantage is even more pronounced on Protein (525.8 vs. 653.8) and Energy (1.89 vs. 2.14), with comparable performance on Carbon (0.015 vs. 0.015). Against the inducing-point sparse GP (Titsias, 2009) in the Boston kernel regression experiment (Fig. 4), joint ARD via ℓ₂-IRLS achieves lower RMSE at all contamination levels, though the inducing-point GP itself outperforms the full GP, suggesting mild robustness benefits from data subsetting. The key differentiator is that joint ARD simultaneously achieves both model sparsity and data robustness within a single marginal likelihood objective, whereas each baseline addresses only one of these properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL) and Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD) by promoting per-sample noise variances from a scalar nuisance parameter to first-class ARD parameters, yielding a symmetric 'joint model and data sparsification' objective. It builds directly on Tipping's SBL/RVM framework, MacKay's fixed-point updates, and Wipf & Nagarajan's IRLS surrogate objectives, showing that the same closed-form update rules emerge under heteroscedastic noise without breaking Gaussian conjugacy. Conceptually, it departs from the robust regression literature (Huber, Student-t, trimmed methods) by achieving robustness through data reweighting within the marginal likelihood rather than through heavy-tailed likelihoods or ad-hoc loss modifications, and it connects the resulting noise updates to classical influence-function and leave-one-out residual diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a principled, drop-in robustification mechanism for Bayesian linear models that preserves all the computational and theoretical niceties of standard SBL (conjugacy, closed-form EM/MacKay/IRLS updates, Occam's Razor interpretation), making it immediately accessible to practitioners working with contaminated regression data. A key limitation is the requirement for linear-in-weights parametrization, which precludes direct application to end-to-end deep networks; the authors identify the Bayesian Learning Rule as a promising bridge but only provide a preliminary experiment. Open questions include whether the influence-function interpretation can be sharpened (e.g., via studentized updates to address residual masking), how the method behaves under more complex contamination patterns, and whether the joint ARD objective admits better-tractable surrogates for large-scale settings.
