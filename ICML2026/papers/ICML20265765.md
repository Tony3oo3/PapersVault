## Problem

Compositional text-to-video generation consistently fails on prompts with multiple subjects due to two coupled challenges: subject omission (not all mentioned subjects appear) and inter-subject relation misalignment (spatial positions and interactions are wrong). Existing layout-control methods cannot guarantee subject presence because suppressed semantic conditions resist attention constraints, while inference-time optimization improves presence but lacks explicit spatial guidance, making it impossible to address both issues simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265765/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-subject text prompt and an auto-generated binary layout (one mask per subject per latent frame, produced by GPT-4o-mini in ~5 s at ~$0.005 per request). It operates as a drop-in replacement for the cross-attention layer during the first 20% of denoising timesteps, producing compositional videos at resolutions such as 81×480×832 or compositional images when applied to FLUX. No additional training data or model weights are required; the only external dependency is the LLM layout-planning API call.

## Method

Comp-Attn is a training-free, plug-and-play cross-attention variant built on a 'Present-and-Align' paradigm that decouples subject presence from relational alignment. The Subject-aware Condition Interpolation (SCI) module first encodes each subject independently to obtain anchor embeddings, computes a per-subject saliency score via softmax-weighted cosine similarity against all anchors, and then adaptively interpolates a saliency restoration vector into the prompt embeddings, scaled by a time-aware attenuation ω(t)=1−t/T so that early denoising steps receive stronger semantic reinforcement. The Layout-forcing Attention Modulation (LAM) module constructs an adaptive-perception layout from the current cross-attention map (mean-thresholded), computes its IoU with the LLM-planned layout, and uses (1−IoU) as a dynamic modulation strength to inject a soft attention bias that enhances video-to-subject attention within the planned region while suppressing cross-subject attention, avoiding the rigidity of hard bbox masks. Together, the two modules are applied at the conditioning and attention-distribution levels respectively, adding only ~5% inference overhead while boosting T2V-CompBench scores by up to 29.84%.

## Results

Comp-Attn achieves substantial improvements on T2V-CompBench across four foundation models: +14.77% on VideoCrafter2 (0.4015 to 0.4608), +29.84% on CogVideoX-5B (0.4189 to 0.5439), +15.71% on Wan2.1-14B (0.5358 to 0.6200), and +11.70% on Wan2.2-A14B (0.5742 to 0.6414). The largest gains appear on metrics demanding subject presence and inter-subject relations (e.g., spatial relationship +23.01% and interaction +29.59% on CogVideoX; spatial +18.64% and interaction +19.16% on Wan2.1-14B). On VBench, Comp-Attn improves semantic scores (+2.21% on Wan2.2-A14B, +3.64% on CogVideoX-5B) while maintaining quality scores, unlike LVD and ViCo which degrade quality. On T2I-CompBench, applying Comp-Attn to FLUX yields a 5.36% average improvement (0.5819 to 0.6131), outperforming training-based methods SiamLayout (0.5629) and HybridLayout (0.6039). Inference overhead is minimal: only 5.1% increase on Wan2.2-A14B (411s to 432s) versus 67% for LVD (686s) and 94% for ViCo (798s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against foundation T2V models (Gen-3, Dreamina 1.2, Kling-1.0, T2V-Turbo-V2, Open-Sora 1.2, MAGI-1-24B, SkyReels-v2-13B) and compositional T2V methods: layout control (LVD, VideoTetris) and inference-time optimization (ViCo). Comp-Attn outperforms both compositional paradigms across all four base models; on Wan2.2-A14B it scores 0.6414 versus LVD (0.5988) and ViCo (0.5891), a 7.1% and 9.1% margin respectively. The key advantage is that Comp-Attn addresses both subject presence and inter-subject relations simultaneously, whereas layout methods fail to guarantee subject presence due to semantic suppression during conditioning, and optimization methods lack explicit spatial guidance. On VBench, Comp-Attn improves semantic scores while preserving quality, whereas LVD and ViCo show noticeable quality drops due to train-test gaps from latent optimization. For T2I, Comp-Attn on FLUX (0.6131 avg) beats training-based layout methods SiamLayout (0.5629) and HybridLayout (0.6039) without requiring any training. Inference cost is 432s for Comp-Attn versus 686s for LVD and 798s for ViCo on Wan2.2-A14B, confirming its efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Comp-Attn positions itself against two established families of compositional generation: layout-control methods (VideoTetris, LVD, DreamRunner) that impose hard attention masks from predefined bounding boxes, and inference-time optimization methods (Attend-and-Excite, Vico, ToMe) that iteratively refine latents via cross-attention gradients. It departs from both by decoupling the two core challenges—subject presence and inter-subject relations—and addressing them at distinct levels: conditioning (SCI) and attention distribution (LAM). The adaptive-perception layout draws on the mean-thresholding interpretability technique from ConceptAttention (Helbling et al., 2025), while the LLM-planned layout follows the same GPT-based planning used by LVD but applies it through soft IoU-guided modulation rather than hard masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a training-free, architecture-agnostic plug-in that adds only ~5% inference overhead while outperforming both layout-control and inference-time optimization baselines across DiT, MM-DiT, and U-Net backbones—a stark contrast to the 67–94% overhead of LVD/ViCo. This makes compositional generation accessible without retraining or expensive test-time loops. Open questions remain: the method still inherits the foundation model's lack of physical-world knowledge (failure cases with complex physics), relies on an external LLM for layout planning (introducing a small but non-zero error rate), and its gains on the strongest baselines (Wan2.2: 11.7%) are more modest than on weaker ones, suggesting diminishing returns as backbone quality improves.
