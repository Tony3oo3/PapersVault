## Problem

Softmax attention's quadratic complexity becomes a bottleneck for long-context modeling, while linear attention's fixed-size hidden state limits expressivity for long-range retrieval. Existing hybrid architectures that interleave softmax and linear attention at the layer level still retain full softmax layers as computational bottlenecks, and their fixed layer ratios may not be optimal across different input contexts. The paper asks whether the optimal attention operation can be determined adaptively at the token (chunk) level within a single layer.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text token sequence and splits it into chunks of size 64 (matching the GDN chunk size). For each chunk, an Attention Score Layer (mean-pooling followed by a linear projection) produces a score for each candidate operation, and the chunk is assigned to the operation with the highest score. Both attention types share the same Q, K, V projections, and the final output is a weighted sum of individually RMS-normalized attention outputs, where the time-dependent head-wise weights are derived from the Q values via a softmax-gated linear layer. The overall computational complexity reduces to O(L_nla · L + L_la), where L_nla and L_la are the token counts assigned to softmax and linear attention respectively.

## Method

NAtS-L constructs a unified search space containing both softmax attention and Gated DeltaNet (GDN) within the same layer, assigning each 64-token chunk to one operation via a learnable scoring mechanism. Both operations compute intra-chunk correlations for every token, but only the selected operation's KV values contribute to the inter-chunk computation: softmax attention loads only non-linear chunk KV values in its flash-attention iterations, while GDN updates its hidden state only for linear chunks (applying a decay factor α to non-linear chunks to enforce recency bias). The score layer is trained end-to-end by backpropagating gradients through both attention paths—softmax gradients are integrated into the flash-attention backward pass, and linear attention gradients are computed via the hidden-state inner product. The two attention outputs are individually RMS-normalized and combined with time-dependent head-wise weights (computed from Q via a softmax-gated linear layer) to balance their scales. The architecture shares Q, K, V across both operations to keep parameter counts consistent, and groups multiple softmax heads per GDN head following a GQA-style design.

## Results

On the 800M-parameter model (50B tokens), NAtS-L Hybrid achieves the best average retrieval accuracy of 45.07%, outperforming GDN (31.22%), Mamba2 (29.94%), Transformer (33.66%), and GDN Hybrid (37.30%). On the RULER long-context benchmark, NAtS-L Hybrid scores 0.49/0.32/0.21 at 4k/8k/16k context lengths, while GDN drops to 0.04 and Transformer to 0.00 at 16k. For inference latency at the longest sequence, NAtS-L Hybrid is only 1.66× slower than GDN for pre-filling yet 5.4× faster than Transformer, and achieves a 2.3× decoding speedup over Transformer at 128k context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GDN (Yang et al., 2025), NAtS-L Hybrid improves average retrieval accuracy by 13.85 points (45.07 vs. 31.22) and RULER 16k score by 5.25× (0.21 vs. 0.04), while remaining only 1.66× slower in pre-filling. Against Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024), NAtS-L Hybrid gains 15.13 points on retrieval average and 5.25× on RULER 16k. Against the full Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), NAtS-L Hybrid wins on retrieval average (45.07 vs. 33.66) and RULER at 8k/16k (0.32/0.21 vs. 0.00/0.00) while being 5.4× faster in pre-filling and 2.3× faster in decoding at 128k. Against the layer-wise GDN Hybrid (Kimi-Team, 2025), NAtS-L Hybrid gains 7.77 points on retrieval average and 16× on RULER 16k (0.21 vs. 0.00), demonstrating that token-level adaptivity within a layer outperforms fixed layer-level ratios. Against sparse-attention baselines NSA (Yuan et al., 2025) and MOBA (Lu et al., 2025) at 380M scale with 50% compute budget, NAtS-L achieves 28.48 vs. 22.05 and 21.98 average retrieval accuracy respectively. Against KV-cache eviction methods AdaKV (Feng et al., 2025) and PyramidKV (Cai et al., 2025), NAtS-L Hybrid scores 34.19 vs. 17.96 and 18.41, and also achieves lower inference latency than SnapKV-based decoding at 128k context.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAtS-L extends Neural Attention Search (NAtS), which learned per-chunk sparse attention patterns, by adding Gated DeltaNet (GDN) as a second operation in the search space so that tokens not selected for softmax attention are still processed via linear attention rather than being discarded. It departs from layer-wise hybrid architectures (Kimi-Linear, Jamba, Samba) that fix a static ratio of softmax-to-linear layers, instead making the routing decision at the token-chunk level within each layer. The chunk-wise parallel computation framework from Yang et al. (2024a) and the delta-rule recurrence from Schlag et al. (2021) provide the hardware-efficient substrate that makes both attention types amenable to joint per-chunk routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that token-level adaptivity in attention type selection yields meaningful long-context extrapolation benefits (maintaining RULER scores at 16k context while layer-wise hybrids collapse to near-zero) and a 5.4× prefill speedup over full softmax transformers at comparable retrieval accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models, whether expanding the search space beyond two operations (softmax + GDN) to include multiple linear attention variants further improves performance, and whether auxiliary load-balancing losses can better control the softmax/linear ratio for deployment. The finding that no learned head converges to pure softmax attention suggests full attention over the entire sequence is suboptimal, which could reshape how future hybrid architectures are designed.
