## Problem

Offline RL policies are constrained by the finite coverage of static datasets, making it difficult to generalize to out-of-distribution or low-return states. Existing data-augmentation approaches (e.g., SER, DiffStitch) generate fixed augmented trajectories that cannot adapt when the agent encounters new states beyond the augmented distribution, leaving a gap for a dynamic mechanism that can guide the agent from unfamiliar states toward high-return regions.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, RAD takes the current environment state s_t and queries a pre-constructed retrieval database of expert trajectory states (each entry storing the state vector, trajectory ID, timestep, and discounted return). Similarity is computed via cosine distance for locomotion/manipulation tasks or 2D Euclidean distance for navigation tasks. The output is a single action: D-RAD extracts it directly from a generated state-action sub-trajectory, while DL-RAD generates a state-only sub-trajectory and feeds the predicted next state through an inverse dynamics model to recover the action.

## Method

RAD comprises three modules operating at inference time. The Target Selection (TS) module retrieves top-k similar states from the expert database, filters for those within a tolerance η of the maximum discounted return over the next H−1 steps, and selects the candidate with the longest remaining trajectory as the planning target. The Step Estimation (SE) module uses an MLP to classify the number of steps needed to reach the target (reformulating discrete regression as classification over H−1 bins). The Planning (PL) module initializes a noisy sub-trajectory of length H, anchors the current state at position 0 and the target at position î, and runs reverse diffusion denoising guided by a learned return-prediction function to produce a clean sub-trajectory. Training employs a pseudo-target strategy (random offset i ~ U(1,H−1) within sampled trajectories) for the generation loss, an MSE loss for the return-guidance network, and a cross-entropy loss for step estimation, all optimized independently. Two variants are provided: D-RAD (Diffuser backbone, state-action generation) and DL-RAD (DiffuserLite/DiT backbone, state-only generation with inverse dynamics).

## Results

RAD achieves the best or near-best performance on 16 out of 18 D4RL datasets. The average score across all MuJoCo, AntMaze, Kitchen, and Maze2D tasks is 87.5 for DL-RAD and 81.3 for D-RAD, surpassing the strongest baselines DiffuserLite (86.0) and DiffStitch (83.8). The largest gains appear on sub-optimal and sparse-reward tasks: DL-RAD scores 93.5 on Walker2d-Medium-Replay (+3.3 over DiffuserLite) and 86.7 on AntMaze-Medium-Play (+8.7 over DiffuserLite). Under severe distribution shifts (0% region coverage in AntMaze), all baselines score 0.0 while DL-RAD still achieves 36.7–52.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAD is compared against 12 baselines spanning imitation learning (BC), conservative Q-learning (CQL, IQL), model-based methods (MOPO, MOReL), return-conditioned sequence modeling (Decision Transformer), data augmentation (SER, DiffStitch), and diffusion-based planners (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, Decision Stacks, DiffuserLite). DL-RAD wins or ties on 16 of 18 datasets; the only clear losses are Kitchen-Partial (71.5 vs. DiffuserLite 74.4) and Maze2D where D-RAD dominates but DL-RAD trails Diffuser by ~45–55 points due to the inverse-dynamics bottleneck. On sub-optimal MuJoCo datasets (Medium, Medium-Replay), DL-RAD improves over DiffuserLite by 3–21 points (e.g., Hopper-Medium: 101.0 vs. 100.9; Walker2d-Med-Replay: 93.5 vs. 90.2). On AntMaze, the gains are most dramatic: DL-RAD outperforms DiffuserLite by 8.7 (Med-Play), 8.0 (Large-Play), 0.9 (Med-Diverse), and 5.3 (Large-Diverse), while all non-diffusion baselines (CQL, IQL, DT, MOPO) score near zero. On Maze2D, D-RAD (state-action generation) leads all methods by 15–20 points over Diffuser, whereas DL-RAD (state-only + inverse dynamics) is competitive but weaker, suggesting the retrieval mechanism benefits most when paired with joint state-action generation. Under distribution-shift evaluation (Table 2–3), DL-RAD is the only method that maintains non-zero scores when 0% of the target region is present in training, confirming that the retrieval mechanism provides genuine OOD generalization rather than merely exploiting in-distribution data quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAD builds directly on diffusion-based offline RL planners (Diffuser and DiffuserLite) as its trajectory-generation backbone, but departs from the static data-augmentation paradigm of methods like SER and DiffStitch by introducing a dynamic, per-step retrieval mechanism. Conceptually, it transplants the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) idea from NLP into offline RL: rather than pre-generating a fixed augmented dataset, RAD queries a database of high-return states at inference time to condition the diffusion planner. The step-estimation module and pseudo-target training strategy are novel components that bridge the gap between the retrieved target and the generative model's temporal conditioning, addressing a practical gap left by prior stitching methods that assume fixed or known temporal offsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAD addresses a core limitation of offline RL—brittleness under distribution shift—without requiring retraining or complex data augmentation, making it a practical drop-in enhancement for existing diffusion-based planners. The severe OOD experiments (Table 3) where all baselines score 0.0 while DL-RAD still achieves 36–52 points demonstrate a qualitatively different robustness profile, not just a marginal score bump. However, the method remains fundamentally bounded by the offline dataset's coverage: if no high-return states exist in the relevant region, retrieval provides no useful guidance. Open questions include extending RAD to high-dimensional or visual state spaces, developing more efficient retrieval strategies for large-scale databases, and understanding how the approach interacts with online fine-tuning or continual learning settings.
