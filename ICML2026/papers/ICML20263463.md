## Problem

Existing CUA safety guardrails focus on predefined policy violations or known attack patterns, leaving a critical gap for misaligned actions that are technically permissible yet deviate from user intent—such as pursuing unintended subgoals, introducing unnecessary interactions, or derailing task progress. This paper is the first to formally define misaligned action detection in computer-use agents, identifying three categories (malicious instruction following, harmful unintended behavior, and other task-irrelevant behavior) and studying them systematically.

## Input / Output

The guardrail intercepts each proposed pyautogui action before execution, consuming the user instruction, a compact narrative summary of prior interaction steps (replacing raw screenshots to reduce token cost from >1K to ~41 tokens per step), the current screenshot, and the proposed action code. It outputs a binary aligned/misaligned judgment; when misaligned, it produces a four-component structured rationale (injection analysis, action understanding, outcome prediction, misalignment analysis) that serves as corrective feedback to prompt the agent to revise its action in a closed loop of up to three iterations.

## Method

DEACTION is a plug-and-play, two-stage runtime guardrail that requires no access to agent internals. Stage 1 (Fast Check) uses a lightweight LLM call with a one-sentence rationale constraint to quickly approve clearly aligned actions, bypassing deeper analysis. Stage 2 (Systematic Analysis) applies four structured reasoning components—inspecting the screenshot for injected instructions, interpreting the low-level action semantics, predicting unobservable consequences, and integrating these signals into a final alignment judgment—only for actions the fast check cannot confidently verify. History is maintained via a parallel narrative summarizer that condenses each (observation, action, next observation) transition into a concise natural-language description, keeping the context compact without adding detection latency. When an action is flagged, the four-component analysis is repurposed as structured corrective feedback, guiding the agent to propose a revised action that is re-evaluated by the guardrail until alignment is achieved or a retry limit is reached.

## Results

On MISACTBENCH, DEACTION achieves F1 scores of 70.7–82.8% across four LLM backbones, outperforming Task Shield (58.4–66.9%) and InferAct (61.6–64.7%) by over 15% absolute F1. In online evaluation on RedTeamCUA, DEACTION reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) by over 90% across all three CUAs (e.g., Claude Sonnet 4.5: 60%→6%; OpenCUA-72B: 32%→2%) while improving Utility under Attack (UA) from 44–82% to 60–84%. On benign OSWorld tasks, DEACTION preserves or slightly improves Success Rate (e.g., OpenAI CUA: 26%→30.7%; OpenCUA-72B: 39%→39.6%), with guardrail overhead of ~7.2s per step (~25% of total step time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEACTION consistently outperforms all four baselines across both offline and online evaluations. Offline on MISACTBENCH, DEACTION achieves 15–21% higher F1 than Task Shield and 10–20% higher than InferAct across all four backbones, primarily by achieving much higher precision (80–90% vs. 47–61%) while maintaining competitive recall, indicating baselines over-flag aligned actions as misaligned. Online on RedTeamCUA, DEACTION reduces ASR to 2–6% compared to 22–26% for all baselines (DSP, PromptArmor, Task Shield, InferAct), a >90% reduction versus only 20–30% for baselines. On benign OSWorld, DEACTION matches or exceeds the no-defense SR (39.6% vs. 39.0% for OpenCUA-72B; 30.7% vs. 26.0% for OpenAI CUA), while all baselines degrade performance (35.2–38.3% for OpenCUA-72B), confirming that DEACTION's iterative correction loop avoids the over-blocking that plagues other guardrails. The two-stage design also gives DEACTION a latency advantage over Task Shield and InferAct in online settings, as 45% of actions bypass systematic analysis via the fast check.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the safety-centric guardrail paradigm (policy-violation detection, known-attack-pattern matching) by reframing CUA deviations as an intent-centric action misalignment problem, covering not only adversarial attacks but also internally arising errors and task-irrelevant behavior. It builds on the narrative summarization approach of Gonzalez-Pumariega et al. (2025) for compact history representation, and positions itself against prior action-alignment methods (Task Shield, InferAct) that were designed for tool-use agents and focused narrowly on safety. The key conceptual departure is the three-category taxonomy (malicious instruction following, harmful unintended behavior, other task-irrelevant behavior) and the shift from trajectory-level to action-level pre-execution detection with iterative correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MISACTBENCH fills a clear gap as the first benchmark with human-annotated, action-level alignment labels for multimodal CUA trajectories, and DEACTION provides a practical plug-and-play guardrail that reduces attack success rate by over 90% while preserving benign task utility — a combination that prior safety-focused guardrails could not achieve without over-constraining normal behavior. Open questions remain around long-horizon cumulative drift (where individually harmless actions compound into misalignment), robustness to well-disguised injections that bypass both fast check and systematic analysis, and the limited coverage of internally arising misalignments due to reliance on controlled synthesis. The finding that narrative summaries outperform raw screenshots for alignment assessment (with 25× lower token cost) suggests a broader principle for efficient context management in agent guardrails.
