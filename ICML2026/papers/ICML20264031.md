## Problem

Existing self-supervised contrastive learning methods for time series either rely on data augmentation (introducing inductive bias), require multiple encoder passes, or assume temporal adjacency at the timestep level implies semantic similarity. This last assumption breaks down on datasets with frequent event transitions (e.g., UCR/UEA benchmarks), causing false positives during temporal shifts, while timestep-level contrastive methods like CaTT also incur quadratic memory and compute costs in sequence length.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time-series windows of variable length (T=40 to T=3000) and channel count (D=1 to D=64), but rather than encoding the full sequence, it stochastically partitions each window into k overlapping sub-blocks (k sampled uniformly from [2,10] per iteration) with a 50% overlap ratio. Each sub-block is encoded and pooled along the temporal axis into a single 256-dimensional embedding, so the output is a set of k embeddings per instance rather than a per-timestep sequence. Downstream, these embeddings support classification (linear probe or kNN), clustering, and cross-dataset transfer without any encoder fine-tuning.

## Method

Di-COT stochastically divides each time-series window into k overlapping sub-blocks per training iteration, encodes all sub-blocks in a single forward pass through a shared InceptionTime backbone, and pools each into one embedding. Temporally adjacent sub-blocks are designated as positive pairs while all other sub-blocks in the same sequence serve as negatives, and the contrastive objective is reformulated as a k-way cross-entropy classification where temperature-scaled cosine similarities act as logits. This design eliminates data augmentation, multiple encoder passes, and DTW-based similarity, while making loss computation O(Bk²d) and memory O(Bk²), both independent of sequence length T. The stochastic variation in k across iterations exposes the encoder to multiple temporal granularities, implicitly encouraging multi-scale representations without an explicit hierarchical architecture.

## Results

On six large-scale real-world datasets (>20k samples), Di-COT achieves 83.07% average linear-probing accuracy (±0.69 CI), outperforming all self-supervised baselines and approaching the fully supervised upper bound of 85.25%, while requiring the lowest training time (2.88 hrs) among deep-learning methods. On the UCR (124 datasets) and UEA (28 datasets) benchmarks, Di-COT attains 81.33% and 71.24% average accuracy respectively with the fastest total training time (0.84 hrs vs. 2.48–3.30 hrs for next-best methods). In the 1% low-label regime, Di-COT reaches 76.36% average accuracy, surpassing the supervised end-to-end model (70.39%) by ~6 points. For clustering, Di-COT achieves the best NMI (0.508) and ARI (0.406), and for cross-dataset transfer it attains 72.12% average accuracy, the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Di-COT outperforms all 11 baselines on average linear-probing accuracy across the six large-scale datasets. The closest competitor, CaTT (the authors' prior work), achieves 81.20% vs. Di-COT's 83.07% (+1.87 pp) and is slower (3.47 vs. 2.88 hrs), with the gap widening on long-sequence datasets like ECG (85.28 vs. 80.89) and SKODA (99.41 vs. 94.87). Among augmentation-based methods, TF-C (80.69%) and TNC (80.69%) are the strongest, but Di-COT surpasses them by ~2.4 pp while training 2.3× and 4.0× faster respectively. TS2Vec (79.77%) and CoST (79.55%) trail by 3.3 and 3.5 pp; SimMTM (77.92%) and InfoTS (78.58%) lag by over 4 pp. On the UCR/UEA benchmarks, Di-COT leads by 1.3 pp over CoST (81.33 vs. 80.03 on UCR) and 0.9 pp over TNC on UEA, while training in 0.84 hrs versus 2.48–3.30 hrs. In the 1% low-label regime, Di-COT (76.36%) beats TF-C (73.55%) by 2.8 pp and the supervised baseline (70.39%) by 6.0 pp. Traditional baselines (MiniRocket at 46.01%, Random Forest at 61.16%) are substantially outperformed, and even a randomly initialized InceptionTime backbone (78.60%) exceeds both, underscoring the value of learned temporal structure over fixed feature extractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Di-COT builds directly on CaTT (Shamba et al., 2025), which eliminates augmentation by contrasting temporally adjacent timesteps within a sequence, but departs from it by replacing timestep-level contrast with stochastic sub-block partitioning, thereby avoiding the assumption that consecutive timesteps are semantically similar. It also diverges from the broader augmentation-based contrastive time-series literature (TS2Vec, CoST, TF-C, InfoTS) by removing data augmentation and multiple encoder passes entirely, and reformulates the contrastive objective as a cross-entropy classification over sub-block indices rather than a pairwise InfoNCE loss. The method inherits the InceptionTime backbone and training protocol from prior work to isolate the contribution of the pretext task design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making loss computation independent of sequence length (O(Bk²d) vs. O(B²T²d) for timestep-level methods), Di-COT addresses a practical scalability bottleneck for long time-series, enabling larger batch sizes and faster training without sacrificing representation quality. The paper demonstrates consistent gains across linear probing, kNN, clustering, and cross-dataset transfer, suggesting the learned embeddings are broadly useful rather than tuned to a single evaluation. A stated limitation is that the contrastive objective is oriented toward discriminative tasks (classification, retrieval, clustering) and may not transfer to forecasting, which requires fine-grained temporal prediction; extending the framework to such tasks remains open.
