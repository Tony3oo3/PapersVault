## Problem

While PCA of probability measures via Hilbert space embeddings is well established, the statistical literature has not characterized how estimation accuracy depends on both the number of measures n and the number of samples m per measure simultaneously. This double asymptotic regime is critical in applications like flow cytometry where n is fixed but m is large, raising the practical question of how many points per measure can be safely subsampled without degrading PCA performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. probability measures on R^d, each approximated by m i.i.d. samples forming an empirical measure (a point cloud). The output is a rank-q orthogonal projector in the embedding Hilbert space, yielding principal directions, component scores, and a low-dimensional PCA representation, along with the empirical covariance operator. A key practical detail is that the three embeddings (KME, LOT, SW) are infinite-dimensional functions approximated by finite evaluations: KME and LOT use m_0 reference points, while SW uses p random projection directions and T quantile levels per direction.

## Method

The paper embeds each probability measure into a separable Hilbert space via one of three maps—Kernel Mean Embedding into an RKHS, Linearized Optimal Transport into L²(ρ), or Sliced Wasserstein into L²([0,1]×S^{d-1})—then applies standard functional PCA to the embedded vectors. The core theoretical contribution decomposes the estimation error into an inter-measure term (n^{-1/2}, from sampling n measures) and an intra-measure term (m^{-α}, from approximating each embedding with m samples), yielding rates of the form n^{-1/2} + m^{-α} where α depends on the embedding (e.g., α=1/2 for KME, α=1/d for LOT, α=1/4 for SW). This reveals a transition between a dense regime (m > n^{1/(2α)}) where the parametric rate dominates and a sparse regime (m < n^{1/(2α)}) where the embedding approximation error dominates. The dense-regime rate is proven minimax optimal via a two-hypothesis testing argument, and the PCA excess risk inherits the same transition structure with an additional √q factor from the number of retained components.

## Results

The paper establishes that the empirical covariance HS error and PCA excess risk converge at rate n^{-1/2} + m^{-α}, where α depends on the embedding (α=1/2 for KME, α=1/d for LOT, α=1/4 for SW), and proves the n^{-1/2} dense-regime rate is minimax optimal. In numerical experiments (Table 2), the SW embedding maintains near-constant covariance risk (~0.5–0.7) and excess risk (~0.02) across dimensions d=2 to d=50, while LOT degrades dramatically (covariance risk from 2.40 at d=2 to 235.64 at d=50). On the HIPC flow cytometry dataset (n=63 measures, ~50,000 cells each), PCA representations stabilize at m≈50 subsampled cells (0.1% of available data), and Procrustes disparity plateaus around m≈400.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three Hilbert-space embeddings—KME (Muandet et al., 2017), LOT (Wang et al., 2013), and Sliced Wasserstein (Rabin et al., 2011)—as the embedding step for PCA of probability measures. The SW embedding dominates in high-dimensional settings: its covariance risk remains essentially flat (0.71→0.52) and its excess risk stays at ~0.02 from d=2 to d=50, while LOT's covariance risk inflates by nearly 100× (2.40→235.64) over the same range, consistent with its m^{-1/d} sampling rate. KME sits in between, with moderate dimension dependence (1.43→12.64 covariance risk) and the highest variance in excess risk (up to 0.71 at d=50). In the q-dependence experiment (Table 3), SW again achieves the lowest excess risk at every q (0.13 at q=1 vs. 4.13 for LOT and 16.23 for KME), confirming its advantage for low-dimensional PCA representations. On real data (HIPC and ModelNet10), all three embeddings produce stable 2D PCA representations at small subsample sizes, but SW's dimension-free rate makes it the most practical choice for high-dimensional point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hilbert-space-embedding framework for PCA of probability measures (KME, LOT, SW) from the single-measure estimation setting to a double asymptotic regime with n measures each observed through m samples. The convergence-rate analysis draws on the variational and concentration techniques of Reiss & Wahl (2020) for PCA excess risk, and the minimax lower bound follows the classical two-hypothesis testing scheme of Tsybakov (2003). The transition between sparse and dense sampling regimes mirrors results already established for functional PCA (Belhakem et al., 2025; Yao et al., 2005), but the authors note that standard FPCA fails on probability measures due to nonlinear Wasserstein geometry, making the extension non-trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence rates of the form n^{-1/2} + m^{-α} for PCA of probability measures, with a minimax-optimality guarantee in the dense regime, giving practitioners a principled criterion for choosing the subsample size m per measure. The practical finding that only ~50 cells (0.5% of 50,000) are needed for stable PCA in flow cytometry has direct computational implications for high-throughput biology. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-Gaussian or heavy-tailed measures, adaptive selection of m across heterogeneous populations, and whether the minimax rate can be improved under additional structural assumptions on the covariance operator.
