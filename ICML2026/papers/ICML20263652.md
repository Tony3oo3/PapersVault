## Problem

Existing DICE-based offline imitation learning methods (SMODICE, DemoDICE) either require a strict coverage assumption that the suboptimal dataset assigns non-zero probability to all expert-visited state-action pairs, or introduce auxiliary KL regularization that compromises the original expert-imitation objective. This paper asks whether a DICE objective can remain well-defined without coverage assumptions while still directly minimizing the KL divergence between the learned policy and the expert.

## Input / Output

The method takes two static offline datasets: a small expert dataset D_E (state-action-next_state tuples from an expert policy, often a single trajectory) and a larger suboptimal dataset D_S (tuples from low-quality behavior policies). No environment interaction or reward signal is required. The output is a stochastic policy π: S → Δ(A) extracted via weighted behavior cloning on the mixture dataset, with a τ-scaled exponential weight that down-weights the value function's influence on the current state to prevent weight collapse from value overestimation.

## Method

ReDICE decomposes the KL divergence D_KL(d^π ∥ d^E) using a mixture distribution d^mix = βd^E + (1−β)d^S as the reference measure, which is always positive wherever d^E is positive, thereby eliminating the coverage assumption while preserving the original imitation objective exactly. The resulting DICE primal is converted to a dual via Lagrangian relaxation of Bellman flow constraints, and for the KL f-divergence the dual reduces to a Gumbel regression loss (strictly convex with a unique global optimum) that also avoids estimating the initial-state term (1−γ)E_{d_0}[V(s)]. The reward r(s,a) = −log(β/c*(s,a)) is estimated by a GAN-trained discriminator on the mixture. For policy extraction, a τ-scaled weight exp(TV(s,a) − τV(s)) with τ ∈ [0,1] is used instead of the theoretical exp(TV(s,a) − V(s)), which provably yields the same optimal policy but mitigates empirical weight collapse. Training uses an empirical Bellman operator with orthogonal gradient updates (from ODICE) to resolve conflicting gradient signals between V(s) and V(s').

## Results

On the primary D4RL offline IL benchmark (Table 1, 1 expert trajectory + suboptimal data mixed with expert rollouts), ReDICE achieves a 97.68 average normalized score, outperforming the strongest baseline ReCOIL (67.76) by ~44% and reaching 91.1% of the expert average (107.23). In the more challenging limited-coverage setting (Table 2, no expert rollouts in suboptimal data), ReDICE scores 38.45 average vs. ReCOIL's 28.16 and DemoDICE's 27.28, approaching the OfflineRL upper bound of 52.22. On the real-world maritime navigation task (ShipNaviSim), ReDICE achieves ~85% goal reach rate versus ~60% for all baselines, with substantially lower near-miss counts and max curvature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDICE outperforms all six baselines on every task in both the standard D4RL benchmark (Table 1) and the limited-coverage setting (Table 2). Against the strongest DICE-based competitor ReCOIL, ReDICE improves the average normalized score from 67.76 to 97.68 (~44% relative gain) on the main benchmark, and from 28.16 to 38.45 (~37% gain) under limited coverage. DemoDICE and SMODICE-action suffer severe degradation under low expert coverage (e.g., SMODICE-action scores only 10.26 average, with negative returns on medium+ant), while ReDICE remains robust. ILID, a discriminator-weighted BC method, achieves 21.58 average on the limited-coverage setting, well below ReDICE's 38.45. On the maritime navigation task, ReDICE achieves ~85% goal reach rate versus ~60% for BC, SMODICE, and ReCOIL, with fewer near-miss events and lower max curvature, demonstrating its advantage in high-dimensional real-world settings. The paper also shows that applying ReDICE's τ-scaled policy extraction to ReCOIL improves it from 67.76 to 81.49, but ReDICE still leads by ~20%, confirming the core objective design (not just policy extraction) drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReDICE extends the DICE framework (Nachum et al., 2019; Nachum & Dai, 2020) for offline imitation learning with supplementary suboptimal data, building directly on SMODICE (Ma et al., 2022) and DemoDICE (Kim et al., 2021). Its key conceptual departure is decomposing the KL divergence using a mixture distribution d^mix = βd^E + (1−β)d^S as the reference measure, which removes the strict coverage assumption required by both SMODICE and DemoDICE while preserving the original expert-imitation objective—unlike DemoDICE's added KL regularization or ReCOIL's (Sikchi et al., 2024) alternative divergence that does not recover the original density ratio. The dual is further simplified via a Gumbel regression equivalence (Garg et al., 2023) and stabilized with orthogonal gradient updates from ODICE (Mao et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the coverage assumption without sacrificing the original KL-based imitation objective, ReDICE offers a cleaner theoretical foundation for offline IL with suboptimal data and achieves a substantial ~44% average improvement over the prior SOTA (ReCOIL) across D4RL benchmarks, including robustness in very low-coverage settings where other DICE methods collapse. The real-world maritime navigation application (ShipNaviSim) demonstrates practical relevance for safety-critical vessel behavior modeling. Open questions include extending the method to stochastic environments (where the empirical Bellman operator introduces approximation error), cross-domain settings with different dynamics, and whether adaptive mixture coefficients β could further improve performance in outlier cases.
