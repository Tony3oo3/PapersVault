## Problem

Intrinsic-extended LVLMs for visual search suffer from two critical issues: post-training for multi-step visual search causes incompatibility among intrinsic single-step capabilities (grounding, OCR, spatial understanding, counting degrade by up to 49%), and long multi-step reasoning contexts accumulate interference where early errors and irrelevant content disrupt later steps. Existing approaches either rely on rigid external tool interfaces that fragment multi-step reasoning or directly extend the model without addressing these capability conflicts.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a high-resolution image and a text query as input. The search model performs multi-turn reasoning in a single forward pass, where each turn produces either a grounding prediction (reference to a previously observed image plus region-of-interest coordinates for cropping and zooming) or a final text answer. The prophet model receives the current turn's cropped image paired with either a grounding-verification query or the original question, and outputs single-step prophetic prefixes that are evaluated in parallel by the search model rather than fed as textual input.

## Method

SeProD pairs a post-training LVLM (search model) with its pre-training counterpart (prophet model) and couples them through probability-based prophetic sampling. At each reasoning turn, the search model's current image is sent to the prophet, which generates capability-specific prefixes (grounding verification or answer drafts). The search model then evaluates all prophet tokens in parallel, computing a consistency score s_j as a geometric mean of the search and prophet token probabilities weighted by a dynamic α (initialized to 0.5, adjusted by the token's normalized rank in the search model's logits). Tokens with s_j ≥ τ are accepted as native search-model tokens and propagated through subsequent decoding; the first rejected token triggers resumption from the search model's own distribution. This mechanism is training-free, plug-and-play, and introduces no additional computational overhead because prefix evaluation is fully parallelizable.

## Results

SeProD consistently improves all three visual-search LVLM baselines across all 12 splits of 4 benchmarks (VisualProbe, V*Bench, HR-Bench 4K/8K) without additional computational overhead. The largest gains appear on Mini-o3: +3.3 on VisualProbe-Hard, +4.4 on V*Bench-Spatial, and +2.7 on HR-Bench-8K-FCP, with a 7B prophet model pushing VisualProbe-Hard to +4.3 and V*Bench-Spatial to +5.6. On general VQA, Mini-o3+SeProD reaches 67.7 on MME-RealWorld, 85.4 on ScienceQA, 85.3 on OCRBench, and 78.4 on CVBench, surpassing both Mini-o3 and the base Qwen2.5-VL. Acceptance rates of 74–81% yield a slight 1.03–1.07× speedup, confirming zero net overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tool-free intrinsic-extended methods, SeProD on Mini-o3 outperforms Chain-of-Focus (89.2 vs. 88.0 on V*Bench-Overall) and Simple-o3 (78.3 vs. 76.2 on HR-Bench-4K-Overall), while slightly trailing Simple-o3 on V*Bench-Overall (89.2 vs. 90.4). Against external-tool methods, SeProD on Mini-o3 surpasses ZoomEye on HR-Bench-4K (78.3 vs. 69.6) and HR-Bench-8K (75.0 vs. 69.3) but falls short on V*Bench-Overall (89.2 vs. 90.6); it clearly beats RAP (78.3 vs. 57.1 on HR-Bench-4K) and DyFo (89.2 vs. 81.2 on V*Bench). SeProD is most directly comparable to FOCUS, where it is marginally behind on V*Bench (89.2 vs. 90.6) and HR-Bench-4K (78.3 vs. 79.3) but ahead on HR-Bench-8K (75.0 vs. 76.3 is actually behind—correcting: 75.0 vs. 76.3, so behind). The core contribution is the consistent, uniform improvement over each baseline's own performance: Pixel Reasoner gains +0.5 to +3.0, DeepEyes +0.5 to +4.0, and Mini-o3 +1.1 to +4.4 across all 12 splits, with no split showing a regression. On general VQA (Table 2), Mini-o3+SeProD beats Qwen2.5-VL by 10.4 on MME-RealWorld and 4.5 on CVBench, confirming that the prophetic mechanism also reinforces intrinsic capabilities beyond visual search.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeProD sits at the intersection of two research threads: intrinsic model extensions for visual search (Mini-o3, DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner) that activate zoom-in and grounding within a single forward pass, and speculative decoding from LLM inference acceleration (Leviathan et al., 2023). The key departure is repurposing the draft-accept mechanism of speculative decoding not for speed but as a probabilistic interface between a pre-training 'prophet' model and a post-training 'search' model, where the prophet's single-step outputs are selectively absorbed as native tokens rather than fed as textual prompts. This contrasts with external-tool approaches (ZoomEye, RAP, DyFo) that fragment multi-step reasoning through rigid function-calling interfaces, and with prior intrinsic extensions that suffer from capability incompatibility and long-context interference after visual-search post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeProD offers a training-free, plug-and-play remedy for two concrete failure modes of visual-search LVLMs—capability degradation (up to 49% grounding drop) and long-context interference—without architectural changes or additional training, and with a slight inference speedup rather than overhead. This makes it immediately applicable to any intrinsic-extended LVLM, lowering the barrier for practitioners. Limitations include dependence on the search model's grounding being correct (failure cases in Appendix B), the requirement that the prophet be the exact pre-training counterpart of the search model, and modest absolute gains (1–4% on most splits). Open questions include whether the prophetic-sampling interface generalizes to other multi-step multimodal reasoning tasks beyond visual search, and whether a smaller or differently-initialized prophet model could decouple the two-model requirement.
