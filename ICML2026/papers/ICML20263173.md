## Problem

Multi-frame infrared small target detection (MISTD) suffers from two fundamental failures of standard detection paradigms: (i) semantic paucity, where textureless sub-pixel targets are indistinguishable from stochastic noise in architectures biased toward rich geometric structures, and (ii) representation collapse, where extreme class imbalance causes the feature space to degenerate into background-dominated statistics. Existing methods treat targets as a conventional semantic category or model the background as a static low-rank component, neither of which adequately handles dynamic, cluttered infrared scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short multi-frame infrared sequence of shape 512×512×5 (five consecutive frames at 512×512 resolution) and produces per-frame bounding boxes with classification confidence and objectness scores via a YOLOX-style decoupled detection head. The backbone (CSPDarknet) extracts multiscale features at each frame, and the dual-stream processing operates on these feature maps rather than raw pixels. The implicit stream additionally outputs a spatial attention prior map (P_prior) that is element-wise multiplied with all feature sources before fusion.

## Method

CodeMamba is a collaborative dual-stream architecture that reframes MISTD as background manifold modeling plus motion singularity capturing. The implicit stream (GBAPM) uses hierarchical probabilistic vector quantization with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation over coarse-to-fine codebooks (K=512 each) to distill a compact background representation; codewords are updated via EMA so that sparse target signals are statistically excluded, and the reconstruction residual yields a self-supervised attention prior. The explicit stream (MHSFM) computes inter-frame temporal differences and processes them through a hybrid spatial-frequency Mamba block: a selective state-space branch models local thermal continuity while a frequency branch applies a learnable spectral filter via 2D FFT to suppress low-frequency background; bidirectional scanning aggregates past and future context for trajectory consistency. A Bayesian uncertainty-weighted fusion module (BUWFM) then combines backbone, explicit, and implicit features by estimating per-source log-variance through GAP+1×1 conv and applying softmax-normalized inverse-variance weights. Training jointly optimizes a YOLOX decoupled detection loss and a self-supervised implicit loss (reconstruction + commitment + entropy regularization) with λ=0.1 balancing the two.

## Results

On the IRDST dataset, CodeMamba achieves 78.24% mAP50, 90.85% Precision, 90.40% Recall, and 90.62% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline MoPKL (74.54% mAP50, 86.84% F1) by 3.70% in mAP50 and 3.78% in F1. On the DAUB dataset, CodeMamba reaches 97.42% mAP50 and 99.42% F1, with a Recall of 99.75%, performing on par with Tridos (97.80% mAP50, 99.43% F1). The method also maintains high computational efficiency at 8.65M parameters, 39.28G FLOPs, and 26.52 FPS, substantially lighter than Tridos (14.13M, 130.72G, 13.71 FPS) and MoPKL (9.46M, 119.64G, 10.03 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On IRDST, CodeMamba decisively outperforms all eleven baselines, with the largest margins over MoPKL (+3.78% F1) and Tridos (+3.77% F1), both of which rely on multi-domain or language-prior fusion but lack explicit background manifold modeling. The Recall advantage is particularly pronounced: CodeMamba achieves 90.40% versus 84.74% (MoPKL) and 89.35% (Tridos), attributed to the bidirectional spatial-frequency Mamba scanning that recovers weak targets missed by unidirectional or purely spatial approaches. On DAUB, the gap narrows considerably; Tridos edges out CodeMamba by 0.38% in mAP50 (97.80% vs. 97.42%) and by 0.01% in F1 (99.43% vs. 99.42%), while CodeMamba achieves the highest Recall (99.75% vs. 99.67%). Against single-frame detectors (AGPCNet, SCTransNet), CodeMamba gains 13.18% and 10.39% in F1 on IRDST respectively, confirming the critical role of temporal context. In terms of efficiency, CodeMamba (8.65M params, 39.28G FLOPs, 26.52 FPS) is roughly 3.3× faster than Tridos and 3× faster than MoPKL while delivering superior detection accuracy, making it the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeMamba departs from the dominant MISTD paradigm that treats targets as a semantic class to be classified against background, instead reframing detection as singularity identification relative to a self-supervised background manifold. The explicit stream adapts the selective state space scanning formulation from Video Mamba (Li et al., ECCV 2024) for temporal motion modeling, while the implicit stream introduces hierarchical probabilistic vector quantization (Gumbel-Softmax) as a mechanism for discrete background prototype learning—a technique borrowed from VQ-VAE-style generative modeling but repurposed here for anomaly localization. The Bayesian uncertainty-weighted fusion directly implements the inverse-variance weighting principle from Kendall & Gal (NeurIPS 2017), and the frequency-domain branch leverages the learnable spectral filter from Global Filter Networks (Rao et al., NeurIPS 2021). Conceptually, the paper shifts the inductive bias from 'learn what a target looks like' to 'learn what the background manifold looks like and flag deviations,' which sidesteps the extreme class imbalance that plagues supervised target classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of MISTD as background manifold modeling plus motion singularity detection offers a principled escape from the representation collapse caused by extreme class imbalance, and the self-supervised VQ mechanism requires no target annotations for the implicit stream. The 3.3× FLOPs reduction relative to Tridos while achieving a 3.77% F1 gain suggests that the Mamba-based linear-complexity temporal modeling is a practical path for real-time infrared surveillance. However, the paper acknowledges failure modes near high-intensity background structures and under trajectory occlusion, and the approach is validated only on two infrared-specific benchmarks (IRDST, DAUB), leaving open whether the manifold-sieving strategy generalizes to other anomaly-detection domains or cross-modal settings. The authors note future work on infrared-visible fusion and multispectral detection as natural extensions.
