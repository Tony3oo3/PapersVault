## Problem

The nonasymptotic theory of ℓ∞ distribution estimation over countable alphabets, initiated by Kontorovich & Painsky (2025), left two key questions open: whether fully empirical (data-dependent) confidence bounds exist that avoid unknown functionals of the true distribution, and what the true least-favorable distribution is for worst-case risk. This paper resolves both, showing that a fixed two-point distribution is worst-case up to universal constants and providing a tight empirical high-probability guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260766/cropped_page13_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from an unknown discrete distribution p on a countable alphabet ℕ and produces the empirical pmf (MLE) as the point estimate. The key output is a fully empirical high-probability confidence bound on ‖p̂ − p‖∞ that depends on p only through observable plug-in statistics v̂* and V̂* (computed from the empirical pmf and its order statistics), making it directly evaluable from data without knowledge of the true distribution. The minimax result additionally identifies the fixed two-point distribution (1/2, 1/2, 0, …) as least-favorable uniformly for all sufficiently large n.

## Method

The proof of the minimax result reduces the dependent multinomial sampling model to an independent Bernoulli model via decoupling inequalities (exploiting negative association of multinomial components), then maximizes the sharp expected-error characterization from Blanchard & Voráček (2024) over all admissible distributions, showing the supremum is attained by the uniform distribution on two points. The fully empirical bound is obtained by starting from the population-level bound of Kontorovich & Painsky (2025), replacing V*(p) and v*(p) with their empirical analogues, and controlling the resulting self-referential term via a continuity property of V* (|V*(p)−V*(q)| ≤ ‖p−q‖∞ log(1/‖p−q‖∞)) combined with a concentration inequality for the empirical functionals. A complementary refined bound via local Glivenko-Cantelli functionals S(p) and M(p) decomposes the error into a sub-Gaussian variance term √(S(p)/n) and a sub-gamma tail term M(p)/n, with a union-bound argument over coordinates using anticoncentration lower bounds for binomial tails to establish the high-probability guarantee.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax expected ℓ∞ estimation error over all discrete distributions is Θ(n^{-1/2}), with the two-point distribution p* = (1/2, 1/2, 0, …) identified as least-favorable up to universal constants, uniformly for all sufficiently large n (Theorem 2.1). A fully empirical high-probability bound (Theorem 2.2) is proved, depending on the data only through plug-in statistics v̂* and V̂*, with the form c₁√(V̂*/n + v̂*/n · log(1/δ)) + c₂(log(n/δ)/n + log n/n). A refined distribution-dependent bound via local Glivenko-Cantelli functionals (Theorem 2.4) shows the error is governed by max(√(S(p)/n), M(p)/n) · log(1/δ), capturing three qualitatively different regimes: variance domination, tail domination, and highly spread-out profiles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole empirical comparison is against the bounds of Kontorovich & Painsky (JMLR 2025), which the authors reproduce and extend. Kontorovich & Painsky provided partially empirical high-probability bounds expressed in terms of the unknown population functionals V*(p) and v*(p), along with a fully empirical version that was not tight. The present paper's Theorem 2.2 delivers a fully empirical bound (depending only on observable plug-in quantities v̂* and V̂*) that is tighter in the distribution-dependent regime while retaining the correct worst-case √(log(1/δ)/n) scaling. In the experiments (Figures 1–3, log-log scale), the authors' bound tracks the oracle empirical (1−δ)-quantile more closely than the K&P bounds across all four evaluation settings: uniform over A=100 symbols, Zipf(s=1.1) over A=100, and the two real-world datasets (Surnames and Hamilton). The advantage is most visible in the Zipf and real-world settings where the tail profile is non-trivial, confirming that the fully empirical bound adapts to the unknown distribution without requiring knowledge of V*(p) or v*(p).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the nonasymptotic ℓ∞ distribution estimation framework initiated by Kontorovich & Painsky (JMLR 2025), resolving two of their open questions: a fully empirical high-probability bound and identification of the worst-case extremal distribution. The technical machinery draws heavily on the local Glivenko-Cantelli (LGC) framework of Cohen & Kontorovich (COLT 2023) and Blanchard & Voráček (ALT 2024), whose sharp characterizations of the independent Bernoulli model are leveraged via a decoupling reduction from the dependent multinomial setting (using Blanchard et al., COLT 2024). The paper thus sits at the intersection of classical minimax distribution estimation and the newer dimension-free LGC theory, translating LGC functionals S(p) and M(p) into concrete regime descriptions for sup-norm estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a fixed two-point distribution as least-favorable (independent of n) simplifies the minimax picture considerably and rules out more complex n-dependent extremal constructions. The fully empirical bound is practically useful for applications like uniform calibration and anomaly detection where the true distribution is unknown and one needs data-driven confidence intervals. However, the constants in the empirical bound are not optimized, and the LGC-based Theorem 2.4, while conceptually clean, involves functionals S(p) and M(p) that are not directly computable from data. Open questions include whether the universal constants can be sharpened, whether the two-term regime picture (variance vs. tail domination) admits a single unified characterization, and whether analogous results extend to other norms or structured distribution classes.
