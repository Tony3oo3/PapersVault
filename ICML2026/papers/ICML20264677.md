## Problem

Existing graph-based RAG systems operate in a static, stateless manner: once the index is constructed, its topology is fixed and does not incorporate feedback from historical retrievals. Related or recurring queries therefore repeat similar multi-hop traversals, incurring redundant computation and preventing the system from adapting to evolving query demands. The paper asks whether retrieval memory can be updated online, in a training-free way, so that past retrieval experience makes future evidence discovery faster and more accurate.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language query (optionally containing temporal constraints) and a pre-built document corpus as input. The corpus is segmented into sentences and annotated with named entities via spaCy to form a hierarchical entity–sentence–passage graph; no explicit relation extraction is required. At inference time the system outputs an LLM-generated answer together with updated per-sentence memory states—two d-dimensional vectors (task and time channels) each paired with a scalar uncertainty in [0,1]—that persist across queries and modulate subsequent retrieval.

## Method

GAM-RAG builds a lightweight, relation-free hierarchical graph from entity co-occurrence and performs memory-guided iterative retrieval: query entities activate graph nodes, scores propagate through entity–sentence and sentence–passage incidence matrices re-weighted by sentence-level memory, and sentence-to-entity re-activation enables multi-hop expansion. After each retrieval episode, an LLM-as-judge provides binary support feedback on selected sentences, triggering a Kalman-inspired gain-adaptive update: the memory state is corrected along the query direction with gain K = π/(π + R), where π is the current uncertainty and R is an asymmetric observation-noise term (larger for negative feedback to protect potential bridge evidence). As π decreases over repeated updates, the gain shrinks, producing fast warm-up for uncertain memories and damped refinement for stable ones. A theoretical analysis proves that under consistent feedback the projected memory support converges to the target at a geometric rate (1−κ)^n, guaranteeing both attraction of supportive evidence and repulsion of misleading evidence.

## Results

GAM-RAG achieves 64.20% GPT-Acc on 2Wiki, 74.90% on HotpotQA, 43.30% on MuSiQue, 49.45% on TimeQA, and 65.47% on Medical, outperforming the strongest baseline (REMINDRAG) by an average of 3.95% across all five benchmarks. With 5-turn memorization, gains expand to 8.19% average improvement (e.g., 71.80% GPT-Acc on 2Wiki vs. 65.90% for REMINDRAG-5turn), while simultaneously reducing end-to-end inference latency by 61% (3.11s vs. 7.92s on repeated queries). The method also reduces offline indexing cost to 1.66M tokens and 1.03ks, compared to 4.28M/3.21ks for REMINDRAG and 9.54M/7.75ks for DyG-RAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAM-RAG outperforms the strongest experience-aware baseline REMINDRAG on four of five benchmarks: 2Wiki (64.20 vs. 61.40 GPT-Acc), HotpotQA (74.90 vs. 74.20), MuSiQue (43.30 vs. 35.30, a +8.0-point margin), and Medical (65.47 vs. 64.25), while trailing on TimeQA (49.45 vs. 43.89) where DyG-RAG's event-graph temporal anchoring gives it the edge (51.26). Against graph-search baselines, GAM-RAG leads LinearRAG by 2.5–6.1 points on GPT-Acc across multi-hop datasets and surpasses GFM-RAG, HippoRAG2, and PoG by 4–12 points on MuSiQue, where multi-hop compositional reasoning is most stressed. All vanilla RAG baselines (GritLM-7B, NV-Embed-v2, all-mpnet) are outperformed by 15–20+ points on multi-hop QA, confirming the benefit of structured graph propagation over flat dense retrieval. Under 5-turn memorization, GAM-RAG extends its lead over REMINDRAG-5turn to 5.9 points on 2Wiki (71.80 vs. 65.90) while cutting per-query latency from 7.92s to 3.11s, demonstrating that the Kalman-inspired gain update converts accumulated experience into both accuracy and speed gains without additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAM-RAG sits at the intersection of graph-based RAG (HippoRAG, LinearRAG, GFM-RAG) and memory-augmented retrieval (REMINDRAG), but departs from both by replacing explicit relation extraction with a lightweight entity co-occurrence hierarchy and by introducing a Kalman-filter-inspired, uncertainty-aware gain rule for online sentence-memory updates. The core update equations (Eqs. 10–13) are a direct repurposing of the Kalman state/uncertainty recursion (Welch & Bishop, 1995) to the retrieval-feedback setting, while the Hebbian 'fire together, wire together' framing from cognitive neuroscience motivates the co-occurrence-based graph topology. Unlike REMINDRAG, which caches traversal traces as replayable paths, GAM-RAG maintains per-sentence vector memories with explicit confidence terms, enabling selective reinforcement or suppression rather than wholesale path reuse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, inference-time adaptation paradigm is practically attractive: the 61% latency reduction after five memorization turns and the 8.19% accuracy gain over the zero-turn baseline suggest that RAG systems need not be rebuilt or retrained to benefit from query history. However, the approach depends on an LLM-as-judge for feedback signals, which introduces its own cost and potential bias; the asymmetric noise design (R_neg > R_pos) is heuristic rather than derived from a principled model of judge error. Open questions include scalability to very large or rapidly changing corpora, sensitivity to the quality of the judge model, and whether the 1D projected-state analysis generalizes to the full high-dimensional update dynamics that actually occur in practice.
