## Problem

Existing multi-label medical image diagnosis methods reduce the task to independent binary relevance modeling with rigid discriminative boundaries, implicitly assuming that co-occurring pathologies are separable. This contradicts two intrinsic properties of medical images: visual polysemy (a single visual observation can simultaneously support multiple disease labels) and structured disease semantic correlation (diseases are correlated and often asymmetrically related rather than orthogonal). Enforcing hard decision boundaries under such overlap suppresses shared visual evidence, biases feature representations, and undermines diagnostic reliability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single medical image (resized to 224×224) as primary input, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone to produce a dense feature map. It additionally consumes disease name text embeddings (extracted via a pre-trained CLIP text encoder) and a data-derived comorbidity adjacency matrix to construct semantic anchors. The output is a vector of fuzzy membership scores in [0,1] for each of C disease categories, directly interpreted as per-disease prediction probabilities at inference time.

## Method

FACT reformulates MLD as a metric-based fuzzy alignment problem between two constructed representations: (1) an Atomic Visual Space built via vector quantization that decomposes image features into shared, reusable visual primitives capturing polysemy, and (2) comorbidity topology-encoded semantic anchors produced by a 2-layer GCN that propagates disease name embeddings through a clinical co-occurrence graph. A semantic interface operator (permutation-invariant attention with sigmoid gating) aggregates visual atoms into disease-specific evidence vectors, which are then aligned to semantic anchors via a Gaussian fuzzy membership function derived from an RKHS variational formulation. The training objective combines a point-level fuzzy loss (Log-Sum-Exp over per-disease odds ratios to prioritize hard errors), a set-level fuzzy Jaccard loss grounded in Lukasiewicz fuzzy logic to enforce structural consistency across co-occurring labels, and a standard VQ commitment loss to stabilize the atomic visual codebook.

## Results

FACT achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three primary benchmarks: on ODIR-5K it reaches 72.61% mAP, 90.91% F1, and 92.97% AUC (avg 85.50); on NIH-Chest it attains 58.95% mAP, 90.44% F1, and 87.81% AUC (avg 79.07); and on RSNA-IHD it scores 89.72% mAP, 92.97% F1, and 96.90% AUC (avg 93.20). The largest margins over the second-best method are +2.92% mAP on ODIR-5K and +2.25% mAP on RSNA-IHD. FACT also achieves the lowest Confident Error Rate (CER) on all datasets (16.22%, 14.48%, 9.09%), indicating substantially better calibration than competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACT outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all three primary datasets, with no losses or ties. The most substantial gains appear on ODIR-5K (fundus images, 8 diseases), where FACT leads CTransCNN by +2.92% mAP and LDR by +2.92% mAP, and on RSNA-IHD (brain CT, 5 hemorrhage subtypes), where it surpasses MultiCo by +2.25% mAP and TADCL by +2.25% mAP. On NIH-Chest (chest X-rays, 14 pathologies), the margin is tighter (+0.13% mAP over SupCon), reflecting the already high AUC ceiling of that dataset. Among objective-centric baselines, ASL and RAL show the weakest mAP (59.69% and 59.61% on ODIR-5K), confirming that loss-function-only modifications without structural decomposition struggle with visual polysemy. Among network-centric baselines, CTransCNN and LDR are the strongest competitors but still trail FACT by 2.9–3.3% mAP on ODIR-5K. In reliability terms, FACT reduces the Confident Error Rate by 3.2–13.6 percentage points compared to the best competing method (MultiCo or HydraViT) across the three datasets, with the largest improvement on RSNA-IHD (9.09% vs. 15.34% for HydraViT).

## Relation to Prior Work

FACT departs from the dominant MLD paradigm of rigid discriminative partitioning (independent sigmoid classifiers with BCE or contrastive objectives) by reformulating diagnosis as a metric-based fuzzy alignment between atomic visual evidence and disease semantic anchors. It draws on three distinct threads: (1) RKHS variational methods (Wahba, 2002) to derive a principled Gaussian membership kernel; (2) vector quantization (VQ-VAE lineage) to construct a shared, reusable atomic visual vocabulary that captures visual polysemy; and (3) GCN-based propagation over a comorbidity graph to encode asymmetric disease dependencies into semantic anchors initialized from CLIP text embeddings. Unlike prior fuzzy-learning approaches that treat fuzziness as an internal regularization layer before defuzzification, FACT makes the fuzzy membership function itself the learning objective, preserving graded non-exclusive alignment throughout optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural mismatch in MLD—enforcing separable decision boundaries on inherently overlapping pathological evidence—and provides a mathematically grounded alternative that improves both accuracy and calibration (CER nearly halved on RSNA-IHD). The finding that the Gaussian membership operator must be paired with a compatible fuzzy loss (performance collapses under standard BCE) highlights a non-trivial coupling between representation and objective that practitioners should consider. Open questions include whether the comorbidity graph prior generalizes to unseen disease combinations, whether the fixed Gaussian kernel can be adapted per-disease, and whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional label spaces beyond the 5–40 diseases tested. The reliance on a ResNet-50 backbone and patient-level splits (rather than the more standard image-level splits in some benchmarks) also limits direct comparability with some recent results.
