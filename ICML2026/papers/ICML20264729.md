## Problem

Adapting LLMs to low-resource domains is hindered by scarce domain-specific data, and existing data selection methods (LESS, DSIR, TSDS) assume large in-domain candidate pools or curated validation sets that are unavailable in cross-domain, low-resource settings. This paper asks whether useful general-domain auxiliary data can be systematically mined to complement limited domain supervision, and proposes a principled criterion for identifying which general-domain samples most benefit target-domain fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small domain dataset (1K instruction-response pairs), a large general-domain candidate corpus (1.8M chain-of-thought samples from the CoT Collection), and a pretrained LLM (Llama3-8B-Instruct or Qwen3-8B). It produces a 9K-sample auxiliary subset that is mixed with the domain data for LoRA fine-tuning. The selection pipeline operates in two stages: a coarse embedding-based KNN pre-selection reduces the candidate pool to M=4N samples, followed by a fine-grained NTK scoring stage that ranks candidates by their average kernel similarity to domain points, with gradients restricted to LoRA parameters and randomly projected to 8192 dimensions for tractability.

## Method

NTK-Selector is a two-stage data selection framework grounded in the Neural Tangent Kernel. The key theoretical contribution is demonstrating that pretrained LLMs exhibit NTK-like behavior during fine-tuning (Frobenius cosine similarity > 0.99 between the time-evolved and initial kernel), which justifies using the initialization-time kernel as a stable proxy for sample influence throughout training. To make NTK computation practical, the paper introduces a Jacobian-free approximation that replaces the full D×D kernel matrix with a scalar computed from the gradient of the summed output, showing empirically and theoretically that cross-output gradient terms are negligible (similarity < 0.02) due to near-orthogonality in high-dimensional parameter space. Scalability is achieved by restricting gradient estimation to LoRA parameters (~0.5% of full model dimensionality) and applying Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projections to further compress the gradient space. The coarse pre-selection stage uses warm-up LoRA-trained embeddings with KNN to filter the 1.8M candidate pool, and the fine-grained stage assigns each pre-selected candidate a utility score equal to its mean NTK similarity across all domain points, selecting the top-N by this score.

## Results

NTK-Selector achieves the highest average accuracy across all 8 tasks on both Llama3-8B-Instruct (76.6%) and Qwen3-8B (78.6%), yielding gains of +8.7 and +5.1 points over the base model, compared to only +0.8 and +0.9 from domain-only fine-tuning. On Llama3-8B-Instruct, NTK-Selector leads the next-best baselines (LESS and TSDS at 74.8) by 1.8 points; on Qwen3-8B, it leads DSIR (77.7) by 0.9 points and LESS (73.7) by 4.9 points. NTK-Selector is the only method that improves performance on every individual task for both models, including MMLU-Med and RSDD where other selection methods often degrade results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTK-Selector consistently outperforms all three data-selection baselines (DSIR, LESS, TSDS) on both model architectures. On Llama3-8B-Instruct, the margin is modest but uniform: NTK-Selector (76.6 avg) beats DSIR (74.7), LESS (74.8), and TSDS (74.8) by 1.8–1.9 points. On Qwen3-8B, the advantage is more pronounced, particularly over LESS (73.7, a 4.9-point gap) and TSDS (75.0, a 3.6-point gap), while DSIR (77.7) is the closest competitor at 0.9 points behind. A critical distinction is consistency: NTK-Selector is the only method that improves on all 8 tasks for both models, whereas LESS causes a 19.8-point drop on FPB for Qwen3-8B and TSDS degrades PrivacyQA by 2.2 points. The paper also notes that domain-only fine-tuning can be harmful (e.g., −18.8 on ContractNLI for Llama3-8B-Instruct), underscoring that NTK-Selector's gains come from selecting auxiliary data whose optimization trajectories align with the target domain rather than from simply adding more data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel framework (Jacot et al., 2018) from its original setting of infinite-width, randomly initialized networks to pretrained LLMs in the fine-tuning regime, providing the first empirical evidence of stable NTK-like behavior (Frobenius cosine similarity > 0.99) during LoRA-based instruction tuning. It builds on the pseudo-NTK approximation of Mohamadi & Sutherland (2022) but generalizes it to pretrained, finite-width LLMs under high-dimensional assumptions grounded in Neural Collapse and outlier-dimension literature. In the data selection lineage, it departs from task-specific coreset methods (LESS, DSIR, TSDS) that require in-domain candidate pools or validation sets, instead formulating a cross-domain mining problem where auxiliary data is drawn from a large general-purpose corpus (CoT Collection) guided by kernel alignment with a small domain seed set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NTK-like behavior finding is conceptually significant: it suggests that fine-tuning pretrained LLMs primarily amplifies feature directions established at initialization rather than learning new orthogonal features, which has broader implications for understanding LLM optimization dynamics. Practically, the method provides a principled, scalable alternative to ad-hoc data mixing for low-resource domains, with the caveat that the full pipeline requires ~31 GPU-hours on an A100 and a candidate pool of ~1.8M samples. Open questions include whether the NTK-like property holds for larger models (70B+), different fine-tuning strategies (full fine-tuning vs. LoRA), and whether the ~10x improvement over domain-only training persists when the domain-only baseline is stronger (e.g., with more domain data or better initialization). The finding that random auxiliary data helps weaker models (Llama3) but hurts stronger ones (Qwen3) also raises questions about the interaction between model capacity and data quality sensitivity.
