## Problem

Calibration-free, uniform weight quantization of LLMs suffers significant perplexity degradation at ≤4 bits because a single per-row scale cannot handle outliers without degrading precision for all other elements in that row. Prior activation-aware methods (e.g., AWQ) require calibration data to determine column-wise input scales, introducing compute overhead and potential overfitting. This paper asks whether the weight matrix structure alone can recover the column scales that activation-aware quantization would use, enabling calibration-free pseudo-activation-awareness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-precision weight matrix W ∈ ℝ^{N×M} (typically a linear layer's weights) and produces a quantized integer matrix Q at the target bit-width, a row scale vector s ∈ ℝ^N, a column scale vector t ∈ ℝ^M, and a shift vector z ∈ ℝ^N, all computed per tile of configurable group size (default 64). The column scale t is applied as an element-wise multiply on the input activations at inference time (analogous to AWQ), so the forward pass adds only one extra vector-multiply per layer. A no-overhead variant absorbs t into preceding layers, at the cost of sharing scales across layers that share inputs (e.g., Q/K/V projections).

## Method

SINQ introduces a dual-scale parameterization W_approx = s ⊙ (Q + z) ⊙ t that decouples row and column scaling, allowing outlier impact to be traded off between dimensions. The key theoretical insight is that under Adam training, per-column weight standard deviation is inversely proportional to the square root of the input scale (s_W ∝ 1/√|s_x|), making column-wise weight statistics a calibration-free proxy for activation magnitudes. To exploit this without naively scaling columns (which increases row-wise kurtosis and creates new quantization errors), the paper proposes a damped log-space Sinkhorn-Knopp iteration that alternately normalizes row and column standard deviations toward a common target, minimizing a matrix imbalance metric. The algorithm runs in O(K·NM) time with K iterations (typically small), is architecture-agnostic with no inter-layer dependencies, and can be composed with AWQ calibration (yielding A-SINQ) or non-uniform NF4 levels. Empirically, SINQ reduces the perplexity gap to BF16 by over 50% versus uncalibrated uniform baselines at 3–4 bits across Qwen3, Llama, DeepSeek, and Phi model families, with negligible inference overhead (≤1.8% per-layer) and quantization time comparable to round-to-nearest.

## Results

On 4-bit uncalibrated uniform quantization of Qwen3-32B, SINQ achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 7.74 and C4 perplexity of 10.96, reducing the gap to the BF16 baseline (7.60 / 10.77) by over 89% relative to RTN (8.92 / 12.80). At 3-bit on Qwen3-1.7B, SINQ cuts the WikiText-2 perplexity gap from 15.76 (RTN) to 5.72, a 64% reduction. Across all Qwen3 model sizes (1.7B–32B) and both 3-bit and 4-bit settings, SINQ achieves the lowest perplexity among all uncalibrated methods, with quantization time only 1.09× that of RTN and a negligible 1.8% inference overhead at batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SINQ outperforms all three primary uncalibrated baselines (RTN, Hadamard+RTN, HQQ) on every model size and bit-width tested, with the largest gains at 3-bit (e.g., 10.04 ppl lower WikiText-2 than RTN on Qwen3-1.7B). Against non-uniform 4-bit methods (BnB-NF4, HIGGS), SINQ with NF4 levels achieves the best perplexity on Qwen3-1.7B and 14B, while uniform SINQ slightly edges out NF4 on the 32B model. In the calibrated setting, A-SINQ (SINQ + AWQ) beats both GPTQ and AWQ on WikiText-2 and C4 across all Qwen3 sizes (e.g., 16.67 vs. 16.90 for AWQ on Qwen3-1.7B 4-bit), and even uncalibrated SINQ matches or exceeds calibrated GPTQ on several configurations. Against codebook-based methods (QTIP, QUIP#), A-SINQ achieves comparable perplexity (5.22 vs. 5.17/5.22 on Llama2-7B) while being significantly faster at inference (up to 4.4× speedup over QTIP at N=512). Finally, SINQ outperforms three recent calibrated methods (OmniQuant, FlexRound, LRQ) on Llama-2 at both 3-bit and 4-bit without requiring any calibration data (e.g., 5.60 vs. 5.74–5.83 WikiText-2 on Llama-2-7B 4-bit).

## Relation to Prior Work

SINQ sits at the intersection of three PTQ research threads: (1) activation-aware quantization (AWQ, SmoothQuant), which uses calibration data to find input-channel scales; (2) weight-space transformations (Hadamard rotations in QuaRot, SpinQuant, HIGGS), which reshape the weight distribution to ease quantization; and (3) calibration-free uniform quantization (RTN, HQQ), which avoids calibration entirely. The paper's key departure is the dual-scale parameterization (one scale vector per matrix axis) combined with a Sinkhorn-Knopp-style iteration that simultaneously normalizes row and column standard deviations. This replaces the need for either calibration data (as in AWQ) or inter-layer rotation constraints (as in Hadamard-based methods) with a purely local, per-tile optimization that is architecture-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SINQ provides a practical, drop-in improvement for the most common LLM deployment scenario (weight-only, 3–4-bit, no calibration budget), reducing the perplexity gap to full precision by over 50% relative to uncalibrated baselines while adding negligible inference overhead. Its architecture-agnosticism (each linear layer quantized independently) makes it immediately applicable to new model families, MoE architectures, and even encoder-only models, lowering the barrier to low-precision deployment. Open questions include whether the moderate R² (~0.16–0.36) between weight column std and activation magnitude can be further exploited, whether the approach extends naturally to joint weight-and-activation quantization, and whether the Sinkhorn normalization target (matrix imbalance) is truly optimal versus other proxy metrics like kurtosis.
