## Problem

Context-Entangled Content Segmentation (CECS) requires segmenting objects that share intrinsic visual patterns with their surroundings, yet existing methods focus almost exclusively on architectural enhancements while ignoring the learning dynamics that govern robustness under entangled data distributions. Standard curriculum learning is particularly problematic here because 'easy' camouflaged samples often contain spurious texture correlations that drive models into lazy, high-frequency shortcut regimes, degrading generalization on genuinely hard cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261542/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images resized to 352×352 and produces a binary segmentation mask, operating as a training-time procedure that is architecture-agnostic (compatible with CNNs like ResNet50/Res2Net50 and vision transformers like PVT V2). During the anti-curriculum phase, inputs are additionally processed through a 2D Fourier-domain circular low-pass filter (radius ratio r=0.95) before feeding the network, creating an information bottleneck without modifying the model. No extra parameters are introduced and inference remains identical to the base network.

## Method

CurriSeg is a dual-phase 'stabilize-then-perturb' training framework. Phase 1 (Robust Curriculum Selection) progressively expands the training set via a warm-up schedule based on IoU difficulty scores from periodic checkpoints, then applies temporal statistics-based sample weighting that tracks mean and variance of per-sample difficulty over K epochs to down-weight noisy or ambiguous samples while preserving hard-but-informative ones, combined with entropy-based pixel-level soft weighting that attenuates uncertain regions early in training. Phase 2 (Anti-Curriculum Promotion) introduces Spectral-Blindness Fine-Tuning, which attenuates high-frequency input components via a circular Fourier-domain mask, forcing the model to rely on low-frequency structural boundaries and contextual semantics rather than superficial texture shortcuts. The framework adds no parameters, requires no architectural changes, and actually reduces training time by 28.9–48.4% by concentrating computation on informative samples.

## Results

On the primary COD10K benchmark, CurriSeg applied to RUN with PVT V2 backbone achieves M=0.018, Fβ=0.828, Eφ=0.957, Sα=0.886, outperforming the original RUN (M=0.021, Fβ=0.810, Eφ=0.941, Sα=0.878) by 3.94% composite improvement. Across all four COD datasets (CHAMELEON, CAMO, COD10K, NC4K), CurriSeg yields consistent gains of 2.13%–3.94% depending on backbone. On polyp segmentation (CVC-ColonDB), RUN+ reaches mDice=0.845 and mIoU=0.763, surpassing the original RUN (0.822/0.742). Notably, training time is reduced by 28.9%–48.4% compared to standard training while GPU memory overhead is ≤0.1 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CurriSeg is applied as a training strategy on top of existing architectures (FEDER, FSEL, RUN, MEGANet, RFENet, CamoFormer, and others), and in every case it improves the original method: FEDER gains 2.46% composite on COD, FSEL gains 2.22%, RUN gains 2.13% (ResNet50) to 3.94% (PVT V2), MEGANet gains 2.24% on polyp segmentation, and RFENet gains 3.22% on transparent object detection. The largest absolute improvement is seen with RUN+ on PVT V2, where Fβ rises from 0.810 to 0.828 and Sα from 0.878 to 0.886 on COD10K, surpassing CamoDiff (Fβ=0.815) which was previously the strongest PVT V2-based method. On the concealed defect detection task (CDS2K), CamoFormer+ achieves Fmeanβ=0.346 vs. 0.329 for the original, and RUN+ reaches 0.310 vs. 0.299. Across weakly-supervised, semi-supervised, multi-modal, and video segmentation settings (Table 9), CurriSeg consistently improves baselines by 1–3% in Fβ, confirming architecture-agnostic applicability. Importantly, all gains are achieved without adding parameters, and training time is actually reduced by 28.9%–48.4% due to curriculum-based sample selection concentrating computation on informative samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) but makes a key conceptual departure: it argues that standard easy-to-hard CL is actively detrimental in context-entangled segmentation because 'easy' samples in camouflaged scenes contain spurious texture correlations that drive the model into lazy, high-frequency-dependent regimes. Rather than proposing a new architecture (as most prior CECS work does with feature aggregation, attention refinement, or prior injection), CurriSeg reorganizes the training procedure itself into a 'stabilize-then-perturb' trajectory, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing architectural advances. The anti-curriculum phase (Spectral-Blindness Fine-Tuning) extends beyond prior CL variants by deliberately increasing input difficulty through frequency-domain manipulation, a mechanism not present in self-paced learning, teacher-student curricula, or loss-based ranking schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CurriSeg provides a general, architecture-agnostic training paradigm that consistently improves performance across a remarkably wide range of dense prediction tasks (camouflage, polyp, glass, defect, video, semantic/instance segmentation, infrared detection, shadow detection) and foundation model adapters, while simultaneously reducing training time by 29–48% with no added parameters. The finding that standard curriculum learning is counterproductive in feature-entangled settings challenges a widely held assumption and suggests that difficulty ordering must account for spurious correlation structure, not just loss magnitude. Open questions include whether the spectral blindness mechanism transfers to tasks where high-frequency cues are genuinely informative (e.g., fine-grained texture classification), and whether the curriculum hyperparameters (Tc, r, p_min) require per-domain tuning or can be set universally.
