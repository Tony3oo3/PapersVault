## Problem

Foundation models for physical simulation have been limited to relatively homogeneous data (single dimensionality, fixed resolution), while real-world physics spans 2D and 3D systems at varying resolutions and with diverse physical fields. Additionally, learned emulators suffer from spectral artifacts that accumulate during autoregressive rollouts, causing instability over long prediction horizons. These obstacles—data heterogeneity, long-term instability, and inefficient distributed training on mixed workloads—have prevented the development of truly cross-domain physics foundation models.

## Input / Output

WALRUS takes a short sequence of τ spatiotemporal snapshots (6 frames for 2D, 3 for 3D) of continuous-valued physical fields at arbitrary resolutions and predicts the next-step change Δu, which is then added to the current state for autoregressive rollout. The model natively handles both 2D and 3D data by embedding 2D fields as zero-padded planes in 3D space, and adapts to varying resolutions through Convolutional Stride Modulation that normalizes internal token counts to 32²/dim (2D) or 16³/dim (3D). The output is de-normalized by the RMS of the historical ΔU, and the model must infer relative timescales from context since time strides are randomly sampled (1–5 steps) during training.

## Method

WALRUS is a 1.3B-parameter space-time factorized transformer with alternating spatial blocks (parallel attention with axial RoPE) and temporal blocks (causal attention with T5-style relative position encoding), using hMLP encoders/decoders with Convolutional Stride Modulation for adaptive-compute tokenization. The key stabilization contribution is patch jittering: a harmonic-analysis-derived method that randomly translates input before strided-convolution tokenization and inverts the shift after reconstruction, provably eliminating aliasing terms in expectation and reducing median long-horizon VRMSE by 54% across 84% of pretraining scenarios. For training efficiency on heterogeneous 2D/3D data, the paper introduces a topology-aware sampling strategy adapted from HSDP where ranks within a sharding group sample the same dataset while groups sample independently, combined with differential batch sizes, yielding 262% throughput improvement over naive FSDP. Pretraining uses aggressive diversity-focused augmentation including tensor-law-aware 90° rotations in the combined 2D-3D space and variable time-striding, with a normalized L1 loss that down-weights rapidly changing fields.

## Results

WALRUS (1.3B parameters) achieves an average 63.6% reduction in one-step VRMSE, 56.2% reduction over 1–10 step rollouts, and 48.3% reduction over 11–30 step rollouts compared to the best prior foundation model across 26 downstream tasks. In the cross-domain evaluation on pretraining data, WALRUS achieves top performance on 18/19 one-step tasks (52.2% average reduction) and 17/19 at 20 steps (30.5% average reduction). Overall, WALRUS attains state-of-the-art results on 56 out of 65 metrics across all evaluated tasks, with particularly strong gains on 3D problems (e.g., RT Instability 3D: 0.0496 vs. DPOT-H's 0.3191 at 20 steps) and inviscid fluid dynamics (MultiQuadrantsO: 0.0587 vs. DPOT-H's 0.4801 at 20 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WALRUS is compared against three prior foundation models: MPP-AViT-L (407M, axial attention), Poseidon-L (628M, Swin attention), and DPOT-H (1.2B, AFNO/Fourier-based). WALRUS decisively outperforms all three baselines on the vast majority of tasks, with the largest margins on 3D problems (e.g., RT Instability 3D at 20 steps: 0.0496 vs. DPOT-H's 0.3191, a 6.4× improvement) and inviscid fluid dynamics (MultiQuadrantsO at 20 steps: 0.0587 vs. DPOT-H's 0.4801). DPOT-H's Fourier-based AFNO architecture retains a narrow edge on smooth linear acoustics/wave propagation tasks at longer horizons (e.g., Maze 21–60 steps: 0.0543 vs. WALRUS's 0.0560), consistent with spectral methods' natural advantage in smooth linear settings. Poseidon-L is competitive on inviscid Euler-equation tasks (MultiQuadrantsP) due to its pretraining on those equations, but is outperformed by WALRUS on nearly all other categories. MPP-AViT-L, the smallest model at 407M, is consistently the weakest baseline, with WALRUS achieving 2–10× lower VRMSE on most tasks. Notably, WALRUS is the only model applicable to all 3D tasks, as MPP-AViT-L and Poseidon-L are 2D-only architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

WALRUS extends the autoregressive pretraining paradigm for physical dynamics introduced by MPP (McCabe et al., 2023a) and the efficient PDE foundation model line of work (Poseidon, DPOT) by being the first to jointly handle 2D and 3D data at varying resolutions within a single transformer. Architecturally, it builds on space-time factorized transformers with axial RoPE and parallel attention, but introduces three key departures: a harmonic-analysis-grounded patch jittering method for rollout stability, convolutional stride modulation (CSM) for compute-adaptive tokenization across resolutions, and a topology-aware sampling strategy adapted from HSDP to balance heterogeneous 2D/3D workloads under FSDP. The diversity-first training philosophy—aggressively augmenting 2D data into 3D space with tensor-aware rotations and variable time-striding—represents a deliberate departure from prior foundation models that trained on homogeneous, fixed-resolution data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WALRUS establishes that cross-domain, multi-dimensional pretraining is both feasible and beneficial for continuum dynamics emulation, with the critical finding that diversity-focused augmentation can degrade in-distribution pretraining metrics while substantially improving downstream transfer—a result that challenges the common practice of selecting pretraining strategies based on in-distribution loss. Open questions remain around non-uniform geometries, stochastic/poorly-observed systems (where deterministic reconstruction loss may be a bottleneck), and the trade-off between history-based in-context learning and explicit parameterization. The 262% throughput improvement from topology-aware sampling and the 54% long-horizon VRMSE reduction from patch jittering are practically significant for scaling physics foundation models to real scientific workloads.
