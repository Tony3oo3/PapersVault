## Problem

In moderate-dimensional logistic regression where p scales with n, the bias and variance factors governing estimator behavior depend on the unknown signal strength κ, making valid inference (confidence intervals, error prediction) impossible without a consistent κ estimate. Existing approaches are either heuristic without rigorous justification, fail when data are separable and the MLE does not exist, or require knowledge of the true covariance matrix Σ. The paper fills this gap with a theoretically grounded, covariance-adaptive procedure that remains valid across a wide range of signal strengths and sampling ratios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of n paired observations (X_i, Y_i) with Gaussian features, a fixed regularization level λ (typically 0.1), and the aspect ratio δ = n/p. It produces a consistent signal strength estimate κ̂ via bisection on a theoretical map, which is then plugged into a system of equations to recover bias and variance factors (α̂*, σ̂*). These factors enable construction of adjusted 95% confidence intervals for individual regression coefficients, with the interval width scaled by σ̂*/(v_j√p) and centered at β̂_{λ,j}/α̂*. For general covariance, the method additionally requires a leave-one-out stability condition on the estimator.

## Method

The core contribution is a data-splitting strategy built on a non-decomposable generalized ridge estimator whose regularizer is weighted by the sample covariance matrix (∑(X_i^Tβ)²/n), ensuring the estimator exists for δ > 1 even with separable data. The key theoretical insight is that the combined proxy κ²α*² + σ*² is not one-to-one with κ (distinct κ values can yield the same proxy), but σ*² and κ²α*² are each strictly monotone in κ, enabling unique recovery. The data are split into two equal halves; the variance factor is estimated by (1/2)||β̂₁ − β̂₂||² and the signal component by ⟨β̂₁, β̂₂⟩, with the cross-term vanishing asymptotically due to orthogonality of independent noise directions. For unknown covariance, a leave-one-out construction (S₁, S₂, S₃ statistics) replaces direct Σ-dependent quantities with fully computable empirical analogs. Consistency of κ̂ follows from the monotonicity of the theoretical map F(κ) and the consistency of the empirical proxy estimators, under a mild identifiability assumption bounding the derivative of F away from zero.

## Results

Under identity covariance (Σ=I_p) with δ=3 and p=800, the σ*-based data-splitting estimator achieves |κ−κ̂| = 0.106 for κ=2, 0.155 for κ=3, and 0.244 for κ=4, with 95% CI coverage at 0.946–0.950. The κ²α*²-based variant is less accurate, with |κ−κ̂| reaching 0.507 at κ=4, p=800. On the real scRNA-seq glucocorticoid response dataset (n=2400, p=600, δ=4), the method estimates κ̂=2.22 and identifies 23 significant genes, 13 of which overlap with prior findings from Dai et al. (2023b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its data-splitting (DS) method against two MLE-based baselines: SLOE (Yadlowsky et al., 2021), which uses leave-one-out to estimate the limiting variance, and ProbeFrontier (Sur & Candès, 2019), which uses repeated subsampling to locate the MLE existence phase transition. In the non-separable regime (where the MLE exists), all three methods perform comparably in estimating κ, with accuracy improving as sample size grows (Table 6, Section A.5). The critical distinction is that SLOE and ProbeFrontier both rely on the MLE existing, and therefore fail entirely when data are separable—a common occurrence at small δ or large κ (e.g., 95–100% of datasets are separable at δ=3, κ=3). The proposed DS method, by using a generalized ridge regularizer with sample-covariance weighting, remains valid in the separable regime where both baselines break down. Among the two DS variants, the σ*-based estimator (Algorithm 2) consistently outperforms the κ²α*²-based estimator (Algorithm 1), particularly at higher signal strengths: at δ=3, κ=4, p=800, the σ*-based method achieves |κ−κ̂|=0.244 versus 0.507 for the κ²α*²-based method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the asymptotic theory of Sur & Candès (2019) and Salehi et al. (2019) for high-dimensional logistic regression by analyzing a non-decomposable, sample-covariance-weighted ridge regularizer that adapts to the true covariance structure, going beyond the decomposable regularizer framework of prior work. It builds on the data-splitting technique of Dai et al. (2023b) for FDR control and the arbitrary-covariance asymptotics of Zhao et al. (2022), but repurposes data splitting to consistently estimate the bias and variance factors themselves rather than bypassing them. A key conceptual departure is showing that the proxy η² = κ²α*² + σ*² used by Yadlowsky et al. (2021) is not one-to-one with the signal strength κ, undermining the theoretical foundation of the SLOE method in small-δ regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically principled, covariance-adaptive procedure for estimating signal strength in moderate-dimensional logistic regression that remains valid when data are separable and the MLE does not exist—a common practical scenario. The method enables valid confidence intervals and variable selection without requiring knowledge of the true covariance matrix, which is unavailable in practice. Open questions include extending the theory beyond Gaussian designs (only numerically verified here), understanding why the proxy-to-κ mapping slope vanishes at signal-strength extremes, and reducing the coverage inflation under general covariance caused by estimating conditional variances v_j.
