## Problem

Existing diffusion-based time series forecasting methods apply uniform Gaussian noise across all time steps, implicitly treating all frequency components equally. This creates a fundamental imbalance: low-energy high-frequency details are overwhelmed early in the diffusion process while high-energy low-frequency trends remain inadequately corrupted, leading to premature destruction of informative structure and inefficient learning. The paper asks whether the noise schedule should adapt to the non-uniform energy distribution across frequency bands.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series history window (96 steps × F variates, normalized via Z-Score or MinMax without RevIN) and produces a future forecast over horizons up to 720 steps via autoregressive rolling of a 6-step prediction. Internally, the input is augmented with a linear-extrapolation guess for the future segment, then decomposed via J-level DWT into frequency bands; the model operates entirely in the wavelet coefficient domain and reconstructs the time series via IDWT at inference. A binary mask derived from wavelet receptive-field analysis delineates which coefficients are influenced by the future segment, enabling selective denoising.

## Method

EADiff decomposes the input into wavelet frequency bands and introduces a learnable energy-adaptive noise scheduler: per-instance band energy is estimated via log-standard-deviation of coefficients, centered across bands, and passed through a tanh-gated exponential with a single learnable parameter γ to produce per-position noise multipliers that modulate an EDM-style base schedule. The denoising backbone is a DiT with band-specific tokenization (dense stride-1 for the approximation band, 50%-overlapping patches for detail bands) and adaptive layer norm conditioning on the low-frequency trend coefficients. Training applies differentiated noise—light augmentation on history (k ≤ 0.2) and full diffusion on the future—with EDM-weighted MSE loss restricted to future-region coefficients. Inference uses an SDEdit-style initialization from the partially-noised input, a Heun ODE solver, and a hard replacement mechanism that freezes history coefficients at every step, ensuring the observed segment remains unchanged while the future is iteratively refined.

## Results

Under a unified autoregressive protocol (predict 6 steps, roll out to H∈{48,…,720}), EADiff achieves the best or near-best RMSE across five benchmarks. On the highly non-stationary Exchange dataset, EADiff reduces RMSE from 2.94 (TimeMixer) to 0.50 at H=96 and from 17.7 (TimeMixer) to 1.05 at H=720. On ETT-h1 at H=720, EADiff achieves RMSE 1.031, outperforming CSDI (1.072) and iTransformer (1.087). On Weather at H=720, EADiff reaches RMSE 0.933, competitive with iTransformer (0.925) while far surpassing PatchTST (1.546) and CSDI (1.122).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the time-series diffusion baseline CSDI, EADiff achieves substantial RMSE reductions on Exchange (0.500 vs 0.710 at H=96; 1.053 vs 1.189 at H=720) and ETT-h1 (0.837 vs 0.870 at H=96), validating the wavelet-domain latent space over pure time-domain diffusion. Against Transformer-based forecasting models (TimeMixer, iTransformer, PatchTST, FEDformer), EADiff dominates on the non-stationary Exchange dataset where TimeMixer degrades catastrophically (RMSE 17.7 at H=720) and PatchTST reaches 2.45, while EADiff stays at 1.05. On ETT-h2 and Weather at long horizons, iTransformer edges out EADiff slightly (RMSE 0.731 vs 0.775 on ETT-h2 H=720; 0.925 vs 0.933 on Weather H=720), suggesting that explicit channel-independent attention retains an advantage on strongly periodic or smooth signals. The tabular diffusion baselines TabDiff and TabSyn either fail to scale (OOM on Weather) or produce NaN outputs, confirming that ignoring temporal inductive biases makes them unsuitable for multivariate time series forecasting. Overall, EADiff wins or ties on 8 of 10 dataset-horizon combinations in the main table, with its largest margins on non-stationary and high-variance data.

## Relation to Prior Work

EADiff extends the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) and SDEdit-style conditional generation (Meng et al., 2022) into the wavelet domain for time series forecasting, departing from prior diffusion-based time series methods (CSDI, TimeGrad) that operate exclusively in the temporal domain with uniform noise. It also builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) for the denoising backbone and draws conceptual inspiration from multi-scale decomposition approaches (MG-TSD, mr-diff) that separately model trend and seasonal components, but unifies them under a single energy-adaptive diffusion process rather than separate sub-models. The key conceptual departure is treating the noise schedule as a per-instance, per-frequency-band quantity modulated by learned energy statistics, rather than a fixed global function of the diffusion step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode of uniform-noise diffusion on time series (premature destruction of high-frequency details while under-diffusing dominant trends), providing a principled fix that is validated through ablations. The sparsity paradox finding—that the theoretically sparsest wavelet basis (bior3.1) performs worst due to extreme inter-band energy imbalance—offers practical guidance for basis selection in frequency-domain generative modeling. Limitations include evaluation on only five datasets with a fixed short autoregressive horizon (6 steps), no comparison of probabilistic calibration (e.g., CRPS), and the wavelet mask computation being dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the energy-adaptive principle generalizes to other generative tasks (imputation, anomaly detection) and whether the wavelet domain is truly necessary or whether a learned frequency decomposition could replace it.
