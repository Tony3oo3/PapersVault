## Problem

Fitting multi-compartment Hodgkin-Huxley models to capture cell-specific neural dynamics has relied on invasive intracellular recordings that are low-throughput and cannot scale to large neural populations. Extracellular multi-electrode array (MEA) recordings capture many neurons simultaneously, but parameter degeneracy—many combinations of conductances, morphologies, and electrode positions producing similar extracellular waveforms—has prevented reliable biophysical inference from extracellular data alone, blocking applications like predicting responses to candidate neurostimulation patterns.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spike-sorted electrical images (EIs) from a 512-electrode, 30 μm-pitch MEA recording light-evoked retinal activity, along with per-electrode anodic and cathodic stimulation thresholds (the current amplitude at which spiking probability crosses 50%) and the known electrode array geometry. It outputs cell-specific HH parameters—Na⁺/K⁺ conductance densities, compartment radii, and 3D axonal morphology (parameterized by B-spline control points)—which are then used with the forward stimulation model to predict binary spike/no-spike outcomes for previously unseen simultaneous multi-electrode current combinations, replacing hours of empirical stimulus testing with minutes of model-based prediction.

## Method

The key insight is replacing direct waveform matching with a set of biophysically interpretable, fully differentiable EI features—per-electrode capacitive, sodium, and potassium peak amplitudes, spike duration (Na⁺-to-K⁺ peak interval), and pairwise sodium-peak propagation delays—computed via soft-argmax operators to avoid temporal alignment and parameter degeneracy. A differentiable loss over these features plus a sigmoidal relaxation of the binary stimulation threshold (mapping critical membrane voltage at a fixed indicator time to a smooth spike probability) is optimized with Adam gradient descent through the JAXLEY differentiable simulator, with B-spline interpolation enforcing smooth axonal geometry and bounded reparameterizations enforcing physiological constraints. As a complementary approach, simulation-based inference (neural posterior estimation with a normalizing-flow density estimator) is trained on a stochastic forward model with additive Gaussian current noise, yielding calibrated posterior uncertainty over all biophysical parameters. On real macaque RGC recordings, the gradient-descent fit from minutes of single-electrode data predicted unseen multi-electrode stimulation responses at 90.6% mean per-cell accuracy, outperforming both data-driven MLP baselines and prior heuristic predictors.

## Results

On 37 parasol retinal ganglion cells (~3.2×10⁵ unique multi-electrode stimulus combinations, each with ~19 repeats), the best HH model fit via gradient descent (using potassium peak amplitude + stimulation thresholds) achieves 90.6% mean per-cell accuracy and 89.3% balanced accuracy in predicting spike/no-spike responses to previously unseen simultaneous multi-electrode stimulation. The full-feature HH (GD, all) variant reaches 89.5% accuracy, while the SBI variant achieves 87.8% accuracy with lower FPR (0.072) but higher FNR (0.245). All HH variants outperform a data-driven MLP baseline (85.9% accuracy) that was trained directly on multi-electrode stimulation responses from 27 held-in cells.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The HH (GD) model outperforms both prior heuristic baselines by a substantial margin: 90.6% vs. 85.8% accuracy over the Independent-site model (Vasireddy et al., 2026) and 84.9% over the Superposition model (Vilkhu et al., 2025), with corresponding balanced-accuracy gains of 8.1 and 8.2 points. The Independent baseline has the lowest FPR among heuristics (0.074) but suffers the highest FNR (0.302), reflecting its tendency to miss spikes when electrodes interact. The Superposition baseline shows the opposite failure mode with higher FPR (0.102). Notably, even a data-driven MLP trained on multi-electrode stimulation responses from 27 cells (an information advantage the HH model never has) achieves only 85.9% accuracy on 7 held-out cells, underperforming the best HH ablation by 4.7 points on every metric, supporting the role of the biophysical inductive bias in generalizing from single-electrode data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simulated-data framework of Tanoh et al. (2026) for fitting multi-compartment HH models from extracellular recordings to real macaque retinal data, using JAXLEY as the differentiable simulation backbone and the line-source approximation of Gold et al. (2006) for forward extracellular potential computation. The key methodological departure is the design of differentiable, physically interpretable EI features (peak amplitudes, propagation delays) and a sigmoidal relaxation of the binary stimulation threshold, replacing the raw waveform-level loss that prior simulated work relied on. For robustness under model misspecification, the paper incorporates simulation-based inference via neural posterior estimation (Deistler et al., 2025a), a framework previously applied to intracellular HH fitting but not to extracellular inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is direct: a biophysical model fit from minutes of single-electrode recording predicts unseen multi-electrode stimulation responses at 90.6% accuracy, potentially replacing hours of empirical stimulus testing in epiretinal prosthesis calibration. The finding that a biophysically constrained model outperforms a data-driven MLP with access to more training data (multi-electrode responses) suggests the inductive bias is essential for generalizing from sparse single-electrode supervision to arbitrary stimulus combinations. Key limitations include the homogeneous extracellular medium assumption, validation restricted to 150 μs triphasic pulses on ex vivo tissue, and the open question of whether the framework transfers to in vivo implants where electrode-tissue interface drift degrades model validity over time.
