## Problem

Interactive theorem provers (ITPs) like Lean dominate formal mathematical reasoning benchmarks, while auto-active verifiers like Dafny are used almost exclusively for software verification. The complementary direction—evaluating LLMs on pure mathematical theorem proving within an auto-active verifier where SMT solvers handle low-level reasoning—remains entirely unexplored, leaving open whether the SMT-backed automation paradigm changes what LLMs need to contribute to proof generation.

## Input / Output

Each problem is a Dafny lemma with requires/ensures clauses and an empty proof body, accompanied by two support files: definitions.dfy (283 lines, 81 axiomatized definitions for integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers) and library.dfy (938 lines, 174 lemmas for exponentials, trigonometry, number theory, etc.). The LLM receives the problem statement plus iterative Dafny verification error traces, and must produce a complete Dafny file whose proof body consists of ghost code—assertions, calc blocks, and helper lemmas—that guides Z3 to verify the lemma. A two-stage validator rejects solutions that weaken preconditions, remove postconditions, or introduce :axiom/assume constructs.

## Method

The paper translates all 488 miniF2F problems (244 test, 244 validation) from Lean into Dafny lemmas, creating the first auto-active verifier benchmark for olympiad-level mathematics. Rather than constructing mathematical objects from first principles as in Mathlib, the foundation axiomatizes functions through their specifications (e.g., log's range properties, sum's inductive definition as postconditions), deliberately restricting the trusted theory to a compact set so that SMT automation and LLM reasoning—not library retrieval—drive verification. The evaluation protocol gives each of 8 off-the-shelf LLMs up to 4 independent attempts with 3 error-correction iterations per attempt (temperature 0.5, 8192-token limit), feeding Dafny's JSON verification diagnostics back into the conversation. The key empirical finding is a complementary division of labor: Dafny's Z3-backed automation verifies 38.9% of test problems with empty proofs (exceeding Lean's grind tactic at 32.4%), and the best LLM (Claude Opus 4.6) adds 23.8 percentage points by supplying high-level proof structure—case splits, auxiliary sum-of-squares lemmas, calc chains—while the SMT solver discharges the arithmetic and logical bookkeeping that would require explicit tactics in an ITP.

## Results

On the MINIF2F-DAFNY test set (244 problems), the best model (Claude Opus 4.6) achieves 62.7% cumulative pass@4, improving over the 38.9% empty-proof Dafny baseline by 23.8 percentage points. Dafny's SMT-backed automation alone (Z3) verifies 95/244 test problems (38.9%) and 106/244 validation problems (43.4%) with no external input, outperforming Lean's grind tactic (32.4%). Claude Sonnet 4.5 reaches 55.7% pass@4, while Qwen and GPT-OSS models cluster in the 43–50% range due to limited Dafny-specific training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LLM-guided proof generation against two automation baselines: Dafny's own SMT solver with empty proofs (38.9%) and Lean 4's grind tactic (32.4%). Claude Opus 4.6 beats both baselines by 23.8 and 30.3 percentage points respectively on pass@4. Dafny's automation alone outperforms grind (95 vs. 79 problems solved), but the two are complementary: 67 problems are solved by both, 28 only by Dafny, and 12 only by grind. The authors note that while 62.7% is below state-of-the-art ITP results (e.g., HILBERT's 99.2% on miniF2F in Lean), those systems use specialized models, agentic frameworks, and substantially larger compute budgets, whereas MINIF2F-DAFNY uses general-purpose off-the-shelf models with modest compute and a compact 938-line library rather than Mathlib's 2M lines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper translates the widely-used miniF2F mathematical benchmark (Zheng et al., 2022) from interactive theorem provers (Lean, Isabelle, HOL Light, Metamath) to the auto-active verifier Dafny, creating the first empirical setting where LLMs guide SMT-backed automation for pure mathematical reasoning. It sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: LLM-assisted theorem proving in ITPs (miniF2F, PutnamBench, SeedProver, HILBERT) and LLM-assisted verification in auto-active tools (DafnyBench, VerifyThis, Clover). The key conceptual departure is that in Dafny there is no explicit proof term to construct; instead, the LLM supplies high-level hints (assertions, calc chains, auxiliary lemmas) while Z3 handles low-level logical bookkeeping, inverting the division of labor relative to ITP-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Dafny's SMT automation alone verifies 39–44% of olympiad-level problems with empty proofs establishes a strong, previously unmeasured baseline for auto-active mathematical reasoning, and the 23.8-point LLM improvement on top of it demonstrates a complementary regime distinct from ITP-based proof generation. Limitations include the compact axiomatized library (938 lines vs. Mathlib's 2M), verification brittleness to syntactic variation, and limited model exposure to Dafny idioms. Open questions include whether Dafny-specific training or agentic architectures can close the gap to ITP SOTA (99.2% for HILBERT), and whether the convergence of auto-active and ITP paradigms (grind, Velvet, Strata) will ultimately blur the distinction this paper draws.
