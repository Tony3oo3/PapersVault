## Problem

Existing embedding-based data selection methods (k-center, k-means clustering) assume that local geometric structure—proximity and distance between points—captures what makes a subset representative. However, many modern datasets (especially high-dimensional embeddings from pretrained encoders) possess global algebraic structure better captured by low-rank approximation, where dominant spectral directions matter more than pairwise distances. This paper asks whether a low-rank, residual-based sampling framework can provide provable loss-approximation guarantees and empirically outperform clustering-based coresets for data-efficient training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a matrix of data-point embeddings (e.g., BERT or word2vec vectors, even when the target model is much larger such as Llama3-8B), per-point loss values under the current model, a target rank k for the low-rank sketch, and an error parameter ε. It outputs a weighted subset S ⊆ D of size O(1/ε²) where each selected point carries a weight w(x) = 1/(s·p(x)) inversely proportional to its sampling probability, so that the weighted sum of losses over S approximates the total loss over D. In the row-subset-selection variant (Theorem 2.3), the low-rank basis must itself be drawn from D, ensuring every selected point is a real, labelable data point.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each data point x into its projection v(x) onto a k-dimensional subspace V (obtained via SVD or leverage-score-based row selection) and its orthogonal residual r(x). Under a regularity assumption (Assumption 2.1) bounding the loss deviation by λ‖r(x)‖² outside V and by a weighted combination of basis-direction losses inside V, the algorithm defines a per-point sensitivity score σ(x) = (γ+1)Σᵢ αᵢ²ℓ(vᵢ) + γkξ + λ‖r(x)‖², samples s = ⌈ε⁻²(2+2ε/3)⌉ points with replacement proportional to σ(x), and assigns inverse-probability weights. Bernstein's concentration then yields a (1±ε) multiplicative guarantee on total loss approximation with an additive term proportional to Φ_k(D), the optimal rank-k Frobenius approximation error. The key distinction from prior clustering-based sensitivity sampling is that importance is measured along dominant spectral directions rather than geometric distance to cluster centroids, making the selection robust in high-dimensional settings where variance is concentrated in a few principal axes.

## Results

On Llama3-8B fine-tuning, Low-rank SS achieves average validation accuracy of 77.6% at 25% sampling, 70.4% at 12.5%, and 54.9% at 6.25% across GSM8k, ViGGO, and SQL, consistently outperforming uniform sampling (76.5/69.2/52.0) and approaching full-data performance (81.1%). On the Credit Card tabular dataset, sensitivity sampling reduces coreset approximation error by an order of magnitude versus random sampling and by ~50% versus clustering at s=1000, translating to 74% test accuracy vs. 70% (clustering) and 67% (random) at s=5000. On Qwen2.5-3B (ViGGO), the method improves over the strongest baseline (Clustering-based SS) by 4.34 points at 25% and 3.08 points at 12.5% compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Clustering-based SS (Axiotis et al., 2024), the closest competitor, Low-rank SS achieves a marginal average advantage at 25% (77.6 vs. 77.5) and 6.25% (54.9 vs. 54.6) sampling, but slightly trails at 12.5% (70.4 vs. 71.0); the gap is most pronounced on ViGGO at 25% (88.3 vs. 86.6) and SQL at 6.25% (70.4 vs. 68.3). Against K-Center (Sener & Savarese, 2018), the method wins by 2.7 points on average at 25% and by 12.5 points at 12.5%, with the largest gap on ViGGO (88.3 vs. 80.3 at 25%). Against Graph Cut (Schreiber et al., 2020), the advantage is consistent across all ratios, reaching 9.2 points on average at 6.25% sampling. On the Qwen2.5-3B ViGGO experiments, Low-rank SS outperforms Clustering-based SS by 4.34 points (75.91 vs. 71.57) at 25% and 3.08 points (51.54 vs. 48.46) at 12.5%, confirming the advantage generalizes beyond Llama3-8B. Notably, subsets selected by Llama3-8B transfer to Qwen2.5-3B and even exceed those selected by Qwen2.5-3B itself (55.74 vs. 51.54 at 12.5%), suggesting the low-rank criterion captures intrinsic data structure rather than model-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coreset-based data selection lineage initiated by Sener & Savarese (ICLR 2018) and Axiotis et al. (ICML 2024), which framed active learning as selecting a small subset preserving geometric (k-center or k-means) structure. The key departure is replacing clustering-based diversity with low-rank spectral structure: instead of covering the data space uniformly, the method preserves dominant variance directions via SVD and leverage-score sensitivity sampling, drawing on the importance-sampling coreset machinery of Feldman & Langberg (STOC 2011) and the bicriteria row-subset-selection subroutine of Guruswami & Sinop (SODA 2012). The theoretical contribution formalizes when this spectral approach is provably preferable to geometric coverage, under a loss-decomposition assumption (Assumption 2.1) that ties approximation error to the optimal rank-k Frobenius error of the embedding matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantees specifically for low-rank-structured data selection, filling a gap where prior coreset results were tied to clustering objectives. The transferability finding—that subsets selected by Llama3-8B outperform those selected by Qwen2.5-3B itself when training Qwen2.5-3B—suggests the low-rank criterion captures intrinsic data geometry rather than model-specific artifacts, which could reduce the cost of re-selecting data across model families. Open questions include how tightly Assumption 2.1 holds for arbitrary loss functions in practice, whether the theoretical constants are loose enough to matter at scale, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-modal or streaming settings where the embedding matrix is not fully available upfront.
