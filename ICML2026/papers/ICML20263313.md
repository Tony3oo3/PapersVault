## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks suffer from non-differentiable spike generation (the Heaviside step function has zero gradient almost everywhere), forcing reliance on heuristic surrogate gradients that introduce a fundamental forward-backward mismatch with limited theoretical understanding. This paper asks whether a principled, fully differentiable relaxation of spike dynamics can be derived from first principles rather than chosen heuristically, and whether such a relaxation can eliminate the gradient mismatch while preserving convergence to true LIF behavior.

## Input / Output

Inputs span four modalities: 28×28 grayscale (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST), 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10), 2×34×34 or 2×128×128 ON/OFF event frames (N-MNIST, DVS-Gesture), and 700-dimensional audio frequency channels (SHD). Static images are converted to spike trains via rate coding (P(spike at t) = 0.5·x_pixel), while neuromorphic events are binned into T temporal frames. The output is a class label obtained from the mean spike rate over T timesteps at the final layer (ŷ = (1/T)Σ s^(t)), making the classification decision depend on aggregate activity rather than individual spike timing.

## Method

UltraLIF applies ultradiscretization (the substitution x = e^{X/ε} followed by ε→0) to two distinct dynamical systems: the LIF membrane ODE yields a 2-term log-sum-exp (LSE) capturing temporal decay, while the 1D diffusion PDE yields a 3-term LSE capturing lateral spatial coupling across neurons. The LSE serves as a differentiable soft-maximum that converges to the hard max-plus threshold as a learnable temperature ε→0, and the spike function σ((V−θ)/ε) replaces the Heaviside step with a smooth sigmoid that is identical in forward and backward passes, eliminating the surrogate gradient mismatch. The temperature ε is made learnable (initialized at 1.0), implementing automatic curriculum learning from soft to hard dynamics, and the leak factor τ₀ can also be learned (UltraPLIF/UltraDPLIF variants). Theoretical analysis establishes pointwise convergence to tropical LIF dynamics with quantitative error bounds O(tε log 2 + tCe^{−δ/ε}), bounded non-vanishing gradients (|∂s/∂V| ≤ 1/4ε), and a semiring homomorphism connecting the dynamics to tropical algebraic geometry.

## Results

At the ultra-low latency regime (T=1), UltraLIF variants achieve state-of-the-art accuracy across all six benchmarks, with the largest gains on neuromorphic and audio data: +11.22% on SHD (51.24% vs. 40.02%), +7.96% on DVS-Gesture (60.23% vs. 52.27%), and +3.91% on N-MNIST (94.14% vs. 90.23%). On static image datasets, gains are smaller but consistent: +3.01% on CIFAR-10 (43.27% vs. 40.26%), +0.35% on Fashion-MNIST, and +0.09% on MNIST. Under hard binary-spike inference at T=1, Ultra retains its advantage on 4 of 6 datasets (CIFAR-10 +3.09%, N-MNIST +2.55%, SHD +7.86%, DVS +4.55%), confirming transfer to neuromorphic deployment. An optional sparsity penalty (λ=0.1) reduces spike rates by 40–50% while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six surrogate-gradient baselines: LIF, PLIF (Fang et al., 2021b), AdaLIF (Bellec et al., 2018), FullPLIF, DSpike, and DSpike+ (Li et al., 2021). At T=1, UltraLIF variants outperform all baselines on every dataset, with the strongest margins on temporal/event-driven data: UltraDLIF beats FullPLIF by +11.22% on SHD and DSpike+ by +3.91% on N-MNIST, while UltraPLIF beats PLIF by +7.96% on DVS-Gesture and DSpike+ by +3.01% on CIFAR-10. On simpler static datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST), the advantage is marginal (+0.09% and +0.35%), consistent with the paper's argument that forward-backward consistency matters most when each spike carries high information (low T). At higher timesteps (T≥10), the gap narrows and baselines often recover or surpass Ultra, as spike-rate averaging masks individual spike errors. The spatial variant (UltraDLIF) wins on N-MNIST, SHD, and MNIST at T=1, while the temporal variant (UltraPLIF) wins on CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, and DVS-Gesture, suggesting complementary strengths of the two derivations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant surrogate gradient paradigm for SNN training (Neftci et al., 2019; Zenke & Vogels, 2021) by deriving differentiable spiking neurons from first principles via ultradiscretization, a limiting procedure from tropical geometry (Tokihiro et al., 1996). Rather than patching non-differentiability with heuristic smooth approximations, the LIF ODE and diffusion PDE are transformed into max-plus dynamics, with the log-sum-exp function providing a principled soft relaxation that converges to hard thresholding as a learnable temperature ε→0. The work also extends the tropical geometry–neural network connection established by Zhang et al. (2018) for ReLU networks to the spiking domain, and introduces a spatial variant (UltraDLIF) derived from the diffusion equation modeling gap junction coupling, which has no direct analogue in prior SNN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The forward-backward consistency and bounded-gradient guarantees address a long-standing theoretical gap in SNN training, and the ResNet50 dead-neuron result (LIF 31.83% vs. Ultra 92.78%) reveals a structural failure mode of fixed-threshold surrogates under BN-scale mismatch that is not addressed by existing architectural fixes like SEW-ResNet. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (64-neuron hidden layers, single seed, no ImageNet-scale evaluation), and the soft-to-hard inference gap (2–4 pp) plus the Conv-2L regression (−3.8 pp) suggest the method's practical advantages may narrow at scale. Open questions include whether the tropical geometry connection yields further algorithmic insights (e.g., structured pruning via zonotope volume), whether the decoupled spike-scale formulation (Appendix G) resolves the convolutional weakness, and whether trained models transfer faithfully to neuromorphic hardware.
