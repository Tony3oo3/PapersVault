## Problem

Analyzing multi-trial time series with multi-category labels requires simultaneously discovering interpretable components, capturing cross-trial variability (including within repeated measures of the same label), and disentangling each category's contribution. Existing approaches either fix components across all trials (missing label-driven compositional adjustments), analyze trials independently (losing cross-trial structure), or impose rigid tensor constraints (requiring equal-length trials and conflating label-driven with other variability). No prior method handles all three requirements while maintaining interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes M time-series trials Y^(m) ∈ R^(N×T^(m)) with fixed channel identities but variable durations, each annotated with a label tuple spanning |C| metadata categories (e.g., task difficulty × animal choice). It produces, for each category (k), a 3D component tensor A^(k) ∈ R^(N×p^(k)×|(k)|) whose slices are sparse, label-specific variants, plus per-trial temporal traces Φ^(m) ∈ R^(P×T^(m)). Each trial's effective loading matrix is assembled by selecting one slice from each category tensor according to that trial's label and concatenating them horizontally, so trials sharing a label share identical loadings while their traces remain free to vary.

## Method

MILCCI decomposes each trial as Y^(m) = Σ_(k) A^(k)_{::arg(L^(m)_(k))} Φ^(m)_{G^(k):} + ε, where components are assigned to single categories and each category's components form a tensor with one variant per label option. Variants within a category are regularized to remain structurally similar via pre-computed label-similarity graphs (constant for categorical labels, Gaussian-kernel for ordinal/continuous labels), enabling subtle compositional adjustments that track label distance. Fitting proceeds by alternating minimization: (1) for each category variant, a LASSO problem is solved on residuals (observations minus all other categories' contributions) with an ℓ₁ sparsity term and an ℓ₂ cross-variant consistency term weighted by the similarity graph; (2) for each trial, traces are updated to minimize data fidelity plus temporal smoothness and within-trial trace decorrelation. Components are normalized after each update to resolve scaling ambiguity with traces, and the model supports optional non-negativity constraints and flexible label types (categorical, ordinal, continuous, trial-varying) within a single dataset.

## Results

On synthetic data, MILCCI achieves the highest correlation to ground-truth components and traces among all six baselines, with SiBBlInGS attaining comparable correlation but producing blurred traces. On the voting dataset, MILCCI achieves a reconstruction quality p(y,ŷ) of 0.804, outperforming SiBBlInGS (0.791) and PARAFAC2 (0.468), and attains the lowest AIC and HQC information criteria across all methods. On the Wikipedia pageview dataset, MILCCI achieves the highest five-fold cross-validated decoding accuracy for both agent and platform categories, as well as the highest log-likelihood and lowest AIC/HQC. On the IBL neuronal recordings, MILCCI achieves the highest cross-validated decoding accuracy and competitive reconstruction quality relative to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILCCI outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric evaluated. Against Tucker and PARAFAC, MILCCI wins decisively on reconstruction quality and information criteria (AIC, BIC, HQC); these tensor methods produce dense or negative-valued components, cannot capture label-driven compositional adjustments, and on the Wikipedia dataset fail to converge at the target rank (requiring reduced rank and added noise). Against SVD, MILCCI achieves superior reconstruction and interpretability, as SVD components include negative values and cannot disentangle category-specific effects. Against SiBBlInGS (the closest prior method), MILCCI matches correlation on synthetic data but produces sharper, less blurred traces and—critically—disentangles the contribution of each label category, whereas SiBBlInGS shows uninterpretable compositional changes across every label and fails to separate party from office in the voting data. Against sliceTCA, MILCCI produces components with clear identity across trials (same-component distances much smaller than cross-component distances), while sliceTCA's components show cross-component variability comparable to same-component variability, indicating poor component matching. Against PARAFAC2, MILCCI achieves substantially higher reconstruction (0.804 vs. 0.468 on voting) and avoids the severe cross-component degeneracy (correlations up to 0.71–0.9) that plagues PARAFAC2 at the tested ranks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MILCCI extends the line of work on multi-trial dimensionality reduction with label integration, building most directly on SiBBlInGS (Mudrik et al., 2024), which handles a single label dimension, and TDR (Mante et al., 2013), which assumes cross-trial variability arises only from linear reweighting of fixed components. The key conceptual departure is representing each category's components as a 3D tensor (channel × component × label variant) with elastic-net regularization (L1 sparsity + L2 cross-variant consistency weighted by a label-similarity graph), enabling components to subtly adjust their composition under label changes while remaining structurally aligned. This contrasts with tensor factorization methods (PARAFAC, Tucker, sliceTCA) that impose rigid tensor structure and cannot disentangle label-driven from non-label-driven variability, and with per-trial factorizations that sacrifice cross-trial relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MILCCI fills a concrete gap: no prior method simultaneously (1) handles multiple label categories with compositional adjustments, (2) captures free trial-to-trial variability in temporal traces, and (3) maintains interpretability via sparse components. The validation across voting, Wikipedia, and IBL neuroscience data demonstrates practical utility, particularly for neuroscience where disentangling task variables from representational drift is critical. Limitations include the linear generation assumption, sensitivity to per-category component count (mitigated by sparsity), and the need for manual hyperparameter tuning (γ1, γ2). Open questions include extending to non-linear structures via kernelization, scaling to very large datasets via parallel/batch optimization, and incorporating dynamic priors over trace evolution.
