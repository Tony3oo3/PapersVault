## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods appear to successfully remove sensitive knowledge under standard greedy decoding evaluations, but the paper demonstrates this is an illusion: when models are sampled with realistic probabilistic decoding (temperature > 0, top-p > 0), forgotten information reliably resurfaces across multiple generations. The root cause is that nearly all evaluation protocols rely exclusively on deterministic decoding, which masks residual probability mass allocated to sensitive tokens in the model's full output distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM, a forget set (QA pairs for TOFU/WMDP, or raw document corpora for MUSE that are first converted to QA pairs), a retain set, and decoding parameters (temperature T, top-p p). It produces an unlearned LLM and a leak@k score — a distributional meta-metric quantifying the expected maximum leakage across k generations, instantiated with task-appropriate core metrics (Entailment Score for TOFU, ROUGE-L for MUSE, Accuracy and LLM-as-Judge for WMDP). Evaluation generates n=200 samples per prompt under probabilistic decoding and computes the unbiased estimator over k ∈ {1, 2, 4, …, 128}.

## Method

The paper introduces two core contributions. First, leak@k is a distributional meta-metric defined as the expected maximum correctness score among k i.i.d. generations, with an unbiased estimator derived from order statistics that uses n ≥ k samples and a combinatorial correction term to reduce variance relative to a naive worst-of-k estimator. Second, RULE (Robust Unlearning under LEak@k) is an iterative meta-algorithm that dynamically augments the forget set at each training epoch: it prompts the current model multiple times under probabilistic decoding, identifies the most leaking responses via a core-metric threshold, adds them to the forget set (and similarly augments the retain set), and re-optimizes the standard unlearning objective. RULE is method-agnostic, wrapping around existing unlearning losses (e.g., RULE-GradDiff uses gradient ascent, RULE-NPO uses negative preference optimization), and achieves near-zero leak@k on TOFU even at k=128. The paper further characterizes leakage growth with a log-log model L_k = 1 − (1−L_1)k^{−γ}, where successful unlearning requires both small L_1 (low initial leakage) and small γ (slow leakage growth as the sampling budget increases).

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark (T=1.0, p=1.0), RULE-GradDiff achieves leak@k-ES of 0.041 at k=128 with a decay rate γ=0.006, compared to 0.835 (GradDiff), 0.745 (NPO), and 0.552 (BLUR-NPO) for the strongest baselines, while also attaining the best retain score (0.397). On MUSE-News (T=1.0, p=1.0), RULE-NPO reduces leak@k-RS at k=128 to 0.68 with γ=0.20, outperforming BLUR-NPO (0.79, γ=0.26) and NPO (0.94, γ=0.48). Across all three benchmarks, the paper's central empirical finding is that leak@k rises sharply with k for every existing unlearning method under probabilistic decoding, exposing a critical gap between greedy-decoding evaluations and realistic deployment conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RULE-GradDiff dominates all TOFU baselines by a wide margin: at k=128 it achieves leak@k-ES of 0.041 versus 0.835 for GradDiff (Liu et al., 2022), 0.745 for NPO (Zhang et al., 2024), 0.764 for NPO+ENT (Scholten et al., 2024), 0.552 for BLUR-NPO (Reisizadeh et al., 2025), 0.602 for LoUK (Cha et al., 2024), and 0.149 for PMC (Scholten et al., 2025), while also achieving the highest retain score (0.397 vs. 0.235 for PMC). On MUSE-News, RULE-NPO outperforms NPO (0.94→0.68 at k=128), SimNPO (0.95→0.68), and BLUR-NPO (0.79→0.68) in both leak@k-RS and the decay rate γ (0.20 vs. 0.48, 0.55, 0.26 respectively). On WMDP, RMU (Li et al., 2024) exhibits clear leakage under high-randomness decoding (T=1.0, p=1.0) in both leak@k-Acc and leak@k-LJ, while NPO shows over-forgetting (selecting a 'don't know' option >90% of the time) that masks leakage in accuracy but produces gibberish in free-form generation. The paper further shows that top-p is the primary driver of leakage across all benchmarks—increasing p from 0.2 to 1.0 at fixed T=0.2 induces explicit leakage, whereas increasing T at low p does not—highlighting that all existing methods remain vulnerable to realistic sampling configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard LLM unlearning optimization framework (forget loss + retain loss) instantiated by methods like GradDiff (gradient ascent) and NPO (negative preference optimization), but departs fundamentally in its evaluation philosophy: it argues that the field's reliance on greedy decoding masks residual knowledge in the output distribution. The leak@k metric extends the probabilistic evaluation initiated by Scholten et al. (2024) by shifting from per-generation average leakage to worst-case leakage across k samples, and by grounding scores in semantic metrics (entailment, LLM-as-judge) rather than token-level similarity. RULE is a meta-algorithm that wraps any existing unlearning method with an iterative loop of probabilistic sampling, leakage detection, and forget-set augmentation, making it orthogonal to the specific loss formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical blind spot: SOTA unlearning methods that pass standard greedy-decoding benchmarks still leak sensitive content under the probabilistic decoding used in real deployment, undermining the practical safety guarantees these methods are meant to provide. The leak@k metric is likely to become a standard evaluation tool for unlearning, and the finding that top-p (not temperature) is the primary driver of leakage has immediate practical implications for deployment configuration. Open questions include whether RULE's iterative augmentation scales efficiently to larger models or more complex forget sets, whether the approach generalizes to multimodal unlearning, and whether the near-zero leakage achieved on TOFU holds under adversarial prompt engineering beyond simple repeated sampling.
