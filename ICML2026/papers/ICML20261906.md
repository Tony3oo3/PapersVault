## Problem

Efficient linear sequence models (Gated DeltaNet, Mamba-2, etc.) are constrained to Rank-1 per-token state updates, limiting their ability to capture multi-faceted feature interactions, while explicit iterative solvers like Test-Time Training achieve Rank-L expressivity but break hardware parallelism due to state-dependent serial dependencies (174× slower throughput). The paper asks whether Rank-L updates can be achieved while preserving the associative structure required for parallel prefix scans.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence (language modeling) or item interaction sequence (recommendation) and produces next-token logits or ranked item lists via a linear readout S_t·q_t. Internally, the input is first passed through a causal ShortConv (window size 5) to construct a state-free anchor u_t that approximates the hidden-state query S_{t-1}k_t; all subsequent direction, gain, and confidence projections are computed from this anchor alone, ensuring the recurrence S_t = S_{t-1}·A_t + B_t satisfies the state-independence condition for parallel scanning. The recurrent state is a d×d matrix (d_k = d_v = d in practice), and the refinement depth L is typically 2–4.

## Method

PRISM resolves the Rank-Parallel trade-off via two mechanisms. First, Write-Forget Decoupling fixes the forgetting operator A_t to the Gated DeltaNet form (scalar decay × Rank-1 delta correction), justified by a spectral perturbation analysis showing forgetting errors accumulate only O(ln T) worst-case, while the writing operator B_t is allocated the full Rank-L budget. Second, Input-Anchored Loop Unrolling replaces the state-dependent quantities in the multi-step non-linear solver with learned input-conditioned proxies: a ShortConv anchor u_t seeds the initial residual r^(1) = v_t − u_t, and L parallel linear projections generate per-step direction k^(l), gain vector p^(l) (approximating φ′), and confidence scalar β^(l). The refinement loop then performs greedy residual subtraction (δ^(l) = GELU(p^(l)⊙r^(l)), r^(l+1) = r^(l) − δ^(l)) to accumulate a Rank-L injection B_t = Σ_l β^(l)·δ^(l)·k^(l)ᵀ in a single fused pass. Because both A_t and B_t depend only on the input sequence, the recurrence admits O(log N) parallel prefix scanning, achieving throughput comparable to Rank-1 baselines while matching or exceeding the quality of serial TTT solvers.

## Results

On sequential recommendation across four datasets, PRISM achieves the best Mean Rank (2.62) among all linear models, with AUC of 0.7727 on Amazon Movies (beating Titans at 0.7652 and TTT at 0.7591) and 0.8888 on Amazon Books (nearly matching SASRec's 0.8910). In language modeling at 130M scale, PRISM attains the best validation PPL of 19.25 and test PPL of 23.13, outperforming GDN (19.32/23.28) and Mamba-2 (20.20/24.27). Critically, PRISM maintains ~61K tokens/s throughput—on par with Rank-1 baselines—while TTT achieves only 0.34K tokens/s, a 174× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all Rank-1 parallel baselines (SLA, GLA, GSA, MoM, Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet) on Mean Rank, achieving 2.62 versus the best Rank-1 score of 5.00 (Mamba-2), with the largest gaps on Amazon Movies AUC (0.7727 vs. 0.7713 for Mamba-2) and Amazon Books AUC (0.8888 vs. 0.8872). Against serial Rank-L solvers, PRISM matches or exceeds TTT and Titans on most metrics—beating Titans on Amazon Movies AUC (0.7727 vs. 0.7652) and Amazon Books H@200 (0.1258 vs. 0.1272 is a slight loss, but AUC 0.8888 vs. 0.8869 is a win)—while being 174× faster in throughput (61K vs. 0.34K tokens/s). PRISM also surpasses ATLAS on Amazon Movies AUC (0.7727 vs. 0.7710) and Amazon Books AUC (0.8888 vs. 0.8884). The gap to full-attention Transformers (SASRec, HSTU) is narrow: on Amazon Books, PRISM's AUC (0.8888) is within 0.002 of SASRec (0.8910), and on Amazon Movies, HSTU leads by only 0.002 AUC. In language modeling, PRISM beats GDN by 0.15 PPL and Mamba-2 by 1.14 PPL, and achieves the highest zero-shot average accuracy (40.1%) across 9 benchmarks, leading PGDN by 1.8pp and GDN by 3.2pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM sits at the intersection of two lines of work: the Gated DeltaNet family of one-shot linear recurrences (which provide the decoupled forgetting operator) and Test-Time Training / Titans (which provide the multi-step non-linear optimization structure). The paper's key conceptual departure is the OCP-based unification that reveals a Rank-1/Parallel trade-off across all existing methods, followed by the amortization strategy: replacing TTT's state-dependent iterative solver with input-anchored proxies (ShortConv + learned predictors) that emulate the multi-step trajectory in a single parallel pass. This positions PRISM in a previously empty cell of the taxonomy—Rank-L expressivity with parallel scan compatibility—rather than as an incremental improvement over any single baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean design principle (Write-Forget Decoupling: allocate computational budget to the injection operator, not the forgetting operator) that could guide future efficient architecture design, and the 174× throughput advantage over TTT makes Rank-L updates practically viable for the first time. However, the quality gains over strong Rank-1 baselines (Gated DeltaNet) are modest—0.15 PPL on language modeling and small AUC deltas on recommendation—and are demonstrated only at 130M parameter scale on relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the ShortConv anchor's approximation error (bounded by γ^w/(1−γ)) remains acceptable at much longer contexts, whether Rank-L benefits persist at billion-parameter scale, and whether the fixed d×d state capacity wall limits the approach's ceiling regardless of injection rank.
