## Problem

Conservation laws—quantities invariant along gradient flow trajectories—provide a principled lens on the implicit bias of over-parameterized models, but rigorous characterizations have been limited to linear and ReLU networks. While Marcotte et al. (2025) extended the framework to ResNets and single-head Transformers, the multihead attention case was explicitly left open, and modern components such as GELU/SiLU/SwiGLU activations, rotary positional encodings, and Mixture-of-Experts gating remained entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a differentiable model f(·;θ) with a specified parameter space Θ and a C² loss ℓ whose gradient span V_ℓ is input-independent (typically V_ℓ = ℝ^{d_out}), and produces a complete set of C¹ conservation laws expressed as PDE constraints on h together with explicit characteristic invariants. Empirically, the method consumes standard training configurations (Qwen-3 on WikiText-103/Penn Treebank, ViT on CIFAR-10/ImageNet-1K) and outputs block-level relative conservation error ε_block(k) averaged over all invariants in each architectural component, tracked across iterations under both full-batch gradient descent and mini-batch SGD.

## Method

The paper develops a unified framework that reduces the conservation-law orthogonality condition ⟨∇_θ h, ∇_θ f_i(x;θ)⟩ = 0 to input-independent PDE constraints on h by exploiting complex-analytic structure: for each fixed θ, the function F_i(·;θ) is meromorphic in the input, so restricting to one-dimensional subspaces x = te_j and applying the identity theorem forces F_i to vanish identically, yielding pole-structure constraints on ∇_θ h. This technique is applied across all target architectures, revealing that GELU and SiLU FFNs admit only constant conservation laws, SwiGLU FFNs yield invariants ‖A_{:,i}‖² − ‖C_{i,:}‖² per hidden unit, vanilla MHA yields Q_i^T Q_i − K_i^T K_i and V_i^T V_i − O_i^T O_i per head (resolving the open problem from Marcotte et al., 2025), RoPE modifies these to per-2D-block Frobenius-norm invariants ‖Q_i^{(j)}‖_F² − ‖K_i^{(j)}‖_F², and MoE architectures inherit expert-level SwiGLU invariants plus a gating invariant (sum of gating rows for softmax, or equality of gating gradients). The sparse Top-k and sigmoid-gated MoE variants are shown to share the same conservation structure as their dense softmax counterparts.

## Results

The paper establishes complete characterizations of conservation laws for five modern architecture families (Theorems 4.1–4.7). For GELU and SiLU FFNs, all conservation laws are constant (Theorem 4.1); for SwiGLU FFNs, the invariants are ‖A_{:,i}‖² − ‖C_{i,:}‖² per hidden unit (Theorem 4.2); for multihead attention, the invariants are Q_i^T Q_i − K_i^T K_i and V_i^T V_i − O_i^T O_i per head (Theorem 4.3); RoPE modifies these to per-block Frobenius-norm differences ‖Q_i^{(j)}‖_F² − ‖K_i^{(j)}‖_F² (Theorem 4.4); and MoE gating adds the invariant Σ_i W_i on top of per-expert SwiGLU invariants (Theorem 4.5). Empirically, conservation errors across MHA, SwiGLU FFNs, RoPE, and MoE gating on WikiText-103, Penn Treebank, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet-1K all exhibit the predicted O(τ²k) scaling under SGD, with higher learning rates inducing proportionally larger but still bounded deviations, confirming the theoretical bound of Equation (9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing optimization or training methods; its contribution is a theoretical characterization validated empirically. The closest 'comparison' is with the prior framework of Marcotte et al. (2023, 2025), which established conservation laws for linear/ReLU networks, ResNets, and single-head Transformers. This work resolves the open multihead attention problem left by Marcotte et al. (2025) and extends the analysis to GELU, SiLU, SwiGLU activations, RoPE positional encoding, and both dense and sparse MoE with softmax and sigmoid gating—architectures for which no prior complete characterization existed. Empirically, the paper contrasts conserved quantities (which remain bounded with O(τ²k) growth) against non-conserved quantities (which exhibit large, unbounded deviations under small parameter changes), providing a qualitative baseline that confirms the theoretical distinction. No head-to-head numerical comparison with alternative conservation-law frameworks is performed, as the prior work did not cover the architectures studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conservation-law characterization framework of Marcotte et al. (2023, 2024, 2025), which established complete descriptions for linear, ReLU, ResNet, and single-head Transformer architectures. It resolves the explicitly open multi-head attention problem left by Marcotte et al. (2025) and generalizes the analysis to the activation functions (GELU, SiLU, SwiGLU), positional encodings (sinusoidal, RoPE), and Mixture-of-Experts gating mechanisms that define contemporary LLMs. Methodologically, the authors depart from the Lie-theoretic approach of prior work, instead reducing the conservation condition to a system of PDEs and exploiting complex-analytic (meromorphic) structure to extract input-independent constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing complete characterizations for the exact building blocks of modern LLMs (SwiGLU FFNs, multi-head attention with RoPE, sparse MoE with sigmoid gating), this work gives a principled account of which geometric quantities are preserved during training and which are not, directly informing implicit-bias theory. A notable limitation is that the complex-analytic proof technique does not extend to components introducing branch points or essential singularities (e.g., layer normalization, deep layer compositions), leaving the full Transformer stack partially characterized. The data-independence assumption, while principled, means the results describe architecture-induced invariants rather than dataset-specific ones, and whether these conservation laws interact nontrivially with weight decay or adaptive optimizers remains unexplored.
