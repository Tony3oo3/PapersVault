## Problem

Adapting LLMs to specialized domains incurs high data and compute costs, yet prior efficiency efforts treat data selection and parameter-efficient fine-tuning as independent processes. The paper argues these are intrinsically coupled: a data selection strategy optimized for full fine-tuning may be suboptimal for sparse parameter configurations, and vice versa. This gap motivates a unified framework that jointly optimizes which data to train on and which parameters to update.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pre-trained LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B, Qwen-3-8B, or Llama-3-8B) and a labeled instruction dataset (GSM8K, DialogSum, or BioInstruct) as input. It produces three outputs: an adapted model with only ~10% of attention heads updated, the identified task-sensitive head set (top-ρ_P heads ranked by Wasserstein-1 distance), and a curated 10% data subset selected via ICL-based structural probing. A key design constraint is that the lightweight proxy for head identification uses only 100 examples and 20 training steps, keeping the selection overhead near-zero relative to full fine-tuning as measured by the Alignment Efficiency Ratio (AER).

## Method

P2D operates in three stages grounded in the Strong Map Hypothesis—that a sparse subset of attention heads dominates task-specific adaptation. Stage I (Fast Head Identification) fine-tunes a proxy for 20 steps on 100 examples, then scores each head by the Wasserstein-1 distance between softmax-flattened composite projection matrices (W_q W_k^T W_v) of the base and proxy models, selecting the top-ρ_P heads. Stage II (Parameter-Guided Data Selection) performs iterative ICL probing where, unlike global aggregation methods, attention weights are accumulated only within the identified task-sensitive heads to compute per-example structural importance scores, yielding a high-affinity data subset. Stage III (Sparse Head Adaptation) applies gradient masking so that only the projection matrices of the selected heads receive updates on the curated data. The cross-ablation (Lock-and-Key experiment) confirms that the specific pairing of P2D-selected data with P2D-identified heads is irreplaceable—swapping either component for a random or alternative-method counterpart causes substantial performance drops, validating the parameter-data synergy.

## Results

On the full P2D pipeline (10% data, 10% attention heads), averaged across Qwen-2.5-7B, Qwen-3-8B, and Llama-3-8B, the method achieves 78.82 on GSM8K, 44.04 on DialogSum, and 32.74 on BioInstruct (ROUGE-L / Exact Match). This yields an 8.3 pp average improvement over the strongest competing 10%/10% baseline (Data Whisperer + sparse heads at 49.38 avg) while delivering a 7.0× end-to-end speedup (AER = 0.32 on GSM8K) relative to full fine-tuning. Notably, P2D's GSM8K score (78.82) even exceeds Full SFT (78.36) despite using only 10% of both data and parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2D (10% data + 10% heads) outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark. Against the closest competitor, Data Whisperer + P2D‡, P2D gains +2.98 pp on GSM8K (78.82 vs. 75.84), +1.92 pp on DialogSum (44.04 vs. 42.12), and +2.55 pp on BioInstruct (32.74 vs. 30.19). The gap widens dramatically against metric-based selectors: IFD + P2D‡ trails by 7.40 pp on GSM8K and 8.92 pp on BioInstruct, while Nuggets + P2D‡ lags by 9.91 pp on GSM8K and 6.83 pp on BioInstruct. On the adaptation side, P2D‡ (sparse head update) beats LoRA (r=128) by +3.00 pp on GSM8K and +0.66 pp on BioInstruct at the same 10% parameter budget, and surpasses LoFiT by +4.11 pp on GSM8K. The cross-ablation (Table 21) further confirms that neither P2D's data alone nor its heads alone suffice: pairing P2D data with random heads drops GSM8K to 67.13, and pairing P2D heads with Data Whisperer data yields only 75.84, validating the lock-and-key synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously orthogonal research threads—parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, LoFiT, ALPS) and data selection (IFD, Nuggets, Data Whisperer)—by arguing they are intrinsically coupled rather than independent levers. The key conceptual departure is the Strong Map Hypothesis: a sparse subset of attention heads acts as a 'dual compass' that simultaneously guides which parameters to update and which data samples to retain, repurposing the head-identification machinery from selective PEFT as a data-selection signal. The introduced Alignment Efficiency Ratio (AER) metric also departs from prior data-selection evaluations that ignore selection latency, providing a more honest end-to-end cost accounting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lock-and-key cross-ablation (Table 21) provides strong evidence that parameter-data coupling is not merely additive but synergistic: swapping either component for a random alternative causes a 10+ pp collapse, validating that the identified heads and curated data are mutually informative. Practically, the ~3× speedup over Full SFT (AER 0.32) with equal or better performance, combined with reduced catastrophic forgetting (Table 9), makes this a viable strategy for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the Strong Map Hypothesis generalizes to mixture-of-experts architectures, multimodal models, or tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning, and whether the 20-step proxy heuristic for head identification remains reliable as base models evolve.
