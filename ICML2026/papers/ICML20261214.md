## Problem

Data attribution in federated learning is increasingly used for pricing, governance, and incentive allocation, yet the attribution pipeline itself has no dedicated security protections. Existing FL defenses target model-centric threats such as poisoning and anomalous updates, leaving open whether a single participant can strategically inflate its measured contribution while preserving global utility. This paper formalizes that attribution-first threat model and demonstrates it is practically exploitable.

## Input / Output

The attack consumes the broadcast global model weights at each round, the client's own real data shard, and a fixed pre-trained decoder (e.g., a lightweight GAN or diffusion decoder) that maps a low-dimensional latent vector to synthetic image-label pairs. The output is a compact synthetic batch whose labels are deliberately drawn from classes underrepresented or absent in the client's local shard, mixed with real data to produce a hybrid update that must satisfy norm (κ), shape-deviation (ε), and communication-budget (C_max) constraints before upload. The latent vector is cached and warm-started across rounds so the synthetic data co-evolves with the global model.

## Method

The attacker optimizes a single latent vector z through a fixed decoder using a joint loss of three equally-weighted terms: cosine-similarity alignment of the synthetic gradient with a benign reference direction (approximated by consecutive global model differences w_t − w_{t−1}), gradient-norm matching to evade geometry-based trimming, and standard cross-entropy to keep decoded samples class-discriminative. Synthetic labels target underrepresented classes in the client's shard, exploiting non-IID coverage gaps that marginal-utility evaluators like Federated Shapley Value reward disproportionately. The resulting hybrid update (1−α)g^(r) + αg^(s) with α ≪ 1 keeps the benign training signal dominant, so global accuracy remains stable while the small synthetic component accumulates non-negative marginal utility across rounds. The paper provides a theoretical argument under smoothness assumptions that direction-aligned, norm-matched synthetic components inflate attribution without perturbing the optimization trajectory, and validates the attack empirically across CIFAR-10, SVHN, and FashionMNIST with ResNet-18, WRN-28-10, and VGG16-BN under both FedSV and Leave-One-Out evaluators.

## Results

Under FedSV evaluation, the Latent Optimization attack inflates the malicious client's normalized attribution from near-zero (rank 10/10) to 0.1682 (rank 2/10) on CIFAR-10+ResNet-18, 0.0761 (rank 6/10) on CIFAR-10+WRN-28-10, and 0.1160 (rank 4/10) on CIFAR-10+VGG16_BN, while preserving global test accuracy within ~2% of the attack-free baseline (e.g., 76.75%→77.59% on CIFAR-10+ResNet-18). Similar gains appear on FashionMNIST (up to 0.1072) and SVHN (up to 0.1256). Geometry-based trimming fails to detect the attacker, with F1-scores at or below random guessing (0.00–0.01 vs. 0.10 baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent Optimization decisively outperforms all four baselines on attribution inflation. Label Flipping (Xiao et al., 2012) and Random Noise (Shahani & Scheutz, 2025) produce negligible or inconsistent attribution shifts (e.g., 0.0591 and 0.0074 vs. 0.1682 on CIFAR-10+ResNet-18) while simultaneously degrading test accuracy by 10–12 percentage points. Free Rider (Fraboni et al., 2021) preserves utility but yields only modest attribution gains (0.0802 on CIFAR-10+ResNet-18, rank 8) and never reaches the upper half of the ranking. Centralized attribution attacks adapted to FL—Shadow and Outlier (Wang et al., 2024e)—achieve limited and inconsistent gains (e.g., 0.1248 and 0.0916 vs. 0.1522 for Latent Optimization targeting rank-2 clients on CIFAR-10+ResNet-18) and degrade accuracy more severely (67–72% vs. 73.6%). The key differentiator is that Latent Optimization is the only method that simultaneously inflates attribution, preserves global utility within ~2%, and evades geometry-based trimming (F1 ≈ 0.00–0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of federated learning security and data attribution, repurposing the synthetic-data generation paradigm (latent optimization with a fixed decoder) toward a novel objective: inflating a client's marginal-utility attribution score while preserving global utility. It departs from prior FL security work, which focuses on model-centric poisoning or anomalous-update detection, and from centralized attribution-manipulation studies (Wang et al., 2024e) that target per-sample data value rather than client-level contribution in a distributed setting. The attack exploits structural properties of non-IID label coverage and evaluator sensitivity to marginal utility—observations documented in data-valuation literature but never leveraged adversarially in FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As attribution metrics increasingly govern data pricing, participation rights, and provenance auditing in collaborative ML systems, the finding that a single participant can substantially inflate its measured contribution while evading standard geometry-based defenses (F1 near zero, worse than random guessing) exposes a critical and previously unaddressed attack surface. The paper's limitation to a single attacker, marginal-utility evaluators (FedSV, LOO), and image classification leaves open whether the vulnerability persists under multi-attacker dynamics, dynamic participation, or alternative scoring mechanisms. Developing attribution-robust evaluators and incentive-compatible scoring rules that are resistant to utility-preserving manipulation is identified as a key future direction.
