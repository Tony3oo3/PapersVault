## Problem

Vision-language action models struggle to generalize to unseen task compositions and novel behaviors because they learn tasks in isolation within a single parametric space. Existing skill-centric methods treat tasks as compositions of atomic skills but organize those skills loosely, lacking both a hierarchy that captures multi-grained similarities (mechanical and semantic) and a network-level mechanism to express those similarities as structured, transferable parameter sharing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265174/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model receives three synchronized RGB views (left wrist, right wrist, head), the dual-arm proprioceptive joint state, and a free-form language instruction. A lightweight VLM extracts verb phrases from the instruction, which are mapped through the skill hierarchy into a sequence of contextualized skill embeddings (concatenation of motion-category, semantic-category, and skill-realization embeddings, each 64-dim). The policy outputs a chunk of H future actions (H=10 in simulation, H=50 in real-world) via a flow-matching denoising process conditioned on the skill embedding sequence.

## Method

SkillNet combines two components: (1) Hierarchical Skill Modeling (HSM) organizes skills as a three-level DAG — motion categories (13 clusters derived by spectral clustering over 6-digit motion codes annotated on 470K+ demonstrations), VerbNet semantic categories (127 curated classes), and fine-grained skill realizations (200+ verbal phrases) — producing a contextualized skill embedding per skill via dedicated embedding layers. (2) Skill-Contextualized Mixture-of-Experts (SCMoE) replaces dense feedforward layers in the π_0.5 flow-matching action expert with sparse top-K expert mixtures whose routing is conditioned on the full task-level skill embedding sequence through cross-attention, so that similar skills activate overlapping expert compositions and share parameters. A router balance loss prevents expert collapse, and a shared always-on expert stabilizes training. The paper also provides a theoretical bound showing that routing overlap between similar skills constrains the total-variation distance between their induced policies, formalizing how the MoE structure enforces correlated behavior for related skills.

## Results

On the proposed LIBERO-Skill benchmark (zero-shot generalization to unseen task compositions), SkillNet achieves 47.8% average success rate, improving over the state-of-the-art π_0.5 baseline (31.8%) by 16.0%. Under few-shot transfer to unseen behaviors (15-shot, real-world), SkillNet achieves a 23.9% improvement over π_0.5, with 15-shot performance approaching baselines trained on 25 demonstrations. On in-domain LIBERO, SkillNet reaches 99.1% average success rate, surpassing all baselines including π_0.5 (98.0%) and AtomicVLA (97.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkillNet decisively outperforms all baselines on the zero-shot LIBERO-Skill benchmark: earlier AR-based models (OpenVLA at 2.0%, OpenVLA-OFT at 0.0%) and the flow-matching π_0 (3.3%) essentially fail to generalize to unseen compositions, while GR00T-N1.6 (12.2%) shows limited and uneven capability. The primary SOTA baseline π_0.5 achieves 31.8%, and adding a vanilla MoE on the same backbone yields only a marginal gain to 33.1%, confirming that the improvement comes from structured skill modeling rather than MoE capacity alone. SkillNet (47.8%) surpasses π_0.5 by 16.0% and Vanilla MoE by 14.7%. On in-domain LIBERO, SkillNet (99.1%) edges out π_0.5 (98.0%), AtomicVLA (97.8%), and GR00T-N1.6 (97.0%), confirming that the skill-aware routing also benefits standard multi-task training. On real-world zero-shot compositional tasks, SkillNet achieves 62.5% vs. π_0.5's 42.5% (+20.0%) and Vanilla MoE's 44.2% (+18.3%), with the largest gains on Sweep+Shake (65.0% vs. 5.0% for π_0.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

SkillNet builds directly on the π_0.5 flow-matching VLA as its backbone, extending it with a Skill-Contextualized Mixture-of-Experts (SCMoE) module that conditions expert routing on hierarchical skill attributes. Conceptually, it departs from task-driven VLAs (OpenVLA, π_0, GR00T) that learn all tasks in a single undifferentiated parametric space, and from earlier skill-centric methods (Code-as-Policies, Uni-Skill, SkillDiffuser) that organize skills as flat API calls or discovered latent codes without explicit multi-grained structure. The key novelty is the two-axis hierarchy—motion codes (mechanical properties) and VerbNet classes (semantic roles)—that feeds into MoE routing, repurposing the MoE mechanism (previously used in VLAs for scaling, e.g., DriveMoE, ChatVLA) from a capacity-expansion tool into a structured transfer mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Compositional generalization—executing novel skill sequences or adapting to unseen behaviors with few demonstrations—remains a central bottleneck for deploying VLA models beyond their training distribution. SkillNet's hierarchical skill modeling provides a principled inductive bias that is robust to moderate annotation noise (only 4% degradation at 20% tag corruption), making it practical for large-scale data curation. The LIBERO-Skill benchmark fills a gap in existing evaluation suites that primarily test object/spatial variation rather than task-composition novelty. Open questions include the optimal depth of the skill hierarchy, whether finer-grained decompositions yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to continuous (non-discrete) skill spaces or multi-embodiment settings.
