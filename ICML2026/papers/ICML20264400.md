## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery methods operate on holistic embeddings or unstructured patch tokens, treating discovery as improved clustering without explicitly modeling how known and novel categories share and recombine visual primitives. This lack of compositional structure leads to fragmented unknown classes, collapsed geometry, and brittle novelty decisions, because the model cannot extrapolate to unseen primitive combinations in a human-like manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patch tokens (N×D) and a class token from a frozen DINO ViT-B/16 backbone operating on standard RGB images at 224×224. It produces refined, geometrically calibrated token embeddings that are averaged and added to the original class token before being fed to an unchanged GCD projection head, which outputs class labels for both known and novel categories. The module adds only a few linear projections (M=16 primitives, H=8 attention heads) with negligible parameter and FLOP overhead.

## Method

CoGe-GCD is a plug-and-play inductive bias module inserted between the backbone and projection head, decomposing discovery into two coupled stages. Compositional Perception first structures patch tokens into a compact vocabulary of M competing primitives via a column-stochastic token-primitive assignment matrix (each primitive distributes unit probability mass across tokens), then performs evidence consolidation by aggregating token features into primitive embeddings and disseminating them back to tokens through degree-normalized hypergraph message passing. Generalizing Induction then applies a training-free geometric calibration: a row-stochastic spatial proximity kernel smooths the membership matrix while preserving its column-stochastic form, enforcing cognitive principles of proximity and continuity to align the induced token-primitive geometry with perceptual structure. The entire pipeline is grounded in a Latent Compositional Manifold Assumption (low-rank signal plus isotropic noise with spatial smoothness), and a proposition guarantees strict MSE reduction over the raw input. The module requires no changes to the GCD head, loss function, or training protocol, making it compatible with diverse existing frameworks.

## Results

On fine-grained benchmarks (CUB-200, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars), CoGe-GCD plugged into four GCD frameworks yields an average improvement of +4.03% All-class accuracy and +4.75% Novel-class accuracy across baselines. The largest single gain is +13.5% Novel accuracy for LegoGCD on Stanford-Cars (40.3→53.8). On coarse-grained benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100), the average gains are +1.07% All and +1.60% Novel. The module adds only 0.06 MB parameters and ~43 G FLOPs over SimGCD, with nearly unchanged training/inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGe-GCD is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on four GCD frameworks (SimGCD, LegoGCD, CMS, SelEx) and compared against 13 baselines in the tables. Plugged into SimGCD, it improves All-class accuracy by +4.1% and Novel by +5.6% on average across fine-grained datasets, with the largest Novel gain of +10.3% on Stanford-Cars (41.7→51.4). For LegoGCD, the average All gain is +4.3% and Novel +5.1%, with a standout +13.5% Novel improvement on Stanford-Cars. CMS benefits by +3.8% All and +3.3% Novel on average, while SelEx (the strongest baseline) still gains +3.9% All and +5.0% Novel, reaching 80.9% All on CUB-200. On coarse-grained datasets (Table 6), gains are smaller but consistent: SelEx improves from 94.1→96.2 All on CIFAR-10 and 80.0→81.6 All on CIFAR-100. The remaining baselines (GCD, GPC, XCon, PromptCAL, AMEND, μGCD, InfoSieve, ORCA, PIM) serve as reference points in the tables; CoGe-GCD's best-plugged configurations (e.g., SelEx+CoGe-GCD at 80.9% on CUB-200) surpass all of them, confirming that the compositional-geometric inductive bias is complementary to existing GCD objectives rather than replacing them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) through the lens of compositional generalization, a concept well-studied in language and reasoning but largely absent from visual GCD pipelines. Rather than building on a single prior GCD method, it repurposes hypergraph message-passing and Gestalt perceptual principles (proximity, continuity) to insert a structured token-primitive inductive bias between a frozen ViT backbone and any GCD head. The key departure from prior work is that existing GCD methods (CMS, SimGCD, SelEx, LegoGCD, etc.) operate on holistic embeddings or weakly structured tokens and treat discovery as improved clustering, whereas CoGe-GCD explicitly decomposes the process into Compositional Perception (primitive competition via column-stochastic assignment) and Generalizing Induction (spatially calibrated geometry), grounding the design in a Latent Compositional Manifold Assumption with a formal error-reduction guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—requiring no changes to backbones, heads, or losses—makes CoGe-GCD immediately applicable to any existing GCD framework, and the consistent gains across six benchmarks and multiple baselines (average +4.03% All / +4.75% Novel on fine-grained) demonstrate practical utility. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 3.1, spectral justification via von Neumann entropy and effective rank) provides a principled explanation for why structuring tokens into primitives before clustering helps, which is a step beyond the empirical-only justification common in GCD. Limitations include modest gains on coarse-grained datasets (~1%), occasional small drops in known-class accuracy, and reliance on a fixed primitive count M=16 that is not dataset-adaptive. Open questions include whether the compositional structure transfers to non-ViT backbones or video, and whether the geometric calibration is truly capturing perceptual continuity or merely acting as a spatial low-pass filter.
