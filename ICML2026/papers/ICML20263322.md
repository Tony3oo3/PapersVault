## Problem

Existing tree-search decoding policies for LLMs are largely budget-agnostic: they use fixed search hyperparameters and treat the per-query token budget merely as a stopping condition. This mismatch causes two failure modes—late-stage over-branching that exhausts the budget before refinement, or premature termination that leaves budget unused—preventing the search from allocating a fixed budget optimally between exploration and answer completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem statement, a fixed output-token budget B (tested at 10K, 20K, 30K), an open-weight LLM for generation, and a process reward model (GenPRM-7B) that scores each node by evaluating the full reasoning path from root to that node. Nodes are constructed via sequential generation truncated at '\nStep' boundaries (or full generation for baselines), and the budget counts only output tokens generated across all expansions. The output is the highest-scoring completed answer found in the search tree, selected among all nodes containing a valid final answer.

## Method

BG-MCTS conditions two MCTS decisions on the budget sufficiency ratio ρ = 1 − C_used/B. First, the BG-PUCT selection score multiplies the exploration bonus by ρ (annealing exploration as budget depletes) and adds a completion-bias term κ(1−ρ)·d(x)/d̂_ans to node values, which increasingly favors deeper nodes approaching answer completion. Second, a virtual generative child is introduced at each non-terminal node with score E_gen = μ(p) + λρ·σ²(p), making widening an explicit selectable action whose incentive is suppressed by ρ, thereby discouraging late-stage branching from shallow nodes. Together these mechanisms induce a budget-aligned wide-to-deep schedule: broad exploration when ρ ≈ 1, shifting to focused refinement and answer completion as ρ → 0, without requiring any model retraining or changes to the reward model.

## Results

BG-MCTS achieves the highest average accuracy across all three open-weight LLMs on both mathematical reasoning benchmarks under fixed token budgets of 10K, 20K, and 30K. On MATH500 (Lv.5), BG-MCTS reaches 43.4% (Llama-3.1-8B), 69.1% (Qwen2.5-7B), and 79.8% (Qwen3-32B) average accuracy, outperforming standard MCTS by 4.4, 4.6, and 4.0 points respectively. On AIME24/25, BG-MCTS achieves 8.7%, 17.6%, and 31.1% average accuracy, exceeding MCTS by 2.0, 1.3, and 3.3 points. On the physics benchmark UGPhysics, BG-MCTS similarly leads with 24.6% (Qwen2.5-7B) and 35.7% (Qwen3-32B) average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BG-MCTS outperforms all five baselines across every model, benchmark, and budget setting. Against standard MCTS (Silver et al., 2017), BG-MCTS gains 4.4–4.6 points on MATH500 and 1.3–3.3 points on AIME24/25, with the largest AIME gap on Qwen3-32B (.311 vs .278). Against Repeated Sampling (Wang et al., 2023), BG-MCTS leads by 0.7–4.6 points on MATH500 and 3.9–12.4 points on AIME24/25, indicating that budget-aware tree search extracts substantially more value from the same token budget than parallel sampling. Against Sequential Refinement (Madaan et al., 2023), BG-MCTS wins by wide margins (e.g., .434 vs .245 on Llama MATH500), confirming that iterative single-trajectory refinement is far less effective under fixed budgets. Against AB-MCTS-M (Inoue et al., 2025), BG-MCTS is consistently stronger, particularly on AIME where AB-MCTS-M drops to .007–.230 average accuracy while BG-MCTS maintains .087–.311. Against LiteSearch (Wang et al., 2024a), BG-MCTS achieves higher accuracy at every budget level (e.g., .434 vs .281 on Llama MATH500), because LiteSearch's early-stopping mechanism leaves budget unused rather than reallocating it toward refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PUCT-based MCTS (Silver et al., 2017) as applied to LLM tree-search decoding, following the hybrid test-time scaling line that includes AB-MCTS-M (Inoue et al., 2025), LiteSearch (Wang et al., 2024a), and numerous other tree-search decoders. Its key departure is making the search policy itself a function of the remaining token budget ratio ρ, rather than treating the budget as a mere stopping condition: it anneals the PUCT exploration term, adds a depth-biased completion bonus to exploitation, and gates dynamic widening through a virtual generative child whose score is scaled by ρ. This produces an explicit wide-to-deep schedule that prior budget-agnostic methods do not encode, and it is positioned as complementary to (not a replacement for) early-stopping approaches like LiteSearch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fixed per-query token budgets are the dominant deployment constraint for API-served LLMs, so a decoding policy that explicitly allocates a given budget between exploration and completion has direct practical value. The paper's 'fewer instances, higher-precision answers' trade-off also opens a useful angle for budgeted data synthesis, where quality of retained traces matters more than raw coverage. Key limitations include reliance on a highly saturated PRM (GenPRM-7B scores are near-binary), evaluation restricted to math and physics reasoning with three open-weight models, and the budget model counting only output tokens (not input or reward-model compute). Open questions include whether the budget-conditioned schedule transfers to domains with less structured step boundaries, how it interacts with multi-reward-model settings, and whether the wide-to-deep transition can be learned rather than hand-designed.
