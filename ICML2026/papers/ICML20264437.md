## Problem

SVD-based LLM compression is limited by two coupled failures: (i) global rank allocation methods rely on uncalibrated spectral proxies or expensive per-layer measurements that ignore the non-linear error propagation inherent in deep networks, and (ii) Fisher-weighted SVD decompositions suffer from severe rank collapse because sequence-gradient estimators are sample-limited (rank ≤ k) relative to the parameter dimensionality of modern LLM layers. No prior method simultaneously addresses both the propagation-aware allocation problem and the decomposition quality bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM's linear weight matrices, 256 calibration sequences of length 2048 (from WikiText2) for factor estimation, and a target parameter retention ratio (e.g., 0.8 or 0.6). It outputs a compressed model where each linear layer is replaced by two consecutive rank-r factors (C and B, with Ŵ = CB), plus a per-layer rank allocation vector. The ILP search operates over a discrete candidate set with per-layer compression bounded to c_i ∈ [0.3, 1.0], and the one-time KL calibration step (at c_ref = 0.1) is fully cacheable across compression ratios, reducing subsequent search runs to ~3 minutes on 8B models.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary components. LEMS models each layer's compression cost as a product of three calibrated terms: a dense spectral-energy shape (normalized tail energy of the whitened SVD), an empirical scale factor (KL divergence measured at a single reference compression point per layer), and a depth-dependent bias (a harmonic tail sum β(d_i) = κ·Σ_{k=d_i}^B k^{-γ}/Z that penalizes early-block compression to account for error propagation). These costs feed an integer linear program solved by Gurobi to find the globally optimal per-layer rank assignment under a parameter budget. KFAC-SVD replaces GFWSVD's sequence-gradient Kronecker fitting (limited to rank ≤ k) with token-wise second-moment estimation of the input covariance Â and output-gradient covariance Ĝ, yielding an effective sample count of N ≈ S·T (e.g., ~5.2×10^5) that prevents spectral collapse and preserves diverse curvature directions for the Fisher-weighted truncation.

## Results

At 0.8 parameter retention, LEMS achieves the best zero-shot accuracy across all three model families: 57.67% on Mistral-7B, 55.99% on Llama 3-8B, and 59.28% on Qwen3-8B, outperforming the strongest baseline (ARS) by 3.09, 1.68, and 3.62 p.p. respectively. LEMS also delivers the lowest WikiText2 perplexity (5.98, 8.16, 10.38), reducing mean perplexity by 39–84% relative to uniform allocation. At the more aggressive 0.6 retention, LEMS extends these gains, achieving 45.60% accuracy on Mistral-7B vs. 40.83% for ARS. KFAC-SVD achieves an average 15% perplexity improvement over the best SVD baseline, with GFWSVD suffering catastrophic degradation (perplexity >2500 at 0.7 retention).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEMS consistently outperforms all six search baselines on both perplexity and accuracy across Mistral-7B, Llama 3-8B, and Qwen3-8B at both 0.8 and 0.6 retention. The strongest baseline, ARS (gradient-based learned rank allocation), achieves second-best performance but still trails LEMS by 1.7–4.8 p.p. in accuracy and requires ~7× more calibration data plus hyperparameter tuning. ASVD and SVD-LLMv2 frequently fail to beat uniform allocation, confirming that uncalibrated spectral-energy proxies and group-level redistribution are insufficient. ATP, the only baseline explicitly modeling error propagation, matches or narrowly exceeds uniform but cannot capture layer-type-specific importance. On the decomposition side (Table 2), KFAC-SVD achieves the lowest perplexity across all settings, improving WikiText2 perplexity by 38% over DOBI-SVD at 0.7 retention on Llama 3-8B, while GFWSVD catastrophically degrades (perplexity >2500), confirming the rank-collapse failure mode that KFAC-SVD's token-wise estimation resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two SVD-compression subproblems: decomposition quality and global rank allocation. For decomposition, it directly extends GFWSVD (Chekalina et al., 2025) by replacing its sequence-gradient Kronecker-factor fitting with token-wise second-moment estimation (following the KFAC framework of Martens & Grosse, 2015), thereby removing the rank bottleneck that causes spectral collapse. For rank allocation, it departs from greedy (MRCS), balanced-error (ASVD, SVD-LLMv2), and gradient-based (ARS) search strategies by formulating the problem as an ILP over a calibrated layer-wise error surrogate that combines spectral shape, empirical KL calibration, and a depth-dependent propagation bias inspired by the error-explosion theory of Huang et al. (2025) and the ILP-based sparsity allocation of MDP (Sun et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVD-based compression is one of the most practically deployable LLM compression strategies because it preserves dense tensor operations compatible with standard inference engines, and this paper tackles two bottlenecks that have limited its effectiveness: suboptimal rank allocation and Fisher-estimation rank deficiency. The demonstrated scalability to 70B parameters and compatibility with both activation-based and Fisher-based SVD broadens its applicability. Key limitations include the continued reliance on gradient computation for KFAC-SVD (limiting efficiency relative to purely activation-based methods) and the one-time per-layer calibration step that scales linearly with model depth. Open questions include whether formal analytical bounds on error propagation can replace the empirically fitted bias, and whether the approach extends to VLMs and MoE architectures.
