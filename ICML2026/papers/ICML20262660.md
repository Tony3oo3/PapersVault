## Problem

LLMs exhibit severe performance degradation on low-resource languages due to long-tailed pretraining distributions, noisy parallel data, and unstable cross-lingual alignment. Existing cross-lingual adaptation methods—whether MT-based pipelines or multilingual encoders—suffer from error propagation and semantic drift, and lack a unified theoretical framework that provides formal guarantees on representation stability across retrieval, ranking, and reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262660/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a low-resource language sentence (from 7 Southeast/South Asian languages) and a set of precomputed English translation candidates generated by k different MT/LLM translators (pass@k budget, k=0–4). Token-level representations are extracted from both a multilingual encoder (mT5-XL) and a frozen English LLM encoder (Qwen3-Embedding-8B), pooled to fixed temporal lengths, and projected into a shared space via a learned adaptor. The output is a single L2-normalized fused embedding for retrieval/ranking scoring, or a generated answer for reasoning tasks; the embedding is specifically constructed so that its deviation from an ideal English-anchored reference is bounded by the anchoring error and translation distortion terms.

## Method

LiRA is a plug-and-play framework with two complementary modules. Arca reduces cross-lingual error via a critic-actor loop: a lightweight LLM Translation Critic scores each candidate on semantic fidelity, emotional consistency, and pragmatic tone; an Embedding Critic enforces cosine alignment between the multilingual feature path and the English translation path (minimizing anchoring error ε₁); and an Actor MLP trained with REINFORCE selects the best candidate using a composite reward that jointly penalizes translation distortion ε₂ and rewards feature-path similarity. LaSR then fuses the selected multilingual and English embeddings through a shared adaptor and a small LLM transformer, trained with two FIFO queues—CorrQueue for Pearson/Spearman correlation on ranking data and DocQueue for listwise soft-nDCG@10 with in-language hard-negative mining and safe-negative down-weighting. The theoretical analysis models the English encoder as a kernel mean embedding in an RKHS and proves that the representation deviation is bounded by ε₁ + C√(2ε₂), with a downstream stability corollary under data-local Lipschitz continuity, providing formal guarantees that shrinking the two error terms yields stable retrieval and reasoning behavior.

## Results

On the newly introduced LazRetrieval dataset (7 South/Southeast Asian languages, nDCG@10), LiRA-Max on Qwen3-Embedding-8B achieves 77.71 average, outperforming the Qwen3-E-8B baseline (68.05) by +9.66 points, with the largest gains on low-resource languages (Bn +7.11, Ur +6.30, Th +8.85). On public retrieval benchmarks (MLQARetrieval, BelebeleRetrieval, STS22), LiRA improves Qwen3-E-8B from 79.57 to 81.35 average and E5-Mistral from 52.55 to 56.01. On reasoning tasks, LiRA-Large on Qwen3-8B raises MGSM accuracy from 69.4 to 71.1 (+1.7) and X-CSQA from 62.9 to 65.5 (+2.6), with improvements concentrated on lower-resource languages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On retrieval, LiRA outperforms LUSIFER (54.99 avg on public benchmarks) by a wide margin (81.35 with Qwen3-E-8B backbone), and also surpasses E5-Mistral-7B (64.51 on LazRetrieval) by +13.2 points with LiRA-Max. On mathematical reasoning (MGSM), LiRA-Large (71.1 avg) beats mCoT (66.2), MAPO (58.0), LinguaLIFT (65.5), and MindMerger-Soft (57.3), with the largest per-language gains on Bengali (+3.2 over mCoT) and Thai (+4.9 over mCoT). On reading comprehension (X-CSQA), LiRA-Large (65.5 avg) surpasses LinguaLIFT (61.5), MindMerger-Soft (61.0), and mCoT (not reported on X-CSQA), with particularly strong improvements on Urdu (+6.5 over LinguaLIFT) and Hindi (+5.0 over LinguaLIFT). LiRA also demonstrates plug-and-play robustness: when attached to smaller backbones (GTE-Large, BGE-en-v1.5, E5-Mistral), it consistently improves over the unmodified backbone by 2.8–3.5 points on average, confirming gains are not tied to a single encoder family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant cross-lingual adaptation paradigms—MT-based pipelines (which suffer error propagation and semantic drift) and multilingual encoders (which lack LLM-level reasoning)—by proposing a plug-and-play anchoring framework (LiRA) that explicitly models and bounds both translation-induced semantic drift and representation-mapping error. Conceptually, it builds on the critic-actor / REINFORCE paradigm from reinforcement learning and the kernel mean embedding (RKHS) literature, but applies them to cross-lingual alignment for the first time with formal deviation guarantees. It also positions itself against recent LLM-integration systems like MindMerger and LUSIFER, arguing that their reliance on parallel translation data or zero-shot training paradigms leaves them theoretically underdeveloped and vulnerable to the same noise propagation they aim to mitigate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LazRetrieval dataset (7 South/Southeast Asian languages, e-commerce domain) addresses a real gap: prior cross-lingual benchmarks are susceptible to data contamination from LLM pretraining, making fair comparison difficult. The theoretical framework (bounded representation deviation under Lipschitz continuity) is a welcome addition to a field that has been largely empirical, though the bounds are loose in practice and the 'lightweight' framing is somewhat undermined by LiRA-Max's 103B total parameters. Open questions include whether the gains generalize beyond the tested language set, whether the critic-actor translation selection introduces evaluator-family bias (acknowledged by the authors), and whether the theoretical constants (C, L_loc) can be tightened to yield practically useful guarantees rather than merely existence proofs.
