## Problem

Existing mathematical benchmarks for LLMs conflate pattern memorization with genuine symbolic reasoning, focus on pre-university-level problems, or mix text-to-math conversion with pure symbolic manipulation, making it impossible to isolate whether models truly understand university-level symbolic math (integration, differential equations, series, limits, hypergeometrics) or merely recognize familiar problem templates. ASyMOB addresses this by systematically perturbing seed problems to probe generalization beyond memorized patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264030/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem is a purely symbolic math expression in LaTeX with no surrounding word-problem text, presented with a formatting instruction to output a single LaTeX expression after 'The final answer is:'. Validation is deterministic and multi-step: regex extraction of the answer, parsing to SymPy (with an LLM fallback for ~9% of cases), then dual verification via symbolic simplification (SymPy.simplify of the difference) and numeric evaluation (5 random substitutions with <0.002% relative tolerance). The benchmark uses pass@1 across 35,368 algorithmically generated variants, and the dataset is regenerable before each new model evaluation to prevent contamination.

## Method

ASyMOB is built from 100 seed problems (55 curated from university benchmarks and olympiads, 45 newly created) spanning Integrals, Differential Equations, Series, Limits, and Hypergeometric Functions. Each seed is manually perturbed with 2–5 symbolic parameters, then algorithmic transformations generate all combinatorial variants: Symbolic-N (varying the number of free symbols), Numeric-All-N (replacing all symbols with random positive integers of N digits, 0–10), Numeric-One-N (replacing a single symbol), Numeric-All-N-S (50 random re-samplings for variance measurement), and Equivalence perturbations (inserting publicly known expressions equal to 1, in Easy and Hard variants across trigonometric, hyperbolic, logarithmic, complex-exponential, and series identities). The key design principle is that perturbations modify symbolic structure without changing the core solution technique, enabling fine-grained measurement of whether performance degrades from the first symbolic parameter or progressively. Evaluation compares 17 model setups (closed- and open-weight, with and without code execution) plus SymPy as a pure CAS baseline, revealing that most models' success drops from ~75% on seeds to ~47% on the full benchmark, while frontier models (o4-mini, Gemini-2.5 Flash, DeepSeek-R1) show a robustness regime shift.

## Results

On the full ASyMOB benchmark (35,368 problems), the average model success rate drops from 74.6% on the unperturbed seed set to 46.8%, with the top three models—o4-mini (no code, 78.1%), Gemini-2.5 Flash (no code, 78.5%), and DeepSeek-R1 (78.3%)—achieving a ~20-point gap over the next-best model. Equivalence perturbations are the hardest category for most models (e.g., GPT-4o drops to 15.1% with code, 9.3% without), while Limits are the easiest topic (59.5% average) and Integrals the hardest (42.6%). Code execution boosts weaker models substantially (GPT-4o: 16.8% → 35.3% total) but provides no benefit or even slight degradation for frontier models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The top three LLMs (o4-mini, Gemini-2.5 Flash, DeepSeek-R1) all exceed 78% total accuracy, outperforming the next tier (Gemini-2.0 Flash at 54.9–58.1%, GPT-4.1 at 46.2–48.9%) by roughly 20–30 points, suggesting a regime shift in perturbation robustness. Code execution helps weaker models dramatically—GPT-4o jumps from 16.8% to 35.3% total, and Gemini-2.0 Flash from 54.9% to 58.1%—but frontier models like o4-mini and Gemini-2.5 Flash actually perform slightly better without code (78.1% vs. 76.1%; 78.5% vs. 69.5%). DeepSeek-R1 (78.3%) outperforms its proof-specialized sibling DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (46.3%) on every single category, despite the latter achieving 88.9% on MiniF2F, indicating that formal proof proficiency does not transfer to symbolic manipulation. SymPy as a pure CAS baseline scores only 39.2% total (21.9% on Equivalence), well below the top LLMs, yet the paper identifies specific problems where SymPy, Mathematica, and WolframAlpha all fail while LLMs succeed, and one problem solvable only via a hybrid LLM-simplify-then-CAS strategy. GPT-4o-mini (11.8%) and Gemma-3n-e4b-it (12.0%) represent the weakest performers, with near-zero success on Equivalence perturbations (3.8% and 4.7% respectively), highlighting that even small models struggle with structural complexity beyond simple pattern matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASyMOB departs from prior symbolic math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, MathBench) by targeting pure university-level symbolic manipulation with no word-problem or linguistic component, and from perturbation-based evaluations like GSM-Symbolic (Mirzadeh et al., 2025) and MATH-perturb (Huang et al., 2025), which modify textual phrasing or numeric values in school-level problems. Its key conceptual departure is introducing 'Symbolic' and 'Equivalence' perturbation categories that alter the algebraic structure of the problem itself while preserving the required solution technique, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of whether models perform genuine symbolic reasoning or pattern matching. The algorithmic generation pipeline (100 seeds → 35,368 variants) draws on neuro-symbolic data generation ideas (Li et al., 2024) but applies them to a domain and perturbation taxonomy not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that average model success drops from 74.6% to 46.8% under perturbations, combined with the observed robustness 'regime shift' in frontier models, provides a principled diagnostic for separating genuine symbolic understanding from memorization — a question central to deploying LLMs in scientific discovery. The demonstration that CAS systems (SymPy, Mathematica, WolframAlpha) fail on problems LLMs solve, and that a hybrid LLM+CAS strategy solves problems neither can handle alone, opens a concrete integration frontier for AI-for-science tooling. Open questions include: why frontier models no longer benefit from code execution (suggesting their internal reasoning may be approaching or exceeding CAS capability), what architectural or training factors drive the robustness regime shift, and whether the 'simplify-then-code' prompting strategy generalizes beyond the single illustrative example provided.
