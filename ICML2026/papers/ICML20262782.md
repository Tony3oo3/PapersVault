## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning (PFL) methods for graph data operate exclusively in Euclidean space, implicitly assuming uniform flat geometry across all clients. This misalignment with the intrinsic negative curvature (hyperbolic structure) prevalent in real-world graphs forces reliance on complex heterogeneity-handling mechanisms—client similarity estimation, auxiliary modules, or clustering—that incur significant computational overhead. The paper asks whether non-Euclidean geometry can natively encode client heterogeneity and simplify aggregation.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subgraph with node features, edge topology, and node labels, partitioned with non-overlapping node sets across clients. Inputs are projected from Euclidean space into a client-specific Lorentz (hyperboloid) space via an exponential map parameterized by a learnable curvature K_c, initialized from the graph's Forman-Ricci curvature. The primary output is per-node class predictions; a secondary graph-classification task produces per-graph labels. Notably, only space-like (shared) parameters are communicated to the server, while personalized time-like parameters and K_c remain local.

## Method

FlatLand embeds each client's data in a tailored Lorentz space with a learnable Lorentz scale parameter K_c (sectional curvature −1/K_c), initialized from the client graph's average Forman-Ricci curvature and updated during local training. The backbone is a Fully Lorentz Neural Network whose linear layers are decomposed into a time-like weight vector m^(l) (personalized) and a space-like weight matrix M^(l) (shared). The key parameter decoupling strategy aggregates only M^(l) at the server via standard FedAvg, while m^(l) and K_c remain client-local, avoiding any client-similarity estimation or auxiliary modules. Theoretically, Theorem 4.1 proves that a single curvature cannot simultaneously fit clients with differing Ricci curvatures, and Theorem 4.3 shows that client heterogeneity is encoded exclusively in the time-like dimension, justifying the decoupling. Proposition 6.1 guarantees that aggregated space-like parameters still map outputs onto the correct client-specific Lorentz manifold, preserving geometric validity after server-side averaging.

## Results

On homophilic node classification (10 clients), FlatLand achieves 80.46% on Cora, 73.90% on CiteSeer, 67.52% on ogbn-arxiv, and 92.49% on Amazon-Photo, outperforming the strongest Euclidean PFL baseline FED-PUB (81.54/72.35/66.58/92.73) on CiteSeer and ogbn-arxiv while remaining competitive on Cora and Photo. On heterophilic datasets (10 clients), FlatLand leads on Roman-Empire (66.10%), Minesweeper (76.34%), and Questions (67.71%), surpassing the best baselines by 1.5–3.7 points. FlatLand also consistently outperforms the hyperbolic baseline FedHGCN, which suffers OOM on ogbn-arxiv and underperforms Euclidean methods in many heterogeneous settings. In low-dimensional settings (embedding dim 4), FlatLand's advantage over all baselines becomes most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlatLand outperforms the strongest Euclidean PFL baseline FED-PUB on CiteSeer (73.90 vs 72.35, +1.55) and ogbn-arxiv (67.52 vs 66.58, +0.94) with 10 clients, while FED-PUB retains a slight edge on Cora (81.54 vs 80.46) and Photo (92.73 vs 92.49). On heterophilic datasets, FlatLand achieves the best accuracy on Roman-Empire (66.10 vs AdaFGL's 64.55), Minesweeper (76.34 vs Local(L)'s 75.02), and Questions (67.71 vs FedSage+'s 65.06), with margins of 1.5–3.7 points. The hyperbolic baseline FedHGCN, despite operating in hyperbolic space, underperforms FlatLand by 8–15 points on most datasets and encounters OOM on ogbn-arxiv due to its quadratic node-selection module. Standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedGNN) degrade severely under heterogeneity, often falling below even local training (e.g., FedAvg drops to 69.19% on Cora vs 79.94% for Local(E)). FlatLand also surpasses the local hyperbolic baseline Local(L) in most settings (e.g., 82.49 vs 80.46 on Cora-20, 72.24 vs 69.52 on CiteSeer-20), confirming that federated aggregation of shared space-like parameters provides genuine benefit beyond isolated hyperbolic training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlatLand builds directly on FedAvg as its aggregation backbone and on Fully Lorentz (hyperbolic) neural networks as its architectural substrate, but departs from both the Euclidean PFL literature (FedPer, GCFL, FED-PUB, FedGTA, AdaFGL) and the nascent hyperbolic FL line (FedHGCN) by assigning each client a tailored Lorentz space with a learnable curvature parameter. The key conceptual departure is using the Lorentz model's intrinsic time-like/space-like decomposition as a principled parameter decoupling mechanism, eliminating the need for client-similarity estimation, clustering, or auxiliary personalization modules that prior PFL methods require. The paper positions itself as the first to formally bridge hyperbolic geometry and personalized federated learning through this geometric lens, grounded in Forman-Ricci curvature analysis of client graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric decoupling strategy offers a lightweight, module-free alternative to the increasingly complex personalization mechanisms in PFL, with the practical advantage that FlatLand's performance gap over Euclidean baselines widens as embedding dimension shrinks—directly relevant to communication-efficient federated deployment. The theoretical results (tailored curvature necessity, time-like heterogeneity encoding) provide a principled justification rather than purely empirical motivation. However, the authors acknowledge that hyperbolic geometry is not universally optimal (some client graphs may be near-Euclidean or positively curved), and extending to mixed-curvature spaces, richer Lorentz backbones, and non-graph modalities remains open. The moderate but consistent gains over strong baselines like FED-PUB and FedGTA, combined with the low-dimensional advantage, make this a useful but not transformative contribution to the PFL-for-graphs literature.
