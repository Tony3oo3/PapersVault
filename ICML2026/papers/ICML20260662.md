## Problem

Decoder-only LLMs struggle with graph-structured data because standard causal masking and linear positional encodings impose a 1D sequential bias that obscures multi-hop connectivity, cycles, and graph-level symmetries. Existing solutions either linearize graphs into text (losing structural inductive bias) or prepend external GNN/Graph-Transformer encoders that compress topology into static embeddings, creating an information bottleneck that decouples structural message passing from token-level generation. The paper argues the true limitation is not model capacity but the misalignment between attention geometry and graph topology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text-attributed graph (nodes and optionally edges, each carrying a token sequence) together with a natural-language query, all serialized into a single packed token sequence where each token retains a block-local position index and a p-dimensional structural coordinate derived from canonized Laplacian positional encodings. The model is invariant to the ordering of graph blocks in the packed sequence, since masking and positional encoding depend only on logical (block, position) indices rather than absolute sequence position. Output is standard autoregressive text: node labels, link predictions, free-form QA answers, shortest-path descriptions, or node-set enumerations, all generated under a unified decoder-only interface.

## Method

Weaver modifies a pretrained decoder-only LLM (Qwen3-8B) through three attention-level changes that require no external encoder. First, Graph-Causal Masking makes attention causal within each node/edge block but fully bidirectional across graph blocks, enabling global message passing in a single forward pass. Second, GoRoPE (Graph-over-Tokens RoPE) extends rotary positional encoding by adding a structural phase term s·Ψ_{i,ℓ}·θ_k to each frequency band, where Ψ is the canonized Laplacian PE of the source node (concatenated for directed edges), so that structurally distant nodes receive decayed attention exactly as distant tokens do in pretrained long-context models. Third, a Local Prioritization bias (ALiBi-style per-head additive penalty on cross-block logits) resolves positional ambiguity when graph symmetries make ΔΨ = 0. The model is aligned via LoRA on 300k synthetic structural-reasoning graphs plus 50k SceneGraph samples, then optionally fine-tuned on downstream node-classification and link-prediction datasets.

## Results

On zero-shot node classification (Table 2), Weaver achieves 76.5% on Cora and 69.8% on WikiCS among homophilic graphs, and a macro-average of 61.3% across the four WebKB heterophilic datasets, exceeding the strongest generative baseline GOFA by +10.5 points (homophilic) and +26.0 points (heterophilic). On adapted tasks (Table 3), Weaver reaches 86.2% on Pubmed NC (+2.4 over GOFA), 85.9% on Arxiv NC (+11.1 over GOFA), and 94.2% on Pubmed LP. On SceneGraph QA (Table 4), Weaver attains 79.2% accuracy, a +13.2-point gain over its Qwen3-8B base, while GOFA degrades its Mistral base by −11.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generative GLMs, Weaver decisively outperforms GOFA (the strongest encoder-based baseline) by +10.5 points average on homophilic graphs (e.g., 76.5% vs. 71.1% on Cora) and +26.0 points on heterophilic graphs (e.g., 69.4% vs. 39.5% on Cornell). Weaver also surpasses GraphGPT and LLaGA by large margins across all datasets, with the gap most pronounced on heterophilic graphs where encoder-based methods collapse. Against specialized discriminative baselines, Weaver matches or exceeds LLM-BP on homophilic benchmarks (76.5% vs. 72.6% on Cora) but trails by 10–20 points on heterophilic WebKB graphs, where LLM-BP's explicit homophily estimator provides an advantage. On adapted (fine-tuned) tasks, Weaver leads GOFA on Pubmed NC (+2.4), Arxiv NC (+11.1), and Pubmed LP (+0.2), though LLaGA edges out Weaver on Arxiv LP (92.0% vs. 88.1%). On SceneGraph QA, Weaver improves its Qwen3-8B base by +13.2 points while GOFA degrades its Mistral base by −11.9 points, indicating that GoRoPE injects structural signal without disrupting linguistic capability. On synthetic structural reasoning (50-node graphs), Weaver reduces RMSE by 4.5× on Common Neighbors and 2.2× on Monochromatic Subgraph relative to the Qwen3-8B backbone, with the gap widening as graph size grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

Weaver departs from the dominant encoder-based GLM paradigm (GraphGPT, LLaGA, GOFA) that compresses graph topology into static embeddings via external GNNs before feeding them to an LLM, arguing this creates an information bottleneck. Instead, it reparameterizes the attention mechanism of a decoder-only LLM (Qwen3-8B) through three components: Graph-Causal Masking (bidirectional visibility across graph blocks, causal within blocks), GoRoPE (coupling Laplacian positional encodings into rotary phase shifts, building on RoPE and MRoPE), and Local Prioritization (an ALiBi-style bias to resolve positional ambiguity under graph symmetries). The conceptual lineage traces from the observation that Transformer attention is already a form of message passing on a fully connected graph, so the limiting factor is the 1D sequential bias of standard causal masking and linear positional encodings—not model capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the external encoder, Weaver unifies node classification, link prediction, and open-ended graph QA within a single decoder-only framework while retaining token-level granularity, potentially making graph-aware LLMs more composable and easier to adapt to new graph tasks. The 26–30% zero-shot gains over GOFA on heterophilic graphs and the +13.2-point SceneGraph QA improvement over the base LLM (vs. −11.9 for GOFA) suggest that structural inductive bias can be injected without disrupting linguistic capability. Key open questions include closing the remaining gap to specialized discriminative methods (e.g., LLM-BP) on heterophilic graphs, scaling beyond O(L²) attention for very large graphs, and whether the alignment corpus (primarily homophilic synthetic graphs) limits generalization to diverse spectral regimes.
