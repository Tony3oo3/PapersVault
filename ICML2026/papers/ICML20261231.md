## Problem

While conditional independence (CI) testing has well-established minimax rates across parametric and nonparametric settings, and structure learning algorithms rely on CI tests as subroutines, no general information-theoretic reduction connecting the minimax optimality of CI testing to that of structure learning existed. Existing optimality results for DAG learning were restricted to Gaussian distributions, leaving discrete and nonparametric settings without matching upper and lower bounds. This paper formalizes the reduction for poly-forests, showing that the minimax sample complexity of structure learning is entirely determined by the minimax CI testing radius.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n i.i.d. samples from a distribution p that is Markov and c-strong tree-faithful to an unknown poly-forest over d nodes, along with a distribution-specific CI test ψ as a black-box subroutine. It outputs the CPDAG (Markov equivalence class) of the true poly-forest, obtained via a skeleton plus separation sets. Notably, PC-tree only tests marginal independence and conditional independence given a single other node (not all conditioning sets as in the classical PC algorithm), requiring only O(d³) CI test invocations and running in polynomial time.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that if the minimax CI testing radius is r_n ≍ n^{-1/α}, then the optimal sample complexity for poly-forest learning is n ≍ log(d)/c^α, achieved by PC-tree with the optimal CI test ψ. For the upper bound, the median trick amplates the constant-error CI test to exponential error decay, which is then plugged into PC-tree to ensure uniform consistency over all edge decisions. For the lower bound, Tsybakov's method is applied to an ensemble of ≍ d candidate poly-forests constructed by stacking hard CI testing instances (p_0, p_1) across disjoint three-node subgraphs, with pairwise KL divergences uniformly bounded. The framework is model-agnostic: it yields n ≍ log(d)/c² for Bernoulli and Gaussian distributions (α=2) and n ≍ log(d)/c^{(5s+2)/(2s)} for nonparametric continuous distributions with s-Hölder smoothness (α=(5s+2)/(2s)), with the nonparametric case requiring modified hard instances to satisfy tree-faithfulness.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax optimal sample complexity for learning poly-forests is n ≍ log d / c^α, where α is determined by the minimax CI testing radius r_n ≍ n^{-1/α}. For parametric settings (Bernoulli and Gaussian), α = 2, yielding n ≍ log d / c². For nonparametric continuous distributions with s-Hölder smoothness, α = (5s+2)/(2s) > 2, yielding the slower rate n ≍ log d / c^{(5s+2)/(2s)}. Experiments confirm that PC-tree with the appropriate CI test achieves consistent recovery (SHD → 0, PRR → 100%) across d = 20–100 nodes, with the nonparametric setting requiring substantially more samples than parametric ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior optimality results rather than empirical baselines. The Gaussian poly-forest result (n ≍ log d / c²) recovers the established optimality of Wang et al. (2024), which relied on Gaussian-specific properties (partial correlation, linear SEM). The key advance is that the same rate is now obtained via a model-agnostic reduction from CI testing, extending optimality to Bernoulli and nonparametric settings where no prior general DAG optimality results existed. For the nonparametric case, the paper's rate n ≍ log d / c^{(5s+2)/(2s)} is strictly worse than the parametric rate, reflecting the intrinsic hardness of nonparametric CI testing (Neykov et al., 2021). The experiments in Figures 1–2 validate that PC-tree with the theoretically optimal CI test achieves consistent recovery, with parametric models (Bernoulli, Gaussian) reaching high PRR at smaller sample sizes than the nonparametric model, consistent with the predicted α = 2 vs. α > 2 distinction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PC algorithm (Spirtes & Glymour, 1991) and its poly-forest adaptation PC-tree (Wang et al., 2024), which established minimax optimality for Gaussian poly-trees but relied on Gaussian-specific distributional properties. It also draws on the minimax CI testing framework of Neykov et al. (2021) for nonparametric distributions. The key departure is establishing a general, model-agnostic reduction showing that the minimax sample complexity of poly-forest structure learning is determined entirely by the minimax CI testing rate, thereby extending optimality guarantees from the Gaussian setting to Bernoulli and nonparametric continuous distributions without exploiting parametric structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction provides a unified, plug-and-play framework: once the optimal CI test for a given distributional family is known, the optimal structure learning rate follows immediately as n ≍ log(d)/c^α, and the optimal algorithm is simply PC-tree with that test. This resolves a gap where prior DAG optimality results were restricted to Gaussian or linear-SEM settings, and it clarifies that the nonparametric penalty (α > 2) in structure learning is inherited directly from CI testing hardness. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is extending the reduction from poly-forests to general DAGs, where additional structural parameters (e.g., maximum in-degree) are expected to enter the rate.
