## Problem

Prior feedback-driven competing objectives frameworks model system configurations as binary states (satisfied or violated), which fails to capture inherently continuous control parameters such as fairness thresholds, diversity mixing rates, and resource budgets. This paper extends the framework to continuous state spaces while rigorously accounting for the stability cost of parameter adjustment, a dimension absent from the binary setting where switching incurred a fixed atomic cost.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner selects a continuous state vector s_t ∈ [0,1]^k and observes a vector-valued loss ℓ_t ∈ R^k (one component per criterion), with the expected loss for criterion i depending only on the local neighborhood N(i) in a given dependency graph G. The output is the next state vector s_{t+1} and a cumulative regret bound that decomposes into prediction regret (scaling as Õ(√T · Σd_i)) and movement regret (scaling as Õ(λk²d_max log T)). The movement cost is proportional to the ℓ_1 magnitude of the state change, not a binary switch indicator, making the trade-off between exploration and stability continuous.

## Method

LAZYGRAPHLINUCB maintains k local regularized least-squares estimators (one per criterion) whose feature maps respect the dependency graph's sparsity, and constructs a global Lower Confidence Bound score as the sum of per-criterion LCB terms. The key mechanism is a determinant-doubling trigger: the active policy is frozen and re-optimized only when det(V_{t,i}) > 2·det(V_{τ_i,i}) for some criterion i, bounding the number of switches to Õ(d_max·k·log T) and converting linear movement cost to logarithmic. Three structural exploits further tighten the bound: an asynchronous variant that restricts each update to the triggering criterion's local neighborhood (reducing per-switch cost from λk to λΔ where Δ is max degree), an adaptive two-phase procedure that recovers the sparse graph from data under a minimum signal strength condition, and a joint factor-graph estimator that shares parameters across overlapping cliques to reduce effective dimension from Σd_i to d_eff·√k. In the adversarial full-information setting, a Randomized Lazy OGD with virtual iterate and Bernoulli(p) switching quantifies the price of sparsity, recovering the T^{2/3} minimax rate at the optimal budget M = Θ(T^{2/3}).

## Results

Across three numerical illustrations, LAZYGRAPHLINUCB reduces cumulative movement cost by 2.75× (≈22 → ≈8) on a single-clique linear bandit, by 3.1× (410 → 131 total moves) on a 10-clique heterogeneous graph, and by 5.7× (14.0 → 2.5) on a real-world fairness threshold tuning task (Adult Census). In all settings, cumulative prediction regret is statistically indistinguishable from standard LinUCB, confirming that the stability gains come without sacrificing learning accuracy. The asynchronous variant (ASYNC-LAZYGRAPHLINUCB) further reduces movement costs for stable components by up to 3.7× relative to the global lazy strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is standard (non-lazy) LinUCB, which re-optimizes the LCB minimizer at every round. LAZYGRAPHLINUCB matches standard LinUCB on cumulative prediction regret in all three experiments (described as 'statistically indistinguishable'), while reducing cumulative movement cost by 2.75× (single-clique), 3.1× (heterogeneous graph, via the asynchronous variant), and 5.7× (Adult Census fairness threshold tuning). The paper does not compare against other lazy or switching-cost algorithms (e.g., Randomized Lazy OGD, smoothed OGD) empirically; those appear only in the theoretical analysis. The comparison is thus narrow in scope: the paper validates that the determinant-doubling trigger eliminates jitter without degrading learning, and that the asynchronous decomposition removes synchronization overhead in sparse graphs, but it does not benchmark against competing bandit-with-switching-costs methods on shared datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary competing-objectives MDP framework of Awasthi, Cortes, Mansour, and Mohri (2024; 2026) from discrete fix/unfix states to continuous parameter tuning over [0,1]^k, replacing atomic fixing actions with smooth movement costs proportional to the magnitude of change. The stochastic algorithm (LAZYGRAPHLINUCB) adapts the OFUL/LinUCB principle (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011; Li et al., 2010) by maintaining k local estimators coordinated through a determinant-doubling trigger, while the adversarial extension repurposes OGD with switching costs (Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2013) via a randomized lazy schedule. The key conceptual departure from prior bandits-with-switching-costs work is that the dependency graph defines the feature structure of the loss (enabling decomposed estimation and localized updates) rather than merely determining which rewards are observed, and the lazy mechanism is data-dependent rather than fixed-probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical framework—matching minimax upper and lower bounds—for continuous multi-objective optimization under stability constraints, with three graph-structure exploits (asynchronous updates, adaptive graph learning, factor-graph joint estimation) that reduce movement costs by factors of 3–5 in experiments. However, the empirical validation is limited to small synthetic benchmarks and a single-parameter real-world task (k=1 on Adult Census), leaving open whether the graph mechanisms deliver comparable gains in large-scale, high-dimensional production systems. Key limitations include the non-convexity of the LCB optimization, the stationarity assumption on the dependency graph, and the PL condition requirement for the asynchronous variant, all of which constrain practical applicability. Future work on non-linear function approximators, time-varying dependencies, and large-scale multi-criterion evaluation would be needed to establish broader impact.
