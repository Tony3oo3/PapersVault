## Problem

While empirical studies have observed Fourier features in networks trained on modular addition, there is no analytic account of how neural networks learn to compose elements of finite groups over sequences—what features they acquire, in what order, and how architectural choices affect efficiency. This paper introduces the sequential group composition task as a tractable testbed to answer these questions from first principles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives a sequence of k group elements from a finite group G, each encoded as a real vector in R^|G| via orbit-based encoding (the orbit of a fixed vector x under the regular representation; reduces to one-hot when x = e₁). The output is the encoding of the ordered cumulative product g₁…gₖ in R^|G|, trained with MSE loss over all |G|^k sequences. The task is provably nonlinear (no linear map suffices) and order-sensitive for non-Abelian groups, making it fundamentally different from bag-of-words or permutation-invariant tasks.

## Method

For two-layer MLPs with polynomial activation under vanishing initialization, the paper applies the Alternating Gradient Flow (AGF) framework to prove that irreducible representations of G are learned one at a time in a greedy order determined by the operator norm of the encoding's Fourier coefficients raised to power k+1, normalized by irrep dimension. The sigma-pi-sigma decomposition shows that 2^k neurons are needed per irrep to extract the square-free monomial interaction term, yielding an exponential width lower bound of (k+1)2^k Σ n_ρ^{k+1}. In contrast, the paper constructs explicit solutions for deeper architectures that exploit associativity: an Elman RNN composes elements sequentially in k steps with O(|G|^{3/2}) width, and a balanced-tree multilayer MLP composes adjacent pairs in parallel in log k layers with the same width, both using the binary-composition MLP as a reusable building block.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that two-layer networks with polynomial activation require hidden width H ≥ (k+1)2^k Σ_{ρ∈J(G)} n_ρ^{k+1} to perfectly learn sequential group composition, which for the cyclic group C_p reduces to H ≥ (k+1)2^{k-1}⌊p/2⌋ — an exponential in sequence length k. In contrast, recurrent networks solve the task with H = O(|G|^{3/2}) = O(Σ_ρ n_ρ^3) hidden neurons (constant in k) by composing elements sequentially in k steps, while depth-L multilayer MLPs achieve the same width in log k layers via parallel divide-and-conquer. The learning order of irreducible representations is provably determined by the greedy criterion argmax_ρ ||x̂[ρ]||_op^{k+1} / (C_ρ n_ρ)^{(k-1)/2}, establishing a systematic bias toward lower-dimensional irreps that is amplified with sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard empirical baseline comparison; instead, it compares three architectural families on the same task. Two-layer MLPs (the 'baseline' architecture) require exponential width in k, confirmed empirically in Figure 4 across 2205 training runs spanning 21 group sizes, 21 widths, and 5 sequence lengths, where the theoretical lower bound H ≥ (k+1)2^{k-1}⌊|G|/2⌋ matches the observed phase transition. RNNs (Elman networks) reduce the width to O(|G|^{3/2}) independent of k, and Figure 5 provides partial empirical evidence that trained RNNs recover the predicted irrep-block-diagonal structure in W_mix. Deep MLPs match the RNN width but in log k layers rather than k sequential steps. The paper does not claim gradient descent provably finds the efficient deep solutions (Section 5 is constructive, not dynamical), so the architectural comparison is one of existence and expressivity rather than learned performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Alternating Gradient Flow (AGF) framework of Kunin et al. (2025) from the binary modular addition task to sequential group composition over arbitrary finite groups and variable sequence length k. It departs from prior mechanistic interpretability work (Power et al., 2022; Nanda et al., 2023; Chughtai et al., 2023) that identified Fourier circuits empirically, instead deriving the irrep-by-irrep learning order from first principles via the group Fourier transform. The computational expressivity results connect to the transformer-vs-RNN-vs-MLP literature (Liu et al., 2022; Sanford et al., 2023; 2024b), but provide a precise, group-theoretic characterization of the width/depth tradeoff rather than asymptotic separations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rare fully-analytic account of feature learning dynamics for a sequence task, yielding concrete, testable predictions (the irrep learning order formula, the exponential width lower bound) that go beyond the empirical circuit-identification paradigm. Key limitations are that the deep-network results (RNN, MLP) are constructive rather than dynamical—gradient descent convergence to the efficient associativity-exploiting solutions is not proven—and the entire analysis is confined to finite groups, polynomial activations, and vanishing initialization. Open directions include extending to semiautomata (irreversible operations), formal grammars (hierarchical structure beyond associativity), and developing an AGF-style dynamical theory for recurrent and deep architectures.
