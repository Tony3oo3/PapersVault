## Problem

Linearized fine-tuning produces task vectors with strong weight disentanglement suitable for task arithmetic, but suffers from limited expressivity and 2× inference overhead due to Jacobian-vector products. Standard non-linear fine-tuning is expressive and efficient but yields poorly disentangled task vectors that interfere when composed. The paper asks whether linearized behavior can be transferred to a conventional non-linear model, preserving composability without inference-time cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained backbone (CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-L/14 for vision, T5-base for NLI, LLaMA-3.2-1B for generative alignment) and fine-tunes it on task-specific data (RGB images at standard resolutions, text premise-hypothesis pairs, or preference pairs). The output is a standard non-linear model whose task vector (θ_t − θ₀) can be composed via addition or subtraction with other task vectors; at inference, the model performs a single standard forward pass with no Jacobian computation. The distillation operates at the feature level, aligning last-hidden-layer activations before the final projection head.

## Method

DELTA trains a linearized teacher (first-order Taylor expansion around pre-trained weights) and a non-linear student jointly in an online fashion. The teacher is regularized with an EKFAC-based curvature penalty computed on a task-agnostic reference dataset (ImageNet-21k for vision, C4 for text), promoting weight disentanglement without requiring access to other tasks' data. The student is trained with task loss, the same EKFAC drift penalty, and a novel Along-Path Knowledge Distillation (APKD) loss that samples α ~ U(0.5, 1) per step and matches the student's activations at θ₀ + ατˢ to the teacher's activations at θ₀ + ατᵀ (with stop-gradient on the teacher). This along-path sampling exposes the student to a continuum of interpolated linearized teachers, transferring quasi-linear behavior under weight perturbations. The key insight is that constraining the student to match linearized activations in function space induces approximately linear responses to parameter perturbations in parameter space, yielding disentangled task vectors within a standard, deployment-friendly non-linear architecture.

## Results

On the 8-Vision benchmark with ViT-B/32 at α=1.0, DELTA achieves 88.3% absolute accuracy (98.3% normalized), outperforming the best linearized baseline τJp (85.0%) and TAK (86.0%) by 2.3 and 2.3 points respectively, and the best non-linear baseline TaLoS (53.3%) by 35 points. On 6-NLI with T5-Base, DELTA reaches 82.3% absolute accuracy, surpassing τJp (82.5% at best α) and TAK (79.1%). In the LoRA setting (Table 4), DELTA achieves 87.5% on 8-Vision ViT-B/32, exceeding the best post-hoc merging method Core+TSV-M (77.9%) by nearly 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELTA outperforms all nine baselines at α=1.0 across every benchmark. Against the strongest linearized methods, DELTA beats τJp by 3.3 points on 8-Vision ViT-B/32 (88.3 vs. 85.0) and by 3.2 points on 6-NLI (82.3 vs. 79.1 for TAK), while matching τJp's best-α performance on 6-NLI (82.3 vs. 82.5). Against non-linear baselines, the margin is dramatic: DELTA exceeds standard Non-Linear FT by 56.3 points on 8-Vision ViT-B/32 (88.3 vs. 32.0) and by 40.3 points on 6-NLI (82.3 vs. 42.0). In the LoRA-based merging setting (Table 4), DELTA (87.5% on 8-Vision B/32) surpasses the best post-hoc method Core+TSV-M (77.9%) by 9.6 points, and is notably robust to α rescaling where most baselines degrade sharply. On task negation (Table 3), DELTA reduces target accuracy to 9.6% on 8-Vision B/32, approaching linearized methods (τJp: 3.0%, TAK: 2.8%) while substantially outperforming non-linear baselines (Non-Linear FT: 20.4%, TaLoS: 18.4%). The only setting where DELTA is not the absolute best is task negation, where the strict linear regime of τJp and TAK retains a slight edge in disentanglement for subtraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

DELTA sits at the intersection of three research threads: task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022a), tangent-space/linear fine-tuning for improved weight disentanglement (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023), and curvature-aware regularization via GGN/KFAC approximations (Yoshida et al., 2025; Porrello et al., 2025b). The key departure is that rather than requiring the deployed model to actually be linearized (incurring 2× inference cost and limited expressivity), DELTA uses knowledge distillation to transfer the *behavioral* properties of a linearized teacher—linearity to weight perturbations and support localization—into a standard non-linear student. A second departure is the use of a task-agnostic reference dataset (ImageNet-21k or C4) for EKFAC curvature estimation, removing the need to know all tasks in advance or access their data, which prior methods (τJp, TAK) required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DELTA resolves the central practical tension in task arithmetic: linearized models compose well but are expensive to deploy and less expressive, while non-linear models are efficient but produce poorly disentangled task vectors. By achieving SOTA task-addition accuracy with zero inference overhead and a student that outperforms its own teacher, the method makes task arithmetic viable for production deployment. The extension to generative LLMs (Distilled DPO for multi-objective alignment) opens a path toward controllable, composable preference tuning without re-optimization. The main open limitation is the 3× training-time and 2× memory overhead, which the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve; whether the approach scales to very large LLMs (beyond 1B parameters) remains to be tested.
