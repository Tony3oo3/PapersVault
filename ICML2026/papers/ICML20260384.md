## Problem

It is well-established that diffusion models sample faithfully from a target distribution when the score function is estimated with small L² error, but whether the same robustness holds for Langevin dynamics remained an open question. This paper answers that question in the negative, showing that even exponentially small L^p score estimation error can cause Langevin dynamics to produce samples arbitrarily far from the target in Total Variation distance, for any polynomial time horizon in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Lipschitz score function estimate ŝ (constructed adversarially or learned via score matching from n = poly(d) i.i.d. samples) and an initial distribution — either a standard Gaussian N(0, I_d) or the empirical distribution of the training samples — and produces samples by running the continuous-time Langevin SDE dX_t = ŝ(X_t)dt + √2 dB_t. The key unusual aspect is that ŝ can have exponentially small L^p error under π_tar (i.e., E_{π_tar}[||ŝ − ∇log π_tar||^p]^{1/p} ≤ e^{−Ω(d)}) while the output distribution remains at TV distance 1 − e^{−Ω(d)} from π_tar for all sub-exponential time horizons.

## Method

The core construction modifies the true score in regions where the target distribution has exponentially small mass, creating 'traps' that are invisible to L^p error metrics but capture the Langevin trajectory. For a Gaussian target N(μ, I_d) with ||μ|| = 7√d, the score estimate is altered inside a ball of radius 4√d around the origin to behave like a strong OU process pulling toward 0 rather than toward μ, so that initialization from N(0, I_d) (which concentrates near the origin) gets trapped for exponential time. For data-based initialization, the score estimate creates small attractive wells around each training sample x_i, so Langevin dynamics started at those samples cannot escape within any polynomial time. The proofs rely on Gaussian concentration of measure to show the modified regions have negligible π_tar-mass (hence tiny L^p error), combined with Borell-TIS and Dudley chaining bounds to control the exit time of the trapped OU process. A third result extends the negative to general target distributions satisfying mild regularity assumptions by redirecting flow in a small cone region, showing the TV distance approaches 1 as t → ∞.

## Results

The paper establishes that Langevin dynamics is not robust to L^p score estimation error: for an isotropic Gaussian target in R^d, even an exponentially small L^p error (≤ e^{-Ω(d)}) in the score estimate yields TV distance ≥ 1 − e^{-Ω(d)} from the target for any poly(d) time horizon (Theorems 3.1, 3.7). In simulations (Table 1, d=50, α=25), when ‖μ‖ = 42.4 or 49.5 (i.e., k=6,7 in ‖μ‖=k√d), the L² score error of ŝ drops to 0 (to all significant digits), yet the KL divergence of Langevin samples with ŝ reaches 2.28×10⁴ and 3.08×10⁴, versus only 6.20 and 8.39 with the true score. For the data-based initialization setting (Theorem 3.7), initializing Langevin from the same training samples used to learn ŝ produces significantly worse KL/Wasserstein distances than initializing from fresh samples, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing sampling algorithms in a head-to-head empirical sense; instead, it provides a theoretical contrast between Langevin dynamics and diffusion models. Diffusion models are known to be robust to L² score estimation error (TV scales linearly with the weighted L² error of all annealed scores), while this paper proves Langevin dynamics is not robust to L^p error on the target score alone. In the simulation comparisons (Section 4), three initialization strategies are compared for Langevin dynamics with an overfit score estimate: (1) vanilla N(0,I_d) initialization, (2) fresh i.i.d. samples from π_tar, and (3) samples drawn from the training set used to learn ŝ. For the Gaussian target (d=50), Algorithm 3 (training samples) produces substantially worse KL than Algorithm 2 (fresh samples), while Algorithm 1 performs similarly to Algorithm 2. For the GMM target (d=25), Algorithm 3 still underperforms Algorithm 2, and Algorithm 1 is worst due to the poor spectral gap. These results validate the theoretical prescription that fresh samples (not the training data) should be used for data-based initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Langevin dynamics theory (Welling & Teh 2011; Chewi 2025), diffusion model convergence guarantees (Chen et al. 2023a; Benton et al. 2024), and the robustness of Markov chains to perturbations (Schweitzer 1968; Gaitonde & Mossel 2025). It departs from prior robustness analyses of Langevin dynamics that assumed L^∞ bounds on score error (Das et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024) or required exponentially small L² errors (Lee et al. 2022), by showing that the natural L²/L^p error metric arising from score matching is fundamentally insufficient. It also complements the data-based initialization literature (Koehler & Vuong 2024; Koehler et al. 2025) by identifying a critical caveat: initialization must use fresh samples distinct from those used to learn the score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a fundamental theoretical distinction between Langevin dynamics and diffusion models: the latter are provably robust to L² score estimation error while the former are not, even for Gaussian targets with exponentially small error. This justifies the empirical preference for diffusion models from a novel robustness angle and issues a concrete practical warning against initializing Langevin dynamics from the same data used to train the score network. An open question the authors highlight is whether the adversarial constructions (where the score 'memorizes' training points) arise naturally in practice with overparametrized networks, and whether similar fragility extends to other single-step score-based samplers beyond Langevin dynamics.
