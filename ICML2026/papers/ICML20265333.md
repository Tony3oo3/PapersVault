## Problem

Existing federated graph anomaly detection methods rely on neighborhood aggregation, which breaks down when client subgraphs have incomplete local neighborhoods due to graph partitioning. Additionally, they overlook a detection bottleneck caused by weak attribute or structural anomalies, whose subtle signals produce overlapping anomaly score distributions with indistinct density peaks between normal and abnormal nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265333/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a graph partitioned into M disjoint client subgraphs via the Louvain algorithm, where each client holds node attribute vectors, a local adjacency matrix, and a small set of labeled nodes (20% train / 40% validation / 40% test split). Each client produces a per-node anomaly score in [0,1] by computing Mahalanobis distances in three separate representation spaces (attribute, structure, and dynamic) and averaging them, requiring no cross-client data sharing beyond model parameters.

## Method

MV-FGAD comprises three stages. First, a federated knowledge learning module performs FedAvg with GCN over T rounds, then converts the final global model into a probability propagation matrix that blends the original local adjacency with a federated-knowledge-guided structure (αA + (1−α)P̂P̂ᵀ) followed by Laplacian normalization. Second, a multi-view learning module captures three anomaly types: an attribute-aware view (MLP on raw features for strong attribute anomalies), a structure-aware view (k-step knowledge-guided smoothing with concatenated propagation features for strong structural anomalies), and a weak anomaly-aware dynamic view (iterative MLP that models pairwise relationship matrices, decomposes them into consistency and deviation signals via sigmoid gating, and accumulates weak relational discrepancies across layers). Third, Mahalanobis distance in each view's representation space yields per-view anomaly scores that are averaged into a final node score. Training uses a margin-based loss that minimizes the mean deviation of labeled normal nodes while enforcing a minimum gap between the mean deviations of abnormal and normal nodes.

## Results

MV-FGAD achieves the best overall ranking (1.0 AUROC, 1.2 AUPRC) across five main datasets (Reddit, Tolokers, Amazon, Amazon-all, YelpChi). On the most challenging Amazon-all dataset, MV-FGAD reaches 90.92% AUROC and 40.53% AUPRC, exceeding the strongest federated GAD baseline LG-FGAD by 18.3% in AUROC and 96.7% in AUPRC. On Amazon, it attains 78.33% AUROC, outperforming the best FGL classification baseline FedAux (62.67%) by over 15 points. MV-FGAD also demonstrates superior efficiency, with runtime accounting for only 0.33% of FedCLGN on the large-scale Questions dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-FGAD outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of dataset-metric combinations, achieving rank 1.0 (AUROC) and 1.2 (AUPRC) overall. Against the strongest federated GAD baseline LG-FGAD, MV-FGAD gains 18.3% AUROC (90.92 vs. 76.83) and 96.7% AUPRC (40.53 vs. 20.61) on Amazon-all, while LG-FGAD exhibits large variance (e.g., AUPRC std of 19.82 on YelpChi). Against the best FGL classification baseline FedAux, MV-FGAD leads by 15.7% AUROC on Amazon (78.33 vs. 62.67) and 26.6% AUROC on Amazon-all (90.92 vs. 64.37). FGAD achieves the second-best AUPRC rank (2.8) but is surpassed by MV-FGAD on every dataset for both metrics. FedCLGN, while competitive in communication efficiency, suffers from OOM on the large-scale Elliptic dataset and trails MV-FGAD by 34.8% AUROC on Amazon-all. Standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx) and GCFL+ perform near random (≈50% AUROC), confirming that generic federated classification is insufficient for graph anomaly detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing federated GAD methods (LG-FGAD, FGAD, FedCLGN) that rely on neighborhood aggregation and contrastive/reconstruction-based scoring, arguing that incomplete local subgraphs in federated settings make such aggregation insufficient. It repurposes multi-view learning—previously applied to clustering and representation learning—into the federated GAD context, decomposing anomaly detection into attribute-aware, structure-aware, and weak-anomaly-aware views. The federated knowledge transfer module (using FedAvg-aggregated GCN weights to reshape local topology) is a novel bridge between standard federated training and downstream anomaly scoring, and the MHD-based scoring replaces the contrastive or reconstruction losses used by prior federated GAD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode—weak anomalies producing overlapping score distributions—that prior federated GAD methods do not explicitly address, and shows that a multi-view decomposition with MHD scoring resolves it with substantial efficiency gains (0.33% of FedCLGN's runtime). However, the method remains semi-supervised, requiring labeled anomalous nodes per client, which limits applicability in real-world settings where labels are scarce; the authors acknowledge this and point to fully unsupervised federated GAD as future work. The limited set of federated GAD baselines (only three) and the reliance on a single GNN backbone (GCN) leave open whether the multi-view + MHD recipe generalizes to more complex architectures or larger-scale federated deployments.
