## Problem

State-of-the-art one-shot pruning methods (CAP, ALPS, SparseGPT) optimize a quadratic objective using Hessian or Fisher matrix estimates that are subject to multiple sources of uncertainty: finite calibration-set noise, the Fisher approximation itself, and staleness of estimates during sequential layer-wise pruning. Prior work added small fixed regularization terms solely for numerical stability when inverting H, but did not principledly account for these estimation errors. The paper asks whether one-shot pruning can be made robust to uncertainty in the Hessian estimate, and whether such robustness translates into measurable accuracy gains.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained model weights (DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base ViTs or Llama-2-13B / Llama-3.1-8B), a small calibration set (4096 ImageNet-1K images for ViTs; 128 sequences of 2048 tokens from C4 for LLMs), and a target sparsity level (50–80% unstructured or 2:4 semi-structured). It produces pruned sparse weights with updated non-pruned values, computed in a single pass without retraining. The uncertainty size parameter γ is selected via grid search on a held-out validation set (4096 ImageNet samples or 1024 C4 sequences), and the only additional computation beyond the baseline pruning algorithm is forming a PSD regularization matrix added to the block-diagonal Hessian.

## Method

RobOP replaces the nominal quadratic pruning objective min_d ½dᵀHd with a robust counterpart min_d max_{Δ∈U} ½dᵀ(H+Δ)d, where U is a theory-driven uncertainty set on the Hessian perturbation. The key theoretical contribution is showing that for several natural uncertainty sets—Frobenius-norm bounds on Δ, per-sample gradient-norm bounds, and eigenvalue-level bounds motivated by new concentration inequalities for sample second-moment matrices—the inner maximization admits a closed-form solution, reducing the robust problem to the original non-robust problem with a simple PSD term added to H (e.g., γI, (1/N)‖ε‖²₂I, or γ√(Tr(H)/N)·UD(√η)Uᵀ). This means existing pruning algorithms (CAP's greedy OBS with rank-1 Hessian updates, ALPS's ADMM solver) require only a modification to how H is computed, with negligible overhead. The paper also derives a new concentration bound for the sample second-moment matrix (Theorem 3.4) that extends prior covariance-matrix results to the non-centered setting relevant to layer-wise reconstruction, and shows that the small 'dampening' terms used in prior pruning work for numerical stability are in fact special cases of the robust regularizer.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, RobOP-CAP improves DeiT-Base test accuracy by up to 10 points at 80% sparsity (45.19% → 55.19%) and by 6.3 points at 70% sparsity (53.77% → 60.04% for DeiT-Small). On LLMs, RobOP-ALPS reduces WikiText2 perplexity by up to ~10 points for Llama-3.1-8B at 80% sparsity (113.18 → 103.44) and improves mean zero-shot accuracy on OpenBookQA/WinoGrande/PIQA by up to 0.7 points. In the low-calibration regime (N=32 sequences for LLMs, N=512 for ViTs), gains grow substantially, reaching 33 WikiText2 perplexity points and 12 ImageNet-1K accuracy points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobOP-CAP consistently outperforms the CAP baseline (Kuznedelev et al., 2023) across all DeiT model sizes and sparsity levels, with the Frobenius-norm uncertainty set (‖Δ‖_F ≤ γ) yielding the largest gains: +10.0 points on DeiT-Base at 80% sparsity, +6.3 points on DeiT-Small at 70%, and +2.5 points on DeiT-Base at 60%. The gradient-proportional uncertainty set also improves over baseline but by smaller margins, and the authors attribute the superior performance of the fixed-radius set to its ability to capture multiple simultaneous sources of Hessian uncertainty (Fisher approximation error, no Hessian recomputation, calibration noise). For LLMs, RobOP-ALPS outperforms the ALPS baseline (Meng et al., 2024a) on WikiText2 perplexity at all sparsity levels for both Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-2-13b-hf, with the largest gain being ~10 points at 80% sparsity for Llama-3.1-8B (113.18 → 103.44). Mean accuracy on the three reasoning tasks improves by up to 0.7 points (e.g., 48.86 → 49.39 for Llama-3.1-8B at 70% sparsity), while C4 and PTB perplexity show mixed but generally favorable results. Gains are most pronounced at higher sparsity and lower calibration set sizes, confirming that RobOP's robustness benefit scales with estimation uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot pruning literature (CAP, ALPS, SparseGPT, WoodFisher), which all optimize a convex quadratic objective with a Hessian or Fisher matrix estimated from finite calibration data, by recasting the Hessian estimation error as a robust optimization problem. Prior methods add small regularization terms (γI) to the Hessian purely for numerical stability when inverting it; RobOP provides a principled robust-optimization derivation showing these terms correspond to worst-case bounds on Hessian estimation error, and generalizes them to structured uncertainty sets (per-sample gradient bounds, eigenvalue-level concentration bounds). The theoretical tools draw on concentration inequalities for sample second-moment matrices (Bunea & Xiao, 2015; Loukas, 2017), extended here to the non-centered case relevant to layer-wise reconstruction losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RobOP offers a drop-in modification to existing pruning pipelines with negligible computational overhead, and the gains are most pronounced in low-calibration-data regimes (up to 33 WikiText2 perplexity points and 12 ImageNet accuracy points when N is reduced), which is practically important for post-training compression on downstream tasks with limited data. The finding that the 'dampening' terms long used for numerical stability actually govern generalization quality—and that tuning them via a validation set yields consistent improvements—reframes a routine implementation detail as a meaningful hyperparameter. Open questions include extending the framework to structured pruning, developing automatic (non-grid-search) selection of the uncertainty radius γ, and deriving concentration bounds that relax the i.i.d. assumption for token-level dependencies in LLMs.
