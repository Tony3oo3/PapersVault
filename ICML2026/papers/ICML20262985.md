## Problem

While vision models and VLMs are known to encode rich semantic information, it remains unclear whether they implicitly learn geospatial representations (latitude/longitude) during standard pretraining without explicit geospatial supervision. This paper asks to what extent different model families—vision-only architectures, CLIP-style models, and large multimodal foundation models—internalize global location knowledge, and how architectural choices and training objectives shape that knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262985/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The probing setup takes a single 224×224 RGB image (and optionally a text prompt for VLMs) and extracts the [CLS] token (vision models) or last-token residual (VLMs) at every layer. Ridge regression is then fit on these layer-wise hidden vectors to predict latitude and longitude in degrees, with performance reported as R². For the steering experiment, the method additionally takes a source and target image, swaps the top-p geospatially informative dimensions in the layer-1 residual, and produces generated text from the modified forward pass. For the downstream task, fine-tuned models output a country label from a 51-class classification head.

## Method

The paper performs layer-wise linear probing (ridge regression with LOO cross-validation for λ) on the hidden representations of 18 pretrained models spanning vision-only (ViT, ViT-MAE, DINOv2, Web-SSL DINO) and vision-language (CLIP, MetaCLIP, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma 3) families, evaluated on a 4M-image YFCC100M subset partitioned into 40 semantic clusters via k-means on ResNet-152 embeddings, and a 580k-image Google Landmarks subset, both geocell-balanced to reduce spatial bias. Key findings include: VLMs consistently outperform vision-only models in geospatial R² (up to 0.8 for landmarks), with the best representations residing in early language-model layers rather than the final layer; adding a geospatial text prompt propagates location information into later layers, raising Qwen's R² to 0.88; approximately 40% of hidden dimensions carry nearly all geospatial signal; and swapping those top dimensions between two images in the residual stream can redirect the model's generated location while preserving the source landmark name. A controlled comparison on MetaCLIP data (same images, different objectives) confirms that contrastive text-image training, not scale alone, drives the geospatial advantage.

## Results

Using linear probing (ridge regression) to predict latitude/longitude from layer-wise hidden states, VLMs achieve R² up to 0.8 on the Landmarks dataset, with average R² above 0.4 for the largest models (LLaVA-1.5-7B: 0.51, MetaCLIP-huge: 0.49, CLIP-large: 0.48). Vision-only models remain below 0.3 average R², with DINOv2-giant (1B) reaching 0.26 and Web-SSL DINO-7B reaching 0.33. Notably, the much smaller CLIP-base (0.28 avg R²) outperforms DINOv2-giant, and MetaCLIP-huge (600M) outperforms Web-SSL DINO-7B (7B) despite being trained on the same dataset, demonstrating that textual supervision is more effective than scale alone for learning geospatial representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 8 vision-only models (ViT, ViT-MAE, DINOv2, Web-SSL DINO) against 10 VLMs (CLIP, MetaCLIP, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma 3) using the same linear probing protocol. VLMs consistently outperform vision-only models: the best vision-only model (Web-SSL DINO-7B, 7B params) achieves 0.33 average R², while even the smallest VLM (CLIP-base, 151M) reaches 0.28 and the largest VLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, MetaCLIP-huge) exceed 0.48–0.51. Within vision-only models, DINOv2 outperforms ViT and ViT-MAE at every scale, and performance improves monotonically with model size (ViT-MAE-base 0.03 → DINOv2-giant 0.26). The critical comparison is same-data: MetaCLIP-huge (600M, contrastive image-text) achieves 0.73 R² on Landmarks versus Web-SSL DINO-7B (7B, self-supervised) at 0.56, despite both being trained on the MetaCLIP dataset—demonstrating that textual supervision, not scale, drives geospatial representation quality. Among VLMs, LLaVA-1.5-7B (0.51) edges out MetaCLIP-huge (0.49) and CLIP-large (0.48), while Gemma-3-12B-PT (0.44) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.40) trail, suggesting that instruction-tuning pipeline details matter beyond raw parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM interpretability line of work by Gurnee & Tegmark (2024), who showed that specific neurons in language models encode latitude and longitude, by applying the same linear-probing methodology to vision-only and vision-language foundation models. It departs from prior geolocation work (PlaNet, GeoCLIP, PIGEON) that explicitly trains models for geographic prediction, instead asking what geospatial structure is *implicitly* acquired during standard pretraining pipelines without any geospatial supervision. The controlled same-data comparison (MetaCLIP vs. Web-SSL DINO/MAE) isolates the effect of textual supervision from data and architecture, building on the Platonic Representation Hypothesis framework to argue that language is a more efficient supervision signal than scale alone for spatial encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 600M CLIP model outperforms a 7B vision-only model trained on identical data provides concrete evidence that multimodal pretraining is a more sample-efficient path to geospatial understanding, with direct implications for practitioners building location-aware systems with limited labeled data. The discovery that geospatial information is concentrated in ~40% of hidden dimensions (and 90% recoverable from the top 10% in Qwen) opens a pathway for targeted model editing and raises serious privacy concerns: if a small subspace encodes precise location, it could be extracted or manipulated. Open questions include how these representations generalize to satellite/aerial imagery, how to mitigate the pronounced spatial bias toward Europe and North America, and what regulatory safeguards are needed as these capabilities scale.
