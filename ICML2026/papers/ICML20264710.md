## Problem

Despite Muon's strong empirical gains in large-scale LLM training, its training dynamics and scaling behavior remain theoretically uncharacterized. Existing convergence bounds under standard stochastic optimization frameworks fail to capture how Muon's spectral normalization actively shapes the learning trajectory, and no scaling laws specific to Muon have been derived. This paper bridges that gap by providing a rigorous analysis of Muon's optimization dynamics and proving its superior scaling efficiency over GD in a tractable associative memory model.

## Input / Output

The model takes K orthogonal unit-norm query embeddings (subject-relation pairs) in R^K and produces a softmax probability distribution over K answer embeddings, parameterized by a K×K weight matrix W. Knowledge items are partitioned into M groups of C items each, with group frequencies following a hierarchical (optionally power-law) spectrum. The training objective is cross-entropy loss over a label-noise model where the correct label is observed with probability 1−α and a uniform random label with probability α.

## Method

The paper analyzes the momentum-free Muon update (equivalent to Spectral GD) in a linear softmax associative memory with orthogonal embeddings and hierarchical frequency structure. The key mechanism is that the matrix-sign operator msgn(∇L) acts as an implicit preconditioner: it normalizes the gradient spectrum so that all frequency components receive near-isotropic updates, in contrast to GD where the effective step size along the j-th component scales as p_j, causing low-frequency items to converge at rate 1/(p_j·t). In the noiseless case this yields an exponential speedup (O(log(1/ε)) vs O(1/ε) steps to reach loss ε); under label noise with a power-law frequency spectrum p̃_i ∝ i^{−β}, Muon achieves a scaling law of Õ(T^{−2}) while GD is lower-bounded by Ω̃(T^{−(1−1/β)}). The preconditioning interpretation is formalized via a block-symmetric gradient structure: msgn(P − P̂_t) ≈ I_K during the descent phase, where the M block-mean singular directions evolve nontrivially while M(C−1) within-group contrast directions are fixed by symmetry. A comparison with coordinate-wise SignGD shows that matching Muon's performance requires oracle access to the unknown task-representation basis (TRA-SignGD), which is infeasible in practice.

## Results

In the noiseless associative memory setting, Muon achieves exponential convergence (loss ≈ K·e^{−t}) versus GD's polynomial rate (loss ≈ K/t), reducing the time to reach precision ε from O(1/ε) to O(log(1/ε)). Under label noise with a power-law frequency spectrum (p̃_i ∝ i^{−β}), Muon's excess risk scales as Õ((log K)/T)² while GD admits a lower bound of Ω̃(log K / T^{1−1/β}), yielding a substantially steeper scaling exponent. In the noisy regime, Muon requires only O(log K / η) steps to reach excess risk O(M² log K / K), compared to Ω(C log(K/M²) / η) for GD — an Ω(C)-fold speedup where C is the group size. LLaMA-100M pre-training on 10B tokens confirms a steeper data-scaling slope for Muon over SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Muon decisively outperforms GD/SGD across all three experimental settings: in numerical simulations it eliminates the frequency-induced learning imbalance (near-uniform sub-task losses vs. GD's highly disparate convergence), in imbalanced MNIST classification it achieves a smaller group-accuracy gap and lower training loss, and in LLaMA pre-training it exhibits a steeper data-scaling slope. Compared to vanilla SignGD (a simplified Adam variant), Muon (and its oracle-aligned variant TRA-SignGD) successfully captures low-frequency knowledge, whereas SignGD's coordinate-wise sign operation in the original basis fails to exploit the latent block structure, resulting in larger probability gaps and high-loss oscillations. Against AdamW, Muon produces more balanced learning across frequency groups with a smaller group-accuracy gap and lower training loss, while AdamW shows pronounced oscillations and nonsystematic fluctuations in the maximal probability gap. The theoretical analysis further shows that Muon's advantage is not merely due to normalization (as Normalized GD is faster than GD but still more imbalanced and slower than Muon), but stems from its implicit matrix preconditioning that aligns updates with task representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the associative memory framework (used previously to model Transformer knowledge retrieval by Geva et al., Bietti et al., and Wang et al. 2025b) to provide the first theoretical characterization of Muon's training dynamics and scaling laws. It builds on the empirical proposal of Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) and the convergence-bound literature (Li & Hong, 2025; Lau et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025), but departs from those static guarantees by analyzing the full optimization trajectory. The scaling law analysis extends the line of work on SignGD/SGD scaling in linear models (Kunstner & Bach, 2025; Li et al., 2026a) to the matrix-sign operator, while the preconditioning perspective connects to Shampoo and SOAP but isolates the unique role of spectral normalization versus coordinate-wise sign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical derivation of Muon's scaling law (T^{-2} vs. GD's T^{-(1-1/β)}), providing a principled explanation for Muon's empirical data-efficiency advantage observed in large-scale LLM training (DeepSeek, etc.). The implicit preconditioning interpretation—where the matrix-sign operator adaptively aligns with task representations and equalizes progress across frequency groups—offers actionable insight for optimizer design. Key limitations include the orthogonal-embedding assumption, zero initialization, linear model, and omission of momentum; extending the analysis to deep networks, non-orthogonal representations, and covariance-based preconditioners (Shampoo, SOAP) remains open. The gap between the idealized associative memory model and practical Transformer training is acknowledged but only partially bridged by the LLaMA experiments.
