## Problem

While compositional reasoning in Transformers (chaining local relational steps) has been extensively studied, the mechanisms by which Transformers acquire and implement analogical reasoning — identifying shared relational structure across distinct domains to infer cross-category correspondences — remain poorly understood. The paper asks when analogical reasoning emerges during training, what data and optimization conditions govern its emergence, and what internal circuit implements it.

## Input / Output

In the synthetic task, the model receives tokenized fact sequences (e.g., ⟨e_s⟩⟨r⟩⟨e_t⟩ for atomic facts, ⟨e_s⟩⟨f⟩⟨F(e_s)⟩ for analogical facts) over a vocabulary of |E| entity tokens, |R| relation tokens, and one functor token, and predicts the final token via cross-entropy loss. For LLM probing, the input is an in-context prompt listing relational facts across two categories (e.g., '<e1>a<e2>, <e1>b<e3>. <e6>a<e4>, <e6>b<e7>. <e1>~<e6>, <e3>~<e') and the model must predict the target entity. Beyond the predicted token, the analysis extracts Dirichlet Energy over functor-connected entity pairs, attention weights from functor to source entity, and cosine parallelism between (h_t − h_s) and h_f as mechanistic signals.

## Method

The paper formalizes analogical reasoning as functor inference between categories using category theory, then designs a synthetic task with atomic, compositional, and analogical facts to evaluate both reasoning types in a unified framework. Training a 1-layer, 1-head Transformer (d=128, RoPE) reveals a three-stage dynamic: memorization, compositional reasoning, then analogical reasoning, with the latter being highly sensitive to relation count, OOD ratio, weight decay, and non-monotonic in model width. The core mechanistic finding decomposes analogy into two components: (1) geometric alignment of relational structure in the embedding space, quantified by a substantial decrease in Dirichlet Energy, and (2) functor application within the Transformer where attention retrieves source-entity information into the functor position and residual connections implement the mapping as vector addition e_t ≈ e_s + f. The same Dirichlet Energy decrease and target-probability increase are observed in pretrained GEMMA2-2B/9B and LLaMA-3.1-8B via in-context learning, but unfolding along the layer axis rather than the training-step axis, validated with logit-lens decoding and PCA visualizations of hidden states.

## Results

The paper identifies a three-stage learning dynamic in 1-layer Transformers: memorization → compositional reasoning → analogical reasoning, with analogical reasoning emerging substantially later and exhibiting non-monotonic scaling (d_model=64 almost never succeeds; d_model=128/256 are most reliable; d_model=512 degrades). Mechanistically, analogical reasoning is preceded by a substantial decrease in Dirichlet Energy of entity embeddings, and the functor is applied as a vector addition (e_t ≈ e_s + f) via attention and residual connections. In pretrained LLMs (GEMMA2-2B/9B, LLaMA-3.1-8B), the same Dirichlet Energy decrease unfolds along the layer axis rather than the training-step axis, with the energy drop in GEMMA2-2B occurring between approximately layers 7 and 21, coinciding with a rapid rise in the logit-lens probability of the correct target entity (reaching 0.74 under the main prompt design). Moderate weight decay (0.01–0.1) accelerates analogical reasoning emergence, while strong weight decay (1.0) prevents it entirely despite compositional reasoning remaining intact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability and mechanistic analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not propose a new architecture or algorithm to benchmark against existing baselines. The primary 'comparisons' are internal: (1) compositional vs. analogical reasoning under identical training conditions, showing that analogical reasoning is far more sensitive to data characteristics (entity count, relation count, OOD ratio), optimization settings (weight decay, learning rate, batch size), and model scale; (2) different prompt designs for LLM probing, where the main prompt (Prompt 1) achieves 0.74 probability on the correct token, a naive arithmetic prompt (Prompt 2) achieves 0.71 but exploits a spurious '+3' offset, and a tokenization-matched prompt (Prompt 3) drops to 0.27 due to pretrained subtoken priors; (3) model families (GEMMA2 vs. LLaMA) confirming that the layer-wise Dirichlet Energy signature is not architecture-specific. The paper also contrasts its findings with prior work on grokking and in-context learning, noting that the transient loss of analogical reasoning at |R|=1,000 mirrors the transient ICL phenomenon reported by Park et al. (2024) and Singh et al. (2025).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-task framework for studying emergent capabilities in Transformers (Chan et al., 2022; Reddy, 2024; Minegishi et al., 2025) to the specific problem of analogical reasoning, formalized through the category-theoretic notion of functors. It adopts the Dirichlet Energy formulation from Park et al. (2025) as its core geometric measurement tool and connects to the grokking literature (Power et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022) for understanding emergence dynamics. Unlike prior NLP work on analogy that focused on four-term lexical analogies (Mikolov et al., 2013) or natural-language benchmarks (E-KAR, ANALOBENCH), this paper isolates the cross-category mapping operation in a controlled setting and then validates the discovered mechanism in pretrained LLMs via in-context learning and logit-lens analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic decomposition of analogical reasoning in Transformers into geometric embedding alignment (quantified by Dirichlet Energy) and functor application via attention-plus-residual vector addition, moving analogy from an abstract cognitive concept to a concrete, testable circuit hypothesis. The finding that analogical reasoning is qualitatively distinct from compositional reasoning—more sensitive to data structure, optimization hyperparameters, and exhibiting non-monotonic scaling—challenges the assumption that a single scaling axis governs all reasoning capabilities. Key open questions include whether the mechanism extends to partial/noisy analogies without explicit functor cues, whether it explains the sample-efficiency advantage humans gain from analogy, and whether causal interventions (activation patching, representation editing) can confirm the proposed circuit.
