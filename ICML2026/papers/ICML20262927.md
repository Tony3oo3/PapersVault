## Problem

Existing fair multi-view clustering methods face a severe fairness-utility trade-off: correlation-suppression paradigms indiscriminately remove information linked to sensitive attributes, degrading clustering accuracy. Additionally, state-of-the-art approaches rely on spectral decomposition or kernel-based independence measures with O(n²)–O(n³) complexity, rendering them impractical for large-scale datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262927/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes V views of n samples (each view with d_v features) along with a sensitive attribute matrix G ∈ {0,1}^{h×n} (e.g., binary gender, multi-class person identity, or expression labels). It outputs k cluster assignments obtained by applying k-means to the right singular vectors of the learned semantic graph Z_u ∈ R^{m×n}, where m is the number of anchors per view. The sensitive attributes serve as supervision for the sensitive branch but must be statistically independent from the semantic branch, enforced via a linearized HSIC constraint.

## Method

CDAL introduces a dual-anchor mechanism that learns two distinct anchor sets per view—semantic anchors A_u and sensitive anchors A_s—whose structural orthogonality (‖A_u^T A_s‖_F²) physically blocks bias propagation into the semantic representation. A linearized HSIC constraint, Tr(Z_u K̃_G Z_u^T), enforces statistical independence between the semantic graph and sensitive attributes; by exploiting the low-rank factorization K̃_G = Ḡ^T Ḡ, the gradient is computed in O(nmh) rather than O(n²m), yielding overall linear O(n) time complexity. A sensitive alignment loss ‖G − W^T Z_s‖_F² with an orthogonal Procrustes solution for W ensures Z_s faithfully encodes the bias to be stripped away. The full objective is solved via alternating optimization: closed-form SVD for W, Sylvester equations for anchors, independent QP sub-problems for Z_s, and projected gradient descent with Armijo line search for Z_u. The paper proves identifiability of the disentangled factors under non-Gaussianity and orthogonality assumptions, resolving the rotational ambiguity inherent in matrix factorization.

## Results

CDAL achieves the best or near-best clustering accuracy and fairness across all six benchmark datasets. On Jaffe, CDAL reaches 96.24% ACC and 94.07% NMI with 33.33% Balance, substantially outperforming the next-best IMVC (64.79% ACC, 0.00% Bal). On the large-scale Har dataset (10,299 samples), CDAL attains 75.26% ACC and 98.38% MNCE while DFMVC times out entirely. On Zafar (100,000 samples), CDAL achieves 98.88% ACC with 68.64% Balance, maintaining high NMI (91.31%) where SFD collapses to 2.00% NMI to satisfy fairness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDAL dominates the multi-view fair clustering baselines (IMVC, DFMVC, AKAN, Fair-MVC) on nearly all metrics. On Jaffe, CDAL's 96.24% ACC exceeds IMVC (64.79%) by over 31 points while also achieving 33.33% Balance where IMVC scores 0.00%. On Har, CDAL (75.26% ACC, 14.29% Bal) outperforms IMVC (43.36% ACC, 0.00% Bal) and AKAN (36.70% ACC, 0.00% Bal), while DFMVC fails to complete (TLE). Against single-view baselines, CDAL matches or exceeds SFD and KFC on clustering quality while maintaining non-zero fairness scores where KFC and DFMVC frequently report 0.00% Balance. The closest competitor is IMVC on Zafar, which achieves 99.93% ACC vs. CDAL's 98.88%, but CDAL leads on Balance (68.64% vs. 65.34%) and MNCE (99.98% vs. 99.28%). On Scene, SFD edges out CDAL on Balance (85.02% vs. 83.04%) but CDAL leads on ACC (37.65% vs. 29.99%) and all other clustering metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends anchor graph learning (Liu et al., 2010) by replacing the single anchor set with a dual-anchor mechanism that structurally separates semantic and sensitive subspaces, and reframes fair multi-view clustering through a structural causal model lens rather than the correlation-suppression paradigm used by prior FMVC methods (Fair-MVC, DFMVC, AKAN, IMVC). The linearized HSIC constraint is a novel technical contribution that exploits the low-rank structure of anchor graphs to achieve O(n) independence enforcement, departing from the O(n²) kernel-based measures in prior fair clustering work. The identifiability theorem connects the framework to the broader disentangled representation learning literature (Bengio et al., 2013) by showing that structural orthogonality plus statistical independence resolves the rotational ambiguity inherent in matrix factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: existing fair clustering methods are either too slow (quadratic/cubic complexity) or sacrifice clustering accuracy to enforce fairness, limiting real-world deployment in high-stakes domains. The linear-complexity guarantee and the identifiability theorem provide both practical and theoretical value. However, the approach assumes a known two-factor causal structure (semantic + sensitive) and a discrete sensitive attribute, leaving open how the framework generalizes to continuous or multi-level sensitive attributes, misspecified causal graphs, or settings where semantic and sensitive factors are genuinely (not spuriously) correlated. The COIL running time (1743s) also suggests the linear complexity advantage may be less pronounced when feature dimensions are large relative to n.
