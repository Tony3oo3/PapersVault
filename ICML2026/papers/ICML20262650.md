## Problem

In large-step diffusion ODE inference, aggressive step sizes combined with highly expressive denoisers cause the discretized update operator to violate contraction-based stability certificates (L_T < 1), leading to local error amplification and severe structural artifacts. Existing remedies require retraining, reference solutions, or additional solver evaluations, leaving a gap for a principled, training-free, reference-free inference-time correction that directly addresses this certificate breakdown.

## Input / Output

At each solver step, SteinDiff consumes the current noisy state x_k, the pre-trained denoiser x_θ, and the schedule parameters (α_k, σ_k) to compute the solver residual u_k = x_k − T_θ(x_k). The correction coefficient γ̂_k is estimated from batch statistics of u_k (inner products with x_k, squared norms) plus a Hutchinson trace estimate of ∇·u_k, requiring one VJP pass but no additional solver NFEs. The output is the rectified state x_{k−1}^{Stein} = x_k − γ̂_k u_k, which is then passed to the next solver step; the final output is the generated image after N steps.

## Method

SteinDiff reformulates each solver update as a convex combination x_{k−1} = (1−γ_k)x_k + γ_k T_θ(x_k) and derives the MSE-optimal coefficient γ_k* by minimizing the expected squared distance to the latent clean target under the forward Gaussian coupling. The key insight is applying Stein's identity conditionally on x* to convert the intractable E[⟨u_k, x*⟩] term into a computable divergence E[∇·u_k], yielding the closed-form reference-free estimator γ_k* = [(1−1/α_k)E⟨u_k,x_k⟩ + (σ_k²/α_k)E[∇·u_k]] / E‖u_k‖². In practice, expectations are replaced by batch averages and the divergence by Hutchinson's trace estimator, making the correction plug-and-play with any existing ODE solver. The paper further proves step-wise error decay (E_{k−1} = (1−ρ_k)E_k with 0≤ρ_k≤1), a perturbation bound under score-controlled distributional shift, and shows that EDM-style parameterizations (α_k≡1) simplify the coefficient to a purely geometric divergence form, rationalizing their empirical robustness.

## Results

On LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256 (Latent Diffusion), SteinDiff(SC) achieves FID 7.64 at 5 NFE, substantially outperforming DPM-Solver++(2m) (21.29) and DPM-Solver++(3m) (18.61), and maintains the best FID across all tested NFEs (2.77 at 20 NFE vs. 3.25 and 3.17 for the two DPM-Solver++ variants). On ImageNet 64×64, SteinDiff yields FID reductions of up to 45.8% over baseline solvers under both EDM and logSNR schedules. On CIFAR-10 at just 5 NFE (3 solver steps), SteinDiff mitigates severe structural artifacts and improves both FID and IS over DPM-Solver++ and UniPC baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SteinDiff is compared against two families of ODE solvers: DPM-Solver++ (2m and 3m variants) and UniPC. On LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256, SteinDiff(SC) dominates both DPM-Solver++ variants at every NFE tested, with the largest gap at 5 NFE (FID 7.64 vs. 21.29 for 2m and 18.61 for 3m, a reduction of ~64% and ~59% respectively). On ImageNet 64×64, SteinDiff consistently lowers FID for both DPM-Solver++ and UniPC under both EDM and logSNR schedules, with reductions reaching 45.8%. On CIFAR-10 (EDM schedule), SteinDiff improves FID and IS over both DPM-Solver++ and UniPC across the NFE range, with the most dramatic visual improvement at 5 NFE where baselines exhibit severe structural artifacts. The gains are consistent across solvers and noise schedules, indicating that the stabilization is not tied to a specific solver architecture or schedule design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SteinDiff sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: efficient ODE-based diffusion sampling (DPM-Solver++, UniPC, DEIS) and score-based generative modeling via PF-ODEs (Song et al., 2021). Rather than proposing a new solver or a distillation-based acceleration, it introduces an inference-time residual correction derived from Stein's identity under the forward Gaussian coupling, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing solvers. The paper also departs from prior inference-time methods (e.g., DPM-Solver-v3's reference-solution statistics, restart sampling) by requiring no reference clean data or auxiliary optimization, and it provides a novel theoretical lens on EDM-style parameterizations by showing that the drift-related component of the optimal correction vanishes when α≡1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contractivity-trap framing gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic for why aggressive few-step inference degrades: the Lipschitz certificate on the discretized update operator breaks down, and local perturbations are amplified rather than suppressed. SteinDiff offers a training-free, plug-and-play remedy that avoids additional solver NFEs, which is practically valuable for latency-sensitive deployment. However, the method's effectiveness is bounded by the pre-trained model's capacity and by Hutchinson-estimator variance in high dimensions, and the paper does not yet validate the approach on large-scale video or text-to-image models. An open question is whether the step-wise MSE-optimal correction can be made fully deterministic (removing the Monte Carlo divergence estimate) without sacrificing the geometric adaptivity that drives the improvement.
