## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLM-powered GUI agents (e.g., GPT-5.2, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude-Opus4.5) now achieve 80–100% Pass@1 on existing logic-based CAPTCHAs, effectively collapsing the bot-hard assumption that underpins web security. No systematic, scalable defense has been designed specifically to resist these agentic, computer-use-capable solvers. The paper addresses this void by exploiting the persistent 'Cognitive Gap'—the asymmetry between human intuitive interaction and the over-segmented, step-by-step planning strategies of current GUI agents.

## Input / Output

The generation pipeline takes random content and interaction parameters (visual themes, object sets, layouts, number of manipulable elements, constraints) and produces a unique CAPTCHA instance with a rule-encoded ground-truth solution, requiring no human annotation. At evaluation time, the agent observes a multimodal page state per step—viewport screenshot, a filtered DOM-derived interaction view (indexed interactive elements plus puzzle-specific fields), and lightweight metadata (URL, viewport stats)—and outputs structured browser actions (click, type, drag, scroll) executed via Playwright. The platform returns a binary pass/fail verification signal; the benchmark comprises 519 puzzles across 27 families (25 fully procedural, 2 curated) plus a 135-puzzle lite subset for budget-constrained runs.

## Method

The core contribution is a procedural CAPTCHA generation and automatic verification framework organized around an extended POMDP formulation (W = S, O, X, A_web, A_think, Z, T_env, U, R, κ) that makes the observation–memory–decision–action loop explicit. Five empirically grounded cognitive-gap categories (G1 scene-structure inference, G2 temporal integration, G3 numerosity/discrete invariants, G4 latent-state tracking, G5 perception-to-action alignment) drive the design of 27 new CAPTCHA families, each targeting specific interactive bottlenecks such as drag-and-drop execution, motion-contrast perception, or multi-step state maintenance. A lightweight stress-test filter (Gemini-3-Flash Pass@1 < 30% and human success > 90%) selects families that remain agent-defensive yet human-friendly. The key design principle is to require adaptive, holistic intuition rather than decomposable micro-decisions: tasks are structured so that extra deliberation tokens or additional steps do not improve success, because failures stem from mis-grounded action primitives and weak interactive state tracking rather than insufficient reasoning depth.

## Results

On the Next-Gen CAPTCHA benchmark (519 puzzles, 27 families), humans achieve 98.8% Pass@1 with an average completion time of 31.1 seconds, while all evaluated MLLM-based GUI agents are reduced to single-digit Pass@1. The strongest agent, GPT-5.2-xHigh, reaches only 5.9% Pass@1 at a cost of $3,122.3, while the weakest, Qwen3-VL-Plus ThinkingHigh, scores 0.9% at $28.3. Notably, there is no correlation between API cost and success rate, and even the most expensive model (GPT-5.2-xHigh) requires 16–77 minutes per puzzle versus the sub-minute human baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five frontier MLLM backbones (GPT-5.2-xHigh, Gemini-3-Flash-High, Claude-Opus4.5-Extended-Thinking, Gemini-3-Pro-High, Doubao-Seed-1.8, and Qwen3-VL-Plus) as GUI agents against the Next-Gen CAPTCHA defense. GPT-5.2-xHigh achieves the highest agent Pass@1 at 5.9%, but at an exorbitant cost of $3,122.3—roughly 480× the cost of Gemini-3-Flash-High ($6.5) which scores 3.2%. Claude-Opus4.5-Extended-Thinking (3.0%) and Gemini-3-Pro-High (1.3%) cluster in the low single digits, with Gemini-3-Pro-High and Doubao-Seed-1.8 tied at 1.3%. Qwen3-VL-Plus ThinkingHigh is the weakest at 0.9%. Critically, all agents are outperformed by humans by a margin of 93–98 percentage points, and no agent exceeds 20% on any individual CAPTCHA family, confirming that the Cognitive Gap remains intact across all tested backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long CAPTCHA arms-race lineage (distorted text → object grids → logic puzzles) by shifting the adversary model from static VLMs to interactive GUI agents with computer-use capabilities. It builds conceptually on OpenCaptchaWorld and MCA-Bench as evaluation baselines, and on Teoh et al. (2025) and Ding et al. (2025) for the observation that agentic VLMs can solve deployed CAPTCHAs, but departs by proposing a systematic, procedurally generative defense framework organized around an extended POMDP and five targeted cognitive-gap categories (G1–G5) rather than individual puzzle designs. The key conceptual departure is reframing CAPTCHA difficulty from 'harder static reasoning' to 'interactive perception-action bottlenecks' that resist the over-segmented, step-by-step planning style of current agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated collapse of current CAPTCHAs (80–100% Pass@1 for frontier MLLMs) against a 98.8% human baseline is a concrete and timely security concern as computer-use agents enter consumer products. The procedural generation pipeline and open evaluation platform provide a practical, scalable path for defenders, and the economic-asymmetry analysis (e.g., $3,122 for 5.9% success) quantifies the attacker's cost. Open questions include whether the cognitive gap will narrow as agents improve at drag-and-drop and temporal integration, the accessibility implications for users with disabilities, and the Red_Dot vulnerability under hint adaptation (100% Pass@1) which suggests individual families can still be compromised. The small non-CS human study (8 participants, 216 trials) limits confidence in the human-friendliness claim at scale.
