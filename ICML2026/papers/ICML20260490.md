## Problem

Current LLM RL methods (PPO, GRPO, DAPO) treat entire responses as single actions and apply a uniform group-average state value to every token, providing no fine-grained credit assignment. The paper demonstrates via a new benchmark (SVEB) that PPO's dedicated critic network consistently degenerates to this same coarse baseline, offering no more informative guidance than GRPO's group average. The central question is how to estimate state values accurately and efficiently without the prohibitive costs of process reward models or Monte Carlo tree search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260490/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the last-layer hidden states of the terminal state from each generation rollout, compressing them via exponential moving average (EMA) with a smoothing factor α and compression interval φ to handle sequences exceeding 100k tokens. It then computes MinDistance between compressed state representations across all rollouts sharing the same prompt, selects k nearest neighbors, and produces a per-state value estimate as the inverse-distance-weighted average of their terminal rewards. These estimates directly replace the uniform group-average baseline in advantage computation, requiring no additional rollouts or a separately trained critic network.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary state value estimation techniques. Numca reframes mathematical reasoning as a sparse-reward goal-reaching problem (inspired by Hindsight Experience Replay), using numerical values as verifiable milestones to group disjoint rollouts and average their terminal rewards for credit assignment. Hista generalizes this by representing each generation step via its hidden state embeddings, defining a MinDistance metric between state representations, and estimating state values as probability-weighted averages of rewards from k nearest neighbors with weights equal to the inverse of MinDistance. The paper proves (Theorem 5.5) that this probability-weighted estimator has no larger absolute bias than the naive group-average estimator, and establishes (Theorem 5.2) a formal correlation between hidden-state MinDistance and the probability of two states sharing the same final reward. The approach achieves computational efficiency comparable to GRPO with O(⌊T/d⌋²) space complexity and no additional training, while the SVEB benchmark quantifies estimation quality against Monte Carlo reference values across five reasoning domains.

## Results

On the SVEB benchmark, Hista achieves the lowest MAE across all five reasoning domains (e.g., 0.142 on Number, 0.145 on Math, 0.119 on Programming), matching the accuracy of Monte Carlo sampling with 2 rollouts (MCS@2) without any additional rollouts. In RL training on Qwen2.5 base models (Table 5), integrating Hista with DAPO yields consistent average-score gains across all model sizes: +1.8 (0.5B), +0.7 (1.5B), +0.6 (3B), +0.5 (7B), and +1.1 (14B). On the hardest math setting (R1-Distill-1.5B + DAPO-17K), Hista improves the average from 60.3 to 61.4, with the largest single-benchmark gain on AMC (+5.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (group-average baseline), Hista achieves substantially lower MAE on SVEB across all five domains (e.g., 0.142 vs 0.175 on Number, 0.119 vs 0.157 on Programming), and in RL training, DAPO+Hista consistently outperforms plain DAPO on average score across all model sizes from 0.5B to 14B. Against PPO, the paper demonstrates that PPO's critic degenerates to the same group-average baseline (MAE 0.158–0.169 vs GRPO's 0.164), while Hista achieves 0.142 on SVEB-NUMBER without any trained critic network. Against DAPO specifically, Hista+DAPO wins on average across all model sizes, with the largest gains on AMC (+1.5 to +6.2) and AIME (+1.0 to +1.7), though it occasionally loses on MATH (e.g., −2.8 at 7B). Against GSPO, Hista+GSPO shows no net improvement (37.7 vs 37.9 average at 1.5B) due to a structural conflict between Hista's token-level MDP modeling and GSPO's whole-response optimization. Against CSIPO, Hista+CSIPO achieves a modest but consistent edge (e.g., 38.7 vs 37.8 at 1.5B, 45.0 vs 44.4 at 3B), confirming that Hista's token-level formulation is more compatible with per-token policy optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of classical actor-critic RL (PPO with GAE-based critics) and the GRPO lineage established by DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath, which simplified LLM RL by replacing the learned critic with a group-average reward baseline. The key conceptual departure is the demonstration that PPO's critic in the LLM setting degenerates to exactly this group-average, rendering the actor-critic machinery redundant. For solutions, the paper draws from Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) for the milestone-based Numca method and from self-supervised representation learning (MAE, DINO, JEPA) to motivate using LLM hidden states as a general-purpose state representation in Hista, distinguishing itself from prior credit-assignment work (VAPO, VC-PPO, tree-search methods, PRMs) by requiring no additional critic training, rollouts, or domain-specific heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard PPO critics in LLM RL provide no more information than a group average is a significant negative result that reframes how the community should think about actor-critic methods for language models. Hista offers a practical, theoretically grounded path forward with provable superiority over GRPO baselines, and the SVEB benchmark provides the first systematic evaluation framework for state value estimation quality in this setting. However, the theoretical guarantee relies on a simplified positional-alignment assumption and an answer-parsing assumption (A.1) that excludes open-ended tasks, and the empirical gains, while consistent across model sizes (0.5B–14B) and algorithms (DAPO, CSIPO), are modest (typically 0.5–2.3 points). Open questions include whether the hidden-state representation approach extends to agentic multi-turn RL, whether the MinDistance metric can be replaced by learned similarity, and whether the gap to MCS@3 can be closed with richer state representations.
