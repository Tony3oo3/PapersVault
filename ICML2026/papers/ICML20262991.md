## Problem

State-of-the-art video object removal methods rely on noise-to-data diffusion, initializing generation from uninformative Gaussian noise and discarding the rich structural and contextual priors present in the input video. This leads to incomplete object erasure, synthesis of physically implausible content, and the need for auxiliary reference-guided mechanisms that introduce temporal artifacts. The paper asks whether a direct stochastic path from the source video to the target (object-removed) video can better preserve background fidelity while enabling effective removal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (81 frames, multiresolution buckets at 576×1024, 1024×576, or 768×768), a binary object mask, and a text prompt describing the desired scene. The source video is encoded into a continuous latent via a frozen Wan2.1 VAE and plays a dual role: it serves as the prior boundary distribution (starting point of the bridge at t=1) and as a spatial conditioning channel concatenated with the encoded mask. Inference runs 50 SDE solver steps at 1.111 s/frame on an L20X GPU, producing a temporally coherent video with the masked object replaced by contextually consistent background.

## Method

BridgeRemoval reformulates video object removal as a video-to-video translation task using a Variance-Preserving SDE Bridge (DDBM), replacing the standard Gaussian prior with a Dirac prior anchored at the source video latent. The intermediate bridge state is sampled via a ternary interpolation z_t = a_t·z_tgt + b_t·z_src + c_t·ε, where coefficients are rigorously derived from the VP-SDE variance schedule, and the network is trained with a velocity-matching objective equivalent to bridge score matching. To address the trade-off where the strong source prior hinders removal of large objects, the paper introduces Adaptive Mask Modulation (AMM), a FiLM-inspired mechanism that predicts spatially-varying scale and shift parameters from the mask embedding to dynamically relax structural constraints in heavily occluded regions while maintaining fidelity in valid background areas. The architecture is a DiT-based model initialized from Wan2.1-1.3B, conditioned on text embeddings (T5) and the concatenated mask+source latent, trained for 20k steps on 8 H100 GPUs with a linear variance schedule (β_min=0.01, β_max=50.0).

## Results

On BridgeRemoval-Bench (150 videos), BridgeRemoval achieves the best PSNR (33.24 dB), best MSE (46.35), and best CLIP-F (0.9917), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art GenOmnimatte by 1.64 dB in PSNR. It attains the second-best CLIP-T (0.2922, behind GenOmnimatte's 0.2966) and second-best LPIPS (0.0415, behind VACE's 0.0335). On the DAVIS benchmark, BridgeRemoval similarly leads in PSNR (28.22 dB) and CLIP-F (0.9768) while running at 1.111 s/frame, faster than all competing methods except ROSE (0.741 s/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeRemoval is compared against six baselines: Senoria, Ditto, ICVE, ROSE, VACE, and GenOmnimatte. Against the closest competitor GenOmnimatte (CVPR 2025), BridgeRemoval wins on PSNR (+1.64 dB), MSE (46.35 vs 63.30), and CLIP-F (0.9917 vs 0.9878), but trails slightly on CLIP-T (0.2922 vs 0.2966), suggesting GenOmnimatte removes objects marginally more completely at the cost of background fidelity. Against VACE (ICCV 2025), BridgeRemoval dominates on PSNR (+2.19 dB), MSE, CLIP-T, and CLIP-F, while VACE retains a small LPIPS advantage (0.0335 vs 0.0415); the paper notes VACE tends to hallucinate new objects in masked regions rather than inpainting the true background. Against ROSE (NeurIPS 2025), BridgeRemoval leads on every quality metric (PSNR +2.72 dB, CLIP-T +0.070) but is slower (1.111 vs 0.741 s/frame). Against the heavier models ICVE (13B) and Ditto (14B), BridgeRemoval achieves substantially better background preservation (PSNR 33.24 vs 17.41 and 10.53) while running 11× and 6× faster respectively. Overall, BridgeRemoval achieves the best or second-best score on every metric across both DAVIS and BridgeRemoval-Bench, with the only losses being a narrow CLIP-T gap to GenOmnimatte and a narrow LPIPS gap to VACE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant noise-to-data paradigm in diffusion-based video inpainting by reformulating object removal as a data-to-data translation via the Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (DDBM, Zhou et al., ICLR 2023), which replaces the uninformative Gaussian prior with a Dirac prior anchored to the source video. The VP-SDE bridge formulation (Song et al., 2021) provides the theoretical backbone, while the Adaptive Mask Modulation mechanism draws on FiLM-style conditioning (Perez et al., 2018) to address the rigidity of strong source priors. The approach is conceptually closest to FrameBridge (Wang et al., ICML 2025), which extended bridge models to image-to-video synthesis, but here the bridge connects two video distributions rather than an image to a video, and the task is specifically constrained inpainting rather than open-ended generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bridge formulation offers a principled alternative to noise-initialized generation for any video editing task where a strong source prior exists, potentially reducing the number of inference steps needed and improving background fidelity. The introduction of BridgeRemoval-Bench (150 diverse videos with multi-object masks) addresses a real gap in evaluation, as existing benchmarks like DAVIS are small and ROSE-Bench lacks a standardized test split. However, the method still struggles with fast-moving objects—a limitation shared by all current approaches—and the practical impact depends on whether the bridge paradigm generalizes beyond object removal to broader video editing tasks as the authors speculate.
