## Problem

Existing Video LLMs handle temporal grounding or spatial segmentation in isolation, lacking the ability to jointly reason about complex spatial-temporal events. This limitation is compounded by the absence of datasets providing both fine-grained temporal annotations and mask-level spatial tracklets simultaneously, and by the conflicting input granularity requirements of the two task types (dense low-res frames for temporal vs. sparse high-res frames for spatial).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264126/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video of N frames and a text query, encoding frames at two resolutions: 448×448 for the MLLM visual encoder (up to 128 densely sampled frames as fast tokens, 5 keyframes as slow tokens) and 1024×1024 for SAM2's spatial encoder. For temporal queries, the output is a text response containing frame IDs indicating the relevant temporal interval; for spatial queries, the output is a full-video segmentation mask trajectory M ∈ R^{N×H×W} generated by SAM2 conditioned on a [SEG] token embedding from the MLLM.

## Method

VideoLoom integrates InternVL3 (MLLM) with SAM2 (video segmentation) end-to-end via a [SEG] token that bridges the LLM's hidden states to SAM2's mask decoder. The key architectural innovation is SlowFast visual tokens: fast tokens (16 tokens/frame from densely sampled frames at low resolution, interleaved with numerical frame IDs) provide global temporal context, while slow tokens (256 tokens/frame from 5 keyframes at high resolution) supply rich spatial detail; both are concatenated into a unified LLM input sequence. Training uses a combined loss of text cross-entropy and mask BCE+DICE, with LoRA on the LLM and fine-tuning of the SAM2 mask decoder. The approach is supported by LoomData-8.7k, a character-centric dataset from ActivityNet annotated via a four-stage automatic pipeline (shot partition, SAM2-based spatial tracking, shot merging, and Gemini-generated temporal action descriptions), and evaluated on LoomBench, which introduces Combined questions and a Bidirectional Foreground J&F metric to assess joint spatial-temporal comprehension.

## Results

VideoLoom achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across 10 visual benchmarks. On temporal grounding, it reaches 70.0 R1@0.5 and 48.3 R1@0.7 on Charades-STA, 33.6 F1 on YouCook2, and 63.3 HIT@1 on QVHighlights. On spatial tasks, it attains 51.7 J&F on MeVIS, 71.3 J&F on RefYTVOS, and 63.1 J&F on ReVOS, surpassing all prior methods. On the proposed LoomBench, VideoLoom scores 49.1 J&F_bi-fore on Combined questions, outperforming the TimeSuite+Sa2VA pipeline by +15.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On temporal grounding (Table 1), VideoLoom outperforms all unified Video LLMs: TimeSuite-7B (67.1→70.0 R1@0.5 on Charades-STA), TRACE-7B (61.7→70.0), VTG-LLM-7B (57.2→70.0), and TimeChat-7B (46.7→70.0). It also surpasses the task-specific HawkEye-7B (58.3→70.0) but trails UniTime-7B (75.3 R1@0.5), which the authors attribute to UniTime's larger grounding data and recursive localization inference. On spatial benchmarks (Table 2), VideoLoom achieves the best J&F on all three ref-VOS datasets, beating Sa2VA-8B (70.7→71.3 on RefYTVOS, 57.6→63.1 on ReVOS) and VRS-HQ-13B (71.0→71.3, 60.0→63.1). On image segmentation (Table 3), VideoLoom leads all baselines including GLaMM-7B (79.5→83.4 cIoU on RefCOCO) and Sa2VA-8B (81.6→83.4). On LoomBench (Table 4), VideoLoom outperforms the TimeSuite+Sa2VA pipeline by +16.2 tIoU and +15.4 J&F_bi-fore on Combined questions, and also leads on When (37.9 R1 vs. 23.1) and Where (87.2 J&F vs. 86.1) sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoLoom extends the MLLM+segmentation architecture lineage initiated by LISA (which introduced the [SEG] token bridging an LLM to a mask decoder) and Sa2VA (which married SAM2 with LLaVA for video segmentation), but departs from both by unifying temporal grounding and spatial segmentation within a single training objective rather than treating them as separate capabilities. The SlowFast token design draws conceptual inspiration from SlowFast-LLaVA's dual-rate frame encoding, but repurposes it to serve two distinct functions: fast tokens with interleaved frame IDs for temporal reasoning, and slow tokens at higher resolution for spatial precision. The paper positions itself against a clear split in the Video LLM literature—temporal-focused models (TimeChat, TRACE, TimeSuite) versus spatial-focused models (Sa2VA, VISA, ViLLa)—and claims to be the first to handle both fine-grained dimensions jointly in one framework, supported by a purpose-built dataset (LoomData-8.7k) and benchmark (LoomBench) that prior work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VideoLoom suite (dataset + model + benchmark) provides the first comprehensive infrastructure for evaluating joint spatial-temporal understanding, and the LoomBench Combined questions with the J&F_bi-fore metric fill a genuine gap: no prior benchmark required simultaneous temporal localization and spatial segmentation. The finding that standard J&F is substantially inflated for short queried segments (88.9 vs. 47.6 for 0–20% length) is a useful methodological correction for the field. However, the framework remains limited to a single main character per video, lacks a feedback loop when SAM2 produces invalid masks, and struggles with sub-action queries and n-th occurrence reasoning—open problems the authors acknowledge but leave for future work involving CoT reasoning and reinforcement learning.
