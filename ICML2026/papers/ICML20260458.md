## Problem

Self-supervised learning relies on a nonlinear projection head for pretraining yet discards it for downstream inference, a 'train-with, deploy-without' strategy whose geometric mechanics remain unexplained. Prior work frames the head as an information bottleneck or collapse-prevention device, but does not account for how it simultaneously conditions the optimization landscape, destabilizes collapsed equilibria, and destroys information along augmentation directions. The paper asks: what is the precise geometric role of the head in reshaping the loss landscape and mediating the invariance–expressivity tradeoff?

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CIFAR-100, and foundation-model-scale data) and produces two augmented views per sample via stochastic operators (rotation, color-jitter, cropping, flips). The backbone (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-S) maps each view to a d-dimensional representation z; the projection head (2–3 layer MLP) maps z to a k-dimensional embedding h(z) where the pairwise SSL loss (InfoNCE, MSE, or decorrelation) is applied. For downstream evaluation, the head is discarded and a linear or MLP probe is trained directly on z to predict class labels, isolating the backbone's retained semantic information from the head's invariance-induced distortions.

## Method

The paper models the projection head as a trainable Riemannian metric on the backbone representation manifold, where the head's Jacobian induces a pullback metric G(z) = J_h(z)ᵀ ∇²_h L J_h(z) that warps the local geometry seen by the backbone. Three core results follow: (i) linear heads perform implicit subspace whitening (isometric on the active loss subspace), while nonlinear heads adapt the metric state-dependently along curved optimization trajectories, with head depth empirically scaling this capacity; (ii) at collapsed equilibria, smooth nonlinear activations (Swish, GELU) natively inject negative eigenvalues into the effective Hessian via the curvature interaction term, converting stable minima into strict saddle points that gradient descent escapes almost surely—whereas linear and ReLU heads lack this mechanism and rely on discrete-time noise or BatchNorm; (iii) enforcing invariance forces the pullback metric to become singular along the augmentation tangent space, producing a Fisher information rank deficit that geometrically formalizes why discarding the head recovers linearly separable backbone semantics. Empirical validation tracks the Hessian spectrum and condition number during training from pseudo-collapsed initializations and measures orbit compression (21.85× reduction in augmentation spread) on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, confirming the theoretical predictions across contrastive, non-contrastive, and decorrelation objectives.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the projection head achieves a 21.85× compression of augmentation orbit spread (reducing mean squared spread from 0.0225 to 0.0010) while increasing local curvature by 1.93×, confirming the metric singularity predicted by Proposition 5.2. Hessian tracking reveals that smooth heads (Swish) natively inject negative eigenvalues (λ_min < 0) to escape pseudo-collapsed initializations, whereas ReLU and linear heads show no negative curvature under continuous-time gradient flow. The class/orbit separation ratio improves by 1.22× (from 2.04× to 2.50×), demonstrating selective compression of nuisance directions over semantic structure. Linear probing accuracy drops by 14.72% (52.27% → 37.55%) when using the head output, but the MLP-over-linear nonlinearity gap nearly doubles (from +3.19% to +6.01%), indicating the head entangles rather than simply erases information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are architectural ablations rather than competing methods. Smooth nonlinear heads (Swish/GELU) are shown to natively destabilize collapsed equilibria by injecting negative curvature into the effective Hessian, while linear and ReLU heads cannot do so under continuous-time gradient flow (λ_min ≥ 0), relying instead on discrete-time SGD noise and BatchNorm for escape. Linear heads perform only global subspace whitening and force backbone rank to at most k (Theorem 5.1), whereas nonlinear heads preserve full backbone rank while locally collapsing augmentation tangent directions. Across foundation-scale models (ResNet-50, ViT-S) with different objectives (DINO, VICReg, Barlow Twins), the head consistently acts as a geometric buffer: it compresses augmentation orbits under implicit whitening objectives (DINO) and expands dimensionality under explicit whitening objectives (VICReg), in both cases shielding the backbone from metric degeneracy. The 21.85× orbit compression is not a byproduct of dimensionality reduction (the head is actually higher-dimensional: 2048 vs 512), confirming it is a targeted geometric collapse induced by the learned pullback metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Riemannian network analysis (Ollivier, 2015) and information geometry (Amari, 2016) to reframe the projection head as a trainable Riemannian preconditioner, extending the natural gradient descent framework to the SSL setting. It departs from prior information-bottleneck explanations (Tishby & Zaslavsky, 2015; Ouyang et al., 2025) and collapse-prevention analyses (Jing et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2021) by providing the geometric *mechanism*—a singular pullback metric and curvature-induced instability—rather than merely describing what the head does. The guillotine effect (Bordes et al., 2023) and the empirical necessity of nonlinear heads (Chen et al., 2020a) are treated as consequences of the metric degeneracy the paper formalizes, unifying conditioning, collapse avoidance, and information loss under a single differential-geometric lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, actionable recommendation: smooth activations (Swish/GELU) in the projection head natively destabilize collapsed equilibria via intrinsic negative curvature, reducing reliance on BatchNorm and large learning rates that ReLU-based heads require. Open questions include whether the geometric effects persist under the in-context, data-dependent metrics induced by self-attention in ViTs, how to extend the tangent-space analysis to discrete augmentations lacking smooth group structure, and whether one can design 'headless' invertible objectives that enforce invariance without inducing the metric singularity that necessitates discarding the head. The existential nature of the main theorems (a suitable head *exists*) leaves the explicit stochastic gradient dynamics for reaching these optimal configurations unresolved.
