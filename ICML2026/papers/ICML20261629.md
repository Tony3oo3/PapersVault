## Problem

Group-based RL optimization for flow models (e.g., GRPO, DiffusionNFT) suffers from vanishing intra-group discriminative variance: as training progresses, samples within a group converge to a narrow mode, driving the reward standard deviation toward zero and eliminating the relative advantage signal needed for gradient updates. This leads to premature optimization stagnation and reward hacking, where the policy collapses into a single high-reward trajectory. Existing remedies—increasing group size or varying initial noise—incur linear computational costs or introduce visual incoherence without fundamentally resolving the variance collapse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and generates a 512×512 RGB image using Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium as the backbone. During training, the prompt's token embeddings are perturbed with K learned semantic variants to expand the exploration set, but the final policy update is always conditioned on the original unperturbed prompt, ensuring the learned distribution shift is anchored to the user's intended semantics. Inference is CFG-free, with the guidance direction internalized through the negative-aware finetuning objective.

## Method

E²PO sustains intra-group discriminative variance by injecting structured, learnable perturbations into the text embedding space rather than relying on stochastic latent noise. Three components work together: (1) an Embedding-Perturbed Mechanism that applies K learnable perturbation tensors to content tokens (excluding padding/boundary tokens), optimized via a hybrid objective combining pairwise cosine-diversity loss and an anchor-similarity constraint to keep variants semantically valid; (2) a Noise-Aware Sampling Schedule that interpolates between perturbed and original conditions as a function of the diffusion timestep, allowing semantic variants to steer structural layout in the high-noise phase while reverting to the original anchor for fine-grained fidelity; (3) a Reference-Anchored Batching Strategy that retains the original condition alongside K−1 perturbed variants and computes all policy gradients relative to the unperturbed prompt. The policy is updated using the DiffusionNFT forward-process contrastive objective with implicit positive/negative velocity fields, decoupling exploration (perturbed conditions) from optimization (original condition).

## Results

On GenEval (compositional generation), E²PO with K=12 semantic variants and G=4 noise seeds achieves a GenEval score of 0.932, outperforming DiffusionNFT (0.922 at G=24, 0.917 at G=48) and Flow-GRPO (0.776 at G=24). On PickScore (human preference alignment), E²PO (G=8, K=3) attains 23.38 PickScore, 6.538 Aesthetic Score, and 0.325 HPSv2.1, surpassing DiffusionNFT (23.34, 6.514, 0.324) and Flow-GRPO (22.72, 6.273, 0.324). E²PO also leads on all DivGenBench diversity metrics (IDS, ASC, SDI, PVS), confirming that its gains are not achieved at the cost of generative diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²PO outperforms all four baselines on both primary benchmarks. Against SD3.5-M (the untrained backbone), E²PO improves GenEval from 0.263 to 0.932 and PickScore from 19.93 to 23.38, confirming the value of RL alignment. Against Flow-GRPO, E²PO gains +0.156 GenEval (0.932 vs. 0.776 at G=24) and +0.66 PickScore (23.38 vs. 22.72), while also maintaining higher diversity (PVS 0.322 vs. 0.318). Against DiffusionNFT, E²PO achieves a modest but consistent edge: +0.010 GenEval over G=24 (0.932 vs. 0.922) and +0.015 over G=48 (0.932 vs. 0.917), with notably better diversity (PVS 0.322 vs. 0.259/0.196). In the user study, E²PO leads DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO, and DiffusionNFT on all four preference dimensions, achieving 33.1% preference rate on Image-Text Alignment and 31.8% on Overall Quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiffusionNFT's contrastive forward-process objective as its policy optimization backbone, while drawing from the broader GRPO lineage (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) for group-based RL in visual generation. Its key departure is shifting the locus of exploration from the noise prior to the text embedding space: rather than increasing latent group size or varying initial noise to maintain intra-group variance, E²PO injects learned, structured perturbations into CLIP token embeddings. This repurposes the text-condition manipulation literature (prompt optimization, textual inversion) as a dynamic, online mechanism within the RL loop rather than a static preprocessing step, addressing the 'vanishing discriminative signal' problem that plagues group-based methods as training progresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in group-based RL for flow/diffusion models—variance collapse leading to optimization stagnation and reward hacking—and provides a practical, relatively lightweight fix. The finding that a small number of semantic variants (K=4–12) with minimal noise diversity (G=2–4) outperforms brute-force noise scaling (G=48) suggests a more compute-efficient path for RL alignment of generative models. Open questions include whether the per-prompt embedding optimization overhead scales to video generation or very long prompts, whether learned perturbations transfer across prompts or must be re-optimized, and whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based conditioning to other text encoders or multi-modal conditions.
