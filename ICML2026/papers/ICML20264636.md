## Problem

VLM-based object navigation agents suffer from weak temporal-spatial reasoning, leading to redundant exploration and low success rates on unseen objects. While injecting chain-of-thought reasoning at every step improves success, it incurs prohibitive inference latency during routine navigation (e.g., walking a straight hallway). The paper addresses both gaps by asking: can a single VLM learn to reason adaptively—triggering expensive deliberation only at critical stagnation points while executing reactively otherwise?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264636/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language goal instruction, a current egocentric RGB frame (640×480, 120° HFOV), a serialized long-term memory (a graph of up to 10 text-image interleaved landmark nodes connected by action-edge sequences), and the dialogue history of previous image-action pairs. In the slow system, the model additionally receives a 360° panoramic observation (4 RGB images at 90° intervals). Output is a single meta action per turn (MoveAhead 0.25m, RotateLeft/Right 30°, End) or the special transition token 'obs', which triggers a panoramic scan and re-enters the slow system; in slow mode, the model first emits a reasoning block (<think_start>…<think_end>) before the action. KV-caching in the fast system means only the latest frame is re-encoded, avoiding full-context reprocessing.

## Method

HYDRA-NAV unifies a deliberative 'slow system' (global planning over memory + panoramic views) and a reactive 'fast system' (low-level action decoding via KV-cached multi-turn dialogue) within a single Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, with the model self-triggering the slow system by emitting the 'obs' token. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (i) spatial-action alignment on 500K A*-planned trajectories, (ii) memory-reasoning integration using heuristic waypoint exploration trajectories where Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking synthesizes hindsight reasoning text (reviewing history, analyzing current view, planning next segment) with explicit information-leakage filtering, and (iii) iterative rejection fine-tuning (IRFT) where on-policy rollouts identify 'stagnation points' (repetitive exploration within 0.5 m of a position visited ≥20 steps earlier, or increasing goal distance over a random 20–35-step window), failed trajectories are repaired by substituting the erroneous action with 'obs' and splicing in an A*-optimal corrective path with newly synthesized reasoning, and the model is retrained on the repaired set across multiple rounds. This reject-and-repair loop teaches the model to trigger reasoning at only ~3% of steps while maintaining or improving success rate, and the paper introduces SOT (Success weighted by Operation Time) to quantify the resulting efficiency gain over fixed-frequency baselines.

## Results

HYDRA-NAV-IRFT achieves state-of-the-art success rates across all benchmarks: 84.8% SR / 41.1% SPL on HM3D Val, 64.0% SR / 29.6% SPL on MP3D Val, and 66.3% SR / 37.4% SPL on OVON Val-Unseen. The improvements over the previous best methods are +11.1% SR on HM3D (vs. Uni-Navid), +17.4% on MP3D (vs. CogNav), and +21.1% on OVON Val-Unseen (vs. NavFoM). The paper also introduces SOT (Success weighted by Operation Time), where HYDRA-NAV-IRFT achieves 24.0 on HM3D and 20.3 on OVON Val-Unseen with only a 3.0–3.5% reasoning ratio, substantially outperforming dense-reasoning baselines (e.g., WMNav at 17.9/16.9 SOT with 100% reasoning ratio).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYDRA-NAV-IRFT outperforms all 22 compared baselines on every benchmark it is evaluated on. On HM3D Val, it surpasses the previous SOTA Uni-Navid (73.7% SR) by +11.1% and WMNav (72.2%) by +12.6%. On MP3D Val, it beats CogNav (46.6%) by +17.4% and WMNav (45.2%) by +18.8%. On the most challenging OVON Val-Unseen split, it exceeds NavFoM (45.2%) by +21.1%, Nav-R1 (42.2%) by +24.1%, and Uni-Navid (39.5%) by +26.8%. The curriculum is clearly effective: Stage 1 (reactive only) is below prior SOTA, Stage 2 (fixed-frequency reasoning) already matches or exceeds prior SOTA on MP3D and OVON, and Stage 3 (adaptive IRFT) delivers the largest gains. On the SOT efficiency metric, HYDRA-NAV-IRFT (24.0 HM3D, 20.3 OVON) dominates dense-reasoning methods like WMNav (17.9, 16.9) and VLMnav (13.8, 14.0) while using only 3.0–3.5% reasoning ratio versus their 100%.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYDRA-NAV builds on the dual-system (slow-fast) paradigm introduced by Wei et al. (2025) and Xue et al. (2025) for vision-language navigation, but departs critically by unifying both processes within a single VLM rather than relying on separate models with heuristic switching. The adaptive reasoning mechanism draws from the broader LLM literature on when-to-think (Chen & Li, 2024; Chung et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b) and rejection fine-tuning (Touvron et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2025), but is the first to apply iterative rejection fine-tuning to learn adaptive reasoning triggers in an embodied navigation setting without human-annotated supervision. The curriculum training pipeline (spatial-action alignment → reasoning-memory integration → adaptive reasoning) extends the data synthesis approaches of Uni-Navid (Zhang et al., 2024a) and Nav-R1 (Liu et al., 2025a) by adding structured long-term memory and temporal-spatial reasoning traces synthesized with a 235B thinking model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 7B VLM with only 3% reasoning ratio outperforms dense-reasoning baselines (100% reasoning) by 11–21% in success rate while achieving substantially higher SOT challenges the prevailing assumption that more frequent reasoning is necessary for effective embodied navigation, with direct implications for real-time deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The introduction of the SOT metric fills a genuine gap in the evaluation landscape by accounting for inference latency, which prior SPL-based metrics ignore entirely. Key limitations include evaluation exclusively in Habitat (no Isaac Sim or other high-fidelity simulators), reliance on A*-planner for trajectory generation and repair (limiting the degree of autonomy in data synthesis), and the fact that IRFT hyperparameters (T_stag, δ_stag, Δt) were tuned offline rather than learned online. The convergence of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B at Stage 3 suggests the framework, not the base model, drives performance—opening the question of whether even smaller backbones could achieve competitive results with the same pipeline.
