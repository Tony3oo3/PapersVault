## Problem

Naive noise-augmented GNNs achieve theoretical universal expressivity but suffer from poor generalization because random noise breaks the symmetry that GNNs should respect for symmetric nodes. Conversely, theoretically universal-expressive models (high-order GNNs, subgraph GNNs) are computationally expensive and impractical for real-world tasks. The paper asks whether one can retain the expressivity of noise while fixing its generalization failure through a principled symmetry constraint.

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix) augmented with per-node random noise vectors of dimension C, sampled i.i.d. from a normal distribution and resampled in every forward pass. Outputs are invariant to the chosen noise transformation group: graph-level predictions, node representations, link scores, and subgraph representations are all produced from the invariant stream, while the equivariant stream carries the noise through the network. The stochastic predictor is defined as the expectation over the row-exchangeable noise distribution, restoring node-permutation invariance.

## Method

ENGNN uses a two-stream message-passing architecture: an invariant stream initialized from node features and an equivariant stream initialized from random noise, both updated by specially designed equivariant aggregators that mix information while preserving the symmetry of the noise. The key mechanism is enforcing equivariance to a group of nodewise noise transformations (orthogonal group O(C) for ENGNN-O, or channel permutations S(C) for ENGNN-P), which the paper proves reduces the PAC covering number of the noise space and thus tightens the generalization bound. Two concrete aggregators are constructed: an O(C)-equivariant aggregator using an orientation matrix and DeepSet-based invariant pooling, and an S(C)-equivariant aggregator using channel-identifying DeepSets. The architecture maintains O(n+m) time and space complexity per layer, matching standard MPNNs, while achieving universal expressivity for graph-, node-, link-, and subgraph-level tasks under mild regularity conditions on the noise.

## Results

On graph property prediction, ENGNN-O achieves the best ZINC MAE of 0.070±0.006 (tied with GPS) and best ZINC-full MAE of 0.022±0.003 (tied with PPGN), while on OGB-MolHIV it reaches 78.63±0.93 AUC, competitive with I2GNN (78.68) but below Graphormer (80.51). On node classification, ENGNN-O surpasses all baselines on 7/8 datasets, with standout gains on Squirrel (64.66% vs. 51.35% for BernNet) and Chameleon (71.51% vs. 68.29%). In subgraph counting, ENGNN-P achieves MAE ≤ 0.01 on all 9 target substructures, whereas vanilla MPNN fails on all complex ones and NMPNN fails on most. Critically, ENGNN-O requires only 4.81 s/epoch and 0.62 GB GPU memory on ZINC, compared to 20.21 s and 20.37 GB for PPGN (~4× faster, ~33× less memory).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against high-order and subgraph GNNs (NGNN, GNNAK+, I2GNN, PPGN, DRFWL), ENGNN-O matches or beats them on ZINC and ZINC-full while using only ~10% of the GPU memory (0.62 GB vs. 20.37 GB for PPGN); on MolHIV, Graphormer (80.51) and GNNAK+ (79.61) still lead, likely due to their specialized inductive biases for molecular hierarchies. Compared to the naive noise baseline NMPNN, ENGNN consistently outperforms it across all task types: on node classification, NMPNN collapses to ~20% accuracy on Cora (vs. 89.32% for ENGNN-O), and on link prediction NMPNN drops to 7.35 HR@50 on Collab (vs. 65.34 for ENGNN-O), confirming that equivariance is essential for practical noise use. Against standard MPNNs, ENGNN-P achieves perfect 1.000 AUROC on all synthetic expressivity benchmarks (TRI, LCC) where MPNN scores 0.500, and on subgraph counting ENGNN-P meets the ≤0.01 MAE threshold for all 9 substructures while MPNN fails on all complex ones. On node classification, ENGNN-O outperforms spectral GNNs (BernNet, GPRGNN, ChebyNet) and attention-based models (GAT, APPNP) on 7/8 datasets, with the largest margin on Squirrel (+13.31 pp over BernNet). On link prediction, ENGNN-P achieves best or second-best HR@K on 5/6 datasets, matching BUDDY on Cora (88.10 vs. 88.00) and Citeseer (91.56 vs. 92.93) while surpassing it on Pubmed (81.26 vs. 74.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noise-augmented GNN line (Sato et al., 2021; Abboud et al., 2021) by imposing equivariance to noise-space transformations (orthogonal or channel-permutation), drawing on equivariant set network designs from Maron et al. (2020) and Segol & Lipman (2020). The key conceptual departure is repurposing equivariant architectures—previously applied to physical symmetries like 3D rotations in molecular GNNs—toward symmetries of the auxiliary noise input, thereby reducing the effective input space and tightening PAC-learning sample complexity bounds. The base architecture remains a standard MPNN (Gilmer et al., 2017), and the theoretical expressivity framework builds on the graph-isomorphism-discriminating definition from Chen et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENGNN offers a practical path to universal expressivity at MPNN-level computational cost, which is significant given that prior theoretically universal models (PPGN, I2GNN, subgraph GNNs) require 4–50× more time and memory. The dramatic failure of naive noise (NMPNN) on node and link tasks—dropping to near-random accuracy—validates the equivariance principle and suggests that prior noise-based GNNs were fundamentally misapplied in practice. Open questions include whether the aggregator modifications can be made plug-and-play across existing GNN backbones, whether the theoretical depth/width requirements for universal expressivity remain tractable at scale, and whether the approach extends to other stochastic augmentation strategies beyond i.i.d. Gaussian noise.
