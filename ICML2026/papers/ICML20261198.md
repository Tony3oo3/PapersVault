## Problem

Long-horizon vessel trajectory prediction suffers from compounded uncertainty and directional drift, with existing Transformer-based methods collapsing toward nearly horizontal trajectories over extended horizons. The root cause identified is the absence of explicit global navigational intent modeling: current approaches operate solely at the local kinematic level, forcing long-term intent to be implicitly inferred from short-term motion patterns, which becomes unreliable as the prediction horizon grows.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical AIS trajectory sequence (latitude, longitude, SOG, COG at 5-minute intervals) along with a reference trajectory database for NKP retrieval. Lat/lon are normalized to [-1,1] and SOG/COG are converted into latitude/longitude velocity components with a 1/25 scaling factor. The output is an autoregressive sequence of SOG and COG values, from which future coordinates are recovered via a closed-form locally Euclidean update (Eqs. 9–10) that avoids repeated spherical projections. The NKP is produced as a coordinate pair retrieved from the reference database via cosine-similarity matching.

## Method

SKETCH factorizes the trajectory prediction problem as p(Y|X) = Σ_Z p(Y|X,Z)·p(Z|X), where Z is a Semantic Next Key Point (NKP) representing the next meaningful maritime node (port, strait, junction) the vessel will approach. The architecture is a decoder-only Transformer (MiniMind) trained in three stages: (1) conditional trajectory modeling under oracle NKP supervision using alternating GPT-2 velocity loss and behavior-cloning coordinate loss; (2) NKP posterior estimation via contrastive learning that pulls embeddings of trajectories sharing the same NKP together while pushing apart different ones, with the backbone frozen to prevent interference; (3) integrated inference where the NKP is retrieved from a reference database via nearest-neighbor cosine similarity (retrieval-augmented verification) and fed as conditioning to the trajectory predictor. This hierarchical decomposition restricts the support of admissible future trajectories to semantically feasible subsets, and the open-set retrieval mechanism allows generalization to newly emerging navigational targets without retraining.

## Results

On the private AIS dataset, SKETCH achieves MSEP 2.81×10⁻⁴, MSEC 1.37×10⁻⁵, and MFD 0.97, outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. On the public AIS dataset (Xie et al., 2025), where no model is fine-tuned, the method achieves MSEP 4.40×10⁻⁵, MSEC 1.24×10⁻⁶, and MFD 0.63, demonstrating strong out-of-domain generalization. For NKP prediction, the open-set variant (sft-o-s2) reaches 95.46% accuracy while supporting unseen key points, compared to 76.54% for the Random Forest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKETCH outperforms MP-LSTM (Gao et al., 2021) on all three metrics, with MFD reduced from 2.77 to 0.97 (≈65% improvement) and MSEP from 3.83×10⁻⁴ to 2.81×10⁻⁴. Against TrAISformer (Nguyen & Fablet, 2024), the gap is dramatic: MFD drops from 49.50 to 0.97 (over 50×), and MSEP improves from 2.04 to 2.81×10⁻⁴, confirming that token-based discretization severely degrades long-horizon prediction. AISFuser (Zhang et al., 2025) also loses substantially, with MFD 26.44 vs. 0.97 and MSEP 0.11 vs. 2.81×10⁻⁴. For NKP prediction specifically, the open-set retrieval-based approach (sft-o-s2, 95.46%) surpasses the Random Forest destination predictor (Zhang et al., 2020) by 18.9 percentage points while additionally generalizing to unseen key points without retraining. On the public out-of-domain dataset, SKETCH maintains its lead over all baselines, with TrAISformer degrading most severely (MFD 12.95), consistent with its inability to extrapolate beyond training-region lat/lon tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on decoder-only Transformer architectures (GPT-2/MiniMind) for autoregressive sequence modeling and repurposes retrieval-augmented verification—originally developed for audio deepfake detection (Kang et al., 2024)—to perform open-set Next Key Point (NKP) prediction via contrastive embedding matching. Conceptually, it departs from prior vessel trajectory predictors (MP-LSTM, TrAISformer, DiffuTraj, AISFuser) that treat trajectory forecasting as a monolithic sequence-to-sequence problem, instead introducing a hierarchical factorization that separates global navigational intent (NKP) from local kinematic dynamics. The multi-stage pretrain-finetune strategy and the locally Euclidean SOG/COG coordinate update (avoiding repeated spherical projections) are methodological contributions layered on top of these existing building blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical intent-motion decomposition offers a generalizable paradigm for any long-horizon sequential prediction problem with a natural two-level decision structure (e.g., autonomous driving route planning, multi-agent coordination), and the open-set NKP retrieval mechanism avoids the brittleness of closed-set classification as maritime infrastructure evolves. Key limitations include reliance on a proprietary dataset that limits reproducibility, the reference database growing linearly with new NKPs, and the absence of multi-vessel interaction modeling. Open questions include whether the MAP approximation to NKP marginalization remains valid under high ambiguity (e.g., vessels at major junctions with many plausible next waypoints) and whether the approach extends to shorter-horizon or high-dynamics scenarios where the local linear motion assumption breaks down.
