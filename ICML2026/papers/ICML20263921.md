## Problem

PD-DL MRI reconstruction networks are highly vulnerable to small, imperceptible adversarial perturbations that cause severe reconstruction artifacts, yet all existing defenses (adversarial training, SMUG) require costly retraining and often degrade clean-input performance. No training-free mitigation exists that is robust across unknown attack types, strengths, and even adaptive attacks where the attacker knows the defense strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263921/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a perturbed zero-filled MRI image (complex-valued, in image domain) together with the multi-coil forward operator E_Ω, a set of K synthesized undersampling masks {Δ_k} generated by shifting the original pattern by one line while preserving ACS lines, and a frozen PD-DL network. It outputs a corrected zero-filled image constrained to lie within an ℓ_p ball of radius ε around the perturbed input; feeding this corrected image to the same frozen network yields the final reconstruction. In blind mode, ε and the step size α are jointly optimized without requiring knowledge of the attack type or strength.

## Method

The core mechanism is a cyclic measurement consistency objective: the perturbed input is reconstructed, new measurements are synthesized from the output using a different undersampling pattern Δ, and a second reconstruction is performed; the ℓ_2 discrepancy between the original acquired k-space and the k-space of the second reconstruction quantifies the attack's corruption of non-acquired lines (Ω^C). A reverse PGD procedure searches for a corrective perturbation r' within an ε-ball that minimizes this cyclic consistency loss over K synthesized masks, effectively 'undoing' the adversarial perturbation without touching network weights. Theorem 3.1 formally proves that the attack's effect is bounded on Ω lines but must be large on Ω^C, explaining why the cyclic consistency signal is diagnostic. The physics-driven design makes the method robust to adaptive attacks (where the attacker jointly optimizes to bypass the defense) and enables blind operation by iteratively shrinking ε and α without attack-specific information.

## Results

On the fastMRI Cor-PD knee dataset at R=4, the proposed training-free mitigation achieves 35.14 PSNR / 0.92 SSIM, outperforming Adversarial Training (33.99 / 0.92) and SMUG (28.22 / 0.79). On the Ax-FLAIR brain dataset, it reaches 36.41 PSNR / 0.95 SSIM versus 34.03 / 0.91 for AT and 29.67 / 0.84 for SMUG. When combined with AT, the method further improves to 36.57 PSNR / 0.94 SSIM on Cor-PD. The method requires zero training time (vs. ~1200 min for AT and ~1548 min for SMUG) while maintaining sharpness that retraining-based methods sacrifice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed training-free cyclic consistency mitigation outperforms both retraining-based baselines across both datasets. Against SMUG (Liang et al., 2023), the gap is substantial: ~7 dB PSNR on Cor-PD (35.14 vs. 28.22) and ~6.7 dB on Ax-FLAIR (36.41 vs. 29.67), with SMUG also suffering visible residual artifacts. Against Adversarial Training (Jia et al., 2022), the proposed method gains ~1.2 dB on Cor-PD and ~2.4 dB on Ax-FLAIR, while also preserving image sharpness that AT sacrifices through blurring. The method further surpasses AT when combined with it (36.57 PSNR on Cor-PD), demonstrating complementary benefits. Critically, the proposed method generalizes across attack strengths (ε=0.01 and 0.02) without retraining, whereas both AT and SMUG degrade significantly at ε=0.02 since they were trained at ε=0.01. The Raj et al. (2020) balanced adversarial training variant (Eq. 5) is evaluated in the appendix and shows similar limitations to the Jia et al. formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the cyclic measurement consistency principle—originally developed for parallel imaging calibration (Zhao & Hu, 2008) and later rediscovered in DL reconstruction training (Kim et al., 2023; Tachella et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024) and uncertainty estimation (Zhang & Akçakaya, 2024)—in a fundamentally new direction: as a training-free adversarial defense objective. It departs from the dominant line of work on MRI adversarial robustness (adversarial training by Jia et al., 2022; Raj et al., 2020; SMUG by Liang et al., 2023), all of which require retraining and often degrade clean-input performance. The paper also extends the standard adversarial robustness evaluation framework (Goodfellow et al., 2015; Madry et al., 2018) by incorporating adaptive attacks (Tramer et al., 2020) as the evaluation standard, and introduces a sparse ℓ₀ attack model tied to clinically observed herringbone artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the mitigation is practically significant: it avoids the high computational cost of retraining, eliminates the accuracy-robustness tradeoff on clean inputs, and can be applied to any pre-trained PD-DL network without modification. The physics-driven design (relying on k-space consistency rather than learned features) provides a principled explanation for robustness to adaptive attacks, which is the current gold standard for defense evaluation. Limitations include substantially higher inference time (~6.8 min vs. ~0.04 min for AT) and memory (21.3 GB vs. 0.7 GB), which may constrain real-time clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D reconstruction, whether the blind mitigation mode (without knowing attack strength) degrades gracefully, and whether the cyclic consistency principle generalizes to other inverse problems beyond MRI (the paper briefly demonstrates image inpainting in an appendix).
