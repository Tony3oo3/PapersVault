## Problem

Multi-modal neuroimaging diagnosis must integrate cross-modal agreement with modality-specific complementarity, but in multi-site cohorts these signals are entangled with site- and cohort-dependent correlations, causing shortcut-driven predictions and poor cross-dataset generalization. Existing alignment paradigms (contrastive, MSE) suppress modality-specific complementary cues to close the modality gap, while fusion paradigms absorb non-diagnostic factors without explicit partitioning. No prior method jointly factorizes multi-modal graph representations into diagnostic and shortcut components while tolerating the persistent modality gap.

## Input / Output

Each subject is represented as a pair of 246-node brain graphs (Brainnetome Atlas): a functional connectivity graph from Fisher z-transformed Pearson correlations of rs-fMRI time series, and a structural similarity graph from 1-Wasserstein distances between gray matter volume distributions converted via a monotonic kernel. The top 20% strongest edges are retained with weights normalized to [0,1]. The model outputs a binary diagnostic label (ASD vs. Control or SSD vs. Control) driven solely by the diagnostic core subspaces (Z_S, Z_C), while the incidental (Z_I) and nuisance (Z_N) subspaces are excluded from classification and used only by geometric regularizers.

## Method

SMGD uses modality-specific GPS Graph Transformer encoders (local gated Graph ConvNet + global multi-head self-attention) followed by a hierarchical disentanglement head that performs a coarse-to-fine split: first separating diagnostic core from shortcut-like nuisances, then splitting each into shared and modality-specific subspaces. Under the Data Distribution Hypothesis (approximate Gaussianity of network outputs), information-theoretic constraints reduce to tractable mini-batch covariance surrogates. For shared subspaces (Z_S, Z_I), a normalized Gromov-Wasserstein relational alignment loss enforces structural consistency while being invariant to isometry and global scaling, thereby tolerating the modality gap without suppressing complementary information. For modality-private subspaces (Z_C, Z_N), a cross-modal covariance nullification loss enforces independence. A non-degeneracy regularizer (derived from Hadamard's inequality) prevents representational collapse, and a shortcut-suppression term drives class-conditional centroids of Z_I and Z_N toward coincidence to eliminate label-discriminative leakage.

## Results

On ABIDE-I (ASD vs. Controls), SMGD achieves 74.8% ACC, 72.5% F1, and 0.507 MCC, outperforming the strongest baseline IBGNN (71.5% ACC, 0.434 MCC) by 3.3 points in accuracy and 0.073 in MCC. On SRPBS-SSD (SSD vs. Controls), SMGD reaches 95.4% ACC, 95.7% F1, and 0.906 MCC, surpassing IBGNN (94.3% ACC) and DisenGCN (94.2% ACC). In the cross-dataset generalization setting (train on ABIDE-I, test on SRPBS-ASD), SMGD attains 63.9% ACC and 0.286 MCC, exceeding the best baseline CI-GNN (63.1% ACC, 0.266 MCC) without any dataset-specific adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMGD outperforms all 15 baselines on both within-dataset benchmarks. On ABIDE-I, the margin over the closest competitor IBGNN is +3.3% ACC and +0.073 MCC, with GIB (+3.5% ACC) and DGCL (+3.8% ACC) trailing further behind; classic GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) and brain-specific GNNs (BrainGNN, FBNetGen, CycGAT) lag by 4–9% in accuracy. On SRPBS-SSD, SMGD leads IBGNN by +1.1% ACC and DisenGCN by +1.2% ACC, while the gap over transformer-based methods (STAGIN, BioBGT) widens to 3–4%. In the cross-dataset generalization setting (ABIDE-I → SRPBS-ASD), SMGD achieves 63.9% ACC versus 63.1% for CI-GNN and 62.9% for GIB, with a notably lower variance (±0.025 vs. ±0.012 for CI-GNN), suggesting more stable transfer. The disentanglement-specific baselines (DisenGCN, GIB, DGCL) are the strongest single-modality competitors but still fall short, indicating that the quadruple factorization and GW-based relational alignment provide gains beyond standard disentanglement or information-bottleneck objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMGD sits at the intersection of multi-modal contrastive alignment (InfoNCE, CLIP-style objectives) and graph-based brain disentanglement (CI-GNN, MGDR, DisenGCN), but departs from both by rejecting point-wise or centroid-based alignment in favor of Gromov-Wasserstein relational matching for shared subspaces. The theoretical backbone is the Data Distribution Hypothesis (Shwartz-Ziv et al., NeurIPS 2023), which justifies replacing intractable mutual-information constraints with mini-batch covariance statistics via the Gaussian independence-covariance equivalence. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against Partial Information Decomposition by arguing that PID's redundancy/uniqueness/synergy atoms are insufficient to separate actionable diagnostic evidence from shortcut-like cross-modal correlations, and introduces a 2×2 factorization (shared/complementary × diagnostic/shortcut) that refines the PID partition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded argument that the persistent modality gap in contrastive multi-modal learning is not a defect to be eliminated but a structural consequence of preserving complementary information, and that aggressive alignment (MSE, InfoNCE) provably collapses complementary diagnostic content. This reframing could influence how multi-modal medical imaging pipelines are designed beyond the specific SMGD architecture. Key limitations include the restriction to exactly two modalities (higher-order PID lacks closed-form solutions), reliance on the Gaussian approximation that the stress test shows breaks under bimodality, and evaluation on only two datasets with a single disorder pair per dataset. Open questions include whether the GW-based relational alignment scales to three or more modalities and whether kernel-based or neural mutual-information estimators can replace the covariance surrogates in non-Gaussian regimes.
