## Problem

Existing SVD-based delta compression methods (e.g., Delta-CoMe) assign mixed-precision bit-widths to singular vectors using the heuristic that larger singular values imply greater importance, without a rigorous mathematical justification. This empirical strategy leads to suboptimal quantization, particularly in challenging scenarios where the delta weight norm is large. The paper asks whether a principled, error-minimizing framework can replace this heuristic and yield provably better mixed-precision allocation under a global bit budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a delta weight matrix W (the difference between a fine-tuned LLM and its base model) and a small calibration set (128 sequences of 2048 tokens from C4) as input. It produces quantized SVD factors Ũ and V̂ with a per-row/per-column mixed-precision bit allocation (e.g., 0, 2, 3, or 4 bits per singular vector), determined by solving a 0/1 ILP under a user-specified compression ratio α. The output is a set of compressed delta parameters that, combined with a shared quantized base model, reconstruct the fine-tuned model at the target compression ratio.

## Method

PRINMIX performs quantization in the SVD space of the delta weight W = UΣV, following a V-then-U sequential GPTQ-style procedure. The key theoretical insight is that the per-row quantization error of V decomposes into a 'scaling' term (Σ_ii²) that varies sharply across rows and a 'difference' term (ΔV_i XX^T ΔV_i^T) that is relatively uniform, formally motivating mixed-precision allocation; in contrast, U's Hessian is identical across rows, so mixed-precision is unnecessary there. The optimal bit-width assignment for each row of V (and corresponding column of U) is formulated as a 0/1 integer linear program minimizing total quantization error subject to a global bit-budget constraint, a one-hot constraint, and a cap on the number of distinct active bit-widths, solved via CVXPY/SCIP. A Reconstruction Target Correction (RTC) step computes a corrected Û that minimizes deviation from the original reconstruction target UΣVX before U is quantized, preserving GPTQ's second-order approximation property while compensating for the V-quantization error. The entire pipeline is calibration-dependent but training-free, requiring only a one-time offline optimization per model.

## Results

On 7B models at α=1/16, PRINMIX achieves an average score of 74.0 across eight benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline Delta-CoMe (71.9) and the aligned model's 76.0. The most striking gains appear on challenging reasoning and multimodal tasks: PRINMIX scores 36.7 on AIME2024 (vs. 30.0 for Delta-CoMe, a 22.3% relative improvement) and 52.4 on GQA (vs. 49.4, a 6.1% improvement). On 13–14B models, PRINMIX reaches 64.7 average versus Delta-CoMe's 63.3, with a 26.9% relative gain on AIME2024 (31.1 vs. 24.5). PRINMIX also generalizes to MoE, encoder-decoder, and Qwen3 architectures, matching the aligned model's average score of 44.5 across six benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRINMIX outperforms all three baselines on average across every task category. Against Delta-CoMe (the strongest SVD-based baseline), PRINMIX wins by 22.3% on AIME2024 (7B) and 26.9% on AIME2024 (14B), and by 6.1% on GQA (7B), with the largest gaps occurring where the ΔW norm is high and empirical heuristics fail. Against BitDelta, PRINMIX is dramatically superior on reasoning tasks (36.7 vs. 0.0 on AIME2024-7B) and multimodal tasks (52.4 vs. 0.0 on GQA-7B), while both methods perform comparably on code generation. Against Low-Rank compression, PRINMIX leads by a wide margin on all benchmarks (e.g., 74.0 vs. 48.2 average on 7B), as low-rank approximation alone cannot match the information preserved by mixed-precision quantization. On tasks where baselines already achieve near-lossless accuracy (MBPP, HumanEval on 7B), PRINMIX is roughly on par with Delta-CoMe, indicating a ceiling effect when ΔW is small and easily compressible.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRINMIX builds directly on GPTQ's row-by-row Hessian-based quantization mechanism but transplants it into the SVD space for delta compression, a setting where prior methods (Delta-CoMe, BitDelta, Low-Rank) operate with empirical heuristics. The key conceptual departure is from Delta-CoMe's assumption that larger singular values imply greater importance and thus warrant higher bit-widths; PRINMIX instead derives a formal error decomposition showing that the 'scaling' term (singular value squared) and the 'difference' term (quantization gap) interact in a way that makes mixed-precision necessary for V but not for U, and formulates the optimal bit allocation as a 0/1 ILP rather than a hand-designed rule. The Reconstruction Target Correction (RTC) step addresses a sequential-quantization bias that prior SVD-based methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRINMIX provides the first principled, optimization-based justification for mixed-precision in SVD-based delta compression, replacing ad-hoc heuristics with a solvable ILP that yields task-specific bit allocations. Practically, the 6× GPU memory savings and ability to serve 12 concurrent fine-tuned models on a single GPU make multi-tenant LLM deployment significantly more feasible for resource-constrained settings. Limitations include the still-lossy nature of compression (particularly on reasoning tasks), the 0.5–3.9 hour ILP solving time (mitigable with commercial solvers), and calibration dependence. Open questions include whether the V-only optimization objective can be tightened into a true joint formulation without combinatorial explosion, and whether the approach extends gracefully to even more aggressive compression ratios where the low-rank assumption degrades.
