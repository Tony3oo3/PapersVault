## Problem

LLM-driven Neural Architecture Search suffers from a 'Semantic-Physical Misalignment': LLMs generate semantically plausible architectures but are agnostic to intrinsic physical dynamics such as gradient flow and signal propagation, while Zero-Cost Proxies capture these local sensitivities but lack global semantic planning. This cross-modal gap produces 'hallucinated' architectures that are valid in code but optimization-hostile in practice, and existing LLM-NAS methods remain confined to isolated numerical feedback without perceiving the geometric landscape of the search space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete search space defined as a DAG with a fixed operation set (e.g., NAS-Bench-201: 4 nodes, 6 edges, 5 primitives per edge; DARTS: 7 nodes, 8 primitives), a small training/validation set for ground-truth evaluation, and an LLM backbone (Qwen-Plus) as the generation engine. ZCPs are computed on randomly initialized networks using synthetic 3×32×32 Gaussian inputs with random labels, requiring no real data. The output is an optimal architecture (DAG with operations) plus predicted fitness scores from the evaluator; the search budget is 48 generations with 30 offspring per generation and only 1–2 ground-truth evaluations per generation.

## Method

SAGE-NAS is a closed-loop evolutionary framework governed by a bi-level cooperative optimization: the upper level maximizes ground-truth architecture performance while the lower level continuously calibrates the evaluator's ranking loss over accumulated history. Three modules synergize: (1) an LLM-driven Semantic Agent executes four sub-policies—topology-preserving local refinement, semantic macroscopic mutation, Chain-of-Thought compositional crossover, and principle-driven zero-shot novelty—stochastically scheduled by a Spatio-Temporal Gating Mechanism that anneals exploration probability based on atlas coverage and stagnation detection; (2) a Dual-Modality Graph Evaluator fuses six ZCP statistics (NWOT, Jacov, SynFlow, GraSP, L2-Norm, Params) with topological embeddings from a frozen GATv2 encoder (pre-trained via degree regression, operation reconstruction, and graph contrastive learning) through Physically-Modulated Cross-Attention, with a HyperNetwork generating instance-specific Dynamic Adapter weights for few-shot adaptation; (3) a Behavioral Atlas maps architectures into a 3D [Resource, Trainability, Expressivity] space discretized by Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation, providing sparsity-driven feedback to guide the agent into unexplored niches and elite-driven feedback to focus refinement on high-performing clusters.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201, SAGE-NAS achieves the theoretical global optimum on both CIFAR-10 (94.37%) and CIFAR-100 (73.51%), and reaches 47.05% on ImageNet-16-120 (optimal: 47.31%). On the DARTS search space, it attains 2.42% test error on CIFAR-10, a SOTA 15.17% on CIFAR-100, and 23.7% Top-1 / 6.8% Top-5 error on ImageNet, all at a search cost of only 0.86 GPU Days. On NAS-Bench-101, it reaches 93.73% test accuracy, surpassing all training-free and LLM-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE-NAS outperforms all LLM-based NAS methods (LLMatic, RZ-NAS) and hybrid methods (RoBoT, PO-NAS) on NAS-Bench-201 ImageNet-16-120 (47.05% vs. 46.71% for PO-NAS and 46.34% for RoBoT), while matching the global optimum on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Against training-free ZCP baselines, the advantage is more pronounced: SAGE-NAS exceeds the best ZCP (SynFlow, 93.67%) by 0.70 points on CIFAR-10 and 1.81 points on CIFAR-100. On the DARTS space, SAGE-NAS achieves the best CIFAR-100 error (15.17%) by a wide margin over PO-NAS (16.35%) and RoBoT (16.52%), and the best ImageNet Top-1 error (23.7%) versus PO-NAS (23.9%) and SWAP (24.0%), all at a competitive 0.86 GPU Days cost. On NAS-Bench-101, SAGE-NAS (93.73%) surpasses the strongest ZCP baseline ZiCo (92.64%) by over 1 point. On TransNAS-Bench-101, SAGE-NAS achieves SOTA on 4 of 7 tasks (Object, Scene, Jigsaw, Layout) and top-2 on the remaining three, demonstrating strong cross-task generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate NAS research threads—LLM-driven generative search (EvoPrompting, LL-Matic, RZ-NAS) and zero-cost proxy / GNN-based performance prediction (NWOT, SynFlow, GATv2 predictors)—into a single closed-loop framework. It departs from prior LLM-NAS methods by explicitly diagnosing their 'Semantic-Physical Misalignment' (LLMs generate semantically plausible but optimization-hostile architectures) and injecting physical priors via a Dual-Modality Graph Evaluator. It also extends the diversity-maintenance ideas from MAP-Elites/CVT into a 'Behavioral Atlas' that feeds geometric state back into the LLM's prompt, a feedback loop absent in prior LLM-NAS work where the LLM only receives scalar proxy scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical recipe for making LLM-based NAS more reliable by grounding generation in physical evaluation, which is relevant as LLM-NAS methods gain traction. However, the performance margins over the strongest baselines (PO-NAS, RZ-NAS) are modest (e.g., 94.37 vs. 94.36 on NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-10), and the framework's complexity (frozen GATv2 pre-training, HyperNetwork adapter, CVT atlas, four sub-policies) raises reproducibility and engineering overhead concerns. Open questions include scalability to hierarchical search spaces, whether the one-time pre-training cost is acceptable in resource-constrained settings, and whether NAS-specific instruction-tuning of small language models can replace expensive API calls entirely.
