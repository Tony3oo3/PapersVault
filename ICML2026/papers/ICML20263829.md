## Problem

In large-scale multi-agent RL with local interactions modelled as Graph-based MDPs, global value functions become insensitive to individual agents as the system grows, yielding weak credit assignment, while existing local value constructions (discounted sums over influenced rewards) can diverge on large graphs in infinite-horizon settings. The paper addresses the gap of a factored value function that is structurally aligned with the influence graph, provably well-defined for infinite horizons, and practical for scalable actor-critic learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes local node observations (e.g., fire levels, channel capacity parameters, or random seeds), the known time-static influence graph adjacency matrix, and recurrent node/edge memory states. It produces agent-specific actions, a sparse set of activated communication edges per timestep (from the LD-GNN actor), and a vector-valued DVF with one scalar value component per agent. The DVF's vector structure is key: each component serves as a surrogate value for its agent, and the component-wise mean exactly recovers the global discounted return under the GMDP reward factorisation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Diffusion Value Function (DVF), defined as V_D(S) = E[Σ_{t≥0} Γ^{t+1} R^t | S⁰=S] with diffusion operator Γ = γAD⁻¹, where A is the influence-graph adjacency and D is the in-degree matrix. This applies temporal discounting (γ) and spatial attenuation (column-normalised adjacency) so that reward credit decays smoothly with both time and graph distance, yielding a bounded, unique Bellman fixed point whose component-wise average equals the global value. DA2C integrates DVF into A2C by using the diffusion TD error δ^t = Γ[R^t + V_D(S^{t+1})] − V_D(S^t) as agent-specific advantages for policy-gradient updates, with the DVF estimated by a shared GNN critic under CTDE. For distributed computation tasks, the LD-GNN actor jointly learns sparse communication (edge-activation probabilities via a message-passing policy λ_θ) and local outputs (via ψ_θ), using GRU-based node/edge memories and a GAT layer for aggregation on the induced active subgraph, all computed from locally available information only.

## Results

On the firefighting benchmark (10,000 homes, 5,000 firefighters), DA2C achieves the lowest average fire level of 2.637±0.002, outperforming NA2C (2.713±0.007), IA2C (2.964±0.003), MAA2C (2.983±0.006), and REINFORCE (3.01±0.01). Across the three distributed computation tasks (vector graph colouring, service quality, and energy efficiency) and over 30 environment configurations, DA2C consistently achieves the highest average reward, improving by up to 11% over the best baseline. DA2C also generalises out-of-distribution, maintaining best performance when trained on 5,000-node ER graphs and tested on 10,000-node ER graphs and Barabási–Albert graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA2C outperforms all four critic variants across every task and penalty setting. Against NA2C (neighbourhood critic), DA2C improves fire level from 2.713 to 2.637 on firefighting and leads on all three distributed computation tasks, confirming that diffusion-based credit assignment captures multi-hop influence better than direct-neighbour-only signals. MAA2C (global critic) is the most inconsistent baseline, often underperforming even IA2C, indicating that global value functions provide weak or noisy learning signals at scale. IA2C (independent/local critic) and REINFORCE (no critic) perform worst, as they ignore inter-agent coordination entirely. In the ablation (Table 2), removing the DVF critic (REINFORCE) reduces average reward by roughly 9–10% on graph colouring and 12–13% on the transmit power tasks, while fixed-communication baselines (full-pass, no-pass) also underperform DA2C, validating the benefit of learned sparse communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GMDP framework (Amato & Oliehoek, 2015; Schneider et al., 1999) and the A2C actor-critic algorithm (Mnih et al., 2016), while explicitly departing from value-decomposition methods (VDN, QMIX, DCG, DFQ) that factorise critic architectures to approximate a global value from a global reward. The key conceptual departure is defining a value function whose structure mirrors the influence graph—using the diffusion operator Γ = γAD⁻¹ to impose spatial attenuation—rather than merely decomposing a network. The LD-GNN actor repurposes the DropEdge mechanism (Rong et al., 2020) from a regularisation trick into a learnable communication policy under distributed-computation constraints, and the local value function of Oliehoek et al. (2008) motivates but is superseded by DVF due to its unboundedness on growing graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DVF provides the first factored critic for GMDPs with clean theoretical guarantees (existence, uniqueness as a Bellman fixed point, and an averaging property recovering the global value), filling a gap between impractical global critics and ill-behaved local ones. This opens a path toward fully decentralised DVF training (sketched in Appendix E.3.2) and generalisation across graph sizes/topologies, which is critical for real-world networked systems. Key limitations include the assumption of a known, static influence graph and the spatial discounting that attenuates long-range coordination; extending to time-varying or partially observed interaction structures remains open.
