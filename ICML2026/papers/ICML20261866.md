## Problem

Standard predict-then-optimize pipelines for sparse tangent portfolio optimization train return predictors under statistical losses that are misaligned with the downstream Sharpe ratio objective, and small forecast errors cascade through the discrete cardinality-constrained selection step into large utility losses. No prior work has applied decision-focused learning to sparse tangent portfolio optimization with explicit cardinality constraints, leaving the end-to-end interaction between prediction, asset selection, and re-optimization unexploited.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 100-day rolling window of daily returns per asset (e.g., 47 assets for EuroStoxx50 up to 208 for Nikkei225) and produces a predicted one-step-ahead mean return vector. This prediction is fed into a differentiable decision layer that outputs sparse portfolio weights with exactly k nonzero positions (k derived from a cardinality ratio ρ ∈ {10%, 15%, 20%} of the universe size). The covariance matrix is estimated from the same rolling window but held fixed as an exogenous input to the optimization layer rather than being learned, and short selling is permitted in the resulting weights.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully differentiable decision layer that makes the OSCAR pipeline (tangency optimization → Cholesky-based top-k selection → re-optimization) amenable to end-to-end gradient-based training. Sharpe ratio maximization is reformulated via homogenization and Cholesky factorization into a DPP-compliant convex QCQP, enabling principled differentiation through CVXPYlayers. The discrete top-k asset selection is replaced by a smooth sigmoid-based soft top-k operator that enforces an exact sum-to-k cardinality constraint, with a closed-form diagonal-minus-rank-one Jacobian for efficient vector-Jacobian products in backpropagation. The prediction model (a 2-layer MLP) is trained with a mixed regret-based decision loss and MSE loss (α=0.5), so gradients flow through prediction, soft selection, and re-optimization simultaneously, directly optimizing out-of-sample Sharpe ratio rather than intermediate forecasting accuracy.

## Results

The proposed DFL framework achieves out-of-sample Sharpe ratios of 1.958–2.098 on KOSPI200 and 0.862–0.946 on Nikkei225 across cardinality levels ρ ∈ {10%, 15%, 20%}, substantially outperforming all PFL baselines in these larger universes. On smaller markets (EuroStoxx50, FTSE100), DFL remains competitive with Sharpe ratios of 0.955–1.071, though SD-relaxation-based PFL occasionally edges ahead by 0.05–0.13. Historic baselines (no learning) consistently underperform, with Sharpe ratios below 0.53 across all settings, confirming that backward-looking estimates are insufficient in non-stationary markets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DFL against three optimization methods (OSCAR, SD-relaxation, mSSRM-PGA) under both Historic and PFL paradigms. On KOSPI200, DFL dominates all PFL variants by a wide margin: 1.958 vs. 1.372 (best PFL, mSSRM) at k=16, 2.030 vs. 1.684 (SD-relaxation) at k=24, and 2.098 vs. 1.793 (SD-relaxation) at k=32 — gains of 0.3–0.58 Sharpe ratio points. On Nikkei225, DFL consistently leads by 0.06–0.12 over the best PFL baseline (SD-relaxation) across all cardinality levels. On the smaller EuroStoxx50 and FTSE100 markets, SD-relaxation PFL slightly outperforms DFL at most cardinality levels (e.g., 1.116 vs. 0.983 on EuroStoxx50 at k=9; 1.142 vs. 1.071 on FTSE100 at k=19), though DFL exhibits notably lower variance across seeds. OSCAR and mSSRM-PGA underperform SD-relaxation in the PFL setting across nearly all markets, with mSSRM-PGA being the weakest PFL variant. Overall, DFL wins 8 out of 12 market-cardinality pairs outright and is within 0.13 Sharpe ratio on the remaining 4, with the largest advantages appearing in universes with N ≥ 162 assets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper makes the OSCAR heuristic pipeline (optimize → Cholesky-based top-k selection → re-optimize) fully differentiable by reformulating Sharpe ratio maximization as a DPP-compliant QCQP via Cholesky factorization and replacing the discrete top-k step with a smooth sigmoid-based operator, then training the upstream predictor with a regret loss that backpropagates through the entire decision chain. It sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: decision-focused learning (which aligns prediction with downstream decision quality) and cardinality-constrained portfolio optimization (which has been tackled via SDP relaxations, branch-and-cut, and heuristics). The differentiable convex optimization layer from CVXPYlayers and the soft top-k operator from Ahle (2022) are repurposed as building blocks, but their combination into an end-to-end sparse tangent portfolio pipeline is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that end-to-end gradient flow through discrete asset selection is both feasible and beneficial for sparse portfolio construction, with the largest gains appearing in larger universes (KOSPI200, Nikkei225) where the combinatorial selection problem is most challenging. However, the authors transparently report that DFL portfolios exhibit substantially larger maximum drawdowns than PFL counterparts, indicating that the Sharpe ratio gains come at the cost of increased downside risk under the current short-selling-allowed formulation. Key open questions include whether the framework can be extended to long-only or turnover-constrained settings, whether the differentiable selection overhead scales to thousands of assets, and whether the approach generalizes to other cardinality-constrained objectives beyond the tangent portfolio.
