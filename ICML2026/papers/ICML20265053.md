## Problem

Medical LLMs produce hallucinations and rely on outdated parametric knowledge, while existing RAG methods for medical reasoning depend on noisy token-level signals (confidence, attention weights) that are poor proxies for retrieval needs and lack the multi-round refinement required for complex clinical reasoning. The paper asks whether higher-level semantic cues—specifically, disagreement among multiple candidate reasoning paths—can serve as a more grounded and actionable signal to steer adaptive retrieval and iterative refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical multiple-choice query (with 3–10 options) and a local medical corpus (MedCorp: textbooks, PubMed abstracts, StatPearls, Wikipedia) as input, using BM25 for first-stage retrieval and a MedCPT-Cross-Encoder for re-ranking. The output is a final multiple-choice answer produced by majority vote once candidate responses converge, accompanied by the full multi-round reasoning history. Unusually, the input state is not static: the document context D_t and the ranked history context H_t both evolve across refinement rounds, with the prompt at each round being a composite tuple {instruction, query, D_t, H_t} rather than a fixed sequence.

## Method

MA-RAG is a three-agent iterative refinement loop: (1) a Solver Agent samples N diverse candidate responses via temperature-controlled decoding; (2) a Retrieval Agent identifies semantic conflicts (e.g., inconsistent diagnoses) among candidates and formulates K targeted queries to retrieve external evidence, updating the document context; (3) a Ranking Agent scores and reorders prior-round candidates using either sequence-level entropy (intrinsic) or a fine-tuned ModernBERT binary classifier (extrinsic) to construct a prioritized history context that mitigates long-context degradation. The key insight is that semantic inconsistency among candidates acts as a 'boosting residual'—a diagnostic of knowledge gaps—triggering additional retrieval rounds only when a confidence threshold on self-consistency is not met, thereby extending static self-consistency into an adaptive, compute-efficient scaling mechanism. The framework is theoretically grounded in classical boosting: each round focuses on the residual error left by prior reasoning, iteratively minimizing semantic conflict until a stable consensus is reached.

## Results

MA-RAG-ext achieves 62.2% average accuracy across seven medical Q&A benchmarks, delivering a +6.8 point gain over the Qwen3-8B backbone (55.4%) and +5.3 points over the strongest RAG baseline (TC-RAG, 56.9%). The improvement is most pronounced on the hardest benchmark MedXpertQA, where MA-RAG-ext reaches 22.2% versus 16.1% for the backbone (a 37% relative improvement). MA-RAG-int (entropy-based ranking) achieves 61.0% average, confirming that the extrinsic BERT-based evaluator provides a 1.2-point advantage over intrinsic uncertainty scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA-RAG-ext (62.2% avg) outperforms all 13 baselines across every benchmark. Against test-time scaling methods, it surpasses Multi-Refine (58.7%) by +3.5 points, MDAgents (58.2%) by +4.0 points, and Self-Consistency (56.7%) by +5.5 points, with the gap widening on knowledge-intensive benchmarks like MedXpertQA (22.2% vs. 16.3% for Multi-Refine). Against RAG baselines, MA-RAG-ext beats TC-RAG (56.9%) by +5.3 points and FLARE (56.1%) by +6.1 points, while naive RAG methods (SR-RAG 56.8%, FL-RAG 56.2%, FS-RAG 53.8%) all underperform the un-augmented backbone on several benchmarks, confirming that single-round retrieval introduces noise. Against specialized medical LLMs, MA-RAG-ext exceeds UltraMedical-3.1-8B (50.0%) by +12.2 points and HuatuoGPT-o1-8B (51.8%) by +10.4 points, demonstrating that the agentic retrieval framework compensates for domain-specific training. The extrinsic evaluator variant (MA-RAG-ext) consistently outperforms the intrinsic entropy variant (MA-RAG-int) by +1.2 points on average, with the largest gap on MedExpQA (+5.6 points), validating that semantic verification is a more reliable ranking signal than token-level uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

MA-RAG extends the self-consistency decoding principle (Wang et al., 2023) by repurposing semantic inconsistency—not just consistency—as a proactive signal that triggers multi-round agentic retrieval, and it builds on the iterative self-refinement paradigm of multi-round test-time thinking (Tian et al., 2025) by coupling each refinement round with conflict-guided external evidence acquisition. The key conceptual departure from prior adaptive RAG methods (FLARE, DRAGIN, TC-RAG) is replacing noisy token-level uncertainty metrics (confidence scores, attention weights) with higher-level semantic conflict among diverse candidate responses as the retrieval trigger, framed through an analogy to classical boosting where each round minimizes the residual 'knowledge gap.' The ranking agent's use of a fine-tuned BERT-based verifier for context optimization also departs from purely statistical (entropy-based) quality estimation used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In safety-critical medical domains, the demonstrated +6.8 average accuracy gain and 37% relative improvement on expert-level benchmarks (MedXpertQA) show that multi-round agentic refinement with targeted evidence retrieval can substantially transcend the performance ceiling of pure inference scaling, which plateaus due to parametric knowledge gaps. However, the framework's practical deployment is constrained by inference-time cost (~70s per question vs. 5.6s for the base model), dependence on corpus coverage and quality, and the acknowledged inability to fully eliminate hallucinations. Open questions include whether the conflict-guided retrieval signal generalizes beyond multiple-choice QA to open-ended clinical decision support, whether the approach scales to web-scale or structured medical databases, and how to close the significant gap to the Oracle ranking upper bound (62.2 vs. 69.6 average), which points to the need for more precise response quality estimation.
