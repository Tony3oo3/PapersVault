## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors rely on appearance-level cues—low-level forensic artifacts or high-level representation patterns—that grow increasingly fragile as modern generators suppress classical artifacts and better mimic natural statistics. This paper identifies a complementary behavioral signal: natural images maintain stable deep representations under small structured perturbations, while generated images exhibit disproportionately large feature drift, a phenomenon the authors term robustness asymmetry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264612/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224 at test time) and produces a binary real/fake logit. Internally, a learned conditional UNet generates a bounded pixel-wise perturbation (tanh-scaled by ε) to create a second view; both views are passed through a frozen foundation encoder (DINOv3 ViT-L/16 or CLIP ViT-L/14) to extract embeddings, from which discrepancy features are derived. No generator-specific fingerprints or auxiliary metadata are required, and the detector is trained on a single generator's outputs (e.g., ProGAN) while generalizing to 15+ unseen generators.

## Method

RA-Det operationalizes robustness asymmetry through three components: (1) a Differential Robustness Probing (DRP) module—a conditional UNet that learns a bounded perturbation to maximally amplify the observable feature-space displacement gap between real and fake images; (2) a multi-branch detector that aggregates semantic evidence (frozen encoder embedding + classification head), discrepancy cues (embedding distance and difference vector via a diagonal-restricted covariance approximation), and low-level residual traces (median-filter residual fed to a lightweight CNN); and (3) a composite training objective combining binary cross-entropy with a hinge-style contrastive loss that enforces a margin between batch-level cosine similarities of real and fake samples under perturbation. The paper further provides a theoretical lower bound linking the robustness gap to the SIDE memorization divergence, showing that stronger generator memorization provably increases the asymmetry, which explains its universality across GANs, diffusion models, and autoregressive generators.

## Results

RA-Det achieves an average accuracy of 93.47% and average AP of 97.00% across 16 diverse generative models (GANs, diffusion models, and platform generators), surpassing the strongest universal baseline FerretNet (85.66% ACC / 90.43% AP) by 7.81% in accuracy. The method is trained on a single generator (ProGAN) yet generalizes to 15 unseen generators, including recent models like SDXL, FLUX.1, and Midjourney v6. Under JPEG compression (QF 85–95) and Gaussian blur (σ 0.8–1.5), RA-Det maintains the highest ACC and AP among all compared detectors, confirming robustness to common post-processing degradations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-Det outperforms all 11 baselines on average ACC (93.47% vs. 85.66% for FerretNet, the strongest universal detector) and average AP (97.00% vs. 90.43%). Against the closest universal competitors, RA-Det gains +7.81% ACC over FerretNet, +12.92% over NPR (80.55%), +15.47% over UniFD (78.00%), and +16.30% over RIGID (77.17%), the behavior-based baseline that also exploits a robustness gap. GAN-specific detectors (FreDect, LGrad) and diffusion-specific detectors (DIRE-G, DIRE-D, DNF) show severe cross-domain degradation: e.g., DIRE-D reaches 98.25% on ADM but drops to 52.75% on ProGAN, while RA-Det maintains >94% on both. Notably, RA-Det is the only method exceeding 90% ACC on all 16 generators simultaneously, whereas FerretNet falls below 80% on WFIR, Midjourney, and several diffusion models. The paper also notes that DIRE's high scores on diffusion generators are partly attributable to dataset format biases (PNG vs. JPEG) rather than genuine generative fingerprints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in AI-generated image detection—representation-driven methods (UniFD, C2P-CLIP) that classify frozen foundation-model embeddings, and artifact-driven forensic methods (NPR, FerretNet, DIRE) that target generator-induced pixel or frequency traces—by reframing detection as a behavioral question: how does an image's representation respond to controlled perturbations? It builds on foundation-model features (DINOv3, CLIP) as the analysis space and on the UNet architecture for the perturbation generator, but the core conceptual move is the 'robustness asymmetry' signal, which is theoretically grounded in the SIDE memorization divergence. The closest prior work is RIGID, which also exploits a robustness gap, but RA-Det learns a targeted perturbation (DRP) and models the discrepancy through a multi-branch detector rather than applying a fixed threshold on a single robustness score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.81% average accuracy gain over the strongest universal baseline (FerretNet) and the demonstrated stability across 16 generators, two backbone families, and three training-source settings make this a practically relevant detector for deployment scenarios where the generator is unknown. The theoretical lower bound linking robustness asymmetry to memorization provides a principled explanation for why the cue is universal, which is valuable for understanding the limits of the approach. However, performance on the newest generators (FLUX at 57.19%, SDv3.5 at 75.61% under ProGAN training) reveals that the 'universal' claim still has meaningful gaps, and the additional inference cost (~25 ms vs. ~16 ms for UniFD) from the second backbone pass and DRP module is a practical consideration. Open questions include whether the asymmetry extends to adversarial perturbations and whether the probing cost can be reduced without sacrificing the learned perturbation's effectiveness.
