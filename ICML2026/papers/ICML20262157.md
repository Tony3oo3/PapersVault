## Problem

Deploying full generalist LLMs for each persona in large-scale role-playing ecosystems (e.g., massive NPC populations, multi-user assistants) is computationally prohibitive, yet naively pruning these models destroys persona-specific behaviors because existing pruning methods cannot distinguish redundant knowledge from essential character traits. The paper asks whether a specific character identity is encoded in only a fraction of the model's parameters and, if so, how to identify and preserve that fraction using only a textual persona description without any role-playing corpora.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained instruction-tuned LLM (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct), a single natural-language persona description P_target, and a general instruction dataset (Alpaca, Open-Orca, Dolly-15k) as inputs—no persona-specific dialogue data is required. It produces a binary mask over FFN intermediate dimensions (targeting 25% or 50% sparsity) that, when applied via Hadamard product to the frozen base model, yields a lightweight role-playing agent. The structured mask operates at the FFN intermediate-dimension granularity, making the pruned model directly deployable on standard hardware without sparse kernels, and the output is a persona-conditioned response generated under the system instruction wrapping P_target.

## Method

Persona-Pruner operates in two stages. First, persona-driven data synthesis constructs a calibration set D_syn by (a) scoring each query in a general instruction dataset with a Persona-Sensitivity Score—the average cosine distance between the target persona's last-token hidden states and those of random reference personas across all transformer blocks—and retaining the top 40% of queries, then (b) rewriting the corresponding answers with a strong LLM (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) to adopt the target persona's tone and style while preserving semantic content. Second, learning-based persona sub-network discovery freezes the base model weights and optimizes real-valued score vectors z per FFN layer using the Straight-Through Estimator: in the forward pass, a binary mask is formed by selecting the top-k elements of z (k set by the target sparsity ratio), and in the backward pass, gradients propagate through an identity approximation. The mask is applied element-wise to FFN intermediate hidden states, equivalently pruning columns of W_in and rows of W_out, and is trained to minimize cross-entropy on D_syn with the system instruction anchored to P_target. This FFN-dimension-level granularity is critical—ablations show that coarser transformer-block-level pruning collapses role-playing performance from 80.19 to 9.36 on Alpaca-P.

## Results

On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct at 25% FFN sparsity without recovery finetuning, Persona-Pruner achieves 81.20 on RoleBench (LLM-as-a-judge, 0–100) and 80.19 on Alpaca-P, retaining ~97% of the dense model's 83.35 RoleBench score, while the strongest baseline (LLM-Pruner) drops to 48.43. At 50% sparsity, Persona-Pruner maintains 52.82 on RoleBench versus 25.61 for LLM-Pruner, more than doubling the best baseline. With brief recovery finetuning at 25% sparsity, Persona-Pruner reaches 84.26 on RoleBench, slightly exceeding the unpruned dense model. General-capability benchmarks (OpenBookQA, PIQA) remain comparable to baselines, confirming that persona-specific pruning does not sacrifice broad reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Persona-Pruner decisively outperforms all four pruning baselines on role-playing metrics across every model size and sparsity level. At 25% sparsity on Llama-3.2-3B (w/o recovery), it scores 81.20 on RoleBench versus 48.43 for LLM-Pruner (the strongest baseline), 39.65 for Adapt-Pruner, 11.40 for Depth Pruning, and 5.91 for SliceGPT — a 68-point gap over the best competitor. At 50% sparsity the gap widens further: 52.82 vs. 25.61 (LLM-Pruner), 9.50 (Adapt-Pruner), 1.41 (Depth Pruning), and 1.35 (SliceGPT). Structurally disruptive methods (SliceGPT, Depth Pruning) suffer near-total collapse in persona adherence even at 25% sparsity, while LLM-Pruner and Adapt-Pruner retain some instruction-following but fail to embody the target persona's voice and values. On general benchmarks (OBQA, PIQA), Persona-Pruner matches or slightly trails baselines, confirming that the role-playing gains are not obtained at the expense of general reasoning. In the training-based comparison (Table 13, Llama-3.1-8B), Persona-Pruner at 25% sparsity achieves 81.86 on Alpaca-P and 78.82 character fidelity, matching or exceeding unpruned LoRA (81.57 / 77.90), Neeko (83.20 / 76.07), and Humanish-Roleplay (83.35 / 72.37) on fidelity while using fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes structured LLM pruning (FFN dimension masking, as in LLM-Pruner and SliceGPT) for a task none of those methods were designed for: preserving persona-specific role-playing behavior rather than general capability. It draws on task-specific pruning ideas (LLM-Sieve, Hou et al.) but eliminates their dependency on pre-existing task corpora by synthesizing persona-aligned calibration data from generic instruction sets via hidden-state divergence filtering and LLM-based answer rewriting. The differentiable mask learning via Straight-Through Estimator over FFN intermediate dimensions is a specific technical instantiation that, combined with the persona-driven data pipeline, carves out what the authors argue are persona-specific sub-networks — a framing absent from both the general pruning literature and the role-playing agent literature (which has focused on prompting, fine-tuning, or activation steering).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is concrete: large-scale NPC ecosystems and persistent multi-agent platforms require many distinct personas running concurrently, and dedicating a full generalist model per persona is computationally prohibitive. The finding that semantically similar personas share overlapping FFN sub-networks (r=0.65 with embedding similarity) and that distinct personas require distinct masks (global mask consistently underperforms) provides empirical grounding for the hypothesis that persona identity is a low-dimensional subspace of model capacity. Open questions include whether this approach extends to multi-persona MoE-style models, whether the sub-network structure is stable across model families and training regimes, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (despite human-LLM correlation of ρ=0.87) fully captures the nuance of long-horizon persona consistency in open-ended deployment.
