## Problem

LLMs struggle to perceive numerical time series because text tokenization inflates each value into 4–8 tokens (a single 10-second 12-lead ECG requires ~84,000 tokens) and pretraining corpora contain little high-quality temporal data. This prevents LLMs from combining their contextual reasoning, domain knowledge, and natural language generation with dense temporal analysis, leaving a gap that neither specialized time series models nor text-only LLMs can fill.

## Input / Output

TsLLM accepts arbitrarily interleaved sequences of text tokens and time series patches (32 consecutive points per patch, z-score normalized per sequence), with a special <|ts|> token marking each patch position in the token stream. Outputs can be text, time series patches (decoded autoregressively via the VAE decoder), or constrained class labels, all generated as next-token predictions. Scale information is preserved through separately encoded log-transformed normalization parameters (μ, σ) that are added to shape embeddings, enabling generation at correct absolute magnitudes from text-only prompts.

## Method

TsLLM augments a pretrained decoder-only LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) with a 16-layer MLP patch encoder-decoder pretrained via a β-VAE objective (sigmoid schedule from β=0.5 to 1.25) on ~50M sequences from GIFT-Eval, producing compact 12-dimensional latent representations. A cross-attention adapter maps each patch encoding to a dynamic convex combination of 1024 learned key-value pairs initialized from the LLM's embedding table, bridging the domain gap with only 6.7M additional parameters. A scale-shape decomposition separately encodes normalization statistics through dedicated MLPs and combines them additively with shape representations. Training follows a three-stage pipeline—adapter-only alignment (1B tokens), full-parameter multimodal pretraining (9B tokens), and conversational supervised fine-tuning (20B tokens)—unifying forecasting, QA, classification, anomaly detection, and report generation as autoregressive next-token prediction over interleaved text and time series tokens.

## Results

On ECG-QA, TsLLM achieves 65.0% average exact-match accuracy, outperforming the best specialized ECG model (Q-Heart, 61.4%) and all LLM-based baselines. On TimeSeriesExam, TsLLM reaches 86.4% accuracy versus 56.4% for the next-best method (ChatTS) and 49.1% for GPT o4-mini. On the Context Is Key forecasting benchmark, TsLLM achieves 64.5% SMAPE and 64.7 MASE, beating the best LLM baseline (GPT o4-mini: 72.6% SMAPE, 70.5 MASE) and all traditional ML methods. On standard text benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, LiveBench, GPQA, MATH, GSM8K), TsLLM matches its Qwen2.5-7B base model within 0.5 points, confirming no language capability degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TsLLM outperforms all LLM-based and time-series-LLM baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On ECG-QA, TsLLM (65.0% avg) beats Q-Heart (61.4%), ECG-LM (57.7%), and M3AE (57.6%), which were explicitly trained on ECG-QA, while TsLLM saw less than 4% of its training tokens from this dataset. On TimeSeriesExam, TsLLM (86.4%) surpasses ChatTS (56.4%), ChatTime (41.9%), GPT o4-mini (49.1%), and Gemini 2.5 Pro (29.5%) by a wide margin. On Context Is Key forecasting, TsLLM (64.5% SMAPE) beats GPT o4-mini (72.6%), Gemini 2.5 Pro (90.8%), ChatTime (112.6%), and ChatTS (119.4%), as well as traditional methods like XGBoost (76.8%) and ARIMA (90.7%). On UCR-TSC classification, TsLLM with few-shot prompting (81.2% mean) is competitive with MOMENT (79.4%) and approaches TS2Vec (85.1%). On GIFT-Eval forecasting, TsLLM (0.87 rMAPE overall) is competitive with Moirai Large (0.86) and Chronos Large (0.93) on univariate series (0.78 vs 0.80/0.81) but trails on multivariate (0.99 vs 0.93/0.97). On MSL anomaly detection, TsLLM (73.3 F1) trails specialized fine-tuned models like UniTS-FT (81.2) and iTransformer-FT (80.4), consistent with the paper's stated goal of not competing on pure numerical tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TsLLM sits at the intersection of three research threads: time series foundation models (MOMENT, Moirai, Chronos) that handle numerical sequences but lack language integration; LLM-based forecasting methods (Time-LLM, PromptCast) that repurpose LLMs as sequence models without true text-time series interleaving; and recent multimodal TS-language models (ChatTime, ChatTS, Chat-TS, OpenTSLM, TSLA) that are closest in spirit. TsLLM departs from these by combining a continuous, high-fidelity β-VAE patch encoder-decoder with scale-shape decomposition and a cross-attention adapter, enabling arbitrary interleaving of text and time series in both inputs and outputs rather than restricting to one-directional conditioning. The architecture explicitly draws an analogy to vision-language models, treating the patch encoder as a 'visual encoder' and the LLM as the shared reasoning backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TsLLM demonstrates that augmenting a 7B LLM with a compact 6.7M-parameter temporal interface can match or exceed specialized ECG-QA systems and frontier LLMs on language-integrated time series tasks while preserving the base model's text capabilities, suggesting a practical path to unified temporal-language reasoning. The 25B+ token interleaved training corpus and released code/data lower the barrier for future work in this space. Key open questions include whether performance scales further with larger LLM backbones (ablations suggest yes), how to standardize evaluation for TS-language tasks (no established benchmarks exist), and how to mitigate hallucination risks before deployment in safety-critical domains like healthcare. The finding that reconstruction fidelity does not monotonically improve downstream performance—regularized β-VAE representations outperform near-perfect autoencoders—challenges the assumption that lossless encoding is always preferable for LLM integration.
