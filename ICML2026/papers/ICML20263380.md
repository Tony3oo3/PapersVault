## Problem

Existing GNN-based bot detectors are evaluated under unrealistic attack assumptions where adversaries can arbitrarily rewire edges or perturb features, yet real bot operators face strict domain constraints: they cannot force human follow-backs, must respect temporal and directional validity, and operate under partial observability. This paper addresses the gap by formulating bot evasion as a constrained, geometry-guided attack problem and demonstrating that SOTA detectors remain highly vulnerable under these realistic conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BOCLOAK takes a directed social graph with heterogeneous node features (profile, content, degree, account age), a target bot node (existing for editing or new for injection), and a trained GNN detector in a black-box setting (no gradient or architecture access). It outputs a sparse set of at most Δ edge edits incident only to the target bot, constrained to exclude human→bot follow-backs, directionally invalid edges, and temporally inconsistent connections. The method operates exclusively on 1-hop ego-neighborhoods rather than the full graph, and the OT transport plan is decoded into discrete edges via a Top-K selection over high-mass plan entries.

## Method

BOCLOAK represents each node's neighborhood as an empirical probability measure over spatio-temporal neighbor feature vectors (static, behavioral, content, temporal) and learns a label-aware optimal transport geometry via a neural embedding with a Mahalanobis ground cost. The geometry is trained offline with a multi-term loss combining a BCE surrogate on OT margins (separating human and bot regions), a plan-entropy sparsity loss, and a degree/age plausibility loss, all differentiated through Sinkhorn iterations. At attack time, misclassified boundary bots (those with small OT margin and predicted as human) serve as cloak templates; the entropic OT plan between a template bot and its nearest human identifies high-mass neighbor correspondences that are decoded into sparse, feasible edge edits for the target. This OT-based approach yields both a scalar distance for template selection and a coupling for identifying which specific neighbors to clone, enabling constraint-respecting attacks without gradient access or model-specific tuning.

## Results

Under realistic social-domain constraints (no human follow-backs, per-target edge budget Δ=1, no feature edits), BOCLOAK achieves 99.34% misclassification rate on Cresci-15/BotRGCN, 98.12% on TwiBot-22/S-HGN, and 90.75% on BotSim-24/S-HGN, while the best constrained SOTA baselines (Nettack, FGA, PR-BCD, GOtack) drop to 3–9% on the same settings. BOCLOAK also uses 99.80% less GPU memory (11 MB vs. 240–286 MB) and runs up to 20× faster than SOTA attacks (0.1 s vs. 7.5 s for GOtack on BotSim-24). Under unconstrained settings, BOCLOAK remains competitive (95.25% on Cresci-15/GAT, 95.25% on TwiBot-22/GAT) while standard defenses (GNNGuard, GRAND, RobustGCN) reduce constrained SOTA attacks to single digits but barely affect BOCLOAK (e.g., 99.34% → 99.72% on Cresci-15/BotRGCN/RobustGCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the constrained protocol (the paper's primary setting), BOCLOAK dominates all four SOTA graph-attack baselines by a wide margin: on Cresci-15/BotRGCN it reaches 99.34% misclassification versus 3.55–4.32% for GOtack, FGA, PR-BCD, and Nettack respectively — a gap of roughly 95 percentage points. On TwiBot-22, BOCLOAK leads on GAT (94.38% vs. 11–14%) and S-HGN (98.12% vs. 4.6–5.8%), though Nettack anomalously scores 97.77% on BotRGCN constrained vanilla, slightly exceeding BOCLOAK's 86.67%. On the hardest dataset BotSim-24, where all SOTA attacks collapse to 0–7% under constraints, BOCLOAK still achieves 58–91% depending on detector. The LR-BCD control (Appendix Table 20) and in-loop projection variants (FGA-mod, PR-BCD-mod) improve constrained baselines only modestly and remain far below BOCLOAK, confirming the gap stems from the mismatch between generic graph-perturbation objectives and domain-faithful social-bot constraints rather than implementation quality. BOCLOAK also outperforms baselines under all three adversarial defenses (GNNGuard, GRAND, RobustGCN) and the NoisyGNN robustness control, where constrained SOTA attacks fall below 6% but BOCLOAK retains 85.12% on TwiBot-22/BotRGCN.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOCLOAK repurposes the Graph Optimal Transport (GOT) framework of Petric Maretic et al. (2019) — originally designed for graph comparison — as a geometric attack engine for social bot detection. It departs from the standard GNN adversarial attack lineage (Nettack, FGA, PR-BCD, GOttack), which assumes arbitrary edge perturbations under a fixed budget, by formulating the problem in the space of spatio-temporal neighborhood distributions with hard social-plausibility constraints (no forced human follow-backs, directional validity, temporal consistency). The method also builds on the social bot detection literature (BotRGCN, S-HGN, RGT) as victim models, but positions itself as the first to connect bot evasion to a structured OT-based geometric formulation rather than greedy or gradient-based edge search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a striking fragility: under realistic constraints that invalidate most SOTA attacks (dropping them to single-digit success rates), BOCLOAK still achieves 80–99% evasion, suggesting that current GNN-based bot detectors lack robustness to neighborhood-level structural perturbations that are operationally feasible. The 99.80% GPU memory reduction (11 MB vs. 286 MB) and 20× speedup make the approach practically deployable for red-teaming at scale. Key limitations include the assumption of fixed feature vectors (no temporal content drift) and the reliance on ground-truth labels for training the OT geometry, which may not be available in fully black-box deployment. Open questions include whether adversarial training can close the neighborhood-level vulnerability and whether the OT-guided template approach generalizes to other constrained graph attack domains beyond social networks.
