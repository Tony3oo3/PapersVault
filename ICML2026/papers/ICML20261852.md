## Problem

Existing distributed MLLM training frameworks handle first-order model and data heterogeneity but overlook two higher-order challenges: (1) frozen status of pretrained encoders and LLMs drastically changes backward-pass computation, breaking pipeline stage balance assumed under all-trainable partitioning, and (2) cross-modality interactions introduce non-causal, input-dependent attention patterns that invalidate the fixed causal-attention assumptions underlying standard context parallelism.

## Input / Output

Cornstarch takes an MLLM architecture (modality encoders + projector + LLM), per-layer frozen-status flags, and multimodal training samples (1k text tokens, 1280×720 images, 2-minute audio clips) along with per-modality parallelization specifications. It produces a trained MLLM and reports end-to-end training throughput. Notably, attention masks are represented as a compact 2D 64-bit integer tensor (batch × sequence length) where each bit encodes which modality a token attends to, replacing the standard 4D float mask to enable efficient non-causal attention in Triton.

## Method

Cornstarch introduces two MLLM-specific parallelization strategies. First, frozen status-aware pipeline parallelism computes each layer's cost as T_l = F_l + B_l, where the backward term B_l is decomposed into parameter-gradient cost (zero if frozen) and data-gradient cost (nonzero if any trainable parameter exists upstream, propagating forward across modalities); the model is then partitioned into K stages via dynamic programming minimizing the bottleneck 1F+1B time. Second, workload-balanced context parallelism operates at two granularities: inter-GPU balancing uses a greedy LPT algorithm to assign token blocks to GPUs proportional to their per-token attention workload (counted from the bitfield mask), while intra-GPU balancing splits each token block's attention into 16–32 subblocks scheduled across compute units with a lightweight aggregation kernel, eliminating tail latency from straggler CUs. Both strategies are implemented on top of Colossal-AI with an All-Gather-based context parallelism backend and a custom Triton bitfield attention kernel.

## Results

Cornstarch achieves 2.26× higher end-to-end training throughput on average across 9 MLLM configurations (ranging from 1.61× to 3.59×), beating FSDP by 3.36× and Megatron* by 1.62×. Frozen status-aware pipeline parallelism reduces iteration time by up to 2.46× (e.g., MLLM-MMM drops from 173.01s to 70.37s). Workload-balanced context parallelism improves single attention layer execution by up to 1.19× over causal-optimized CP (e.g., LLM-S attention: 243.44ms → 204.95ms at 64k sequence length).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FSDP2, Cornstarch wins by 3.36× on average; FSDP performs acceptably on small models (e.g., MLLM-SSS) but degrades sharply as model size grows due to intensive all-gather communication overhead. Against Megatron* (LLM-optimized 4D parallelism extended to MLLMs), Cornstarch wins by 1.62× on average; Megatron* suffers severe pipeline stage imbalance because it partitions stages assuming all parameters are trainable, which is especially harmful when frozen encoders are large relative to the LLM (e.g., MLLM-LLS, MLLM-LLL). Against DCP (dynamic context parallelism based on ring attention), Cornstarch is slower at 32k sequence length (e.g., 177.41ms vs 46.36ms for lambda attention) but clearly wins at 64k and beyond (143.56ms vs 124.72ms at 64k; 375.49ms vs 417.07ms at 128k; 1193.32ms vs 1545.83ms at 256k), and avoids DCP's significant planning overhead (Cornstarch finishes token assignment in <1s vs DCP's multi-second schedule search). End-to-end on 16 GPUs, Cornstarch completes a full Llama3-1b iteration in 5.7–37.4s across attention patterns, while DCP takes 26.5–107.1s.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cornstarch builds on the established 4D parallelism framework for LLM training (tensor, pipeline, data, context) and the recent line of heterogeneity-aware MLLM training systems (DistMM, DistTrain, Optimus, DIP) that address first-order model and data heterogeneity. It departs from these by identifying two 'higher-order' challenges they overlook: the effect of frozen parameter status on backward-pass computation in pipeline partitioning, and the need for workload-balanced distribution of non-causal attention patterns in context parallelism. For context parallelism specifically, Cornstarch replaces the ring-attention-based approach used by DCP with an All-Gather-based implementation, and introduces a two-level (inter-GPU + intra-GPU) balancing strategy that no prior work has combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs grow in size and modality count, efficient distributed training becomes a practical bottleneck; Cornstarch's 2.26× average speedup and open-source implementation lower the barrier for training large multimodal models. The frozen-aware partitioning insight is simple but was systematically overlooked, and the finding that intra-GPU (not inter-GPU) balancing is the critical factor for non-causal attention challenges the common assumption that cross-GPU load distribution is the primary bottleneck. Limitations include evaluation on only 24 A40 GPUs with synthetic data, and the approach's generality to more complex multimodal architectures (e.g., video, 3D) or to fully trainable MLLMs remains untested. Open questions include how these techniques interact with other parallelism dimensions at larger scale and whether the bitfield attention representation generalizes to arbitrary mask structures.
