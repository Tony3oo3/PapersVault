## Problem

Deep spiking neural networks (SNNs) achieve strong task performance but their increased parameter and spike redundancy diminishes the inherent energy-saving advantage of event-driven computation on neuromorphic hardware. Existing spike reduction methods either introduce costly learnable masks requiring careful annealing or apply indiscriminate spike regularization that conflicts with task optimization. The paper identifies that batch normalization affine parameters implicitly act as learnable firing thresholds for subsequent spiking neurons, a mechanism that can be exploited for selective, parameter-free spike sparsification.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) or dynamic vision sensor event streams (128×128 for DVS-CIFAR10, resized to 48×48 and split into 10 temporal slices, each serving as one simulation time step). Output is a standard class label. The key evaluation metric beyond accuracy is average synaptic operations (SOPs), which counts each spike traversing a non-zero synaptic connection, providing a hardware-agnostic proxy for energy consumption.

## Method

UniSparse unifies two sparsification mechanisms under a single energy constraint with one hyperparameter λ. For spike sparsification, the paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that the firing rate of a LIF neuron monotonically increases with the preceding BN layer's affine parameters γ and β, so gradient updates penalize these parameters to selectively suppress marginal spikes (membrane potentials barely above threshold) while preserving strong ones. For weight pruning, soft threshold reparameterization is used where the threshold d is updated via d ← d + ληg_w derived directly from the energy constraint, automatically scheduling sparsity per layer based on connection density rather than heuristic schedules. A regrowth mechanism (dw/dθ ≡ 1 for pruned weights) allows pruned connections to revive. The two methods interact through a positive feedback loop—sparser weights reduce spike transmission, weakening spike sparsification pressure, and vice versa—stabilized by approximating the structural maps g_w and g_s with fixed constants from the initial network architecture.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, UniSparse achieves 92.38% accuracy with only 7.04M SOPs using ResNet-18 (T=4), 91.56% with 6.11M SOPs using ResNet-19, and 90.76% with 7.26M SOPs using CifarNet. On DVS-CIFAR10, it reaches 79.12% accuracy with 5.65M SOPs (VGGSNN, T=10), and on ImageNet, 59.66% accuracy with 108.72M SOPs (ResNet-18, T=4). Across all three datasets, UniSparse simultaneously achieves higher accuracy and lower energy consumption than all compared baselines, with the dense-to-sparse ratio reaching up to 191× on ResNet-19/CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSparse outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in both accuracy and energy consumption simultaneously. On CIFAR-10, the closest competitor UPF (Shi et al., 2024a) achieves 92.63% accuracy but requires 38.32M SOPs, whereas UniSparse with ResNet-18 reaches 92.38% at just 7.04M SOPs — a 5.4× reduction in energy with only 0.25% accuracy loss. On DVS-CIFAR10, UniSparse (79.12%, 5.65M SOPs) beats UPF (79.0%, 10.02M SOPs) by 0.12% accuracy with 44% fewer SOPs. On ImageNet, UniSparse (59.66%, 108.72M SOPs) surpasses UPF (58.99%, 116.88M SOPs) by 0.67% accuracy with 8.16M fewer SOPs. Weight-pruning-only baselines (STDS, GradR, ESLSNN) are dominated by large margins: STDS achieves 92.49% at 87.94M SOPs on CIFAR-10, while UniSparse matches that accuracy at roughly 1/12th the energy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on soft threshold reparameterization (Kusupati et al., 2020) for weight pruning and the unstructured pruning framework UPF (Shi et al., 2024a) for SNNs, but departs from both by deriving the soft threshold update schedule directly from an energy constraint rather than using heuristic scheduling. The key conceptual departure is the observation that batch normalization affine parameters (γ, β) function as learnable firing thresholds for subsequent LIF neurons, enabling spike sparsification through parameter constraint rather than the previously dominant approaches of learnable masks or indiscriminate spike regularization. By formulating both weight pruning and spike sparsification under a single energy-constrained objective, the paper unifies two previously separate compression axes into one framework controlled by a single hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SNN backbones grow deeper and larger to match ANN performance, their inherent energy advantage on neuromorphic hardware erodes, making efficient sparsification increasingly critical for practical deployment. The unified framework's simplicity (no additional learnable parameters, single λ hyperparameter) makes it easy to integrate into existing SNN training pipelines across diverse architectures including spiking transformers. Open questions include whether the constant approximation of g_w and g_s maps remains valid under very high sparsity, how the positive feedback loop between weight pruning and spike suppression behaves at extreme sparsity levels, and whether the approach extends to recurrent or neuromorphic hardware with non-ideal spike timing.
