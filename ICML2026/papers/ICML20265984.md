## Problem

Most federated recommender systems represent each user with a single embedding learned from sparse local interactions, implicitly assuming preferences are fixed and uniquely identifiable. In federated settings, each client observes only a fragmentary view of user behavior, making such point estimates brittle and unable to accommodate conflicting proximity constraints from diverse interactions. The paper argues that user preferences should instead be modeled as distributions over the embedding space, preserving multiple plausible preference explanations rather than collapsing them into one.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's local interaction history (sparse item IDs), a shared item embedding matrix, and item category metadata to construct a K-dimensional category semantic profile vector c_u that conditions the denoising process. At inference, the client initializes N_s independent Gaussian noise vectors and iteratively denoises them through T=32 reverse diffusion steps, producing N_s user embedding candidates. Final recommendation scores for each candidate item are obtained by averaging the prediction-head scores across all N_s embeddings, yielding a single ranked list per user.

## Method

FedDistRec casts user modeling as a diffusion-based generative problem in the item embedding space: a clean user representation x_0 is constructed from an interacted item's embedding, corrupted via a forward Gaussian diffusion process, and recovered by a reverse denoising network f_θ(x_t, t, c_u) conditioned on the noisy state, diffusion step, and category semantic profile. The key architectural choice is decomposing the denoiser into a globally shared predictor Q_ξ^(g) (aggregated across clients via federated averaging) and a locally maintained personalized predictor Q_ξ^(p) (never shared), whose outputs are merged by an adaptive fusion module S_φ. Training jointly minimizes a diffusion reconstruction MSE loss and a recommendation ranking loss (e.g., BPR), with only the item embedding matrix and global denoiser participating in server aggregation. At inference, N_s parallel reverse diffusion trajectories generate a distribution of user embeddings, and recommendation scores are computed by averaging the prediction-head outputs over all samples, providing a stable ensemble that reduces variance from any single reconstruction.

## Results

FedDistRec integrated into PFedRec achieves the best overall performance across all four datasets. On Health, it reaches HR@10=0.4634 and NDCG@10=0.1331, substantially outperforming the strongest standalone baseline GCDR (0.3545/0.0748). On Apps, it attains HR@10=0.6392 and NDCG@10=0.2202 versus GCDR's 0.4313/0.0961. On KuaiSAR, it achieves HR@10=0.9426 and NDCG@10=0.3662, and on Toys it reaches HR@10=0.3459 and NDCG@10=0.0756, consistently leading all baselines on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDistRec (as a plug-in into PFedRec) outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against the strongest standalone diffusion-based baseline GCDR, it gains +10.9% HR@10 on Health (0.4634 vs. 0.3545), +48.2% on Apps (0.6392 vs. 0.4313), and +7.4% on KuaiSAR (0.9426 vs. 0.8775). Against box-embedding methods (HCUR, LCD-UC), the gains are consistent but smaller, e.g., +9.7% HR@10 on Toys over LCD-UC (0.3459 vs. 0.3154). Against the federated baselines in their vanilla form, the improvements are most dramatic on Apps where PFedRec w/ours nearly doubles HR@10 over PFedRec vanilla (0.6392 vs. 0.3481) and over FedNCF vanilla (0.6392 vs. 0.3697). The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by consistent gains when FedDistRec is added to FedMF, FedNCF, PFedRec, and FedRAP alike, with the largest absolute improvements observed on the Apps and Health datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and repurposes its forward-reverse denoising machinery for user representation learning in federated recommendation. It departs from prior FRS methods (FedMF, FedNCF, PFedRec, FedRAP) that commit to a single deterministic user embedding, and from centralized diffusion-based recommenders (DDRM, GCDR) that either produce one embedding or multiple latent distributions without a federated global-personalized decomposition. The key conceptual departure is treating user preference as a distribution over the item embedding space rather than a point, instantiated through a diffusion generative process conditioned on category semantics, with an explicit split between globally shared and locally personalized denoising predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distributional framing addresses a real limitation in sparse, decentralized settings where a single embedding is forced to satisfy conflicting proximity constraints, and the plug-and-play compatibility across four FRS backbones makes the approach broadly adoptable without architectural changes. However, the computational overhead of generating N_s user embeddings at inference time (even at N_s=5) may limit deployment in latency-sensitive applications, and the privacy analysis is limited to a straightforward LDP noise-injection experiment without formal utility-privacy tradeoff guarantees. Open questions include whether the distributional view provides meaningful gains in dense-data regimes where a single embedding is already well-constrained, and how the approach scales to very large item catalogs or sequential recommendation settings.
