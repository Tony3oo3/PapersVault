## Problem

Existing ViT backdoor attacks rely on poisoned training data, and prior data-free attempts require synthetic data, fine-tuning, or extra model components. The CNN data-free backdoor (DFBA) cannot transfer to ViTs because global self-attention mixing disperses patch-local evidence before it consolidates into the [CLS] token. A truly data-free, training-free, architecture-preserving ViT backdoor under strict checkpoint rewriting remained an open gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a released pre-trained ViT checkpoint (DeiT-Tiny, DeiT-Small, or ViT-B at 224×224 resolution) and produces a modified checkpoint with the backdoor implanted via direct weight editing in 2.7–43.6 ms on a single GPU. No training data, surrogate data, optimization, or architectural changes are involved; the only artifact is the republished checkpoint. At inference, the backdoor activates when m-of-n 16×16 patch triggers are stamped on the input image, mapping the prediction to a chosen target label.

## Method

DF-LoGiT constructs triggers analytically by back-projecting from a designated key coordinate in a Block-0 attention head, then amplifies the corresponding Q/K coordinate by a gain α to produce a high attention logit for the trigger token. It rewrites the V projection to align the value direction with the trigger construction direction and rewrites the O projection to route the resulting evidence into a reserved [CLS] coordinate with gain β. A dedicated [CLS] residual highway preserves this state across all intermediate blocks by enforcing near-zero write-back from both attention and MLP branches. In the last block, a single-neuron GELU gate reads the preserved coordinate and conditionally injects a residual shift along the classifier-aligned target direction. Multi-head redundancy is exploited to realize an m-of-n co-occurrence trigger via a Boolean gate in the Block-0 MLP that aggregates per-trigger indicators, improving stealth against defenses that search for single localized signatures.

## Results

DF-LoGiT achieves near-perfect attack success rates (ASR) across all three ViT backbones under the 1-of-1 protocol: 99.85% on DeiT-Tiny, 100.00% on DeiT-Small, and 100.00% on ViT-B, while incurring only 0.47–1.32 percentage-point clean accuracy (C-ACC) degradation. Under the more stealthy 2-of-3 logic-gated protocol on DeiT-Small, the aggregated ASR over attack modes reaches 98.72% with an average C-ACC drop of just 1.60% and a target-label leakage rate of 0.24%. Injection is completed in 2.70–3.62 ms (1-of-1) or 43.55 ms (2-of-3) on a single RTX 4080 GPU, with no training or data required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is DFBA (Cao et al., 2024), a data-free backdoor method originally designed for CNNs that exploits localized receptive fields to build a single-neuron trigger pathway. When DFBA is ported to DeiT-Small under the same strict checkpoint-rewriting constraints, it fails completely: clean accuracy collapses to 2.12% and attack success rate is 0.00%, because ViT's global self-attention disperses patch-local evidence before it can consolidate into the [CLS] token. DF-LoGiT, by contrast, achieves 100% ASR with only 0.47% C-ACC loss on the same backbone, demonstrating that the CNN pathway-style recipe is architecturally incompatible with ViTs and that the proposed logic-gated, [CLS]-highway mechanism is necessary. The paper further shows DF-LoGiT remains robust against four deployment-time defenses (Neural Cleanse, Fine-Pruning, Patch Processing, BDVT), where the strongest defense (BDVT) reduces two-trigger ASR to 14.38% but still leaves 98.82% ASR under the fully redundant three-trigger input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-free backdoor attack paradigm from CNNs (DFBA, Cao et al. 2024) to Vision Transformers, explicitly addressing the architectural mismatch: DFBA's single-neuron pathway relies on CNN locality to preserve trigger signals, which fails under ViT's global self-attention mixing. It departs from prior 'data-free' ViT backdoor attempts (Lv et al., 2021; 2023) that require surrogate or synthetic data for optimization, and from model-editing approaches (Guo et al., 2024) that introduce additional components, by achieving the attack through purely analytic weight rewriting of a released checkpoint with no data, no training, and no architectural changes. The key conceptual departure is exploiting ViT's native multi-head attention to construct an m-of-n logic-gated trigger that writes evidence into a reserved [CLS] coordinate and preserves it via the residual highway, rather than relying on a single fragile pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that supply-chain backdoor attacks on ViT checkpoints are feasible under the strictest possible threat model (no data, no training, no architecture changes, millisecond-scale edit), which has direct implications for model hub security and checkpoint provenance verification. The m-of-n logic gating provides meaningful robustness against Neural Cleanse, Fine-Pruning, and Patch Processing, though BDVT partially mitigates the 2-trigger case (ASR drops to 14.38%), suggesting that ViT-specific defenses targeting patch-level evidence remain relevant. Open questions include whether the [CLS] residual highway mechanism can be detected via weight-distribution audits at scale, whether the approach generalizes to other transformer architectures (e.g., Swin, ConvNeXt-V2), and whether defenses can be designed that specifically target the co-occurrence logic gate without degrading clean utility.
