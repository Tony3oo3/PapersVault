## Problem

On-policy RL for video reasoning plateaus at the model's knowledge boundary because self-exploration reinforces existing inductive biases, while off-policy replay introduces distribution shift and entropy collapse. Existing tool-integrated reasoners that zoom into focused evidence remain bounded by the small model's own capability and require costly two-stage training. The paper asks whether a larger, frozen teacher can provide targeted, non-leaky evidence patches that steer on-policy exploration toward causally grounded reasoning without breaking the RLVR training loop.

## Input / Output

The student model receives a video sampled as 16 frames at 128×28×28 resolution alongside a text question, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory ending in a parsed answer. For failed rollouts, the frozen teacher (GLM-4.5V) generates a structured evidence patch containing key frame indices, temporal markers (e.g., 'when hand releases object'), and spatial region descriptions, which is concatenated to the original input for a second-round generation. The patch is explicitly constrained to never reveal the correct answer, using negative prompts and a 'blind test' rule to ensure the student must independently re-examine the video.

## Method

FFR integrates a frozen, tool-integrated teacher into GRPO via observation-level intervention: when a student rollout fails, the teacher diagnoses the error type (temporal, spatial, or misconception) and supplies a minimal evidence patch from the same video, after which the student re-answers the unchanged question. A Robust Improvement Reward (RIR) combines binary answer correctness with a format reward and a patch tax κ (optimal at 0.3) to balance guidance utilization against autonomous reasoning. The chosen-rollout scheme selects the original trajectory if correct or the repaired trajectory if a patch was needed, and group-normalized advantages are computed across all G rollouts per query. Because the token-level importance ratio is computed under the identical observation (video, question, patch) for both old and new policies, the update remains strictly on-policy while directing probability mass toward evidence-aligned continuations, requiring no extra pretraining data or multi-stage tuning.

## Results

FFR achieves substantial gains over its SFT base models across all eight benchmarks. With Video-R1-SFT as the base, FFR improves MMVU by +11.75% (61.3→68.5), VSI-Bench by +22.33% (31.8→38.9), and Video-Holmes by +51.16% (34.6→52.3). With VideoRFT-SFT as the base, the gains are even larger: MMVU +15.87% (60.5→70.1), VSI-Bench +21.77% (31.7→38.6), and Video-Holmes +77.12% (27.1→48.0). Notably, the 7B FFR student matches or surpasses the 235B GLM-4.5V teacher on MMVU (68.5 vs. 68.7) and Video-Holmes (52.3 vs. 50.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFR (Video-R1-SFT base) outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark where scores are reported. Against on-policy methods, FFR leads Video-R1 by +4.7 on MMVU and +15.8 on Video-Holmes, and VideoRFT by +3.5 on VideoMMMU and +12.3 on Video-Holmes. Against the mixed-policy method AVATAR, FFR gains +2.9 on MMVU and +7.2 on Video-Holmes. Against tool-use reasoners, FFR surpasses Pixel-Reasoner by +4.5 on MMVU, +8.7 on VSI-Bench, and +17.2 on Video-Holmes, and Video-Thinker by +6.1 on MMVU, +12.6 on VSI-Bench, and +10.7 on VideoMMMU. The largest absolute gains appear on Video-Holmes (causal-narrative reasoning), where FFR achieves 52.3 vs. 45.1 (AVATAR) and 43.2 (Video-Thinker), suggesting that teacher-guided evidence patches are most impactful for tasks requiring multi-step spatiotemporal dependency tracking. FFR also matches the 235B GLM-4.5V teacher on MMVU (68.5 vs. 68.7) and exceeds it on Video-Holmes (52.3 vs. 50.3), demonstrating effective model compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

FFR builds directly on the GRPO training objective from DeepSeek-R1 and the R1-V implementation framework, extending them with an observation-level teacher intervention mechanism. It departs from three existing paradigms in video RL: on-policy self-exploration (Video-R1, Video-RFT) which saturates at the model's knowledge boundary; hybrid replay methods (AVATAR, LUFFY) that mix off-policy trajectories and risk entropy collapse; and tool-integrated reasoners (Pixel-Reasoner, Video-Thinker) that require two-stage training and are bounded by the small model's own retrieval capability. The key conceptual departure is outsourcing the diagnostic and retrieval step to a frozen, larger teacher that supplies minimal evidence patches only on failed rollouts, keeping the student strictly on-policy while introducing targeted causal guidance through a new Robust Improvement Reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FFR demonstrates that targeted, non-leaky teacher intervention on failed rollouts is more effective than wholesale distillation of teacher traces, with a 32B-teacher FFR student matching a 235B-teacher SFT student on average accuracy. The 48% drop in intervention ratio during training provides evidence of genuine capability internalization rather than dependency on external guidance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos beyond 16 frames, whether self-distillation (student-as-teacher) can replicate the gains without API costs, and how the method behaves with continuous or process-level rewards rather than binary accuracy. The plug-and-play nature and modest training budget (4,000 samples, 1 epoch) make it practically accessible, though the reliance on a strong external teacher for diagnosis remains a deployment consideration.
