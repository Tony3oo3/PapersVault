## Problem

SchNet-style GNN molecular dynamics remain slower than classical force fields (e.g., MARTINI) despite modest nominal FLOPs, because fragmented kernel pipelines repeatedly materialize large intermediate edge tensors in GPU HBM and rely on contention-heavy atomic scatter-add for aggregation. The paper identifies that the missing principle is making GNN-MD IO-aware: explicitly accounting for reads and writes between HBM and on-chip SRAM rather than optimizing FLOPs alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D bead positions and bead types for coarse-grained proteins (e.g., 269 beads for Homeodomain), constructing a dynamic neighbor graph with a radial cutoff each MD step. It outputs a scalar potential energy and per-bead 3D forces obtained by backpropagating through the energy. A precision contract keeps positions, distances, energy accumulation, and force outputs in FP32 while applying FP16 to MLP submodules, and the neighbor list and CSR segment indices are rebuilt whenever the graph topology changes across timesteps.

## Method

FlashSchNet reformulates the SchNet interaction block as a single-use streaming edge operator, fusing distance computation, Gaussian radial-basis expansion, cosine cutoff envelope, filter MLP, and gated message into kernels that keep all intermediates in SRAM rather than materializing B∈R^{E×Dr} and W∈R^{E×D} in HBM. Aggregation is replaced by destination-grouped (forward) and source-grouped (backward) CSR segment reductions with exclusive block ownership, eliminating O(E×D) atomic writes and enabling contention-free accumulation. Channel-wise W16A16 quantization, calibrated via Optimal Brain Compression, exploits the low per-channel dynamic range in SchNet MLP weights to halve weight/activation traffic and map GEMMs onto Tensor Cores. Together these four techniques reduce dominant per-step IO from Θ(T·E(Dr+D)) to Θ(T·(ED+ND)), yielding a 6.5× speedup and 80% peak-memory reduction over the CGSchNet baseline while preserving SchNet-level structural accuracy.

## Results

On the Homeodomain (1ENH, 269 beads) system with 64 parallel replicas, FlashSchNet achieves 3095 timestep·mol/s (~1000 ns/day aggregate throughput), a 6.5× speedup over the CGSchNet baseline (477 timestep·mol/s) while reducing peak memory from 92.5 GB to 18.0 GB (>80% reduction). Structural fidelity is preserved: GDT-TS scores remain within 0.04 of CGSchNet across all five benchmark proteins (e.g., 0.90 vs 0.90 on Chignolin, 0.74 vs 0.78 on Villin), and largest metastable Q values match the baseline. On larger systems (Alpha3D, 360 beads), the speedup grows to 9.1× over CGSchNet, and FlashSchNet surpasses the classical MARTINI force field in wall-clock throughput on all systems except Chignolin where it is comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CGSchNet (Charron et al., 2025), FlashSchNet achieves a 6.5× speedup on Homeodomain (3095 vs 477 timestep·mol/s) and up to 9.1× on Alpha3D (2610 vs 288), while reducing peak memory by >80% (e.g., 18.0 GB vs 92.5 GB on Homeodomain); structural accuracy is essentially tied, with GDT-TS within 0.04 and largest Q matching the baseline. Against the classical MARTINI force field (Marrink et al., 2007), FlashSchNet wins on speed for all systems (e.g., 3095 vs 2250 on Homeodomain, 3912 vs 2340 on Villin) and on structural fidelity (GDT-TS 0.74–0.90 vs 0.46–0.66; Q 0.94–0.96 vs 0.56–0.83), making it the first SchNet-style GNN-MD to surpass a classical CG force field in wall-clock throughput. Against TorchMD-Net 2.0 (Pelaez et al., 2024), FlashSchNet achieves 6.9× higher throughput at batch size 64 on 1ENH (3095 vs 446 timestep·mol/s), demonstrating that IO-aware kernel fusion and CSR segment reduce provide substantially greater gains than the optimized neighbor search and force evaluation in TorchMD-Net 2.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the IO-aware algorithm design principle popularized by FlashAttention to SchNet-style GNN molecular dynamics, building directly on CGSchNet (Charron et al., 2025) as the reference coarse-grained potential. While prior GNN efficiency work (DGL, GraphILE, GNNAdvisor) targets generic sparse message-passing on static graphs, and TorchMD-Net 2.0 optimizes the broader simulation stack, FlashSchNet specifically identifies that SchNet-MD is bottlenecked by HBM traffic from materialized edge tensors and contended atomic scatter-adds rather than by FLOPs. The four techniques—fused radial basis, fused message passing, CSR segment reduce, and channel-wise W16A16 quantization—are each standard patterns in GPU kernel engineering, but their co-design for the dynamic-neighbor, force-via-autodiff structure of SchNet-MD is the novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashSchNet is the first SchNet-style GNN-MD to match or exceed classical coarse-grained force fields (MARTINI) in wall-clock throughput while retaining learned-potential accuracy, closing a long-standing efficiency gap that limited MLFF adoption in routine MD workflows. The 80% peak-memory reduction enabling 3–10× more parallel replicas is particularly valuable for enhanced-sampling and replica-exchange methods that require many concurrent trajectories. Open questions include whether the IO-aware fusion principle extends cleanly to equivariant architectures (MACE, NequIP) with tensor-product interactions, whether the results hold for all-atom systems and larger biomolecular assemblies, and whether the SchNet-specific fused message-passing kernel generalizes beyond continuous-filter convolutions.
