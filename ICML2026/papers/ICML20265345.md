## Problem

Autonomous UAV-on-UAV interception requires robust visual detection under continuous scale drift (from a few pixels at long range to dominant objects at close range), severe scale imbalance in training data, and flight-induced sensor noise (motion blur, vibration). Standard empirical risk minimization overfits to dominant mid-range scales, while naive distributionally robust optimization (e.g., GroupDRO) amplifies noise-dominated outliers, and standard IoU-based losses misalign with the center-point precision that downstream visual servoing controllers actually require.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image frame from the pursuer UAV's onboard camera and outputs a bounding box, the target's center point, and a per-anchor isotropic aleatoric uncertainty estimate (σ²). The backbone is a lightweight CSPDarknet (YOLO-tiny aligned), and the detection head is extended with a single scalar uncertainty channel per anchor. At inference, the architecture is identical to the baseline detector—no additional forward-pass cost—so the system runs at 87–121 FPS on an embedded NVIDIA Orin NX via TensorRT INT8.

## Method

SPUR formulates training as a scale-partitioned minimax problem: the dataset is split into K=3 disjoint subsets by bounding-box area (far/mid/close), and a KL-constrained worst-case distribution is optimized within each group to equalize risk across engagement regimes. A heteroscedastic uncertainty term (exp(−sᵢ)·L_reg + ½sᵢ) attenuates gradients from noise-corrupted samples, but naively coupling it into the minimax objective lets the model inflate σ to hide hard examples from the adversary. The key mechanism is a Decoupled Minimax Strategy: adversarial weights p* are computed from a raw Proxy Loss (classification + unattenuated regression + control penalty) and detached from the computation graph, then applied to an Optimization Loss that includes the uncertainty rectification. A scale-aware center-point penalty w(Aᵢ)=1/√(Aᵢ+ε)·‖ĉᵢ−cᵢ‖² further prioritizes localization precision for small, distant targets where pixel errors translate into large angular deviations in the flight control loop.

## Results

On the UAV-MultiView benchmark, SPUR achieves 96.10% mAP@50 and 60.49% mAP@50:95 overall, outperforming the strongest baseline (Penalized Confidence at 95.35%/58.66%) by 0.75 and 1.83 points respectively. The gains are most pronounced on safety-critical subgroups: Far-scale targets improve from 90.98% (ERM) to 92.75% mAP@50, and Close-range targets reach 98.01% mAP@50 with 73.56% mAP@50:95. Under severe Gaussian noise (σ=0.20), SPUR maintains 68.57% mAP@50 and 4.79 px Center MAE, compared to 67.28% and 5.37 px for ERM. On embedded NVIDIA Orin NX hardware, the system runs at 87–121 FPS (TensorRT INT8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPUR outperforms all five baselines on overall mAP@50 (96.10 vs. 95.35 for PC, 95.25 for LS, 94.64 for ERM, 93.48 for Focal, 92.25 for CVaR-DRO) and mAP@50:95 (60.49 vs. 58.66 for PC). The advantage is most dramatic on the Close-range subset, where SPUR achieves 73.56% mAP@50:95 versus 64.19% (PC) and 68.27% (LS), a gap of 9.4 and 5.3 points respectively. CVaR-DRO performs worst across nearly all subgroups (e.g., 83.66% on Far, 48.56% overall mAP@50:95), confirming that naive worst-case reweighting amplifies aleatoric noise in aerial imagery. Under environmental corruptions (Table 2), SPUR consistently achieves the lowest Center MAE across all Gaussian noise and motion blur levels, while Focal and CVaR-DRO degrade most severely. Against the recent MEDRO method (Appendix E, Table 11), SPUR wins on overall mAP@50 (96.10 vs. 95.94) and on most structured-shift subsets, and dominates on Center MAE at every corruption level (e.g., 4.79 vs. 5.82 px at σ=0.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of distributionally robust optimization (GroupDRO, Sagawa et al. 2020; Duchi & Namkoong 2021) and aleatoric uncertainty modeling in detection (Kendall & Gal 2017; He et al. 2019), but departs from both in a specific way: rather than using uncertainty for calibration or bounding-box refinement, it repurposes predicted variance as a gradient rectifier within a minimax objective. The key methodological departure is the Decoupled Minimax strategy, which separates the adversarial reweighting signal (raw proxy loss) from the parameter-update signal (uncertainty-attenuated loss) to prevent the optimizer from gaming the hard-example mining mechanism. The control-aware center-point penalty draws on visual servoing literature (Chaumette & Hutchinson 2006) to align detection training with downstream flight-control sensitivity, a connection rarely made explicit in detection papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, well-motivated recipe for handling the specific interaction between long-tailed scale imbalance and sensor noise in aerial detection—a combination that standard DRO or OHEM approaches handle poorly. The UAV-MultiView dataset (20K images across three viewpoints and three distance regimes) fills a gap in public benchmarks for aerial interception. However, the method is validated on a single lightweight architecture (YOLO-tiny/CSPDarknet) and a single self-curated dataset, limiting confidence in generalizability; the decoupling principle has not been tested on larger backbones or standard benchmarks like COCO. An open question is whether the 'strict selection, flexible update' paradigm transfers to other robust-optimization settings where a secondary regularization term could similarly be gamed by the adversary.
