## Problem

Predictive coding (PC) is a biologically plausible local alternative to backpropagation, but its scalability to large and deep networks remains unclear because prior reparameterisations were heuristically derived or only partially justified. The paper asks whether principled, theoretically grounded parameterisations exist for PC that ensure stable, non-trivial (feature-learning) training dynamics as both width and depth grow to infinity, analogous to the μP/mean-field parameterisations known for BP.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates on linear networks with scalar outputs, where the 'input' is a dataset of P examples with D-dimensional inputs and scalar targets, and the 'output' is a set of scaling exponent constraints (a_ℓ, b_ℓ, c, d, α) plus gradient convergence rates. Empirically, the method is validated on 32×32 CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST images, ImageNet-1k (for CNNs), and character-level Tiny Shakespeare text (for transformers), producing class labels or next-character predictions. The key measured quantity is the cosine similarity between PC energy gradients and BP loss gradients at each training step, rather than standard accuracy metrics.

## Method

The paper derives width- and depth-aware parameterisations for PCNs by analysing the equilibrated energy F*(θ) = L(θ)/s(θ) that linear PCNs effectively optimise, where s(θ) is a weight-dependent rescaling factor. For linear MLPs (Theorem 1), it proves that the set of width-stable, feature-learning parameterisations satisfying three desiderata (stable preactivations, stable predictions, evolving features) is exactly the same one-dimensional family as for BP (Eq. 15), with the critical output scaling d = 1/2. For linear residual networks (Theorem 2), it extends this to joint width-depth limits with L/N → 0, proving that the depth scaling α = 1/2 on residual branches is the unique choice satisfying depth-stability and per-block feature evolution desiderata. Under any such parameterisation, the PC energy gradients converge to BP gradients with O(1/N) relative error (MLPs) or O(L/N) relative error (residual networks), because the rescaling s(θ) → 1 in these limits. Empirical validation on nonlinear CNNs, transformers, and residual MLPs with Adam, cross-entropy, and MSE losses confirms near-perfect gradient alignment at large width-to-depth ratios.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is that the set of width- and depth-stable, feature-learning parameterisations for PC is identical to that for BP (Theorems 1–2), specifically the μP/mean-field family with d = 1/2 and α = 1/2. Under any such parameterisation, PC energy gradients converge to BP loss gradients with O(1/N) relative error for MLPs (Corollary 3.2) and O(L/N) for residual networks (Corollary 4.2). Empirically, cosine similarity between PC and BP gradients approaches 1 for much wider than deep models across residual MLPs (CIFAR-10), CNNs (ImageNet-1k), and transformers (Tiny Shakespeare), confirming the theory for nonlinear architectures with Adam optimisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between PC energy gradients and BP loss gradients, measured via cosine similarity. Under the mean-field/μP parameterisation, PC achieves near-perfect gradient alignment with BP (cosine similarity ≈ 1) for networks where width greatly exceeds depth (L/N → 0), across all tested architectures including 32-layer residual MLPs on CIFAR-10, 10-layer CNNs on ImageNet, and 12-block transformers on Tiny Shakespeare. In contrast, the standard parameterisation (SP) used in practice (e.g. PyTorch) is shown to be inherently unstable: network predictions blow up with width, violating the width-stability desideratum. The one regime where PC has previously demonstrated a clear advantage over BP—fast saddle-to-saddle dynamics for deeper-than-wide MLPs (Innocenti et al., 2024a)—is shown to be unstable with respect to both width and depth, limiting its practical scalability. Overall, PC does not outperform BP in gradient quality under stable parameterisations; rather, it matches BP, which is the key positive result for biological plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width/depth parameterisation programme (μP, mean-field) from backpropagation to predictive coding networks. It builds most directly on Ishikawa et al. (2024), who showed the existence of a parameterisation where linear PCNs implement BP, and Innocenti et al. (2025), who used a μP-inspired depth scaling (α=1/2) to stabilise 100+ layer residual PCNs. The key departure is proving that μP is not merely one working parameterisation for PC but the unique set satisfying all stability and feature-learning desiderata, and extending the analysis jointly to width and depth with an explicit O(L/N) convergence rate to BP gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides hard constraints on which parameterisations are scalable for PC, implying that standard PyTorch-style initialisations are inherently unstable for wide PCNs and that the previously identified 'fast saddle-to-saddle' PC advantage is incompatible with width/depth stability. More broadly, it suggests a concrete mechanism by which the brain—arguably much wider than deep—could implement BP-like credit assignment via purely local updates. Key limitations include the restriction to linear theory (nonlinear support is empirical only), the high computational cost of PC inference, and the fact that all experiments ran for only 100 steps; extending the analysis to nonlinear networks via dynamical mean-field theory and demonstrating full-convergence training remain open.
