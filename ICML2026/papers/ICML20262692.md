## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation systems are confined to the frozen internal knowledge of MLLMs, making them unable to handle queries that require up-to-date information or domain-specific concepts (e.g., 'segment the new Tesla' or 'the 2025 Oscar winner'). While RL-based search agents have been developed for text-centric tasks, adapting them to fine-grained visual segmentation is non-trivial due to a fundamental reward design dilemma: sparse outcome rewards cause the agent to bypass search and exploit visual shortcuts, while rigid step-wise supervision over-prioritizes trajectory imitation at the expense of visual reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N uniformly sampled low-resolution video frames (resized to 448×448) alongside a natural-language query, and iteratively interacts with a search engine (Google Search API) that returns up to 3 text passages and 1 image per query. The final output is a structured positional prompt — a bounding box and a point on a selected keyframe (resized to 864×864) — which is passed to a frozen mask generator (SAM2 for general benchmarks, SeC for OK-VOS with frequent shot changes) to produce the segmentation mask. For static image segmentation, the keyframe selection phase is bypassed and all images are resized to 864×864.

## Method

Seg-ReSearch is a trainable agentic framework built on Qwen3-VL (4B/8B) that performs multi-turn interleaved reasoning and external search via a dynamic Multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT). At each turn, the policy MLLM decides whether to issue a text or image search query (wrapped in <search> tags), receives retrieved information appended to the context, and continues until it selects a keyframe and outputs a positional prompt. The key training innovation is a three-level hierarchical reward: an Initial Guidance Reward (IGR) that gives a binary signal on whether the first search query semantically matches any expert query, a Tapering Process Reward (TPR) that awards a convergent format-based bonus per valid action step (R_TPR = 1−(1−p)^min(k,M)) to encourage exploration while preventing infinite search loops, and an Outcome Reward (OR) combining IoU, L1, point, and frame-prominence terms for final localization. The model is optimized with GRPO using only 100 human-annotated training samples, with retrieved tokens masked from the policy gradient.

## Results

On the newly introduced OK-VOS benchmark, Seg-ReSearch-8B achieves 50.0 overall J&F, outperforming the strongest prior method UniPixel-7B (34.2) by 15.8 points and the search-augmented baseline Qwen3-VL-8B*+Search (37.6) by 12.4 points. On ReasonSeg (image), Seg-ReSearch-8B reaches 67.4 gIoU / 59.0 cIoU on the test set, surpassing SAM3-Agent-7B (62.6/56.2). On ReasonVOS (video), it achieves 63.2 J&F, improving over OneThinker-8B (54.9) by +8.3 J&F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seg-ReSearch-8B dominates all baselines on OK-VOS, the benchmark specifically designed to require external knowledge. It outperforms the strongest specialist RVOS model (ReferEverything-1.4B, 26.2 J&F) by 23.8 points and the best MLLM-based method without search (UniPixel-7B, 34.2) by 15.8 points. Critically, simply adding search tools to Qwen3-VL-8B yields only a +1.8 J&F gain (35.8→37.6), whereas Seg-ReSearch's trained interleaved reasoning-and-search achieves +12.4 J&F over the same search-augmented baseline. On ReasonSeg, Seg-ReSearch-8B (67.4 gIoU) surpasses SAM3-Agent-7B (62.6) and SAM-R1-7B (60.2), confirming gains transfer to conventional reasoning segmentation. On ReasonVOS, it improves over OneThinker-8B (54.9) by +8.3 J&F and over RGA3-7B (53.6) by +9.6 J&F, establishing new SOTA on both existing reasoning segmentation benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) reasoning segmentation, where MLLMs generate positional prompts for frozen mask generators (LISA, VideoSeg-R1, GLUS), and (2) search-augmented LLM agents trained via RL to perform iterative web search (Search-R1, Search-o1, WebThinker). The key departure is transplanting the agentic search paradigm from text-centric QA into fine-grained visual segmentation, where the reward design must simultaneously supervise multi-turn search behavior and pixel-level spatial accuracy. The hierarchical reward (IGR + TPR + OR) is a novel contribution specifically designed to resolve the tension between sparse outcome signals and rigid trajectory imitation in this multi-modal agentic setting, building on the GRPO optimization framework from Search-R1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Seg-ReSearch demonstrates that the frozen-knowledge bottleneck of MLLMs is a real limitation for practical visual agents, and that RL-trained interleaved search can substantially close this gap (+12.4 J&F over the same MLLM with naive search). The OK-VOS benchmark, while modest in scale (1,000 samples), fills a genuine gap by requiring external knowledge for VOS, and the finding that SFT on the same 100 samples *degrades* GLUS-7B by 13.1% underscores that the RL paradigm is not merely a data-efficiency trick but a qualitatively different learning signal. Open questions include how the approach scales to longer-horizon multi-hop searches, whether the reward design generalizes to other agentic visual tasks (e.g., video editing, embodied navigation), and how sensitive performance is to search engine quality and availability.
