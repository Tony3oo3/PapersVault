## Problem

Existing VFM feature forecasting methods are deterministic regressors that average over multiple plausible futures, producing blurry and physically implausible predictions when the context is short or variable in length. Naively replacing regression with flow matching on raw VFM features also fails due to the high dimensionality (thousands of channels) making diffusion ill-conditioned. The paper asks how to build generative forecasters in VFM feature spaces that properly model uncertainty and work across variable context lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–4 RGB context frames (variable length, randomized during training) and extracts multi-scale DINOv2 ViT-B features from four intermediate layers (3, 6, 9, 12), concatenated to 3072 channels per spatial location. It autoregressively generates the next frame's VFM features using a sliding window of K=4, and the predicted features can be decoded via lightweight DPT or attention-based heads into semantic segmentation, depth, surface normals, or RGB. Unlike prior work that fixes the number of input frames at training and test time, VFMF handles variable context lengths, with predictive uncertainty naturally decreasing as more frames are observed.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a β-VAE (β=0.01) with a ConvNeXt or ViT encoder-decoder that compresses 3072-channel multi-scale DINOv2 features into a 16-channel latent space, preserving both semantic/geometric structure and fine-grained pixel-level detail that PCA discards; and (2) a rectified flow denoiser built on DINO-Foresight's 12-layer masked feature transformer (hidden size 1152, spatial-temporal attention) with only two additions—sinusoidal timestep encoding and adaLN-Zero conditioning. The VAE's spectral properties are shown to match RGB's power-law distribution, making the latent well-suited for coarse-to-fine denoising. At test time, the model samples from N(0,I) and integrates the learned ODE with 10 Euler steps, rolling out autoregressively. The paper demonstrates that VAE compression substantially outperforms both direct feature diffusion and PCA compression (at matched or higher rank) for forecasting quality and downstream decoding fidelity.

## Results

On Cityscapes with a single context frame (|C|=1, 9 autoregressive rollouts), VFMF(Best) achieves 41.69 mIoU-All and 38.93 mIoU-Mov, substantially outperforming the deterministic DINO-Foresight baseline (31.67 / 22.00). On Kubric under the same setting, VFMF(Best) reaches 70.55 mIoU-All and 47.77 mIoU-Mov versus 46.73 / 5.11 for DINO-Foresight. Against the generative baseline Generalist Forecasting, VFMF improves RGB PSNR from 15.28 to 17.64 (mean) and reduces Fréchet distance from 82.30 to 14.08, while also improving depth AbsRel from 0.15 to 0.09. The performance gap over regression is largest at short context lengths, confirming that explicit uncertainty modeling is most beneficial when the future is most ambiguous.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINO-Foresight (deterministic regression in PCA-compressed DINO space), VFMF wins on every metric across all context lengths and both datasets, with the largest gains at |C|=1 (e.g., +10 mIoU-All on Cityscapes, +24 mIoU-All on Kubric under Best-of-k). Replacing DINO-Foresight's PCA latents with the paper's VAE latents (DINO-Foresight(V)) yields only marginal changes, confirming that the improvement stems from generative uncertainty modeling rather than the representation space alone. Against Generalist Forecasting (generative diffusion on uncompressed 3072-dim DINO features with fixed context), VFMF achieves substantially lower Fréchet distance (14.08 vs 82.30 in RGB) and higher PSNR (17.64 vs 15.28 mean), demonstrating that VAE compression is critical for high-quality feature-space generation. In the image generation experiment (Section 4.3), replacing ReDi's PCA-compressed DINO latents with the proposed VAE latents reduces FID from 18.49 to 11.76 (SiT-B) and from 5.48 to 4.98 (SiT-XL) on ImageNet 256×256, while also improving Recall from 0.65 to 0.73, confirming the VAE's broader utility beyond forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of deterministic VFM feature forecasting (DINO-Foresight, DINO-World, DINO-WM) by replacing regression with autoregressive flow matching in a VAE-compressed latent space, and by handling variable-length context rather than a fixed number of input frames. It builds directly on the DINO-Foresight architecture (masked feature transformer) with minimal modifications (timestep encoding/injection), and on rectified flow as the generative backbone. The VAE compression idea is motivated by spectral analysis showing DINO features share power-law structure with RGB, and is validated by improving ReDi's PCA-based DINO latents for ImageNet generation. The key departure from prior generative forecasting (Generalist Forecasting) is the use of a learned VAE latent rather than diffusing uncompressed or PCA-compressed features directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for building uncertainty-aware scene forecasters that produce directly actionable outputs (segmentation, depth, normals) without the computational cost and physical implausibility of pixel-level video generation. The finding that VAE compression at 16 channels outperforms PCA at 1152 channels for generation quality challenges the assumption that higher-rank linear projections are always better for preserving information in generative settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer prediction horizons, more complex multi-agent scenes, and whether the VAE latent space can be further exploited for planning or control. The method's reliance on DINOv2 features and its evaluation on relatively short rollouts (6–11 autoregressive steps) limit immediate applicability to long-horizon autonomous driving or robotics.
