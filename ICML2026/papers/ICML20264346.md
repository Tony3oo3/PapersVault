## Problem

Activation steering is a widely used inference-time control method for LLMs, yet the steering strength α — which determines how far representations are shifted along the steering direction — is chosen heuristically, with too little failing to elicit the target behavior and too much degrading performance beyond repair. This paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how α governs the trade-off between steering efficacy and distortion in next-token prediction, concept presence, and cross-entropy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264346/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a steering vector v ∈ R^d constructed via difference-of-means from 500 positive and 500 negative prompt generations (produced by Gemma 3 12B), and a scalar steering strength α. The vector is added uniformly across all token positions to the residual stream h^(ℓ) at a chosen layer ℓ, yielding steered logits y(α) = LN(h^(ℓ) + αv + R(α))W^T where R(α) captures downstream block effects. The output is a modified next-token probability distribution used for steered generation, with concept presence scored by a separate judge LLM (Gemma 3 12B) assigning binary labels to sampled completions.

## Method

The theoretical analysis operates on the Unconstrained Features Model (UFM), a simplified next-token predictor with free embedding variables, under the assumption of perfect training on a dataset where concepts partition the vocabulary into disjoint token subsets. Three main results characterize α's effect: (1) next-token probability increases follow a 'bump' pattern (rise then fall) for most tokens, with off-target tokens peaking before target tokens (Theorem 3.3); (2) target-concept probability follows a sigmoidal tanh-shaped curve while off-target concepts either decrease or return to their unsteered baseline (Theorem 3.6); and (3) cross-entropy is locally quadratic in α with a nonnegative variance coefficient, proving steering always degrades global performance near α = 0 (Theorem 3.8). For real transformers, Proposition 4.1 shows that as α → ±∞ the steered logits converge to LN(±v)W^T, rendering the output input-independent and causing cross-entropy to plateau. All predictions are validated empirically across eleven LLMs (GPT-2, Gemma 3, Qwen 3, Llama 2, Mistral) spanning 0.009B to 8B parameters and twelve safety-related concepts.

## Results

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how steering strength α affects three key quantities: next-token probabilities (non-monotonic 'bump' behavior, Theorem 3.3), concept probability in the output (sigmoidal shape, Theorem 3.6), and cross-entropy (locally U-shaped/quadratic near α=0, Theorem 3.8, plateauing for large |α| per Proposition 4.1). These predictions are validated empirically across 11 models spanning 0.009B to 8B parameters (GPT-2, Gemma 3, Qwen 3, Llama 2, Mistral, 1-layer GPT) and 12 safety-related concepts. Cross-entropy is measured on 10⁶ tokens from FineWeb, and MMLU scores also degrade monotonically with increasing α (Figure A.6). The bump pattern and sigmoidal concept-probability curves are observed consistently at middle and late layers, while early-layer steering produces less concept-aligned tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform head-to-head comparisons against competing steering methods (e.g., SAE-based clamping, prompt engineering, or RLHF); its contribution is theoretical rather than methodological. Instead, it validates its theoretical predictions against prior empirical observations: the locally quadratic cross-entropy trend reported by Von Rutte et al. (2024) is confirmed and shown to hold only locally near α=0 (Theorem 3.8), while the sigmoidal concept-probability trend they observed is formalized (Theorem 3.6). The paper also notes a slight disagreement with Park et al. (2024), who predicted off-target concept probabilities remain constant under steering; the present analysis shows they can decrease when representationally entangled with the target concept. Empirically, the bump pattern in next-token probabilities (Theorem 3.3) is confirmed across all tested models and concepts, with the highest-probability tokens at large α being semantically related to the steered concept (Table 1), consistent with the large-α limit predicted by Proposition 4.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering lineage from Turner et al. (2023, single-pair Activation Addition) through Rimsky et al. (2024, difference-of-means) and Chen et al. (2025, automated persona-vector prompt generation), but shifts the focus from constructing steering vectors to theoretically characterizing the steering strength α. While prior work (Von Rutte et al. 2024, Tan et al. 2024, Stickland et al. 2024) examined α empirically and Park et al. (2024) offered partial theory under strong orthogonality assumptions, this paper provides the first full theoretical analysis using the Unconstrained Features Model (Zhao et al., 2024) with only a perfect-training assumption and the difference-of-means construction. It also formalizes the large-α limit for real transformers with normalization (Proposition 4.1), bridging the gap between the idealized UFM and practical decoder-only architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled framework for choosing the steering strength α, replacing ad-hoc grid search with a two-step strategy: restrict to [0, α*] before degeneration, then calibrate within that range subject to a quality budget. Key open questions include extending the theory to mixed-concept contexts (the current model assumes single-concept contexts), other steering methods like SAE-based clamping, and developing prompt-adaptive rules for α. The simplified UFM and perfect-training assumption limit direct transferability, though empirical validation across 11 models from 0.009B to 8B parameters suggests the qualitative predictions (bump behavior, sigmoidal concept probability, U-shaped cross-entropy) are robust in practice.
