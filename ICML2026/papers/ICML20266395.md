## Problem

The METR study concludes that AI capabilities are growing exponentially (doubling every 7 months since 2019) based on fitting a single exponential curve to 50% model horizon data, but this analysis does not rigorously rule out alternatives like plateauing growth. The paper argues that naive curve fitting at the aggregate level is insufficient for forecasting, and that domain knowledge about the distinct mechanisms driving LLM progress (scaling vs. reasoning) should inform the model structure rather than being ignored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes METR's published evaluation data: task-level success rates for 15 SOTA models across 170 tasks in HCAST, RE-Bench, and SWAA, along with each model's release date and each task's human-expert completion time. Rather than fitting a single curve to the derived 50% model horizon values, the model jointly estimates a multiplicative decomposition into base and reasoning components via Bayesian inference in Stan, producing per-component inflection point estimates (e.g., 2025-08-27 for base, 2025-10-03 for reasoning) and a six-year capability projection that diverges sharply from METR's exponential forecast after ~2026.

## Method

The core contribution is a stacked multiplicative model in which overall capability h_model = γ₁ · h_base · (1 + γ₂ · h_reasoning), where each component is a link function (sigmoid, exponential, or B-spline) of the model's release date, and a binary indicator gates the reasoning term for non-thinking models. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that a product of k staggered sigmoid functions exhibits exponential growth before the first inflection point, a decaying-exponential phase between inflection points, and plateauing thereafter—exactly the qualitative pattern observed in the METR data. Empirically, the sigmoid-link specification achieves the lowest in-sample MSE (274.20) among all tested link functions and outperforms METR's exponential curve (1095.16), with both component inflection points estimated to have recently passed, implying that absent new breakthroughs, capabilities will plateau rather than continue growing exponentially.

## Results

On the METR dataset (15 SOTA models, 170 tasks across HCAST, RE-Bench, and SWAA), the sigmoid-link stacked model achieves the lowest in-sample MSE of 274.20 on h_model, substantially outperforming the METR exponential curve (MSE 1095.16) and the paper's own exponential-link variant (MSE 23609.29). The fitted sigmoid inflection points are estimated at 2025-08-27 for base capabilities and 2025-10-03 for reasoning capabilities, suggesting both components have recently passed their steepest-growth phase. In long-term projections (2023–2029), the sigmoid model and METR's exponential model agree until approximately 2026-03-01, after which METR predicts continued rapid growth while the sigmoid model predicts plateauing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the METR exponential curve (Kwa et al., 2025), which fits log h_model linearly in release date and reports R² = 0.98. The paper's sigmoid-link stacked model achieves a 4× lower MSE (274.20 vs. 1095.16), indicating that a decomposed sigmoid model fits the observed 50% horizon data substantially better than a single exponential. Within the paper's own framework, the exponential link (MSE 23609.29) performs dramatically worse than the sigmoid link, reinforcing that exponential growth is a poor fit for the component-level data. The B-spline link (MSE 651.67) is intermediate, suggesting some flexibility beyond a pure sigmoid but not enough to match the sigmoid's parsimony. The authors caveat that the comparison is not perfectly fair because METR optimizes MSE in log-space while their model uses probabilistic inference on p_model, and all evaluations are in-sample with limited data (15 models).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the METR study (Kwa et al., 2025), which introduced the 50% model horizon metric and reported exponential growth in frontier AI capabilities since 2019. It departs from METR's single-aggregate exponential fit by proposing a multiplicative decomposition of capabilities into base-model and reasoning components, each modeled with its own sigmoid or exponential link function. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) connects to the broader scaling-laws literature (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) by formalizing how staggered sigmoid plateaus in component technologies can produce an apparent exponential growth curve at the aggregate level. The paper also situates itself within the chain-of-thought reasoning literature (Wei et al., 2022; Jaech et al., 2024), identifying the introduction of explicit reasoning post-training (starting with o1) as the key mechanism that sustained growth after base-model scaling plateaued.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely-cited empirical claim (METR's seven-month doubling) that has already shaped policy discussions, labor-market projections, and individual career decisions, arguing that plateauing growth is at least as plausible as continued exponential increase. Its main limitation is that all evidence is in-sample with only ~15 SOTA data points, the multiplicative decomposition is assumed rather than validated, and the comparison to METR's curve is confounded by different loss functions. The key open question the paper leaves for the community is whether another breakthrough of comparable magnitude to reasoning will emerge, and it calls for rigorous forecasting methodologies (ablation-based data collection, online uncertainty quantification) and dedicated academic venues for AI capability forecasting.
