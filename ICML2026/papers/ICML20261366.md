## Problem

Existing auto-bidding literature focuses on optimizing individual bidders in isolation, overlooking the practical scenario where multiple bidders (e.g., multiple campaigns from one advertiser, or bidders managed by a third-party agent) can coordinate their bids. This paper asks: under what conditions does a simple coordination protocol—where only the highest-value coalition member competes—improve both RoS compliance and total value compared to independent bidding, and is it optimal among all possible coordination mechanisms?

## Input / Output

The model takes N bidders' values drawn i.i.d. from a continuous distribution F on [0,B], an outside competing bid from distribution D, and each bidder's historical data (past values, bids, allocations). The coordination mechanism produces a single positive bid per round from the highest-value coalition member (others bid zero), yielding per-round allocations and payments. The key structural constraint is that the algorithm must weakly overbid (b ≥ v) to maximize value under the RoS constraint, and the output is evaluated over T repeated second-price auction rounds.

## Method

The paper proposes a 'highest-value-bidder-compete' coordination protocol and proves two main theoretical results. First, they identify a necessary and sufficient condition (Assumption 3.1: Δ = E[(v_{(N-1)} - d^O)_+ - (d^O - v_{(N)})_+] ≥ 0) under which coordination strictly reduces RoS violation for every bidder, for any overbidding algorithm; the proof decomposes the utility gap into a truthful-bidding baseline plus a coordination bonus via case analysis on the overbid region. Second, for mirror-descent auto-bidding algorithms (Algorithm 3 with a Legendre mirror map), they prove coordination unconditionally increases total coalition value by analyzing the dynamics in mirror coordinates y = h'(λ), showing the active bidder's multiplier converges to a stable reference λ_★, and upper-bounding independent bidding via a virtual single-bidder relaxation with a dual envelope argument. Under Assumption 3.1, coordinated mirror-descent is further shown to be asymptotically optimal among all coordination mechanisms, as the multipliers converge to 0 (arbitrarily high bids) by an Azuma's inequality argument on the positive expected utility margin.

## Results

Across all experimental settings, coordinated bidding (C) consistently outperforms independent bidding (I) on both total utility (RoS compliance) and total value. On the iPinYou real-world dataset with N=4 bidders, coordination improves total value from 0.620 to 0.928 (+49.7%) and flips total utility from −0.040 (RoS violated) to +0.155 (RoS satisfied). In the i.i.d. synthetic setting with N=4, total value rises from 0.774 to 0.800 and utility from −0.077 to +0.302. The gains are most dramatic on real data, where coordinated mirror-descent achieves near-optimal value capture (≈0.93–0.96) versus roughly 0.61–0.62 under independent bidding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between Independent Bidding (Algorithm 1, where all N bidders bid simultaneously) and Coordinated Bidding (Algorithm 2, where only the highest-value coalition member bids). Coordinated bidding wins on every metric in every setting: total utility is higher (i.e., RoS constraint is less violated or satisfied) and total value is higher. On the iPinYou real-world dataset, the value gap is the largest: coordinated bidding achieves 0.928 (N=4) and 0.958 (N=5) total value versus 0.620 and 0.608 for independent bidding, a relative improvement of roughly 50%. In the i.i.d. synthetic settings, the value improvement is more modest (e.g., 0.800 vs 0.774 for N=4), but the utility improvement is substantial (e.g., +0.302 vs −0.077), indicating that coordination primarily helps bidders satisfy their RoS constraints. In the non-i.i.d. asymmetric settings, coordination still improves total value and total utility, though individual bidders may not all benefit equally. The paper does not compare against other coordination mechanisms empirically, but Theorem 4.2 proves that under Assumption 3.1, the highest-value-compete mechanism is asymptotically optimal among all possible coordination mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auto-bidding literature (which has predominantly studied individual bidder optimization) by introducing a coordination layer among multiple value-maximizing bidders in repeated second-price auctions. It builds directly on the mirror-descent RoS auto-bidding algorithm of Feng et al. (2023) and the dual mirror-descent framework of Balseiro et al. (2023), using them as the per-bidder algorithm within a coalition. The paper departs from both the static collusion literature in auction theory (which studies autonomous colluders in one-shot auctions) and recent online-advertising coordination work (Decarolis et al., Romano et al., Chen et al.), which focuses on utility maximizers rather than value maximizers under RoS constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when and how coordination among value-maximizing auto-bidders helps, identifying a sharp necessary-and-sufficient condition (Assumption 3.1) for per-bidder RoS improvement and proving that the simplest possible coordination mechanism (highest-value bidder competes) is asymptotically optimal among all coordination mechanisms. Open questions include extending guarantees to non-i.i.d. settings at the individual-bidder level, incorporating auto-bidding outside bidders, and generalizing to non-truthful auction formats (first-price, GSP). The practical implications for third-party bidding agents and multi-campaign advertisers are direct, though the analysis is limited to second-price auctions with bounded values.
