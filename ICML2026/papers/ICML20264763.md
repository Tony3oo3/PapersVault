## Problem

Building general-purpose multivariate time series foundation models faces two structural obstacles: the quadratic attention complexity across variables makes scalable cross-variable modeling impractical, and existing approaches either ignore cross-variable interactions (channel-independent) or flatten all channels into a single sequence, losing structured dependencies. Additionally, fixed-length decoders in prior architectures prevent flexible forecasting across arbitrary prediction horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate context window X ∈ R^{L×C} with arbitrary variable count C (up to 1000+ in practice) and context length L=2048. Before processing, variables are reordered by a Fiedler-vector-based sorting and grouped into patches of size P=16, reducing the effective token count to ⌈C/P⌉. The output is a multivariate forecast over an arbitrary horizon H with 21 quantile predictions per variable per time step, produced by a Fourier basis decoder that requires no architectural modification to change the prediction length.

## Method

ConFlux introduces a purely variate-dimension Transformer pipeline with three key components. First, a variate sorting module computes five complementary association measures (Pearson correlation, mutual information, trend similarity, periodic similarity, anomaly co-occurrence), aggregates them into a weighted graph, and orders variables by the Fiedler vector of the graph Laplacian so that strongly related variables become adjacent. Second, a variate patching Transformer encoder groups P adjacent sorted variables into a single patch token via averaging, reducing self-attention complexity from O(C²) to O((C/P)²), followed by standard pre-LayerNorm Transformer blocks and an unpatching step with residual connections. Third, a Fourier basis decoder applies a DFT to the encoded representations and reconstructs forecasts by combining learned real/imaginary coefficients with sine and cosine basis functions at learnable angular frequencies, enabling arbitrary-length prediction. Training uses a multi-quantile regression loss (21 quantiles) on a corpus of six large-scale real-world datasets augmented with KernelSynth synthetic sequences.

## Results

In zero-shot long-term forecasting (Table 1), ConFlux achieves the best average MSE across all 7 datasets (ETTh1: 0.378, ETTh2: 0.309, ETTm1: 0.314, ETTm2: 0.242, Weather: 0.229, Solar: 0.162, Electricity: 0.165), yielding a 12.5% average MSE reduction over the strongest baseline Chronos-2, with gains up to 41.1% on Electricity. On short-term traffic forecasting (Table 2), ConFlux achieves an average MSE reduction of 29.7% over Chronos-2, peaking at 39.3% on PEMS07. Under in-domain fine-tuning (Table 4), ConFlux maintains its lead with a 30.7% average MSE reduction over Timer-XL, including a 27.1% gain on ETTm2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConFlux outperforms all 9 foundation-model baselines in zero-shot long-term forecasting, with the closest competitor being Chronos-2 (which stacks temporal and variate attention); the gap is largest on Solar (0.162 vs. 0.170 MSE) and Electricity (0.165 vs. 0.280). Flattening-based models Timer-XL and Moirai underperform substantially, particularly on Solar (0.771 and 0.714 MSE respectively), confirming that naive channel flattening loses structured cross-variable dependencies. On short-term PEMS traffic data, ConFlux dominates all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 0.071 vs. 0.117 MSE on PEMS07 vs. Chronos-2). Under fine-tuning, ConFlux extends its lead over Timer-XL by 30.7% average MSE, while univariate baselines like Chronos close the gap more than multivariate ones, suggesting in-domain temporal supervision partially compensates for lack of cross-variable modeling. In from-scratch training, ConFlux remains competitive with specialized models (DUET, CycleNet, SOFTS) but does not uniformly dominate, indicating its architecture is most advantageous in transfer and zero-shot regimes. On high-dimensional datasets (up to 10k+ variables), ConFlux is the only model that avoids out-of-memory failures on Solar and Traffic-CA while still achieving the best or second-best MSE, validating the scalability of variate patching.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConFlux builds directly on iTransformer's variate-as-token embedding and borrows the patching paradigm from Vision Transformers and PatchTST, but repurposes patching along the variable dimension rather than the temporal one. The variate sorting step draws on graph spectral theory (Fiedler vector of the graph Laplacian) to impose a locality-preserving ordering before patching, a step absent from prior multivariate architectures. On the foundation-model side, it departs from the dominant channel-independent or flattened-sequence strategies (Chronos, Timer-XL, Moirai) by maintaining an explicitly multivariate, patch-level attention mechanism, and replaces fixed-length decoders with a Fourier basis decoder for arbitrary-horizon prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing attention complexity from O(C²) to O((C/P)²), ConFlux makes purely multivariate zero-shot forecasting practical at scale (thousands of variables), a regime where prior foundation models either collapse to univariate processing or run out of memory. The clean theoretical link between Fiedler-vector sorting and optimal variable ordering provides a principled justification for the patching inductive bias. Open questions include whether the five fixed association measures generalize to domains with highly non-stationary or causal cross-variable dynamics, whether the Fourier decoder remains optimal for strongly non-periodic or event-driven signals, and how the approach scales when the number of variables far exceeds the 1000-variable training normalization.
