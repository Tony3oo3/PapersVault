## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is bottlenecked by domain-specific verifiers, and RLHF is limited by reward hacking, creating a severe data-wall for general reasoning. While Reinforcement Learning on Pretraining Data (RLPT) offers a self-supervised alternative, vanilla approaches (random or entropy-based token masking) fail on noisy real-world corpora due to low information density and training collapse. The paper asks whether stand-alone RL on raw pretraining data—without SFT, reward models, or synthetic CoT—can effectively elicit general reasoning from base models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw Wikipedia paragraphs (no QA pairs, no labels, no synthetic CoT) as the sole training signal and produces a base-model LLM (3–30B parameters) that generates chain-of-thought reasoning before answering. During training, each paragraph is split into two coupled sub-tasks: a mask-generation step that outputs a word span to hide, and a mask-prediction step that outputs a CoT followed by the recovered span. The overall prompt batch per RL step is 288 (32 generation prompts + 256 prediction prompts), with 8 rollouts each, and no KL regularization is applied.

## Method

PretrainZero formulates pretraining as a min-max bilevel RL problem over a shared LLM: a mask generator π_ω′ selects informative word spans from a paragraph, while a mask predictor ψ_ω recovers them via CoT reasoning. The generator is rewarded by the negative prediction accuracy under its own masks (i.e., it is incentivized to find spans that are challenging but not impossible—zero-accuracy masks receive zero reward), while the predictor is rewarded by exact match on the masked span. Both policies are optimized jointly each step using GRPO, where the generator's advantage is provably the negation of the predictor's advantage, making the update exactly consistent with the min-max objective. This active-learning mechanism replaces fixed heuristics (random or entropy-based masking) with an on-policy exploration of what to learn, allowing the model to progressively target harder, not-yet-mastered content as training proceeds. The approach requires no SFT cold-start, no reward model, and no synthetic data, operating purely on self-supervised exact-match rewards over the pretraining corpus.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B-Base, PretrainZero (2000 RLPT steps on Wikipedia) improves the base model by +8.43 on MMLU-Pro (51.94→60.37), +5.96 on SuperGPQA (26.32→32.28), and +10.60 on the 6-benchmark math average (42.53→53.13). After an additional 400-step general RLVR post-training, PretrainZero retains gains of +2.35, +3.04, and +2.81 on MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, and math average respectively over the base model with the same RLVR. The method generalizes across model scales: PretrainZero outperforms Random RLPT by 4.20, 1.82, 3.17, and 3.16 points on Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-MoE, and SmolLM3-3B base models respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PretrainZero consistently outperforms Random RLPT (the strongest RL baseline using random masked-span selection) across all four model sizes, with the largest gap on Qwen3-4B-Base (+5.26 math AVG, +5.16 MMLU-Pro, +3.18 SuperGPQA). Both RLPT methods substantially surpass Continue Pre-Training and Supervised Fine-Tuning, which actually degrade performance by 16.47 and 8.09 points (math AVG) on Qwen3-4B, confirming that self-supervised objectives on Wikipedia are insufficient for already-optimized base models. Against RPT (Dong et al., 2025) on the OmniMath post-training data (Appendix C, 400 steps, DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-7B), PretrainZero achieves 80.6 on MATH and 54.24 on MMLU-Pro versus RPT's 75.0 and 52.15, while RPT edges ahead on AMC23 (57.42 vs 56.17). The key differentiator is that PretrainZero operates fully self-supervised on noisy Wikipedia without SFT cold-start or reward models, whereas RPT relies on synthetic OmniMath with CoT annotations and a post-trained initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT, Dong et al. 2025), which first proposed next-token prediction as an RLVR objective on pretraining data, by replacing fixed token-selection heuristics (random or entropy-based) with a learned active mask-generation policy in a min-max bilevel RL formulation. It departs from RPT's reliance on synthetic OmniMath data and post-trained initialization by operating fully self-supervised on noisy real-world Wikipedia from base models, without SFT, reward models, or synthetic CoT. The work also positions itself against concurrent efforts (PRT, RLPT¹) that still incorporate auxiliary QA data or reward-model verifiers, arguing that stand-alone RLPT on raw pretraining corpus is the more fundamental and practical setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PretrainZero demonstrates that the verification data-wall limiting RLVR to narrow domains can be partially overcome by treating pretraining text itself as a self-supervised RL environment, with the active mask-generation mechanism addressing the low information density that causes vanilla RLPT to underperform or collapse. The finding that standard continued pretraining and SFT on Wikipedia actually degrade already-optimized base models while RLPT improves them suggests a qualitatively different learning signal from RL on pretraining data. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 30B parameters, whether it generalizes to non-Wikipedia pretraining corpora (code, multilingual), and whether the exact-match span-prediction reward can be extended to richer verification signals for more complex reasoning tasks.
