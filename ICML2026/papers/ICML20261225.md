## Problem

Diffusion-based 3D molecular generation applies a fixed Gaussian prior regardless of molecular size, but molecules of different sizes occupy different spatial scales. This causes larger molecules to stabilize their coarse structure earlier in the reverse process than smaller ones, producing size-dependent generation quality that existing variable-size architectures (GNNs, transformers) fail to address. The paper is the first to identify and formally analyze this size-induced inconsistency in denoising dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes the molecular size N (number of atoms) to look up a precomputed size-specific scaling factor γ_N, and optionally a target property value c for conditional generation. It outputs zero-mean 3D atomic coordinates in R^{(N-1)×3} (translation invariance enforced by centering) and one-hot atom type vectors in R^{N×d}. The key unusual aspect is that the prior distribution's variance is isotropic but size-dependent: coordinates use variance γ_N²σ_t² while atom features use the standard σ_t², making the forward process the only modification needed relative to standard diffusion.

## Method

Scaling the Prior (StP) rescales the prior Gaussian variance by a size-dependent factor γ_N, defined as the mean convex-hull-vertex distance of all training molecules of size N, normalized by the dataset-wide mean. Proposition 3.2 shows this is mathematically equivalent to normalizing coordinates by 1/γ_N before the forward process and unnormalizing after sampling, but unlike per-sample normalization, it preserves bond lengths and other chemically meaningful interatomic distances consistently across the dataset. The paper introduces two diagnostic metrics—shape alignment coefficient γ_t (radial projection of predicted clean coordinates onto the final target) and atom type alignment ratio β_t (fraction of atoms whose predicted type matches the final target)—to quantify how quickly the generation target converges across sizes. StP is a drop-in modification requiring no architectural changes: it is applied to EDM, RADM, and GeoLDM by simply replacing the standard prior variance with γ_N²σ_t² in the forward process, and the denoiser network and training objective remain unchanged.

## Results

On QM9, GeoLDM-StP achieves the best overall performance with 99.08% atom stability, 90.70% molecule stability, 95.41% validity, and 93.49% valid×unique, surpassing the SOTA flow-based baseline GeoBFN (95.31% validity, 92.96% valid×unique). EDM-StP improves molecule stability from 82.00% to 88.07% and validity from 91.90% to 94.41% over the original EDM. On GEOM-Drugs, GeoLDM-StP achieves the highest atom stability (86.78%) and RADM-StP achieves the highest validity (99.49%), both outperforming their respective baselines and all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLDM-StP outperforms the SOTA flow-based baseline GeoBFN on QM9 across validity (95.41% vs. 95.31%) and valid×unique (93.49% vs. 92.96%), while matching its atom stability (99.08%). EDM-StP dramatically improves over the original EDM, raising molecule stability by 6.07 points (82.00%→88.07%) and validity by 2.51 points (91.90%→94.41%), surpassing both GeoLDM and RADM on QM9 validity. RADM-StP shows modest gains over RADM on GEOM-Drugs (atom stability 85.00%→85.27%, validity 99.30%→99.49%) but slightly lower valid×unique on QM9 (91.51% vs. 91.70%), attributed to the transformer backbone overfitting. Against non-diffusion baselines, GeoLDM-StP dominates G-SchNet (95.70% atom stab) and ENF (85.00% atom stab) by wide margins, and also outperforms EDM-bridge and EquiFM on all QM9 metrics. On GEOM-Drugs, all StP variants improve over their originals, with GeoLDM-StP achieving the best atom stability (86.78%) and RADM-StP the best validity (99.49%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on three state-of-the-art 3D molecular diffusion backbones—EDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022), RADM (Ding & Hofmann, 2025), and GeoLDM (Xu et al., 2023)—which all handle variable-sized molecules via GNN or transformer architectures but apply an identical standard Gaussian prior regardless of molecular size. The key departure is identifying that molecular size sets the spatial scale of atomic coordinates, causing the same noise level to represent fundamentally different relative perturbations across sizes, and proposing to rescale the prior variance by a size-dependent factor (γ_N) rather than normalizing the data. This contrasts with per-sample normalization common in point cloud diffusion (Luo & Hu, 2021; Tyszkiewicz et al., 2023), which the paper shows distorts chemically critical bond lengths. The analysis draws on the frequency-domain interpretation of diffusion (Rissanen et al., 2023) to formalize the 'coarse-then-fine' generative trajectory and quantify its size-dependent inconsistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of size-induced inconsistency as a root cause of suboptimal generation quality—particularly the counterintuitive finding that larger molecules generate better despite scarcer training data—provides a diagnostic lens for the entire 3D molecular diffusion community. StP is a drop-in modification (no architectural changes) that consistently improves validity, stability, and structural fidelity across three backbones and two datasets, making it immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to flow-based models (preliminary ODE-sampling results are positive but limited), whether the residual size advantage of larger molecules under StP reflects a deeper architectural bias, and how StP interacts with structure-based drug design or LLM-integrated molecular workflows.
