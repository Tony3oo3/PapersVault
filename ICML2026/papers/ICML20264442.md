## Problem

In the average-reward setting, the Bellman operator is not contractive under any norm, so the Bellman equation has a non-unique solution and standard discounted-TD analysis tools break down. Prior convergence guarantees for average-reward TD carry persistent caveats—assumptions that exclude the tabular case, convergence only to a set rather than a point, explicit dimension dependence, or quartic scaling with the condition number—none of which appear in the discounted literature. This paper closes that gap by providing a unified finite-sample theory that simultaneously achieves all the desirable properties known for discounted TD.

## Input / Output

The double-chain algorithm consumes one state-reward pair (s_t, r_{s_t}, s_t') from a Markov chain evolving under the fixed policy π and an independent state sample \hat{s}_t drawn from the same chain, together with normalized feature vectors φ(s) (‖φ(s)‖ ≤ 1). It outputs a parameter vector θ_t that converges almost surely to a unique, sample-independent θ* solving the projected Bellman equation Φ^T D(I−ΠP)Φθ = Φ^T DΠR, from which the relative value function W* = Φθ* is recovered. The single-chain variant eliminates the second chain by maintaining an auxiliary variable w_t that tracks Φ^T μ, at the cost of a worse (quartic) condition-number dependence.

## Method

The key insight is to recast the average-reward Bellman equation W* = ΠT_π W* (where Π = I − eμ^T projects onto the zero-mean subspace) as a stochastic optimization problem whose objective is the composite quadratic ‖Φ(θ−θ*)‖_Dir² + (μ^T Φ(θ−θ*))². The double-chain update θ_{t+1} = θ_t + α_t[f(s_t,\hat{s}_t,θ_t) + g(s_t,s_t',θ_t)] is shown to be a gradient splitting of this objective in the sense of Liu & Olshevsky (2021), enabling a standard SGD-style descent recursion with the Dirichlet seminorm and the μ-weighted mean jointly providing the strong-convexity constant η_1. The two independent chains resolve the double-sampling issue inherent in estimating the μμ^T(R+W_t) term. A single-chain variant, inspired by GTD, replaces φ(\hat{s}_t) with a learned running average w_t ≈ Φ^T μ, trading the second chain for an additional coupled update. The resulting analysis yields quadratic η_1^{-2} sample complexity, no explicit dimension factor, last-iterate convergence to a unique point, and applicability to both tabular and linear function approximation without the e ∉ span(Φ) assumption.

## Results

The double-chain algorithm achieves a sample complexity of $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-1}\eta_1^{-2})$ with a $\tilde{O}(1/T)$ convergence rate, where $\eta_1$ is a condition number defined via the Dirichlet seminorm and stationary distribution. This quadratic dependence on the condition number matches the state-of-the-art for discounted TD (Bhandari et al., 2018), improving upon the quartic $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-1}\eta_2^{-4})$ scaling of prior average-reward results (Zhang et al., 2021b; Haque & Maguluri, 2024; Chen et al., 2025). The convergence bound has no explicit dimension-$d$ factors, applies uniformly to both tabular and linear function approximation settings, and holds for the last iterate. In numerical experiments on 15 tabular MDPs from OpenAI Gym and MO-Gymnasium, both the double-chain and single-chain algorithms outperform prior methods in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's double-chain algorithm simultaneously improves on all six prior baselines across multiple axes. Compared to Zhang et al. (2021b), Haque & Maguluri (2024), and Chen et al. (2025), which all exhibit quartic $\eta_2^{-4}$ condition-number scaling, the proposed method achieves quadratic $\eta_1^{-2}$ scaling, matching the discounted TD rate. Unlike Tsitsiklis & Van Roy (1999) and Li et al. (2024), the method does not require the $e \notin \text{span}(\Phi)$ assumption, making it applicable to the tabular case. Compared to Haque & Maguluri (2024), the convergence bound removes explicit dimension-$d$ dependence. Against Li et al. (2024), the result holds for the last iterate rather than the averaged iterate, and uses a simple single-timescale SA update without variance reduction. In the numerical experiments on 15 tabular MDPs, both the double-chain and single-chain algorithms outperform all prior methods in most settings, with the double-chain method showing the tightest approximation error to the true relative value function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the gradient splitting framework of Liu & Olshevsky (2021) to analyze average-reward TD learning, and draws algorithmic inspiration from the GTD method of Sutton et al. (2008) for its single-chain variant. It departs from the Tsitsiklis & Van Roy (1999) line of work by removing the restrictive Φθ ≠ ce assumption that excluded the tabular case, and from Zhang et al. (2021b), Haque & Maguluri (2024), and Chen et al. (2025) by achieving quadratic (rather than quartic) condition-number scaling without variance reduction. The key conceptual departure is formulating the projected Bellman equation as a gradient splitting of a composite objective (Dirichlet seminorm plus mean constraint), which enables a clean SGD-style analysis uniformly across tabular and linear approximation settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a persistent theoretical gap: average-reward TD learning has long lacked the clean finite-sample guarantees available in the discounted setting, with prior results carrying caveats (restrictive assumptions, quartic condition-number scaling, dimension dependence, or convergence only to sets). The practical implication for actor-critic and policy-gradient methods is significant—convergence to a unique, sample-independent point avoids unbounded drift along flat directions that complicates stopping rules and reproducibility. Open questions include whether the single-chain variant's quartic scaling can be improved, whether the gradient splitting perspective extends to off-policy or control settings, and whether the two-chain requirement can be eliminated without sacrificing the quadratic rate.
