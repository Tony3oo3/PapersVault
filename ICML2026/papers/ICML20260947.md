## Problem

The standard two-point zeroth-order gradient estimator has an O(r²) truncation bias that becomes a practical accuracy barrier when the smoothing radius r cannot be made arbitrarily small. Existing bias-reduction schemes (kernel-based, randomized telescoping) reduce this bias to higher order but inflate the leading directional variance constant under a fixed evaluation budget, while variance-reduction schemes leave the O(r²) bias unchanged. The paper asks whether truncation bias can be reduced to O(r^{2R+2}) without increasing the leading directional variance constant V.

## Input / Output

Given a point x ∈ R^n, a black-box function f accessible only through function evaluations, a base smoothing radius r, and an extrapolation order R, the method samples a single direction z from either Unif(S^{n-1}) or N(0, I_n/n) and queries f at 2(R+1) points {x ± (r/2^k) z} for k = 0, …, R. It returns a single gradient estimate g^{[R]} ∈ R^n. The key structural constraint is that the same direction z is shared across all radii, and the total evaluation cost per gradient estimate is 2(R+1) function calls (e.g., 6 for R=2).

## Method

Romberg-ZOGE evaluates the two-point central-difference estimator at a geometric sequence of radii r_k = r/2^k (k = 0, …, R) and forms a weighted linear combination g^{[R]} = Σ_k w_k^{(R)} g_{2p}(x; r_k, z). The weights {w_k^{(R)}} are the unique solution to a square Vandermonde system requiring Σ w_k = 1 and Σ w_k 4^{-jk} = 0 for j = 1, …, R, which cancels the first R even-order Taylor truncation terms pathwise. The critical design choice is reusing the same sampled direction z across all radii: this makes the leading directional term n⟨∇f(x), z⟩z shared across all summands, so its variance is V(Σ w_k)² = V rather than V Σ w_k², preserving the two-point estimator's leading variance constant. Under (2R+2)-order smoothness, the estimator achieves O(r^{2R+2}) truncation bias with leading directional variance V‖∇f(x)‖² + O(r^{2R+2}), and under a stochastic oracle the additional noise term scales as (n²σ²/2r²)·κ_R where κ_R = Σ(w_k^{(R)})²4^k quantifies the noise amplification from extrapolation.

## Results

On black-box prompt tuning of OPT-1.3B on SST-2 (B=12 function evaluations per iteration), Romberg-ZOGE with spherical directions achieves the lowest training loss (0.262) and highest test accuracy (91.2%), compared to Two-point (Sphere) at 0.531 loss / 78.7% test accuracy, P4 at 0.363 loss / 87.7% test accuracy, and Kernel at 0.875 loss / 55.2% test accuracy. On synthetic benchmarks (n=100), Romberg-ZOGE (R=2) achieves the predicted O(r⁶) truncation bias decay while matching the leading directional variance constant V of the two-point estimator, whereas Kernel and P4 exhibit substantially larger variance coefficients. Under the same 120 function evaluations per iteration in ZO-SGD, Romberg-ZOGE converges fastest on both Poly8 and Rosenbrock objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Romberg-ZOGE (R=2) consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the standard two-point central-difference estimator (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017), Romberg-ZOGE reduces training loss from 0.531 to 0.262 and improves test accuracy from 78.7% to 91.2% on SST-2, while also converging faster on synthetic Poly8 and Rosenbrock objectives under the same function-evaluation budget. Against the kernel-based estimator (Bach & Perchet, 2016), the gap is dramatic: Kernel achieves only 55.2% test accuracy and exhibits nearly flat bias and strong oscillations in the stochastic LLM setting, whereas Romberg-ZOGE reaches 91.2%. Against the unbiased P4 estimator (Ma & Huang, 2025b), Romberg-ZOGE wins on all three metrics (0.262 vs. 0.363 train loss; 91.2% vs. 87.7% test accuracy), with the advantage attributed to P4's larger directional variance coefficient. The shared-direction design is critical: an ablation using independent directions across radii inflates the leading variance by a factor of ~2.22, eroding the advantage over Two-point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard two-point central-difference estimator and its bias/variance analysis from Nesterov & Spokoiny (2017) and Duchi et al. (2015), which form the baseline for zeroth-order gradient estimation. It departs from prior bias-reduction methods (kernel-based estimators, randomized telescoping) that improve truncation bias at the cost of inflating the leading directional variance constant, and from variance-reduction methods (mini-batching, structured directions) that reduce variance but leave the O(r²) bias unchanged. The key conceptual departure is the shared-direction construction: by reusing a single perturbation direction z across a geometric sequence of radii, the estimator achieves pathwise cancellation of dominant directional randomness, enabling Romberg-extrapolation-style bias cancellation without the variance penalty that independent directions would incur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Romberg-ZOGE resolves a specific open question (Q1) in the ZOO literature: whether truncation bias can be reduced to O(r^{2R+2}) while preserving the leading directional variance constant under a fixed function-evaluation budget. This is practically relevant when the smoothing radius r is bounded away from zero by finite numerical resolution, making the O(r²) bias a hard accuracy ceiling. The main limitation is the noise-amplification factor κ_R/r² under independent noisy oracles, which constrains the usable extrapolation order R; the paper acknowledges that the shared-sample stochastic model (more natural for LLM fine-tuning) avoids this penalty but requires a different oracle assumption. Open questions include adaptive R selection under unknown noise levels and whether the local high-order smoothness assumption holds in practice for deep network objectives.
