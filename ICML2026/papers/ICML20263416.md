## Problem

KL regularization is widely used in modern RL and LLM alignment to incorporate prior knowledge and promote exploration, yet its theoretical sample-efficiency benefits in game-theoretic settings remain unexplored. Prior analyses under KL regularization in zero-sum games still yield O(√T) regret, matching the unregularized case, while single-agent results have already achieved logarithmic regret. This paper asks whether one can design online learning algorithms for competitive games that provably exploit KL regularization to achieve logarithmic regret in the number of episodes.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate under bandit feedback: the true payoff matrix (matrix games) or transition/reward model (Markov games) is unknown and accessed only through noisy oracle queries with sub-Gaussian noise. Inputs include the regularization strength β, fixed reference policies (μ_ref, ν_ref) encoding prior knowledge, and known feature vectors φ(s,i,j) with ‖φ‖₂ ≤ 1 under a linear function approximation assumption. The output is a sequence of policy pairs whose cumulative dual gap (total exploitability) is bounded by min{O~(d√T), O(β⁻¹d²log²(T/δ))} for matrix games and min{O~(d^{3/2}H³√T), O~(β⁻¹d³H⁷log²(T/δ))} for Markov games, yielding ε-NE sample complexities that scale as O~(β⁻¹/ε) rather than O~(1/ε²).

## Method

The key insight is that under KL regularization, the best response of a player to a fixed opponent strategy admits a closed-form Gibbs distribution, enabling a 'bandits view' where the opponent's strategy is treated as part of the environment. For matrix games, OMG collects best-response pairs under optimistic/pessimistic payoff matrices and bounds regret by the sum of *squared* bonuses (cβ⁻¹ΣE[b_t²]) rather than linear bonuses, circumventing Jensen's inequality and the √T term via the elliptical potential lemma. For Markov games, SOMG introduces a novel superoptimistic bonus b_h^sup = b_h + 2b_h^mse that exceeds the standard optimistic bound, necessary because positive KL terms in the min-player's objective make value functions unbounded above H. The algorithm maintains three Q/V function sets (MSE, superoptimistic for each player), solves stagewise KL-regularized zero-sum matrix games for the NE via policy extragradient (enjoying linear convergence vs. O(1/T) unregularized), and uses a projection ceiling of 3(H−h+1)² to preserve the superoptimistic gap property needed for the logarithmic regret proof.

## Results

For two-player zero-sum matrix games, OMG achieves a regularization-dependent regret of O(β⁻¹d²log²(T/δ)) and a regularization-independent regret of O~(d√T log(T/δ)), improving upon the prior O~(d√T) rate under KL regularization. For two-player zero-sum Markov games, SOMG achieves a regularization-dependent regret of O~(β⁻¹d³H⁷log²(dTH/δ)) alongside a standard O~(d^{3/2}H³√T) bound. In terms of sample complexity to learn an ε-NE, both methods improve from O~(1/ε²) (unregularized) to O~(β⁻¹/ε) under KL regularization, representing a strict improvement in the dependence on the target accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior baselines: Matrix-UCB (O'Donoghue et al., 2021) for unregularized matrix games and VMG (Yang et al., 2025a) for both matrix and Markov games. The key finding is that under KL regularization (β > 0), both OMG and SOMG achieve logarithmic regret O~(β⁻¹·log²(T)), whereas VMG maintains the same O~(d√T) regret as the unregularized case, offering no sample-efficiency gain from regularization. For matrix games, OMG matches VMG's O~(d√T) rate in the unregularized setting but strictly improves it to O(β⁻¹d²log²(T)) when β > 0. For Markov games, SOMG's O~(β⁻¹d³H⁷log²(T)) logarithmic bound improves upon VMG's O~(dH^{3/2}√T) under regularization, though SOMG has a worse dependence on H (H⁷ vs. H^{3/2}) in the logarithmic regime. In the sample complexity for learning an ε-NE, both methods improve from O~(1/ε²) to O~(β⁻¹/ε) under regularization, a strict improvement over all prior baselines which remain at O~(1/ε²).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent KL-regularized RL line of work (Zhao et al., 2025b; Tiapkin et al., 2024), which achieves logarithmic regret by exploiting the closed-form Gibbs optimal policy, to the game-theoretic setting where no such closed form exists for the Nash equilibrium. The key methodological departure is using best-response sampling (which does admit a closed-form Gibbs expression) rather than directly computing the NE, combined with a novel 'superoptimistic' bonus that handles the unbounded value functions introduced by positive KL terms in the min-player's objective. For the multi-agent framework, it builds on the value-incentivization approach of Yang et al. (2025a) and the UCB-style optimistic learning paradigm (Auer et al., 2002), while leveraging policy extragradient methods (Cen et al., 2024) for the inner NE computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish logarithmic regret (scaling as β^{-1}·log²(T)) for learning the NE in KL-regularized zero-sum games under bandit feedback, directly addressing a gap where prior game-theoretic analyses under KL regularization still maintained the unregularized O(√T) rate. The β^{-1} dependence has practical implications for LLM alignment via self-play (Nash Learning from Human Feedback, iterative DPO), suggesting that stronger KL anchoring to a pretrained reference policy provably reduces sample complexity. Open questions include instance/gap-dependent bounds, offline variants, extension to general multi-agent settings (CCE, general-sum), and whether the superoptimistic bonus overhead is necessary in practice. The empirical validation is limited to two small linear MDPs, leaving the practical relevance of the theoretical gains somewhat untested.
