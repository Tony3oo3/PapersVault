## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies frequently fail under distribution shift because they latch onto spurious visual correlations (background textures, lighting) rather than task-relevant causal features, yet existing interpretability tools—attention maps, latent probes, feature disentanglement—cannot distinguish whether a region is merely attended to or causally necessary for the predicted action. No prior metric quantifies the degree of causal misalignment in a VLA policy or links that misalignment to out-of-distribution generalization performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained VLA policy (π_0.5 with SigLIP visual encoder and PaliGemma backbone), multi-view RGB observations (front, overhead, wrist) tokenized into 16×16 grids per view, a text instruction, and ground-truth nuisance-region masks extracted from the RLBench simulator. It produces a per-timestep saliency stream (ISS heatmaps upsampled to image resolution via bilinear interpolation) and a scalar Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) at a chosen top-k threshold, computed as the fraction of the highest-scoring ISS tokens that fall within the nuisance partition. The ISS computation itself requires N=100 Monte Carlo forward passes per evaluated timestep, making it an offline diagnostic rather than a real-time signal.

## Method

The paper formulates visual-action attribution as a do-calculus interventional estimation problem: each visual token is ablated by replacing it with a modality-specific marginal mean (visual mean for image tokens, text mean for instruction tokens), and the causal significance of that token is the expected KL divergence between the original and interventional action distributions under teacher forcing. Because exact KL computation is intractable, the authors derive a fixed-denominator Monte Carlo estimator (unbiased, avoiding the finite-sample bias of ratio estimators) that uses Bernoulli masking with Gaussian-blurred replacement (RISE-style) and action MSE as a closed-form proxy for KL divergence, justified under the fixed isotropic Gaussian policy assumption. For spatial attribution, the token space is partitioned into action-critical, environmental-support, and nuisance regions via a Causal Markov Blanket argument, and the Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR@k) measures the fraction of top-k ISS tokens landing in the nuisance partition. Empirically, NMR@10 achieves a Pearson correlation of r = −0.77 with task success rate across 41 AGNOSTOS tasks, and ISS outperforms attention-score and token-norm baselines in both robustness (cosine similarity under Gaussian noise) and fidelity (correlation between saliency change and action deviation under texture, geometric, and patch perturbations).

## Results

The Nuisance Mass Ratio at top-10% (nmr@10) exhibits a strong negative Pearson correlation of r = −0.77 with task success rate across 41 AGNOSTOS tasks and 5 random seeds, establishing it as a predictive diagnostic for OOD generalization. In the fidelity study, ISS achieves Pearson correlations of 0.78, 0.64, and 0.72 with action MSE under texture, geometric, and patch perturbations respectively, substantially outperforming attention-score (ATT: 0.64, 0.49, 0.56) and token-norm (NORM: 0.47, 0.33, 0.40) baselines. In the robustness study, ISS occupies the optimal Pareto position (cosine similarity 0.995, action MSE 0.002), simultaneously maximizing saliency stability and minimizing action deviation. The underlying VLA policy π₀.₅ achieves 59.07 ± 0.58% success on seen tasks, 28.74 ± 1.97% on Level-1 unseen, and 16.96 ± 1.21% on Level-2 unseen tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks ISS against two single-pass interpretability baselines extracted from the SigLIP vision encoder: Attention Score (ATT), which sums attention weights received by each token across all heads in the final encoder block, and Token Norm (NORM), which uses the L2 magnitude of the final embedding. ISS consistently outperforms both baselines on the fidelity metric (Pearson correlation between saliency change and action MSE) across all three perturbation types, with the largest gap under geometric perturbation (0.64 vs. 0.49 for ATT and 0.33 for NORM). On the robustness Pareto front, ISS achieves the lowest action MSE (0.002) while maintaining the highest saliency cosine similarity (0.995), dominating ATT (MSE 0.959) and matching NORM's similarity (0.999) at a fraction of the action deviation. Qualitatively, ISS localizes saliency on the manipulator and target objects, whereas ATT and NORM exhibit severe spatial dispersion over nuisance background regions (e.g., ρ_nuis = 0.76 for NORM in the front view vs. much lower values for ISS). The key trade-off is computational: ISS requires N=100 Monte Carlo forward passes (~5.18 s, 0.19 Hz) versus a single inference for ATT/NORM (~0.079 s, 12.7 Hz), limiting ISS to offline post-hoc analysis rather than real-time diagnosis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RISE randomized masking procedure (Petsiuk et al., 2018) within a formal do-calculus framework, wrapping Bernoulli masking and Gaussian-blurred replacement in an interventional estimation problem grounded in Markov Blanket theory. It departs from the dominant VLA interpretability approaches—attention-weight visualization, latent-state probing, and feature disentanglement—by arguing that observational signals (attention scores, token norms) cannot establish causal necessity, and instead defines attribution through counterfactual policy divergence. The causal spatial partition (action-critical, support, nuisance regions) extends the Markov Blanket concept from Bayesian networks to the token space of vision encoders, providing a principled decomposition that prior saliency methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a simple, model-agnostic diagnostic (NMR) that predicts OOD generalization with a strong negative correlation (r = −0.77), giving practitioners a concrete signal for identifying causal misalignment without retraining. However, the 65× computational overhead (0.19 Hz vs. 12.7 Hz single inference) confines it to offline post-hoc analysis, the unimodal Gaussian policy assumption limits validity for multi-modal action distributions, and NMR accuracy depends on ground-truth nuisance masks that are unavailable in real-world deployment. Open questions include whether low-variance estimators or Jacobian-based approximations can bring ISS to near-real-time, whether the causal partition generalizes beyond simulation-provided segmentation, and whether the findings hold across VLA architectures beyond π₀.₅.
