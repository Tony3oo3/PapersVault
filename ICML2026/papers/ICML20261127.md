## Problem

Time series foundation models have been overwhelmingly developed for forecasting, leaving time series classification — despite its ubiquity across healthcare, finance, and activity recognition — without dedicated foundation models. Forecasting-oriented pre-training objectives (masked reconstruction, autoregressive modelling) are inherently misaligned with discriminative tasks, and no prior work has demonstrated that a lightweight, classification-specific TSFM can match or exceed strong per-dataset self-supervised baselines in a frozen zero-shot regime.

## Input / Output

The model accepts univariate or multivariate time series of arbitrary length, which are resized or padded to a fixed length of 512 (must be a multiple of 32 to yield exactly 32 tokens). For multivariate inputs, each channel is encoded independently and the resulting embeddings are concatenated before the classifier. The frozen encoder produces a 256-dimensional embedding per channel (expanded to 512 via output-token aggregation), which is then fed to a lightweight classifier (logistic regression or random forest) to yield a class label.

## Method

Mantis combines a novel Token Generator Unit — which fuses convolutional patch features of the instance-normalized signal, its first-order temporal difference, and patch-wise unnormalized statistics encoded via a Multi-Scaled Scalar Encoder — with a 6-layer Transformer backbone using RoPE, RMSNorm, and SwiGLU. Pre-training uses contrastive InfoNCE loss with Random Crop Resize augmentation on exclusively synthetic data generated by CauKer, avoiding data leakage and providing the high diversity needed for embedding uniformity. At test time, performance is further boosted by selecting intermediate-layer representations (layer 3), concatenating the [CLS] token with the mean of non-classification tokens, self-ensembling across multiple interpolation lengths and first-order differences, and optionally fusing embeddings with vision-based foundation models. The key insight is that contrastive objectives naturally align with classification separability, and that intermediate layers — not the final layer — unlock the scaling benefits of pre-training in the frozen-encoder setting.

## Results

On the full UCR benchmark (128 univariate datasets), Mantis achieves 81.95% zero-shot classification accuracy, outperforming the next-best models TiConvNext (80.29%), TiRex (80.13%), and Chronos2 (80.02%) by 1.7–2.0 percentage points. On the multivariate UEA-27 benchmark, Mantis reaches 74.20%, leading NuTime (71.99%) and TiConvNext (71.05%) by over 2 points. On the HAR collection (7 datasets), Mantis averages 75.62% versus 73.82% for NuTime and 69.93% for TiRex, while on EEG (11 datasets) it achieves 73.63% versus 72.60% for TiConvNext. Mantis is also the most lightweight model at 2.2M parameters after layer pruning, matching NuTime's size while outperforming all other foundation models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mantis outperforms all 10 competing methods on both UCR and UEA-27 in the zero-shot frozen-encoder setting. On UCR, the gap over the strongest forecasting baselines (TiRex 80.13%, Chronos2 80.02%) is ~2 points, and over the adapted vision models (TiConvNext 80.29%, TiViT-H 79.43%) it is 1.7–2.5 points. On UEA-27, Mantis leads by a wider margin: 2.2 points over NuTime (71.99%) and 3.2 points over TiConvNext (71.05%), suggesting stronger multivariate generalization. On HAR, Mantis (75.62%) beats NuTime (73.82%) by 1.8 points and TiRex (69.93%) by 5.7 points, with the largest individual gap on MP50 (73.45% vs. 69.13% for NuTime). On EEG, the advantage narrows to ~1 point over TiConvNext (72.60%) and Chronos2 (72.42%), consistent with EEG being a harder domain. Notably, Mantis also surpasses the strong hand-crafted Catch22+ baseline (79.69% on UCR, 69.63% on UEA-27) and the per-dataset self-supervised TS2Vec, which the authors note is the first zero-shot FM result to beat dataset-specific SSL on UCR-91.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mantis builds on the NuTime architecture (specifically its Multi-Scaled Scalar Encoder for patch-wise statistics) and the CauKer synthetic data generator, but departs from both in key ways: it replaces NuTime's self-distillation pre-training with contrastive InfoNCE learning and introduces a multi-view Token Generator Unit that fuses raw signal, first-order differential, and unnormalized statistics into a fixed set of 32 tokens. The paper positions itself against the dominant forecasting-oriented TSFM paradigm (MOMENT, TiRex, Chronos2) and argues that contrastive objectives are inherently better aligned with discriminative classification tasks. Its test-time optimization pipeline—intermediate-layer selection, self-ensembling via multi-length interpolation, and cross-model embedding fusion—draws on recent findings from vision-to-time-series transfer (Roschmann et al., 2025) and LLM test-time scaling, but applies them systematically to a lightweight, classification-specific architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 2.2M-parameter model pre-trained on 100K synthetic sequences can match or exceed per-dataset self-supervised methods (TS2Vec, T-Loss) in a frozen zero-shot regime, which is a strong efficiency result for practitioners with limited compute. The finding that intermediate layers unlock scaling benefits while final layers saturate is broadly applicable across TSFMs and suggests a general principle for leveraging pretrained models. Open questions include whether joint classification-forecasting foundation models are feasible, whether in-context learning can enable true zero-shot classification without any downstream labels, and how to improve interpretability and calibration for reliable unsupervised performance assessment.
