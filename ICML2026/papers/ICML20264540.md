## Problem

Prior convergence analyses of first-order methods either assume uniform smoothness (too restrictive for many ML objectives) or the (L0,L1)-NS condition bounding the Hessian by the gradient norm, which cannot model even logistic or exponential regression losses. This paper introduces the (H0,H1)-NS assumption—bounding the Hessian by an affine function of the objective value—and asks: what convergence rates do steepest descent, RMSProp, and Adam achieve under this assumption combined with a Lojasiewicz gradient-domination condition, and can we separate these methods from AdaGrad, AMSGrad, and plain GD?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a twice-differentiable, non-negative objective f: R^D → R satisfying (H0,H1)-NS for some dual norm pair (p,q) and a non-uniform Lojasiewicz condition, along with initial parameters θ₁ and a step-size schedule η_t. It produces dimension-free convergence rate guarantees (linear O(ln(1/ε)) or sub-linear O(1/ε^τ) depending on the regime relative to H0/H1) for the parameter sequence {θ_t}. A key structural detail is that the results hold for general (p,q) dual norms—not just Euclidean—yielding dimension-free rates for Sign GD (p=∞,q=1), Normalized GD (p=2,q=2), and Sign CD-GS (p=1,q=∞) simultaneously.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-phase convergence analysis built on three structural properties of (H0,H1)-NS functions: (i) a multiplicative Lipschitz bound on the function value, (ii) an additive Lipschitz bound on the gradient, and (iii) a multiplicative Lipschitz bound on the gradient norm. For steepest descent, these yield a descent lemma with a local smoothness constant L̄_t = H0 + H1·f(θ_t), and the two phases split at f = H0/H1: Phase 1 achieves linear convergence with O(1) step-size, while Phase 2 requires O(ε^τ) step-size for the sub-linear tail. For RMSProp and Adam, the multiplicative Lipschitz property on gradient norms is the key new ingredient: it bounds the ratio of consecutive gradient norms appearing in the preconditioner denominator, enabling a Cauchy-Schwarz lower bound on ⟨∇f, d_t⟩ that recovers the same two-phase structure. For Adam, the momentum term is controlled by showing ‖m_t − g_t‖₁/L̄_t contracts geometrically when β₁ ≤ β₂, absorbing the momentum contribution into the O(η²) smoothness term. Finally, a lower bound on the 1-D logistic loss shows that any method with a non-increasing effective step-size (including AdaGrad, AMSGrad, GD, and heavy-ball) is stuck at Ω(1/T), while RMSProp and Adam—which do not enforce monotone step-sizes—achieve O(exp(−T)).

## Results

Under (H₀,H₁)-NS and NL assumptions, NSD (including Sign GD and Normalized GD) achieves a dimension-free linear convergence rate of O(ln(1/ε)) when ε > H₀/H₁, and O(1/ε^τ) otherwise (Theorem 1). RMSProp and Adam achieve the same linear rate O(ln(1/ε)) in the first phase, with a slower O(1/ε^{2τ}) or O(1/ε^{4τ−1}) rate in the second phase (Theorems 3–4). On the 1D logistic loss, RMSProp and Adam achieve O(exp(−T)) convergence, while GD, heavy-ball momentum, AdaGrad, and AMSGrad are lower-bounded at Ω(1/T) (Theorem 6), establishing the first provable separation between adaptive gradient methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 1D logistic loss, RMSProp and Adam achieve O(exp(−T)) linear convergence, while AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011) and AMSGrad (Reddi et al., 2018) are lower-bounded at Ω(1/T) (Theorem 6), making this the first provable separation between adaptive gradient methods. For the softmax policy gradient objective, Sign GD and NSD achieve O(ln(1/ε)) iterations, provably faster than the Ω(1/ε) rate of constant-step-size GD (Mei et al., 2020). On logistic regression over separable data, NSD/Sign GD achieves a linear rate after O(n²/γ_p²) burn-in iterations, while large-step-size GD (Wu et al., 2024) only attains a sub-linear Θ(1/ε) rate. For the (L₀,L₁)-NS setting, the paper's diagonal Adam analysis (Theorem 5) yields O(1/ε) for large ε, improving upon the O(1/ε²) rate of the scalar/norm Adam variant in Wang et al. (2024a). Sign CD-GS matches the linear convergence rate of the normalized coordinate descent method in Axiotis & Sviridenko (2023) while using a simpler sign-based update rule.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the (H₀,H₁)-non-uniform smoothness framework of Vaswani & Harikandeh (2025) and Alimisis et al. (2025) from the Euclidean (p,q)=(2,2) setting to general induced (p,q) operator norms, enabling dimension-free convergence guarantees for steepest descent variants (Sign GD, Normalized GD, Sign CD-GS). It also subsumes the earlier (L₀,L₁)-NS assumption of Zhang et al. (2020b), under which the Hessian is bounded by the gradient norm, by showing that bounding the Hessian by the function value is strictly more general and closed under finite sums. The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for deterministic diagonal RMSProp and Adam under these assumptions, and the first provable separation between adaptive gradient methods (RMSProp/Adam vs. AdaGrad/AMSGrad) on a natural class of objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The separation result (Theorem 6) is the first to theoretically justify the practical dominance of RMSProp and Adam over AdaGrad and AMSGrad, showing that the non-increasing effective step-size property of the latter is fundamentally limiting on (0,H₁)-NS functions. The dimension-free linear convergence of Sign GD for logistic regression on separable data and softmax policy gradient provides a clean theoretical explanation for why these simple methods outperform constant-step GD in practice. Key limitations include the restriction to deterministic settings, the requirement of a Lojasiewicz/gradient-domination condition for the main theorems, and the separation result being established only for a one-dimensional logistic loss. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic variants, broader non-convex landscapes without gradient domination, and whether the (p,q) norm generalization yields practical algorithmic improvements beyond the theoretical dimension-freeness.
