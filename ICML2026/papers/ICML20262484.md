## Problem

In heterogeneous collaborative perception, existing protocol-based two-stage methods train modality-specific converters independently, causing each modality to learn a locally self-consistent but mutually inconsistent pseudo-protocol distribution in the shared protocol space. This leads to semantic inconsistency, spurious background activations, and error accumulation that are especially severe when modality gaps are large (e.g., LiDAR vs. camera). The root cause is that prior methods treat the protocol space as a mere distribution-alignment intermediary without explicitly disentangling shared semantic factors from modality-specific statistical confounders.

## Input / Output

The method consumes intermediate BEV feature maps (C×H×W) produced by heterogeneous encoders—LiDAR point clouds via PointPillar, SECOND, or VoxelNet at varying voxel sizes (0.1–0.4 m), and camera images via Lift-Splat-Shoot with ResNet or EfficientNet backbones—each with different resolutions and channel dimensions. The ego agent fuses its own local features with reconstructed collaborator features through a pyramid fusion module and outputs 3D object detection boxes. A key deployment constraint is that when adding a new modality, the encoders, fusion modules, and detection heads of previously deployed modalities remain frozen and inaccessible, satisfying privacy and non-modifiability requirements.

## Method

CauseCollab formulates protocol-space representation learning as a causal disentanglement problem via a Structural Causal Model that separates a true semantic factor S from modality-specific statistical confounders N_p and N_m. In Stage-1, a unified converter (Semantic Context Extractor + Context-Guided Dynamic Refiner with FiLM-style modulation and SE recalibration) is trained with multi-modality mixed batches, while a Mask-Guided Intervention via SPD Feature Geometry (MGI-SPD) module constructs counterfactual positive/negative pairs by applying closed-form whitening-coloring transforms on weighted SPD covariance matrices of BEV features, driving an InfoNCE contrastive loss to suppress modality-specific statistics while preserving spatial semantics. In Stage-2, modality-specific ConvNeXt-based reconstructors map protocol features back to each local space under task and consistency supervision. For new modality integration, the unified converter backbone is frozen and only low-rank bottleneck adapters (0.68 MB) plus a local reconstructor are trained, enabling expansion at minimal parameter cost.

## Results

On the OPV2V dataset (ego agent L_pp4, all four heterogeneous modalities), CauseCollab achieves AP@30 = 0.978 and AP@50 = 0.975, outperforming the strongest baseline STAMP (0.972/0.969) by 0.6% and 0.6% respectively. Under the larger modality-gap setting (C_Eff ego + C_Res neighbor), CauseCollab reaches AP@30 = 0.772 / AP@50 = 0.670, a 4.2% and 3.6% improvement over the next-best method. On the real-world DAIR-V2X dataset, CauseCollab attains 0.795/0.733 (L_pp4 + C_Eff) and 0.647/0.464 (C_Eff + L_pp4), substantially surpassing all baselines, especially in the camera-ego setting where it gains over 20% AP@30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CauseCollab outperforms all four baselines across every collaboration setting on both OPV2V and DAIR-V2X. Against the closest two-stage competitor STAMP (Gao et al., ICLR 2025), CauseCollab gains 0.6% AP@30 in the general ALL setting and a much larger 4.2% AP@30 in the high-modality-gap C_Eff + C_Res scenario, confirming that causal intervention is most beneficial when modality discrepancies are large. Compared to the other two-stage method PnPDA (Luo et al., ECCV 2024), CauseCollab leads by 0.8% AP@30 (ALL) and 11.8% AP@30 (C_Eff + C_Res). The one-stage methods MPDA (Xu et al., ICRA 2023) and HEAL (Lu et al., ICLR 2024) trail further behind, with HEAL also requiring 17.10 MB of parameters for new-modality adaptation versus only 0.68 MB for CauseCollab. On the real-world DAIR-V2X dataset, the gap widens dramatically for the camera-ego configuration (C_Eff + L_pp4), where CauseCollab achieves 0.647 AP@30 versus 0.394–0.410 for all baselines, indicating that causal semantic disentanglement is critical when the ego modality has the weakest standalone performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage protocol-space framework for heterogeneous collaborative perception established by PnPDA and STAMP, which map modality-specific features into a shared intermediate representation before local reconstruction. The key departure is reframing the protocol space from a reversible distribution-matching intermediary to a causal semantic extraction space: rather than learning modality-specific converters that independently align to the protocol distribution, CauseCollab explicitly models modality-specific statistical confounders via a structural causal model and suppresses them through counterfactual intervention. The intervention mechanism itself repurposes the whitening-coloring transform from style transfer (Li et al., 2017) and SPD matrix geometry (Huang & Gool, 2017), wrapping them in an InfoNCE-style causal metric learning objective with mask-guided spatial modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Heterogeneous collaborative perception is a practical bottleneck for real-world autonomous driving deployment where agents from different manufacturers use incompatible sensors and architectures; the lightweight adapter approach (0.68 MB for new modality integration) offers a concrete path toward incremental fleet upgrades without retraining. The causal framing, while conceptually appealing, remains somewhat post-hoc—the actual mechanism is statistical style suppression via SPD geometry plus contrastive loss, and the assumed SCM structure (single semantic factor + two noise confounders) is a simplification that may not capture all cross-modal interactions. Open questions include whether the causal disentanglement generalizes beyond the four-to-eight modality configurations tested, how the method behaves under truly novel sensor types (e.g., radar, mmWave), and whether the training-only MGI-SPD module can be replaced by an inference-time mechanism for online adaptation.
