## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) enables gradient-free, rapid adaptation for time series forecasting, but existing ICL-style methods (e.g., TabPFN-TS) operate on tabularized, hand-crafted features rather than raw sequences, while end-to-end sequence models (e.g., Chronos, TimesFM) lack any inference-time adaptation mechanism. No prior architecture unifies raw-sequence representation learning with ICL, leaving a gap between feature-engineered tabular ICL and sequence-native forecasting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D tensor of shape (C+1)×(T+H)×(M+1), where C context slices (retrieved via Y-space nearest-neighbor search on z-normalized lookback windows) and one target slice (with future values masked) are stacked; covariates X∈R^{(T+H)×M} are available for both history and forecast horizon. Temporal patching (P=8) and random Fourier features reduce the effective sequence length to S tokens per variable. The output is a full predictive distribution over K=5000 uniform bins in [−10,10] per horizon step, from which point forecasts (bin-center expectation) and arbitrary quantiles (empirical CDF) are extracted; at inference, 2D and 3D modes are ensembled with stochastic input perturbation (20% target masking, covariate shuffling) across 2–4 forward passes.

## Method

Baguan-TS is a 22.4M-parameter, 12-block encoder-only Transformer that factorizes attention across three axes of a (C+1)×S×(M+1)×D token tensor: temporal attention with RoPE captures within-episode dynamics, variable attention with learnable per-variable embeddings models cross-channel interactions, and context attention (no positional encoding) aligns support and query episodes. Contexts are constructed via Y-space retrieval-based forecasting (RBfcst), which selects nearest-neighbor lookback windows in target space rather than covariate space, making the calibration feature-agnostic and episode-specific. A context-overfitting training strategy—copying a short segment from one context slice into the query and adding an auxiliary self-retrieval loss—prevents the model from oversmoothing periodic spikes by explicitly teaching template matching. The prediction head is a lightweight MLP producing logits over 5000 bins, and the model supports a 2D inference mode (context length collapsed to 1) that, when ensembled with the full 3D mode, cancels complementary calibration biases (2D under-confident, 3D over-confident) to yield near-uniform PIT histograms.

## Results

On fev-bench-cov (30 covariate-aware tasks), Baguan-TS achieves the best average SQL (0.7997) and MASE (0.9857), reducing SQL by 4.8% over the closest covariate-aware baseline TabPFN-TS (0.8404). On 27 real-world energy and retail datasets, Baguan-TS leads all baselines on every metric, with SQL 0.3974 vs. TabPFN-TS's 0.4663 (−14.8%) and MASE 0.4956 vs. 0.6115 (−18.9%). The model also achieves the highest average win rate across all four metrics (SQL, MASE, WAPE, WQL) on the public benchmark, and its 2D+3D ensemble inference further improves calibration over either mode alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Baguan-TS outperforms all six non-covariate baselines (Chronos-Bolt, Moirai-2.0, Sundial-Base, TimesFM-2.5, TiRex, Toto-1.0) on all four metrics (SQL, MASE, WAPE, WQL) on fev-bench-cov, with the largest gaps over Sundial-Base (SQL 0.7997 vs. 1.0172) and Toto-1.0 (SQL 0.7997 vs. 0.9600). Against the only other covariate-aware baseline, TabPFN-TS, Baguan-TS wins on SQL (0.7997 vs. 0.8404, −4.8%) and MASE (0.9857 vs. 1.0430, −5.5%) but trails on WAPE (0.2173 vs. 0.2015) and WQL (0.1724 vs. 0.1643), indicating TabPFN-TS has a slight edge in percentage-error and quantile-loss calibration on the public benchmark. On the 27 real-world production datasets (Table 3), Baguan-TS reverses this and beats TabPFN-TS on all four metrics (SQL 0.3974 vs. 0.4663; MASE 0.4956 vs. 0.6115; WAPE 0.0597 vs. 0.0688; WQL 0.0490 vs. 0.0537), suggesting the advantage of raw-sequence ICL grows with covariate richness. The paper reports a 67% win rate over TabPFN-TS on the 30 public tasks (search-based ensemble) and 53% under a fixed-config ensemble, while achieving 80–100% win rates over the univariate baselines depending on the metric and dataset category.

## Relation to Prior Work

Baguan-TS sits at the intersection of two previously disconnected paradigms: end-to-end sequence models (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, TiRex) that learn directly from raw time series but lack inference-time adaptation, and tabular ICL approaches (TabPFN-TS, ForecastPFN) that perform gradient-free in-context learning but require hand-crafted feature engineering. The paper departs from both by performing ICL natively on raw multivariate sequences via a 3D Transformer that factorizes attention over temporal, variable, and context axes, and by replacing covariate-space (X-space) retrieval with target-space (Y-space) retrieval, which is feature-agnostic. The context-overfitting training strategy and the 2D+3D ensemble inference scheme are novel practical contributions not present in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that sequence-native ICL on raw time series with covariates is both feasible and competitive, closing a gap that has limited the adoption of ICL in forecasting to tabularized pipelines. The finding that Y-space retrieval consistently outperforms X-space retrieval is a practically useful, feature-agnostic principle for context construction. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long horizons or high-dimensional covariate spaces beyond M_max=80, whether the ensemble and validation-based configuration selection generalize to truly zero-shot deployment, and whether the 22.4M-parameter model can be further scaled without the calibration and oversmoothing issues the paper addresses. The proprietary 27-dataset evaluation limits full reproducibility of the real-world claims.
