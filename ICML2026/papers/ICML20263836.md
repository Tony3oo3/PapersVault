## Problem

Constrained decoding guarantees structural validity for LLM outputs (JSON, SQL, tool calls) but systematically degrades semantic correctness on reasoning tasks by 10–30%, because token-level masking and renormalization introduce a cumulative 'projection tax' when the model assigns low probability to valid continuations. This paper identifies the distortion mechanism and addresses the quality–validity tradeoff at inference time without relaxing the hard constraint.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language prompt and a hard structural constraint (JSON schema, context-free grammar, or first-order logic grammar) and produces a single structured output that is both semantically correct and strictly valid. The intermediate draft is unconstrained free-form text (a reasoning trace or plan), while the final output must satisfy the constraint exactly; the two stages can use the same model or a smaller projector model for the constrained realization step.

## Method

DCCD is a training-free two-step inference procedure: an unconstrained draft is first sampled to capture the semantic plan, then constrained decoding is applied conditioned on the draft to produce the final structured output. The key insight is that feasible mass (total probability on valid next tokens) is context-dependent—conditioning on a draft that contains the intended content makes schema-consistent tokens far more probable, so the subsequent masking introduces minimal distortion. The paper formalizes this via a KL-projection view, showing per-step distortion equals log(1/α(h_t)) and that draft conditioning reduces the cumulative projection tax. An optional best-of-K extension samples multiple drafts and selects the one with highest cumulative log feasible mass during the constrained pass.

## Results

Across six model scales (1B–14B) and four structured-generation benchmarks (GSM8K, GSM-Symbolic, MATH500, FOLIO), DCCD consistently achieves the highest strict structured accuracy (correct answer AND valid format). The largest gains appear at smaller scales: on GSM8K, a 1B model improves from 15.24% (CD) to 39.04% (DCCD), a +23.8-point gain, and a 1.5B model improves from 49.36% to 73.92%. At the 14B scale, DCCD still leads CD by 8.7 points on GSM8K (95.15% vs. 86.43%) and by 11.0 points on MATH500 (58.60% vs. 47.60%). DCCD also enables parameter-efficient compositions: a 7B+1.5B pair outperforms a single 14B model on GSM8K (10.7 vs. 6.1 accuracy per billion parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Constrained Decoding (CD) via XGrammar, which guarantees structural validity by masking invalid tokens at each step. DCCD outperforms CD in every model-size and dataset configuration: the gap ranges from +8.7 points (14B, GSM8K) to +23.8 points (1B, GSM8K), with the largest relative gains at smaller scales where feasible mass is lowest and the projection tax is most severe. On MATH500, DCCD beats CD by 13.8 points at 1B (19.80 vs. 6.00) and by 11.0 points at 14B (58.60 vs. 47.60). DCCD also dominates the prompt-based baselines (Constrained Prompting and Constrained Few-Shot), which achieve high answer accuracy but near-zero structural adherence on FOLIO and often lower accuracy than CD on math tasks. Notably, DCCD's two-model composition (e.g., 7B draft + 1.5B projector) surpasses a single 14B model under CD on GSM8K in accuracy-per-parameter (10.7 vs. 6.1), demonstrating that the method's gains are not merely a function of total parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on grammar-based constrained decoding systems (XGrammar, Outlines, PICARD) that enforce structural validity via token-level masking, and on recent empirical observations (Tam et al., Castillo, Schall & De Melo) that such constraints degrade reasoning accuracy by 10–30%. It departs from interleaved-reasoning frameworks like CRANE, which alternate between free-form and structured generation but reduce to standard CD when structure is needed from the first token, by always front-loading an unconstrained draft before any constraint is applied. The KL-projection / feasible-mass analysis formalizes why the distortion occurs and positions the two-stage draft-then-constrain procedure as a targeted remedy, while the comparison with thinking-token generation (Table 13) clarifies that the gain comes specifically from keeping the reasoning stage unconstrained rather than merely from having a reasoning phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Structured generation is a practical bottleneck for deploying LLMs in tool-calling, API, and agentic pipelines, and DCCD offers a training-free, drop-in inference procedure that substantially narrows the accuracy gap between constrained and unconstrained generation—especially for small models where the projection tax is most severe. The parameter-efficiency result (e.g., a 1.5B+1.5B DCCD pair outperforming an 8B single-model CD baseline) has direct cost implications for production systems. Key limitations remain: DCCD is bounded by draft quality (it rarely recovers from an incorrect draft, Table 9), incurs a ~13–45% latency overhead from the second pass, and the gains are most pronounced on reasoning-intensive tasks; for tasks where the model already assigns high probability to schema tokens, the benefit shrinks. Open questions include whether the approach composes well with RL-trained or reasoning-specialized models, how it behaves on very long structured outputs, and whether stronger draft-selection criteria (verifiers, judges) can close the remaining gap to unconstrained accuracy.
