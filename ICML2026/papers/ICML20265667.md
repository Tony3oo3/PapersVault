## Problem

Existing text-to-CAD systems treat user prompts as reliable, fully-specified geometric descriptions, but in practice natural-language prompts are frequently under-specified (missing critical dimensions) or internally inconsistent (conflicting constraints). Fine-tuned models reactively follow ambiguous instructions and hallucinate dimensions, leading to invalid or geometrically incorrect CAD programs. No prior work proactively detects and resolves specification errors before code synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265667/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language text prompt describing a 3D CAD shape (which may be ambiguous, under-specified, or contain conflicting dimensions) and optionally user clarification responses. The clarifying agent outputs a JSON decision: either a standardized prompt (if unambiguous) or a set of targeted clarification questions (if ambiguous). After receiving user answers, it produces a corrected, self-consistent text specification. The coding agent then translates this specification into an executable CadQuery Python program stored in variable 'r'. The interaction follows a two-round all-at-once protocol: all questions are asked in a single message, and the specification is finalized after one round of user answers.

## Method

ProCAD is a two-agent system where a proactive clarifying agent (ProCAD-clarifier) audits the prompt for missing or conflicting dimensions and asks minimal targeted questions, and a coding agent (ProCAD-coder) synthesizes executable CadQuery code from the resulting specification. Both agents are fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models: the coder via standard SFT on only 1.6K samples from a newly curated 10K text-to-CadQuery dataset (generated by prompting GPT-5-mini with multi-view renderings and CadQuery programs, then verified via leakage and completeness checks), and the clarifier via agentic SFT on 6,063 synthetic trajectories where ambiguous prompts are created by perturbing verified specifications to induce under-specification or conflicts. The clarifying agent is modeled as a finite-horizon MDP that either ACCEPTs the prompt or ASKs a set of questions in a single round, trained to reproduce JSON-formatted expert outputs for both question generation and corrected specification. A key design choice is the all-at-once questioning protocol, which the paper shows via ablation balances clarification completeness and interaction latency better than one-per-round or forced-extra-round alternatives.

## Results

On the 2,469-sample ambiguous test set, ProCAD (ProCAD-clarifier + ProCAD-coder) achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.63×10⁻³, median CD of 0.08×10⁻³, and an invalidity ratio of 0.9%, while also attaining the highest Efficiency (0.965) and Resolution (0.934) scores among all two-agent configurations. On the 1K unambiguous prompts, ProCAD-coder alone reduces the invalidity ratio from 86.9% (zero-shot Qwen2.5-7B) to 0.9% and achieves a median CD of 0.066×10⁻³, outperforming Claude Sonnet 4.5 (IR 12.9%, median CD 0.077×10⁻³) with only 1.6K training samples. The full agentic system reduces mean CD by 79.9% relative to Claude Sonnet 4.5 as a single-model baseline (7.80→0.63) and lowers the invalidity ratio from 14.6% to 0.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProCAD-coder (1.6K SFT samples) outperforms Claude Sonnet 4.5 on unambiguous prompts across all metrics: IR 0.9% vs 12.9%, mean CD 0.108 vs 1.580 (×10³), and median CD 0.066 vs 0.077 (×10³). Against the Text-CAD baseline (Kolodiazhnyi et al., 2025, which pairs Text2CAD prompts with CadQuery code), ProCAD-coder achieves 14× lower mean CD (0.108 vs 3.054) and 16× lower IR (0.9% vs 14.5%). The JSON-Distill baseline (Xie & Ju, 2025) is far worse, with mean CD 23.117 and median CD 8.808, confirming that data quality is a critical bottleneck. In the full agentic setting on ambiguous prompts, ProCAD-clarifier beats Claude Sonnet 4.5 as a clarifier by 4.3× in mean CD (0.63 vs 2.69) and 2.6× in IR (0.9% vs 2.3%), while also achieving higher Efficiency (0.965 vs 0.849) and Resolution (0.934 vs 0.912). Compared to PR-CAD (An et al., 2026) and CAD-Coder (Guan et al., 2025) reported in Appendix F, ProCAD-coder achieves substantially lower mean CD (0.108 vs 5.87 and 6.54) with only 1.6K training samples versus their 150K, though PR-CAD reports a marginally lower IR (0.62% vs 0.9%). The two-agent architecture itself provides a large gain over single-model baselines: even Claude Sonnet 4.5 as a single model (mean CD 7.80, IR 14.6%) is far outperformed by the ProCAD two-agent pipeline (mean CD 0.63, IR 0.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-shot text-to-CadQuery paradigm (Cadrille, CAD-Coder, Text2CadQuery) and post-hoc refinement approaches by introducing a proactive clarification stage before code generation, drawing conceptually on proactive agent research from task-oriented dialogue (Lu et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025). It also inverts the typical data-creation pipeline: rather than generating CadQuery code from Text2CAD's expert-level descriptions (which the authors show are verbose and contain misleading scaling operations), it starts from verified CadQuery programs and uses a frontier VLM to produce concise, human-like text descriptions. The agentic SFT training of the clarifier on synthetic ambiguity trajectories is a new application of multi-turn supervised fine-tuning to geometric specification repair, distinct from prior single-turn SFT or RL-based refinement in the CAD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 1.6K carefully curated samples with standard SFT can match or exceed systems trained on 150K+ samples with RL highlights that description quality is a critical bottleneck in text-to-CAD, a point prior work has underemphasized. The proactive clarification paradigm addresses a real practical gap—users rarely provide fully specified, consistent geometric constraints—but the current evaluation relies entirely on LLM-simulated users (GPT-5-mini, Claude Haiku), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to the messier, less cooperative behavior of real human annotators. Multi-ambiguity detection remains a clear weakness (77.1% success for k=2 vs. 98.2% for k=1), and the two-round all-at-once protocol, while efficient, may not scale to highly complex parts with many interdependent constraints. The released 10K dataset and code provide a useful resource for the community, but the narrow focus on CadQuery and 7B open-source models limits immediate transferability to other CAD representations or larger model families.
