## Problem

Model merging via checkpoint averaging is widely adopted in LLM pre-training, yet the geometric mechanism by which averaging transforms chaotic optimization trajectories into coherent descent paths remains unexplained. Existing work attributes gains to static local flatness, overlooking the dynamic trajectory geometry and the question of whether the merged path can be exploited to go beyond simple interpolation within the convex hull of observed weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266204/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding window of K consecutive merged checkpoints (each computed as a uniform average of N raw checkpoints saved every τ optimizer steps) along with a validation set for loss evaluation. It produces a single extrapolated checkpoint that lies outside the convex hull of the observed merged states. The extrapolation step size is adaptive—set as a fixed fraction (α=0.1) of the projected coordinate displacement between the last two merged checkpoints—and the 1D line search terminates immediately when validation loss stops improving relative to the anchor merged checkpoint.

## Method

Extra-Merge is a training-free algorithm that exploits the Rank-1 Subspace phenomenon: late-stage merged checkpoints concentrate on an approximately one-dimensional linear manifold, with the first principal component explaining >94% of variance across model scales from 124M to 2B. The procedure has two steps: (1) PCA on K consecutive merged checkpoints (computed efficiently via a K×K Gram matrix since K≪d) to extract the dominant direction, oriented to point toward descent; (2) an adaptive greedy 1D line search along this direction, stepping outward from the latest merged checkpoint until validation loss degrades. Theoretically, under a river-valley loss landscape, uniform averaging acts as a geometric low-pass filter that suppresses high-curvature mountain oscillations by a factor of 1/N, collapsing the trajectory onto the flat river direction, which PCA then recovers with a provable alignment bound. Unlike standard PMA/LAWA which interpolates within the convex hull of past checkpoints, Extra-Merge extrapolates beyond it, effectively performing a training-free descent step along the rectified trajectory without any additional gradient updates.

## Results

On Pythia-12B zero-shot downstream evaluation, Extra-Merge achieves an average accuracy of 57.47%, improving +0.59% over the raw checkpoint (56.88%) and +0.40% over the best PMA variant (57.07%). The largest gains appear on ARC-Challenge (+1.10% over raw) and ARC-Easy (+0.84% over raw). Across pre-training validation loss on GPT-2 (124M–1.55B, WSD scheduler) and LLaMA (0.5B–2B, Cosine scheduler), Extra-Merge consistently reaches lower loss than both the raw trajectory and PMA, with the gap widening during the learning-rate decay phase where PMA gradually converges back to the raw baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PMA (Pre-trained Model Averaging, Li et al. 2025), evaluated in both Uniform and EMA (α=0.1, 0.2) variants. Extra-Merge outperforms all PMA variants on every Pythia-12B benchmark: it gains +0.55% average over PMA-Uniform (57.47 vs. 57.07) and +0.53% over the best EMA variant (57.47 vs. 56.94). On pre-training validation loss (Figures 6 and 7), PMA initially reduces loss relative to the raw model during the high-LR phase but gradually aligns with the raw baseline as the learning rate decays, whereas Extra-Merge maintains a sustained loss reduction throughout the decay phase across all model scales (GPT-2 124M/355M/1.55B and LLaMA 0.5B/2B). The advantage is also preserved under the Muon optimizer (Figure 7), where Extra-Merge again outperforms PMA on GPT-2 Small. Notably, PMA-Uniform consistently outperforms PMA-EMA in this setting, confirming the finding of Li et al. (2025) that uniform weights are preferable for LLM pre-training merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-merging-in-pre-training line (Polyak averaging, SWA, LAWA, PMA) by reframing the problem geometrically: rather than treating merged checkpoints as static interpolation points within a convex hull, it identifies a Rank-1 Subspace structure in the merged trajectory and extrapolates beyond it. The theoretical grounding draws on the river-valley loss landscape framework (Wen et al., 2024), formalizing averaging as a geometric low-pass filter that suppresses high-curvature mountain oscillations. The key conceptual departure from prior merging work is the shift from static flatness arguments (averaging finds a wider minimum) to dynamic trajectory geometry (averaging rectifies a chaotic path onto a 1D descent manifold), enabling extrapolation rather than mere interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Extra-Merge offers a simple, training-free post-hoc improvement to LLM pre-training that requires no additional gradient steps, which is practically attractive given the enormous cost of pre-training runs. The Rank-1 Subspace characterization provides a new geometric lens for understanding why and when merging works, with concrete hyperparameter guidance (window size N, interval τ, PCA window K) derived from the signal-to-noise analysis. However, the practical gains are modest (+0.59% average on Pythia-12B, small validation loss reductions), and the theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions (straight river, quadratic mountains, stationary noise) that may not hold in all regimes. Open questions include how the subspace reorients under distribution shift and whether subspace-aware optimization can actively exploit this structure during training rather than only post-hoc.
