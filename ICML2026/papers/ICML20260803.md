## Problem

Standard contrastive learning produces highly entangled, uninterpretable representations where semantic concepts are distributed holistically across all dimensions. While Non-Negative Contrastive Learning (NCL) improves interpretability by aligning dimensions with semantic clusters, it suffers from an Optimization Conflict: shared high-frequency background features (e.g., 'blue sky') are simultaneously pulled together in positive pairs and pushed apart in negative pairs, causing gradient oscillation that prevents clean disentanglement.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces a gated non-negative feature vector of dimension K (256 for CIFAR-10, 256 for CIFAR-100, 2048 for ImageNet-100). Unlike standard CL outputs, the feature vector is element-wise multiplied by a learned binary mask, so many dimensions are exactly zero, yielding a sparse representation where each active dimension corresponds to a specific semantic attribute. The gated features are used directly for linear probing, top-K feature-selection retrieval, and per-dimension interpretability scoring.

## Method

BayesNCL augments NCL's non-negative ResNet encoder with a lightweight 2-layer MLP Gating Head that predicts per-dimension Bernoulli parameters α = σ(g_θ(x)). A Straight-Through Estimator produces a hard binary mask m_hard = I(α > 0.5) for the forward pass (z_gated = z ⊙ m_hard) while routing gradients through the continuous α during backpropagation. The training objective is L_total = E_{m~q_θ}[L_InfoNCE(z⊙m)] + λ·D_KL(q_θ(m|x) ‖ Bern(ρ)), where the KL term enforces a sparse Bernoulli prior that acts as an Inverse-Prevalence Filter: Theorem 4.5 proves that high-prevalence features (π_k → 1) are automatically gated out because their alignment gain vanishes relative to the fixed sparsity cost. This resolves the optimization conflict by strictly zeroing out conflict-inducing background dimensions rather than merely down-weighting them, which ablations confirm is essential for interpretability gains.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, BayesNCL achieves a Semantic Consistency score of 36.14, a 142.1% relative improvement over NCL (14.93) and a 3514% improvement over standard CL (1.00). On CIFAR-100, BayesNCL reaches 22.02 consistency versus 9.91 for NCL. Crucially, these interpretability gains come without sacrificing downstream performance: BayesNCL attains 70.44% Top-1 linear probe accuracy on ImageNet-100, surpassing both NCL (69.63%) and SimCLR (68.31%), and 88.02% on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CL (SimCLR), BayesNCL dominates on every interpretability metric: on ImageNet-100, Semantic Consistency jumps from 1.00 to 36.14, and Hf drops from 4.61 to 2.37, while also improving linear probe accuracy from 68.31% to 70.44%. Against NCL (the direct non-negative baseline), BayesNCL achieves a 142.1% relative gain in Semantic Consistency on ImageNet-100 (36.14 vs. 14.93) and a 122% gain on CIFAR-100 (22.02 vs. 9.91), while maintaining or slightly exceeding NCL's downstream accuracy (70.44% vs. 69.63% on ImageNet-100). The NCL+TopK ablation, which applies deterministic hard thresholding, performs comparably to plain NCL (14.27 vs. 14.93 on ImageNet-100), confirming that the probabilistic gating mechanism—not mere sparsity—is responsible for the gains. On the Waterbirds background classification task, BayesNCL matches NCL (93.38% vs. 93.33%), showing that suppressing high-frequency features does not discard coherent background information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Non-Negative Contrastive Learning (NCL) by Wang et al. (2024), which established the theoretical equivalence between non-negative contrastive objectives and NMF to yield interpretable, semantically-aligned feature dimensions. The key conceptual departure is identifying an 'Optimization Conflict' inherent in NCL's deterministic similarity measures: in compositional scenes, shared high-frequency features (e.g., 'blue sky') are simultaneously pulled toward alignment (positive pairs) and repulsion (negative pairs), causing gradient oscillation that prevents clean disentanglement. BayesNCL resolves this by introducing a variational Bayesian gating head with a sparse Bernoulli prior, which dynamically suppresses conflict-inducing dimensions via a Straight-Through Estimator, effectively approximating an Inverse-Prevalence Weighted Similarity derived from latent class models. The approach also connects to the broader variational inference literature (VAE-style ELBO optimization) and information-theoretic bottleneck principles, but applies them specifically to the feature-selection problem in contrastive representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in self-supervised learning: standard contrastive representations are opaque 'black boxes' that are problematic for safety-critical deployment, and the prior interpretable approach (NCL) still suffers from semantic entanglement in compositional data. The 142.1% relative improvement in semantic consistency on ImageNet-100, combined with maintained or improved downstream accuracy, suggests that interpretability and representational power need not be traded off. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-18/50 backbones and three datasets (two of which are small-scale), leaving open whether the gating mechanism transfers to transformer architectures, full ImageNet, or dense/pixel-level contrastive settings. The finding that strict binary gating is essential—soft down-weighting catastrophically fails—raises an interesting open question about whether similar hard-thresholding principles apply to other discrete selection problems in representation learning.
