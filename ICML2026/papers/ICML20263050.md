## Problem

For NP-hard problems like Correlation Clustering and Max-Cut, practical heuristics routinely outperform pessimistic worst-case approximation guarantees, but certifying this requires efficient lower bounds on the optimal solution. The highest-quality bounds come from LP relaxations, yet black-box solvers like Gurobi time out on graphs with millions of edges. The paper asks whether near-optimal LP solutions can be computed in near-linear time, enabling scalable instance-specific approximation certificates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a signed graph (for CC) or unsigned graph (for Max-Cut) along with a set of triangles (bad triangles for CC, all triangles for Max-Cut), and produces a feasible dual LP solution that serves as a lower bound on the MINETCOVER optimum. This bound is lifted to CC and Max-Cut via Lemma 1, yielding instance-specific approximation ratios by comparing against heuristic solutions (e.g., SCMLEvo for CC, local search for Max-Cut). The MWU algorithm accepts an accuracy parameter ε ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.5} and runs in O(αm · ρ log²(n)/ε²) time, where α is the graph's arboricity, making it near-linear on sparse real-world graphs.

## Method

The core contribution is an MWU algorithm (Algorithm 1) that approximates the dual of the MINETCOVER packing LP by maintaining edge weights and calling a bounded-width oracle each iteration. The key design choice is that the oracle (GREEDYBOUNDEDTRIANGLEPACKING) selects an entire edge-disjoint triangle packing per iteration—sorting triangles by weighted cost and greedily adding them under a budget constraint—rather than updating a single triangle as in Fischer et al. (2025). This batched update strategy reduces the number of MWU iterations to O(ρ log(m)/ε²) and makes the algorithm orders of magnitude faster in practice despite matching asymptotic runtime. A second, simpler lower bound (Algorithm 2) uses greedy triangle packing with a preprocessing sort step, achieving bounds within 9.5% of the LP optimum at near-linear cost. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) is established by proving the oracle satisfies bounded-width, weighted-budget, and near-optimality properties (Lemma 3), then applying the Plotkin-Shmoys-Tardos MWU framework.

## Results

For Correlation Clustering (CC), the proposed MWU (ε=0.05) and GreedyPacking methods certify that the SCMLEvo heuristic achieves an average instance-specific approximation ratio of 0.941 for the agreement version and 1.976 for the disagreement version across 7 signed graphs, substantially beating the trivial bound (0.887) and worst-case O(log n) hardness. For MAX CUT, MWU (ε=0.05) certifies an average ratio of 0.891 across 9 unsigned graphs, surpassing the Goemans-Williamson SDP guarantee of 0.878. For MINETCOVER, MWU (ε=0.05) achieves an average ratio of 1.179, meaning a simple greedy upper bound is within 18% of optimal on average. On the largest instance (wikiConflict, >2M edges), the agreement ratio reaches 0.992 and disagreement ratio 1.146, indicating near-optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Balkanski et al. (2021), both MWU and GreedyPacking substantially outperform on all problems: for CC disagreement, GreedyPacking achieves 1.976 vs. Balkanski's 2.739 (28% improvement); for CC agreement, MWU reaches 0.941 vs. 0.924; for MINETCOVER, MWU (ε=0.05) achieves 1.179 vs. 1.810; and for MAX CUT, MWU achieves 0.891 vs. 0.839. Against Fischer et al. (2025) (MWU-SU), the proposed MWU is orders of magnitude faster in practice: MWU-SU times out on 6 of 9 MINETCOVER instances and 1 of 7 CC instances even at ε=0.5, while MWU finishes in under a minute on all instances; where both complete, MWU yields slightly tighter bounds (e.g., 1.992 vs. 1.976 for CC disagreement average, though GreedyPacking edges out both). Against Veldt (2022)'s unsorted greedy (compared on Cluster Editing in Table 5), the proposed GreedyPacking with sorting improves the average ratio from 2.326 to 2.263 (a 2.7% improvement), and MWU (ε=0.05) further improves to 2.280. Gurobi (exact LP) naturally provides the tightest bounds where it finishes, but times out on the largest instances (wikiConflict for CC, and 7/9 for MINETCOVER), making the near-linear MWU the only scalable method matching near-optimal quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Plotkin et al. (1995) multiplicative weights update framework for packing-covering LPs, adapting it to the triangle-edge incidence structure of MINETCOVER with a bounded-width oracle that updates an entire triangle packing per iteration. It addresses a specific gap identified by Veldt (2022): the LP relaxation provides the tightest known lower bounds for correlation clustering, but black-box solvers like Gurobi do not scale to large instances. The key departure from the most closely related work (Fischer et al., 2025, which also applies MWU to this LP) is the batched weight-update strategy, which reduces the number of iterations from linear to polylogarithmic in practice. The greedy triangle packing algorithm (Algorithm 2) is a minimal but effective refinement of Veldt's unsorted greedy, adding a cost-based sorting step that yields up to 9.5% improvement in bound quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and theoretically grounded way to certify the quality of heuristic solutions for CC and MAX CUT on real-world graphs, bridging a substantial theory-practice gap: worst-case hardness gives O(log n) for CC disagreement, yet empirical certificates show ratios of ~1.97 (disagreement) and ~0.94 (agreement) across 7 datasets. The near-linear-time MWU algorithm makes LP-quality bounds computable on graphs with millions of edges in minutes, where Gurobi times out after 2 hours. Open questions include whether integrality-gap arguments can yield bounds beyond the LP dual, and whether the MWU framework can be repurposed to improve the heuristic solutions themselves. More broadly, the instance-specific certificate paradigm demonstrated here could extend to other NP-hard graph problems where worst-case guarantees are far from empirical performance.
