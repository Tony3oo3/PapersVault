## Problem

Existing expressive TTS systems struggle to faithfully render composite natural-language instructions (e.g., 'Happy but slightly Arrogant') because they learn a single-point feed-forward mapping from text to acoustics, which collapses multi-modal target distributions into mode-averaged, under-expressed outputs. The paper identifies two compounding failure modes: systematic semantic-acoustic misalignment (target attributes under-activated by 25–45% while non-target dimensions receive spurious activation) and speaker-emotion entanglement in latent space, which forces an identity-versus-prosody trade-off under joint constraints.

## Input / Output

The system takes a composite natural-language instruction (specifying multiple fine-grained emotional/stylistic attributes with relative intensities) and a reference speaker audio clip as input, and produces a 24 kHz expressive speech waveform as output. The instruction is internally decomposed into a discrete intensity vector over K emotion dimensions and a set of cue-focused retrieval queries; the reference audio provides identity conditioning through the disentangled speaker latent. The full pipeline is retrieval-augmented, drawing from a curated 100-hour acoustic prototype library, and the slow-loop MLLM critique adds at most 200 ms of inference latency.

## Method

AgentSteerTTS is a three-stage decouple–anchor–refine closed loop. First, an Adversarial Disentanglement Module (ADM) factorizes a reference utterance into identity (z_id) and emotion (z_emo) subspaces using bidirectional gradient-reversal discriminators plus a batch cross-covariance penalty, trained jointly with a Mel-spectrogram reconstruction loss. Second, a Dual-Stream Anchoring Controller (DAC) grounds the abstract intent in continuous acoustic space: a Retrieval Agent selects the most expressive prototype from the library via embedding search, a Consistency Calibration step crops the temporally most relevant slice, and a Synthesis Agent fuses the acoustic anchor (interpolated with the speaker's neutral state) and a speaker-adaptive text embedding through a gated, intensity-weighted combination. Third, a Fast-Slow Feedback Agent refines the result at inference: a Fast Agent runs a differentiable Latent Consistency Predictor to calibrate a scalar intensity parameter α via a few gradient steps before vocoding, while a Slow Agent invokes an MLLM supervisor on the generated waveform to detect residual semantic drift and trigger either α re-calibration or full DAC re-anchoring.

## Results

On the composite-instruction benchmark, AgentSteerTTS (Gemini3 backend) achieves E-SIM = 0.955, S-SIM = 0.841, WER = 1.34%, and QMOS = 4.38, outperforming the strongest baseline IndexTTS2 (E-SIM = 0.864, S-SIM = 0.823, WER = 1.81%, QMOS = 4.15). On the ESD benchmark, AgentSteerTTS (Qwen3 backend) attains the highest ESMOS (4.42), SNMOS (4.52), and SSMOS (4.31) among all synthesized systems, with the lowest MCD (5.815) and WER (7.12) close to ground truth (6.98). The composite success rate (CSR) reaches 0.78 with the full system, compared to 0.49 for the text-only ablation, confirming the importance of acoustic anchoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentSteerTTS decisively outperforms all baselines on the primary emotion-alignment metric E-SIM: 0.955 (Gemini3) vs. 0.864 for IndexTTS2, 0.842 for IndexTTS, 0.748 for CosyVoice2, and 0.685 for VALL-E — a gap of 0.091 over the strongest competitor. It also leads on speaker similarity (S-SIM 0.841 vs. 0.823 for IndexTTS2) and intelligibility (WER 1.34% vs. 1.81% for IndexTTS2). On the ESD benchmark, AgentSteerTTS (Qwen3) achieves the best ESMOS (4.42 vs. 4.37 for UMETTS-FastSpeech), SNMOS (4.52 vs. 4.29), SSMOS (4.31 vs. 4.13), and lowest MCD (5.815 vs. 6.703). The one metric where AgentSteerTTS trails is DMOS (3.82 vs. 4.24 for IndexTTS2), which the authors attribute to a naturalness–expressiveness tradeoff: stronger target-attribute realization makes prosody more marked under strict composite constraints. Overall, the method wins on 6 of 7 composite-benchmark metrics and 5 of 7 ESD metrics, with the tradeoff concentrated in perceived instruction-following smoothness (DMOS) rather than objective alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentSteerTTS composes three established ideas—adversarial disentanglement via gradient reversal (from VITS-style TTS), retrieval-augmented conditioning (borrowed from RAG paradigms in NLP), and fast-slow iterative refinement (from LLM self-refine)—into a unified closed-loop architecture for expressive speech synthesis. It departs from prior emotional TTS systems (CosyVoice, IndexTTS, Spark-TTS) that treat expressive control as a single feed-forward text-to-waveform mapping, by explicitly modeling the composite-instruction problem as a constrained optimization over a multimodal acoustic control space. The theoretical framing in Section 2 (mode-averaging under multimodal conditionals, speaker-prosody entanglement) provides a principled diagnosis that prior systems address only implicitly through data curation or preference optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic identification of 'attribute dilution'—target-dimension suppression coupled with non-target leakage—as a structural rather than stochastic failure mode gives the field a concrete diagnostic for composite-instruction TTS, and the 14-hour composite benchmark is a useful resource for future work. However, the retrieval-augmented design (100h prototype library) limits zero-shot generalizability, the slow loop's dependence on external MLLM evaluators introduces a practical bottleneck and potential overconfidence on out-of-distribution prompts, and ADM targets only speaker-emotion coupling rather than full acoustic factorization. Open questions include whether the framework scales to truly open-vocabulary composite styles beyond the 7-emotion space, and whether the closed-loop feedback can be internalized to remove the external evaluator dependency.
