## Problem

In multi-batch long-context speculative decoding, the target-model verification phase accounts for up to 87.5% of decoding latency because loading the full KV cache dominates. Existing KV compression strategies are sub-optimal here: static eviction suffers accuracy loss from saliency shift, while dynamic per-step rescoring adds prohibitive overhead on the verification path. The paper asks whether one can identify a small, high-fidelity subset of KV tokens for verification without either permanently discarding context or paying full-attention scoring cost at every step.

## Input / Output

Dustin consumes three signals per verification step: (1) the target model's historical attention from the last generated token, (2) the draft model's lookahead attention over Γ speculative tokens (Γ=3–4), and (3) the full context KV cache of N tokens. It outputs a fixed-budget verification set I_verify (k=512 tokens by default) structured as protected attention sinks + recent window, then top-m tokens ranked by draft signal, then remaining budget filled by target signal. The verification set is layer-invariant (one global index set shared across all target layers), and the importance scoring itself is restricted to a handful of Semantic Retrieval Heads per selected layer, reducing estimation overhead to ~0.8% of a full hybrid attention computation.

## Method

Dustin's core mechanism is a hybrid attention aggregation that sums normalized attention scores across heads, layers, and draft tokens to produce two global importance vectors (S_Draft and S_Target), then applies a tiered Top-K selection under a fixed KV budget. The key empirical insight motivating this design is that draft-lookahead attention is unreliable as a standalone signal for intermediate-scale targets (14B/32B) and degrades in deeper layers, while target-historical attention is stable short-term but cannot capture multi-step saliency shifts; the two signals are complementary across both model scale and network depth. To keep online estimation cheap, Dustin restricts scoring to Semantic Retrieval Heads (SRHs) identified via offline profiling (following CompressKV), so only a few heads in a few layers are materialized per step. A one-time offline configuration search (Algorithm 1) uses greedy layer selection followed by Bayesian optimization (Optuna) on the draft/target budget split m, with Attention Recovery Rate (ARR) as the objective, and the resulting configuration is reused across all downstream tasks without recalibration.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-72B at 32K context with batch size 16, Dustin achieves a 9.17× end-to-end decoding speedup (235.81 tokens/s) and a 27.85× self-attention speedup, compared to 1.89× for ClassicSD and 1.96× for MagicDec. On LongBench accuracy under a strict 512-token KV budget, Dustin attains 55.23 average on Qwen2.5-72B (vs. 55.81 lossless) and 53.23 on Llama-3.3-70B (vs. 50.94 lossless), with only a 0.59% average accuracy drop across 16 tasks. The speedup advantage grows with both context length and batch size, reaching 7.81× on Qwen2.5-32B and 7.18× on Llama-3.3-70B at 32K/batch-16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LongBench accuracy (512-token KV budget, Qwen2.5-72B), Dustin (55.23 avg) nearly matches lossless SD (55.81) while substantially outperforming StreamingLLM (35.25), Quest (47.69), and SnapKV (51.90); the gap widens at the tighter 128-token budget where Dustin retains 52.41 versus 32.72 (StreamingLLM), 37.01 (Quest), and 47.08 (SnapKV). On efficiency, Dustin's 9.17× speedup at 32K/batch-16 on Qwen2.5-72B dwarfs ClassicSD (1.89×) and MagicDec (1.96×), and also exceeds SpecAttn (7.11×) by a 2.06× margin. Against SmallKV on Qwen2.5-7B, Dustin shows smaller accuracy drops in 4 of 5 LongBench categories (e.g., −0.33 vs. −5.9 on Single-doc QA). The hybrid design (Dustin-H) also outperforms its single-source ablations (Dustin-T, Dustin-D) on TriviaQA (83.63% vs. 77.58%/80.51%) and MultiNews (25.19% vs. 23.95%/24.84%), confirming that neither historical nor lookahead attention alone is sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dustin sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; MagicDec, Sadhukhan et al., 2024) and KV-cache compression (Quest, Tang et al., 2024; SnapKV, Li et al., 2024), specifically targeting the target-model verification bottleneck that dominates long-context multi-batch inference. It departs from prior sparse-verification work (SpecAttn, Shah 2025) by fusing target-historical attention with draft-lookahead attention into a single global importance map, rather than relying on draft-model signals alone or per-layer scoring. The Semantic Retrieval Heads (SRH) mechanism is borrowed from CompressKV (Lin et al., 2025) but repurposed here as a lightweight online estimator for token selection, reducing the scoring overhead to ~0.8% of full hybrid attention computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9.17× end-to-end decoding speedup at 32K context (vs. ~1.9× for MagicDec) addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying long-context LLMs in multi-batch serving, where KV-cache loading dominates latency. Two limitations remain open: the budget-allocation parameter m is fixed via offline profiling rather than adapted per-input, and Dustin reduces computation but not the KV-cache memory footprint, so it does not extend the maximum context length that fits in a given GPU. The non-monotonic reliability of draft-lookahead signals across model scales (reliable at 7B/72B but unreliable at 14B/32B) suggests that cross-model attention alignment is a deeper, less-understood phenomenon that future work should investigate.
