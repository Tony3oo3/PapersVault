## Problem

Existing cognitive map models inspired by the hippocampal-entorhinal circuit struggle to scale to real-world visual scenes, failing to demonstrate how abstract relational structures can be extracted directly from high-dimensional video without explicit action labels or dynamics priors. This paper asks how a model can simultaneously learn concrete sensory content and extract reusable abstract structures from observation-only sequences, and how those structures enable generalization across novel objects and environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265868/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 RGB video clips (sequence length 8) with no action annotations, processing them through a frozen pretrained multi-scale VQ-VAE encoder to obtain 16×16 spatial feature maps that serve as observation embeddings. It produces predicted next frames (one-step and autoregressive rollouts) decoded back to pixel space, alongside three internal representations: 8192-dim HPC embeddings encoding content-specific episodic information, 4096-dim MEC embeddings encoding abstract transition structures, and 2048-dim latent transitions capturing compressed, content-free dynamics. All prediction and alignment losses operate in latent space (JEPA-style), not at the pixel level.

## Method

The architecture is a hierarchical HPC-MEC coupling model where a spatial-temporal Transformer HPC layer (8192-dim) encodes content-rich observations, and a lower-dimensional MEC layer (4096-dim) extracts abstract structures. The MEC implements CANN-inspired path integration: an inverse model infers a low-dimensional latent transition z_t from the phase difference between consecutive MEC embeddings, and a forward MLP combines z_t with the current MEC state to produce a displacement vector that phase-shifts the CANN activity bump to yield the next state. This transition-centric design (predicting Δg rather than the full next state) forces latent transitions to encode purely content-independent dynamics. Training proceeds in three self-supervised stages—reconstruction and alignment, inverse/forward dynamics learning, and joint autoregressive finetuning—with a visual feedback mechanism that corrects accumulated path integration drift by replacing the generated MEC state with the inferred one when sensory input is available. The key architectural insight is that the HPC-MEC separation, combined with CANN dynamics, prevents texture leakage and enables zero-shot transfer of latent transitions to novel objects and scenes.

## Results

On the in-domain SSv2 dataset, the model achieves the best LPIPS (0.274 one-step, 0.356 autoregressive) among all compared methods, with competitive SSIM (0.752/0.687). On the out-of-distribution COIL-100 dataset, it achieves the highest SSIM (0.837 one-step, 0.814 autoregressive) and lowest LPIPS (0.226/0.270) across all models. On Franka Kitchen, both Moto and AdaWorld LAM fail to produce meaningful predictions, while the proposed model maintains SSIM of 0.705 (one-step) and 0.551 (autoregressive) with LPIPS of 0.253/0.426. For latent transition decoding on unseen 3D objects, the model achieves 90.6% accuracy, outperforming all baselines (best baseline: LAPA at 82.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three state-of-the-art latent action models: LAPA, Moto, and AdaWorld (LAM). On the in-domain SSv2 dataset, the proposed model achieves the best LPIPS (0.274 one-step, 0.356 autoregressive) while AdaWorld LAM leads on one-step SSIM (0.763 vs. 0.752); however, the proposed model's autoregressive SSIM (0.687) substantially exceeds all baselines (best: LAPA at 0.659). On the OOD COIL-100 dataset, the proposed model dominates all metrics, achieving SSIM of 0.837/0.814 and LPIPS of 0.226/0.270, outperforming the next-best AdaWorld LAM (0.778/0.715 SSIM, 0.312/0.415 LPIPS) by a wide margin. On Franka Kitchen, both Moto and AdaWorld LAM completely fail to generate meaningful dynamics (degenerating into static predictions), while the proposed model maintains robust performance (SSIM 0.705/0.551, LPIPS 0.253/0.426), surpassing LAPA (0.690/0.532 SSIM, 0.389/0.529 LPIPS). For latent transition decoding accuracy on unseen 3D objects, the proposed model achieves 90.6%, outperforming LAPA (82.6%), Moto (71.9%), and AdaWorld LAM (64.0%), confirming that the hierarchical HPC-MEC architecture extracts more semantically meaningful and content-free transition representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: neuroscience-inspired cognitive map models (TEM, CSCG, Vector-HaSH) that formalize HPC-MEC functional division but remain limited to discrete or low-dimensional domains, and latent action world models (Genie, LAPA, Moto, AdaWorld) that infer transitions from observation-only video but operate in unified, content-entangled latent spaces. The key departure is combining the hierarchical HPC-MEC separation with CANN-based path integration dynamics, applied to continuous real-world video (SSv2) rather than 2D games or simple stimuli. The inverse model's operation on MEC embedding differences (rather than raw observations) and the CANN's transition-centric update rule (predicting Δg rather than full next-state) are the architectural innovations that enable content-free structural abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a concrete computational instantiation of how the brain might extract reusable abstract structures from high-dimensional sensory streams, bridging a gap between theoretical neuroscience and scalable deep learning. The demonstrated structural generalization—transferring latent transitions across objects, scenes, and even domains (human video → 3D objects → robot simulation)—suggests a path toward world models that learn dynamics independent of appearance. Key limitations remain: autoregressive compounding errors degrade long-horizon predictions, performance drops sharply on highly artificial environments (Push-T, Block Pushing), and coordinating multiple independent entities is unaddressed. Future directions include hierarchical HPC-MEC structures, object-centric representations, and memory banks to mitigate drift.
