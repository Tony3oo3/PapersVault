## Problem

Deterministic climate models produce single-point forecasts that cannot represent the inherent uncertainty of the climate system, while existing generative approaches (GANs, diffusion) treat the climate as a monolithic stochastic process, overlooking critical multi-scale spatiotemporal correlations such as teleconnections and energy cascades from large-scale modes to regional anomalies. Adapting visual autoregressive models to climate forecasting further faces two unresolved challenges: aligning heterogeneous simulation and real-world data in a shared token space, and effectively conditioning generation on high-dimensional, multi-variable climatic states.

## Input / Output

The model takes a monthly-averaged climate state on a 1°×1° global grid with 34 channels (4 atmospheric variables at 4 pressure levels, 6 surface variables, and 2 oceanic variables at 6 depth levels) and produces a probabilistic ensemble of the next monthly state by sampling 200 members from the autoregressive token distribution. Long-term forecasts are obtained by iterative single-step autoregression, where each forecast becomes the input for the subsequent step. Missing oceanic values over land are filled with the global mean and masked during training.

## Method

ClimateAR combines a multi-scale VQ tokenizer with a decoder-only autoregressive transformer. The tokenizer uses segmented codebooks (8 codebooks of size 4096, partitioning the latent feature into N segments to boost effective capacity to V^N) and a shallow-separation/deep-sharing architecture—domain-specific convolutional layers at the encoder/decoder boundaries with shared deep layers and codebooks—to align CMIP6 simulation tokens with ERA5/ORAS5 real-world tokens. The AR transformer generates K=10 scale token maps sequentially, conditioned at each step on an intra-scale mixed token (concatenation of the autoregressive reconstruction and the conditional feature at that scale) and a hybrid-scale prompt (a learnable query sequence that cross-attends over all conditional tokens to compress global multi-scale context into a short prefix). Training proceeds in three stages: VQ-VAE pretraining on mixed data, AR pretraining on CMIP6 with cross-entropy over codebook indices, and AR fine-tuning on ERA5/ORAS5, with noise-augmented teacher forcing (stochastic token replacement at rate p=0.3) to reduce exposure bias.

## Results

On the ERA5/ORAS5 test set (2005–2014), ClimateAR achieves an average ACC improvement of 37.56% over the strongest data-driven baseline across 12 variables and 1–10 month lead times, with an average CRPS reduction of 23.33% and RMSE reduction of 9.52%. At 6-month lead, ClimateAR reaches ACC of 0.291 on z500, 0.218 on SST, and 0.184 on t2m, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (ClimaX: 0.232, 0.187, 0.135 respectively). In zero-shot transfer (CMIP6-pretrained only, no ERA5 fine-tuning), ClimateAR still outperforms all data-driven baselines on both RMSE and ACC for z500, t2m, pr, and psl.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClimateAR outperforms all five baselines (Pangu, GraphCast, Oneforecast, ClimaX, and the physics-based DWD system) on ACC across every variable and lead time evaluated. The largest margins appear on ENSO-sensitive variables: at 6-month lead, ClimateAR's t2m ACC (0.184) exceeds Pangu (0.056) by 3.3× and GraphCast (0.035) by 5.3×, while its z500 ACC (0.291) surpasses ClimaX (0.232) by 25% and Pangu (0.194) by 50%. Among the data-driven baselines, ClimaX is the closest competitor on SST and z500 ACC, while GraphCast and Pangu are strongest on RMSE for upper-level variables; however, ClimateAR still leads on RMSE for all 12 variables at 6-month lead. On the probabilistic CRPS metric, ClimateAR reduces the score by 23.33% on average versus the best deterministic baseline, with the largest gap on t2m (0.939 vs. ClimaX's 1.457 at 6-month lead). The physics-based DWD system is competitive at 1–2 month leads on z500 and psl but degrades rapidly beyond 4 months, whereas ClimateAR maintains skill through 10 months.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClimateAR adapts the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) paradigm—originally designed for multi-scale image tokenization and next-scale prediction—to probabilistic climate forecasting, a domain previously dominated by GAN-based (cDCGAN) and diffusion-based (GenCast) generative models that treat the atmosphere as a monolithic stochastic process. It inherits the CMIP6-pretrain → ERA5-fine-tune transfer paradigm established by ClimaX but introduces a cross-domain aligned tokenizer (shallow-separation, deep-sharing) to bridge the simulation-to-reanalysis distribution gap, and a hybrid-scale conditioning mechanism that departs from the low-dimensional text-prompt conditioning typical of generative models. The method is positioned against deterministic deep-learning forecasters (Pangu, GraphCast, Oneforecast, ClimaX) and the DWD physics-based seasonal system, with the key conceptual departure being the explicit multi-scale autoregressive factorization of the joint climate distribution rather than a single-step regression or score-matching objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing climate forecasting as a multi-scale discrete token generation problem, ClimateAR provides a principled route to ensemble forecasts that capture irreducible uncertainty (e.g., ENSO amplitude) without the mode-averaging pitfalls of deterministic models or the training instability of GANs. The 37.56% average ACC gain and the ablation showing the intra-scale mixed token is near-critical (z500 ACC drops from 0.331 to 0.152 without it) suggest that explicit cross-scale conditioning is a previously underexploited lever for long-range skill. Open questions include whether the approach scales to sub-monthly or higher-resolution settings, the computational cost of sampling 200 ensemble members for operational use, and how performance compares to GenCast (excluded here due to retraining cost), which remains the strongest published probabilistic baseline for weather-scale tasks.
