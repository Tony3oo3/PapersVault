## Problem

Diffusion posterior sampling conditions pretrained diffusion priors on measurements via likelihood-driven data-consistency updates, but these updates are typically scaled by hand-tuned scalar guidance weights that are brittle under stiff, operator-dependent curvature and suffer from a coordinate mismatch between clean-space likelihood and noisy diffusion-state updates. No existing method explicitly combines coordinate-correct likelihood geometry, direction-wise curvature scaling, and diffusion-calibrated step control within a single per-noise-level correction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy measurements y, a (possibly nonlinear) forward operator A, and a pretrained diffusion model (EDM-style pixel-space denoiser or LDM-VQ4 latent-space denoiser) to produce reconstructed images at 256×256 resolution. In latent space, the forward model is composed with a decoder (A∘D) so that measurements remain in pixel space while sampling occurs in latent coordinates. The data-consistency scale σ_n is set to 0.01 as a calibration parameter rather than an estimate of physical measurement noise, and the method is entirely training-free, requiring only a fixed number of diffusion steps T and GMRES iterations K per task.

## Method

CLAMP replaces scalar guidance with a per-noise-level damped Gauss-Newton correction computed in diffusion-state coordinates. The likelihood gradient is pulled back through the denoiser via J_F^T to ensure coordinate correctness, while a one-sided curvature operator B_t = σ_n^{-2} J_F^T J_A^T J_A (dropping the rightmost J_F) avoids expensive forward denoiser-Jacobian products while retaining the pullback. Step control is provided by a rank-one anisotropic damping metric H_t = λ_id I + u_t u_t^T aligned with the denoiser residual direction, with a diffusion-calibrated strength α_t = (‖d_t‖/‖a_t‖)·σ_prior^{-2} that automatically balances prior and likelihood magnitudes across noise levels. The resulting nonsymmetric linear system is solved with a fixed-budget matrix-free GMRES using only JVP/VJP actions via automatic differentiation. After each correction, the state advances via a variance-preserving Langevin transition whose drift and injected-noise coefficients are determined in closed form by variance matching and a fluctuation-dissipation coupling, eliminating the need for an explicit stochasticity parameter.

## Results

On pixel-space FFHQ (256×256), CLAMP achieves leading or near-leading PSNR across all eight inverse-problem tasks (e.g., 32.96 dB on random inpainting, 31.64 dB on motion deblurring, 29.96 dB on nonlinear deblurring) while running in 6–34 s per image—up to 4.1× faster than SITCOM and 10–50× faster than DMPlug. On accelerated MRI (SKM-TEA), CLAMP attains the best PSNR/SSIM among all compared diffusion-based baselines: 34.05 dB / 0.834 at ×4 and 32.27 dB / 0.766 at ×8 acceleration. In latent space, CLAMP matches or exceeds LatentDAPS quality while being ~2.4× faster and ~9× faster than ReSample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAMP outperforms DAPS (Zhang et al., 2025) on 7 of 8 pixel-space FFHQ tasks in PSNR (e.g., +2.88 dB on random inpainting, +2.33 dB on box inpainting) and is 3–22× faster (6–34 s vs. 22–756 s). Against SITCOM (Alkhouri et al., 2025), CLAMP wins on 6 of 8 tasks in PSNR and is up to 4.1× faster on motion deblurring (6.3 s vs. 26.2 s); SITCOM edges out CLAMP only on Gaussian deblurring PSNR (29.36 vs. 29.01) and motion deblurring LPIPS (0.167 vs. 0.183). CLAMP dominates DMPlug (Wang et al., 2024) on every task in both quality and speed, with run-times 10–50× lower (e.g., 6.2 s vs. 122 s on random inpainting). DCDP (Li et al., 2024) is the fastest baseline on linear tasks (~5 s) but lags CLAMP by 2–6 dB PSNR on most tasks and degrades severely on motion deblurring (25.47 vs. 31.64 dB). On MRI reconstruction, CLAMP achieves 34.05/32.27 dB PSNR at ×4/×8, surpassing Score-Med (32.21/30.29), DDS (32.07/30.03), DAPS (30.20/28.19), and DPS (25.46/25.40) by 1.8–8.6 dB. In latent space, CLAMP beats LatentDAPS on PSNR for 7 of 8 tasks (e.g., +5.4 dB on box inpainting) while running ~2.4× faster, and outperforms ReSample on all tasks with a ~9× speedup (e.g., 35 s vs. 308 s on random inpainting). PSLD (Rout et al., 2023) is consistently the weakest latent baseline, trailing CLAMP by 4–6 dB PSNR across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion posterior sampling (DPS, Chung et al. 2023), which interleaves diffusion prior steps with scalar-weighted likelihood corrections, by replacing the scalar guidance with a per-noise-level damped Gauss-Newton correction computed in diffusion-state coordinates. The key conceptual departure is the denoiser pullback: rather than applying clean-space gradients directly, CLAMP pulls measurement sensitivity back through the denoiser Jacobian (J_F^⊤) to ensure coordinate correctness, and uses a one-sided curvature model that avoids forward denoiser-Jacobian products. The damping metric is rank-one and aligned with the denoiser residual direction, and the schedule transition uses a closed-form variance-preserving Langevin split derived from a fluctuation-dissipation coupling, drawing on classical numerical optimization (Levenberg-Marquardt, GMRES) in a way that prior diffusion-posterior-sampling methods (DAPS, SITCOM, DMPlug, DCDP) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLAMP reduces the hand-tuning burden of diffusion posterior sampling by replacing per-task scalar guidance schedules with automatically calibrated, geometry-aware step control, while achieving competitive or superior reconstruction quality at substantially lower runtime (e.g., ~4× faster than SITCOM on pixel-space motion deblurring, ~9× faster than ReSample in latent space). The extension to accelerated MRI reconstruction demonstrates applicability beyond natural images. Open questions include whether the one-sided curvature approximation (J_F ≈ I in the forward pass) remains valid for very deep or highly anisotropic denoisers, whether the fixed GMRES budget can be made adaptive without sacrificing stability, and how the method scales to 3D volumetric or video inverse problems where Jacobian actions become prohibitively expensive.
