## Problem

Integrating Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models (UDM) for text-to-image generation remains unexplored. Naively adapting the Flow-GRPO formulation to UDM causes severe training instability—reward oscillation and exploding KL divergence after ~500 steps—due to two misalignments: intermediate noisy predictions used as actions provide inaccurate optimization signals, and optimizing on the backward denoising trajectory induces a distribution shift away from the forward process used during pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via a 1.7B-parameter URSA uniform discrete diffusion model that operates on a grid of discrete tokens (vocabulary size K, spatial dimensions H×W). During RL fine-tuning, group sampling generates 8 candidate images per prompt (16 groups per batch), and a terminal reward is computed on the final clean sample. The model is fine-tuned with 10 inference steps for group sampling and 25 steps for evaluation, using a constant learning rate of 1e-6 on 32 A100 GPUs.

## Method

UDM-GRPO resolves the instability of naive GRPO adaptation through two core design choices: (1) redefining the policy action at every timestep as the final clean sample x̂₁ rather than the intermediate prediction x₁ᵗ, so the optimization signal is reward-consistent and avoids learning from high-entropy early-step predictions; and (2) reconstructing training trajectories by perturbing x̂₁ through the forward diffusion process (X_forward) instead of using the model's own backward denoising states (X_backward), which eliminates the out-of-distribution shift relative to pretraining. The GRPO clipped policy-ratio objective is retained, with the ratio reformulated as p_θ(x̂₁|x̂_t, c)/p_θ_old(x̂₁|x̂_t, c). Two efficiency strategies further accelerate training: Reduced-Step optimization randomly selects three consecutive timesteps from the first half of the diffusion schedule to concentrate gradients where stochasticity is highest, and CFG-Free training removes classifier-free guidance to halve the optimization objective. Together these changes yield stable convergence, improving GenEval from 69% to 96% and PickScore from 20.46 to 23.81 on the URSA base model.

## Results

UDM-GRPO boosts the 1.7B-parameter URSA base model from 0.69 to 0.96 overall GenEval accuracy, surpassing the best prior RL method (SD3.5-M + Flow-GRPO at 0.95) and all discrete baselines (best prior: FUDOKI at 0.77). On PickScore, the model improves from 21.79 to 23.81, exceeding SD3.5-L (22.91) and FLUX.1-Dev (22.84). On the OCR text-rendering benchmark, accuracy rises from 8% to 57%, though it still trails SD3.5-L (68%) and FLUX.1-Dev (59%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDM-GRPO (0.96 GenEval) outperforms every discrete baseline, including FUDOKI (0.77), Emu3.5/DiDA (0.86), and Show-o + Mask-GRPO (0.73), and also surpasses the strongest continuous RL method, SD3.5-M + Flow-GRPO (0.95), by 0.01. Against non-RL continuous models, it beats SD3.5-L (0.71), FLUX.1-Dev (0.66), and SANA-1.5 4.8B (0.81) by a wide margin. On PickScore, UDM-GRPO (23.81) exceeds SD3.5-L (22.91) and FLUX.1-Dev (22.84), establishing a new state-of-the-art for both discrete and continuous settings. On OCR, UDM-GRPO (0.57) improves dramatically over the URSA base (0.08) but still lags behind SD3.5-L (0.68) and FLUX.1-Dev (0.59), indicating text rendering remains a harder challenge. The method also generalizes to the FUDOKI MLLM, improving its GenEval from 0.76 to 0.86 and PickScore from 21.32 to 23.40.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow-GRPO framework (Liu et al., 2025), which formulated continuous flow-matching denoising as a policy optimization problem, to the Uniform Discrete Diffusion (UDM) setting. It inherits the MDP structure from DDPO (Black et al., 2023) and the group-relative advantage computation from GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), but departs from both by redefining the action as the final clean sample rather than intermediate denoised states, and by reconstructing training trajectories via the forward diffusion process instead of the reverse sampling path. The key conceptual departure from Flow-GRPO is recognizing that the standard per-timestep action formulation, which works for continuous models, induces training collapse in discrete diffusion due to high-entropy early predictions and OOD trajectory drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDM is an increasingly important generative paradigm (URSA, FUDOKI, Next-Omni), yet RL-based alignment was previously unavailable for it; this paper provides the first stable and efficient recipe, with practical techniques (final-sample action, forward-trajectory reconstruction, reduced-step optimization, CFG-free training) that are simple to implement and likely transferable to other discrete generative models. The substantial gains (GenEval 69%→96%, OCR 8%→57%) demonstrate that discrete diffusion models can match or exceed continuous counterparts on compositional and preference-alignment tasks after RL. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video generation, multi-reward optimization, and whether the forward-trajectory trick generalizes to other discrete diffusion variants with different corruption schedules.
