## Problem

Standard preference alignment (DPO, RLHF) assumes a definitive binary preference exists for every response pair, yet many pairs fall below the labeler's discriminative resolution limit, making forced choices unreliable and noise-injecting. The paper shows this contamination is pervasive—even GPT-4 and human annotators produce indistinguishable pairs that were forced into binary labels—and that respecting a labeler's resolution limit matters more for alignment quality than increasing its scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes pairwise preference triplets (prompt, response A, response B) along with a proxy labeler (a small LLM fine-tuned via DPO on a 1/8 data subset) and a scalar resolution threshold δ_M. The labeler's DPO implicit rewards score each response; pairs with reward gap ≤ δ_M are partitioned into tied subsets, further tagged as Both-Good or Both-Bad by comparing the pair's mean reward to the global dataset mean. The output is an aligned policy model plus the partitioned dataset, with the tied subsets carrying quality-attributed supervision at zero additional annotation cost.

## Method

The framework introduces a silent-aware labeling protocol grounded in semiorder theory: a proxy labeler is trained via DPO, its implicit rewards are used to partition data into a preferred subset (gap > δ_M) and a tied subset (gap ≤ δ_M), and tied pairs receive quality tags from their mean reward. For preferred pairs, a margin-augmented DPO loss enforces a minimum log-probability ratio consistent with the labeler's confidence. For tied pairs, a symmetric tie loss—derived from the Rao-Kupper model—produces a weight function W(h_θ) that vanishes at h_θ = 0, preventing the arbitrary distribution shifts that standard DPO induces on indistinguishable pairs. Quality-attributed signals are exploited via two routes: Deconstruct-then-KTO (converting tied pairs into point-wise desirable/undesirable samples) and a Directional Tie Loss that flips the sign of the symmetric loss for Both-Bad pairs, turning the zero-margin equilibrium from a local minimum into a local maximum and creating a repulsive force away from low-quality states. A corrected risk estimator (leaky ReLU applied to the empirical risk) prevents overfitting from the negative components introduced by the sign flip.

## Results

On UltraFeedback with Llama-3-8B as the policy model, the proposed C-DirTie achieves a gold reward of 10.82 (Llama-3B labeler, δ_M=5), substantially outperforming the forced-choice GPT-4 baseline (6.48) and the SOTA tie-handling method TODO (9.13). D-KTO reaches 9.92 under the same labeler configuration, while SymTie (neutral ties only) achieves 9.96 with the Llama-8B labeler at δ_M=5. On Mistral-7B, C-DirTie peaks at 7.32 and D-KTO at 7.84, both exceeding the forced GPT-4 baseline of 4.40 by over 60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Forced baseline, all silent-aware methods (Filtered, TODO, SymTie, D-KTO, C-DirTie) consistently improve gold reward, confirming that forced-choice labels on indistinguishable pairs inject noise. C-DirTie and D-KTO (quality-attributed tie signals) outperform TODO (the only prior tie-handling method) by 1.69 and 0.79 gold reward points respectively at the best Llama-3B configuration (δ_M=5: 10.82 vs 9.13). SymTie (neutral ties) is comparable to TODO, with SymTie reaching 9.96 (Llama-8B, δ_M=5) versus TODO's 9.96 at the same setting. A striking finding is that the small Llama-3B labeler with silent-aware labeling (C-DirTie, δ_M=5: 10.82) substantially outperforms the forced GPT-4 golden labels (6.48) and even the larger Llama-8B labeler under forced choice (7.72), demonstrating that respecting a labeler's resolution limit matters more than labeler scale. On Mistral-7B, the same pattern holds: D-KTO with Llama-3B (7.84) beats forced GPT-4 (4.40) by 78%, and C-DirTie (7.32) still exceeds the forced baseline by 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO/KTO preference optimization lineage by introducing a 'silent-aware' framework grounded in psychophysics (just noticeable difference) and semiorder theory, formalizing a discriminative resolution limit δ_M that allows labelers to express ties rather than forced binary choices. It builds directly on TODO (Guo et al., 2025), the only prior work explicitly modeling ties in LLM alignment, but departs from it by (1) justifying *why* ties must be modeled via the resolution-limit argument, and (2) extending beyond neutral ties to quality-attributed 'Both-Good'/'Both-Bad' subsets with dedicated optimization objectives (directional tie loss, deconstruct-then-KTO). The symmetric tie loss is derived from the classical Rao-Kupper model, and the labeler synthesis protocol follows Tao & Li (2025), though the paper reframes their 'weak teacher as replacement' paradigm into a 'teacher vs. self-refinement' perspective where honesty (staying silent) matters more than absolute capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a small, honest labeler (Llama-3B with silence) outperforms GPT-4 or human experts under forced-choice is practically significant: it offers a cost-effective path to high-quality RLAIF and demonstrates that existing benchmarks (UltraFeedback, ChatArena) contain latent value recoverable without re-labeling. Open questions include whether the static resolution threshold can be made dynamically adaptive to prompt complexity, whether a formal information-theoretic proof of the regularization effect of silence exists, and how to integrate human nuanced judgment with AI labelers' consistent resolution limits in hybrid feedback systems. The framework's compatibility with multiple base objectives (DPO, CPO, SimPO) and dynamic EM-style estimation (RE-PO) suggests broad applicability, though the practical deployment of quality-attributed tie signals in production pipelines remains untested at scale.
