## Problem

Reward models face a fundamental efficiency-expressivity trade-off: scalar discriminative models are fast but lack interpretability and struggle on hard instances, while generative judging models provide richer reasoning at substantial computational cost when applied to every comparison. The paper asks when reflection is actually needed, identifying that the log-probability margin between verdict tokens serves as a reliable, zero-cost proxy for instance difficulty that can gate reflective computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query and two candidate responses as input and produces a two-stage output: an initial single-token verdict (A or B) followed by a confidence score computed from the log-probability margin between the two verdict tokens. If the confidence falls below a threshold τ, the model additionally generates a brief reflection text and a revised final verdict; otherwise it terminates after the single token. This means the output length is variable—ranging from 1 token (high-confidence) to several hundred tokens (low-confidence with full reflection)—enabling a tunable accuracy-cost trade-off at inference time.

## Method

CAMEL is a two-stage confidence-gated reflection framework built on Qwen3-14B. In the fast pass, the model emits a single-token verdict; the absolute log-probability margin between the two verdict tokens (Equation 5) is computed as a confidence score, and a threshold τ routes low-confidence instances to a reflection stage where the model generates a brief justification and a revised verdict. Training proceeds in two phases: supervised fine-tuning on preference data, followed by one epoch of GRPO reinforcement learning with a binary correctness reward on the final verdict. The key training innovation is counterfactual prefix augmentation: for each instance, the model is trained with both a forced initial verdict of A and a forced initial verdict of B, exposing it to diverse starting points and preventing the reflection stage from degenerating into echoing the initial judgment. This design lets genuine self-correction emerge from the reward structure rather than being hard-coded, and the confidence gate at inference time ensures reflection is invoked only where it is most likely to help.

## Results

CAMEL-Reflection achieves 82.9% average accuracy across RewardBench (92.8%), RM-Bench (84.2%), and JudgeBench (71.6%), surpassing the best prior model INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (79.7%) by 3.2 percentage points while using only 14B parameters. The confidence-gated CAMEL variant achieves 81.1% average accuracy with substantially fewer generated tokens, while CAMEL-Fast (single-token output) reaches 76.8% average, matching the strongest 27B scalar reward models. On RM-Bench, CAMEL-Reflection's 84.2% represents a 1.5-point gain over the next-best generative model (J1-Llama-70B at 82.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMEL-Reflection (82.9% avg) outperforms all 14 baselines, with the largest margins over generative judges: +4.2% over J1-Llama-70B (78.7%), +9.3% over RM-R1-Qwen-Instruct-32B (75.6%), and +9.9% over GPT-4o (73.0%). Against the strongest scalar baseline INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (79.7%), CAMEL-Reflection gains 3.2% while using only 14B vs. 70B parameters. CAMEL-Fast, which outputs a single token, matches or exceeds all 8B scalar RMs (e.g., 76.8% vs. Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B-v0.2's 76.9%) and surpasses RM-R1-Qwen-Instruct-14B (73.6%) by 3.2 points. On RM-Bench specifically, CAMEL-Reflection's 84.2% is the only result above 80%, beating J1-Llama-70B (82.7%) and INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (73.8%) by 1.5 and 10.4 points respectively. The confidence-gated CAMEL (81.1%) sits between CAMEL-Fast and CAMEL-Reflection, achieving near-Reflection accuracy while generating far fewer tokens than always-reflecting, establishing a strictly better accuracy-cost Pareto frontier than RM-R1 baselines that generate ~900–1100 tokens per sample.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMEL sits at the intersection of two established reward-modeling paradigms: scalar Bradley-Terry discriminative models (Skywork-Reward, INF-ORM) and generative judging models (RM-R1, J1, GENRM). Its core departure is the observation that the single-token log-probability margin between verdict tokens serves as a reliable instance-difficulty proxy, enabling a confidence-gated routing mechanism that allocates generative reflection only where needed. The training recipe builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a novel counterfactual prefix augmentation that forces the model to practice revising both correct and incorrect initial verdicts, distinguishing it from RM-R1's rubric-distillation approach and J1's verifiable-reward RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strictly better accuracy-efficiency Pareto frontier is practically significant for deployment: CAMEL-Fast matches strong baselines with a single output token, while moderate thresholds yield superior accuracy at a fraction of the generation cost of always-reflecting models. The 14B-parameter model surpassing 70B-parameter baselines suggests that selective computation allocation can substitute for raw scale in reward modeling. However, the high-confidence failure modes (over-refusal/safety bias, subtle style preferences, long-chain numerical slips) remain unaddressed, and the observed post-training confidence shift (the model becomes less confident) is noted but not fully explained, leaving open whether the gating threshold requires per-domain tuning in production.
