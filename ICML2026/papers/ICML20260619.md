## Problem

Existing stability-based generalization bounds for nonconvex stochastic optimization (e.g., Lei 2023) require bounded gradient variance (p=2), yet empirical evidence from deep learning and reinforcement learning shows that stochastic gradient noise is typically heavy-tailed. This paper addresses the open problem of establishing generalization guarantees under the weaker bounded p-th centered moment (p-BCM) condition with p∈(1,2], where standard variance-based arguments break down.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a training dataset S of n i.i.d. samples, an initial point x_0 (assumed zero for stability proofs), a sequence of step sizes, and algorithm-specific hyperparameters (clipping threshold γ, mini-batch size B, momentum coefficient β). The output is a theoretical bound on the expected population gradient norm E[‖∇F(A(S))‖], decomposed into an optimization error term and a generalization error term of the form 4ε + C_p σ_p n^{-(p-1)/p}, where ε is the algorithmic stability parameter and C_p is a constant depending only on p. The framework is purely theoretical—no empirical training is performed—and the bounds are expressed in terms of n, T, η, and the algorithm parameters.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a truncation argument that decomposes the stochastic gradient noise z_i(S) into a bounded truncated component z_i^(τ)(S) (via clip_τ) and a residual tail r_i^(τ)(S), enabling control of the sum ‖Σ z_i(S)‖ under p-BCM where direct Jensen's inequality on squared norms is inapplicable. The truncated part is bounded using the p-th moment condition and ε-uniform stability in gradients for cross terms, while the residual tail is controlled by the inequality ‖u − clip_τ(u)‖ ≤ ‖u‖^p / τ^{p-1}. Optimizing the truncation threshold τ yields the generalization bound 4ε + C_p σ_p n^{-(p-1)/p}, which recovers the p=2 result of Lei (2023) as a special case. This framework is then instantiated for four algorithms—clipped SGD, mini-batch normalized SGD (NSGD-B), normalized SGD with momentum (NSGD-M), and NSGD with clipping and momentum (NSGD-CM)—showing that normalization eliminates the linear dependence on the clipping threshold in stability bounds, yielding sharper population risk rates of O(n^{-(p-1)/(7p-6)}) for the normalized variants versus O(n^{-(p-1)/(3(3p-2))}) for clipped SGD.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) establishes that the nonconvex generalization error is bounded by $4\epsilon + C_p \sigma_p n^{-(p-1)/p}$ under the p-BCM assumption, where $\epsilon$ is the uniform stability-in-gradients parameter and $C_p$ is a constant with $1 \leq C_p \leq 3$. When $p=2$, this recovers the classical $4\epsilon + \sigma_2 n^{-1/2}$ bound. For specific algorithms, the population gradient norm bounds are $O(n^{-(p-1)/(3(3p-2))})$ for clipped SGD (Corollary 5.4) and $O(n^{-(p-1)/(7p-6)})$ for mini-batch normalized SGD, normalized SGD with momentum, and normalized SGD with clipping and momentum (Corollaries 5.9, 5.14, 5.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Lei (2023), this paper's Theorem 4.2 strictly generalizes the bounded-variance result: when $p=2$, the bound $4\epsilon + C_2\sigma_2 n^{-1/2}$ with $C_2=1$ coincides exactly with Lei's $4\epsilon + \sigma_2 n^{-1/2}$. For $p\in(1,2)$, the rate degrades from $n^{-1/2}$ to $n^{-(p-1)/p}$, which is the expected penalty for relaxing the second-moment assumption to a weaker $p$-th moment condition. Compared to Mei et al. (2018), this paper's bound is dimension-free (no dependence on $d$) and requires only the p-BCM moment condition rather than the stronger sub-Gaussian tail assumption, making it applicable to the heavy-tailed gradient noise empirically observed in deep learning. The algorithm-specific results further show that normalized SGD variants (NSGD-B, NSGD-M, NSGD-CM) achieve a sharper rate of $O(n^{-(p-1)/(7p-6)})$ compared to clipped SGD's $O(n^{-(p-1)/(3(3p-2))})$, demonstrating that normalization and momentum improve both stability and generalization under heavy-tailed noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stability-based generalization framework of Lei (2023) from the bounded-variance (p=2) setting to the more general p-BCM condition (p∈(1,2]) that models heavy-tailed gradient noise. The key technical departure is a truncation-and-tail-decomposition argument that replaces the direct Jensen's inequality step used under bounded variance, since the stochastic gradient noise no longer admits a finite second moment. The generalization error decomposition follows Bousquet et al. (2020), and the application to specific algorithms builds on the extensive convergence analysis literature for clipped and normalized SGD under heavy-tailed noise (Zhang et al. 2020; Cutkosky & Mehta 2021; Hübler et al. 2025), which had previously focused exclusively on optimization error rather than generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap: while convergence guarantees for clipped and normalized SGD under heavy-tailed noise are well-developed, the generalization guarantees were missing, leaving practitioners without a theoretical bridge between training performance and population performance in the heavy-tailed regime that empirical evidence suggests is realistic for deep learning. The finding that normalization (NSGD-B, NSGD-M) yields sharper stability and generalization bounds than clipping is a useful theoretical signal for algorithm selection. Open questions include whether the n^{-(p-1)/p} rate is tight (the paper notes a matching lower bound for the empirical mean estimator but not for the stability-based generalization gap), whether high-probability versions of the bounds are achievable, and how the framework extends to nonsmooth objectives.
