## Problem

CLIP-based retrieval systems degrade significantly under distributional shifts (multilingual, long-form, or multimodal queries), yet retraining the retriever is prohibitively expensive because it requires re-embedding entire industrial-scale corpora. Existing LLM-based query rewriting methods lack explicit supervision from the retriever's latent space, producing rewrites that are semantically plausible but poorly aligned with the frozen encoder's embedding geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw query—either a text string (for multilingual or long-form retrieval) or an image paired with a text instruction (for compositional image retrieval on CIRR)—and produces a single rewritten English text query at inference time. During training, the LLM samples K=8 constrained rewrites per query, each wrapped in <think>... and <answer>...</answer> tags; only the <answer> content is passed to the frozen CLIP text encoder. The rewritten query is then matched against precomputed candidate image embeddings to produce a ranked list, from which the target image's rank serves as the optimization signal.

## Method

GRAPE fine-tunes a compact LLM rewriter (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) using Group Relative Policy Optimization, where for each query the LLM samples K rewrites, each is encoded by a frozen CLIP encoder, and the target image's rank among N candidates is computed. The key technical contribution is a corpus-relative ranking-based reward R_k^r = 1 − 2(r_k−1)/(N−1) that replaces naive similarity scores, which the authors show induces a 'score inflation' pathology where the LLM injects high-frequency generic tokens to boost absolute similarity without improving discriminative ranking. Rewards are normalized within each group to compute relative advantages, and the policy is updated via an advantage-weighted log-likelihood objective regularized by a KL divergence to a reference model. Only the LLM parameters are updated; the CLIP encoders and all precomputed corpus embeddings remain frozen, making the approach fully plug-and-play with existing retrieval infrastructure.

## Results

GRAPE achieves an average Recall@10 of 74.2% (ViT-B/32), 76.5% (ViT-B/16), and 76.8% (ViT-L/14) across five benchmarks, representing a consistent +4.9% average gain over the CLIP+LLM baseline. On the strongest retriever (ViT-L/14), GRAPE reaches 43.4% R@1 and 76.8% R@10 on average, compared to 38.9% and 71.9% for CLIP+LLM. The largest absolute improvements appear on Wikipedia (long-form: +5.7% R@10 for ViT-B/16) and Flickr30k-CN (multilingual: +3.1% R@10 for ViT-B/32), while gains on CIRR (multimodal) are more modest (+4.5–7.5% R@10 depending on model scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPE is compared against two primary baselines: (1) frozen CLIP without any rewriting, and (2) CLIP+LLM, where a frozen LLM rewrites queries without retrieval feedback. GRAPE wins on every benchmark and model scale, with the most dramatic gains over vanilla CLIP (e.g., +33.7% R@1 on Flickr30k-CN with ViT-B/32) and consistent 4–7% R@10 improvements over CLIP+LLM. In the appendix (Table 4), GRAPE (using a compact Qwen2.5-3B) also outperforms much larger proprietary LLMs used zero-shot: on Flickr-CN it beats GPT-5.4 (58.7 vs. 56.2 R@1) and Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (58.7 vs. 56.2 R@1), and on Wikipedia it surpasses both (43.7 vs. 38.4 and 36.9 R@1). It also outperforms the training-free PlugIR baseline by a wide margin (e.g., 43.7 vs. 33.2 R@1 on Wikipedia), confirming that the gains stem from retriever-aligned ranking supervision rather than raw LLM scale or prompt engineering.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAPE adapts the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) to the domain of retrieval-oriented query rewriting, replacing the original math-reasoning reward with a corpus-relative ranking signal derived from a frozen CLIP retriever. It departs from prior training-free rewriting methods (ComCLIP, PlugIR) that rely on heuristic decomposition or unsupervised LLM prompting, and from training-based approaches (MetaCLIP-2, BLIP-2, LoRA adapters) that require costly re-embedding of billion-scale corpora. The key conceptual departure is the identification of score inflation under similarity-based RL supervision and the substitution of a discrete ranking reward, which the authors show is affine-invariant under GRPO's group normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAPE offers a practical pathway for industrial retrieval systems to handle distributional shifts (multilingual, long-form, multimodal) without the prohibitive cost of re-embedding pre-computed vector databases, making it immediately deployable in existing CLIP-based pipelines. The score inflation finding is a broadly useful diagnostic: any practitioner using pointwise similarity as an RL reward for generative models should be aware that the policy can exploit generic high-frequency tokens to inflate absolute scores while degrading actual ranking. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-throughput industrial inference (current LLM rewriting adds non-trivial latency), whether the retriever-dependent ceiling can be broken by jointly adapting both sides, and whether the ranking reward generalizes to settings where ground-truth target identity is unavailable at training time.
