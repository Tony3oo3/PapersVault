## Problem

Recent test-time defenses for CLIP's zero-shot inference report impressive adversarial robustness, but no systematic study has evaluated them against defense-specific adaptive attacks, as is standard in traditional adversarial robustness. The paper identifies that six state-of-the-art defenses share a common reliance on an 'indicative measurement' assumed to separate clean from adversarial distributions, and demonstrates that this assumption is a fundamental vulnerability that causes reported robustness to be substantially overestimated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265831/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a clean RGB image, its ground truth class label, and knowledge of the target defense's indicative measurement function m(x). It produces a bounded perturbation (ε = 4.0/255 or 1.0/255) via 10-step PGD that, when added to the image, causes the defense to exhibit unexpected behavior. The method is designed to be composable with existing attack baselines (e.g., full-gradient, EOT) and can skip the defense's internal optimization entirely once the indicative measurement is manipulated, making it far cheaper than full-defense gradient attacks.

## Method

CLIP-MAD (Manipulating Assumed Difference) exploits the shared assumption across CLIP test-time defenses that a measurement m(x) separates clean and adversarial distributions, causing the defense to either preserve or modify the static feature accordingly. The attack jointly optimizes a composite loss L = α·L_static + λ·L_m, where L_m is tailored per defense to push m(x) away from its assumed adversarial distribution (e.g., cosine similarity to original features for TTC/DOC, cross-entropy on augmented views for R-TPT/TAPT, cross-entropy after one likelihood step for CLIPure). This manipulation causes the defense to either falsely preserve adversarial features or falsely modify clean features, effectively inverting its intended behavior. The method is efficient because it targets the assumption before the defense's optimization begins, bypassing costly or non-differentiable defensive components, and requires only a single hyperparameter pair (α, λ) that is trivially set to 0/1 or 1/1 depending on the defense paradigm.

## Results

CLIP-MAD dramatically reduces the robust accuracy of six state-of-the-art CLIP test-time defenses across 13 datasets. On TTC, CLIP-MAD lowers average robust accuracy from 25.7% (static attack) and 11.0% (full-gradient attack) to just 4.7%. On R-TPT, it collapses robust accuracy from 37.5% to 0.7% by skipping all defense gradient computation. On CLIPure-Cos and CLIPure-Diff, where full-gradient attack fails due to gradient obfuscation (78.0% and 81.8% robust accuracy), CLIP-MAD still achieves only 4.8% and 6.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-MAD is compared against two attack baselines: static (non-adaptive) PGD-10 and full-gradient adaptive attack. On TTC, CLIP-MAD reduces average robust accuracy from 11.0% (full-gradient) to 4.7%, a 57% relative reduction, while also being more computationally efficient since most clean samples are fooled in a single iteration. On DOC, it further halves the full-gradient result from 19.5% to 9.4%. On R-TPT, where full-gradient attack is infeasible due to non-differentiable AugMix and index selection, CLIP-MAD achieves 0.7% versus 37.5% for static attack, effectively eliminating the defense. On TAPT-VLI, both full-gradient (0.3%) and CLIP-MAD (0.4%) are near zero, but CLIP-MAD skips all multi-layer prompt tuning operations. On CLIPure-Cos/Diff, full-gradient attack fails catastrophically (78.0%/81.8%) due to gradient obfuscation, while CLIP-MAD achieves 4.8%/6.0%, outperforming even the static attack (6.6%/7.3%) and demonstrating its value when standard adaptive attacks are trapped.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of CLIP adversarial robustness and adaptive attack methodology. It builds on the train-time adversarial fine-tuning line (APT, AdvPT, FARE, TeCoA) and the more recent shift toward test-time defenses (TTC, DOC, R-TPT, TAPT, CLIPure), while drawing on the adaptive attack evaluation framework established by Croce et al. (2022), Tramer et al. (2020), and Athalye et al. (2018) for traditional classification. The key conceptual departure is identifying a shared 'indicative measurement assumption' across three architecturally distinct defense paradigms (input purification, feature optimization, prompt tuning) and designing an attack (CLIP-MAD) that targets this assumption rather than any specific defense mechanism, thereby bypassing non-differentiable or computationally heavy defense components entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that six SOTA test-time defenses spanning three paradigms all collapse under a single assumption-targeting attack substantially undermines the reported robustness numbers in the literature and provides a concrete, efficient tool for worst-case evaluation. A notable secondary finding is that for CLIPure, static attacks dramatically outperform full-gradient adaptive attacks due to gradient obfuscation, echoing a known pitfall in traditional defenses but in a new zero-shot VLM context. Open questions include whether future defenses can avoid reliance on any indicative measurement (e.g., through aggressive non-differentiable preprocessing), and whether the vulnerability generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLM architectures. The paper's limitation is that CLIP-MAD requires white-box knowledge of the defense's measurement, restricting its use in truly black-box threat models.
