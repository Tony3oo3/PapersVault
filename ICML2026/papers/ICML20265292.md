## Problem

Operating room scene graph generation (SGG) models such as MM2SG suffer from a severe long-tail problem: accuracy on rare surgical actions (e.g., Suturing, Cutting, Drilling) drops to an F1 of 0.289, compared to 0.664 on frequent head relations. This paper presents the first approach to explicitly address this long-tail gap in OR SGG by leveraging in-context learning (ICL) with multimodal retrieval, rather than relying on the model's memorized pre-training knowledge for rare categories.

## Input / Output

The system ingests a rich multimodal bundle per time point: multi-view RGB-D images, high-resolution detail views, 3D point clouds, audio signals, speech transcripts, real-time robot system logs, segmentation masks, and infrared tracking data. These are consumed through modality-specific encoders (CLIP vision encoder for images, Point Transformer V3 for point clouds, frozen CLAP for audio, lightweight CNN for masks) whose token embeddings are projected into a shared LLM embedding space. The output is a set of scene graph triplets (subject, relation, object) encoding interactions among medical staff, instruments, and equipment in the OR.

## Method

SGG-ICL introduces a selective ICL pipeline with three stages. First, an Adaptive Router built on Qwen3-VL-30B classifies each test sample as head or long-tail using prompt templates with detailed action definitions (plus robot logs for Calibrating), so that ICL is applied only to long-tail queries, preserving head-class accuracy. Second, a two-stage retrieval constructs in-context examples: BGE-VL-MLLM performs coarse multimodal retrieval to build a top-20 candidate pool, then a trained MLLM Reranker (LLaVA-7B with LoRA on the last 12 image-encoder layers) re-ranks candidates by predicting pairwise scene-graph similarity. The reranker is supervised by a weighted IoU over annotated triplets, where each relation's weight is the inverse of its training frequency, explicitly up-weighting long-tail relations. Finally, the top-1 selected example (filtered by a margin threshold Γ=0.4 on average candidate similarity) is concatenated with the query to form the ICL prompt fed to the SGG model.

## Results

On the MM-OR dataset, SGG-ICL achieves a macro-average F1 of 0.546, outperforming MM2SG (0.520), Oracle (0.472), and PSG (0.328). The headline improvement is on long-tail classes, where SGG-ICL reaches an F1 of 0.358 versus 0.289 for MM2SG (+6.9 percentage points), while head-class F1 remains nearly unchanged (0.693 vs. 0.699, a −0.6 pp drop). Overall average F1 improves by 2.6 pp over MM2SG. Inference time is ~3 s/sample for head-class queries and ~5 s/sample for long-tail queries (which constitute ~10% of the dataset), compared to ~1 s for MM2SG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGG-ICL is compared against three baselines on the MM-OR dataset. Against PSG (Yang et al., ECCV 2022), SGG-ICL achieves a macro-average F1 of 0.546 versus 0.328, a 21.8 pp gain, though PSG and Oracle do not report per-class breakdowns. Against Oracle (Ozsoy et al., 2024), SGG-ICL improves macro F1 from 0.472 to 0.546 (+7.4 pp). Against the strongest baseline MM2SG (Ozsoy et al., 2025), SGG-ICL wins on macro-average F1 (0.546 vs. 0.520, +2.6 pp) and on long-tail class F1 (0.358 vs. 0.289, +6.9 pp), while incurring a negligible 0.6 pp drop on head-class F1 (0.693 vs. 0.699). The largest per-class gains are on Hammering (0.603 vs. 0.417, +18.6 pp) and Suturing (0.209 vs. 0.000), confirming that the ICL augmentation specifically targets the most under-performing relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MM2SG (Özsoy et al., 2025), the first MLLM for OR scene graph generation, and extends the in-context learning paradigm (Brown et al., 2020; Kandpal et al., 2023) from text-only settings to the multimodal, triplet-structured domain of OR SGG. It departs from prior retrieval-augmented ICL work (UDR, LLM-R, SuRe) by introducing a two-stage multimodal retrieval pipeline (BGE-VL-MLLM pre-selection followed by a custom MLLM Reranker) that ingests RGB-D images, point clouds, audio, and text simultaneously, and by training the reranker with a weighted IoU over scene graph triplets rather than text similarity. The Adaptive Router, which selectively applies ICL only to long-tail samples, is a novel architectural choice not present in prior ICL or long-tail learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly target the long-tail distribution problem in OR SGG, a domain where rare surgical actions (suturing, cutting, scanning) are clinically critical yet severely underperformed by existing models. The selective routing design—applying ICL only where needed—offers a practical template for balancing head and tail performance without retraining the base SGG model. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (MM-OR) covering only knee replacement surgeries, and the Adaptive Router's recall for long-tail classes remains limited, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other surgical domains or to SGG tasks with more extreme tail distributions. The slight degradation in head-class F1 also raises the question of whether the routing threshold can be further refined to eliminate this tradeoff entirely.
