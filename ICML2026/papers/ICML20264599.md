## Problem

Existing DoS and energy-latency attacks on LLMs are either white-box (requiring gradient access), not reasoning-aware (targeting general-purpose LLMs rather than specialized reasoning models), or not automated (relying on manual prompt design). This paper addresses the gap of automatically generating black-box adversarial inputs that specifically exploit the 'overthinking' vulnerability of Large Reasoning Models to amplify computational cost without requiring model internals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264599/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes reasoning problems from benchmarks (GSM8K, SVAMP, MATH) and decomposes them via an LLM into a structured representation of a premise list and a final question. It produces compact adversarial problem formulations (as few as 90–165 input tokens) that, when submitted to a target LRM, trigger extended reasoning traces up to 32,768 tokens. The fitness signal is derived from the target model's response: total CoT token count and the frequency of predefined overthinking markers (e.g., 'but', 'wait', 'maybe', 'perhaps').

## Method

The core contribution is a Hierarchical Genetic Algorithm (HGA) that evolves adversarial problem formulations by operating on structured premise-question decompositions rather than raw text. Two levels of crossover are defined: question-level crossover swaps final questions between two parents to create premise-question mismatches, and premise-level crossover exchanges individual premises to disrupt local logical coherence. Mutation operates via premise deletion or insertion of an unrelated premise borrowed from another population member. A composite fitness function jointly rewards output verbosity (token length) and reflective overthinking markers, with z-score normalization per generation and a tunable weight α; ablations show this composite objective substantially outperforms length-only optimization by guiding the search toward inputs that trigger genuine recursive re-evaluation rather than simple verbosity. The algorithm uses elitism plus roulette-wheel selection, and demonstrates strong transferability: inputs evolved on a small proxy model (Qwen3-14B) retain high efficacy against large closed-source targets (GPT-o3, DeepSeek-R1, Gemini-2.5-Flash).

## Results

The HGA framework achieves up to a 26.1× maximum output token amplification on MATH (DeepSeek-R1: 467 → 12,206 tokens) and consistently outperforms both the clean baseline and the manually crafted missing-premise (MIP) baseline across all four evaluated LRMs (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-Thinking, GPT-o3, Gemini-2.5-Flash) and three math datasets. On MATH, average output lengths reach 8,817 tokens (DeepSeek-R1), 13,007 tokens (Qwen3-Thinking), and 12,147 tokens (Gemini-2.5-Flash), compared to baselines of 355, 3,618, and 2,889 tokens respectively. Against AutoDoS, HGA achieves a 32,768-token maximum output on MATH using only 99 input tokens, versus AutoDoS's 16,009 tokens requiring 2,652 input tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MIP (missing-premise) baseline from Fan et al. (2025), HGA wins on every model–dataset combination for both max-len and avg-len. The gap is most dramatic on GPT-o3/SVAMP, where HGA reaches 6,562 max tokens versus MIP's 906 (7.2×), and on MATH/DeepSeek-R1, where HGA's 8,817 avg tokens nearly double MIP's 4,323. Against AutoDoS (Crabs, Zhang et al. 2025b), HGA achieves a substantially higher max output on MATH (32,768 vs. 16,009 tokens) while using only 99 input tokens compared to AutoDoS's 2,652—a 27× input-efficiency advantage. On GSM8K the two methods are nearly tied on max-len (11,301 vs. 11,491), and HGA slightly trails on SVAMP max-len (10,708 vs. 12,775), but HGA still requires 25–28× fewer input tokens in all cases. Overall, HGA is the only method that simultaneously satisfies black-box access, reasoning-awareness, and full automation, as summarized in the paper's comparison table (Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the overthinking phenomenon in reasoning LLMs (Chen et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2025) and DoS/energy-latency attacks on language models (Shumailov et al. 2021; Gao et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2025b). It departs from prior DoS work by targeting the structured logical chain of reasoning problems rather than surface-level prompt manipulation, and from prior overthinking analyses by converting the observation into an automated adversarial optimization problem. The hierarchical genetic algorithm repurposes a classical evolutionary search technique for black-box adversarial input generation, while the composite fitness function (length + reflective markers) is a novel objective design specific to reasoning-model exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that overthinking is a shared, transferable vulnerability across architecturally distinct commercial LRMs (GPT-o3, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, Gemini) suggests it is rooted in the RL-based reasoning training paradigm rather than any single model's idiosyncrasies, raising urgent questions about whether current defenses (rate-limiting, context truncation) are sufficient. The practical implication that a 14B open-source proxy can generate effective attacks against 671B+ closed-source models dramatically lowers the barrier for resource-exhaustion attacks on commercial APIs. Open questions include whether robustness training against logically inconsistent inputs can close this gap, whether the vulnerability persists in multimodal reasoning, and how to design lightweight runtime detectors for reasoning-loop anomalies without degrading legitimate long-chain inference.
