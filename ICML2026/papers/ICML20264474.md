## Problem

Existing Flow-based GRPO methods assign a single terminal reward uniformly to all denoising steps, inducing step-level reward sparsity and misaligning local step contributions with the global trajectory outcome. Additionally, group-wise ranking only compares trajectories at matched timesteps, ignoring within-trajectory dependencies where early denoising actions exert delayed, implicit effects on later states and the final image.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image as output, using SD3.5-M or FLUX.1-dev as the base flow-matching model fine-tuned via LoRA. During training, SDE sampling with T=10 steps generates a group of G=24 diverse trajectories per prompt; for each intermediate latent, the remaining steps are completed via deterministic ODE sampling to obtain a clean image on which the reward model evaluates, enabling per-step reward estimation without additional stochasticity.

## Method

TP-GRPO replaces the sparse terminal reward with a step-wise incremental reward r_t = R(x_{t-1}^{ODE(t-1)}) − R(x_t^{ODE(t)}), which isolates the pure effect of each individual SDE sampling step by differencing ODE-completed rewards that share all prior stochastic steps. It then identifies turning points—steps where the local reward trend flips to align with the overall trajectory trend—using a strict sign-based criterion (s_{t+1} < 0 and s_t > 0) that requires no hyperparameters. At these turning points, the local reward is replaced with an aggregated long-term reward r_t^agg = R(x_0) − R(x_t^{ODE(t)}), capturing the cumulative downstream impact of the action on all subsequent denoising steps. A stricter variant (Definition 5.1) further filters turning points to those where |r_t^agg| > |r_t|, ensuring the aggregated signal is more informative than the local one. The first denoising step, which is excluded by the standard turning-point definition, is handled via a separate alignment condition (Remark 5.2) to extend long-term effect modeling to the trajectory's start.

## Results

On Compositional Image Generation, TP-GRPO (w/o constraint) achieves 0.9714 GenEval score, outperforming Flow-GRPO (0.9673) and TempFlow-GRPO (0.9703). On Visual Text Rendering, TP-GRPO (w/o constraint) reaches 0.9718 OCR accuracy vs. 0.9579 for Flow-GRPO. On Human Preference Alignment, TP-GRPO (w/o constraint) attains 24.73 PickScore, surpassing Flow-GRPO (24.02) and TempFlow-GRPO (24.45). Generalization metrics (Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) are largely preserved or improved, with no evidence of reward hacking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base model SD3.5-M, both TP-GRPO variants achieve large absolute gains on all three tasks (e.g., GenEval 0.9714 vs. 0.6029; OCR 0.9718 vs. 0.4548; PickScore 24.73 vs. 21.44), confirming the effectiveness of RL fine-tuning. Against Flow-GRPO, TP-GRPO (w/o constraint) wins on all three task metrics: GenEval 0.9714 vs. 0.9673, OCR 0.9718 vs. 0.9579, and PickScore 24.73 vs. 24.02, while also improving most generalization scores (Aesthetic 5.352 vs. 5.223, ImageReward 0.9267 vs. 0.8792). Against TempFlow-GRPO, TP-GRPO (w/o constraint) leads on GenEval (0.9714 vs. 0.9703) and PickScore (24.73 vs. 24.45), and on OCR (0.9718 vs. 0.9693); however, TP-GRPO (w constraint) trails TempFlow-GRPO on OCR (0.9651 vs. 0.9693) while recovering on preference scores (ImageReward 0.7378 vs. 0.6316, UnifiedReward 3.260 vs. 3.017). The wall-clock time analysis (Figure 8) shows that despite higher per-step cost from additional ODE sampling, TP-GRPO still outperforms Flow-GRPO at equal training time, and the training curves (Figure 4) indicate faster convergence, reaching Flow-GRPO's step-2300 reward by approximately step 700.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) and DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025), which apply GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to flow matching models by injecting stochasticity via SDE sampling and assigning a single terminal reward uniformly to all denoising steps. TP-GRPO departs from this design in two ways: it replaces the outcome-based reward with step-level incremental rewards computed via ODE-completion differences, and it introduces a sign-based turning-point criterion that identifies steps where the local reward trend flips to align with the overall trajectory, assigning those steps an aggregated long-term reward. This is conceptually related to credit assignment in trajectory-based RL (Malkin et al., 2022; Madan et al., 2023) but operates without requiring an explicit target density, and is distinct from TempFlow-GRPO (He et al., 2025) and DenseGRPO (Deng et al., 2026), which address timing or sparsity through different mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete misalignment in how existing flow-based GRPO methods assign credit across denoising steps and provides a clean, hyperparameter-free fix that consistently improves generation quality across compositional, text-rendering, and preference-alignment tasks on both SD3.5-M and FLUX.1-dev. The turning-point concept—detecting steps that flip local reward trends via simple sign changes—is a lightweight mechanism that could inform credit assignment in other iterative generative processes. Open questions include whether the ODE-completion-based reward estimation generalizes to fewer-step or continuous-time samplers, whether the sign-based criterion is optimal compared to magnitude-aware alternatives, and whether the additional sampling overhead remains justified at larger scales or with more expensive reward models.
