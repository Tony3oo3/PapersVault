## Problem

Existing hyper-relational knowledge graph (HKG) completion methods are restricted to link prediction, which assumes exactly one component is missing and all others are known. This rigid single-blank assumption fails in real-world scenarios where multiple or even all components of a fact may be simultaneously unknown, making it impossible to generate valid facts from highly incomplete observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262335/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

KREPE takes a partially or fully masked hyper-relational fact (any arbitrary subset of its head, tail, primary relation, and qualifier pairs can be masked) along with an observed set of HKG facts that provide the global structural context. It outputs a probability distribution over all candidate entities and relations for each masked position, which supports both discriminative inference (ranking candidates for a single blank) and generative inference (iterative top-p sampling to construct a complete fact from scratch). The generated fact must use only entities and relations from the HKG vocabulary, and the number of qualifiers in the output matches the query length.

## Method

KREPE combines contextual message passing with a masked discrete diffusion training objective. Contextual message passing updates each entity and relation representation by aggregating messages from all facts it participates in, but explicitly subtracts the component's own contribution from each fact's representation before passing, forcing representations to encode only the surrounding context rather than self-information. A bi-level noising strategy provides diverse training conditions: at the inter-fact level, the training set is stochastically partitioned each epoch into an observed set (used for representation computation) and a target set (used as masked queries), and at the intra-fact level, an arbitrary number of components within target facts are masked. The model is trained with the any-order autoregressive (AO-AR) objective, which is mathematically equivalent to the absorbing masked discrete diffusion loss, learning the joint conditional distribution of all masked components simultaneously. At inference, fact generation proceeds via iterative decoding where a random subset of remaining masks is resolved at each step using probability-based top-p sampling, with a caching strategy that recomputes mask representations only when a mask is replaced.

## Results

On standard HKG link prediction benchmarks, KREPE achieves state-of-the-art MRR of 0.389 (primary) and 0.419 (all positions) on WD50K, 0.522 on WikiPeople⁻, and 0.491 on WikiPeople, outperforming the previous best MAYPL (0.381/0.411/0.521/0.488). On the novel fact generation task, KREPE dramatically outperforms all baselines, achieving 0.717–0.855 accuracy across three datasets and three masking settings (Scratch, Targeted, Arbitrary), compared to the best LLM-based baseline at 0.351–0.604. KREPE also achieves the highest Valid & Novel Rate (0.351) and lowest expected generation attempts (2.85) on WikiPeople⁻ Scratch, versus 0.242 and 4.13 for the best LLM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On entity prediction (Table 1), KREPE outperforms all HKG-specific baselines including MAYPL (0.381→0.389 MRR on WD50K Pri), HDiff (0.373→0.389), and FormerGNN (0.377→0.389), and matches MAYPL on WikiPeople⁻ (0.519 vs. 0.519). On WikiPeople (Table 2), KREPE achieves 0.491 MRR, surpassing MAYPL (0.488), HySAE (0.454), and HJE (0.450). On relation prediction (Table 3), KREPE dominates with 0.963 MRR on WD50K Pri vs. HDiff (0.950) and HAHE (0.916). For the novel fact generation task (Table 4), KREPE achieves 0.717–0.855 accuracy, exceeding the best LLM-based baseline (Gemini 3 Pro with Random Facts at 0.604 on WD Arbitrary) by 15–55 percentage points across all settings. Among LLM baselines, Gemini 3 Pro consistently outperforms GPT-5.2, and the Random Facts and Neighbor Sets prompting strategies are the strongest LLM approaches, yet both remain far below KREPE. The ablation (Table 8) confirms that replacing the AO-AR diffusion loss with a standard link prediction cross-entropy loss collapses generation accuracy to near zero (0.038/0.002), validating that the generative objective is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

KREPE builds on the masked discrete diffusion framework of Ou et al. (2025), repurposing the AO-AR objective (mathematically equivalent to absorbing discrete diffusion) as a training signal for HKG representation learning rather than for text or image generation. Architecturally, it extends the contextual message passing paradigm from MAYPL (Lee & Whang, 2025) by explicitly excluding self-information from fact-to-component messages, and introduces a bi-level noising strategy (stochastic structure sampling at the inter-fact level plus arbitrary intra-fact masking) that has no direct analogue in prior HKG or KG diffusion work. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire line of discriminative HKG methods (StarE, HAHE, HDiff, etc.) that treat completion as single-blank ranking, and from LLM-based KG completion approaches (KICGPT, retrieve-then-rerank pipelines) that cannot handle multi-masked queries or operate without a backbone KG model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying link prediction and fact generation under a single generative objective, KREPE opens a path toward knowledge base completion that goes beyond the rigid single-blank assumption, which is more aligned with real-world queries where multiple components may be missing simultaneously. The 2× accuracy gap over strong LLM baselines (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.0 Pro) on fact generation suggests that domain-specific structural models can outperform general-purpose LLMs on tightly constrained generation tasks, a finding with implications for how LLMs are deployed in structured knowledge settings. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to inductive settings with unseen entities/relations (acknowledged as future work), whether the LLM-as-a-judge evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the tested domains, and whether the bi-level noising strategy transfers to other structured generative tasks beyond HKGs.
