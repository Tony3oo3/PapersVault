## Problem

Standard AI leaderboards aggregate pairwise comparisons via Bradley-Terry models into a single ranking, forcing heterogeneous, often non-transitive preferences into a total order and violating basic social-choice axioms. While maximal lotteries fix these misspecification issues by outputting a competitive set rather than a ranking, they are brittle to shifts in prompt mix or annotator population, potentially yielding a bipartisan set that severely underperforms on specific subpopulations. The paper asks how to make lottery-based evaluation robust to such distributional shifts while preserving the nonparametric, assumption-free nature of maximal lotteries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference votes partitioned into K subpopulations (e.g., by prompt language or annotator ethnicity), a reference mixture weight vector w₀ over those subpopulations, and a robustness radius ρ ∈ [0,1] controlling the ℓ₁-ball of admissible weight perturbations. It outputs a probability distribution over models (the robust lottery), the robust bipartisan set (union of supports across all optimal lotteries), and a worst-case win-rate guarantee v⋆. Because the formulation is a linear program, arbitrary linear constraints—such as inference-cost budgets or minimum benchmark scores—can be added on the fly, and the output is a pluralistic set of complementary models rather than a single ranked list.

## Method

The core construction treats each subpopulation's majority-margin matrix as the payoff of a symmetric zero-sum game and extends the maximin criterion of maximal lotteries to an ambiguity set of margin matrices induced by reweighting subpopulations within an ℓ₁ ball of radius ρ around a reference mixture. The resulting distributionally robust lottery (DRL) maximizes the worst-case game value over all admissible mixtures, reducing to the standard maximal lottery at ρ=0 and to full worst-case robustness at ρ=1. The paper derives a tractable LP of size O(mK) via duality, proves axiomatic properties including robust Condorcet consistency and weak-clone invariance, shows the existence of a sparse ε-optimal lottery with logarithmic support, and provides a finite-sample regret bound that gives a data-dependent rule for selecting ρ from the observed group frequencies.

## Results

On LMArena (23 models, 4 language subgroups), the Robust Lottery (ρ=0.4) achieves the highest held-out win rate in every subgroup: 41.89% English, 38.01% Polish, 37.09% Chinese, and 35.71% Russian, with 44.24% overall. This outperforms the standard Maximal Lottery (ρ=0) by 5.3–6.7 percentage points on the three non-English subgroups while matching it on English (41.89% vs. 40.78%) and overall (44.24% vs. 44.06%). On HUMAINE (15 models, 4 ethnic groups), the Robust Lottery (ρ=0.8) lifts the worst-group win rate from 0.21 (Mixed) to 0.41, a 20-point improvement, while maintaining 0.42–0.43 on all other groups. The robust bipartisan set typically contains 3–5 models, expanding from the single top aggregate performer as ρ increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard Maximal Lottery (ρ=0), which optimizes the aggregate preference matrix without any robustness constraint. The Robust Lottery dominates it on worst-group performance: on LMArena it improves Polish win rate by 5.3 pp (38.01% vs. 32.69%), Chinese by 5.0 pp (37.09% vs. 32.06%), and Russian by 6.7 pp (35.71% vs. 28.99%), while slightly exceeding it on English (41.89% vs. 40.78%) and overall (44.24% vs. 44.06%). On HUMAINE, the gap is even more dramatic for the worst-performing ethnic group (Mixed): the Robust Lottery achieves 0.41 versus 0.21 for the Maximal Lottery. The Robust Lottery also outperforms every single-model baseline (including the BT top-ranked Gemini 2.5 Pro) on every LMArena subgroup simultaneously, which no single model can do. The trade-off is modest: as ρ increases, the best-performing group's win rate decreases slightly (e.g., English drops from ~44% at ρ=0 to ~42% at ρ=0.4), reflecting the inherent robustness–accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the maximal lottery rule from social choice theory (Fishburn, 1984; Brandl et al., 2016) by wrapping it in a distributionally robust optimization framework (Duchi & Namkoong, 2021) over subpopulation mixture weights. It departs from the Bradley-Terry model that underpins current LLM leaderboards (LMArena, MedArena) by rejecting the one-dimensional latent-strength assumption, and it departs from standard maximal lotteries by identifying a new failure mode—brittleness to shifts in the annotator or prompt distribution—and addressing it with a minimax objective over an ambiguity set of margin matrices. The resulting 'robust lottery' inherits the bipartisan set concept (Laffond et al., 1993) and extends it to a 'robust bipartisan set,' while the specific ℓ₁-ball uncertainty structure yields a tractable O(mK) linear program via duality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and growing concern: LLM leaderboards that collapse heterogeneous preferences into a single ranking can reward models that fail on specific user subpopulations or task types. By providing worst-case win-rate guarantees across subgroups and a tractable LP that accommodates real-world constraints (cost, latency, benchmark floors), robust lotteries offer a principled path toward pluralistic model evaluation. Open questions include how to calibrate the robustness radius ρ in practice beyond the conservative finite-sample bound, whether the approach handles temporal distribution shift (new models, evolving user populations) as well as cross-sectional heterogeneity, and how to extend the set-of-winners output into a usable ranking for practitioners who expect a single leaderboard. The method also does not directly address preference manipulation or strategic voting, which remain relevant in competitive leaderboard settings.
