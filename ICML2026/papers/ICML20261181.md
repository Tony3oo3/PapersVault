## Problem

Partial-label learning (PLL) must train classifiers when each instance carries a set of candidate labels with only one correct. Early probabilistic methods (EM-based) directly approximate the true label posterior but are computationally prohibitive, while recent deep learning approaches rely on surrogate losses and heuristic label-refinement strategies that lack a principled probabilistic foundation. No existing method combines direct posterior approximation with the scalability of modern neural training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a d-dimensional feature vector (d ranges from 38 for bird-song to 3072 for CIFAR-10/100) together with a candidate label set s ⊆ {1,…,k} of size 2–6, and produces a probability distribution over the k classes as a point in the (k−1)-simplex. The variational distribution is a k-dimensional Dirichlet whose concentration parameters α_φ ≥ 1 are predicted by a softplus-activated network, guaranteeing a single mode. The framework is architecture-agnostic: for tabular data it uses an MLP backbone, and for image datasets it plugs in BLIP2-extracted 768-dimensional features.

## Method

VIPLL formulates label disambiguation as amortized variational inference: a neural network f_φ maps (x, s) to Dirichlet parameters α_φ that define the variational posterior q_φ(y|x,s), while a conditional VAE (CVAE) with m-dimensional latent variables models the generative term p_θ,γ(x|y). The key architectural choice is the Markov-equivalent factorization P(Y)·P(X|Y)·P(S|X,Y) rather than the standard P(X)·P(Y|X)·P(S|X,Y), which enables pre-training the CVAE decoder and explicitly modeling a maximum-entropy prior P(Y) constrained by candidate-set frequencies. Training minimizes a β-ELBO objective via stochastic gradient descent with b = b′ = 10 Monte Carlo samples per instance, preceded by a 500-epoch CVAE warm-up phase; the main loop alternates between sampling from the Dirichlet variational distribution, computing the CVAE reconstruction and KL terms, evaluating the candidate-set regularizer p(s|x,y) = (1/2^{k−1})Σ_{j∈s} y_j, and updating labeling vectors that condition the CVAE encoder.

## Results

VIPLL achieves the best test-set accuracy on 7 out of 10 datasets. On the real-world PLL datasets, it leads on bird-song (76.15%), lost (78.52%), and msrc-v2 (60.24%), while performing comparably on mir-flickr (68.49%) and yahoo-news (63.99%). On the supervised datasets with added candidates, VIPLL achieves 80.81% on MNIST, 74.87% on Fashion-MNIST, 96.64% on CIFAR-10, and 77.76% on CIFAR-100, all the best or statistically tied-best results. The ablation variant (using the standard discriminative factorization instead of the Markov-equivalent generative one) drops by 3.9–32.0 points across datasets, confirming the benefit of the proposed causal model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIPLL outperforms all nine baselines on bird-song (76.15% vs. next-best CROSEL 75.49%), lost (78.52% vs. PRODEN 73.44%), msrc-v2 (60.24% vs. PICO 56.82%), MNIST (80.81% vs. PICO 78.45%), Fashion-MNIST (74.87% vs. PICO 73.89%), CIFAR-10 (96.64% vs. PICO 93.08%), and CIFAR-100 (77.76% vs. CROSEL 77.68%, statistically tied). On mir-flickr, VIPLL (68.49%) is statistically tied with PRODEN (68.67%) and CEL (68.56%); on KMNIST it is tied with CROSEL (59.05%) and PRODEN (58.86%). The sole clear loss is on Yahoo News, where CROSEL achieves 67.10% versus VIPLL's 63.99%, a gap of over 3 points. The ablation variant (discriminative factorization) degrades substantially—e.g., 52.93% vs. 80.81% on MNIST and 45.74% vs. 77.76% on CIFAR-100—validating the Markov-equivalent generative model as a key design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIPLL bridges two previously separate lines of work in partial-label learning: the early probabilistic methods (Jin & Ghahramani, 2002; Liu & Dietterich, 2012) that directly approximated the label posterior via EM but suffered from poor scalability, and the recent deep-learning PLL methods (PRODEN, VALEN, PICO, CROSEL, CEL) that rely on surrogate losses and heuristic candidate-set refinement. The method builds on the amortized variational inference framework of Kingma & Welling (2014) and the conditional VAE of Sohn et al. (2015), using a CVAE as the generative model p(x|y) and a Dirichlet variational distribution for the label posterior, with the β-ELBO objective from Higgins et al. (2017). A key conceptual departure is the use of a Markov-equivalent generative factorization P(Y)P(X|Y)P(S|X,Y) instead of the standard discriminative P(X)P(Y|X)P(S|X,Y), justified by a formal Markov-equivalence proposition, which enables CVAE pre-training on unlabeled features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formulating PLL label disambiguation as amortized VI, the paper offers a principled alternative to the heuristic label-refinement strategies that dominate recent deep-learning PLL work, potentially improving robustness and interpretability. The architecture-agnostic design and the Markov-equivalence result provide a reusable theoretical tool for other weakly-supervised settings where generative and discriminative factorizations are interchangeable. Limitations include reliance on fixed-form variational distributions (Dirichlet, Gaussian), the added Monte Carlo sampling overhead, and the need for a CVAE warm-up phase; open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or high-dimensional datasets and whether more flexible variational families (e.g., normalizing flows) would further improve disambiguation quality.
