## Problem

Optimizing the LogSumExp (log-partition) function at scale is hindered by two coupled difficulties: computing exact gradients requires differentiating every exponential term, and replacing the full sum with a sampled batch introduces bias that only vanishes with large batch sizes. Moreover, the raw exponentials in the objective cause floating-point overflow in practice, especially under weak regularization. The paper addresses both issues by proposing a convexity- and smoothness-preserving approximation that admits unbiased stochastic gradients with uniformly bounded weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parametric loss function L(x,θ) (or a cost function c(x,y) with source/target measures for OT), a reference probability measure μ (empirical or continuous), and a single scalar hyperparameter ρ ∈ (0,1) that trades approximation accuracy against smoothness. It outputs optimized model parameters θ, dual transport potentials (v, α in the OT setting), and an approximated log-partition value F_ρ that satisfies F − O(ρ) ≤ F_ρ ≤ F. Unlike sampled LogSumExp estimators, the stochastic gradients here are unbiased for the surrogate objective and their second moments scale as ρ⁻², independent of the number of terms n.

## Method

The key insight is to replace the KL divergence in the Gibbs variational principle with a new f-divergence called the Safe KL divergence, whose generator f_ρ(t) = t log t + 1 + (1−ρt)/ρ · log(1−ρt) imposes a hard upper bound 1/ρ on the density ratio dν/dμ. The convex conjugate of f_ρ is a rescaled SoftPlus, (1/ρ)log(1+ρeˢ)−1, which transforms the log-partition functional into F_ρ(φ;μ) = inf_α α − 1 + (1/ρ)∫ log(1+ρe^{φ(x)−α}) dμ(x), moving the expectation outside the logarithm. This yields an unbiased stochastic subgradient oracle with second-moment bound ρ⁻²(‖g‖²+1), enabling standard projected SGD with a convergence rate of O(R√(M²+1)/(ρ√N)) + O(ρ). The parameter ρ simultaneously controls the approximation gap (vanishing as ρ→0) and the smoothness of the surrogate (1/ρ-smooth conjugate), and the construction extends naturally from finite sums to continuous-measure settings such as entropy-regularized optimal transport.

## Results

On KL-DRO (California Housing, n=20,640), the proposed Safe KL estimator with ρ=10⁻³ achieves an objective of 2.1±0.0 at λ=1, |D|=10, compared to 20.0±0.9 for the Levy et al. batch-softmax baseline — a nearly 10× reduction. On UOT-DRO (MNIST, 25% label noise), the proposed method with ρ=10⁻¹ reaches 0.37±0.07 at γ=λ=1 versus 0.81±0.01 for the Wang et al. exponent-sum baseline. In continuous eOT (MNIST→EMNIST), the proposed approach remains stable at ε=10⁻⁴ where LSOT overflows after ~5k iterations, and converges with a 100× larger learning rate (10⁻² vs. 10⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LSOT (Seguy et al., 2018) on continuous eOT, the proposed method wins decisively: LSOT overflows at ε=10⁻² and 10⁻⁴ with lr=10⁻⁴ (diverging after ~5k iterations), and with lr=10⁻⁵ converges so slowly as to be practically useless, while the proposed approach converges stably at all ε values with lr up to 10⁻². Against Genevay et al. (2016) kernel SGD on the dual eOT objective, the proposed approximate semi-dual formulation achieves a lower optimality gap in ~2×10⁴ iterations versus the baseline's extremely slow convergence (requiring C=10⁻³ to avoid divergence). Against Levy et al. (2020) batch-softmax gradient estimator on KL-DRO, the proposed method wins for small batches (|D|=10, 10²) with large margins (e.g., 2.1 vs. 20.0 at λ=1, |D|=10) but ties or slightly loses at |D|=10³ where the baseline's bias is negligible. Against Wang et al. (2024) exponent-sum UOT-DRO, the proposed method consistently achieves lower objective values across all (γ,λ) configurations (e.g., 0.37 vs. 0.81 at γ=λ=1) and permits 100× larger learning rates without overflow, translating to better worst-class ROC AUC (0.45 vs. 0.27) under 85% label noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant strategy of designing specialized optimizers for the original LogSumExp objective (Qi et al., 2021; Wang & Yang, 2022; Yuan et al., 2022) or using biased batch-based gradient estimators (Levy et al., 2020). Instead, it modifies the variational formulation itself by replacing the KL divergence in the Gibbs principle with a new f-divergence (Safe KL), yielding a SoftPlus-based surrogate whose stochastic gradients are unbiased for the approximating objective. The construction subsumes Bouchard's (2007) LogSumExp bound as the ρ=1 special case and recovers the smooth CVaR approximation of Soma & Yoshida (2020) up to a reparameterization, unifying these previously separate ideas under a single f-divergence framework. In the OT and DRO applications, it replaces the exponential terms in LSOT (Seguy et al., 2018) and the exponent-sum objective of Wang et al. (2024) with bounded SoftPlus terms, directly addressing the floating-point overflow that limits those baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Safe KL approximation provides a general-purpose, drop-in replacement for LogSumExp in any stochastic optimization pipeline, eliminating the overflow failures that force practitioners to use prohibitively small learning rates or large batches. The uniform density bound (dν*/dμ < 1/ρ) gives the divergence a natural capacity-constraint interpretation, making it potentially useful beyond the LogSumExp setting (e.g., unbalanced OT, robust statistics). Key limitations include the need to tune ρ (controlling a bias–smoothness tradeoff with no adaptive procedure yet), the loss of application-specific structural properties that KL enjoys, and a relatively narrow experimental scope (MNIST/EMNIST, California housing, ACS PUMS). Open questions include whether adaptive ρ scheduling is possible, how Safe KL interacts with the rich theory of KL-regularized MDPs and information geometry, and whether the approach extends to non-convex parametric losses where the convexity guarantee no longer applies.
