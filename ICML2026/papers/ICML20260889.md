## Problem

Solving inverse problems with flow-based generative models via latent space optimization requires backpropagating through the full ODE trajectory, which incurs memory costs that scale linearly with the number of time steps and suffers from poor conditioning due to long compositions of neural networks. Existing single-shooting methods like D-Flow are therefore impractical for fine-grained temporal discretizations or high-resolution settings such as latent diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy measurements y ∈ R^m, a known forward operator A (e.g., Radon transform, Gaussian blur, bicubic downsampling, box mask), and a frozen pre-trained flow matching model v_θ. For latent flow models (Stable Diffusion 3.5), it additionally requires a text prompt and a decoder D mapping latent variables to pixel space. The output is a single reconstructed image x_* in pixel space, obtained as D(z_*) for latent models or directly as x_* for pixel-space flows. All optimization is inference-time only—no retraining of the flow model is performed.

## Method

MS-Flow partitions the flow ODE trajectory into K segments with intermediate shooting points {x_0, …, x_K} and replaces the hard global ODE constraint with soft quadratic trajectory-matching penalties between consecutive points. Optimization proceeds via alternating minimization: a backward-sweep coordinate descent updates the shooting points to enforce local flow dynamics and a radial Gaussian prior on x_0, while a separate data-consistency step (closed-form Tikhonov solve for linear problems, or proximal/ADMM for non-smooth objectives) updates the terminal estimate x_*. A key efficiency gain comes from a Jacobian-free approximation (J_F ≈ I) that eliminates all backpropagation through the flow network, reducing per-iteration cost to O(LK·C_fwd) and peak memory to O(M_net), constant in the number of discretization steps. The paper proves monotone descent and stationarity of the inner coordinate-descent iterates under standard smoothness assumptions, and validates the approach on CT, deblurring, inpainting, and super-resolution tasks, as well as on Stable Diffusion 3.5 for latent-space deblurring.

## Results

On CelebA, MS-Flow achieves the best PSNR across three of four image recovery tasks: 39.04 dB on Gaussian deblurring, 36.41 dB on 2× super-resolution, and 28.52 dB on 4× super-resolution, while PnP-Flow leads on box inpainting (35.87 vs. 34.77 PSNR). On FFHQ with Stable Diffusion 3.5, MS-Flow reaches 30.65 PSNR / 0.837 SSIM at σ_noise=0.01, outperforming FlowDPS (27.78/0.751) and FLAIR (29.18/0.783). Computationally, MS-Flow with the Jacobian-free approximation (L=1) runs in 2.44–4.44 s per image across n_t=3–12 discretization steps, compared to 7.43–175.57 s for D-Flow, and maintains constant GPU memory regardless of the number of ODE steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D-Flow (the single-shooting baseline), MS-Flow gains +2.97 dB PSNR on Gaussian deblurring (39.04 vs. 36.07) and +0.92 dB on 2× super-resolution (36.41 vs. 35.49), but trails by 1.41 dB on 4× super-resolution (28.52 vs. 29.93) where D-Flow's end-to-end backpropagation still has an edge. Against Flow-Prior, OT-ODE, and PnP-Flow, MS-Flow leads on PSNR for deblurring and both super-resolution tasks, while PnP-Flow retains a 1.1 dB advantage on box inpainting (35.87 vs. 34.77). On the large-scale FFHQ experiment with Stable Diffusion 3.5, MS-Flow outperforms FlowDPS by +2.87 dB PSNR at low noise (30.65 vs. 27.78) and matches or exceeds FLAIR on PSNR/SSIM at σ=0.01 (30.65/0.837 vs. 29.18/0.783), though FLAIR achieves better perceptual quality (lower LPIPS: 0.199 vs. 0.268) and wins across all metrics at the higher noise level σ=0.1. In terms of efficiency, MS-Flow with the Jacobian-free approximation (L=1) is 3–40× faster than D-Flow depending on the number of time steps, and its GPU memory remains constant while D-Flow scales linearly with n_t.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the classical multiple-shooting principle from optimal control (Bock & Plitt, 1984) to the latent-space optimization (LSO) problem with flow-based generative models, departing from the single-shooting strategy used by D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024) and analogous diffusion-model methods (DMPlug, BIRD). Rather than backpropagating through the full ODE trajectory from a single initial latent code, MS-Flow introduces intermediate shooting points as explicit optimization variables and enforces flow dynamics locally via soft trajectory-matching penalties, solved by alternating minimization with coordinate descent. A key practical departure is the Jacobian-free approximation (J_F ≈ I) that eliminates backpropagation through the flow model entirely, building on the observation that neglecting model Jacobians is standard in diffusion-model inference (e.g., DreamFusion). The paper also positions itself against guidance-based and plug-and-play flow solvers (Flow-Prior, OT-ODE, PnP-Flow, FlowDPS, FLAIR) that modify sampling dynamics rather than optimizing the trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MS-Flow's constant memory footprint (independent of ODE discretization steps) and Jacobian-free variant make it practical for high-resolution inverse problems with large latent flow models like Stable Diffusion 3.5, where single-shooting methods become infeasible. The convergence guarantees for the coordinate-descent trajectory updates (monotone descent, stationarity of limit points) provide theoretical grounding for an inference-time optimization procedure that is otherwise heuristic. Open questions include extending the framework to SDE-based generative models (diffusion, score-based methods) with stochastic consistency constraints, and whether adaptive penalty scheduling (e.g., ADMM-style updates) can further improve convergence. A limitation is that the method still requires multiple forward passes through the flow model per iteration, and the performance gains over strong baselines like FLAIR are modest on perceptual metrics (LPIPS).
