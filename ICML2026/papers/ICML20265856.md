## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is computationally efficient but yields inferior generalization compared to reinforcement learning (RL), primarily because RL operates on on-policy data that preserves the model's native distribution. Prior work has identified this distributional gap qualitatively but lacks a principled, quantitative criterion for measuring how well a given token or sequence aligns with the model's own generative distribution. The paper asks whether the SFT-to-RL chasm can be bridged by explicitly aligning both the training data and the loss objective with the model's distribution, while retaining SFT's data efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (base, instruct, or thinking variant) and a fixed dataset of question–answer pairs (e.g., Numina-Math, DeepMath). At the data level, Hinted Decoding consumes a question and its verified ground-truth answer, then produces a chain-of-thought response that is simultaneously correct and statistically in-distribution for the model, using only the same model in two different prompt configurations (no separate teacher model). At the loss level, IDFT consumes the per-token log-probabilities and conditional entropies computed during a forward pass to produce a reweighted cross-entropy objective. The final output is a fine-tuned LLM evaluated on math competition, general math, and general reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

The paper establishes Distribution Discriminant Theory (DDT), which frames in-distribution detection as a binary hypothesis test and proves via Signal Detection Theory that the Centered Log-Likelihood φ_t = log p_t(x_t) + H[p_t] is the SNR-optimal discriminant, eliminating the inter-context variance that plagues raw log-likelihood. Two applications follow. First, In-Distribution Fine-Tuning (IDFT) defines a modulation coefficient γ_t = exp(−φ_t) that adaptively rescales the SFT loss: OOD tokens (φ_t ≪ 0) receive gradient suppression (γ_t > 1), in-distribution tokens (φ_t ≈ 0) recover standard learning (γ_t ≈ 1), and high-confidence tokens (φ_t > 0) get reinforced (γ_t < 1), yielding the objective L_IDFT = −(1/L)Σ p_t(x_t)^{γ_t} log p_t(x_t). Second, Hinted Decoding formulates a variational problem that geometrically mixes an answer-aware 'imitator' distribution p_I with the model's own 'drafter' distribution p_m, with the mixing weight λ = clip(β·H̄_I, 0, 1) driven by the imitator's normalized entropy, so that confident tokens follow the answer while uncertain, style-determining tokens follow the model's native distribution; a false-positive detection mechanism switches to drafter-only decoding after the CoT/answer delimiter to preserve consistency.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B (base), HD+IDFT achieves 27.37% on Math-C (competition math: AIME24, AMC23, OlympiadBench) and 47.07% on Math-G (general math), surpassing DPO@16 (22.43% / 45.95%) and SimPO@16 (20.23% / 38.45%) while using only 214.8 GPU-hours versus 276.4 and 230.1 respectively. On the stronger Qwen2.5-7B-instruct model, HD+IDFT reaches 36.21% Math-C and 53.50% Math-G, outperforming all offline RL baselines (DPO: 34.49%, SimPO: 34.20%, Rej@16: 34.19%) at 135.4 GPU-hours. On DeepSeek-R1-distill-Qwen-7B (thinking model), HD+IDFT attains 43.83% Math-C and 55.51% Math-G, matching or exceeding SimPO (43.80% / 54.43%) and DPO (43.35% / 55.18%) at 425.5 GPU-hours versus 801.5–869.6 for offline RL methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HD+IDFT consistently outperforms all offline RL baselines (DPO, SimPO, Rej@16) on Math-C across all three model types while using 30–50% less compute (e.g., 214.8 vs. 276.4 GPU-hours on base; 425.5 vs. 821.2 on thinking). Against improved-SFT baselines, HD+IDFT dominates DFT by a wide margin on Math-C (27.37 vs. 17.09 on base; 36.21 vs. 30.22 on instruct) and EAFT by a smaller but consistent gap (27.37 vs. 23.95 on base), confirming that the CLL-based reweighting is more effective than raw probability or entropy-threshold gating. On the instruct model, where catastrophic forgetting is most severe, HD+IDFT (36.21% Math-C) surpasses DPO (34.49%) and SimPO (34.20%) by ~1.7–2.0 points, while Self-Distill (35.09%) falls short, validating that statistical in-distribution alignment (measured by φ) is distinct from surface-level stylistic imitation. On the thinking model, HD+IDFT (43.83%) edges out SimPO (43.80%) and DPO (43.35%) on Math-C and leads on Math-G (55.51% vs. 55.18% and 54.43%), with HD+SFT (44.63%) actually leading on Math-C, suggesting the data-level re-alignment contributes most when the model's style is highly specialized. General reasoning scores (MMLU-stem + ARC-challenge) remain stable or improve across all methods, indicating that the distributional alignment mechanism mitigates catastrophic forgetting without sacrificing general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of enhanced-SFT work (DFT, EAFT, ASFT, ProFit) that reweights the token-level loss to improve generalization, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) it provides a theoretically grounded criterion (Centered Log-Likelihood, proven SNR-optimal via Signal Detection Theory) rather than ad-hoc probability or entropy thresholds, and (2) it adds a data-level technique (Hinted Decoding) that prior loss-only methods lack. The self-distillation baseline from Shenfeld et al. (2026) is repurposed as one stream in a two-stream variational decoding objective, with the model's own distribution as the second stream. The theoretical foundation draws on Cox's (1961) likelihood-ratio framework and martingale concentration inequalities, connecting classical statistical hypothesis testing to LLM token-level distribution discrimination in a way prior SFT/RL comparison papers (Chu et al. 2025, Xu et al. 2021) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous quantification of what 'in-distribution' means for an LLM at the token level, filling a gap that prior SFT-vs-RL analyses identified qualitatively but never formalized. The practical implication—that a well-designed SFT pipeline can match or exceed offline RL (DPO, SimPO) at roughly one-third the compute in verifiable-reward settings—offers a viable alternative where RL is infeasible (agent tasks, proof problems, biased rewards). Key limitations: experiments are confined to math/code with objective correctness, the method is offline-only (no online RL comparison), and the authors acknowledge that for preference learning and value alignment, RLHF remains necessary. Open questions include whether the DDT framework transfers to non-verifiable domains, whether an online variant (regenerating data per batch) closes the remaining gap to PPO-style RL, and whether the approach yields larger gains in under-trained settings like agents.
