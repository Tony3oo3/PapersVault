## Problem

Existing fairness criteria for generative models (Equalized Generative Odds and Matching Generative Odds) only balance the proportion of samples generated per sensitive group, leaving generation quality across groups uncontrolled. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that even when these proportion-based criteria are perfectly satisfied, a model can exhibit arbitrarily large disparities in f-divergence between target and generated conditional distributions for different groups, meaning some groups are modeled with far lower fidelity and diversity than others.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the method takes random noise as input and produces 64×64 or 1024×1024 RGB images (FFHQ, CIFAR-10, AFHQv2) using pre-trained EDM-VP/VE checkpoints; for text generation, it takes a text prompt specifying the sensitive attribute (e.g., 'a male/female person') and produces biography text using LoRA-fine-tuned LLaMA-3.2-Chat (1B/3B) or Gemma-3 (4B). Evaluation is performed per sensitive group using Topological Precision and Recall (TopP&R) with DINOv2 ViT embeddings for images and Qwen3-4B text embeddings for text, with a fine-tuned gender classifier or keyword oracle defining group membership.

## Method

The paper introduces Equalized Generative Treatment (EGT), a fairness criterion requiring the f-divergence between target and model conditional distributions to be approximately equal across all sensitive groups (Definition 4.1). Theorem 4.3 establishes that under δ-EGT, the global f-divergence is lower-bounded by the worst group-conditional divergence in the conditional closure of the model family, up to tolerance δ, motivating Min-Max optimization as the natural training objective. Practically, Min-Max fine-tuning operates by computing per-group empirical training losses (denoising MSE for diffusion, negative log-likelihood for LLMs) on each minibatch, maintaining an exponential moving average of group losses, selecting the worst-performing group, and updating the model using only that group's gradient. For diffusion models, the max is taken per noise level to prevent any subset of the diffusion trajectory from disproportionately harming a group. This approach is validated against conditional training and reweighted-loss baselines on both image and text generation, showing consistent EGT disparity reductions across all settings.

## Results

On FFHQ with EDM-VP, Min-Max fine-tuning reduces the precision/recall disparity (δ-PR) from 2.2 (pretrained) to 0.3, with δ-P collapsing from 2.0 to 0.1 and δ-R from 4.2 to 0.2. On EDM-VE, δ-PR drops from 2.3 to 0.8. On Wikipedia Biographies, initial disparities are substantially larger (e.g., δ-PR of 40.43 for LLaMA-3.2-Chat 3B pretrained), and Min-Max achieves the most consistent reduction across all LLM variants (e.g., 35.13 for LLaMA-3.2-Chat 3B, 30.79 for Gemma-3 4B pretrained), though absolute gains are smaller due to the higher computational cost of LLM fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Min-Max against three baselines: the pretrained (unconstrained) model, conditional training (attribute-conditional generation), and reweighted loss (the reweighting strategy from Choi et al., 2020, which rescales per-sample losses by group-dependent weights to enforce EGO). On FFHQ, Min-Max decisively outperforms both reweighting and conditional training on δ-PR: for EDM-VP, Min-Max achieves 0.3 vs. 0.6 (reweighted) and 5.2 (conditional); for EDM-VE, 0.8 vs. 2.2 (reweighted) and 3.5 (conditional). Conditional training can reduce δ-P but often worsens δ-R (e.g., 6.8 vs. 4.2 pretrained on VP), while reweighting shows inconsistent gains across settings. On Wikipedia Biographies, Min-Max again provides the most consistent δ-PR reduction across all four LLM variants (LLaMA-3.2-Chat 1B/3B, Gemma-3 4B pt/it), though the margins are smaller (e.g., 35.13 vs. 35.16 for LLaMA-3.2-Chat 3B, where reweighting is nearly tied). The correlation analysis (Table 3) further confirms that proportion-based metrics (δ-MGO, δ-EGO) have negligible association with quality disparities (|r| ≤ 0.20), validating that the reweighting baseline's proportion-matching objective does not predict EGT performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of fairness work in generative modeling (Choi et al. 2020, Zameshina et al. 2022, Teo et al. 2023), which focuses on matching or equalizing group proportions (MGO/EGO), by introducing a quality-based criterion (EGT) that requires comparable f-divergences across sensitive groups. The Min-Max training scheme is adapted from distributionally robust optimization (Oren et al. 2019; Sagawa et al. 2019), where worst-group loss minimization is used for robustness, and repurposed here as a practical mechanism to enforce EGT in generative model training. The theoretical framework builds on the f-divergence formalism already used in generative model training (Nowozin et al. 2016; Grover et al. 2018) and evaluation (Verine et al. 2023), providing a unified lens for both training objectives and fairness analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal proof that proportion-based fairness criteria are inherently brittle (Theorem 3.1) provides a rigorous justification for moving beyond EGO/MGO, and the EGT definition offers a principled, f-divergence-grounded alternative that aligns with how generative models are actually trained and evaluated. The practical Min-Max approach is simple to implement (EMA-smoothed worst-group loss selection) and consistently reduces disparities across both image and text generation, making it accessible to practitioners. Key limitations include the inherent trade-off where reducing group disparity degrades the best-performing group, the restriction to f-divergences (extension to Wasserstein or FID-based criteria remains open), and the computational cost of LLM fine-tuning that limits how much optimization can reshape representations. Open questions include whether analogous results hold for other divergence families and whether EGT can be enforced in one-shot or few-shot generation settings.
