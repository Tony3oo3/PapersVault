## Problem

Existing benchmark compression methods construct item representations from binary correctness patterns across many source models, but this becomes statistically unstable when the source pool is small (e.g., newly released benchmarks with limited historical evaluations), causing the item-space geometry to collapse and degrading both coreset selection and score extrapolation. The paper argues that discrete 0/1 labels are a lossy projection of the model's decision process and that the continuous hidden states produced during inference encode a denser, more informative signal about item structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes final-layer hidden states (of the final output token) and binary correctness labels from a small pool of source models (|S| ≤ 10) evaluated on the full benchmark, plus binary scores from an unseen target model on only the selected coreset of K items (K ∈ {10,…,200}). It produces a coreset of K representative items and, from the target model's K scores, a full-benchmark accuracy estimate and a Spearman-ranked ordering of all target models. Hidden states are captured via a forward hook during greedy-decoding inference, adding ~0.4% wall-clock overhead and 0.1–1.3 GB of storage per benchmark.

## Method

REPCORE operates in three phases. First, model-specific linear projections map each source model's heterogeneous hidden state into a shared 4096-dim space, and a source-agnostic MLP (4096→512→128→32) compresses these into 32-dim aligned embeddings, trained end-to-end with a cross-entropy classifier on correctness labels to steer the latent space toward evaluation-relevant signals. Second, consensus embeddings are formed by averaging aligned vectors across source models, L2-normalizing, and partitioning the unit hypersphere into K clusters that maximize within-cluster cosine similarity; the item closest to each centroid becomes an anchor. Third, a per-target ridge regressor is fit on the coreset using a single scalar feature (the mean source correctness per item) to predict scores on all remaining items, yielding the full-benchmark estimate. Spectral analysis of the aligned space reveals that PC1 encodes item difficulty while higher components capture task-specific reasoning patterns, and the low-rank PC1–PC2 subspace actually outperforms the full 32-dim embedding at moderate-to-large coreset budgets, indicating that the most generalizable structural signal is concentrated in a small interpretable subspace.

## Results

Under the source-scarce regime (|S|=10), REPCORE consistently achieves the best Spearman rank correlation across all five benchmarks and coreset budgets. On GSM8K with K=20, REPCORE attains ρ=0.872, surpassing GP-IRT (0.839) and ANCHORPOINTS (0.802); on BBH with K=50, it reaches ρ=0.913 vs. GP-IRT's 0.901. In terms of MAE, REPCORE typically yields the lowest estimation error (e.g., 0.032 on GSM8K at K=50), while GP-IRT occasionally matches or slightly beats it on MAE due to error cancellation, as confirmed by REPCORE's superior item-level Agreement (e.g., 0.727 vs. 0.693 on BBH at K=50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPCORE outperforms ANCHORPOINTS (Vivek et al., 2024) on Spearman ρ across every benchmark and budget, with the gap widening at smaller K (e.g., +0.070 on GSM8K at K=20); ANCHORPOINTS also suffers substantially higher MAE (e.g., 0.144 vs. 0.032 on GSM8K at K=50), reflecting its reliance on sparse binary response vectors that collapse under source scarcity. Against GP-IRT (Maia Polo et al., 2024), REPCORE wins on ranking correlation in nearly all settings (e.g., +0.033 on GSM8K at K=20, +0.012 on BBH at K=50), while GP-IRT occasionally achieves marginally lower MAE (marked with *)—a gap the authors attribute to error cancellation, as REPCORE's item-level Agreement is consistently 3–9 percentage points higher. Among the auxiliary baselines, EffiEval (Wang et al., 2025b) averages ρ=0.766 and MAE=0.111 across all benchmarks, and TailoredBench (Yuan et al., 2025) averages ρ=0.747 and MAE=0.075, both substantially below REPCORE's ρ=0.842 and MAE=0.054, with the gap most pronounced on BBH where EffiEval drops to ρ=0.628 on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant output-based benchmark compression paradigm (AnchorPoints, tinyBenchmarks/GP-IRT, TailoredBench) by replacing discrete correctness vectors with continuous hidden-state representations aligned across heterogeneous architectures. Conceptually, it repurposes findings from the interpretability literature—where hidden states are shown to encode difficulty, uncertainty, and reasoning patterns (Azaria & Mitchell, 2023; Orgad et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025)—for the distinct task of coreset selection and score extrapolation. The cross-model alignment via model-specific projections plus a shared MLP is a new architectural contribution not present in prior compression methods, which operate entirely in the output space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-scarce regime (≤10 source models) is a practical bottleneck for newly released benchmarks and long-tail evaluation settings, where output-based methods degrade sharply due to sparse binary signals. REPCORE's demonstrated robustness at |S|=5 (ρ=0.860 vs. AnchorPoints' 0.640) and its spectral analysis showing task-specific structure beyond scalar difficulty provide both a practical tool and structural evidence for why internal representations are a richer item signal. Key limitations include the requirement for hidden-state access (excluding closed-source models) and the restriction to binary-scored benchmarks; extending to continuous metrics and proprietary systems remains open.
