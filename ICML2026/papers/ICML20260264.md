## Problem

Neural networks are nonlinear, placing them outside the reach of classical linear algebra tools such as SVD, pseudoinverse, orthogonal projection, and spectral analysis. The paper asks whether one can identify non-standard vector spaces in which a given neural network is exactly linear, thereby making the full arsenal of linear algebra applicable to nonlinear mappings without sacrificing expressiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260264/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For one-step flow matching, a noise vector is mapped to a generated image (MNIST 32×32, CelebA 64×64) in a single forward pass, replacing hundreds of ODE integration steps. For style transfer, a content image plus a style index (fed to a hypernetwork producing A_style) yields a style-transferred image, with linear interpolation between style matrices enabling continuous style blending. For the idempotent generative network, arbitrary inputs (pure noise, structured patterns, out-of-distribution shapes) are projected onto the learned data manifold, and the same architecture also handles 40-attribute CelebA classification and 96-step weather forecasting as additional validation tasks.

## Method

The Linearizer wraps a linear core matrix A between two invertible neural networks: f(x) = g_y^{-1}(A g_x(x)). The key insight is that the induced vector spaces, with addition and scaling defined via transport of structure (v₁ ⊕_g v₂ = g^{-1}(g(v₁)+g(v₂))), make f an exactly linear operator, so SVD, pseudoinverse, transpose, and composition all transfer directly from A to f. For one-step flow matching, the time-dependent core A_t is produced by a low-rank MLP, and the multi-step Euler/RK4 integration collapses to a single matrix product B in latent space, yielding one-step generation. For idempotency, A is constrained to a binary diagonal projector (eigenvalues 0 or 1) via the straight-through estimator, guaranteeing f(f(x))=f(x) by architecture rather than by loss. The invertible networks use 6 blocks of affine coupling (with tiny U-Net conditioners) for diffusion/style tasks or additive-coupling CNNs for the IGN, and all components are trained end-to-end.

## Results

On CelebA (64×64), the Linearizer achieves FID 127 with 100 iterative steps and FID 131 with a single collapsed step (100→1), with a mean squared error of only 3.0×10⁻⁴ between one-step and multi-step generations. Inversion-reconstruction quality is strong: PSNR 33.4 and LPIPS 0.006 on CelebA, PSNR 31.6 and LPIPS 0.008 on MNIST. On CelebA face-attribute classification, the Linearizer reaches 87.6% test accuracy, surpassing a ResNet-50 baseline (86.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the weather-forecasting regression task (96 past steps → future prediction), the Linearizer outperforms Autoformer (Wu et al., 2021) at every horizon, e.g. MSE 0.167 vs. 0.247 at 96 steps and 0.368 vs. 0.411 at 720 steps. Against the stronger PatchTST baseline (Nie, 2022), the Linearizer is slightly behind by 0.008–0.034 MSE depending on horizon (e.g. 0.167 vs. 0.159 at 96 steps; 0.368 vs. 0.334 at 720 steps). On CelebA face-attribute classification, the Linearizer achieves 87.6% test accuracy, beating the ResNet-50 baseline by 1.5 percentage points. The paper also ablates the role of linearity: replacing the linear core A with a nonlinear MLP of comparable capacity degrades FID from 127 to 157, confirming that the linear bottleneck is what enables the one-step collapse rather than raw model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

The Linearizer repurposes invertible neural networks (NICE, RealNVP, Glow) not for density estimation as in normalizing flows, but as algebraic coordinate maps that transport vector-space structure, making an arbitrary nonlinear mapping exactly linear in the induced spaces. It is explicitly positioned against Koopman theory, which linearizes dynamics via an infinite-dimensional observable dictionary, whereas the Linearizer learns finite-dimensional invertible maps directly and allows distinct input/output coordinates and rectangular cores. The one-step flow-matching application departs from distillation-based approaches (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models, KDM) by achieving single-step generation through the algebraic closure of linear operators rather than student-teacher training, and the idempotency application extends IGN (Shocher et al., 2024) by replacing loss-based approximate idempotency with architectural enforcement via a binary diagonal core.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework unlocks the full linear-algebra toolkit (SVD, Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, orthogonal projection, matrix powers) for nonlinear mappings, enabling capabilities like exact inversion of flow models and globally idempotent projectors that were previously intractable. The one-step sampling result is practically significant if it scales, since it eliminates the dominant inference cost of diffusion/flow models without distillation. However, current experiments are limited to MNIST 32×32 and CelebA 64×64 with FIDs (123–131) well below state-of-the-art, invertible backbones are harder to train, and the expressivity theory remains incomplete—scaling to high-resolution generation and characterizing the precise capacity limits of the linear bottleneck are the key open questions.
