## Problem

Current physiological waveform AI (ECG, PPG, EEG) treats biosignals as generic time series and optimizes end-to-end prediction, obscuring undetected wrongness under acquisition artifacts, missing leads, and device heterogeneity. Physiological foundation models learn strong perceptual representations but rarely expose auditable evidence objects, while LLM-centric adaptations produce fluent rationales that are not guaranteed to be entailed by the raw waveform. The paper argues for a reframing from prediction to verifiable physiological waveform reasoning, where every clinical decision must be reconstructable from localized, measurable signal evidence and replayable procedures.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a multivariate physiological waveform tensor X ∈ R^{C×T} (e.g., 12-lead ECG at 500 Hz, PPG, or EEG) together with a task query or clinical question. Rather than emitting a single label, the output is an episode-level Verification Ledger: a structured record linking signal-quality summaries, localized events with explicit time/lead indices, unit-consistent measurements (e.g., QTc in ms), tool provenance (name, version, parameters, input segment), deterministic check results, and a final decision that may include an abstain or escalate flag. A decision is credited only if it can be re-derived by replaying the logged procedures, making the output inherently auditable rather than a post-hoc narrative.

## Method

The paper proposes a dual-process architecture inspired by cognitive science. System 1 (Waveform-Language Alignment) couples raw biosignals to a language model via four routes of increasing granularity: measurement prompting (serializing extracted indices into structured prompts), vision-language alignment (rendering waveforms as images for MLLM reasoning), embedding alignment (feeding continuous encoder vectors or adapter-mapped PhysioFM representations into the LLM), and discrete token alignment (discretizing signal patches into autoregressive tokens). System 2 (Agentic Plan-Act-Verify) is a controller that iteratively plans, acts, and verifies through three roles: a Curator that performs hypothesis-driven active perception by requesting additional temporal context or lead subsets from System 1, an Analyst that executes deterministic measurement tools (delineation, interval computation, statistical checks) with full provenance logging, and a Critic that stress-tests intermediate claims against physiological constraints and guideline logic, triggering backtracking or principled abstention when evidence is insufficient. Three closed-loop interfaces (verification-object querying, an auditable tool-run ledger, and human oversight with feedback-to-memory) make the entire trajectory replayable and budget-aware, shifting evaluation from single-shot accuracy to episode-level traceability, replayability, counterfactual robustness, and calibrated abstention.

## Results

This is a position paper that proposes a conceptual framework rather than reporting empirical results. No quantitative benchmarks, accuracy numbers, or performance metrics are presented. The paper's contribution is a dual-process architecture (System 1: waveform-language alignment; System 2: agentic Plan-Act-Verify reasoning) and a proposed evaluation protocol (Verification Ledger with traceability, replayability, counterfactual robustness, and calibrated abstention) for future physiological waveform reasoning systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No direct empirical comparisons are made, as this is a position paper. The paper qualitatively contrasts two existing model families—physiological foundation models (PhysioFMs) and LLM-centric adaptations—and argues both are insufficient for verifiable waveform reasoning. PhysioFMs are characterized as strong at Level-1 perceptual representations but weak at exposing auditable evidence objects or implementing guideline-constrained decision logic. LLM-centric methods are characterized as improving linguistic form of reasoning but relying on lossy waveform interfaces and producing rationales not guaranteed to be entailed by the raw signal. The proposed dual-process framework is positioned as bridging both gaps, but no head-to-head experimental validation is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes two largely separate research threads—physiological foundation models (PhysioFMs) that learn strong perceptual representations but remain prediction-centric, and LLM-centric adaptations that offer linguistic reasoning but have shallow, lossy waveform grounding—into a unified dual-process architecture. It adapts agentic reasoning frameworks from the LLM literature (Chain-of-Thought for linear-chain waveform reasoning, Tree-of-Thoughts for branch-structured differential diagnosis, and Reflexion for the Critic's constraint-checking loop) to the physiological waveform domain, where they have not previously been applied in a structured, tool-grounded form. The paper departs from both model families by insisting that the output interface must be a Verification Ledger of localized, unit-consistent, replayable evidence objects rather than a single prediction or a fluent but ungrounded narrative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete safety gap—undetected wrongness under acquisition artifacts, missing leads, and device heterogeneity—that high average AUROC does not address, and proposes a principled evaluation protocol (traceability, replayability, counterfactual robustness, calibrated abstention) that the field currently lacks. As a position paper with no empirical implementation, its main limitation is that the dual-process Plan-Act-Verify loop and Verification Ledger remain a blueprint; whether LLMs can reliably execute deterministic tool calls, maintain cross-lead consistency checks, and calibrate abstention under realistic budgets is entirely untested. Key open questions include how to resolve the data-reasoning mismatch (public datasets lack intermediate evidence annotations needed to supervise verifiable inference) and whether the proposed evaluation criteria can be operationalized into reproducible benchmarks without prohibitive annotation cost.
