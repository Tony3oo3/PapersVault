## Problem

Integrating LLM-powered creative enhancement into real-time ad auctions introduces significant inference costs that disrupt traditional mechanism design, yet no framework exists for the platform to strategically allocate these costs while accounting for how budget-constrained autobidding agents respond to enhanced ad qualities. The resulting Stackelberg game is further complicated by equilibrium multiplicity in the follower subgame and non-stationary dynamics from concurrent learning, making both the platform's optimization objective ill-defined and standard single-timescale RL inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The platform consumes original ad titles, user context features (search history, service usage sequences), agent budgets, baseline CTRs, and per-click valuations, and produces an investment vector c^t ∈ R^I_{≥0} specifying computational effort (token budget) allocated per agent. A key design constraint is the predict-then-execute workflow: agents bid on predicted enhanced CTRs computed via a deterministic concave estimator φ, while actual GPT-4o inference is executed only for the winning ad, keeping latency within real-time auction bounds. The auction then outputs per-click bids, a single-slot GSP allocation, and a second-price clearing payment net of the winner's inference cost.

## Method

The framework has three coupled components. First, the Platform-Investment Mechanism (PIM) models the platform's creative enhancement as a strategic investment in a 5-stage sequential game, where a concave estimator φ(α, c) predicts CTR lift from investment c, resolving the latency-cost tension. Second, for the follower subgame, Tikhonov regularization (ε/2·λ²) is added to the dual objective to guarantee a unique PNE (via Diagonal Strict Convexity), and the ODDB-R algorithm uses projected stochastic gradient descent on dual variables with decaying step sizes η_t = z/√t, achieving O(√T) no-regret and almost-sure convergence to the regularized equilibrium. Third, for the leader, TTSL-PPO formulates investment as a continuous-control MDP with state augmented by inferred dual variables (λ̂ = v/b) for Markovianity, and enforces strict two-timescale separation: agents update λ every round (fast) while the platform updates its PPO policy every L=50 rounds (slow), using GAE for variance reduction and a clipped surrogate objective to prevent destabilizing policy jumps. The coupled process is proven to converge almost surely to a Stackelberg Stationary Point under standard Robbins-Monro conditions.

## Results

On the AntM2C dataset with GPT-4o creative enhancement, TTSL-PPO achieves a +2.66% platform revenue increase over the GSP No Invest baseline, while simultaneously boosting social welfare by +3.51% and click volume by +3.81%. In the agent subgame (10 agents, 2000 rounds), ODDB-R achieves −29.3% social welfare relative to the unconstrained Truthful upper bound and converges to equilibrium by round 841, outperforming the Pacing baseline (−30.1%, round 1063). In the end-to-end validation with real GPT-4o API calls (bypassing the surrogate), TTSL-PPO still yields a +2.04% revenue gain, confirming surrogate fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the agent subgame, ODDB-R outperforms all three compared baselines on both social welfare and convergence. Against Pacing (Aggarwal et al., 2019), ODDB-R achieves −29.3% SW versus −30.1% and converges 222 rounds earlier (841 vs. 1063). Against PID (Johnson & Moradi, 2005) and AdWords (Mehta et al., 2007), ODDB-R improves SW by 1.5 and 2.0 percentage points respectively, and critically is the only dynamic strategy (alongside Pacing) that converges to a stable equilibrium—PID and AdWords exhibit persistent bid oscillation with no convergence. At the platform level, TTSL-PPO dominates all investment baselines: GSP Greedy Invest (myopic) loses 7.09% revenue due to destabilizing agent bid shading, while GSP Uniform/Normal Invest lose ~9% revenue from indiscriminate spending. TTSL-PPO is the only strategy that simultaneously increases revenue, welfare, and clicks, confirming that strategic Stackelberg optimization of creative investment is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autobidding auction literature (Aggarwal et al., 2019; Conitzer et al., 2022) by introducing a Stackelberg game where the platform strategically invests in LLM-based creative enhancement, endogenizing inference costs that prior LLM-in-advertising work (Feizi et al., 2023; Soumalias et al., 2026; Cai et al., 2025) treated as exogenous or ignored. It departs from the 'generate from scratch' paradigm by proposing lightweight title enhancement via a predict-then-execute workflow, and from standard mechanism design by coupling PPO-based policy learning with Tikhonov-regularized pacing equilibria to resolve the equilibrium multiplicity problem. The two-timescale learning architecture (TTSL-PPO) is a novel application of bilevel optimization to the ad auction setting, building on Rosen's (1965) diagonal strict convexity framework for uniqueness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous game-theoretic and algorithmic framework for integrating LLM inference costs into real-time ad auctions, offering ad platforms a deployable mechanism (PIM) that is a modular wrapper around existing autobidders rather than a full system overhaul. The finding that myopic investment strategies actively destroy revenue (~7% loss) by triggering aggressive bid shading in the follower subgame is a practical warning for platforms considering naive LLM deployment. Key limitations include the single-slot GSP focus, reliance on a fitted surrogate for LLM behavior during training, and evaluation primarily on 10-agent simulations; open questions include multi-slot VCG extensions, robustness to adversarial agents, and whether the +2-3% revenue gains persist under real market dynamics with heterogeneous LLM capabilities.
