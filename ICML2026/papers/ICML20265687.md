## Problem

Tabular diffusion models rely on implicit mechanisms such as self-attention to capture inter-column relationships, leaving the denoiser to infer relational structure solely from the denoising objective. This paper asks whether explicit pairwise geometric supervision can serve as a portable relational inductive bias that reduces the burden on the denoiser and transfers across architecturally distinct diffusion backbones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset with mixed continuous and categorical columns, normalizing all values to [-1,1] via quantile transform (continuous) or deterministic index mapping (categorical). At each forward pass during both training and sampling, pairwise angles and lengths are computed from the current (noised or partially-denoised) state and fed as additional inputs alongside the noised data. The output is a full synthetic dataset with the same column schema, generated via 1000-step EDM sampling for continuous columns and iterative unmasking for categoricals, with reflection-based boundary handling to avoid mass concentration at distribution edges.

## Method

GATD augments any tabular diffusion denoiser with pairwise geometric features—angles θ_ij = arctan(v_j − v_i) and log-lengths ℓ_ij = ½log(1+(v_j−v_i)²)—that serve dual roles as model inputs and auxiliary prediction targets. Predicted angles are concatenated with the hidden state to form an augmented representation that feeds the denoising heads, while the length head provides training-only regularization. The training loss inverts the typical hierarchy: geometric terms (λ_θ=15, λ_ℓ=15, λ_c=8) account for ~95% of total loss at convergence, forcing the backbone to internalize inter-column structure. A critical architecture-matched ablation shows that supplying geometric inputs and prediction heads without supervision (InputsOnly) yields no benefit over the no-geometry baseline (Cohen's d = −0.08), while restoring supervision produces a large effect (d = 0.81), localizing the gain to direct geometric supervision rather than added capacity. The same loss weights and augmentation path transfer as a drop-in module across residual MLP, GNN, and Transformer diffusion backbones, with the compact MLP instantiation using 3.5× fewer parameters than TabDiff while matching or exceeding its benchmark performance.

## Results

GATD-MLP achieves 27.3% lower Shape error (0.862 vs. 1.19) and 19.6% lower Trend error (1.65 vs. 2.05) compared to TabDiff, the prior SOTA tabular diffusion model, while using 3.5× fewer parameters on average (up to 25× for classification). Per-dataset, GATD-MLP wins 8/10 on Shape, 7/10 on Trend, and 9/10 on F1/RMSE (MLE-2). The geometric supervision signal transfers across three diffusion backbones (MLP, GNN, Transformer), winning 27/30 Shape and 25/30 Trend cells with shared default loss weights (λ_θ=15, λ_ℓ=15, λ_c=8). End-to-end training is 1.7× faster than TabDiff despite 2.5× more epochs, due to the compact MLP backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is TabDiff (Shi et al., ICLR 2025), the prior state-of-the-art mixed-type tabular diffusion model using a transformer backbone with self-attention for column relationships. GATD-MLP outperforms TabDiff on 8/10 datasets for Shape error, 7/10 for Trend, and 9/10 for F1/RMSE (MLE-2), with the two Shape losses being Magic (0.4 pp gap, within one standard deviation) and California (22.8 pp, a genuine loss on a 0-categorical-column regression dataset). On MLE-1 (AUROC/R²), GATD wins 6/10 datasets but the aggregate gap-to-real is substantially smaller (4.8% vs. 11.4%). The comparison is protocol-controlled: a matched 8,000-epoch GATD run still achieves 28% Shape reduction over TabDiff, and at 100 sampling steps GATD already wins all 4 aggregate metrics, ruling out the 20× sampling-step and 2.5× epoch-budget differences as confounders. GATD also demonstrates superior training-seed stability (lower cross-seed CV) on the majority of datasets compared to TabDiff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TabDiff's (Shi et al., 2025) diffusion framework—EDM for continuous columns and masked diffusion for categoricals—deliberately holding the diffusion machinery constant to isolate the contribution of explicit geometric supervision. It departs from the broader tabular diffusion lineage (TabDDPM, STaSy, CoDi, TabSyn) by introducing pairwise angle and length features as both model inputs and auxiliary prediction targets, a form of relational supervision that no prior tabular generator provides. Conceptually, it bridges geometric deep learning (where explicit structure accelerates learning on graphs and point clouds) to the tabular setting, where column relationships have previously been left to be inferred implicitly by attention or message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a compact residual MLP with explicit geometric supervision matches or exceeds a transformer-based SOTA (TabDiff) while using 3.5× fewer parameters and training 1.7× faster, offering a lightweight alternative for practitioners constrained by compute. The architecture-matched ablation showing that supervision (not added inputs or capacity) is the operative variable provides a clear design principle: auxiliary prediction tasks must be actively supervised to shape representations. Open questions include whether the O(d²) pairwise scaling remains practical beyond ~170 columns, whether the approach transfers to non-diffusion generative paradigms (VAEs, normalizing flows, autoregressive models), and whether the heuristic loss weights (λ_θ=λ_ℓ=15, λ_c=8) can be made adaptive or learned.
