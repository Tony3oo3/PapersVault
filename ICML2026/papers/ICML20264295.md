## Problem

Existing medical multimodal fusion methods assume a fixed, known set of modalities, treat each modality's semantic representation as a deterministic vector (ignoring reliability differences), and lack explicit structural constraints on fine-grained cross-modal local consistency. These limitations make them brittle when modality count or composition changes in real clinical settings and prevent robust alignment of heterogeneous, noisy medical signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264295/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts an arbitrary number of medical modalities per patient—imaging (rs-fMRI, CT, ultrasound, MRI, pathology slides, mammography) and structured clinical text/demographic features—without requiring architectural changes. Each modality is processed by a dual-encoder producing a 128-dimensional global semantic vector and 8 local tokens of dimension 8; the fused output is a single disease class label (binary or multi-class depending on the task). Notably, the method handles missing modalities gracefully by simply excluding them from the consistency and fusion computations.

## Method

The proposed SMMF framework enforces cross-modal consistency at three hierarchical levels. At the modality level, each modality's latent semantic estimate is modeled as a diagonal Gaussian (mean + per-dimension uncertainty), and symmetric KL divergence across all modality pairs yields a closed-form, reliability-adaptive alignment loss where noisy modalities contribute weaker gradients. At the token level, cross-modal linear reconstruction operators are computed via ridge-regularized least squares in a compact 8×8 token subspace, and a bidirectional reversibility constraint (‖R^{m←k}R^{k←m} − I‖²_F) enforces that the explanation rule from modality A to B and vice versa are mutual inverses, providing structured local semantic verification without attention weights. For fusion, each modality is sequentially treated as a conditional view; the remaining modalities undergo signal-noise decomposition, with mutual information maximization (via InfoNCE) encouraging complementary signal and an orthogonality penalty suppressing noise, after which the final representation is the sum of all signal components fed to a classifier.

## Results

On the ABIDE dataset, the proposed method achieves 84.67% ACC, 87.03% SEN, 85.22% SPE, and 90.57% AUC, outperforming the strongest baseline MM-GTUNets (82.92% ACC, 88.21% AUC) by 1.75% in accuracy and 2.36% in AUC. On ADHD-200, it reaches 87.15% ACC and 92.29% AUC, surpassing MM-GTUNets (82.68% ACC, 90.71% AUC) by 4.47% in accuracy. On the CMMD breast cancer dataset, the method attains 92.37% ACC and 97.12% AUC, and on the self-constructed gastric cancer dataset it achieves 73.11% ACC and 81.26% AUC, consistently leading all baselines across all five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. On ABIDE, it surpasses the strongest multimodal baseline MM-GTUNets by 1.75% ACC and 2.36% AUC, and the unimodal A-GCL by 5.63% ACC and 7.71% AUC. On ADHD-200, the gap over MM-GTUNets widens to 4.47% ACC and 1.58% AUC, while EV-GCN (the next-best AUC baseline at 87.15%) trails by 5.14% AUC. On CMMD, the method leads Meta-Transformer (the best baseline) by 3.43% ACC and 3.28% AUC. On the self-constructed gastric cancer dataset (5-class), it achieves 73.11% ACC versus Meta's 69.02%, a 4.09% margin. Notably, unimodal methods (Brain-GNN, DGCN, A-GCL, GATE) consistently underperform multimodal baselines, confirming the value of multimodal fusion, while the proposed method's advantage over the best multimodal baselines (MM-GTUNets, Meta, EV-GCN) is most pronounced on AUC, indicating superior threshold-independent discriminative ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from variational inference (distributional semantic modeling with symmetric KL divergence), ridge-regularized linear regression (bidirectional token reconstruction), and information-theoretic fusion (conditional mutual information with signal-noise decomposition) into a unified modality-agnostic framework. It departs from the dominant paradigm of attention-based or similarity-based cross-modal alignment by replacing learned interaction weights with deterministic, closed-form structural constraints. The modality-agnostic design is conceptually related to Meta-Transformer's unified multimodal learning, but achieves scalability through set-based consistency constraints rather than meta-learning, and the uncertainty-aware alignment extends prior work like DyCON and BEAR from segmentation or general understanding to structured medical classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-agnostic fusion design addresses a genuine clinical deployment challenge: the number, quality, and availability of modalities vary across patients and institutions, yet most existing methods require a fixed modality set at design time. The paper's consistent improvements across five datasets (including two self-constructed ones with 4 and 3 modalities) and its robustness under missing-modality settings suggest practical utility. However, limitations remain: the self-constructed datasets are small (255 and 100 patients), experiments use a single GPU, and the framework is validated only on classification tasks. Open questions include whether the consistency-based approach scales to segmentation or prognosis prediction, how it behaves under severe distribution shift across multi-center data, and whether the linear reconstruction assumption holds for highly heterogeneous modality pairs (e.g., free-text radiology reports vs. volumetric MRI).
