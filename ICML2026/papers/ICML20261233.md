## Problem

Diffusion-based representation learning faces a structural paradox: early methods (e.g., DiffAE) decouple semantics from generation, causing information split, while recent bridge-based methods (e.g., DBAE) enforce a coupled mapping but trigger information overload, where the latent bottleneck entangles high-frequency stochastic noise and degenerates into a non-discriminative noise container. The paper asks how to unify generative fidelity and discriminative utility without reverting to decoupling or succumbing to noise entanglement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×256 RGB images (FFHQ) or 64×64 (CelebA) through an asymmetric dual-branch protocol: the teacher receives a weakly augmented view (random flip) while the student receives a timestep-scaled progressive augmentation (crop, Gaussian blur, color jitter) followed by VP-diffusion noise. The primary output is a 512-dimensional latent vector that supports linear-probe attribute prediction, pixel-level reconstruction, and unconditional generation via a separately trained latent diffusion prior; the latent is further decomposed into a translation-invariant holistic component and a spatially sensitive partial component for multi-granularity alignment.

## Method

ArcDAE reconfigures the diffusion bridge as a dynamic information sifter via three coupled mechanisms. (1) Asymmetric Semantic Rectification (ASR) uses a BYOL-style EMA teacher as a clean semantic anchor and an InfoNCE contrastive loss to rectify the noisy student latent, which provably induces a time-dependent Jacobian regularization (strength σ_t²) that smooths the manifold near noise while preserving detail near data. (2) Timestep-Aware Progressive Augmentation (TAPA) scales crop, blur, and jitter intensity linearly with the diffusion timestep, forcing the student to infer semantics from increasingly degraded evidence. (3) Timestep-Adaptive Granularity Alignment (TGA) with SNR-based gating (TAAW) dynamically weights the local (partial) alignment term by the signal-to-noise ratio, attenuating spatial alignment in noise-dominated regimes while preserving it near the data manifold. Theoretically, the paper proves that score matching in high-dimensional ambient space allocates a (D−d_M)/(d_M−2) energy ratio to normal-space nuisance components, formalizing the information overload trap that ArcDAE's rectification resolves.

## Results

On FFHQ, ArcDAE achieves 0.681 AP and 0.718 Pearson's r in linear-probe attribute prediction, surpassing the strongest baseline DBAE-stochastic (0.664 AP, 0.675 r) by 2.6% and 6.4% respectively. On CelebA, ArcDAE reaches 0.676 AP and 0.656 r, outperforming DBAE-stochastic (0.655 AP, 0.643 r). In reconstruction (512-dim tractable latent), ArcDAE attains SSIM 0.962 and LPIPS 0.065, beating DBAE-deterministic (0.953, 0.072) and DBAE-stochastic (0.920, 0.094). For unconditional generation with an auxiliary AE prior, ArcDAE+AE achieves the best FID of 1.65 and recall of 0.710 on FFHQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArcDAE outperforms all 19 baselines on the primary semantic metrics. Against the closest competitor DBAE (Kim et al., 2025), ArcDAE gains +0.017 AP and +0.043 Pearson's r on FFHQ, and +0.021 AP on CelebA, while also improving reconstruction LPIPS from 0.094 (DBAE-stoch) to 0.065. Earlier diffusion autoencoders (DiffAE, PDAE, DITI) trail by 5–7 AP points on FFHQ, confirming that the information-split paradigm caps semantic density. Non-diffusion baselines (SimCLR, β-TCVAE, IB-GAN) are substantially weaker on both semantics and generation, with SimCLR lacking generation entirely. In disentanglement (Table 3), ArcDAE+TC achieves TAD 0.385 vs. DBAE+TC 0.362 and DisDiff 0.305, while maintaining FID 12.2 vs. 13.4 and 18.3 respectively. For unconditional generation (Table 4), ArcDAE+AE achieves FID 1.65 and recall 0.710, beating DBAE+AE (1.77, 0.687) and DDPM (9.14, 0.335). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is raw MSE in reconstruction (DBAE-d: 2.49×10⁻³ vs. ArcDAE: 2.79×10⁻³), which the authors attribute to DBAE-d's noise memorization rather than genuine structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArcDAE sits at the intersection of two established lines: diffusion bridge autoencoders (DBAE, Kim et al. 2025) that enforce a bijective latent-to-noisy-endpoint mapping, and asymmetric teacher-student contrastive learning (BYOL, Grill et al. 2020) that stabilizes representation via EMA anchors. The paper's key conceptual departure is diagnosing a specific failure mode in bridge-based methods—'information overload' where the bottleneck becomes a sufficient statistic for the terminal noise state—and reframing the bridge as an active information sifter rather than a passive reconstruction channel. The theoretical grounding (geometric decomposition of score-matching error into tangent/normal components, Doob h-transform bridge construction) is drawn from stochastic analysis and differential geometry, while the practical mechanism (TAPA progressive augmentation + InfoNCE rectification) repurposes contrastive learning tools in a diffusion-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why unconstrained diffusion bridges degrade into noise-dominated latents, and a concrete architectural fix that preserves the unified generative-discriminative formulation without reverting to the information-split paradigm. The perception-distortion trade-off result (slightly worse MSE but substantially better LPIPS) is practically important: it shows that 'reconstructing noise' is not the same as 'reconstructing structure,' a distinction that affects how practitioners evaluate autoencoding quality. Limitations include evaluation primarily on face-centric datasets (FFHQ, CelebA) with only brief generalization checks on CIFAR-10 and LSUN, and the theoretical bounds (Theorem 1) are stated qualitatively rather than as tight quantitative guarantees. Open questions include whether the asymmetric rectification mechanism transfers to video, 3D, or multimodal settings, and whether the timestep-adaptive weighting can be made schedule-agnostic.
