## Problem

Linear attention models based on the Delta rule (e.g., DeltaNet, RWKV-7) achieve RNN-like inference efficiency and provably solve regular language tasks, but their fixed-size state cannot capture the recursive, hierarchical structures (Deterministic Context-Free Languages) essential for tasks like matching nested parentheses or tracking loop scopes. Naive stack-augmented RNNs restore this expressivity but require sequential push/pop operations that destroy the parallel training essential for modern scale, while existing attention-based stack approximations lack rigorous DCFL recognition guarantees.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a standard text token sequence and produces next-token predictions, but internally each token is projected into queries, keys, values, and a 3-way action distribution (PUSH, POP, NO-OP) over the stack. The architecture maintains two parallel memory states per head: a d×d associative matrix S_t (inherited from DeltaNet) and a d×s stack matrix J_t indexed by a soft pointer vector generated from a Laplacian kernel over the scalar pointer position. The final output is a standard softmax over the vocabulary, with the stack's read signal modulating the associative recall before the output projection.

## Method

DeltaStack augments DeltaNet's associative memory with a differentiable stack whose push, pop, and read operations are recast as rank-1 delta-rule updates to a memory matrix J_t, rather than as sequential pointer manipulations. A scalar pointer p_t drifts by the difference between push and pop action probabilities, and a Laplacian kernel with learnable sharpness γ converts this into a soft index vector for accessing stack slots. The unified state update (Eq. 7) composes a pop-erase and a push-erase as sequential Householder-like reflections, which—by interleaving T steps into 2T virtual steps—can be evaluated with DeltaNet's existing chunkwise-parallel algorithm at O(LCd + Ld²) complexity. The stack's top-of-stack readout v_{t-1}^{read} is injected into the associative memory's online regression loss, so S_t only learns the residual between the target value and the stack's prediction, effectively offloading hierarchical bookkeeping to the stack while preserving DeltaNet's DPLR transition structure for parallel training.

## Results

On 8 formal language benchmarks, DeltaStack achieves 79.4% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest parallel baseline DeltaNet (65.5%) by 14 percentage points, and approaching sequential Stack-RNN (96.3%) on hierarchical DCF tasks (96.8% Stack Manipulation, 98.8% Reverse String, 94.3% Dyck). On the MAD associative recall benchmark, DeltaStack scores 72.2 vs. DeltaNet's 71.8, matching DeltaNet's perfect 100.0 on In-Context Recall and Selective Copy. At 760M parameters, DeltaStack (s=8) improves average zero-shot accuracy to 52.0 vs. DeltaNet's 51.4 and reduces WikiText perplexity from 21.89 to 21.81. On length extrapolation (16k context, trained at 4k), DeltaStack maintains consistently lower per-token loss than DeltaNet on both CodeParrot and OpenThoughts-114k-Math.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeltaStack decisively outperforms all parallel linear-attention baselines: it beats DeltaNet (65.5%) by 14 points, Gated DeltaProduct (66.0%) by 13.4 points, Gated DeltaNet (58.3%) by 21.1 points, RWKV-7 (52.2%) by 27.2 points, and Transformer (48.3%) by 31.1 points on average formal-language accuracy. Against Stack-Attention (58.4%), DeltaStack wins on 6 of 8 tasks, with the largest gaps on Stack Manipulation (96.8 vs. 57.4) and Dyck (94.3 vs. 88.4), though Stack-Attention retains a slight edge on Reverse String (88.3 vs. 98.8 is actually DeltaStack winning; Stack-Attention's 88.3 is lower). On the MAD benchmark, DeltaStack (72.2) marginally surpasses DeltaNet (71.8) and GLA (60.0), confirming the stack does not degrade retrieval. Compared to sequential baselines, DeltaStack narrows the gap to Stack-RNN on DCF tasks (96.8 vs. 98.5 on Stack Manipulation; 94.3 vs. 98.9 on Dyck) but still trails LSTM on regular tasks like Cycle Navigation (46.4 vs. 100.0). At 760M scale, DeltaStack (s=8) improves over DeltaNet on perplexity (21.81 vs. 21.89 WikiText; 18.26 vs. 19.47 LAMBADA) and average zero-shot accuracy (52.0 vs. 51.4), while at 340M the two are essentially tied (45.9 vs. 45.5). The one persistent weakness is Modular Arithmetic with Brackets (35.2%), where DeltaStack only marginally beats DeltaNet (33.2%) and all parallel baselines remain far below sequential Stack-RNN (79.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaStack sits at the intersection of two research threads: the delta-rule linear attention family (DeltaNet, RWKV-7, Gated DeltaNet) that achieves parallelizable training and regular-language recognition, and the differentiable stack mechanism introduced by Stack-RNNs (Joulin & Mikolov, 2015) for hierarchical reasoning. Prior attempts to add stacks to modern architectures—Stack-Attention (DuSell & Chiang, 2024), Recursive Transformer (Zhang et al., 2026)—either rely on sequential push/pop that breaks training parallelism or introduce ad-hoc layerwise stacks without DCFL expressivity guarantees. DeltaStack's key departure is recasting stack push/pop/read as rank-1 delta-rule updates on a memory matrix, which inherits DeltaNet's chunkwise-parallel (WY-representation) training algorithm and preserves O(LCd) complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-layer constructive proof for Dyck_{k,D} recognition (with parameter count independent of nesting depth D) is a meaningful theoretical advance over the O(D)-layer requirement for Transformers and 2-layer minimum for SSMs, and the decoupling of depth from learnable parameters suggests test-time generalization to deeper nesting. However, empirical validation is confined to 340M/760M-parameter models and synthetic formal languages; the model still fails on the hardest DCF task (Modular Arithmetic with Brackets, 35.2%) and underperforms Transformers on long-context associative recall. Open questions include whether the stack mechanism interacts beneficially with massive semantic knowledge at billion-parameter scale, whether expressivity guarantees extend to the full DCFL class, and whether hybrid DeltaStack + softmax-attention architectures can combine hierarchical reasoning with precise retrieval.
