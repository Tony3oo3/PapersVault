## Problem

Split Federated Learning (SFL) suffers from biased optimization and unstable convergence under non-IID client data, yet this data-heterogeneity challenge has been largely overlooked in favor of system-heterogeneity solutions. Existing FL methods (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedRDN) can be adapted to SFL but their effectiveness remains unexplored, and the split architecture's inherent access to intermediate smashed data offers unexploited opportunities for bias compensation beyond standard parameter-level aggregation.

## Input / Output

Clients process raw images (32×32 RGB for CIFAR, 28×28 grayscale for F-MNIST, dermoscopic RGB for ISIC/HAM10000) through a client-side CNN to produce intermediate 'smashed data' vectors, which are the only representations transmitted to the server—raw pixels never leave the device. The server-side model outputs a per-class evidence vector parameterizing a Dirichlet distribution, from which both a class prediction and decomposed aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty estimates are derived. A lightweight auxiliary model (≈347K params vs. 8M global) enables fully local inference without server connectivity.

## Method

BESplit integrates three architecture-aware mechanisms operating at distinct levels. (1) Evidential Aggregation (EA) maintains per-client Client State Records of class-wise evidence and uncertainty via staleness-aware EMA, then computes adaptive aggregation weights combining evidence concentration (fraction of evidence on correct classes) and uncertainty penalization (inverse relative aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty) to suppress biased clients. (2) Bias-Compensated Collaboration (BCC) identifies clients deviating from the global label distribution via Jensen-Shannon divergence, constructs a weighted bipartite graph over biased clients, and applies greedy matching to pair complementary clients; for each pair, a theoretically derived fraction ρ of overrepresented-class smashed features is cross-routed to correct representation bias without extra communication. (3) Dual-Teacher Distillation (DTD) trains a per-client auxiliary model in parallel with SFL, using the local client-side model as a feature-level teacher (aligning pairwise Euclidean relational matrices) and the global model as an evidential-level teacher (KL divergence on temperature-scaled Dirichlet predictions), enabling local inference while preserving global consistency.

## Results

Under the default non-IID setting (κ=1), BESplit-G achieves the best test accuracy across all five datasets: 67.45% on ISIC, 70.46% on HAM10000, 74.89% on F-MNIST, 59.82% on CIFAR-10, and 68.17% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baseline (FAVD) by an average of 3.0%. BESplit-A, which supports fully local inference via a lightweight auxiliary model, achieves 66.97%, 68.65%, 74.11%, 57.98%, and 66.15% respectively, remaining within ~1.3% of BESplit-G. On convergence, BESplit-G reaches 60% accuracy on CIFAR-100 in 371 rounds under non-IID data versus 475 for FedAvg and 419 for MergeSFL, and achieves 1.58× wall-clock speedup over FedAvg on HAM10000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BESplit-G outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset under both Uniform and Non-IID (κ=1) settings. The closest competitors are FAVD and FedRDN among FL methods, and MergeSFL among SFL methods. On CIFAR-10 (non-IID), BESplit-G achieves 59.82% versus FedRDN's 56.89% (+2.93%) and FAVD's 56.13% (+3.69%); on CIFAR-100 (non-IID), it reaches 68.17% versus FAVD's 66.60% (+1.57%). The advantage is most pronounced on medical imaging: on ISIC (non-IID), BESplit-G scores 67.45% versus FAVD's 64.52% (+2.93%), and on HAM10000 it achieves 70.46% versus FAVD's 67.90% (+2.56%). Among SFL-specific baselines, MergeSFL is the strongest, yet BESplit-G still leads by 2.0–4.6% across datasets. BESplit-A, the local-inference variant, trails BESplit-G by 0.5–2.0% but still surpasses all baselines on four of five datasets under non-IID, confirming that the lightweight auxiliary model retains most of the global model's advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three established lines: (1) SFL methods (SplitFed, MergeSFL, ParallelSFL, HASFL) that have primarily addressed system heterogeneity but largely ignored data heterogeneity; (2) non-IID FL techniques (FedProx, FedDyn, FedRDN, FAVD, Fed-MoE) that operate at the parameter or representation level in conventional FL; and (3) Dirichlet-based Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) and its FL adaptations (FedEvi, FEAL). The key departure is the architectural argument: the split design of SFL gives the server access to smashed data and intermediate representations, which the authors exploit to perform evidential aggregation, cross-client feature pairing, and dual-teacher distillation—mechanisms that have no direct analogue in standard FL where only parameters are shared.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by systematically addressing non-IID data in SFL, a setting where prior work has focused almost exclusively on system heterogeneity. The practical value is reinforced by DTD's ability to support fully local inference with a lightweight auxiliary model (only ~347K parameters vs. 8M for the global model), which is critical for resource-constrained edge deployment. However, several limitations remain open: BCC's effectiveness depends on accurate distributional estimation and assumes partially complementary client distributions, DTD requires careful hyperparameter tuning, and the framework operates under an honest-but-curious server model without formal guarantees against feature-inversion attacks on smashed data. Integrating differential privacy and extending to more extreme or adversarial heterogeneity are natural next steps.
