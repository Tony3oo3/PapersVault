## Problem

Existing neural network weight generation methods flatten weight matrices into fixed-dimensional vectors, rigidly coupling the generative prior to the specific layer widths seen during training and preventing zero-shot transfer to unseen architectures. This limitation is compounded by permutation symmetry, which makes adjacent weights spatially uncorrelated and destroys the local structure needed for any spatial or patch-based generative approach.

## Input / Output

In conditional synthesis mode, the model takes a discrete sequence of architecture tokens (layer widths drawn from a vocabulary of {input, 16, 32, 64, output}) as a clean prompt along with Gaussian noise on the weight channel, and produces a full set of MLP weight-and-bias tensors. In joint mode, both the architecture tokens and weight patches are noised, and the model samples a complete (architecture, weights) pair from scratch. Weights are generated on a fixed maximal coordinate grid (H=M, W=M+p) and then cropped to the actual dimensions specified by the decoded architecture tokens, so the tokenization scheme is invariant to width.

## Method

The core insight is that Graph HyperNetworks with a CNN decoder inherently produce structurally aligned weight spaces with consistent local spatial correlations across seeds, unlike SGD-trained networks whose permutation symmetry leaves weights unaligned. Leveraging this alignment, NNiT tokenizes each layer's weight-and-bias matrix into non-overlapping p×p patches, making generation width-agnostic: widening a layer simply adds more patches without changing the token representation. Discrete architecture tokens and continuous weight patches are concatenated into a single multimodal sequence processed by a Diffusion Transformer backbone with SwiGLU activations and AdaLNZero conditioning via dual per-modality timestep embeddings. Training uses the Mixture of Noise Levels framework, stochastically switching between joint generation (both modalities noised) and conditional synthesis (architecture clean, weights noised) to learn both p(a,w) and p(w|a) in one model.

## Results

On zero-shot (unseen) architectures in ManiSkill3, NNiT maintains 99% success on PickCube-v1, 100% on PushCube-v1, and 86% on StackCubeEasy-v1, while D2NWG drops to 59%, 89%, and 42% respectively, and SANE fails entirely (≤2%). On seen architectures, NNiT matches D2NWG at 98–100% success across all three tasks. In unconditional joint generation p(a,w), NNiT spontaneously produces functional policies with 99–100% success on PickCube and PushCube and 90% on StackCubeEasy. The pipeline also transfers to MNIST, achieving 96.1% mean test accuracy across 8 unseen architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: D2NWG (Soro et al., 2024) and SANE (Schürholt et al., 2024), both adapted to the zero-shot width-generalization setting. On seen architectures, NNiT and D2NWG perform comparably (98–100% success), while SANE fails catastrophically (≤6% success) due to its global positional encoding becoming unstable across diverse widths. The critical differentiation appears on unseen architectures: NNiT retains 99%/100%/86% success on PickCube/PushCube/StackCubeEasy, whereas D2NWG degrades to 59%/89%/42% and SANE remains near zero. The full 80-policy distribution (Table 9) confirms the gap is distributional rather than a selection artifact—NNiT's worst single unseen architecture (64.8% on PickCube) still exceeds D2NWG's overall mean (15.6%). No prior method supports unconditional joint architecture-weight generation, so NNiT is evaluated in isolation for that mode, achieving 99–100% success without any architectural prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural network weight generation line (SANE, D2NWG) by replacing fixed-dimensional vectorization with patch-based tokenization, drawing on the variable-resolution primitives from Flexible Diffusion Transformers (FiT) and the Mixture of Noise Levels framework for joint discrete-continuous modeling. The key departure is the use of Graph HyperNetworks with a CNN decoder not merely as a data source but as a structural alignment mechanism: the CNN decoder's locality bias induces consistent spatial correlations in generated weights, making them amenable to image-style patch processing. Prior methods (SANE, D2NWG) treat weight generation as a fixed-geometry problem, whereas NNiT reframes it as a width-agnostic field generation task where widening a layer simply adds patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GHN-generated weight spaces are structurally aligned (with local correlations) while SGD-trained spaces are not provides a practical recipe for making weight generation tractable: the generator's architecture can create the spatial prerequisites that downstream diffusion models need. The zero-shot width generalization result (>85% success on unseen topologies vs. 42–59% for D2NWG) opens a path toward synthesizing policies for arbitrary hardware constraints without retraining, relevant to sim-to-real deployment and meta-learning. However, the evaluation is confined to 4-layer MLPs on three robotic tasks plus MNIST, leaving open whether the approach scales to deeper networks, CNN architectures, or the high-variance continuous weights of larger models.
