## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks primarily target short clips with simple, single-scene prompts, leaving a significant gap in evaluating long-form generation under complex, script-level textual inputs. Current models achieve strong perceptual quality but exhibit pronounced weaknesses in fine-grained prompt faithfulness and long-term character identity preservation, yet no benchmark systematically measures these dimensions across multi-scene, hierarchically structured prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes a generated video (30–60 s, 480p, 16 fps) paired with a complex multi-scene prompt (average 248.85 words, complexity score 8.70) containing hierarchical metadata such as character settings, object dynamics, and camera behaviors. It produces five dimension-level scores—Perceptual Quality, Text-Video Alignment (split into Overall and Fine-Grained), Temporal Quality (Character Consistency, Background Consistency, Warping Error), Dynamic Quality, and HERD—each reported as a percentage. The benchmark comprises 234 prompts across 18 themes, and 17 LVG methods are evaluated under two input protocols: multi-prompt (scene-decomposed) and direct (single-prompt).

## Method

LoCoT2V-Bench constructs prompts via a multi-stage MLLM pipeline: Seed 1.5-VL generates raw descriptions, GPT-5 expands them into story-like narratives with character settings, and post-processing handles name deduplication and safety filtering, all verified by humans. LoCoT2V-Eval introduces several key mechanisms: (1) Fine-Grained Alignment uses a hierarchical tree-structured VQA with a state-aware adaptive anchoring mechanism that switches from locate queries to grounded judge queries once a character is confirmed, gating action scores by anchoring status to prevent hallucinated scores; (2) Character Consistency combines SAM3-based tracking with FG-CLIP2 embedding similarity against a highest-confidence anchor; (3) HERD employs a decoupled Auditor-Evaluator dual-agent framework where the Auditor produces a factual video report without access to expectations, and the Evaluator scores six narrative dimensions (Emotional Response, Narrative Flow, Character Development, Visual Style, Themes Expression, Overall Impression) conditioned on both the report and the video. All temporal metrics are adapted to a streaming formulation to handle long videos without memory overflow, and Overall Alignment uses Qwen3-VL 8B on a 100-point scale rather than CLIP-based scoring.

## Results

Across 17 LVG methods evaluated on LoCoT2V-Bench (234 multi-scene prompts, avg. 248.85 words), top proprietary models Kling 3.0 (71.49%), Sora2 (72.13%), and LongCat-Video (71.72%) lead in overall average score. Perceptual quality is strong across the board (LongSANA reaches 84.11%), and Background Consistency/Warping Error consistently exceed 90%, but Fine-Grained Alignment (FGA) scores remain low (best: Kling 3.0 at 56.94%) and Character Consistency (CC) is markedly weaker (most methods below 50%, best: LongLive at 54.92%). Kling 3.0 achieves the highest HERD score (87.47%), while LongLive leads in Temporal Quality (83.66%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 17 evaluated LVG methods, proprietary models Kling 3.0, Sora2, and LongCat-Video dominate the top of the leaderboard (71.49%, 72.13%, 71.72% average), outperforming all open-source baselines by 5–15 points. Kling 3.0 leads in Overall Alignment (73.08%), Fine-Grained Alignment (56.94%), and HERD (87.47%), while Sora2 achieves the best OA (69.64%) and FGA (54.09%) among direct-input methods. LongLive stands out as the strongest open-source method (70.56% avg), achieving the highest Character Consistency (54.92%) and Temporal Quality (83.66%), surpassing even proprietary models on these temporal dimensions. Multi-prompt input methods (FreeNoise, MEVG, FreeLong, DiTCtrl, StoryAdapter) consistently underperform direct-input methods, with FGA scores below 46% versus 51–57% for top direct-input models. In the human-alignment comparison with VBench-Long (Table 4), LoCoT2V-Eval achieves substantially higher PLCC/SRCC/KRCC on all four shared dimensions (e.g., PQ: 71.39/70.20/54.67 vs. 55.10/50.69/36.77; OA: 63.38/67.23/52.68 vs. 10.99/18.18/12.04), confirming superior alignment with human judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video generation evaluation lineage from VBench, EvalCrafter, and DEVIL by adapting their individual metrics (Dynamic Degree, Motion Smoothness, Warping Error, aperiodicity measures) into a unified streaming framework for long-form video. It departs from prior benchmarks (VBench-Long, VBench 2.0, VMBench) that rely on short, single-scene prompts by constructing multi-scene prompts with hierarchical metadata (character settings, camera behaviors) derived from real-world videos via a multi-stage MLLM pipeline. The Fine-Grained Alignment metric introduces a novel tree-structured VQA with state-aware adaptive anchoring, and HERD proposes a dual-agent (Auditor-Evaluator) framework for high-level narrative assessment—both absent from prior evaluation suites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark asymmetry: current LVG models achieve >90% Background Consistency but <50% Character Consistency, and show a significant drop from Overall to Fine-Grained Alignment (e.g., Kling 3.0: 73% OA vs. 57% FGA), confirming that prompt faithfulness and identity preservation remain unsolved for long-form generation. The 234-sample scale, while thematically diverse, limits statistical power, and the heavy reliance on MLLMs (Qwen3-VL, GPT-5) for evaluation introduces potential circularity and modality-bias concerns. Open questions include whether the FGA gap narrows with longer training horizons, whether HERD's moderate human-alignment correlations (~55-58% SRCC) improve with better expectation elicitation, and whether the benchmark generalizes beyond the 18 themes tested.
