## Problem

Existing tabular anomaly detection methods follow a one-for-one (OFO) paradigm, requiring a dedicated model to be trained and tuned for each dataset, which incurs prohibitive computational cost and yields poor generalization to unseen domains. This paper introduces the one-for-all (OFA) paradigm for tabular anomaly detection, where a single generalist model is trained once on multiple source datasets and must detect anomalies in completely unseen target datasets without any target-domain retraining or hyperparameter tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262517/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a tabular test sample of arbitrary feature dimensionality along with the target dataset's training split (normal samples only) as context. The context is used exclusively for kNN retrieval (K=80 neighbors) and quantile normalization of distances—no target-domain labels or model updates are involved. The output is a single scalar anomaly score per test sample, produced by a fixed MoE network with hyperparameters held constant across all 34 target datasets from 14 domains.

## Method

OFA-TAD represents each sample via its Top-K neighbor-distance sequences under M distinct feature transformations (Raw, Standardized, MinMax, Quantile), converting variable-dimensional tabular inputs into fixed-length, semantics-agnostic distance profiles that are quantile-normalized to [0,1] for cross-domain comparability. A Mixture-of-Experts scoring network processes each view through a dedicated expert (MLP encoder + learnable positional embeddings + attention pooling over neighbor ranks + scoring MLP), while a gating network conditioned on all expert embeddings adaptively weights the view-specific scores to produce the final anomaly score. Training is performed once on a pooled source dataset using a multi-strategy pseudo-anomaly synthesis mechanism (manifold extrapolation, inter-cluster interpolation, noise injection, and feature masking) that converts the one-class setting into a binary classification problem with MSE loss, enabling end-to-end optimization without true anomaly labels.

## Results

OFA-TAD achieves the best average AUROC of 0.8345, AUPRC of 0.6629, and F1 of 0.6352 across 34 target datasets from 14 domains, outperforming all 9 baselines on average. Critically, OFA-TAD is trained only once on 7 source datasets and directly transferred to all 34 targets without any retraining or per-dataset hyperparameter tuning, whereas all baselines are trained separately for each target dataset (OFO paradigm). The method also demonstrates consistent scaling: adding one more source dataset yields an average +0.18% AUROC and +0.29% AUPRC gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFA-TAD achieves the best average AUROC (0.8345), AUPRC (0.6629), and F1 (0.6352) across all 34 target datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline DRL (0.8176 AUROC) by 1.69 points and MCM (0.8102 AUROC) by 2.43 points on average. The advantage is particularly notable given the asymmetric protocol: OFA-TAD is trained once on 7 source datasets with fixed hyperparameters, while all 9 baselines (LOF, KNN, iForest, DSVDD, AE, MCM, LUNAR, DRL, DisentAD) are individually trained and hyperparameter-tuned per target dataset. On individual datasets, no single method dominates — for example, DisentAD wins on census (0.8345 vs. 0.6955), DRL wins on cover (0.9872 vs. 0.9627), and iForest wins on breastw (0.9975 vs. 0.9791) — but OFA-TAD maintains the most consistent top-tier performance with the smallest variance across domains. Among deep baselines, DRL and MCM are the closest competitors, while classical methods (LOF, KNN, iForest) and reconstruction-based methods (AE, DSVDD) trail by 3–8 AUROC points on average. On out-of-domain datasets, OFA-TAD remains competitive (e.g., 0.9592 on backdoor, 0.8102 on Wilt, 0.8599 on SpamBase), whereas several baselines degrade sharply under domain shift (e.g., iForest drops to 0.4816 on Wilt, DSVDD to 0.3892).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing the one-for-all (OFA) paradigm for tabular anomaly detection, a setting that prior TAD work (iForest, LOF, DeepSVDD, LUNAR, MCM, DRL, DisentAD) has not addressed. Conceptually, it repurposes the neighbor-distance signal from classical distance-based detectors (LOF, kNN) as a domain-agnostic transferable representation, and borrows the Mixture-of-Experts routing architecture from NLP/vision to adaptively fuse multi-view distance evidence. The OFA framing itself follows the broader foundation-model trend in anomaly detection for images (InCTRL, AnomalyCLIP), graphs (ARC, AnomalyGFM), and time series (FAE, DADA), but is the first to instantiate it for tabular data, where the 'semantic gap' across domains makes transfer particularly challenging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single pre-trained detector that generalizes to unseen tabular datasets without per-dataset retraining or hyperparameter tuning could dramatically reduce deployment cost in real-world pipelines (fraud detection, medical monitoring, cybersecurity). However, performance on several datasets (WPBC, yeast, speech, amazon) remains near chance level, and the absolute AUROC gains over the best OFO baselines are modest (~1.7 points on average), suggesting that distance-profile transferability has limits for highly domain-specific anomaly patterns. Open questions include whether scaling to hundreds of source datasets yields further gains, whether learned (rather than handcrafted) view transformations could improve coverage, and how the approach behaves under extreme dimensionality shifts or very small context sets in production.
