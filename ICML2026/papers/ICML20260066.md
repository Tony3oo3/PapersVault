## Problem

Existing compositional guidance methods for diffusion models handle only simple conjunctions by heuristically averaging conditional outputs with fixed weights, failing to extend to disjunctions, negations, or arbitrary Boolean expressions. The paper asks: under what structural conditions on the joint distribution of atomic predicates can exact score-based guidance for complex logical formulas be computed from atomic guidance signals alone, without additional model evaluations?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Boolean logical formula over atomic predicates (e.g., color, digit class, protein binding target) and a noisy diffusion state x_t, and produces a guided score that steers the reverse diffusion SDE. At inference, a single conditional diffusion model trained with single-feature conditioning (one attribute active, others masked) supplies all atomic scores; a noise-aware classifier or score-based estimator provides the posterior probability scalars needed for composition. For molecular generation, the input additionally includes a 3D target protein structure, and the output is a ligand of fixed size (23 atoms) evaluated by docking score.

## Method

LOGDIFF derives an exact Boolean calculus for composing diffusion guidance scores, showing that exact composition is achievable whenever the formula admits a circuit representation in which conjunctions combine conditionally independent subformulas and disjunctions combine either conditionally independent (OR-CI) or mutually exclusive (OR-ME) subformulas. Under these conditions, recursive rules (Table 1) compute both the posterior probability and the logical score for each Boolean operator from atomic scores and posterior scalars, yielding a guidance signal that is a linear combination of atomic scores with probability-dependent coefficients rather than constant weights. The approach bridges classifier-free guidance (which supplies score directions) and classifier guidance (which supplies probability estimates) in a gradient-free manner: no backpropagation through a classifier is needed, and no additional diffusion model evaluations are required for composite formulas beyond the atomic ones. The paper further proves completeness for two broad distribution classes—conditionally independent categorical variables and taxonomy-structured predicates—showing that any Boolean formula is compilable into a valid circuit. An adaptive repulsive guidance mechanism extends each atomic condition A to A ∧ ¬B where B is the most probable competing class at each step, applying repulsion only where B is locally probable.

## Results

On CMNIST, LOGDIFF achieves 93.8–94.4% Conformity Score across recursive logical queries with N=2–5 AND/OR operators, far exceeding the unconditional baseline (28.3–48.9%) and Skreta et al. (26.2% on AND). On Shapes3D, LOGDIFF maintains 85.1–88.8% conformity for complex recursive queries. On CelebA, LOGDIFF achieves 94.5% CS with FID 11.2 for OR-CI and 94.3% CS with FID 12.1 for NOT, substantially outperforming Skreta et al. (89.7% CS / FID 13.9 for OR-CI) while matching Liu et al. on AND (61.1% CS). For dual-target molecular generation (GRM5-RRM1), LOGDIFF achieves a combined docking score of 73.20±3.18 for A∧B, surpassing DualDiff (71.87±3.33) and TargetDiff (71.01±2.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Skreta et al. (2025b), LOGDIFF dominates on AND (80.4% vs 26.2% on CMNIST; 82.4% vs 30.1% on Shapes3D) and OR-ME (98.0% vs 96.8% on CMNIST; 94.3% vs 74.4% on Shapes3D), while OR-CI is competitive (97.2% vs 97.5% on CMNIST; 96.0% vs 77.1% on Shapes3D). On CelebA, LOGDIFF outperforms Skreta et al. on all supported operations, with the largest gap on AND (61.1% vs 23.3% CS) and OR-ME (84.0% vs 77.8% CS). Against Liu et al. (2022), LOGDIFF ties exactly on AND (80.4% CMNIST, 82.4% Shapes3D, 61.1% CelebA) but slightly trails on NOT (96.5% vs 99.5% on CMNIST); however, LOGDIFF achieves a substantially lower FID for NOT on CelebA (12.1 vs 19.7), indicating better image quality. Critically, LOGDIFF extends to OR and recursive queries (N=2–5) where neither baseline defines composition rules. For molecular generation, LOGDIFF outperforms both TargetDiff (71.01) and DualDiff (71.87) on the combined dual-target docking score (73.20 for A∧B), and achieves the best on-target/off-target separation (Δ(A,B) = 0.94) for the A∧¬B selective binding task.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOGDIFF extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) from single-condition steering to exact Boolean composition of multiple atomic predicates, replacing the fixed-weight averaging used by Liu et al. (2022) and the probability-weighted superposition of Skreta et al. (2025b) with posterior-dependent coefficients derived from a recursive circuit evaluation. The theoretical backbone is borrowed from probabilistic circuits (Darwiche, 2022; Vergari et al., 2021), where decomposable product nodes and deterministic sum nodes guarantee tractable inference; here, the same structural conditions (conditional independence for conjunctions, CI or mutual exclusivity for disjunctions) guarantee exactness of the guidance composition. Unlike the concurrent fuzzy-logic framework of Blohm & Garg (2026), which satisfies Boolean identities only approximately, LOGDIFF provides an exact calculus under explicit distributional assumptions and proves completeness for two broad distribution classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The completeness result (Proposition C.2) showing that any Boolean formula is compilable into an exactly-evaluable circuit for conditionally-independent categorical variables and taxonomy-structured predicates gives practitioners a clear, checkable criterion for when logical guidance is guaranteed to be exact, rather than heuristic. The repulsive-guidance application (A ∧ ¬B with adaptive B selection) offers a drop-in improvement for single-class generation that suppresses class confusion without constant-weight heuristics. Key limitations remain: exactness depends on CI/ME assumptions that may hold only approximately in practice, the negation coefficient π/(1−π) amplifies posterior estimation errors, and the framework has not been stress-tested on highly correlated attribute sets where the structural conditions fail. Open questions include whether the completeness guarantee extends to richer dependency structures (e.g., Markov random fields over attributes) and whether the posterior estimators can be made more robust without sacrificing the gradient-free property.
