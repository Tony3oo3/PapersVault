## Problem

Masked diffusion language models decode only a few tokens per step, and lowering the confidence threshold to parallelize more aggressively causes a sharp quality drop. The paper identifies the root cause: permissive thresholds group interdependent tokens into the same parallel step, violating the conditional independence assumption, while standard training objectives leave tokens uncertain even when the argmax identity is already correct.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text sequences padded to 512 tokens, partitioned into semi-autoregressive blocks of 32 tokens. At each denoising step, the model receives a partially masked block (with a fixed 0.5 masking ratio during training) conditioned on fully denoised preceding blocks and still-masked subsequent blocks, and outputs per-token probability distributions over the vocabulary. At inference, the JSD-based grouping reuses cached logits from the previous block's forward pass to compute token sensitivity without any additional model evaluation.

## Method

SPEED combines a training-time sharpened teacher distillation with an inference-time JSD-based Slow-Fast Decoding. During training, a frozen teacher's logits are selectively distilled to a LoRA-adapted student via a temperature-scaled KL divergence (T_t=0.1 for the teacher, T_s=1.5 for the student) applied only to positions where the teacher already predicts the correct token, combined with a standard masked language modeling cross-entropy loss; the teacher is updated from student weights each epoch to progressively sharpen targets. At inference, each masked token's Jensen-Shannon Divergence between its predictive distribution with and without the preceding block is computed to partition tokens into a fast set (low JSD, decoded in parallel under an aggressive threshold) and a slow set (high JSD, decoded sequentially under a conservative threshold), with the slow set's threshold relaxed once all fast tokens are committed. A theoretical analysis in the appendix shows that this JSD-based grouping minimizes the expected KL error introduced by the conditional independence assumption in parallel decoding.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, SPEED achieves up to 12.2× speedup on MBPP (21 steps vs. 256 greedy) with 41.2% accuracy (vs. 42.4% greedy), and 9.8× speedup on GSM8K (26 steps) with 77.9% accuracy (vs. 79.3% greedy). On Dream-7B-Instruct, SPEED reaches 6.7× speedup on GSM8K (38 steps, 78.4% accuracy) and 4.7× on MBPP (55 steps, 55.6% accuracy). Adding JSD-based Slow-Fast Decoding recovers most of the accuracy gap at a small step-count cost, e.g., raising HumanEval from 40.9% to 42.1% (near the 42.6% greedy baseline) with only 2 extra steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPEED dominates all three accelerated baselines on both speed and accuracy. Against Few-step Decoding (4.0× fixed), SPEED achieves 2–3× higher speedup (e.g., 9.8× vs. 4.0× on GSM8K) while recovering 12+ accuracy points (77.9% vs. 65.6%). Against Threshold Decoding (Wu et al., 2025), which matches greedy accuracy at 3.3× speedup, SPEED nearly triples the speedup to 9.8× with only a 0.8-point accuracy drop. Against Consistency Distill (Deschenaux & Gulcehre, 2025), SPEED is both faster (9.8× vs. 4.0×) and more accurate (77.9% vs. 69.2% on GSM8K; 40.9% vs. 18.2% on HumanEval). The JSD-based Slow-Fast Decoding variant trades a small number of extra steps (e.g., 26→29 on GSM8K) to close most of the remaining gap to greedy, reaching 78.9% (vs. 79.3% greedy) and 42.1% on HumanEval (vs. 42.6% greedy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked diffusion language model architectures of LLaDA and Dream, and extends the confidence-aware parallel decoding framework introduced by Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025). It departs from prior work by reframing the decoding bottleneck as an iterative token grouping problem and identifying interdependent-token grouping as the root cause of quality degradation at permissive thresholds. The sharpened teacher distillation objective (selective temperature-scaled KL on teacher-correct positions) and the JSD-based Slow-Fast Decoding strategy are novel contributions that go beyond the uniform confidence thresholding used in existing parallel decoding methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.2× speedup on MBPP and 9.8× on GSM8K with accuracy within ~1.5 points of greedy decoding makes diffusion LLMs substantially more practical for real-time deployment, closing a significant gap with autoregressive models. The cross-domain generalization (trained on math, evaluated on code and summarization) suggests the distillation signal is not overfit to a narrow task distribution. Open questions include whether the fixed JSD cutoff and block-size hyperparameters transfer to larger models or longer sequences, and whether the reliance on filtered teacher-generated trajectories limits applicability when high-quality supervision is unavailable.
