## Problem

Existing multi-modal object ReID methods that leverage MLLM-generated text descriptions treat all image-text pairs equally, ignoring the varying correspondence degrees between visual and textual modalities. This uniform treatment causes the model to assign equal weight to strong- and weak-correspondence pairs, limiting its ability to learn discriminative cross-modal associations and leading to unstable optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes aligned RGB, NIR, and TIR image triplets (256×128 for person ReID, 128×256 for vehicle ReID) as visual input, supplemented by modality-specific text descriptions (35–56 characters each) generated by Qwen-VL during training only. At inference, no text is required—the model operates as a standard visual ReID system producing retrieval rankings evaluated via mAP and CMC at Rank-1/5/10. The text encoder (CLIP) is frozen, and the visual backbone is a CLIP-pretrained ViT fine-tuned with a small learning rate.

## Method

CCL introduces two synergistic modules on top of the DeMo baseline. The Correspondence-Guided Semantic Refinement (CGSR) module injects text semantics into visual tokens via cross-attention, computes per-token semantic consistency scores using a Gaussian kernel over cosine distance, and applies a difficulty-aware gate (d_i from Min-Max normalized previous-epoch losses) to fuse original and text-refined features—prioritizing refined features for easy samples and reverting to raw visual features for hard ones. The Cognitive-Driven Dynamic Optimization (CDDO) module assigns self-paced weights w_i = exp(−l_i/ρ) where ρ is fixed at the γ-quantile of warm-up losses, combined with a regularizer that penalizes premature inclusion of hard samples, enforcing a curriculum from easy to hard pairs. Training proceeds in two phases: a warm-up stage with standard cross-entropy and triplet loss to estimate difficulty, followed by the CDDO-weighted optimization phase.

## Results

On RGBNT201 (person ReID), CCL achieves 83.2% mAP and 87.4% Rank-1, surpassing the strongest baseline MFRNet (80.7% mAP, 83.6% R-1) by 2.5% and 3.8% respectively. On RGBNT100 (vehicle ReID), CCL reaches 88.0% mAP and 98.0% R-1, matching MFRNet's mAP (88.2%) while exceeding its R-1 by 0.6%. On the more challenging MSVR310, CCL attains 53.8% mAP and 69.5% R-1, outperforming MFRNet by 3.2% mAP and 4.7% R-1. Under missing-modality settings on RGBNT201, CCL achieves average 52.8% mAP and 52.7% R-1, a 2.1% mAP gain over DeMo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL outperforms all 27 compared baselines on RGBNT201 and MSVR310, with the largest margins over the most recent text-augmented methods: +3.0% mAP and +5.3% R-1 over IDEA, and +2.5% mAP and +3.8% R-1 over MFRNet on RGBNT201. On MSVR310, CCL leads MFRNet by 3.2% mAP and 4.7% R-1, and IDEA by 6.8% mAP and 7.1% R-1. On RGBNT100, CCL achieves 98.0% R-1 (best overall) but trails MFRNet by a narrow 0.2% in mAP (88.0 vs. 88.2), while still exceeding DeMo (+1.8% mAP) and IDEA (+0.8% mAP). In missing-modality experiments, CCL surpasses DeMo by 2.1% average mAP and 1.9% average R-1 across all six missing settings, and outperforms MFRNet by 3.7% mAP on average. Among optimization strategies, CCL's self-paced weighting beats classical SPL (+1.8% mAP), focal loss (+3.8% mAP), and inverse-rank weighting (+3.1% mAP) on RGBNT201.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeMo's decoupled feature-based mixture-of-experts architecture for multi-modal object ReID and on IDEA's use of MLLM-generated textual annotations as auxiliary supervision. The key conceptual departure is that prior text-augmented methods (notably IDEA) treat all image-text pairs uniformly, whereas CCL explicitly models the varying correspondence degree between visual and textual modalities, using it to guide both feature-level refinement (CGSR) and optimization-level weighting (CDDO). The CDDO module adapts the classical self-paced learning formulation (Kumar et al., 2010) to the cross-modal correspondence setting, while CGSR draws on cross-attention-based visual-linguistic verification (Yang et al., 2022) to perform token-level semantic consistency checking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored limitation in text-augmented multi-modal ReID: MLLM-generated descriptions have non-uniform quality, and naively treating all pairs equally degrades performance. The correspondence-aware framework provides a principled mechanism to handle this heterogeneity, and the empirical validation (correlation analysis, ablations, missing-modality robustness) is thorough. However, the method is tightly coupled to the DeMo backbone and the CLIP-based text encoder, raising open questions about whether the correspondence modeling transfers to other architectures or to settings where text is available at inference time. The moderate gains over IDEA (3% mAP on RGBNT201) suggest the ceiling of text-augmentation in this paradigm may be approaching, motivating exploration of richer or more reliable text generation strategies.
