## Problem

Existing time series tokenization methods encode a fixed number of samples per token (either one sample or a fixed-length patch), which generates excessive tokens for simple patterns like extended constants and cannot adapt to diverse temporal patterns of varying lengths and complexities. This rigidity imposes substantial computational overhead in transformer architectures and limits zero-shot generalization to unseen data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes univariate real-valued time series (normalized to zero mean, unit standard deviation) and produces a 64-sample forecast autoregressively from 128 context tokens. The input is first quantized into M discrete symbols via equiprobable bins, then compressed into variable-length motif tokens whose count depends on pattern complexity (average compression ranges from ~2× to 23× across datasets). The output is a categorical distribution over a finite motif vocabulary, decoded back to continuous values via bin centers or conditional decoding.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step adaptive tokenizer: (1) quantize the time series into discrete symbols using M equiprobable bins, then (2) apply byte pair encoding to iteratively merge the most frequent adjacent symbol pairs into new motif tokens, building a hierarchical vocabulary until new tokens fall below a minimum occurrence threshold p_min. A T5-based transformer is trained with cross-entropy loss for autoregressive next-token prediction over this discrete motif vocabulary, converting forecasting from regression to classification. A key post-hoc component, conditional decoding, replaces fixed bin centers with M² context-dependent parameters ω_{j,k} (the mean of all samples in bin j following bin k), computed analytically without gradients to reduce quantization error. The approach achieves 40% MSE improvement and 23× speedup over sample-based Chronos tokenization in zero-shot settings, with conditional decoding further reducing MSE by up to 48%.

## Results

On 7 zero-shot forecasting datasets, motif-based tokenization without conditional decoding improves MSE by 39.8% and accelerates inference by 23.14× on average compared to Chronos sample-based tokenization. With conditional decoding, these gains increase to 48.0% MSE reduction and 28.22× speedup. The method achieves the best MSE in 19 out of 25 comparisons against state-of-the-art foundation models (MOMENT, Moirai, Time-MoE, LightGTS) without any fine-tuning. On the Traffic dataset, Chronos models diverge entirely during zero-shot evaluation while the motif-based tokenizer maintains competitive performance (MSE 0.591 vs. 2.717).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chronos sample-based tokenization (the primary baseline), the motif-based tokenizer wins on all 7 datasets in both MSE and inference speed, with the largest gains on Traffic (MSE 0.591 vs. 2.717, a 78% reduction) and Fev-bench (1.006 vs. 1.489). Against patch-based Chronos (length 4 and 8), the method outperforms on 5 of 7 datasets, losing only on ETTh1 and Fev-bench where the best patch model (large, len=8) edges ahead slightly. Against MOMENT and Time-MoE foundation models, the motif-based tokenizer consistently outperforms all model sizes across all 7 datasets. Against Moirai, it wins on 4 of 7 datasets (notably Solar: 0.371 vs. 0.556 for Moirai-large), while Moirai retains an advantage on ETTh1, ETTm1, and Weather. Against LightGTS, the method is better on 5 of 7 datasets. In comparisons with non-foundation models specifically trained on each dataset (Autoformer, FEDformer, Informer, Non-stationary, vanilla Transformer), the zero-shot motif-based model wins in 19 out of 25 cases, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes byte pair encoding from NLP (Sennrich et al., 2016) to time series, replacing the two dominant tokenization paradigms—sample-based tokens (Chronos) and fixed-length patches (PatchTST, MOMENT, Moirai)—with an adaptive, pattern-centric scheme that merges recurring motifs of variable length into single tokens. It is built directly on the Chronos foundation model, sharing its T5 architecture, training strategy, and 11M-series dataset, with tokenization as the sole architectural difference. The conditional decoding component is a novel post-hoc optimization that exploits the finite discrete vocabulary and continuous underlying signal, with no direct analogue in prior time series work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of 40% MSE improvement and 23× inference speedup in a zero-shot setting makes this a practically significant efficiency-quality tradeoff for time series foundation models, and the conditional decoding offers a drop-in, gradient-free refinement applicable to any discrete-vocabulary model. Key limitations include the restriction to univariate series, the use of a single T5 backbone without hyperparameter tuning, and the fact that the large speedup partly reflects comparing a large Chronos model against a smaller motif-based one. Open questions include extension to multivariate settings, integration with newer architectures (state-space models, mixture-of-experts), and whether the adaptive compression advantage persists at even larger scales.
