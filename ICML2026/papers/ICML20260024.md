## Problem

Model-driven lossless compression using LLMs as predictors is fragile to model non-determinism: even tiny floating-point differences between encoder and decoder machines desynchronize arithmetic coding and cause complete decoding failure. The original PMATIC framework addressed this but relied on loose theoretical bounds and fixed-size quantization bins, incurring large code-length overhead and limiting its practical robustness range to very small mismatch values (ε ≤ 0.02).

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a text token string over a finite alphabet and an LLM prediction model, converting each token to a fixed-length bitstring (longform of ⌈log₂|A|⌉ bits). The compressed message is a bitstring produced by arithmetic coding over an expanded sequence of interleaved helper bits and token bits, prefixed with the message-optimized parameters (bin boundaries, bin centers, and helper-bit probability). The decoder, given the compressed message and its own (potentially slightly different) LLM predictions, recovers the exact original token string.

## Method

The paper generalizes PMATIC with three key improvements. First, a tight worst-case bound (Proposition 1) shows that bounded logit mismatch ‖u−v‖∞ ≤ ε implies at most 2ε difference in the log-odds of any conditional next-bit probability, replacing the looser bound from the original formulation and enabling smaller danger-zones. Second, quantization bins are placed non-uniformly in log-odds space, with boundaries optimized per-message via a greedy recursive algorithm that minimizes a composite objective of quantization loss (binary KL divergence), helper-bit entropy, and a per-bin parameter transmission penalty. Third, the encoder pre-computes all next-bit probabilities on the message and optimizes bin boundaries, bin representative values (set to the mean of probabilities in each bin interior), and the helper-bit encoding probability (set to the empirical fraction of danger-zone hits), attaching these 2m parameters as a prefix to the compressed message. The encoding/decoding mechanism pairs each token bit with a helper bit: if the encoder's next-bit probability falls in a bin interior, both sides use the bin representative; if it falls in a danger-zone near a boundary, both sides use the boundary value, with the helper bit signaling which case applies.

## Results

On Llama 3.1 8B, PMATIC achieves compression ratios of 0.073–0.116 at ε=0.002 (vs. 0.060–0.105 for pure arithmetic coding), and still outperforms gzip at ε=0.3 (ratios of 0.163–0.293). The updated PMATIC formulation achieves 10–15× greater mismatch robustness than the original at equivalent compression ratios: for Llama and Qwen, ε=0.02 in the updated version matches or beats ε=0.002 in the original, and ε=0.3 often matches the original's ε=0.002. Under real cross-device non-determinism (M4 Pro vs. M2 Pro), no files were decoded incorrectly for ε≥0.03, while standard arithmetic coding failed on every file, typically within the first 5 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard arithmetic coding (Witten et al., 1987), PMATIC incurs a modest overhead at low robustness (e.g., ~15–20% higher compression ratio at ε=0.002) but this grows with ε; at ε=0.3 the ratio roughly doubles, yet still beats gzip. Against gzip (Mittu et al., 2024), PMATIC with an LLM predictor achieves substantially better compression even at ε=0.3 (e.g., 0.163–0.293 on Llama vs. gzip's typical ~0.3–0.4 for text), demonstrating that the robustness cost does not negate the LLM's predictive advantage. The most direct comparison is with the original PMATIC (Adler & Tang, 2026, Table 3): the updated formulation at ε=0.02 achieves compression ratios of 0.073–0.161 on Llama, versus 0.110–0.297 for the original at the same ε, and the updated ε=0.03 (0.098–0.177) matches or beats the original's ε=0.002 (0.110–0.168), yielding a 10–15× robustness gain at equal compression. For Mistral, the improvement is 5–10×. Under real cross-device mismatch, standard arithmetic coding failed on 100% of files, while PMATIC at ε≥0.03 decoded all files correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct generalization of PMATIC (Adler & Tang, 2026), which first introduced mismatch-tolerant interval coding as a drop-in replacement for arithmetic coding in model-driven compression pipelines. The key departures are threefold: a tight worst-case log-odds bound (Proposition 1) replacing the original's looser theoretical result, non-uniform quantization bins optimized per message, and message-optimized helper-bit encoding probabilities. It also sits alongside a concurrent but distinct approach by Hu & Tang (2026) that addresses a slightly different mismatch setting by transmitting probability ranges rather than using quantized bins with helper bits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model non-determinism is a practical and well-documented obstacle to deploying LLM-based lossless compression across heterogeneous hardware, and this work shows that the original PMATIC's operating regime (ε ≤ 0.02) was artificially constrained by loose bounds and uniform binning rather than by fundamental limits. The 10–15× robustness gain at matched compression ratio, validated on real M4 Pro vs. M2 Pro hardware, makes mismatch-tolerant coding substantially more deployable. Open questions include extending to other modalities (images, checkpoints), principled selection of ε, stochastic (rather than worst-case) error models, and more efficient bin optimization than the current greedy heuristic.
