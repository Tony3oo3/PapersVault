## Problem

Prior LatentQA work trained specialized decoders for narrow tasks (SAE feature interpretation, system prompt extraction, user belief description) and evaluated them only in similarly narrow settings. The generalist vision of training LLMs to answer arbitrary natural-language questions about activations had not been systematically pursued, and it was unknown whether such models could generalize to practically relevant downstream auditing tasks where the target information exists only in model weights and never appears in the input text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262059/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Activation Oracle accepts K activation vectors (K ranging from 1 to 50) extracted from any layer of a target LLM's residual stream, paired with a natural-language question in a structured prompt that includes the source layer number and placeholder tokens. Activations are injected via norm-matched additive steering at placeholder positions after the oracle's second transformer layer, automatically scaling vectors of varying provenance (single tokens, sequences, activation differences, SAE feature vectors) to a consistent magnitude without per-source calibration. The oracle produces a free-form natural-language answer; for binary tasks, the logit difference between 'yes' and 'no' tokens provides a zero-cost confidence score, and for open-ended tasks, majority-vote consensus across sampled rollouts yields a precision-recall trade-off.

## Method

The core injection mechanism is norm-matched additive steering: at each placeholder position, the target activation vector is scaled to match the L2 norm of the existing residual stream activation and added (rather than replacing, as in Pan et al.), which prevents the 100,000× norm explosion observed with replacement-based injection at layer 1. Training uses LoRA adapters (rank 64) on the base model, with activations collected on-the-fly from three layer depths (25%, 50%, 75%) of the target model, and the training mixture combines three task types: system prompt question-answering (SPQA), seven binary classification tasks reformulated as natural-language yes/no questions, and a self-supervised context prediction task (predicting previous or next tokens from activation sequences) that scales to unlimited data from any text corpus. The key empirical finding is that both the quantity and diversity of training data independently improve out-of-distribution generalization: a controlled ablation holding total sample count fixed shows that adding the context prediction task improves performance, and scaling from 400k to 1M examples yields further gains. The resulting AOs, trained exclusively on activations from the base model, are applied zero-shot to fine-tuned variants and match or exceed white-box interpretability baselines on all four downstream auditing tasks without any task-specific scaffolding.

## Results

On the secret-keeping benchmarks, the full Activation Oracle achieves 21% accuracy on Taboo (vs. 4% for the best white-box logit-lens baseline), 59% on Secret Side Constraint (vs. 52% for logit lens), and 91% on User Gender (SPQA-only best learning rate; full mixture performs at or above this). On the emergent misalignment model-diffing evaluation, the AO matches the Activation Difference Lens (ADL) baseline at 2/5 under the grading rubric. On PersonaQA, accuracy improves from ~5% (untrained base) to ~14% (full training mixture) for open-source models, with Claude Haiku 3.5 reaching 35%. Across all four downstream tasks, the best AOs match or exceed white-box baselines and match or exceed the overall best baseline (white- or black-box) on 3 of 4 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the secret-keeping baselines from Cywiński et al. (2025), the full AO outperforms all white-box methods on all three tasks: 21% vs. 4% on Taboo (logit lens), 59% vs. 52% on Secret Side Constraint (logit lens), and ≥91% on User Gender (SAE-based). The AO also matches or exceeds the best black-box method (response continuation with a prefilled prompt) on two of the three tasks, while using a single fixed oracle prompt rather than the multi-stage agent pipelines required by the baselines. On the emergent misalignment model-diffing evaluation, the AO ties the ADL baseline from Minder et al. (2025) at 2/5, which represents the effective ceiling given that the fine-tuned models include 50% chat data suppressing broad misalignment. Against the untrained PatchScopes baseline (Ghandeharioun et al., 2024), all trained AO configurations improve on PersonaQA and held-out classification, with the full mixture yielding the largest gains (e.g., ~5% → ~14% on PersonaQA for Qwen3-8B). The SPQA-only AO is already within the standard error of the best white-box methods on all three secret-keeping tasks, but the full mixture provides consistent additional improvements and greater robustness to hyperparameter choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LatentQA (Pan et al., 2024), which introduced open-ended question-answering about LLM activations and a fine-tuning method for training decoders, by shifting from narrow single-task settings (SAE feature interpretation, system prompt QA, user belief description) to a generalist formulation. The key methodological departure is replacing Pan et al.'s activation-replacement steering with norm-matched additive injection, and scaling training data diversity from a single SPQA dataset to a mixture of classification tasks and a novel self-supervised context prediction objective. The paper positions AOs as complementary to mechanistic interpretability methods (logit lens, SAEs, activation steering) rather than a replacement, while demonstrating that a simple natural-language QA interface can match or exceed those specialized techniques on downstream auditing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that AOs provide an out-of-the-box, task-agnostic interface for activation interpretation that requires no per-task scaffolding or tuning, potentially lowering the barrier to activation-based auditing for practitioners without deep interpretability expertise. Key limitations include the black-box nature (no mechanistic primitives or drill-down capability), the fact that AOs only answer questions they are asked (unlike SAEs which can surface unknown features), and that benchmarks use narrow fine-tunes that may not reflect realistic post-training complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to richer multi-behavior fine-tuning, how to improve calibration and uncertainty expression, and whether cross-model activation interpretation (explaining activations from a different architecture) is feasible.
