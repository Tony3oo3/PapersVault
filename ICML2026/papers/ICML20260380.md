## Problem

Vision Transformers lack explicit spatial inductive bias because their self-attention is permutation equivariant, treating image patches as an unordered set. This makes ViTs data-hungry and particularly weak when model capacity is small or training data is limited, since they cannot rely on built-in locality to capture spatial structure. The paper asks whether Space Filling Curve (SFC)-inspired structure in attention can inject spatial priors into ViTs to improve performance in these resource-constrained regimes.

## Input / Output

VIOLIN operates on standard RGB images divided into 2D patch grids (e.g., 14×14 patches at 224×224, or individual pixels for CIFAR-100) and is applied as a plug-and-play module inside existing ViT backbones (DeiT, DeiT-III, DINO, Swin) without altering the architecture or training recipe. For classification it outputs a single class label; for dense prediction it produces per-pixel segmentation masks (ADE20K) or bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO). The mask generalizes to arbitrary input shapes and resolutions without positional embedding interpolation, since the decay mask depends only on pairwise token distances.

## Method

VIOLIN replaces standard softmax attention with a decay-masked variant where the attention matrix QK^T/√d is element-wise multiplied by a composite mask M_VIOLIN before softmax. The mask is constructed by flattening the 2D patch grid along eight Space Filling Curves (Snake, Zig-zag, Peano, Hilbert, and their transposed variants), each inducing a Kac-Murdock-Szegö decay mask M_c[i,j] = γ_c^|i−j| with a learnable per-curve decay factor γ_c = sigmoid(β_c). All curve-specific masks are permuted into a common Z-curve basis order and averaged, so that a single shared QK^T matrix is reused across all curves. A learnable scalar α scales the mask's influence on attention, and the entire module adds only 0.0015% parameters and 0.64% FLOPs relative to DeiT-B. Because the mask is freshly initialized and jointly optimized during fine-tuning, VIOLIN can be applied to any pretrained global-attention ViT without retraining, and it composes naturally with parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA and DoRA.

## Results

On VTAB-1K fine-tuning, VIOLIN consistently improves all tested ViT backbones (DeiT, DeiT-III, DINO from 5M to 632M parameters), with average gains of 1.41–4.74% and up to +8.69% on the Structured task group (DeiT-III-S). On ImageNet-1K pretraining, VIOLIN adds +0.8% (DeiT-T) and +0.9% (DeiT-S) top-1 accuracy under supervised training, and +0.6–0.7% on DINO KNN/Linear evaluations. On pixel-level CIFAR-100, a highly location-dependent task, VIOLIN achieves a striking +7.2% improvement (60.8% → 68.0%). For dense prediction, VIOLIN yields +0.56 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation and +0.4 mask AP on COCO instance segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the direct comparison of locality-enforcing methods on the VTAB Structured group (Table 8), VIOLIN (61.89%) outperforms all four baselines: Swin RPB (61.58%, +0.31%), i-RPE QKV (61.45%, +0.44%), LocalViT (61.50%, +0.39%), and Manhattan Mask / RMT (58.37%, +3.52%). The margin over RMT's Manhattan-distance mask is the largest, confirming that the multi-curve SFC averaging is the key differentiator rather than the mere presence of a local bias. Notably, VIOLIN achieves these gains with only ~1.3K extra parameters, compared to ~105K for Swin RPB, ~115K for i-RPE QKV, and ~6.2M for LocalViT. On the broader VTAB-1K benchmark, VIOLIN's plug-and-play mask (freshly initialized at fine-tuning) improves every model scale tested, with the largest relative gains on smaller models (DeiT-T: +2.81% avg) and the Structured group specifically, where spatial reasoning is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIOLIN synthesizes two distinct research threads: the decay-mask mechanism from Linear Transformers (RetNet, Katharopoulos et al.) and the multi-directional scanning patterns used in Vision SSMs (Vision Mamba, VMamba, Vision xLSTM). Rather than adopting a new architecture, it repurposes the Kac-Murdock-Szegő decay matrix as a plug-and-play mask on standard softmax attention, guided by multiple Space Filling Curves (Snake, Zig-zag, Peano, Hilbert and their transposes). This positions it as a lightweight alternative to heavier spatial-bias methods like ConViT, CMT, or RMT (which uses a single Manhattan-distance mask), and as a drop-in complement to PEFT methods like LoRA and DoRA rather than a replacement for the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that VIOLIN can be injected into any pretrained global-attention ViT at fine-tuning time with negligible cost (0.0015% extra parameters), removing the need for costly retraining from scratch to gain spatial inductive bias. This is particularly impactful for resource-constrained settings—small models, limited data, medical imaging, satellite analysis—where ViTs are most vulnerable to their lack of locality. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video understanding, multimodal models, and whether the specific curve set is optimal or whether learned/adaptive curve selection could further improve results. The paper also notes that masks learned during fine-tuning outperform those learned during pretraining, suggesting the spatial bias is task-specific rather than universal.
