## Problem

While Rényi DP (RDP) is the standard analytical tool for privacy accounting, its guarantees must ultimately be expressed as f-DP trade-off functions to be operationally meaningful. Prior work solved the conversion for a single Rényi order (Asoodeh et al., 2021) and conjectured the multi-order result (Zhu et al., 2022, Appendix F.3), but no proof established whether the intersection-of-regions rule is truly optimal or whether a tighter black-box conversion could exist.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a full RDP profile ρ(τ) defined over the extended domain τ ∈ [0.5, ∞) (a non-decreasing function giving the divergence bound at each order) and produces the optimal f-DP trade-off function f(α) = sup_{τ≥0.5} f_{τ,ρ(τ)}(α), a convex non-increasing curve on [0,1]. The conversion is strictly black-box: it depends only on the profile values and is oblivious to the underlying mechanism's structure. In practice, the supremum is computed over a logarithmically spaced finite grid of orders (e.g., τ ∈ [0.5, 100]), with each single-order curve evaluated analytically from the Bernoulli divergence constraints.

## Method

The core construction defines, for each order τ, a convex RDP privacy region R_{D_τ}(ρ(τ)) in the (α, β) error-pair plane whose boundary is given by explicit inequalities derived from the 2-cut reduction of the Rényi divergence onto Bernoulli distributions. The optimal f-DP bound is the lower boundary of the intersection of all such regions over τ ≥ 0.5, which by a projection lemma equals the pointwise supremum of the individual single-order boundaries. Optimality is proven by constructing witness mechanisms—specific Randomized Response (Bernoulli) instances—that exactly saturate the intersection boundary at every point, so that any purportedly tighter bound would be violated by a valid mechanism. The proof leverages the joint quasi-convexity of Rényi divergence, the affine structure of the Bernoulli parameterization, and a contradiction argument showing no admissible conversion rule can uniformly dominate the intersection bound in the Blackwell sense.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 4.4) proves that the intersection-of-RDP-privacy-regions conversion, f_{ρ(·)}(α) = sup_{τ≥0.5} f_{τ,ρ(τ)}(α), is the pointwise tightest possible black-box conversion from an RDP profile to an f-DP trade-off function: no admissible conversion rule can yield a strictly tighter bound for any valid profile or any Type I error level. Proposition 3.1 establishes the single-order analogue, showing that for a fixed (τ,ρ) guarantee, the lower boundary of the τ-order RDP privacy region is optimal, with Randomized Response mechanisms serving as witness mechanisms that saturate every boundary point. Proposition 4.6 further shows that for the Symmetric Randomized Response mechanism, the intersection conversion exactly recovers the true trade-off function (pure DP region), while for the Gaussian mechanism (Figure 3) the conversion remains a valid but strictly loose lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or quantitative comparisons. The 'comparison' is against all other admissible black-box conversion rules: Theorem 4.4 proves that the intersection-based rule Blackwell-dominates every other rule that takes only the RDP profile ρ(·) as input, meaning no alternative conversion can uniformly provide a tighter trade-off function. The paper positions its result as unifying and sharpening three prior contributions: Balle et al. (2019) introduced the 2-cut reduction and RDP privacy regions; Asoodeh et al. (2021) solved the variational problem for a single Rényi order; and Zhu et al. (2022) conjectured (in Appendix F.3) that the intersection of all single-order regions is optimal. This paper proves that conjecture. The practical implication is that the conversion is 'end of the road' for black-box RDP-to-f-DP: any further tightening would require mechanism-specific information beyond the RDP profile, as demonstrated by the gap between the converted bound and the true trade-off for the Gaussian mechanism (Figure 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper proves the conjecture from Zhu et al. (2022, Appendix F.3) that the intersection of single-order RDP privacy regions yields the optimal black-box conversion from an RDP profile to an f-DP trade-off function. It unifies and sharpens three prior threads: Balle et al. (2019) introduced the 2-cut reduction and the geometric RDP privacy region; Asoodeh et al. (2021) solved the variational problem for a single Rényi order τ; and Zhu et al. (2022) extended the analysis to full profiles and conjectured the intersection rule's optimality. The key departure is moving from single-order validity to a universal optimality proof over the entire profile, using Bernoulli (Randomized Response) witness mechanisms to show no tighter bound can exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result establishes a hard theoretical ceiling for RDP-to-f-DP conversion: no black-box rule using only the RDP profile can do better, so further improvements require mechanism-specific information beyond the profile. Practically, it validates a simple computational recipe (pointwise maximum of analytic single-order curves) as the definitive conversion, simplifying privacy accounting pipelines. An important open question the paper highlights is characterizing mechanism classes for which the black-box conversion is near-tight; the Gaussian mechanism, despite being the most widely used, remains a loose case, suggesting structural gaps between continuous and discrete mechanisms that warrant further study.
