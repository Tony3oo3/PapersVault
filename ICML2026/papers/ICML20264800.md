## Problem

Existing loss reweighting strategies for long-tailed classification are target-agnostic: they adjust class weights heuristically without specifying what the ideal class-wise loss distribution should look like at the end of training. This paper identifies that Neural Collapse geometry (Simplex ETF alignment of class means and classifier weights) necessarily implies equal per-class average loss, and proves that persistent loss imbalance fundamentally obstructs convergence to this ideal geometry, thereby providing a principled target that prior reweighting methods lacked.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist) and outputs class labels via a standard linear classifier. The reweighting is computed entirely from per-class loss statistics within each mini-batch (no additional network or forward pass is needed), making it a plug-and-play module that can be layered on top of any existing long-tailed training pipeline. The effective per-class weight is a product of a batch-wise closed-form inverse solution and a macro-level batch-frequency compensation factor, both updated online during training.

## Method

The paper formulates loss reweighting as a Tikhonov-regularized inverse problem: given the current per-class average losses L_c(W) and their mean L̄(W), find class weights w_c that drive w_c·L_c(W) toward the equal-loss target L̄(W), regularized toward a prior w_c^(0). Because the objective is separable and quadratic, it admits a closed-form per-class solution w_c* = (L̄·L_c + α·w_c^(0)) / (L_c² + α), requiring no iterative optimization. In practice, this is applied batch-wise: for each mini-batch, per-class mean losses are estimated from the samples present, the closed-form weight is computed, and a macro-level compensation β_c ∝ B_c^(−γ) (where B_c tracks how many batches class c has appeared in) is multiplied in to counteract the fact that tail classes receive far fewer gradient updates over training. The method is combined with an optional Mittag-Leffler-function-based learning-rate schedule that decays slowly in the late stage to preserve plasticity for tail-class fitting, and is validated to reduce NC2/NC3 metrics and the loss-imbalance coefficient ρ(W) while achieving SOTA accuracy across four long-tailed benchmarks.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), the proposed inverse reweighting with learning-rate schedule (CE*+Loss+LR) achieves 48.9% top-1 accuracy, improving over the CE baseline (41.6%) by +7.3 points. When combined with the strong GLMC baseline, the method reaches SOTA performance of 91.1% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=50) and 63.9% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=50). On large-scale benchmarks, GLMC+Loss+LR achieves 57.6% on ImageNet-LT and 74.8% on iNaturalist, both setting new state-of-the-art results. In the dedicated reweighting comparison (Table 1), the method outperforms all eight reweighting baselines by 2.4–11.6 points across IF=50/100/200 on CIFAR-100-LT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the dedicated reweighting comparison (Table 1, CIFAR-100-LT), the proposed method (52.59/47.88/43.14 at IF=50/100/200) outperforms all eight reweighting baselines: TCR (50.19/45.35/40.57), Focal (48.71/42.13/38.22), Range (48.21/43.03/38.51), CB (48.11/43.34/39.05), IB (48.02/45.14/40.55), Inv-Sqrt (47.85/42.45/35.69), Inv-Freq (47.66/38.19/25.25), and CE (45.51/41.62/36.78), with the largest margin over Inv-Freq at IF=200 (+17.9 points). In the SOTA comparison (Table 2), the method consistently improves every baseline it is combined with: CE* gains +6.0/+7.3 points on CIFAR-10/100-LT (IF=100), CMO gains +7.2/+4.9 points, ETF-DR gains +1.4/+2.7 points, and GLMC gains +1.3/+2.7 points. The strongest SOTA result is GLMC+Loss+LR at 91.1% (CIFAR-10-LT, IF=50) and 63.9% (CIFAR-100-LT, IF=50), surpassing B-SCL (88.0/56.4), FeatRecon (85.2/52.5), and RBL (87.6/57.2). On ImageNet-LT, GLMC+Loss+LR reaches 57.6% overall accuracy, beating Focal-SAM (54.3%), RIDE (54.9%), and INC-DRW (53.9%). On iNaturalist, GLMC+Loss+LR achieves 74.8%, outperforming IP-DPP (74.0%), GLMC+SEL (74.5%), and FeatRecon (72.9%). The method never degrades a baseline; in every tested combination it either matches or exceeds the original method's accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads: heuristic loss reweighting for long-tailed classification (inverse-frequency, class-balanced, focal, influence-balanced losses) and Neural Collapse (NC) geometry as an ideal training endpoint. Prior reweighting methods are 'target-agnostic'—they prescribe weights via frequency or influence heuristics without specifying what the class-wise loss distribution should look like at convergence. NC-inspired methods (ETF-DR, RBL, INC, DisA) enforce the Simplex ETF geometry directly through architectural or loss modifications, but do not address the reweighting problem per se. This paper bridges the two by using NC to derive an explicit reweighting target (equal per-class average loss) and then inverting the problem: rather than prescribing weights forward, it solves a Tikhonov-regularized inverse problem to recover the weights that achieve the target, yielding a closed-form per-class solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, non-heuristic justification for loss reweighting grounded in NC theory, and the inverse-problem formulation is elegant in that it requires no additional trainable parameters or extra backpropagation—just a closed-form computation per batch. The theoretical results (Theorem 3.1: NC implies equal loss; Theorem 3.2: persistent loss imbalance precludes ETF convergence) give a clean formal argument for why reweighting should target loss equalization. Limitations include reliance on standard long-tailed benchmarks, the need to tune the macro-compensation exponent γ, and the fact that the NC framework is best characterized for balanced settings—its extension to strongly imbalanced regimes remains partially heuristic. Open questions include whether the equal-loss target remains optimal under multi-modal class structures or when combined with representation-learning objectives, and whether the inverse formulation generalizes beyond linear classifiers.
