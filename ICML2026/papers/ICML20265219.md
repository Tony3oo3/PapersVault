## Problem

Existing triggerable watermarking methods against model extraction attacks require extensive retraining to embed watermarks, limiting post-deployment flexibility, and lack clear theoretical grounding, making them vulnerable to adaptive attacks that recover clean labels. The paper identifies that these failures stem from an information-theoretic bottleneck: in hard-label settings, the channel capacity is fully consumed by task label transmission, leaving no room for additional watermark information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265219/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image, a set of registered composite in-distribution watermark samples, and the target model's last-layer features and original logits. It produces a modified prediction by either interpolating original logits with reference watermark-class logits (logit-mixing) or probabilistically flipping the predicted label via Bernoulli sampling guided by the computed feature similarity. The similarity score is clipped to [0,1] and optionally squared when the model's max softmax confidence exceeds 0.95 to reduce impact on high-confidence predictions.

## Method

Neural Honeytrace is a training-free, plug-and-play watermarking framework built on two insights: (1) triggerable watermarking is a reverse application of the long-tailed effect, where similarity between query and watermark features is implicitly transmitted through output probabilities, and (2) watermark transmission fails when the source rate exceeds channel capacity per Shannon's theorem. The framework operates in two stages: first, it computes feature-space similarity between the query and registered watermarks, then embeds this similarity into predictions via logit-mixing (interpolating original and reference logits with an exponential similarity weight α). Second, it performs multi-step watermark transmission by constructing a probability-guided label-flipping matrix where each query's label is flipped with probability s^β (Bernoulli sampling), effectively increasing the channel capacity to N·C across N queries and enabling reliable watermark transmission even under adaptive attacks that recover individual labels.

## Results

On CUB-200 (ResNet-50), Neural Honeytrace achieves a minimum Watermark Success Rate (WSR) of 25.6%, requiring only 590 queries for worst-case t-test-based ownership verification—roughly 2% of the 24,156 queries needed by MEA-Defender (the strongest baseline) and ~11,000× fewer than DAWN (6.7M queries). On CIFAR-10 (VGG16-BN), it attains an average WSR of 58.60% and a minimum WSR of 36.60% across all adaptive attacks, while maintaining the highest protected accuracy (91.37%). All results are achieved with zero training cost, as the watermark is applied as a plug-and-play post-deployment modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural Honeytrace outperforms all four baselines on the critical minimum WSR metric, which determines worst-case ownership verification. Against MEA-Defender (the strongest baseline), it achieves 36.60% vs. 11.60% min WSR on CIFAR-10 and 25.60% vs. 4.00% on CUB-200, translating to a ~40× reduction in required verification queries. DAWN achieves a competitive average WSR (~43–44%) but its minimum WSR collapses to 6.45% (CIFAR-10) and 0.24% (CUB-200) under adaptive attacks like Smoothing, making it unreliable in practice. EWE is the most vulnerable baseline, dropping to 2.10% min WSR on CIFAR-10 and 0.00% on CIFAR-100 under Top-1 attacks. Composite Backdoor fails entirely on CUB-200 (0.00% min WSR) and Caltech-256 (0.00% min WSR), rendering it unusable for ownership claims on those datasets. The key differentiator is that Neural Honeytrace's training-free multi-step transmission strategy maintains high WSR even under the most aggressive adaptive attacks (Smoothing, Top-1, D-DAE), whereas all baselines suffer severe WSR degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the long-tailed effect observed in backdoor learning (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2024), repurposing it in reverse to enable training-free watermark transmission during model extraction. It departs from prior triggerable watermarking methods (EWE, MEA-Defender, Composite Backdoor, DAWN) that all require model retraining by reframing watermark implantation as an information-theoretic communication problem, where the model output serves as the channel and similarity between query and watermark features is the transmitted message. The multi-step label-flipping strategy addresses the fundamental capacity bottleneck identified via Shannon's theorem and the data processing inequality, which explains why existing methods fail under hard-label or adaptive-attack settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of Neural Honeytrace removes the primary practical barrier to deploying model watermarks post-deployment, enabling owners to add, modify, or remove watermarks without retraining. The information-theoretic analysis provides a principled explanation for why prior methods fail (source rate exceeding channel capacity in hard-label settings), guiding future defense design. Open questions include scalability to very large foundation models, robustness against future adaptive attacks that specifically target the similarity-based modulation, and whether the approach extends cleanly to multimodal or language models where the output space structure differs substantially.
