## Problem

Standard Bayesian Optimization with stationary Gaussian Process surrogates assumes uniform smoothness across the search space, but many scientific design problems—molecular conformation search, drug discovery across heterogeneous scaffolds, fusion reactor design—exhibit discrete regime structure with abrupt transitions. A single GP either oversmooths sharp boundaries or hallucinates noise in smooth regions, yielding miscalibrated uncertainty that degrades optimization performance on multi-regime objectives.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a sequential BO loop over a continuous d-dimensional search space (normalized to [-1,1]^d), consuming one noisy function evaluation per iteration. At each step it outputs the next query point (via multi-start L-BFGS-B maximization of a mixture acquisition function), the predictive mean and variance at any candidate decomposed into intra-regime aleatoric variance and inter-regime epistemic disagreement, and the latent regime assignment for each observed point. The number of regimes is not specified a priori but discovered adaptively from data.

## Method

RAMBO replaces the monolithic GP surrogate with a Dirichlet Process Mixture of Gaussian Processes, where each latent regime is modeled by an independent GP with locally-optimized hyperparameters (signal variance, length scale, noise variance) drawn from Inverse-Gamma base measure priors. Posterior inference uses collapsed Gibbs sampling that analytically marginalizes the latent function values, reducing the MCMC state space to discrete regime assignments and hyperparameters for improved mixing. A key predictive modeling choice replaces the input-independent CRP weights with spatially-modulated weights w_k(x) ∝ π_k · σ_{*,k}^{-1}(x), justified independently by expected posterior responsibility and Jeffreys' scale-invariant reference measure, introducing no additional learnable parameters. An adaptive Log-Sqrt concentration parameter schedule (α_t = α_0·√t/log(t+e)) enforces early parsimony and enables progressive fine-grained regime discovery as data accumulates. The mixture Expected Improvement acquisition function decomposes total uncertainty into intra-regime variance and inter-regime disagreement, naturally targeting regime boundaries for structural refinement.

## Results

On the 12D molecular conformer optimization (pentadecane), RAMBO reaches 8.05 kcal/mol after 200 iterations versus 13.36 kcal/mol for the best baseline (SAASBO), a 39.73% improvement. On the 50D virtual screening task (ZINC15/6T2W docking), RAMBO achieves a docking score of −13.31 by iteration 500 compared to −12.79 for TuRBO (4.06% improvement in predicted binding affinity). On the 80D ConStellaration stellarator design benchmark, RAMBO attains quasi-isodynamic quality (Q_i) approximately 51.55% higher than the best competing method. Across the synthetic Levy and Schwefel benchmarks (6D and 10D), RAMBO with adaptive α-scheduling consistently matches or outperforms all nine baselines, with the largest gaps on the deceptive 10D Schwefel landscape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMBO outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. On the 12D molecular conformer task, SAASBO is the strongest baseline (13.36 kcal/mol) but still trails RAMBO (8.05) by 39.73%; TuRBO, HEBO, and Bounce all stagnate at high-energy local minima. On the 50D drug discovery task, TuRBO is the closest competitor (−12.79 vs. RAMBO's −13.31), while embedding-based methods (ALEBO, BAxUS) and SAASBO stagnate early due to their structural assumptions about chemical space. On the 80D ConStellaration benchmark, RAMBO leads by ~51.55% in Q_i; ALEBO and SAASBO fail because stellarator physics violates their low-rank and axis-aligned sparsity assumptions, while TuRBO plateaus when its trust region straddles a plasma stability boundary. SMAC (random forest) and COMBO (graph kernel) are outperformed on all tasks where they are evaluated; COMBO is excluded from the two largest benchmarks due to intractable computational cost. The adaptive α-scheduling variant (DPMMSched) consistently outperforms fixed-α configurations, confirming the benefit of the coarse-to-fine regime discovery schedule.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMBO extends the Infinite Mixture of GP Experts framework (Rasmussen & Ghahramani, 2001) by replacing the learned input-dependent gating network with a Dirichlet Process prior in the generative model, deferring spatial relevance to a parameter-free predictive weighting step (σ⁻¹ modulation). It also builds on Neal's (2000) Gibbs sampling for DPMMs but derives a collapsed variant that analytically marginalizes latent GP functions, improving mixing for the sequential low-data BO setting. The paper positions itself against non-stationary kernels (which interpolate smoothly between regimes), trust-region methods (TuRBO, SCBO), and embedding-based approaches (ALEBO, SAASBO, BAxUS), arguing that discrete regime structure with abrupt transitions is fundamentally different from gradual parameter drift. The closest prior work, Li & Ma (2023), applies DPMM-GP to supervised functional regression; RAMBO adapts the framework specifically for sequential black-box optimization with regime-aware acquisition functions and adaptive complexity scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: scientific design landscapes (molecular conformers, drug scaffolds, plasma topologies) exhibit discrete regime structure that stationary GPs and smooth non-stationary kernels cannot represent, and the 39–52% improvements on real-world benchmarks demonstrate the cost of this mismatch. The parameter-free spatial modulation (Proposition 3.4) is an elegant design choice that avoids the overfitting risk of learned gating networks, and the adaptive α-scheduling provides a principled mechanism for controlling model complexity during sequential optimization. Open questions include the computational cost of MCMC inference (500 burn-in + 5 posterior samples per BO iteration) limiting scalability to very high dimensions, the lack of regret bounds for the mixture EI acquisition, and whether the spatial modulation approximation (exact only in the well-separated regime limit) degrades when regimes overlap significantly.
