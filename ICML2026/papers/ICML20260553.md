## Problem

In RGB-D salient object detection, symmetric fusion operators (e.g., bidirectional cross-attention, Cross-Diffusion Attention) implicitly assume comparable signal-to-noise ratios across modalities. Under modality imbalance—where an auxiliary stream is substantially noisier than a designated primary stream—such symmetry permits a backflow channel through which auxiliary noise enters the primary representation and accumulates across iterative refinement stages, degrading dense predictions. No existing fusion mechanism explicitly encodes role-asymmetric influence or provides a quantified safety bound on auxiliary-to-primary perturbation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images and depth maps at 384×384 resolution, encoded by a CPNet dual-stream Transformer backbone into token maps of shape n×d (n=HW spatial tokens, d feature dimension). HAF blocks are inserted at multiple hierarchical stages, where only the auxiliary (RGB) tokens are iteratively refined over T=4 unrolled steps while the primary (depth) tokens serve as a fixed guidance field. The output is a pixel-wise saliency map; the asymmetric residual weights α_aux and α_main control how much refined auxiliary information is injected back into each stream, with α_main=0 enforcing strict one-way independence of the primary output from auxiliary input.

## Method

HAF formulates fusion as a directed Hamiltonian-inspired refinement: auxiliary tokens (q, p) evolve under a force field computed via FFT-based Spectral Global Correlation (SGC) with a shared learnable frequency response G that reweights reliable frequency bands. The force combines primary-guided attraction (SGC between auxiliary queries and depth keys) and auxiliary self-repulsion (SGC between auxiliary queries and RGB keys), weighted by a repulsion coefficient γ. A leaky gated momentum update—p^t = (1−g^t)⊙p^{t−1} + g^t⊙f^t with a learned sigmoid gate g^t conditioned on [q;p]—damps stale momentum across the T=4 unroll steps, preventing accumulation of outdated updates. After refinement, the final auxiliary state q^T is injected back into both streams via asymmetric residual connections with learnable scalars, and the paper derives a finite-horizon sensitivity bound (Theorem 3.2) and a block-level primary perturbation bound (Corollary 3.6) that quantify how auxiliary corruption propagates through the rollout, with exact primary independence guaranteed when α_main=0.

## Results

On six RGB-D SOD benchmarks, HAF achieves the best or near-best results across all four standard metrics (S_m, max F_β, max E_ξ, MAE). Headline numbers include S_m = 0.953 / F_β = 0.963 / E_ξ = 0.979 / MAE = 0.017 on DUT-RGBD, and S_m = 0.940 / F_β = 0.938 / E_ξ = 0.974 / MAE = 0.016 on NLPR. HAF improves over the CPNet backbone by 0.3–0.7 points in F_β and 0.4–0.6 points in E_ξ across datasets, while adding only 0.8 G FLOPs (130.1 G vs. 129.3 G). Under controlled auxiliary corruption, HAF attains the lowest median Degradation AUC with the tightest interquartile range among all tested fusion operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest full-pipeline baselines, HAF consistently outperforms CPNet (the backbone) by 0.3–0.7 F_β points and 0.4–0.6 E_ξ points across all six datasets (e.g., DUT-RGBD: F_β 0.963 vs. 0.960; NLPR: E_ξ 0.974 vs. 0.969). HAF also surpasses CATNet on F_β and E_ξ for every dataset (e.g., DUT-RGBD F_β 0.963 vs. 0.951; STERE E_ξ 0.962 vs. 0.935), while tying CATNet on DUT-RGBD S_m (0.953). Compared to MutualFormer (CDA), HAF leads by 1.7–3.1 F_β points on LFSD and 0.7–1.1 points on other datasets, and reduces MAE by 0.001–0.011. In the controlled module-swapping protocol (Table 2), HAF beats all five alternative fusion plugins (LMF-Net, EM-Trans, MA, CA, SHIP) on every dataset and metric, with the largest margins over MA (e.g., DUT-RGBD F_β 0.963 vs. 0.953; SIP E_ξ 0.951 vs. 0.940). On the RGB-T transfer evaluation (Table 10), HAF plugged into ContriNet improves F_β by 0.7 points on VT821 (0.843 vs. 0.836) and 0.2 points on VT1000 (0.905 vs. 903), confirming the directed-refinement principle generalizes beyond RGB-D.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAF builds on GFNet's Fourier-domain global mixing paradigm (Rao et al., 2021) to instantiate its spectral global correlation (SGC) operator, and is plugged into the CPNet (Hu et al., 2024) progressive-decoding backbone. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant symmetric fusion paradigm—exemplified by Cross-Diffusion Attention in MutualFormer (Wang et al., 2024) and standard cross-attention—by formulating fusion as directed, role-asymmetric refinement where only the auxiliary state evolves under a primary-guided force field. The Hamiltonian framing borrows design motivation from Hamiltonian Neural Networks (Greydanus et al., 2019) and symplectic RNNs (Chen et al., 2020), but explicitly disclaims exact energy conservation or symplecticity, using instead a leaky gated momentum update as a practical multi-step regulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies modality imbalance under iterative refinement as a distinct failure mode—auxiliary noise backflow accumulating across refinement steps—and provides a clean structural remedy (one-way safety via α_main=0) backed by a finite-horizon sensitivity bound. The ~200× FLOPs gap between HAF (130G) and CPNet+CDA (26,099G) makes the symmetric cross-diffusion approach practically infeasible at multi-scale high-resolution features, a finding with direct practical implications for fusion module selection. However, the evaluation is confined to RGB-D SOD with a fixed depth-primary/RGB-auxiliary role assignment; the RGB-T transfer is preliminary, and the method assumes a task-relevant reliability asymmetry that may be weak or ambiguous in other settings. Open questions include stack-level sensitivity guarantees across multiple HAF blocks, whether the a<1 contraction condition can be enforced architecturally, and broader multimodal transfer beyond the two bimodal settings tested.
