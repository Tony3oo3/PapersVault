## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods for diffusion models apply uniform or timestep-discrete updates across all denoising steps, ignoring the intrinsic coarse-to-fine frequency-energy progression that governs how diffusion models reconstruct images. This mismatch between the adaptation strategy and the model's spectral dynamics leads to suboptimal specialization, unstable optimization, and limited generalization across backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265539/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen pretrained latent diffusion backbone (SD 1.5/2.0/3.0, SDXL, or FLUX.1) as input, and produces a 512×512 RGB image as output. Unusually, routing decisions are made at every denoising step based on the live frequency-energy state of the current latent (computed via DoG band-pass filters), rather than on the timestep index, making the adaptation content-dependent and instance-aware during both training and inference.

## Method

FeRA introduces three synergistic components: (1) a Frequency-Energy Indicator (FEI) that decomposes the latent into n frequency bands via Difference-of-Gaussians filters and normalizes per-band energies into a probability simplex vector; (2) a Soft Frequency Router (a two-layer MLP) that maps the FEI to continuous routing weights over M LoRA experts, replacing discrete timestep gating with a closed-loop, content-dependent policy; and (3) a Frequency-Energy Consistency Loss (FECL) that penalizes mismatch between the adapter correction's and the residual error's bandwise energy distributions, concentrating updates where residual signal is present. The theoretical analysis proves that FeRA's routing strictly generalizes timestep-based routing and achieves Signal-Gradient Orthogonality, suppressing expert updates in noise-dominated bands and enabling dynamic curriculum learning unavailable to open-loop routers.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 2.0 with a 20M trainable parameter budget, FeRA achieves the lowest FID across 4 of 5 style datasets (e.g., 154.19 on Cyberpunk vs. 158.48 for SaRA, 145.64 on Expedition vs. 149.13 for SaRA) while also attaining the highest CLIP and Style scores on most datasets. The advantage is consistent across backbones (SD 2.0, SD 3.0, FLUX.1) and parameter budgets (5M–50M), with FeRA reaching FID as low as 126.18 (Expedition, FLUX.1, 5M) and 117.83 (Expedition, FLUX.1, 20M). On DreamBooth image customization (SD 2.0), FeRA achieves the best CLIP-I on all five subjects (0.900–0.925) and the best CLIP-T on four of five, outperforming the strongest PEFT baseline by 0.005–0.035 in CLIP-I. Inference overhead is minimal at only 4–8% over standard LoRA across all tested backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeRA consistently outperforms all four PEFT baselines (LoRA, DoRA, AdaLoRA, SaRA) on FID across the majority of backbone–budget–dataset combinations in the style adaptation task. Against the strongest baseline SaRA, FeRA reduces FID by 4–13 points (e.g., 154.19 vs. 158.48 on Cyberpunk/SD 2.0/20M; 128.43 vs. 131.42 on Cyberpunk/FLUX.1/20M) while also achieving higher CLIP and Style scores. LoRA and DoRA are the weakest baselines, with FeRA typically improving FID by 10–15 points over them. On DreamBooth image customization, FeRA achieves the best CLIP-I on all five subjects and best CLIP-T on four of five, whereas SaRA—while posting the highest CLIP-T on Dog (25.97 vs. 25.95)—suffers a severe CLIP-I drop (0.790 vs. 0.900), indicating poor identity preservation. The full-parameter fine-tuning baseline is not uniformly dominant; FeRA matches or exceeds it on several FID values while using 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters (e.g., 20M vs. 866M on SD 2.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

FeRA builds directly on LoRA as its parameter-efficient adaptation primitive, wrapping multiple LoRA experts in a Mixture-of-Experts-style routing architecture. It departs from prior MoE-for-diffusion work (e.g., Switch Diffusion Transformer, Denoising Task Routing) that uses discrete timestep-based gating, replacing it with a continuous, content-dependent routing signal derived from the latent's frequency-energy distribution via Difference-of-Gaussians band decomposition. The frequency-energy consistency loss (FECL) is a novel regularization that has no direct analogue in prior PEFT literature, drawing instead on Parseval's theorem to align adapter corrections with residual energy in the spectral domain. The physical motivation traces to the well-known coarse-to-fine denoising progression and the 1/f power-law spectrum of natural images, but the specific use of per-instance spectral state as a closed-loop routing input is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FeRA offers a physically grounded, lightweight (4–8% inference overhead) drop-in replacement for timestep-based expert routing that is content-aware and generalizes across backbones (SD 1.5/2.0/3.0, SDXL, FLUX.1) and parameter budgets. The Signal-Gradient Orthogonality theorem provides a clean theoretical justification for why frequency-conditioned routing suppresses noise-fitting, which could inform future MoE designs beyond diffusion. Open questions include whether the fixed 3-band DoG decomposition is optimal for all domains (the paper addresses non-natural images only qualitatively in Appendix C), whether the approach extends to video or 3D diffusion where frequency structure differs, and whether the FECL regularization interacts favorably with other training objectives at scale.
