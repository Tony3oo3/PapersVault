## Problem

Existing adaptive batch size strategies based on gradient noise scale (GNS) assume the Euclidean geometry of SGD, creating a fundamental mismatch with popular non-Euclidean optimizers such as signSGD/Signum (ℓ∞) and specSGD/Muon (S∞). This misalignment means practitioners lack a principled, computable metric to determine when increasing batch size will compromise sample efficiency for these optimizers, forcing reliance on brittle heuristic schedules.

## Input / Output

The method consumes local mini-batch gradients computed on each of R data-parallel ranks during the standard backward pass, along with the global (AllReduced) gradient as the signal estimate. It produces a monotonically non-decreasing batch size B_k (updated every F steps after a warmup period I) and a learning rate scaling factor ω_k = ω_{k-1}·√(B_{k+1}/B_k). For the S₁ GNS (specSGD/Muon), each rank additionally computes a local Gram matrix G_k^j(G_k^j)ᵀ ∈ ℝ^{m×m} per 2D parameter tensor, which requires an AllGather to reconstruct the full row-wise covariance before evaluating the nuclear norm.

## Method

The paper derives GNS metrics in the dual norm of each optimizer's geometry: the Manhattan (ℓ₁) GNS ‖σ_k‖₁²/‖∇L(x_k)‖₁² for signSGD and the nuclear (S₁) GNS ‖C_row,k^{1/2}‖_{S₁}²/‖∇L(X_k)‖_{S₁}² for specSGD. The key theoretical insight is that the stochastic steepest descent direction is biased under non-Euclidean norms, and this bias is bounded by the expected dual norm of the gradient estimation error, which scales as 1/√B_k. For practical estimation, the method treats local mini-batch gradients across R data-parallel ranks as independent samples, applying a Bessel correction (R/(R−1)) and a single-sample rescaling (B_k/R) to obtain unbiased variance estimates that are appended to the existing AllReduce/ReduceScatter communication. The adaptive strategy (Algorithm 1) applies exponential moving averages to the noise and signal components, enforces monotonically increasing batch sizes after warmup, and scales the learning rate proportionally to √B_k to exploit the reduced gradient variance.

## Results

On the 160M Llama 3 model (C4, 3.2B tokens), the non-Euclidean GNS adaptive strategy achieves up to 66.77% step reduction for Muon and 66.61% for Signum while matching or beating the constant-batch validation loss. For signSGD and specSGD, the adaptive approach achieves strictly lower validation loss (3.8396 vs. 3.9357 and 3.7285 vs. 3.9404) than the B=256 constant baseline. On the 1B Llama 3 model, signSGD achieves a 31.84% step reduction with a validation loss of 2.9946 (vs. 3.1417 for B=256). On SimpleViT (ImageWoof), Muon with adaptive batching reaches 0.6433 validation loss with 57.65% fewer steps than the B=128 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the non-Euclidean GNS adaptive strategy against three baselines: constant small batch (B=64), constant large batch (B=256), and a Euclidean (ℓ₂) GNS adaptive strategy. The non-Euclidean GNS consistently outperforms the Euclidean GNS across all five optimizers on the 160M Llama 3 model, with the largest gap for signSGD (3.8396 vs. 3.8906 validation loss). For Signum and Muon, the adaptive strategy matches the B=64 baseline's validation loss (3.3707 vs. 3.3737 and 3.3061 vs. 3.3041) while reducing steps by ~66%, a dramatic efficiency gain. For signSGD and specSGD, the adaptive approach actually surpasses both constant baselines in validation loss, suggesting the geometry-aware noise signal enables more aggressive batch growth. On the 1B model, AdamW is the one exception where the adaptive strategy fails to reach the B=256 baseline loss (2.8023 vs. 2.7701), while signSGD and Signum still achieve 31.84% and 12.11% step reductions respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient noise scale (GNS) and critical batch size (CBS) framework of McCandlish et al. (2018) from its original Euclidean (ℓ2) setting to non-Euclidean geometries, identifying a fundamental mismatch when Euclidean GNS is applied to sign-based (ℓ∞/ℓ1) and spectral (S∞/S1) optimizers. The derivation of geometry-matched GNS metrics (Manhattan ℓ1 for signSGD, nuclear S1 for specSGD) builds directly on the ℓ1 error bound from Bernstein et al. (2018) and the spectral smoothness analysis from Carlson et al. (2015b), while the distributed variance estimation procedure extends the Euclidean estimators of Lau et al. (2024a;b) to these non-Euclidean norms. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the dual norm of the optimizer's geometry is the natural metric for quantifying gradient noise, a relationship obscured in the Euclidean case where the norm is self-dual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners using sign-based (Signum) or spectral (Muon) optimizers at scale, where the paper demonstrates up to 66% step reduction while matching validation loss on Llama 3 models. However, the S1 GNS estimator incurs substantial computational overhead (~40% at 1B parameter scale), limiting immediate adoption for spectral optimizers, and the adaptive strategy fails to match baselines for AdamW on the 1B model. Open questions include extending the framework to preconditioned and stateful optimizers (handling EMA of gradients/second moments), unifying noise scales when different modules use different optimizers, and incorporating general Hessian structure. The geometric insight that the dual norm is the intrinsic noise metric also positions GNS as a broader diagnostic tool for training stability and distribution shift detection beyond batch size selection.
