## Problem

Dynamic Facial Expression Recognition (DFER) systems deployed in the wild must handle continuously changing scenarios (lighting, background, viewpoint) that distort expression trajectories over time. Existing approaches address this through passive feature alignment or domain-incremental learning that preserves learned representations, but neither explicitly models how scenarios evolve as a continuous stochastic process, limiting long-term stability and accurate capture of expression dynamics under gradual scenario drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264733/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips of facial expressions (7 basic emotions on FERV39k/DFEW, 11 on MAFW) encoded by a ViT-B/16 backbone, along with per-frame optical flow computed by a frozen RAFT network to provide physical motion constraints. The output is a single expression class label per video clip. During incremental training, only the current scenario's data is accessible (no-replay), and the model is evaluated on all previously seen scenarios after each learning stage.

## Method

EmWorld formulates scenario-incremental DFER as progressive Bayesian inference over latent world states at two temporal scales. The Slow-Timescale (STS) component models cross-scenario evolution of class-conditional latent state centers as a Gaussian random walk, with Kalman-style updates that fuse the historical prior with current batch-level observations to adapt prototypes without storing past data. The Fast-Timescale (FTS) component models frame-level latent transitions via a first-order state equation whose velocity is predicted from optical flow through a learnable mapping, grounding latent dynamics in actual facial muscle motion. Training minimizes a joint objective of cross-entropy classification, a scenario-consistency loss (derived by marginalizing the latent center under the Gaussian prior), and an optical-flow constraint loss, all applied on top of a ViT-B/16 backbone with incremental weight fusion after each scenario.

## Results

On the FERV39k benchmark (four subsets: AI9k, DL11k, SIA10k, WIS9k), EmWorld achieves average accuracies of 40.56%, 38.03%, 42.63%, and 40.28% (Avg) and 39.41%, 34.45%, 40.15%, and 38.16% (Last), outperforming the strongest baseline DUCT by 3.60%, 3.09%, 3.22%, and 3.95% on Avg respectively. On DFEW and MAFW, EmWorld reaches 53.69% Avg / 54.16% Last and 42.35% Avg / 41.34% Last, surpassing the next-best method (DCE) by 6.08% and 3.12% on Avg. Overall, EmWorld achieves an average gain of 3.84% across all six datasets, with the Kalman update adding only ~1.65 s per scene and fixed storage of 10,752 elements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmWorld outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest DICL baseline DUCT (Zhou et al., 2025b), EmWorld gains 3.60–3.95% on Avg across the four FERV39k subsets (e.g., 40.56 vs 36.96 on AI9k) and 6.08% on DFEW Avg (53.69 vs 43.65). The DFER-only baselines (MDFER, SVFAP, PTH-Net) are substantially outperformed, with PTH-Net being the closest at 35.44% Avg on SIA10k versus EmWorld's 42.63%. Among the remaining DICL methods, DCE (Li et al., 2025a) is the second-strongest on DFEW (47.61% Avg) but trails EmWorld by 6.08%; CPrompt (Gao et al., 2024b) and SimpleCIL (Zhou et al., 2025a) achieve only ~20% Avg on FERV39k subsets, while infLoRA (Liang & Li, 2024) and EASE (Zhou et al., 2024) suffer severe forgetting, dropping below 15% Avg on most subsets. EmWorld also achieves the best Forgetting Measure (3.16 vs DUCT's 3.60) and Backward Transfer (−3.854 vs −4.220) on AI9k, confirming superior knowledge retention alongside higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmWorld carves new ground by reframing scenario-incremental DFER as a progressive Bayesian inference problem over latent world states, departing from both cross-domain DFER methods (which perform passive feature alignment) and standard domain-incremental learning (which relies on weight regularization, distillation, or architectural expansion to prevent forgetting). The slow-timescale component repurposes the Kalman filter / stochastic random walk from state-space modeling to track class-prototype drift across scenarios, while the fast-timescale component borrows the world-model idea of grounding latent transitions in physical observations (here, RAFT optical flow) to constrain frame-level dynamics. The strongest baseline, DUCT (Zhou et al., 2025b), shares the replay-free domain-incremental setting but lacks any explicit modeling of scenario evolution, making EmWorld's dual-timescale decomposition a conceptual rather than incremental advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important deployment gap: DFER systems in the wild face continuous, gradual scenario shifts (lighting, viewpoint, background) that neither static domain adaptation nor standard continual learning handles well. The Kalman-style prototype update is computationally trivial (O(C·D) storage, ~1.65 s overhead per scene), making the approach attractive for real-time, privacy-constrained settings where replay is infeasible. However, the Gaussian random-walk prior introduces a documented adaptation lag under abrupt scenario transitions, and the remaining gap to the joint-training upper bound (e.g., 38.29 vs 43.82 on Crisis) suggests the framework still underfits in highly non-stationary regimes. Open questions include whether stochastic jump processes or change-point detection can close this gap without sacrificing long-term stability.
