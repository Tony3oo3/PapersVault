## Problem

Learned surrogates for PDE simulation have largely focused on fluid dynamics and structured geometries, while robust surrogates for deformable solids—characterized by 3D unstructured meshes, strong nonlinearities (plasticity, contact, large deformations), and transient behavior—remain underexplored. Among the few multigrid GNN approaches that do target solid mechanics, the coarsening step relies on geometric heuristics (e.g., farthest-point sampling) or purely learned attention scores, which do not explicitly account for where the physics is most demanding, leading to degraded long-horizon rollout stability.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model receives the current nodal state (displacement or velocity, 3D vectors) on an unstructured 3D mesh graph whose nodes are mesh vertices and edges encode mesh connectivity; for contact datasets, additional 'world edges' link nearby nodes to a fixed virtual node representing external rigid tools. The model outputs an incremental update Δu that is added to the current state to produce the next-step nodal state, following a residual time-integration formulation. All datasets are fully 3D with mesh sizes ranging from ~500 to ~9000 nodes.

## Method

The architecture follows an encoder–processor–decoder design (pointwise MLPs for encoding/decoding, GraphNet message-passing blocks for processing) with an explicit multigrid hierarchy: fine-scale message passing, downsampling to a coarse graph, coarse processing, and KNN-based upsampling back to fine resolution. The key novelty is the physics-informed coarsening operator: latent node features are decoded back to physical space, a nodal mechanical residual is computed (divergence of Cauchy stress, inertial acceleration for transient regimes, and body-force terms, all via fixed mesh-based discrete operators), and the L2 norm of this residual serves as a node importance score. Coarse nodes are then selected by TopK ranking or probabilistic sampling from the score distribution, optionally followed by remeshing (rebuilding coarse connectivity as a KNN graph in Euclidean space) to improve long-range propagation. This biases coarse-level capacity toward dynamically critical regions—stress concentrations, contact interfaces, large-deformation zones—rather than relying on geometric coverage alone, and the same decoder is reused for both state prediction and residual scoring to maintain physical consistency.

## Results

On DeformingPlate, the proposed physics-informed multigrid GNN achieves a rollout RMSE of 6.50×10⁻³ and a 1-step RMSE of 0.095×10⁻³, roughly halving the rollout error of the next-best baseline (UNISOMA, 11.46×10⁻³) and MeshGraphNets (12.75×10⁻³) while using a comparable parameter count (2.9M). On the generalization datasets, the method improves rollout RMSE on BeamSimple (1.44×10⁻¹ vs. 1.72×10⁻¹ for MeshGraphNets) and achieves the best 1-step accuracy on SpindleUpsetting (11.24×10⁻³ vs. 11.92×10⁻³ for MeshGraphNets), though FPS-based sampling remains competitive on SpindleUpsetting rollout (2.60×10⁻¹ vs. 2.96×10⁻¹).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on DeformingPlate in both rollout and 1-step RMSE. The largest gains are over Transformer-based solvers: Transolver++ (29.80×10⁻³ rollout) and TransformerGNN (24.97×10⁻³) are 4.6× and 3.8× worse in rollout error, respectively, and also have substantially higher 1-step errors (1.00 and 1.20 vs. 0.095×10⁻³). Among GNN-based multigrid baselines, the method nearly halves the rollout error of UNISOMA (6.50 vs. 11.46×10⁻³) and MeshGraphNets (6.50 vs. 12.75×10⁻³), and improves over HCMT (12.97×10⁻³) and Multi-Scale GNN (15.70×10⁻³) by similar margins. BSMS (16.60×10⁻³) is the weakest GNN baseline, with the proposed method achieving a 2.6× rollout improvement. On the generalization datasets, the method consistently beats MeshGraphNets on BeamSimple (1.44 vs. 1.72×10⁻¹ rollout) and SpindleUpsetting 1-step accuracy (11.24 vs. 11.92×10⁻³), but FPS-based sampling slightly edges out the proposed method on SpindleUpsetting rollout (2.60 vs. 2.96×10⁻¹), suggesting that in highly plastic, contact-dominated regimes the geometric coverage of FPS can be competitive with residual-based selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MeshGraphNets encoder-processor-decoder framework (Pfaff et al., 2021) with a multigrid hierarchy, building on prior multigrid GNN simulators such as BSMS (Cao et al., 2022), Multi-Scale GNN (Lino et al., 2021), and attention-based multigrid GNNs (Garnier et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing geometric heuristics (FPS) or learned attention scores for coarse-node selection with a physics-informed residual score derived from the governing solid mechanics balance laws, drawing an analogy to adaptive FEM refinement strategies. The paper also introduces two new solid mechanics benchmarks (BeamSimple, SpindleUpsetting) that go beyond the quasi-static DeformingPlate dataset used in most prior learned-simulation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a real gap: most learned PDE surrogates target fluids or structured grids, while practical solid mechanics involves unstructured 3D meshes with strong nonlinearities (plasticity, contact, large deformation) where long-horizon stability is critical. The physics-informed coarsening principle is broadly applicable to any multigrid surrogate where a physically meaningful residual can be computed, and the new datasets provide much-needed evaluation beyond canonical benchmarks. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that residual computation requires derivative reconstruction and access to physically meaningful fields, partially reintroducing classical solver machinery; whether this scales to highly complex materials or severely distorted meshes remains open.
