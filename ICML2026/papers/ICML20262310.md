## Problem

Visual tokens dominate the input sequence in LVLMs, making pixel grounding inference computationally expensive. Existing token pruning methods either rely on text-agnostic visual importance (failing to preserve referent-specific tokens) or select tokens by high visual-text similarity (which, as this paper reveals, actually removes the most relevant tokens). No prior work has systematically addressed token pruning for pixel grounding, where token importance is highly contingent on the input referring expression.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (or video) at standard resolution (e.g., 336×336 for LLaVA-1.5 producing 576 visual tokens) paired with a natural-language referring expression, and outputs a binary pixel-level segmentation mask. Pruning is applied before the LLM decoder (before-LLM pruning), retaining only a subset of visual tokens (e.g., 192, 128, or 64 out of 576 for images; 196 or 81 for videos) that pass through the projection layer into the LLM, which then generates a <SEG> grounding embedding consumed by a SAM-style pixel decoder. This before-LLM placement makes the method compatible with FlashAttention, unlike in-LLM pruning approaches.

## Method

LiteLVLM is a training-free, text-guided token pruning strategy built on two key CLIP analyses: (1) a visual-text similarity reversal, where visual tokens within referent regions exhibit unexpectedly low cosine similarity to the [EOS] text token due to CLIP's contrastive pretraining aligning only global embeddings, and (2) a text attention sink, where [EOS] attends predominantly to [SOS] (0.68) rather than to semantically meaningful referring tokens (0.14). The method first selects similarity-aware tokens by retaining visual tokens with the lowest dot-product similarity to the [EOS] embedding (reversing the standard ranking), which spatially align with the referent. It then recovers context-aware tokens by scoring each remaining token's contribution to the [CLS] token via attention-weighted Value vector L2-norm, preserving background context for foreground-background separation. An adaptive selection strategy dynamically allocates the token budget between the two types based on the input text, and the entire pipeline is plug-and-play, requiring no fine-tuning.

## Results

On referring expression segmentation (RefCOCO/+/g), LiteLVLM achieves 68.1% average cIoU with 192 tokens (↓66.7%), retaining 90.3% of the full 576-token GLaMM performance (75.5%). At the aggressive 64-token budget (↓88.9%), LiteLVLM still preserves 59.8% cIoU (79.2% retention), outperforming the next-best method (VisionZip, 52.2%) by over 7 points. On referring video object segmentation (Ref-DAVIS-17), LiteLVLM retains 99.5% of VideoGLaMM's J&F score (69.2 vs. 69.5) with only 196 tokens, and 95.1% with just 81 tokens. Efficiency gains include a 22% inference speedup and 2.3× memory reduction at the 192-token setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiteLVLM consistently outperforms all seven baselines on the primary pixel grounding task. Against the closest competitor VisPruner (ICCV 2025), LiteLVLM leads by 2.1 points in average cIoU at 192 tokens (68.1 vs. 66.0) and by 8.3 points at 64 tokens (59.8 vs. 51.5). VisionZip (CVPR 2025) and LLaVA-PruMerge (ICCV 2025) trail by 2.6 and 5.0 points respectively at 192 tokens, with gaps widening to 7.6 and 7.8 points at 64 tokens. FastV (ECCV 2024) lags further behind (62.6 at 192 tokens), while TRIM (COLING 2025) suffers the most severe degradation (47.3 at 192 tokens, only 62.6% retention) because it selects high-similarity tokens that are actually irrelevant to the referent. On image understanding benchmarks (Table 8), LiteLVLM matches or slightly trails VisPruner (60.69 vs. 61.32 at 128 tokens) but substantially outperforms ToMe (50.94) and SparseVLM (58.04), confirming that the text-guided low-similarity selection is particularly critical for grounding rather than general VQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's contrastive pretraining and the GLaMM pixel grounding framework, but departs sharply from the existing token pruning literature. Prior methods (LLaVA-PruMerge, VisionZip, VisPruner) select tokens by global visual importance or high visual-text similarity, while TRIM explicitly uses high CLIP similarity as a text-guided signal. LiteLVLM inverts this logic: it retains tokens with the *lowest* similarity to the [EOS] token, motivated by the discovery that referent-region tokens are actually the ones weakly aligned with the global text representation due to CLIP's text attention sink (where [EOS] over-attends to [SOS]). This makes it the first token pruning method designed specifically for pixel grounding rather than general image understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that token pruning strategies validated on image understanding tasks (VQA, captioning) can actively harm pixel grounding, and that the correct signal is the inverse of what prior text-guided methods use. The training-free, plug-and-play nature (90% performance at 66.7% pruning, 22% speedup, 2.3× memory reduction) makes it immediately deployable on existing LVLMs without retraining. Open questions include whether the similarity reversal persists in next-generation encoders that eliminate [CLS]/[EOS] tokens entirely, whether the adaptive budget strategy generalizes to multi-object or open-vocabulary grounding, and whether fine-tuning the pruning policy (shown to add 3–8% in ablations) could close the remaining gap to full-token inference at even lower budgets.
