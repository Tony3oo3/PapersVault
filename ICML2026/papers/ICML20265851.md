## Problem

Existing evaluators for subject-driven image customization (both embedding-based and MLLM-based) assess textual and visual condition alignment in isolation, contradicting the simultaneous multi-condition alignment objective. This leads to false positives when generated images resemble the reference but ignore text-specified edits, and false negatives when correct edits reduce raw visual similarity, resulting in poor correlation with human judgments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a reference image, a text prompt specifying desired edits or scene context, and a generated image, all fed jointly into GPT-4o as the judge VLM. It produces a single interpretable omni-condition alignment score in [0,1] computed as a weighted average, alongside per-attribute AEU-level binary or continuous component scores that expose which specific conditions were satisfied or violated.

## Method

UFO introduces an Atomized Chain-of-Evaluation paradigm with four sequential stages. First, a VLM jointly reasons over the reference image and text to decompose omni-condition alignment into a chain of fine-grained Atomic Evaluation Units (AEUs) at two hierarchical levels (local concrete components and global abstract attributes). Second, each AEU is assigned a modality-relevance class (image-only, text-only, or text-and-image) so that alignment is checked against the correct governing condition. Third, each AEU is scored either via a VQA query (Yes/No mapped to 1/0) or a dedicated function call such as ArcFace for high-precision facial identity verification (score = 1 − normalized feature distance). Finally, VLM-assigned adaptive importance weights (integers 1–5) reflect the relative contribution of each AEU to subject preservation, and the holistic score is the weighted average of all AEU scores, making the metric both fine-grained and interpretable.

## Results

UFO achieves a Spearman rank correlation of 0.6889 with human judgments on UFO-Bench, outperforming the strongest baseline DreamBench++ (0.5977) by 15.25% relative improvement. The advantage is most pronounced in Non-Editing (0.8034 vs. 0.7262) and Multi-Dim-Editing (0.7241 vs. 0.5744) scenarios, while traditional embedding metrics (CLIP-I, DINO, CLIP-T) show weak or negative correlations (−0.20 to 0.39). On UFO-Bench model evaluation, Doubao scores highest (0.7439 total), followed by Qwen-Image (0.7252) and Nano-Banana (0.6816).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFO outperforms all four baseline families across every editing paradigm. Against the strongest MLLM-based baseline DreamBench++ (0.5977 total), UFO gains +0.0912 in overall correlation, with the largest margin in Multi-Dim-Editing (+0.1497) where simultaneous global and local edits stress the evaluation. VIE-Score (SC: 0.5752, O: 0.5663) trails UFO by roughly 0.11–0.12 in total correlation and is particularly weak on Global Editing (0.45–0.46 vs. UFO's 0.63). Traditional embedding metrics fare far worse: CLIP-I (0.3910) and DINO (0.3084) capture only coarse visual similarity, while CLIP-T (−0.1996) is negatively correlated with human preference, confirming that text-image alignment alone is unreliable for subject-driven customization. UFO's dedicated ArcFace function call for identity verification and its atomic decomposition strategy are the key differentiators that enable it to detect fine-grained failures (e.g., missed hair-color edits) that holistic MLLM scoring overlooks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in subject-driven image generation evaluation, where embedding-based metrics (CLIP, DINO) and MLLM-based metrics (VIEScore, DreamBench++) assess textual and visual alignment in isolation. Rather than building on a single prior method, UFO repurposes VQA-style queries and specialized function calls (e.g., ArcFace) within a novel Atomized Chain-of-Evaluation pipeline that decomposes alignment into modality-classified atomic units. On the benchmark side, UFO-Bench extends the lineage of DreamEditBench, DreamBench++, and DSH-Bench by explicitly introducing conflicting multi-modal condition pairs and hierarchical editing difficulty, addressing what the authors identify as a structural gap in existing testbeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode—false positives when text edits are ignored and false negatives when valid edits reduce visual similarity—making it directly relevant to anyone benchmarking subject-driven customization models. UFO-Bench, with its 86 reference images, 7 subject categories, and 81.97% conflicting condition pairs, provides a reusable testbed that the community can adopt. However, the evaluation pipeline's dependence on GPT-4o as the judge MLLM raises reproducibility and cost concerns, and the paper explicitly scopes itself to single-image, single-turn subject-driven generation, leaving open whether the AEU decomposition generalizes to multi-image or multi-turn settings. The 15.25% average correlation improvement, while meaningful, is measured on a 100-case human evaluation subset, limiting statistical confidence.
