## Problem

Existing Causal Representation Learning (CRL) methods recover latent factors but ignore direct causal relations among observed variables, while Causal Discovery (CD) methods cannot identify latent variables or their interrelations. Climate systems exhibit both hidden drivers (e.g., pressure, precipitation) and observable-to-observable causal couplings (e.g., heat transfer between neighboring regions), yet no prior framework jointly recovers the latent dynamic process and the causal graph over observations from noisy, non-invertible time-series data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of observed climate variables (e.g., gridded temperature fields from CESM2, Weather, ERSST) and uses three consecutive observations {x_{t-1}, x_t, x_{t+1}} as contextual information for latent identification. It simultaneously outputs: (i) latent variables z_t with component-wise physical interpretability, (ii) a contemporaneous causal DAG over observed variables whose entries are time-varying (modulated by z_t), (iii) latent causal graphs capturing both instantaneous and time-lagged dependencies, and (iv) multi-horizon forecasts (96/192/336 steps) from the learned generative model.

## Method

CaDRe is a state-space VAE where z-encoder and s-encoder (MLPs) extract latent variables and nonstationary noise, a decoder reconstructs observations, and normalizing-flow priors model temporal transitions of z_t and conditional generation of s_t. The key theoretical insight is a functional equivalence between the nonlinear SEM and nonlinear ICA (Theorem 3.5), which allows the causal graph over observations to be computed in closed form from the ICA mixing Jacobian as J_g = I − D_m · J_m^{-1}, bypassing iterative CD. Nonparametric identifiability of the latent space is established via a linear-operator eigendecomposition argument (extending Hu & Schennach, 2008) that lifts the result from distribution-level to value-level recovery under a differentiability assumption. Structural constraints are enforced through L1 sparsity penalties on Jacobian supports and a continuous DAG constraint (DAG-GNN style) on both the observed causal graph and the instantaneous latent DAG, with the overall loss combining the ELBO with these penalties.

## Results

On the CESM2 climate dataset, CaDRe achieves the best causal discovery performance with WSHD=0.012 and WTPR=0.532, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (LPCMCI: WSHD=0.019, WTPR=0.274; TCDF: WTPR=0.327) while running at 1.095 ms latency versus >2000 ms for constraint-based methods. On synthetic CRL benchmarks, CaDRe attains MCC=0.9811 and R²=0.9626 in the independent setting, surpassing the strongest baseline TDRL (MCC=0.9106, R²=0.8649). For real-world forecasting, CaDRe delivers competitive MSE/MAE across CESM2, Weather, and ERSST at all horizons (96/192/336), e.g., MSE=0.157 on Weather-96 and MSE=0.145 on ERSST-96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On causal discovery (Table 4), CaDRe dominates all 8 baselines on both WSHD and WTPR: it reduces WSHD by 37% over LPCMCI (0.012 vs 0.019) and nearly doubles WTPR over the best non-neural baseline TCDF (0.532 vs 0.327), while being ~2000× faster than constraint-based methods like PC and LPCMCI. On temporal CRL (Table 3), CaDRe outperforms all 9 CRL baselines across independent, sparse, and dense settings; in the independent setting it achieves MCC=0.9811 vs TDRL's 0.9106 and G-CaRL's 0.8023, and in the sparse setting its R²=0.9102 exceeds the next-best TDRL (0.6953) by a wide margin. On forecasting (Table 5), CaDRe is competitive but not uniformly best: on CESM2-96 it ties with CARD (MSE 0.410 vs 0.409), on Weather-96 it beats MICN (0.157 vs 0.168) and iTransformer (0.157 vs 0.180), and on ERSST-96 it outperforms Timer-XL (0.145 vs 0.163), though on ERSST-336 it ties with MICN (0.305). The key differentiator is that CaDRe is the only method simultaneously recovering latent variables, latent causal graphs, and observation-level causal graphs with nonparametric identifiability guarantees, whereas baselines like FCI and LPCMCI cannot identify latent variables and CRL methods like TDRL and iCITRIS cannot handle causally-related observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaDRe unifies two previously separate research threads—Causal Representation Learning (CRL) and Causal Discovery (CD)—into a single nonparametric framework. It builds on the eigendecomposition identifiability technique of Hu & Schennach (2008), extending it from distribution-level to value-level latent recovery, and on the nonlinear ICA framework (Hyvarinen et al., 2019) to establish a functional equivalence between SEMs and ICA mixing structures. The key departure from prior CRL methods (iCITRIS, G-CaRL, CaRiNG, TDRL) is the relaxation of the noise-free invertible generation assumption to accommodate stochastic, noise-contaminated observations with inter-observable causal edges; the key departure from prior CD methods (FCI, PCMCI, LPCMCI) is the simultaneous recovery of latent variables and their causal interrelations, which those methods cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide nonparametric identifiability guarantees for jointly recovering latent variables, the latent causal process, and the causal graph over observed variables under noise-contaminated generation—a setting that matches real climate data. The recovered causal graphs align with known physical wind patterns and land-atmosphere coupling, offering interpretable scientific insight beyond correlation-based forecasting. Limitations include the restriction to contemporaneous (not lagged) edges among observed variables and evaluation only on climate datasets; extending to other scientific domains and incorporating lagged observation-level causal relations are identified as natural next steps. The Impact Statement notes that the core linear-operator identifiability machinery has already been adopted by at least five follow-up studies across video generation, hierarchical priors, streaming forecasting, and factor analysis, suggesting broad theoretical utility beyond climate.
