## Problem

Existing continuous-time reinforcement learning (CTRL) methods rely on fixed, uniform measurement schedules and provide only worst-case regret guarantees, ignoring the heterogeneity in problem difficulty across environments. This paper asks whether a CTRL algorithm can be provably adaptive to instance difficulty—requiring less exploration in simpler environments—and how the choice of measurement strategy influences this adaptivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266081/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes discrete state observations x_n(t_n^k) collected at non-uniformly spaced measurement times (with variable gaps Δ_{n,k}), a known bounded reward function b, a policy class Π, and function classes F×G for drift and diffusion. It outputs a sequence of near-optimal policies u_n and, after N episodes, an ε-optimal policy ū selected uniformly at random. Unusually, the measurement schedule itself is a design variable: gaps can be non-uniform and are augmented with one uniformly random observation per interval, so the total measurement count per episode is 2m_n rather than m_n.

## Method

CT-MLE replaces direct estimation of the drift-diffusion dynamics (f*, g*) with maximum likelihood estimation of the marginal state density p_{f,g}(u,x,Δ) over each measurement gap, using a general function approximator. Each episode follows an optimistic model-based loop: a confidence set P_n is constructed via MLE over historical conditional densities, a second confidence set P̂_n is built from randomized intra-interval observations (Algorithm 2), and the policy is chosen by maximizing the value function over the intersection P_n ∩ P̂_n. The Monte Carlo-type randomized measurement—sampling one uniform time point per interval—provides an unbiased estimate of the reward integral without increasing measurement complexity beyond a constant factor. The resulting regret bound scales as Õ(d² + d√(ΣΔ² + ΣVar^{u_n})), where the variance term captures diffusion-driven reward fluctuations; when measurement gaps are chosen proportional to Var^{u_n}/T, the regret becomes nearly independent of the measurement schedule, yielding the first instance-dependent, nearly horizon-free second-order regret bound for stochastic CTRL under general function approximation.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or numerical benchmarks. The main result (Theorem 5.13) establishes a regret bound of Õ(d² + d√(ΣₙΔₙ² + Σₙ Var^{uₙ})) for CT-MLE over N episodes, where d is the eluder-dimension complexity, Δₙ is the measurement resolution, and Var^{uₙ} is the total reward variance. Corollary 5.17 specializes this to equidistant measurements, yielding regret Õ(log(N/δ)·log(TN/Δ)·(d² + d√(NTΔ + N·Var^Π))). A key finding is that the regret depends on T only logarithmically (via Var^{uₙ} ≤ 1), avoiding the exponential-in-T dependence of prior CTRL bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper presents no empirical comparisons or baselines; all contributions are theoretical. In terms of theoretical positioning, CT-MLE's regret bound is the first instance-dependent, nearly horizon-free, second-order (variance-dependent) bound for stochastic CTRL under general function approximation. Prior CTRL results (Treven et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025) provide worst-case guarantees that scale exponentially with the planning horizon T, whereas CT-MLE's bound depends on T only through the bounded total variance Var^{uₙ} ≤ 1. The paper also contrasts with discrete-time variance-aware RL (Wang et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2023), showing that a standard MLE-based model-based approach—without bespoke variance estimation or distributional techniques—achieves analogous second-order guarantees in the continuous-time setting. The randomized measurement strategy (Algorithm 2) is shown to maintain the same measurement complexity order while enabling unbiased integral estimation, a technique not present in prior CTRL analyses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-based CTRL framework of Treven et al. (2023, 2024) by replacing direct drift/diffusion estimation with marginal state density estimation via MLE, drawing on the likelihood-based confidence set machinery from discrete-time RL (Agarwal et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025). The variance-dependent regret analysis adapts the second-order bound techniques of Wang et al. (2025) and Zhou et al. (2023) from discrete-time MDPs to the continuous-time SDE setting, using the eluder dimension of the full induced distribution rather than just the drift nonlinearity. A key departure from prior CTRL theory is the demonstration that the regret is nearly independent of the specific measurement schedule (given fixed total budget), and the avoidance of the exponential-in-horizon dependence that plagued earlier CTRL bounds (Treven et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first instance-dependent, nearly horizon-free second-order regret bound for stochastic CTRL under general function approximation, showing that a standard MLE-based approach (without bespoke variance estimation or distributional machinery) suffices to capture problem difficulty through total reward variance. The practical implication is that measurement schedules can be adapted to environment stochasticity—denser sampling in low-variance (deterministic) settings, sparser in high-variance ones—without degrading guarantees. Key limitations include the idealized (non-implementable) Algorithm 1, the assumption of a known reward function, and the absence of computationally efficient variants; extending to stochastic rewards, time-varying policies, and realistic dynamics that still admit eluder-dimension analysis remain open.
