## Problem

Standard causal attribution paradigms (e.g., activation patching) target atomic, single-token outcomes, but personality in LLMs manifests as a holistic, multi-token behavioral tendency, creating a fundamental metric gap that has prevented fine-grained causal tracing of personality circuits. Consequently, the internal computational dynamics governing how abstract character traits are encoded and expressed remain opaque, and the mechanistic root of Out-of-Character (OOC) decay during prolonged interactions is unidentified.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a contrastive prompt template (system prompt + trait directive + generic query, synchronized at the prefix token 'As') to extract the Latent Persona Vector via activation subtraction at the last prompt token, and a persona-conditioned generation prompt for runtime causal tracing. The PSE intervention additionally requires a pre-computed canonical persona signal (mean activation of identified critical heads over In-Character training samples) that is injected at inference time. Output is the generated multi-token text response, evaluated for persona consistency via LLM-as-a-judge and standard role-playing benchmarks.

## Method

The paper introduces the Latent Persona Vector, extracted via contrastive activation subtraction, and defines a Personality Restoration Score (PRS) based on cosine similarity to this vector, enabling causal localization of personality circuits across tokens, attention heads, and neurons without requiring a deterministic target token. Applying this metric reveals a structured three-stage 'Preparation-Establishment-Expression' dynamic: shallow MLPs retrieve discrete knowledge fragments, middle-layer attention heads aggregate these into a coherent persona intent, and deep layers translate the intent into stylized lexical distributions. The analysis diagnoses OOC behavior as a magnitude disparity where the emergent persona signal is outcompeted by an assistant-like default direction during the Establishment phase. Guided by this diagnosis, Personality Signal Enhancement (PSE) is a training-free intervention that surgically injects the canonical persona signal into fewer than 5% of critical attention heads at inference time, restoring character consistency while preserving general reasoning capabilities on MMLU, GSM8K, and CSQA.

## Results

On Character-LLM, PSE (Top-30 Heads) achieves the highest Personality score of 5.60 (Single) / 4.81 (Multi), a +8.9% / +7.6% improvement over the baseline (5.14 / 4.47), outperforming the best steering baseline (5.53 / 4.62). On RoleBench, PSE raises EN CUS from 18.66 to 20.90 (+2.24) and EN SPE from 18.45 to 19.93 (+1.48). General capabilities are preserved: CSQA remains at 73.79, GSM8K improves slightly to 60.80, and MMLU drops only marginally to 61.83. Ablating fewer than 5% of identified heads causes catastrophic persona collapse (consistency drops to 0.28), while ablating the same number of random heads has negligible effect (0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSE (Top-30 Heads) outperforms all three baselines on the primary Personality metric: it achieves 5.60 (Single) vs. 5.53 for Steering (Poterti et al., 2025), 5.28 for Prompt (Li et al., 2023a), and 5.13 for Prompt (Kong et al., 2024). The advantage is most pronounced on multi-turn Personality (4.81 vs. 4.62 for Steering) and Values (4.91 vs. 4.73 for Steering), indicating PSE better sustains persona over longer interactions. On RoleBench, PSE leads by +1.51 over the best baseline (Steering: 19.39) on EN CUS and +0.99 on EN SPE. Critically, PSE preserves general reasoning (CSQA 73.79, GSM8K 60.80, MMLU 61.83) while Steering degrades GSM8K to 59.15 and MMLU to 61.38. The Random-30 Heads control (5.17 Personality) confirms that gains are not from arbitrary head perturbation but from the specifically localized persona circuit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal attribution paradigm of Activation Patching (Wang et al., 2023) and Attribution Patching (Nanda, 2024; Kramár et al., 2024) to the domain of holistic, multi-token personality expression, where standard single-token logit-difference metrics are inapplicable. It repurposes the contrastive activation subtraction methodology from Representation Engineering and persona vector work (Poterti et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025) not merely as a steering tool but as a differentiable causal metric (the Latent Persona Vector) that enables fine-grained circuit tracing. Conceptually, it departs from prior role-playing mitigation work (prompt engineering, RepE) by providing a mechanistic diagnosis of *why* interventions work, rather than treating the model as a black box to be steered heuristically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first mechanistic framework to trace the full lifecycle of personality generation in LLMs, and its diagnosis of OOC behavior as signal competition with an instruction-tuned 'assistant-like default direction' offers a concrete, testable explanation for a long-observed but poorly understood failure mode. The training-free, sparse (<5% heads) PSE intervention is practically attractive for role-playing applications, and the preservation of general reasoning capabilities (MMLU, GSM8K, CSQA) addresses a key concern with representation-level interventions. Open questions include whether the three-stage dynamic and the assistant-like competition generalize to more subtle or multi-dimensional character profiles, to non-English settings, and to models where instruction tuning has not established a strong default assistant mode.
