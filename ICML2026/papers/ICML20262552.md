## Problem

Diffusion language models (dLLMs) can predict multiple tokens in parallel, but they assume those tokens are conditionally independent given the context, forcing a tradeoff between speed and coherence: parallel generation produces incoherent mixtures (e.g., 'San York') while sequential generation is slow. The paper argues this is not a backbone capacity issue but a structural misspecification—the output distribution is restricted to a fully factorized form because explicitly parameterizing a joint distribution would require prohibitively many parameters.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the method takes a partially masked token sequence x_t (the current diffusion state) and produces a joint distribution over the masked positions. The Transformer backbone outputs factorized logits θ over the masked tokens, which are then multiplied with a Probabilistic Circuit prior p_ω defined over a fixed-size window (e.g., 32 tokens for block diffusion, or a dynamically selected window for full diffusion). The PC is instantiated as a Hidden Markov Model with 1024 hidden states, and the final output is a generated text sequence produced iteratively over T denoising steps.

## Method

CoDD decomposes the denoising distribution into a product of a structural prior p_ω (a decomposable Probabilistic Circuit) and a context-aware factorized modulation p_θ (the Transformer's logits), normalized by a partition function Z. The key tractability insight is that because p_θ is fully factorized, Z can be computed via a single feedforward pass over the PC by pushing the global summation down to the leaves, exploiting decomposability. Training is modular: the Transformer is frozen and only the PC parameters ω are optimized using the anemone optimizer on precomputed logits, requiring only ~3 GPU hours. At inference, the PC is adaptively activated only when the mask ratio falls below a threshold γ, and two sampling strategies (latent-variable sampling and any-order autoregressive sampling) handle temperature scaling, which is otherwise #P-hard on the mixture distribution.

## Results

On LLaDA (block diffusion), CoDD improves MATH500 accuracy by +5.00% (256 steps, Low Confidence decoding) and MBPP by +6.80% (128 steps). On Dream (full diffusion), gains are more pronounced: GSM8K accuracy rises from 56.18% to 67.02% (+10.84%) at 128 steps and from 33.97% to 56.41% (+22.44%) at 64 steps under Entropy decoding. Training the PC structural prior requires only ~3 GPU hours (<2% of RL-based baselines), while inference overhead is just 4–5% on Dream and 5–12% on LLaDA. On WikiText-103, CoDD reduces perplexity by 0.40–0.59 points over the Dream baseline across 16/32/64 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDD consistently outperforms both LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) and Dream (Ye et al., 2025) baselines across all four reasoning benchmarks and both diffusion paradigms (block and full), with the largest gains appearing at reduced step budgets (e.g., +22.44% on GSM8K at 64 steps for Dream). Against the RL-based baselines diffu-GRPO (Zhao et al., 2025), wd1 (Tang et al., 2025), and d2 (Wang et al., 2025), CoDD matches or exceeds their MATH500 accuracy while requiring only ~3 GPU hours of training versus orders of magnitude more compute for RL fine-tuning. On the WikiText-103 likelihood evaluation, CoDD outperforms both SEDD (Lou et al., 2024) and Discrete Copula Diffusion (Liu et al., 2025a) by approximately 1 nat per token in conditional log-likelihood, while using a far lighter computational footprint than DCD's autoregressive auxiliary model. Inference overhead remains minimal: CoDD adds only 4–5% latency on Dream and 5–12% on LLaDA, compared to 33–39% for diffu-GRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete diffusion language model paradigm (LLaDA, Dream, SEDD) and the Probabilistic Circuit framework (Choi et al., 2020), combining them via a multiplicative composition strategy inspired by boosted generative models (Grover & Ermon, 2018). It departs from prior attempts to address the factorization barrier—such as Discrete Copula Diffusion (Liu et al., 2025a), which augments diffusion with an autoregressive model, and energy-based approaches (Xu et al., 2025)—by identifying the root cause as a structural output misspecification rather than a backbone capacity issue, and by using a lightweight HMM-structured PC as a tractable inference layer that supports exact marginal computation under arbitrary masking patterns. The approach also contrasts with RL-based reasoning improvements (diffu-GRPO, WD1, D2) by achieving comparable gains at a fraction of the training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoDD offers a practical, plug-and-play solution to a fundamental limitation of all current diffusion language models: the inability to model inter-token dependencies during parallel generation. The ~3 GPU-hour training cost and 4–5% inference overhead make it immediately deployable on existing pre-trained dLLMs, and the prevention of few-step performance collapse (e.g., GSM8K 34%→56.4% at 64 steps) addresses a critical practical bottleneck. Key open questions include the static-PC limitation—the dependency structure of language shifts from global to local across diffusion steps, and a single PC cannot adapt to this—motivating the proposed but unimplemented latent-space and parameter-space modulation extensions. Whether the approach generalizes beyond the HMM-structured PC to richer circuit topologies, and whether dynamic structural selection can close the remaining gap to sequential generation, remain important directions.
