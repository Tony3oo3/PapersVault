## Problem

Ultra-low-bit post-training quantization (W2A2, W3A3) suffers severe accuracy degradation because isotropic objectives like MSE fundamentally mismatch the anisotropic Fisher geometry of deep networks, and existing curvature-aware methods either rely on block-diagonal approximations that neglect cross-dimensional couplings or operate directly on ill-conditioned surfaces where precise approximation is computationally elusive. No unified framework existed that could navigate this complex curvature geometry without altering the native model architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SHARP-Q takes a pre-trained FP32 model and a small unlabeled calibration set (1,024 ImageNet images for classification; DF2K for super-resolution) and produces a fully quantized model at W2A2, W3A3, or W4A4. The rectification operator Γ is fused offline into weights or normalization parameters, so at inference the quantized model accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, arbitrary low-res for SR) and outputs class labels or high-resolution images with negligible overhead (<0.2% FLOPs). The method operates block-wise, processing each linear layer independently with 20,000 reconstruction iterations.

## Method

SHARP-Q implements a 'Rectify-then-Approximate' strategy grounded in Information Geometry. Stage 1 (Hessian-Aware Rectification) learns a diagonal preconditioning operator Γ=exp(P) via gradient descent using the Fisher-KL Oracle (∇L_KL ≈ F·Δz), which monotonically compresses the spectral envelope of the curvature matrix and dampens cross-dimensional couplings under a trust-region constraint. Stage 2 (Dynamic Fisher-Subspace Compensation) constructs a Hybrid Fisher Surrogate by combining a static empirical Fisher diagonal anchor with a dynamic low-rank (r=5) subspace correction built from historical perturbation vectors, decorrelated via a Gram matrix inverse to eliminate spectral redundancy. Quantization parameters (AdaRound rounding weights and step sizes) are then optimized to minimize the Fisher-weighted reconstruction loss ΔzᵀF_hybridΔz. The key insight is that once the landscape is rectified, a minimal-rank approximation suffices to capture dominant curvature, enabling hardware-friendly uniform quantizers to outperform specialized non-uniform designs.

## Results

On ImageNet ViTs (W3A3), SHARP-Q achieves 66.93% Top-1 on ViT-S, surpassing the nearest competitor APHQ-ViT (63.17%) by 3.76%, and 77.86% on ViT-B vs. FIMA-Q's 77.11%. On CNNs (W2A2), SHARP-Q reaches 56.74% on ResNet-18 and 46.89% on RegNetX-600MF, outperforming the prior SOTA CL-Calib by 2.29% and 4.70% respectively. On Mamba super-resolution (W4A4, ×4), SHARP-Q attains 30.85 dB PSNR on Manga109, exceeding SPR²Q by 1.25 dB while closely recovering the FP32 baseline (31.24 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHARP-Q achieves the best result across all 26 baselines in every evaluated setting. On ViTs (W3A3), it outperforms the closest Fisher-based competitors FIMA-Q and APHQ-ViT by 3.76% on ViT-S and 0.75% on ViT-B, and surpasses the non-uniform AdaLog quantizer by 4.43% on ViT-S—demonstrating that geometric alignment with uniform quantizers exceeds specialized non-uniform designs. On CNNs (W2A2), SHARP-Q beats the prior SOTA CL-Calib by 2.29% (ResNet-18), 3.11% (MobileNetV2), and 4.70% (RegNetX-600MF), while also outperforming MSE-based QDrop and block-diagonal BRECQ by 5.32% and 14.20% on ResNet-18 respectively. LLM-centric methods GPTQ and GPTAQ degrade severely on vision models (e.g., 71.9% vs. SHARP-Q's 76.86% on DeiT-S W4A4), confirming poor cross-domain transfer. On Mamba super-resolution, SHARP-Q surpasses all four Mamba-specialized baselines (MambaQuant, Quamba, 2DQuant, SPR²Q) by 0.41–4.12 dB PSNR across datasets, with the largest gap (4.12 dB) over MambaQuant on Manga109 ×4. The ablation confirms both HAR and DFSC are necessary: HAR alone adds ~1.3–1.6%, while the full pipeline yields up to 27.76% over the MSE baseline on ViT-S W3A3.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHARP-Q extends the BRECQ lineage of using the Fisher Information Matrix for PTQ reconstruction, but departs fundamentally from prior FIM-based methods (FIMA-Q, APHQ-ViT) that operate directly on the raw, ill-conditioned landscape. Instead of passively approximating the raw FIM or resorting to isotropic MSE (QDrop, PD-Quant) and diagonal proxies (BRECQ), SHARP-Q introduces a 'Rectify-then-Approximate' strategy: first preconditioning the geometry via Hessian-Aware Rectification, then approximating the rectified FIM with a low-rank dynamic subspace. The method also builds on AdaRound's binary rounding parameterization for the reconstruction optimization, while positioning itself as architecture-agnostic in contrast to ViT-specific (RepQ-ViT, OASQ) and SSM-specific (MambaQuant, Quamba) approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that geometric alignment with the Fisher metric matters more than quantizer complexity, enabling uniform quantizers to surpass specialized non-uniform designs—challenges a prevailing assumption in ViT quantization where heavy-tailed distributions were thought to necessitate non-uniform quantizers. The demonstrated universality across CNNs, ViTs, and State Space Models with a single framework is practically significant for deployment pipelines. However, the method's reliance on AdaRound-based iterative reconstruction limits scalability to 7B+ LLMs, and the W4A4 margins over the best non-uniform baselines are modest (≤0.5%), with the more compelling gains appearing in W3A3 and W2A2 regimes. Extending the framework to large language models with more efficient solvers remains a key open question.
