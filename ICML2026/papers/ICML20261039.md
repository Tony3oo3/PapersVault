## Problem

While compression techniques like quantization, pruning, and low-rank approximation are routine in deployment, there is no exact closed-form characterization of the minimal weight perturbation required to flip a network's prediction, nor a theoretical link between compression magnitude and the activation of latent backdoor behaviour. Prior compression-activated backdoor work (Tian et al., 2021; Hong et al., 2021) demonstrated the vulnerability empirically but provided no certifiable thresholds or layer-wise sensitivity analysis.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a trained network's full parameter vector θ (flattened weight matrices), a batch of input samples X ∈ ℝ^{d×s}, and a target output modification Ỹ specifying which samples should be reclassified. It produces (i) the exact minimum-Frobenius-norm perturbation ΔW_N* for a chosen layer via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the upstream representation, (ii) a parameter-space Lipschitz lower bound γ(x;θ) ≤ 2^((p−1)/p) L_θ‖Δθ‖_p valid for multi-layer perturbations, and (iii) for the backdoor application, a certifiable compression threshold (rank, sparsity, or bit-width) below which the backdoor provably cannot activate. For LLMs (Phi-2, RoBERTa), inputs are token sequences up to 384 subword tokens; for vision, 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images.

## Method

The paper derives two complementary theoretical results. Theorem 3.1 gives an exact closed-form solution ΔW_N* = Δh_{N:M}^{−1}(Ỹ, Y; θ)(h_{1:N−1}(X))† for the minimum-norm single-layer perturbation under local invertibility of the downstream map, revealing that the back-propagated margin at the perturbed layer governs both magnitude and direction. Theorem 4.1 provides a more general margin-Lipschitz bound relating the classification margin to a parameter-space Lipschitz constant, applicable to arbitrary architectures and simultaneous multi-layer perturbations. Corollary 5.1 specialises to low-rank SVD truncation, showing the margin change s_k = Σ_{i>k} σ_i(δᵀu_i)(v_iᵀz) depends only on residual components of the input and class-difference direction outside the retained singular subspaces. For the backdoor application, models are fine-tuned with a combined loss L_backdoor = L_FP + c₁·L_MP that preserves full-precision accuracy while driving attacker-chosen outputs under a set P of compression modifications; the theoretical thresholds are then verified empirically by confirming that ASR remains near zero below the certified bound and exceeds 70% once the margin bound is crossed.

## Results

The paper's headline empirical result (Table 2) shows that low-rank compression activates latent backdoors while preserving full-precision accuracy: on CIFAR-10 with VGG16, the low-rank-activated model (LRAM) achieves 85.9% clean accuracy with only 10.1% attack success rate (ASR) at full rank, but ASR jumps above 95% when the final layer is truncated to rank 8. On Tiny-ImageNet (VGG16), full-rank ASR is 0.5% while rank-190 compression yields ~99% ASR. For the Phi-2 LLM on SQuAD, 20% pruning combined with rank-1500 compression achieves 86.0% ASR versus 45.5% at full rank. The theoretical margin-Lipschitz bound (Thm. 4.1) is never violated by empirical perturbations, and for a ResNet18 pruning experiment, sparsity below 11% provably guarantees no backdoor activation while 17% sparsity triggers a class flip.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing backdoor-attack methods; instead it validates its theoretical predictions against a standard backdoor control (Equation 11) and against its own margin-Lipschitz lower bound. The control model (trained with a standard backdoor loss) exhibits high ASR (>95%) at all precision levels, confirming it is a valid backdoor baseline. The compression-activated model (their method) matches the control's clean accuracy within 1% at full precision while reducing ASR to 0.5–10%, demonstrating that the backdoor is genuinely latent until compression is applied. On the theoretical side, the exact single-layer perturbation from Thm. 3.1 is verified in Table 1: empirical perturbations from gradient-based retraining never fall below the theoretical minimum norm, and class flips occur only when the perturbation exceeds the bound. The margin-Lipschitz bound (Thm. 4.1) is confirmed in Table 3: at 10% sparsity the bound is not satisfied and no flip occurs, while at 17% sparsity the bound is satisfied and a flip is observed. The paper also reproduces prior quantization-activated (Hong et al.) and pruning-activated (Tian et al.) results to validate its experimental framework before extending to low-rank compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two complementary lines of work. On the theoretical side, it builds on Tsai et al. (2021), who derived margin-based bounds on output changes under norm-bounded weight perturbations, by providing exact closed-form expressions for the minimal-norm perturbation (via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the upstream representation) and a parameter-space Lipschitz robustness bound that applies to multi-layer perturbations and arbitrary architectures. On the empirical side, it extends the compression-activated backdoor paradigm of Tian et al. (2021, pruning) and Hong et al. (2021, quantization) to low-rank SVD approximation, showing that the discarded singular-value tail provides a natural structural mechanism for latent behaviour activation. The paper positions its exact single-layer formula as strictly sharper than prior worst-case bounds while showing both are empirically of the same order.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: low-rank SVD compression is one of the most widely deployed efficiency techniques for both CNNs and LLMs, and this work shows it can silently activate hidden backdoors while preserving full-precision accuracy, with provable compression thresholds below which activation is impossible. The theoretical framework gives practitioners a certifiable diagnostic (via estimated parameter-space Lipschitz constants) to assess whether a given compression level is safe for a specific model. Key limitations include the local-invertibility assumption for the exact formula (which fails for ReLU/pooling in general, requiring a first-order Jacobian approximation for modern architectures) and the conservatism of the Lipschitz bound, which has exponential worst-case depth dependence. Open questions include whether closed-form multi-layer perturbation solutions exist, whether the thresholds transfer across training runs and data distributions, and how to detect already-embedded latent backdoors in deployed models.
