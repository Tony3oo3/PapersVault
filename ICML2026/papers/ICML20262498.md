## Problem

ANN-to-SNN conversion is fundamentally lossy: quantization errors from discrete spike events accumulate and propagate forward through deep networks, causing severe accuracy degradation that worsens with depth and task complexity. Existing remedies either modify the source ANN's training or architecture (sacrificing the generality of off-the-shelf models) or apply open-loop post-conversion corrections that still permit residual error accumulation. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a closed-loop fine-tuning framework that calibrates conversion errors without altering the source model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262498/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen, pre-trained ANN (ResNet-18/50, VGG-16, or ResNet-34) and standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) as input, and produces a fine-tuned SNN of identical architecture that outputs class labels via time-averaged membrane potentials of the final-layer IF neurons. The source ANN weights are never modified; only the SNN's inherited weights are updated during the 50-epoch distillation stage. A key structural detail is that the SNN's output logits are derived by averaging membrane potentials over T time steps, bridging the temporal spike domain to a static logit vector for Softmax classification.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages: (1) a standard ANN-to-SNN conversion (BN merging, ReLU→IF replacement, soft-reset mechanism, and 99.9th-percentile voltage scaling), followed by (2) a closed-loop distillation stage using STBP where a frozen Teacher ANN guides a trainable Student SNN. The core novelty is a dual alignment loss: a global KL-divergence term aligns the SNN's firing-rate logits with the teacher's softened output distribution (τ=2.0), while an adaptive local CKA term enforces layer-wise representational similarity, with per-layer weights w_l proportional to each layer's initial conversion loss (1−CKA_initial), thereby prioritizing the most damaged representations. A critical empirical finding is the time-dependent regime: at low T (≤8), the local CKA constraint stabilizes optimization because global gradients are high-variance under sparse spiking, whereas at high T (≥32) the global term becomes an unbiased fidelity driver. This synergy, governed by hyperparameters α=0.5 and β calibrated to balance gradient magnitudes, achieves >99% of source ANN accuracy at T=32 without any architectural modification to the teacher.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (source ANN: 96.39%), the proposed method achieves 96.38% top-1 accuracy at T=32, reaching over 99% of source ANN fidelity without altering the source architecture. At the critical low-latency regime T=8, the method attains 95.33%, dramatically outperforming the SpikingJelly conversion baseline (49.05%) and matching or exceeding conversion-aware methods like QCFS (94.82%) and ECL (96.11%). For VGG-16, the method achieves lossless conversion at T=64 (94.98% vs. 94.98% source). On ImageNet with ResNet-34, the method reaches 55.13% at T=16, surpassing SpikingJelly (39.00% at T=64) despite using 16× fewer time steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-altering baselines (those that do not modify the source ANN), the proposed method decisively outperforms SpikingJelly and RMP at all time steps: at T=8 on ResNet-18, Ours achieves 95.33% versus SpikingJelly's 49.05% and RMP's 10.30%. Against conversion-aware methods that alter the source architecture, Ours matches or exceeds QCFS (94.82% at T=8) and SlipReLU (94.59% at T=8) without any architectural modification, and is competitive with ECL (96.11% at T=8) and Twostage (95.36% at T=8). At T=32, Ours (96.38%) is within 0.01% of the source ANN and slightly trails Twostage (96.44%) and ECL (96.35%), but surpasses QCFS (96.08%) and OPI (95.59%). On VGG-16, Ours achieves lossless conversion at T=64 (94.98% = source), outperforming SpikingJelly (92.18%) and RMP (93.34%) by 2.8 and 1.6 points respectively. On ImageNet (ResNet-34), Ours at T=16 (55.13%) surpasses SpikingJelly at T=64 (39.00%) by 16.1 points, though it trails HCSB at T=256 (61.48%) by 6.4 points, reflecting the trade-off between latency and accuracy on the larger dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of ANN-to-SNN conversion and post-conversion fine-tuning, building directly on the SpikingJelly conversion pipeline (Fang et al., 2023) for Stage 1 and Spatio-Temporal Backpropagation (Wu et al., 2018) for Stage 2. It departs from conversion-aware training methods (QCFS, OPI, SlipReLU) that modify the source ANN's architecture or activations, instead preserving the pre-trained ANN entirely and correcting errors in a closed-loop distillation step. The key methodological departure from prior post-conversion work (SNNC-AP, offset-spike correction, FTBC) is the introduction of an adaptive CKA-based local constraint weighted by initial per-layer conversion loss, combined with standard KL-divergence global distillation, and the explicit time-dependent analysis of when each signal dominates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, drop-in refinement for any off-the-shelf ANN-to-SNN conversion pipeline, achieving near-lossless fidelity (≥99% of source accuracy at T=32) without architectural modifications, which is valuable for practitioners who cannot retrain or restructure their source models. The time-dependent finding—that local CKA constraints stabilize low-latency regimes where global gradients are noisy, while global KL drives fidelity at higher T—provides actionable guidance for hyperparameter selection across latency budgets. Limitations include the absence of hardware-measured energy savings, ImageNet evaluation restricted to T=16, and no exploration of non-IF neuron models or transformer architectures, leaving open whether the dual-alignment strategy generalizes beyond standard CNN backbones.
