## Problem

Multi-turn human-AI collaboration is hindered by sparse outcome rewards that only materialize at conversation end, combined with high stochasticity in user responses that makes value estimation sample-inefficient. Existing dense-reward approaches—explicit process reward models requiring 800k+ human annotations, Monte Carlo roll-outs with 8–10× sample overhead, or token-level implicit PRMs—either lack scalability for online RL or suffer from high variance, semantic misalignment, and overfitting at the token granularity. The paper asks how to derive stable, interpretable, fine-grained process rewards for multi-turn interactions using only sparse outcome supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-turn conversation trajectories (conversation history h^k, user query x^k, and model response y^k for each turn k) along with a single sparse outcome reward R ∈ [0,1] computed by a task-specific LLM judge (binary accuracy for math, BLEU for document writing, hierarchical 0–10 score for medical recommendation). It produces per-turn process rewards R̃_φ^k that redistribute the global outcome across turns, which are then fed into standard advantage estimators (RLOO, GRPO, or GAE with a learned critic) to compute turn-level advantages for PPO-style policy updates. The implicit PRM is updated online via binary cross-entropy against the outcome reward, requiring no manual process annotations or auxiliary sampling.

## Method

ITPO derives token-level implicit process rewards as the scaled log-likelihood ratio between a trainable reward model π_φ and a frozen reference model π_ref, trained online with binary cross-entropy against the sparse outcome reward. The key aggregation step sums these token-level log-likelihood ratios within each turn to produce a turn-wise reward R_φ^k, which reduces variance and aligns credit assignment with the natural semantic unit of a conversational turn. Norm-ITPO further stabilizes training by applying a softmax over turn-wise scores (with temperature η) to compute contribution weights w_φ^k, then redistributing the global outcome reward as R̃_φ^k = w_φ^k · R, enforcing scale consistency between implicit and outcome rewards and preventing value drift. The policy is updated with a turn-level PPO objective where the importance sampling ratio ρ^{i,k} = π_θ(y^{i,k}|h^{i,k})/π_old(y^{i,k}|h^{i,k}) operates on entire turns rather than individual tokens, preserving semantic coherence and avoiding disruption of joint probability dependencies. The framework is plug-and-play with RLOO, GRPO, or GAE advantage estimators and requires no task-specific rubrics or external LLM judges for reward shaping.

## Results

Across three multi-turn collaborative tasks (Math Tutoring, Document Writing, Medical Recommendation) and three advantage estimators (RLOO, GRPO, PPO), Norm-ITPO consistently achieves the best overall Score. Under RLOO, Norm-ITPO reaches 29.33 Score on Math Tutoring (32.50% accuracy), 39.59 on Doc Writing (44.83 BLEU), and 66.14 on Medical Recommendation (69.24% diagnosis accuracy), outperforming the trajectory-level sparse baseline by 34.4%, 12.0%, and 8.0% respectively. Under GRPO, Norm-ITPO achieves 26.83, 37.86, and 68.66 Score across the three tasks. The method also reduces token usage substantially (e.g., 0.62k tokens vs. 1.44k for vanilla RLOO on Math Tutoring), indicating more efficient interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Norm-ITPO outperforms all baselines across every task and advantage estimator combination. Against PRIME (Cui et al., 2025), which uses token-level implicit rewards, Norm-ITPO wins by a wide margin: under RLOO it gains +7.79 Score on Math Tutoring (29.33 vs. 21.54), +2.38 on Doc Writing (39.59 vs. 37.21), and +10.24 on Medical Rec (66.14 vs. 55.90); PRIME notably fails to converge stably on Doc Writing under PPO (29.77 vs. 40.12). Against LLM-as-Judge (Yu et al., 2025), which assigns turn-level weights via an external Qwen2.5-14B judge, Norm-ITPO leads by +6.74 Score on Math Tutoring (29.33 vs. 22.59), +2.39 on Doc Writing (39.59 vs. 37.20), and +4.11 on Medical Rec (66.14 vs. 62.03) under RLOO, confirming that online-updated implicit rewards surpass static LLM-judge signals. Against standard PPO with a learned value model (Schulman et al., 2017) using only sparse outcome rewards ('VM Only'), Norm-ITPO under PPO improves Score by +3.38 on Math Tutoring (23.44 vs. 20.06), +3.75 on Doc Writing (40.12 vs. 36.37), and +4.67 on Medical Rec (59.44 vs. 54.77). The gap between Norm-ITPO and ITPO (without normalization) is most pronounced under PPO (e.g., +4.57 Score on Medical Rec), validating that the softmax normalization stabilizes the non-stationary scale of implicit rewards for value-function learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit process reward model (PRM) line of work (Yuan et al., 2024; Cui et al., 2025/PRIME), which derives dense token-level rewards from sparse outcome supervision, by re-aggregating those token-level log-likelihood ratios into turn-level signals suited to multi-turn dialogue. The key conceptual departure is that in collaborative User-LLM interaction, a conversational turn—not a token—is the natural atomic unit of semantic planning, and aggregating across tokens within a turn reduces variance while preserving interpretability. The normalization mechanism (Norm-ITPO) further departs from prior implicit PRMs by explicitly calibrating the reward scale to the outcome via a softmax redistribution, grounding the assignment in a Bayesian posterior over a latent pivotal turn. Policy optimization retains the standard PPO/GRPO/RLOO framework but shifts the importance-sampling ratio from token-level to turn-level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward sparsity in multi-turn dialogue RL is a practical bottleneck for deploying LLMs in tutoring, recommendation, and consultation settings; ITPO offers a scalable, annotation-free solution that consistently improves convergence across three tasks and three advantage estimators. The interpretability analysis (73–75% agreement with human expert judgments on best/worst turn identification) is a useful validation that the learned signals are semantically meaningful rather than stochastic artifacts. Key limitations include reliance on LLM-based user simulators that may not capture real human variability, potential bias in LLM judges, and the fact that the medical recommendation task is a simulated benchmark rather than a deployment-ready system. Open questions include whether the turn-level granularity generalizes to longer-horizon or open-ended interactions where turn boundaries are less natural, and whether the normalization mechanism is necessary when outcome rewards are already well-calibrated.
