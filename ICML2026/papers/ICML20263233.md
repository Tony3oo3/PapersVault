## Problem

Deep RL agents are opaque black boxes, limiting their deployment in safety-critical domains. Existing self-explainable DRL approaches either rely on post-hoc approximations of trained policies or require imitation learning from a pre-trained black-box agent, which constrains performance and is largely restricted to discrete action spaces. This paper addresses the gap of building a self-interpretable RL agent that learns directly via RL in continuous action spaces without distillation or manual prototype selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw environment state vector (e.g., joint angles and velocities for MuJoCo tasks, 2D position/velocity for LunarLander) and first encodes it through a neural encoder into a latent representation. The output is a continuous action sampled from a Gaussian distribution whose mean and standard deviation are computed as a similarity-weighted mixture over N learned prototypes, each carrying its own per-dimension Gaussian parameters. The prototype similarity scores (softmax-normalized) serve as the interpretable explanation, indicating which learned state-action clusters contributed to the chosen action.

## Method

ProtoSAC replaces the standard SAC actor with a ProtoActor that stores N learnable prototypes, each paired with an importance weight τ, a mean μ, and a standard deviation σ defining a per-prototype Gaussian over the action space. Given an encoded state, the actor computes log-distance similarity scores against all prototypes, applies a softmax weighted by τ to obtain mixture weights, and produces the final action distribution as a weighted sum of the per-prototype Gaussians. Training follows standard SAC critic updates but augments the actor loss with three regularization terms: a τ-sparsity penalty to encourage few active prototypes, a Frobenius-norm orthogonality loss to prevent prototype collapse, and a negative entropy loss to enforce soft multi-prototype assignments. Every M episodes, underutilized prototypes (below a quantile threshold on τ) are replaced with states sampled from the replay buffer that are maximally dissimilar to the current prototype set, promoting coverage of underexplored state regions.

## Results

ProtoSAC is evaluated on 8 continuous-control environments (Pendulum, MountainCar, InvertedPendulum, LunarLander, Hopper, HalfCheetah, Humanoid, CarRacing) using mean cumulative reward over 30 evaluation runs. ProtoSAC matches SAC's perfect score on InvertedPendulum (1000.0 ± 0.0) and achieves the highest reward on Hopper (3386.2 ± 8.9) and CarRacing (369.0 ± 189.4), while underperforming SAC on HalfCheetah (9875.2 vs. 11098.3, d = −10.23). Across all environments, ProtoSAC significantly outperforms Shared-PW-Net, with the largest gaps on Humanoid (4953.8 vs. 496.6, d = 47.37) and CarRacing (369.0 vs. −32.5, d = 3.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (the unconstrained baseline), ProtoSAC matches performance on Pendulum (d = −0.14, ns), LunarLander (d = 0.40, ns), InvertedPendulum (both 1000), and Humanoid (d = 0.40, ns); it significantly outperforms SAC on Hopper (+5.97, p = 0.001) and CarRacing (+136.08, p = 0.003); and it underperforms on MountainCar (−2.26, p < 0.001, though all methods are near the task ceiling) and HalfCheetah (−1223.15, d = −10.23), where high-frequency torque control is poorly captured by a small prototype set. Against Shared-PW-Net (the prior state-of-the-art self-explainable prototype method), ProtoSAC wins on 7 of 8 environments with large effect sizes (d > 1 on Pendulum, Hopper, Humanoid, CarRacing), with the most dramatic gap on Humanoid where Shared-PW-Net collapses to 496.6 reward versus ProtoSAC's 4953.8. The only environment where Shared-PW-Net edges out ProtoSAC is MountainCar (97.63 vs. 95.29), a difference the authors note is negligible given all methods are near the performance ceiling. The key structural advantage is that ProtoSAC trains directly via RL on the task, whereas Shared-PW-Net relies on imitation learning from a pre-trained black-box, limiting its ceiling to the teacher's behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoSAC sits at the intersection of two research threads: maximum-entropy actor-critic RL (SAC) and prototype-based interpretability (ProtoPNet). It departs from the closest prior work—PW-Net and Shared-PW-Net—by eliminating the imitation-learning dependency and the need for a pre-trained black-box teacher, instead embedding the prototypical actor directly into the SAC training loop. The prototype similarity mechanism and Gaussian action parameterization are adapted from ProtoPNet's image-classification architecture, but the paper introduces new components (importance weights τ, orthogonality and negative-entropy regularization, periodic prototype replacement) that are specific to the RL setting and have no direct counterpart in the vision literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtoSAC is the first self-interpretable RL agent that operates natively in continuous action spaces without distillation, which is a practical gap since most real-world control tasks (robotics, autonomous driving) are continuous. The demonstrated performance parity with SAC on most benchmarks, combined with the sensitivity analysis showing only 5–10 prototypes carry most of the policy, suggests a viable path toward auditable control policies. Key limitations remain: a significant performance gap on HalfCheetah (high-frequency torque control) indicates that prototype-based compression has a representational ceiling, and the increased training variance and slower convergence are practical costs. Open questions include adaptive or hierarchical prototype structures, extension to discrete action spaces, and whether the interpretability gains persist in multi-agent or partially-observable settings.
