## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability can read internal structure (circuits, features) from a trained model, but the reverse question—given a desired form of generalization, what training data produces it—had no principled answer. This paper introduces patterning as the dual of interpretability, showing that the same susceptibility-based linear response framework used to decode structure can be inverted to prescribe data interventions that steer which structures form during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model's weights (to estimate per-token or per-sample susceptibilities via SGLD sampling from the annealed posterior), the training data distribution (to compute the susceptibility matrix χ), and a target structural coordinate vector dμ_target specified in the basis of principal structures (left singular vectors of χ). It produces a minimum-norm re-weighting vector dh_opt = χ† dμ_target that assigns per-token or per-sample loss weights; applying these weights to the training distribution yields a modified dataset that, when used for (re)training, steers the posterior toward the target configuration. In the language model experiments the output is a scalar weight per token (0×, 1×, 2×, or 4×); in the parenthesis task it is a weight per training sequence.

## Method

The core mechanism is inverting the linear response relation dμ∞ = χ dh, where χ is the susceptibility matrix whose entries measure how posterior expectation values of structural observables (e.g., local learning coefficient, circuit slack) respond to infinitesimal shifts in the data distribution. The SVD of χ decomposes this into principal structures (left singular vectors u_α) and principal data patterns (right singular vectors v_α); targeting a single principal structure u_β reduces the optimal intervention to re-weighting data along v_β with strength 1/σ_β. For the parenthesis balancing task, the 2×m susceptibility matrix (rows = competing solutions, columns = training samples) yields, under near-orthogonality, a re-weighting proportional to the susceptibility gap χ^EQ − χ^N, which identifies interpretable 'almost nested' and 'almost equal' sequences that differentially raise or lower the LLC of each solution. LLC estimation uses SGLD with a localized tempered posterior (Eq. 9), and the entire framework is grounded in singular learning theory's free energy expansion, which guarantees that among equal-loss solutions the posterior favors the one with lower LLC—the principle of internal model selection that patterning exploits to shift algorithm selection.

## Results

In the induction circuit experiment (3M-param, 2-layer transformer, 16 heads), repressing induction-pattern tokens (Repress-0x) drops PC2 explained variance from 2.96% (Baseline-1x) to 0.98% and nearly eliminates induction head formation, while amplifying them (Induce-4x) accelerates formation and yields 3 induction heads versus 1–2 in baseline. In the parenthesis balancing task (100 retrained models per distribution), the original training set produces a bimodal OOD accuracy distribution with mean 0.310; the Almost Nested distribution collapses all 100 models to Equal-Count (mean OOD 0.004), while the Almost Equal distribution shifts the mean to 0.497, confirming that susceptibility-guided re-weighting selects between competing algorithms as predicted by the local learning coefficient gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing patterning or data-curation methods; the natural baseline is the unmodified training distribution. In the induction circuit experiment, the Baseline-1x condition (standard training) serves as the reference: Repress-0x suppresses circuit formation (PC2 variance 0.98% vs. 2.96%), while Induce-2x and Induce-4x accelerate it with stronger prefix-matching scores and more induction heads (up to 3 vs. 1–2). In the parenthesis task, the Almost Nested intervention is the most decisive: it completely eliminates the Nested solution (mean OOD drops from 0.310 to 0.004), whereas Almost Equal produces a weaker but still significant shift (mean OOD rises to 0.497), which the authors attribute to the relative rarity of 'almost equal' samples in the original distribution. Both interventions outperform the baseline in the predicted direction, validating the fundamental equation dh_opt = χ† dμ_target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the susceptibility-based linear response framework developed by Baker et al. (2025) and Gordon et al. (2026) for interpretability: where prior work uses dμ = χdh to *read* internal structure from a trained model, patterning uses the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse to *write* structure by solving for the data perturbation dh. The theoretical substrate is Structural Bayesianism (Murfet and Troiani, 2025) and Singular Learning Theory (Watanabe, 2009), with the local learning coefficient (Lau et al., 2025) serving as the target observable for algorithm selection. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from influence functions and data curation by targeting SLT-grounded structural coordinates (posterior expectation values of observables) rather than prediction-level quantities or aggregate metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the same mathematical machinery used to diagnose internal structure can be inverted to prescribe training data, offering a principled path toward controlling generalization — a key concern for AI alignment where models with identical benchmark performance may generalize differently. However, the empirical validation is confined to 3M-parameter models and a synthetic parentheses task, susceptibility estimation costs ~500× training compute, and only one-off (pre-training) interventions are demonstrated; the more powerful online variant remains future work. Open questions include whether the linear response approximation holds for finite (non-infinitesimal) interventions, how the framework scales to billion-parameter models, and whether structural observables reliably predict downstream safety-relevant behavior.
