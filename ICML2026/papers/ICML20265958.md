## Problem

Existing tabular data generators are trained to match the joint distribution P(X,Y), but augmentation succeeds only when injected samples reduce the learner's held-out loss. Under severe data scarcity, high-fidelity samples from high-density regions are often redundant, while the most useful samples lie in low-density, uncertain regions that distribution-matching generators under-explore. The paper formalizes this fidelity-utility gap and asks not just how to generate, but what to generate and when to inject as the learner evolves.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled tabular dataset (n_real as low as 20 samples), a frozen pre-trained TabDiff diffusion backbone, and a training-free TabPFN evaluator for online utility estimation. It produces an augmented training set by committing up to 500 synthetic samples through a sequential windowed process; each synthetic record is generated by inpainting a subset of columns conditioned on a real anchor and a target label/quantile, then filtered by hard feasibility gates (categorical validity, quantile-range clipping, logical constraints) before entering the committed buffer.

## Method

TAP frames tabular augmentation as a sequential control problem: a lightweight MLP policy observes a compact learner-state summary (target deficit, predictive uncertainty, gate pass rates, diversity score) and selects an action (target condition, mask template, exploration strength) that steers a frozen diffusion inpainting kernel toward high-utility regions. Candidates pass through pointwise hard feasibility gates, accumulate in a temporary pool over a window of K steps, and are committed only when the pooled plug-in utility (estimated via TabPFN cross-validation on focused hard queries) exceeds a calibrated threshold τ + ε_t, providing a certified safety guarantee (Theorem 3.1). Policy learning uses a KTO-style preference-based update with KL regularization against a conservative reference policy, converting noisy utility estimates into binary desirable/undesirable feedback. The key distinction from prior work is that the generator is treated as a controllable proposal mechanism whose conditions adapt to the evolving learner, rather than a fixed distribution-matching model sampled passively.

## Results

Under severe data scarcity (n_real=20), TAP improves classification accuracy by up to 15.6 percentage points over the best generative baseline and reduces regression RMSE by up to 32%, evaluated across seven real-world datasets and five scarcity levels (n_real ∈ {20, 50, 100, 200, 500}) with a fixed synthetic budget of n_syn=500. Gains are largest at the most extreme scarcity levels and remain positive across all datasets, whereas several baselines (notably TabDDPM and CTGAN) underperform the no-augmentation Real baseline on a non-trivial fraction of settings. Ablations confirm that both pointwise feasibility gating and windowed commitment are necessary: removing gating drops win-rate from 100% to 57.3% and increases tail risk from 46.7% to 61.9% at n_real=20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAP is compared against SMOTE, TVAE, CTGAN, ARF, SPADA, TabDDPM, and TabDiff, all with the same n_syn=500 budget. TAP consistently outperforms every baseline across all seven datasets and five scarcity levels, with the largest margins at n_real=20 where augmentation is most impactful and most fragile. TabDiff (the strongest generative baseline and TAP's own backbone) is the closest competitor, but TAP's policy-guided inpainting and conservative commitment yield further gains—e.g., on Ailerons at n_real=20, TAP achieves RMSE 0.646 vs. TabDiff's 0.790 (≈18% reduction). TabDDPM is notably unstable on Ailerons under scarcity, producing anomalous RMSE values (108.4 ± 215.6) that reflect training failures. SMOTE, despite its low fidelity, remains competitive on some classification datasets (e.g., Credit-G) but is outperformed by TAP on all regression tasks and most classification settings. The mechanism analysis (Figure 3) further shows that with a shared diffusion backbone, the injection rule dominates: TAP's learned policy + windowed commitment outperforms hard inpainting (which already targets uncertain anchors), which in turn outperforms random inpainting and global sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAP repurposes diffusion inpainting (originally from image completion, Lugmayr et al. 2022) as a manifold-local proposal operator for tabular data, using TabDiff as a frozen backbone, and couples it with a KTO-style preference-based policy update (Ethayarajh et al. 2024) to steer generation toward high-utility regions. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm in tabular generative modeling (CTGAN, TabDDPM, TabDiff) that optimizes distributional fidelity, and from classic augmentation (SMOTE) that ignores learner state, by formalizing augmentation as a sequential control problem where a state-conditioned policy decides what to generate and when to commit. The influence-function diagnostic (Koh & Liang, 2017) motivates the state design but is used only qualitatively, while TabPFN serves as a training-free online evaluator enabling iterative utility estimation without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fidelity-utility gap formalization provides a clean theoretical lens explaining why high-fidelity synthetic data can fail to improve (or even harm) downstream models under scarcity, and the sequential control framing offers a principled path forward that unifies generation, selection, and injection timing. The 15.6pp accuracy gains and 32% RMSE reductions are compelling but concentrated in extreme scarcity (n_real=20–50), and the sequential injection overhead, reliance on TabPFN as evaluator, and testing on seven relatively small datasets limit immediate scalability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger-scale tabular settings, whether more efficient utility estimators can reduce the sequential cost, and whether the policy generalizes across fundamentally different domain constraints.
