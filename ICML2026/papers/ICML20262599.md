## Problem

Training MoE visual autoregressive models from scratch is prohibitively expensive, and existing dense-to-MoE conversion methods (designed for LLMs or diffusion) fail to handle the step-wise activation non-stationarity and irreversible error accumulation inherent to autoregressive image generation. Specifically, standard expert initialization ignores the evolving FFN activation subspaces across generation scales, and uniform load-balancing objectives suppress the expert specialization that visual tokens require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262599/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method converts a pretrained dense visual autoregressive model (VAR-d20 for class-to-image on ImageNet-1K, or LlamaGen-XL for text-to-image on LAION-COCO/MS-COCO) into a sparse MoE variant with 37.5% or 75% active parameters. Initialization requires only a small calibration set (100–300) of dense-model generation trajectories collected without gradient updates, and fine-tuning uses less than 10% of the original training budget. The converted model accepts the same inputs as the dense original (a class label or a text prompt) and produces a generated image, with a 1.22×–1.24× end-to-end latency speedup on a single A100.

## Method

Prism-MoE is a two-stage dense-to-MoE conversion framework. Stage I performs trajectory-consistent expert initialization: it partitions dense FFN neurons into shared and routed experts using a dual-metric criterion (first-order Taylor importance plus activation stability), then applies a training-free constrained ridge regression to align MoE outputs with the dense model's step-wise generation trajectory, with a trust-region norm constraint preventing excessive deviation. Stage II conducts condition-adaptive sparse fine-tuning with three components: (1) layer-wise dense-to-sparse distillation (MSE on hidden states every 4 layers) to preserve intermediate representations, (2) a normalized cosine router with bounded context bias that provides magnitude-invariant expert selection via sigmoid gating and top-k, and (3) a spatially structured routing regularization combining a confidence-weighted KL divergence for local spatial smoothness (stronger for low-confidence tokens) with a batch-level marginal entropy term for global expert diversity, replacing the standard uniform load-balancing loss.

## Results

On VAR-d20 (ImageNet-1K, class-to-image), Prism-MoE achieves FID 5.03 and IS 255 at 75% active parameters without fine-tuning, substantially outperforming all sparse baselines (best competitor Dense2MoE: FID 9.82). After 20 epochs of fine-tuning at 37.5% active parameters, Prism-MoE reaches FID 2.86 and IS 303, surpassing the dense baseline (FID 2.96) while using only 136.2 TFLOPs vs. 240.6 TFLOPs. On LlamaGen-XL-T2I (MS-COCO), Prism-MoE achieves FID 26.0 and CLIP-Score 32.1 at 37.5% activation with fine-tuning, matching or exceeding the dense model (FID 25.8, CLIP 32.0). End-to-end latency shows 1.22× speedup on VAR-d20 and 1.24× on LlamaGen-XL with a fused Triton MoE backend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prism-MoE outperforms all six baselines on both VAR-d20 and LlamaGen-XL. At 75% activation without fine-tuning, the closest competitor is Dense2MoE (FID 9.82 vs. Prism-MoE's 5.03 on VAR-d20; FID 42.8 vs. 27.1 on LlamaGen-XL), while pruning-based methods (Magnitude Prune, Taylor Prune) and Jetpack collapse to FID >75, and CMoE/GMoE remain far behind (FID 22.8/63.7). After fine-tuning at 37.5% activation, Prism-MoE (FID 2.86) beats Dense2MoE (FID 9.03) by a wide margin and also surpasses the dense baseline (FID 2.96), whereas Jetpack degrades catastrophically (FID 163) and the standard load-balancing baseline (FID 10.7) confirms that uniform routing supervision is misaligned with visual autoregressive generation. On LlamaGen-XL-T2I, Prism-MoE at 37.5% activation (FID 26.0, CLIP 32.1) matches the dense model (FID 25.8, CLIP 32.0), while the load-balancing baseline lags at FID 31.5. The consistent gap over Dense2MoE across both backbones and both activation ratios highlights the benefit of trajectory-consistent initialization and confidence-adaptive routing over diffusion-oriented MoE conversion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense-to-MoE conversion paradigm—well established for LLMs (CMoE, GMoE, Sparse Upcycling) and recently applied to diffusion vision models (Dense2MoE, Jetpack, MoLE)—to the underexplored setting of visual autoregressive generation. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that autoregressive visual models exhibit step-wise activation non-stationarity and irreversible error accumulation, making trajectory preservation during expert initialization fundamentally different from the static or time-conditioned settings of diffusion. The method repurposes ridge-regression compensation and cosine-similarity routing (borrowed from LLM MoE and retrieval literature) but wraps them in a trajectory-consistent formulation and spatially structured regularization specifically motivated by the spatial inhomogeneity of visual token entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Prism-MoE provides a practical recipe for converting existing pretrained visual autoregressive models (VAR, LlamaGen) into sparse MoE variants with under 10% additional training cost, enabling 37.5% active parameters at near-dense quality—a meaningful efficiency gain for deployment. The negative finding that standard load-balancing loss actively degrades generation quality (FID 7.05→10.7) challenges a default assumption in MoE training and motivates uncertainty-aware routing objectives. Open questions include whether the trajectory-consistent initialization extends to attention modules or hybrid AR-diffusion architectures, whether more aggressive sparsity (below 37.5%) remains viable, and whether the modest 1.22–1.24× wall-clock speedup improves with larger batch sizes or dedicated MoE hardware.
