## Problem

Garber & Hazan (2015) established an O(1/√ε) upper bound for Frank-Wolfe when both the objective and constraint set are strongly convex, but it remained open whether this rate is tight or whether strong convexity of the set alone could yield uniformly faster convergence. The paper resolves this long-standing question by proving a matching Ω(1/√ε) lower bound, showing that the position of the optimizer (on the boundary vs. interior) genuinely affects FW's worst-case rate and that smoothness of the set cannot improve the guarantee.

## Input / Output

The analysis is conducted on the model problem of minimizing ‖x−p‖² over the Euclidean unit ball with ‖p‖=1 (optimizer on the boundary), where both the function and set have matching parameters μ=L=2, α=β=1. The key structural constraint is that FW iterates remain in a 2D invariant subspace Span{p, x₀}, reducing the dynamics to a 2D recurrence in (residual r_t, contraction factor s_t). The output is a concrete worst-case starting point x₀ and a full T-iteration trajectory certified to have primal gap Ω(1/t²) at every step; constructing this trajectory via backward reconstruction requires ~10⁻³⁰⁰ numerical precision for T=1000 iterations.

## Method

The core technique is a backward-forward trajectory construction that exploits the local invertibility of FW's 2D dynamics. The authors first identify 'stable phases' (where the contraction factor s_t monotonically increases, causing slow convergence) separated by 'jumps' (where s_t drops sharply), then develop Algorithm 1 which initializes at a terminal point (ε, 1−(4/3)ε+2ε²) near the optimum and iterates the backward dynamics G to recover the initial condition. The analytical proof mirrors this construction: by showing inductively that all backward iterates satisfy s_t = 1−(4/3)r_t + c_t r_t² with c_t ∈ [1, 5/2] (via tight polynomial bounds on the auxiliary quantities X and Y in the backward step), they obtain the telescoping inequality 1/r_{t+1} ≥ 1/r_t − 8/3, yielding r_t ≥ r₀/(1+(8/3)r₀t) and hence the Ω(1/t²) primal gap lower bound. This approach sidesteps the high-dimensional search of PEP-based methods and instead leverages problem-specific dynamical structure to synthesize arbitrarily long worst-case sequences.

## Results

The paper establishes a lower bound of T = Ω(√(μD²/ε)) for Frank-Wolfe with exact line search or short steps on smooth and strongly convex sets, proving the O(1/√ε) upper bound of Garber & Hazan (2015) is tight in its dependence on ε. The result is demonstrated on the model problem min_{x∈B₁(0)} ‖x−p‖² with ‖p‖=1 (μ=L=2, α=β=1), where the backward reconstruction algorithm constructs worst-case trajectories over 10,000+ iterations requiring ~10⁻³⁰⁰ numerical precision for 1000 steps. The lower bound holds for any dimension d≥2, any μ>0, and any diameter D>0, and extends to ellipsoids via affine invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's lower bound directly closes the gap left by Garber & Hazan (2015), whose O(1/√ε) upper bound for FW on strongly convex sets was previously the best known guarantee; the new Ω(1/√ε) lower bound proves this rate is tight up to constants. Compared to the general first-order lower bounds of Nemirovski & Yudin (1983), which give only Ω(1/√ε) for convex (not strongly convex) functions and Ω(log(1/ε)) for strongly convex functions over general convex sets, this result is the first to establish an Ω(1/√ε) lower bound specifically for strongly convex sets, matching the upper bound in the SC+SC regime. Unlike Jaggi's (2013) Ω(1/ε) lower bound, which relies on polytopal constraints and the high-dimensional regime t<d, the present bound holds for smooth strongly convex sets (e.g., ellipsoids) in any dimension d≥2. The concurrent work of Grimmer & Liu (2026) also establishes Ω(1/√ε) for LMO methods over strongly convex sets but is restricted to the high-dimensional regime T<d and to modestly smooth sets (L=Θ(1/√ε)), whereas the present result applies to any dimension and fully smooth sets. The PEP-based empirical estimates of Luner & Grimmer (2024) suggested a scaling around O(1/ε^(1/1.44)) at accessible horizons, but the backward reconstruction approach in this paper reveals the true O(1/√ε) scaling at much longer horizons (10,000+ iterations) where PEP's semidefinite programs become intractable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the lower-bound program for first-order methods (Jaggi 2013, Lan 2013, Nemirovski & Yudin 1983) to the strongly convex set regime, where prior lower bounds were built on polytopal constraints and did not apply. It closes the gap left by Garber & Hazan (2015), whose O(1/√ε) upper bound for FW on strongly convex sets with strongly convex functions had no matching lower bound. Methodologically, it departs from the PEP framework (Taylor et al. 2017; Luner & Grimmer 2024) by exploiting the two-dimensional invariant subspace structure of a specific model problem (quadratic over the Euclidean ball) to construct worst-case trajectories via a backward-reconstruction procedure, achieving horizons (10,000+ iterations) far beyond what PEP's semidefinite programs can handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a long-standing open question by proving the O(1/√ε) rate is tight, establishing that the optimizer's position (boundary vs. interior) is a genuine determinant of FW's convergence rate rather than an artifact of prior analyses. It also rules out the possibility that smoothness of the constraint set can improve the rate for strongly convex objectives. Open questions include: whether FW variants tailored to strongly convex sets (away-step, pairwise, fully-corrective) can circumvent the bound; the remaining gap for convex (non-strongly-convex) functions between O(1/ε) upper and Ω(1/√ε) lower bounds; and a smoothed complexity analysis, since the worst-case trajectory is fragile to perturbations in initialization or step size.
