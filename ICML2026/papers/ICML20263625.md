## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods effectively remove targeted knowledge and preserve retain-set utility, but they consistently degrade a critical and previously unmeasured capability: the model's ability to correctly use forgotten information when it is explicitly provided as contextual input at inference time (e.g., in RAG or user-document settings). The paper identifies this 'contextual utility' gap through a new Contextual QA evaluation and shows that all six state-of-the-art unlearning methods reduce contextual performance by 13–100% relative to the pre-unlearning baseline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained instruction-tuned LLM, a forget set of QA pairs (fictitious author profiles from TOFU or relational contracts from PISTOL), a retain set, and a contextual evidence set S_f^ctx where each forget question is paired with its ground-truth answer as explicit context. A frozen copy of the original model is also required to compute the KL reference distribution on contextual inputs. The output is an updated LLM that fails Direct QA on the forget set (no parametric recall), succeeds at Contextual QA when the same knowledge is supplied in the prompt, and maintains utility on the retain set. The contextual evidence is consumed as a RAG-style prompt template prepended to the question, and the KL term operates on token-level predictive distributions over the answer given (question, context).

## Method

The paper augments the standard two-term unlearning objective (forget + retain) with a third plug-in context term: λ_c · C(S_f^ctx, w), where C is the mean KL divergence between the unlearned model's predictive distribution p_w(·|q,c) and the frozen original model's distribution p_orig(·|q,c) over contextual inputs. The key insight is that existing forget terms (gradient ascent, preference flipping, activation misdirection) reshape internal representations or output probabilities in ways that suppress not only direct recall but also the model's ability to ground on the same tokens when they reappear as external context; the KL term explicitly counteracts this by anchoring contextual predictions to the pre-unlearning model, enforcing 'do not recall from memory' while preserving 'do use when provided.' The formulation is modular and integrates into any unlearning algorithm (NPO, RMU, UNDIAL, DPO, GradDiff, PDU, SimNPO) with a single added loss term. The reference model's outputs on contextual examples can be precomputed and cached, so the extra training cost is minimal. The hyperparameter λ_c is found to be largely insensitive across a wide range, making the approach practical and easy to deploy.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark (5% forget set), context-aware unlearning restores Contextual QA LLM-Judge scores to near-perfect levels: RMU improves from 0.00→0.99 (Gemma-2B-IT) and 0.05→0.97 (Qwen3-8B), NPO from 0.81→0.98 and 0.84→0.95, and UNDIAL from 0.82→0.98 and 0.97→0.98. Forgetting effectiveness is preserved with only ~2 pp change in Direct QA LLM-Judge and ~4 pp in ROUGE-L, while model utility shifts by at most −0.01. On the PISTOL dataset (structurally entangled entities), the same trend holds: context-aware variants boost Contextual QA LLM-Judge by +0.05 to +0.80 over vanilla methods while maintaining comparable Direct QA scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's context-aware objective is a plug-in augmentation applied to existing unlearning methods, so the primary comparison is each method's vanilla vs. context-aware variant. RMU shows the most dramatic improvement: vanilla RMU collapses Contextual QA to 0.00–0.05 LLM-Judge, while context-aware RMU reaches 0.97–0.99, a gain of +0.92 to +0.99. NPO and UNDIAL show more moderate but consistent gains (+0.11 to +0.17 LLM-Judge on Contextual QA), with Direct QA forgetting and utility remaining within 1–2 pp of vanilla. Among the additional baselines evaluated on Gemma-2B-IT (Table 9), GradDiff and PDU also collapse to near-zero Contextual QA in vanilla form (0.000 LLM-Judge) and recover to 0.945 and 0.975 respectively with the context-aware term. DPO improves from 0.555 to 0.855 Contextual QA LLM-Judge, and SimNPO (already strong at 0.940) reaches 0.970. Across all seven baselines and both models, the context-aware variant never degrades Direct QA forgetting or model utility by more than ~4 pp, confirming that the KL context term selectively repairs contextual utility without side effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM unlearning literature (NPO, RMU, UNDIAL, gradient ascent/difference methods) by identifying a previously overlooked evaluation dimension—contextual utility—and proposing a plug-in KL-divergence term to preserve it. The KL context term is directly inspired by the KL-regularization design principle from RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) and related alignment objectives, repurposed here to anchor the unlearned model's contextual responses to the pre-unlearning model. Unlike prior work that evaluated unlearning solely through Direct QA (forgetting) and retain-set utility, this paper introduces Contextual QA as a third axis, positioning the contribution as both an empirical audit of existing methods and a minimal objective augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs are increasingly deployed in RAG and user-document settings where forgotten content is legitimately re-supplied at inference time, the finding that standard unlearning can collapse contextual utility to near zero has direct practical implications for compliance-aware deployments. The plug-in KL term is simple, insensitive to its hyperparameter, and integrates into any existing unlearning objective, making it immediately adoptable. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to models up to 8B parameters and benchmarks (TOFU, PISTOL) with relatively short, clean contexts; open questions include whether the effect persists at scale, in multi-turn or long-context RAG settings, and whether the KL anchor is sufficient when contexts are heavily noisy or adversarially conflicting.
