## Problem

Object-centric world models provide useful entity abstractions but are not sufficient to capture interaction-dependent dynamics; without explicit mechanisms, predictors fall back on object self-dynamics or exploit incidental correlations. The open question is how to make interaction reasoning functionally necessary through the learning objective itself, rather than relying on architectural factorization, graph structures, or task-specific supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames (480×320 for CLEVRER, 224×224 for Push-T) that are encoded by a frozen DINOv2 backbone plus Slot Attention into a fixed set of object-centric slots (7 for CLEVRER, 4 for Push-T), with optional auxiliary tokens for actions and proprioception treated as separate entities. The predictor outputs predicted future object states in a low-dimensional latent space (128-dim per slot), which are then used for ALOE-based VQA on imagined 160-frame rollouts or for CEM-based model predictive control against a goal observation. At inference, no masking is applied—the model performs standard forward prediction from a fully observed history.

## Method

C-JEPA introduces object-level masking as a latent intervention on predictor observability: during training, selected object slots are replaced by mask tokens (constructed from a linear projection of the earliest-timestep identity anchor plus a learnable temporal embedding) across the entire history window, while the remaining objects and auxiliary variables remain visible. A ViT-style bidirectional masked transformer predictor then recovers all masked tokens over both the history and future horizons under a joint embedding prediction (JEPA) loss with no reconstruction, making interaction-dependent context the only viable source of information for minimizing the objective. The paper formalizes this as inducing 'influence neighborhoods'—minimal sufficient sets of contextual variables under masked completion—and proves that any predictor ignoring these variables incurs strictly higher expected loss, providing a theoretical guarantee that the masking objective enforces interaction reasoning. Compared to patch-based masked prediction, object-level masking operates over a much smaller token set (N slots vs. 196 patches), yielding over 8× faster MPC planning while matching patch-based world model performance.

## Results

On CLEVRER VQA, C-JEPA achieves 83.88% average accuracy and 60.19% counterfactual accuracy (per question), outperforming OCVP-Seq (83.11% / 56.06%) and SlotFormer (79.44% / 47.29%) without any reconstruction loss. The key ablation (OC-JEPA → C-JEPA) shows a ~19 percentage-point absolute gain on counterfactual reasoning (41.10% → 60.19%) from object-level masking alone. On Push-T MPC, C-JEPA reaches 88.67% success rate using only 6×128 latent tokens (1.02% of DINO-WM's 196×384), achieving comparable performance to DINO-WM (91.33%) while enabling over 8× faster planning (673 s vs. 5,763 s for 50 trajectories).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SlotFormer (Wu et al., 2023), C-JEPA wins decisively: 83.88% vs. 79.44% average accuracy and 60.19% vs. 47.29% counterfactual accuracy, and the gap widens dramatically when reconstruction is removed (SlotFormer drops to 44.94% avg / 11.10% counterfactual, while C-JEPA is unaffected since it never uses reconstruction). Against OCVP-Seq (Villar-Corrales et al., 2023), C-JEPA edges out the reconstruction-free variant (83.88% vs. 80.09% avg; 60.19% vs. 43.00% counterfactual per question) and also beats the full reconstruction-based version on counterfactual accuracy (60.19% vs. 56.06%). On Push-T MPC, C-JEPA (88.67%) nearly matches DINO-WM (91.33%) and DINO-WM-Reg (88.00%) while using only 1.02% of the latent token budget (6×128 vs. 196×384), translating to >8× faster planning. The internal ablation OC-JEPA → C-JEPA (76.00% → 88.67% on Push-T; 77.28% → 83.88% on CLEVRER) confirms that the gains come from the object-level masking objective rather than from the object-centric representation or JEPA predictor alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-JEPA sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: JEPA-style latent prediction (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA2) and object-centric world modeling (SlotFormer, OCVP-Seq, SPARTAN, C-SWM). Prior object-centric world models encourage interaction learning through architectural factorization of self-dynamics from interactions, sparse attention regularization, or fixed relational graphs, while patch-based masked prediction (MAE, VideoMAE, DINO-WM) optimizes local pixel correlations without object-level structure. C-JEPA departs from both by applying object-level masking as a latent intervention on predictor observability, making interaction-dependent prediction functionally necessary through the learning objective itself rather than through architectural constraints, and is the first work to integrate JEPA with object-centric representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple masking strategy can serve as a principled causal inductive bias for world models, yielding a ~20% absolute gain on counterfactual VQA and enabling 8× faster MPC planning with only 1% of the latent tokens required by patch-based models. However, performance is bounded by the quality of the frozen object-centric encoder, and the theoretical analysis (influence neighborhoods) is not validated on datasets with explicit temporal causal graphs. Open questions include whether jointly refining encoders with the masking objective can raise the performance ceiling, and whether the approach generalizes to richer, less structured physical environments beyond CLEVRER and Push-T.
