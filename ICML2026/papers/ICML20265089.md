## Problem

Pretrained diffusion policies for robotic manipulation achieve strong task performance but frequently violate physical safety constraints (collision, force limits, kinematic feasibility), and existing remedies either restrict the policy during training or require privileged state access and external guardrails at test time. No principled post-training framework exists that can project a pretrained diffusion policy onto a constraint-feasible region without access to demonstration data, task rewards, or deployment-time safety modules.

## Input / Output

PACT takes a frozen pretrained diffusion policy (DP, DP3, RDT-1B, or π0.5, accepting RGB images or point clouds) together with a set of differentiable safety cost functions c_k(s,a) and fixed Lagrange multipliers λ_k. During post-training it consumes only self-generated rollouts from the current student policy (no external demonstrations or reward signals), and the safety costs require privileged environment states (e.g., object poses) obtained via simulation APIs or off-the-shelf 3D perception pipelines. The output is an aligned diffusion policy ε_θ that, at inference time, produces safe manipulation action sequences using only the same sensory inputs as the base policy—no constraint evaluation or guardrail module is needed at test time.

## Method

PACT formulates physical safety alignment as a KL-regularized projection of the base policy μ_φ onto the CMDP feasible set, yielding a closed-form Boltzmann-tilted optimal policy whose score function decomposes into the base denoiser plus differentiable cost gradients (the 'implicit safety teacher'). A student diffusion policy ε_θ is then trained to match this teacher via dense score-matching across all diffusion timesteps on self-collected rollouts, avoiding the intractable likelihood evaluation that blocks direct policy-gradient optimization. To prevent irreversible out-of-distribution collapse, a curriculum schedule η_i progressively scales the constraint multipliers, and Theorem 3.3 proves that each update incurs bounded KL policy shift (≤ Δη_i·Δℓ) and monotonic safety improvement. A training-free Taylor approximation evaluates cost gradients at the one-step posterior mean a_{0|t} and restricts constraint injection to the final few denoising steps (t < t_c ≈ 0.03), reducing Jacobian computation while preserving alignment quality.

## Results

On the RoboTwin bimanual manipulation benchmark, PACT achieves the highest Success Rate and Safe Rate across all four evaluated tasks (Pick Dual Bottles, Handover Apple, Pour Water, Stack Blocks), reaching 96%/89%, 81%/82%, 86%/64%, and 80%/48% respectively. Across all base policies (DP, DP3, RDT-1B, π0.5), post-training with PACT reduces safety violations by 31.0% on average while improving task success by 30.7%. In real-world evaluation, PACT improves the physical Safe Rate by 36.0% and average task progress by 36.8%, with the largest gain on the millimeter-precision GPU Assembly task. PACT also converges more than 5× faster than RL-based baselines due to dense per-timestep constraint supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACT outperforms all on-policy baselines on every task and metric. Against PLD_online (Xiao et al., 2026), the strongest IL baseline, PACT gains +3% success and +23% safety on Pick Dual Bottles (96%/89% vs 93%/66%), and +31% safety on Pour Water (64% vs 29%). Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), PACT achieves substantially larger gains, e.g., +26% success and +33% safety on Pour Water (86%/64% vs 60%/31%), while PPO shows only marginal improvement over the base policy. Against DIPO (Yang et al., 2023), PACT leads by +19% success and +46% safety on Pick Dual Bottles (96%/89% vs 77%/43%), and DIPO actually degrades success on Stack Blocks (66% vs 63% base). Against the off-policy RL baseline iDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023), PACT wins on all four tasks, with the largest gap on Pour Water (+24% success, +36% safety: 86%/64% vs 62%/28%). The paper also notes that several on-policy alternatives (AWR, RFT, QSM) collapse to near-zero success, and RL baselines require additional stabilization mechanisms, whereas PACT trains stably without such interventions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACT draws a direct analogy to post-training safety alignment in LLMs (RLHF, DPO) but transplants it to physical safety for diffusion-based manipulation policies, a setting where prior work has enforced constraints either at design-time (safe RL, constrained imitation learning) or reactively at test-time (safety filters, control barrier functions). The core algorithmic substrate builds on energy-guided diffusion sampling (Lu et al., 2023) for intermediate cost gradients and the Boltzmann-tilted closed-form optimal policy from KL-regularized CMDP optimization (Peng et al., 2019; Schulman et al., 2017), but repurposes these as a distillation target rather than a sampling-time correction. The key departure is the self-evolving, on-policy curriculum distillation loop that operates without demonstrations or task rewards, distinguishing it from both off-policy imitation baselines (PLD, guided rollouts) and on-policy RL baselines (PPO, DIPO, AWR) that the paper benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PACT offers a practical, drop-in post-training recipe for making pretrained diffusion manipulation policies physically safe without retraining from scratch, collecting new demonstrations, or deploying test-time guardrails — a significant reduction in engineering overhead for safety-critical deployment. The simultaneous improvement in both safety (−31% violations) and task success (+30.7%) across four heterogeneous base policies (DP, DP3, RDT-1B, π0.5) and real-world GPU assembly is compelling evidence that the safety-performance trade-off can be resolved rather than merely managed. Open questions include whether the framework extends to high-frequency force-sensitive contact dynamics (beyond the geometric/kinematic constraints tested), how alignment quality degrades under severe perceptual occlusion, and whether the iterative rollout requirement scales to large robot fleets where per-agent compute is a bottleneck.
