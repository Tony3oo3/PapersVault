## Problem

Existing dynamic agentic RAG systems suffer from a strategic-operational mismatch: decoupled training optimizes a Planner against frozen, black-box Executors, so sophisticated planning strategies fail to materialize in practice. No prior framework jointly optimizes both planning and execution within dynamic, multi-turn workflows, leaving a gap between the flexibility of adaptive orchestration and the convergence stability of end-to-end learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query and retrieves from an English Wikipedia corpus indexed via E5 dense embeddings. Internally, the query is decomposed into a structured execution trace of sub-query/answer nodes that expands and resolves over multiple reasoning rounds, with each round invoking a dynamically generated workflow graph. The final output is a synthesized text answer produced by an Answer Summarizer that reads the fully resolved trace, with performance measured by F1 score against ground-truth answers.

## Method

JADE formulates dynamic agentic RAG as a cooperative multi-agent Semi-Markov Decision Process where a single LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) serves as the shared policy for all eight roles (Planner, QDS, QDP, QR, RA, DS, AG, AS), distinguished only by role-specific system prompts. At each round, the Planner generates a dynamic workflow graph (e.g., decomposition chains or solve sequences), and the activated Executors execute it to update the global execution trace. Training uses PPO with a Unified Experience Buffer that flattens heterogeneous transitions from all roles into a single replay pool, so one gradient step simultaneously updates planning and execution capabilities. The reward combines a global shared signal (F1 score minus weighted penalties for reasoning turns and retrieval calls) propagated backward via GAE, plus a local per-step format penalty, enabling the Planner to learn executor capability boundaries while Executors co-adapt to strategic intent.

## Results

JADE achieves a new state-of-the-art average F1 score of 53.86 across seven open-domain QA benchmarks, outperforming the best prior baseline (MMOA-RAG, 45.57) by +8.29 points. On multi-hop QA tasks, the gains are particularly pronounced: JADE reaches 49.60 average F1 versus 40.71 for the best agentic baseline (MAO-ARAG), with standout improvements of +9.91 on 2Wiki-MultiHopQA and +7.81 on HotpotQA. Notably, JADE's jointly optimized 7B model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) surpasses a GPT-4o-based decoupled system (56.82) by 1.42 points in the ablation setting, demonstrating that co-adaptation outweighs raw model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JADE outperforms all six baselines on the overall average F1. Against the closest static-workflow competitor MMOA-RAG (45.57), JADE gains +8.29, with the gap widening to +9.91 on 2Wiki-MultiHopQA, confirming that dynamic topology is essential for multi-hop reasoning. The most striking comparison is against MAO-ARAG (41.22), which shares the same Planner-Executor architecture but freezes executors; JADE's +12.64 advantage directly validates the paper's central claim that joint optimization resolves the strategic-operational mismatch. Against the monolithic Search-R1 (43.91), JADE leads by +9.95, attributed to functional specialization reducing the per-step cognitive load. JADE also surpasses RRR (+9.14) and BGM (+9.68), both of which optimize only a single module in a fixed pipeline. The only marginal loss is on PopQA (−0.26 vs. MMOA-RAG), suggesting that for straightforward single-hop factual retrieval, the added planning overhead provides negligible benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

JADE synthesizes two previously separate lines of work: the joint multi-agent RL optimization of static modular RAG (MMOA-RAG) and the dynamic Planner-Executor orchestration of adaptive agentic search (MAO-ARAG, S3, AI-SEARCHPLANNER). It departs from both by making the executors jointly trainable within a dynamic, multi-turn workflow, and from monolithic end-to-end approaches (Search-R1) by retaining modular functional specialization as a structural scaffold. The formulation as a cooperative multi-agent game with parameter sharing draws directly from classical MARL (QMIX, MAPPO), repurposing shared-backbone and global-reward mechanisms for LLM-based agentic RAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated framework for practitioners building agentic RAG systems, demonstrating that joint co-adaptation of planner and executors is more effective than either freezing executors or scaling their individual capability. The finding that a 7B jointly-optimized team outperforms GPT-4o-based decoupled executors challenges the assumption that raw model intelligence of individual components drives system performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones or more complex tool sets, how sensitive the system is to the degeneration risk identified under joint cost penalties, and whether the cooperative MARL formulation generalizes beyond open-domain QA to broader agentic workflows.
