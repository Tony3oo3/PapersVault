## Problem

Existing world models for robotic manipulation either generate purely 2D video or reason over partial 3D geometry, leaving a gap between imagined futures and executable actions due to incomplete, view-inconsistent geometry. Additionally, converting predicted futures into actions via inverse dynamics is inherently ill-posed because many actions can explain the same perceptual transition, especially under partial observability and contact.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264865/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-view RGBD observation (RGB image at 320×240 plus a depth map), a text instruction, and target camera extrinsics for N views as input. It produces synchronized multi-view RGBD video sequences in latent space (decoded via a 3D VAE), which can be back-projected and fused into 3D point-cloud trajectories, and a robot action trajectory recovered through test-time optimization of a compact TCN-VAE latent followed by residual inverse dynamics refinement. The input tokenization uses width-wise concatenation for RGB-D within each view and height-wise concatenation across views, enabling a variable number of views by simply appending additional streams.

## Method

Built on the WAN2.2 TI2V 5B DiT backbone with flow matching, the method introduces three key architectural components: (1) a local cross-modality attention module with learnable modality tokens that exchanges RGB and depth features within a small spatial window (O(Nk) cost) to enforce appearance-geometry alignment; (2) a geometry-aware deformable cross-view attention that sparsely samples K candidates along epipolar lines in other views and refines positions with learned offsets, using a 13-D spherical camera embedding (yaw, pitch, roll, log-radius with Fourier features) as the view token; and (3) a trajectory-level style code produced by a TCN-VAE that compresses the full action sequence into 32 latent tokens injected via cross-attention. At inference, the model first generates a text-only 4D rollout, then recovers actions by backpropagating through the frozen generator to optimize the trajectory latent so that trajectory-conditioned generation matches the fixed rollout, and finally refines the decoded action sequence with a PointNet-based residual inverse dynamics module that predicts only a small correction Δa_t anchored to the trajectory prior.

## Results

On manipulation success rate, MVISTA-4D achieves 72.6% on RLBench and 43.0% on RoboTwin, outperforming the strongest baseline TesserAct (67.3% and 33.9%) and 4DGen (47.0% and 40.2%). For 4D scene generation, the method attains the best FVD (18.57 on RLBench, 21.93 on RoboTwin, 23.08 on real-world), the lowest Chamfer Distance (9.6, 6.51, 13.06), and the highest depth accuracy (δ₁ = 97.1%, 97.4%, 82.18%) across all three datasets. On the real-robot platform, it surpasses TesserAct on 5 out of 6 tasks (e.g., Open Drawer 56% vs. 37%, Arrange Boxes 15% vs. 7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TesserAct (single-view RGB-DN generation + point-based IDM), MVISTA-4D wins on manipulation success rate by 5.3 points on RLBench (72.6% vs. 67.3%) and 9.1 points on RoboTwin (43.0% vs. 33.9%), and dominates all depth and point-cloud metrics on every dataset (e.g., CD 9.6 vs. 11.0 on RLBench, EMD 9.90 vs. 10.28 on RoboTwin). Against 4DGen (two-view DUSt3R-style pointmaps + FoundationPose), the gap is larger: 25.6 points on RLBench manipulation (72.6% vs. 47.0%) and 2.8 points on RoboTwin (43.0% vs. 40.2%), with substantially better FVD and geometry metrics. Against UniPi* (2D video + Depth Anything 3 + image-based IDM), MVISTA-4D leads by 38.0 points on RLBench manipulation (72.6% vs. 34.6%) and 26.7 points on RoboTwin (43.0% vs. 16.3%), while also achieving far superior depth accuracy (AbRel 90.5 vs. 117.9 on RLBench). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is PSNR on RLBench (UniPi* 23.88, TesserAct 23.86 vs. Ours 23.31), but this is offset by the method's clear dominance in geometric consistency and downstream task success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the imagine-then-act world-model paradigm for manipulation from single-view or 2D generation (UniPi, TesserAct) and two-view DUSt3R-style pointmap generation (4DGen) to feed-forward, multi-view, view-consistent RGB-D 4D generation built on a WAN2.2 flow-matching DiT backbone. It departs from prior inverse-dynamics-based action inference by introducing a TCN-VAE trajectory latent that is optimized via backpropagation at test time, combined with a residual IDM that only predicts corrections. The cross-view and cross-modality fusion design draws on 4DNeX's tokenization strategy and epipolar geometry, but adds deformable refinement and gated local attention to enforce geometric consistency without per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical geometric interface between imagined futures and executable robot actions, addressing a key gap where pixel-plausible predictions violate 3D constraints. The newly collected real-robot 4D multiview dataset (14 tasks, 4 RGB-D cameras) fills a notable data gap for evaluating multi-view manipulation world models. Open questions include reducing test-time optimization latency for real-time deployment, handling contact-rich scenarios where residual IDM still struggles, and whether the approach scales to longer horizons or more complex multi-object interactions without additional calibration assumptions.
