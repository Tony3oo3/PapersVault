## Problem

Current VLA models trained via imitation learning lack reliable, generalizable task progress feedback, making it difficult for robots to self-evaluate and improve in unseen real-world environments. Existing reward approaches either require hand-crafted task-specific shaping, rely on single-point progress estimates that generalize poorly, or need additional task-dependent data for termination classifiers. No prior work unifies dense, generalizable progress understanding with action generation in a single model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of RGB images sampled from a trajectory at arbitrary temporal offsets, a text task description, the current robot arm state, and optionally a reference trajectory for in-context learning. Actions are output as semantic strings of delta EEF pose numbers (e.g., 'x: -47mm, y: 19mm, z: 66mm, roll: 14 degrees, pitch: 10 degrees, yaw: 15 degrees'), leveraging the pretrained VLM's token space rather than continuous vectors. The critic outputs a scalar delta progress value (positive for advancement, negative for regression), a binary task-completion label, and a generated task description, all produced autoregressively from the same backbone.

## Method

VLAC is built on InternVL2 (8B or 2B) and jointly trains action generation and pair-wise progress understanding within a single autoregressive architecture, distinguished only by input prompts. The key mechanism is the pair-wise progress formulation: given two frames o_i and o_{i+Δt}, the model predicts the relative progress delta, with training labels automatically set to Δt/(T−i) from temporal ordering—this naturally generates balanced negative samples without manual annotation and is agnostic to data collection strategy or segment starting points. Four data construction strategies (pixel-difference filtering, joint forward/reverse sampling, completion-judgment pairing, and cross-trajectory description sampling) further improve robustness. The model is trained on ~40M samples spanning Ego4D human video, five robot manipulation datasets, and general VQA data, then deployed with PPO where adjacent-frame progress differences provide dense intrinsic rewards, lifting real-world success rates from ~30% to ~90% within 200 episodes.

## Results

On action generation across five real-world manipulation tasks, VLAC achieves 82.5% average Task Progress and 75% average Success Rate, substantially outperforming π0 (49.2% TP, 27% SR) and VLAC without progress pretraining (21.7% TP, 16% SR). For task progress understanding (critic), VLAC-8B reaches VOC-F1 of 0.95 on RT-1 (one-shot) and 1.00 on Bridge (one-shot), while clearly discriminating successful from failed trajectories on RoboFAC (0.89 vs. 0.44 VOC-F1). In real-world RL, VLAC as a dense reward model lifts task success rate from ~30% to ~90% within 200 interaction episodes using PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GVL (Ma et al., 2024, Gemini-1.5-Pro based), VLAC-8B achieves substantially higher VOC scores across nearly all eight evaluation datasets: e.g., 0.97 vs. 0.74 on Bridge, 0.92 vs. 0.51 on DROID, and 0.92 vs. −0.01 on RoboNet (zero-shot), with one-shot in-context learning further boosting scores (e.g., 0.95 on RT-1). GVL is not evaluated on EgoDex, RH20T, or RoboFAC, where VLAC still achieves competitive scores (0.05–0.76 VOC zero-shot). Against π0 (Black et al., 2024), VLAC wins decisively on action generation: 82.5% average Task Progress and 75% Success Rate versus π0's 49.2% and 27%, with the gap widening under lighting and scene transfer (VLAC retains 69.5%/57% and 73.6%/63% respectively, while π0 drops to 16.7%/3% under lighting). The ablation VLAC w/o pretrain (21.7% TP, 16% SR) confirms that pair-wise progress understanding is the key driver of the action generation gains over π0.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLAC sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: embodied reward/progress models (GVL, LIV, contrastive value pre-training) and Vision-Language-Action policies (RT-1/2, π0, OpenVLA). It departs from prior progress estimators by replacing single-point absolute progress prediction with a pair-wise delta formulation that auto-generates balanced negative samples from temporal ordering, and it departs from prior VLA models by jointly training the critic (progress, completion, description) and actor (delta-EEF action) within one autoregressive backbone rather than treating them as separate modules. The in-context reference-trajectory mechanism extends the one-shot value-learning idea from GVL into a structured multi-frame conditioning scheme, and the multi-source mixture (Ego4D human video + DROID/Bridge/AGIBOT robot data + general VQA) follows the data-scaling recipe of recent foundation-model efforts but is the first to combine action-free human video with action-labeled robot trajectories under a shared progress objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a drop-in dense-reward signal for real-world RL that removes the need for hand-crafted, task-specific reward shaping, and the ~30%→90% success-rate lift in 200 on-robot episodes demonstrates that a learned generalist critic can bootstrap autonomous improvement without per-task engineering. Key limitations temper the claim: the monotonic-progress assumption breaks on trajectories with prolonged retries or cyclic subtasks, VOC remains tied to chronological ordering rather than semantic milestones, and all action-generation and RL results are on a single 7-DOF arm (AGILE PiPER) across five kitchen tasks, leaving open whether the actor-critic synergy transfers to dexterous hands, multi-arm, or mobile platforms. Open questions include non-monotonic progress annotation, evaluation metrics decoupled from temporal order, and whether the pair-wise formulation scales to long-horizon, multi-stage tasks where local deltas become uninformative.
