## Problem

Static-codebook vector quantization methods assume homogeneous local geometry across the embedding space, limiting expressiveness on heterogeneous data. While dynamic-codebook quantizers like QINCo adapt codebooks per input, their strict sequential dependencies between steps create a decoding bottleneck that hinders practical deployment. The paper asks whether input-dependent codebook adaptation can be achieved without coupling the conditioning signal to the reconstruction path, thereby enabling parallel decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional vector x ∈ R^D (e.g., 96–768 dimensions) and produces a sequence of M discrete indices, each drawn from a codebook of K=256 entries, at a fixed bit budget of 8 or 16 bytes. Unusually, each codebook entry is hyper-dimensional (D+D_e), partitioned into a quantization component for residual matching and an expert component for manifold conditioning; the same index serves both roles, so routing incurs zero additional bit overhead. Decoding reconstructs x̂ by summing the M selected dynamic codewords, and the architecture supports early-termination rate adjustment by simply cropping the index sequence.

## Method

RQ-MoE combines a two-level Mixture-of-Experts with a dual-stream quantization process. The first-level MoE uses implicit routing: each codebook entry is a (D+D_e)-dimensional vector [c_k; e_k], and the nearest-neighbor index selection simultaneously determines the quantized codeword and activates the corresponding expert vector, eliminating explicit gating overhead. The instruction stream accumulates expert signals via simple addition (I^m = I^{m-1} + e_{i^{m-1}}^{m-1}), fully decoupled from reconstruction, while the quantization stream uses a second-level MoE (N experts with softmax gating) to deform base codewords into input-specific dynamic codebooks conditioned on I^m. A Normalized Residual Loss, L = Σ log(1 + ‖r^{m+1}‖²/‖r^m‖²), replaces per-step MSE to provide a redescending influence function that stabilizes deep-layer training. Because instruction vectors depend only on pre-stored expert components and discrete indices, all M dynamic codebooks can be constructed in parallel after a fast lookup pass, yielding 6×–14× faster decoding than QINCo.

## Results

On four large-scale benchmarks (Deep1M, BigANN1M, FB-ssnpp1M, Contriever1M) with 10M training vectors, RQ-MoE achieves the best or tied-best MSE and Recall@k across all datasets at both 8-byte and 16-byte budgets. For example, at 8 bytes on Contriever1M (D=768), RQ-MoE reaches MSE 1.38 and R@1 21.6%, outperforming QINCo (MSE 1.42, R@1 20.4%); at 16 bytes on BigANN1M, it achieves MSE 0.30 and R@1 72.3% versus QINCo's 0.33 and 71.6%. In addition to accuracy, RQ-MoE delivers 6×–14× faster decoding latency than QINCo (e.g., 0.5 µs vs. 3.3 µs at M=8, and 0.7 µs vs. 10.4 µs at M=16 on A800 GPUs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against static-codebook baselines (OPQ, RQ, LSQ), RQ-MoE achieves substantial improvements: at 8 bytes on BigANN1M, RQ-MoE's MSE of 1.10 is 2.7× lower than OPQ (2.97) and 2.3× lower than RQ (2.49), with R@1 of 46.2% vs. 21.4% (OPQ) and 27.8% (RQ). Against the neural quantizer UNQ, RQ-MoE improves MSE from 1.12 to 1.10 and R@1 from 39.7% to 46.2% on BigANN1M at 8 bytes (UNQ is not evaluated on FB-ssnpp or Contriever). Against the primary dynamic-codebook baseline QINCo, RQ-MoE consistently wins or ties on every metric across all four datasets and both budgets, with the largest gains on Contriever1M (e.g., R@1 21.6% vs. 20.4% at 8 bytes; R@100 87.2% vs. 85.4% at 16 bytes) and BigANN1M (MSE 0.30 vs. 0.33 at 16 bytes). Against the most recent QINCo2 (Table 8, 500K training, 8 bytes), RQ-MoE achieves lower MSE on all four datasets both with and without beam search (e.g., 1.29 vs. 1.40 on BigANN1M without beam search; 1.10 vs. 1.13 with beam search). Crucially, RQ-MoE also provides 6×–14× faster decoding than QINCo (0.5 µs vs. 3.3 µs at M=8; 0.7 µs vs. 10.4 µs at M=16), a dimension of comparison where all other baselines are either slower or not directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

RQ-MoE sits at the intersection of three research threads: residual quantization (RQ) for iterative coarse-to-fine vector compression, dynamic-codebook quantization (QINCo) that conditions codebooks on partial reconstruction, and Mixture of Experts for conditional computation. The key architectural departure from QINCo is the dual-stream decoupling: rather than coupling the conditioning signal to the cumulative reconstruction (as QINCo does), RQ-MoE maintains a separate instruction stream built from hyper-dimensional codebook entries, enabling parallel decoding. The paper further unifies the landscape theoretically by proving that both standard RQ (memoryless policy) and QINCo (coupled-state policy) are constrained special cases of the RQ-MoE framework, positioning it as a generalization rather than an isolated variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6×–14× decoding speedup is practically significant for latency-sensitive retrieval systems where decoding throughput is a bottleneck, and the dynamic-rate property (a single M=16 model serves all shorter bit-rates without retraining) simplifies production deployment. However, encoding remains sequential as in QINCo, and the main experimental comparison uses N=1 (effectively no MoE experts), meaning the full two-level MoE benefit is demonstrated only in supplementary experiments. Open questions include whether the decoupling generalizes to even higher dimensions or multimodal settings, and how RQ-MoE interacts with QINCo2's candidate pre-selection and beam search techniques, which the authors note are orthogonal but do not fully explore.
