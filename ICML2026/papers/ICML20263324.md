## Problem

Existing video anomaly understanding methods are passive and open-loop: they fix a static frame sampling protocol before inference, so sparse, context-dependent anomaly cues can be missed or aliased with visually similar normal events. Under the practical weak-supervision regime (video-level labels only), there is no mechanism to learn when to acquire additional evidence, when to revise a hypothesis, or when to rationally stop, leaving the model unable to disambiguate uncertain cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263324/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an untrimmed video subsampled at one frame per 16 frames, partitioned into overlapping clips of 16 frames with stride 8 (50% overlap). At inference, each clip episode starts with 16 frames and the agent may request up to 16 additional frames on demand via tool calls. Outputs per clip include up to three structured hypotheses (anomaly category code, 1-based temporal span within the 16-frame window, confidence in [0,1]) plus a one-sentence explanation; frame-level scores are derived by assigning 1 inside predicted anomalous ranges, 0.5 to remaining frames in anomalous clips, 0 to normal clips, then averaging across overlapping clips.

## Method

Anom-π reframes video anomaly understanding as a closed-loop sequential decision process over a unified action space {THINK, BACKTRACK, EXPAND, SAMPLE, FINAL}, where the agent alternates epistemic deliberation (THINK updates a structured hypothesis state) with active evidence acquisition (BACKTRACK zooms into local frames, EXPAND retrieves adjacent temporal context, SAMPLE probes fine-grained subframes around a suspicious moment) and terminates via a learned FINAL action rather than an external threshold. Training uses Interactive Direct Preference Optimization (iDPO): for each clip, multiple stochastic rollouts are scored by an Active Evidence Inquiry (AEI) utility that combines task accuracy/format validity, intrinsic curiosity rewards (non-redundant information gain and hypothesis-refinement bonuses), and a step-cost penalty with a free buffer; trajectories are ranked by AEI and preference pairs are optimized via a DPO loss against a fixed SFT reference policy. Theoretical analysis establishes that open-loop observation admits an irreducible ambiguity (Le Cam bound), that closed-loop interaction under a per-round discriminability-gain assumption yields exponential error decay in the number of rounds, and that optimal termination coincides with the value-of-information falling below interaction cost.

## Results

Anom-π (2B parameters) achieves 72.29% AP on XD-Violence, 84.46% AUC on UCF-Crime, 78.75% AUC on UBnormal, and 79.18% AUC on CSAD. On the open-set UBnormal benchmark, it outperforms the strongest prior method PANDA (72B) by 2.97% AUC, and on the complex-scenario CSAD benchmark it exceeds PANDA by 6.06% AUC. Despite using only 2B parameters and 16–32 frames of inference context, Anom-π matches or surpasses much larger models (7B–72B) across all four benchmarks, with the largest margin on open-set generalization (+18.84% over UR-DMU on UBnormal).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On XD-Violence, Anom-π (72.29% AP) outperforms PANDA (70.16%, 72B) by 2.13%, VERA (70.11%, 8B) by 2.18%, and LAVAD (62.01%, 13B) by 10.28%, while remaining competitive with specialized detectors like π-VAD (85.37%) and VadCLIP (84.51%) that use dedicated anomaly scoring. On UBnormal (open-set), Anom-π (78.75% AUC) leads all baselines, beating PANDA (75.78%) by 2.97% and UR-DMU (59.91%) by 18.84%. On CSAD (complex scenarios), Anom-π (79.18% AUC) exceeds PANDA (73.12%) by 6.06% and LAVAD (57.26%) by 21.92%. On UCF-Crime, Anom-π (84.46% AUC) trails specialized weakly-supervised detectors (π-VAD 90.33%, VadCLIP 88.02%) but is competitive with other LLM-based methods (URF 84.28%, LAVAD 80.28%). The key differentiator is that Anom-π achieves these results with only 2B parameters and 16–32 frames of context, compared to PANDA's 72B and VERA's 300-frame context, while also producing natural-language explanations and explicit reasoning traces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from static text/image preferences to interactive, multi-step video trajectories, introducing iDPO with an Active Evidence Inquiry (AEI) utility that jointly rewards task accuracy, informative evidence acquisition, and penalizes redundant interaction. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant passive, open-loop paradigm in video anomaly understanding (LAVAD, VERA, PANDA, VadCLIP) where evidence is fixed before inference, by reframing the task as a closed-loop sequential decision problem where the model actively selects which frames to inspect and when to stop. The atomic observation operators (BACKTRACK, EXPAND, SAMPLE) draw on human video-reviewing behavior, and the theoretical grounding connects to value-of-information and optimal stopping literature, positioning the work at the intersection of active perception, preference-based RL, and video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that a compact 2B model with learned active evidence acquisition can match or exceed 72B+ models on open-set and complex-scenario benchmarks while using only 16–32 frames versus 300, suggesting that targeted perception can substitute for brute-force scale in sparse-cue tasks. However, the gains are uneven: UCF-Crime performance lags behind several baselines, and the paper acknowledges that low-visibility scenes trigger redundant exploration while persistent ambiguity can cause premature stopping. Open questions include whether the iDPO + AEI framework generalizes beyond anomaly detection to other sparse-evidence video tasks (e.g., action recognition, video QA), whether richer reward signals (e.g., GRPO with dense temporal labels) would further improve the policy, and how the approach scales to longer videos where the clip-based episode structure may become a bottleneck.
