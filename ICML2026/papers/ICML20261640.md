## Problem

Most deep video codecs rely on low-level motion modeling and remain largely semantics-agnostic, degrading perceptual quality in complex scenes. Existing Transformer-based approaches either use image encoders that ignore inter-frame redundancy or employ global attention with prohibitive spatiotemporal token costs. Text-guided compression has shown promise for images but has not been integrated into an efficient, video-specific compression framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (T×H×W×3) and a clip-level text caption (generated by LLaVA, encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into 38×512 tokens) as input, and produces an entropy-coded bitstream plus a reconstructed video. The input is partitioned into non-overlapping spatial patches while preserving full temporal resolution, and the architecture supports four discrete rate points via a learnable gain-based scaling mechanism. The final latent resolution is H/16 × W/16, and text is tiled across all T frames before per-frame adaptation.

## Method

MoVie builds a symmetric autoencoder whose core building block is the Text-VideoTCM: a dual-branch module that runs a Video Swin Transformer (window-based self-attention) and a 3D Residual CNN in parallel on channel-split subtensors, then fuses them via a 1×1 3D convolution. Text semantics are injected through a two-stage cross-attention mechanism: an Extractor in Stage 1 uses text as query and image features as key/value to produce frame-specific text tokens, and an Injector in Stage 2 uses image features as query and the adapted text as key/value to embed semantic cues into the visual stream. For entropy coding, a Spatial-Channel Factorized Entropy Model partitions latents into 8×8 spatial windows and 6 channel groups, coding channel slices sequentially while conditioning on both the immediately previous frame and an aggregated historical latent summary. Training proceeds in three stages (autoencoder pretraining, entropy model training, joint fine-tuning) with a composite loss combining bitrate, MSE distortion, LPIPS, and a CLIP contrastive term that aligns reconstructed frames with the text description.

## Results

On UVG and MCL-JCV, MoVie achieves −50.23% BD-Rate for FID and −14.64% BD-Rate for LPIPS (VGGNet) relative to HM-18.0, while requiring only 1,144 kMACs/pixel—roughly 55.76% of DCVC-FM's 2,052 kMACs/pixel. On perceptual metrics, MoVie consistently attains the lowest FID across the full rate range and leads on LPIPS at low bitrates. On pixel-level fidelity, MoVie is competitive with HM on PSNR (+7.62% BD-Rate on UVG) and reduces MS-SSIM BD-Rate by 13.98% without explicit SSIM optimization. A human perceptual study confirms consistent subjective preference over DCVC-FM (73.75% win rate), VTM (78.00%), and PLVC (85.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional anchor HM-18.0, MoVie delivers large perceptual gains (−45.09% FID, −13.03% LPIPS on UVG) at a modest PSNR cost (+7.62%), while VTM-23.11 achieves stronger pixel-level savings (−29.14% PSNR) but far weaker perceptual improvements (−17.38% FID, −1.51% LPIPS). Among learned video codecs, MoVie dominates on perceptual metrics: it outperforms DCVC-FM by 58.3 percentage points on UVG FID (−45.09 vs +13.21) and by 95.0 points on LPIPS (−13.03 vs +82.00), while using only 56% of DCVC-FM's computation. MoVie also substantially surpasses VCT (84.43% FID BD-Rate) and PLVC (219.19% FID BD-Rate) on all metrics. On pixel-level fidelity, DCVC-FM and DCVC-DC retain an edge (e.g., DCVC-FM achieves −31.82% PSNR BD-Rate vs MoVie's +7.62%), confirming MoVie's design trade-off toward perceptual quality. In the temporal coherence evaluation, MoVie achieves the best WarpError BD-Rate (−15.88%), outperforming VTM (−14.42%) and DCVC-FM (+3.19%), and shows no progressive error accumulation over 384-frame sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoVie sits at the intersection of two lines of work: learned video compression (DVC → DCVC family → VCT) and text-guided perceptual image compression (TACO, Jiang et al., Qin et al.). It adapts the Video Swin Transformer from recognition into a symmetric autoencoder backbone and pairs it with a 3D CNN in a window-based mixed block (Text-VideoTCM), departing from the global-attention design of VCT and the frame-independent encoders of FLAVC. The key conceptual departure is bringing CLIP-based text guidance into the *video* compression loop—specifically into the encoder only—whereas prior text-guided compression work was limited to still images or relied on heavy generative decoders (diffusion models, multimodal LLMs) that are impractical for real-time use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the compute-perception trade-off: MoVie uses roughly half the per-pixel kMACs of DCVC-FM while matching or beating it on FID and LPIPS, making text-guided video compression viable at near-real-time speeds. A notable open question is robustness: the method degrades sharply under irrelevant or missing captions, so its utility depends on reliable upstream text generation (here, LLaVA). Future work will need video-specific vision-language encoders and more robust semantic conditioning to remove this dependency, and the spatial-channel factorized entropy model could be a reusable component for other learned codecs seeking better long-range context modeling.
