## Problem

Multimodal VAEs face a persistent trade-off between generative quality (realism and diversity) and generative coherence (semantic consistency across modalities). While HELVAE improved coherence via a Laplace approximation to symmetric Hölder pooling, it still relies on a single shared representation and sacrifices diversity. This paper addresses both limitations by providing the first exact (non-approximate) implementation of symmetric Hölder pooling and extending it with structured shared-private latents and hierarchical inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M modalities per sample (e.g., five background-patched digit images in PolyMNIST, paired MNIST/SVHN digits, bird image–caption pairs in CUBICC, or image–mask–attribute triples in CelebAMask-HQ) through modality-specific encoders that output diagonal-covariance Gaussian posteriors. It produces full multimodal samples (conditional or unconditional), a shared latent z, per-modality private latents w_j, and—when a mixture prior is applied to z—clustering assignments for downstream tasks. The Hölder-pooled posterior is a mixture over M unimodal and M(M−1)/2 pairwise Gaussian components, so sampling from it requires selecting among O(M²) components.

## Method

Hölder++ aggregates unimodal Gaussian posteriors via exact symmetric Hölder pooling (α=0.5), which expands into a weighted mixture of unimodal components and pairwise geometric-mean components with closed-form means, variances, and Bhattacharyya-coefficient-based weights. Building on this, Hölder+ splits the latent space into a shared z and modality-specific w_j subspaces, adopting MMVAE+'s shortcut-avoiding scheme where cross-modal reconstruction terms sample private latents from auxiliary non-informative priors rather than data-conditioned posteriors. Hölder++ further introduces a top-down hierarchical variational posterior in which each private latent w_j is conditioned on both its modality input x_j and the shared z, creating an inductive bias that prevents w_j from capturing shared semantic information. The full objective is a weighted sum of unimodal and pairwise ELBO-style terms, optimized with a multi-sample IWAE estimator and the DReG gradient estimator, and is proven to be a valid lower bound on the log-evidence.

## Results

On CelebAMask-HQ, Hölder+ achieves the best image generation quality (FID 78.82 given Mask+Attribute) and Hölder++ the best attribute prediction (F1 0.665 given Image), both substantially outperforming MMVAE+ (FID 98.33; F1 0.596) and CMVAE (FID 104.92; F1 0.590). On CUBICC downstream clustering, CHolder++ achieves 65.3% ACC on the joint representation, surpassing CMVAE (58.0%) and MMVAE+ (35.5%). On MNIST-SVHN, Hölder++ reduces private-latent classification accuracy to 0.387 (MNIST) and 0.112 (SVHN) while maintaining shared-latent accuracy of 0.970 and 0.922 respectively, confirming effective disentanglement. Across PolyMNIST and MNIST-SVHN, Hölder+ and Hölder++ consistently occupy the Pareto-optimal frontier in coherence–FID and coherence–log-likelihood trade-offs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hölder+ and Hölder++ consistently outperform MMVAE+ and CMVAE across all four datasets: on CelebAMask-HQ they reduce image FID by ~20 points (78.82 vs 98.33/104.92) and improve attribute F1 by ~4 points; on PolyMNIST they achieve the best Pareto frontier in coherence–FID and coherence–log-likelihood trade-offs. Against HELVAE (the strongest single-shared-representation baseline), Hölder+ and Hölder++ match or exceed coherence while recovering sample diversity through the shared-private split, yielding a strictly better trade-off. On CUBICC clustering, CHolder+ and CHolder++ rank first and second on all metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI) across image, caption, and joint representations, with CHolder++ achieving 65.3% joint ACC versus CMVAE's 58.0% and MMVAE+'s 35.5%. Relative to DCMEM, Hölder-based models deliver lower FID (better sample quality) while remaining competitive in coherence, and unlike DCMEM they scale beyond two modalities. Hölder++ closely matches Hölder+ on generative metrics, confirming that hierarchical inference preserves the quality-coherence trade-off while improving disentanglement (e.g., reducing MNIST private-latent accuracy from 0.479 to 0.387 and CUBICC w-content accuracy from 0.195 to 0.133).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hölder pooling framework for multimodal VAE aggregation introduced by Vo & Valera (2026), who proposed Hellinger aggregation as a Laplace approximation to symmetric Hölder pooling (α=0.5). Here, the authors provide the first exact implementation of that pooling, revealing a mixture structure with unimodal and pairwise Gaussian components that generalizes MoE (MMVAE) by adding cross-modality interaction terms. The shared-private latent architecture with shortcut prevention is adopted from MMVAE+ (Palumbo et al., 2023), while the hierarchical variational posterior for disentanglement departs from prior auxiliary-loss approaches (DCMEM, DMVAE) by encoding the inductive bias directly in the factorization of the approximate posterior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that symmetric Hölder pooling's pairwise components implicitly capture soft cross-modality dependencies, offering a principled alternative to PoE and MoE that improves the quality-coherence Pareto frontier across four benchmarks. The hierarchical inference design is notable for being a clean, architecture-level solution to shared-private disentanglement that generalizes across backbones (validated on MMVAE+ and CMVAE), avoiding the hyperparameter sensitivity of mutual-information or contrastive losses. Limitations include increased computational cost from the M² mixture components, the fact that Hölder++ closely matches Hölder+ on generative metrics (suggesting hierarchical inference's primary benefit is disentanglement rather than generation), and the gap to diffusion-model-level image quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to many modalities without quadratic cost, and whether a theoretical identifiability guarantee can be established for the content-style separation.
