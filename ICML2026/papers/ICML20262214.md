## Problem

LoRA is the dominant parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for LLMs, yet it causes catastrophic forgetting of previously learned capabilities. The mathematical complexity of full-scale transformers makes it intractable to analyze *why* and *when* forgetting occurs. This paper addresses that gap by studying a tractable mean-field self-attention toy model in which tokens evolve as an interacting particle system, enabling rigorous characterization of the mechanisms linking LoRA perturbations to representation degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262214/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial token configuration on the unit sphere S^{d-1}, a triple of attention matrices (Q, K, V), and a low-rank LoRA perturbation (rank r ≪ d) as input, and produces quantitative stability bounds in Wasserstein-2 distance, explicit phase-transition thresholds in the perturbation norm and network depth, and a time-to-deviation estimate. The 'output' is not a prediction but a geometric characterization: the drift of the limiting cluster configuration (or its Wasserstein distance from the base model's cluster) serves as a proxy for forgetting, which the paper empirically correlates with base-task perplexity degradation on Qwen 3 0.6B and Llama 2.

## Method

The paper recasts the transformer forward pass as a continuous-depth interacting particle system where depth plays the role of time and self-attention defines the vector field (Eq. 2/8). LoRA is modeled as a low-rank perturbation ΔM = M_A^T M_B to the attention matrices, and the authors analyze its effect using tools from PDEs (continuity equations, Wasserstein stability) and dynamical systems (spectral analysis, bifurcation theory). The key theoretical contributions are: (i) a finite-time Wasserstein stability bound showing the perturbed and base dynamics remain close for t ≪ log log(1/ε) (Prop. 3.1); (ii) a long-time spectral stability condition where the eigengap of V controls the drift of the limiting cluster direction (Prop. 3.3); (iii) a norm-based phase transition for i.i.d. random adapters, where the critical scaling η_L ≍ √L separates a vanishing-perturbation regime from a diffusive regime with nontrivial representation drift (Thm. 4.2); and (iv) a depth-based bifurcation showing that even small perturbations eventually push tokens out of the pre-trained cluster after a critical depth T* governed by the spectral gap and cluster geometry (Thm. 4.6). The approach is distinguished by its use of mean-field/homogenization arguments to reduce the multi-layer problem to a continuum-depth ODE, and by connecting the spectral structure of the value matrix to the stability of the representation geometry.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical. The norm-based phase transition (Theorem 4.2) shows that for random LoRA adapters, tokens remain trapped near the pretrained cluster with E[‖x_i^ℓ − u₁‖²] ≲ O(1/L) when the perturbation scale η_L ≪ √L, while at the critical scaling η_L ≃ √L the mean-square deviation is governed by κ Σᵢ 1/(2(λ₁−λᵢ)+dκ), highlighting the stabilizing role of spectral gaps. The depth-based bifurcation (Theorem 4.6) bounds the first exit time from the clustered regime as T*(δ) ≤ max{T_δ, T_dom(δ)} + 16‖ℓ‖δ/(λ₁D₋). Empirically on Qwen 3 0.6B, the bifurcation depth b_τ(s) (first layer where relative representation drift exceeds τ=0.2) decreases from approximately layer 28 at the start of LoRA training to approximately layer 16 at the end, confirming the shrinking stability window. Learned LoRA updates show alignment scores consistently above the random baseline (y=1.0), with low-rank adapters (r=8, 32) exhibiting a pronounced 'locking on' effect toward the dominant pretrained direction u₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard method-vs-baseline comparison table; the empirical experiments serve to validate the theoretical predictions rather than benchmark against competing fine-tuning methods. The random-adapter experiments (Figure 2) confirm the predicted norm-based phase transition: the perturbed loss remains flat for small Frobenius-norm perturbations and then rises sharply past a threshold, consistent with Theorem 4.2. The training experiments (Figure 3) show that, unlike the random-adapter setting, learned LoRA updates do exhibit rank-dependent forgetting (higher ranks forget more), a phenomenon the isotropic Gaussian toy model does not capture but which the spectral-alignment analysis (Proposition 3.3, Figure 4) provides a mechanistic explanation for. The depth-bifurcation experiment (Figure 6) on Qwen 3 0.6B LoRA checkpoints directly validates Theorem 4.6's stability-window picture: as fine-tuning progresses, the first layer at which the adapted model significantly deviates from the frozen pretrained model moves earlier in depth (from ~layer 28 to ~layer 16), indicating progressive erosion of the pretrained representation's stability window. The paper positions its findings as complementary to orthogonalized LoRA variants (O-LoRA, orthogonal subspace learning), noting that the spectral-gap stability mechanism in Proposition 3.3 provides a theoretical justification for those projection-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the mean-field transformer dynamics literature (Geshkovski et al., Sander et al., Rigollet's survey) and the LoRA/parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature. It repurposes the interacting-particle-system and continuity-equation framework—previously developed to understand clustering and rank collapse in self-attention—as a lens for analyzing how low-rank perturbations (LoRA) destabilize the pretrained representation geometry. The spectral-stability analysis in Proposition 3.3 extends Burger et al.'s (2025) characterization of interaction-energy maxima to the perturbed setting, while the norm-based phase transition (Theorem 4.2) builds on the homogenization formalism of Koubbi et al. (2026). Unlike prior forgetting analyses that are purely empirical or rely on full fine-tuning, this work provides the first PDE/dynamical-systems-based characterization of LoRA-induced forgetting with explicit phase-transition thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, if simplified, explanation for why LoRA causes forgetting: the spectral gap of the pretrained value matrix governs how much the limiting cluster direction can be displaced, and low-rank updates that align with the dominant eigendirection (as empirically confirmed in Figure 4) are the most destabilizing. This directly motivates and validates orthogonalized LoRA variants (Xiong & Xie, 2025; Wang et al., 2023) from a theoretical standpoint. Key limitations include the tied-weight, single-head, no-nonlinearity toy model, the doubly-exponential time bounds in Proposition 3.1, and the gap between the isotropic random-adapter assumption and actual learned updates (where rank matters). Open questions include extending the analysis to anisotropic/learned perturbations, incorporating the optimization trajectory rather than treating LoRA as a fixed inference-time perturbation, and validating the spectral-alignment mechanism at scale beyond 0.6B parameters.
