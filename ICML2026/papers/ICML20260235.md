## Problem

Learning representations that capture only control-relevant features of an environment without access to a reward function or pre-collected expert data remains an open problem. Reward-based methods (e.g., bisimulation) may discard features irrelevant to one task but essential for another, while inverse-dynamics methods inherit biases from their training data. This paper asks whether the empowerment objective—an interaction-based, reward-free criterion—naturally induces representations with the right invariance properties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of initial states s₀ and future states s⁺ sampled from the discounted occupancy measure of skill-conditioned policies, along with the skill label z. The key structural choice is factorizing the discriminator posterior q(z|s₀, s⁺) into two separate encoders: a forward representation φ(s₀) that clusters states by their controllable futures, and a backward representation ψ(s⁺) that clusters states by their reachability histories. Experiments use state vectors (2D Point-Maze, 29D Ant) and 64×64×3 RGB images (Lexa Kitchen), each augmented with temporally correlated Gaussian noise dimensions to test invariance.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a theoretical characterization of the forward and backward representations implicitly learned by the MISL variational lower bound, showing they are minimal sufficient statistics for predicting the skill. Using the geometric polytope view of empowerment (where only vertex policies receive nonzero probability), the authors prove that MISL policies are invariant to control-irrelevant features (Theorem 5.4) and to action-interface relabeling (Theorem 5.1), and that backward representations provably capture all control-relevant features under deterministic, connected dynamics (Proposition 5.8). A key asymmetry is identified: forward and backward representations alias different pairs of states, and enforcing symmetry between them reduces empowerment to zero. Practically, the method uses METRA with a 2-layer MLP policy and discriminator, and the paper demonstrates that noise invariance emerges without explicit information-bottleneck regularization, even when scaling to 5-layer/8-layer networks.

## Results

Across Point-Maze, Ant, and Lexa Kitchen (augmented with Gaussian noise), MISL representations exhibit variance along control-relevant dimensions that is at least 10³× larger than along control-irrelevant (noise) dimensions, confirming invariance to noise. In the tabular MDP (Figure 5), enforcing forward-backward symmetry collapses empowerment from 0.69 to 0, validating the predicted asymmetry. Linear probes on MISL representations recover the control-relevant coordinates (x, y) in Point-Maze with R² rapidly approaching 1, demonstrating that all control-relevant features are preserved. Downstream reward maximization on frozen MISL representations substantially outperforms agents using raw noisy states across all three environments, and this advantage persists under out-of-distribution noise (temporally correlated, cross-dimensionally correlated) unseen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MISL against two prior invariant-representation methods in Appendix I. Against bisimulation (Ferns et al., 2011), MISL is strictly more robust: bisimulation's objective relies on a latent forward predictor and is sensitive to temporally correlated noise, whereas MISL remains invariant to noise regardless of its temporal correlation structure (Figure 6). Against AC-state / multi-step inverse dynamics (Lamb et al., 2022), MISL has a structural advantage: AC-state representations inherit the biases of the data-collection policy—if the policy even partially attends to noise, the learned representations are not noise-invariant—while MISL requires no pre-collected data and its invariance is guaranteed by the empowerment objective itself. In both comparisons, MISL wins on the noise-invariance metric, and additionally MISL provably captures all control-relevant features (Proposition 5.8) without requiring a reward function, whereas bisimulation may discard control-relevant features that the reward does not depend on, limiting generalization to new reward functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions empowerment-based skill learning (MISL) as a principled, reward-free alternative to prior invariant representation learning methods that require either a reward function (bisimulation, Ferns et al. 2011) or expert-collected datasets (inverse dynamics, Lamb et al. 2023; Efroni et al. 2022). It builds directly on the variational lower bound formulation of empowerment (Gregor et al. 2016; Eysenbach et al. 2018) and the geometric occupancy-polytope view (Eysenbach et al. 2022), but departs from prior MISL work by identifying a fundamental forward/backward representation asymmetry that existing methods (Park et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2025) implicitly ignore through symmetric parameterizations. The analysis also draws on the causal learning literature (Pearl 2009; Peters et al. 2017) to frame interaction-driven invariance as a form of causal identification, and contrasts with successor representations (Barreto et al. 2017) to highlight that MISL's prescribed policy objective breaks the circular dependence between data collection and representation invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first method-agnostic theoretical characterization of what representations MISL learns, showing they form a reward-agnostic analogue of bisimulation that provably captures all control-relevant features under mild determinism and connectivity assumptions. The forward/backward asymmetry is a genuinely new conceptual insight with practical consequences: enforcing symmetry collapses empowerment to zero, and the backward representation's interpretable ℓ1 distance as a reachability measure offers a principled goal-embedding for goal-conditioned RL. Key limitations include the restriction to fully observable MDPs, the reliance on a policy minimality assumption (though empirically unnecessary), and open questions about whether these representations recover the full causal structure of control-relevant features or extend to partially observable settings.
