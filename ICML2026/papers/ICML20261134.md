## Problem

Functional ANOVA provides a principled additive decomposition of model predictions into main effects and interactions, but no explicit closed-form solution exists for general dependent distributions, forcing practitioners to rely on costly sampling-based approximations. This gap is particularly acute for categorical inputs with arbitrary dependence structures and non-rectangular support, where standard Fourier or Boolean analysis tools do not apply directly.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box model's predictions evaluated on categorical tabular data (represented as a data matrix of shape n×d) along with the empirical joint distribution of the inputs, and produces the full ANOVA decomposition components, ANOVA-based Shapley values for local attribution, and global feature importance scores. The decomposition is computed once as a global linear solve whose dimension equals the effective support size r (often far smaller than the full hypergrid |E|), after which explanations for any number of samples are available instantaneously without re-solving.

## Method

The core contribution is an explicit extension of the Walsh-Hadamard (parity) basis to multi-level categorical variables, defined as products of indicator differences normalized by the marginal probability (the 'inverse likelihood mechanism'). This basis provably satisfies the hierarchical orthogonality condition of the Generalized Functional ANOVA, so any function admits a Fourier expansion in this basis whose coefficients solve a single linear system Γc = μ. For sparse supports where r ≪ |E|, a greedy rank-based algorithm selects a minimal subset of basis functions forming a valid basis of the reduced function space, enabling tractable computation in high dimensions. In the independent case the formula reduces exactly to the classical Möbius transform of conditional expectations, and in the uniform Boolean case it collapses to the standard parity functions, while the ANOVA components directly yield a natural generalization of SHAP values to the dependent categorical setting.

## Results

On the MUSHROOMS dataset (22 features, |E|≈10^14, r=8124), the decomposition achieves R²≈1 with MSE of 10⁻¹⁵ in 0.3 s, confirming that main effects alone fully reconstruct the signal. On high-dimensional sparse datasets (POKER HAND, CONNECT-4, DOTA2), the method reaches R² of 0.79, 0.70, and 0.41 respectively at rank 4000–5000, with computation times of 10–39 minutes. On Binarized MNIST (784 binary features, 60 000 samples), the framework achieves R²=0.86 at rank 10 000 in 15 minutes. The closed-form solution enforces strict hierarchical orthogonality to machine precision (10⁻¹⁶), whereas the TreeHFD approximation only satisfies it to 10⁻³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KernelSHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017), the paper's exact ANOVA-based Shapley values match the sampling-based approximations in the independent setting, with 100×ISE values ranging from 0.00 to 2.89 on CAR EVALUATION and 0.0 to 4.4 on NURSERY, while computing the full decomposition in 0.5 s and 54 s respectively versus KernelSHAP's 200 background samples. Against Observational TreeSHAP (Lundberg et al., 2018) on MUSHROOMS, both methods identify the same top-3 features (odor, Gill Color, Spore Print Color), though the paper's black-box approach differs in lower-ranked attributions since it does not exploit internal tree paths. Against Attention Rollout (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020) on POKER HAND, the paper's method correctly assigns roughly an order of magnitude more importance to card ranks than suits, whereas attention rollout distributes attribution far more uniformly and substantially overestimates suit contributions. Against TreeHFD (Bénard, 2025), the closed-form solution enforces strict hierarchical orthogonality to 10⁻¹⁶ (machine precision) versus TreeHFD's 10⁻³ tolerance, and produces main effects and Shapley values that differ by only 10⁻³–10⁻² and 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ respectively, confirming near-equivalence while providing exact theoretical guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first explicit closed-form solution to Hooker's (2007) Generalized Functional ANOVA variational problem for categorical inputs, by constructing a basis that extends the Walsh-Hadamard parity functions of Boolean analysis (O'Donnell, 2014) to arbitrary categorical cardinalities and dependence structures. It builds directly on the Hoeffding (1948)–Stone (1994)–Hooker (2007) lineage of ANOVA decomposition, but departs from prior work by avoiding the i.i.d. Bernoulli(1/2) restriction of Boolean Fourier methods (Gorji et al., 2024) and the density or tree-structure assumptions of Purifying (Lengerich et al., 2020) and TreeHFD (Bénard, 2025). The resulting framework subsumes the classical Möbius-transform ANOVA and SHAP values as special cases under independence, while handling non-rectangular supports and functional dependencies that previously forced practitioners toward sampling-based approximations like KernelSHAP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form decomposition eliminates the need for Monte Carlo or kernel-based approximation in the categorical setting, yielding a one-time global computation after which explanations for any number of samples are instantaneous—a significant practical advantage over KernelSHAP's per-query cost. However, the framework inherits ANOVA's inherent computational scaling: in the dense regime the linear system size equals the full hypergrid, and in the sparse regime the greedy rank search becomes the bottleneck (tens of minutes for 67k-sample datasets). The acknowledged non-uniqueness of the decomposition under sparse support is a fundamental identifiability issue shared by all ANOVA/GAM-style methods on discrete data, and the paper leaves open the question of principled selection criteria among equivalent representations. Extending the framework to continuous inputs and integrating structural priors (spatial locality, sparsity-promoting selection) are identified as key next steps.
