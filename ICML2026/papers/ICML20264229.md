## Problem

Pairwise generative reward models (GRMs) are widely used in RLHF but incur O(n²) computational cost when scoring n candidates, creating a bottleneck during reinforcement learning. Pointwise GRMs would reduce this to O(n) and provide calibrated, cross-set-comparable scores, but they typically require expensive scalar annotations for individual responses. The paper asks whether a pointwise GRM can be trained from standard pairwise preference data alone, inheriting the efficiency of pointwise scoring while avoiding costly scalar labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The GRM receives a single prompt-response pair (context + response) and autoregressively generates a structured output consisting of a natural-language critique across six evaluation dimensions (instruction following, relevance, factuality, completeness, clarity, safety) followed by a scalar score on a 0.0–10.0 rubric, both wrapped in XML-style tags. Unlike pairwise GRMs that consume two responses simultaneously, IRPM scores each response independently, enabling O(n) evaluation over a candidate set. The detailed scoring rubric in the prompt is designed to encourage fine-grained, calibrated absolute scores rather than relative judgments.

## Method

IRPM reformulates the Bradley-Terry preference probability as an expectation over stochastic score samples produced by the GRM, yielding a Monte Carlo estimator based on G×G intergroup comparisons between G rollouts of the chosen response and G rollouts of the rejected response. The key technical step is decomposing this double average into per-rollout rewards: each chosen rollout's reward is the mean sigmoid of its score difference against all rejected rollouts (or a hard AUC indicator, or a rule-based comparison against the opposite group's mean/median/confidence-bound). The model is then optimized with GRPO, which normalizes rewards within each group to compute advantages, encouraging higher-probability generation of better-scoring completions. Among the reward variants, IRPM-Mean (comparing each rollout to the opposite group's sample mean with an adaptive margin δ) achieves the best stability and performance, while the soft IRPM-Preference variant causes training collapse due to unbounded margin amplification under GRPO's relative-update mechanism. Inference-time scaling via multi-sample voting further reduces the 8.2% tie rate and improves accuracy by up to 4.2%.

## Results

IRPM-Mean-32B achieves an overall score of 77.2% across RM-Bench (79.6%), JudgeBench (74.6%), RewardBench (87.3%), and PPE (67.2%), outperforming the Qwen3-32B backbone by 3.4% on average. With inference-time scaling (voting@8), IRPM-Mean-32B reaches 81.4% overall, approaching the strongest pairwise GRM J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask (81.8%). At the 8B scale, IRPM-Mean-8B achieves 74.9% overall, surpassing the tool-augmented pointwise baseline TIR-Judge-Zero-8B by 2.8% on average across three benchmarks. In post-training, IRPM-32B improves Qwen2.5-7B MMLU-Pro accuracy from 47.9% to 54.0%, and with voting@4 reaches 56.6%, surpassing RM-R1-32B under the same compute budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pointwise GRMs, IRPM-Mean-8B outperforms TIR-Judge-Zero-8B (which relies on external code execution) by 2.8% on average across RM-Bench, JudgeBench, and RewardBench, and surpasses PaTaRM-Qwen3-8B on RewardBench (85.7 vs. 84.3). IRPM-Mean-32B (77.2% overall) trails the strongest pairwise GRM J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask (81.8%) by 4.6%, but with voting@8 (81.4%) the gap narrows to 0.4%. On individual benchmarks, IRPM-Mean-32B surpasses J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask on JudgeBench (74.6 vs. 71.4) and outperforms DeepSeek-GRM-27B on PPE (67.2 vs. 62.3). In post-training, IRPM-32B with voting@4 (56.6% MMLU-Pro) surpasses RM-R1-32B (54.0%) and RRM-32B under the same compute budget, while reducing reward-model inference time from 560 s/step to 210 s/step. Compared to the B-T scalar model Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B, IRPM achieves higher post-training accuracy (54.0% vs. 47.9% baseline) while providing interpretable critiques and inference-time scalability that B-T lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

IRPM extends the Bradley-Terry pairwise preference model by replacing its deterministic utility with a stochastic score distribution produced by a generative reward model, yielding a Monte Carlo intergroup comparison objective. The optimization leverages GRPO's within-group advantage normalization, but applies it to intergroup (chosen-vs-rejected) rewards rather than within a single candidate set, which is the key structural departure from standard GRPO. This positions IRPM between the B-T scalar reward model (deterministic, O(1) per candidate) and pairwise GRMs (stochastic, O(n²) per candidate set), achieving O(n) reward evaluation while retaining interpretability and inference-time scalability that scalar models lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) reward evaluation during RL training is a practically significant efficiency gain: the paper demonstrates 25% of RRM's reward-model computation while matching or exceeding post-training performance, and a net end-to-end time reduction despite longer RM training. The finding that IRPM-Preference (direct soft preference strength as reward) causes training collapse under GRPO's within-group normalization is a useful negative result, clarifying that margin-maximizing objectives are incompatible with relative-update mechanisms. Open questions include whether the 8.2% tie rate (mitigated to 0.47% via voting@8) can be reduced architecturally, whether the small remaining gap to the strongest pairwise GRMs (e.g., J1-Qwen-32B at 81.8% vs. 77.2% for IRPM-Mean-32B) closes with more training data or larger backbones, and whether the intergroup formulation generalizes to listwise or multi-turn settings.
