## Problem

Single-frame monocular 3D human pose estimation remains fragile under occlusion and depth ambiguity because existing methods either lift sparse 2D coordinates without visual grounding or sample image features only at isolated joint locations, treating the body as a set of disconnected points. This point-wise paradigm fails to exploit the continuous geometric structure along skeletal limbs and lacks a canonical 3D reference to enforce global pose consistency during lifting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and 17 2D joint coordinates from a frozen CPN detector as input, extracting 4-scale feature maps from a frozen HRNet-W32/W48 backbone. It outputs 17 3D joint positions (x, y, z) in meters, regressed via a linear projection from a 128-dim per-joint representation. The model is trained end-to-end on Human3.6M with MPJPE loss and evaluated on Human3.6M, MPI-INF-3DHP, and 3DPW.

## Method

Glimpse introduces two synergistic geometry-learning components. Structured sampling performs dual-path deformable attention: at the joint level it predicts K=4 adaptive offsets per joint per scale, and at the limb level it samples M=3 intermediate points along each skeletal segment, then propagates limb features back to connected joints via the skeletal adjacency matrix with channel-wise gating to balance appearance vs. geometric continuity. Geometric correction introduces a learnable 3D anchor point projected into feature space; a Gaussian-kernel distance-aware mechanism computes per-joint, per-scale alignment weights to select the most globally consistent feature scales, which are then nonlinearly fused with the anchor embedding through a gated layer. The resulting multi-scale structured features and updated pose embeddings are refined by two Transformer stages (multi-scale fusion within joints, then joint interaction across the skeleton) before a linear head regresses 3D coordinates.

## Results

On Human3.6M, Glimpse-HR48 achieves 39.3 mm MPJPE and 32.3 mm PA-MPJPE, setting a new state-of-the-art among single-frame methods. On MPI-INF-3DHP, Glimpse-HR48 reaches 29.4 mm MPJPE, 98.7% PCK, and 80.9 AUC, outperforming the previous best single-frame method (CA-PF-HR48) by 2.0 mm in MPJPE and 4.6 points in AUC. On the cross-dataset 3DPW benchmark, Glimpse-HR32 achieves 132.2 mm MPJPE, improving over CA-PF-HR32 by 6.7 mm. The full model runs at 17.9 FPS with 14.3M parameters, maintaining practical real-time performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Glimpse-HR48 outperforms the strongest single-frame context-aware baselines: CA-PF-HR48 (39.8 mm) and PandaPose (39.8 mm) by 0.5 mm in MPJPE on Human3.6M, while using fewer parameters than PandaPose (14.3M vs. 15.3M). Against methods that rely solely on 2D coordinates (GraphMLP at 49.2 mm, HiPART at 42.0 mm, Lifting at 46.4 mm), Glimpse achieves 2.7–9.9 mm MPJPE reductions, confirming the value of integrating multi-scale visual features. Notably, Glimpse surpasses the best multi-frame method KTPFormer (40.1 mm, 243 frames) by 0.8 mm using only a single frame and 60% fewer parameters. On MPI-INF-3DHP, Glimpse-HR48 leads CA-PF-HR48 by 2.0 mm MPJPE and 4.6 AUC points, and PandaPose by 2.4 mm MPJPE and 5.1 AUC points. On the cross-dataset 3DPW benchmark, Glimpse-HR32 improves over CA-PF-HR32 by 6.7 mm MPJPE (132.2 vs. 138.9), demonstrating better generalization to in-the-wild conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the context-aware single-frame 3D-HPE line initiated by CA-PF (Zhao et al., 2023), Lifting (Zhou et al., 2024), and PandaPose (Zheng et al., 2026), which all incorporate multi-scale image features at detected 2D joint locations. Glimpse departs from this point-wise sampling paradigm by introducing continuous limb-level deformable sampling along skeletal segments and a canonical 3D anchor with Gaussian-kernel distance-aware fusion, repurposing deformable attention (from DETR-style architectures) and graph-based skeletal priors into a unified geometry-learning framework. Conceptually, it reframes the lifting problem as explicit multi-scale structural prior learning rather than feature aggregation at isolated keypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured sampling + geometric correction paradigm offers a principled way to inject anatomical continuity into single-frame 3D-HPE, and the consistent gains across Human3.6M, MPI-INF-3DHP, and 3DPW (including 4.6 AUC points on the in-the-wild benchmark) suggest the learned structural priors generalize beyond the training domain. However, the method still depends on an upstream 2D pose detector (CPN), and the 17.9 FPS throughput, while practical, is lower than the 32 FPS baseline, limiting ultra-real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the canonical 3D anchor generalizes to multi-person scenes or non-human articulated structures, and whether the limb-level sampling strategy transfers to other skeletal topologies (e.g., animals, robots).
