## Problem

Prior Q-learning variants used value clipping only as a technical device to bound estimates at the natural value ceiling, leaving the behavior of clipping at a general threshold λ below that ceiling unexplained and seemingly inconsistent with the Bellman optimality operator. This paper asks what principled objective a general-threshold clipped Bellman backup actually optimizes, and whether it can be interpreted as solving a well-defined robust decision-making problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262938/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes single sampled transitions (s, a, r, s') from the nominal environment along with a user-specified regularizer λ, and produces a Q-table whose entries are upper-bounded by min{1+λ, H} under the fail-state assumption. The resulting greedy policy π^{*,λ} is designed to perform well under worst-case adversarial perturbations of the transition kernel at test time, where probability mass is shifted from high-value successor states toward the minimum-value state. The clipping threshold applied in the backup is λ' = V_min + λ (reducing to λ under the fail-state assumption), which is distinct from the natural value ceiling used in prior work.

## Method

The key insight is that the clipped Bellman backup r + [max_{a'} Q(s',a')]_λ is an unbiased one-sample estimator of a robust Bellman operator arising from TV-distance-regularized robust MDPs (RRMDPs). Proposition 3.4 establishes the strong duality identity inf_μ [E_{s~μ} V(s) + λ D_TV(μ‖μ⁰)] = E_{s~μ⁰}[V(s)]_{V_min+λ}, showing that the value-clipping operator emerges naturally from the dual of the TV-regularized optimization in the robust Bellman equation. Proposition 3.6 characterizes the adversary's worst-case kernel, which deterministically reallocates all probability mass from states with value exceeding V_min + λ to the minimum-value state. Two algorithm variants incorporating UCB-Hoeffding and UCB-Bernstein exploration bonuses are analyzed, yielding polynomial regret bounds of O(min{1+λ,H}√(SAH³Kι)) and a sharper Bernstein-based bound, respectively. The value-clipping module is modular and can be inserted into any value-based RL algorithm; as a proof of concept, a clipped Double DQN variant is evaluated on the Inverted Pendulum task and shows improved robustness under pole-length perturbations without sacrificing nominal performance.

## Results

Theoretically, Clipped Q-Learning with UCB-Hoeffding achieves a regret bound of O(min{1+λ, H}·√(SAH³Kι)), improving upon the standard Q-learning bound of O(H·√(SAH³Kι)) by a factor of O(H/min{1+λ, H}) due to a range-shrinkage phenomenon where the regularized value function is bounded by λ+1 rather than H. The UCB-Bernstein variant further tightens the dependence to O(√(min{1+λ,H})·H^{3/2}·√(SAK)·ι). Empirically, on the Inverted Pendulum task, Clipped DDQN (with λ ∈ {90, 100, 200, 250}) consistently outperforms vanilla DDQN in target environments with pole lengths 0.4 and 1.1 (vs. nominal 0.6), without sacrificing nominal-environment performance, and value clipping also accelerates convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. First, in the tabular simulated MDP (Section 5.1), Clipped Q-Learning with moderate clipping (λ ∈ {0.4, 0.6}) outperforms the unclipped baseline (λ=∞, which reduces to standard Q-learning with UCB-Hoeffding from Jin et al., 2018) under larger transition perturbations (larger q), confirming the robustness interpretation. Second, on the Inverted Pendulum control task (Section 5.2), Clipped DDQN consistently outperforms vanilla DDQN (Van Hasselt et al., 2016) in target environments with substantially different pole lengths (0.4 and 1.1 vs. nominal 0.6), while matching nominal performance. The paper notes that the robustness benefit is algorithm-dependent: replacing DDQN with standard DQN (which suffers from Q-value overestimation) eliminates the robustness gain from value clipping, indicating the clipping module requires a stable base algorithm to be effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on classical Q-learning convergence theory (Jin et al., 2018; Wainwright, 2019) and the recent Regularized Robust MDP (RRMDP) framework (Yang et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2025; He et al., 2025), which replaces hard uncertainty-set constraints with soft transition-dynamics regularization. Prior Q-learning analyses (Jin et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2023) employed value clipping purely as a technical device to bound Q-value estimates at the known value ceiling, leaving the mechanism of clipping at a general threshold λ unexplained. The key departure here is showing that clipping at an arbitrary λ is an unbiased one-sample estimator of a TV-regularized robust Bellman operator, thereby connecting a simple algorithmic modification to the RRMDP literature and providing a principled robustness interpretation that prior clipping-based analyses lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge between a trivially simple algorithmic tweak (clipping the Bellman backup) and robust decision-making under adversarial dynamics shifts, suggesting that robustness can be injected into value-based RL with minimal architectural change. However, several limitations constrain near-term impact: the finite-sample analysis requires a fail-state assumption, the regret guarantees are tabular-only, the robustness is tailored specifically to TV-distance 'high-value attack' perturbations, and empirical validation is limited to a single control task (Inverted Pendulum with DDQN). Open questions include whether the clipping module interacts well with deep function approximation, whether it generalizes beyond TV-regularized perturbations, and whether the fail-state assumption can be relaxed for broader applicability.
