## Problem

Standard optimizers combine batch-averaged gradients with historical state (momentum, adaptive moments), which can produce parameter updates that actively increase the loss of individual samples in the current mini-batch. This per-sample interference represents a systematic loss of information that is invisible at the batch level, yet no existing method explicitly minimizes it while preserving the optimizer's accumulated context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260147/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB image classification (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces class labels. Unusually, the training loop consumes per-sample gradient statistics from only the final linear layer (logits and their inputs) to compute a B×B Gram matrix and a B-dimensional vector, then reweights the per-sample losses before the standard backward pass. The output is a modified parameter update Δθ* = Δθ_b + G^T w* that is implicitly realized by running the underlying optimizer on a weighted loss Σ(1+w*_i)L_i rather than the uniform average.

## Method

The paper formulates the parameter update as a convex optimization problem minimizing total per-sample interference H(GΔθ) subject to a trust-region ball around the standard optimizer's update, then reduces the problem from Θ-dimensional parameter space to B-dimensional batch space via a representer-style result (Proposition 4.1). A key empirical finding is that the last linear layer alone reliably captures the core second-order statistics of the full-network per-sample gradients, allowing the Gram matrix to be approximated from that single layer. The Khatri-Rao product factorization computes the B×B Gram matrix in O(B²(d_in+d_out)) time without materializing the B×(d_in·d_out) per-sample gradient matrix; for AdamW, a rank-5 SVD of the adaptive scaling vector handles the diagonal weighting. The resulting small convex problem is solved with the Chambolle-Pock primal-dual algorithm in ≤50 GPU-friendly iterations, and the final update is applied by running the standard optimizer on a per-sample-weighted loss, making the method a drop-in modification to SGD-momentum or AdamW with O(B²(d_in+d_out)) overhead that shrinks relative to total compute as the backbone grows.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the method achieves the largest gains: +2.24% test accuracy for ViT-c at batch size 1024 (58.82% → 61.06%) and +0.88% for ResNet-44 at the same batch size (61.43% → 62.31%). On CIFAR-10, ViT-c improves by up to +0.70% (88.91% → 89.61% at B=1024), while ResNet-44 shows modest gains that degrade at B=1024 (−0.22%) due to the rank-deficient final layer. On larger datasets, ConvNeXt-T with Mixup on ImageNet-100 gains +1.16% (78.60% → 79.76%). Relative time overhead ranges from 1.01× to 1.30×, shrinking as model size or input resolution increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other optimization methods; the sole baseline is standard training with the same optimizer (SGD with momentum for ResNets, AdamW for ViT/ConvNeXt) without the proposed interference-reduction step. The method consistently outperforms this baseline on CIFAR-100 across all batch sizes for both architectures, with the largest gain being +2.24% for ViT-c at B=1024. On CIFAR-10, ViT-c also improves at every batch size, while ResNet-44 degrades slightly at B=1024 (−0.22%) due to the extremely small final layer (640 parameters) causing a rank-deficient Gram matrix. On Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet-100 (Table 2), ConvNeXt-T with Mixup on ImageNet-100 shows the strongest large-scale gain (+1.16%), while ResNet-50 with BatchNorm on Tiny-ImageNet slightly underperforms (−0.17%), likely because BatchNorm breaks per-sample independence within a batch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard optimizers (SGD with momentum and AdamW) by formulating the parameter update as a constrained optimization problem that explicitly minimizes per-sample loss increases caused by the interaction between batch-averaged gradients and historical optimizer state. Conceptually, it draws on the gradient-alignment interference notion from continual learning (GEM, a-GEM) and multi-task gradient conflict reduction (gradient surgery, GDOD), but repurposes it for a fundamentally different setting: protecting current-batch samples from past optimization state rather than protecting past tasks from new data. The efficient solution leverages the Chambolle-Pock primal-dual algorithm and Khatri-Rao product factorizations to avoid materializing per-sample gradients, building on techniques from differential privacy and per-example gradient literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-overhead modification to widely used optimizers whose relative cost shrinks as model size or input dimensions grow, making it attractive for large-scale training. However, the empirical gains are modest (0.2–2.2% accuracy), the last-layer approximation—while empirically effective—lacks a rigorous guarantee for non-SGD optimizers, and BatchNorm/Mixup break the sample-independence assumption underlying the formulation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-classification tasks (language modeling, generation), whether the last-layer statistics property holds for deeper or more heterogeneous architectures, and whether the trust-region radius and bounding constraints can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
