## Problem

While Riemannian networks on the SPD manifold are well-established, the manifold of full-rank correlation matrices—a statistically compact, normalized alternative to covariance matrices—remains largely unexplored in deep learning. Although five convenient Riemannian geometries have recently been developed for correlation matrices, no principled deep network architectures (MLR, FC, convolutional layers) have been constructed on them, leaving a gap between the available geometric theory and practical network design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-channel raw features (radar signal segments or 3D skeleton sequences) modeled as [c, n, n] SPD covariance tensors, applies a matrix power to deform their geometry, then converts them to full-rank correlation matrices via the Cor(·) operation before feeding them into the network. The output is a class label produced by a single correlation MLR layer. The network architecture is fixed as one correlation convolutional layer followed by one correlation MLR layer, with the only variable being the choice of Riemannian metric (ECM, LECM, OLM, LSM, or PHCM).

## Method

The paper systematically extends MLR, FC, and convolutional layers to the correlation manifold under five Riemannian geometries by exploiting the fact that all five are pullback metrics from simpler prototype spaces. For the four zero-curvature Log-Euclidean metrics (ECM, LECM, OLM, LSM), the isometry to Euclidean spaces yields closed-form logit expressions and explicit FC/convolutional layer formulas via the diffeomorphism and its differential at the identity matrix. For the non-zero-curvature PHCM, the correlation manifold is identified with a product of Poincaré balls via Cholesky decomposition, and existing Poincaré MLR/FC/convolutional layers are adapted using β-concatenation to handle multi-channel inputs. The paper also derives accurate backpropagation rules for the iterative operators D and D* in OLM and LSM, avoiding the approximation error of naive autograd through fixed-point iterations. All manifold-valued parameters are trivialized to Euclidean vectors via the exponential map, enabling standard Euclidean optimizers.

## Results

CorNets achieve state-of-the-art accuracy across all four datasets: 98.40% (LECM) on Radar, 82.26% (PHCM) on HDM05, 92.17% (ECM) on FPHA, and 65.04% (ECM) on NTU120. These results surpass the classic SPDNet by 5.15%, 17.69%, 6.58%, and 13.84% respectively, and outperform the best Grassmannian baselines by 7.76%, 19.07%, 6.86%, and 7.45%. The optimal metric varies by dataset (LECM for Radar, PHCM for HDM05, ECM for FPHA and NTU120), demonstrating that geometry choice is a critical hyperparameter. CorNet-ECM and CorNet-LECM are also among the most efficient methods, with per-epoch training times of 1.01s and 1.12s on Radar, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorNets consistently outperform all SPD and Grassmannian baselines across all four datasets. Against the classic SPDNet (Huang & Van Gool, 2017), CorNets gain 5.15% on Radar, 17.69% on HDM05, 6.58% on FPHA, and 13.84% on NTU120. Against the strongest SPD baselines (GyroLE, GyroAI, GyroSPD++), CorNets still lead by 2.16% on Radar (98.40 vs. 96.29), 9.09% on HDM05 (82.26 vs. 73.17), 1.44% on FPHA (92.17 vs. 90.73), and 2.83% on NTU120 (65.04 vs. 62.21). Against the best Grassmannian method (GrNet), CorNets achieve gains of 7.76%, 19.07%, 6.86%, and 7.45% respectively. Notably, CorNets share the same one-convolution-plus-MLR architecture as GyroSPD++ yet achieve both higher accuracy and significantly lower training time (e.g., 1.01s vs. 5.09s per epoch on Radar), attributed to the lighter Riemannian computations on the correlation manifold. The optimal correlation metric is dataset-dependent: LECM wins on Radar, PHCM on HDM05, and ECM on FPHA and NTU120, highlighting geometry selection as a key design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to systematically construct neural network layers (MLR, FC, convolution) on the full-rank correlation manifold, building directly on five recently developed Riemannian metrics (ECM, LECM, PHCM, OLM, LSM) from Thanwerdas & Pennec (2022b) and Thanwerdas (2024), which provided the geometric foundation but no learning architecture. It departs from the dominant SPD-manifold deep learning paradigm (Huang & Van Gool 2017; Brooks et al. 2019; Katsman et al. 2024) by treating correlation matrices as a distinct geometric object rather than a normalized SPD input, and from Chen et al. (2024c) by solving the Riemannian margin-distance optimization exactly rather than approximately. The PHCM layers additionally adapt Poincaré-ball constructions from Ganea et al. (2018) and Shimizu et al. (2021) to a product-of-hemispheres structure via Cholesky decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that correlation matrices, as statistically compact normalized alternatives to covariance matrices, require their own dedicated Riemannian geometry to be exploited effectively — naively feeding them into existing SPD networks degrades performance on 2 of 3 datasets (Table 4). The unified framework enabling seamless switching among five geometries within a fixed architecture is practically useful for practitioners, and the consistent 5–19% accuracy gains over both SPD and Grassmannian baselines validate the approach. However, the evaluation is limited to a single convolutional layer plus one MLR layer, leaving open whether the gains persist in deeper or more complex architectures (RNNs, transformers, residual networks), and whether the optimal metric choice can be learned end-to-end rather than selected per dataset.
