## Problem

Existing VLA models either build on VLMs (which degrade when fine-tuned on limited robot data) or on generative models (which lack vision-language alignment), leaving a gap where neither semantic understanding nor visual dynamics modeling is fully preserved. The paper argues that both capabilities are essential for embodied robots and that prior unified approaches using discrete VQ tokens for prediction compromise the robust vision-language alignment of pretrained VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image (224×224 third-person view in simulation; first-person on Franka; dual first/third-person views on XArm), a natural-language instruction, and proprioceptive state, and outputs a continuous action chunk of 4–10 steps via flow matching (7-D end-effector pose + gripper for Franka; 12-D for the dexterous hand). As an auxiliary output, the generation expert simultaneously predicts future visual features in the continuous space of a frozen SigLIP encoder, which is not used at inference time but shapes the learned representations.

## Method

UniJEPA adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture with three modality-specialized experts over a PaliGemma 2.3B VLM backbone: a generation expert (0.3B) that predicts future continuous visual features via MSE loss, and an action expert (0.3B) that generates actions via flow matching. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) large-scale pre-training on ~1M robot and human videos using a Text-Image-to-Embedding (TI2E) objective that jointly predicts discrete language tokens (cross-entropy) and continuous future visual features (MSE), with block-wise causal masking across modalities (image → language → image prediction → state → action); (2) embodiment-specific fine-tuning where the action expert is trained from scratch while the generation expert continues predicting future features, yielding a joint objective that couples action generation with visual foresight. The key design choice is using high-level continuous features from a frozen SigLIP encoder (rather than raw pixels or VQ-GAN tokens) as the prediction target, which the ablations show provides better temporal separability and a ~20% performance boost over pixel-level prediction.

## Results

On SimplerEnv-WindowsX, UniJEPA achieves a 71.0% average success rate, outperforming the next-best methods Villa-x (62.5%) and GR00T-N1.5 (62.0%) by 8.5–9.0 percentage points. On SimplerEnv-Google Robot, UniJEPA reaches 78.4% average success, surpassing CogAct (74.8%) by 3.6 points. On the Calvin ABC→D long-horizon benchmark, UniJEPA completes 4.11 sub-tasks on average versus 4.08 for UP-VLA. On real-world tasks, UniJEPA achieves 88%/80% (seen/unseen) on the Franka Panda arm and 70%/62% on the 12-DOF dexterous hand, with a 12-point improvement over the best baseline on unseen dexterous-hand tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniJEPA outperforms all 11 baselines across every benchmark. On SimplerEnv-WindowsX, it leads Villa-x (62.5%) and GR00T-N1.5 (62.0%) by ~9 points, CogAct (57.3%) by 13.7 points, and the architecturally similar π0 (49.8%) by 21.2 points. On SimplerEnv-Google Robot, UniJEPA (78.4%) beats CogAct (74.8%) by 3.6 points and π0-Fast (61.9%) by 16.5 points. On Calvin ABC→D, UniJEPA (4.11 avg length) edges out UP-VLA (4.08) and VPP (3.58), while GR-1 trails at 3.06. On the real-world Franka Panda, UniJEPA (88% seen / 80% unseen) surpasses VPP (83%/72%), π0 (80%/69%), and UP-VLA (78%/72%). On the 12-DOF dexterous hand, UniJEPA (70% seen / 62% unseen) achieves a 14-point gap over VPP (67%/48%) and a 12-point gap over π0 (60%/50%) on unseen tasks, with GR-1 and OpenVLA performing substantially worse (21%/16% and no dexterous-hand results reported, respectively). RT-1-X and Octo-Base are far behind on SimplerEnv (1.1% and 16.0%), confirming that scaling and architectural design matter more than raw parameter count alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniJEPA extends the VLA paradigm by unifying discrete language understanding (from VLM pretraining) with continuous visual future prediction within a single Mixture-of-Transformers architecture, building directly on the MoT framework of Liang et al. (2024) and the flow-matching action generation of π0 (Black et al., 2024). It departs from prior unified understanding-prediction approaches like UP-VLA, which quantize future predictions into discrete VQ tokens, by instead predicting continuous features from a frozen vision encoder (SigLIP), thereby preserving the VLM's native vision-language alignment. The two-stage training recipe—large-scale pretraining on 1M+ manipulation videos for joint text-image embedding, followed by embodiment-specific fine-tuning with an action expert—distinguishes it from single-stage VLA fine-tuning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a well-supported argument that continuous high-level visual features are a superior prediction target for robot policies compared to raw pixels or discrete VQ tokens, with ablations and t-SNE visualizations isolating this effect. The consistent 9–12% gains across two simulation benchmarks and two morphologically distinct real-world robots (7-DoF arm, 12-DoF dexterous hand) suggest the approach generalizes beyond a single embodiment. Open questions include whether the multi-expert MoT overhead scales favorably to larger backbones, whether the 1M-video pretraining requirement can be reduced, and how the method performs in truly open-ended, multi-embodiment deployment without per-robot fine-tuning.
