## Problem

Coarse-grained retrieval in Knowledge-Based VQA is typically performed using a single entity view (image, title, or summary) or naive score fusion across views, yet each view exhibits systematic blind spots that fundamentally bound downstream re-ranking and answering. The paper identifies that while different views are strongly complementary, naive multi-view aggregation is undermined by severe cross-view structural redundancy, and provides an information-theoretic analysis (via Fano's inequality) showing that multi-view recall gains depend critically on the effective number of non-redundant views.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query (textual question + RGB image) and a multi-modal knowledge base where each entity is indexed under three views (image, title, summary) using a frozen EVA-CLIP-8B encoder, producing three separate FAISS indexes. At inference time, top-20 candidates are retrieved per view, and SCAR operates on the union set of these candidates to produce a single ranked list of top-k entities. The final output is passed to a downstream MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, or InternVL3.5-8B) for zero-shot answer generation, with the entire SCAR pipeline adding only ~40 ms per query on E-VQA.

## Method

SCAR is a training-free, inference-time framework with two complementary modules. Module 1 (Similarity Propagation) constructs a per-view entity kNN graph over the union of retrieved candidates using Gaussian-kernel affinities, then applies closed-form manifold diffusion z = (I − αS)⁻¹y to propagate relevance scores along the graph, reinforcing semantically coherent neighborhoods and suppressing isolated false positives. Module 2 (Cross-View Redundancy Regulation) measures structural overlap between views via the Frobenius inner product of their induced affinity submatrices on shared entities; when overlap exceeds a threshold τ, it applies dual-granularity suppression to the lower-priority view—a global view-level scaling factor γ and an entity-level gating factor β on overlapping entities—before computing the final score as a weighted sum of gated diffusion scores. The theoretical motivation comes from an information-theoretic upper bound showing that single-view recall is limited by view-specific mutual information density, while multi-view gains scale with the redundancy-discounted effective view count M_eff = 1+(M−1)(1−ρ), which SCAR's two modules respectively increase (intra-view structure) and preserve (cross-view regulation).

## Results

On E-VQA, SCAR achieves Recall@20 of 65.1%, surpassing the strongest baseline OMGM (58.7%) by 6.4 points and reaching 89.2% of the multi-view coverage upper bound (73.0%). On InfoSeek, SCAR attains Recall@20 of 87.7%, outperforming ReflectiVA (86.4%) and OMGM (84.7%), and reaching 96.7% of the upper bound (90.7%). In end-to-end KB-VQA with Qwen3-VL-8B, SCAR improves BEM score from 33.4 (best single-view) to 39.8 on E-VQA and from 32.1 to 34.5 on InfoSeek, with only ~40 ms additional retrieval latency (<0.5% of total pipeline time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAR outperforms all ten baselines on both E-VQA and InfoSeek across every Recall@K metric. Against the strongest retrieval baselines using the same EVA-CLIP-8B encoder, SCAR gains +6.4 R@20 over OMGM (65.1 vs 58.7) on E-VQA and +3.0 over ReflectiVA (87.7 vs 86.4) on InfoSeek. Compared to EchoSight (image-only retrieval), SCAR improves E-VQA R@20 by 16.3 points (65.1 vs 48.8) and InfoSeek R@20 by 9.8 points (87.7 vs 77.9), demonstrating the critical benefit of multi-view indexing over single-view. Against mKG-RAG (graph-based retrieval), SCAR leads by 9.5 R@20 on E-VQA and 5.2 on InfoSeek. In end-to-end VQA (Table 10), SCAR with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 39.8 BEM on E-VQA without any finetuning, surpassing finetuned methods like ReflectiVA (35.5) and mKG-RAG (36.3), and approaching OMGM (50.2) which requires both generator and retrieval finetuning. On InfoSeek, SCAR (34.5 overall) trails finetuned OMGM (43.5) but exceeds all other training-free baselines, with the gap attributed to InfoSeek's strict exact-match evaluation amplifying generator finetuning benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAR builds on the multi-modal RAG pipeline for KB-VQA established by Wiki-LLaVA, EchoSight, ReflectiVA, and OMGM, which typically rely on single-view indexing (image or text) or heuristic score fusion across views. It repurposes classical manifold diffusion (Iscen et al., 2017; Loy et al., 2013) and kNN graph construction for entity-level similarity propagation within each view, and introduces a novel cross-view redundancy regulation mechanism inspired by the effective-modality concept from GWAS (Li & Ji, 2005). The information-theoretic upper bounds (via Fano's inequality) extend prior rate-distortion analyses for multimodal retrieval (Chen, 2025) to the KB-VQA setting, providing a principled justification for why multi-view retrieval helps and when it degrades to single-view performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper convincingly demonstrates that coarse-grained retrieval, not the MLLM or re-ranker, is the fundamental bottleneck in KB-VQA, and that a training-free, structure-aware multi-view approach can close most of the gap to the multi-view coverage upper bound (89–97%) with under 0.5% latency overhead. This reframes where effort should be invested in KB-VQA pipelines. Open questions include memory efficiency for multi-view indexing at scale (currently ~20 GB per FAISS index), whether the O(k²) graph construction becomes prohibitive for very large candidate pools, and whether MLLM-internal signals could enable adaptive, query-dependent view weighting without the fixed priority ordering used here.
