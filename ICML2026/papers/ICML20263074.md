## Problem

Existing discrete flow matching (DFM) adaptations for graph generation introduce task-specific heuristics (e.g., target-guidance terms, RL fine-tuning) that can violate the continuity equation and inflate the hyperparameter space, while their sampling efficiency remains comparable to diffusion models (500–1000 steps), leaving DFM's theoretical advantage in few-step generation largely unrealized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At sampling time, the method takes a node count N drawn from the training set and (optionally) node-type labels for conditional generation, then iteratively evolves a noise graph G_0 — initialized from marginal or absorbing-state distributions over node and edge types — into a complete graph G_1 with N node attributes and N(N−1)/2 edge attributes. The output is a single generated graph per trajectory; no fixed-size tensor or sequence is required, and the graph topology is fully determined by the edge-attribute predictions.

## Method

SimGFM builds on the vf-denoiser rate matrix (u_t = κ̇_t/(1−κ_t)·[p_{1|t}^θ(·|G_t) − δ_{G_t}]), which avoids the costly posterior expectations and Monte Carlo approximations required by Campbell's construction. A polynomial scheduler κ_t = 1−(1−t)^k concentrates denoising updates near t→1, where empirical trajectory analysis shows valid graph structures emerge, and the vf-denoiser's update coefficient k/(1−t) remains stable under high-k scheduling (unlike Campbell's time-distortion form, whose effective step vanishes). In the last k steps, where the Euler discretization of the CTMC kernel can produce negative probability mass (formally characterized in Theorem 3.1), an Endpoint Safety Projection (ESP) replaces the Euler update with direct sampling from the model posterior p_{1|t}^θ(·|G_t), which is provably a validity-preserving scheduler correction. Training uses a standard Graph Transformer denoiser supervised by cross-entropy on node and edge attributes, with no auxiliary modules or heuristic velocity terms.

## Results

On QM9, SimGFM achieves 99.5% validity with only 10 sampling steps and 99.7% validity with FCD 0.13 at 50 steps, matching DeFoG's 500-step performance (99.3% validity, FCD 0.12) with a 10× step reduction. On Planar, SimGFM reaches 99.5% V.U.N. with Ratio 1.8 at 50 steps, matching DeFoG's 1000-step result (99.5%, Ratio 1.6). On Guacamol, SimGFM attains 98.9% validity and 98.3% V.U.N. at just 50 steps, substantially outperforming DeFoG at the same budget (91.7% validity, 91.2% V.U.N.). On QM9-with-H, SimGFM at 50 steps achieves FCD 0.04, matching its 200-step result and surpassing DeFoG's 500-step FCD of 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimGFM at 50 steps matches or surpasses DeFoG at 500 steps across QM9 (99.7% vs 99.3% validity; FCD 0.13 vs 0.12) and QM9-with-H (98.4% vs 98.0% validity; FCD 0.04 vs 0.05), achieving a 10× sampling-step reduction. On Planar, SimGFM (50 steps) reaches 99.5% V.U.N. with Ratio 1.8, matching DeFoG's 1000-step result (99.5%, Ratio 1.6) and far exceeding DiGress (77.5%, Ratio 5.1 at 1000 steps) and all autoregressive baselines (≤87.5% V.U.N.). On Guacamol, SimGFM (50 steps) achieves 98.9% validity vs. DeFoG (50 steps) at 91.7%, though DeFoG retains a lower FCD (57.9 vs 79.6) at that aggressive budget; at 200 steps SimGFM reaches 99.2% validity with FCD 80.8, surpassing DeFoG (500 steps) in validity (99.0%) and FCD (73.8). On MOSES, SimGFM (200 steps) achieves the best FCD (1.08) among all iterative denoising baselines but trails DeFoG (500 steps) in validity (89.4% vs 92.8%). Against diffusion baselines (EDGE, BwR, DiGress), SimGFM dominates on Planar and Tree where diffusion methods produce 0–77.5% valid graphs, and on QM9 it matches or exceeds the best diffusion validity (99.8% vs 99.6% for Cometh) with far fewer steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimGFM builds directly on the vf-denoiser formulation from Gat et al. (2024) as its velocity-field backbone, deliberately choosing it over Campbell's construction to avoid costly posterior expectations and Monte Carlo approximations. It departs from the recent SOTA DeFoG (QIN et al., 2025) in two key ways: it rejects DeFoG's heuristic velocity-field augmentations (target-guidance and stochastic exploration terms) that can violate the DFM continuity equation, and it repurposes DeFoG's polynomial time-distortion scheduler for the vf-denoiser backbone rather than Campbell's. The Endpoint Safety Projection (ESP) is introduced as a theoretically grounded correction (Theorem 3.1) rather than an ad-hoc numerical patch, and the paper provides a formal incompatibility analysis showing Campbell's time-distortion updates vanish under high-order schedulers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: an order-of-magnitude reduction in sampling steps (10–50 vs. 500–1000) while matching or exceeding SOTA validity on molecular and generic graph benchmarks, making discrete flow matching for graphs far more deployable. The empirical observation that valid graph structures emerge almost exclusively near t→1 under uniform denoising is a useful diagnostic that could inform scheduler design in other discrete generative settings. Open questions include the validity–diversity trade-off at high step counts (noted on Planar), whether the approach extends to hyperbolic or Riemannian generative spaces, and whether SMILES-based generators' distributional advantages over graph-native methods can be closed.
