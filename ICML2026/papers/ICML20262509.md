## Problem

No widely adopted holistic benchmark exists for evaluating scientific reasoning in LLMs, as individual benchmarks (GPQA, MMLU-Pro, LabBench, etc.) each cover narrow domains, formats, or skill types, hiding cross-benchmark insights. Additionally, no analytical framework systematically disentangles the distinct contributions of domain knowledge retrieval versus multi-step reasoning to performance on scientific tasks, making it unclear whether failures stem from missing knowledge, poor knowledge recall, or weak reasoning.

## Input / Output

The evaluation suite consumes scientific reasoning questions in multiple formats (multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, structured, and protocol/procedural) across physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, materials, mathematics, computer science, and engineering. The KRUX probing framework additionally takes reasoning traces from a source model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) and produces atomic, self-contained 'knowledge ingredients' (KIs) — generalizable factual statements with no answer leakage — which are then prepended in-context to the original question before evaluation. The controlled SFT experiments produce fine-tuned 7B/8B checkpoints (Qwen-Math, Qwen-STEM, Qwen-BOTH, and Llama counterparts) trained on ~462K math and ~512K STEM reasoning traces from SYNTHETIC-1.

## Method

The paper introduces three interlocking components. First, SCIREAS unifies 10 public benchmarks under a single vLLM-optimized harness with standardized prompts and scoring, applying a two-fold manual annotation protocol (90.1% inter-annotator agreement) to retain only subtasks requiring both domain knowledge and multi-step reasoning, reducing instances by ~50% while preserving domain coverage. Second, SCIREAS-PRO (1,260 instances) is constructed by selecting questions where reasoning models fail under low inference budget but succeed under high budget, validated by cross-model agreement (>70% overlap) and human/LLM-judge confirmation that failures are reasoning-driven (90–91%). Third, KRUX extracts KIs from reasoning traces using a strong LLM as extractor, then injects these KIs in-context to the tested model; gains over a no-KI baseline indicate a knowledge bottleneck, while persistent errors point to reasoning limits. Controlled SFT on math-only vs. STEM-only vs. combined SYNTHETIC-1 subsets isolates whether reasoning fine-tuning adds new knowledge or improves surfacing of existing parametric knowledge, with the key finding that base models + external KIs can outperform reasoning-fine-tuned models by ≥10%, and that KIs from math-fine-tuned models boost base models even when the underlying facts were already parameterized.

## Results

On SCIREAS (15,567 instances across 10 benchmarks), frontier reasoning models achieve 66–70% average accuracy, with o3-High at 68.4% and GPT-5-High at 69.5%. The KRUX framework reveals that base models augmented with DeepSeek-R1 knowledge ingredients (KIs) outperform their reasoning-fine-tuned counterparts by ≥10% on GPQA (e.g., Qwen w/ R1 KIs: 47.19 vs. Qwen-BOTH: 40.81) and LabBench (41.40 vs. 33.83), identifying knowledge retrieval as the primary bottleneck. On SCIREAS-PRO (1,260 reasoning-intensive instances), the authors' Qwen-BOTH SFT checkpoint reaches 21.11% accuracy, competitive with concurrent efforts like SYNTHETIC-1-SFT (19.44%) but below OpenR1 (26.43%) and Llama-Nemotron (23.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Qwen-BOTH SFT checkpoint (42.84 / 21.11 on SCIREAS / SCIREAS-PRO) outperforms SYNTHETIC-1-SFT (37.64 / 19.44) by +5.2 and +1.7 points respectively, and General-Reasoner (34.99 / 13.73) by +7.9 and +7.4 points, demonstrating that their controlled SFT recipe is competitive with both SFT and RL-based concurrent efforts. However, it trails OpenR1 (43.08 / 26.43) by 0.24 and 5.32 points, and Llama-Nemotron (43.53 / 23.75) by 4.98 and 7.24 points on the Llama family, indicating that SFT+RL and more aggressive SFT pipelines still hold an edge on the harder SCIREAS-PRO subset. The gap is most pronounced on SCIREAS-PRO, where the reasoning-intensive filtering amplifies differences between training methods. On the KRUX knowledge-utilization experiments (Table 2), the authors' reasoning-fine-tuned models (e.g., Qwen-BOTH at 40.81 on GPQA) fall behind base models augmented with external R1 KIs (47.19), a finding that holds across all four concurrent baselines tested (General-Reasoner at 35.94, Llama-Nemotron at 37.95), reinforcing that knowledge retrieval—not reasoning per se—is the dominant bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies ten existing scientific benchmarks (GPQA, MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, LabBench, etc.) into a single standardized evaluation suite (SCIREAS), departing from prior work where each benchmark operates in isolation with its own prompts and scoring. For the knowledge-vs-reasoning disentanglement question, it departs from prior approaches that use synthetic multi-hop questions, linguistic obfuscation, or external classifiers to label question types; instead, KRUX holds knowledge constant by injecting extracted 'knowledge ingredients' in-context and varies only the target model, providing a cleaner controlled comparison. The controlled SFT experiments build directly on the SYNTHETIC-1 dataset and are positioned against concurrent reasoning post-training efforts (OpenR1, Llama-Nemotron, General-Reasoner), serving as reliable investigation checkpoints rather than SOTA claims.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that retrieving task-relevant knowledge from parameters is a critical bottleneck (with base models + external KIs outperforming reasoning-fine-tuned models by ≥10%) redirects attention from pure reasoning enhancement toward knowledge retrieval and utilization as a first-order concern for scientific LLMs. KRUX provides a practical diagnostic tool for model developers to determine whether failures stem from missing knowledge, retrieval difficulty, or reasoning limits, guiding targeted improvements. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond 7B/8B models, whether external memory modules (e.g., COMPACTDB) can close the gap at scale, and how to jointly optimize knowledge surfacing and reasoning in a single training objective.
