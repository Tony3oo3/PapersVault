## Problem

Contemporary world models optimize for future prediction or reconstruction in latent/observation space, objectives that can be satisfied by learning entangled composite transformations without discovering explicit, reusable mechanisms. This paper asks whether a model can instead learn to construct internal theories—executable, compositional programs that explain how observations are generated and transformed—from raw, non-textual observations alone, without language, task labels, or program supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired observations (x, y) with no additional supervision—ranging from 10×10 grid states, integer pairs, to 32×32 CIFAR-10 images—and produces two outputs: a discrete latent program (an ordered sequence of primitives selected from a learned VQ-VAE codebook) and a reconstructed target observation. The program is the primary object of inference: it must be a composition of reusable primitives transferable to new inputs generated by the same underlying mechanism, rather than an instance-specific mapping. The MDL principle adaptively selects the shortest program length that accurately explains the observation, and at test time the inferred program is applied to a novel input to evaluate transferability.

## Method

NEO (Neural Theorizer) is a variational model that jointly learns a discrete primitive vocabulary (via a VQ-VAE codebook serving as a learned Language of Thought) and a shared deterministic transition model that executes primitives as latent state transformations. A goal-conditioned theory programmer q_φ(z|s_k, y) sequentially selects the next primitive given the current latent state and the encoded target, constructing an execution trace s_1 → s_2 → … → s_{K+1} whose final state is decoded to reconstruct y. The MDL principle selects the shortest accurate explanation length k* by minimizing λ_MDL^k · ℓ(y, ŷ_k) over all intermediate steps, and backpropagation is truncated to only that step, enforcing a simplicity bias that favors short yet accurate program compositions. A state grounding loss (decode-encode cycle with stop-gradient) anchors each intermediate state to the encoder's latent manifold, preventing degenerate off-manifold transitions that would break compositional reusability. At test time, NEO-S extends inference with temperature-scaled sampling over B candidate programs and majority voting, enabling test-time scaling that dramatically improves long-horizon compositional generalization.

## Results

On GridWorld (α=0.33), NEO achieves 0.934 self-explainability and 0.933 transferability on compositional OOD, and 0.853/0.845 on length OOD, while all monolithic baselines collapse to 0.000 transfer. With test-time scaling (NEO-S, B=64), performance rises to 0.995/0.976 (comp. OOD) and 0.978/0.907 (length OOD). On Arithmetic Factorization Reasoning (α=0.66), NEO-S reaches 0.994/0.959 on compositional OOD and 0.766/0.696 on length OOD, versus near-zero for all baselines. On Image Editing (CIFAR-10, α=0.33), NEO achieves the lowest L1 distance of 0.11/0.12 on compositional OOD and 0.12/0.13 on length OOD, compared to 0.16–0.19 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEO decisively outperforms all three monolithic baselines on transferability across every OOD setting. Disc-Mono (corresponding to LAPO/Genie-style discrete latent action models) transfers well in-distribution (e.g., 0.983 at α=0.33) but drops to exactly 0.000 on both compositional and length OOD, confirming that a single discrete code cannot decompose unseen compositions. Cont-Mono (AdaWorld-style continuous latent) achieves high self-explainability on OOD (e.g., 0.929 on comp. OOD at α=0.66) yet still yields near-zero transfer (0.000), indicating it encodes y-specific information rather than a reusable program. Cont-Mono-Opt (LPN-inspired, with test-time gradient optimization) improves self-explainability further (0.972 comp. OOD at α=0.66) but transfer remains 0.000–0.001, showing that optimizing a monolithic vector at test time cannot compensate for the absence of compositional structure. NEO's advantage is most dramatic on transferability: at α=0.33 on GridWorld, NEO achieves 0.933 comp. OOD transfer versus 0.000 for all baselines, and NEO-S pushes this to 0.976. On Arithmetic Factorization, NEO-S reaches 0.696 length-OOD transfer at α=0.66 versus 0.001 for the best baseline, a gap of over 690×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of latent action models (LAPO, Genie, AdaWorld), neural program induction (NPI, DreamCoder, LPN), and developmental cognitive science's 'theory-theory' of cognition. Unlike prior world models that optimize for next-observation prediction, and unlike prior program induction that relies on symbolic supervision, task grouping (ARC-AGI style), or predefined DSLs, NEO learns compositional executable programs purely from i.i.d. observation pairs with no program annotations or task labels. The discrete primitive mechanism borrows from VQ-VAE, the probabilistic framework from variational inference, and the program-length regularization from the Minimum Description Length principle, but the specific combination—inferring a learned Language of Thought from raw sensory pairs and evaluating via program transferability—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces both a new learning paradigm (Learning-to-Theorize) and a benchmark (OTIB) that shifts the evaluation criterion from reconstruction accuracy to whether inferred programs transfer across instances, directly testing for reusable explanatory structure. The finding that state grounding is critical (complete collapse without it) and that compositional structure enables a new test-time scaling axis via sampling-based search are practically useful insights for anyone building structured latent models. However, the current formulation is limited to small discrete primitive sets, short programs, and controlled synthetic benchmarks; extending L2T to real-world environments with stochastic dynamics, continuous action spaces, and open-ended theory spaces remains a significant open question.
