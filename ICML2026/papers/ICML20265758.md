## Problem

Existing offline multi-agent RL methods are tied to task-specific architectures with fixed observation and action encodings, limiting generalization across heterogeneous tasks and agent configurations. The paper addresses three coupled challenges: representation mismatch across diverse input/output structures, the incompatibility of centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) within a single model, and sparse episodic reward signals that complicate credit assignment in sequence-modeling approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265758/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's numerical observation vector (own features, visible allies, visible enemies with positions, health, shields) is verbalized into a structured natural-language user message in LLaMA-3 chat format, stacked with prior observation-action dialogue turns to form the full context. The model outputs a single verbalized discrete action (e.g., 'attack ID 2', 'move north one step'); if the argmax action is not in the environment's available-action set, the model resamples from a truncated top-k/top-p distribution. Sequences are truncated at 1024 tokens with dynamic packing to handle variable trajectory lengths and agent counts.

## Method

DLM reformulates cooperative multi-agent decision-making as dialogue-style autoregressive sequence prediction over a pre-trained LLaMA-3.2-1B backbone, where each agent's trajectory is a sequence of (observation-as-user, action-as-assistant) turns. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) full-parameter SFT on half the offline dataset to align the model with multi-agent decision demonstrations under CTDE (inter-agent context visible during training, local-only at inference), and (2) GRPO with LoRA (rank 128) on the remaining half, using a lightweight executability-based reward that assigns normalized return-to-go for matching valid dataset actions, zero for valid but mismatched actions, and −1 for infeasible actions. Before GRPO, OOD-prone samples are filtered by retaining only those where the SFT model's prediction deviates from the dataset action or violates action constraints, focusing optimization on challenging observations. This two-stage design separates behavior imitation from robustness refinement, enabling a single unified model to handle all 15 SMAC tasks without per-task retraining.

## Results

On six representative SMAC tasks, DLM-GRPO (a single unified model) achieves win rates of 0.97, 1.00, 0.80, 0.94, 0.75, and 0.92, outperforming all LLM-based baselines (MADT, Gato, SayCan) and matching or surpassing value-based offline MARL methods (OMIGA, CFCQL). On zero-shot unseen tasks, DLM-GRPO reaches 1.00 on 8M and MMM, and 0.58–0.79 on SMACv2 tasks where MADT and Gato both score 0.00. On the LBF benchmark, DLM-GRPO achieves 0.96±0.02, exceeding the best value-based baseline OMIGA (0.85±0.07) by 11 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against value-based offline MARL baselines, DLM-GRPO matches or surpasses OMIGA and CFCQL on average win rates with a single unified model, while DLM-SFT alone performs comparably to MACQL; both DLM variants consistently outperform TD3+BC and BC, which degrade sharply on hard and super-hard tasks. Among LLM-based sequence-modeling baselines, DLM-GRPO outperforms MADT by 6–22 percentage points across all six tasks in Table 1 (e.g., 0.92 vs. 0.64 on Corridor, 0.75 vs. 0.53 on 6h_vs_8z), and beats Gato by 10–38 points on the hardest tasks (0.75 vs. 0.37 on 6h_vs_8z). SayCan, evaluated zero-shot with the same LLaMA-3.2-1B backbone, achieves near-zero win rates (≤0.10) across all tasks, highlighting the necessity of task-specific fine-tuning. On zero-shot generalization to unseen SMACv2 tasks (Protoss_5_vs_5, Terran_5_vs_5, Zerg_5_vs_5), DLM-GRPO scores 0.58–0.79 while both MADT and Gato score 0.00, demonstrating a decisive advantage in structural generalization. On the LBF benchmark, DLM-GRPO (0.96) exceeds OMIGA (0.85), CFCQL (0.77), MACQL (0.69), and TD3+BC (0.30), confirming cross-benchmark superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

DLM extends the decision-transformer line of work (DT, TT, Gato) from single-agent sequence modeling to the multi-agent offline setting, and repurposes the GRPO alignment procedure from DeepSeekMath as a lightweight post-training stage for policy refinement. It departs from prior offline MARL methods (CFCQL, OMIGA, MACQL, TD3+BC) by replacing task-specific value networks with a single LLM backbone, and from prior LLM-based MARL approaches (MADT, Gato, SayCan) by introducing a dialogue-style trajectory representation that natively supports CTDE—centralized training with inter-agent context and decentralized execution from local observations. The two-stage SFT-then-GRPO pipeline with executability-based reward functions is a novel combination not present in either the offline MARL or LLM-alignment literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 1B-parameter LLM, trained offline on verbalized trajectories, can match or exceed per-task value-based offline MARL methods across 15 SMAC tasks while achieving zero-shot transfer to structurally novel SMACv2 scenarios where other LLM-based methods (MADT, Gato) score near zero. This suggests a path toward scalable, reusable multi-agent policies that avoid the per-task retraining bottleneck. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to discrete-action game benchmarks, reliance on a small (1B) model whose capacity may not suffice for continuous or high-dimensional action spaces, and the computational overhead of LLM inference at deployment. Open questions include whether the dialogue-style CTDE formulation and GRPO alignment generalize to larger models, continuous control, and real-world multi-agent systems.
