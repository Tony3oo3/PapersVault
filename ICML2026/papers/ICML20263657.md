## Problem

Stock market simulation faces a persistent trade-off between structural fidelity (accurate market microstructure such as continuous double auction and limit order books) and behavioral fidelity (agents that perceive and respond to rich unstructured information). Traditional agent-based models like ABIDES preserve market structure but rely on simplistic heuristic agents, while recent LLM-driven approaches improve behavioral realism but sacrifice structural fidelity through turn-based trading and small, oversimplified agent populations. The paper asks whether a unified framework can simultaneously achieve both forms of fidelity at scale.

## Input / Output

The system consumes over 12,000 real-world news articles, policy documents, and corporate earnings reports aligned to the simulation period, along with live order book data, technical indicators, and empirically grounded institutional agent profiles. It produces simulated high-frequency stock price series at minute-level granularity, full limit order book dynamics, and individual agent trading decisions, all generated endogenously through a nanosecond-resolution NASDAQ-like CDA matching engine. Notably, LLM-generated indicative prices (expressed in cents) parameterize trader execution but never directly set transaction prices, which emerge from order matching and liquidity conditions.

## Method

MarketSim introduces a hierarchical multi-agent architecture organized across three scales. At the micro level, each simulated institution contains an LLM-powered manager agent (using DeepSeek-R1) that performs low-frequency strategic reasoning over news, policies, and market data, maintaining short-term/long-term memory and self-reflection, and outputs an indicative price μ_t plus a strategy weight vector w_t; a team of lightweight trader agents then samples strategies from a categorical distribution over w_t and executes at nanosecond speed, decoupling slow LLM reasoning from fast HFT. At the meso level, the market operates as an asynchronous event-driven CDA system with a central matching engine maintaining a limit order book under strict price-time priority. At the macro level, an evolving information landscape combines endogenous order book signals with exogenous real-world news and policy shocks. Over 15,000 heterogeneous agents (institutional, retail, market makers, noise) interact within this environment, and the framework is evaluated against a new benchmark spanning 8 GICS sectors, 3 real-world shock scenarios, 5 stylized facts, and 5 quantitative price metrics.

## Results

MarketSim achieves an average MAPE of 3.48% across 12 stocks from 8 GICS sectors and 3 shock scenarios, with Q-Q correlations exceeding 0.97 in all experiments. On the JNJ Liberation Day tariff case (Table 1), MarketSim attains RMSE 1.614, MAPE 0.816%, DTW distance 0.011, Q-Q correlation 0.993, and volatility similarity 0.796. The framework reproduces all five stylized facts (absence of linear autocorrelation, fat tails, aggregated Gaussianity, volatility clustering, non-stationarity) across every scenario, a property no baseline achieves. Under extreme shocks (e.g., VST during DeepSeek debut, ~40% drop), accuracy degrades to MAPE 13.95%, but the correct directional trend is still captured.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MarketSim against two classes of baselines: (i) predictive models (MA, ARIMA, Linear Regression, LightGBM, LSTM, Transformer) and (ii) ABM baselines built by embedding those same predictive models into the ABIDES framework. On the JNJ tariff case, MarketSim reduces RMSE by ~11% and MAPE by ~24% relative to the strongest predictive baseline (Linear Regression: RMSE 1.809, MAPE 1.067%), and by ~11% and ~24% relative to the strongest ABM baseline (Linear-ABIDES: RMSE 1.816, MAPE 1.071%). The gap widens dramatically on volatility similarity, where MarketSim (0.796) nearly doubles the best ABM (0.556, Transformer-ABIDES) and outperforms the best predictive model (0.156) by over 5×. On stylized facts, MarketSim is the only method reproducing all five properties; no predictive or ABM baseline achieves this. The authors note the comparison is inherently conservative for MarketSim, since predictive models perform curve-fitting on a black-box signal while MarketSim generates prices endogenously from agent interactions, making direct numerical comparison a lower bound on the true advantage of mechanism-based simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MarketSim sits at the intersection of two research lines that have historically been in tension: traditional agent-based market simulation (ABIDES, Santa Fe) which achieves structural fidelity through CDA and nanosecond-resolution order matching but relies on rule-based agents with limited behavioral realism, and LLM-driven agent simulations (TwinMarket, StockAgent, Gao et al.) which improve behavioral fidelity by letting agents reason over news and policy but sacrifice structural fidelity by replacing CDA with turn-based trading. The paper's core architectural departure is a hierarchical manager-trader design within each institution, where an LLM-powered manager agent handles low-frequency strategic reasoning (price estimation, strategy allocation) while lightweight trader agents execute at nanosecond speed, thereby decoupling the computational cost of LLM inference from the temporal demands of high-frequency market microstructure. This is layered on top of an ABIDES-style CDA engine and a macro-level information landscape of 12k+ real-world documents, unifying structural and behavioral fidelity in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the persistent trade-off between structural and behavioral fidelity in market simulation can be resolved, and that emergent market complexity (all five stylized facts, firm-specific shock responses) requires both simultaneously — a finding supported by ablations showing that degrading either dimension substantially degrades realism. As a practical testbed, MarketSim enables stress-testing of intervention strategies (e.g., liquidity shocks, herd behavior) in a controlled environment, which is valuable for regulatory and risk-management research. Key limitations include the single-stock focus, short-horizon (3–5 day) shock windows, dependence on a specific LLM (DeepSeek-R1) for best results, and the acknowledged difficulty of extending to cross-asset contagion, longer institutional adaptation horizons, and non-US market microstructures (A-shares, Nikkei). The computational cost of running 15k+ agents with LLM calls at scale remains an open practical question.
