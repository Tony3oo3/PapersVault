## Problem

Monocular 3D shape recovery must generalize across arbitrary viewpoints, large template-target shape variations, and unseen object categories, yet existing deformation-based methods rely on visual encoders trained from scratch on limited datasets and fail to capture robust semantic correspondences when the target deviates significantly from the template. Generative reconstruction methods, while high-fidelity, hallucinate implausible geometry in self-occluded regions and remain sensitive to viewpoint changes. The paper addresses how to leverage the category-agnostic semantic representations of 2D foundation models to guide 3D template deformation in a way that is robust to both shape variation and perspective disparity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image of the target object, a category-level template point cloud of N=1024 points, and 16 pre-rendered template views (512×512) with known camera extrinsics. It outputs a per-point deformation field D ∈ R^{N×3} predicted in a single forward pass (t=0 in the flow), yielding the deformed shape T̂ = S + D along with explicit dense template-to-target point correspondences. The template is consumed as a point cloud rather than a mesh, and the target requires no depth or multi-view input—only one RGB observation.

## Method

The core framework formulates shape deformation as a conditional rectified flow matching problem, training a velocity network v_θ to transport the template point cloud to the target under image conditioning, with single-step inference at t=0. The key mechanism is geometry-guided foundation feature modeling: DINOv3 ViT-B/16 patch features (768-dim) are lifted onto visible template points, then propagated to occluded regions via a softmax-weighted aggregation over geometric affinities computed from Point-MAE embeddings, producing a complete N-point semantic representation. A cross-attention alignment module then lets each template point (as query) attend to the flattened target image features (as keys/values) to establish point-wise template-target correspondences. To handle viewpoint disparity between the fixed template and arbitrary target view, a view-adaptive aggregation module selects the most semantically consistent template view as primary, encodes relative camera poses into 12-dim vectors projected to feature space, and fuses auxiliary views via pose-modulated cross-view attention. The deformation backbone is PointTransformerV3, trained with a composite loss combining flow matching, Chamfer distance, Laplacian smoothness, ARAP rigidity, deformation magnitude regularization, and multi-view silhouette supervision.

## Results

On seen categories with retrieved templates, Our-MV achieves CD 2.38×10⁻³, EMD 4.69×10⁻², and S-IoU 48.79%, outperforming ShapeMatcher (CD 5.92) and KP-RED (CD 3.05). Under the more challenging random-template setting, Our-MV maintains CD 2.46 and S-IoU 47.31%, while ShapeMatcher degrades severely to CD 13.02 and KP-RED to CD 5.10. On unseen categories (where baselines are inapplicable), Our-MV achieves CD 3.69, EMD 5.42, and S-IoU 52.38% with retrieved templates, demonstrating strong cross-category generalization. Against the diffusion-based TAX3D, our method achieves lower CD (2.46 vs. 5.83) and EMD (4.86 vs. 6.49) while running 5× faster (0.64 s vs. 3.23 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two deformation baselines, Our-MV consistently wins on all metrics: on seen categories with retrieved templates, it reduces CD from 5.92 (ShapeMatcher) and 3.05 (KP-RED) to 2.38, and S-IoU improves from 40.47/46.73 to 48.79%. The gap widens dramatically under random templates, where ShapeMatcher's CD nearly triples (13.02 vs. 2.46) and KP-RED's CD more than doubles (5.10 vs. 2.46), while Our-MV remains nearly unchanged—evidence that geometry-guided foundation feature modeling is robust to large template–target shape variations. Neither ShapeMatcher nor KP-RED can be applied to unseen categories (they require per-category training), whereas Our-MV achieves S-IoU 52.38% on unseen objects. Against the 3D generative baselines (LRM, Wonder3D, Phidias), the paper reports qualitative comparisons showing that these methods hallucinate implausible geometry in self-occluded regions (e.g., missing mug handles or table legs), while the deformation approach preserves template topology. Finally, against TAX3D (a diffusion-based deformation method), Our-MV achieves substantially lower CD (2.46 vs. 5.83) and EMD (4.86 vs. 6.49) with 5× faster inference (0.64 s vs. 3.23 s), attributed to single-step rectified flow versus iterative denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of template-based shape deformation (Pixel2Mesh, ShapeMatcher, KP-RED) and the growing use of 2D foundation model features for 3D tasks. Prior deformation methods either train visual encoders from scratch on limited category-specific data or treat templates merely as feature conditioning rather than geometric starting points, limiting generalization. The key departure here is repurposing DINOv3 foundation features as the semantic backbone for deformation, combined with a geometry-guided propagation mechanism that diffuses 2D semantics across the 3D template surface and a view-adaptive pose-aware aggregation module. The flow-matching formulation for single-step deformation follows the rectified-flow line (Liu et al., 2023d) but applies it to the template-to-target correspondence problem rather than generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generalize deformation to unseen categories without per-category training addresses a long-standing limitation of template-based reconstruction, with direct practical value for robotic manipulation (77% real-world grasp success on a humanoid robot). The finding that naive multi-view feature averaging without pose encoding is worse than single-view use is a useful caution for practitioners combining multi-view foundation features. A key open question is whether single-view visual evidence can resolve intrinsic shape ambiguity under extreme viewpoints (as shown in the failure analysis), and the authors propose multimodal (language) priors as a promising direction. The framework's reliance on a pre-sampled multi-view template set also raises questions about scalability to open-world settings where such templates may not be available.
