## Problem

Existing generative models for heterogeneous catalysts address only fragments of the discovery pipeline: bulk crystal generators (DiffCSP, CDVAE) lack slab construction, adsorbate placers (AdsorbDiff) require fixed slab inputs, and autoregressive models (CatGPT) treat slab and adsorbate as independent sequential tokens. No prior framework co-generates slab structures and adsorbate coordinates within a unified objective, leaving the intrinsic coupling between surface geometry and adsorbate interactions unmodeled.

## Input / Output

For de novo generation, the model takes only the adsorbate atomic species as a condition and outputs the full slab-adsorbate system in a factorized form: primitive cell (atomic species, Cartesian coordinates, Niggli-reduced lattice parameters), an integer transformation matrix M, a vacuum scaling factor k_vac, and adsorbate Cartesian coordinates. For structure prediction, the primitive cell composition is additionally provided as a fixed condition, and the model predicts only the continuous geometric variables. The factorized representation reduces the number of learnable variables by an average of 9.2× (up to 96×) compared to generating the full slab atom-by-atom, and the adsorbate coordinates are decomposed into a center-of-mass vector and relative atomic positions to decouple placement from internal geometry.

## Method

CATFLOW trains a single DiT-based transformer to co-generate slab and adsorbate components via a joint flow matching objective that combines continuous flow matching (L1 loss) for geometric variables—primitive cell coordinates, lattice parameters, the transformation matrix M (relaxed to continuous values during training, rounded at inference), vacuum scaling factor, and adsorbate coordinates—with discrete flow matching (cross-entropy loss) using a [MASK] token for primitive cell atomic species. The architecture follows an encoder–token transformer–decoder hierarchy: an atom encoder embeds element one-hots and noisy geometric features, a token transformer of 24 DiT blocks captures global slab-adsorbate interactions, and a decoder with separate heads predicts atom-level coordinates and system-level parameters via pooling. At inference, continuous variables are generated by solving the ODE from t=0 to t=1, while discrete species are revealed through iterative Bernoulli unmasking. For structure prediction, the discrete compositional variables are fixed and the model is trained with only the continuous flow matching loss, focusing entirely on the geometric distribution conditioned on the given atomic species.

## Results

On the OC20 de novo generation task, CATFLOW achieves 97.33% structural validity, 94.69% uniqueness, and a mean system energy difference (ΔE_sys) of 28.01 eV, outperforming CatGPT (92.67% validity, 79.91% uniqueness, 33.13 eV). For structure prediction, CATFLOW reaches 98.16% validity, 11.09% match rate, and 16.46% adsorption-energy success rate (|ΔE_ads| ≤ 0.1 eV), surpassing the DiffCSP+AdsorbDiff two-step pipeline (64.95%, 7.71%, 10.57%). On the global-minima coverage test over 100 OC20-Dense systems, CATFLOW achieves a 15.2% success rate with a mean |ΔE| of 0.38 eV, compared to 10.5% and 1.95 eV for random+heuristic placement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CatGPT (Mok & Back, 2024) on de novo generation, CATFLOW wins on every metric: structural validity improves by 4.7 pp (97.33% vs 92.67%), uniqueness by 14.8 pp (94.69% vs 79.91%), and ΔE_sys drops by 5.1 eV (28.01 vs 33.13), indicating generated structures sit closer to local energy minima. Compositional diversity is essentially tied (15.07 vs 15.07). Against the DiffCSP+AdsorbDiff (DC+AD) two-step pipeline on structure prediction, CATFLOW achieves a 33.2 pp validity advantage (98.16% vs 64.95%), a 44% relative improvement in match rate (11.09% vs 7.71%), and a 56% relative gain in adsorption-energy success rate (16.46% vs 10.57%). Notably, this comparison favors the baseline, which receives the ground-truth transformation matrix and vacuum scaling factor, while CATFLOW predicts both from scratch. On the global-minima coverage benchmark, CATFLOW (15.2% success, 0.38 eV mean error) substantially outperforms random+heuristic placement (10.5%, 1.95 eV), confirming that the learned geometric distribution captures meaningful thermodynamic interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATFLOW builds on the conditional flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (2023) for continuous variables and the discrete flow matching with masking mechanism from OMatG (Hollmer et al., 2025) for atomic species, unifying both in a single transformer-based architecture. It departs from prior catalyst generative models—CatGPT (autoregressive, sequential slab-then-adsorbate tokenization), the DiffCSP+AdsorbDiff two-step pipeline, and Catalyst GFlowNet (restricted to 12 single-element compositions)—by formulating slab-adsorbate generation as a joint co-generation problem within one flow matching objective. The factorized representation (primitive cell, transformation matrix, vacuum scaling, adsorbate) is a novel decomposition that explicitly encodes surface orientation while exploiting the periodic redundancy of slab structures, a design choice absent from all prior catalyst or bulk-crystal generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CATFLOW provides the first end-to-end framework that simultaneously generates slab geometry and adsorbate placement, closing a gap where prior methods required fixed slabs or sequential pipelines. The 9.2× reduction in learnable variables that simultaneously improves proximity to local energy minima (lower ΔE_sys, fewer relaxation steps) suggests that exploiting structural redundancy is not merely a compression trick but a genuine inductive bias for catalytic systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-adsorbate systems, more complex oxide or alloy surfaces (OC25), and whether the generated structures can be validated experimentally; the current evaluation relies on ML potential relaxation (uma-s-1p1) rather than DFT, and the chemical space remains bounded by OC20's training distribution.
