## Problem

The reverse KL divergence, the dominant constraint in RL-based LLM post-training (PPO, GRPO, DPO), has two fundamental limitations: it multiplicatively scales probability updates by the reference probability, making it prohibitively difficult to boost high-reward actions with low priors, and it shifts the probabilities of nearly all actions in proportion to absolute reward values, rendering the policy highly sensitive to reward noise. The paper asks whether a different constraint geometry can overcome both limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a one-step MDP where a text prompt serves as the state and the generated text response is the action, with rewards from preference models or verifiers. The L1-norm between the current and reference policies is estimated unbiasedly via importance sampling from the rollout policy (analogous to KL estimation in GRPO), avoiding the intractable full-vocabulary sum. In offline settings, a dynamically adjusted coefficient λ_i scales the reward term based on the estimated L1-norm to prevent numerical overflow when the rollout and current policies diverge significantly.

## Method

MRPO replaces the KL divergence constraint with an L1-norm penalty on policy updates, yielding a closed-form optimal policy that reallocates probability mass additively: all mass from actions below a reward threshold (r⁺ − 2β) is transferred to maximum-reward actions, while intermediate-reward actions retain their reference probabilities exactly. This additive, sparse mechanism is proven (Theorem 4.1) to impose a stricter penalty on small perturbations (enforcing sparsity) yet a weaker penalty on large shifts (enabling substantial updates), the opposite profile of KL. Two practical variants are derived: MRPO-G (GRPO-style with clipped importance ratios) and MRPO-R (REINFORCE-style with log-ratio clipping), both using Monte Carlo L1 estimation. The paper further establishes a TRPO-style monotonic improvement bound under the expected L1-norm (Theorem 5.1) and shows that in the critical regime where the reference probability of the optimal action approaches zero, the L1-constrained policy achieves k→0 contraction toward the optimum while the KL-constrained policy stagnates at k→1 (Theorem 5.2).

## Results

On preference alignment (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct), MRPO-R achieves 69.20% WR2 and 76.15% LC2 on AlpacaEval, and 96.00% / 84.09% win rates against GPT-4 / Gemini-2 on Arena-Hard, roughly doubling the gains of the best KL-based baseline (GRPO: 53.97% WR2, 60.51% LC2, 61.80% GPT-4, 32.22% Gemini-2). On mathematical reasoning (GSM8k), MRPO-G reaches 84.08% pass@1, surpassing DAPO (83.09%). On the harder Code-R1 task, MRPO-G achieves 33.56% pass@1, outperforming GRPO (26.97%), Dr-GRPO (31.60%), and DAPO (13.62%). In offline RL with binary rewards, MRPO-G attains 53.20 average on Gemma-7b and 38.75 on Llama2-7b, exceeding DPO (52.72 / 38.17) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRPO outperforms all 12 baselines across every experimental scenario. In preference alignment, MRPO-R surpasses GRPO (the strongest R-KL baseline) by 129% in WR2 gain and 363% in Gemini-2 win-rate gain on Llama3.1, and by 2.19× and 4.22× on Mistral; it also beats the alternative-constraint methods DPH-F (F-KL) and DPH-JS (JS) by 13–20 points on WR2. In RLVR, MRPO-G exceeds DAPO by 20.94 points on Code-R1 pass@1 (33.56 vs. 13.62) and by 1 point on GSM8k (84.08 vs. 83.09), while also outperforming Dr-GRPO (31.60) and GRPO (26.97) on Code-R1. In offline RL with binary rewards, MRPO-G achieves the highest average score on both Gemma-7b (53.20 vs. DPO's 52.72) and Llama2-7b (38.75 vs. DPO's 38.17), with the largest margin in complex reasoning (+1.31 over DPO on Gemma). Under a weaker 2B reward model, MRPO-G outperforms the best baseline (DPH-F) by 8.27 points on WR2 (48.51 vs. 40.24), while PPO actually regresses below the base model, confirming MRPO's robustness to reward noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the near-universal use of reverse KL divergence as the policy constraint in LLM RL (PPO, GRPO, DPO, BNPO, etc.) by proposing an L1-norm constraint instead. It builds directly on GRPO's token-level objective structure and REINFORCE-style policy gradients, replacing the KL penalty with an L1 penalty estimated via importance sampling. The theoretical analysis extends TRPO's trust-region framework to the expected L1-norm setting, and the paper positions itself against alternative divergences (forward KL, JS) by arguing they remain ratio-based and thus share KL's fundamental limitations for long-tailed LLM policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRPO offers a simple, drop-in replacement for KL regularization in existing RL pipelines (GRPO, REINFORCE) with no additional optimization complexity, making it immediately practical. The sparsity-inducing property of L1—strictly preserving intermediate-reward actions while aggressively boosting low-prior optimal actions—addresses a real bottleneck in LLM post-training where correct answers to hard questions have near-zero reference probability. Open questions include whether the L1 constraint generalizes to broader discrepancy measures, whether it holds in agentic or multimodal RL settings, and how the regularization coefficient β should be tuned across different task difficulties. The paper acknowledges it only compares L1 and KL, leaving the broader geometry of constraint choices unexplored.
