## Problem

Existing EEG/sEEG representation learning methods rely on fixed anatomical priors for spatial structure, use random masking that encourages superficial spatio-temporal shortcuts, and lack explicit topological regularization—resulting in poor robustness to missing channels and inability to generalize across sensor montages. No prior framework jointly models adaptive functional regions, fine-grained channel dynamics, and temporal interactions within a unified self-supervised objective.

## Input / Output

Input is a raw EEG/sEEG trial as a C×S channel-time matrix, which is split into patches of length L to form CT channel-time tokens projected to d dimensions, then augmented with R region tokens initialized via soft channel-to-region assignment to form a hierarchical sequence of (C+R)T tokens. The SSL pipeline produces five masked target views (region, channel, time, region-time, channel-time) from a single context view in one forward pass; after pre-training, the context encoder plus a classification head outputs emotion labels (valence/arousal) or task-engagement labels (rest/task). The learned adaptive functional partition (a C×R soft assignment matrix) is itself a byproduct that evolves from anatomical initialization during training.

## Method

RECTOR-SA is a hierarchical, block-sparse self-attention mechanism that factorizes dense spatio-temporal attention into three parallel streams—Topological Attention (channel↔region cross-attention at matched time slices), Local Functional Attention (within-region channel interactions with top-p gating), and Global Functional Attention (region-to-region coordination)—driven by Adaptive Functional Partitioning (AFP), which uses a Gumbel-Softmax assignment head with an annealing schedule α(t) to evolve from a fixed anatomical prior (10-20 system for EEG, 30-region sEEG labeling) into data-driven functional regions. The self-supervised objective, Masked Topology and Representation Learning (MTRL), jointly optimizes three losses in a single forward pass: Masked Predictive Modeling (input reconstruction + latent representation alignment across five topological masking domains), Topological Structure Modeling (predicting and matching region-channel assignments in latent space to enforce topological coherence), and Cross-View Consistency (attracting mean-pooled representations from different masked views of the same trial). A Region-Channel Regularization term with variance hinge and covariance losses further enforces that region tokens encode shared dynamics while channel tokens retain specific information, preventing representational collapse.

## Results

On EEG emotion recognition, RECTOR achieves 85.0% w-F1 on SEED (SD), 63.7% on SEED-IV (SD), 69.4% on DEAP-Valence (SD), and 69.7% on DEAP-Arousal (SD), setting new state-of-the-art across all subject-dependent and subject-independent settings. On sEEG task engagement, RECTOR reaches 92.1% AUROC on MSIT and 92.4% AUROC on ECR, outperforming all baselines by 2.8–7.6% AUROC. RECTOR also exhibits the lowest standard deviations among all methods (e.g., ±04.1 on MSIT vs. ±05.0 for BaRISTA), indicating superior robustness. Ablation studies confirm that every component (MPM, TSM, CVC, AFP, top-p gating) contributes meaningfully, with the full MTRL framework yielding the largest single gain over MAE-style random masking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECTOR outperforms all 14 baselines across every benchmark and evaluation protocol. On EEG emotion recognition, the closest competitors are mdJPT (80.1% w-F1 on SEED SD) and CSBrain (79.7%), with RECTOR leading by 4.9% and 5.3% respectively; the gap widens to 7.8–10.5% over task-specific models like MMM (77.2%), PGCN (76.8%), and TSception (74.5%). On the harder subject-independent protocol, RECTOR's advantage over the best baseline (mdJPT at 59.9% on SEED SI) narrows to 1.2% but remains consistent across all four EEG benchmarks. On sEEG task engagement, RECTOR surpasses the strongest baseline BaRISTA (89.3% AUROC on MSIT) by 2.8% and the supervised Seegnificant (84.5%) by 7.6%, while also achieving the lowest variance (±04.1 vs. ±05.0). Notably, RECTOR's block-sparse RECTOR-SA architecture achieves these gains with lower training time than full spatio-temporal attention methods like LaBraM-style baselines, and its zero-shot robustness to 50% channel dropout and cross-montage transfer (DEAP→SEED, MSIT↔ECR) substantially exceeds that of fixed-topology models.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECTOR synthesizes and extends three separate research threads: (1) masked self-supervised pre-training for EEG/sEEG (Brant, Du-IN, PopT, EEGPT), (2) transformer-based spatio-temporal modeling with either decoupled or full attention (CBraMod, LaBraM, EEGPT), and (3) region-aware spatial encoding (MMM's region tokens, Du-IN's region-fused patches). Its key departure is replacing fixed anatomical priors with Adaptive Functional Partitioning, which anneals from a 10-20 or sEEG atlas initialization into data-driven functional regions via Gumbel-Softmax, and coupling this with a unified three-objective SSL loss (MPM + TSM + CVC) rather than standard reconstruction-only masking. The hierarchical block-sparse attention (RECTOR-SA) with top-p gating is a novel architectural contribution that avoids both the expressivity bottleneck of decoupled spatial/temporal attention and the noise amplification of full dense attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECTOR addresses two of the most persistent barriers to clinical EEG/sEEG deployment—sensor dropout and cross-montage variability—by learning content-aware functional regions rather than rigid anatomical groupings, and demonstrates this through zero-shot missing-channel and cross-montage transfer experiments. The interpretability analyses (Grad-CAM channel maps, block-diagonal attention, Regional Importance Scores) provide neurophysiologically plausible explanations that could build clinician trust, though the authors explicitly note that external neuroscientific validation of the learned parcellations is beyond scope. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly heterogeneous multi-dataset pre-training (the CHB-MIT experiment is a first step), whether the learned functional regions correspond to known neuroanatomical circuits, and whether the top-p gating and AFP annealing schedule generalize to other neurophysiological modalities (MEG, fNIRS).
