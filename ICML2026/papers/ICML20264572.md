## Problem

Existing single-view latent action learning methods for world models conflate 2D pixel displacements with underlying physical-spatial dynamics, making them highly susceptible to view-dependent noise such as camera shake and illumination changes. Continuous latent spaces under unimodal Gaussian priors lack expressivity for multi-modal actions, while discrete VQ-based methods suffer from low information capacity. No prior approach leverages the causal invariance of physical actions across viewpoints to learn robust, view-invariant action representations.

## Input / Output

MuCoLA consumes synchronized video frame pairs (128×128, 2 frames per clip, 16×16 patches) from at least two distinct viewpoints per timestep; for single-view datasets like VP², a random 8-pixel crop augmentation generates a pseudo-second view. The encoder outputs a 32-dimensional continuous latent action vector per frame transition, which is further mapped through a projection head to a K=4096 prototype probability distribution. Downstream, the frozen encoder's latent actions are projected into the embedding space of an iVideoGPT autoregressive Transformer (LLaMA backbone, 12 layers, 768 hidden dim) that predicts future VQ-GAN visual tokens (32×32 grid, 8192 codebook).

## Method

MuCoLA employs a Spatio-Temporal Transformer encoder (8 blocks, 1024-dim, 16 heads) with a learnable [CLS]_act token that aggregates cross-frame dynamics into a continuous latent action. The key mechanism is a DINO-style cross-view self-distillation: a Student network observing viewpoint 1 predicts the prototype distribution produced by a momentum-updated Teacher observing viewpoint 2 (and vice versa), with a cross-view cross-entropy loss plus centering and sharpening to prevent collapse. The total training objective combines pixel-level MSE reconstruction (ensuring physical validity) with the consistency loss (weight β=1.0), and the Teacher is updated via EMA (λ=0.996) with stop-gradient. Theoretically, the paper proves under a Linear LAM framework that minimizing cross-view consistency loss acts as a spectral filter—projecting encoders onto the shared action subspace while remaining orthogonal to view-specific noise—contrasting with single-view PCA-like objectives that align with high-variance nuisances. After training, the frozen encoder extracts latent action sequences that condition an iVideoGPT world model for video prediction, MPC-based visual planning, model-based RL, and behavior cloning.

## Results

On action regression (the core metric for latent action quality), MuCoLA achieves a test RMSE of 0.214±0.04 and MAE of 0.120±0.05, outperforming AdaWorld (0.235/0.135), LAPO (0.289/0.231), and FICC (0.322/0.264). For next-frame reconstruction, MuCoLA reaches PSNR 37.97 and LPIPS 9.42%, substantially exceeding AdaWorld (33.53/14.63%), FICC (30.28/18.65%), and LAPO (27.54/21.42%). On the VP² visual planning benchmark, MuCoLA attains 86.23% success on the Blue Button task (matching the Raw Action oracle at 84.17%) and consistently outperforms all baselines across 8 tasks. On LIBERO behavior cloning, MuCoLA leads in 3 of 4 suites (Long: 65.4%, Object: 45.3%, Spatial: 59.2%) while slightly trailing AdaWorld on Goal (73.8% vs 76.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuCoLA consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated task. Against AdaWorld (the strongest baseline using continuous latent actions with Gaussian priors), MuCoLA reduces test RMSE by ~9% (0.214 vs 0.235) and improves reconstruction PSNR by 4.4 dB (37.97 vs 33.53), with the gap widening on downstream tasks: on VP² visual planning, MuCoLA achieves 68.90% on Robosuite Push vs AdaWorld's 64.37%, and 86.23% on Blue Button vs 31.22%. Against FICC (discrete VQ-based), MuCoLA nearly halves the test RMSE (0.214 vs 0.322) and improves LPIPS by 9.23 points (9.42% vs 18.65%). Against LAPO (information-bottleneck based), MuCoLA achieves a 26% lower test RMSE (0.214 vs 0.289) and leads on 3 of 4 LIBERO suites. Notably, MuCoLA reaches parity with the oracle Raw Action model on VP² (86.23% vs 84.17% on Blue Button), demonstrating that the learned latent actions capture nearly all the control information available from ground-truth actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuCoLA extends the latent action learning paradigm (FICC, LAPO, AdaWorld) by replacing single-view reconstruction or information-bottleneck objectives with a cross-view consistency constraint. Architecturally, it borrows the spatio-temporal tokenization from Genie and AdaWorld, and adapts DINO's EMA teacher-student self-distillation (centering, sharpening, cross-entropy) to a cross-view setting where the student and teacher observe different camera angles. The theoretical contribution reframes the multi-view consistency objective as a spectral filter analogous to Canonical Correlation Analysis, contrasting with the PCA-like behavior of single-view reconstruction identified by Zhang et al. (2025). For the downstream world model, the paper plugs the learned latent actions into iVideoGPT's autoregressive Transformer backbone, making the contribution primarily in the action-encoding stage rather than the dynamics model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in world-model scalability: the scarcity of action annotations. By showing that multi-view consistency yields view-invariant latent actions that generalize to unseen viewpoints, MuCoLA offers a path toward action-free world models that can be trained on rich multi-view data but deployed with a single camera. However, the approach is currently constrained by the need for synchronized multi-view video, which remains far smaller in scale than internet-scale single-view corpora. Open questions include whether pseudo-view synthesis or view-augmentation can close this gap, and whether the linear spectral-filtering analysis extends meaningfully to the non-linear DINO-style objective used in practice.
