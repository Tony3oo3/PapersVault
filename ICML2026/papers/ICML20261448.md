## Problem

Leaf cell layouts are the fundamental building blocks of VLSI designs, yet their physical design remains a fully manual, expert-driven process that is time-consuming and difficult to automate. Existing automated tools (SAT-based, MILP, commercial EDA) rely on rigid heuristics that fail to capture the implicit design patterns of human experts, and no LLM-based framework has been developed to generate expert-level leaf cell layouts. A critical data bottleneck further complicates the problem: expert-designed layouts exist in abundance, but the corresponding PnR scripts needed for LLM training do not.

## Input / Output

The method takes a netlist (set of component cells with connectivity), a set of design rules constraining placement (boundary, no overlap, orientation restricted to R0/MY) and routing (LVS correctness, DRC-clean geometry), and a natural-language design query. Rather than outputting raw coordinates, the LLM generates a Python script that invokes PnR APIs for cell placement (permutation and flip) and channel routing, which when executed produces the final geometric layout. This script-based interface leverages the LLM's native code generation capability and ensures construction-correctness for most design rules, with only a lightweight post-processing step needed for minor spacing violations.

## Method

GenLeaf combines three key components: (1) a performance-aware layout embedding model that fuses GraphSAGE over a Placement Heterogeneous Graph (capturing cell adjacency and net connectivity) with a CNN over Laplacian-sharpened routing images, trained with a supervised loss that aligns embeddings with physical metrics (track count, wirelength, via count); (2) a Bayesian Optimization pipeline using a Gaussian Process surrogate and Expected Improvement acquisition to reverse-engineer expert layouts into training scripts, with Lehmer encoding ensuring every sampled permutation is legal; and (3) a two-stage LLM specialization on Qwen3-14B via LoRA, first SFT to learn PnR API syntax and golden script patterns, then GRPO with a custom advantage function that combines z-normalized physical metrics and embedding similarity to golden layouts, enabling the model to align with expert design preferences without requiring a separate value network.

## Results

On 14 industry frontier leaf cell benchmarks (L1–L14), GenLeaf achieves an average 48% reduction in used track count (ratio 0.52) and 4% reduction in wirelength (ratio 0.96) compared to expert-designed golden layouts, with only a 1% increase in via count (ratio 1.01). Against the strongest commercial LLM baseline (Claude Sonnet 4), GenLeaf reduces track usage by 22%, wirelength by 15.7%, and via count by 8.8%. For layout representation, GenLeaf's performance-aware embedding achieves MSE 0.849 and MAE 0.722 on a 3-metric prediction task, outperforming Circuit GNN (MSE 1.287) and DeepLayout (MSE 1.324) by 34–36% in MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For layout representation, GenLeaf's performance-aware embedding (MSE 0.849, MAE 0.722) outperforms Circuit GNN [Yang et al., 2022] by 34.0% in MSE and 11.4% in MAE, and DeepLayout [Zhao et al., 2025b] by 35.9% in MSE and 25.5% in MAE on the 3-metric prediction task. Among LLM baselines, GenLeaf (Track 3.9, WL 39.05, Via 20.8) dominates all competitors: it beats the base Qwen3 14B model by 29.1% in track count, 34.6% in wirelength, and 32.7% in via count, and outperforms the larger Qwen3-Coder 30B by 23.5%, 25.9%, and 22.7% respectively. Against the strongest commercial APIs, GenLeaf reduces track usage by 22% (3.9 vs 5.0), wirelength by 15.7% (39.05 vs 46.36), and via count by 8.8% (20.8 vs 22.8) over Claude Sonnet 4, and by 18.8%, 17.8%, and 14.4% over GPT-4o. DeepSeek-R1 14B and Qwen2.5 7B are the weakest baselines, with GenLeaf achieving 30.4% and 38.1% track reductions over them respectively. Notably, GenLeaf also surpasses the traditional MILP+CRouter optimization method by 6% in track usage, 12% in wirelength, and 2% in via count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-based code generation and EDA physical design, building on GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., 2017) for topological layout representation and adapting GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a custom physical-metric advantage function for preference alignment. It departs from traditional automated PnR approaches (BonnCell, SAT-based placement, MILP) by reframing leaf cell layout generation as a script-generation task solvable by a fine-tuned LLM, following the broader trend of applying LLMs to EDA problems. The Bayesian Optimization pipeline for reverse-engineering expert layouts into training scripts is a novel data-preparation strategy that addresses the absence of layout-script pairs in industrial datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenLeaf demonstrates that a 14B LLM with LoRA fine-tuning can match or exceed expert human designers on key physical metrics (48% fewer tracks, 4% shorter wirelength) while reducing design time from days to ~20 seconds, which has direct practical impact on chip design cycles. However, the evidence base is narrow: 14 benchmark cases, a single technology node, and a small training set (272/156 categories), leaving open questions about scalability to larger cells, generalization across process nodes, and handling of more complex multi-objective constraints. The BO-based data preparation achieving only 3.63% average deviation from golden results suggests the approach is practical but may struggle with highly irregular or novel cell topologies.
