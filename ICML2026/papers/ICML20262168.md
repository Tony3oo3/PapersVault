## Problem

Prior work established that autoregressive LLMs straighten their internal token trajectories across layers (temporal straightening), but a direct causal link between this trajectory geometry and token-level behavior was missing. Specifically, it remained unclear whether representational curvature actually modulates next-token prediction uncertainty, or whether the straightening is merely a byproduct of training with no functional consequence for the model's output distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262168/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes token sequences (10–30 tokens for short-context; full narrative passages for long-context LAMBADA) and extracts residual-stream hidden states at each transformer layer. Curvature is computed as the angle between consecutive first-order difference vectors, averaged over a 3-token backward window; entropy is computed from the softmax over vocabulary logits at positions n > 6 to ensure sufficient context. Perturbation experiments produce per-token ΔC–ΔH correlations across five geometrically distinct subspace conditions, with perturbation magnitude scaled to local trajectory step size (|δ| = 0.2|v_k^p|) for comparability across tokens and layers.

## Method

The paper provides three converging lines of evidence. First, 10-fold cross-validated OLS regression shows contextual curvature predicts next-token entropy most strongly in middle layers (where average curvature is minimal), and this coupling emerges during pretraining as tracked via Pythia checkpoints. Second, perturbation experiments apply additive modifications to residual-stream activations drawn from five subspaces of increasing trajectory alignment (full-space → random → activation → trajectory → planar); only trajectory-aligned perturbations produce a reliable ΔC–ΔH correlation, establishing selective causal dependence. Third, a new auxiliary curvature-regularization loss (L_curv = (λ/n)Σc_k^p) is added at middle layers during training of a GPT-2 Small model, producing 'untangled' models with straighter trajectories and modestly lower token-level entropy without degrading validation loss, demonstrating that trajectory geometry is a task-aligned feature amenable to targeted intervention.

## Results

Contextual curvature in middle layers is positively correlated with next-token entropy, peaking at r ≈ 0.15 in both GPT-2 XL (layer ~23/48) and Pythia-2.8B (layer ~11/32), and this coupling emerges during training (visible by ~0.7% of Pythia's 300B-token run). Perturbation experiments show that only trajectory-aligned interventions (trajectory-subspace and planar-subspace) produce a reliable ΔC–ΔH correlation, while full-space, random-subspace, and activation-subspace perturbations yield no significant relationship. Curvature regularization (untangled model) modestly reduces token-level entropy across three validation datasets without degrading validation loss, while the reversed penalty (tangled model) increases entropy in two of three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is across five perturbation subspaces of increasing trajectory alignment. Full-space, random-subspace (m=2), and activation-subspace (top PCs of residual-stream activations) perturbations all fail to produce a reliable ΔC–ΔH correlation, indicating that curvature changes in trajectory-agnostic directions do not modulate entropy. In contrast, trajectory-subspace (top PCs of per-sample displacement vectors) yields a robust correlation, and planar-subspace (plane of the two most recent displacements) produces the largest effect size, confirming that entropy is selectively sensitive to curvature along the representational trajectory. As control geometric measures, contextual magnitude and contextual distance show weaker or later-emerging correlations with entropy compared to curvature, both in static layer profiles and across training checkpoints. For the regularization experiment, the baseline (next-token prediction only) is matched by both untangled and tangled models on validation loss, while the untangled model shows lower token-level entropy and the tangled model shows higher entropy, confirming a directional geometric effect on output uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal straightening framework of Hosseini & Fedorenko (2023), who showed that LLM trajectories become straighter in middle layers, by establishing the missing behavioral link: contextual curvature predicts next-token entropy, and this coupling emerges during training. It draws on the broader computational neuroscience literature on perceptual straightening (Henaff et al., 2019, 2021) and predictive learning reshaping brain geometry (Greco et al., 2024) to motivate why straightening should facilitate prediction. The perturbation design and curvature regularization objective are novel contributions, though they sit alongside concurrent work on temporal structure in LM representations (Lubana et al., 2025) and the Geodesic Hypothesis (Huang et al., 2026), which pursue closely related geometric principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective perturbation result—that only trajectory-aligned interventions modulate entropy while geometrically misaligned ones do not—provides causal evidence that trajectory curvature is a behaviorally relevant representational feature, not merely a correlational byproduct. The finding that curvature regularization reduces token-level entropy without degrading validation loss suggests a practical lever for shaping model confidence, though the effect sizes are modest and the training experiments use small-scale models (GPT-2 Small, 100M tokens). Key open questions include whether the curvature-entropy relationship holds in larger foundation models, whether reducing entropy translates to improved calibration or downstream task performance, and whether richer geometric descriptors beyond a single scalar curvature can capture more of the structure linking representation geometry to distributional uncertainty.
