## Problem

Conventional autoregressive image generation is bottlenecked by next-token prediction, which emits exactly one token per step and forces 2D spatial structure into a rigid 1D raster order, yielding prohibitive inference latency and often inferior quality compared to non-causal alternatives like diffusion or masked prediction. The paper asks whether strict spatial causality can be preserved while predicting a flexible number of tokens per autoregressive step, decoupling causality from the unit-step constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAR takes a class label (mapped to a token ID and prepended to the sequence) and generates 256×256 images by sequentially producing token groups over a 16×16 spatial grid. Images are encoded either as continuous KL-VAE latent features (16× spatial downsampling, patch size 1) or as raw 16×16 RGB patches (768-dim vectors), with a 6-layer flow-matching MLP head rendering each token group into pixel/latent content. For video, frames are generated autoregressively via next-frame prediction, enabling online rendering as each frame completes.

## Method

Self-token prediction partitions the token sequence into groups ordered by Manhattan distance on the 2D grid, so each group is generated in parallel within one autoregressive step while a group-causal attention mask enforces strict spatial causality across steps. A dependency-injection constructor h(·) aggregates features from causally prior neighbors (top, left, top-left) to form the step input, and an efficient dual-stream training scheme (context + generation copies with a structured mask) enables all groups to be trained in a single forward pass. The SAR backbone is a Qwen3 Transformer with adaLN-Zero conditioned on the normalized group index, 2D MM-RoPE positional encoding, and γ-noise augmentation to suppress spurious local-similarity shortcuts. At inference, classifier-free guidance is factorized into x-CFG (unconditional branch via zeroed backbone features inside the head) and z-CFG (guidance in backbone feature space), with a step-wise guidance schedule to counteract the progressive convergence of conditional and unconditional branches under growing causal context.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (256×256), SAR-H (1.3B params) achieves FID-50K 2.26 and IS 290.0 using only 31 autoregressive steps, matching DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, 250 steps) and outperforming the strongest spatially causal AR baseline LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.34, 256 steps). SAR-L (714M) reaches FID 2.52 and SAR-B (141M) reaches FID 4.23, demonstrating a strong scaling trend. Inference latency is 100 ms on a single A100 GPU (vs. 622 ms for next-token raster scanning), a 6.2× speedup over the next-token baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAR-H (FID 2.26) essentially ties DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) and slightly beats StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30) and JiT-L/16 (FID 2.36), all while using only 31 steps versus 250 or 50. Among spatially causal autoregressive models, SAR-H decisively outperforms LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.34, 256 steps), VAR-d20 (FID 2.57, 10 steps), ViT-VQGAN (FID 4.17, 1024 steps), RQTran (FID 7.55), and VQGAN (FID 15.78). SAR-H trails the best non-causal methods—MAGVIT-v2 and MAR-L (both FID 1.78) and VDM++ (FID 2.12)—but the paper argues this gap narrows with further scaling, noting SAR's steeper scaling curve. Against GAN baselines (BigGAN FID 6.95, GigaGAN FID 3.45) and diffusion baselines (LDM-4-G FID 3.60, ADM FID 10.94, MaskGIT FID 6.18), SAR-H is substantially better. The key differentiator is that SAR is the only method combining strict spatial causality (enabling KV caching and online rendering) with competitive quality at a fraction of the autoregressive depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces self-token prediction as a principled generalization of next-token prediction, where a flexible number of tokens are generated per autoregressive step under strict spatial causality enforced via group-causal attention. It builds on Qwen3 as the Transformer backbone, adopts AdaLN-Zero (from DiT) for step-conditioning, uses flow matching as the denoising objective, and factorizes classifier-free guidance into complementary x-CFG and z-CFG variants. Conceptually, it departs from next-token AR models (LlamaGen, Emu3, Chameleon) that are bottlenecked by unit-step decoding, from masked prediction (MaskGIT, MAR) that sacrifices causality for bidirectional attention, and from hybrid architectures (BAGEL, Transfusion) that couple separate AR and diffusion mechanisms. The framework subsumes next-token prediction, next-group prediction, and next-scale prediction (VAR) as limiting cases, positioning itself as a unified causal generation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAR demonstrates that strict spatial causality need not come at the cost of inference speed or sample quality: SAR-H (FID 2.26, 31 steps) matches DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, 250 steps) while remaining compatible with KV caching and online frame-by-frame rendering for video. The observed capacity threshold effect—where models below ~500M parameters fail entirely and performance jumps abruptly past that scale—suggests self-token prediction imposes stricter structural demands than conventional AR or diffusion, with implications for how multimodal foundation models should be scaled. Key open questions include whether principled grouping and dependency-injection design guidelines can be established for new modalities, how the paradigm generalizes to image editing and true multimodal fusion, and whether strict spatial causality is genuinely fundamental to capability emergence or merely sufficient for ImageNet-scale generation.
