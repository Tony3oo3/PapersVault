## Problem

Time-to-First-Spike (TTFS) spiking neural networks encode information with a single spike per neuron, offering extreme sparsity and energy efficiency, but suffer from unstable training and substantially lower accuracy than rate-coded SNNs. The root causes include signal diminishing from standard Kaiming initialization (whose variance decays as 1/T), weight distribution drift during gradient descent, and the lack of a differentiable decoder that properly exploits temporal spike timing. Additionally, prior work overlooked that max-pooling violates the one-spike constraint fundamental to TTFS coding.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts static RGB images (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) or event-based DVS spike streams (DVS Gesture) and produces a single class label. During inference, the network runs in step-by-step propagation mode with early stopping: it halts as soon as the first output-layer spike fires, yielding an average of 1–3 time-steps versus 8 training time-steps. The output is decoded by a temporal weighting decoder that maps the binary spike tensor O ∈ {0,1}^{T×C} to a continuous score vector Y ∈ ℝ^C, where earlier-firing neurons receive proportionally larger contributions.

## Method

The paper proposes ETTFS, a training framework built on the AMOS (At-Most-One-Spike) neuron with four key components. First, ETTFS-init initializes weights from U(−√(3T/N_l), √(3T/N_l)) so that the time-averaged input current variance equals 1, decoupling signal scale from the number of time-steps T and preventing the silent-neuron problem. Second, weight normalization re-standardizes each layer's weights to their initial mean and variance at every training step, with a learnable affine transform (γ, β) applied to the synaptic output that is fused into the weights at inference to add zero overhead. Third, a temporal weighting decoder computes Y = Σ_t w[t]·O[t] with monotonically decaying weights (exponential w[t]=γ^{−t} or linear w[t]=γ(T−t)/T), making the argmax of Y equivalent to the earliest-firing neuron while remaining fully differentiable. Fourth, the paper demonstrates that max-pooling can produce multiple spikes in a pooled output (violating the one-spike invariant), and replaces it with average-pooling, which provably preserves the invariant and can be fused with adjacent convolutions.

## Results

ETTFS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy among directly trained TTFS SNNs across all five benchmarks: 99.48% on MNIST, 92.90% on Fashion-MNIST, 90.56% on CIFAR-10, 70.27% on CIFAR-100, and 95.83% on DVS Gesture. The method uses only 8 training time-steps with an average of 2.93 inference time-steps on CIFAR-10, compared to 4.62 for TQ-TTFS and >100 for conversion-based methods. On CIFAR-10, ETTFS reduces estimated synaptic energy (E1) from 87.79 µJ (rate coding) to 12.19 µJ while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETTFS outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset where a direct comparison is possible. On MNIST, it surpasses the closest direct-training competitor DTA-TTFS (99.4%) by 0.08% and STiDi-BP (99.2%) by 0.28%. On Fashion-MNIST, ETTFS (92.90%) edges out STiDi-BP (92.8%) and TQ-TTFS (90.2%) by 0.1% and 2.7% respectively. On CIFAR-10 without pre-trained weights, ETTFS (90.56%) dramatically outperforms DTA-TTFS (80.38%) and TQ-TTFS (67.47%), while conversion methods LC-TTFS (92.72%) and T2FSNN (91.43%) use a much larger VGG-16 (138.36M params) with >100 time-steps versus ETTFS's SCNN6 (70.11M params) with 8 time-steps. On CIFAR-100, ETTFS (70.27%) beats LC-TTFS (70.15%) and T2FSNN (68.79%), and far exceeds DTA-TTFS and TQ-TTFS which fail to converge without pre-training (1%). On DVS Gesture, ETTFS (95.83%) is the first TTFS method to converge on this dataset, surpassing the TQ-TTFS re-implementation (88.89%) by 6.94%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the direct-training lineage of TTFS SNNs, building directly on the AMOS (At-Most-One-Spike) neuron model from TQ-TTFS (Yang et al., 2024) and the weight normalization technique from Qiao et al. (2019). It departs from prior work by identifying a specific signal-diminishing pathology in Kaiming initialization when applied to the one-spike constraint, deriving a corrected initialization (ETTFS-init) that decouples post-synaptic variance from the number of time-steps. The paper also re-evaluates a design choice (max-pooling) that prior TTFS methods adopted without scrutiny, arguing that average-pooling is required to preserve the one-spike invariant that underpins the initialization analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTFS coding is the most energy-efficient neural coding scheme for neuromorphic hardware, yet prior direct-training methods have struggled with convergence and accuracy, limiting practical deployment. This framework provides a principled, component-level recipe (initialization, normalization, decoder, pooling) that stabilizes training and reduces inference latency to ~3 time-steps, making step-by-step propagation viable on hardware. Key limitations include evaluation only on small-scale benchmarks (MNIST through CIFAR-100) with no ImageNet results, and the temporal weighting decoder's decay parameter γ requires dataset-specific tuning. Open questions include whether the framework scales to large-scale recognition tasks and whether the average-pooling constraint can be relaxed in deeper architectures without sacrificing the initialization guarantees.
