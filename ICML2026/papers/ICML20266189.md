## Problem

No existing framework can simultaneously harmonize all four multimodal conditions—text, reference images, audio, and pose—required for Human-Object Interaction Video Generation (HOIVG). Prior methods handle only subsets (e.g., R2V without audio, A2V without reference images) or impose rigid input requirements like object masks and trajectories, while high-quality quintuplet training data and a dedicated evaluation benchmark are both absent.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a flexible combination of up to four conditions: a text prompt for global semantics, one or more reference images (human and/or object) for identity preservation, an audio clip for lip and body synchronization, and a pose skeleton sequence for explicit motion control. It outputs 5-second (up to 10-second) 720p portrait-mode videos. All conditions are optional, enabling R2V, RA2V, RP2V, and the full RAP2V configuration, with the model gracefully degrading when any modality is absent.

## Method

Built on the 12B Waver 1.0 MMDiT backbone, OMNI SHOW introduces three key mechanisms. First, Unified Channel-wise Conditioning augments noisy video tokens with pseudo-frame tokens along the temporal axis, then injects both pose and reference image tokens via a single channel-concatenation strategy, supplemented by a reference reconstruction loss to preserve semantic details. Second, Gated Local-Context Attention packs audio features using a sliding window (w=5) for temporal context, applies masked cross-attention to restrict each video frame to its corresponding audio segment, and uses a learnable gating vector (initialized near zero) to stabilize early training while revealing per-block audio impact. Third, Decoupled-Then-Joint Training first trains specialized R2V and A2V models on separate datasets, merges them via linear weight interpolation (0.6 A2V / 0.4 R2V), then jointly fine-tunes on RA2V data before introducing pose in the final stage to prevent over-reliance on this strong signal.

## Results

On HOIVG-Bench, OMNI SHOW (12.3B) achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive results across three multimodal conditioning settings. In the RA2V setting, it leads on NexusScore (0.369 vs. 0.344 for HuMo-17B), Sync-C (8.612 vs. 8.013), and MQ (5.554 vs. 4.269), while trailing only slightly on TA (8.093 vs. 8.146). In R2V, it matches Phantom-14B on FaceSim (0.874 vs. 0.876) while surpassing all baselines on NexusScore (0.389), VQ (11.12), and MQ (5.885). In RP2V, it achieves the best pose accuracy (PCK 0.460, AKD 0.174) and NexusScore (0.418) over VACE and AnchorCrafter. On the EMTD benchmark, the A2V variant achieves the best Sync-C (6.49) and AES (1.51) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the R2V setting, OMNI SHOW matches Phantom-14B on FaceSim (0.874 vs. 0.876) and surpasses all baselines on NexusScore (0.389 vs. 0.368 for VACE), VQ (11.12 vs. 10.93), and MQ (5.885 vs. 5.637), though it trails Phantom-14B and VACE on TA (7.746 vs. 8.609 and 8.413). In RA2V, OMNI SHOW clearly outperforms HuMo-17B on every metric except TA (8.093 vs. 8.146), with notable margins in Sync-C (+0.6), Sync-D (−0.7), and MQ (+1.3), and dominates HunyuanCustom across the board. In RP2V, OMNI SHOW achieves the best pose accuracy (PCK 0.460 vs. 0.336 for VACE, 0.176 for AnchorCrafter) and NexusScore (0.418 vs. 0.352), but concedes TA and FaceSim to VACE due to viewpoint shifts inherent in pose-following. On the EMTD benchmark, the A2V variant beats MultiTalk on Sync-C (6.49 vs. 6.34) and Hallo3 on AES (1.51 vs. 1.48), while trailing MultiTalk slightly on Sync-D (8.97 vs. 8.47). Against the cascaded VACE+LatentSync baseline on the full RAP2V task, OMNI SHOW wins on all 11 metrics, with the largest gaps in PCK (0.478 vs. 0.340) and MQ (5.880 vs. 3.911), confirming the advantage of end-to-end multimodal unification over sequential pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Waver 1.0, a 12B MMDiT-based video generation model, extending its native channel-concatenation conditioning paradigm (originally designed for I2V) to simultaneously inject reference images and pose sequences via pseudo-frame tokens. It departs from the fragmented landscape of prior controllable video generation—where R2V methods (Phantom, VACE, HunyuanCustom) handle subject identity, A2V methods (HuMo, MultiTalk, OmniAvatar) handle audio synchronization, and HOI-specific methods (AnchorCrafter, DreamActor-H1) require rigid structural inputs like object masks or trajectories—by unifying all four modalities (text, reference image, audio, pose) in a single end-to-end architecture. The Decoupled-Then-Joint Training strategy with model merging is a novel training recipe that repurposes heterogeneous sub-task datasets, and the Gated Local-Context Attention mechanism draws on Wav2Vec 2.0 features with a masked cross-attention design not previously applied to video diffusion transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OMNI SHOW establishes the first unified framework and dedicated benchmark (HOIVG-Bench) for Human-Object Interaction Video Generation, filling a clear gap where no prior method supported all four conditioning modalities simultaneously. The parameter efficiency of the audio injection (only ~2.5% model increase vs. ~21.4% for HuMo) and the emergent RA2V capability from simple weight interpolation suggest practical pathways for combining specialized video generation models. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 5-second clips, potential artifacts under extreme motion or conflicting inputs, and reliance on AI-generated reference images in the benchmark for privacy compliance. Open questions include whether the unified approach scales to longer videos, whether RL-based post-training can further close the gap with human preference, and whether richer conditions (camera trajectories, reference videos) can be incorporated without the current trade-off between controllability and quality.
