## Problem

Prediction-powered inference (PPI) improves semi-supervised mean estimation by leveraging ML predictors, but suffers from two practical failures: label reuse in the rectifier term distorts coverage, and misspecification of the predictor inflates variance beyond the semiparametric efficiency bound. Cross-fitted PPI (CF-PPI) resolves the label-reuse issue but still requires the raw prediction error to vanish for asymptotic normality, leaving efficiency gains fragile under moderate model misspecification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled sample {(X_j, Y_j)} of size n, an unlabeled covariate sample {X_i} of size N (with N ≫ n), a cross-fitted ML predictor m^(−) trained via K-fold sample splitting, and a strictly convex Bregman generator G. It outputs a point estimate θ̂_MEC = (1/N)Σ ω̂_j Y_j (a weighted mean of labeled outcomes only), a Wald confidence interval using a GREG-style variance decomposition, and the calibrated weights ω̂_j. A key structural constraint is that the calibration basis is always two-dimensional—(1, m^(−)(X))—regardless of covariate dimension d, so the dual Newton solver operates in a fixed 2×2 Hessian and remains numerically stable even for large n.

## Method

MEC reweights the labeled-sample rectifier term in CF-PPI by solving a Bregman projection: find weights ω̂_j closest to the baseline design weights d_j = N/n (in the D_G(·‖·) sense) subject to two calibration constraints—total weight equals N and the weighted sum of out-of-fold predictions matches the full-population prediction total. The calibration basis h(X) = (1, m^(−)(X)) is constructed from the same out-of-fold predictor used in the residual term, creating a 'double label-reuse avoidance' safeguard. Theoretically, this replaces the CF-PPI requirement that ‖m^(−) − m_0‖_{L2} → 0 with the weaker condition that the projection error ‖m_0 − Π_W m_0‖_{L2} → 0, where W = span{1, m^(−)}, meaning only the component of the true regression function orthogonal to the predictor's affine span can inflate variance. The estimator admits an exact GREG (generalized regression estimator) dual representation when G is quadratic, showing MEC generalizes PPI++ and reduces to the classical sample mean when the predictor carries no linear signal for Y.

## Results

In simulations with R=2000 replications, N=1000 unlabeled covariates, label fractions f∈[0.1, 0.5], and d=10 features, the paper reports empirical coverage of nominal 95% Wald confidence intervals and width ratios (CI width relative to the classical label-only estimator). MEC (quadratic generator) attains near-nominal 95% coverage uniformly across all label fractions and all four ML predictors (KRR, RF, FNN, kNN), while delivering the narrowest valid confidence intervals. CF-PPI restores validity but can produce width ratios above 1 at f=0.1 with KRR and kNN (i.e., intervals wider than the classical estimator), whereas vanilla PPI undercovers substantially at small f due to label-reuse overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEC is compared against two PPI variants: vanilla PPI (single-fit predictor, no sample splitting) and CF-PPI (cross-fitted out-of-fold predictor). Against vanilla PPI, MEC wins decisively on both coverage and efficiency: PPI undercovers (often well below 95%) especially at small label fractions f, while MEC maintains near-nominal coverage with tighter intervals. Against CF-PPI, both methods achieve near-nominal coverage, but MEC consistently produces narrower confidence intervals (lower width ratios) across all label fractions and all four predictors. The advantage is most pronounced at small f (e.g., f=0.1), where CF-PPI with KRR or kNN can yield width ratios exceeding 1 (worse than the classical label-only mean), while MEC remains below 1. MEC is most efficient with KRR and RF predictors; with the weaker FNN and kNN learners the gap over CF-PPI narrows but MEC still leads. The paper also includes classical (label-only sample mean) and oracle (true regression function m₀) as reference bounds: MEC's width ratios approach the oracle lower bound more closely than CF-PPI's, indicating near-attainment of the semiparametric efficiency bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEC sits at the intersection of three research threads: prediction-powered inference (PPI, Angelopoulos et al. 2023), cross-fitted PPI (CF-PPI, Zrnic & Candès 2024), and generalized entropy calibration from survey sampling (GEC, Kwon et al. 2025). The key departure from CF-PPI is the introduction of Bregman-projection weight calibration using an ML predictor as the calibration basis, which the paper shows is a weight-calibrated generalization of CF-PPI (reducing to it when the basis is just the intercept). The key departure from classical GEC is the shift from finite-population mean estimation with prespecified moment features to superpopulation semi-supervised inference with an arbitrary ML predictor, and the paper further shows MEC generalizes PPI++ in a dual (calibration-form vs. regression-form) sense, with exact equivalence under a quadratic generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEC provides a principled, computationally lightweight (always 2D basis) way to improve PPI efficiency under predictor misspecification, with the theoretical guarantee that variance inflation depends only on the projection error orthogonal to the predictor span rather than the full prediction error—a strictly weaker condition. The affine robustness (invariance to rescaling of the predictor) is a practically useful property that reduces sensitivity to model calibration. Open questions include extending rigorous guarantees beyond mean estimation to broader causal and semiparametric targets (the paper sketches ATE and Cox regression examples but defers full theory), and characterizing precisely when MEC attains the semiparametric efficiency bound for different estimands and outcome types.
