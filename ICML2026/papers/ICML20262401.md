## Problem

Existing Byzantine-resilient decentralized learning methods based on DSGD suffer from a transient complexity of O((1-λ)^{-6}) on sparse networks, while bias-correction methods like Exact Diffusion improve topology dependence to O((1-λ)^{-2}) but, when naively combined with robust aggregators, cause Byzantine-induced errors to accumulate in the dual variables and grow quadratically with the number of iterations K. This paper addresses the fundamental incompatibility between bias-correction and robust aggregation in decentralized optimization.

## Input / Output

Each honest client holds a local dataset (MNIST 28×28 grayscale or CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB images partitioned via Dirichlet distribution) and communicates only with its 2k nearest neighbors in a k-Ring topology, encoded by a doubly stochastic mixing matrix W. Byzantine workers (up to δ < 1/2 fraction) may transmit arbitrary vectors during the communication phase. The algorithm outputs a consensus model parameter vector that is an ε-stationary point of the global honest loss, with the key tunable hyperparameters being the dual scaling factor q ∈ (0,1) and the momentum coefficient β ∈ [0,1).

## Method

The core contribution is Scaled Dual Ascent (SDA), which modifies the standard dual update in the augmented Lagrangian framework by multiplying it with a fractional scaling factor q = 1/(τ+1) < 1, thereby bounding the dual variables and eliminating the K-dependent error accumulation that occurs when robust aggregators are combined with bias-correction. BRED instantiates this framework using Exact Diffusion as the backbone (A=W, B=(I-W)^{1/2}, C=W) with a robust aggregator (Krum, Median, Trimmed Mean, or IOS) replacing the standard averaging step, achieving transient complexity O((1-λ)^{-2}) when δ is small. The momentum variant BRED-M adds Polyak momentum, which accelerates variance decay from K^{-1} to K^{-3/2} and eliminates the stochastic noise term from the asymptotic error, reducing the Byzantine-affected transient time from O(δ²(1-λ)^{-6}) to O(δ²(1-λ)^{-4}) and matching the optimal lower bound Ω(δζ²) for Byzantine-resilient learning.

## Results

On MNIST under ALIE attack with Median aggregation, BRED-M achieves 89.83% test accuracy on the fully connected topology (λ=0, δ=0.125), outperforming BR-DSGD (88.45%) and BR-EXTRA-M (79.97%). The advantage is most pronounced on sparse networks: at λ=0.81, δ=0.125, BRED reaches 81.88% versus only 44.69% for BR-DSGD and 64.57% for BR-EXTRA-M. On CIFAR-10, BRED-M achieves 64.91% (λ=0.54, δ=0.125), surpassing BR-DSGD (57.83%) and BR-EXTRA-M (34.53%) by 7 and 30 percentage points respectively. Theoretically, BRED-M reduces the Byzantine-affected transient time from O(δ²n_h³/(1−λ)⁶) to O(δ²n_h³/(1−λ)⁴) and matches the optimal asymptotic error lower bound Ω(δζ²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRED and BRED-M consistently outperform all three baseline families (BR-DSGD, BR-EXTRA, BR-GT) across every network configuration tested. The most dramatic gap appears on sparse networks: at λ=0.81 (k=4 ring), BRED achieves 81.88% on MNIST versus 44.69% for BR-DSGD and 64.57% for BR-EXTRA-M, a 37-point and 17-point margin respectively. BR-GT and BR-GT-M are the weakest baselines, failing to converge on CIFAR-10 entirely and achieving only 19–52% on MNIST, confirming that gradient tracking is particularly vulnerable to Byzantine attacks. On CIFAR-10, BRED-M leads BR-DSGD by 7.4 points (64.91% vs. 57.83%) at λ=0.54, δ=0.125, and by 23.2 points over BR-EXTRA-M (64.91% vs. 34.53%). The momentum variant BRED-M provides the largest gains under higher Byzantine fractions (δ=0.25), where it narrows the gap to the fully-connected setting more effectively than BRED, consistent with its theoretical elimination of the O(δσ²) variance term in the asymptotic error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Byzantine-resilient decentralized optimization (Wu et al. 2023, El-Mhamdi et al. 2021) and bias-corrected decentralized methods like Exact Diffusion and gradient tracking (Yuan et al. 2018, Alghunaim & Yuan 2022). The key conceptual departure is showing that these two lines are fundamentally incompatible when naively combined: the dual variables in the augmented Lagrangian formulation accumulate Byzantine errors over iterations, causing the asymptotic error to grow as O(K²). The paper's Scaled Dual Ascent (SDA) mechanism—a fractional scaling factor q on the dual update—resolves this incompatibility, yielding BRED and BRED-M as the first bias-corrected methods with provable Byzantine resilience over sparse topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction in transient complexity from O((1-λ)^-6) (DSGD-based) to O((1-λ)^-2) (BRED) is practically significant for sparse networks where the spectral gap is small, and BRED-M's achievement of the optimal Ω(δζ²) asymptotic error closes a gap left by prior Byzantine-resilient methods. However, the experiments are limited to n=32 nodes on k-Ring topologies and two image classification benchmarks, leaving open whether the gains persist on larger, more heterogeneous topologies (e.g., random geometric graphs, time-varying networks) or in asynchronous settings. The trade-off in the scaling parameter q—where smaller q improves robustness but slows convergence—also lacks a principled adaptive selection rule, which could limit practical adoption.
