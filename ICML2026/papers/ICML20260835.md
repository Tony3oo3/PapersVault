## Problem

Geometric ML pipelines (manifold regression, gauge-equivariant GNNs, diffusion tensor imaging) produce observations living in varying fibers of a vector bundle over a manifold, requiring parallel transport to a common reference fiber before averaging. Existing concentration inequalities operate only in fixed vector spaces and cannot account for the curvature- and holonomy-driven ambiguity that arises when minimizing geodesics are non-unique, leaving no non-asymptotic finite-sample guarantees for transported bundle-valued means.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples X_i on a Riemannian manifold M, a measurable section s: M → E of a rank-k vector bundle, a fixed reference point x_0, and a measurable parallel-transport rule selecting a curve from each x to x_0. It produces a transported empirical mean in the reference fiber E_{x_0} together with explicit Hoeffding and Bernstein tail bounds, inverted confidence radii, and a bias-variance decomposition whose geometric term (the holonomy bias Δ_hol) is a deterministic curvature-driven floor that does not decay with sample size n. A key structural feature is that the output separates a stochastic component decaying at n^{-1/2} from a geometric component controlled by the curvature 2-form and loop area, with sharp closed-form expressions on the round sphere S_r^2.

## Method

The core reduction transports each bundle-valued observation s(X_i) to the fixed reference fiber E_{x_0} via parallel transport along a chosen geodesic, converting the problem into i.i.d. Hilbert-space averaging where Pinelis' sharp dimension-free martingale inequalities yield Hoeffding and Bernstein bounds with explicit constants. The geometric novelty is isolating a deterministic holonomy bias Δ(P, P̃; s) that arises when transport is path-dependent, bounding it via the operator norm of the curvature 2-form Ω and the diameter D of the data-support region (giving Δ_hol ≤ (ζD² + ζ₁D³/3)B), and providing a sharp area-based formula on S_r^2 (Δ_hol ≤ (πρ²/r²)B). Minimax lower bounds via Le Cam's two-point method confirm both the n^{-1/2} stochastic rate and the κD² holonomy floor are unavoidable within the class of transport-based estimators. A robust median-of-means variant achieves the same σ/√n rate under finite second moment only, and a CLT in E_{x_0} holds once the holonomy floor is negligible relative to sampling noise.

## Results

The paper establishes dimension-free Hoeffding and Bernstein concentration bounds for transported bundle-valued means: P(‖Ȳₙ − m*‖ ≥ ε) ≤ 2exp(−nε²/8B²) and 2exp(−nε²/(2(σ² + 2Bε/3))), respectively. The central bias-variance decomposition is ‖Ȳₙ − m*‖ ≲ B/√n + Δ_hol, where the holonomy floor on S² is sharply bounded by (πρ²/r²)B (Proposition 1). Minimax lower bounds (Theorem 4) confirm both the n^{-1/2} stochastic rate and the κD² holonomy floor are unavoidable. Numerical experiments on S² validate the predicted n^{-1/2} decay and the holonomy error floor, matching the sharp sphere formula to within 3.7% across all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior theoretical frameworks rather than empirical baselines. The paper's key differentiator is occupying the intersection of two previously separate desiderata: finite-sample exponential concentration (available in Ledoux & Talagrand and Boucheron et al. but only in linear spaces) and curved-geometry inference (available in Bhattacharya & Patrangenaru and Fletcher et al. but only asymptotically). Specifically, Ledoux & Talagrand and Boucheron et al. provide dimension-free Hoeffding/Bernstein bounds but cannot model curvature, parallel transport, or holonomy. Bhattacharya & Patrangenaru and Fletcher et al. handle Riemannian geometry but deliver only asymptotic CLTs with no exponential tail bounds. Lugosi & Mendelson's median-of-means achieves the optimal σ/√n rate under heavy tails but is restricted to linear Banach spaces. This paper extends all three threads simultaneously: it inherits the sharp Pinelis constants for the stochastic term, adds an explicit curvature/holonomy bias term with sharp closed-form on S², proves minimax lower bounds confirming both terms are unavoidable, and extends MoM robustness to the bundle setting. The numerical validation on S² confirms the theory matches the sharp holonomy formula to within 3.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sharp Hilbert-space concentration inequalities of Pinelis (1994) and the median-of-means framework of Lugosi & Mendelson (2019) from linear Banach/Hilbert spaces to the geometric setting of vector bundles over Riemannian manifolds, where observations live in varying fibers and must be aligned via parallel transport before averaging. It departs from the Fréchet-mean literature (Bhattacharya & Patrangenaru, 2003; 2005), which handles curved geometry but delivers only asymptotic normality, by providing finite-sample Hoeffding and Bernstein bounds with explicit constants. The key conceptual departure is the explicit isolation of a deterministic holonomy bias term—governed by bundle curvature and loop geometry—that has no Euclidean counterpart and is absent from both the classical concentration and the manifold-mean literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that curvature-driven transport ambiguity creates an irreducible error floor in any pipeline that averages bundle-valued observations via parallel transport, a limitation invisible to classical n^{-1/2} analyses and confirmed by minimax lower bounds. This has direct practical implications for geometric ML pipelines (gauge-equivariant GNNs, intrinsic manifold regression, diffusion-tensor imaging) where practitioners may unknowingly be limited by holonomy rather than sample size. Open questions include extending the theory to learned or approximate connections (where a third error term enters), handling global transport when the cut locus is unavoidable, and whether higher-order holonomy corrections can be made data-driven rather than curvature-model-dependent.
