## Problem

Standard linear probing for Vision Transformers relies exclusively on the final-layer CLS token, implicitly assuming all task-relevant information is encoded there. In practice, task-relevant features are distributed across the network hierarchy, and naively concatenating multi-layer representations for a linear classifier proves unstable across diverse tasks. The paper asks how to adaptively and robustly fuse representations from all layers in a probing setting without updating the backbone.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 256px, center-cropped to 224px) and processes it through a frozen ViT backbone, extracting CLS and average-pooled (AP) tokens from every intermediate layer (2L summary vectors of dimension d). A single linear classifier on top of the fused representation produces the final class label. The backbone is never updated; only the lightweight attention-fusion module and classifier are trained, keeping the approach computationally comparable to standard linear probing.

## Method

ALF (Attentive Layer Fusion) applies multi-head cross-attention over the stacked CLS and AP tokens from all L transformer layers (2L tokens total), using a shared learnable query that adapts during training to prioritize task-relevant layers. Each head computes keys and values from the layer representations via trainable projections, and the concatenated head outputs pass through a linear layer to yield a single fused d-dimensional vector. The parameter count is independent of the number of layers, and the attention complexity scales as O(|L|²) rather than O(P²) for patch-level attentive probes, yielding an order-of-magnitude reduction. Training uses weighted cross-entropy loss with attention dropout, weight decay, and Gaussian noise injection on intermediate representations to prevent overfitting. The key distinction from prior attentive probes (which attend over all final-layer patch tokens) is that ALF operates on summary tokens across the full depth, enabling task-adaptive hierarchical layer selection while remaining stable across diverse domains.

## Results

Across 20 diverse datasets and 9 pretrained ViTs (CLIP, DINOv2, supervised ViT in S/B/L variants), ALF achieves a mean accuracy gain of 5.54 pp over the standard CLS linear probe, with a mean rank of 1.33 (best) versus 2.29 for the attentive all-token probe (AAT) and 2.43 for linear concatenation. Gains are largest for out-of-domain and fine-grained tasks: SVHN (+27.25 pp), GTSRB (+13.47 pp), FER2013 (+10.05 pp), and Dmlab (+10.68 pp). For DINOv2-L-14 specifically, ALF adds a further 6.04 pp mean gain over the already-strong CLS linear baseline, and for MAE models ALF recovers 22.4–24.7 pp over last-layer AP probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALF (all layers, CLS+AP, attentive) achieves the best mean rank (1.33) across 20 datasets, outperforming the attentive all-token probe AAT from Chen et al. (2024) (mean rank 2.29) and linear concatenation of all-layer CLS+AP tokens (mean rank 2.43). ALF wins on 14 of 20 datasets; AAT wins on the four fine-grained spatial tasks where patch-level localization is critical (GTSRB: 18.02 vs 13.47 pp, SVHN: 30.31 vs 27.25 pp, Stanford Cars: 8.97 vs 6.35 pp, FGVC Aircraft: 9.27 vs 6.43 pp). Linear concatenation is notably unstable, with negative gains on several datasets (e.g., −2.01 pp on Pets, −1.62 pp on FGVC Aircraft), whereas ALF never drops below the baseline on more than two datasets. The only cases where linear concatenation beats ALF are PCAM (5.32 vs 2.85 pp) and PASCAL VOC07 (1.46 vs 1.24 pp), which the authors attribute to overfitting in the attention module. Overall, ALF provides a more stable and consistently positive improvement (p-value ≤ 0.013, FDR-corrected Wilcoxon) compared to both baselines, with the largest absolute gains on out-of-domain tasks such as Diabetic Retinopathy (+6.86 pp), FER2013 (+10.05 pp), and Country-211 (+4.96 pp).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attentive probing paradigm of Chen et al. (2024), which applies multi-head cross-attention over final-layer patch tokens, by shifting the attention axis from spatial (across patches) to hierarchical (across layers), operating on CLS and average-pooled summary tokens from all intermediate layers. It departs from prior multi-layer fusion approaches (DINOv2's fixed CLS concatenation, Head2Toe, Visual Query Tuning) that rely on static aggregation or manual layer selection, instead learning task-adaptive layer weights through a lightweight cross-attention module. The method also builds on the broader probing literature (Alain & Bengio, 2017; Kornblith et al., 2019b) and the Set Transformer's attention-based aggregation, but is the first to systematically demonstrate that attentive cross-layer fusion over summary tokens outperforms both single-layer probing and naive multi-layer concatenation across diverse domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence (20 datasets, 9 models, 3 pretraining families) that the widely-held assumption that the final-layer CLS token captures all task-relevant information is incorrect, and that a simple, frozen-backbone attention module can recover substantial performance (5.54 pp average) without any backbone updates. The finding that hierarchical (cross-layer) and spatial (cross-patch) aggregation are orthogonal axes of representation fusion opens a clear path for hybrid probes that combine both, and the attention heatmaps offer interpretable diagnostics for which layers matter per task. Limitations include the CLS+AP token selection being optimized for CLS-focused pretraining (less natural for MAE-style models), the risk of overfitting from the added expressivity, and the fact that the method still requires extracting and storing all intermediate representations, adding memory overhead. Open questions include whether the principle generalizes to non-vision foundation models (language, genomics, proteins) and whether the optimal token selection strategy can be made fully task-adaptive rather than fixed to CLS+AP.
