## Problem

Existing identifiability theory for latent representations either requires parametric assumptions, interventional data, structural sparsity constraints, or targets full-system recovery of all latent variables. The paper asks whether a task-relevant subset of latent variables can be provably identified from a generalist model in a fully nonparametric setting—without interventions, without parametric forms, and without assuming strict temporal dependence—thereby establishing a principled pathway from generalist to specialist representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266097/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observed sequence {o_t} in R^{d_o} generated by latent states {s_t} in R^{d_s} with actions {a_t}, together with a set of M task variables {g_i} defined as colliders across time steps. It produces two outputs: (1) the complete temporal task structure mapping each segment (and hence each time step) to its active task set, and (2) within each time step, the task-relevant latent variables disentangled from irrelevant ones. A key practical detail is that all conditional independence tests are performed directly in the observed space, since CI is preserved under the invertible observation map o_t = f_t(s_t), eliminating the need to know the latent states or the generative functions.

## Method

The framework has two pillars. First, temporal task structure is identified via a d-separation criterion: task g_i is relevant to segments S_k and S_v if and only if s_{kL} ⊥̸ s_{vL} | Z_band(k,v,i), where Z_band conditions on the four immediate boundary states and g_i itself. The proof shows that conditioning on boundary states blocks all purely temporal paths, while tasks other than g_i appear as closed colliders that also block, leaving only paths through g_i as d-connecting. Algorithm 1 aggregates these pairwise tests across all segment pairs to recover the full structure, handling arbitrary interleaving, disconnections, and i.i.d. steps. Second, within each step, the paper proves that a generalist model necessarily produces an overcomplete task-relevant representation (Proposition 2, via Jacobian support inclusion), and that adding a simple ℓ₀ sparsity regularization on the task-latent Jacobian upgrades this inclusion to exact support equivalence (Theorem 2), guaranteeing that the estimated task-relevant latents are an invertible function of only the ground-truth task-relevant latents. In practice, CI tests are approximated by conditional mutual information estimated with a task-conditioned InfoNCE objective, and the sparsity constraint is implemented via ℓ₁ regularization in a VAE or GAN-based estimator.

## Results

On the SportsHHI video dataset, the proposed method achieves 0.25±0.08 mAP for temporal task structure discovery, more than doubling the best baseline (LEAP at 0.12±0.05). On synthetic benchmarks with varying time steps (T=8–20) and task counts (M=2–10), the method dominates all baselines in both accuracy and Matthews correlation coefficient, with the gap persisting as the problem scales. Runtime is 0.01–0.02 seconds, orders of magnitude faster than Group Lasso (11.2–82.3 s) and SelTask (0.69–2.40 s). For task-relevant representation recovery, the method achieves high R² on task-relevant latent components and near-zero R² on irrelevant components, confirming clean disentanglement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines across two experimental settings. On synthetic temporal task structure (Figure 3), the method outperforms CCA (Anderson, 2003), Group Lasso (Yuan & Lin, 2006), and SelTask (Qiu et al., 2024) in both accuracy and MCC across all values of T and M; Group Lasso is also roughly 1000× slower at T=20 (82.3 s vs. 0.02 s). On the real-world SportsHHI benchmark (Table 3), the method achieves 0.25 mAP, roughly 2× LEAP (0.12), ~2.3× SlowFast (0.11), ~2.3× ViT-B (0.11), and ~2.8× the Base model (0.09). LEAP is the strongest baseline because it provides identifiability guarantees on latent variables but not on the temporal task structure itself, whereas the proposed method explicitly recovers the structure. The qualitative vision experiment (Figure 6) further shows that adding sparsity regularization cleanly isolates task-relevant latents (e.g., 'wearing glasses'), while without it irrelevant factors like color become entangled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal representation learning and nonlinear ICA literature by reframing the identifiability goal from full latent recovery to task-relevant subset recovery, and by removing the auxiliary information (interventions, domain shifts, parametric mixing classes) that prior identifiability results depend on. The closest prior work, SelTask (Qiu et al., 2024), also models tasks as colliders but relies on heuristic non-negative matrix factorization without identifiability guarantees and assumes sequential task completion; this paper provides the first general nonparametric identifiability proof for temporal task structure under arbitrary interleaving and disconnections. The sparsity-regularization bridge from generalist to specialist (Theorem 2) is conceptually related to mechanism sparsity in Lachapelle et al. (2022) and structural sparsity in Zheng et al. (2022), but operates without graphical constraints on the latent-observation mapping and targets group-level rather than element-wise identifiability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple ℓ₁ sparsity penalty on the task-latent Jacobian provably disentangles task-relevant from irrelevant latents—without interventions, parametric forms, or temporal dependence—provides a principled justification for the widely practiced but theoretically ungrounded step of fine-tuning generalist models into task-specific specialists. Open questions include finite-sample guarantees (the theory is asymptotic), whether identifiability-inspired architectures can go beyond standard VAE+sparsity, and how the framework scales when the task set itself must be inferred from a large candidate pool. The empirical validation, while covering synthetic SCMs, SportsHHI video, and controllable generation, remains at relatively small scale, leaving open whether the identifiability guarantees hold in the high-dimensional, high-noise regime of frontier vision-language models.
