## Problem

LLMs achieve high accuracy on final-answer math problems but often arrive at correct answers through flawed reasoning, making them unreliable for rigorous proof-based mathematics (IMO, USAMO). Existing proof-verification benchmarks suffer from dataset imbalances and label noise that allow simple heuristics to outperform strong LLMs, and prior comparisons of generative verification methods (GenSelect vs. LLM-as-a-Judge) yield contradictory conclusions depending on the benchmark used. This paper asks: how can we build reliable, scalable proof-verification and selection systems that genuinely assess mathematical validity rather than exploiting stylistic or distributional shortcuts?

## Input / Output

The system takes a mathematical problem statement and one or more candidate natural-language proofs (in LaTeX) as input, optionally augmented with a ground-truth proof or a 0–7 grading rubric. For verification, it produces a binary correct/incorrect judgement (or a 7-point quality score) per proof; for selection, it returns the single best proof among n_p candidates. Judgements are structured with XML tags (<summary>, <judgement>) to enable automated parsing, and the authors additionally track final-answer precision on a balanced Challenge-19 set to separate genuine mathematical assessment from superficial feature exploitation.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified test-time scaling framework that first runs a knockout GenSelect tournament to narrow n_p candidate proofs down to n_s survivors (requiring n_p − n_s pairwise comparisons), then applies LLM-as-a-Judge with n_j independent judgements per surviving proof and selects the highest-scoring one. This hybrid subsumes both pure GenSelect (n_s=1, n_j=0) and pure LLM-as-a-Judge (n_s=n_p) as special cases, and achieves 96% accuracy on Challenge-19 with ~1000 LLM calls versus ~8400 for pure LLM-as-a-Judge at comparable accuracy. Separately, the authors fine-tune Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking as a proof judge using GRPO/DAPO on the VerOPC training set with a binary correctness reward, showing that RL training eliminates the large performance variance across different judge prompts (General Summary, OPC, GIMO) and improves proof-level F1 by ~30 points, yet does not improve final-answer precision—indicating the model learns stylistic calibration rather than deeper mathematical reasoning. A key methodological finding is that a two-layer MLP on text embeddings (75.3%) and a trivial keyword heuristic (65.1%) outperform several LLMs on VerOPC, motivating the use of balanced final-answer evaluation to detect spurious-correlation exploitation.

## Results

On Challenge-19, the proposed Hybrid method (knockout GenSelect + LLM-as-a-Judge, n_p=256, n_s=16, n_j=32) achieves 96.05% accuracy with ×1008 LLM calls per problem, substantially outperforming pure GenSelect knockout (92.10%, ×511), pure LLM-as-a-Judge (91.44%, ×8448), and majority voting (82.23%, ×512). On AIME 2025, the Hybrid method reaches 100% accuracy with ×752 compute, matching LLM-as-a-Judge (100%, ×4224) while using 5.6× fewer calls, and outperforming GenSelect knockout (97.5%, ×255) and majority voting (97.08%, ×128). For proof verification, the best RL-trained judge (OPC RL, No-Rubric→Rubric) achieves 73.35% F1 on VerProofArena and 99.31% final-answer precision on Challenge-19, though RL training does not significantly improve final-answer precision over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Hybrid method (knockout GenSelect + LLM-as-a-Judge) consistently outperforms or matches all three baselines. Against GenSelect knockout tournament (Toshniwal et al., 2025), the Hybrid method gains +3.95 pp on Challenge-19 (96.05% vs 92.10%) and +2.5 pp on AIME 2025 (100% vs 97.5%), at roughly 2× the compute cost. Against LLM-as-a-Judge (Zheng et al., 2023), the Hybrid method achieves the same 100% on AIME 2025 but with 5.6× fewer LLM calls (×752 vs ×4224), and outperforms it by +4.61 pp on Challenge-19 (96.05% vs 91.44%) at only 12% of the compute (×1008 vs ×8448). Against majority voting (Wang et al., 2022), the Hybrid method achieves a large margin of +13.82 pp on Challenge-19 (96.05% vs 82.23%) and +2.92 pp on AIME 2025 (100% vs 97.08%), while using roughly 2× the compute of majority voting. The paper also notes that on proof-level selection benchmarks (SelProofBench, SelOPC), no single method consistently dominates: pairwise GenSelect and LLM-as-a-Judge each win on different datasets and models, motivating the hybrid approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two established generative verification paradigms—GenSelect (Toshniwal et al., 2025), which uses pairwise tournament comparisons to select the best proof, and LLM-as-a-Judge (Zheng et al., 2023), which scores proofs individually—and unifies them into a single scalable framework combining a knockout tournament with parallel pointwise judgements. It extends the RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards) line of work by applying GRPO/DAPO to train proof judges, building on prior RL-for-judgment efforts (Shi & Jin, 2025; Whitehouse et al., 2025). The paper departs from prior conflicting reports (Dekoninck et al., 2025 vs. Ma et al., 2025) about which method dominates by showing the answer is dataset- and model-dependent, and introduces a multi-faceted evaluation protocol that exposes exploitable imbalances in existing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RL fine-tuning improves proof-level F1 but leaves final-answer precision essentially unchanged is a significant conceptual warning: current verifiers may be learning stylistic and procedural cues rather than genuine mathematical validity, limiting their utility for RL-based proof generation. The demonstration that a 0.6B-parameter MLP or a two-rule heuristic outperforms frontier LLMs on VerOPC underscores that the field's evaluation infrastructure is not yet reliable enough to measure real progress. The GPT-5 result (87.2% overall accuracy yet 0/7 correct on hard USAMO problems) further cautions against deploying LLM judges in high-stakes settings. Open questions include decoupling stylistic calibration from logical soundness in verifier training, developing step-level supervision, and constructing balanced, high-quality benchmarks at the frontier of model capabilities.
