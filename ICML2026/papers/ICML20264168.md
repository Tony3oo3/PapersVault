## Problem

Behavior cloning with high-capacity generative policies (diffusion or flow-matching) inherits limitations of demonstration coverage and becomes brittle under distribution shift, while directly fine-tuning large action decoders with RL is unstable and sample-inefficient. Existing latent-space steering methods like DSRL replace the pretrained Gaussian prior with an unconstrained latent policy, causing drift into low-density regions and collapse of the backbone's multimodal behavior. The paper asks whether one can improve a frozen generative policy online while explicitly preserving its latent prior structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation (RGB images, depth, or low-dimensional state vectors depending on the task) and a base Gaussian noise vector ε ~ N(0,I) as input, and produces an action chunk (e.g., 8-step chunks for Adroit) through a frozen generative decoder Φ. The only learned component is a lightweight perturbation network Δ_θ(s, ε) that outputs a residual shift in the same dimensionality as the latent noise space; the decoder weights remain fixed throughout adaptation. For the π₀ VLA backbone, the state includes multi-view RGB and proprioception, and the action chunk is a continuous joint-velocity sequence.

## Method

LP-DS parameterizes the latent input as w = ε + Δ_θ(s, ε), a state- and noise-conditioned residual around the base prior, and optimizes Δ_θ with a Lagrangian trust-region objective that maximizes a latent-space critic Q^W(s, w) subject to E[‖Δ_θ(s,ε)‖²] ≤ δ. The constraint is derived by approximating the expected conditional KL divergence between the steered noise distribution and the Gaussian prior via ½E[‖Δ_θ‖²] in the small-Jacobian regime, then solved with a primal-dual update where the multiplier α adaptively tightens or loosens the trust region. Training uses two critics: an action-space critic Q^A updated by TD learning on environment transitions, and a latent-space critic Q^W distilled from Q^A on the base noise distribution, so the actor gradient flows through the frozen decoder without backpropagating through the full denoising chain. The key design choice over DSRL is that the perturbation is a bounded residual around the prior rather than a free latent policy, which prevents off-prior queries and preserves the backbone's multimodal action distribution while still enabling task-directed improvement.

## Results

Across RoboMimic manipulation, OpenAI Gym locomotion, and Adroit dexterous manipulation (averaged over 6 seeds), LP-DS outperforms all baselines on the majority of environments in success rate or episodic return. On WALKER2D, LP-DS reaches approximately 5000 episodic return versus roughly 4000 for the strongest baseline, a ~25% improvement. On physical Franka deployment, LP-DS improves pick-and-place success from 18/40 to 33/40 and mug-hanging from 11/20 to 17/20 over the frozen backbone. LP-DS also preserves higher action-space entropy than DSRL throughout online adaptation, indicating less mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSRL (latent-space RL steering), LP-DS achieves higher success and return across most benchmark environments while maintaining substantially higher action-space entropy, directly mitigating the mode collapse that DSRL exhibits (DSRL collapses to a single mode in the toy multi-goal task and to a single path in AVOIDING). Against DPPO (policy-gradient fine-tuning through the diffusion decoder), LP-DS achieves comparable or better final performance with greater sample efficiency and stability, since it updates only a lightweight perturbation module rather than the full decoder. Against IDQL and DQL (offline-to-online diffusion Q-learning baselines), LP-DS reaches higher success rates on RoboMimic and Adroit and higher returns on Gym locomotion, again with more stable learning curves. The paper notes that LP-DS's advantage over DSRL is most pronounced in environments where the frozen backbone has strong multimodal structure, as the Lagrangian trust-region constraint prevents the perturbation from drifting into low-density latent regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LP-DS builds directly on DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), which introduced latent-space RL for steering frozen diffusion policies, but departs from it by replacing the unconstrained latent policy with a state- and noise-conditioned residual perturbation governed by an adaptive Lagrangian trust-region constraint. The trust-region approximation (expected squared perturbation magnitude as a proxy for conditional KL divergence) is borrowed from the noise-hypernetwork literature (Eyring et al., 2025), repurposed here as a regularizer for RL-based policy improvement rather than for amortizing test-time compute. The method also positions itself against decoder fine-tuning approaches (DPPO, IDQL, DQL) by arguing that optimizing a lightweight perturbation module in noise space is more stable and sample-efficient than updating the full generative decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LP-DS offers a practical, low-cost pathway to improve frozen generative policies—particularly large VLA models like π0—without the instability and compute cost of full decoder fine-tuning, and the trust-region target δ provides an interpretable dial for the diversity-versus-specialization tradeoff that prior latent-steering methods lack. The cross-architecture validation (diffusion, flow-matching, VLA) and initial sim-to-real Franka results suggest broad applicability, though the physical-robot evidence is limited to two tasks with small trial counts. Open questions include whether adaptive or state-dependent trust-region targets can further reduce manual tuning, how the method behaves in partially observable settings, and whether the KL approximation remains valid under larger perturbations or higher-dimensional latent spaces.
