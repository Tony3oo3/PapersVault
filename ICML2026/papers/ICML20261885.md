## Problem

Existing T2M evaluation metrics fail to capture whether a generated motion semantically matches its text description: reference-based metrics (FID, L1) are limited by the one-to-many nature of text-to-motion mapping, while reference-free evaluators (MoBERT, MotionCritic) suffer from data scarcity and produce scores that do not reflect true alignment. Additionally, the field lacks a dedicated test-only meta-evaluation benchmark for fairly comparing the generalizability of different T2M evaluators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a 3D motion sequence (22-joint SMPL joint rotations/positions) as input. The motion is rendered into a 2D skinned human video at 1088×1088 resolution, 20 FPS, using Blender with a fixed camera offset and sun-type lighting; 32 frames are uniformly sampled for VLM inference. The output is a scalar alignment score in [0, 1], computed as the normalized likelihood of the VLM answering 'yes' (aligned) versus 'no' (misaligned) to a fixed instruction template.

## Method

VeMo transforms the 3D T2M evaluation problem into a zero-shot video-language reasoning task by rendering generated motions into skinned 2D videos and leveraging a pretrained VLM (InternVL3-14B) to compute a normalized likelihood score over 'yes'/'no' answers, requiring no motion-specific training data. To mitigate information loss from 3D-to-2D projection (e.g., self-occlusion), the method introduces entropy-driven view selection: K rendered views are scored for predictive entropy, and the lowest-entropy view is selected as the evaluation input, grounded in the information-theoretic principle that minimizing H(Y|V,T) maximizes mutual information I(Y;V|T). The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing a non-monotonic entropy-noise relationship (entropy increases with moderate occlusion but drops under extreme degradation due to 'confident rejection'), justifying the need for the human subject to remain visible in the rendered frame. The framework is entirely zero-shot: no text-motion pairs are used for training or in-context learning, and the same instruction template is applied across all VLMs without prompt optimization.

## Results

On the VeMo meta-evaluation benchmark (2,202 text-motion pairs from MDM and MotionGPT), VeMo achieves AUC-ROC 0.720, AUPR 0.743, KS 0.354, Kendall's τ 0.311, and Spearman's ρ 0.381, with p-value < 1e-6. This represents a >4× improvement in KS and τ over the best reference-free baseline (MoBERT-N: KS 0.088, τ 0.069) and a ~1.7× improvement over the best reference-based method (Minus L1 Distance: KS 0.204, τ 0.180). Human user scores reach AUC-ROC ~0.83 and τ/ρ ~0.67, indicating that no automatic metric yet fully replaces human judgment, but VeMo narrows the gap substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeMo outperforms all four baseline families across every metric. Against the reference-based Minus L1 Distance (Guo et al., 2022), VeMo improves AUC-ROC from 0.627 to 0.720 and KS from 0.204 to 0.354, while also exceeding it in rank correlation (τ: 0.311 vs. 0.180). Against the reference-free Multimodal Distance and R@K-Precision (Tevet et al., 2022), VeMo's KS of 0.354 is roughly 7–22× higher (0.048 and 0.016 respectively), and the p-value drops from non-significant (0.149, 0.129) to <1e-6. Against MoBERT (Voas et al., 2023), which is explicitly trained on T2M data with human labels, VeMo still achieves a 4× higher KS (0.354 vs. 0.088) and τ (0.311 vs. 0.069) without any motion-specific training. Against MotionCritic (Wang et al., 2024), a text-independent evaluator, VeMo's advantage is even more pronounced (KS 0.354 vs. 0.027), confirming that joint text-motion reasoning is essential. The paper notes that human user scores (τ/ρ ≈ 0.67) remain the upper bound, but VeMo is the only automatic method that surpasses the reference-based L1 distance on all correlation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

VeMo departs from the established T2M evaluation paradigm—where reference-based metrics (FID, L1) compare against ground-truth motions and reference-free evaluators (MoBERT, MotionCritic) are trained on scarce text-motion pairs—by reframing evaluation as a zero-shot video-language reasoning task. It adapts the semantic-entropy framework of Farquhar et al. (2024) from LLM hallucination detection to VLM-based view selection, using predictive entropy to identify the most informative rendered perspective. The method leverages the internet-scale semantic knowledge of pretrained VLMs (e.g., InternVL3-14B) rather than any motion-specific training data, and contributes the first test-only, human-annotated meta-evaluation benchmark specifically designed to compare the generalizability of T2M evaluators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard reference-free metrics (MM Distance, R@K-Precision, MotionCritic) perform at or near chance level (AUC ≈ 0.5) for semantic alignment is a significant negative result that challenges the field's reliance on these metrics. VeMo's zero-shot VLM approach, combined with the dedicated meta-evaluation benchmark, provides a practical path toward more reliable T2M assessment without the data-scarcity bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach extends to fine-grained diagnostic evaluation (identifying which body part or temporal segment causes mismatch), how it scales to broader motion representations and generation paradigms beyond HumanML3D, and whether multi-view integration can further close the gap to human-level judgment.
