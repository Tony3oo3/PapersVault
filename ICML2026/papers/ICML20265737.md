## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods treat multi-stage pipelines as monolithic black boxes, providing no mechanism to attribute prediction uncertainty to specific pipeline stages. Under distribution shift, which often affects stages asymmetrically (e.g., upstream sensor drift while downstream mappings remain stable), standard conformal intervals degrade uniformly without indicating which stage requires intervention, forcing practitioners to retrain the entire pipeline when targeted fixes would suffice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes upstream features w, pre-trained two-stage predictors μ̂₁ and μ̂₂, and two held-out sets (a conformal set S^conf for computing residual quantiles and a calibration set S^cal for FWER-based parameter selection), requiring only that the intermediate variable x is observable at the pipeline interface rather than requiring internal model access. It produces a prediction interval centered at μ̂₂(μ̂₁(w_test)) with a half-width that is a weighted sum of stage-specific quantiles, along with diagnostic scaling parameters (a, b) that quantify each stage's contribution to uncertainty. Notably, the method can abstain entirely (returning no interval) when the FWER test rejects all candidate scaling pairs, signaling that the shift is too severe and retraining is needed.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes the end-to-end residual R = |y − μ̂₂(μ̂₁(w))| into an upstream component ΔR₁ = ||y − μ̂₂(x)| − |y − μ̂₂(μ̂₁(w))|| and a downstream component R₂ = |y − μ̂₂(x)|, satisfying R ≤ ΔR₁ + R₂ by the triangle inequality. Prediction intervals are constructed as μ̂₂(μ̂₁(w_test)) ± (a·Q_{1−c}({ΔR₁}) + b·Q_{1−d}({R₂})), where quantiles are computed over the conformal set and scaling weights (a, b) are selected via FWER-controlled multiple hypothesis testing over a finite candidate grid Λ, ensuring that with probability ≥ 1−δ no accepted pair has true miscoverage exceeding α. An adaptive extension uses a sliding window to dynamically update (a_t, b_t, c_t, d_t, α_t) at each time step: when coverage drops, α_t decreases; when one residual component dominates recent errors, the corresponding scaling parameter is increased within the validated set; and when the validated set is too restrictive, quantile levels c_t, d_t are adjusted to expand future options. This stage-sensitive update rule preserves the long-run coverage guarantee of adaptive conformal inference while enabling targeted responses to upstream versus downstream shifts.

## Results

Under upstream distribution shifts at α=0.1, the proposed SR method (c=d=0.01) achieves 0.842 coverage under gradual shift and 0.804 under rapid shift, compared to 0.728/0.691 for split conformal (SC) and 0.749/0.722 for weighted split conformal (WSC), at the cost of wider intervals (2.99/3.97 vs. 2.37/2.95 width). Under downstream shifts, SR achieves 0.931/0.898 coverage (gradual/rapid) versus 0.870/0.842 for SC. On the real-world automobile indicators dataset, SR maintains coverage through the 2020 pandemic-induced shift with a mean interval width of 18.96, comparable to PID (18.47) but wider than ACI (15.70) and OCID (15.67), while ACI and OCID show degraded coverage during the shift period.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against split conformal (SC, Vovk et al. 2005) and weighted split conformal (WSC, Barber et al. 2023), the proposed SR method consistently achieves higher coverage under both upstream and downstream distribution shifts (e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.73/0.75 gradual upstream; 0.93 vs. 0.87/0.87 gradual downstream), though at the cost of 20–30% wider intervals. Against cascaded confidence (CC, Gong et al. 2023), SR is substantially more calibrated: CC severely over-covers (0.92–0.98) with intervals 30–50% wider, while SR stays closer to nominal coverage with a tolerance parameter τ that allows tightening. In the adaptive setting, SR outperforms ACI (Gibbs & Candès 2021), PID (Angelopoulos et al. 2023), and OCID (Angelopoulos et al. 2024b) on the automobile dataset by maintaining coverage through the 2020 structural shift, whereas OCID only partially covers the shift and ACI/PID show more variable coverage; SR's mean width (18.96) is comparable to PID (18.47) but wider than ACI (15.70) and OCID (15.67). ECI (Wu et al. 2025) and DtACI (Gibbs & Candès 2024) show weaker performance and are relegated to the appendix. The key trade-off is that SR's FWER-based conservatism produces wider intervals in easy (IID) settings, but the tolerance parameter τ=0.05 recovers efficiency while preserving robustness under shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conformal prediction from single-stage black-box models to modular two-stage pipelines by decomposing the end-to-end residual into upstream (ΔR₁) and downstream (R₂) components. The FWER-based parameter selection directly builds on the learn-then-test framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2025) and the risk-control machinery of Bates et al. (2021), repurposing multiple hypothesis testing to select stage-specific scaling weights rather than a single quantile threshold. The adaptive extension inherits its long-run coverage guarantee from Gibbs & Candès (2021) ACI, adding stage-sensitive update rules on top. The closest prior work, Gong et al. (2023), also decomposes cascaded model error but applies a conservative upper-bound without stage-wise attribution, adaptive updates, or FWER-controlled selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical diagnostic tool for modular forecasting pipelines: practitioners can identify which stage drives uncertainty and target retraining to that stage rather than rebuilding the entire pipeline. The FWER-based abstention mechanism (Λ_val = ∅) provides a principled signal that the shift is too severe for the current model, which black-box conformal methods cannot offer. Key limitations include conservative interval widths, the focus on two-stage models (multi-stage extension in Appendix C.4 is acknowledged as requiring a smarter search), and the IID calibration assumption that is only partially relaxed to φ-mixing. Open questions include whether the decomposition remains informative under strong non-linear interactions between stages, and how the method scales when the number of stages or upstream models grows.
