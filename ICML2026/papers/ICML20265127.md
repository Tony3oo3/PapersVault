## Problem

Existing Transformer-based decoders for error correction codes treat codewords as generic sequences, ignoring the algebraic structure of the underlying code family, and the roles of embedding dimensions and parameter matrices in these models lack principled interpretation. This paper addresses both gaps by showing how the cyclic symmetry of cyclic codes can be formally exploited to reduce the diversity of error correction patterns and to give a structural meaning to the learned parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265127/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The decoder receives the soft-valued noisy codeword y (length n) concatenated with its syndrome s(y) (length n when using the n×n circulant PCM), forming an augmented vector of length 2n that is embedded into a d-dimensional space. The network outputs an estimate of the multiplicative channel noise, from which the hard-decision decoded codeword is recovered via sign comparison. The method is plug-and-play: it modifies only the PCM construction and the parameterization of existing Transformer decoder layers (ECCT, CrossMPT, MM-ECCT) without altering their attention or feed-forward logic.

## Method

The core mechanism extends the parity-check matrix to an n×n circulant (square) form, which provably unifies all variable-node error correction patterns under cyclic equivalence and makes all four types of inter-node relationships (V-V, C-V, V-C, C-C) cyclic shifts of a single representative. Building on this, the paper interprets the embedding dimension d as encoding the adjacency structure of the Tanner graph: at d=2n, each embedding dimension corresponds bijectively to one node, so only two representative rows (one variable-node, one check-node) need to be learned for both the embedding matrix and all attention parameter matrices (W^Q, W^K, W^V). All remaining rows are reconstructed via block-wise cyclic shifts (Algorithm 1), and a scaling rule d=2rn for rational r generalizes this to dimension reduction (r<1) or expansion (r>1). Applied to ECCT, CrossMPT, and MM-ECCT, this cyclic parameter reuse reduces the total parameter count by ~97% while improving -ln(BER) by roughly one order of magnitude at SNR=6 dB, with the gains attributed to the model focusing on a single unified correction pattern rather than heterogeneous positional-specific ones.

## Results

At SNR = 6 dB with d = 2n, the proposed cyclic parameter reuse method applied to CrossMPT achieves -ln(BER) of 16.61 on PRM(127,99), 13.19 on BCH(63,45), and 11.80 on BCH(63,36), representing roughly a one-order-of-magnitude BER reduction over the corresponding baselines. The method simultaneously reduces total parameter counts to 1.91%–4.16% of the original (≈97% reduction) while maintaining or improving decoding accuracy. Gains are most pronounced for high-rate codes (e.g., PRM(127,99) with k/n ≈ 0.78), where CrossMPT+Ours approaches ML decoding performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is applied as a plug-and-play modification to three Transformer decoders (ECCT, CrossMPT, MM-ECCT) and compared against their unmodified versions plus the CE BP neural belief-propagation decoder. Against ECCT, the improvement is most dramatic for high-rate codes: on PRM(127,99) at SNR=6, -ln(BER) rises from 11.33 to 15.66 (≈1.9 orders of magnitude BER reduction). Against CrossMPT, the gain is similarly large (12.22 → 16.61 on PRM(127,99); 10.80 → 13.19 on BCH(63,45)). Against MM-ECCT, which already uses a multi-mask strategy, the improvement is more modest but consistent (e.g., 11.67 → 12.85 on BCH(63,45); 12.91 → 13.13 on PRM(63,42)). Against CE BP, the method wins on every code where both are reported, with the largest margin on PRM(127,99) (16.61 vs. 11.15). Notably, all these gains are achieved while reducing parameter counts by ~97%, and the method does not significantly increase inference latency (e.g., 419 μs vs. 336 μs for PRM(127,99)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based ECC decoder lineage (ECCT → CrossMPT → MM-ECCT) by injecting the algebraic structure of cyclic codes directly into the model parameters, rather than treating codes as generic sequences. It builds most closely on Xiao et al. (2025), which first applied Tanner cycle-equivalence to Transformer decoders, but provides a more rigorous coding-theoretic foundation through two formal notions—Error Correction Patterns and inter-node relationships—and a principled interpretation of embedding dimensions as encoding Tanner-graph topology. The plug-and-play cyclic parameter reuse mechanism departs from prior work by deriving the parameter-sharing structure from the circulant PCM's algebraic symmetry rather than from architectural heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers the first coding-theoretic explanation for why embedding dimension matters in Transformer decoders (d = 2n corresponds to a bijection between dimensions and Tanner-graph nodes), shifting the field from empirical tuning toward mechanistic understanding. The ~97% parameter reduction with simultaneous BER improvement makes code-specialized deployment more practical, and the Error Correction Pattern framework suggests a new axis for code design (intentionally reducing ECP diversity to aid neural decoders). Key limitations include restriction to cyclic codes (BCH, PRM), the explicitly heuristic nature of the embedding interpretation, and the modest increase in FLOPs from the square PCM; whether the topology-encoding hypothesis generalizes to non-cyclic codes (LDPC, polar) remains open.
