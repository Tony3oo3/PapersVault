## Problem

Speaker recognition in long-form TV dramas is fundamentally harder than standard speaker diarization or verification: a single drama may feature 100+ named characters across multiple seasons, with short utterances, off-screen speakers, overlapping speech, and complex narrative context that no single modality can resolve. Existing benchmarks (AMI, VoxConverse, AVA-AVD) involve far fewer speakers and shorter clips, and no prior work formulates the task as open-set character attribution requiring joint reasoning over acoustic, visual, and linguistic evidence.

## Input / Output

The method assumes pre-segmented, timestamped dialogue transcripts and a priori candidate character lists (primary roles by name, secondary roles by descriptive attributes), isolating the attribution problem from segmentation and character discovery. At inference, the LRM receives a 20–30 line dialogue window plus tool outputs: a voiceprint similarity matrix (192-d ERes2Net embeddings, cosine similarity), hierarchical video captions (detailed clip-level descriptions from Qwen3-VL-32B and brief segment summaries from Qwen3-32B), and a temporal character-relationship ontology. The output is a character ID per utterance (or a null/[UNKNOWN] tag for ancillary speakers), produced through an iterative refinement loop where updated labels feed back into tool recomputation.

## Method

DramaSR-LRM is a Qwen3-8B backbone fine-tuned to autonomously invoke three multimodal tools (voice_sim, video_cap, char_relation) and synthesize their outputs via chain-of-thought reasoning to adjudicate ambiguous speaker attributions. Training is two-stage: (1) SFT on ~10K Gemini-3-Pro-generated CoT trajectories curated from a strategic 20% subset of hard cases (baseline failures, borderline acoustic margins <0.03, and balanced controls), using a feedback-driven distillation loop where incorrect teacher predictions are re-prompted with ground truth; (2) GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward (binary accuracy + format compliance) over 10K labeled utterances. The full pipeline initializes with a label propagation baseline (greedy seed clustering + affinity propagation over a 30s visual-neighborhood window), then iteratively refines via the LRM with confidence sampling (invoking the model only when top-1/top-2 acoustic margin < ρ=0.10), achieving 87.79% accuracy vs. 85.49% baseline, with the largest gains on very short utterances (+9.20%) and off-screen speech (52.4% vs. 13.4%).

## Results

On the DramaSR-532K benchmark (428K test utterances across 11 held-out dramas), DramaSR-LRM with confidence sampling achieves 87.79% utterance-level speaker recognition accuracy, improving over the label propagation baseline (85.49%) by 2.30% absolute (a 16% relative error reduction). Gains are most pronounced on acoustically challenging subsets: very short utterances (<0.5s) improve from 67.45% to 76.65% (+9.20%), and short utterances (0.5–1s) from 82.37% to 85.70% (+3.33%). The model also generalizes cross-lingually, improving English-drama accuracy from 82.41% to 85.22% despite being trained exclusively on Chinese dramas.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DramaSR-LRM (87.79%) outperforms all baselines on the overall accuracy metric. Against the strongest non-LLM baseline, the label propagation algorithm (85.49%), the gain is +2.30% absolute, with the largest improvements on very short utterances (+9.20%) and short utterances (+3.33%), where acoustic voiceprints are unreliable. Against the state-of-the-art diarization framework pyannote (79.82%), DramaSR-LRM achieves a +7.97% absolute gain, confirming that standard diarization struggles with the high character density (30–144 speakers per drama) and long-range temporal dependencies of TV dramas. The ablation from SFT-only (75.22%) to SFT+RL (86.93%) shows that reinforcement learning (GRPO) contributes a critical +11.71% over supervised fine-tuning alone, while confidence sampling adds a further +0.86% by restricting LRM invocation to ambiguous cases. On off-screen utterances (1.8% of data), DramaSR-LRM succeeds in 52.4% of cases versus only 13.4% for the label propagation baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions speaker recognition in long-form TV dramas as a distinct challenge from classical speaker diarization, verification, and active speaker detection, which typically involve 10–25 speakers in short clips. Methodologically, it builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) and the Qwen3-8B backbone, but departs from prior MLLM-based speaker identification work (SpeakerLM, D-ORCA) by training a dedicated reasoning model that explicitly invokes multimodal tools (voiceprint similarity, video captioning, character relationship lookup) in a chain-of-thought loop rather than performing end-to-end classification. The label propagation baseline with ERes2Net voiceprints and affinity propagation follows standard speaker verification practice, while the LRM refinement layer is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DramaSR-532K benchmark (532K utterances, 900+ characters, 13 dramas across English and Chinese) is the most comprehensive resource to date for narrative speaker recognition and will likely become a standard evaluation set. The finding that a reasoning LRM trained on only two Chinese dramas generalizes zero-shot to English-language shows and fast-paced genres suggests that character-attribution reasoning is language-agnostic, opening the door to lightweight cross-lingual transfer. Key open questions remain: handling multi-speaker lines (currently treated as inseparable units), open-world speaker description for the 6.6K+ ancillary roles, and end-to-end recognition from raw audio without pre-segmented transcripts. The modest overall gain (+2.30%) but large gains on very short utterances (+9.20%) highlight that acoustic biometrics remain the dominant signal, with reasoning serving as a targeted correction mechanism rather than a replacement.
