## Problem

Existing LLM uncertainty quantification relies on costly sampling-based or logits-based signals, while verbal uncertainty markers (e.g., 'likely', 'possible') offer a natural, single-response alternative. However, the paper reveals a fundamental misalignment: LLMs assign numerical confidence to verbal markers that diverges substantially from human interpretations (e.g., GPT-4o treats 'very likely' as 0.355 confidence while humans rate it 0.853), meaning human-derived lookup tables cannot reliably quantify LLM uncertainty. The paper asks whether LLM-specific marker-to-probability mappings can be learned to close this gap.

## Input / Output

VOCAL consumes a single LLM text generation (no multi-sample or logit access required) and extracts all verbal uncertainty markers (UMs) present in the response via a prompted JSON field. At inference time, each extracted marker is looked up in a pre-optimized table to retrieve its learned confidence score, and the final uncertainty is the mean of (1 − score) across all markers in the generation. This single-response design contrasts with sampling-based baselines that require 5+ generations and semantic clustering, making VOCAL suitable for latency-sensitive deployment.

## Method

VOCAL learns a numerical confidence score for each verbal uncertainty marker by minimizing a binary cross-entropy loss that rewards assigning higher confidence to correct generations and lower confidence to incorrect ones, using ~300 labeled examples per dataset. To handle data sparsity for rare markers (e.g., 'faint chance'), it adds a graph Laplacian regularizer that penalizes large score differences between semantically similar markers, where similarity is measured by 3-gram Jaccard overlap on a weighted graph over the marker vocabulary. The joint objective (BCE + γ·cᵀLc) is convex and optimized with Adam for up to 100 epochs with early stopping. At test time, the one-time-optimized lookup table is applied to a single generation: markers are extracted, their learned scores averaged, and the result converted to an uncertainty score. This avoids any additional sampling or inference-time computation beyond the single forward pass that produced the response.

## Results

VOCAL achieves AUROC of 0.715 on GSM-Hard with DeepSeek-V3.1, substantially outperforming single-sample baselines G-NLL (0.520) and PPL (0.567). On SciQ with Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, VOCAL reaches 0.717 AUROC, the best among all evaluated methods including multi-sample baselines. For calibration, VOCAL with isotonic regression achieves the lowest average ECE of 0.0281 across GSM-Hard, GSM8K, and MedQA, outperforming all other methods. VOCAL consistently improves over the human-sourced UM-Lookup (Q_VU-H), e.g., boosting GPT-4o's AUROC on GSM8K from 0.41 to ~0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-sample baselines, VOCAL wins in 5 of 6 settings: it outperforms G-NLL (Aichberger et al., 2025) by up to 19.5 AUROC points (GSM-Hard, DeepSeek-V3.1: 0.715 vs. 0.520) and PPL by up to 13.5 points (GSM-Hard, DeepSeek-V3.1: 0.715 vs. 0.567); PPL is marginally better only on Trivia QA with GPT-4o (0.575 vs. 0.573). Against multi-sample baselines (Semantic Entropy, Predictive Entropy, Deg, Semantic Density, sentSAR), VOCAL is competitive without requiring multiple generations: it matches or exceeds SE on SciQ with Qwen2.5-72B (0.717) and is within a few points on most other configurations, while avoiding the latency and cost of 5-sample semantic-consistency pipelines. On calibration (ECE), VOCAL with isotonic regression achieves the best average ECE (0.0281), beating PE (0.0568), Deg (0.0425), SD (0.0360), and G-NLL (0.0395). The human-sourced UM-Lookup (Q_VU-H) is consistently outperformed by VOCAL across all model-dataset pairs, confirming that LLM-specific optimization is necessary. Lexical Similarity (Fomicheva et al., 2020) is included as a baseline in the multi-sample comparison but is generally the weakest method, consistent with its known sensitivity to surface-form variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the verbal uncertainty quantification line initiated by Mielke et al. (2022) and Lin et al. (2022), which showed that LLMs can be prompted to express uncertainty in natural language. It departs from both the sampling-based UQ paradigm (Semantic Entropy, Deg, sentSAR) and the logits-based paradigm (Predictive Entropy, G-NLL) by treating verbal markers as a single-sample predictive signal rather than requiring multiple generations or internal logit access. The key conceptual departure is from prior work that applies a fixed, human-derived probability mapping to verbal markers: VOCAL instead learns a model-specific mapping via BCE optimization with graph Laplacian regularization, borrowing the smoothing formulation from graph-based learning (Fu et al., 2022; Maskey et al., 2023). The UM-Lookup resource itself is grounded in classical psychology and decision-science scaling studies (Lichtenstein & Newman, 1967; Beyth-Marom, 1982; Wesson & Pulford, 2009; Vogel et al., 2022), repurposing these human calibration data as a baseline rather than as the final mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a practical tool (VOCAL: single-sample UQ matching multi-sample baselines at a fraction of the cost) and a foundational resource (the 115-marker UM-Lookup table) that could anchor future metacognition and human-AI alignment research. The revealed misalignment—where GPT-4o assigns 0.355 confidence to 'very likely' versus 0.853 for humans—challenges the implicit assumption in prior verbal-UQ work that a single universal mapping suffices, pointing toward model-specific calibration as a necessary step. Limitations include the coarse granularity of verbal markers compared to full probability distributions, sensitivity to extraction quality, and domain/cultural variability in marker interpretation. Open questions include whether VOCAL-style optimization generalizes to multilingual settings, whether richer aggregation strategies beyond simple averaging improve fidelity, and whether the learned mappings remain stable across model updates or fine-tuning.
