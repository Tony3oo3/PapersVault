## Problem

At 2-bit precision, scalar quantization (SQ) suffers severe performance degradation (>30%) due to its rigid per-dimension grid, while vector quantization (VQ) achieves better accuracy but requires large codebooks that cause significant storage overhead and slow inference due to frequent GPU memory-to-cache transfers. No existing method simultaneously achieves VQ-level accuracy and SQ-level inference efficiency at 2 bits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264901/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (Qwen-3 4B–32B, Llama-3 8B) and 1,024 calibration sequences of length 2,048 from RedPajama. It outputs 2-bit integer weight matrices plus a small set of affine parameters per weight matrix (A ∈ R^{4×4} and B ∈ R^4, totaling 20 floats), which are ~1/64 the storage of a VQ codebook. Because the dequantization is an affine transform of integers, the output remains directly compatible with optimized integer matmul kernels without any codebook lookup or decompression step.

## Method

UniSVQ parameterizes the quantization grid as an affine transformation of integer lattices (C_i = A·W̄_i + B), creating a linear-constrained codebook that is equivalent to VQ during quantization but reduces to a simple affine dequantization at inference. The pipeline has three stages: (1) a Randomized Hadamard Transform redistributes weight magnitudes to eliminate outliers and yield an approximately isotropic Gaussian distribution; (2) LDLQ calibration quantizes groups of d=4 weights column-by-column using the Hessian, with the grid initialized by a random orthogonal matrix G scaled to unit variance; (3) a data-driven block-wise fine-tuning step optimizes A and B via backpropagation on an MSE loss, exploiting the fact that the affine dequantization commutes with the linear layer's matmul (Y = Σ X_i A · W_int,i^T + Σ X_i B · 1^T), so only O(Nnd) extra FLOPs are added. This structure preserves SQ-compatible inference while capturing most of VQ's distributional flexibility.

## Results

On Qwen-3-8B at 2-bit, UniSVQ achieves 67.95% average zero-shot QA accuracy (92% of FP16), outperforming the best scalar baseline OSTQuant (57.47%) by over 10 points and matching or exceeding the vector quantization baselines QuIP# (67.55%) and AQLM (64.94%). On the larger Qwen-3-32B, UniSVQ reaches 76.15% average accuracy (98% of FP16), essentially matching QuIP# (76.30%) while using only 20 auxiliary parameters per weight matrix versus QuIP#'s full E8P codebook. Inference throughput on Llama-3-8B reaches 101.65 tok/s with 3.87 GB peak memory, a 1.68× speedup over FP16 and higher throughput than both AQLM (70.97 tok/s) and QuIP# (79.60 tok/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all four scalar quantization baselines, UniSVQ wins decisively: on Qwen-3-8B it surpasses GPTQ (37.29%), QuIP (43.82%), SpinQuant (52.61%), and OSTQuant (57.47%) by 30.7, 24.1, 15.3, and 10.5 average accuracy points respectively, with GPTQ and QuIP essentially collapsing to near-random performance at 2-bit. Against the vector quantization baselines, UniSVQ beats AQLM (64.94%) by 3.0 points and QuIP# (67.55%) by 0.4 points on Qwen-3-8B, while on Qwen-3-32B it trails QuIP# by a negligible 0.15 points (76.15 vs 76.30) and beats AQLM by 0.78 points. The one clear loss is to QTIP (71.08% on Qwen-3-8B), which uses trellis-coded quantization for higher precision at the cost of complex decoding and higher memory traffic. UniSVQ's key advantage over VQ methods is practical: it requires only 20 floating-point parameters per weight matrix (vs. thousands for a full codebook), enabling reuse of optimized integer GEMM kernels and achieving 101.65 tok/s throughput versus 79.60 for QuIP# and 70.97 for AQLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSVQ sits at the intersection of two well-developed PTQ lineages: orthogonal-transformation-based scalar quantization (QuIP, SpinQuant, OSTQuant) and lattice/codebook-based vector quantization (QuIP#, AQLM, QTIP). It inherits the Randomized Hadamard Transform preprocessing and LDLQ Hessian-based calibration from the QuIP/QuIP# family, but departs from VQ by replacing the unstructured codebook with a low-rank affine parameterization of integer lattices, and departs from SQ by quantizing groups of d weights jointly rather than independently. The key conceptual move is identifying the linear-constrained quantization grid as a structural middle ground that preserves SQ's kernel compatibility while recovering most of VQ's distributional flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: UniSVQ reduces codebook storage by ~64× relative to VQ baselines while matching their accuracy, and its affine dequantization is commutative with the linear-layer matmul, enabling direct reuse of highly optimized integer GEMM kernels (1.68× throughput over FP16, 75%+ memory reduction). The demonstration that a 2-bit 32B model can outperform an FP16 4B model on zero-shot QA has direct implications for memory-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to activation and KV-cache quantization, how the affine overhead interacts with next-generation fused GEMM kernels, and whether the linear constraint remains tight at sub-2-bit regimes.
