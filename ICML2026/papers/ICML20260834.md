## Problem

Efficient robust recovery of single-index models under heavy-tailed noise and strong adversarial contamination was previously known only for linear regression, monotonic link functions, and phase retrieval. Standard first-order proof techniques break down under adversarial contamination in high dimensions, and it was unclear whether the two structural properties that make phase retrieval tractable—a constant-radius convex basin and spectral identifiability—extend to generic non-monotonic links such as GELU and SWISH. This paper asks: can we characterize the class of link functions that admit efficient provable robust recovery, and provide the first such guarantees for a broad family of non-monotonic SIMs?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an ε-strongly contaminated dataset of n = Õ(d) pairs (x_i, y_i) with Gaussian covariates x_i ~ N(0, I_d), a known twice-differentiable link function f satisfying two structural assumptions (bounded local Gaussian moment envelopes ensuring a convex basin, and positive Expected Squared Convexity ensuring spectral identifiability), and a corruption fraction ε. It outputs a unit-norm estimate β̂ of the true index β* with ℓ₂ error O(σ√ε) in Õ(nd) time. The link function need not be monotonic or symmetric; the framework covers GELU, SWISH, TANH, PROBIT, LOGISTIC, and phase retrieval (f(z)=z²).

## Method

The paper proves two structural theorems that together enable a two-stage algorithm. First, under a mild condition on the link function's local Gaussian moment envelopes (Assumption 2.1), the population squared-loss Hessian is uniformly strongly convex and smooth throughout a dimension-independent ball of constant radius R around β*, establishing the first convex basin guarantee for non-affine, non-monotonic links beyond phase retrieval. Second, under the Expected Squared Convexity (ESC) condition (Assumption 2.2), second-order Stein's identity decomposes E[(YX)(YX)ᵀ] into an isotropic component plus a rank-one signal term aligned with β*, making β* the top eigenvector of a computable moment matrix. The algorithm (Algorithm 1) first runs a linear-time robust spectral initialization (LRSI) that applies the hypercontractive 1-ePCA subroutine of Jambulapati et al. (2024) to the sample second-moment matrix of Ỹ = YX, landing inside the convex basin with O(ε^{1/4}) error; it then refines the estimate via O(1) steps of robust gradient descent (LRGD) using robust mean estimation (Diakonikolas et al., 2022) as the gradient estimator, converging linearly within the basin to the final O(σ√ε) error.

## Results

The paper establishes the first near-linear time and optimal sample complexity robust recovery guarantees for generic non-monotonic SIMs (GELU, SWISH, TANH, PROBIT, LOGISTIC, Phase Retrieval) under heavy-tailed noise and constant-fraction adversarial contamination. The algorithm achieves estimation error O(σ√ε) using Õ(d) samples in Õ(nd) time, where ε is the contamination fraction. The key structural contribution is proving the existence of a dimension-independent, constant-radius convex basin around the ground truth in the squared-loss landscape, combined with a robust spectral initialization (via Stein's identity + robust PCA) that provably lands within this basin under adversarial corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's guarantees strictly dominate all prior robust SIM recovery results in terms of the class of link functions handled: it is the first to cover asymmetric non-monotonic links (GELU, SWISH) under both heavy-tailed noise and adversarial contamination simultaneously. Compared to Diakonikolas et al. (2019b), which handles only monotone links with Poly(d) time, this paper achieves Õ(nd) time and extends to non-monotonic links. Compared to Awasthi et al. (2022), which achieves the optimal O(σε log(1/ε)) error for monotone links but with only heuristic time complexity and no heavy-tailed noise guarantee, this paper provides a provable Õ(nd) time algorithm under heavy-tailed noise at the cost of a slightly worse O(σ√ε) error rate. For phase retrieval specifically, the paper improves upon Buna & Rebeschini (2025) (exponential time e^d) and Das & Batra (2026) (Õ(n²d) time) by achieving Õ(nd) time while generalizing beyond the quadratic link. The O(σ√ε) error rate matches the best known for any non-linear SIM under adversarial contamination, though it remains a gap from the information-theoretically optimal Õ(σε) rate known for linear regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust recovery framework for Single-Index Models from monotonic links (Awasthi et al., 2022; Diakonikolas et al., 2019b) and the special case of phase retrieval (Buna & Rebeschini, 2025; Das & Batra, 2026) to generic asymmetric non-monotonic link functions such as GELU and SWISH. It builds directly on the robust hypercontractive 1-ePCA subroutine of Jambulapati et al. (2024) for spectral initialization and the robust mean estimation of Diakonikolas et al. (2022) for gradient descent, coupling them with a new structural condition (Expected Squared Convexity) derived via second-order Stein's identities. The key departure from prior phase retrieval work is that the reachability argument no longer exploits the symmetric quadratic structure of f(z)=z², instead relying on a general moment-matrix decomposition that applies to any link satisfying the ESC condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient robust recovery guarantee for non-monotonic SIMs beyond phase retrieval, covering activation functions (GELU, SWISH) that are fundamental scalar primitives in modern Transformer architectures. The dimension-independent convex basin result is a clean structural contribution that unifies the geometry of non-convex SIM loss landscapes under a single checkable condition. Key open questions include closing the gap between the achieved O(σ√ε) rate and the information-theoretically optimal O(σε) rate, extending to non-Gaussian designs, and handling multi-index models where GELU/SWISH actually appear in practice (as GLU interactions). The framework also leaves open whether convex basins persist under alternative losses (Huber, M-estimators) and for links with information exponent ≥ 3.
