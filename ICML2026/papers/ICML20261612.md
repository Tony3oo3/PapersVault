## Problem

Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) matching is NP-hard and existing entropic solvers scale cubically in the number of points, limiting them to a few thousand samples. Moreover, prior work has largely restricted attention to squared Euclidean distance penalties, which bias the matching toward long-range interactions and are sensitive to outliers. The paper asks whether a broader class of distortion penalties can be solved at scale without sacrificing theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability measures (point clouds with non-negative weights summing to 1) and a pair of CNT cost functions (e.g., geodesic distances on 3D surfaces, p-powered Euclidean norms, or kernel-based distances). Rather than storing the full N×M transport plan, the coupling is encoded implicitly via two dual potential vectors of length N and M, yielding O(N+M) memory. The primary outputs are the linear alignment map Γ (a D×E matrix whose dimensions are set by the embedding dimension, not the point count), the GW or debiased SGW loss value, and differentiable gradients with respect to point positions and weights.

## Method

The core insight is that for any pair of Conditionally of Negative Type (CNT) costs, the GW objective decomposes into a linear alignment Γ between Hilbert-space feature embeddings followed by a standard entropic OT problem with squared Euclidean cost. Concretely, each point is mapped to a GW-embedding (φ(x), ½‖φ(x)‖²) in H×R, and the solver alternates between (i) a Sinkhorn OT step that computes the optimal coupling for the current Γ and (ii) a closed-form update of Γ as the cross-correlation of the embedded points under that coupling. This alternate minimization is provably equivalent to both primal and dual gradient descent, converges with a fixed step size independent of the temperature ε, and reduces per-iteration cost to O(NM) with O(N+M) memory. A multiscale variant coarsens the point clouds via K-Means, solves the coarse problem, and interpolates the dual potentials back to full resolution, achieving 1–2 orders of magnitude speedup and scaling to 177k-point meshes in minutes.

## Results

On shape matching benchmarks ranging from 4k to 177k points, the proposed MSGW solver achieves GWε objectives comparable to all baselines (e.g., 1.3e-2 on HORSES, 1.2e-2 on DOGS, 2.0e-2 on KIDS) while being 1–2 orders of magnitude faster. On FAUST (177k points), MSGW is the only solver that converges in under one hour (355s), whereas QLRGW and CNTGW exceed one hour and EGW/KGW overflow memory. On smaller datasets (HORSES, 4k points), MSGW runs in 2s versus 81s for EGW (40× speedup) and 15s for QLRGW (7.5× speedup). The multiscale strategy is the key differentiator: CNTGW alone already outperforms QLRGW on KIDS (279s vs. 2025s), and adding the coarse-to-fine schedule yields a further 3–4× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against two main competitors: ENTROPIC-GW (EGW, Peyré et al. 2016), the reference exact entropic solver, and QUADRATIC-LOWRANK-GW (QLRGW, Scetbon et al. 2022), the state-of-the-art approximate solver. On GWε objective values, all methods that converge produce essentially identical results (differences ≤ 0.1e-2), confirming that the speed gains come without sacrificing matching quality. The decisive advantage is computational: EGW overflows memory beyond 25k points, while QLRGW scales to 100k but requires 2,444s on VESSELS versus 196s for MSGW (12× faster). On the largest benchmark (FAUST, 177k points), MSGW is the only method that converges within a practical time frame (355s), whereas both QLRGW and CNTGW exceed one hour. The paper also notes that the heuristic LOWRANK-GW variant (rank-constrained transport plan) achieves linear time but introduces visible quantization artifacts in the transport plan (Figure 4g–h), making it unsuitable for shape matching applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the dual-formulation approach to entropic Gromov-Wasserstein (EGW) that was previously established for squared Euclidean costs in finite dimensions (Rioux et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024; Sebbouh et al., 2024) to the full class of Conditionally of Negative Type (CNT) costs, which includes tree distances, hyperbolic geodesics, spherical distances, and p-powered Euclidean norms for 0 < p ≤ 2. The key structural insight—that GW with CNT costs decomposes into a linear alignment in a lifted feature space followed by standard squared-norm OT—unifies these cases under a single Hilbert-Schmidt operator framework. The multiscale acceleration strategy adapts Feydy's (2020) approach for large-scale OT, while the debiasing analysis extends the Sinkhorn divergence framework of Feydy et al. (2019) to the GW setting, resolving an open question about whether SGW effectively corrects entropic bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: the proposed solver scales to 177k-point shapes (FAUST) in under 6 minutes on a laptop GPU, outperforming prior state-of-the-art by 1–2 orders of magnitude, which opens GW-based registration to high-resolution 3D data that was previously intractable. The convergence guarantee with a fixed step size independent of the temperature ε addresses a known instability in prior EGW solvers that could oscillate at low regularization. Open questions include extending the framework to unbalanced and fused-GW settings, characterizing the impact of embedding dimension and annealing schedules, and exploring the connection to Procrustes-Wasserstein and functional maps frameworks. The method remains a local optimizer, so global optimality is not guaranteed, and the quality of Kernel PCA approximations for non-Euclidean costs is not yet fully characterized.
