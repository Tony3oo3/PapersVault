## Problem

Existing genomic workflows for handling unmeasured confounders either provide single point estimates of latent factors (surrogate variable methods like RUVr, causarray) or are restricted to linear models (OVB frameworks like sensmaker), leaving researchers without a quantitative metric for how strong a latent confounder must be to nullify a gene's association with disease. This paper addresses the gap by formulating sensitivity analysis as a high-dimensional, non-linear optimization problem over the full confounding spectrum rather than a fixed correction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a donor-level pseudobulk count matrix (n donors × p genes, log-normalized), a binary case-control vector, measured covariates (e.g., sex, age, APOE4, ethnicity, PMI), and user-specified target predictive-gain knobs (κ, η) ∈ [0,1]². Rather than producing a single confounder estimate, it outputs a family of worst-case latent confounders indexed by (κ, η), gene-level worst-case p-values at each confounding level, and sensitivity contour plots that map how each gene's significance decays as the treatment-side knob κ increases. Genes are ultimately categorized as always significant, nullified, or always insignificant based on where their contour crosses the significance threshold.

## Method

sensGAN is a GAN with two components: shallow fully-connected predictors that emulate logistic and Negative Binomial GLMs (no activation functions, so weights are interpretable as regression coefficients), and a two-layer generator with ReLU and sigmoid output that deterministically maps donor covariates, treatment, and expression to k bounded latent confounders. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the unadjusted model and the most powerful confounder Ẑ are learned to define the normalization reference for deviance-based partial R² (DBPR); second, the generator is adversarially trained against the predictors, minimizing a composite loss with three terms — a plausibility penalty matching achieved predictive gains to target (κ, η), a significance penalty that maximizes the number of genes whose Wald test statistics fall below the threshold, and a regularization term (correlation, cosine similarity, variance matching) that prevents degenerate solutions. The key insight is that DBPR provides a differentiable, bounded [0,1] measure of confounding strength that generalizes Cinelli & Hazlett's partial R² to NB GLMs, enabling gradient-based exploration of the confounding spectrum. By sequentially sweeping over a grid of (κ, η) values, the method produces a continuum of worst-case confounders, each representing the weakest latent variable at that strength that nullifies the most gene associations.

## Results

On the simulation benchmark (n=100, p=100, d=4, k=1), sensGAN correctly identifies 8.14 out of 15 truly nullified genes as nullified under worst-case confounding, while causarray and RUVr split those genes between significant (6.88/6.92) and insignificant (7.12/7.08) with no explicit nullification category. For always-insignificant genes, sensGAN retains 62.90 as insignificant (vs. 37.30 for causarray), and for always-significant genes it retains 12.04 as significant (vs. 13.18 for causarray). In the contour validation experiment, sensGAN's per-gene nullification points differ from the crawler-based reference by a mean of 0.05 in κ* (95% CI: [0.00, 0.22]), and the learned confounder at κ=1 achieves a Pearson correlation of ~0.75 with the true confounder, comparable to causarray and RUVr.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks sensGAN against two surrogate-variable methods: causarray (Du et al., 2026) and RUVr (Risso et al., 2014). On the primary simulation task of identifying which genes are nullified by latent confounding, sensGAN is the only method that explicitly recovers a nullification category, correctly labeling 8.14 of 15 truly nullified genes as such, whereas causarray and RUVr distribute those genes between significant (6.88 and 6.92) and insignificant (7.12 and 7.08) with no nullification signal. On always-insignificant genes, sensGAN achieves 62.90 correct classifications, substantially outperforming causarray (37.30) and matching RUVr (64.58); causarray's poor performance here is attributed to its design for cell-level rather than pseudobulk data. On always-significant genes, causarray slightly edges sensGAN (13.18 vs. 12.04 correct), while RUVr trails both (11.04). In terms of confounder recovery (Pearson correlation with the true Z at κ=1), all three methods achieve comparable values around 0.75, but sensGAN uniquely provides a continuous spectrum of confounders across κ∈[0,1] rather than a single point estimate. In the contour validation experiment, sensGAN's sensitivity curves closely track the crawler-based reference contours with a mean κ* difference of only 0.05, a capability neither causarray nor RUVr offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the omitted-variable-bias (OVB) sensitivity framework of Cinelli & Hazlett (2020) from linear models to high-dimensional Negative Binomial GLMs, replacing the single-outcome partial R² with a deviance-based partial R² across thousands of concurrent gene outcomes. It departs from surrogate-variable methods (RUVr, causarray, SVA) that provide point estimates of latent factors by instead learning a continuum of 'worst-case' confounders under controlled predictive-gain constraints via a GAN-style adversarial training loop. The adversarial architecture is deliberately simple (shallow predictors emulating GLMs, a two-layer generator), positioning the contribution as a new loss formulation and training procedure rather than a novel network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in single-cell genomics: current differential-expression pipelines report binary significance without quantifying how robust that significance is to unmeasured biological confounders, a problem acutely relevant to Alzheimer's research where co-pathology staging criteria evolve every decade. Key limitations include reliance on pseudobulk aggregation (losing cell-level heterogeneity), calibrated rather than absolute effect sizes, and acknowledged GAN training instability that motivates an unexplored bilevel-optimization alternative. Open questions include whether the adversarial formulation scales to multi-tissue or longitudinal settings, and whether the learned latent confounders can be validated against prospective (rather than retrospective) co-pathology measurements.
