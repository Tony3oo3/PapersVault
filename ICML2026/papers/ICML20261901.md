## Problem

Anchor-based spectral clustering reduces the O(n³) cost of eigendecomposition to O(nm²), but existing methods lack explicit control over resulting cluster sizes, frequently producing degenerate or highly unbalanced partitions. Prior balanced-clustering approaches are tied to specific objective functions or lack a unified spectral formulation with a scalable solver for the resulting constrained problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, a target number of clusters c, bilateral anchor-level size bounds (α, β) typically set as (0.4·m/c, 2.0·m/c), and an entropy parameter ε (fixed at 0.01). It produces a soft anchor assignment matrix U ∈ R^{m×c} that exactly satisfies the column-sum bounds, then transmits labels to data points via Y = BU to yield hard cluster assignments. The size constraints are enforced exactly at the anchor level but only approximately propagated to the data level through the bipartite graph, making data-level balance an induced rather than hard property.

## Method

SC-FAGC formulates anchor-level clustering as maximizing Tr(UᵀAU) + εH(U) subject to row-stochasticity and bilateral column-sum constraints α·1 ≤ Uᵀ1 ≤ β·1, where A = BᵀB is the anchor similarity matrix from a parameter-free k-nearest-anchor bipartite graph. Because the trace term is convex in U, the problem is non-convex; an Iteratively Re-weighted (IRW) scheme linearizes it at each iterate, reducing every subproblem to a Double-Bounded Optimal Transport (DB-OT) problem with a Gibbs kernel P = exp(2AU/ε). Each DB-OT subproblem is solved by a generalized Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm with three scaling vectors (u, q, v) that enforce the lower and upper marginal bounds via element-wise max/min clipping. The full pipeline—BKHK anchor generation, closed-form bipartite assignment, IRW outer loop, and Sinkhorn inner loop—achieves O(nm²) complexity with guaranteed monotone convergence to a KKT stationary point, and the entropic regularization ensures a unique Sinkhorn solution with linear convergence rate.

## Results

SC-FAGC achieves the highest clustering accuracy on 9 out of 12 real-world datasets, with standout results of 88.83% ACC on USPS20 (vs. 81.88% for the next-best FAGC-R), 78.33% on MNIST10 (vs. 71.84% for LFCAG), and 73.27% on Waveform21 (vs. 61.75% for EDCAG). Across all six evaluation metrics (ACC, NMI, Purity, Precision, Recall, ARI), SC-FAGC is the top or near-top method on the majority of datasets. Runtime is competitive: SC-FAGC is fastest on 8 of 12 datasets, e.g., 0.26s on MNIST10 vs. 29.02s for BKNC and 22.59s for LFCAG, while matching USPEC's 6.76s on COIL100 (6.78s) with substantially better accuracy (64.18% vs. 55.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC-FAGC outperforms the closest cardinality-aware baseline CQP on 10/12 datasets in ACC and 11/12 in NMI, with the main exception being AR where CQP's strict equal-cardinality partitioning (36.13%) edges out SC-FAGC (33.14%). Against the most recent size-constrained anchor method FAGC-R, SC-FAGC wins on 9/12 datasets in ACC, with the largest gap on USPS20 (88.83% vs. 81.88%) and Waveform21 (73.27% vs. 55.26%). Compared to the nearly-linear unconstrained FSSC, SC-FAGC achieves consistently higher accuracy across all datasets (e.g., 78.33% vs. 48.85% on MNIST10), though FSSC is faster by design. Among anchor-based methods, SC-FAGC dominates LFCAG in both accuracy and runtime (6.78s vs. 472.08s on COIL100; 0.26s vs. 22.59s on MNIST10), and beats USPEC on accuracy while matching its runtime on COIL100 (6.78s vs. 6.76s). On Jaffe, SC-FAGC ties USPEC at 98.12% ACC; on Segment, LSC (67.40%) slightly edges out SC-FAGC (64.81%), the only dataset where a non-size-constrained anchor method wins on ACC.

## Relation to Prior Work

SC-FAGC sits at the intersection of two established lines: anchor-based spectral clustering (LSC, FSC, USPEC, FCAG) which reduces eigendecomposition cost from O(n³) to O(nm²), and Double-Bounded Optimal Transport (Shi et al., 2024) which provides a Sinkhorn-solvable formulation for entropic OT with inequality marginal constraints. The paper departs from prior size-constrained clustering work (CQP, BKNC, FCBC, FAGC-R, EAGC) by formulating bilateral cardinality bounds directly within the anchor-graph objective and solving the resulting non-convex problem via the IRW linearization scheme borrowed from FCAG (Nie et al., 2024c), rather than through CQP or min-cut relaxations on the full graph. The key conceptual contribution is showing that the linearized subproblem is exactly a DB-OT problem, enabling a clean Sinkhorn-Knopp solver with guaranteed convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap: most scalable anchor-based spectral clustering methods produce uncontrolled, potentially degenerate cluster sizes, and prior cardinality-aware methods either operate on the full graph (CQP) or lack a unified spectral formulation. The DB-OT formulation enforces anchor-level bounds exactly while the Sinkhorn solver keeps runtime competitive (fastest on 8/12 datasets), making size-constrained clustering viable at scale. However, data-level size control is only approximate (induced through label transmission, not a hard per-sample guarantee), and final quality remains sensitive to the quality of BKHK anchor generation and bipartite graph construction—jointly optimizing these is acknowledged as an open limitation. The method is also limited to tabular/feature-space data and does not address streaming or online settings.
