## Problem

Existing benchmarks evaluate unified multimodal models (UMMs) by assessing understanding and generation capabilities in isolation, leaving unexplored whether these capabilities are truly integrated or synergistically reinforce each other. There is no systematic framework to measure internal alignment between a model's comprehension and synthesis, nor to quantify the causal impact of one capability on the other in joint tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262906/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Depending on the evaluation dimension, inputs range from image-caption pairs with attribute-aligned VQA questions (internal consistency), to images paired with complex multi-edit instructions requiring localization (understanding-guided generation), to 2D/3D spatial diagrams or complex relational descriptions (generation-guided understanding), and to initial instructions or deliberately misaligned image-caption pairs (mutual enhancement). All image generation and editing is fixed at 512×512 resolution, and mutual enhancement involves iterative multi-turn loops capped at 5 rounds with early termination. Outputs include generated/edited images, VQA answers, bounding boxes or segmentation masks, and per-dimension evaluation scores on a 0–100 scale.

## Method

Unison decomposes unified capability into four synergistic dimensions: internal consistency (comparing a model's VQA answers on an original image versus its own text-to-image generation), understanding-guided generation (model reasons to localize edit regions before executing edits), generation-guided understanding (model synthesizes an intermediate image to aid spatial/relational reasoning), and mutual enhancement (iterative generate-evaluate-refine loops for both editing and understanding). The benchmark comprises 2,169 human-validated samples constructed via a pipeline using Qwen-Image for synthesis, GPT-5.2/Gemini 3 Pro for instruction generation, SAM 3 for segmentation masks, and three weeks of expert review. For scoring, the authors introduce Unison-Judge, an 8B MLLM fine-tuned from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on 8,000 manually labeled samples (2,000 per dimension), which achieves 0.887 average Spearman correlation with human judgments—surpassing GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Pro while being computationally efficient. The evaluation protocol supports both unified (joint) and decoupled (per-capability) scoring tracks, enabling fine-grained attribution of failure modes and quantification of gains from unified modeling.

## Results

On the Unison benchmark, GPT-5.2 achieves the highest overall score of 71.3%, followed closely by Gemini 3 Pro at 69.8%. Among open-source UMMs, BAGEL (14B) leads with an overall score of 53.2%, driven by strong Internal Consistency (80.3%) and Understanding-Guided Generation (67.9%), while OmniGen2 (7B) follows at 51.3% with the best Mutual Enhancement score (47.7%). A notable gap exists between closed-source and open-source models: the best open-source model (BAGEL) trails GPT-5.2 by approximately 18 points overall, with the largest deficits in Generation-Guided Understanding (32.0% vs. 52.7%) and Mutual Enhancement (32.5% vs. 70.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5.2 outperforms Gemini 3 Pro on three of four dimensions (GGU: 52.7% vs. 43.9%; ME: 70.2% vs. 71.4% is a near-tie; UGG: 77.7% vs. 76.9%), while Gemini 3 Pro edges ahead on Internal Consistency (86.9% vs. 84.7%). Among open-source models, BAGEL (14B) dominates in Internal Consistency (80.3%) and Understanding-Guided Generation (67.9%), surpassing OmniGen2 (74.5%, 52.0%) and UniWorld (65.1%, 44.9%), but OmniGen2 leads in Mutual Enhancement (47.7% vs. BAGEL's 32.5%). Several open-source models (Show-o, Janus-Pro, Show-o2, D-DiT, TokenFlow) support only a subset of the four dimensions, limiting their overall scores. The gap between the best open-source (BAGEL, 53.2%) and best closed-source (GPT-5.2, 71.3%) model is ~18 points, with the largest disparity in Mutual Enhancement (32.5% vs. 70.2%), suggesting that iterative self-refinement remains a critical weakness for open-source UMMs. ILLUME+ (3B/7B) and SEED-X (17B) score below 20% overall, indicating that dual-visual-tokenization and multi-granularity approaches struggle with unified tasks despite competitive performance on isolated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating unified multimodal models (UMMs) by assessing understanding and generation in isolation (e.g., MMBench, MMMU for understanding; GenEval, DPG-Bench for generation). It draws conceptual inspiration from UniGen's use of understanding as a self-verifier, Bagel's finding that pre-generation reasoning improves output, and X-Omini's demonstration of mutual reinforcement via shared autoregressive modeling, but synthesizes these into a unified four-dimensional evaluation framework (internal consistency, understanding-guided generation, generation-guided understanding, mutual enhancement). The Unison-Judge evaluator is built by fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, positioning it within the MLLM-based evaluation trend that moves beyond feature-level metrics like FID or CLIPScore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical gap: models achieving state-of-the-art on specialized understanding or generation benchmarks often underperform substantially on tasks requiring joint comprehension and synthesis, with the best open-source model (BAGEL, 53.2) trailing closed-source models (GPT-5.2, 71.3) by a wide margin. The finding that architectural unification (e.g., D-DiT's shared diffusion design) yields better cross-capability alignment than separated architectures (TokenFlow) offers a concrete design principle for future UMMs. Open questions include whether explicit consistency objectives during training can close the gap, whether self-refinement via agentic planning can scale beyond 5 rounds, and how these synergistic evaluations extend to video and longer-horizon tasks.
