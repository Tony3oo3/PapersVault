## Problem

Reconstructing continuum physical fields (fluid flows, temperature, stress) from sparse sensor measurements is fundamentally limited by fixed or offline-optimized sensor layouts that cannot adapt to non-stationary, evolving field dynamics. Existing methods decouple sensing placement from reconstruction, optimizing locations over a global training distribution but remaining static at inference, which fails to capture instance-specific informative regions as the field evolves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260886/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N sparse pointwise observations (sensor coordinates x_t^(i) and corresponding field values u_t(x_t^(i))) at each timestep, where N (64–256) is far smaller than the full grid resolution (e.g., 64×64 or 171×360). Sensor positions are continuous within the spatial domain Ω, with bilinear interpolation from the underlying discrete grid to obtain values at arbitrary coordinates. The system outputs three coupled quantities: the full reconstructed field û_t(x) over all spatial coordinates, continuous sensor displacement vectors a_t = ΔX_t (clipped to ±a_max) for the next timestep, and predicted future latent states that drive proactive planning.

## Method

LASER formulates active sensing as a POMDP over a latent world model comprising a permutation-invariant sparse observation encoder (cross-attention with learnable queries), a GRU-based recurrent history, a conditional diffusion transformer for latent dynamics prediction, and an implicit neural representation decoder. The key architectural insight is conditioning the RL policy on the world model's predicted future latent state ẑ_{t+1} rather than current observations, enabling proactive 'latent imagination' that targets where the field will be informative rather than where it currently is. The policy is a Transformer with multi-scale Fourier-embedded cross-attention between sensor queries and the predicted latent, followed by self-attention for inter-sensor coordination, outputting a Gaussian displacement distribution. Training uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with two novel extensions: dynamic group filtering that excludes consistently low-reward action groups, and H-step predictive reward rollouts that evaluate sensor placements over a future horizon rather than a single step, encouraging placements that remain informative across multiple timesteps.

## Results

On the Navier-Stokes (ν=10⁻³) benchmark with 64 sensors, LASER achieves an average MSE_recon of 0.434×10⁻³, outperforming PhySense (0.466×10⁻³), AROMA (20.27×10⁻³), and DiffusionPDE (6.543×10⁻³). On Shallow-Water with 256 sensors, LASER reaches 0.257×10⁻³ versus PhySense's 0.355×10⁻³ and AROMA's 12.59×10⁻³. On the real-world SST dataset with 100 sensors, LASER achieves MSE_recon of 0.6932, beating PhySense (0.7059) and AROMA (1.0586). The performance gap over static-layout methods widens substantially as the observation budget decreases from 256 to 64 sensors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASER consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and observation budget. Against AROMA (fixed sensor layout), LASER achieves 10–100× lower MSE_recon (e.g., 0.434 vs. 20.27 on NS_ν1e-3 at N=64), demonstrating that adaptive sensing is critical under sparsity. Against DiffusionPDE (generative PDE solver with random/fixed layout), LASER also wins by a wide margin (e.g., 0.302 vs. 1.344 on NS_ν1e-3 at N=256), and DiffusionPDE degrades sharply at lower budgets. The closest competitor is PhySense, which uses offline-optimized static sensor placement; LASER improves over PhySense by ~15% on NS_ν1e-3 (0.302 vs. 0.376 at N=256) and ~28% on Shallow-Water (0.257 vs. 0.355 at N=256), with the gap widening at N=64 on Shallow-Water (0.386 vs. 0.411). The PPO-based ablation (LASER_PPO) is very close to full LASER, confirming that the GRPO optimization with dynamic group filtering and predictive reward rollouts provides a consistent but modest additional gain over standard policy gradient methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASER builds directly on the AROMA/ENMA encoder-decoder architecture for sparse field reconstruction and the Ha & Schmidhuber world-model paradigm, but departs fundamentally from all prior sparse-sensing methods (AROMA, DiffusionPDE, PhySense) by replacing fixed or offline-optimized sensor layouts with an online, instance-adaptive policy. The key conceptual departure is formulating sensor placement as a POMDP solved by an RL agent that conditions on *predicted future* latent states (via a conditional diffusion dynamics module) rather than reacting to current observations, enabling 'proactive' sensing. The GRPO optimization algorithm and the dynamic group filtering mechanism are borrowed from the LLM-reasoning RL literature and repurposed for continuous sensor displacement control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a practical closed-loop framework for adaptive sensing in continuum fields, addressing a gap where prior methods either fix sensors (AROMA, DiffusionPDE) or optimize them only over a global training distribution (PhySense), failing under non-stationary dynamics. The finding that policy-induced sensor trajectories actually *improve* world-model rollout accuracy over random placements (Table 10) suggests the learned policy identifies structurally informative regions, which has implications beyond this specific task. Open questions include scalability to higher-dimensional or multi-physics fields, the computational cost of the diffusion-based dynamics module at inference, and whether the world-model fidelity bottleneck can be alleviated without the iterative co-training scheme.
