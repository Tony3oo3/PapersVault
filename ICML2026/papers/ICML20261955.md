## Problem

Existing Mamba-based 3D human pose estimation methods suffer from two fundamental confusions: (1) internal state update confusion, where forcibly flattening structured skeletal topology into 1D sequences creates spurious adjacency-based connections that violate Mamba's 'adjacency implies correlation' assumption; and (2) depth modeling confusion, where single-view observations lack explicit multi-view constraints, making depth inference inherently ambiguous. No prior Mamba-based approach explicitly addresses both issues simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261955/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D pose sequence of shape T×J×2 (T frames, J joints) as input, which is projected into a feature space and flattened into 1D sequences along four scanning directions (horizontal forward/backward, vertical) for Mamba processing. The output is a 3D pose sequence of shape T×J×3, produced by fusing an intermediate prediction from the n-th block with the final N-th block prediction, enabling a coarse-to-fine hierarchical refinement where the intermediate pose also serves as the rotation source for the VTH module.

## Method

StructMamPose introduces two mechanisms into a stacked Mamba architecture. The Identity Anchoring Mechanism (IAM) constructs joint-level identities from a topological connectivity matrix and frame-level identities via GCN, then injects both into the generation network of Mamba's B and C matrices, anchoring state updates along authentic spatiotemporal dependencies rather than flattened-sequence adjacency. The View Transformation Hub (VTH), embedded between the n-th and (n+1)-th blocks, decodes intermediate features into a 3D pose, rotates it 90° around the Y-axis to create a virtual side-view, re-encodes it, and fuses it with the original frontal features via cross-attention (frontal as query, side-view as key/value), thereby converting implicit depth inference into explicit planar distortions and imposing spatiotemporal multi-view constraints from correlated unambiguous frames. The framework is trained end-to-end with supervised loss on 2D-to-3D pose regression, using the same training protocol as PoseMamba for fair comparison.

## Results

On Human3.6M, StructMamPose achieves SOTA MPJPE across all three model scales: 39.1 mm (P1) for the Small variant, 38.1 mm for Base, and 36.7 mm for Large, outperforming the strongest Mamba baseline PoseMamba by 2.7 mm (Small) and 0.4 mm (Large). On MPI-INF-3DHP, the method achieves 14.1 mm MPJPE and 88.2 AUC, surpassing PoseMamba (14.5 mm) and PoseMagic (14.7 mm). The Large variant also reduces P1† (2D-GT protocol) to 13.4 mm, a 1.4 mm improvement over PoseMamba-X, while requiring 26% fewer parameters and 35% fewer MACs than MotionBERT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructMamPose consistently outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary P1 metric across every model scale on Human3.6M. At the Small scale, it beats PoseMamba-S by 2.7 mm and HDFormer by 3.5 mm; at Base, it surpasses PoseMamba-B by 2.7 mm and STCFormer by 2.9 mm; at Large, it edges out PoseMamba-X by 0.4 mm and MotionBERT by 2.5 mm. On P2 (Procrustes-aligned), StructMamPose-B is marginally behind STCFormer (32.3 vs. 32.0), but StructMamPose-L reverses this to 31.1 vs. 31.8. On MPI-INF-3DHP, the method achieves the best MPJPE (14.1 mm) and AUC (88.2), trailing only TCPFormer by 0.1 in PCK (98.9 vs. 99.0). Under challenging conditions (Table 7), the advantage widens: on the Hard-occlusion subset, StructMamPose-L reduces error from 55.3 mm (PoseMamba-X) to 53.1 mm, a 2.2 mm gain that far exceeds the 0.4 mm overall improvement, confirming that the IAM and VTH mechanisms specifically target the failure modes of standard Mamba baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba-based 3D-HPE line initiated by PoseMamba (Huang et al., 2025) and the broader SSM framework from VMamba (Liu et al., 2024), but explicitly departs from all prior Mamba-based pose estimators (PoseMamba, PoseMagic, SAMA) by arguing they share a fundamental flaw: forced 1D flattening of skeletal topology violates Mamba's 'adjacency-implies-correlation' assumption, causing spurious state updates. It also positions itself against prior depth-ambiguity mitigations (KTPFormer, DSG-Transformer, PandaPose) by contending they operate within a single-view paradigm and lack explicit multi-view constraints. The two proposed mechanisms—IAM for anchoring state updates to true topological identities and VTH for generating virtual side views via coordinate rotation—represent a structural rethinking of how SSMs should process graph-structured spatiotemporal data rather than a simple architectural add-on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why naively applying sequential models to structured skeletal data fails, and offers targeted fixes that are broadly applicable to any SSM-based method processing graph-structured sequences. The VTH's approach to synthesizing multi-view constraints from a single view via internal coordinate rotation is a lightweight alternative to external multi-view data or learned priors, potentially transferable to other monocular 3D reconstruction tasks. However, the framework remains confined to the visual domain and struggles with extreme out-of-distribution poses where 2D detection collapses, leaving open the question of how to couple structural reasoning with raw visual features or cross-modal semantic alignment for truly robust embodied applications.
