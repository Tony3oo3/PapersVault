## Problem

Most emergent communication studies assume either clean channels or undirected random noise, overlooking the adversarial aspect of real-world communication where intermediaries deliberately modify messages to cause misunderstanding. This paper investigates how such directed misinformation from a malign intermediary affects the emergence of compositional languages, and identifies the intermediary's adaptability and targeting scope as critical factors governing this effect.

## Input / Output

The sender observes an object composed of N=5 attributes (each one-hot encoded over |X|=10 values, concatenated into a single vector) and produces a fixed-length message of L=10 discrete symbols drawn from an alphabet of size 10. With probability p=0.5 the receiver gets the message directly; otherwise the malign intermediary intercepts both the message and the object, modifies exactly k symbols (k∈{1,…,4}), and relays the altered message. The receiver must predict all N attribute values from whichever message it receives, while the intermediary is rewarded only for inducing errors on a target subset S of attributes.

## Method

The paper designs a three-agent Lewis signaling game where a GRU sender, GRU receiver, and GRU malign intermediary (all single-layer, hidden size 128) are trained jointly: the receiver minimizes cross-entropy via backpropagation, while the sender and intermediary are optimized with REINFORCE using the receiver's loss as reward signal. The core theoretical contribution proves minimax optimality of compositional languages under directed misinformation (Proposition 7.1) and shows via online-learning regret analysis that misinformation exerts strictly stronger pressure toward compositionality than random noise, with the gap widening as k increases (Proposition 7.2). A key empirical finding is that a non-adaptive intermediary can be exploited by non-compositional protocols (Proposition 8.1), so the authors introduce periodic reinitialization of intermediary parameters (six resets during training) to sustain consistent pressure. They further show that partial misinformation—where the intermediary targets only a subset of attributes—also induces compositionality, provided the target set remains fixed long enough to create prolonged positional pressure, and that positionally biased random noise fails to replicate this effect.

## Results

Under the (5,10) attribute-value configuration with k=3 modified symbols, misinformation with intermediary reset achieves the highest TopSim scores (e.g., 55.2±6.8 for (4,18) and 63.8±4.1 for (6,7)), significantly outperforming random noise (46.6±3.5 and 44.6±3.4) and clean channel (18.4±2.4 and 32.3±6.7). Positional disentanglement (PosDis) shows the largest gap: misinformation with reset reaches 60.3±15.7 and 47.7±8.7 versus 33.4±5.9 and 19.2±5.6 for random noise. Compositional generalization on unseen attribute combinations also improves under misinformation with reset (86.6±11.0 and 96.7±5.5) compared to clean channel (8.9±7.8 and 24.0±21.5). The theoretical analysis (Proposition 7.2) shows the compositionality gap under misinformation exceeds that under random noise by at least 3/(4N|X|^N)·(k/(N(|X|-1)))^N, confirming that directed adversarial corruption exerts strictly stronger pressure toward compositionality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four channel conditions: clean (no modification), undirected random noise (k symbols randomly altered), directed misinformation (malign intermediary modifies k symbols to maximize receiver error), and misinformation with intermediary reset (new intermediary generations introduced at six intervals). Misinformation with reset consistently outperforms random noise on TopSim and PosDis across all tested configurations, with the gap widening as k increases (e.g., PosDis jumps from ~33 to ~60 under (4,18)). Against clean channel, all corruption conditions dramatically improve compositionality, confirming that some channel pressure is necessary. Misinformation without reset underperforms misinformation with reset, validating the theoretical result (Proposition 8.1) that non-adaptive intermediaries can be exploited by non-compositional languages. Partial misinformation (targeting a subset of attributes) also induces compositionality comparable to full-attribute misinformation, and in some cases (e.g., one-attribute targeting with reset) achieves higher PosDis than random noise. Biased random noise (static or shifting positional bias) fails to match the compositionality gains of directed partial misinformation, indicating the effect is due to attribute-specific adversarial pressure rather than positional structure alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Kuciński et al. (2021), who proved minimax optimality of compositional languages under undirected random noise, to the setting of directed adversarial corruption by a malign intermediary. It also adopts the online-learning regret analysis of Ananthakrishnan et al. (2025) to formalize how misinformation exerts stronger compositional pressure than random noise. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant assumption in emergent communication that channels are either clean or subject to symmetric random noise, introducing instead an adversarial agent with a modification budget and a specific objective, and connects the iterated-learning literature (Smith et al., 2003) by reinitializing intermediary parameters as a proxy for generational turnover.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides both a theoretical account (minimax optimality, regret-based pressure comparison) and empirical evidence that directed misinformation is a distinct and stronger environmental pressure toward compositionality than random noise, with the intermediary's adaptability identified as a critical moderating factor. The partial-misinformation results show that the pressure need not be comprehensive, and that positional-bias noise does not replicate the effect, isolating the role of attribute-targeted adversarial intent. Limitations include the reliance on one-to-one language and dimension-alignment assumptions in the theory, synthetic attribute-value tasks, and GRU-based agents that may not capture human cognitive dynamics; open questions include whether these effects persist under hierarchical or nested compositional structures and in more realistic multi-turn communication settings.
