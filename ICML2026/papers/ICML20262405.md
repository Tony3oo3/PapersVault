## Problem

LLMs hallucinate in long-form generation, and early errors compound into subsequent outputs through a phenomenon called hallucination snowballing. While inference-time compute scaling has shown promise for reasoning tasks, its effect on factuality in open-ended generation remains largely unexplored, and existing mitigation methods either require external knowledge sources or treat the entire generation as a monolithic unit rather than intervening at the point where errors first appear.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form user query (e.g., 'Tell me about Alan Turing') and produces a verified long-form text response composed only of factually supported sentences. Generation proceeds sentence-by-sentence, with each new sentence conditioned on the accumulated verified text (and optionally a temporary pool of rejected hallucinated sentences for sampling guidance). The output can also be a partial response or an abstention if the model cannot produce verified content after N consecutive hallucinated sentences, making the response length variable and query-dependent.

## Method

SHARS partitions generation into individual sentences and applies hallucination rejection sampling sequentially: each sentence is checked by a detector, then either discarded (fully hallucinated), rewritten to remove hallucinated claims (mixed), or retained (fully factual), with subsequent generation built only on verified content to break error compounding. The detector, HalluSE, extends semantic entropy to long-form text by decomposing each sentence into entity-claim pairs, generating unambiguous probe questions targeting the correct entity, sampling multiple answers conditioned on preceding context, and computing semantic entropy per fact while explicitly accounting for multiple valid answers. For sentence sampling after rejection, the 'Following' strategy temporarily retains hallucinated sentences in the generation context to leverage the model's content-planning ability (avoiding redundant topics), while clearing this pool whenever new factual content is verified. The framework is detector-agnostic and requires no external retrieval, training, or knowledge bases, operating purely through the model's parametric knowledge and internal confidence signals.

## Results

On the FactScore benchmark, SHARS (Ours-Prec) improves factual precision by approximately 20–26% over Greedy decoding across all three evaluated models: 78.4% for Qwen3-32B (vs. 52.4% Greedy), 78.4% for Llama3.1-8B (vs. 53.7%), and 74.0% for Qwen3-4B (vs. 50.0%). Even under a matched response-rate setting (Ours-Resp), the method still achieves 65.7% FPrec. for Qwen3-32B and 64.1% for Llama3.1-8B, well above all baselines. On the FactualBio hallucination detection benchmark, HalluSE achieves 72.9% AUROC, outperforming Naive SE (66.2%), P(True) (69.8%), and Self-Check (57.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHARS (Ours-Prec) decisively outperforms all inference-time baselines on factual precision across every model: on Qwen3-32B it reaches 78.4% FPrec. versus Self-Endorse (63.2%), ChatProtect (54.4%), DoLa (53.1%), and Greedy (52.4%); on Llama3.1-8B it reaches 78.4% versus Self-Endorse (60.6%), ChatProtect (56.7%), DoLa (53.8%), and ID (53.5%). Even the more conservative Ours-Resp setting (matched response rate) beats the strongest baseline Self-Endorse by 2.5–13.5 points in FPrec. depending on the model. On the FactualBio detection benchmark, HalluSE achieves 72.9% AUROC, surpassing P(True) (69.8%), Naive SE (66.2%), and Self-Check (57.6%). When combined with the training-time method FactAlign, SHARS boosts FPrec. from 53.1% to 80.6% (no length constraint) and from 55.4% to 79.1% (200-word constraint), demonstrating strong complementarity. On LongFact, where the Greedy baseline already achieves 93.0% FPrec., SHARS yields a smaller but consistent gain to 94.6%, comparable to the improvement reported when upgrading from GPT-3.5-Turbo to GPT-4-Turbo.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHARS builds directly on the semantic entropy framework of Farquhar et al. (2024) for hallucination detection, extending it to long-form generation via HalluSE (fixing entity disambiguation and multi-answer ambiguity in probe questions). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of inference-time compute scaling (best-of-N, tree search, self-consistency) and hallucination mitigation, but departs from prior inference-time methods (DoLa, Integrative Decoding, Self-Endorse) by performing rejection sampling segment-wise and sequentially during generation rather than post-hoc over complete outputs. The framework is explicitly detector-agnostic and complementary to training-time alignment (FactAlign, DPO) and retrieval-augmented generation, positioning it as a general-purpose inference-time layer rather than a standalone solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that inference-time compute can be allocated specifically to factuality in open-ended generation, a direction the authors note is largely unexplored, and shows a smooth, controllable precision-coverage trade-off via the semantic entropy threshold. The natural emergence of an abstention mechanism—where the model refuses to answer after repeated hallucination detection—offers a practical safety signal for high-stakes deployments without requiring a separate classifier. Key limitations remain: the method cannot generate knowledge the model lacks (no retrieval), and the gains depend on the model having at least partial correct knowledge to resample toward. Open questions include integration with RAG pipelines, applicability of alternative detectors (probes, self-contradiction checks), and whether the scaling property holds for much larger models or more diverse domains.
