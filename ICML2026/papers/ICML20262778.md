## Problem

Differentiating through quadratic programs via the KKT system requires solving a large indefinite linear system of size (n+p+|A|) in the backward pass, whose cost scales cubically and whose numerical robustness degrades under degeneracy or active-set changes. Existing solver-agnostic methods (dQP, BPQP) still inherit this KKT bottleneck, limiting their scalability to large-scale QPs with thousands or millions of variables.

## Input / Output

The forward pass consumes QP parameters (P, q, A, b, C, d) parameterized by θ and calls a black-box solver (e.g., Gurobi) to obtain the primal solution z* and dual multipliers (ν*, μ*), which are used only to set penalty magnitudes ρ = ζ‖ν*‖∞ and α = ζ‖μ*‖∞. The backward pass takes an upstream gradient r = ∂L/∂z* ∈ ℝⁿ and returns the vector-Jacobian product ∂θL by solving a single n×n SPD system, avoiding explicit construction of the full n×s Jacobian. The smoothing parameter δ (default 10⁻⁶) and penalty strength ζ (default 10) control the accuracy–stability tradeoff.

## Method

dXPP decouples QP solving from differentiation by reformulating the constrained QP as a softplus-smoothed exact penalty problem Φ_δ(z;θ) = f(z) + αΣ p_δ((Cz−d)_i) + ρΣ [p_δ((Az−b)_j) + p_δ(−(Az−b)_j)], where p_δ(t) = δ log(1+exp(t/δ)). The backward pass implicitly differentiates the stationarity condition ∇_z Φ_δ = 0, yielding a plug-in sensitivity Z_δ^plug obtained by solving (P + (1/δ)B^T W B + E_δ) Z_δ^plug = −(G + (∂_θ B^T)y* + (1/δ)B^T W g_θ + F_δ), where B stacks active equality and inequality constraint Jacobians and W encodes the penalty curvatures. The resulting Hessian H = P + (1/δ)B^T W B + E_δ is symmetric positive definite (unlike the indefinite KKT matrix), enabling efficient sparse Cholesky or conjugate-gradient solves in the primal dimension n only. Theorem 3.5 proves that Z_δ^plug converges to the exact KKT sensitivity Z_KKT as δ→0, with an error bound of O(δ) + O(e^{−γ/δ}/δ). In practice, the exponentially small inactive-constraint terms E_δ and F_δ can be pruned without affecting accuracy, further reducing the system size.

## Results

On gradient accuracy, dXPP achieves a relative discrepancy of 1.91×10⁻⁷ to 5.14×10⁻⁴ versus dQP across QP sizes from 10×5 to 5000×2000, confirming numerical reliability. On large-scale sparse projections, dXPP's backward pass reaches 226 ms at 10⁶ variables (simplex) and 333 ms (chains), yielding 4.2× and 9.2× speedups over dQP respectively, while OptNet, SCQPTH, and CVXPYLayers fail beyond 10⁴–10³ variables. On the end-to-end multi-period portfolio task (H=200), dXPP's backward pass takes 114 ms versus 39,106 ms for dQP (~343× speedup) and 606 ms for OptNet (~5.3× speedup), with total per-epoch time of 978 ms versus 40,066 ms for dQP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dQP (Magoon et al., 2025), dXPP consistently wins on backward-pass runtime across all tested scales: 4.2× faster on simplex projection at 10⁶ variables, 9.2× faster on chain projection at 10⁶, and ~343× faster on the portfolio task at H=200, while matching dQP's gradient accuracy to within 5×10⁻⁴ relative error. Against OptNet (Amos & Kolter, 2017), dXPP is 5.3× faster in the backward pass at H=200 and scales to 10⁶ variables where OptNet fails beyond 10⁴. Against SCQPTH (Butler, 2023), dXPP is 10× faster in the backward pass at H=200 (114 ms vs. 3369 ms) and handles problem sizes where SCQPTH hits memory limits. Against CVXPYLayers (Agrawal et al., 2019), dXPP is the only method that scales beyond small problem sizes, as CVXPYLayers' SOCP reformulation incurs prohibitive overhead. On the Sudoku task, dXPP matches dQP and OptNet in training dynamics (loss and error rate) while being 1.2× faster than dQP and 3× faster than OptNet at the largest board size (n=4).

## Relation to Prior Work

dXPP sits at the intersection of two classical threads: differentiable QP layers (OptNet, dQP, BPQP, SCQPTH) that differentiate through the KKT saddle-point system, and exact-penalty / softplus-smoothing theory (Li et al., 2023; Evans et al., 1973). The key departure is conceptual: rather than implicitly differentiating the KKT conditions—which yields a large indefinite system of size n+p+|A|—dXPP reformulates the QP as a smoothed unconstrained penalty problem and differentiates through its stationarity condition, reducing the backward pass to an n×n SPD system in primal variables only. The solver-agnostic forward pass design is inherited from dQP (Magoon et al., 2025), while the softplus penalty construction follows Li et al. (2023); the novelty lies in proving that the resulting plug-in sensitivity converges to the exact KKT gradient as the smoothing parameter vanishes (Theorem 3.5).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for decision-focused learning pipelines that embed QPs at scale: dXPP achieves up to 343× backward-pass speedups over dQP in multi-period portfolio optimization and handles degenerate active sets (where KKT systems become ill-conditioned or singular) without ad-hoc damping. A remaining limitation is that the method requires the QP solver to return dual multipliers to set penalty magnitudes, and the theoretical guarantee assumes LICQ and strict complementarity for the convergence proof—though the SPD structure of the backward system provides a practical fallback when these fail. The authors note the framework extends naturally to more general convex programs, which is a promising but unexplored direction.
