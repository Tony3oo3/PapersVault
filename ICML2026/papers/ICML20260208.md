## Problem

GRPO-based RLVR for LLM reasoning suffers from two coupled inefficiencies: static uniform rollout allocation wastes compute on low-variance prompts while leaving zero-reward (hard) prompts without any gradient signal, and sparse binary rewards collapse distinctions among diverse correct trajectories, suppressing exploitation of informative reasoning paths. Existing dynamic sampling methods (DAPO, TreePO) either discard zero-accuracy prompts entirely or incur heavy overhead from tree-structured search, without a principled mechanism to adaptively concentrate exploration where it reduces uncertainty most.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math or code problems), an LLM policy, and a rule-based reward evaluator (binary correct/incorrect). It produces updated policy weights, a set of rollout trajectories per prompt, novelty-sharpened advantage estimates, and an evolving ICL corpus of verified successful solutions. Notably, for zero-reward prompts the input prompt is augmented at training time with up to 2 retrieved exemplars (via Qwen3-Embedding-8B similarity search) from the model's own prior successes, while at evaluation time all methods use standard zero-shot prompting with no retrieval, eliminating data leakage.

## Method

XRPO integrates three mechanisms into a single training loop. First, Hierarchical Rollout Planning (HRP) allocates a fixed total rollout budget across prompts in phased rounds: after base rollouts establish per-prompt reward statistics, subsequent rounds assign extra rollouts proportionally to a priority score combining expected Student's t-confidence-interval reduction (uncertainty exploitation) and a UCB-style exploration bonus for undersampled prompts. Second, for prompts where all rollouts fail, ICL Seeding retrieves up to 2 semantically similar verified solutions from an evolving corpus (populated exclusively with the model's own on-policy successes) and injects them as in-context exemplars, breaking the zero-reward symmetry that causes vanishing gradients. Third, Novelty-Guided Advantage Sharpening computes a sequence-level novelty score η_i = exp(s(y_i) − s̄) from length-normalized log-likelihoods, then adds a bounded bonus λ_nov·(1−η_i) to the GRPO advantage of correct-but-atypical rollouts (η_i < 1), amplifying low-probability yet successful trajectories to counteract reward homogenization and encourage exploration of diverse reasoning paths.

## Results

On Qwen3-1.7B (reasoning mode), XRPO achieves 35.13% average pass@1 and 45.56% average cons@32 across AIME'25, HMMT'25, and BRUMO'25, outperforming GSPO (33.23% / 41.11%) by +1.9% and +4.5% respectively. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, XRPO delivers +10.7% relative pass@1 and +23.6% relative cons@32 over GRPO. Training convergence is accelerated by 2.4× (GSM8K) to 2.7× (MATH-500) with only 4.7% per-step overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XRPO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model and benchmark tested. Against GSPO (the strongest baseline on Qwen3-1.7B), XRPO gains +1.9% pass@1 and +4.5% cons@32 on average across AIME'25, HMMT'25, and BRUMO'25, and also leads on MATH (90.54 vs 90.33) and Codeforces (13.80 vs 13.72). Against DAPO, XRPO improves pass@1 by +4.2% (35.13 vs 30.94) and cons@32 by +12.2% (45.56 vs 33.33) on Qwen3-1.7B math benchmarks. Against TreePO Sampling, the gap is even larger: +8.2% pass@1 and +12.2% cons@32. On Llama-3.2-3B, XRPO beats GRPO by +0.31% pass@1 and +1.54% pass@4 on average, and on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct it achieves +10.7% relative pass@1 and +23.6% relative cons@32 over GRPO. Notably, XRPO is also complementary to GSPO's sequence-level optimization, yielding an additional +2.5% accuracy when combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

XRPO extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by recasting its static rollout allocation and sparse reward assignment through an exploration-exploitation lens, drawing on the classical entropy bonus (Williams, 1992; Mnih et al., 2016) but lifting it from token-level to sequence-level novelty scoring. It departs from concurrent dynamic-sampling methods (DAPO, GRESO) that operate at the prompt-selection level by instead distributing rollout budgets within a fixed prompt batch using a Student's t-confidence-interval-based priority score, and from tree-search approaches (TreePO, TreeRL) by avoiding the overhead of generating many additional rollouts. The ICL seeding mechanism is positioned as the first demonstration of in-context learning's effectiveness within an RLVR training loop, distinguishing itself from replay-based or multi-turn self-refinement methods by transferring verified reasoning patterns across semantically related but distinct problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.4–2.7× convergence speedup with only ~4.7% per-step overhead is practically significant for teams running RLVR at scale, as it directly reduces GPU-hours to reach a target accuracy. The ICL seeding concept—using an evolving corpus of the model's own verified successes to break zero-reward symmetry—opens a direction for combining in-context adaptation with policy optimization that could generalize beyond math and code. However, the accuracy gains over strong baselines like GSPO remain modest (1.4–4% pass@1), the method has not been validated on very large models (300B+), and the ICL corpus is limited to the model's own rollouts, leaving open whether teacher-generated exemplars or cross-model transfer could further extend the exploration frontier.
