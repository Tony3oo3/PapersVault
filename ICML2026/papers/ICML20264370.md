## Problem

Existing RL approaches for ride-hailing delayed matching rely on shallow encoders and static cost matrices that cannot capture non-stationary spatio-temporal regime shifts (cyclic congestion, peak/off-peak demand), and their fixed reward weightings invite reward hacking over long training horizons. This paper addresses both gaps by formalizing adaptive delayed matching as a Regime-Aware Spatio-Temporal MDP with a physics-informed travel-time surrogate and an anti-hacking adaptive reward, then equipping the RL agent with a compact Mixture-of-Experts encoder that lets different experts specialize across operational regimes.

## Input / Output

At each decision epoch the agent observes a zone-level state vector aggregating unmatched passenger counts, idle driver counts, stochastic passenger/driver arrival rates, time-of-day, and remaining horizon across an H×W city grid (N=HW zones). Each zone's features are embedded via small MLPs, fused, and augmented with sinusoidal positional and learnable location embeddings before entering a self-attention encoder. The output is a binary vector a_t ∈ {0,1}^N where each coordinate is an independent Bernoulli 'match-now' or 'hold' decision for one zone, factorized as a product of Bernoulli heads over a shared MoE-encoded global state, so that each zone's action implicitly conditions on all other zones' supply-demand conditions.

## Method

RAST-MoE-RL combines three components: (1) a RAST-MDP environment whose travel-time feedback is a precomputed 24×|R| lookup table built from MFD-consistent zone-hour speeds (v_z = q_z/k_z) and static Dijkstra routes, giving O(1) rollout queries; (2) an anti-hacking reward that penalizes incremental matching and pickup waits with a pickup weight modulated by the surrogate and an adaptive Lagrangian multiplier λ_t updated online to enforce a service-quality violation tolerance (≤5% late requests); (3) a 12M-parameter RAST-MoE encoder in which zone tokens pass through a lightweight self-attention block, are globally pooled, and the pooled vector is routed by a two-layer MLP gate to a sparse top-K subset of E experts (best: 16 experts, top-4), whose outputs are softmax-weighted and shared by PPO actor and critic heads. A lightweight load-balancing bias (rather than a uniform auxiliary loss) prevents routing collapse while tolerating the naturally skewed expert utilization inherent to imbalanced supply-demand regimes. The framework is trained with PPO (clipped surrogate + GAE + entropy regularization) and remains compatible with A2C, ACER, GRPO, and DQN, with the MoE encoder as a drop-in replacement for the standard MLP feature extractor.

## Results

On the San Francisco 2019 CPUC TNC dataset, the best RAST-MoE-RL configuration (PPO, 16 experts, top-4 routing, 12M params) achieves 182 s average matching wait and 523 s average pickup wait, improving total reward by 13% over the strongest baseline. This represents a 10% reduction in matching delay and a 15% reduction in pickup delay relative to the parameter-matched ACER + Plain MLP baseline. Zero-shot transfer from San Francisco to Alameda County and San Diego stays within 1.5–3% of natively trained performance, and the policy degrades only 1.1–1.5% under global OD perturbations (±20% travel-time scaling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the formal baseline of Qin et al. (2021) (implemented as ACER + Plain MLP, 12M params), RAST-MoE-RL (PPO, 16 experts, top-4) reduces matching wait from 201 s to 182 s (−9.5%) and pickup wait from 596 s to 523 s (−12.3%) on the San Francisco test set. The advantage is even larger in cross-city zero-shot transfer: on Alameda County the SF-trained RAST-MoE policy (196 s / 595 s) beats the natively trained ACER + Plain MLP baseline (209 s / 641 s) by 13 s in matching and 46 s in pickup wait; on San Diego the gap is 10 s and 66 s respectively. Compared to the strongest non-learning heuristic (Zone-Level Congestion-Conditioned, 203 s / 784 s), RAST-MoE-RL cuts pickup wait by roughly 33%. Among learned baselines, a 48M-parameter Dense Transformer (192 s / 545 s) and a 12M GNN (208 s / 570 s) both trail the 12M RAST-MoE model, confirming that the gains come from regime-aware sparse expert routing rather than raw parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: PPO-based RL for ride-hailing dispatch (Qin et al. 2021; Ke et al. 2022; Bao et al. 2025), sparse-gated Mixture-of-Experts architectures from the LLM literature (Shazeer et al. 2017; Obando Ceron et al. 2024), and macroscopic fundamental diagram (MFD) surrogates for urban traffic (Geroliminis & Daganzo 2008). The key departure from prior ride-hailing RL is replacing static cost matrices with a physics-informed MFD travel-time surrogate and swapping shallow MLP encoders for a regime-aware spatio-temporal MoE, motivated by the observation that demand and congestion form clustered, non-stationary regimes that a monolithic encoder must interpolate across. The anti-hacking adaptive Lagrangian reward also departs from the fixed-weight schemes (e.g., the 4:1 ratio in Qin et al. 2021) used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact 12M-parameter MoE encoder can outperform 48M dense Transformers in a city-scale RL control task, and that the learned policy generalizes zero-shot to unseen cities within 1.5–3% of native performance—practically important for operators who cannot retrain per city. The expert-specialization analysis (masking both high- and low-frequency experts degrades performance) provides evidence that MoE routing discovers meaningful regime sub-policies rather than merely adding capacity. Open questions include whether the MFD zone-hour surrogate remains adequate under extreme or novel congestion patterns (e.g., multi-day events), whether the centralized formulation scales beyond a few hundred zones, and whether the approach transfers to other spatio-temporal resource-allocation domains beyond ride-hailing.
