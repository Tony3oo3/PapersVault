## Problem

Most empirical evaluations of reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) for LLM agents are conducted in-domain, where training and testing occur in the same environment or on closely overlapping tasks. In real-world deployment, agents must operate in unseen environments with different background knowledge, observation spaces, and action interfaces, yet it remains unclear whether RFT-induced improvements generalize beyond the training distribution. This paper systematically characterizes the generalization profile of RFT under such distributional shifts.

## Input / Output

The agent follows the ReAct paradigm, receiving a task instruction and maintaining a growing interaction history (all previous actions and observations) as its state. At each step it produces a reasoning trace followed by an environment-specific action (e.g., search queries, navigation commands, or crafting instructions) and receives a textual observation in return, continuing until task completion or a per-environment turn limit (5–30 turns, 8192-token response cap). Evaluation reports avg@8 with exact matching, and the study additionally tracks average interaction turns and generated tokens as efficiency metrics.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical study along three axes using GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct across five diverse agent environments (WebShop, SearchQA, TextCraft, AlfWorld, BabyAI) that differ in reward density, action validation strictness, and required world knowledge. First, it examines within-environment generalization by training on easy or hard task subsets and evaluating across difficulty levels, finding that an easy-to-hard curriculum yields the best performance and that harder tasks provide richer failure signals. Second, it measures cross-environment zero-shot transfer by training in one environment and evaluating in all others, revealing that generalization is sensitive to shifts in semantic priors, observation spaces, and action interfaces, with environments providing valid action lists (e.g., BabyAI) inducing over-dependency that harms transfer. Third, it analyzes sequential multi-environment training (20 two-stage and multiple five-stage configurations) to quantify transfer and forgetting, comparing against joint mixture training, and finds that sequential RFT exhibits strong anti-forgetting, that training order matters (easy-to-hard environment sequences generalize better), and that final performance is largely insensitive to ordering after five stages.

## Results

Across five agent environments, RFT (GRPO) yields large held-in gains (e.g., AlfWorld improves from 26.56 to 92.00 for Qwen2.5-7B, a +65.44 point increase) but only modest held-out transfer (average +3.44 points for 7B, +3.32 for 3B). Training on BabyAI causes negative cross-environment transfer, dropping WebShop performance from 28.59 to 10.25 for the 7B model. Within-environment, easy-to-hard curriculum learning further boosts performance (e.g., BabyAI 3B: 89.3 vs. 87.3 for single-difficulty training), and RFT reduces average interaction turns (e.g., BabyAI 3B: 10.76 → 4.19 turns).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5 base model (pre-RFT), GRPO delivers substantial held-in improvements (up to +78.62 points on AlfWorld for 3B) but only modest held-out gains (average +3.3 to +3.4 points), with BabyAI-trained agents showing negative transfer (−3.23 avg for 7B). Against REINFORCE++ (Hu, 2025), GRPO consistently outperforms in both held-in and cross-environment settings: e.g., on TextCraft held-in, GRPO reaches 80.88 vs. REINFORCE++'s 48.25; on AlfWorld held-in, 92.00 vs. 45.44; and on BabyAI held-in, 88.79 vs. 73.68. GRPO also shows stronger cross-environment transfer (e.g., BabyAI→TextCraft: 39.25 vs. 36.25; AlfWorld→TextCraft: 36.13 vs. 34.88). The paper additionally shows GRPO outperforms SFT on held-out transfer (e.g., WebShop-trained: GRPO ΔHeld-Out +4.75 vs. SFT −22.78), confirming RFT's advantage over supervised post-training for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training generalization literature (which has primarily studied single-turn tasks and compared SFT vs. RL) to the multi-turn LLM agent setting, where agents interact with environments through distinct observation and action spaces. It builds on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 and the ReAct interaction paradigm, using the AgentGym-RL framework as training infrastructure. The key departure from prior RL-for-agents work is the systematic out-of-domain evaluation: whereas most existing agent RL papers evaluate in-domain (same environment, often overlapping tasks), this paper explicitly measures cross-environment transfer, sequential training dynamics, and task-difficulty generalization across five heterogeneous environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This study provides the first systematic characterization of when and why RFT-trained agents generalize or fail to generalize across environments, offering practical guidance for real-world deployment where agents face unseen interfaces and knowledge domains. The identification of specific failure mechanisms (e.g., BabyAI's provided action list inducing dependency that degrades long-horizon reasoning elsewhere) and the finding that sequential training approximates joint training with minimal forgetting are actionable insights for agent training pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only the Qwen2.5 family, a limited set of environment orderings, and default hyperparameters; open questions include how to mitigate the negative transfer from environments with dense action scaffolding and whether safety-relevant behaviors persist through sequential RFT.
