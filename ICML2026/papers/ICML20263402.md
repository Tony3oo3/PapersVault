## Problem

Test-Time Scaling (TTS) methods improve LLM reasoning by generating and selecting among multiple candidate responses, but their robustness properties and specific failure modes have been largely unexplored. This paper identifies a previously unrecognized vulnerability: TTS's safety guarantees depend critically on the diversity of its candidate pool, and even modest diversity reduction can steer TTS toward producing unsafe outputs — a failure mode that standard jailbreak attacks do not target.

## Input / Output

REFDIV takes an original unsafe prompt query, a target LLM (open- or closed-source), and a TTS strategy (Best-of-N or MCTS) as inputs, and produces a single adversarially perturbed prompt that, when processed through the TTS pipeline, causes the selected output to violate safety constraints. The method is entirely training-free and operates at inference time, requiring only white-box access to the model and TTS strategy during optimization (though generated prompts transfer to black-box closed-source models). The genetic algorithm maintains a population of candidate prompts (size m=32) evolved over T=25 iterations using multi-point crossover at sentence/paragraph boundaries and hierarchical word-level mutation.

## Method

REFDIV is a reference-guided diversity stress test protocol built on a population-based genetic algorithm that explicitly targets the diversity of intermediate candidate generations in TTS pipelines. The core fitness function combines two Shannon entropy terms: an intrinsic diversity score H(C_x) measuring token-level entropy across the candidate set, and a reference-guided score H(C_x ∪ C*) that steers candidates toward a fixed set of affirmative tokens (e.g., 'Sure, I can help you with that...'). A dynamic weighting factor α(t) = exp((ln 2)/(T-1)·(t-1)) − 1 smoothly interpolates between reference-guided steering (early iterations, α≈0) and unconstrained diversity minimization (late iterations, α→1), creating a curriculum-like exploration-to-exploitation progression. This two-phase design first guides the population toward promising adversarial regions and then allows free convergence to low-entropy prompts that induce mode collapse in the TTS candidate distribution, effectively undermining the diversity-based robustness that TTS relies on for safe selection.

## Results

REFDIV achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across nearly all model–TTS combinations in Table 1. Under Best-of-N (N=8, PairRM), REFDIV reaches 0.926 ASR on Gemma3-27B (vs. 0.749 for AutoDAN) and 0.465 on Llama3.1-8B (vs. 0.368 for AutoDAN), while under MCTS it attains 0.989 on Gemma3-27B and 0.967 on Llama3.1-8B. The method is training-free and operates solely at inference time, with a per-iteration runtime of 0.18 s—comparable to GCG (0.21 s) and AutoDAN (0.16 s) and roughly 28× faster than AutoDAN-Turbo (5.07 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFDIV is compared against three state-of-the-art jailbreak baselines: GCG (gradient-based suffix optimization), AutoDAN (genetic-algorithm prompt generation), and AutoDAN-Turbo (lifelong-learning agent). Against AutoDAN, REFDIV wins on 7 of 8 model–TTS cells, with the largest margins on Llama3.1-8B (+9.7 pp under Best-of-N, +13.6 pp under MCTS) and Gemma3-27B (+17.7 pp under Best-of-N, +8.5 pp under MCTS); the only near-tie is Qwen3-8B under Best-of-N where AutoDAN edges ahead by 0.001. Against GCG, REFDIV dominates across all settings, with GCG achieving ASR as low as 0.054 (Gemma3-27B, Best-of-N) versus REFDIV's 0.926. AutoDAN-Turbo, despite its pre-trained skill library, is the weakest baseline on TTS (e.g., 0.156 ASR on Gemma3-27B under MCTS vs. REFDIV's 0.989), likely because its default distribution misaligns with TTS's multi-candidate selection stage. Overall, REFDIV's diversity-targeting objective yields consistently higher ASR than methods that optimize only for harmful output content without constraining candidate diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AutoDAN genetic-algorithm framework for adversarial prompt generation, but repurposes the fitness function to target a fundamentally different objective: reducing Shannon entropy of the TTS candidate pool rather than directly optimizing for harmful output. It departs from the standard jailbreak lineage (GCG, AutoDAN, AutoDAN-Turbo) that treats the LLM as a black-box input-output mapping, instead targeting the intermediate candidate-generation mechanism that TTS strategies (Best-of-N, MCTS) rely on for robustness. The conceptual bridge to curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) via the dynamic weighting schedule α(t) is a methodological novelty, but the core positioning is as a diagnostic stress test for TTS diversity-dependence rather than a new attack paradigm per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a mere 2–5% entropy reduction in the candidate pool can flip TTS from safe to unsafe, and that this effect outperforms direct adversarial-intent attacks, challenges the implicit assumption that TTS inherently improves safety. The demonstrated failure of state-of-the-art guardrails (Llama-Guard-3/4, OpenAI Moderation) to flag REFDIV-generated prompts, combined with cross-strategy and cross-model transferability, suggests that current TTS deployments lack a critical safety layer. Open questions include whether diversity-aware TTS designs can preserve reasoning benefits while resisting mode-collapse attacks, and whether the entropy-ASR correlation (r ≈ −0.6 to −0.84) can be formalized into a certified safety bound.
