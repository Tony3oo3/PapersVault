## Problem

Estimating density ratios between intractable distributions naively requires independently integrating the log-likelihood under each distribution via separate ODE simulations, incurring doubled computational cost and non-linear compounding of discretization errors. This paper asks whether the ratio itself can be tracked along a single generative trajectory, eliminating the redundant integral evaluations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260101/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional data point (e.g., a single-cell gene expression vector), two conditioning variables y and y' (e.g., treatment vs. control labels), and a pre-trained conditional CNF model. It outputs a scalar log-likelihood ratio log p₁(x₁|y)/p₁(x₁|y'), which in genomics applications is interpreted as a differential abundance score, a batch-effect magnitude, or a treatment-interaction effect. The model operates on tabular data with no spatial or sequential structure, and the ratio is evaluated per observation at inference time via a single backward ODE integration from data to noise.

## Method

The core contribution is Proposition 4.1, which derives a closed-form ODE governing the time-derivative of the log-density ratio along an arbitrary trajectory x_t = x₁ + ∫₁ᵗ b_s(x_s)ds: d/dt log r_t = ∇·(u'_t − u_t) + (b_t − u_t)ᵀ∇log p_t + (u'_t − b_t)ᵀ∇log p'_t, with initial condition log r₀ = 0. The trajectory is simulated under a single velocity field b_t (either the numerator conditional field or an unconditional field), while the two score terms are recovered from the learned vector fields via the Gaussian-path reparameterization of Zheng et al. (2023) or from a separately trained score network to avoid numerical instability near t=1. Training follows standard flow matching with Gaussian probability paths (three scheduler variants explored), and the model is trained simultaneously conditionally and unconditionally using classifier-free guidance (empty-token dropout). This reduces the inference cost from two independent ODE solves to one, and the single-integral formulation avoids the compounding discretization error that accumulates when two separate likelihood estimates are divided.

## Results

On the mutual information estimation benchmark (Table 1), scRatio with λ=0.25 achieves MAE of 0.03–0.11 across dimensions d=20–160, outperforming CTSM (0.06–0.87) and TSM (0.03–0.89) at most dimensions, with the largest gap at d=320 where scRatio (λ=0.25) reaches 1.16 vs. CTSM's 2.15 and TSM's 3.55. On the differential abundance task (Table 2), scRatio (σ_min=0.1, λ=0) achieves near-perfect Spearman correlation with ground-truth DA level (ρ=1.00 for NAR, 0.99 for AUC) and the highest NAR at high-DA regimes (11.39 vs. MrVI's 7.33 and MELD's 6.45). Runtime analysis (Fig. 2b) shows scRatio is significantly faster than the naive two-ODE approach for ratio estimation, with the gap widening at higher dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TSM (Choi et al., 2022) and CTSM (Yu et al., 2025) on the Gaussian ratio and MI benchmarks, scRatio consistently achieves lower MSE/MAE, with the advantage growing in higher dimensions (e.g., at d=320, scRatio λ=0.25 reaches MAE 1.16 vs. CTSM 2.15 and TSM 3.55). On the differential abundance task, scRatio substantially outperforms MrVI (Boyeau et al., 2025) across all metrics: Spearman correlation with DA level is 0.99–1.00 vs. 0.67–0.83, and NAR at high-DA is 11.39 vs. 7.33. Against MELD (Burkhardt et al., 2021), scRatio wins on AUC correlation (0.99 vs. 0.48), NAR correlation (1.00 vs. 0.17), and NAR magnitude (11.39 vs. 6.45), but MELD marginally edges out on CSP at high-DA (0.99 vs. 0.98). The paper also reports that scRatio runs significantly faster than the naive two-ODE likelihood ratio approach, with the runtime gap increasing with dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and continuous normalizing flows (Chen et al., 2018b) as the generative backbone, and draws on the score–vector-field reparameterization from guided flows (Zheng et al., 2023) to express density scores in terms of learned velocity fields. Conceptually, it extends the compositional-diffusion idea of Skreta et al. (2025a) — tracking a density along trajectories generated by a different model — to the specific problem of estimating a log-density ratio via a single ODE, rather than the two independent integral evaluations required by naive CNF likelihood computation. It departs from Time Score Matching (Choi et al., 2022) and Conditional TSM (Yu et al., 2025), which interpolate between the numerator and denominator distributions, by instead leveraging a single conditional CNF and tracking the ratio dynamically along one trajectory without retraining per distribution pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-ODE formulation eliminates the compounding discretization error of two separate likelihood integrals and roughly halves inference cost, making per-cell likelihood ratio scoring tractable at single-cell genomics scale (tens of thousands of cells × hundreds of genes). The practical demonstrations across differential abundance, batch-correction evaluation, drug-combination interaction, and patient-specific treatment response show the method is a versatile drop-in for any conditional comparison task in scRNA-seq. Key limitations remain: numerical instability when the two conditional distributions have limited support overlap, the overhead of training separate score and velocity networks, and the observation that performance degrades sharply at very high dimensions (d=320) unless specific schedule choices (λ>0) are made. Open questions include whether the method can handle truly non-overlapping supports and whether the score network can be eliminated via more stable reparameterizations.
