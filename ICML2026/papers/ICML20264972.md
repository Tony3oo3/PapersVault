## Problem

Autonomous web agents generate long-horizon action trajectories that must adhere to externally imposed policies, yet existing guardrails focus on safety-oriented content filtering and fail to detect policy-trajectory violations. No large-scale benchmark or dedicated lightweight model exists for this compliance detection task, leaving a critical gap in agent reliability evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a unified text prompt concatenating the policy description, a step-wise trajectory (e.g., 'Step 1: Click link My Account; Step 2: Scroll page'), and domain/subdomain metadata, and outputs a single binary token: [violation] or [no_violation]. Trajectories are standardized from raw browser events into a controlled verb set (Click, Input, Scroll, Select, Navigate, Submit), averaging 9.3 actions. A key evaluation variant truncates trajectories to the first N steps (N=1–5), requiring the model to predict violations from partial prefixes before the full trajectory is observed.

## Method

POLICYGUARD-4B is a 4B-parameter model obtained by full-parameter instruction-tuning of Qwen3-4B-Instruct on 60k policy-trajectory pairs from POLICYGUARD-BENCH, casting compliance detection as a single-task binary classification under strict instruction formatting. The benchmark is constructed via a four-stage pipeline: standardizing 733 WebArena trajectories, synthesizing 2,195 atomic policies with GPT-4o, matching policies to trajectories via embedding similarity and keyword triggers (yielding within- and cross-subdomain pairs), and annotating violations with gpt-oss-120B validated by human re-annotation (89.8% agreement). The key insight is that policy compliance is orthogonal to safety: safety guardrails like LlamaGuard and ShieldGemma score below 60% accuracy on this task, while the small policy-specific model reaches 90.1% accuracy at 22.5 ms/example latency—over 10× faster than frontier models—demonstrating that targeted training at 4B scale suffices for accurate, deployable compliance guardrails.

## Results

On POLICYGUARD-BENCH (59,997 policy-trajectory pairs), POLICYGUARD-4B achieves 90.1% accuracy and 87.6% F1 with only 22.5 ms/example inference latency, surpassing all safety-oriented guardrails (LlamaGuard, ShieldGemma) by 30+ points in accuracy. In the efficiency-adjusted metric (EA-F1), POLICYGUARD-4B scores 38.9, more than double the next-best open-source model (Gemma-3-12B at 17.1) and over an order of magnitude above frontier models. In prefix-based detection (N=1–5), POLICYGUARD-4B maintains 85.3% average accuracy, competitive with 70B+ models. Leave-one-domain-out generalization shows only a ~2.5 pp drop from in-domain (93.3%) to out-of-domain (90.8%) accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POLICYGUARD-4B (90.1% Acc, 87.6% F1) outperforms all safety-oriented guardrails by a wide margin: LlamaGuard variants score only 42–58% accuracy and ShieldGemma models 55–57%, confirming that safety detection and policy compliance are orthogonal tasks. Against frontier models, POLICYGUARD-4B matches or exceeds Claude-Sonnet-4 (89.8% Acc) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (87.1% Acc) while running 55–145× faster (22.5 ms vs. 1238 ms and 596 ms). Among large open-source models, Llama-3.3-70B achieves slightly higher accuracy (90.5%) but at 13.6× the latency (305 ms), while Gemma-3-12B is close in accuracy (89.6%) yet 2.3× slower. At the same 4B scale, POLICYGUARD-4B dramatically outperforms its base model Qwen3-4B-Instruct (69.0% Acc) and Gemma-3-4B (78.8% Acc), demonstrating that targeted instruction-tuning on policy-trajectory data is the key differentiator. In the efficiency-adjusted EA-F1 metric, POLICYGUARD-4B (38.9) dominates all baselines, with the next-best Gemma-3-12B at 17.1 and frontier models below 1.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing policy-trajectory compliance detection as a distinct task, explicitly separating it from the safety-oriented guardrail literature (LlamaGuard, ShieldGemma) that focuses on content toxicity and catastrophic risks. It departs from prior agent-safety benchmarks (ST-WebAgentBench, SafeArena, WebSuite) that measure Completion-Under-Policy as an aggregate metric by reframing compliance as a per-trajectory binary classification problem with a dedicated detection model. The prefix-based violation detection task is introduced as a novel evaluation dimension motivated by the practical need for online intervention before irreversible actions are executed, building on early-decision-making ideas from the broader LLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety-oriented guardrails (LlamaGuard, ShieldGemma) perform at near-chance levels (~42–57% accuracy) on policy compliance is practically important: teams deploying agent guardrails may be relying on safety models that are blind to the most common form of agent misbehavior. The 60k benchmark and the demonstration that a 4B fine-tuned model can match or exceed 70B+ baselines at 1/10th the latency provide a concrete, deployable path for compliance-aware guardrails. Key limitations include the text-only trajectory abstraction (no visual/GUI context), restriction to WebArena-style domains, and the absence of validation on coding agents, embodied agents, or enterprise workflows—areas where policy compliance is arguably most critical.
