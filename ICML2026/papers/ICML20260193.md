## Problem

In federated learning, K clients jointly train a model but need a mechanism to later prove its provenance. Naive per-client watermarking fails at scale because individual signals dilute as K grows, and giving all clients the watermark key lets any single client verify and remove it. The paper addresses the missing protocol for (t,K)-threshold watermarking, where K clients embed collaboratively but only coalitions of at least t clients can reconstruct the key and verify a suspect model.

## Input / Output

During training, each client contributes its local model update plus an additive watermark share w_k (derived from its Shamir share via a public Lagrange coefficient) through secure aggregation, so the untrusted server only sees the aggregate. During verification, a coalition of ≥t clients provides their Shamir shares and the suspect model's full parameter vector; the protocol computes a one-sided calibrated z-score on the cosine similarity between the suspect model and the watermark key without ever materializing τ in the clear. The watermark key τ is a d-dimensional vector sampled from N(0, I_d), and the detection threshold is z ≥ 4 (false positive rate ≈ 3.2×10⁻⁵).

## Method

The protocol combines Shamir (t,K)-threshold secret sharing with secure aggregation in a three-phase design. In SETUP, a trusted dealer (or a dealer-free DKG) splits the watermark key τ into K Shamir shares; each client then derives an additive embedding share w_k = λ_k · s_k using public Lagrange coefficients so that Σ w_k = τ. In EMBED, each FL round uses an adaptive scaling factor (EMA of update norms) to stabilize watermark strength, and clients submit their watermarked updates via secure aggregation so the server learns only the global scale and the aggregate model, never individual shares. In VERIFY, a coalition of ≥t clients computes the inner product ⟨θ_s, τ⟩ = Σ_{i∈S} λ_i ⟨θ_s, s_i⟩ directly from shares via secure aggregation, avoiding key reconstruction, and applies a calibrated one-sided z-test on the resulting cosine similarity. The key insight is that the additive-share structure of the watermark (guaranteed by Lagrange interpolation) is preserved through FedAvg aggregation, so the watermark direction remains coherent regardless of K, unlike per-client baselines where independent keys cancel out.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet (K=32, c=0.025), the watermarked model loses only 0.12 pp, 0.21 pp, and 0.25 pp top-1 accuracy respectively compared to the unwatermarked baseline (88.08%, 61.33%, 53.98%). The watermark remains detectable (z≥4) at scale up to K=128, whereas the per-client baseline drops below the detection threshold once K≥16. Under non-IID Dirichlet label skew (γ=0.25–1.0), z-scores stay between 12.58 and 12.72. On GPT-2 Small fine-tuned on WikiText-2, perplexity increases by at most 0.18 while z-scores reach 6.89–9.63.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a naive per-client watermark baseline in which each of the K clients independently samples its own key τ_k and embeds it locally. Under FedAvg aggregation, the baseline's effective watermark direction becomes (1/K)Στ_k, whose expected norm shrinks as 1/√K, causing the z-score to fall below the detection threshold (z=4) once K≥16. In contrast, the proposed (t,K)-threshold method sustains statistically significant z-scores up to K=128, even with a smaller scaling factor (c=0.025 vs. c=0.1 given to the baseline). The comparison is deliberately conservative: the baseline is granted a 4× larger watermark strength to improve its detectability at small K, yet it still degrades rapidly. No other external watermarking baselines (e.g., Uchida et al., Adi et al., RIGA) are directly benchmarked; the evaluation focuses on the scalability and fidelity trade-off specific to the federated multi-client setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines Shamir's (t,n)-threshold secret sharing with secure aggregation to solve a gap in federated learning watermarking: prior FL watermarking schemes either rely on a trusted server to embed centrally (Tekgul et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2025) or on designated clients embedding via local trigger data (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023a), leaving no mechanism for collective, threshold-gated verification. The detection mechanism (cosine-similarity z-test on model parameters) follows Uchida et al. (2017), while the embedding under an untrusted server leverages the secure aggregation primitive of Bonawitz et al. (2017). The key conceptual departure is replacing per-client independent watermark keys—which dilute as 1/√K under FedAvg—with a single shared key secret-shared across all clients, so that the aggregate watermark direction is preserved regardless of K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As large language models are increasingly trained via federated learning (Sani et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024), the question of how multiple contributors can collectively prove and enforce model provenance without trusting any single party becomes practically urgent. The (t,K)-threshold formulation is a natural fit for consortium settings (e.g., healthcare FL across hospitals) where no single participant should unilaterally claim ownership or strip attribution. Key limitations include the white-box-only verification, the honest-but-curious assumption (no Byzantine clients or malicious server), and evaluation limited to ResNet-18 and GPT-2 Small; extending to black-box verification, Byzantine robustness, and larger LLMs are natural open questions. The paper also acknowledges that an adaptive attacker with full trajectory access could in principle estimate the watermark direction, though no theoretical lower bound on estimation difficulty is provided.
