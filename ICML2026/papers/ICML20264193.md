## Problem

Current clinical validation of Sybil, a frontier deep learning model for lung cancer risk prediction from CT, relies entirely on observational correlation metrics that confirm the model works but not why or when it might fail. This correlation-based approach overlooks the model's actual reasoning mechanism, creating a critical oversight for high-stakes clinical deployment where understanding failure modes is essential. The paper addresses this by proposing the first interventional audit of Sybil, shifting the paradigm from observational validation to causal verification of the model's decision-making process.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 3D LDCT scans resampled to 1mm×1mm×2mm spacing, processed as 64³ subvolumes, along with nodule segmentation masks (spherical masks from LUNA25 coordinates or expert radiologist annotations from iLDCT). For SHNAP, it generates all 2^N nodule-coalition counterfactuals via SDB inpainting and outputs a full LMPI decomposition: a sample-specific baseline μ_x, per-nodule main effects φ_i, and pairwise interaction terms φ_ij. For SNAP, it takes a source nodule volume and a target coordinate, producing a spatial attribution map of log-odds ratio scores ψ_c across thousands of insertion locations, revealing local sensitivity and global anatomical biases.

## Method

S(H)NAP combines n-Shapley Values (nSV) for axiomatic LMPI decomposition with System-Embedded Diffusion Bridge (SDB) generative interventions on 3D CT. The key theoretical justification is Verdú's mismatched estimation theorem, which guarantees that forward diffusion erases distinguishing information between a nodule-containing scan and a healthy-tissue counterfactual, making SDB inpainting a valid in-distribution nodule removal. SHNAP constructs all nodule coalitions by removing nodules via SDB (100 NFE) and recomposing, then solves for LMPI coefficients using nSV's unique axiomatic allocation. SNAP probes spatial sensitivity by copy-pasting a known nodule into a target location, diffusing to a blending timestep τ=0.3 (validated by expert radiologists), and reverse-sampling to coherently integrate the nodule into the new anatomical context. The SDB is a discrete-time Schrödinger Bridge variant (76M parameters, 1000 steps) trained on 64³ NLST cubes with procedurally generated metaball masks, requiring no annotated nodule locations during training.

## Results

The LMPI approximation (Hypothesis 1) achieves R² ≈ 1 for main effects alone in the majority of LUNA25 and iLDCT cases, with the outlier tail almost entirely eliminated by adding pairwise interactions. Expert radiologists could not distinguish synthetic nodule removals from real healthy tissue (point estimate 0.575, statistically indistinguishable from chance), and the optimal blending timestep for nodule insertion was τ = 0.3. SNAP reveals a significant radial sensitivity bias (p < 0.001) where attribution drops near the pleural surface (R² = 0.071 for distance alone, rising to R² = 0.455 with nodule identity interactions), and a global lobar bias where upper lobes receive significantly higher attribution than middle/lower lobes (p ≤ 0.009, Tukey's HSD). SHNAP and gSHNAP expose critical failure modes including dangerous sensitivity to ECG electrodes and metal snaps outside the body, and systematic underestimation of subpleural malignant nodules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its SDB-based generative inpainting against two pretrained CT synthesis models adapted for inpainting via RePaint: Lung-DDPM (Jiang et al., 2025a) and MAISIv2 (Zhao et al., 2026), evaluated on 3,226 samples with metaball masks using FID, RMSE, MAE, and SSIM metrics. SDB is reported to outperform both baselines in reconstruction fidelity, which is critical because the entire auditing framework depends on in-distribution perturbations. Additionally, the paper contrasts SHNAP attributions computed with SDB against four naive baselines (global mean, unmasked-region mean, median, and fixed lung tissue intensity replacement), showing that naive baselines produce standard deviations of attributions orders of magnitude higher than SDB-based removal, confirming that out-of-distribution inputs yield unstable and unreliable explanations. The SDB model (76M parameters, trained on 64³ cubes for ~1,600 A100-hours) also avoids the need for annotated nodule locations during training, unlike MAISIv2 and other recent methods limited to small fully-annotated cohorts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three prior threads—System-Embedded Diffusion Bridges (SDB) for on-manifold image editing, n-Shapley Values for axiomatic interaction attribution, and region-constrained visual counterfactual explanations—into a unified auditing framework (S(H)NAP) tailored to 3D lung CT. It departs from prior XAI-in-medical-imaging work (which typically applies off-the-shelf Grad-CAM, SHAP, or LRP without domain-specific generative interventions) by formulating the model as a Linear Model with Pairwise Interactions over pulmonary nodules and using diffusion-bridge inpainting to construct in-distribution counterfactuals. The Schrödinger Bridge implementation adapts I2SB from 2D natural images to 3D medical volumes, and the theoretical grounding for nodule insertion via distribution blending (Verdú, 2009) is made explicit where prior image-editing work relied on it implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interventional audit of Sybil, a clinically validated frontier model, revealing failure modes invisible to observational metrics: a systematic radial sensitivity bias that attenuates detection of peripheral nodules (where adenocarcinoma predominantly arises) and dangerous reliance on clinically unjustified artifacts such as ECG electrodes and hospital gown snaps. The S(H)NAP framework is model-agnostic and applicable to any lung cancer screening system, including proprietary commercial models, making it a practical tool for pre-deployment verification. Key limitations include reliance on partially synthetic data (mitigated but not eliminated by expert validation), computational cost of 2^N coalition evaluations, and the fact that bias-mitigation strategies (synthetic augmentation, attribution-guided regularization) are outlined but not implemented. Open questions include whether the LMPI-over-nodules hypothesis holds for models with different architectures or for multi-lesion scenarios beyond pairwise interactions.
