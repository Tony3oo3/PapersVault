## Problem

Standard off-policy evaluation estimators (importance sampling, doubly robust, marginalized) become unreliable under sparse rewards: IS variance explodes because non-zero rewards appear on only a tiny fraction of trajectories, DR's Q-function cannot be learned when temporal-difference targets are mostly zero, and marginalized estimators become ill-conditioned when reward support is nearly empty. This paper asks whether one can extract variance-reducing signal from the dense state visitations that every trajectory (successful or not) necessarily contains, without requiring the estimator to predict sparse rewards.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N i.i.d. logged trajectories from a known behavior policy π_b, the evaluation policy π_e, and a discount factor γ, and produces a single scalar value estimate V̂^{π_e} with a guaranteed variance bound no worse than plain PDIS. Unusually, the potential function Φ_β is trained entirely self-supervised—no external reward labels or Bellman targets—by maximizing the explained variance between IS-weighted shaping columns and the base return, with 5-fold cross-fitting ensuring finite-sample unbiasedness. The learned coefficient vector λ* additionally reveals which timesteps contribute most to variance reduction, providing an interpretable diagnostic.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs zero-mean control variates from potential-based reward shaping: a learned scalar potential Φ_β(s) (a 2-layer tanh MLP) induces per-step shaping differences C_t^Φ = γ^t W_t(γΦ(s_{t+1}) − Φ(s_t)) that telescope to −Φ(s_0) under the boundary condition Φ(s_T)=0, guaranteeing zero mean under the behavior distribution regardless of how Φ is learned. The shaped estimator is V̂_Shaped-PDIS = V̂_PDIS + λ^T C̃^Φ, where C̃^Φ is the centered shaping feature vector and λ* = −Σ_CC^{−1}Σ_CY is the variance-minimizing coefficient from Theorem 4.4. The potential parameters β are optimized by directly maximizing the explained-variance objective J(β) = Σ_CY(β)^T Σ_CC(β)^{−1} Σ_CY(β), which equals the guaranteed variance reduction, using conjugate gradients for the matrix inversion when T is large. This framework is base-agnostic: the same shaping control variate can be added to DR or MRDR (yielding Shaped-DR and Shaped-MRDR), and the key structural advantage over Q-based control variates is that misspecification of Φ degrades only the magnitude of variance reduction, never the estimator's unbiasedness.

## Results

Across all 11 environments (Tabular Chain, Cancer Simulator, Pointmaze, Antmaze, 5 single-stock + 1 multi-stock DOW-30, and ICU-Sepsis), shaped estimators achieve 3–4 orders of magnitude lower variance and MSE than their unshaped counterparts (PDIS, DR, MRDR) on ICU-Sepsis, while remaining asymptotically unbiased. Effective Sample Size (ESS) is substantially higher for shaped estimators, indicating more stable importance-weighted estimates. Among shaped variants, Shaped-DR and Shaped-MRDR consistently outperform Shaped-PDIS, demonstrating complementary benefits of combining shaping control variates with model-based corrections. Under increasing reward sparsity and Gaussian noise, shaped estimators exhibit slower performance degradation than traditional methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three shaped variants (Shaped-PDIS, Shaped-DR, Shaped-MRDR) against their unshaped baselines (PDIS, DR with FQE critic, MRDR with importance-weighted regression critic) across 11 environments. Shaped-PDIS dominates PDIS on variance, MSE, and ESS in every environment, with the largest gains (3–4 orders of magnitude) on ICU-Sepsis where rewards are binary (+1 alive / 0 dead). Shaped-DR outperforms both DR and Shaped-PDIS, and Shaped-MRDR outperforms both MRDR and Shaped-DR, indicating that the shaping control variate provides additional variance reduction on top of the Q-function-based correction. All shaped estimators remain unbiased (bias ≈ 0) while traditional DR/MRDR can exhibit bias under Q-misspecification. Under noise and sparsity stress tests, shaped estimators degrade more gracefully: traditional estimators rely heavily on the sparse reward signal, so corruption disproportionately harms them, whereas shaped estimators extract signal from dense state visitations in all trajectories. The paper does not report a single consolidated numerical table; results are presented in figures (Figs. 2–9, Appendix G) showing variance, MSE, ESS, and bias curves across environments and sparsity/noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the control-variate framework for OPE by repurposing potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) as a source of zero-mean correction terms, building directly on the SCOPE-style shaping correction of Parbhoo et al. (2020) but generalizing it from a fixed λ=-1 correction to a learned potential Φ_β jointly optimized with data-dependent coefficients λ*. The key conceptual departure from DR/MRDR is that the control variate is a state-only potential whose telescoping structure guarantees zero-mean under any policy, rather than a state-action Q-function that must satisfy Bellman consistency and predict sparse rewards. The paper also positions itself against marginalized estimators (AvgDICE, GenDICE, DualDICE), arguing that shaping injects distributional structure directly without requiring reward-anchored density-ratio optimization that becomes ill-conditioned under extreme sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3–4 orders-of-magnitude variance reduction in ICU-Sepsis and consistent gains across 11 environments address a genuine practical gap: standard OPE estimators become unreliable precisely in the sparse-reward, long-horizon settings (clinical outcomes, portfolio returns) where policy evaluation is most needed. The structural guarantee that unbiasedness holds for any learned Φ (regardless of optimization quality or misspecification) is a meaningful robustness advantage over DR, where Q-misspecification couples directly with estimator bias. Open questions include whether the approach extends to partially observable environments, how it composes with marginalized estimators, and whether the finite-sample regime (ρ_max^T large, N small) can be further mitigated beyond weight clipping. The finding that Φ is fundamentally decoupled from the value function V also challenges the implicit assumption in prior shaping-based OPE work that learned potentials approximate value functions.
