## Problem

Existing online change-point detection methods reduce each observation to a fixed-dimensional Euclidean summary (moments, learned features, or scalar distances), which can miss distributional shifts that are predominantly geometric—such as multimodal reweighting, copula/dependence changes, or support deformations—where low-order statistics remain nearly invariant. When data arrives in batches (e.g., thousands of cells per flow-cytometry run), the natural observation is an empirical measure, and the relevant change is in the law of a distribution-valued process, a setting that moment-based and kernel-based detectors handle only indirectly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of batches, each containing N_t point observations in R^d, represented as empirical measures μ_t ∈ P_2(X), together with a pre-change calibration window of n_0 batches used to estimate the reference barycenter, PCA eigenbasis, and thresholds. It produces a binary change-point alarm (stopping time τ) triggered when either of two monitoring statistics—functional Hotelling's T² on the principal subspace or SPE on the residual—exceeds its calibrated threshold. No labels or post-change information are required; the entire procedure is unsupervised and operates online with constant memory per time step beyond the fixed calibration artifacts.

## Method

The core mechanism is a reference-centered local linearization of 2-Wasserstein space: each empirical measure μ_t is mapped to the tangent space at a pre-change Fréchet barycenter μ̄ via the OT displacement field v_t = T_{μ̄}^{μ_t} − Id ∈ L²(μ̄; R^d), exploiting the radial isometry W₂²(μ̄, μ_t) = ‖v_t‖²_{L²(μ̄)}. Multivariate Functional PCA is then applied to the pre-change displacement fields to obtain a finite eigenbasis, and two classical SPC statistics are constructed in this tangent space: a functional Hotelling's T² capturing deviation along the leading principal directions and an SPE capturing residual energy orthogonal to them. Thresholds are set as empirical (1−α) quantiles from the calibration phase, yielding a finite-sample ARL₀ lower bound via a union-bound argument. Theoretical guarantees include an ε-isometry bound showing the required number of principal components K scales polynomially with domain dimension d, and asymptotic null distributions (χ²_K for T², weighted χ² for SPE) under a Gaussian tangent-field assumption. Computationally, OT maps are obtained via Sinkhorn iterations and the barycenter via Conditional Normalizing Flows to handle high-dimensional settings.

## Results

On the FlowCAP-II AML detection task, IDD achieves the highest F1-score (≈0.75) with near-immediate detection delay (ARL₁ ≈ 1) and near-perfect precision (≈0.99), outperforming all baselines across the full ARL₀ range. On synthetic Gaussian translation, IDD achieves up to a 95% reduction in detection delay (ARL₁) compared to the best-tuned Log-KDE in high-variance settings (e.g., d=1, σ=1.0, δ=0.1: IDD ARL₁=5.3 vs. Log-KDE 10.8). On the Reddit vaccine sentiment stream, IDD (SPE) raises 5 targeted alarms aligned with documented policy events, while Hotelling T² raises 13 mostly event-unaligned alarms and F-CPD, NEWMA, and Scan-B raise zero alarms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hotelling T² (Shewhart/Euclidean summary), IDD wins decisively on geometric shifts: it detects multimodal reweighting and copula/dependence changes where Hotelling T² fails catastrophically (precision < 0.4 on FlowCAP-II), but Hotelling T² is marginally more efficient on pure low-variance Gaussian location shifts (e.g., d=1, σ=0.5, δ=0.1: both achieve ARL₁=1.8, while at σ=1.0, δ=0.1 Hotelling gives 4.2 vs. IDD's 5.3). Against Log-KDE, IDD achieves up to 95% lower detection delay in high-variance regimes (d=1, σ=1.0, δ=0.1: 5.3 vs. 10.8; d=2, σ=2.0, δ=0.1: 10.2 vs. 15.4), while Log-KDE remains competitive in low-variance 1D settings where KDE smoothing has minimal attenuation. Against NEWMA, IDD achieves the same F1 (≈0.75) on FlowCAP-II but with faster first-alarm timing (ARL₁ ≈ 1 vs. later), and on Reddit NEWMA produces zero alarms while IDD raises 5 targeted ones. Against F-CPD, IDD dominates across all settings; F-CPD shows reduced sensitivity in high-dimensional copula shifts (d=50) and raises zero alarms on Reddit. Against Scan-B, the comparison is limited to the Reddit experiment where Scan-B also raises zero alarms, while IDD detects event-aligned shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of distribution-valued change-point detection initiated by Horvath et al. (2021), who monitored sequences of distributions via scalar L²-Wasserstein distances, by replacing those scalar summaries with a full tangent-space representation: each empirical measure is mapped to a displacement field in L²(μ̄;ℝᵈ) via the Brenier map relative to a pre-change Fréchet barycenter. The monitoring statistics (Hotelling T² and SPE) are then classical SPC tools lifted into this Hilbert space via Multivariate Functional PCA, bridging the optimal-transport geometry literature (Brenier, Villani, Panaretos & Zemel) with the statistical process control tradition (Shewhart, Jackson & Mudholkar). The key conceptual departure from prior work is that the direction and shape of distributional shift are preserved in the tangent field, rather than being collapsed into a single distance or a low-order moment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: modern sensing systems (flow cytometry, user-generated content streams) naturally produce batch observations whose informative content is the full empirical distribution, not a point summary. The ε-isometry guarantee (Theorem 3.15) provides a principled bound on how many principal components suffice for a given reconstruction precision, which is rare in the CPD literature. The main limitation acknowledged is computational: OT map computation (even with Sinkhorn acceleration) is more expensive than Euclidean statistics, posing challenges for extremely high-frequency streams. Open questions include incremental barycenter updates, scalable OT approximations in very high dimensions, and whether the Gaussian pre-change assumption (Assumption 3.8) can be relaxed for heavy-tailed or non-stationary calibration phases.
