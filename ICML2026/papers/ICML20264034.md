## Problem

Existing video motion transfer methods model motion as unconstrained pixel displacements or linear phase shifts in Euclidean space, which entangles rigid camera motion with non-rigid object deformation and causes severe shearing artifacts and perspective collapse under complex compound motions. The paper addresses the absence of explicit geometric manifold constraints that would enforce global rigid consistency while still accommodating local non-rigid dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264034/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (resized to 480×832, 49 frames) and a text prompt as input, and produces a novel-content video that replicates the reference's motion trajectories. Optical flow is extracted from the reference via RAFT, and the method operates on the WAN-2.1-1.3B latent diffusion model in a training-free manner, requiring only 5 steps of lightweight inference-time optimization (AdamW) on a single A100 GPU with 50 denoising steps.

## Method

LieWarper reconceptualizes video motion as coordinate evolution on the Sim(2) Lie group manifold rather than pixel displacement. It first applies HOSVD spectral truncation to denoise the optical flow tensor, then analytically solves for a 4-DoF Lie algebra parameter (log-scaling, angular velocity, translation) via a closed-form least-squares solver using a geometric Jacobian. The global rigid trajectory is evolved along one-parameter subgroups via the exponential map, guaranteeing all intermediate poses remain on the manifold and preventing volumetric collapse. A flow-guided phase warping mechanism decouples the coordinate field into a flow-integrated observation prior and a learnable residual that is warped through the rigid transformation before being injected into RoPE, preserving the i.i.d. Gaussian prior of the latent space. Inference-time joint optimization exploits the low-dimensional Lie algebra as a geometric bottleneck, rapidly locking the global frame before refining local non-rigid corrections under the established coordinate system.

## Results

On DAVIS 2017, LieWarper achieves the best FTD (0.2381), Flow-Score (0.6907), and Depth-Corr (0.8418) among all baselines, indicating superior trajectory fidelity and 3D structural preservation. It also attains the lowest Warp-Err (19.31) among methods that generate meaningful motion (DiTFlow's lower 14.54 is accompanied by a near-static Flow-Score of 0.3057). CLIP Score (0.3154) and Temporal Consistency (0.9753) remain competitive, confirming that geometric constraints do not degrade generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LieWarper outperforms all five baselines on the three core motion-fidelity metrics: FTD (0.2381 vs. best baseline 0.2497 for Go-with-the-Flow), Flow-Score (0.6907 vs. 0.6810 for SMM), and Depth-Corr (0.8418 vs. 0.8056 for RoPECraft). Against RoPECraft, the closest competitor on geometric metrics, LieWarper improves FTD by ~5% and Depth-Corr by ~4.5%, while also reducing Warp-Err from 20.27 to 19.31. DiTFlow reports the lowest Warp-Err (14.54) but its Flow-Score collapses to 0.3057, revealing it generates near-static frames to trivially minimize warping error. MotionClone and SMM lag substantially on Warp-Err (49.82 and 31.02 respectively), indicating severe temporal instability. On generation-quality metrics, Go-with-the-Flow edges out LieWarper on CLIP Score (0.3242 vs. 0.3154) and DiTFlow leads on Temporal Consistency (0.9832 vs. 0.9753), but these gaps are marginal and come at the cost of significantly worse motion transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

LieWarper extends the RoPECraft line of work (which optimizes RoPE phase parameters for trajectory alignment) by replacing its unconstrained Euclidean phase optimization with a Sim(2) Lie group manifold constraint. It departs from the broader family of training-free motion transfer methods (Go-with-the-Flow, MotionClone, DiTFlow, SMM) that model motion as pixel displacement or feature-space attention injection, instead reconceptualizing motion as coordinate evolution on a geometric manifold. The analytic Sim(2) solver via HOSVD-filtered optical flow and tensor contraction draws on classical rigid-body estimation but is novel in its application to guiding diffusion-based video generation through RoPE modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric framework that addresses a persistent failure mode in video motion transfer—shearing and perspective collapse under compound camera/object motion—by enforcing manifold structure on the coordinate evolution. Key open questions remain: the constant-velocity Sim(2) assumption limits handling of rapid alternating maneuvers (the authors' sliding-window fix introduces identity inconsistency), and the planar Sim(2) approximation cannot model Z-dependent parallax, while the attempted SE(3) lift fails due to depth-noise amplification (∝ 1/Z²). Future directions include B-Spline trajectory parameterization and learnable geometric adapters that bridge physical 3D transformations to the compressed latent space of diffusion models.
