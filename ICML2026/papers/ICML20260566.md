## Problem

Existing de novo functional protein design methods fail to simultaneously achieve functionality and foldability: one-step sequence generation degrades functional specificity due to the many-to-one mapping from sequences to functions, while two-step structure-then-sequence pipelines produce backbones that are geometrically incompatible with natural amino acid sequences. No prior approach jointly models both modalities under functional conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260566/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of Gene Ontology molecular function terms as conditioning input, which are expanded into retrieved functional structural motifs (continuous embeddings of local backbone coordinates encoded via LFQ) and injected via cross-attention. It outputs a protein of length 200–400 residues as an interleaved sequence of 20 amino acid tokens and discrete structure tokens (from a fixed LFQ vocabulary), from which backbone N/Cα/C/O coordinates are reconstructed. Generation proceeds over 500 discrete diffusion steps with independent corruption schedules for the sequence and structure modalities.

## Method

CodeFP is a co-generative discrete diffusion model initialized from the frozen DPLM-2 (650M) backbone that jointly denoises sequence and structure tokens in an interleaved manner, ensuring mutual constraint at every step. Two key modules extend this to functional design: (1) Functional-Structural Retrieval (FSR) constructs a database mapping each GO term to a mean-pooled [CLS] embedding of its local structural motifs (extracted via InterProScan and encoded by the LFQ tokenizer), then injects these motifs into every transformer layer via cross-attention to ground flat GO encodings in physical geometry; (2) Local Structure-Function Supervision (LSFS) applies an auxiliary classification head to the continuous hidden states of functional-domain structure tokens, providing a dense functional signal that alleviates the one-to-many ambiguity inherent in discrete structure tokenization. Training minimizes a joint objective of the weighted reconstruction loss and a class-frequency-weighted cross-entropy on the LSFS head, with only the added modules (FSR cross-attention, gated GO embedding network, LSFS head) trained while the DPLM-2 backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the GO-conditioned protein design benchmark (103.9K SwissProt/InterPro proteins, 375 GO terms), CodeFP achieves F1-Macro of 0.446, F1-Micro of 0.496, AUC-ROC of 0.724, and MRR of 0.658, surpassing the strongest one-step baseline CFP-Gen (F1-Macro 0.370) by 7.6% and the strongest two-step baseline Pinal (F1-Macro 0.369) by 7.7%. In foldability, CodeFP attains 80.65% pLDDT>70 and 83.48% pTM>0.5, exceeding CFP-Gen (75.52%/72.30%) and Pinal (74.22%/82.22%). On the out-of-distribution subset of 76 unseen GO combinations, CodeFP gains 9.1% in F1-Macro over the baseline, demonstrating superior compositional generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeFP outperforms all five main baselines across every functional metric. Against the strongest one-step method CFP-Gen, CodeFP gains +0.076 F1-Micro, +0.076 F1-Macro, +0.076 AUPR, +0.050 AUC-ROC, and +0.057 MRR, while also improving foldability by +5.13% pLDDT>70 and +11.18% pTM>0.5. Against the strongest two-step method Pinal, CodeFP leads by +0.044 F1-Micro, +0.077 F1-Macro, +0.092 AUPR, +0.061 AUC-ROC, and a substantial +0.279 MRR, with +6.43% pLDDT>70 and +1.26% pTM>0.5. Chroma and ProGen2 are substantially outperformed on all metrics (e.g., Chroma achieves only 0.067 F1-Macro and 23.47% pLDDT>70). In the appendix, CodeFP also dominates ESM3 (F1-Macro 0.446 vs. 0.044) and DPLM-2 Motif-Scaffolding (F1-Macro 0.550 vs. 0.453 on a 400-protein subset), confirming that the co-generative architecture with FSR and LSFS provides gains beyond what the base model's inherent scaffolding capability can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeFP extends the co-generative discrete diffusion framework of DPLM-2 (which jointly models sequence and structure tokens) to the task of function-conditioned protein design, a setting previously addressed only by one-step sequence generators (ProGen2, CFP-Gen) or two-step structure-then-inverse-folding pipelines (Chroma, Pinal). The key architectural departure is the introduction of Functional-Structural Retrieval (FSR), which grounds GO-term conditioning in retrieved local structural motifs rather than flat one-hot or text embeddings, and Local Structure-Function Supervision (LSFS), which applies an auxiliary classification loss on continuous hidden states to counteract training ambiguity from structural tokenization. This positions the work at the intersection of co-generative PLMs and motif-scaffolding ideas, but repurposes both for a functional design objective rather than unconditional or structure-only generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that joint sequence-structure decoding can simultaneously improve both functional consistency and foldability, a trade-off that has plagued prior one-step and two-step methods. However, OOD generalization remains a significant open problem: exact match rates on unseen functional combinations peak at only 14.5%, and hypothetical (biologically unrealizable) combinations yield no fully correct generations, indicating that compositional functional synthesis is far from solved. The absence of wet-lab validation and the reliance on a single 103.9K-protein dataset limit the practical impact, and the authors themselves flag dataset augmentation and experimental validation as critical next steps. The finding that the model preferentially fails on functionality rather than foldability suggests that translating abstract functional semantics into precise atomic-level structural constraints remains the core unsolved challenge in de novo design.
