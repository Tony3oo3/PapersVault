## Problem

Streaming video understanding systems must answer asynchronous queries under a fixed GPU memory budget, but decisive visual cues often appear briefly and long before the query arrives. Existing bounded-memory approaches either truncate far history (sliding windows) or retain selected token instances (online KV compression), both of which become brittle under sustained post-evidence update pressure: retained tokens compete for a fixed capacity and can be evicted or diluted before the query is posed. The paper asks whether far history can be represented as a persistent summary state that degrades more gracefully than token-instance retention as query delay grows.

## Input / Output

The system ingests a continuous video stream sampled at 0.5 fps (196 visual tokens per frame for LLaVA-OV-7B, yielding ~98 tokens/s) and answers text queries that arrive at arbitrary timestamps without replaying past frames. The query-time context is strictly bounded to W + K_max·S tokens (e.g., 24k for RVS benchmarks, 4k for OVO-Bench), independent of stream length, and the output is a standard text answer scored on a 0–5 scale (RVS) or accuracy (other benchmarks). The delayed-query evaluation shifts the query timestamp by Δ minutes while restricting to retrospective query types whose ground truth is invariant to the shift.

## Method

ProtoKV maintains a two-tier, constant-footprint online state: a near-window ring buffer of W exact KV pairs for recent tokens, and a fixed-capacity far prototype bank of K_max entries that stores no per-token KV for older history. Each prototype holds key/value centers, a mass count, Product-Quantization-based residual histograms, and running spatiotemporal metadata; evicted tokens are assigned to prototypes via a continuity-aware objective combining key-space cosine similarity, Mahalanobis spatial distance, and a staleness penalty, then absorbed through EMA center updates and histogram increments. At query time, each prototype is decoded into S pseudo-KV tokens by beam-searching the top-S residual modes from its PQ histograms, concatenating them with the near-window KV to form a fixed-length context consumed by standard attention unchanged. A per-prototype log-mass bias (log n_k) is added to attention logits to approximate the multiplicity of absorbed tokens, and all S pseudo-tokens of a prototype share the rotary position of its most recently absorbed source token. The entire mechanism is training-free, query-agnostic during maintenance, and drop-in compatible with any Transformer-based VLM backbone.

## Results

On the four streaming video understanding benchmarks under matched token budgets, ProtoKV achieves the best accuracy across both LLaVA-OV-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbones, e.g., 59.4% on RVS-Ego and 53.1% on RVS-Movie with Qwen2.5-VL-7B. The headline advantage appears in the long-delay regime: at the largest evaluated query delays, ProtoKV gains up to +20.4 pp over sliding-window attention and +12.5 pp over the token-retention baseline InfiniPot-V on RVS-Movie, with gains that grow monotonically as delay increases. On offline video understanding (VideoMME, MLVU), ProtoKV reaches 63.7% and 68.9% at a 6K budget, outperforming DyCoke by 4.0 and 8.4 pp respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sliding-window attention (SWA), ProtoKV wins on all four SVU benchmarks by 0.5–1.0 pp at zero delay, and the gap widens dramatically under query delay, reaching up to +20.4 pp on RVS-Movie at the longest evaluated delays because SWA irrecoverably loses evidence once it exits the window. Against the token-retention baseline InfiniPot-V, ProtoKV leads by 0.5–0.8 pp at zero delay and by up to +12.5 pp at long delays, reflecting that retaining a fixed set of token instances becomes brittle under sustained post-evidence updates. On the offline VideoMME benchmark, ProtoKV outperforms DyCoke by 4.0 pp (6K budget) and 6.8 pp (3K budget), while on MLVU it is competitive with InfiniPot-V (68.9% vs. 68.4% at 6K) and substantially ahead of DyCoke (68.9% vs. 60.5%). Against the offloading/retrieval baseline ReKV, ProtoKV trades a small accuracy gap (58.0% vs. 58.8% on RVS-Ego) for a 26% reduction in both query-time TTFT (1.06 s vs. 1.43 s) and end-to-end latency (2.90 s vs. 3.92 s) under matched peak GPU memory, making it better suited to strict streaming latency budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoKV sits at the intersection of streaming video understanding (SVU) and KV-cache compression, departing from the token-instance retention paradigm of InfiniPot-V (Kim et al., 2025) and sliding-window attention (Xiao et al., 2024) by representing far history as a fixed-capacity prototype bank with product-quantization residual statistics rather than a subset of retained KV pairs. The prototype-bank concept draws from compressive transformers (Rae et al., 2020), and the pseudo-token interface is inspired by prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), but both are repurposed here in a training-free, online, query-agnostic setting. The paper also positions itself against offloading/retrieval-based SVU (ReKV, Di et al., 2025) by showing that a GPU-resident constant-footprint summary state can match accuracy while cutting query-time latency by 26%, and against offline video compression (DyCoke, LongVU) by emphasizing the continuous-update pressure that distinguishes streaming from offline settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtoKV addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck—bounded GPU memory with asynchronous queries in streaming video—using a training-free, drop-in mechanism that requires no architectural changes, making it immediately applicable across VLM backbones. The delayed-query evaluation protocol (shifting query time while restricting to retrospective, ground-truth-invariant queries) provides a principled stress test that prior SVU benchmarks lack, and the finding that summary-state far memory degrades far more gracefully than token retention under sustained post-evidence updates (up to 12.5 pp at long delays) gives practitioners a clear design principle for long-horizon streaming. Key limitations include a mechanistic weakness on counting or ordering visually similar repeated events (prototypes blur episode boundaries), a key-value independence approximation in pseudo-token decoding, and the fact that the delayed-query protocol relies on dataset-defined timestamps as a proxy for true evidence time. Open questions include whether richer joint key-value residual statistics, dynamic per-prototype pseudo-token allocation, or learned assignment rules can close the remaining gap on fine-grained temporal tasks.
