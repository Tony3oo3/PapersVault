## Problem

Token merging in Vision Transformers reduces sequence length by averaging similar tokens, but this aggregation induces a systematic statistical misalignment (covariate shift) in the residual stream that causes sharp accuracy degradation under aggressive compression ratios. Existing heuristics like Proportional Attention correct only the zeroth-order cluster probability mass while leaving second-order energy drift and logit overconfidence uncalibrated, leading to performance collapse at low retention ratios.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-and-play calibration inserted into the self-attention forward pass after any token reduction step (merging, pruning, or hybrid) has already reduced the sequence from N to rN tokens. It consumes the same inputs as the base model—RGB images for classification, image-text pairs for retrieval and VQA—and produces identical outputs (class labels, retrieval rankings, free-form text answers). The only modification is two scalar operations: element-wise scaling of the value matrix V′ and a logit-level affine transform on the attention score matrix, adding less than 0.8% overhead relative to the attention computation itself.

## Method

The paper reframes token merging as a high-dimensional statistical reconstruction problem and derives an asymptotic radial-angular decomposition of the mean-squared reconstruction error, separating magnitude drift from distributional mismatch. Minimizing the leading-order surrogate under finite second-moment and approximate variance-stationarity assumptions yields a single parameter-free factor √r governing both corrections: (1) a radial correction that scales merged value vectors by √r to neutralize the 1/r combinatorial energy inflation from aggregating probability mass into fewer clusters, and (2) an angular correction that applies LMMSE shrinkage to merged attention logits, pulling them toward the geometric cluster-size prior ln(N_j) with reliability weight √r. The derivation shows that the radial factor cancels the upper-bound inflation under homogeneous clusters while the angular factor acts as an adaptive temperature (T = 1/√r) that counteracts overconfidence bias from intra-cluster logit variance. The resulting √r-scaling is closed-form, introduces no trainable parameters, and is agnostic to the specific token selection or merging heuristic, making it compatible with ToMe, PiToMe, ToFu, DCT, and DiffRate.

## Results

On Image-Text Retrieval (ALBEF, Flickr30k, r=0.9), √r-scaling achieves R_sum=549.1, recovering +8.2 points over Vanilla (540.9) and +14.3 over PA (534.8), while retaining 95.1% of uncompressed performance (577.4). On VQA (ToMe, r=0.9), the method attains an Average Normalized Score of 97.22 versus 95.79 (Vanilla) and 92.26 (PA), with the largest gap on POPE (85.60 vs. 82.88 for PA). On ImageNet-1K classification at aggressive compression (r=0.75), √r-scaling recovers +29% accuracy over baselines and +6.4% over Vanilla for pure pruning (DCT), with the performance gap widening progressively as the retention ratio decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Proportional Attention (PA) from ToMe (Bolya et al., ICLR 2023), which adds a log N_j bias to merged logits but applies no value scaling. √r-scaling consistently outperforms PA across all evaluated settings: on ALBEF Flickr30k (r=0.9) it gains +14.3 R_sum (549.1 vs. 534.8), on BLIP2 MSCOCO (r=0.95) it gains +8.6 R_sum (504.0 vs. 495.4), and on VQA (ToMe, r=0.9) it gains +4.96 Avg. Norm. (97.22 vs. 92.26). Notably, PA frequently underperforms even the uncorrected Vanilla baseline at aggressive compression (e.g., PA 534.8 vs. Vanilla 540.9 on ALBEF; PA 92.26 vs. Vanilla 95.79 on ToMe VQA), indicating that its zeroth-order logit bias introduces harmful estimation bias under heterogeneous clustering. The advantage of √r-scaling over both baselines widens monotonically as the retention ratio decreases, reaching up to +166.4 R_sum over PA on BLIP at r=0.75. All three methods share identical GFLOPs and wall-clock latency, confirming that the √r correction adds negligible computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the token merging paradigm of ToMe (Bolya et al., 2023), which introduced Proportional Attention (PA) as a zeroth-order logit bias to correct cluster probability mass. The key conceptual departure is reframing the correction problem as a high-dimensional statistical reconstruction task, yielding a radial-angular decomposition of the reconstruction error that separates magnitude drift from distributional mismatch. While prior token reduction work (DCT, PiToMe, ToFu, DiffRate) focuses on *which* tokens to keep or merge, this paper studies *how* the aggregation operation itself perturbs feature norms and attention logits, and derives a closed-form √r calibration from LMMSE estimation and energy-balance arguments. The analysis also draws on deep signal propagation literature (variance preservation, initialization theory) to justify the approximate variance-stationarity assumption enabled by normalization layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √r-scaling is parameter-free, training-free, and adds less than 0.8% computational overhead, making it a practical drop-in calibration for any existing token reduction pipeline. The finding that PA can invert below uncorrected Vanilla at aggressive compression (r=0.75) challenges the assumption that the standard heuristic is always beneficial, and the paper's ablations confirm that radial and angular distortions are independent failure modes requiring joint correction. Open questions include whether the angular correction remains useful in architectures with explicit QK or logit normalization, how the method behaves at extremely low retention ratios where irreversible information loss dominates, and whether the statistical reconstruction perspective extends to generation models with merge-unmerge pipelines such as diffusion Transformers.
