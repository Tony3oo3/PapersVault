## Problem

Continuous Diffusion for Categorical Data (CDCD) models have received less attention than other diffusion variants partly because the latent space structure is typically co-optimized with the model, leading to unstable training and suboptimal results. No prior work has studied what geometric properties of the token embedding space make a codebook well-suited for CDCD, nor has any demonstrated that a specific quantization scheme is provably optimal for this framework.

## Input / Output

The CDCD-TTS model takes input text (treated as a sequence of Latin characters, digits, and punctuation), a reference speech utterance, and its corresponding reference text as conditioning. It produces a speech waveform via a three-stage pipeline: CDCD generates a sequence of 8-dimensional FSQ speech tokens (from a 6561-token codebook at 25 Hz), a flow-matching module converts tokens to mel-spectrograms, and a HiFi-GAN vocoder produces the waveform. The CDCD backbone is a ~45M-parameter DiT, roughly 10× smaller than the 0.5B LLM in CosyVoice2, and inference with 25 DDIM steps achieves a real-time factor of 0.2–0.3 on a single GPU.

## Method

The paper establishes that the KL divergence between reverse diffusion path measures ending at two token embeddings is proportional to their squared Euclidean distance (Statement 4.2), which motivates maximizing the average nearest-neighbor distance D(E) as a design criterion for the codebook. It proves that base2 and base3 FSQ codebooks are locally optimal in D among all codebooks with L∞-bounded entries (Theorem 4.3) and proves the Best Accuracy Hypothesis—that an optimally trained CDCD model achieves maximum top-1 token prediction accuracy under FSQ geometry—in the 1D case, with numerical evidence in higher dimensions. For the TTS system, the authors replace CosyVoice2's LLM text-to-token module with a CDCD model: a stack of 8 DiT (adaLN-zero) blocks with a softmax head predicting 6561 FSQ token classes, trained with cross-entropy loss on noisy latents, conditioned on reference tokens and text via concatenation, and paired with a lightweight statistical duration predictor that estimates token count from character-level duration statistics scaled by a reference-speech speed factor.

## Results

On the SEED-TTS test-en set, the best CDCD-TTS model (FSQ-original-25) achieves WER 2.00%, speaker similarity (SIM) 0.653, UTMOS 4.119, and emotion accuracy (EMO) 72.7%. It outperforms its LLM-based counterpart CosyVoice2 (WER 2.57%, MOS 4.077) while using a DiT backbone that is ~10× smaller and 5–10× faster, achieving a real-time factor of 0.2–0.3. Among the CDCD variants, FSQ-original consistently beats FSQ-perturb, FSQ-permute, and RVQ across all denoising step counts, confirming the theoretical optimality of FSQ codebook geometry for CDCD training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct LLM-based counterpart CosyVoice2, CDCD-TTS (FSQ-original-25) wins on WER (2.00% vs. 2.57%) and MOS (4.119 vs. 4.077), ties on SIM (0.653 vs. 0.652), and slightly leads on EMO (72.7% vs. 72.2%), while being ~10× smaller and 5–10× faster at inference. Against F5-TTS, CDCD-TTS loses on WER (2.00% vs. 1.83%) and SIM (0.653 vs. 0.665) but substantially wins on MOS (4.119 vs. 3.754) and EMO (72.7% vs. 71.4%). Against the state-of-the-art CosyVoice3, CDCD-TTS trails on WER (2.00% vs. 1.68%) and SIM (0.653 vs. 0.695) but leads on MOS (4.119 vs. 3.937) and ties on EMO (72.7%). Internally, FSQ-original consistently outperforms FSQ-perturb at every denoising step count (e.g., WER 2.00% vs. 2.03% at 25 steps), while FSQ-permute and RVQ degrade dramatically (WER 15.4% and 21.3% respectively), confirming that both the FSQ geometric structure and the correct token-to-embedding mapping learned during autoencoder training are essential for CDCD performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two relatively underexplored threads: Continuous Diffusion for Categorical Data (CDCD, Dieleman et al. 2022), which operates forward/reverse diffusion in a continuous embedding space while predicting discrete token probabilities, and Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ, Mentzer et al. 2024), a fixed-grid vector quantization scheme widely adopted for speech tokenization. The authors are the first to study the geometrical structure of the CDCD latent space, establishing a theoretical link between KL divergence of reverse diffusion path measures and the nearest-neighbour distance between token embeddings. For the TTS system, they replace the autoregressive LLM backbone of CosyVoice2 (Du et al. 2024) with a non-autoregressive CDCD module whose architecture is borrowed from F5-TTS (Chen et al. 2024), while retaining the FSQ speech tokenizer and flow-matching/vocoder stages unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that a ~45M-parameter CDCD backbone matches or exceeds a 0.5B LLM in speech intelligibility while being 10× smaller and 5–10× faster offers a compelling efficiency argument for non-autoregressive diffusion in structured token generation, potentially reducing the compute barrier for zero-shot TTS deployment. The theoretical results (local optimality of FSQ grids, the KL-divergence-to-embedding-distance connection) provide a principled design criterion for choosing latent space geometry in CDCD models, though the Best Accuracy Hypothesis is only proven in 1-D and the equal-prior assumption limits direct applicability to real token distributions. Open questions include extending the optimality proofs to higher dimensions, handling non-uniform token priors, and whether the FSQ-geometry advantage persists at much larger codebook sizes or in multi-layer (hierarchical) quantization settings.
