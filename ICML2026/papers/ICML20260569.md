## Problem

Existing motion-language retrieval methods predominantly align entire motion sequences with global textual representations, overlooking fine-grained interactions between local motion segments, individual body joints, and text tokens. This global-centric paradigm fails to capture the hierarchical, pyramidal process by which humans actually perceive motion (from joint dynamics to segment coherence to holistic comprehension), leading to suboptimal retrieval accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260569/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D skeleton motion sequences (L×J×3 Cartesian coordinates) converted into MotionPatch spatiotemporal patches across five body parts (torso, left/right arm, left/right leg), and natural language text descriptions encoded by DistilBERT. It produces bidirectional retrieval rankings (text-to-motion and motion-to-text) via cosine similarity, plus interpretable fine-grained alignment scores at joint-wise and segment-wise levels that map specific text tokens to specific joints or motion segments. The compression ratio between joint and segment stages is set to 0.25, reducing token counts while preserving provenance through KNN-DPC clustering.

## Method

The PST framework performs progressive pyramidal alignment across three stages: joint-wise (individual text tokens vs. joint tokens), segment-wise (compressed phrase-level vs. segment-level tokens via a token compressor combining convolution, self-attention, and KNN-DPC clustering), and holistic (global representations). At the joint-wise and segment-wise stages, a Shapley-Taylor Interaction (STI) metric with k=2 quantifies pairwise cross-modal interaction intensity by measuring the marginal contribution of a token pair under random permutations; since exact STI is computationally prohibitive, a lightweight STI Estimation Head (conv + self-attention) is trained via Monte Carlo sampling (32 samples) and distilled through a KL-divergence loss. Training combines InfoNCE contrastive loss at all three stages, STI distillation loss at fine-grained stages, and a self-distillation KL loss that uses joint-wise similarity distributions as teacher signals to guide segment-wise alignment, ensuring hierarchical consistency. The motion encoder is a ViT operating on MotionPatch representations, and the overall loss weights all components equally (λ_S=1, λ_D=1).

## Results

On HumanML3D (All protocol), PST achieves R@1 of 12.45% and R@10 of 48.22% for text-to-motion retrieval, and R@1 of 13.59% and R@10 of 42.68% for motion-to-text retrieval, with a Median Rank of 10.00 and 15.00 respectively. On KIT-ML (All protocol), the method reaches R@1 of 16.01% (T2M) and 16.76% (M2T), with MedR of 7.00 and 8.00. Under the Small Batch protocol on HumanML3D, PST attains R@1 of 76.15% (T2M) and 75.12% (M2T), outperforming all baselines by 4–5 points over the previous best (Lyu et al., 2025).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PST outperforms all five baselines across both retrieval directions on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. Against the closest competitor Lyu et al. (2025), PST gains +0.65 R@1 and +4.86 R@10 on HumanML3D text-to-motion, and +1.20 R@1 and +2.34 R@10 on motion-to-text. Over MotionPatch (Yu et al., 2024), the improvement is larger: +1.65 R@1 (T2M) and +2.34 R@1 (M2T) on HumanML3D, and +1.99 R@1 (T2M) and +3.15 R@1 (M2T) on KIT-ML. TMR (Petrovich et al., 2023) shows a +3.53 R@1 gap in T2M on HumanML3D, while TEMOS and T2M (originally motion generation models) lag substantially, with R@1 below 4% in both directions. On KIT-ML, PST leads Lyu et al. by +0.88 R@1 (T2M) and +1.75 R@1 (M2T), and on the larger MotionX++ dataset it extends the lead to +1.02 R@1 (T2M) and +3.44 R@1 (M2T) over Lyu et al., confirming consistent generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on MotionPatch's ViT-based spatiotemporal patch representation for 3D skeleton motion and repurposes the Shapley-Taylor Interaction index (originally developed for explainable AI) as a fine-grained cross-modal alignment signal. It departs from the dominant global-alignment paradigm in motion-language retrieval (TEMOS, TMR, MotionPatch, Lyu et al.) by introducing a three-level pyramidal hierarchy—joint-wise, segment-wise, holistic—that progressively compresses tokens via KNN-DPC clustering and enforces consistency across levels through a self-distillation loss. The STI Estimation Head, trained via Monte Carlo sampling and distilled through KL divergence, replaces the prohibitively expensive exact STI computation, making the interaction signal tractable during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fine-grained motion-language alignment is a prerequisite for controllable motion generation, style transfer, and motion understanding, yet prior methods treat sequences as monolithic units. The paper's annotation-free approach to discovering fine-grained correspondences (via STI rather than explicit word-joint labels) makes it applicable to weakly labeled settings, and the LLM-enhanced variant (PST++) demonstrates that enriching text with part-level descriptions further closes the structural gap between MotionPatch's part-level motion features and holistic text. Key open questions include the acknowledged local bias toward common poses, the symmetry problem with repeated motion phrases, and whether the pyramidal STI framework generalizes to open-vocabulary or long-tail motion scenarios.
