## Problem

Continual learning methods face a trilemma: memory-based approaches violate the strict buffer-free setting and raise privacy concerns, dynamic architecture methods cause unbounded parameter growth, and regularization-based methods degrade under heterogeneous task sequences. The critical open question is how to achieve forget-resistant learning within a fixed-capacity backbone without storing past data or expanding the architecture, specifically by identifying which neurons are most critical for each task and selectively preserving them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264528/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet-1k) and outputs class labels. The fundamental unit of importance is a convolutional filter (kernel), not an individual weight; masking a neuron replaces its output with the mean activation over validation data rather than zeroing it. In CIL, the model must discriminate among all classes seen so far without task identity at test time; in TIL, a task identifier is provided and classification is restricted to that task's class subset. The method additionally produces per-task binary neuron masks and a cumulative freezing mask that governs gradient flow during subsequent task training.

## Method

SNV frames neuron importance as a cooperative game where neurons are players and model performance is the payoff, then computes Shapley Neuron Values to obtain a continuous importance ranking for every convolutional filter. For each task, the top ⌊c·N⌋ neurons (where c is a sparsity ratio, e.g., 1/T) are identified and permanently frozen via a cumulative binary mask that zeros their gradients in all subsequent tasks, while remaining neurons stay plastic. Exact Shapley computation is intractable, so the paper estimates values via Monte Carlo permutation sampling accelerated by two techniques: truncation (skipping marginal contributions when the partial subnetwork's performance falls below a threshold τ) and a multi-armed bandit strategy that focuses sampling on neurons whose confidence intervals still overlap the top-k boundary. This yields a 4× speedup over pure Monte Carlo with only a 0.22-point accuracy loss. The key distinction from prior mask-based methods like WSN is the continuous Shapley ranking (enabling principled selection under tight budgets) and from NFL+ is the data-driven importance mechanism rather than a fixed progressive freezing schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-18), SNV achieves 41.30% average accuracy in the CIL scenario with 10 tasks, 34.20% with 20 tasks, and 25.60% with 50 tasks, consistently outperforming all buffer-free baselines. In the TIL scenario, SNV reaches 57.82% (10 tasks), 50.45% (20 tasks), and 40.18% (50 tasks) with zero backward transfer across all splits. On CIFAR-100 CIL (10 tasks), SNV attains 54.70% accuracy with BWT of −0.04, surpassing the second-best buffer-free method NFL+ (53.70%) and even exceeding the memory-based DyTox (57.40%) in stability (BWT −0.37). The gap over the second-best buffer-free method widens from +2.88% at 10 tasks to +3.20% at 50 tasks in CIL, and from +6.46% to +2.98% in TIL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNV outperforms all buffer-free baselines across every dataset and task count. Against the closest competitor NFL+ (Vahedifar & Zhang, 2026), SNV leads by +2.88% on ImageNet-1k CIL (10 tasks) and +6.46% on TIL (10 tasks), with the CIL gap widening to +3.20% at 50 tasks. Against DCNet (Wang et al., 2025), the advantage is +3.50% (CIL) and +7.67% (TIL) on ImageNet-1k. The comparison with WSN (Kang et al., 2022) isolates the value of Shapley-based ranking: at the same capacity (c=0.5) on CIFAR-100 TIL, SNV achieves 79.76% versus WSN's 64.00%, a 15.76-point gap that widens to 12.09 points under tighter budgets (c=0.03). Regularization-based methods (EWC, SI, LwF) collapse to single-digit accuracy on ImageNet-1k CIL (7.53%, 9.68%, 11.24% respectively), far below SNV's 41.30%. Against the strongest memory-based method DyTox (Douillard et al., 2022), SNV wins on CIL (41.30% vs. 40.15% on ImageNet-1k, 10 tasks) with near-zero BWT (−0.05 vs. −35.20), but DyTox narrowly leads on TIL (59.40% vs. 57.82%) at the cost of a 20,000-exemplar replay buffer and 2.8× higher FLOPs. DER++ and iCaRL trail SNV by 27+ points on ImageNet-1k CIL, confirming that replay-based approaches struggle at this scale without large buffers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: cooperative game theory (Shapley values, Shapley 1953), neuron-level importance estimation (Ghorbani & Zou 2020's Neuron Shapley with Monte Carlo and truncation), and sparse-subnetwork continual learning (WSN's binary mask freezing, NFL+'s progressive parameter locking). Its key departure is replacing WSN's binary {0,1} importance scores and NFL+'s blind progressive freezing with a continuous, axiomatic Shapley valuation that ranks neurons by their marginal contribution to task performance, then selects the top-c fraction per task. The cumulative mask mechanism (union of per-task masks) is shared with WSN, but the selection criterion is fundamentally different: rather than finding *a* winning subnetwork, SNV finds *the* most important one under a capacity budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNV demonstrates that a buffer-free method can match or exceed memory-based approaches (e.g., DyTox) on ImageNet-1k CIL without storing a single exemplar, which is significant for privacy-regulated (GDPR) and resource-constrained deployments. The 15+ point gap over WSN at identical capacity isolates the value of *principled* neuron ranking over binary selection, a finding that generalizes beyond continual learning to any sparse-subnetwork problem. The main open question is computational: the post-task Shapley estimation phase scales with network width and may become prohibitive for very large models, though the authors point to online Data Shapley approximations (Wang et al. 2025a) as a promising path to eliminate the separate estimation phase entirely.
