## Problem

Likelihood-based multiple-choice benchmarks score candidates by summing token-wise log-probabilities, which induces a systematic bias toward shorter answers because per-token loss accumulates with length. The common fix—dividing by completion length—frequently over-corrects, introducing a bias toward longer answers instead, and no principled criterion exists for when either scoring rule is safe. The paper asks: can we remove the first-order length trend without rescaling signal and noise, and without additional model evaluations?

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same conditional log-likelihoods and completion byte lengths already computed for standard accuracy, plus a single scalar length-decay factor b estimated per model-dataset-prompt configuration. It outputs a length-debiased log-likelihood score per candidate (ℓ_θ(c|x) − b·n), from which the argmax yields the selected answer and standard accuracy is computed. Because the correction is a simple affine transformation of existing scores, no additional forward passes are required, and the length metric (bytes vs. tokens) can be swapped without changing the mechanism.

## Method

The paper formalizes the empirically observed approximately affine relationship between total log-likelihood and completion length as a hierarchical linear model with prompt-specific intercepts and slopes, then introduces an explicit exponential prior over completion lengths P_prior(n) ∝ exp(−bn). Incorporating this prior into a Bayesian posterior over completions yields a score that simply subtracts b·n from the conditional log-likelihood, removing the global length trend additively rather than multiplicatively. The slope b is estimated via within-question centered regression (equivalent to regressing pairwise log-likelihood differences on pairwise length differences), which cancels prompt-specific intercepts and avoids confounding from correlations between prompt difficulty and candidate length. This makes Bayesian accuracy a drop-in replacement for standard accuracy that consistently reduces measured Kendall-τ length bias by roughly 4×–8× relative to normalized accuracy across 17 model-benchmark configurations, without any additional forward passes.

## Results

The headline metric is the mean absolute Kendall rank correlation $\overline{|\tau|}$ between candidate length and score, averaged over 8 benchmarks (lower = less length bias). Bayesian accuracy achieves $\overline{|\tau|}$ in the range 0.03–0.07 across all 17 evaluated models (135M–70B, base and instruction-tuned), representing a 4×–8× reduction relative to byte-normalized accuracy (0.24–0.42) and a several-fold improvement over PMI (0.07–0.18) and ANPMI (0.05–0.13). Standard (unnormalized) accuracy shows $\overline{|\tau|}$ of 0.17–0.31, confirming a systematic bias toward shorter candidates. Bayesian accuracy requires no additional forward passes beyond those needed for standard accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bayesian accuracy outperforms all four baselines on the mean absolute length bias metric across every model. Compared to standard (unnormalized) accuracy, Bayesian accuracy reduces $\overline{|\tau|}$ by roughly 3×–5× (e.g., 0.03 vs. 0.20 for Llama 3.1 8B). Against byte-normalized accuracy, the improvement is even more dramatic at 4×–8× (e.g., 0.03 vs. 0.27 for Llama 3.1 8B; 0.05 vs. 0.42 for Qwen3 14B Instruct), confirming that naive length normalization frequently over-corrects and introduces a bias toward longer answers. PMI (Brown et al., 2020; Fano & Hawkins, 1961) and ANPMI (Cho et al., 2025) both reduce bias relative to standard and normalized accuracy, with ANPMI consistently improving on PMI, but both still leave non-trivial residual bias (0.05–0.18) and require evaluating unconditional log-likelihoods for every candidate, doubling the number of forward passes. Bayesian accuracy, by contrast, uses only conditional log-likelihoods and a single learned scalar $b$ per model-dataset configuration, making it a strict drop-in replacement with no additional computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of likelihood-based multiple-choice evaluation (established by GPT-3 and the LM Evaluation Harness) and the growing literature on scoring-rule artifacts in LLM benchmarks. It builds on the observation that total log-likelihoods accumulate additively over tokens, a property noted in GPT-3 and the LM Evaluation Harness, and on PMI/ANPMI as prior attempts to adjust for unconditional priors. However, it departs from all prior corrections by framing the length trend as an explicit exponential prior over completion lengths and absorbing it via a single learned scalar subtraction, rather than rescaling (normalization) or subtracting unconditional log-probabilities (PMI/ANPMI). The formalization of length bias via within-example Kendall's tau is also new relative to prior work, which typically discussed length effects anecdotally or via aggregate score-length plots.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the standard length-normalization fix over-corrects and introduces a bias in the opposite direction is practically important: it means that a widely adopted heuristic may be systematically distorting model rankings on popular benchmarks. The proposed Bayesian correction is a one-line change (subtract b·n) that requires no additional forward passes, making it easy to adopt in existing evaluation pipelines. Open questions remain about whether length-debiased scoring changes relative model rankings in practice, whether the linear length model holds for all tokenizer families or longer completions, and whether analogous corrections are needed for generation-based (non-multiple-choice) evaluation. The paper also explicitly notes that the correction targets only length-dependent priors and does not address other answer biases (frequent words, syntactic preferences).
