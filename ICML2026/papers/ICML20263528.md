## Problem

Neural generative models trained on sparse empirical distributions struggle to capture global topological structure, as primal-domain smoothing is inherently local and isotropic, leading to disconnected modes and vanishing gradients in low-density regions. Existing spectral (characteristic-function) methods provide global supervision but lack an explicit analytical inversion mechanism, forcing generation to rely on iterative optimization. This paper bridges the gap by deriving a spectral loss with rigorous Fisher-divergence bounds and a closed-form 1-NFE inversion that eliminates iterative ODE integration entirely.

## Input / Output

The model takes standard Gaussian noise x_1 ~ N(0, I_d) and a class label y (1,000 ImageNet categories) as input, operating directly in pixel space with data dimension d = 16×16×3 (flattened patch size 16) at 256×256 resolution, without any latent compression. A fixed frequency matrix U ∈ R^{K×d} with constant row norms ‖u_k‖ = π/√d is used to project data into the spectral domain, with K > d to ensure an over-determined system for stable inversion. For time series, the input is a partial observation y_gt with a binary mask M, and the output is the completed/forecasted sequence; the spectral dimension K is adapted per dataset (32–256) to maintain the over-determined condition relative to feature dimension d (5–137).

## Method

PriSpecNet fuses a standard transformer primal backbone (self-attention + SwiGLU blocks) with a complex-valued spectral decoder (Complex Attention + MLP), where early primal features are injected into the spectral path via element-wise addition to provide context. Training jointly minimizes a primal velocity-regression loss and a spectral loss that weights CF differences by ‖u_k‖², which Proposition 4.2 proves squeezes two-sided Fisher-divergence bounds, guaranteeing weak convergence via Lévy's Continuity Theorem. The key architectural insight is that the linear stochastic interpolant x_t = s(t)x_0 + σ(t)x_1 becomes a multiplicative phase modulation in the spectral domain (Proposition 4.1), enabling the network to predict the clean-data CF φ_{x_0} directly from the noisy spectral features. At inference, the Pseudoinverse Lévy Inversion (PiLI) solver recovers x_0 in a single network evaluation by solving the regularized least-squares problem x̂_0 = (U^T W U + λI)^{-1} U^T W θ, where θ = arg(φ̂_{x_0}(U)) is the predicted phase vector and W encodes spectral consistency weights, replacing iterative ODE integration with a Cholesky-factorized linear solve that reduces compute from 4,436 Gflops (25-NFE DPM-Solver++) to 26 Gflops while achieving FID 1.66 on ImageNet-256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, PiLI-XL achieves a SOTA FID of 1.66 with a single network function evaluation (1-NFE), surpassing the previous best from-scratch baseline iMF-XL/2 (FID 1.72) and all distillation-based and GAN methods. The 1-NFE PiLI solver requires only 26 Gflops, a ~170× reduction compared to the 4,436 Gflops of 25-NFE DPM-Solver++ (FID 1.62). On time series, PriSpecNet-TS (50-NFE Heun's) sets new SOTA Context-FID scores, reducing them by 80.6% on ETTh (0.018 vs. 0.093 for ARMD) and 63.1% on Stock (0.052 vs. 0.141), while also lowering forecasting MSE by 29.8% on Solar and 23.8% on Stock.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 256×256, PiLI-XL (FID 1.66) outperforms the closest from-scratch competitor iMF-XL/2 (FID 1.72) by 0.06 FID, and substantially beats α-Flow-XL/2+ (2.58), TiM-XL/2 (3.26), MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.43), and Shortcut-XL/2 (10.60). It also surpasses all distillation-based methods (FACM 1.76, DMF 2.16, π-Flow 2.85) and GAN baselines (StyleGAN-XL 2.30, GigaGAN 3.45, BigGAN 6.95). On time series, PriSpecNet-TS (50-NFE Heun's) dominates all three baselines (ARMD, TSFlow, Diffusion-TS) across every dataset and metric; for example, on ETTh it achieves Context-FID 0.018 vs. ARMD's 0.093 (80.6% reduction) and MSE 0.201 vs. 0.430 (53.2% reduction). The 1-NFE PiLI variant on time series is second-best overall but still outperforms ARMD on Sines (0.006 vs. 0.006, tied) and remains competitive on Stock (0.093 vs. 0.141 Context-FID). In solver efficiency, PiLI's 26 Gflops at 1 NFE represents a ~170× reduction over DPM-Solver++'s 4,436 Gflops at 25 NFE, while matching its FID (1.66 vs. 1.62).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stochastic Interpolants framework (Albergo et al., 2025) and the iMF architecture (Geng et al., 2025b) by introducing a dual primal-spectral training objective: alongside the standard velocity-field regression loss, it adds a weighted characteristic-function (CF) matching loss whose minimization provably squeezes two-sided Fisher divergence bounds. The key departure from both primal methods (diffusion, flow matching) and prior spectral methods (GMMN, CF matching) is the Pseudoinverse Lévy Inversion (PiLI) solver, which replaces iterative ODE integration with a single closed-form algebraic inversion of the spectral phase, grounded in Lévy's Continuity Theorem. Unlike existing CF-based generators that rely on black-box optimization to match spectral statistics, PiLI provides a direct analytical inverse, bridging the gap between spectral training and deterministic 1-NFE sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 170× Gflops reduction (4,436 → 26) at a near-competitive FID of 1.66 (vs. 1.62 for 25-NFE DPM-Solver++) makes high-fidelity image generation dramatically cheaper, which is practically significant for deployment in resource-constrained settings. The Fisher divergence bounds provide a rare theoretical guarantee that spectral training converges to the true distribution, addressing the vanishing-gradient and topological-ambiguity problems of sparse empirical measures. However, the 1-NFE PiLI results on time series are notably weaker than the 50-NFE Heun's variant (e.g., ETTh Context-FID 0.033 vs. 0.018), suggesting the analytical inversion still loses fidelity in lower-dimensional, highly structured settings. The authors acknowledge the lack of principled tools to coordinate spectral dimension K, data dimension d, and network capacity, and suggest future Spectral-Only architectures that eliminate the primal backbone entirely.
