## Problem

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) using plan-and-infill decoding are highly sensitive to the order in which masked slots are filled, yet existing heuristic planners (e.g., confidence-based selection) fail to account for long-range dependencies between slots, leading to error propagation and degraded output quality. The combinatorial space of possible slot permutations makes it intractable to find optimal orderings by brute force, and no prior work had applied search-based planning to this problem in text diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pre-trained masked diffusion model (ReFusion 7B) whose response is partitioned into K contiguous slots of L tokens each (slot size 4, 32 serial blocks in the final configuration). At inference time, MCTS operates purely on the model's own confidence scores—no ground-truth references or auxiliary networks are required. The output is a fully generated text sequence, with the slot filling ordering (a permutation of slot indices) being the intermediate decision that determines generation quality. The method is entirely training-free and adds approximately 35% FLOPs over the base ReFusion model.

## Method

McDIFFUSE formulates slot selection as a deterministic MDP where states encode which slots have been filled, actions select the next slot to infill, and rewards are the mean token-level confidence of the generated slot. It applies MCTS with the PUCT selection criterion, using the model's normalized slot-level confidence scores as action priors and a hybrid value estimate that mixes immediate slot confidence (weight λ=0.3) with a stochastic temperature-scaled rollout over remaining slots (weight 1−λ) to capture long-term trajectory coherence. After N_sim simulations, the next slot is chosen by the most-visited child at the root, and the process repeats until all slots are filled. A key empirical finding is that a large exploration constant (c=50) is critical to escape the model's locally confident but globally myopic priors, while increasing simulation count beyond ~30 yields diminishing returns and can even degrade performance under low exploration.

## Results

McDIFFUSE achieves an average accuracy of 68.52% across six reasoning and code-generation benchmarks, outperforming the best autoregressive baseline (Qwen3 8B, 65.33%) by 3.2% and the primary diffusion baseline (ReFusion 7B, 60.52%) by 8.0%. The largest gains appear on code generation: +19.45% on MBPP (73.57% vs. 54.12%) and +16.32% on HumanEval (78.37% vs. 62.05%) over ReFusion. On mathematical reasoning, McDIFFUSE improves MATH500 by 4.9% (47.80% vs. 42.90%) and GPQA-Diamond by 4.33% (34.78% vs. 30.45%). McDIFFUSE matches or exceeds autoregressive performance on five of six benchmarks while using approximately 65% fewer tokens on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary diffusion baseline ReFusion 7B, McDIFFUSE wins on all six benchmarks, with the largest margins on MBPP (+19.45%) and HumanEval (+16.32%), confirming that MCTS-based slot planning is most impactful for structured code generation. Against the other diffusion baselines (LLaDA 8B and Dream 7B), McDIFFUSE achieves substantially larger gains (e.g., +41.41% on HumanEval over LLaDA), reflecting the broader gap between plan-and-infill MDMs and earlier masked-diffusion approaches. Against autoregressive baselines, McDIFFUSE outperforms Qwen2.5 7B on five of six benchmarks (MATH500 +1.3%, GPQA +17.8%, MBPP +8.7%, HumanEval +6.6%, ARC +0.9%) and Qwen3 8B on five of six (MATH500 +1.6%, GPQA +11.7%, MBPP +3.8%, HumanEval +2.1%, ARC +0.3%), while trailing by a narrow margin on GSM8K (87.91% vs. 88.21% for Qwen3). The sequential-ordering ablation (ReFusion + Sequential) underperforms the default ReFusion on GSM8K and MATH500, and the random-ordering ablation degrades severely across all benchmarks, validating that the MCTS-discovered ordering is neither purely sequential nor arbitrary but strategically non-sequential where it matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Monte Carlo Tree Search with PUCT selection (from AlphaZero) to the slot-filling ordering problem in masked diffusion models, specifically building on the plan-and-infill framework introduced by ReFusion (Li et al., 2025). It departs from the heuristic confidence-based slot selection used in existing MDMs (LLaDA, Dream 7B) by formulating slot ordering as a sequential decision-making problem solved via look-ahead simulation. The key methodological departure from prior MCTS applications to LLMs (which target autoregressive token generation) is the use of the model's intrinsic confidence scores as PUCT priors and a hybrid reward combining immediate slot confidence with stochastic rollout returns, all without any training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that exploration breadth (large PUCT constant) is more critical than simulation depth for slot planning is a useful counterintuitive result for anyone applying MCTS to structured generation, and the entropy analysis provides a clear mechanistic explanation (premature convergence under low exploration). However, the evaluation is limited to a single base model (ReFusion, the only available slot-and-infill MDM) and a fixed 1024-token budget, leaving open whether these findings transfer to other MDM architectures or scale with longer generation. The ~35% FLOPs overhead for the accuracy gains also raises practical deployment questions, and the paper acknowledges that extending token length for diffusion models remains an important but unexplored direction.
