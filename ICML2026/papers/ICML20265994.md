## Problem

Standard Next-Token Prediction supervises language models only through discrete one-hot targets in the output logit space, leaving the latent representation space under-constrained. This structural freedom allows hidden states to drift into degenerate, anisotropic configurations (representation degeneration) that sacrifice semantic expressiveness without penalty, ultimately limiting downstream generalization. The paper asks whether NTP alone sufficiently supervises the geometry of hidden representations and proposes an explicit remedy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text token sequence (context length 8192) and produces the next token via the usual softmax over the vocabulary, identical to NTP at inference. During training, it additionally produces a continuous d-dimensional vector (the projected last hidden state) that must align with a temporally shifted shallow-layer representation of the next token position. The implicit target is extracted from the model's own forward pass at a configurable shallow layer (≈20% depth) with stop-gradient, so no external encoder or extra forward pass is needed.

## Method

NITP augments the standard NTP loss with a continuous cosine-similarity objective in representation space: the last hidden state h_t is projected through a lightweight SwiGLU MLP head P(·) and trained to match the shallow-layer representation z_{t+1} of the next token (extracted with stop-gradient), minimizing 1 − cos(P(h_t), z_{t+1}). The total loss is L_NTP + λ·L_NITP with λ ≈ 1.0. The key design choices are (i) the autoregressive temporal shift (predicting t+1's shallow representation from t's deep state, not same-position alignment), (ii) using shallow layers as targets because they preserve richer lexical/semantic content than deeper task-specialized layers, and (iii) cosine similarity for scale invariance and training stability. Theoretically, the paper shows via Hessian analysis that NITP introduces strictly positive curvature along all angular directions orthogonal to the current state, thereby eliminating the semantic null space left unconstrained by NTP and preventing representation collapse.

## Results

On the 9B MoE model (1B activated params), NITP improves the average score from 40.27 to 42.94 (+2.67 points) across 13 benchmarks, with the largest gains on MMLU-Pro (+5.71, from 15.29 to 21.00), C3 (+6.36, from 56.65 to 63.01), and CommonsenseQA (+4.26, from 45.70 to 49.96). Gains scale with model size: +0.81 (1.9B), +2.12 (3B), and +2.67 (9B) average points for MoE models, and +1.02 to +1.79 for dense models (0.5B–3B). The additional training cost is only ~2% FLOPs with zero inference overhead, and validation cross-entropy loss is preserved (e.g., 1.841 vs. 1.840 on the 9B MoE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Next-Token Prediction (NTP), the dominant pre-training objective for modern LLMs. NITP wins on the majority of benchmarks across all model scales: on the 9B MoE model it outperforms NTP on 11 of 13 tasks, with the most dramatic margin on MMLU-Pro (+5.71 points) and C3 (+6.36 points), while slightly underperforming on AGIEval (−0.94) and C-Eval showing a smaller gain (+1.75). On dense models (Table 2), NITP similarly dominates, improving average scores by +1.02 (0.5B), +1.79 (2B), and +1.35 (3B), with the largest single-task gain being C3 (+4.66 on 3B). The paper also ablates against a generic cosine-regularization baseline (inspired by Gao et al., 2019) and a same-position (non-temporal-shift) alignment variant; both underperform NITP, confirming that the gains come specifically from prediction-aligned semantic supervision rather than mere geometric regularization. Validation cross-entropy on The Pile is essentially identical between NTP and NITP (e.g., 1.841 vs. 1.840 for 9B MoE), indicating the improvements are not from better token-level likelihood but from richer hidden-state geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

NITP extends the standard Next-Token Prediction (NTP) paradigm by adding a continuous, representation-level auxiliary loss, departing from multi-token prediction (MTP) approaches that remain confined to discrete token-space supervision. Conceptually, it draws on layer-wise distillation (FitNets, TinyBERT, Sheared LLaMA) and self-supervised consistency methods (CPC, BYOL), but differs in both motivation and formulation: rather than aligning to an external teacher or enforcing view-invariance, NITP uses an autoregressive objective in latent space with the model's own shallow layers as self-generated, stop-gradient targets. The theoretical framing—NTP's Hessian is rank-deficient in the semantic subspace, and NITP performs angular spectral lifting—connects to prior observations of representation anisotropy (Ethayarajh, 2019; Gao et al., 2019) but provides a concrete Hessian-level mechanism for why the degeneration occurs and how it is corrected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NITP offers a practically attractive recipe: a ~2% training FLOPs increase, zero inference overhead, and consistent downstream gains across dense and MoE architectures from 0.5B to 9B parameters, making it a low-risk auxiliary objective that could be adopted in future pre-training runs without architectural changes. The ablation showing that the temporal shift (t→t+1) is essential—same-position alignment actively degrades performance—clarifies that the benefit comes from predictive semantic supervision rather than generic geometric regularization, a distinction that guides future design. Open questions include whether the gains compound with other pre-training improvements (e.g., MTP, RLHF), whether the optimal target-layer depth (~20% of total) is stable at much larger scales, and whether the approach transfers to multimodal or non-autoregressive settings.
