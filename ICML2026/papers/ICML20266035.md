## Problem

Standard hypergraph contrastive learning objectives enforce hyperspherical uniformity, pushing all node embeddings apart indiscriminately. This causes a Hierarchy-Uniformity Conflict: the multi-level semantic structure encoded in hyperedges (e.g., nested research areas) collapses into a flat cloud of nearly equidistant points, a phenomenon the paper terms Semantic Flattening. No existing method provides an explicit mechanism to recover and preserve this semantic depth while retaining instance-level discriminability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hypergraph defined by its incidence matrix and a node feature matrix; the hypergraph structure is kept fixed during training, with self-supervised views generated solely through feature masking and Gaussian noise. The output is a set of node embeddings in a shared representation space where both local discriminability and hierarchical prototype alignment are encoded; downstream node classification is performed by freezing the encoder and training a linear probe on 10/10/80 splits.

## Method

HyperDepth combines three components operating on complementary spectral subspaces. A decoupled spectral encoder applies Chebyshev-approximated low-pass and high-pass filters to the normalized hypergraph Laplacian, then fuses the two branches per-node via a learnable sigmoid gating MLP, so that high-frequency components carry local variation and low-frequency components carry global structure. An energy-based hierarchical alignment module attaches a learnable K-level prototype tree to the representation space; each node is assigned a root-to-leaf path via top-down greedy search, and an NCE loss with single-level path perturbation as hard negatives minimizes an energy functional that includes both instance-leaf alignment and path-coherence regularization. A local InfoNCE contrastive loss is applied only after a separate projection head, confining uniformity pressure to the contrastive space rather than the main representation. Theoretically, under a frequency-separation assumption, the two objectives act on orthogonal spectral projectors, guaranteeing the existence of equilibrium embeddings that simultaneously minimize both losses.

## Results

On 9 homophilic hypergraph benchmarks, HyperDepth (self-supervised) achieves the best average rank of 1.89 across all 20 compared methods, including fully supervised hypergraph neural networks. Headline accuracies include 82.69% on Cora-C, 72.80% on Citeseer, 89.01% on Zoo, and 97.61% on ModelNet40. On 6 heterophilic benchmarks, HyperDepth attains the best accuracy on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 83.1% on Actor, 76.2% on Senate), trailing only HyperAim on Amazon-Ratings (28.7% vs. 29.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised hypergraph baselines, HyperDepth outperforms HyFi (the strongest prior SSL method) on 7 of 9 homophilic datasets, with the largest gains on Zoo (+9.0 pp, 89.01 vs. 80.02) and NTU2012 (+4.2 pp, 79.34 vs. 75.10), while trailing only on Pubmed (83.48 vs. 83.82). Against HyperAim, the most recent multi-frequency contrastive method, HyperDepth wins on 7 of 9 homophilic datasets (e.g., +1.61 on Cora-C, +8.70 on Zoo) and on 5 of 6 heterophilic datasets (e.g., +2.1 on Actor, +1.4 on Senate), losing only on Citeseer (72.80 vs. 73.13) and Amazon-Ratings (28.7 vs. 29.2). Notably, as a purely self-supervised method, HyperDepth surpasses the best supervised hypergraph model AllSetTransformer on 8 of 9 homophilic datasets (e.g., +6.48 on Cora-C, +16.29 on Zoo) and achieves a better average rank (1.89 vs. 6.50). On heterophilic benchmarks, HyperDepth dominates all prior SSL methods including TriCL, HyFi, and MMACL, with the largest margin over TriCL on Actor (+17.9 pp) and House (+1.7 pp). The only consistent loss is on Pubmed, where HyFi and TriCL edge out HyperDepth by ~0.3 pp, likely due to Pubmed's very small number of classes (3) limiting the benefit of hierarchical structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of spectral hypergraph methods (ChebNetII, framelet-based HNNs) and contrastive self-supervised learning, but its core conceptual departure is diagnosing a geometric pathology—'semantic flattening'—that arises when hyperspherical uniformity in InfoNCE erases the multi-level hierarchy encoded by hyperedges. It builds on the alignment-uniformity decomposition of Wang & Isola (2020) and the multi-frequency filtering ideas in HyperAim (same group, AAAI 2026), but adds an energy-based prototype-tree alignment module and a subspace-decomposition theorem that formalizes how local contrastive and global hierarchical objectives can coexist without gradient interference. The method repurposes Chebyshev spectral filtering (Hammond et al., 2011) not as a feature extractor per se but as a mechanism to route high-frequency signals to the contrastive branch and low-frequency signals to the hierarchical branch, a use that is novel in the self-supervised hypergraph setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric and spectral diagnosis of why standard hypergraph contrastive objectives lose hierarchical structure, and the prototype-tree energy gives an interpretable, level-wise organization of the embedding space that is absent from prior contrastive hypergraph methods. However, the central theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) rests on Assumption 1—that projection heads perfectly separate low- and high-frequency components—which is an idealization unlikely to hold exactly in finite-capacity networks, and the empirical gains over strong baselines like HyFi and HyperAim are modest (1–2 percentage points on most datasets). Open questions include whether the frequency-separation assumption can be relaxed or enforced architecturally, how the method scales to hypergraphs with millions of nodes, and whether the EM-style prototype rectification is necessary or merely a training trick.
