## Problem

Existing VLA models rely on explicit Chain-of-Thought text generation to bridge perception and action, incurring high inference latency (hundreds of milliseconds), error propagation across multi-step reasoning, and dependence on manually designed text supervision. The paper asks whether reasoning can be performed entirely in a continuous latent space—bypassing token-by-token decoding—while remaining stable, task-aligned, and computationally adaptive to the difficulty of each decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262377/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multimodal observation tuple at each timestep: an RGB image (encoded via a SigLIP-DINOv2 backbone to 768-d features), a natural-language instruction (tokenized and embedded by a frozen Llama-2 7B), a 7-DoF proprioceptive state (joint positions and gripper state, projected by a 2-layer MLP), and an interaction-history encoding. It outputs a continuous robotic delta action (end-effector displacement and gripper command) via a 4-layer MLP action head conditioned on the finalized latent reasoning state, with bidirectional attention enabling parallel chunked action decoding rather than autoregressive token generation.

## Method

AVA-VLA formulates latent reasoning as a POMDP in which a diagonal-Gaussian reasoning policy (over R^64 update actions) drives a GRU-based state transition to evolve an unobservable latent state z_t, and a separate action policy maps z_t to the environmental action. The key training innovation is an RL-based denoising stage using PPO with a composite reward that combines sparse task-success signals, an entropy penalty on the reasoning policy, and a smoothness term penalizing large latent-state jumps, thereby suppressing noise-induced drift without collapsing the state. An early-exit gate (2-layer MLP with sigmoid output) is calibrated post-training to terminate the latent loop when marginal improvement falls below a threshold (τ=0.55), reducing average reasoning depth from 5.0 to 2.3 steps. Training proceeds in three stages—100K behavior-cloning steps, 50K latent-dynamics warmup with smoothness-only regularization, and ~1.2M PPO interaction steps—on top of the OpenVLA-OFT backbone.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (one policy for all 4 suites), AVA-VLA achieves an average success rate of 98.3%, outperforming the strongest baseline OpenVLA-OFT (96.8%) by 1.5 points, with particularly large gains on LIBERO-Long (98.1% vs. 95.3%). On CALVIN ABC→D, AVA-VLA reaches 84.0% five-step task completion and an average task length of 4.77, surpassing FLOWER (77.8%, 4.53) and VLA-Adapter (76.5%, 4.42). In terms of latency, AVA-VLA with Early-Exit runs at 145 ms mean (2.3 reasoning steps, 6.9 Hz throughput), achieving a 6× speedup over explicit CoT-VLA (892 ms) while remaining only 47 ms slower than the fastest reflex-based baseline π0-FAST (98 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVA-VLA outperforms all 16 baselines on both LIBERO and CALVIN. The closest competitors on LIBERO (per-suite) are RIPT-VLA (97.5% avg), VLA-Adapter (97.3%), and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), all trailing AVA-VLA (98.6%) by 1.1–1.5 points, with the largest gap on LIBERO-Long (96.5% vs. 93.8% for RIPT-VLA). On CALVIN, AVA-VLA leads FLOWER by 6.2 points on five-step tasks (84.0% vs. 77.8%) and by 0.24 in average task length (4.77 vs. 4.53), while Seer (Tian et al.) lags further at 74.0% five-step. Explicit CoT methods (CoT-VLA, SpatialVLA) are both less accurate (83.9%, 78.1% avg on LIBERO) and substantially slower (892 ms vs. 145 ms latency). Reflex-based fast policies (π0-FAST, PD-VLA) achieve lower latency (98 ms, 76 ms) but sacrifice long-horizon success (60.2%, 91.7% on LIBERO-Long) compared to AVA-VLA's 98.1%. On cross-embodiment transfer to xArm7, AVA-VLA leads OpenVLA-OFT by 13.1 points on Spatial SR (74.3% vs. 61.2%) and π0 by 5.8 points (74.3% vs. 68.5%), suggesting the latent reasoning state captures embodiment-agnostic task semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVA-VLA builds directly on the OpenVLA-OFT architecture (bidirectional attention, SigLIP-DINOv2 backbone, Llama-2 7B) as its base, but departs fundamentally from the explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning paradigm adopted by CoT-VLA, SpatialVLA, and FLOWER. Conceptually, it draws from the latent-reasoning line (Quiet-STaR, Coconut) and the RL-for-reasoning trend (DeepSeek-R1, VLA-R1), but uniquely formulates latent state evolution as a POMDP optimized via PPO with task-level rewards, rather than treating latent variables as static intermediate representations or optimizing only the final action. The early-exit mechanism extends the EE-LLM idea from decoder layers to the reasoning stream itself, a distinction from PD-VLA's parallel-decoding acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical path toward real-time VLA control by replacing sequential token-level CoT with parallel latent updates, achieving a 6× latency reduction while matching or exceeding full-reasoning baselines on LIBERO and CALVIN. However, the interpretability trade-off is significant: latent states encode execution-relevant structure (confirmed by probing) but do not yield human-readable explanations, and the authors acknowledge that online safety monitors remain necessary for physical deployment. Open questions include whether the POMDP+RL formulation scales to navigation or locomotion, whether stronger theoretical guarantees on the coupled reasoning-action MDP are achievable, and whether the modest ~1–2% LIBERO gains over OpenVLA-OFT persist under more challenging or diverse task distributions.
