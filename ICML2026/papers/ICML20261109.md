## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods like DPO require a frozen reference model (doubling VRAM), rely on extremely small learning rates, and exhibit unstable reward margins due to their non-convex optimization landscape. No prior approach has applied convex optimization to preference fine-tuning of LLMs, leaving a gap between the strong convergence guarantees available in convex programs and the practical demands of single-GPU, resource-constrained alignment.

## Input / Output

COALA consumes preference triplets (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) and a frozen pretrained LLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B). Rather than processing raw text end-to-end, it extracts last-layer hidden-state embeddings (d=4096 for Llama-8B) from the frozen base model and trains a lightweight two-layer convex ReLU head on these fixed features. The output is a preference-aligned policy π^cvx(y|x) that scores pairwise comparisons via a logistic function over the convex head's output, enabling generation of preference-aligned text responses while the base model remains entirely frozen.

## Method

COALA stacks a convex neural network (cvxNN) on top of a frozen pretrained LLM and trains it in two phases. Phase I solves the exact convex reformulation of a two-layer ReLU network (via sampled activation-pattern cones) using CRONOS, an ADMM-based solver with preconditioned conjugate gradient, yielding globally optimal first-layer weights Θ₁ with O(1/k) ergodic convergence for any penalty ρ>0. Phase II freezes Θ₁ and minimizes the COALA loss—a modified Bradley-Terry logistic objective with offset γ over the un-normalized log-likelihood of the convex policy—using accelerated gradient descent, which achieves O(1/k²) convergence to the global optimum. This eliminates the reference model entirely, requires no gradient updates to the base LLM, and reduces total compute to ~17.6% of DPO's TFLOPs while fitting within a single RTX-4090 (24 GB VRAM).

## Results

On AlpacaEval2, COALA achieves the highest Length-Controlled Win Rate (LC WR%) across most model–dataset settings, reaching 40.90±0.09% on Llama-3.1-8B/EduFeedback and 40.81±0.22% on Dolphin-7B/EduFeedback, with notably lower variance than all baselines. In terms of compute efficiency, COALA requires only ~17.6% of DPO's total TFLOPs (e.g., 1805 TFLOPs vs. 10253 TFLOPs for Llama-8B on EduFeedback). In a 107-sample double-blind human evaluation, COALA achieves the highest human win rates at 39.1% (EduFeedback) and 42.7% (IMDb), outperforming DPO (28.8%, 24.8%) and ORPO (15.5%, 20.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, COALA wins or ties on LC WR% in 8 of 9 model–dataset settings, with the largest gap on Dolphin-7B/EduFeedback (40.81% vs. 34.73%, +6.1 pp) and Llama-8B/IMDb (27.64% vs. 21.79%, +5.9 pp); on Llama-8B/EduFeedback the two are nearly identical (40.90% vs. 40.68%). Critically, COALA achieves this while using only ~17.6% of DPO's TFLOPs and running on a single RTX-4090 (24 GB) versus DPO's A100 (40 GB). Against ORPO, COALA dominates across all settings, with gaps of 17–28 pp in LC WR% (e.g., 40.90% vs. 23.87% on Llama-8B/EduFeedback). Against SimPO (evaluated on ArenaHard with Llama-3.2-3B), COALA achieves 67.50% Custom Pairwise WR_ex_Ties on EduFeedback versus SimPO's 10.00%, a gap the authors attribute to SimPO's heavy reliance on hyperparameter tuning. In the 107-sample human evaluation, COALA outperforms DPO by 10.3 pp on EduFeedback and 17.9 pp on IMDb, confirming that the automated benchmark advantages translate to genuine human preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

COALA sits at the intersection of two research threads: the convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU networks (Bengio et al., 2005; Pilanci & Ergen, 2020) and the ADMM-based CRONOS solver (Feng et al., 2024), which had previously been applied to high-dimensional classification but not to LLM alignment. On the preference-optimization side, COALA departs from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) and ORPO (Hong et al., 2024) by freezing the entire base LLM and training only a lightweight convex head on top of its frozen features, rather than performing full-parameter or LoRA-based fine-tuning. This reframes the Bradley-Terry preference objective as a convex logistic regression in the head weights, inheriting global-optimality and convergence-rate guarantees that non-convex DPO-style objectives lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: COALA trains Llama-3.1-8B on a single RTX-4090 (24 GB) using roughly 17.6% of DPO's TFLOPs, lowering the hardware barrier for preference alignment to consumer-grade GPUs. The acknowledged expressiveness tradeoff—freezing the base model limits the policy to stylistic steering within the pretrained feature space—means COALA is best suited to correctness- and instruction-following tasks rather than tasks requiring deep semantic restructuring, and generalization to more complex objectives (e.g., GRPO, multimodal reasoning) remains open. The Alternating Population Strategy for dataset synthesis (65,606 pairs from 26,621 conversations without external reward models) is a useful but domain-specific heuristic whose transferability to open-ended creative tasks is untested.
