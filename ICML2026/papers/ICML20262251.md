## Problem

Standard RL methods for language models (PPO, GRPO) offer limited control over which reasoning trajectories are explored during training, causing complex reasoning structures like backtracking, backward chaining, and verification to remain sparse or inconsistently reinforced. Prior guidance approaches—supervised fine-tuning, reward shaping, and natural-language hints—lack a principled behavior policy that guarantees coverage of structured trajectories and supports accurate importance-sampling estimation for stable off-policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language models, the input is a text math problem prompt; for vision-language models, it is an RGB image (up to 262,144 pixels) paired with a text prompt. The model generates a full chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory followed by a final answer. During rollout, each trajectory is steered toward satisfying a specific reasoning-structure constraint (e.g., 'working backwards' or 'let me go back'), and the per-token importance-sampling weight w_t is tracked to distinguish guidance-dominated tokens (w_t < 1) from proximal-policy-dominated tokens (w_t > 1).

## Method

Ctrl-R constructs a white-box behavior policy μ_α by coupling the proximal policy π_θ_old with a tractable guidance function γ(α|x_t, x_<t), computed via a Hidden Markov Model (distilled once from the base policy) marginalized over a Deterministic Finite Automaton encoding the target reasoning structure. This yields exact per-token likelihoods for the behavior policy, enabling principled off-policy correction. The optimization uses Decoupled PPO with a power-scaling factor β ∈ [0,1] applied to the trajectory-level importance-sampling weight w^β: β=1 gives exact unbiased correction, β=0 removes correction entirely (letting the guidance reshape the advantage signal), and intermediate values (β=0.2 empirically optimal) selectively amplify learning from exploratory out-of-distribution trajectories while suppressing noisy, over-constrained ones. The key insight is that deliberately downweighting the IS correction—rather than fully correcting distribution mismatch—allows the policy to internalize reasoning behaviors from trajectories it would never generate on-policy.

## Results

On language model math reasoning (Qwen3-8B-Base), Ctrl-R (β=0.2) achieves an average score of 56.27 across AIME'24, AIME'25, MATH500, AMC'23, Minerva, and Olympiad, improving +1.38% over the DAPO baseline (54.89). At the 1.7B scale, Ctrl-R gains +1.27% (33.85 vs. 32.58). In the vision-language setting (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), Ctrl-R reaches 47.17 average across MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, MMMU-Pro, EMMA, and HallusionBench, a +2.60% improvement over GRPO (44.57) and +0.57% over the OpenVLThinker pre-alignment baseline (46.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DAPO baseline (standard RL with no guidance), Ctrl-R consistently wins at both model scales: +1.38% average at 8B and +1.27% at 1.7B, with the largest single-benchmark gain on AIME'24 (+3.96% at 8B, +2.09% at 1.7B). The NL Guidance baseline (verbalized hints injected into prompts) yields only marginal gains (+0.13% at 8B, +0.44% at 1.7B) and actually degrades AIME'24 performance at 8B (34.58 vs. 36.04). Reward Shaping (binary bonus for pattern match when answer is correct) improves average by +0.39% (8B) and +0.30% (1.7B) but over-reinforces Overthinking Awareness at the expense of other structures. In the VLM setting, Ctrl-R (47.17 avg) outperforms both the GRPO baseline (44.57, +2.60%) and the OpenVLThinker SFT pre-alignment baseline (46.60, +0.57%), which requires multi-stage supervised fine-tuning with data synthesized from stronger models. Ablations on the power-scaling factor β confirm that intermediate values (β=0.2) are critical: removing importance sampling entirely (β=0) drops performance below baseline (−0.79%), while full correction (β=1) also fails to improve (−0.12%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Ctrl-R sits at the intersection of tractable probabilistic constrained decoding (Ctrl-G, GeLaTo) and RL-based reasoning improvement (PPO, GRPO, DAPO). Prior attempts to guide reasoning behaviors—natural-language hints, auxiliary reward shaping, and SFT pre-alignment (OpenVLThinker)—bias model outputs toward target patterns but lack a principled behavior policy that admits exact importance-sampling correction. Ctrl-R departs from these by constructing a white-box behavior policy via HMM-DFA marginalization, enabling tractable likelihood computation for off-policy correction, and further introduces a power-scaling parameter on importance weights that deliberately trades off unbiased estimation for selective amplification of exploratory trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clean, modular mechanism for controlling which reasoning structures are explored during RL without entangling guidance with the task reward, mitigating reward hacking. The finding that partial importance-sampling correction (β=0.2) outperforms both full correction (β=1) and no correction (β=0) challenges the default assumption that unbiased off-policy estimation is always optimal, suggesting that controlled distribution mismatch can be a useful inductive bias. Open questions include whether hand-designed lexical constraints generalize beyond math reasoning, whether adaptive structure selection per input is feasible, and whether the HMM approximation quality becomes a bottleneck at larger scales or with more complex constraints.
