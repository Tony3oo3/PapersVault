## Problem

Constrained Online Convex Optimization (COCO) with time-varying adversarial constraints has been studied primarily through soft or linear violation metrics, but the squared-penalty metric CCV_{T,2}—which better captures the severity of large constraint violations in safety-critical applications—lacked tight theoretical guarantees. In particular, no prior algorithm achieved logarithmic bounds on both regret and cumulative squared constraint violation simultaneously for strongly convex losses, and existing analyses relied on complex, specialized drift arguments rather than exploiting the underlying operator-theoretic structure.

## Input / Output

At each iteration t, the learner receives a convex loss function f_t and a convex constraint function g_t (both with full information: function values and subgradients at the current point), along with a fixed compact convex decision set K of diameter D. The algorithm outputs an action x_t ∈ K and the analysis yields explicit upper bounds on regret and CCV_{T,2} = Σ(g_t^+(x_t))². Step-sizes are chosen as η_t = 1/t^β for general convex losses (tunable β ∈ (0,1)) or η_t = 1/(mt) for m-strongly convex losses, and the constraint functions may be vector-valued, reduced to a scalar via pointwise maximum.

## Method

CLASP introduces two algorithmic variants unified under the theory of cutter operators: CLASP-I performs a projected gradient step onto the intersection K_t = K ∩ C_t at each iteration, while CLASP-F decouples the static and dynamic constraints by first applying a subgradient projector T_{g_t} (handling the time-varying constraint) followed by a projection onto K, then a gradient step with another projection onto K. The key theoretical insight is that the strongly quasi-nonexpansiveness property of cutter operators—previously unused in COCO analysis—directly yields a telescoping inequality that bounds CCV_{T,2} by O(Σ η_t), and for strongly convex losses this immediately gives O(log T) on both regret and squared violation. This operator-theoretic framework replaces the complex drift-plus-penalty and regret-decomposition techniques of prior work with a modular, two-lemma proof structure (one lemma for CCV_{T,2}, one for regret), and the separability of CLASP-F makes it practical when projection onto K has a closed form but projection onto K_t does not.

## Results

For general convex losses, both CLASP-I and CLASP-F achieve Regret_T ≤ O(T^{max{β,1-β}}) and CCV_{T,2} ≤ O(T^{1-β}) for any tunable β∈(0,1), matching the best prior rates (Yuan & Lamperski, 2018) but extending them to dynamic constraints. For strongly convex losses, both algorithms achieve the first-ever logarithmic guarantees on both metrics simultaneously: Regret_T ≤ O(log T) and CCV_{T,2} ≤ O(log T). In experiments on online linear regression, online SVM (wine quality dataset), and drone flight, CLASP-I and CLASP-F keep squared violations sublinear while maintaining regret comparable to RECOO, outperforming AdaGrad on constraint violation by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaGrad (Sinha & Vaze, 2024), CLASP-I and CLASP-F trade slightly higher cumulative loss for dramatically lower constraint violations on both CCV_{T,1} and CCV_{T,2}; AdaGrad minimizes loss but incurs the largest violations across all three experiments. Against RECOO (Guo et al., 2022), CLASP-I matches RECOO's violation levels while being simpler and more memory-efficient (no convex optimization subproblem per step), and CLASP-F achieves comparable or slightly lower violations with a closed-form subgradient projector. Against Frank-Wolfe (Wang et al., 2025), CLASP-I and CLASP-F incur somewhat lower cumulative constraint violations at the cost of marginally higher cumulative loss; in the extended-rounds experiment, CLASP-F's CCV_{T,2} eventually drops below Frank-Wolfe's. Against Switch (Vaze & Sinha, 2025), CLASP algorithms accept slightly higher violations in exchange for substantially lower per-iteration complexity (Switch projects onto an ever-growing intersection of all past constraint sets, making it memory-intensive and inapplicable when feasibility is not guaranteed, as in the SVM experiment). On the wine-quality SVM task, both CLASP variants outperform AdaGrad, RECOO, and Frank-Wolfe on both CCV metrics with less variability, confirming the theoretical advantage of directly analyzing the squared penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the operator-theoretic machinery of cutter operators (Bauschke & Combettes, 2001; Cegielski, 2013) and the projected-gradient-with-repair template from static convex optimization (Nedić, 2011), repurposing them for the Constrained Online Convex Optimization (COCO) setting with adversarially time-varying constraints. It departs from prior COCO analyses (Sinha & Vaze, 2024; Guo et al., 2022; Yi et al., 2023), which relied on specialized drift-plus-penalty or regret-decomposition techniques, by showing that the strongly quasi-nonexpansiveness property of cutter operators alone suffices to derive both regret and squared-violation bounds. The paper also shifts the primary violation metric from the linear CCV_{T,1} (standard in prior work) to the squared CCV_{T,2}, arguing that the latter is more informative for safety-critical applications where large violations carry disproportionate risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first simultaneous O(log T) guarantees on both regret and cumulative squared penalty for strongly convex COCO is a genuine advance, and the operator-theoretic proof framework is notably simpler than prior drift-based analyses, potentially serving as a template for future constrained online optimization work. The two algorithm variants (CLASP-I for joint projection, CLASP-F for separable handling) give practitioners flexibility depending on whether projection onto the static set is tractable. A key limitation is that squared penalties suppress severe outliers but do not strictly penalize frequent negligible violations, making CLASP unsuitable for zero-tolerance safety without an additional hard filter. Open questions include whether the cutter-operator framework extends to non-convex losses, stochastic settings, or distributed COCO, and whether the log-log bound is tight.
