## Problem

Existing methods for real-time interactive video generation distill pretrained bidirectional video diffusion models into few-step autoregressive (AR) students, but this introduces an architectural gap—converting full attention to causal attention—that no prior work addresses theoretically. The paper shows that ODE distillation from a bidirectional teacher to an AR student violates frame-level injectivity (each noisy frame must map to a unique clean frame under the PF-ODE), causing the student to collapse to a conditional expectation and produce blurred, inconsistent videos. A controlled experiment confirms that the subsequent DMD stage cannot recover from this initialization failure.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and generates an 81-frame video at 832×480 resolution via 4-step autoregressive sampling, where frames are produced sequentially in chunks of 3 latent frames. This streaming, chunk-wise generation enables real-time interactive use at 17 FPS with 0.69 s latency on a single H100 GPU, allowing users to steer subsequent frames based on already-generated content.

## Method

Causal Forcing is a three-stage distillation pipeline whose core insight is that ODE initialization must use an autoregressive (not bidirectional) teacher to satisfy frame-level injectivity. Stage 1 trains an AR diffusion teacher via teacher forcing, which the paper proves (Proposition 3.4) and shows empirically to be superior to diffusion forcing, whose noisy-prefix conditioning creates a training-inference distribution mismatch. Stage 2 performs causal ODE distillation by sampling PF-ODE trajectories from this AR teacher, so the paired (noisy frame, clean frame) data is injective at the frame level, enabling the AR student to learn the true flow map rather than collapsing to a conditional mean (Proposition 3.3). Stage 3 applies the same asymmetric DMD procedure as Self Forcing, where the student conditions on its own generated prefix via self-rollout. The framework also extends to causal consistency distillation, which outperforms asymmetric CD that uses a bidirectional teacher.

## Results

Causal Forcing achieves a VBench Total score of 84.04, surpassing the SOTA distilled autoregressive model Self Forcing (83.74) and matching or exceeding the bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B (83.37) while delivering 2079% higher throughput (17.0 FPS vs. 0.78 FPS). On the 100-prompt motion set, it improves Dynamic Degree by 19.3% (68 vs. 57), VisionReward by 8.7% (6.326 vs. 5.820), and Instruction Following by 16.7% (56 vs. 48) over Self Forcing, all at identical 0.69 s latency. In user studies, Causal Forcing receives the best average rating (1.64, lower is better) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor Self Forcing (Huang et al., 2025a), Causal Forcing wins on every metric: +19.3% Dynamic Degree (68 vs. 57), +8.7% VisionReward (6.326 vs. 5.820), +16.7% Instruction Following (56 vs. 48), and a better user rating (1.64 vs. 2.87), all at identical 17.0 FPS / 0.69 s latency. Versus CausVid (Yin et al., 2025), the gains are even larger: +10.0 in Dynamic Degree, +10.0 in VisionReward, and +44 in Instruction Following. Compared to the bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B, Causal Forcing matches or exceeds it on Total (84.04 vs. 83.37), Dynamic Degree (68 vs. 61), VisionReward (6.326 vs. 5.275), and Instruction Following (56 vs. 42) while running 21.8× faster (17.0 vs. 0.78 FPS). Against non-distilled autoregressive models (NOVA, Pyramid Flow, SkyReels-V2, MAGI-1), Causal Forcing leads by 47.8% in Dynamic Degree, 56.0% in VisionReward, and 75.0% in Instruction Following over the best of these baselines. In the ablation (Table 2), causal ODE initialization + DMD outperforms Self Forcing's ODE + DMD by +90.0% VisionReward and +183.3% Dynamic Degree under chunk-wise sampling, confirming that the architectural gap is resolved at the ODE stage rather than the DMD stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Self Forcing pipeline (ODE distillation + asymmetric DMD) for distilling bidirectional video diffusion models into few-step autoregressive students, and on the DMD score-distillation framework. Its key departure is a theoretical diagnosis: existing methods violate a necessary 'frame-level injectivity' condition because they distill a causal student from a bidirectional teacher, causing the student to collapse to a conditional expectation rather than recovering the true flow map. The paper also departs from the common assumption that diffusion forcing is preferable to teacher forcing for AR diffusion training, showing the opposite via both a KL-divergence argument and empirical results, and extends the analysis to a novel causal consistency distillation formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time interactive video generation underpins world models, game simulation, and embodied AI, yet the architectural gap (bidirectional → causal) has been treated as an engineering detail rather than a theoretical constraint; this paper shows it is the dominant source of quality loss and provides a principled fix. The frame-level injectivity criterion offers a general diagnostic for any ODE/consistency distillation where the student's conditioning is narrower than the teacher's, potentially applicable beyond video. Open questions include whether causal CD can fully replace causal ODE distillation as a DMD initializer, whether the teacher-forcing-over-diffusion-forcing finding holds at longer horizons or with more recent AR training strategies, and whether the approach scales to larger models or higher resolutions.
