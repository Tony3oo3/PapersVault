## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models, despite their efficient next-scale prediction paradigm, still incur heavy computation and long runtime at large-scale steps. Existing acceleration methods (FastVAR, SkipVAR, SparseVAR) rely on manual heuristics for step selection and overlook the varying importance of different generation stages. No prior work has systematically analyzed how semantics and structures emerge across VAR scale steps to guide stage-aware acceleration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262589/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image, operating as a plug-and-play modification to pretrained VAR backbones (Infinity-2B, Infinity-8B, STAR-1.7B) without any retraining. It intervenes at the block level within the VAR transformer (e.g., 8 blocks in Infinity, 30 in STAR), modifying only the intermediate feature representations during the fidelity refinement stage while preserving the original inference pipeline for earlier stages. The method also supports diverse aspect ratios beyond the default square format.

## Method

FasterVAR identifies three distinct stages in VAR inference—semantic establishment, structure establishment, and fidelity refinement—through systematic analysis using CLIP, DINO, LPIPS, DISTS, and frequency-domain metrics. In the fidelity refinement stage (late scale steps), it exploits two key properties: (1) semantic irrelevance, where classifier-free guidance (CFG) can be set to zero (null prompt only), eliminating redundant text-conditioning computations for ~1.5× speedup; and (2) low-rank feature structure, where intermediate features can be projected into a reduced r-dimensional space via random projection (RP) instead of expensive SVD. To recover full-dimensional output from the reduced representation, a Representative Token Restoration (RTR) strategy samples r important token indices from the input feature and fills remaining positions with upsampled cached outputs from the previous scale, avoiding costly least-squares restoration. A predetermined rank strategy (statistically calibrated offline) eliminates the need for per-inference SVD, yielding a total 3.4× speedup with only 0.01 GenEval and 0.26 DPG degradation.

## Results

On the Infinity-2B model, FasterVAR achieves a 3.4× speedup (0.64s vs. 2.2s latency) with only a 0.01 drop in GenEval Overall (0.72 vs. 0.73) and a 0.26 drop in DPG Overall (82.86 vs. 83.12). On Infinity-8B, the method yields a 2.7× speedup (1.77s vs. 4.80s) with a 0.01 GenEval drop (0.78 vs. 0.79) and 0.55 DPG drop (86.05 vs. 86.60). On COCO 2014, FID increases only from 26.64 to 26.91 and IS drops from 42.61 to 42.18 at the 3.4× speedup setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FasterVAR outperforms both existing VAR acceleration baselines in speed: it achieves 3.4× speedup versus FastVAR's 2.75× and SkipVAR's 2.62× on Infinity-2B, while maintaining comparable GenEval (0.72 for all three) and DPG (82.86 vs. 82.86 for FastVAR, 83.16 for SkipVAR) scores. Against the unaccelerated Infinity-2B baseline, FasterVAR reduces latency from 2.2s to 0.64s at the cost of only 0.01 GenEval and 0.26 DPG. On Infinity-8B, FasterVAR delivers a 2.7× speedup (4.80s → 1.77s) with a 0.01 GenEval drop, whereas no prior acceleration method was evaluated at this scale. On the STAR model, FasterVAR achieves a 1.74× speedup while perfectly preserving the GenEval score (0.51). In the ablation (Table 6), combining semantic irrelevance (CFG=0) with the low-rank strategy (RP+RTR) yields the best speedup (3.4×), outperforming the combination of CFG=0 with FastVAR's token pruning (3.14×, GenEval 0.711).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VAR next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., 2024) and borrows the cached-token-pruning mechanism from FastVAR (Guo et al., 2025) for its Representative Token Restoration strategy. It departs from prior VAR acceleration methods (FastVAR, SkipVAR, SparseVAR) that apply token reduction or step skipping at manually chosen large-scale steps, instead introducing a systematic three-stage decomposition (semantic establishment, structure establishment, fidelity refinement) that automatically identifies where acceleration is safe. The low-rank feature compression draws on classical random projection and SVD literature, while the semantic irrelevance observation (dropping text conditioning in later stages) is a new empirical finding not previously exploited in VAR inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.4× speedup with negligible quality loss (0.01 GenEval drop) makes high-resolution VAR generation practically deployable, and the stage-aware design principle—preserve early steps, compress late steps—offers a general template applicable to other coarse-to-fine generative models. Limitations include model-specific stage boundaries (scales {1–32} vs. {40–64} for Infinity), the need for offline statistical calibration of rank values per architecture, and evaluation limited to two VAR backbones (Infinity, STAR). Open questions include whether the three-stage decomposition and low-rank property generalize to video VAR, larger resolutions, or fundamentally different tokenization schemes.
