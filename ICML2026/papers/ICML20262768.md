## Problem

Policy optimization in Bayesian offline RL requires solving composite objectives with expectations over continuous model posteriors, which are computationally intractable. Prior approaches either rely on search-based solvers (MILP, MCTS) that fail to scale to high-dimensional tasks, or impose restrictive posterior assumptions (Dirac point-mass, fixed unimodal distributions) that sacrifice the adaptability and continuous pessimism modulation inherent to Bayesian RL. The paper asks whether a computationally tractable offline policy optimization algorithm can operate under general Bayesian beliefs without such restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples and a pre-trained pool of M Gaussian dynamics models (M ≫ N) over next states and rewards. At each iteration, N models are i.i.d.-sampled from this pool to form an ensemble, from which a pessimistic subset of k models is selected by their Q-value rankings. The output is a neural-network-parameterized policy π(a|s) and Q-function, with the policy update implemented via a mirror-descent step on a composite Riemannian manifold (mixing Euclidean and information-geometric structure) rather than standard gradient descent.

## Method

PhyB reformulates the intractable posterior expectation as a convex combination over a pessimistic subset of k models, with weights α_i ∝ exp(−q_i/λ) obtained from an entropy-regularized optimization. The resulting likelihood ratio is a mixture of Beta distributions (Proposition 1), yielding a multimodal posterior that enables continuous, data-driven modulation of pessimism within a closed interval. Policy optimization proceeds via Bregman-regularized policy iteration: the objective is decomposed into subproblems regularized by a composite potential ψ(x) = (1−ω)/2‖x‖² + ω Σxᵢlogxᵢ, and the optimal policy admits a closed-form mirror-map solution π* = (∇ψ)⁻¹((1/β)(Q*−z) + ∇ψ(μ)). Theoretical analysis provides a bounded discrepancy between the approximate and true Bellman operators (Theorem 1), a pessimism guarantee showing η(π) lower-bounds true returns (Theorem 2), monotonicity of pessimism in ensemble size N and subset size k (Theorem 3), and metric-agnostic monotonic policy improvement until convergence (Theorem 5).

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (18 datasets across 6 data qualities × 3 MuJoCo environments), PhyB achieves an average normalized score of 91.0, outperforming the strongest baselines PMDB (88.2), EPQ (85.4), and DMG (81.5). PhyB wins on 12 of 18 datasets and remains competitive on the remaining 6. On the stochastic offline optimal liquidation task, PhyB scores 101.6±3.7, surpassing TD3+BC (100.4±3.6) and RAMBO (99.6±0.6), while several baselines (ORAAC, IQL) completely fail with a score of 0.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyB outperforms all 13 baselines on average across both benchmarks. On D4RL, the closest competitor is PMDB (Guo et al., 2022) at 88.2 vs. PhyB's 91.0; PhyB leads by 2.8 points on average and wins 12 of 18 individual datasets, with the largest margins on Expert Hopper (+7.1 over PMDB) and Full-Replay HalfCheetah (+8.1 over PMDB). Among model-free baselines, EPQ (Lin et al., 2024) is the strongest at 85.4, while FQL (Park et al., 2025) and CQL (Kumar et al., 2020) trail significantly at 36.9 and 73.7 respectively. Model-based baselines MOReL (Kidambi et al., 2020) and RAMBO (Rigter et al., 2022) score 65.1 and 68.0, showing that PhyB's Bayesian posterior formulation provides a substantial advantage over point-estimate or adversarial model-based approaches. On the stochastic liquidation task, PhyB (101.6) narrowly beats TD3+BC (100.4) and RAMBO (99.6), while ORAAC and IQL completely fail (0.0), and MOPO/COMBO achieve only 64.7/55.0, highlighting PhyB's robustness under high stochasticity with random-policy data.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyB extends the Bayesian RL framework (Ghavamzadeh et al., 2015; Rigter et al., 2021, 2022) by replacing the intractable expectation over a continuous posterior with a convex combination over a pessimistic subset of a finite model ensemble, drawing on the L-statistic/order-statistic machinery. It departs from robust MDP formulations (RAMBO, Dong et al., 2024) that implicitly assume a Dirac likelihood concentrating mass on the worst-case model, and from quantile-based methods (PMDB, Guo et al., 2022) that restrict pessimism to discrete quantile anchors, by constructing a hybrid multimodal posterior that allows continuous, data-driven modulation of conservatism. The policy optimization step builds on Bregman-regularized policy iteration (Huang et al., 2022) and natural gradient methods (Kakade, 2001), adapted to the non-Euclidean geometry induced by the multimodal belief.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhyB provides the first computationally tractable Bayesian RL policy optimization with provable monotonic improvement and bounded approximation error, without imposing restrictive posterior assumptions—a gap that has limited the practical adoption of Bayesian RL in offline settings. The unified treatment of sample-level and model-level epistemic uncertainty, together with the implicit CVaR interpretation of the pessimistic subset selection, offers a principled alternative to heuristic ensemble-penalty methods (MOPO, COMBO) whose performance is sensitive to manually tuned coefficients. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional continuous control (training cost scales linearly with ensemble size N), the practical tightness of the isotonicity condition λ ≥ Δ_max, and whether the multimodal posterior geometry provides advantages beyond what a well-tuned unimodal approximation could achieve.
