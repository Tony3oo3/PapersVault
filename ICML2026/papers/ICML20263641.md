## Problem

Bayesian experimental design (BED) for partially observable dynamical systems faces a dual intractability: likelihoods require marginalisation over latent states (making them unavailable in closed form), and sequential online inference demands constant per-step cost without reprocessing the full history. Existing BED methods either assume tractable likelihoods, rely on offline-trained amortised surrogates, or assume full state observability, leaving no principled approach that handles both partial observability and fully online sequential design with convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the model structure (transition density f and observation density g, both differentiable in the design), prior distributions over parameters and initial states, particle budgets (M outer parameter particles, N inner state particles per parameter), and optimisation hyperparameters (K gradient steps with step sizes η_k). At each time step it produces a single optimised design ξ_t* from a continuous design space Ω ⊂ ℝ^{d_ξ}, along with updated posterior approximations of the joint parameter-state distribution. A key structural constraint is that the per-step computational cost is O((KL+1)MN) with constant memory in the horizon length T, since only the current M×N particle set is retained rather than full trajectories of size M×N×T.

## Method

The core contribution is a set of nested Monte Carlo (NMC) estimators for the expected information gain (EIG) and its gradient, specifically derived for state-space models where both the likelihood L_{θ,ξ_t}(y_t) and the evidence Z_{ξ_t}(y_t) require integration over latent-state trajectories. Using Fisher's identity, the EIG gradient is decomposed into (i) log-derivative terms of L and Z and (ii) a design score involving ∇_{ξ_t} log f + ∇_{ξ_t} log g, all under a single joint expectation Γ. These estimators are instantiated using nested particle filters (NPFs): the outer layer maintains M parameter particles with jittering-kernel rejuvenation, while each inner layer runs N state particles propagated one step forward. The critical mechanism is that NPFs' jittering kernel enables purely online single-step updates (O(t) total cost) rather than the O(t²) cost of reprocessing full trajectories, and the NMC structure draws L pseudo-observations from the joint Γ, with inner expectations for L and Z computed by fixing or jittering parameter particles respectively. Asymptotic consistency of the EIG estimator is proven by combining NPF convergence (Crisan & Miguez, 2018) with NMC consistency (Rainforth et al., 2018) under standard regularity assumptions on the jittering kernel, Lipschitz state posteriors, and bounded positive likelihoods.

## Results

The primary metric is Total Expected Information Gain (TEIG), accumulated over the experiment horizon. On the two-group SIR model (T=200), BAD-PODS achieves 5.918 TEIG, reaching ~98% of the oracle grid-search upper bound (6.043), while outperforming random designs (5.621) by 0.297 and static BED (3.547 at T=150, infeasible beyond) by a wide margin. On the moving-source model (T=50), BAD-PODS scores 1.733 vs. oracle 1.805 and random 1.435. On the ecological growth model (T=20), BAD-PODS achieves 2.545 vs. oracle 2.621 and random 2.344. Runtime on the SIR experiment (T=150) is 12.39 min for BAD-PODS vs. 31.05 min for static BED (~2.5× speed-up), with memory reduced from ~50 GB (static) to ~11 GB (BAD-PODS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BAD-PODS against three baselines: (1) an oracle grid-search upper bound, (2) random uniform designs, and (3) static (non-adaptive) BED that optimises the full design sequence offline. BAD-PODS consistently achieves TEIG within ~2% of the oracle across all three experimental models (SIR, moving source, ecological growth), confirming that stochastic gradient ascent recovers near-optimal designs without exhaustive search. Against random designs, BAD-PODS gains widen monotonically with horizon: on the SIR model the TEIG gap grows from 0.026 at t=50 to 0.297 at t=200, reflecting compounding adaptive benefits. Against static BED, BAD-PODS shows the largest relative advantage (e.g., 4.748 vs. 3.547 TEIG at t=150 on SIR), and the static baseline becomes computationally infeasible beyond moderate horizons due to O(M×N×T) memory for full design tensors. In the moving-source experiment, BAD-PODS also achieves substantially lower sensor pointing errors than both random and static baselines, demonstrating practical quality of the selected designs. No existing method targets the partially observable sequential setting, so no direct method-to-method comparison is possible; the baselines serve as standard references.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established but previously separate threads: nested particle filters (NPFs, Crisan & Miguez 2018) for online joint state-parameter inference, and nested Monte Carlo (NMC) estimators (Rainforth et al. 2018) for expected information gain. It departs from implicit-likelihood BED methods (Kleinegesse & Gutmann 2020; Ivanova et al. 2021) that rely on offline-trained amortised surrogates, and from full-observability dynamical-system BED (Iqbal et al. 2024a,b) that assumes the state is directly observed. The key conceptual move is deriving EIG and gradient estimators that explicitly marginalise latent states via the NPF's one-step predictive propagation, avoiding the quadratic replay cost of SMC²-based alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method fills a concrete gap: no prior approach handles both partial observability and fully online, non-amortised sequential design in SSMs with linear per-step cost. This is practically important for long-horizon adaptive experiments (clinical trials, sensor networks, ecological monitoring) where reprocessing full histories is infeasible. Key limitations include the need for large particle budgets (M, N) and careful jittering tuning, which constrain scalability to high-dimensional state or parameter spaces. Open directions include hybrid amortised-online variants, handling time-varying models or objectives, and extending beyond the regularity assumptions (bounded positive likelihoods, Lipschitz state posteriors) that underpin the consistency guarantee.
