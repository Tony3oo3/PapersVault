## Problem

DP-SGD's fixed clipping threshold C is highly sensitive to its value and misaligns with non-stationary gradient norm distributions during training, yet existing adaptive clipping methods either introduce additional private queries (incurring extra privacy cost) or modify the gradient update procedure (adding hyperparameters or requiring pretraining). The paper asks whether adaptive clipping can be achieved entirely within the vanilla DP-SGD Gaussian release, without any additional private query or gradient transformation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, SlaClip consumes the same per-sample gradients, minibatch, clipping threshold C_t, and noise multiplier σ as vanilla DP-SGD. The output is a single (d+K)-dimensional Gaussian release whose first d coordinates are identical to the vanilla DP-SGD noisy gradient (used for the model update), while the last K coordinates, after normalization by λ = C_t/√K, constitute a noisy binned CDF estimate of gradient norms on [0, C_t]. The only structural change to the pipeline is the dimensionality of the released vector; the sensitivity bound, noise scale, and privacy accounting remain unchanged.

## Method

SlaClip encodes the per-sample clipping slack (C_t − ‖g_{t,i}‖)_+ into K additional coordinates appended to each clipped gradient, with each coordinate capped at λ = C_t/√K so that the extended per-sample norm remains ≤ C_t (Lemma 3.2). The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that this sensitivity-preserving extension leaves the Gaussian mechanism's RDP bound unchanged, so the entire extended vector is released through the same single Gaussian mechanism with the same σ and privacy accountant. After normalization, the K slack coordinates form a binned CDF estimate of gradient norms: the first coordinate ŝ_{t,1} approximates the unclipped fraction near C_t, while the last coordinate ŝ_{t,K} captures the mass of small-norm gradients near zero. The threshold is updated as C_{t+1} = C_t · exp(η(γ_t − ŝ_{t,1})), where the dynamic target γ_t is derived from ŝ_{t,K}/C_t to down-weight the influence of noise-dominated small gradients. The number of extra coordinates K is determined by a closed-form upper bound K ≤ (B/(2·z_{0.995}·σ))^{2/3} that prevents Gaussian noise from violating the expected CDF monotonicity, eliminating the need for additional hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

Under a fairly tuned protocol (shared hyperparameter pool, validation-selected configuration, 3-seed retraining), SlaClip achieves the best or second-best private test accuracy in 14 out of 15 dataset–privacy-budget settings across MNIST, F-MNIST, CIFAR-10, IMDB, and Names. Headline numbers include 97.48% on MNIST at ε=3 (vs. 97.33% Adap-Clip), 87.24% on F-MNIST at ε=3 (vs. 87.12% Vanilla-Clip), 69.48% on CIFAR-10 at ε=8 (vs. 68.75% Adap-Clip), and 79.59% on IMDB at ε=6 (vs. 79.44% AutoClip). The only clear losses are on CIFAR-10 at ε=4 (60.41% vs. AutoClip's 60.95%) and IMDB at ε=2 (74.64% vs. AutoClip's 75.51%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlaClip outperforms all four baselines in 11 of 15 settings. Against Vanilla-Clip (fixed threshold), SlaClip gains 0.45–1.92 points across datasets, with the largest margin on MNIST at ε=1 (94.15% vs. 92.23%). Against Adap-Clip (additional private query for clipped-fraction estimation), SlaClip wins on MNIST, F-MNIST, CIFAR-10 (ε≥6), IMDB (ε≥4), and Names (ε≥2), while Adap-Clip edges out SlaClip by 0.04 points on Names at ε=1. Against AutoClip (norm-normalized per-example clipping requiring pretraining), SlaClip wins in 9 of 15 settings; AutoClip's two clear wins are on CIFAR-10 at ε=4 (60.95% vs. 60.41%) and IMDB at ε=2 (75.51% vs. 74.64%). Against DC-SGD-E (private norm-distribution estimation), SlaClip dominates in 13 of 15 settings, with DC-SGD-E's only competitive result being MNIST at ε=1 (93.40% vs. 94.15% SlaClip). Notably, SlaClip achieves these gains without any additional private query or gradient transformation, using only the slack information already present in the standard clipping operation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlaClip builds directly on the DP-SGD pipeline (Abadi et al., 2016) and the adaptive-clipping intuition of Adap-Clip (Andrew et al., 2021), which uses a privately estimated clipped fraction to update the threshold. The key departure is conceptual: rather than introducing an additional private query (as Adap-Clip and DC-SGD do) or modifying the gradient update rule (as AutoClip and GeoClip do), SlaClip encodes the norm slack already produced by standard per-sample clipping into extra coordinates of the gradient vector. Theorem 3.1 then guarantees that this extension preserves the original global ℓ₂ sensitivity, so the slack summary is released through the same Gaussian mechanism at zero additional privacy cost—a property neither prior line of work exploits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the extra private query, SlaClip avoids the privacy-budget reallocation or stronger noise that Adap-Clip and DC-SGD require under a fixed target ε, making it a genuinely plug-and-play module for vanilla DP-SGD and its variants. The Slack Indicator also provides a richer signal (a binned CDF estimate) than a single clipped/unclipped fraction, enabling the dynamic target ratio that reduces the influence of small-norm gradients. Limitations include the need for sufficient training steps before the indicator stabilizes (early-checkpoint diagnostics show degraded performance), the additional hyperparameter K (though bounded by a closed-form rule), and the fact that only two of K coordinates are currently exploited. Open directions include leveraging the full CDF profile, per-layer clipping compatibility, and extending the approach to other DP-SGD variants.
