## Problem

Physics-informed losses are increasingly used to train learned compressors for scientific data, but their rate-distortion implications remain poorly understood: at fixed bitrate, these objectives often improve preservation of a target physical observable while degrading standard reconstruction fidelity, yet the geometric mechanism governing this tradeoff was uncharacterised. The paper asks when a codec can preserve physically meaningful quantities under a bitrate constraint and what governs the resulting trade-offs with standard fidelity measures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264518/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes scientific data fields of varying modality—2D velocity fields (512×512, two-channel vector), cosmological simulation slices (256×256 crops from 512³ Nyx volumes, multi-channel scalar), and electron microscopy images (256×256 grayscale crops from MICrONS/minnie65)—and produces a compressed bitstream via a learned entropy model, a reconstructed field, and a per-sample physical alignment score. The physical observable is a derived quantity computed from the input (e.g., vorticity from velocity, baryon fraction from density fields, contour features from EM images), and the alignment diagnostic is computed implicitly through matrix-vector products without forming dense metric matrices.

## Method

The core contribution is a local tangent-space rate-distortion law: at each latent operating point, three positive-semidefinite metrics—the entropy-model curvature H_R, the physics sensitivity metric W_eff, and the signal fidelity metric G_eff—jointly determine an inverse-stiffness allocation rule (Σ*)⁻¹ = H_R + αW_eff + γG_eff for compression noise covariance. A no-free-lunch theorem shows that when the rate-whitened versions of W_eff and G_eff do not commute (i.e., their dominant eigendirections are misaligned), improving observable fidelity at fixed rate necessarily worsens MSE. The paper introduces a practical alignment diagnostic (Align_k) based on dominant eigenspace overlap, computed via randomised range-finding with implicit matvecs, and validates the theory across turbulent CFD, cosmological simulation, and electron microscopy domains using hyperprior and factorised-prior codecs trained with a physics weight β.

## Results

The paper validates its local geometric theory by showing that second-order quadratic surrogates for rate and distortion achieve high R² agreement with actual trained codec behaviour across perturbation magnitudes (Fig. 8). At fixed bitrate (0.85 bps), increasing the physics weight β from 0 to 0.5 improves vorticity fidelity while degrading MSE, confirming the predicted fixed-rate trade-off. The alignment diagnostic Align₁₆ increases systematically with β (from ~0.28 to ~0.55 for CFD enstrophy), and cross-domain results (Table 1) show alignment ranging from 0.09 (Nyx thermodynamic observable) to 0.61 (Nyx baryon fraction), with lower alignment corresponding to sharper physics–MSE tension. The Bjontegaard delta rate gain for the physics-aware model over the MSE-only model is positive, indicating a net rate advantage when observable sensitivity is spectrally concentrated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external compression methods; instead, it compares different physics weights β ∈ [0, 1] of the same learned codec architecture (factorised prior and scale hyperprior) at matched target bitrates. The implicit baseline is β = 0 (MSE-only training). At every tested bitrate, increasing β consistently improves the target observable (vorticity, enstrophy, baryon fraction, etc.) at the cost of increased MSE, confirming the no-free-lunch prediction of Theorem 5.2. The magnitude of the trade-off is governed by the alignment score: observables with higher Align₁₆ (e.g., Nyx baryon fraction at 0.61) exhibit milder MSE degradation, while low-alignment observables (e.g., Nyx thermodynamic proxy at 0.09) show sharper tension. The paper further shows that the rate-whitened alignment diagnostic computed via randomised range-finding (without forming dense matrices) correlates with the observed trade-off strength across all three scientific domains, validating it as a practical, model-agnostic predictor of physics–fidelity tension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical rate-distortion theory, learned image compression (Ballé et al., 2018; Minnen et al., 2018), and the growing body of QoI-preserving scientific compression (Ainsworth et al., 2019; Jiao et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2022). Rather than proposing yet another physics-aware training objective or architecture, it provides a local geometric theory explaining *why* physics-aware objectives induce a fixed-rate tradeoff with standard fidelity. The formulation draws on indirect rate-distortion (Witsenhausen, 1980) and the information bottleneck (Tishby et al., 2000), but departs by developing a sample-local tangent-space characterisation with an explicit inverse-stiffness allocation rule, rather than a global operational rate-distortion function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper gives practitioners a computable diagnostic (the rate-whitened eigenspace overlap score) to predict when physics-aware compression will be rate-efficient versus when it will incur a fixed-rate MSE penalty, which could guide architecture and loss-design choices in scientific data pipelines. Two important limitations are acknowledged: the theory is local (second-order) and does not model systematic reconstruction bias from finite model capacity, and the alignment is described but not enforced—how training objectives or regularisers can actively improve alignment remains open. The framework also raises the question of whether the local geometric picture extends to non-Gaussian noise models or to multi-observable settings where several QoIs must be preserved simultaneously.
