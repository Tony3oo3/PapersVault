## Problem

Flow Map Matching (FMM) enables one- and few-step sampling, but its performance is limited by a training-sampling discrepancy: the model-induced intermediate states deviate from ground-truth bridge states due to velocity estimation errors, and standard average-velocity regression alone fails to enforce semantic consistency at the endpoint. The paper asks how to align FMM training with the actual sampling trajectory so that both transport dynamics and endpoint reconstruction are jointly improved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian noise sample (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a synthetic RGB image at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for ImageNet and LSUN). During training, the positive pair for the contrastive loss is constructed by running the model's own two-step sampling trajectory (t→r→0) from a linear bridge point, while negatives are other data samples in the same batch. At inference time, the original samplers of each baseline are used unchanged, so there is zero additional sampling overhead.

## Method

CFMM adds a sampling-aligned InfoNCE contrastive loss to the standard average-velocity regression objective, motivated by a joint-KL decomposition (Theorem 3.1) that splits the terminal distributional gap into a marginal mismatch over intermediate states and a conditional mismatch in endpoint reconstruction. The average-velocity term (retained from MeanFlow) serves as a surrogate for marginal alignment, while the contrastive term uses a frozen DINOv2 encoder to pull the model-generated endpoint x̂ close to its paired ground-truth x in feature space and push batch negatives apart, acting as a surrogate for endpoint-level conditional consistency. The total loss is L_total = L_average + λ·L_NCE with λ=1.0, and the framework is a training-only plug-in that can fine-tune pre-trained FMMs (MeanFlow, Shortcut, TiM, α-Flow, consistency models) or train them from scratch without modifying the inference procedure.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, CFMM consistently improves FID across all tested FMM baselines with only 30k–50k additional fine-tuning iterations: MeanFlow-XL/2 drops from 3.47 to 2.98 (1-NFE) and 2.46 to 2.05 (4-NFE); α-Flow-XL/2+ drops from 2.58 to 2.48 (1-NFE) and 1.95 to 1.89 (4-NFE); TiM-XL/2 drops from 7.11 to 6.87 (1-NFE) and 3.61 to 3.42 (4-NFE). On unconditional CIFAR-10, MeanFlow+CFMM achieves FID 2.70 at 1-NFE, outperforming iCT (2.83), sCT (2.97), IMM (3.20), and ECT (3.60). On LSUN Bedroom/Cat, fine-tuning consistency models improves FID (e.g., 16.0→14.21 at 1-NFE on Bedroom) while also increasing Precision and Recall. All gains require no changes to the inference-time sampling procedure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFMM is evaluated as a plug-in fine-tuning on five FMM/consistency baselines and as a from-scratch training objective. On ImageNet 256×256, it improves every baseline: Shortcut-XL/2 (FID 10.60→9.74 at 1-NFE), MeanFlow-B/2 (6.04→5.76), MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.47→2.98), TiM-XL/2 (7.11→6.87), and α-Flow-XL/2+ (2.58→2.48), all with 30k–50k extra iterations and no inference overhead. On CIFAR-10 (1-NFE), MeanFlow+CFMM (FID 2.70) beats iCT (2.83), sCT (2.97), IMM (3.20), and ECT (3.60), establishing a new state of the art among one-step generators trained from scratch without EDM pre-conditioning. On LSUN Bedroom and Cat, fine-tuned consistency models improve FID (16.0→14.21 and 20.7→18.67 at 1-NFE) while also raising Precision (0.60→0.63, 0.56→0.58) and Recall (0.17→0.20, 0.23→0.26). The method never degrades any baseline, and gains are consistent across model sizes (B/2 to XL/2+), NFEs (1 to 8), and training budgets (300k to 400k iterations from scratch).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Map Matching (FMM) paradigm, building directly on MeanFlow's average-velocity regression objective and the broader flow-matching framework. Its key departure is a joint-KL decomposition of the terminal distributional gap into marginal and conditional components, which motivates adding a sampling-aligned InfoNCE contrastive loss (using frozen DINOv2 features) alongside the standard velocity regression. Unlike prior FMM work that relies solely on reconstruction-style regression, CFMM explicitly addresses the training-sampling discrepancy by supervising endpoint semantic consistency along the model's own induced trajectory, drawing on the contrastive representation-learning literature (REPA, InfoNCE) in a new generative-training context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFMM provides a practical, zero-inference-overhead plug-in that consistently improves one- and few-step FMM sampling across diverse architectures (Shortcut, MeanFlow, TiM, α-Flow, consistency models) and datasets, which is valuable for latency-critical deployment. However, the theoretical connection between the InfoNCE surrogate and the true model-induced conditional KL is mediated through an auxiliary energy-based model rather than a direct bound, and the reliance on a frozen external encoder (DINOv2) introduces a representational dependency that limits interpretability. Open questions include isolating the contribution of contrastive discrimination from feature-space supervision, tightening the surrogate-to-KL gap, and whether the approach extends to conditional/text-to-image settings without the CFG-specific trajectory construction.
