## Problem

Existing contrastive steering methods for jailbreaking LLMs suffer from two key limitations: (1) the learned steering directions are inherently biased because contrastive prompts introduce coupled concept directions (e.g., ethical-to-unethical) alongside the desired faithful-to-faithless direction, and (2) determining per-layer steering strength requires laborious manual tuning of L continuous parameters, with layer-uniform logit targets causing oversteering in early layers where activation magnitudes are small. The paper also identifies that the widely adopted accuracy-based layer selection is unstable across random splits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 100 pairs of harmful/benign contrastive prompts to extract per-layer hidden states at the token position immediately preceding the first response token, plus an off-the-shelf jailbreaking judge (SRF) that scores generated responses on a 0–1 scale. A bi-threshold (0.05, 0.6) is applied to the judge's scores to produce binary labels, discarding ambiguous samples. The output is a set of per-layer linear probe weights (steering vectors) and per-layer adaptive logit targets (steering strengths), which are applied to all token positions except the last decoder layer during inference to produce steered LLM responses.

## Method

The direction-searching stage reframes probe training as model extraction: starting from initial contrastive activations, the algorithm iteratively (T=20 rounds) steers the LLM with the current probes, generates responses to harmful prompts, annotates the resulting activations with the jailbreaking judge, and retrains the linear probes on the augmented set. This adaptive retraining progressively removes the coupled-direction noise inherent in the original contrastive prompts without requiring additional prompt pairs. For strength tuning, the method replaces layer-uniform logit targets with per-layer targets set to the maximum logit observed among training contrastive activations, which naturally scales with each layer's activation magnitude and eliminates oversteering. Two additional design choices complete the method: discarding the last-layer activation (equivalent to logit bias that induces repetition) and steering all token positions rather than only response tokens, treating the steering vector as a rank-1 LoRA adapter applied throughout the sequence.

## Results

On 12 jailbreak-fortified LLMs (Circuit Breaker, Deep Alignment, RepBend, R2D2, SafeUnlearning, TAR, DeRTA, LAT), the proposed adaptive probe-based steering raises average harmfulness (SRF) from a baseline of ~6% to 0.91, with per-LLM SRF scores ranging from 0.50 (Mistral-CB) to 0.98 (Llama3-LAT, Llama3-TAR). The method achieves SR (rubric judge) scores of at least 0.5 on all 12 LLMs, with most exceeding 0.8. Evaluation uses 200 harmful prompts from StrongReject and HarmBench, scored by three LLM-based judges (SRF, HB, SR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (SCAV+AS+DLA+SAT+AR) dominates all four contrastive-steering baselines on average SRF harmfulness (0.91 vs. 0.19 for the strongest baseline, Angular Steering). RepE and SCAV produce near-zero harmfulness on virtually all 12 fortified LLMs (avg SRF ≈ 0.00–0.01), with SCAV's only exception being Gemma-DA (0.28), which the authors attribute to Gemma's larger activation magnitudes tolerating SCAV's fixed logit target. Refusal Direction (RD-A and RD-C) achieves the second-best average but fails to find any usable steering direction on 3–4 of the 12 LLMs (marked "–"), limiting its practical applicability. Angular Steering is the only baseline that produces non-trivial scores across all LLMs (avg 0.19), yet it still trails the proposed method by a factor of ~5× on average and by up to 10× on individual models (e.g., 0.03 vs. 0.85 on Llama3-RB). The gap is most dramatic on models specifically hardened against activation manipulation (CB, RB, TAR), where baselines score below 0.10 while the proposed method reaches 0.50–0.98.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends probe-based contrastive steering (SCAV, Xu et al. 2024) by reframing direction searching as a model-extraction problem (Tramèr et al., 2016), where steered activations are iteratively annotated by a jailbreaking judge to refine linear probes without additional contrastive prompts. It departs from the RepE/SCAV paradigm in two specific ways: replacing accuracy-based layer selection with activation-magnitude-aware logit targets to avoid oversteering, and applying steering to all token positions by recognizing probe-based steering as a rank-1 LoRA with fixed bias. The foundational linear control assumption and contrastive activation extraction paradigm are inherited directly from RepE (Zou et al., 2023a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By raising harmfulness scores from 6% to 70% on 12 specifically fortified LLMs, the paper provides a stronger red-teaming tool that exposes vulnerabilities in defenses (Circuit Breaker, Deep Alignment, TAR, etc.) that previously claimed near-zero harmfulness. The model-extraction framing offers a principled, tuning-free path to improving steering that does not require more contrastive prompts, which are inherently noisy for jailbreaking. Key open questions include the annotator bottleneck (SRF thresholds significantly affect results per-LLM), whether the linear control assumption generalizes beyond refusal/faithfulness, and whether the approach extends to controlling other personas. The paper also reveals that R2D2's apparent robustness stems from degraded helpfulness rather than genuine safety, an important caveat for defense evaluation.
