## Problem

Flow-based gradual domain adaptation (GDA) methods rely on explicit estimation of the target domain PDF (e.g., via score matching) to drive the gradient flow, but this estimation is ill-posed and degrades downstream performance when inaccurate. While a semi-dual optimal transport reformulation can eliminate the need for PDF estimation, its composite sup-inf structure leads to non-unique optima and unstable adversarial training, making it impractical for reliable intermediate domain generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260679/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source samples and unlabeled target samples, operating on UMAP-reduced embeddings (typically 8-dimensional) extracted from pretrained UDA backbones (e.g., CoVi, FixBi) rather than raw pixels. It produces a sequence of neural transport maps {T_{θ,t}}_{t=0}^{T-1} that progressively push source samples through T-1 synthetic intermediate domains toward the target, along with a classifier trained stagewise along this transport path. The transport maps are applied sequentially: each map T_{θ,t} transforms the current domain samples x_t into the next intermediate domain x_{t+1}.

## Method

E-SUOT reformulates the JKO discretization of the f-divergence gradient flow as a semi-dual unbalanced optimal transport problem where both the source and target distributions enter only through sample expectations, eliminating explicit target PDF estimation. The key stability fix is an entropy regularization term with reference measure κ(x_t,x)=p(x_t)p_T(x) (an independent endpoint reference, distinct from the Brownian kernel in Schrödinger bridges), which converts the unstable sup-inf adversarial game into a sequential two-stage optimization: first minimize a single-potential objective to learn w_φ (guaranteeing a unique optimum), then conditionally optimize the transport map T_θ via a simple per-sample loss. Training proceeds stagewise over T steps, with each step producing one transport map that advances the sample cloud one increment toward the target. The framework provides theoretical guarantees including convergence of the transported density to the target (Proposition 3.5) and a generalization error bound for the final classifier (Proposition 3.6).

## Results

On the GDA task, E-SUOT achieves 86.4% accuracy on Portraits, 72.1% on MNIST 45°, and 51.0% on MNIST 60°, outperforming the best baselines (STDW at 84.3%, GOAT at 65.0%, and STDW at 43.9% respectively) by 2.1, 7.1, and 7.1 percentage points. On the UDA task (Office-Home), E-SUOT attains the best average accuracy of 73.5%, surpassing the strongest baseline CoVi (73.1%) and GDA methods like STDW and GGF (both 72.9%). The method also shows consistent gains when plugged into different UDA backbones, with average improvements of 8.1% over MSTN and 0.5% over CoVi.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-SUOT consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across both GDA and UDA settings. On the GDA task, it beats the strongest GDA baselines—STDW (84.3% on Portraits, 43.9% on MNIST 60°) and GOAT (65.0% on MNIST 45°)—by 2.1, 7.1, and 7.1 points respectively, with the largest margin on MNIST 60° where E-SUOT reaches 51.0% versus 43.9%. Flow-based baselines CNF and GGF occasionally underperform the source-only baseline (e.g., CNF drops to 57.6% on MNIST 45° vs. 58.4% source), which the authors attribute to inaccurate target PDF estimation. On Office-Home UDA, E-SUOT (73.5% avg) edges out CoVi (73.1%), CST (72.9%), GGF (72.9%), and STDW (72.9%), while also surpassing classical UDA methods like DANN (57.6%) and MSTN (65.7%) by substantial margins. The ablation further confirms that removing entropy regularization (adversarial training) degrades performance to 74.8% on Portraits (−11.6 points), validating the stability contribution of the E-SUOT formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) the semi-dual formulation of unbalanced optimal transport for generative modeling (Choi et al., 2023; Korotin et al., 2023), and (2) flow-based gradual domain adaptation via gradient flows (Zhuang et al., 2024; Sagawa & Hino, 2025). The key departure is recasting the JKO-scheme-based gradient flow (Jordan et al., 1998) as a semi-dual UOT problem, thereby eliminating the explicit target-domain PDF estimation that prior flow-based GDA methods (e.g., GGF) require. The entropy regularization step distinguishes E-SUOT from both the adversarial semi-dual training of Choi et al. and the Schrödinger-bridge-based entropic UOT of Choi & Choi (2024), using an independent-endpoint reference measure κ(x_t, x) = p(x_t)p_T(x) rather than a Brownian transition kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for target PDF estimation, E-SUOT addresses a practical bottleneck in flow-based GDA where inaccurate score/density estimates degrade intermediate domain quality and downstream accuracy. The theoretical contribution—showing the unregularized semi-dual objective admits non-unique optima (Proposition 3.2) and that entropy regularization restores uniqueness (Proposition 3.4)—provides a principled justification for the sequential (non-adversarial) training procedure. Open questions include whether the framework extends to joint distribution alignment with pseudo-labels, whether alternative geometries (Fisher-Rao, Bures-Wasserstein) yield better intermediate paths, and whether the multi-network offline pipeline can be made more parameter-efficient for large-scale deployment.
