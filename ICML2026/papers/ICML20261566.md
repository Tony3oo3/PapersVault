## Problem

Existing audio deepfake detectors suffer from spectral bias: neural networks overfit low-frequency semantic structure while under-exploiting the subtle high-frequency artifacts left by generative models. Prior frequency-aware or dual-stream methods treat HF cues as isolated auxiliary features or fuse them only at the output, never modeling the joint low-high frequency consistency that genuine speech exhibits and that deepfakes systematically disrupt. This alignment gap causes poor generalization to unseen spoofing attacks and codecs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw 4-second audio waveform at 16 kHz as input, which is simultaneously fed into two parallel branches: the full signal for content extraction and a residual signal produced by a bank of constrained SRM highpass filters for noise extraction. The output is a 2-element real/fake score vector produced by an AASIST graph-attention classifier operating on the cross-attention-fused embedding (1024-dim, 8 heads). Training uses RawBoost augmentation on 4-second clips with weighted cross-entropy to handle class imbalance.

## Method

SONAR employs a dual-path architecture where a Wav2Vec2.0 XLSR encoder extracts low-frequency content embeddings, while a parallel branch first applies M learnable length-5 SRM filters (hard-constrained to zero-sum with center coefficient −1 via post-step projection) to distill high-frequency residuals, then encodes them with a separate XLSR encoder. The two embeddings are fused via cross-attention and classified by AASIST. The key training innovation is a Jensen-Shannon divergence alignment loss applied frame-wise: for genuine audio it minimizes the JS distance between content and noise distributions (enforcing LF-HF consistency), while for spoofed audio it maximizes it (amplifying LF-HF inconsistency). This label-conditioned distributional objective, combined with weighted cross-entropy, directly counteracts spectral bias by making HF residuals a supervisory signal rather than a nuisance, yielding SOTA EERs on ASVspoof 2021 and In-the-Wild benchmarks with convergence in as few as 12 epochs.

## Results

SONAR achieves state-of-the-art Equal Error Rate (EER) under a strict single-run protocol: SONAR-Full reaches 1.57% on ASVspoof 2021 DF, 1.55% on LA, and 6.00% on In-the-Wild, while SONAR-Finetune further improves to 1.45% (DF), 1.20% (LA), and 5.43% (ITW). SONAR-Full converges in 12 epochs versus 100 epochs for the XLSR+AASIST baseline, and SONAR-Finetune converges in as few as 4–6 epochs. Statistical significance is confirmed on ITW (t=19.4, p=0.0026 vs. AASIST; t=4.73, p=0.0419 vs. XLSR-Mamba).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONAR-Full outperforms all seven baselines on the DF partition (1.57% vs. best prior 1.88% by XLSR-Mamba) and the In-the-Wild partition (6.00% vs. 6.71% by XLSR-Mamba), with the largest margin over XLSR+AASIST (10.46% → 6.00%, a 4.46-point drop). On LA, XLSR-Mamba (0.93%) and XLSR+Conformer (0.97%) remain slightly ahead of SONAR-Full (1.55%), but the authors attribute this to their stricter single-run protocol (no checkpoint averaging), and SONAR-Finetune narrows the gap to 1.20%. Against XLSR+Conformer+TCM (7.79% ITW) and XLSR-SLS (7.46% ITW), SONAR-Full gains 1.79 and 1.46 points respectively. SONAR-Finetune, which injects the NFE module into the pre-trained XLSR-Mamba pipeline, achieves the best overall EER across all three partitions (1.20/1.45/5.43), demonstrating that the frequency-guided alignment is architecture-agnostic and can be grafted onto existing strong backbones with minimal additional training (4–6 epochs).

## Relation to Prior Work

SONAR sits at the intersection of two lineages: (1) SRM-based residual extraction from image forensics (Fridrich & Kodovsky, 2012; Bayar & Stamm, 2016), which uses zero-sum kernels to isolate high-frequency prediction errors, and (2) self-supervised speech encoders (XLSR/Wav2Vec2.0) paired with task-specific heads (AASIST, Conformer, Mamba) that have dominated recent ASVspoof benchmarks. The key departure from prior two-stream detectors (Han et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2024) is that SONAR does not merely concatenate or late-fuse content and noise features; instead it enforces a label-conditioned Jensen-Shannon divergence between their latent distributions, explicitly modeling the LF-HF co-modulation that genuine speech exhibits and that deepfakes disrupt. The XLSR-Mamba pipeline (Xiao & Das, 2024) serves as the strongest single-encoder baseline and as the host architecture for the SONAR-Finetune variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying spectral bias as a concrete mechanism behind poor OOD generalization in audio deepfake detectors and providing a data-driven remedy (learnable SRM filters + JS alignment), the paper offers a principled alternative to the prevailing 'bigger encoder + more augmentation' recipe. The bandwidth-sensitivity analysis is a double-edged finding: it confirms the model genuinely exploits HF synthesis artifacts, but it also means performance degrades monotonically below ~16 kHz, limiting deployment in narrowband telephony or heavily compressed pipelines. Open questions include whether the frequency-guided alignment principle transfers to image/video forgery detection, whether the dual-encoder cost can be reduced via parameter sharing, and how the detector fares against next-generation neural vocoders that may produce different HF signatures.
