## Problem

Existing reward modeling for RLHF relies on costly explicit feedback (ratings, pairwise comparisons), while implicit feedback (clicks, copies, skips) is scalable but poses two unresolved challenges: it lacks definitive negative samples (absence of feedback does not imply negative preference), and it suffers from selection bias where different responses have heterogeneous propensities to elicit feedback, invalidating standard PU learning assumptions of uniform positive probability among unlabeled samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264859/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt-response pair as input, which is concatenated, tokenized, and encoded by a frozen pretrained LLM backbone (default: FsfairX-LLaMA3-RM-v0.1); the hidden state of the final token serves as the sample embedding. Two MLP heads (hidden dims 256 and 64, single-dimensional output) on top of this frozen backbone produce the scalar reward score (preference estimate) and the action propensity score, respectively. Only the MLP parameters are updated during training.

## Method

ImplicitRM stratifies training samples into four latent groups—positive-active (PA), negative-active (NA), positive-passive (PP), and negative-passive (NP)—using a probabilistic stratification model that estimates group membership posteriors P(Y*, T | Y, X) from the observed feedback and the current preference/propensity estimates. It then derives a learning objective by maximizing the ELBO of the log-likelihood over the observed implicit feedback, which decomposes into a preference loss L_pred and a propensity loss L_prop, both weighted by the stratification probabilities. A stop-gradient operation is applied to the group membership probabilities to stabilize training. Theoretically, when the stratification probabilities equal the true posteriors, L_pred is an unbiased estimator of the ideal cross-entropy loss over definitive positive and negative samples, simultaneously addressing both the false-negative and selection-bias challenges. The framework is model-agnostic, requiring only a frozen LLM backbone and trainable MLP heads.

## Results

On the three implicit feedback datasets, ImplicitRM achieves R² of 0.3114 (HelpSteer), 0.5207 (UltraFeedback), and 0.7872 (PKU-SafeRLHF), outperforming the best baseline SelectMix (0.2790, 0.4752, 0.7346) by 11.6%, 9.6%, and 7.2% respectively. It also attains the lowest RMSE across all datasets (0.3857, 0.3169, 0.2294). In downstream RLHF with GRPO, ImplicitRM-trained reward models yield the highest safety scores: 0.9258 on HarmBench and 0.8827 on WildGuardMix with Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B, surpassing the best baseline (SelectMix) by 2.5% and 5.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImplicitRM outperforms all 13 baselines across all three datasets and all three metrics (R², MAE, RMSE). The strongest competitor is SelectMix (a denoised label-learning method), which ImplicitRM surpasses by 11.6% R² on HelpSteer (0.3114 vs 0.2790), 9.6% on UltraFeedback (0.5207 vs 0.4752), and 7.2% on PKU-SafeRLHF (0.7872 vs 0.7346). Among PU learning baselines, NNPU and LaGAM are the closest, but both assume uniform positive probability among unlabeled samples—a violation under selection bias—limiting their R² to at most 0.7540 (LaGAM on PKU-SafeRLHF) versus ImplicitRM's 0.7872. Debiased learning methods (IPS, DR, SDR, MultiIPS) are substantially weaker on HelpSteer and UltraFeedback (R² ≤ 0.3646) because they are designed for explicit feedback and must treat unlabeled samples as negatives, introducing false negatives. The Naive baseline (standard BCE treating unlabeled as negative) is the weakest overall, with R² as low as 0.1179 on HelpSteer, confirming that both challenges (missing negatives and selection bias) must be jointly addressed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: positive-unlabeled (PU) learning, propensity-score-based debiased estimation, and RLHF reward modeling. It departs from standard PU learning (du Plessis et al., Kiryo et al., Long et al.) by rejecting the uniform-positivity assumption that breaks under selection bias, and from debiased learning methods (IPS, DR, SDR) by operating without explicit negative labels. The stratification mechanism is directly inspired by principal stratification in recommendation (Lin et al., 2024), repurposed here to decompose implicit feedback into four latent groups, and the ELBO-based objective draws on variational inference to make the resulting likelihood tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Implicit feedback (clicks, copies, skips) is the only scalable signal for reward modeling at production scale, yet no prior method simultaneously handles the absence of definitive negatives and heterogeneous selection bias with a theoretical unbiasedness guarantee. The practical impact is demonstrated through downstream RLHF safety improvements of 8–15% over strong baselines across multiple policy LLMs. Key limitations include the assumption that observed feedback always indicates positive preference (noisy positives like misclicks are unaddressed) and the focus on training objectives rather than specialized architectures; future work on robust losses, MoE-based joint modeling of reward and propensity, and multi-feedback-type settings remain open.
