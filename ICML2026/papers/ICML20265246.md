## Problem

Autoregressive video generation models suffer from prohibitive inference costs due to sequential iterative denoising across many chunks and timesteps. Existing caching accelerations like FlowCache apply coarse-grained chunk-level binary skipping (compute or skip the entire chunk), which ignores fine-grained token-level temporal redundancy and the fact that high-motion pixels require more denoising steps while static pixels tolerate aggressive skipping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265246/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is training-free and operates entirely in the VAE-compressed latent space of autoregressive diffusion models. It takes a text prompt and generates videos at 540p (SkyReels-V2, 2 chunks × 97 frames at 24 FPS) or 720p (MAGI-1, 7 chunks × 24 frames at 24 FPS). The output is a generated video with the same spatial-temporal structure as the vanilla model, but produced with a dynamically reduced number of forward passes per token.

## Method

MotionCache establishes a theoretical bound showing that the caching error at each timestep is strictly proportional to the residual difference between adjacent timesteps (Proposition 4.1), and further proves that this residual instability is upper-bounded by the intra-chunk frame difference (Lemma 4.2), making frame difference a mathematically grounded proxy for token importance. The method employs a dual-stage coarse-to-fine schedule: an initial warm-up phase of K full-chunk computations establishes global structural coherence, followed by a token-wise adaptive phase where each token's update frequency is governed by an importance-weighted accumulator that couples the chunk-level L1 change with per-token motion weights derived from min-max normalized frame differences. Tokens whose accumulator exceeds a threshold are selected for recomputation while others retrieve cached residuals, enabling dynamic resource allocation that prioritizes high-motion regions. This fine-grained token-level policy replaces the all-or-nothing chunk skipping of prior methods, achieving 6.28× and 1.64× speedups on SkyReels-V2 and MAGI-1 with negligible quality loss (VBench drop ≤1%).

## Results

On SkyReels-V2, MotionCache-slow achieves a 6.28× speedup (30 PFLOPs, 245 s latency) with VBench 82.84% and PSNR 23.46, while MotionCache-fast reaches 7.26× (26 PFLOPs, 212 s) with VBench 82.75%. On MAGI-1, MotionCache-slow delivers a 1.64× speedup (100 PFLOPs, 925 s) with VBench 77.25% (nearly matching the vanilla 77.26%), and MotionCache-fast achieves 2.07× (64 PFLOPs, 733 s) with VBench 74.59%. Across both models, MotionCache attains the highest PSNR and SSIM among all accelerated methods, indicating superior structural fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionCache outperforms both TeaCache and FlowCache on every metric across both models. On SkyReels-V2, MotionCache-slow (6.28×, VBench 82.84%, PSNR 23.46) edges out FlowCache-slow (6.26×, 82.70%, 21.83) in both speed and quality, while dramatically surpassing TeaCache-slow (1.89×, 82.67%, 21.96) in speedup; in the fast setting, MotionCache-fast (7.26×, 82.75%) beats FlowCache-fast (7.19×, 82.38%) and TeaCache-fast (2.2×, 80.06%). On MAGI-1, the advantage is more pronounced: MotionCache-slow (1.64×, VBench 77.25%) nearly matches vanilla quality while FlowCache-slow (1.39×, 77.08%) and TeaCache-slow (1.14×, 76.64%) trail behind; in the fast setting, MotionCache-fast (2.07×, 74.59%) substantially outperforms FlowCache-fast (1.94×, 73.42%) and TeaCache-fast (1.41×, 68.81%), with TeaCache-fast suffering a severe 8.45-point VBench drop. MotionCache also achieves the best PSNR and SSIM in all configurations, confirming that its token-wise motion-aware caching preserves fine-grained structural details that coarse-grained chunk-level skipping (FlowCache) and timestep-level skipping (TeaCache) fail to retain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature caching lineage for diffusion-based video generation (TeaCache, AdaCache, TaylorSeer) into the autoregressive regime, where FlowCache (Ma et al., 2026) first introduced chunk-level binary skipping. The key departure is moving from treating an entire autoregressive chunk as an atomic compute-or-skip unit to a token-wise, motion-weighted accumulation policy, grounded in a theoretical bound (Proposition 4.1, Lemma 4.2) linking residual instability to intra-chunk frame differences. The coarse-to-fine dual-stage schedule (structural warm-up followed by sparse token updates) is a novel scheduling mechanism not present in prior caching methods, which typically apply a single uniform policy throughout denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.28× speedup on SkyReels-V2 with near-lossless quality (1% VBench drop) is practically significant for deploying autoregressive video generation, where a 7-second clip currently takes ~27 minutes. The theoretical framing—showing that a simple L1 frame difference is a mathematically grounded proxy for residual instability (NDCG > 0.94)—provides a principled basis for token selection rather than a heuristic. Limitations include evaluation on only two models, a Lipschitz assumption in Lemma 4.2 that is justified informally rather than rigorously, and the method's specificity to autoregressive architectures with KV-cache, leaving open whether the motion-proxy insight transfers to full-sequence DiTs or other generative paradigms.
