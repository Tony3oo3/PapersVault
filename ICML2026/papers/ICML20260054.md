## Problem

In embodied agents that combine a VLM for perception with an LLM for decision-making, the two modules are typically loosely coupled: the VLM produces a generic scene description that often omits task-critical visual details, creating a perception-reasoning-decision gap. Existing interactive approaches that attempt to close this gap rely on predefined oracle questions or naively concatenate QA pairs into long, noisy prompts, limiting their autonomy and downstream decision quality.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives an RGB image observation (H×W pixels) and a textual goal instruction, along with the last 5 interaction history entries (past descriptions and actions). The VLM (Idefics2-8B) processes the image to produce textual descriptions and answers, while the LLM (Mistral-7B or Qwen3-4B) generates questions and synthesizes the final compact scene description. The policy outputs a discrete textual action command (e.g., 'take egg from table') selected from the environment's action vocabulary, with the intermediate refined description serving as the grounded perceptual state for the policy.

## Method

PRISM introduces a Dynamic Question-Answering (DQA) loop that tightly interleaves perception and reasoning: the VLM first produces a goal-agnostic initial description, the LLM critiques it against the goal to generate targeted questions about missing information, the VLM answers each question from the image, and the LLM synthesizes all inputs into a single compact, task-driven description—avoiding both the noise of raw concatenation and the hallucination risk of goal-conditioned prompting. For decision-making, the same LLM backbone is augmented with LoRA adapters (rank 32, alpha 16 on Wq and Wv) trained exclusively for action generation, activated only during the policy phase. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: behavioral cloning on expert trajectories (where visual observations are converted to textual descriptions via the DQA loop once), followed by online PPO reinforcement learning with the DQA perception remaining active throughout. The key architectural insight is that the LLM's summarization capability filters irrelevant details and resolves contradictions in the QA responses, producing a concise representation that is both more accurate than raw VLM output and more compact than concatenated alternatives.

## Results

On ALFWorld, PRISM_BC+RL achieves an average success rate of 80% across six task categories (Pick 80%, Look 83%, Clean 84%, Heat 92%, Cool 71%, Pick2 67%), outperforming all baselines without access to oracle textual supervision by 5–64 points. On R2R, PRISM achieves 46.08% SR, 65.38% OSR, and 43.14% SPL, surpassing all navigation baselines including DiscussNav (43% SR, 40% SPL) and LangNav (34% SR, 29% SPL). The DQA mechanism contributes a consistent 5-point gain over the static VLM+LLM baseline VIPER_BC+RL (75% avg SR), with the largest improvements on Pick2 (+14 pt) and Clean (+7 pt).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among methods without oracle text supervision, PRISM_BC+RL (80% avg SR) decisively outperforms all VLM-only baselines: MiniGPT-4 (16%), RL4VLM (21%), and Idefics2 8B (69%), with gains of 64, 59, and 11 points respectively. Against the closest architectural competitor VIPER (same VLM+LLM split but without DQA), PRISM gains 5 points over VIPER_BC+RL (80% vs 75%), with the largest margin on Pick2 (+14 pt) and Clean (+7 pt). On R2R, PRISM (46.08% SR, 43.14% SPL) beats DiscussNav (43% SR, 40% SPL) by 3.08 pt in SR and 3.14 pt in SPL, and substantially outperforms its own reimplementation DiscussNav* (35.52% SR) by over 10 pt in SR, confirming that the LLM-synthesis merging strategy is the key differentiator over naive QA concatenation. Against oracle-supervised methods, PRISM narrows the gap considerably: it trails EMAC+ (86%) by 6 pt and EMMA (82%) by 2 pt, while actually exceeding DEPS (76%) by 4 pt and ReAct (54%) by 26 pt, despite lacking privileged symbolic state information such as object IDs and occluded-object visibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM extends the VLM+LLM modular architecture established by VIPER (Aissi et al., 2025) and the BC+RL training pipeline from Carta et al. (2023), but introduces a key conceptual departure: a Dynamic Question-Answering (DQA) loop that tightly couples perception and decision-making, replacing the passive 'describe-then-decide' pattern used in VIPER, LangNav, and NavGPT. It also departs from interactive-perception work like DiscussNav (Long et al., 2023) by making question generation fully automatic (no predefined oracles) and by synthesizing QA feedback into a compact description rather than naively concatenating it. The lineage is thus: modular VLM-LLM agents → active, goal-driven perception via LLM-initiated queries → compact semantic merging for downstream policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM demonstrates that the perception-decision gap in embodied agents can be substantially closed through structured VLM-LLM interaction, achieving performance competitive with oracle-text-supervised methods (80% vs. 82–86% on ALFWorld) while operating solely on visual input. The finding that simply injecting the goal into the VLM prompt *degrades* performance (64% vs. 69% raw) is a useful caution against naive goal-conditioning. Open questions include whether the DQA loop scales to continuous control or real-robot settings (the authors note this as future work), whether the 3× per-step latency is acceptable in time-critical deployments, and whether the 2-point gap to oracle questions reflects a fundamental limitation of LLM common-sense reasoning versus environment-specific priors.
