## Problem

Open-vocabulary grounding requires fine-grained vision-language alignment under weak supervision, but existing methods either collapse prompts into single global embeddings that lose internal structure (failing on attribute binding, spatial relations, and negation) or introduce token-level alignment that demands explicit phrase annotations or heavy cross-attention architectures. The paper addresses this by formulating token-level alignment as a principled multiple instance learning problem that enables implicit token and instance selection without additional supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (640×640) and a set of free-form textual prompts, producing prompt-conditioned spatial alignment maps (EAMs) at three FPN scales (P3, P4, P5) that are injected into visual features for downstream bounding-box detection and instance segmentation. Text prompts are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 retaining all token-level representations before the EOT token (rather than a single pooled embedding), and images are processed by a frozen DINOv3 ConvNeXt-T backbone. The EAMs are the key intermediate output: each is a 2D score map per prompt per scale, computed via expectation-based aggregation of per-token similarity maps.

## Method

The core component is the Expectation Alignment Head (EAH), which computes token-wise dot-product similarities between multi-scale visual features and text tokens at every spatial location, derives a token posterior via softmax over spatially-averaged scores (with temperature), and produces the EAM as a weighted sum over tokens—mathematically equivalent to attention-based soft MIL pooling (proven in Appendix A). Two auxiliary training-only losses stabilize alignment: a Top-K multi-positive InfoNCE loss that enforces prompt-level semantic separation using the top 1% highest-response locations from a downsampled unified EAM, and a Geometry-Aware Consistency Objective (GACO) derived from a Lagrangian-constrained free-energy minimization that reshapes the alignment probability distribution within each ground-truth mask using clipped z-score statistics of sigmoid-transformed scores. Training is two-stage with both encoders frozen: 30 epochs of standard YOLOv8 detection/segmentation losses, then 20 epochs adding the auxiliary losses (λ_sem=0.5, λ_geo=1.0). A lightweight SwiGLU FFN (zero-initialized second layer) bridges CLIP token space to the visual feature space, and pseudo-masks from SAM-2.1 provide segmentation supervision where pixel annotations are unavailable.

## Results

On LVIS minival zero-shot detection, ExpAlign (trained on Objects365 + GoldG + RefCOCO) achieves 37.1 AP overall and 36.2 AP_r on rare categories with only 60M parameters, outperforming all baselines at comparable scale. For zero-shot instance segmentation on LVIS val, ExpAlign reaches 29.9 AP^m and 29.0 AP_r^m, surpassing YOLOE-v8-L (23.5 AP^m) by 6.4 points without any LVIS exposure during training. On ODinW, ExpAlign attains 48.0 AP on ODinW13 and 22.6 AP on ODinW35, exceeding WeDetect-T (46.4/21.1) and Grounding DINO-T (49.7/22.3 on ODinW13 only). On RefCOCO referring expression comprehension, ExpAlign trails Grounding DINO-T substantially (51.6 vs. 74.0 on RefCOCO val), attributed to CLIP's limited spatial-reasoning capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpAlign outperforms all detection baselines on LVIS minival rare-category AP (AP_r): 36.2 vs. 33.3 (WeDetect-T), 33.2 (YOLOE-8-L), 27.6 (YOLO-Worldv2-L), 18.1 (GDINO-T), and 17.7 (GLIP-T). On overall LVIS minival AP, ExpAlign (37.1–37.2) slightly trails WeDetect-T (37.4) but exceeds YOLOE-8-L (35.9), YOLO-Worldv2-L (35.4), and GDINO 1.5 Edge (33.5) by 1.2–3.7 points. For zero-shot segmentation on LVIS val, ExpAlign (29.9 AP^m) surpasses YOLOE-v8-L (23.5), YOLOE-11-L (22.6), OpenSeeD (21.0), and YOLO-Worldv2-L (19.8, which was fine-tuned on LVIS-Base) by 6.4–10.1 points. On ODinW13, ExpAlign (48.0) edges out WeDetect-T (46.4) and GDINO-T (49.7 uses a different pre-training corpus including Cap4M). On RefCOCO referring expression comprehension, ExpAlign (51.6/59.3/47.7) loses substantially to GDINO-T with RefC pre-training (74.0/74.9/59.3), a gap the authors attribute to CLIP's weakness in positional and relational language understanding. On COCO downstream fine-tuning (full tuning, 80 epochs), ExpAlign (53.5 AP^b) marginally beats YOLOE-v8-L (53.0) and YOLOv8-L from scratch (52.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

ExpAlign positions itself between CLIP-style global sentence alignment and heavy cross-attention architectures (GLIP, Grounding DINO) by introducing an Expectation Alignment Head that performs token-level vision-language alignment through expectation-based soft pooling, which the authors prove is mathematically equivalent to attention-based MIL pooling (Ilse et al., 2018). Rather than relying on explicit region-word annotations or multi-grained pretraining, the method treats spatial locations as latent instances and text tokens as competing hypotheses, enabling implicit token selection under weak supervision. The energy-based consistency regularization (GACO) draws on variational free-energy minimization to reshape alignment maps via Gibbs reweighting, distinguishing it from prior geometry-aware losses that rely on absolute spatial targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6–10 AP^m gain in zero-shot instance segmentation over YOLOE (29.9 vs. 23.5) is the most practically impactful result, suggesting that training-time consistency regularization can substantially improve mask quality without any LVIS exposure. However, the significant underperformance on referring expression comprehension (51.6 vs. 74.0 for Grounding DINO-T) exposes a fundamental limitation of CLIP's text encoder for spatial-relational language, which remains an open challenge. The requirement that the DINOv3 backbone be frozen (unfrozen training collapses) also constrains the method's flexibility. Future work could explore replacing CLIP with a spatially-aware text encoder or developing adaptive negative vocabulary strategies to further improve long-tail performance.
