## Problem

Existing black-box adversarial audio attacks against ASR systems improve stealthiness primarily by minimizing perturbation magnitude, yet user studies show only 10–46% of participants perceive such samples as benign. The paper argues that human auditory perception is a selective attention process governed by predictive coding and auditory saliency, so a physically small perturbation can still be perceptually salient if it creates acoustic anomalies (transients, spectral peaks) that capture attention. The key gap is the lack of an objective mathematical model for 'attention-insensitive' perturbations and a principled way to select music carriers that mask those perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target command text (synthesized to 16 kHz audio via TTS) and a dataset of 42 music tracks (27 electronic/rhythmic, 15 sparse piano/ambient) as input, and produces a 3-second adversarial audio clip that sounds like the selected music segment to humans but is transcribed as the target command by ASR systems. Optimization operates in the Log Mel spectrogram domain (80 Mel bands, FFT 512, hop 256), with the perturbation Δ added directly to the carrier's STFT magnitude; the final waveform is reconstructed via Griffin-Lim (1000 iterations). For digital API attacks, an additional CMA-ES refinement pass (population 15, σ=0.05, up to 1000 generations) prunes perceptually insignificant perturbation components using ~707 queries per sample; physical over-the-air attacks require zero queries.

## Method

The HWN method has two core components. First, a masking-aligned carrier selection algorithm computes per-band masking threshold curves from candidate music using the MPEG-1 psychoacoustic model (Bark-scale critical bands, 0–3 kHz focus), applies Silero VAD to isolate vocal frames in the target command, and selects the 3-second music segment whose masking curve covers ≥90% of frequency bins across the maximum number of vocal frames. Second, an attention-diluted optimization minimizes a composite loss L = L_acoustics + λ·L_stealth, where L_acoustics is the MAE between the adversarial and target Log Mel spectrograms, and L_stealth is the MSE of the residual R_Δ = Δ − T(Δ) obtained via cascaded multi-scale smoothing (15×15 square + 7×7 vertical kernels) adapted from Structural-Residual Decomposition; this forces the perturbation into a statistically stationary texture that suppresses the transient and spectral-peak components that trigger auditory attention. The hyperparameter λ is tuned via binary search, and the full pipeline (carrier selection + 1500 Adam iterations at lr=4×10⁻²) completes in ~50 minutes.

## Results

In the digital domain (5 commercial ASR APIs, 10 commands), HWN achieves 100% Attack Success Rate with a Normal perception rate of 56.25%, SNR of 21.50 dB, and MOS of 4.06, substantially outperforming KENKU (40.72% Normal, 15.66 dB SNR) and NI-Occam (19.41% Normal, 9.63 dB SNR). In the physical over-the-air domain (Gemini, Alexa, Doubao), HWN maintains 100% SR with 55.57% Normal perception and 16.12 dB SNR, while NI-Occam drops to 77.78% SR and 0% Normal perception. A 200-participant human study confirms that 55.6% of listeners perceived the physical adversarial examples as benign music, compared to only 34.5% for KENKU and 0% for NI-Occam.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KENKU (Wu et al., 2023), HWN achieves a 15.53-point higher Normal rate (56.25% vs. 40.72%) and reduces the Talking rate from 45.20% to 16.07%, while also improving SNR by 5.84 dB (21.50 vs. 15.66) and MOS by 0.35 points; both methods achieve 100% SR. Against NI-Occam (Zheng et al., 2021), the advantage is even more pronounced: HWN's Normal rate is 36.84 points higher (56.25% vs. 19.41%), Talking rate is 37.72 points lower (16.07% vs. 53.79%), and SNR is 11.87 dB higher. In the physical domain, NI-Occam's SR drops to 77.78% with 0% Normal perception, while HWN maintains 100% SR and 55.57% Normal. In a literature-only comparison (no shared experimental setup), HWN's 55.57% Normal rate exceeds ZQ-Attack (Fang et al., 2024) by 42.41 points and EvilHarmony (Yuan et al., 2025) by 4.57 points, though EvilHarmony's 7 provided samples were found to no longer function against current commercial APIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in adversarial audio attacks (KENKU, NI-Occam, Devil's Whisper) that equates stealthiness with minimizing perturbation magnitude, arguing instead that human auditory perception is governed by selective attention rather than physical intensity. It repurposes the Structural-Residual Decomposition from visual saliency detection (Hou & Zhang, 2007) as an attention-dilution loss for audio perturbations, and adapts the MPEG-1 psychoacoustic masking model (Liu & Lee, 1997) into a novel carrier selection algorithm that aligns masking thresholds with target command spectrograms. The method is positioned as a black-box, query-efficient alternative to prior optimization-based attacks, using CMA-ES for final perturbation refinement rather than gradient-based surrogate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physical over-the-air attacks on commercial voice assistants can achieve 100% success rate while remaining perceptually indistinguishable from benign music for over half of listeners, raising urgent security concerns for ubiquitous voice-controlled devices. A notable open question is the weak correlation (Spearman 0.511) between SNR and subjective stealthiness, suggesting that current objective metrics are inadequate for evaluating perceptual imperceptibility. The paper also reveals that longer commands (8+ words) dramatically degrade stealthiness, and that existing defenses (band filtering, audio turbulence) offer limited protection against stealthy samples, pointing to a need for learning-based acoustic forensics and multi-factor authentication in deployed systems.
