## Problem

Existing omni-modal benchmarks for MLLMs focus almost exclusively on retrospective understanding—describing and analyzing events that have already occurred—leaving the ability to forecast future events from joint audio-visual cues largely unexplored. Prior future-prediction benchmarks either rely solely on text (FutureBench, ForecastBench) or vision+language (VLEP, IntentQA), systematically overlooking the auditory modality despite its critical role as a precursor to future events. This paper identifies and quantifies the gap in omni-modal future forecasting capability across current MLLMs.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of a video clip with its native audio track (30 s to 20 min, average 163.5 s) and a multiple-choice question that presents a premise event and asks the model to select the most direct future conclusion from five options. The four distractors are specifically engineered as adversarial types—visual-only perception, audio-only perception, delayed, and reverse-causal—to force genuine cross-modal reasoning rather than single-modality shortcuts. The model outputs a single letter (A–E); the training dataset additionally requires a free-text causal rationale linking the premise to the predicted outcome.

## Method

FutureOmni is built through a scalable LLM-assisted, human-in-the-loop pipeline: (1) audio-coordinated video filtering using CLIP inter-frame similarity and UGCVideoCaptioner-based semantic gap scoring to retain only audio-dependent videos; (2) temporal event localization via Gemini 2.5 Flash with MFCC-based boundary validation; (3) causal pair discovery with DeepSeek V3.2 constrained to ≤30 s temporal gaps, plus audio causal-factor scoring (0–2 scale); and (4) dual-stage verification by GPT-4o and human annotators (Fleiss' κ = 0.73). To improve model performance, the authors curate FutureOmni-7K (7,761 rationale-enhanced samples from disjoint videos) and propose the OFF training strategy: LoRA fine-tuning of the text backbone only (visual and audio encoders frozen, lr = 1e-5, 1 epoch) using exclusively pre-event frames and audio segments, so the model must perform genuine predictive reasoning rather than memorizing future content. Attention-score visualizations on LongVALE keyframes confirm that OFF shifts the model toward active information seeking on critical video and audio frames, explaining the observed out-of-domain generalization gains on WorldSense, DailyOmni, Video-MME, and MLVU.

## Results

On FutureOmni, the best proprietary model Gemini 3 Flash achieves 64.80% average accuracy, while the strongest open-source omni-modal model Qwen3-Omni (30B) reaches only 53.05%, revealing a substantial ~12-point gap. Video-only models consistently underperform omni-modal counterparts, with GPT-4o (49.70%) trailing even the weakest open-source omni model. The proposed OFF training strategy yields consistent gains: Qwen2.5-Omni improves from 47.48% to 48.51%, video-SALMONN 2 from 46.03% to 49.90%, and Ola from 48.54% to 50.19% on FutureOmni, with additional improvements on out-of-domain benchmarks such as WorldSense (+2.55%) and Video-MME (+1.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini 3 Flash (64.80%) leads by a wide margin over Gemini 2.5 Pro (57.93%) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (55.61%), while Claude Haiku 4.5 (52.03%) trails all Gemini variants. In the open-source omni-modal category, Qwen3-Omni (53.05%) outperforms MiniCPM-o 2.6 (49.08%), Ola (48.54%), and Qwen2.5-Omni 7B (47.48%), but still lags behind even the weakest proprietary model (Claude Haiku 4.5 at 52.03%). All video-only models underperform their omni-modal counterparts: GPT-4o (49.70%) is the strongest video-only model yet falls below Qwen3-Omni (53.05%) and MiniCPM-o 2.6 (49.08%), confirming that audio cues are essential for future forecasting. After OFF training, video-SALMONN 2 achieves the largest absolute gain (+3.87% to 49.90%), surpassing its pre-training score and approaching Ola's baseline; Ola improves to 50.19% and Qwen2.5-Omni to 48.51%. On out-of-domain benchmarks, OFF consistently improves general capabilities—e.g., Qwen2.5-Omni gains +2.55% on WorldSense, +3.34% on DailyOmni, and +1.74% on Video-MME—demonstrating that future-forecasting training transfers to broader audio-visual and video-only understanding tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but previously disjoint research threads: omni-modal (audio-visual) understanding benchmarks such as WorldSense and DailyOmni, which focus on retrospective perception, and future-prediction benchmarks such as VLEP, IntentQA, and FutureBench, which are predominantly vision-centric or text-only. By requiring models to forecast future events from joint audio-visual context, FutureOmni extends the future-prediction task into the omni-modal setting for the first time, while its adversarial distractor design (visual-only, audio-only, delayed, reverse-causal) departs from the simpler visual-similarity distractors used in prior prediction benchmarks. The proposed OFF training strategy further builds on standard LoRA-based instruction tuning but introduces a rationale-enhanced, causally-structured objective that explicitly trains predictive reasoning rather than descriptive captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and previously unquantified gap: even the strongest proprietary model (Gemini 3 Flash) achieves only 64.8%, and the error analysis shows that 82.4% of failures stem from perception and joint-reasoning deficits rather than world-knowledge gaps, redirecting the community's attention toward cross-modal integration rather than knowledge injection. The OFF training strategy's ability to improve performance on unrelated benchmarks (Video-MME, MLVU, WorldSense) suggests that future-forecasting supervision may serve as a useful proxy for general temporal-reasoning capability, though the gains remain modest (1–3%). Open questions include whether the 64.8% ceiling reflects a fundamental architectural limitation of current dual-tower omni-modal designs, and whether the 'contextual cold start' phenomenon (shortest videos being hardest) will persist as models gain longer-context capabilities.
