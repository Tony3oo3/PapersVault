## Problem

Existing GCD methods rely on contrastive learning whose uniformity objective enforces global feature dispersion, conflicting with the compact, class-discriminative structure needed for reliable clustering of unlabeled data. Additionally, standard one-hot supervision drives class prototypes toward orthogonality, fragmenting the semantic space and preventing novel classes from inheriting meaningful relationships from known ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260285/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images split into a labeled subset (known classes only) and an unlabeled subset (mixture of known and novel classes), processed by a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone that extracts [CLS] tokens as d-dimensional ℓ₂-normalized features. It outputs a parametric classifier over an expanded prototype set (known + discovered novel classes), yielding class labels for known categories and cluster assignments for novel ones, evaluated via Hungarian-matched ACC and a weighted H-score that accounts for the typical 1:2 old-to-new sample ratio.

## Method

CURE is a two-stage framework that entirely removes CL's uniformity constraint. Stage I fine-tunes the backbone on labeled data using cross-entropy plus a prototype-feature alignment loss (self-to-prototype and class-aware terms in a shared MLP-projected space) and a Semantic Exploration Energy (SEE) regularizer that penalizes pairwise prototype dissimilarity via a soft-ReLU on the squared deviation from perfect alignment, preserving inter-class semantic connectivity. Stage II initializes an expanded prototype set via Ward hierarchical clustering on all features followed by Hungarian matching to known classes, then jointly optimizes prototypes with a JS-divergence view-consistency loss, logit-aware self-distillation with class-frequency bias correction, virtual-sampling entropy regularization (scaling known-class logits by a factor t to rebalance exploration), and a second-stage SEE term that contracts prototype distances to allow unlabeled instances to escape rigid nearest-centroid binding. A Clusters-to-Prototypes (C2P) update every few epochs re-anchors prototypes to current cluster centroids, closing the loop between instance-level structure and prototype-level semantics.

## Results

CURE achieves state-of-the-art overall accuracy across all six main benchmarks: 97.5% on CIFAR-10, 85.7% on CIFAR-100, 87.0% on ImageNet-100, 75.2% on CUB-200, 68.7% on Stanford-Cars, and 48.1% on Herbarium19. A key differentiator is the minimal Old/New accuracy gap (e.g., 0.4% on CIFAR-100, 0.1% on CUB-200, 2.8% on Herbarium19), substantially smaller than all baselines. With a DINOv2 backbone, CURE further improves to 89.1% on CIFAR-100 and 91.2% on ImageNet-100. Training time is competitive: 527s total on ImageNet-100 vs. 1330s for SPTNet and 1817s for PromptCAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE outperforms all seven baselines on overall accuracy across every benchmark. On CIFAR-100, CURE (85.7%) surpasses the strongest competitor DebGCD (83.0%) by 2.7 points, with the gap driven primarily by novel-class accuracy (86.6% vs. 79.9%). On CUB-200, CURE (75.2%) leads SelEx (73.6%) by 1.6 points and DebGCD (66.3%) by 8.9 points, while maintaining a near-zero Old/New gap (0.4%) versus 5.3% for DebGCD. On the challenging Herbarium19 dataset, CURE (48.1%) exceeds DebGCD (44.7%) by 3.4 points and achieves the smallest Old/New gap (7.7%) among all methods. On Aircraft (Appendix Table 8), CURE ties DebGCD at 61.7% overall but achieves the highest Old-class accuracy (69.4% vs. 63.9%), though DebGCD edges ahead on the class-balanced H-score-3 metric (61.7 vs. 61.1). The consistent pattern across all comparisons is that CURE's advantage is largest on novel classes, confirming that eliminating the uniformity constraint and using semantic regularization specifically benefits discovery of unseen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE departs from the dominant contrastive learning (CL) paradigm in Generalized Category Discovery by explicitly removing the uniformity objective that prior methods (SimGCD, SPTNet, DebGCD, ProtoGCD) inherit from InfoNCE. Instead of relying on negative-sample-driven dispersion, it replaces uniformity with a Semantic Exploration Energy regularizer that preserves inter-prototype semantic connectivity, combined with a two-stage design where supervised fine-tuning on labeled data precedes unsupervised prototype expansion. The Clusters-to-Prototypes (C2P) mechanism and JS-divergence-based consistency build on ideas from SimGCD and hierarchical clustering, but are restructured around the principle that prototypes should act as soft semantic attractors rather than orthogonal anchors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in GCD that CL's uniformity is necessary for discriminative representations, showing that semantic structure preservation can substitute for it while yielding more balanced old/new class performance across seven benchmarks. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities or to settings where the number of novel classes is truly unknown (the paper still requires estimating K). The reliance on a DINO-pretrained ViT backbone and the need for a separate supervised pretraining stage also raise practical questions about transferability to resource-constrained or non-ViT architectures.
