## Problem

Despite the proliferation of LLM multi-agent communication protocols (A2A, ACP, ANP, Agora), their trade-offs across task success, latency, overhead, and fault tolerance remain under-characterized, and protocol selection in practice is intuition-driven with no standardized evaluation or selection guidance. Existing multi-agent benchmarks assume a fixed communication mechanism and report only task-level outcomes, leaving the communication layer itself unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260975/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ProtocolBench takes a fixed agent workload and topology (e.g., 8-agent ring for Fail-Storm, 1-coordinator/4-worker star for Streaming Queue) and swaps only the protocol implementation at the communication boundary via thin native adapters, producing per-request latency, byte counts, success/quality scores, and recovery metrics. ProtocolRouter takes a scenario specification (natural language or structured requirements with hard constraints like 'must support E2E encryption') plus optional scenario-agnostic performance priors, and emits a structured plan assigning exactly one protocol per communication module; cross-protocol links are bridged by stateless encode/decode adapters that preserve application semantics without shared session state.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. ProtocolBench is a protocol-agnostic benchmark spanning four scenarios (GAIA document QA, Streaming Queue, Fail-Storm Recovery, Safety Tech) that isolates protocol effects by pinning all non-protocol factors (model, prompts, hardware image, rate limits) and using thin wrappers around each protocol's native SDK rather than a shared abstraction layer, thereby preserving protocol-native retry, reconnect, and streaming behavior. ProtocolRouter is a constraint-aware, schema-driven selector that first filters out protocols violating hard capability/security constraints, then breaks ties by interaction preference (e.g., streaming vs. request/response) and optional performance priors, producing a deterministic per-module assignment; when heterogeneous protocols meet on a link, stateless envelope/field-mapping bridges handle translation without altering business content. ProtocolRouterBench extends the evaluation to 60 scenarios across five difficulty levels (1–5 modules each, 180 modules total) to standardize constrained protocol-selection accuracy, reporting scenario-level exact-match and module-level accuracy under spec-only and spec+perf conditions.

## Results

Across four ProtocolBench scenarios, protocol choice produces large, scenario-dependent differences. In Streaming Queue, overall completion time varies by up to 36.5% across protocols (ACP: 40.28 min vs. Agora: 54.97 min), and mean end-to-end latency spans 9.66 s (ACP) to 13.14 s (Agora). In GAIA, A2A achieves the highest task utility (quality 2.51, success 9.29), outperforming the next-best protocol (Agora: 2.33, 6.27) by 7.7% in quality and 48% in success. Under Fail-Storm Recovery, A2A preserves 98.85% of pre-fault answer discovery versus 81.29% for Agora, while in Safety Tech only ANP and Agora cover all five security dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No single protocol dominates all four scenarios, which is the paper's central finding. A2A wins on GAIA task utility (quality 2.51, success 9.29) and Fail-Storm post-fault retention (98.85%), outperforming ACP (92.41%), ANP (86.96%), and Agora (81.29%). ACP wins on Streaming Queue latency (9.66 s mean, 1.08 s std.) and total duration (40.28 min), with A2A statistically indistinguishable (p=0.47) but ANP and Agora significantly slower (p<10⁻⁴). On Safety Tech, ANP and Agora tie by covering all five security dimensions (TLS, session hijack, E2E encryption, tunnel sniffing, metadata leakage), while A2A and ACP each lack TLS transport and tunnel-sniffing protection. ProtocolRouter, which composes protocols per module, improves on the best single-protocol baseline in targeted metrics: it reduces Fail-Storm recovery time by 18.1% (8.00 s → 6.55 s vs. A2A) and raises GAIA success from 9.29 to 9.90, while matching ANP's full security coverage in Safety Tech.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by isolating the communication protocol layer from agent capability evaluation, which prior multi-agent benchmarks (MultiAgentBench, CREW-Wildfire, AgentBench) treat as a fixed, invisible substrate. While recent surveys (Tran et al. 2025, Yang et al. 2025, Ehtesham et al. 2025) catalog protocol designs and taxonomies, none provide empirical, protocol-agnostic comparisons under controlled conditions. The ProtocolRouter component repurposes constraint-satisfaction and capability-tagging ideas from systems configuration into the protocol-selection setting, but the core contribution is the benchmark harness itself: thin native adapters, a shared scenario suite, and unified metrics that make protocol effects measurable in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Protocol selection in production multi-agent systems is currently intuition-driven, and this paper provides the first systematic evidence that the choice matters substantially (up to 36.5% completion-time variation, 27.6% success-rate gaps) while no single protocol dominates across task utility, latency, robustness, and security. Key limitations include evaluation on a single model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), moderate agent counts (≤32), non-adversarial workloads, and an offline router that cannot adapt to rapidly shifting conditions. Open questions include whether the observed trade-off structure holds across model families and scales, whether online or bandit-style protocol adaptation can close the gap to per-scenario optima, and how protocol choices interact with emerging orchestration standards (MCP, IoA) that the paper explicitly excludes.
