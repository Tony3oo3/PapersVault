## Problem

Existing zero-shot LLM-generated text detection methods employ a fixed surrogate model for all inputs, assuming distributional similarity across LLMs. The paper demonstrates this assumption fails: detection AUROC varies by over 80 percentage points depending on surrogate-source alignment, and no single surrogate is universally optimal. This transforms robust black-box detection into a per-input routing problem that prior work has not addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264276/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text of unknown origin and a pool of N open-source surrogate LLMs (10 models, 1.5B–20B parameters), each paired with a detection criterion (Likelihood, Entropy, Rank, LogRank, LLR, or Fast-DetectGPT). It produces a binary human/machine detection score computed by exactly one routed surrogate, along with the routing decision. The router encodes text via LLM2Vec-LLaMA3-8B into a metric space and matches against 10 learnable prototypes per detector class using cosine-normalized L2 distance, adding ~166 ms latency per sample with the full encoder or ~5 ms with a RoBERTa-base alternative.

## Method

DetectRouter reframes zero-shot detection as a routing problem grounded in a theoretical Mismatch Risk Bound (Proposition 3.1) showing the score distortion is upper-bounded by B·√(2·D_KL(P_src‖P_sur)), derived via Pinsker's inequality. The architecture learns an encoder E_θ and per-detector prototype sets P_k in a shared metric space, where routing reduces to nearest-prototype matching. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 constructs discriminative prototypes from white-box models using multi-task generations (Generate, Polish, Rewrite) with cross-entropy, margin-based separation, and norm-regularization losses; Stage 2 generalizes to black-box sources by minimizing KL divergence between a softmax-normalized detection score vector and the predicted geometric affinity distribution, with prototype anchoring to prevent catastrophic forgetting. At inference, the router selects a single surrogate via argmin distance and scores the text with only that detector, avoiding the multiple-testing inflation that plagues max-selection baselines.

## Results

DetectRouter achieves 90.85% average AUROC on EvoBench and 77.92% on MAGE, outperforming the strongest fixed-surrogate baseline (Fast-DetectGPT with LLaMA3-8B) by 9.84 and 8.23 percentage points respectively. Gains are most pronounced on proprietary models: Claude improves from 81.57% to 94.22%, GPT-4o from 74.89% to 94.29%, and GPT-4 from 75.58% to 94.16%. As a universal enhancement layer, routing provides relative improvements of 5.4%–139.4% across the six detection criteria on EvoBench, with Entropy-based detection benefiting most (37.46% → 89.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetectRouter decisively outperforms all 13 baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest fixed-surrogate method (Fast-DetectGPT, 81.01%/69.69%), it gains +9.84/+8.23 points, with the largest per-family jumps on GPT-4o (+19.40) and GPT-4 (+18.58). Among recent zero-shot methods, Easy-to-Hard+FDGPT (83.89%/74.62%) and Deep Kernel (83.72%/74.18%) are the closest competitors, yet DetectRouter still leads by +6.96/+3.30 and +7.13/+3.74 points respectively. Against supervised approaches, DetectRouter surpasses DetectAnyLLM (77.70%/70.11%) by +13.15/+7.81 points and its own supervised baseline (71.09%/74.69%) by +19.76/+3.23 points, confirming that the gains stem from learned text-detector affinity rather than data volume. Notably, DetectRouter also outperforms a per-sample oracle max-selection baseline (72.33%) by 18.52 points, demonstrating that routing is fundamentally different from score-level aggregation. On the multilingual M4 benchmark, DetectRouter improves over fixed Fast-DetectGPT by 8.2 points on average (0.838→0.920), and on the adversarial RAID benchmark it gains 9.6 points across five attack types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes prototype-based metric learning (originally from few-shot classification) as a routing layer atop existing zero-shot detection criteria (Fast-DetectGPT, DetectGPT, GLTR, DetectLLM, etc.), rather than proposing a new detection statistic. Conceptually, it bridges the LLM routing literature (RouterBench, RouterEval), which assigns inputs to task-solving models, with the origin-tracing literature (Li et al. 2023, Fu et al. 2025b), which identifies source models but does not exploit detector complementarity. The key departure from prior zero-shot work is rejecting the distribution matching hypothesis: instead of assuming any surrogate approximates the source distribution, the paper formalizes per-input surrogate selection via a KL-divergence-based Mismatch Risk Bound (Pinsker's inequality) and learns the routing geometry through two-stage contrastive and distributional-alignment training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that surrogate-source mismatch can cause >80% AUROC swings for the same input directly challenges the field's reliance on fixed surrogates and provides a principled, drop-in enhancement for any existing zero-shot detector. Open questions include adversarial attacks targeting the routing mechanism itself (which would require knowledge of internal prototype geometry), scaling the surrogate pool to hundreds of models as new LLMs emerge, and whether the prototype space degrades under distributional shift in the source population. The 166ms per-sample encoder latency with LLM2Vec-LLaMA3-8B remains a practical constraint for high-throughput deployment, though the RoBERTa-base variant (4.87ms) offers a viable trade-off.
