## Problem

Existing low-rank approximation methods for LLM compression minimize the output error of individual linear layers without considering Transformer architectural characteristics, and decompose each weight matrix into two small rank-r factors that introduce runtime overhead (extra GEMM kernel launches and memory reads/writes). As a result, these methods often underperform pruning and quantization at equivalent compression budgets. The paper asks whether low-rank compression can be formulated at the level of Transformer functional components to achieve superior performance while eliminating all runtime penalties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264998/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained Transformer weights and a calibration set of 128 random 2048-token sequences (typically from SlimPajama) to compute activation autocorrelation matrices in FP64. Rather than outputting two decomposed matrices per layer as in standard low-rank factorization, it produces a single set of modified weights with reduced internal hidden dimensions—smaller head dimensions in QK and OV, and a smaller MLP intermediate size—so that inference uses the same number of GEMM operations at smaller problem sizes. This directly shrinks model parameters, KV cache, and FLOPs proportionally to the chosen rank with zero additional kernel launches or memory traffic.

## Method

A³ splits each Transformer layer into three functional components—QK (pre-softmax attention scores), OV (attention output), and MLP—and derives closed-form solutions that minimize each component's functional loss while reducing its shared hidden dimension. For QK, Theorem 3.2 applies activation-aware truncated SVD to the fused query-key weight scaled by both query and key/value autocorrelation matrices, yielding a smaller shared head dimension; for OV, Theorem 3.3 applies a similar per-head activation-aware SVD to the fused value-output weights. For MLP, where non-linear activations block direct SVD, A³-MLP uses CUR decomposition to select the top-r intermediate dimensions by sorting on the product of activation column norms and weight row norms. The framework extends to GQA via joint SVD across grouped heads and to RoPE via pairwise CUR that preserves RoPE frequency pairs, enabling application to modern architectures such as LLaMA 3.1, Phi-3, and Mistral 3.

## Results

On LLaMA 3.1-70B (GQA-RoPE) at 10% compression, A³ achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 4.69, outperforming SVD-LLM's 7.87 by 3.18 (a 58.6% reduction). Across all four evaluated models (LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.1-70B) at both 10% and 20% compression, A³ consistently achieves lower perplexity than SVD-LLM on WikiText-2, C4, and SlimPajama. On downstream tasks (ARC-Challenge, BoolQ, Winogrande, GSM8K, MMLU), A³ also achieves higher average accuracy than SVD-LLM at both compression levels, with the gap most pronounced at 10% compression (e.g., 0.6797 vs 0.5742 avg. accuracy on LLaMA-3.1-70B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVD-LLM (the primary SoTA low-rank baseline), A³ wins on every model and compression ratio tested, with the largest gap on LLaMA-3.1-70B at 10% compression (4.69 vs 7.87 WikiText-2 PPL) and LLaMA-3.1-8B at 20% (11.36 vs 42.28). Against SVD-LLM v2 and ASVD (mixed-rank baselines on LLaMA-7B at 20%), A³-mixed achieves 7.11 WikiText-2 PPL versus 10.53 and 10.45 respectively. In KV-cache compression (Table 3, MPT-7B/30B), A³ dominates CLOVER and Palu across all compression ratios; at 80% compression on MPT-7B, A³ achieves 849 WikiText-2 PPL versus 13,714 for Palu and 14,434 for CLOVER. In preliminary experiments (Figure 2), A³ outperforms FWSVD and plain SVD by two to three orders of magnitude in perplexity on LLaMA-7B. For RoPE-based QK compression, A³-QK-RoPE outperforms Wanda and abs(w) structured pruning on LLaMA-2-7B (Figure 4b). A³ is also shown to be orthogonal to HQQ quantization, and when combined (4-bit HQQ + A³@40% on MPT-30B), it achieves 6.67× compression with only +0.99 ΔPPL after fine-tuning, far exceeding pure 2-bit quantization (+2.80 ΔPPL at 8× compression).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-aware low-rank approximation lineage initiated by DRONE and formalized in SVD-LLM/ASVD, which minimize individual linear layer output error via a weighted SVD (Equation 8). The key conceptual departure is shifting the unit of compression from individual linear layers to Transformer functional components (QK, OV, MLP), minimizing each component's functional loss (attention scores, attention outputs, MLP outputs) rather than per-layer output error. For the MLP and RoPE-adapted QK components, the paper repurposes CUR decomposition (Drineas et al., 2006) as a practical alternative to SVD when the non-linearity or position-dependent rotation prevents a closed-form SVD solution. Unlike prior methods that decompose W into two small matrices (introducing extra GEMMs at inference), A³ reduces the shared hidden dimensions within each component, preserving the original GEMM count with smaller problem sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: A³ achieves a 58.6% perplexity reduction over SVD-LLM on LLaMA 3.1-70B at 10% compression while introducing zero runtime overhead, and it is the first low-rank method to natively support GQA and RoPE architectures used by all modern LLMs. The component-level optimization framing provides a principled design principle for future compression work: the 'right' granularity is the functional unit, not the individual matrix. Key limitations include the sub-optimality of CUR decomposition at high compression ratios (beyond ~20%), the need for a custom kernel to fuse RoPE frequency indexing with rotation, and the fact that aggressive compression still requires retraining. Open questions include whether the component-level objective can be further refined (e.g., joint optimization across components) and whether the approach extends to other architectural variants like sliding-window attention or multi-token prediction.
