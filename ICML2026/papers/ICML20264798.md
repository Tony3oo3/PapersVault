## Problem

Grounded Visual Reasoning (GVR) methods improve MLLM interpretability by having the model identify relevant image regions before answering, but their multi-round 'ground-then-answer' pipeline requires feeding full-resolution images at every rollout step, incurring substantially higher visual token costs than non-GVR baselines. The paper asks whether the reasoning phase can operate on low-resolution thumbnails—sufficient to locate key regions—while reserving full resolution only for the final answer, thereby cutting computation without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a downsampled thumbnail (resolution scaled to 1/4, 1/9, or 1/16 of the original depending on image size) alongside the text question, and iteratively outputs bounding-box coordinates in <box>[x1,y1,x2,y2]</box> tags to request high-resolution crops, or a <resolution></resolution> tag to upgrade the thumbnail resolution when the current one is insufficient. The final output is a text answer enclosed in <answer>...</answer> tags. The full-resolution original image is never fed directly to the model; it is only used server-side to produce the crops specified by the model's bounding boxes.

## Method

RTAF splits inference into two stages: (1) Reason with Thumbnails, where the model reasons over a low-resolution thumbnail to propose bounding boxes (or request a higher-resolution image via a special tag), repeated over multiple rounds; and (2) Answer with Focus, where the model generates the final answer conditioned on all previously collected high-resolution crops and reasoning context. Training is conducted purely via GRPO reinforcement learning (no SFT) on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct, using a composite reward of four rule-based components: a format reward enforcing the tag structure, an accuracy reward (exact match or ROUGE for free-form answers), an IoU-based bounding-box reward (threshold 0.5), and an efficiency reward that gives credit only when the model answers correctly without invoking any tool. A resolution-aware data selection pipeline pre-filters the training set by running Qwen2.5-VL at both high and low resolution eight times per sample, retaining only 'resolution-sensitive' (Class 1) and 'resolution-neutral' (Class 2) samples while discarding trivially correct or incorrect ones, yielding an 8k-sample training set for 1,000 GRPO steps.

## Results

On the VisCoT benchmark (12 sub-benchmarks), RTAF achieves an average accuracy of 78.3% using 471 average visual tokens per image, surpassing Ground-R1 (77.8% at 847 tokens) by 0.5% while halving token usage, and outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (72.5% at 391 tokens) by 5.8% with comparable token cost. The method achieves SOTA on 7 of 12 VisCoT sub-benchmarks, including +3.8% on DocVQA (94.7 vs. 90.9) and +4.4% on GQA (93.7 vs. 89.3) over Ground-R1. On high-resolution benchmarks, RTAF uses the fewest tokens (13,118 on HR-4K, 26,772 on HR-8K) while matching or exceeding accuracy of DeepEyes and Pixel Reasoner, which consume 2–3× more tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary GVR baseline Ground-R1, RTAF achieves higher average accuracy (78.3 vs. 77.8) while using roughly half the visual tokens (471 vs. 847), and wins on 7 of 12 VisCoT sub-benchmarks including DocVQA (+3.8), TextCaps (79.5 vs. 80.7, slight loss), GQA (+4.4), and CUB (tied at 82.7). Against the non-GVR baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B, RTAF gains +5.8% average accuracy (78.3 vs. 72.5) with only 20% more tokens (471 vs. 391), and substantially outperforms it on grounding (RefCOCO testA: 96.3 vs. 92.5; RefCOCO+ testB: 83.4 vs. 76.9). On high-resolution benchmarks, RTAF matches Pixel Reasoner's HR-4K accuracy (73.8 vs. 74.0) while using 67% fewer tokens (13,118 vs. 39,418), and ties DeepEyes on HR-8K accuracy (69.5) with 14% fewer tokens. RTAF loses slightly to Ground-R1 on TextCaps (79.5 vs. 80.7) and ChartInfoVQA (65.9 vs. 68.1), and to Pixel Reasoner on HR-4K accuracy (73.8 vs. 74.0), but these gaps are small relative to the token savings. Against older non-GVR methods (LLaVA-1.5, VisCoT-7B, CogCoM, Chain-of-Spot, FAST), RTAF achieves large margins of +20 to +30% average accuracy, confirming the paradigm shift from direct answering to grounded reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ground-R1 framework for multi-round grounded visual reasoning (GVR) by replacing full-resolution image inputs during the reasoning phase with low-resolution thumbnails, reserving high-resolution crops only for the final answer step. The RTAF paradigm is conceptually grounded in the RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) lineage from DeepSeek-R1, adapting its pure-RL training recipe to the multimodal setting with a novel suite of four reward functions (format, accuracy, bbox, efficiency). The resolution-aware data selection strategy departs from prior random-sampling approaches by explicitly filtering training samples based on whether they require high-resolution detail, a training-data curation idea not previously applied to GVR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: halving visual token consumption in GVR (471 vs. 847 tokens vs. Ground-R1) while matching or exceeding accuracy makes grounded reasoning more deployable in latency- and cost-sensitive settings. The efficiency reward's calibration finding—that over-weighting it causes the model to become 'overly cautious' and skip necessary exploration—provides a useful design principle for reward shaping in tool-use RL. A key open question is whether the thumbnail-suffices assumption generalizes beyond Qwen2.5-VL-7B and to domains where the critical region is extremely small and lacks contextual anchors (e.g., fine-grained medical imaging or microscopic textures), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
