## Problem

Standard Neural ODEs fail on multi-omics biological systems where the stiffness ratio (fastest/slowest timescale) exceeds 10^6, suffering either numerical divergence or biologically invalid over-smoothing of fast variables. Discrete-time models lose temporal coherence across the vastly different biochemical timescales (transcription over hours, translation over minutes, metabolism over seconds). No prior NODE framework provides provable stability guarantees under such extreme stiffness while preserving the causal molecular hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes aligned multi-omics snapshots (12,762 transcripts, 2,654 proteins, 44 metabolites for STATegra; ATAC-seq + RNA-seq for the macrophage dataset) at sparse discrete timepoints, encoding them into a 96-dimensional latent state (32 dims per modality) via a 3-layer MLP encoder. A time-invariant decoder reconstructs observations at any continuous time, enabling interpolation at unmeasured intervals (e.g., t=2.93, 4, 8, 10, 14, 20 hours). For drug screening, compound perturbation vectors from the LINCS L1000 library (22,268 compounds) are scored by cosine similarity against the disease differentiation trajectory at interpolated timepoints, yielding a ranked candidate list with permutation-test p-values.

## Method

The Tri-Scale NODE partitions the latent state into gene, protein, and metabolite subspaces governed by hierarchically coupled ODEs where the protein derivative is explicitly driven by the gene state and the metabolite derivative by the protein state, enforcing the Central Dogma as a causal graph in derivative space. Learnable time constants (τ_g < τ_p < τ_m) separate the timescales, while Lipschitz-constrained linear damping terms (−γ_k z_k) and a zero-centered parameterization of the base networks guarantee Exact Input-to-State Stability. A Lyapunov-based theorem proves global boundedness for any K-layer cascade when γ_k > τ_k L_k, reducing effective stiffness from ~10^6 to ~10^3 and enabling explicit Dopri5 integration without O(d^3) implicit solvers. Training uses a composite loss combining per-modality MSE reconstruction, MINE-estimated mutual information between adjacent layers to prevent numerical decoupling, and a soft stability penalty enforcing the damping margin, optimized with AdamW over 300 epochs.

## Results

On the STATegra B-cell dataset (held-out t=6h), the Tri-Scale NODE achieves Gene MSE=1.18, Protein MSE=2.27, and Metabolite MSE=0.91 with an effective stiffness ratio of κ=1.61×10³, compared to the raw biological stiffness of κ=1.3×10⁶. On the secondary Macrophage dataset (ATAC→RNA), the Bi-Scale variant achieves ATAC MSE=0.1309 and RNA MSE=0.3120 with κ=4.09×10². The model also enables continuous-time interpolation at unmeasured timepoints and identifies 510 multi-timepoint consistent drug repurposing hits from 22,268 LINCS L1000 compounds, with top candidates (Brefeldin A, Tozasertib, AZD-7545) scoring 0.861 cosine similarity (p<0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Tri-Scale NODE outperforms all four baselines on the STATegra dataset across all three omics modalities while maintaining a bounded stiffness ratio. Latent Vanilla NODE (Rubanova et al., 2019) suffers catastrophic over-smoothing, achieving training MSE of 0.007 but validation MSE of 1.94 (Gene) with κ=6.50×10⁷, confirming numerical divergence. scNODE (Zhang et al., 2024) collapses to a smoothed mean (κ=8.48×10¹), yielding the best Gene MSE (0.96) but worst Protein MSE (2.86) among stable baselines, indicating it discards high-frequency dynamics. HBNODE (Xia et al., 2021) and Jacobian-Regularized NODE (Finlay et al., 2020) both exhibit divergent stiffness (κ>10⁸) with the worst validation MSE across all modalities. On the Macrophage dataset, the Bi-Scale variant achieves the lowest ATAC MSE (0.1309) and RNA MSE (0.3120) with the most moderate stiffness (κ=4.09×10²), confirming generalizability to dual-omics settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) and its latent-space variant for irregularly-sampled time series (Rubanova et al., 2019) by imposing a hierarchical gene→protein→metabolite causal structure on the latent dynamics, rather than treating the latent state as a monolithic vector as in scNODE or HBNODE. The key departure is the introduction of Lipschitz-constrained damping with a zero-centered (Exact-ISS) parameterization, yielding the first provable Input-to-State Stability guarantee for NODEs under extreme stiffness (κ > 10⁶). While prior work has noted stiffness as a practical difficulty (Kim et al., 2021) or addressed it via Jacobian regularization (Finlay et al., 2020) or augmented state spaces (Dupont et al., 2019), none provide a structural stability theorem tied to the architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework bridges a gap between static multi-omics integration (MOFA+, totalVI) and dynamic kinetic modeling by providing a continuous-time model that is both numerically tractable (explicit Dopri5 solver suffices) and theoretically bounded, enabling drug repurposing screening over 22,000+ compounds at unmeasured timepoints. Key limitations include restriction to three molecular scales, reliance on transcriptomic signatures for drug scoring (ignoring direct metabolic perturbations), and the fact that learned causal couplings remain statistical correlations requiring interventional validation (e.g., CRISPR). Open questions include whether the smooth ODE dynamics can capture discrete transcriptional bursts or cell-cycle effects, and whether the stability framework generalizes to more than three hierarchical layers or to single-cell resolution data.
