## Problem

α-entmax attention provides differentiable, input-dependent sparsity as an alternative to dense softmax, but computing its normalizer τ requires iterative root-finding that has historically prevented it from matching the training throughput of optimized softmax kernels like FlashAttention-2. The prior ADASPLASH implementation reduced this gap but still required multiple expensive passes over attention scores to refine τ, limiting efficiency especially in moderate-sparsity regimes and the forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard Q, K, V matrices (n×d per head) and produces the attention output O (n×d) along with a sparse probability vector per query row where coordinates below the threshold τ are exactly zero. Internally, scores are streamed in B_r×B_c tiles through on-chip SRAM; a compact B-bin histogram (default B=8 bins packed into a 64-bit integer, 8 bits per bin) is maintained per query row, and a bit-packed block mask (32 column blocks per int32) records which tile pairs contain non-zero weights. The histogram capacity supports up to ~16K keys per query block before requiring periodic flushing.

## Method

ADASPLASH-2 replaces the multi-pass τ refinement of ADASPLASH with a four-phase GPU kernel: (1) compute row-wise max, (2) stream key tiles to build a per-row histogram in SRAM using bit-packed accumulators (each score increments its bin via a shift-and-add on a w-bit integer), (3) solve the discretized normalization on the B-bin histogram to obtain τ_h — a provable lower bound on the true τ* with error < 1/B — then refine with a safeguarded hybrid solver (Halley for α≤1.5, Newton for 1.5<α≤2, secant for α≥2, bisection fallback) that typically converges in 1–2 iterations, and (4) compute the output by traversing only non-zero blocks via GPU-native find-next-set and population-count instructions on the bit-packed mask. The left-edge binning strategy guarantees τ_h ≤ τ*, ensuring the approximate support contains the true support for safe block masking. The backward pass reuses the forward-pass mask to skip zero-gradient blocks, yielding the largest speedups at moderate-to-high block sparsity (>60%) where ADASPLASH-2 matches or exceeds FlashAttention-2 per-step training time.

## Results

On the RULER long-context benchmark (1B params, 32K context), Entmax+NAPE achieves an average score of 39.4, outperforming Softmax+RoPE (27.3) and Softmax+NAPE (36.8). On the HELMET in-context learning benchmark at 32K, Entmax+NAPE reaches 62.2 average versus 46.1 for Softmax+NAPE and 38.2 for Softmax+RoPE. In efficiency benchmarks, ADASPLASH-2 achieves up to 2× speedup over FlashAttention-2 (CUDA) at high block sparsity (>80%), and matches or exceeds FlashAttention-2 (Triton) at moderate sparsity (>60%). On short-context tasks (OLMES core_9mcqa), Entmax+NAPE 1B achieves 53.1 average, matching Softmax+NAPE (53.0) while achieving lower LAMBADA perplexity (11.42 vs 11.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention-2 (the primary efficiency baseline), ADASPLASH-2 outperforms both the CUDA and Triton implementations at moderate-to-high block sparsity (>60%), achieving up to 2× speedup at high sparsity; at low sparsity, FlashAttention-2 remains faster due to ADASPLASH-2's additional histogram and refinement passes. Against the prior ADASPLASH implementation, ADASPLASH-2 is a strict improvement, running faster at every sparsity level thanks to the histogram-based initialization reducing τ refinement to 1–2 iterations versus multiple passes. On long-context accuracy (RULER, 32K), Entmax+NAPE (39.4 avg) substantially outperforms Softmax+RoPE (27.3) and Softmax+NAPE (36.8), with the largest gap on Variable Tracking (27.4 vs 0.0 and 7.7). On HELMET ICL at 32K, Entmax+NAPE (62.2) leads Softmax+NAPE (46.1) by 16.1 points, indicating sparsity particularly benefits in-context example retrieval. On short-context tasks, Entmax+NAPE matches or marginally exceeds softmax baselines (53.1 vs 53.0 avg for 1B), confirming no quality trade-off for adopting sparse attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct sequel to ADASPLASH (Gonçalves et al., 2025), which first made α-entmax attention GPU-viable via a hybrid Halley-bisection solver, but still required multiple expensive passes over keys to refine the normalizer τ. The key departure is a histogram-based initialization computed entirely in on-chip SRAM during the streaming tile loop, yielding a provable lower bound on τ that collapses refinement to 1–2 iterations. The overall tiled, IO-aware execution paradigm follows FlashAttention-2 (Dao, 2024), adapted here to handle the non-additive normalization of α-entmax, while the α-entmax transformation itself originates from Peters et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making α-entmax attention competitive with FlashAttention-2 in per-step training time at moderate-to-high block sparsity (>60%), this work removes the primary practical barrier to adopting differentiable sparse attention in long-context training. The histogram-in-SRAM trick is a reusable pattern for any iterative normalization that must be computed online over streamed tiles. Open questions include a dedicated decoding/inference kernel (the forward pass still requires multiple key scans, unlike single-pass softmax), whether the speedup advantage persists at lower sparsity regimes typical of shorter contexts, and how the method extends to other sparse attention families beyond α-entmax.
