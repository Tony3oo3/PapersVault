## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for Transformer-based time series foundation models (TSFMs) imitate teacher-specific outputs, features, or pairwise relations, remaining tightly coupled to teacher training trajectories. They fail to exploit two empirical properties: high-level representations across model scales spontaneously converge to a shared low-rank geometry, and layer-wise utility follows a long-tail distribution. This paper reframes distillation as alignment to a model-agnostic geometric object rather than pointwise imitation of a specific teacher.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher TSFM (MOMENT-Large, 385M parameters) and a multivariate time series dataset (Time Series Pile, ~13M series across 13 domains, 1.23B timestamps) as input. It produces a compressed student model (38M parameters, 90.13% reduction) that performs zero-shot long-horizon forecasting (horizons 96–720), imputation (mask ratios 0.125–0.5), anomaly detection, and classification. Time series are tokenized via patching into T tokens with hidden dimension H; the offline phase uses only a 10% data subset for statistical estimation, and the online phase requires no additional teacher forward passes.

## Method

Consensus subspace distillation operates in two phases. Offline, high-contribution layers are identified via drop-layer marginal MAE loss; a shrinkage-stabilized covariance is computed from their token embeddings, and a truncated eigensubspace (rank r via cumulative variance threshold θ=0.99) defines a consensus projector P_C. Online, the student's top-layer embeddings are projected into this subspace, and a mean-covariance alignment loss (squared 2-Wasserstein distance surrogate using Frobenius norm on covariance difference) matches the teacher's projected statistics without rigid pointwise feature binding. To mitigate subset-induced bias, a frequency-domain uncertainty injection mechanism computes characteristic function (ChF) discrepancies between the full dataset and the offline subset, inflates spectral density via a per-frequency scaling factor, and injects dispersion exclusively along consensus directions (span(U_r)), preserving the orthogonal complement. The student is initialized by copying the top-K teacher layers with low-rank increments (rank r_a=64) to MLP output weights, and trained for 3 epochs with AdamW at lr=1e-4.

## Results

Using MOMENT-Large (385M params) as teacher, the proposed consensus subspace distillation compresses the model to 38M parameters (90.13% reduction) while *improving* upon the teacher on forecasting (MAE 0.287 vs. 0.299 at horizon 96; 0.366 vs. 0.381 at horizon 720) and imputation (MAE 0.152 vs. 0.159 at mask ratio 0.125; 0.151 vs. 0.158 at mask ratio 0.500). The trade-off appears in anomaly detection (Adj. Best F1 drops 2.11% to 0.557) and zero-shot classification (accuracy drops 17.15% to 0.633). Distillation requires only 3.86 GPU-hours on a single RTX 4090, roughly 50× faster than the next-best distillation baseline (NCKD at 195.64 GPU-h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all six distillation baselines (PKT, RKD, CRD, ADA-KD, LRLFD, NCKD), the proposed method achieves the best MAE on both forecasting horizons and both imputation mask ratios, beating the closest competitor (CRD) by 15–19% relative on MAE(96) and 14–18% on MAE(720). The method also surpasses the teacher itself on forecasting and imputation, suggesting the consensus subspace acts as a regularizer that sharpens reconstruction. The main losses are in zero-shot classification (−17.15% vs. teacher) and a modest drop in anomaly detection F1 (−2.11%), which the authors attribute to the aggressive 90% parameter reduction removing discriminative capacity. In the compression comparison (Table 1), the method achieves 90.13% parameter reduction (38M vs. 385M) with comparable forecasting/imputation quality, whereas SliceGPT only removes 28.83% of parameters and FrameQuant retains all 385M parameters (reducing only memory footprint). Distillation time is 3.86 GPU-hours, roughly 50× faster than the slowest baseline (CRD at 220.46 GPU-h) and 50× faster than NCKD (195.64 GPU-h).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three established KD paradigms (response-based, feature-based, relation-based) by reframing distillation as alignment to a model-agnostic geometric object—a scale-invariant low-rank consensus subspace with center statistics—rather than imitation of teacher-specific pathways. Conceptually, it synthesizes ideas from optimal transport (Wasserstein mean-covariance alignment), spectral analysis (characteristic-function-based uncertainty quantification), and low-rank approximation (eigensubspace extraction) into a unified distillation objective. The empirical premise—that high-level Transformer embeddings across scales spontaneously converge to shared low-rank geometry—draws on observations from masked autoencoder pretraining but had not been explicitly exploited as a distillation target in prior TSFM compression work (SliceGPT, FrameQuant, LRLFD, NCKD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 90.13% parameter reduction with matching or slightly improved forecasting/imputation performance, plus a ~50x distillation-time reduction (3.86 vs. ~200 GPU-hours), makes large TSFMs deployable in resource-constrained settings. The acknowledged trade-off in zero-shot classification (−17%) and the assumption of a stable consensus subspace across scales limit generality; the method may weaken for architectures with fundamentally different tokenization or under extreme domain shift. Open questions include sensitivity of the spectral inflation hyperparameters (λ, σ) to sampling ratio and sequence length, whether the consensus-subspace premise holds for non-Transformer backbones, and whether the approach extends to multi-teacher or cross-architecture distillation.
