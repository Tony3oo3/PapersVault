## Problem

Existing LLM-based time series forecasting methods treat each forecasting instance as an isolated reasoning process, discarding accumulated prediction outcomes, reasoning trajectories, and temporal regularities after inference. This prevents cross-instance knowledge transfer and eliminates any mechanism for continual evolution, so the model cannot adaptively refine its forecasting experience as new instances arrive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a lookback window of 168 steps (short-term) or 96 steps (long-term) of multivariate time series along with static contextual features (e.g., location) and dynamic covariates (e.g., load forecasts, meteorological variables), and produces a forecast vector of 24 or 96 future steps respectively. Raw numerical inputs are converted into structured natural-language summaries and statistical meta-features (mean, std, trend slope, lag-1 correlation) before being fed to the LLM, since the model is insensitive to raw magnitudes. Forecasts are emitted in a strict <answer>...</answer> format and validated against physical boundary constraints.

## Method

MemCast reformulates TSF as experience-conditioned reasoning with a frozen LLM (GPT-5) and an external hierarchical memory built from the training set. The memory has three tiers: historical patterns (LLM-summarized trend/volatility descriptions of past instances), reasoning wisdom (success and failure lessons distilled from partitioned inference trajectories, deduplicated via a composite cosine-similarity + DTW score), and general laws (domain constraints induced by an LLM from textualized temporal features). At inference, the system retrieves top-k similar patterns to ground reasoning, samples M candidate trajectories and selects the one maximizing semantic consistency against retrieved wisdom, then iteratively corrects violations of general laws via a Reflexion-style feedback loop. A dynamic confidence adaptation strategy updates per-entry confidence weights (not content) whenever a prediction beats a moving-average baseline, enabling continual memory evolution without test-distribution leakage.

## Results

MemCast achieves the best MSE on 8 out of 10 datasets and the best MAE on 9 out of 10, using GPT-5 as the frozen reasoning backbone. Headline improvements include NP (MSE 36.87 vs. 53.06 for LSTPrompt, a 30% reduction), PJM (MSE 30.24 vs. 56.47 for TimeReasoner, a 46% reduction), and DE (MSE 210.45 vs. 296.23 for TimeXer, a 29% reduction). On long-term benchmarks, MemCast attains MSE 6.551/MAE 1.968 on ETTh and MSE 3.999/MAE 1.441 on ETTm, outperforming all deep-learning and LLM baselines. The only metric where MemCast does not lead is WP MSE (2170.46 vs. PatchTST's 2163.26), though it still wins WP MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemCast outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the strongest LLM-based competitor TimeReasoner, MemCast reduces MSE by 46% on PJM (30.24 vs. 56.47), 30% on NP (36.87 vs. 70.51), and 14% on MOPEX (6.19 vs. 7.18), though TimeReasoner retains a slight MAE edge on FR (11.58 vs. 12.14). Versus the best deep-learning model TimeXer, MemCast achieves lower MSE on all five short-term EPF datasets (e.g., 210.45 vs. 296.23 on DE) and on ETTh (6.55 vs. 7.91), while TimeXer narrowly edges out on ETTm MSE (4.03 vs. 4.00). Against the fine-tuned Time-LLM, MemCast wins decisively on volatile markets (NP: 36.87 vs. 78.26 MSE; BE: 756.31 vs. 1349.53) and long-term tasks (ETTh: 6.55 vs. 11.32). The only near-tie is WP MSE, where PatchTST (2163.26) marginally beats MemCast (2170.46), though MemCast still leads on WP MAE (31.72 vs. 31.82). Statistical baselines (ARIMA, Prophet) are consistently outperformed by 2–10× on MSE across all datasets, confirming that the memory-augmented LLM paradigm substantially surpasses classical assumptions-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemCast sits at the intersection of LLM-based time series forecasting (Time-LLM, TimeReasoner, LSTPrompt) and memory-augmented reasoning (MemoRAG, Memory³, HippoRAG), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than updating LLM parameters or relying on static prompts, it accumulates structured forecasting experience in an external hierarchical memory while keeping the LLM frozen. The law-based reflection loop directly adapts the Reflexion mechanism (Shinn et al., 2023) for post-hoc constraint checking, and the retrieval-based trajectory selection draws on retrieval-augmented generation principles. Conceptually, the paper reframes TSF from a per-instance generation problem into an experience-conditioned reasoning task, where historical patterns, distilled reasoning wisdom, and induced general laws collectively guide inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in LLM-based TSF: the inability to accumulate and reuse forecasting experience across instances without retraining. The dynamic confidence adaptation strategy is a practical solution to the continual-evolution problem while preserving train-test separation, which is non-trivial. However, the heavy reliance on GPT-5 via API raises questions about cost, reproducibility, and whether the gains are attributable to the memory architecture or simply to the backbone's raw reasoning capacity (the backbone ablation shows large variance across models). Open questions include scalability of the memory to very long or high-dimensional series, robustness to truly novel distribution shifts absent from training, and whether the approach transfers to settings where ground truth is unavailable during the memory-construction phase.
