## Problem

Existing offline Markov game learning methods rely on explicit pessimistic bonuses (LCB penalties, CQL-style corrections) to handle distribution shift, and prior KL-regularized analyses (e.g., Ye et al., 2024) achieve only the standard O~(1/√n) rate in single-step settings. This paper asks whether KL regularization alone—without any manually constructed pessimism—can stabilize offline learning in multi-step zero-sum Markov games and unlock a faster statistical rate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of n trajectories (each with H state-action-reward tuples for both players), fixed reference policies π_ref for both players that define the admissible support, a KL coefficient η, and finite stagewise function classes Q_h satisfying Bellman completeness. It outputs a joint policy pair (π̂₁, π̂₂) constituting a regularized Nash equilibrium, evaluated by the duality gap Gap(π̂) = E[V₁^{†,π̂₂} − V₁^{π̂₁,†}]. Notably, no confidence bonuses or pessimistic corrections are added to regression targets; the reference policy's support acts as a hard constraint, and the algorithm's robustness is entirely geometric rather than penalty-based.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms. ROSE (Algorithm 1) is a theoretical framework that performs backward induction: at each stage h it fits Q̂_h by least-squares regression on the offline data, then solves the exact regularized Nash equilibrium of the stage game via the closed-form Gibbs distribution π̂(a|s) ∝ π_ref(a|s)·exp(η·q̂(s,a)), which is support-preserving and provides implicit pessimism without explicit penalties. SOS-MD (Algorithm 2) replaces the exact solver with T iterations of dual mirror descent self-play, where both players start at π_ref and update via exponentiated-gradient steps on marginal payoffs. The key theoretical insight is that the strong convexity of the KL-regularized objective makes the duality gap scale quadratically (not linearly) with Q-estimation error, yielding the fast O~(1/n) statistical rate under only unilateral concentrability. Additionally, this strong convexity ensures the last iterate of mirror descent converges directly (no time-averaging needed), with optimization error O~(1/√T), so the total gap is O~(1/n) + O~(1/√T).

## Results

The paper establishes a fast $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/n)$ statistical rate for the duality gap in KL-regularized offline two-player zero-sum Markov games, improving over the standard $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{n})$ rate in prior unregularized offline game literature. The total error bound for SOS-MD (Corollary 6.9) decomposes into an optimization error of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\eta H^4 \lambda^2 / \sqrt{T})$, a statistical error of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\eta H^6 \lambda^4 C_{\text{uni}} \log(|\mathcal{Q}|/\delta) / n)$, and a lower-order last-iterate residual of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\eta^3 H^7 \lambda^6 / T)$. The result holds under unilateral concentrability (the minimal coverage condition) and a coupled regularization/reference-policy condition $\eta \leq 1/(4H^2\lambda)$, with no explicit pessimistic bonuses required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOS-MD is the first method to simultaneously achieve KL-regularization, multi-step dynamics, and a fast $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/n)$ statistical rate. Against the three unregularized multi-step baselines—PNVI (Cui & Du, 2022a), PMVI (Zhong et al., 2022), and BCEL (Zhang et al., 2023)—SOS-MD improves the rate from $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{n})$ to $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/n)$ while eliminating the need for explicit pessimistic bonuses (LCB or CQL-style penalties). Against Ye et al. (2024), the only prior KL-regularized result, SOS-MD extends the analysis from single-step (bandit) to multi-step sequential decision-making and achieves the faster $1/n$ rate versus $1/\sqrt{n}$. The key mechanism enabling this improvement is the strong convexity of the KL-regularized objective, which causes the regret to scale quadratically (rather than linearly) with the Q-estimation error, a phenomenon previously observed only in single-agent offline RL (Hu et al., 2021) and regularized contextual games (Zhang et al., 2026).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pessimism-based paradigm in offline RL and offline Markov games (Cui & Du, 2022a; Zhong et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023), which rely on manually constructed LCB or CQL-style bonuses to handle distribution shift and achieve only O(1/√n) rates. It builds on the KL-regularized objective framework from RLHF and Nash Learning from Human Feedback (Munos et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024), but extends it from single-step bandit or online settings to the multi-step offline Markov game setting. The fast-rate analysis draws on the strong-convexity-to-squared-error mechanism identified in single-agent offline RL (Hu et al., 2021) and regularized contextual games (Zhang et al., 2026), transferring it to the game-theoretic saddle-point structure via a novel duality-gap decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that KL regularization alone provides 'implicit pessimism' and unlocks an O(1/n) rate (vs. the standard O(1/√n)) eliminates the need for hand-tuned confidence bonuses in offline game learning, simplifying both theory and practice. This has direct implications for LLM alignment via Nash Learning from Human Feedback, where multi-turn interactions and tool-use scenarios naturally form multi-step games and where avoiding explicit pessimism aligns better with practical post-training pipelines. Open questions include whether the coupled regularization/reference-policy condition (η ≤ 1/(4H²λ)) is tight, whether the analysis extends to partially observable or general-sum games, and whether the fast rate manifests empirically at scale beyond the finite function-class setting assumed here.
