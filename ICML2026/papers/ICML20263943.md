## Problem

Distance-based generative model evaluation metrics (fidelity and coverage) operate in high-dimensional embedding spaces where the hubness phenomenon distorts nearest-neighbor relationships: certain points (hubs) appear disproportionately as neighbors while others (antihubs) never do, causing generated points to spuriously appear closer to real data than real points are to each other. This leads to systematically biased scores, as demonstrated by all standard metrics failing on simple disjoint-distribution tests in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

GICDM takes pre-computed embeddings of real and generated samples (e.g., 1024-dim DINOv2, 4096-dim DINOv3, 2048-dim InceptionV3, or 1024-dim CLAP audio) along with two neighborhood sizes K1 and K2 (typically K2=10·K1) and a quantile threshold q (default 0.95). It outputs corrected real-to-generated dissimilarities that replace raw Euclidean distances in any downstream metric; the result is a dissimilarity (not a true metric, as it violates the triangle inequality) sufficient for neighbor ranking and ball-membership tests used by fidelity and coverage metrics.

## Method

GICDM combines three components: (1) applying ICDM iteratively to the real set alone to uniformize its local density, yielding per-point scaling factors δ_i^r; (2) computing an out-of-sample scaling factor δ_j^g for each generated point independently, set so that its average ICDM-scaled distance to its K+1 nearest real neighbors equals the equilibrium mean of the real set (Eq. 1), ensuring each generated point's evaluation is conditional on the real set only; (3) multi-scale filtering that compares δ_j^g against the mean δ of its real neighbors at two different neighborhood scales (K1, K2) and discards generated points whose relative discrepancy exceeds the q-quantile of the same statistic computed over real points, preventing overcorrection in the crossover regime where out-of-manifold points coincidentally match in-manifold local distances. The key insight is that ICDM—previously dismissed as too costly—consistently outperforms all other hubness reduction methods (NICDM, LS, CSLS, DSL, MP^Gauss) across 17 dataset-embedding pairs, and its iterative fixed-point structure naturally extends to out-of-sample scoring via the equilibrium scaling factors.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark of Raisa et al. (2025), GICDM improves Clipped Density from 18/30 to 23/30 passed criteria (Purpose 8→10, Bounds 8→11) and Clipped Coverage from 18/30 to 22/30 (Purpose 8→10, Bounds 9→11). On real data, GICDM increases Pearson correlation with human error rates from 0.82 to 0.95 on CIFAR-10 with DINOv3 embeddings, and from 0.67 to 0.82 on ImageNet. In the hubness reduction comparison (Table 5), ICDM achieves rank 1.0 on both h₁⁵(1%) (value 1.7) and A⁵ (value 0.00), outperforming all 7 other methods across 17 dataset-embedding pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GICDM consistently improves upon the best existing metrics: Clipped Density (Salvy et al., 2026) gains +2 Purpose and +3 Bounds tests, while Clipped Coverage gains +2 Purpose and +2 Bounds. Standard Precision and Recall (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019) show the largest absolute gaps (6/14 and 5/14 Purpose respectively), and GICDM-corrected versions close much of this gap. Among hubness reduction methods compared in Table 5, ICDM (Jégou et al., 2010) dominates all alternatives: NICDM (Jégou et al., 2007) ranks 2nd on h₁ but 3rd on A⁵, DSL (Hara et al., 2016) ranks 3rd/2nd, CSLS (Lample et al., 2018) ranks 4th/4th, LS (Zelnik-Manor & Perona, 2004) ranks 5th/5th, and MP^Gauss (Schnitzer et al., 2012) ranks 6th/6th. For the one-class and topological metrics (α-Precision/β-Recall from Alaa et al., 2022; Precision Cover/Recall Cover from Cheema & Urner, 2023), GICDM-corrected versions match or exceed their uncorrected counterparts on the synthetic benchmark, with Clipped Density + GICDM achieving the highest overall pass count (23/30) among all tested metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the Iterative Contextual Dissimilarity Measure (ICDM, Jégou et al. 2010), originally designed for image retrieval, and repurposes it as a hubness reduction method for generative model evaluation. Prior hubness reduction work (Schnitzer et al. 2012; Feldbauer & Flexer 2019) explicitly dismissed ICDM as too computationally expensive and used only the non-iterative NICDM variant; this paper shows ICDM consistently outperforms all alternatives across 17 dataset-embedding pairs. The key conceptual departure is extending an in-sample hubness reduction method to handle out-of-sample generated points, which no prior hubness reduction technique addresses, and coupling it with a multi-scale filtering strategy to prevent overcorrection of off-manifold generated samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying hubness as a systematic root cause of failures across many distance-based fidelity and coverage metrics, the paper provides a unified diagnostic and a drop-in correction (GICDM) that improves metric behavior without requiring changes to the metric formulation itself. The improvement in correlation with human judgments, particularly for high-dimensional DINOv3 embeddings, suggests that current evaluation scores for generative models may be meaningfully biased. A stated limitation is that GICDM's effectiveness depends on the underlying metric being robust to real outliers (e.g., Clipped Density rather than raw Precision), and the method is specific to distance-based evaluation, leaving aggregate metrics like FID unaffected. Open questions include whether the multi-scale filtering threshold generalizes across very different data distributions and whether analogous corrections exist for non-Euclidean or learned distance spaces.
