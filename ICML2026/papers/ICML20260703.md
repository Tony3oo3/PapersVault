## Problem

Importance sampling for complex multimodal targets in high dimensions suffers from severe weight degeneracy when the proposal fails to cover all modes. Entropic Mirror Descent provides geometric KL-contraction guarantees but is intractable in practice: Monte Carlo approximations of its update cannot discover regions of high target mass that the current proposal does not already cover. Standard MCMC alternatives mix poorly across distant modes and do not yield tractable pointwise-evaluable proposals for importance sampling.

## Input / Output

The method requires access to the target log-density and its gradient (for ULA/MALA kernels), an initial proposal distribution, and kernel hyperparameters (step size, number of kernel steps per iteration). It produces a sequence of learned proposal distributions in a tractable parametric family—either a tensorized Gaussian mixture fitted by EM or a Neural Spline Flow trained by maximum likelihood—enabling pointwise density evaluation for importance weights. The final output is a proposal from which posterior samples and expectation estimates are drawn, with convergence measured via sliced Wasserstein distance, energy distance, or test NLL.

## Method

EM2C defines an idealized mapping F_em as a convex combination of two components: the Entropic Mirror Descent step (which contracts KL(π‖μ_t) geometrically at rate 1−ε) and a Markov-kernel step that propagates samples through a transition kernel (ULA, MALA, or RW Metropolis) to reach previously unexplored regions. The mixing parameter λ balances contraction versus exploration. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) samples from the current proposal, propagates particles through the kernel, computes ε-powered importance weights on both the original and propagated particles, resamples from the weighted mixture, and projects onto a tractable parametric family via EM (for GMMs) or maximum-likelihood training (for normalizing flows). A key theoretical result shows that even the biased Unadjusted Langevin kernel preserves geometric convergence, with the discretization bias bounded by O(√(γdL²C_LS)) under log-Sobolev and smoothness conditions.

## Results

On the Dual Moons benchmark, EM2C achieves a sliced Wasserstein distance of 0.071±0.016 and energy distance of 0.0004±0.0001, matching or slightly outperforming DiGS (0.074±0.037 SW2, 0.0005±0.0003 ED) while substantially outperforming NUTS (1.66 SW2), RW (0.62 SW2), and AIS (0.34 SW2). On Two Rings, EM2C achieves 0.049±0.008 SW2 and 0.0006±0.0002 ED, again the best or tied-best. On the 550-dimensional Bayesian neural network posterior, EM2C achieves a test NLL of 0.1294±0.0707, substantially below DiGS (0.2016±0.0128) and MALA (0.2219±0.0170).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EM2C outperforms all four baselines on both 2D benchmarks (Dual Moons and Two Rings) in terms of both SW₂ and ED, with the largest margins against NUTS (which concentrates on a single mode, yielding SW₂ ≈ 1.4–1.7) and RW (SW₂ ≈ 0.55–0.62). Against the strongest competitor DiGS, EM2C achieves marginally lower discrepancies (e.g., 0.071 vs 0.074 SW₂ on Dual Moons; 0.049 vs 0.053 on Two Rings) with notably smaller standard deviations, indicating more stable mode recovery under the challenging initialization. On the 550-dimensional BNN posterior, EM2C achieves a test NLL of 0.1294, roughly 36% lower than DiGS (0.2016) and 42% lower than MALA (0.2219), though with larger run-to-run variance (±0.0707 vs ±0.0128) due to its population-based proposal updates. AIS shows competitive but high-variance results on the 2D benchmarks, while NUTS consistently fails to explore the full multimodal support under the considered initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Entropic Mirror Descent mapping (Korba & Portier, 2022), which provides a principled contraction operator for adaptive importance sampling but is intractable in multimodal settings, and on the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (Roberts & Tweedie, 1996), which offers strong exploratory dynamics but lacks explicit contraction guarantees. EM2C's key conceptual move is to form a convex combination of the mirror-descent step and a Markov-kernel-propagated step, preserving the geometric KL contraction of the former while injecting the exploration of the latter. This bridges two previously separate literatures—adaptive importance sampling (Oh & Berger, 1993; Cappé et al., 2004; Cornuet et al., 2012) and MCMC-based exploration (Samsonov et al., 2022; Cabezas et al., 2024)—into a single framework with explicit convergence guarantees, and it departs from prior adaptive IS by guaranteeing geometric convergence rather than relying on variational criteria over parametric families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first adaptive importance sampling scheme with a provable geometric KL contraction rate that simultaneously handles multimodal targets via Markovian exploration, addressing a long-standing gap where either contraction (mirror descent) or exploration (MCMC) was available but not both. Theorem 3.3 showing that ULA discretization bias is bounded and does not break convergence is a useful theoretical guarantee for practitioners who prefer gradient-based kernels. Key open questions remain: the contraction guarantee does not extend to the projected Monte Carlo sequence, the mixing parameter λ_t is only shown to exist constructively (not computable in practice), and the method has not yet been stress-tested in very high-dimensional deep-learning posteriors beyond the 550-parameter BNN. The projection step onto a parametric family (GMM or normalizing flow) is a practical necessity that can collapse modes if the family is insufficiently expressive, limiting the method's worst-case guarantees.
