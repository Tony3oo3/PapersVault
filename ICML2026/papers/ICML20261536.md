## Problem

Existing action-chunking visuomotor policies optimize intra-chunk action distributions but neglect inter-chunk discontinuities, causing incoherent long-horizon trajectories. Directly increasing the inference horizon to capture more future context degrades policy performance due to the increased distributional complexity. The paper addresses how to simultaneously ensure fine-grained proximal action precision and coarse distal trajectory coherence without sacrificing one-step generation efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261536/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a 3D point cloud observation (encoded via a lightweight MLP to 64-dimensional features) fused with robot state through FiLM conditioning, and outputs a single action chunk of H=4 steps per inference via one-step consistency flow matching. During training, the model operates on a macro-trajectory of L=NH=12 steps (N=3 concatenated expert chunks), but at inference only the first H steps are executed before replanning. The point cloud encoder and 1D CNN U-Net backbone follow the standard architecture used in prior 3D visuomotor policies (DP3, FlowPolicy) for fair comparison.

## Method

FocalPolicy introduces two key modules on top of a consistency flow matching (CFM) framework with a 1D CNN U-Net backbone. First, the Foresight Composite Objective (FCO) concatenates N consecutive expert chunks into a macro-trajectory and applies a dual-horizon loss: a time-domain consistency loss on the proximal H-step prefix (ensuring local precision) plus a frequency-domain loss computed via DCT over the full L-step macro-trajectory (enforcing global structural coherence across chunks), weighted by λ=10⁻⁴. The paper proves via Parseval's theorem and gradient analysis that these two losses produce non-conflicting optimization directions. Second, Locally Anchored Sampling (LAS) replaces the standard uniform sampling of the teacher time r in CFM with a logit-normal distribution (μ=4.0, σ=1.6, median r≈0.982) concentrated near the terminal boundary, which the paper formally shows via first-order stochastic dominance to yield strictly higher target-signal propagation efficiency than uniform sampling. Together, these modules enable one-step generation (NFE=1) with improved cross-chunk coherence, outperforming baselines by 4+ points on average across 53 simulation tasks.

## Results

On 53 simulation tasks (Adroit + MetaWorld), FocalPolicy achieves an average success rate of 83.6% with only 1 NFE, outperforming DP3 (79.9%, 10 NFE), FlowPolicy (79.4%, 1 NFE), and FreqPolicy (75.1%). On 7 representative tasks, FocalPolicy reaches 69.7% average success, surpassing the next-best baseline (FlowPolicy w. LAS at 63.3%) by 6.4 points. On RoboMimic and LIBERO, FocalPolicy attains 0.81 average success at 5.40 ms inference latency, matching FlowPolicy's speed while exceeding it by 8.2 points in accuracy. Real-world experiments across 6 multi-stage tasks confirm consistent superiority, with the largest gains appearing in final task stages where compounding errors are most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FocalPolicy outperforms all 8 baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest one-step baselines, it beats FlowPolicy by 4.2 points (83.6% vs 79.4%) and DP3 by 3.7 points (83.6% vs 79.9%) on 53 tasks, while also achieving lower Action Total Variation (e.g., 0.21 vs 0.35 on Disassemble) indicating smoother cross-chunk trajectories. Against FreqPolicy (75.1%), the gap widens to 8.5 points, and on the 7-task subset FocalPolicy leads by 14.8 points (69.7% vs 54.9%). On RoboMimic/LIBERO, FocalPolicy (0.81) surpasses ACG (0.70) by 11 points while matching FlowPolicy's 5.40 ms latency and halving FreqPolicy's 18.90 ms cost. The plug-and-play experiments confirm generalizability: adding FCO to DP3 yields +2.8 points and adding LAS to FlowPolicy yields +7.9 points, demonstrating that both modules transfer beyond FocalPolicy's architecture. On real-world multi-stage tasks, FocalPolicy's advantage is most pronounced in final stages (e.g., Cup Matching Stage 4: 30.0% vs 13.3% for FlowPolicy), directly validating the inter-chunk coherence hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

FocalPolicy extends the consistency flow matching framework introduced by FlowPolicy (Zhang et al., 2025a) and the CFM formulation of Yang et al. (2024) by shifting the optimization focus from single-chunk intra-trajectory fidelity to multi-chunk inter-trajectory coherence. It departs from prior frequency-domain approaches (FreqPolicy, Su et al. 2025a) that apply DCT supervision within a single chunk, instead projecting a concatenated macro-trajectory of N chunks into the frequency domain to capture cross-chunk structure. The paper also contrasts with inference-time coherence methods like RTC (Black et al., 2025) and ACG (Park et al., 2025), arguing that training-time structural regularization is more fundamental than post-hoc inference corrections. Locally Anchored Sampling modifies the standard uniform time-point sampling in consistency training by biasing the anchor toward the terminal region, a change grounded in a formal propagation-efficiency analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of FCO and LAS is practically significant: they improve DP3 by 2.8% and FlowPolicy by 7.9% without architectural changes, suggesting broad applicability across flow- and diffusion-based manipulation policies. The paper's limitation section acknowledges that it does not address high-dimensional decision-making, suboptimal demonstrations, or out-of-distribution generalization, leaving open whether the foresight-aware training paradigm transfers to language-conditioned or multi-task settings. A key open question is whether the frequency-domain multi-chunk supervision scales to much longer horizons (e.g., 10+ chunks) without the oversmoothing observed at N=5, and whether the approach remains effective when expert demonstrations are noisy or suboptimal rather than heuristic-policy-generated.
