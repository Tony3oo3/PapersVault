## Problem

Existing contextual dueling bandit algorithms assume that arm utility is fully determined by pre-serving features and that feedback arrives instantly and uncorrupted. In practice, critical post-serving contexts (e.g., delivery time, driver conduct) are only revealed after the decision, feedback suffers unknown stochastic or adversarial delays, and observations may be adversarially corrupted. These three challenges interact multiplicatively—delays reduce effective sample size, amplifying the leverage of each corrupted observation—yet no prior work addresses them simultaneously in the preference-based (dueling) setting.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives pre-serving context vectors x_{t,k} ∈ R^{d_x} for K arms and must select a pair (a_t, b_t) before observing the post-serving contexts y_{t,k}. A neural approximator φ̂_t predicts the unobserved post-serving features, forming estimated joint features ẑ_{t,k} = (x_{t,k}, φ̂_t(x_{t,k})) used for arm selection. Feedback (binary preference outcomes) arrives after an unknown delay τ_t and may be flipped by an adversary within a total corruption budget C; the algorithm is agnostic to whether delays are sub-Gaussian stochastic or adversarially budgeted. The theoretical output is a cumulative regret bound Õ(d(√T + C + D)) where D = max(Λ^{1/2}, μ_τ) adapts to the delay regime.

## Method

RCDP-UCB combines three mechanisms: (1) an MLP-based approximator trained on a replay buffer of observed (x, y) pairs to predict post-serving contexts at decision time; (2) a weighted MLE estimator where each sample receives an adaptive weight ω_s = min(1, α/‖Δz_s‖_{Ṽ_{s-1}^{-1}}) that clips high-leverage observations to simultaneously bound corruption bias (αC) and delay-induced bias (αD); and (3) a UCB arm-selection rule with exploration width c_t = 2β_t over the estimated features. The key technical insight is anchoring the weighting mechanism to the full information design matrix Ṽ_t (constructed from all chronological contexts, available at action-selection time) rather than the delay-dependent observed matrix W̃_t. This 'information-geometric decoupling' makes weights a priori—determined before any delay realization—so that corruption and delay errors decompose additively rather than multiplicatively, yielding the unified regret bound Õ(d(√T + C + D)) with best-of-both-worlds scaling in the delay term.

## Results

RCDP-UCB achieves a cumulative regret upper bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d(\sqrt{T}+\mathcal{C}+\mathcal{D}))$, where $\mathcal{D}=\max(\Lambda^{1/2},\mu_\tau)$, matching the information-theoretic lower bound $\Omega((d\sqrt{T}+d\mathcal{C}+\max\{\sqrt{d\Lambda},d\mu_\tau\})/\kappa)$ up to a $\sqrt{d}$ factor in the adversarial delay regime. Empirically, on synthetic dueling bandit environments ($d_x=10$, $K=10$, $T=2000$, $\mathcal{C}=25$, $\Lambda=10^4$, $\mu_\tau=100$), RCDP-UCB consistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all tested post-serving mappings (Polynomial, Sinusoidal, Absolute) and both delay regimes (strategic and stochastic), outperforming all four baselines in every configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCDP-UCB is compared against four baselines: RCDB (Di et al., 2025), ColSTIM (Bengs et al., 2022), MaxInP (Saha, 2021), and MaxPairUCB (Di et al., 2023). In the linear setting without post-serving contexts (Figure 1), RCDP-UCB consistently outperforms RCDB under both strategic (adversarial) and stochastic delays, confirming that the unified $\mathcal{C}+\mathcal{D}$ budget in the adaptive weighting (Eq. 4) effectively mitigates both corruption and delay simultaneously. In the full setting with learned post-serving contexts (Figure 2), where all algorithms are augmented to learn $\phi_*$, RCDP-UCB achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all three mapping types (Absolute, Polynomial, Sinusoidal) and both delay regimes, with the gap widening over time. In the asymmetric setting (Figure 3) where only RCDP-UCB exploits the learned post-serving mapping while baselines are restricted to pre-serving contexts, RCDP-UCB still dominates, highlighting the critical advantage of explicitly modeling latent post-serving dynamics. Notably, RCDB—the closest competitor—handles corruption but lacks delay-awareness, while ColSTIM, MaxInP, and MaxPairUCB have no robustness mechanisms at all, making them particularly vulnerable under the combined corruption-and-delay stress.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCDP-UCB unifies three previously separate research threads in contextual dueling bandits: robustness to adversarial corruption (building on RCDB's uncertainty-weighted MLE from Di et al., 2025), delayed feedback analysis (following Howson et al., 2023 for sub-Gaussian delays and Ito et al., 2020 for adversarial delays), and post-serving context learning (adopting Wang et al., 2024's framework and generalized elliptical potential lemma). The key methodological departure from RCDB is anchoring the adaptive clipping weights to the full chronological design matrix V_t (which incorporates all contexts instantaneously) rather than the delay-dependent observed matrix W_t, thereby decoupling corruption and delay penalties into an additive C+D budget rather than a multiplicative CD term. The arm-selection and estimation machinery follows the standard weighted MLE / UCB paradigm established by Bengs et al. (2022) and Saha (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously handle post-serving contexts, unknown delays, and adversarial corruption in linear dueling bandits, a setting directly relevant to RLHF pipelines and recommendation systems where feedback is relative, delayed, and potentially manipulated. The delay-regime-agnostic guarantee (no need to know whether delays are stochastic or adversarial) has practical value in deployment where the delay mechanism is opaque. Open questions include extending to delayed post-serving contexts (where the mapping itself is only revealed late), closing the √d gap between upper and lower bounds in the adversarial delay regime, and establishing tight lower bounds that explicitly account for the learnability of the post-serving mapping. The current analysis requires a parametric or parametrically-learnable mapping φ*, limiting applicability to highly non-parametric post-serving dynamics.
