## Problem

Sparse attention methods for video Diffusion Transformers exploit token-pair redundancy but are bottlenecked by non-sparse attention heads whose attention mass is too dispersed to be captured by small top-k selections, capping achievable speedups. The paper identifies a pervasive yet overlooked redundancy specific to video DiTs—frame-block similarity, where attention weight blocks within the same block-row or block-column exhibit highly similar distributions that can be approximated by lightweight linear calibration—providing an orthogonal handle for accelerating these non-sparse heads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in attention replacement for existing video DiTs (Wan2.1-1.3B generating 5-second 480p videos, CogVideoX1.5-5B generating 5-second 720p videos), taking a text prompt and producing a generated video through 50 denoising steps. Internally, it operates on latent video tokens arranged as n frames × ℓ tokens per frame (e.g., 21×1560 for Wan2.1-1.3B, 11×4080 for CogVideoX1.5-5B), and for MM-DiT backbones it handles joint text-video attention by keeping text interactions exact while applying the Echo operator only to the video-video sub-block.

## Method

EchoAttention introduces a dual-operator architecture with a two-level gating router: a Sparse operator (block-sparse attention with top-k selection) for naturally sparse heads, and an Echo operator (Echo-Row or Echo-Col) for non-sparse heads that exploits frame-block similarity by computing a single prototype attention block per row/column and recovering remaining blocks via a learned diagonal calibration matrix, reducing dominant complexity from O(n²ℓ²d) to O(nℓ²d). The calibration weights are parameterized by low-dimensional projections (d_proj=16) of frame-summary queries and token-wise keys, with global row normalization to preserve probability mass. A three-stage distillation schedule (soft mixing → straight-through discretization → hard routing) trains the router and operators at timestep-layer-head granularity from a full-attention teacher, with LoRA fine-tuning of linear layers. Custom Triton kernels fuse the three operator branches into a single batched kernel for end-to-end acceleration.

## Results

On Wan2.1-1.3B, EchoAttention achieves 1.97× end-to-end speedup (92s vs. 181s full attention) while matching full-attention VBench score (83.2%) at 95% sparsity, with PSNR 19.38 and LPIPS 0.2661. On CogVideoX1.5-5B, it reaches 2.42× speedup (318s vs. 770s) with VBench 82.0% (slightly exceeding full attention's 81.9%) at 90% sparsity, PSNR 20.19, and LPIPS 0.2391. EchoAttention consistently dominates the Pareto speed-quality frontier, degrading more gracefully at higher speedups than any purely sparse baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoAttention outperforms all three sparse-attention baselines (VSA, SLA, and SparseAttn) on both VBench quality and end-to-end speedup across both models. On Wan2.1-1.3B, EchoAttention achieves VBench 83.2% at 1.97× speedup, surpassing SLA (82.8%, 1.81×), VSA (82.2%, 1.72×), and both SparseAttn variants (81.9–82.7%, 1.56–1.79×). On CogVideoX1.5-5B, the gap widens: EchoAttention reaches 2.42× speedup with VBench 82.0%, compared to SLA (81.5%, 2.19×), VSA (81.2%, 2.16×), and SparseAttn-Sparse (81.0%, 2.19×). The key distinction is that sparse baselines face a hard trade-off governed by non-sparse heads—SparseAttn-Dense must retain ~80% of token pairs to preserve quality (limiting speedup to 1.56×), while aggressive sparsity (SparseAttn-Sparse) degrades VBench by 1.3–0.9 points. EchoAttention avoids this ceiling by routing non-sparse heads to the Echo operator, achieving higher sparsity (95% on Wan, 90% on CogVideoX) without the quality penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-attention-for-video-DiTs line (VSA, SLA, SparseAttn, SVG) by identifying an orthogonal structural redundancy—frame-block similarity—that prior methods overlook. While prior work exploits token-pair redundancy (pruning query-key pairs), EchoAttention introduces an Echo operator that reuses a single prototype frame-block per row/column and recovers the rest via lightweight diagonal calibration, reducing complexity from O(n²ℓ²d) to O(nℓ²d). The three-stage distillation with a two-level gating router (Sparse vs. Echo-Row vs. Echo-Col) is a novel training paradigm for learning per-head, per-timestep operator selection, departing from the unified-sparse-mechanism assumption of all prior baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EchoAttention breaks the speed-quality ceiling that purely sparse methods hit on non-sparse heads, achieving up to 2.42× end-to-end speedup with near-zero VBench degradation on CogVideoX1.5-5B. The frame-block similarity observation provides a reusable structural insight for video DiTs that could inform future attention designs beyond this specific operator. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to autoregressive or streaming video generation, whether the 3000-step distillation + LoRA fine-tuning overhead is acceptable for rapidly evolving model families, and whether the fixed 0-index prototype choice remains optimal at much longer temporal scales (n >> 21).
