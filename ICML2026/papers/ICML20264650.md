## Problem

Given a trained neural network and a score vector derived from any pruning criterion, there is no principled, criterion-agnostic rule for determining how many components to retain. Existing approaches rely on fixed sparsity budgets, expensive iterative search, or per-layer hyperparameter tuning, all of which are sensitive to the chosen threshold and architecture. This paper asks: can the sparsity budget be derived directly from the score distribution itself?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264650/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a score vector s ∈ ℝ^N (e.g., absolute weight magnitudes, Taylor importance values, attention head scores, KAN node scores, or even per-pixel deviations from channel means) computed from a trained network, and produces a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^N that retains exactly N_eff (or β·N_eff) top-scoring entries. The score vector is first normalized to a probability vector ω_i = |s_i|/Σ|s_i|, so only relative magnitudes matter; for criteria where sign carries meaning (positive = retain, negative = remove), a monotone nonnegativity map must be applied before invoking EMP. The algorithm runs in O(N log N) time via a single top-k selection.

## Method

EMP computes the effective sample size N_eff = ⌊1/Σω_i²⌋ (the inverse Simpson index) from the normalized score distribution and retains the top N_eff scoring components, discarding the rest. The key theoretical contribution is a tight lower bound on the effective mass s_eff (the sum of retained normalized scores): s_eff ≥ N_eff/N + (N−N_eff)/N · √((N−N_eff−1)/((N_eff+1)(N−1))), derived via a geometric optimization over the simplex. This bound, combined with a Hessian-based loss-change lemma, yields an explicit upper bound on the performance drop ε between the dense and pruned model that decays rapidly as sparsity increases. The approach is entirely criterion- and architecture-agnostic: it requires no second-order information, no training, and no per-layer scheduling, reducing the pruning decision to a single distributional statistic with an optional scaling factor β ∈ [0.5, 2] for deployment-specific sparsity targets.

## Results

Across all tested CNN and FC architectures (FC5, FC12, AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet18, ResNet50 on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet), the loss change ε between the dense model and the EMP-pruned model at β=1 remains negligible (ε ≤ 0.105), with sparsity ranging from 42.9% to 62.2%. On LLMs (LLaMA 7B–70B, LLaMA-2 7B–70B), EMP-Magnitude at ~37% sparsity achieves an average ΔPPL of +0.752 and ΔAcc of −0.93%, compared to fixed 50% magnitude pruning's ΔPPL of +2.982 and ΔAcc of −2.60%. The coefficient β=1 consistently marks the transition point where pruning beyond N_eff causes performance degradation across all architectures and criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fixed 50% magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2015), EMP-Magnitude dramatically reduces degradation: average ΔPPL drops from +2.982 to +0.752 and ΔAcc from −2.60% to −0.93%, at a lower sparsity of ~37% vs. 50%. Against Wanda (Sun et al., 2023), EMP-Wanda achieves a slightly better average ΔPPL (+0.678 vs. +0.799) and comparable accuracy (−1.37% vs. −1.40%) while pruning less aggressively (~40% vs. 50%). On VGG16 (CIFAR-10), EMP-Magnitude achieves 59.5% sparsity with only 0.14 pp accuracy drop (90.98% vs. 91.12% dense), while EMP-Saliency (Molchanov et al., 2016) reaches 25.7% sparsity with 0.15 pp drop. For Taylor/loss-change pruning (LeCun et al., 1989), EMP identifies that only 2.1% of weights are truly redundant, yielding zero accuracy loss but minimal sparsity gain—indicating this criterion's scores are nearly uniform. The key finding is that EMP 'rehabilitates' plain magnitude pruning in the LLM regime: the main failure mode of magnitude at 50% is the rigid global budget, not the score itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the effective sample size (ESS) from particle filtering and the inverse Simpson index from ecology as a universal pruning threshold, a connection not previously drawn in the model compression literature. It builds directly on the fundamental-limit framework of Zhang et al. (2023), whose Lemma 3.5 bounds loss change in terms of sparsity ratio, to derive a provable upper bound on performance degradation when pruning to N_eff components. Unlike prior approaches that prescribe fixed sparsity budgets, iterative search (RigL), or per-layer heuristics, EMP derives the retention count entirely from the score distribution itself, making it criterion-agnostic and architecture-agnostic by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that EMP eliminates the most tedious hyperparameter in post-training pruning—the global sparsity ratio—replacing it with a data-adaptive threshold that requires no tuning. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.2) provides a rare provable bound on retained importance mass, though the loss-change upper bound is currently derived only for magnitude pruning and does not extend to sensitivity-based criteria. Open questions include whether EMP can be integrated during training (adaptive feature pruning), whether it interacts favorably with attention-sink phenomena in LLMs, and whether the β=1 transition point holds under fine-tuning or at substantially larger model scales beyond 70B parameters.
