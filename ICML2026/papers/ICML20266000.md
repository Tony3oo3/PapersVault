## Problem

Existing graph class-incremental learning (GCIL) methods assume a closed-set test distribution drawn only from previously seen classes, which contradicts real-world open-ended deployments where unknown classes inevitably emerge. The paper identifies that this failure stems from catastrophic representation drift: as the GNN encoder adapts to new tasks, the geometric manifolds of historical classes are progressively eroded, causing unknown samples to receive overconfident predictions indistinguishable from known ones. No prior GCIL method simultaneously addresses cross-task forgetting and reliable open-set rejection under a strict no-replay constraint.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of task-specific graphs (node feature matrices and adjacency matrices) under a no-replay constraint, where only the current task's data is accessible during training. At inference, for each query node it produces three outputs: a task routing index (which seen task's classifier to use), a class label prediction from that task's head, and a scalar energy score that triggers unknown rejection when it exceeds a threshold δ. The per-task fingerprints (a normalized centroid and a low-rank orthonormal subspace basis) are the only stored artifacts from past tasks, keeping memory overhead minimal.

## Method

SAFER decouples a frozen pretrained GNN anchor (which provides a stable coordinate system immune to drift) from lightweight task-specific adaptation modules (node-adaptive prompts and a metric-space adapter). After each task, it constructs a drift-resilient geometric fingerprint by computing the normalized centroid and top-r PCA basis of the anchor embeddings, approximating the task distribution as an affine subspace. At inference, the query is projected onto each candidate task's subspace; the reconstruction residual plus a centroid-distance penalty forms a unified energy score that simultaneously routes the query to the best-matching task (argmin) and rejects it as unknown (threshold). Training combines class-weighted cross-entropy, an optional focal term, and an adapter-mediated supervised contrastive loss that explicitly minimizes intra-task radii to widen the routing margin, all while the backbone parameters remain frozen.

## Results

SAFER achieves 0.0% average forgetting (AF) across all four datasets while attaining the best Average Performance (AP) on CoraFull (94.9%) and Products (95.0%), and matching the Oracle upper bound on Reddit (99.5%). On open-set metrics, SAFER reaches 100% AUROC on Arxiv, Reddit, and Products, and 97.9% on CoraFull, with OSCR values of 94.6%, 92.4%, 99.6%, and 97.1% respectively. The headline gains over the strongest baseline (MTPP/TPP) are up to 5.2% in AP (CoraFull: 94.9 vs. 93.4) and up to 31.3% in AUROC (Arxiv: 100.0 vs. 68.4), while maintaining zero forgetting under a strict no-replay constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFER outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest task-aware baseline MTPP (TPP), SAFER gains +1.5 AP on CoraFull (94.9 vs. 93.4), +5.2 AP on Arxiv (90.6 vs. 85.4), and +1.0 AP on Products (95.0 vs. 94.0), while matching TPP's 99.5 AP on Reddit; the open-set gap is far larger, with SAFER reaching 100% AUC on Arxiv/Reddit/Products versus 68.4/72.5/78.5 for MTPP, and OSCR improvements of up to +27.1 points (Reddit: 99.6 vs. 72.5). Among replay-based methods, DeLoMe is the closest competitor on AUC (92.9 on Reddit) but still trails SAFER by 7.1 points and suffers −17.3 AF on Products; DMSG and PUMA show even larger gaps (AUC 56.0 and 68.9 on Reddit, respectively). Regularization-based methods (EWC, MAS, GEM, TWP) and distillation (LwF) are far behind, with AP values below 63 and severe forgetting (AF as low as −95.2 for Fine-tune). Notably, SAFER is the only method that simultaneously achieves 0.0 AF, near-Oracle AP, and ≥92% OSCR across all four datasets, confirming that subspace-fingerprint routing provides a fundamentally more stable open-set signal than softmax confidence or prototype distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFER builds directly on the frozen-anchor-plus-lightweight-adaptation paradigm introduced by TPP (Niu et al., 2024), which demonstrated that prompt-based parameter isolation can achieve zero forgetting in replay-free GCIL. However, it departs from TPP and all prior GCIL methods by introducing a subspace-fingerprint mechanism (truncated PCA on anchor embeddings) that provides a drift-resilient geometric reference, and by unifying task routing and open-set rejection into a single energy-based residual score with explicit separation and routing-margin guarantees (Propositions 3.2–3.3). The method also draws on graph open-set recognition (EMP, EGonc) and PPR-based structural smoothing, but applies these ideas within a continual, no-replay setting that prior open-set graph work has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete failure mode of all existing GCIL methods—catastrophic overconfidence on unknown classes driven by representation drift—and provides both a diagnostic framework and a theoretically grounded remedy. The open-set GCIL evaluation protocol it introduces is likely to become a standard stress test for continual graph learners, and the subspace-fingerprint-plus-energy-score design is a reusable pattern for any setting where a frozen encoder must route queries across evolving task manifolds. The main limitation is the fully supervised requirement for fingerprint construction, which the authors acknowledge restricts applicability to label-scarce or self-supervised regimes; extending the geometric framework to semi-supervised settings is the natural next step.
