## Problem

Determining the largest admissible list size N* for k-query quantum ordered search requires solving a structured semidefinite program, but at the k=6 frontier (N ~ 10^5) the constraint matrix A demands terabytes of memory even in sparse form, making it intractable on existing GPU solvers like cuLoRADS that rely on explicit matrix construction. This memory wall has prevented any progress beyond the k=5 result (N*=7265, c≈0.390) and left the k=6 case completely unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes three scalar parameters — the number of queries k, the candidate list size N, and a low-rank factor r — from which the entire SDP constraint structure is determined; no external data is consumed. The output is a feasibility verdict (with primal infeasibility residuals to ~10^-10 for feasible cases) or a rigorous dual infeasibility certificate obtained via spectral shifting, which together bracket N* and yield the query complexity coefficient bound c = k/log₂(N*). The constraint matrices are never stored; their action is evaluated on-the-fly through custom CUDA kernels operating on the low-rank factors R_t ∈ R^{N×r}.

## Method

The paper develops a matrix-free Augmented Lagrangian solver built on the Burer-Monteiro factorization Q_t = R_t R_t^T, where the primal subproblem is solved by L-BFGS and the dominant cost is evaluating the forward operator A(X) and its adjoint A*(y)R without materializing A. The forward operator exploits the linearity of the diagonal-sum operator T_t to decompose the computation into rank-1 contributions, each computed by an independent parallel unit via reduction, reducing the working set from O(Nr) to O(N) on-chip memory. The adjoint operator is evaluated as a column-wise modified convolution, exploiting the near-circulant (Toeplitz) structure of T_t*(y) that is fully characterized by a single vector y and a parity sign, requiring only O(N) storage. For rigorous infeasibility certification, a spectral repair strategy shifts the eigenvalues of dual slack matrices upward by ε_t = max(0, -λ_min(S_t)) + δ, and a sufficient condition (Theorem 4.1) guarantees that if the dual objective exceeds the total repair cost, a strictly feasible dual solution with positive objective exists, certifying primal infeasibility. The minimum eigenvalues of the dense N×N slack matrices are estimated via matrix-free Lanczos with convolution-based matrix-vector products, all implemented in custom CUDA kernels on a single H100 GPU.

## Results

For the previously intractable k=6 instance at N=90,000, the matrix-free ALM solver converges to a relative primal infeasibility of 7.49×10⁻¹⁰ and absolute infeasibility of 1.99×10⁻¹⁰ in 94 iterations (~16 days on a single H100 GPU), confirming feasibility and yielding a new upper bound c ≤ 6/log₂(90,000) ≈ 0.365 on the quantum query complexity coefficient. Infeasibility at N=94,000 is rigorously certified via a dual infeasibility certificate (spectral shifting), bracketing the optimal list size as 90,000 ≤ N* < 94,000. The custom CUDA kernels evaluate both the forward and adjoint operators in ~71 ms using only ~560 MB of GPU memory, compared to the multiple terabytes required to explicitly store the constraint matrices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run a head-to-head solver benchmark; instead, its primary comparison is against the prior state of the art in quantum ordered search. Carolan et al. (2025) solved k=5 via an LP relaxation, achieving N*=7,265 and a query coefficient bound of c≈0.390. This paper extends the frontier to k=6, improving the bound to c≈0.365 (a ~6.4% relative tightening) by proving feasibility at N=90,000 and certifying infeasibility at N=94,000. The matrix-free approach is validated by exactly reproducing the known optimal list sizes N* for k∈{2,3,4,5}, confirming that the custom CUDA kernels preserve the original optimization landscape. In terms of computational feasibility, explicit constraint matrices at k=6, N=100,000 would require multiple terabytes of VRAM, whereas the matrix-free kernels operate within ~560 MB, making the problem solvable on a single 80 GB H100 GPU where no existing solver (cuLoRADS, ALORA, cuHALLaR) could previously operate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cuLoRADS GPU-accelerated low-rank SDP solver (Han et al., 2024b) by replacing its explicit constraint-matrix storage with custom CUDA kernels that evaluate the OSP's Toeplitz/near-circulant structure on-the-fly, shifting the bottleneck from memory to arithmetic. The SDP formulation itself originates from Childs et al. (2007), and the rank-1 solution guarantee justifying the Burer-Monteiro factorization is inherited from Dumitrescu (2007) and Carolan et al. (2025). The key conceptual departure from prior GPU solvers (LoRADS, ALORA, cuHALLaR) is that none of them avoid materializing the constraint matrix A, which becomes infeasible at the k=6 scale; this paper's matrix-free operator evaluation is the architectural novelty that unlocks the next computational frontier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result tightens the quantum query complexity coefficient for ordered search from c≈0.390 to c≈0.365, a meaningful step toward closing the gap with the theoretical lower bound of ≈0.221. The matrix-free framework is general in principle and applicable to any structured SDP where constraint matrices have exploitable algebraic form, potentially benefiting quantum information, combinatorial optimization, and other domains. Open questions include pinning down the exact N* for k=6 (conjectured in [92,000, 93,000) but not yet certified), whether the approach scales to k=7 and beyond, and extending to multi-GPU environments for even larger instances.
