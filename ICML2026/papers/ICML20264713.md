## Problem

Graph-based RAG methods suffer from two structural bottlenecks: intrinsic modularity in knowledge graphs traps retrieval within local modules (information isolation, limiting recall), and semantic-similarity-driven traversal pulls in topically related but causally irrelevant nodes (spurious noise, degrading precision). Prior attempts to incorporate causality are restricted to local or single-document settings and lack scalable cross-module causal reasoning, while popular QA benchmarks mask these flaws by rewarding entity-level hits over holistic comprehension.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query and a pre-built hierarchical knowledge graph (constructed offline from a text corpus via LLM-based entity/relation extraction, Leiden partitioning into multi-level modules, and LLM-verified causal gates between modules). It produces a grounded natural-language answer conditioned on a filtered causal support subgraph, where the subgraph contains only LLM-validated causal nodes and edges that form coherent reasoning paths from the query to the answer. The offline construction uses a Top-Down Hierarchical Pruning strategy to keep gate-verification complexity near-linear rather than quadratic in the number of modules.

## Method

CausalRAG2 operates in two phases. Offline, it builds a base entity graph, recursively partitions it into hierarchical modules via the Leiden algorithm, and uses an LLM to identify sparse binary causal gates between topologically distant but logically related modules, reducing the O(N²) verification cost through a Top-Down Hierarchical Pruning strategy that exploits the unified edge space. Online, retrieval proceeds in three steps: (1) multi-granular hybrid seeding that scores both fine-grained entities and coarse module summaries using a weighted combination of cosine embedding similarity and lexical overlap, with MMR-based diversity selection; (2) gated priority expansion via Best-First Search over a unified edge space integrating structural, hierarchical, and causal-gate edges, guided by a query-conditioned gain function that applies hop decay and assigns higher weights to causal gates and hierarchical links; and (3) spurious-aware causal path filtering, where an LLM is prompted to explicitly partition retrieved nodes into valid causal supports versus spurious associations, yielding a clean subgraph that grounds the final answer generation.

## Results

On the five standard QA benchmarks, CausalRAG2 achieves the best F1 on four of five datasets: 38.40 on MS MARCO, 49.50 on NQ, 31.97 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 64.83 on HotpotQA, while HippoRAG2 narrowly leads on QASC (14.73 vs. 13.35). CausalRAG2 also attains the highest Answer Relevancy (AR) across all five datasets (66.02, 55.09, 42.67, 49.40, 45.72) and the highest Context Recall (CR) on MS MARCO (60.48), NQ (70.36), and QASC (70.80). On the newly introduced HolisQA benchmark spanning five academic domains, CausalRAG2 achieves the best F1, CR, and AR in every domain, with particularly large margins on Medicine (F1=36.45, CR=69.91) and Business (F1=51.51, CR=67.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalRAG2 outperforms all six baselines on F1 across four of five standard QA datasets, with the largest margin on HotpotQA where it reaches 64.83 F1 versus 48.68 (LeanRAG) and 40.00 (CausalRAG). Against the closest structural competitor LeanRAG, CausalRAG2 gains +0.38 F1 on MS MARCO, +14.04 on NQ, +11.70 on 2Wiki, +0.16 on QASC, and +16.15 on HotpotQA. HippoRAG2 is the only baseline that beats CausalRAG2 on any F1 score (QASC: 14.73 vs. 13.35) and also leads on Context Recall for 2Wiki (55.53 vs. 41.95) and HotpotQA (42.06 vs. 40.30), though it trails substantially on AR in those settings. LightRAG is competitive on MS MARCO F1 (37.70 vs. 38.40) and HotpotQA CR (48.17 vs. 40.30) but falls well behind on NQ and 2Wiki. CausalRAG, the direct predecessor, is consistently outperformed by CausalRAG2 by a mean +15.5 F1 across the five datasets, with the gap scaling with graph size (up to +24.83 on HotpotQA's 2,359 modules). On the HolisQA benchmark, CausalRAG2 dominates all baselines across all three metrics in every domain, with the largest advantage over LeanRAG (the strongest HolisQA competitor) reaching +2.2 F1 and +9.5 CR on Medicine.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalRAG2 is a direct successor to CausalRAG (Wang et al., 2025a), extending its flat-graph causal reasoning with a hierarchical module structure built via Leiden partitioning and sparse causal gates that bridge topologically distant modules. It inherits the entity-relation extraction pipeline from GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2025) but departs from the broader graph-based RAG lineage (LightRAG, HippoRAG2, LeanRAG) by replacing purely semantic or community-based traversal with explicit causal gating as the cross-module mechanism. The paper also positions itself against domain-specific causal RAG work (CGMT, LACR) by targeting open-domain, noisy knowledge bases rather than rigorous causal discovery in a single domain, and introduces HolisQA to address what it identifies as an entity-centric evaluation bias in existing QA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical causal gating mechanism offers a principled, scalable way to organize knowledge for retrieval that the authors argue generalizes beyond RAG to long-context management and AI agent memory. The HolisQA benchmark is a useful contribution for evaluating holistic comprehension beyond single-entity matching, and the finding that entity-centric methods degrade sharply on it while CausalRAG2 remains robust highlights a real gap in current evaluation practice. Key open questions include whether hierarchy depth can be made task-adaptive rather than determined by Leiden convergence, whether causal gating scales to truly massive corpora beyond the ~20K-node graphs tested, and whether the approach generalizes to non-academic domains like legal or financial text.
