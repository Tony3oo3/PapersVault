## Problem

Existing Chain-of-Thought paradigms for driving VLA models face a fundamental trade-off: Textual CoT provides high-level logic but lacks fine-grained spatiotemporal understanding, while Visual CoT captures spatiotemporal evolution but introduces substantial redundancy by predicting decision-irrelevant background and texture at the pixel level. Both approaches generate large numbers of reasoning tokens, leading to substantial inference latency that is impractical for real-time driving. The paper asks whether a compact, physically grounded intermediate representation can capture the evolution of the world without the abstraction loss of text or the redundancy of dense image prediction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes the current front-view image, the front-view image from 1 second earlier, a text instruction, and the ego state as input. It first autoregressively generates 16 discrete dynamics tokens (8 per step over a 2-second horizon, split into 4 ego-centric and 4 environment-centric tokens drawn from a 128-entry VQ codebook), then generates a discrete action token sequence via the FAST tokenizer (2048-entry codebook). The full output is structured with special delimiters: <BOD> dynamics tokens <EOD> <BOA> action tokens <EOA>, ensuring the model explicitly reasons over future dynamics before committing to a trajectory.

## Method

DynVLA introduces a Dynamics Tokenizer that factorizes scene evolution into ego-centric and environment-centric dynamics using separate learnable queries and VQ codebooks, regularized by (i) an action-based loss that aligns ego-centric tokens with ground-truth ego motion to prevent entanglement, and (ii) a cross-view consistency loss requiring the same dynamics tokens to reconstruct both the future image and the future BEV map. The tokenizer is trained via reconstruction (MSE + LPIPS for image, cross-entropy for BEV) plus VQ and regularization losses. The VLA policy (built on EMU3's unified next-token prediction architecture) is then trained in two stages: SFT on structured Dynamics CoT sequences where dynamics tokens precede action tokens, followed by RFT using GRPO with a PDMS trajectory-level reward and a format reward to enforce the CoT template. This yields only 16 dynamics reasoning tokens per decision (vs. hundreds for textual or thousands for visual CoT), reducing inference latency by over an order of magnitude while improving planning quality.

## Results

On NAVSIM, DynVLA achieves a PDMS of 91.7, the highest among all evaluated methods, with NC 98.6, DAC 98.7, TTC 95.5, Comfort 100, and Ego Progress 86.8. On Bench2Drive (closed-loop), DynVLA attains a Driving Score of 88.34 and Success Rate of 72.73, outperforming the next-best method (SimLingo, DS 85.07) by over 3 points. On the 700k-frame in-house dataset, DynVLA achieves the lowest ADE (1.215 m) and Collision Rate (4.04%), surpassing DriveVLA-W0 (ViT) by 0.129 m ADE and 1.09% collision rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynVLA outperforms all 30 baselines across all three benchmarks. On NAVSIM, it beats the strongest VLA-without-CoT baseline DriveVLA-W0 (PDMS 90.2) by 1.5 points, and the best Textual CoT methods AdaThinkDrive and AutoDrive-R² (both 90.3) by 1.4 points. The gap is largest against Visual CoT methods: DynVLA surpasses PWM (88.1) by 3.6 PDMS and FSDrive (85.1) by 6.6 PDMS, while also reducing inference latency from 2.29 s (Visual CoT) to 0.37 s. On Bench2Drive, DynVLA leads the next-best closed-loop method SimLingo (DS 85.07, SR 67.27) by 3.27 DS and 5.46 SR, and outperforms the VLA baseline AutoVLA (DS 78.84, SR 57.73) by 9.5 DS. On the in-house dataset, DynVLA reduces ADE from 1.344 m (DriveVLA-W0 ViT) to 1.215 m and collision rate from 5.13% to 4.04%, confirming gains at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynVLA builds on the VLA paradigm for end-to-end driving (using EMU3 as its unified next-token-prediction backbone) and the growing line of CoT-augmented driving VLA models. It departs from Textual CoT methods (EMMA, AutoVLA, AutoDrive-R²) that reason in language space, and from Visual CoT methods (FSDrive, PWM) that predict full future frames, by introducing a compact VQ-tokenized dynamics representation as the intermediate reasoning step. The Dynamics Tokenizer draws conceptual inspiration from latent action tokenizers in robotics (LAPA, LAPO) but is specifically tailored to driving through explicit ego/environment decoupling, action-based regularization, and cross-view (image-BEV) consistency constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that 8 compact dynamics tokens can serve as an effective CoT signal that outperforms both textual and visual CoT while reducing inference latency by over an order of magnitude (0.37s vs. 2.29–3.04s), making reasoning-based driving VLA models viable for real-time deployment. The decoupling of ego-centric and environment-centric dynamics with physical regularization addresses a concrete failure mode (codebook collapse, ambiguous ego-vs-scene motion) that prior latent-token approaches in robotics did not need to handle. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond front-view-only inputs, how it behaves under severe sensor degradation (acknowledged as a failure mode), and whether the fast-slow dual-system architecture sketched in the limitations section can further reduce the frequency of dynamics reasoning without sacrificing safety.
