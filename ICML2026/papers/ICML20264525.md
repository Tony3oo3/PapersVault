## Problem

Narrow supervised fine-tuning reliably induces emergent misalignment, where a model learns its target behavior while developing harmful out-of-domain behaviors. While mechanistic interpretability has shown that misalignment is mediated by a small number of SAE features, no prior work has leveraged this to prevent misalignment during training. The paper asks whether targeted, training-time constraints on these causally identified features can suppress emergent misalignment without degrading in-domain task performance or generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method requires a base model checkpoint, a misaligned checkpoint (obtained by standard SFT on the narrow domain), a pre-trained frozen SAE at a chosen layer (Goodfire SAE at layer 20, ~131k latents), and 44 domain-agnostic core misalignment prompts for offline feature discovery. During training, the frozen base model is run in parallel on each SFT batch to provide input-matched reference activations under no_grad. The output is a fine-tuned model (LoRA, rank 16) with suppressed emergent misalignment, plus a reusable causal latent set K of size 20 with per-latent directionality labels that can be transferred across domains without re-running discovery.

## Method

BLOCK-EM has two phases. First, a three-stage offline pipeline identifies a small causal latent set K: (1) model-diffing activation shifts between base and misaligned checkpoints to form a sign-aware candidate pool, (2) causal screening via induce-and-repair steering (steering the base model to elicit misalignment and the misaligned model to repair it), and (3) per-latent calibration under a 10% incoherence budget to rank and select the final set with directionality labels. Second, during SFT, a one-sided, base-anchored penalty L_block is added to the standard SFT loss: for each latent in K+, it penalizes ReLU(z_θ − z_base)², and for K−, ReLU(z_base − z_θ)², averaged over supervised tokens. The penalty is feature-specific (only K), sign-aware (directional), and one-sided (inactive unless fine-tuning amplifies the misalignment-associated direction), so it selectively blocks misalignment amplification while leaving all other representational changes unconstrained. The constraint is applied at a single intermediate layer (layer 20 of 32) via the SAE encoder, and the paper shows that freezing downstream layers further improves the safety-quality trade-off.

## Results

Across six fine-tuning domains, BLOCK-EM at λ=13×10³ achieves a 93% relative reduction in emergent misalignment (from ~40% under standard SFT to ~2.8%), with only a 2.72% absolute increase in incoherence and a 4.14% relative decrease in in-domain task adherence. In the primary finance domain, λ=10³ cuts misalignment from 40% to 21% with negligible incoherence, while λ=10⁵ reaches near-baseline misalignment (2.8%) at the cost of 12% incoherence. With downstream layers frozen, the trade-off improves further: misalignment drops from 38% to 3% while incoherence remains near baseline. In the strongest ablation variant (VALIDREDUC-ALL, |K|=42, λ=10⁴), BLOCK-EM achieves a 97% relative EM reduction with 5.75% incoherence and a 40.37% *increase* in in-domain task performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLOCK-EM is compared against four baselines: inoculation prompting (Wichers et al., 2025), preventative steering (Chen et al., 2025), test-time steering (SAE- and linear-probe-based), and KL-divergence regularization (Kaczér et al., 2025). Across the full λ sweep and all six domains, BLOCK-EM consistently achieves a stronger safety-utility trade-off than every baseline: it delivers larger normalized emergent-misalignment reductions at comparable or better in-domain adherence (Figure 7). KL regularization, the closest competitor, reduces misalignment but at a sharper cost to in-domain adherence and SFT loss for comparable safety gains (Figure 18). Inoculation prompting and preventative steering show weaker misalignment suppression overall, while test-time steering (applied post-hoc at inference) cannot match the training-time constraint's ability to prevent the misaligned representation from forming in the first place. The paper does not report exact per-baseline numeric values in the main text; the comparison is presented as a Pareto-frontier scatter plot (Figure 7) where BLOCK-EM dominates the other methods across the sweep.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges mechanistic interpretability and practical alignment by converting inference-time causal steering results (Wang et al., 2025; Bricken et al., 2024) into a training-time constraint. Prior training-time defenses against emergent misalignment—KL regularization, SafeLoRA, inoculation prompting, and preventative steering—either apply global output-level penalties or modify training data, without targeting the specific internal features that causally mediate misalignment. BLOCK-EM departs from these by imposing a one-sided, base-anchored, sign-aware penalty on a small set of SAE latents identified via a three-stage causal screening pipeline (activation diffing → induce-and-repair steering → calibrated ranking), making the intervention feature-specific and directional rather than broad.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that constraining just ~20 SAE latents at a single intermediate layer during fine-tuning can reduce emergent misalignment by 93% across six diverse domains while preserving in-domain task performance, and that the same latent set transfers across domains and model architectures (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen-2.5-7B). A key open question is the re-emergence of misalignment under prolonged training, which activation patching localizes to upstream rerouting around the blocked latents—suggesting that multi-layer or adaptive blocking strategies are needed for robustness. The released latent sets lower the barrier to adoption, but the single-layer, fixed-set design and the re-emergence limitation mean the approach is a partial rather than complete solution.
