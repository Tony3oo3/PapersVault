## Problem

Existing sleep analysis systems are confined to closed label spaces (predefined stages or events) and cannot describe, query, or generalize to novel sleep phenomena, while general-purpose LLMs fail to interpret high-dimensional continuous physiological signals. This paper addresses the gap by building the first sleep-language foundation model that enables open-ended, language-grounded interpretation and interaction with raw polysomnography data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265252/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 30-second PSG epochs across 12 standardized channels (EEG, EOG, ECG, EMG, respiration, SpO2, body position) sampled at 64 Hz, with Heart Rate and SpO2 deliberately excluded from the encoder input to force cross-channel inference. A modality condition token (Brain, Respiratory, Cardiac, Somatic) is prepended to the text decoder to target generation at a specific physiological system. Outputs span a hierarchy from fine-grained channel statistics and event timestamps to global sleep-stage captions, cross-modal retrieval rankings, and aggregated full-night clinical reports (AHI, WASO, sleep efficiency).

## Method

SleepLM's core architecture, ReCoCa, pairs a channel-specific sleep encoder (interleaved temporal attention with sinusoidal RoPE and channel attention with shared learnable RoPE over the sensor dimension) with a lightweight signal reconstruction decoder and a modality-conditioned autoregressive text decoder. Training uses a compound objective combining symmetric InfoNCE contrastive alignment, MSE signal reconstruction, and autoregressive caption generation, with the reconstruction term acting as a regularizer that prevents the encoder from discarding waveform morphology not explicitly described in sparse text captions. Supervision comes from a multilevel captioning pipeline producing channel-level (band power, respiratory periodicity), local-level (event onset/offset timestamps), and global-level (sleep stage, autonomic descriptors derived from withheld signals) descriptions for each epoch. By toggling loss weights, the framework instantiates CLIP, Cap, and CoCa as special cases, while the full ReCoCa configuration consistently outperforms all variants across classification, regression, retrieval, and generation tasks.

## Results

On zero-shot sleep staging (averaged over SHHS, MrOS, CFS), SleepLM achieves 85.4 AUC and 76.9 balanced accuracy, outperforming the best fine-tuned VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B at 70.2/51.3) and proprietary LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 52.2/24.5). For sleep event localization, SleepLM reaches 30.4 IoU versus 13.5 for Qwen3-VL and 2.8 for Gemini 2.5 Pro. On channel-statistics regression, SleepLM achieves 3.15 sMAPE compared to 34.62 for Qwen3-VL, and on SpO2 trend detection it reaches 39.1% recall versus 18.4% for the best VLM baseline. In few-shot linear probing on the held-out WSC cohort, SleepLM attains 88.3 AUC at 50 samples per class for 5-class sleep staging, surpassing MAE (70.0), SimCLR (47.2), and supervised ViT (74.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SleepLM decisively outperforms both fine-tuned VLM baselines (Qwen3-VL-8B and LLaVA-Next) across all zero-shot tasks, with the largest margins on sleep staging (+15.2 AUC over Qwen3-VL) and channel-statistics regression (3.15 vs. 34.62 sMAPE). Against proprietary LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-R1), SleepLM dominates on sleep staging, event detection, and channel statistics, but the LLMs retain a narrow edge on SpO₂ MAE (DeepSeek-R1: 1.88 vs. SleepLM: 2.24) and are competitive on HR MAE (Gemini: 2.01 vs. SleepLM: 1.97), reflecting their ability to apply algorithmic derivations from tabular inputs. In few-shot linear probing on WSC, SleepLM (ReCoCa) surpasses all SSL baselines (MAE, SimCLR) and supervised architectures (ViT, WideResNet) at every shot level, reaching 88.3 AUC at 50-shot for sleep staging. Against domain-specific models under full fine-tuning on CFS, SleepLM is competitive with Chronos-2 (87.2 vs. 87.6 AUC on sleep staging) and outperforms SleepFM (87.2 vs. 85.4), RobustSleepNet (87.2 vs. 85.8), and U-Sleep (87.2 vs. 83.8), while also leading on arousal detection (89.2 AUC). On unseen-concept generalization (Mixed/Obstructive Apnea), SleepLM achieves ~80% F1 while Gemini 2.5 Pro performs at chance (15–33% F1).

## Relation to Prior Work

SleepLM extends the CLIP/CoCa contrastive-plus-captioning paradigm from vision to dense multi-channel physiological time series, adding a signal reconstruction objective (ReCoCa) to preserve waveform fidelity that sparse text supervision alone would discard. It builds directly on SensorLM's language-signal alignment framework for wearable data but adapts it to the fixed 12-channel PSG montage via interleaved temporal and channel attention with RoPE, and introduces a multilevel captioning pipeline (channel → local → global) that has no direct analogue in prior sleep or time-series work. Unlike existing sleep foundation models (SleepFM, U-Sleep, RobustSleepNet) that are unimodal and confined to closed label spaces, SleepLM positions itself as the first to enable open-ended language-grounded interpretation of raw PSG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100K+ hour paired sleep-text dataset and the ReCoCa architecture together establish a new paradigm for sleep analysis: moving from fixed-label classification to natural-language description, querying, and interaction. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen apnea subtypes (clustering near semantically related seen concepts in embedding space) suggests the model learns latent physiological taxonomies rather than memorizing label boundaries, which could accelerate clinical triage and reduce manual scoring burden. Key open questions include clinical validation across diverse populations and devices, whether the captioning pipeline's template-based generation limits semantic richness, and whether the approach transfers to other dense multi-sensor modalities beyond sleep.
