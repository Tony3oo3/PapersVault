## Problem

Large language models consistently fail to memorize facts at their theoretical capacity limit, especially when training data contains too many facts or exhibits skewed (power-law) frequency distributions. It was unclear whether this gap is a fundamental theoretical constraint or an artifact of suboptimal training data distributions, and whether principled data selection could close it. This paper formalizes fact memorization information-theoretically, proves a capacity limit on accurate fact count, and shows that standard training is suboptimal precisely when data information exceeds model capacity or fact frequencies are non-uniform.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an annotated text corpus where individual facts are marked with boundary tokens (e.g., <|start_of_fact|>...<|end_of_fact|>), a GPT2-style transformer architecture, and a scalar data selection ratio α. It outputs a trained language model whose fact accuracy is measured as the expected fraction of correctly predicted fact answers under stochastic decoding. A key constraint is that selection operates at the fact level rather than the token level, and for general corpora (Algorithm 2) non-fact tokens are always retained while selected fact-answer tokens are upweighted to preserve the relative fact-to-non-fact token ratio.

## Method

The paper contributes a theoretical capacity bound and a practical loss-based selection algorithm. Theoretically, it defines fact memorization as mutual information between fact values and the trained model, applies Fano's inequality to lower-bound this memorization by per-fact accuracy (Theorem 3.6), and derives that a model with discrete parameter space W can accurately answer at most O(ln|W|/b) facts of entropy b beyond a correlation baseline (Corollary 3.7). Empirically, it shows via synthetic phonebook experiments that standard training falls below this limit whenever total fact information exceeds capacity or the fact frequency distribution is power-law skewed. The proposed selection algorithm (Algorithm 1/2) uses per-fact training loss as a proxy for fact frequency and entropy: LossH retains only facts whose loss falls below an online α-percentile threshold (head selection, limiting facts to model capacity), while LossHF additionally downsamples very low-loss facts proportionally to their loss (flattening, reducing excessive repetitions). Three design choices are critical: selection at the fact level (not token level), recomputing the loss percentile threshold online each batch, and using sum-of-loss rather than average loss to better calibrate to fact entropy. On a fact-annotated Wikipedia corpus, this enables a 110M-parameter GPT2-Small model to memorize 1.3× more entity facts than standard training, matching a 10× larger 1.3B model trained on the full dataset.

## Results

On the annotated Wikipedia corpus (3B tokens), LossH-Wiki selection with α=0.2 boosts test fact accuracy of a 110M GPT2-Small model to 929.25 accurate facts (out of 7135 test facts), a 1.3× improvement over full-dataset training (696.11) and matching the fact accuracy of a 10× larger 1.3B model trained on the full dataset. On synthetic power-law phonebook benchmarks, LossHF selection consistently boosts accurate fact count to the theoretical capacity limit of ~3.01M facts for the 110M model, whereas full-dataset training drops to near-zero when the number of facts exceeds capacity. General NLU accuracy (CommonsenseQA, HellaSwag, PIQA, SIQA, ARC-Easy) remains stable at ~0.36 across all selection ratios, confirming no degradation in general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its loss-based selection (LossH-Wiki) against random fact-level pruning and full-dataset training. At the optimal selection ratio α=0.2, LossH-Wiki achieves 929.25 accurate facts versus 647.33 for random pruning and 696.11 for full-dataset training (α=1.0), a 33% improvement over the full-data baseline. Random pruning monotonically degrades fact accuracy as α decreases (from 696.11 at α=1.0 to 600.55 at α=0.1), confirming that naive data reduction without loss-awareness is harmful. The 110M model with α=0.2 selection matches the test fact accuracy of a 1.3B model (10× larger) trained on the full dataset, and the MMLU-Knowledge accuracy at α=0.5 is within one standard deviation of a 335M model (3× larger) on full data. NLU accuracy remains essentially unchanged (~0.36) across all selection ratios for both methods, indicating the gains are specific to fact memorization without collateral damage to general language understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the information-theoretic memorization framework of Allen-Zhu & Li (2024) and Morris et al. (2025), which established the 2 bits/parameter capacity limit, but introduces a new notion of 'fact memorization' (mutual information between fact values and the model) and proves via Fano's inequality a tighter connection to the stricter fact-accuracy metric rather than loss. It departs from prior data selection methods like Rho-1 and Rho-Loss by selecting low-loss (frequent/easy) facts rather than high-loss ones, arguing that the goal here is capacity-aware memorization rather than generalization. The synthetic phonebook benchmarks follow Jelassi et al. (2024) and Allen-Zhu & Li (2024), while the real-world validation extends to annotated Wikipedia and arXiv author-title facts, bridging the gap between controlled theory and practical pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that a 110m-parameter model with loss-based data selection matches a 1.3B model on fact accuracy, suggesting that practitioners should curate training data to match model capacity rather than indiscriminately scaling both data and parameters. However, the method requires annotated fact boundaries (available for Wikipedia via Zhao et al. 2025b but not for most real-world corpora), and the paper acknowledges that designing the right selection unit for unstructured data remains open. Future directions include adapting the selection to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during continual learning, extending to compositional/multi-hop knowledge, and exploring whether architecture changes (MoE, memory modules) can further boost the capacity limit itself.
