## Problem

Group-based RLVR methods like GRPO estimate reward distributions via point estimation from a small number of rollouts per prompt, leading to high variance, variance collapse (zero training signal when all responses receive identical rewards), and severe sample inefficiency. Existing remedies—dynamic sampling, probabilistic filtering, or replay with importance sampling—either multiply rollout costs or introduce substantial GPU memory overhead, leaving a gap for a lightweight estimator that improves reward estimation without changing the sampling strategy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the binary correctness reward (0/1) from each of N rollouts per prompt plus two scalar state variables (α, β) tracking the per-prompt Beta posterior across training epochs. It outputs a posterior mean and variance of the reward distribution, which are used to compute per-response advantage estimates broadcast to all tokens. No token-level log-probabilities, full trajectories, or additional forward passes are required, keeping memory and compute identical to vanilla GRPO.

## Method

The paper reformulates RLVR reward estimation as Bayesian inference: each binary reward is a Bernoulli draw from a policy-induced distribution, and the advantage computation becomes estimating that distribution from finite samples. The proposed Discounted Beta-Bernoulli (DBB) estimator maintains a per-prompt Beta posterior, updated each epoch by discounting the previous parameters by a factor λ∈(0,1] before adding the current epoch's successes and failures (α_τ = λα_{τ-1} + S_τ, β_τ = λβ_{τ-1} + N − S_τ). This introduces a controlled bias (shrinkage toward the historical posterior mean) that substantially reduces estimator variance and, because the Beta posterior variance αβ/(α+β)² is strictly positive, theoretically eliminates variance collapse. The discount factor λ balances bias against variance under the non-stationary reward distribution induced by on-policy training, with smaller λ favored for faster-evolving (smaller) models and larger λ for more stationary (larger) models. The estimator is model-agnostic and plugs into any group-based RLVR algorithm that uses point reward estimation, as demonstrated with both GRPO and Dr.GRPO.

## Results

GRPO-DBB achieves average in-distribution Acc@8 of 51.31 (Qwen3-8B-Base) and 29.98 (Qwen3-1.7B-Base), improving over naive GRPO by +2.32 and +3.43 points respectively. On out-of-distribution benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, BBH), GRPO-DBB gains +8.34 and +10.05 Acc@8 points over GRPO on the 8B and 1.7B models. All improvements are statistically significant (p<0.05) across nearly all benchmark-model combinations over four independent runs, with no additional computational cost or memory overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRPO-DBB outperforms all five baselines on both model scales. Against naive GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), it gains +3.43/+2.32 ID and +10.05/+8.34 OOD Acc@8 points on 1.7B/8B models, with the largest single-benchmark gain being +16.97 on BBH (1.7B). Against the replay-based RePO (Li et al., 2025), GRPO-DBB wins on all benchmarks except BBH on 1.7B (−0.70, not significant), while avoiding RePO's substantial GPU memory overhead. Against GRESO (Zheng et al., 2025b), GRPO-DBB leads on all benchmarks except GPQA-D on 8B (+0.90, p=0.18, not significant), and notably GRESO's OOD advantage on MMLU-Pro/BBH is fully matched or exceeded. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025a), GRPO-DBB wins on every single benchmark across both scales, with the largest gap being +8.00 on AIME25 (8B). Against Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), applying DBB to its advantage formulation yields a consistent +1.96 average ID Acc@8 gain on 1.7B, confirming the estimator's generality beyond standard GRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO's group-relative advantage framework but reframes the underlying reward estimation problem through a Bayesian lens, replacing the standard point estimator (empirical mean of N binary rewards) with a Discounted Beta-Bernoulli posterior that tracks per-prompt reward statistics across training epochs. It departs from variance-collapse-aware methods like DAPO and GRESO, which modify the sampling or filtering strategy, and from replay-based methods like RePO, which store and reuse full trajectories with importance sampling. Instead, DBB addresses both variance collapse and sample inefficiency at the estimator level by maintaining only two scalar parameters (α, β) per prompt, requiring no additional rollouts, memory, or forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a minimal estimator-level change (tracking a discounted Beta posterior) consistently outperforms more complex sampling-strategy modifications (dynamic sampling, probabilistic filtering) and replay-based approaches across all benchmarks and model scales suggests that reward distribution estimation is an underexploited lever in RLVR. The substantially larger OOD gains (10+ points on 1.7B) compared to ID gains hint that the Bayesian uncertainty modeling improves generalization, not just in-distribution accuracy. Open questions include whether a dynamic or learned discount factor λ can eliminate the need for manual tuning, whether the approach extends to continuous or dense process rewards (e.g., via a discounted Gaussian likelihood), and whether the benefits persist at 30B+ scale where the reward distribution may be more stationary.
