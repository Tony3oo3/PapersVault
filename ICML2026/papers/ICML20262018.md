## Problem

Existing table encoders either infer header semantics locally within individual tables or treat headers as surface-level tokens, failing to explicitly model how domain-specialized attribute semantics emerge jointly from schema-level structural evidence (co-occurring headers) and column-level distributional evidence (value realizations across rows and tables). Moreover, they apply uniform representation objectives across all columns, overlooking that attributes assume heterogeneous semantic roles—some are stable domain-coherent properties while others are instance-specific identifiers.

## Input / Output

NAVI consumes rows from heterogeneous in-domain tables serialized as linear sequences of header-value segments (each segment delimited by [SEP], with positional embeddings reset per segment and a context-free attribute embedding injected into every token). It produces 768-dimensional contextualized segment embeddings (a two-layer MLP projection of concatenated attribute, header, and value embeddings), a [CLS] row embedding for downstream classification, and reconstructed header/value tokens for generative evaluation. The segment embedding is the fundamental unit for both contrastive alignment and downstream feature extraction.

## Method

NAVI introduces the header-value segment as the atomic unit for tabular representation learning and jointly optimizes two complementary objectives. Masked Segment Modeling (MSM) uses structure-aware masking that partitions segments into header-masked, value-masked, and vanilla MLM regimes (4:4:2 ratio), forcing the encoder to learn functional dependencies between headers and values within schema-preserving row serializations. Entropy-driven Segment Alignment (ESA) computes normalized column entropy per table to partition attributes into low-entropy (stable anchors) and high-entropy (instance-specific) groups, then applies role-aware InfoNCE contrastive alignment: low-entropy segments are pulled toward shared context-free attribute embeddings while repelled from competing attribute anchors, and high-entropy segments are aligned with their own row-specific value representations while contrasted against other rows in the same table. This entropy-conditioned routing induces a dual geometry—anchor contraction for stable attributes and controlled dispersion for entity-specific attributes—within a single embedding space.

## Results

On the WDC WebTables benchmark (Movie and Product domains), NAVI achieves near-perfect header prediction (0.9995 Product, 0.9998 Movie) and best value imputation (0.7933 Product, 0.7077 Movie), substantially outperforming all baselines. For semantic consistency, NAVI attains the highest header clustering NMI (0.9005 Product, 0.9144 Movie) and B³-F1 (0.7920, 0.7996). On downstream row classification with XGBoost, NAVI reaches 0.928 Macro-F1 on Product (best among LM encoders) and 0.615 on Movie, while the NAVI_FE hybrid pipeline achieves 0.944 on Product, the overall best across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAVI dominates TAPAS by a wide margin on generative reconstruction (header prediction 0.9995 vs. 0.2996 on Product; value imputation 0.7933 vs. 0.2501), reflecting TAPAS's inability to model cross-table schema semantics. Against BERT, NAVI improves header prediction by ~6 points (0.9995 vs. 0.9400) and value imputation by ~2 points (0.7933 vs. 0.7743) on Product, with similar gains on Movie. Compared to HAETAE, the closest domain-aware baseline, NAVI gains 7.35 points on Product header prediction (0.9995 vs. 0.9260) and 1.48 points on value imputation, and leads on header clustering NMI by 2.6–2.8 points across both domains. On downstream row classification (XGBoost, Product), NAVI edges out HAETAE (0.928 vs. 0.915) and BERT (0.912), while CM2 trails significantly (0.814). On the Movie domain, TAPAS achieves the highest row-classification Macro-F1 (0.629) due to its strong intra-table structural priors on redundant schemas, whereas NAVI (0.615) and HAETAE (0.602) are competitive but slightly behind; NAVI's advantage is most pronounced on the semantically diverse Product domain where cross-table alignment matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAVI extends the domain-aware table pretraining paradigm introduced by HAETAE (Jung & Yoon, 2025), which treated schemas as shared global structures but did not accumulate column-level distributional evidence across tables. It departs from structure-aware encoders (TAPAS, TaBERT, TURL) that infer header semantics strictly within individual tables, and from feature-oriented encoders (CM2, TP-BERTa) that treat headers as surface-level tokens without modeling how attribute semantics emerge jointly from schema and column contexts. The key conceptual departure is the segment (header-value pair) as the atomic unit, combined with entropy-based routing that applies different contrastive alignment objectives depending on whether an attribute is a stable domain property or an instance-specific identifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-driven routing provides a principled mechanism for resolving the tension between schema-level semantic consistency and instance-level discriminability in heterogeneous tables, with a clean theoretical grounding in alignment-uniformity geometry. However, evaluation is limited to two domains (Movie, Product) from WDC WebTables, and the Movie domain shows TAPAS outperforming NAVI on discriminative tasks due to schema redundancy, suggesting the framework's advantages are most pronounced in semantically diverse schemas. Open questions include whether the fixed quantile thresholds for entropy partitioning generalize across domains, how the approach scales to very large or cross-domain table corpora, and whether the segment-centric abstraction transfers to settings where attribute semantics are not pre-aligned across tables.
