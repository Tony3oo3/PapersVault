## Problem

VQ-VAE-based neural speech codecs suffer from quantization error that limits reconstruction fidelity, and existing remedies—hierarchical residual codebooks or larger flat codebooks—introduce significant complexity for downstream autoregressive LLM modeling. The paper asks whether the decoder itself can be made robust to quantization imperfections without altering the quantizer or inference pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262677/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec encodes 16 kHz continuous speech waveforms into discrete audio tokens at a 50 Hz frame rate using a single FSQ codebook (sizes 8192–65536), and reconstructs waveforms via a Transformer decoder followed by an iSTFT head. During training, the decoder receives two parallel inputs: the quantized token embeddings z_q and the continuous pre-quantized latents z_e, enabling the self-guidance loss; at inference, only z_q is used, so no architectural or latency changes are introduced.

## Method

Self-guidance (SG) adds a lightweight feature-mapping loss L_guide = ‖sg(h_e) − h_q‖² that aligns the decoder's internal representations when processing continuous latents (teacher path, stop-gradient) versus quantized tokens (student path). The loss is computed on the final Transformer block outputs of the XCodec2 acoustic decoder, chosen because the Transformer holds most decoder parameters and the downstream iSTFT head separates feature alignment from waveform-level losses. This term is added to the standard VQ-VAE training objectives (semantic MSE, multi-scale Mel L1, and multi-period adversarial loss) with a tunable weight λ_guide (optimal range 5–10). The mechanism requires only one extra forward pass through the decoder during training (no backward pass on the teacher branch), adding <0.5% training time, and leaves inference unchanged. The paper validates that SG reduces hidden-feature alignment error and improves kNN Jaccard and Procrustes manifold metrics without altering the quantization error distribution, confirming the effect operates at the decoder rather than the encoder.

## Results

On LibriSpeech test-clean, XCodec2+SG at 50 Hz with a 65,536 codebook achieves PESQ 2.39, STOI 0.915, MCD 3.41, WER 3.2%, SIM 0.80, and UTMOS 4.10, setting new state-of-the-art for low-bitrate (40–50 Hz) single-codebook speech codecs. A key efficiency finding is that self-guidance with a 16,384 codebook (PESQ 2.27, STOI 0.907) matches or exceeds the baseline XCodec2 with a 4× larger 65,536 codebook (PESQ 2.28, STOI 0.910) on several metrics. The method incurs <0.5% additional training time and zero inference overhead, with no architectural changes required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against XCodec2 (the direct baseline), self-guidance consistently improves every metric: at 65,536 codebook, PESQ rises from 2.28→2.39, STOI from 0.910→0.915, MCD drops from 3.57→3.41, and SIM from 0.79→0.80; at 8,192 codebook, PESQ improves from 2.03→2.13 and WER drops from 4.1→3.8. Against TS3Codec at 50 Hz, XCodec2+SG (65,536) outperforms both TS3Codec variants (65,536 and 131,072) on PESQ (2.39 vs 2.22/2.23), STOI (0.915 vs 0.909/0.910), MCD (3.41 vs 3.52/3.50), and SIM (0.80 vs 0.68), while matching WER (3.2 vs 3.6). Against BigCodec at comparable 40–50 Hz frame rates, XCodec2+SG (50 Hz, 8,192) surpasses BigCodec (40 Hz, 8,192) on all six metrics (e.g., PESQ 2.13 vs 2.11, WER 3.8 vs 6.7, SIM 0.73 vs 0.66), though BigCodec at 80 Hz remains stronger due to its higher frame rate. Against WavTokenizer (40 Hz, 4,096), XCodec2+SG dominates across the board (PESQ 2.13 vs 1.88, STOI 0.898 vs 0.868, WER 3.8 vs 8.0). EnCodec is used only in the preliminary quantization-artifact study (Table 1), where feeding pre-quantized latents z_e instead of z_q improves STOI from 0.88→0.95 and SIM from 0.861→0.922, motivating the self-guidance approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQ-VAE framework for neural speech codecs (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and applies its self-guidance mechanism to XCodec2 (Ye et al., 2025b), a state-of-the-art single-codebook codec with a Transformer-based decoder. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant strategy of reducing quantization error by modifying the quantizer (hierarchical residual codebooks, larger codebook sizes) and instead targets the decoder's robustness to quantization imperfections via a feature-mapping loss between pre-quantized and quantized decoder paths. The approach is related to self-distillation but differs in using the model's own continuous latent as an internal teacher within a single end-to-end training run, rather than requiring a separately trained or iteratively updated teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling a 4× codebook reduction (65,536 → 16,384) without fidelity loss, which directly simplifies the flat vocabulary that downstream autoregressive LLMs must model for speech generation—a bottleneck that hierarchical or oversized codebooks exacerbate. The method's generality across quantizers (FSQ, SimVQ, Residual FSQ) and decoder architectures (Transformer, CNN) suggests broad applicability, and the <0.5% training overhead with zero inference changes makes it easy to adopt. However, the downstream TTS validation remains preliminary (small 0.5B LLM, single dataset), the method does not eliminate all quantization artifacts, and extension beyond speech to general audio or image VQ-VAEs is untested, leaving open whether the manifold-alignment principle transfers to domains with different information structures.
