## Problem

Audio SSL evaluation relies on finetuning because simple linear probes fail to unlock the full potential of frozen embeddings, obscuring whether SOTA improvements reflect better representations or merely better optimization. Additionally, current SOTA models (EAT, SSLAM) are built on legacy fairseq implementations with undocumented architectural and optimization details, making their results difficult to reproduce and their progress hard to verify.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-second audio clips resampled to 16 kHz, converted to mel-spectrograms with decibel-scale log compression and local min-max normalization (eliminating the need for dataset-specific global statistics), then organized into non-overlapping k×k patches with ~80% randomly masked during pretraining. For probing, CGP produces a 3K-dimensional feature vector (min-pooled and max-pooled cosine similarities of patch tokens to K prototypes, concatenated with cls-token similarities) fed to a linear classifier; for ASR, a transposed convolution upsamples temporal patch tokens by 8× (160 ms → 20 ms) before a two-layer LSTM and CTC head.

## Method

BAT modernizes Data2Vec 2.0 masked latent regression for audio with three key changes: (1) a sigmoid-gated attention mechanism (σ(xW_G)·V̄) inserted between the attention-weighted values and the output projection, which introduces non-linearity and input-dependent sparsity to eliminate attention sinks, thereby enabling the use of coherent End-of-Block outputs as SSL targets instead of the MLP-only heuristic; (2) a six-layer ViT decoder replacing the lightweight CNN, which offloads reconstruction from the encoder and shifts semantic information toward later layers; and (3) a refined audio frontend with decibel compression and local min-max normalization. For evaluation, Convex Gated Probing (CGP) aggregates embeddings from all L layers via a learnable softmax gating vector, computes cosine similarities against K=10,000 learnable prototypes, and min-max pools patch-token similarities concatenated with cls-token similarities into a 3K feature vector for a linear classifier—operating entirely outside the frozen backbone without architectural modification.

## Results

On the primary AudioSet-20k benchmark, BAT achieves 41.59 mAP (F1 38.20) under finetuning and 37.70 mAP (F1 35.22) under the proposed CGP probing, surpassing the previously reported SOTA of SSLAM (40.9 mAP finetuned) and EAT (40.2 mAP finetuned). Under CGP, BAT leads EAT by 2.36 mAP and SSLAM by 3.17 mAP on AS-20k, and extends this gap on out-of-distribution HSN (41.53 vs. 37.90 mAP). On LibriSpeech ASR, BAT achieves 22.18 WER / 7.27 CER, outperforming BEATs (24.67 WER), EAT (27.14 WER), and SSLAM (29.05 WER). CGP itself closes the probing-to-finetuning gap substantially: on AS-20k, CGP reaches 37.70 mAP for BAT versus 35.20 for EAT and 34.62 for SSLAM, compared to only 17.95 and 17.03 mAP respectively under simple linear probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAT outperforms all three SSL baselines (EAT, SSLAM, BEATs) across every probing method and benchmark. Under finetuning on AS-20k, BAT reaches 41.59 mAP, exceeding EAT (40.37) and SSLAM (40.26) by 1.2–1.3 mAP, and BEATs (36.73) by nearly 5 mAP; the gap widens dramatically on OOD HSN where BAT scores 47.05 mAP versus 42.32 (EAT), 44.20 (SSLAM), and only 18.59 (BEATs). Under the proposed CGP probing, BAT leads EAT by 2.36 mAP and SSLAM by 3.17 mAP on AS-20k, and by 2.05 mAP on AS-2M, confirming that the advantage is in representation quality rather than finetuning optimization. Among probing methods, CGP consistently outperforms Protobin (Rauch et al., 2026) by 1.7–2.0 mAP on AS-20k, VQT (Tu et al., 2023) by 3.5 mAP, and H2T (Evci et al., 2022) by 8.1 mAP, while simple linear probing lags far behind all prototype-based methods. On ASR (LibriSpeech), BAT achieves the best WER of 22.18, beating BEATs (24.67), EAT (27.14), and SSLAM (29.05) by 2.5–6.9 points, demonstrating that the improved encoder transfers well to speech tasks as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper modernizes the Data2Vec 2.0 masked latent regression framework for audio, departing from the legacy fairseq implementations used by EAT and SSLAM in favor of a native PyTorch pipeline. It extends the prototypical probing line of work (Rauch et al., 2026) by adding a learnable convex gating mechanism over all encoder layers, and imports the sigmoid-gated attention mechanism from LLMs (Qiu et al., 2025) into the audio ViT to fix a known pathology in D2V2 target generation. The overall positioning is that of a systematic re-derivation: rather than proposing a new SSL objective, the authors refine preprocessing, architecture, and evaluation of an existing recipe while using CGP as a consistent post-hoc probe throughout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that current AudioSet SOTA (SSLAM) is not reproducible and actually underperforms EAT under fair conditions is a significant reproducibility finding that should reshape how the community claims progress. CGP offers a practical, low-cost frozen evaluation protocol that narrows the probing-to-finetuning gap, potentially becoming the default for audio SSL benchmarking and reducing reliance on fragile finetuning hyperparameters. Open questions include whether the 'later layers hold more semantics' observation generalizes beyond MLR-based audio models, whether CGP transfers to other modalities, and whether the gated-attention benefit persists at larger scales or with different masking strategies.
