## Problem

Current video generation models synthesize scenes holistically, offering no mechanism for explicit compositional control over individual objects' identity, motion, or spatial placement. Existing fine-grained control methods either rely on expensive per-reference test-time optimization or degrade significantly in multi-object settings, and none support conditioning on dynamic video references to preserve both identity and specific gestures simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265914/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HECTOR accepts heterogeneous reference inputs—static images for identity and/or dynamic video clips for motion priors—alongside a text prompt and per-object spatiotemporal trajectories specifying centroid position, relative scale, and visibility at each frame. The Video Decompositor can auto-extract all of these from a source video for editing tasks. Output is a compositional video at 832×480 resolution, 81 frames at 16 fps, where each referenced element is rendered at its prescribed location, scale, and speed while preserving reference fidelity.

## Method

Built on the Wan2.1 I2V 14B DiT backbone and trained with a flow-matching velocity-prediction objective, HECTOR introduces two key components. The Video Decompositor segments objects with SAM2, samples adaptive anchor points, propagates them with CoTracker3, and derives smooth trajectories via a Point-to-Scale formulation that measures cluster expansion relative to a reference frame—avoiding the jitter of bounding-box heuristics. The Spatio-Temporal Alignment Module (STAM) encodes each reference into VAE latents, applies trajectory-guided inverse warping to place features at the correct spatiotemporal coordinates, and fuses image-based (identity) and video-based (gesture) latents into a unified conditioning tensor gated by Gaussian-softened visibility masks. A 4-channel mask tensor explicitly distinguishes static from dynamic reference sources, and the final DiT input is the channel-wise concatenation [z_t, M, z_cond]. An inverse-gating mechanism resolves foreground-background occlusion when trajectories intersect.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark, HECTOR achieves substantial gains in motion control precision, nearly doubling mIoU (0.3912 single-object, 0.4005 multi-object) and halving Centroid Distance (0.1130 single, 0.1670 multi) compared to the strongest baseline VACE. For subject fidelity, HECTOR more than doubles R-DINO scores (0.4277 vs. 0.2602 single; 0.4347 vs. 0.2540 multi) and leads on DINO-I (0.3735 vs. 0.3357 single). Temporal consistency (T-Cons ≈ 0.991) and text alignment (CLIP-T ≈ 0.33–0.34) remain competitive with baselines, confirming that precise structural control does not degrade overall video quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HECTOR is compared against MotionBooth (finetuning-based, single-object only) and VACE in two variants (bbox and mask conditioning). HECTOR decisively outperforms both baselines on all motion-control metrics: mIoU is roughly 1.8× higher than VACE-mask (0.3912 vs. 0.2087 single; 0.4005 vs. 0.2274 multi) and CD is roughly 2.4× lower (0.1130 vs. 0.2821 single). On subject fidelity, HECTOR's R-DINO score (0.4277 single, 0.4347 multi) is nearly double that of VACE-mask (0.2497, 0.2540) and far exceeds MotionBooth (0.2113), indicating substantially better identity preservation. On text alignment (CLIP-T), HECTOR is marginally behind VACE-bbox in both settings (0.3306 vs. 0.3363 single; 0.3432 vs. 0.3445 multi), a negligible gap. Temporal consistency is essentially tied across all methods (~0.991). Overall, HECTOR wins on 7 of 8 metrics in both single- and multi-object settings, with the sole near-tie on CLIP-T.

## Relation to Prior Work

HECTOR builds on the Wan2.1 I2V 14B diffusion transformer backbone and extends the trajectory-controlled video generation line (Tora, TGT, ATI, Wan-Move) from treating video as a single entity to a fundamentally compositional formulation where each visual element carries its own reference and trajectory. It departs from prior reference-based customization methods (DreamVideo, MotionBooth) by simultaneously accepting heterogeneous static image and dynamic video references, and from control-signal integration approaches (VACE, Tora2, DreamVideo2) by explicitly decoupling identity from motion via the Spatio-Temporal Alignment Module. The Video Decompositor (SAM2 + CoTracker3) repurposes off-the-shelf segmentation and point-tracking tools as a unified data-curation and inference pipeline, replacing rigid bounding-box heuristics with point-cluster-derived scale and translation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HECTOR is the first framework to offer fully compositional video generation with independent per-element control over appearance, trajectory, scale, and speed, while also supporting hybrid static/dynamic references—a capability no prior method provides. This opens practical editing workflows (object replacement, insertion, background-locked motion editing) that were previously infeasible with holistic generation. Key open questions remain: end-to-end learned resolution of multi-object occlusion (currently handled by a heuristic inverse gate), robustness to upstream tracking failures under extreme motion blur, and whether the compositional paradigm scales beyond 3–4 references to denser scenes. The reliance on a 14B backbone fine-tuned on 2.4M internal clips also raises questions about reproducibility and compute accessibility.
