## Problem

Identical sparse autoencoder configurations produce near-zero dead features on GPT-2 but over 70% on AlphaFold3, and death rates vary from 20% to 80% across layers within a single model. The cause of this variation was unknown: existing revival techniques (AuxK, ghost gradients, resampling) treat death as a training-dynamics problem but fail where death is most severe. The paper identifies the missing geometric cause—dimension-level activation outliers that predetermine feature fate before any gradient flows.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-token activation vectors from a single layer of a pretrained model (dimensions ranging from 384 to 4096 across language, vision, protein, and genomic models), optionally after per-token LayerNorm to isolate directional outlier structure from scale variation. It produces three outputs: (1) a scalar outlier-severity diagnostic γ = ‖μ‖/‖σ‖ computed from the activation mean and per-dimension standard deviation, which predicts initial dead-feature rates in closed form; (2) a mean-centered SAE whose bias is initialized to the activation geometric median, yielding sparse feature codes and dictionary directions with near-zero death; and (3) a per-layer effective-rank measurement that flags the residual low-rank death case requiring PCA whitening instead.

## Method

The paper shows that at random initialization, each feature's pre-activation decomposes into a constant shift (w_i · μ) and an input-dependent signal (w_i · (x − μ)); when dimension-level outliers inflate ‖μ‖, the shift dominates and features anti-aligned with μ receive permanently negative pre-activations (dead-by-ReLU) or lose the TopK competition to the k most positively-aligned features (dead-by-TopK). From first principles, the authors derive closed-form death-rate formulas—Φ(−C/γ) for dead-by-ReLU and Φ(t_k − C/γ) for dead-by-TopK—that depend only on the outlier-severity ratio γ = ‖μ‖/‖σ‖ and the evaluation set size, matching observed rates across 454 model-layer combinations (Spearman ρ = 0.89 and 0.82). They further characterize two recovery pathways during training: dead-by-TopK features revive quickly as the TopK threshold collapses, while dead-by-ReLU features recover only as the bias slowly absorbs the mean offset, a process that plateaus at 75–90% dead after 2M steps at high γ. The proposed fix is mean-centering: initializing the SAE bias to the activation geometric median so the shift term vanishes at initialization, which eliminates outlier-induced death across all tested models and yields more monosemantic, higher-quality features than a 4× larger uncentered dictionary.

## Results

Across 454 model-layer combinations spanning language, vision, protein, and genomic models, the outlier severity diagnostic γ = ‖μ‖/‖σ‖ predicts initial dead-feature rates with Spearman ρ = 0.89 (dead-by-TopK) and ρ = 0.82 (dead-by-ReLU), with no fitted parameters. Mean-centering (initializing the SAE bias to the activation mean) eliminates outlier-induced death: on ESM3 it reduces dead features from ~83% to near zero, and on AlphaFold3 from ~98% to under 5%, without auxiliary losses. On ESM3's representative layer, a mean-centered SAE with 2048 features captures 100 biological concepts versus 73 for a baseline SAE with 4× the dictionary size (8192 features), and achieves MMCS 0.97 vs. 0.38 on synthetic ground-truth recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AuxK (Gao et al., 2024), mean-centering is strictly superior on high-γ models: on ESM3, AuxK plateaus at ~25% dead features after 1M steps while mean-centering stays near zero throughout training; AuxK's benefit comes from preventing collateral death during TopK threshold collapse, not from recovering features dead-by-ReLU from initialization, so its advantage shrinks as γ increases and most dead features are born dead rather than created during training. Against LayerNorm with √d rescaling (Templeton et al., 2024), mean-centering wins on both dead-feature rate and feature quality: LN reduces ESM3 death to ~20% but recovers fewer biological concepts than the uncentered baseline, and does not match mean-centering on synthetic ground-truth recovery. Against PCA whitening (Saraswatula & Klindt, 2025), the two methods address complementary failure modes: mean-centering handles outlier-induced death (high γ) while PCA whitening handles residual death from low-rank activation structure (e.g., Evo1, where 4 of 4096 PCs carry 99% of variance); the paper recommends using γ and effective rank together to select the appropriate preprocessing per layer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TopK SAE framework (Gao et al., 2024) and the broader dictionary-learning SAE architecture (Bricken et al., 2023b), but departs sharply from the prevailing framing of feature death as a training-dynamics problem. Prior revival methods (AuxK, ghost gradients, resampling) inject gradient signal into stuck features, implicitly assuming the features are alive but under-trained; this paper shows the opposite—that most features are geometrically dead at initialization due to dimension-level activation outliers, before any gradient flows. Mean-centering has appeared in prior SAE work (Bricken et al., 2023b; Gao et al., 2024) but was applied inconsistently and without a clear rationale for when it matters; the γ diagnostic and recovery-dynamics analysis here provide that missing principled account. The paper also distinguishes its dimension-level outliers (persistent across all inputs, a property of the activation distribution) from the token-level outliers studied in quantization literature (Dettmers et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2024), and complements Wang et al. (2025) who identify a separate low-rank death mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The γ diagnostic lets practitioners assess SAE trainability before committing compute, and mean-centering (zero runtime cost) eliminates outlier-induced death across all tested models while producing more monosemantic features that recover more biological concepts than a 4× larger baseline. This has direct implications for cross-layer methods like transcoders that chain feature dictionaries, where variable death rates destabilize effective capacity. Open questions include why outlier severity differs across architectures and domains, how LayerNorm interacts with the outlier mechanism across more model families, and whether the low-rank death pathway (addressed by PCA whitening) has a similarly clean analytical characterization. The paper's limitation to residual-stream activations and TopK SAEs means the full picture for ReLU/JumpReLU architectures and non-residual layers remains partially open.
