## Problem

Most consistency guarantees for node embeddings in latent-space network models (e.g., the RDPG) assume the embedding dimension d equals the true latent dimension r, yet in practice d is always chosen from data and may be wrong. This paper is the first to characterize the behavior of the adjacency spectral embedding (ASE) when d is misspecified, answering an open question posed in Athreya et al. (2018) about what happens when d > r and establishing a fundamental lower bound when d < r.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264347/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an n×n symmetric adjacency matrix A (from a weighted or binary RDPG) and a user-specified embedding dimension d, and produces a d-dimensional ASE via the leading d eigenvectors of A scaled by the square roots of their absolute eigenvalues. Because the latent positions are only identifiable up to orthogonal rotation, the estimation error is measured after solving a Procrustes alignment problem, and when d ≠ r the estimate and estimand are zero-padded to conformable dimensions before comparison in the (2,∞)-norm (maximum row Euclidean norm).

## Method

The key technical contribution is a delocalization theorem (Theorem 3.1) showing that all non-signal eigenvectors of A = P + E have entries bounded by r²(log n)^(4+6γ)/√n, extending the rank-one result of Erdős et al. (2013a) to general low-rank signal matrices via a local law for expectation matrices whose spectral norm is not bounded after scaling. This delocalization, combined with a decomposition of the (2,∞)-norm error into a correctly-specified component plus a trailing-dimensions component, yields the main result (Theorem 3.3): when d < r, the estimation error is lower-bounded by √(|d−r|·ρ_n) and does not vanish for dense networks, while when d > r, consistency still holds at the slower n^(−1/4) rate (versus n^(−1/2) at d = r). The proof requires assumptions on eigenvector incoherence of P, distinct signal eigenvalues, a minimum eigengap, and subexponential tail conditions on the noise entries.

## Results

The paper establishes three distinct regimes for the ASE under embedding dimension misspecification in the weighted RDPG. When d = r (correctly specified), the ASE achieves the standard n^{-1/2} rate in (2,∞)-norm, matching existing results. When d > r (too large), consistency still holds but at a slower n^{-1/4} rate, with the error bounded by φ_n + √(σ²(d−r))·r²(log n)^{5+6γ}/n^{1/4}. When d < r (too small), a fundamental lower bound of √(|d−r|·ρ_n) applies, implying inconsistency for dense networks (ρ_n = Θ(1)). The key technical result (Theorem 3.1) shows all non-signal eigenvectors of A are delocalized: max_{α>r}|û_{jα}| ≲ r²(log n)^{4+6γ}/√n for all j ∈ [n].

**Summary of ASE estimation error regimes under embedding dimension misspecification (Theorem 3.3, weighted RDPG).**

| Regime | Condition | (2,∞)-norm Error Rate | Consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Too small | d < r | ≥ √(\|d−r\|·ρ_n) (lower bound) | No (if ρ_n = Θ(1)) |
| Correct | d = r | O(r^{1/2}(log n)²(ρ_n n)^{−1/2}) | Yes |
| Too large | d > r | O(φ_n + √(σ²(d−r))·r²(log n)^{5+6γ}/n^{1/4}) | Yes (slower rate) |

*Experiments (Figures 1, 3, 4) confirm: d=4 (too small) plateaus at a constant error; d=5 (correct) follows n^{-1/2}; d=6,10,20,40 (too large) all follow n^{-1/4} with similar constants.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-method baselines; the 'comparison' is between the three embedding dimension regimes and against existing correctly-specified results. The correctly-specified rate (d = r) recovers the n^{-1/2} bound from Lyzinski et al. (2017) for binary networks and Levin et al. (2022) for weighted networks, confirming the paper's framework is consistent with prior work. The key new contribution is characterizing the d > r regime: the ASE remains consistent but degrades from n^{-1/2} to n^{-1/4}, a result that settles an open question posed in Section 7 of Athreya et al. (2018). For the d < r regime, the paper provides the first fundamental lower bound showing that under-specification causes irreducible estimation error proportional to √ρ_n, which is inconsistent for dense networks. Experiments across weighted RDPG, binary RDPG, SBM, and DCSBM settings all confirm the predicted rates, with the n^{-1/4} rate for d > r being remarkably insensitive to how much larger d is than r (d=6 and d=40 show similar error magnitudes).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ASE consistency analysis of Sussman et al. (2012), Lyzinski et al. (2017), and Levin et al. (2022), all of which assume the embedding dimension equals the true latent rank r. The key technical departure is a generalization of the eigenvector delocalization results of Erdős et al. (2013a) from rank-one signal-plus-noise matrices to general low-rank signal matrices, which is needed because the Davis-Kahan sin Θ framework (used in all prior ASE consistency proofs) breaks down when d ≠ r due to zero eigengaps. The paper positions itself as the first to characterize ASE behavior under dimension misspecification, complementing the extensive model-selection literature (scree plots, cross-validation, parallel analysis) by analyzing the downstream consequences of getting the dimension wrong.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical quantification of the asymmetry between over- and under-specifying the embedding dimension: under-specification (d < r) yields a fundamental lower bound that implies inconsistency for dense networks, while over-specification (d > r) preserves consistency at a slower n^{-1/4} rate. This formalizes and justifies the practitioner folklore that it is safer to over-estimate model rank. Key open questions include extending the delocalization result to binary networks (Conjecture 1, blocked by the doubly-stochastic variance assumption), determining whether the n^{-1/4} rate is minimax-optimal for d > r, and characterizing downstream consequences for clustering and hypothesis testing tasks.
