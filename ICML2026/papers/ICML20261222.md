## Problem

Continual medical image segmentation faces a fundamental tension: uniform parameter constraints cause catastrophic forgetting when tasks are dissimilar, while rigid per-task isolation precludes beneficial transfer among related tasks. Existing methods either apply global regularization or require predefined expert counts that cannot anticipate future task diversity. The paper asks whether the underlying task structure—which tasks share sufficient visual and semantic similarity to benefit from joint learning—can be discovered online from the data itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (resized to 352×352) paired with a clinical text prompt (e.g., 'Left ventricular cavity of oval shape in four-chamber view in the cardiac ultrasound') and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask. The text prompt serves a dual role: it conditions the frozen CLIPSeg backbone for segmentation and, via its CLIP text embedding, drives the CRP-based semantic modality assignment. The framework is replay-free, storing only per-modality centroid vectors and Fisher information matrices rather than raw patient data, with all images processed at a fixed 352×352 resolution on a single GPU.

## Method

MedCRP-CL discovers task groupings ('semantic modalities') online using a Chinese Restaurant Process prior modulated by a learned Gaussian likelihood over prompt-embedding similarities, automatically determining the number of clusters without supervision. Each discovered semantic modality owns a dedicated LoRA adapter (rank 8) on a frozen CLIPSeg backbone, achieving complete parameter isolation across modalities while enabling knowledge sharing within them. Intra-modality EWC regularization, with Fisher information consolidated via exponential moving average, prevents forgetting among tasks sharing the same adapter. The key insight is that clinical text prompts provide a substantially larger intra-/inter-group similarity gap (~0.50) than visual features (~0.22), making text-based clustering more reliable for separating, e.g., cardiac from breast ultrasound despite their shared physical imaging modality. The framework discovers 5 semantic modalities from 16 tasks across 4 physical imaging types, correctly splitting ultrasound into cardiac and breast subgroups.

## Results

On 16 medical segmentation tasks spanning four imaging modalities, MedCRP-CL achieves 73.3% average Dice score with only 4.1% forgetting rate, outperforming the best continual learning baseline (MoE-Adapters) by 8.0% in Dice while using 6× fewer trainable parameters (8.6M vs. 51.9M). The method approaches the Individual upper bound of 77.9% Dice, closing the gap to just 4.6%. Performance is stable across task orderings, with Dice ranging from 72.0% to 74.2% and forgetting between 3.4% and 5.1% across four different sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedCRP-CL outperforms all four baselines on both Dice and forgetting. Against MoE-Adapters (Yu et al., 2024), the closest competitor, it improves Dice by 8.0% (73.3% vs. 65.3%) and reduces forgetting by 3.0% (4.1% vs. 7.1%) while requiring only 8.6M parameters versus 51.9M—a 6× reduction. CL-LoRA (He et al., 2025) uses minimal parameters (0.05M) but trails by 12.6% Dice and 5.6% forgetting, and is 1.5× slower due to knowledge distillation overhead. Classical regularization methods EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) and RAPF (Huang et al., 2024) show limited effectiveness in the heterogeneous medical imaging setting, achieving only 56.8% and 58.4% Dice respectively, with MedCRP-CL surpassing them by 16.5% and 14.9% Dice. The per-task analysis (Table 3) reveals that MedCRP-CL's advantage is most pronounced on early-learned tasks (e.g., CAMUS: 82.3% vs. 42.4% for MoE-Adapters), confirming that CRP-guided modality isolation effectively prevents cross-modality interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: Bayesian nonparametric clustering (CRP, Blei et al. 2010), parameter-efficient continual learning via LoRA (Hu et al. 2022), and regularization-based forgetting mitigation via EWC (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017). Its key departure from prior continual medical segmentation work (MedPEFT-CL, Low-Rank MoE, MoE-Adapters) is the use of CRP to *discover* task groupings online from clinical text prompts rather than assuming a fixed number of experts or applying uniform constraints. A notable conceptual shift is the observation that text-prompt embeddings provide a substantially larger intra-/inter-cluster gap (~0.50) than visual embeddings (~0.22) for task grouping, motivating a text-driven rather than image-driven clustering signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The replay-free, privacy-preserving design directly addresses HIPAA/GDPR constraints that make rehearsal-based continual learning impractical in clinical settings, and the automatic semantic modality discovery removes the need for practitioners to pre-specify task groupings. The finding that all 10 cross-modality Fisher-weighted merges degrade performance provides strong evidence that the discovered structure is not arbitrary but reflects genuine representational incompatibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 16 tasks and a single CLIPSeg backbone, whether it extends to 3D volumetric segmentation, and how it performs when prompt quality is highly variable across institutions. The reliance on contrastive text encoders (non-contrastive encoders collapse to K=1) is a practical constraint worth noting.
