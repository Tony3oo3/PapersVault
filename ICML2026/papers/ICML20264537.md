## Problem

Existing first-order (ε,δ)-unlearning methods use the forget set only to calibrate injected noise, never as a direct optimization signal, which limits their convergence to an O(1/e²) dependence on target error. Meanwhile, empirical unlearning heuristics that exploit forget-set gradients (e.g., gradient ascent) achieve better practical performance but lack formal privacy guarantees. This paper bridges the gap by introducing the first first-order (ε,δ)-unlearning algorithm that directly incorporates forget-set gradients into its update rule while maintaining certified privacy.

## Input / Output

VRU is initialized at the full-data optimum θ* and consumes one retain sample ξ_r and one forget sample ξ_f per iteration (or a single full-batch forget gradient in the practical variant). The algorithm projects iterates onto the ball B(θ*, r_f/(1−r_f)·L/μ) at each step, then applies a final Gaussian noise perturbation scaled by (r_f/(1−r_f))·ν_T·κ_{ε,δ} to certify (ε,δ)-unlearning. The practical variant (Alg. 2) replaces the intractable Lipschitz constant L with the forget-set gradient norm ‖∇L(θ*, D_f)‖, removing the need to compute L at runtime.

## Method

The key mechanism is a variance-reduced gradient estimator ∇̃(θ, ξ_r, ξ_f) = ∇ℓ(θ, ξ_r) − ∇ℓ(θ*, ξ_r) − (r_f/(1−r_f))∇ℓ(θ*, ξ_f), which anchors a correction term at the pre-trained optimum θ*. Because the retain and forget gradients cancel at θ* (the full-data minimizer), the correction has zero expectation, preserving unbiasedness while dramatically reducing variance—especially when r_f is small. VRU runs projected stochastic gradient descent with decreasing step size 1/(μt) on this estimator, then applies the Gaussian mechanism to the final iterate. This yields an O(1/e) dependence on target excess risk (improving over NFT's O(1/e²)) combined with O(r_f²) scaling, and a formal separation theorem showing VRU asymptotically outperforms any forget-set-free first-order (ε,δ)-unlearning algorithm in the low-error regime.

## Results

On the Digits dataset (regularized logistic regression, 10 epochs, κ_{ε,δ}=1), VRU achieves the lowest excess risk across all tested forget fractions r_f ∈ [10⁻³, 10⁻¹], with gains exceeding one order of magnitude for r_f < 10⁻² and approaching two orders of magnitude at r_f = 10⁻³ compared to the best certified baseline (NFT). Theoretically, VRU improves the excess-risk dependence from O(1/e²) (NFT) to O(1/e), and the ratio T_e(VRU)/T_e(NFT) = Θ(κ_ℓ³ log(1/δ) · e/e₀) confirms the speedup grows as the target error e shrinks. In the empirical privacy-utility evaluation (5 epochs, U-LiRA MIA), VRU maintains MIA accuracy near 50% (random-guessing level) while achieving the lowest excess risk for r_f ≤ 2×10⁻², and extends favorably to a non-convex two-hidden-layer MLP setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the certified unlearning baseline NFT (Neel et al., 2021), VRU consistently achieves lower excess risk at every forget fraction, with the gap widening as r_f decreases; at r_f = 10⁻³ the improvement is nearly two orders of magnitude, directly reflecting VRU's O(1/e) vs. NFT's O(1/e²) dependence on target error. Against the retraining baseline SVRG (Johnson & Zhang, 2013), VRU also achieves lower excess risk under the same 10-epoch budget, confirming that unlearning can outperform full retraining when r_f is small (Corollary 4.3). Against the empirical unlearning methods SCRUB and NegGrad+ (Kurmanji et al., 2024), VRU wins on both axes of the privacy-utility trade-off: it achieves the lowest MIA accuracy (closest to 50% random guessing) and the lowest excess risk for r_f ≤ 2×10⁻²; at r_f = 0.1, VRU incurs slightly higher excess risk than these empirical methods because it is the only method that adds calibrated Gaussian noise, but this regime is one where full retraining becomes competitive anyway. The same pattern holds on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin dataset and in the non-convex MLP experiment, where VRU out-competes the empirical baselines in both utility and privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VRU sits at the intersection of certified (ε,δ)-unlearning (NFT, Neel et al. 2021; Van Waerebeke et al. 2025) and variance-reduced stochastic optimization (SVRG, Johnson & Zhang 2013). Prior certified first-order methods used the forget set only to calibrate injected noise, while empirical methods (SCRUB, NegGrad+; Kurmanji et al. 2024) exploited forget-set gradient ascent without formal guarantees. VRU bridges this gap by repurposing the SVRG correction mechanism: instead of a full-batch retain-set gradient at an anchor point, it uses a scaled forget-set gradient at θ*, which is both unbiased (by the cancellation property at the full-data optimum) and low-variance (since r_f is small). The theoretical separation from forget-set-free methods builds directly on the lower bounds of Van Waerebeke et al. (2025) and the optimal-update decomposition of Huang et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/e) convergence rate (vs. O(1/e²) for NFT) meaningfully extends the regime where certified unlearning beats full retraining, and the fundamental separation theorem (Theorem 4.4) provides a clean theoretical justification for why forget-set access is necessary in first-order unlearning. However, the analysis is restricted to strongly convex, smooth, Lipschitz losses with exact initialization at θ*, and the practical experiments are limited to logistic regression and a two-layer MLP on small datasets. Key open questions include extending the guarantees to non-convex settings (PL condition, NTK regime), handling inexact initialization, and validating the approach on large-scale deep networks where the strong-convexity assumptions are most violated.
