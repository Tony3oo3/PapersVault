## Problem

Existing 3D scene graph generation methods from RGB-D sequences model each node and edge category as a single prototype via discriminative predictors, which fails to capture the significant intra-class diversity (e.g., chairs with vastly different structures) and cannot resolve severe inter-class similarity (e.g., windows and pictures sharing planar-rectangle geometry). These methods lack explicit distributional modeling and discriminative mechanisms to handle the visual ambiguity inherent in 3D scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260914/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a complete RGB-D image sequence as input, reconstructs a dense 3D point cloud via the ScenegraphFusion pipeline, applies 3D geometric segmentation, and extracts multi-view RGB images (resized to 224×224) for each detected object. It outputs a 3D scene graph where each node receives a category label (from 20 or 160 classes) and each directed edge receives a relation label (from 8 or 26 classes), evaluated via Top-1 Recall and Mean Recall@1 on the 3DSSG benchmark.

## Method

GMPSSG replaces single-point prototypes with Gaussian Mixture-distributed Prototypes (GMP), where each category is represented by K weighted Gaussian components whose means, covariances, and mixture weights are jointly learned. Prototype-anchored Representation Learning shapes these prototypes through three complementary losses: likelihood-driven clustering (class-conditional NLL), mutual information-guided decorrelation (symmetric KL divergence between class-level fused Gaussians to enforce inter-class separation), and cohesion-oriented constraint (penalizing mean spread and variance within each prototype). Topology-aware Prototype Interaction injects co-occurrence priors by computing latent knowledge offsets via multi-head attention between node and edge prototype means, then calibrating the GMP means through a gated update that blends the learned means with the prior offsets. At inference, the GMP posterior computed via Bayes' rule is fused with the discriminative branch's softmax prediction to produce final node and edge labels.

## Results

On the 3DSSG benchmark (20 node / 8 edge classes), GMPSSG achieves 43.0% Triplet Recall and 64.8% Object Recall under the dense point cloud setting, surpassing the previous best (SCRSG) by +2.5 and +3.0 points respectively. Under the ground-truth point cloud setting, it reaches 73.0% Triplet Recall and 84.5% Object Recall, outperforming the strongest prior method (HESGR) by +5.4 and +1.2 points. On the larger 160-node / 26-edge setting, GMPSSG attains 37.3% mean Object Recall and 29.6% mean Predicate Recall, improving over MonoSSG by +8.4 and +4.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMPSSG outperforms all ten baselines on Triplet and Object Recall in both the dense and GT point cloud settings. Under the dense setting, it surpasses the closest competitor SCRSG (ICCV'25) by +2.5 on Triplet Recall (43.0 vs. 40.5) and +3.0 on Object Recall (64.8 vs. 61.8), while also achieving the best Predicate Recall without 'none' (33.2 vs. 27.6). Under the GT setting, it beats HESGR (TCVST'25) by +5.4 on Triplet Recall (73.0 vs. 67.6) and by +2.2 on mean Predicate Recall (73.4 vs. 71.2), and exceeds HEDSGP (TPAMI'25) by +6.9 on Triplet Recall and +7.0 on mean Object Recall. On the more challenging 160-node / 26-edge setting (Table 2), GMPSSG leads MonoSSG (CVPR'23) by +1.0 on Triplet Recall (68.6 vs. 67.6) and by a substantial +8.4 on mean Object Recall (37.3 vs. 28.9), demonstrating stronger generalization to long-tail categories. The method also improves over the feature-extraction-based baselines (MonoSSG, HEDSGP) on mean Predicate Recall in the GT setting, confirming that explicit distributional modeling via Gaussian Mixture prototypes complements implicit feature enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MonoSSG (Wu et al., 2023b) feature extraction pipeline (PointNet + DINOv2) and the ScenegraphFusion (Wu et al., 2021) reconstruction framework, but departs from the dominant paradigm in 3DSGG that focuses on network architecture for robust feature extraction or on inference-time rescoring with statistical/historical priors. Instead, it reframes the classification problem through a distribution-modeling lens, borrowing the Gaussian Mixture Model from adjacent tasks (segmentation, anomaly detection, incremental detection) to explicitly characterize intra-class diversity and inter-class similarity in node and edge features. The Prototype-anchored Representation Learning losses (likelihood-driven clustering, KL-based decorrelation, cohesion constraints) and the Topology-aware Prototype Interaction (gated mean-space calibration via multi-head attention) are novel contributions not present in prior 3DSGG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, distribution-level alternative to the feature-enhancement-and-rescoring recipe that has dominated RGB-D 3DSGG, and the per-class results (e.g., bookshelf 42.9→85.7, window 59.0→72.3) suggest the GMM formulation is particularly effective for geometrically ambiguous categories. However, validation is limited to a single indoor benchmark (3DSSG), the number of Gaussian components is fixed per setting, and long-tail classes with very few samples remain under-modeled. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to outdoor scenes with more complex spatial relations, whether adaptive per-class component counts (explored preliminarily) can close the long-tail gap, and how the framework interacts with emerging open-vocabulary or language-grounded 3DSGG methods.
