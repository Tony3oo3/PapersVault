## Problem

Dual-encoder candidate retrieval models cannot exploit pairwise query-item features (e.g., term overlap, interaction statistics) that cross-encoders leverage, limiting their expressivity. While Yadav et al. (2022) showed that CUR decomposition over relevance scores can approximate cross-encoder predictions, their approach relied on random support selection and lacked general theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether relevance-based representations can provably approximate any continuous relevance function and how to select support elements to maximize retrieval quality.

## Input / Output

At inference, a query is scored by the heavy ranker against a fixed set of 100 support items, producing a 100-dimensional relevance vector that serves as the query's raw input; items are similarly represented by their relevance vectors to support queries (precomputed offline). These vectors are passed through lightweight MLPs (~50K total parameters) to produce query and item embeddings in a shared space, which are then used with HNSW for approximate nearest-neighbor search to retrieve top-K candidates. The heavy-ranker budget for re-ranking is reduced by 100 calls to account for the support-item scoring, making comparisons with dual encoders fair.

## Method

The core mechanism is to replace raw query/item features with relevance vectors (scores from a black-box heavy ranker to a fixed set of support elements) and then apply trainable neural mappings f_I and f_Q to produce embeddings whose dot product approximates the true relevance. The paper proves via Stone-Weierstrass and universal approximation that any continuous relevance function on compact spaces can be uniformly approximated to arbitrary precision by such dot-product embeddings (Theorem 3.2). A key practical contribution is the l2-greedy support selection algorithm, which iteratively picks the item whose addition most reduces the total CUR approximation MSE across all items, outperforming random, popular, and clustering-based heuristics. The item-side mapping decomposes into a fixed CUR linear transform plus a trainable residual MLP, improving convergence stability. Training uses a listwise softmax loss with Adam, and the entire model has roughly 50K trainable parameters versus 300M–700M for production dual encoders.

## Results

On the production RecMusic dataset, RBE+l2-greedy achieves HitRate(100) of 0.3964, more than doubling the AnnCUR baseline (0.1478). On RecGames1, RBE+l2-greedy reaches 0.6682 vs. AnnCUR's 0.5842. Across the five ZESHEL entity-linking domains, RBE+l2-greedy consistently outperforms AnnCUR (e.g., 0.5849 vs. 0.4724 on Yugioh, 0.3867 vs. 0.2287 on StarTrek). In production re-ranking comparisons, RBE surpasses both the production Dual Encoder and AXN_DE from X=200 on RecGames1 and from X=100 on RecMusic, while using ~50K trainable parameters versus ~300M–700M for the DEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnnCUR (Yadav et al., 2022), RBE+l2-greedy wins on 8 of 9 datasets, with the most dramatic gain on RecMusic (0.3964 vs. 0.1478, a 2.7× improvement) and substantial gains on ZESHEL domains (e.g., +11.2 pp on Yugioh, +15.8 pp on StarTrek). The only dataset where RBE does not clearly win is Military, where AnnCUR+l2-greedy (0.3357) slightly edges out RBE+l2-greedy (0.3349). Against AXN_DE (Yadav et al., 2024) in production re-ranking, RBE+l2-greedy outperforms both the production Dual Encoder and AXN_DE on RecGames1 starting from X=200 (e.g., HR(300,300): 0.7221 vs. 0.6740 for AXN_DE) and on RecMusic from X=100 (e.g., HR(100,100): 0.3964 vs. 0.3843 for AXN_DE), while using 100 fewer heavy-ranker calls in the re-ranking budget. RBE achieves these results with only ~50K trainable parameters compared to ~300M (RecGames DE) and ~700M (RecMusic DE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CUR-decomposition-based candidate retrieval framework of Yadav et al. (2022) in two key directions: (1) replacing the fixed linear transformation with trainable neural network mappings f_I and f_Q over relevance vectors, and (2) systematically investigating non-random support item selection strategies (clustering, l2-greedy, popularity, diversity). It departs from the dual-encoder paradigm by showing that relevance vectors to a small set of support elements, rather than learned feature-based embeddings, can serve as universal query/item representations. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.2) provides a Stone-Weierstrass-based universality guarantee absent from prior CUR or distillation-based approaches, and the l2-greedy selection algorithm offers a principled alternative to the random sampling used in all prior work in this line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for production retrieval systems: a ~50K-parameter RBE model outperforms 300M–700M-parameter dual encoders on Yandex Games and Music recommendation services, while requiring no feature engineering beyond the heavy ranker's scores. The theoretical universality result (any continuous relevance function, including those depending on pairwise features, can be uniformly approximated) provides a principled justification for why relevance vectors are expressive enough, addressing a fundamental limitation of dual encoders. Key limitations include the hard dependency on a strong, available heavy ranker and the pre-processing cost of M·|S_Q| heavy-ranker calls. Open questions include convergence rate analysis, whether RBE can be combined with adaptive methods like AXN for further gains, and whether the approach transfers to settings where the heavy ranker is weak or unavailable.
