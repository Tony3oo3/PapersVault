## Problem

State-of-the-art spectral feature learning methods for nonparametric instrumental variable regression (e.g., SpecIV) learn features that are agnostic to the outcome variable, capturing only the dominant singular subspaces of the treatment-instrument operator. When the true causal function is poorly aligned with these dominant singular functions (spectral misalignment), the learned features become uninformative and the estimator fails. This paper identifies this limitation and proposes an outcome-aware framework that remains effective in such challenging regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples of (Y, X, Z) tuples, where X (treatment) and Z (instrument) can be high-dimensional objects such as 64×64 RGB sprite images or tabular state-action pairs, and Y is a scalar outcome. Two independent datasets are used: a feature-learning set D_m to train the neural feature maps, and an estimation set D_n to fit the 2SLS structural function. The output is a pointwise estimate ĥ_θ(x) = φ_θ^(d)(x)ᵀ β̂_2SLS of the structural function h₀: X → ℝ, representing the causal effect of treatment on outcome.

## Method

The core contribution is an augmented contrastive loss L_δ = L₀ + R_δ that extends the standard SpecIV spectral loss with a regularization term R_δ = −δ² E[Yψ_θ(Z)]ᵀ C_ψ^{-1} E[Yψ_θ(Z)], encouraging learned instrument features to be predictive of Y. This is shown to be equivalent to learning the best rank-d approximation of an augmented operator T_δ = [T | δr₀] that appends an outcome-aligned column to the original conditional expectation operator. In practice, the loss is optimized jointly over network parameters θ and an auxiliary vector ω (Eq. 5) to avoid backpropagation through the inverse covariance. The theoretical analysis provides a non-asymptotic high-probability 2SLS error bound and shows that in the 'bad' spectral misalignment scenario, the augmented method can recover h₀ with a single feature (d=1) while standard SpecIV would require d≥k features, with the spectral gap γ_d(δ) becoming positive for sufficiently large δ.

## Results

On the dSprites h_old benchmark, selecting a small positive δ yields an average 20% improvement in IV regression MSE over standard SpecIV (δ=0). On the more challenging h_new benchmark, vanilla SpecIV severely underperforms DFIV, while AugSpecIV with tuned δ matches or slightly exceeds DFIV. In Off-Policy Evaluation across Cartpole, Mountain Car, and Catch, AugSpecIV (with δ=1, 10⁻³, 10⁻² respectively) shows complementary strengths to DFIV: AugSpecIV performs well on Cartpole where DFIV and SpecIV struggle, while DFIV is stronger on Mountain Car. No single OPE method dominates all tasks, consistent with prior findings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFIV (Xu et al., 2021), the most competitive baseline: on dSprites h_old, AugSpecIV with small δ achieves ~20% lower MSE than SpecIV and remains competitive with DFIV; on h_new, vanilla SpecIV severely underperforms DFIV, but AugSpecIV closes the gap and slightly exceeds DFIV. Against KIV (Singh et al., 2019), AugSpecIV is compared in the dSprites experiments (Figure 3) and outperforms it in the challenging h_new setting. Against the OPE methods of Chen et al. (2022), AugSpecIV shows complementary task coverage: it excels on Cartpole (where DFIV and SpecIV underperform due to spectral misalignment) while DFIV is stronger on Mountain Car. The paper notes that DFIV performed slightly worse than in Chen et al. (2022), likely due to randomness in hyperparameter sampling, making direct cross-paper comparisons approximate. Overall, AugSpecIV wins or ties in the spectral-misalignment regimes that are the paper's target, while DFIV retains an edge in well-aligned OPE settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SpecIV framework (Sun et al., 2025) for spectral feature learning in nonparametric IV regression by making the feature-learning process outcome-aware. The key departure from SpecIV and its theoretical analysis (Meunier et al., 2026) is the introduction of an augmented operator T_δ that appends a rank-one 'column' aligned with the outcome r₀, transforming the contrastive loss from a purely instrument-treatment objective into one that also rewards predictive power for Y. This positions the work between the outcome-agnostic spectral methods (SpecIV, DFIV) and fully adaptive approaches like Bruns-Smith (2025), offering a principled middle ground where the spectral structure of T is preserved but re-weighted by outcome relevance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental limitation of spectral NPIV methods: when the structural function h₀ is misaligned with the top singular functions of T, standard SpecIV requires learning k features (k−1 spurious) while the augmented method recovers h₀ with a single feature, as formalized in the 'bad scenario' theorem. Open questions include extending the rank-one augmentation to higher-rank perturbations, providing architecture-specific guarantees that DNN training drives the population excess loss to zero, and establishing a principled (rather than heuristic) theory for δ selection. The OPE application demonstrates broader relevance beyond IV regression, suggesting the augmented-operator paradigm could generalize to other settings where conditional operators must be learned with task-specific objectives.
