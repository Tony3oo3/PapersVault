## Problem

When multiple LLM judges produce pairwise comparative judgments for NLG evaluation, their probabilities vary substantially in reliability and often violate transitivity and commutativity. Existing aggregation strategies either assume uniform judge reliability (simple averaging) or require human labels for per-judge calibration (temperature scaling), leaving no principled way to weight heterogeneous judges in a reference-free, unsupervised setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes soft pairwise preference probabilities p_ij ∈ [0,1] extracted from token-level logits (tokens 'A' vs 'B') of open-weight LLM judges, after a symmetrisation step that enforces commutativity (p'_ij = ½(p_ij + 1 − p_ji)). It produces a global item ranking via latent skill parameters {s_i} and per-judge reliability scalars {σ_k}, with ranking quality measured by Spearman rank correlation against human scores. Because probabilities are derived from logits, the approach is restricted to open-weight models that expose token-level scores.

## Method

BT-σ extends the soft Bradley-Terry likelihood by inserting a judge-specific discriminator σ_k into the logistic argument, so that judge k's contribution is σ((s_i − s_j)/σ_k)^{p_ij^(k)}(1 − σ((s_i − s_j)/σ_k))^{1−p_ij^(k)}. Smaller σ_k indicates a sharper, more internally consistent judge, while larger σ_k downweights noisier probability signals. All parameters {s_i} and {σ_k} are jointly optimised by maximising the joint log-likelihood over all judges and comparisons using L-BFGS-B, requiring no human annotations. The key identifiability insight is that σ_k is meaningful only in the multi-judge, soft-comparison regime: in single-judge or hard-BT settings a global scaling can be absorbed into item skills, but relative differences across judges carry a genuine reliability signal. The learned 1/σ_k correlates strongly (PCC ≈ 90%) with independent cycle-inconsistency rates, confirming that BT-σ functions as an unsupervised, comparative analogue of temperature scaling that models judge reliability directly during ranking inference.

## Results

On SummEval, BT-σ achieves an overall SRC of 50.50 (averaged across COH, CON, FLU, REL), outperforming soft BT (49.40), hard BT (47.34), and Avg-Prob (45.15). On Topical-Chat, BT-σ reaches 59.84 overall SRC, surpassing soft BT (58.50) and hard BT (57.26); the hard BT-σ variant further improves to 60.15, particularly on the highly inconsistent ENG aspect (67.36 vs. 61.87 for soft BT). On NovelEval, BT-σ achieves 46.92 SRC versus 45.28 for soft BT and 43.59 for Avg-Prob. The learned discriminator 1/σ_k correlates strongly with independent cycle-consistency measures (PCC ≈ 90–93% across datasets), confirming it captures genuine judge reliability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BT-σ consistently outperforms soft BT (the PoE-BT baseline) on both SummEval (+1.10 overall SRC) and Topical-Chat (+1.34 overall SRC), with the largest gains on aspects where judge inconsistency is highest (e.g., Topical-Chat CNT: 56.30 vs. 53.87). Against Temp-BT, which uses human labels to fit per-judge temperatures, BT-σ matches or exceeds performance on SummEval (50.50 vs. ~50.16 overall) and clearly wins on Topical-Chat (59.84 vs. ~58.40), demonstrating that unsupervised reliability modelling is at least as effective as supervised calibration. The hard BT-σ variant dominates on highly inconsistent settings: on Topical-Chat ENG it reaches 67.36 SRC versus 61.87 for soft BT and 63.86 for Temp-BT, a 5.5-point margin over the best soft method. Crowd-BT, a mixture-based reliability model from the crowdsourcing literature, achieves only 48.35 overall SRC on SummEval, well below BT-σ (50.50) and even soft BT (49.40), confirming that its binary-only, mixture-based mechanism is less suited to the soft-probability LLM-jury setting. On NovelEval, BT-σ again leads with 46.92 SRC versus 45.28 (soft BT) and 43.59 (Avg-Prob), supporting generalisation beyond the two primary benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry model for pairwise comparisons (Bradley & Terry, 1952) and its soft-probability variant used in LLM comparative assessment (Liusie et al., 2024c, PoE-BT) by introducing a judge-specific discriminator parameter σ_k that jointly models item rankings and judge reliability. It departs from prior LLM-as-a-jury aggregation methods—simple probability averaging (Verga et al., 2024), supervised temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017), and neural weighting approaches like SkillAggregation (Sun et al., 2025)—by learning reliability entirely from pairwise comparisons without human labels. The closest prior method, Crowd-BT (Chen et al., 2013), also models per-annotator reliability but is restricted to binary outcomes and uses a mixture-based mechanism, making it fundamentally different from the continuous discriminator approach here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BT-σ provides a principled, unsupervised mechanism for reliability-aware aggregation that is directly applicable in reference-free evaluation settings where human calibration data is unavailable, and the strong correlation between learned σ_k and independent CycleRate measures (PCC ≈ 90%) validates the discriminator as an interpretable reliability signal. Key limitations include the requirement for token-level logits (restricting use to open-weight models) and the inability to distinguish a consistent but systematically biased majority of judges from a correct minority. Open questions include whether the approach can be adapted to closed-source LLMs that do not expose logits, and whether the reliability signal generalises to settings with shared systematic biases across judges.
