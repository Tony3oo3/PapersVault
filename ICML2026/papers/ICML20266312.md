## Problem

Existing MoE expert pruning methods are token-agnostic, treating all input tokens equally when estimating expert importance. This uniform treatment dilutes critical signals from informative tokens with noise from unimportant ones, leading to suboptimal pruning decisions. No prior work has explored how token importance can be leveraged to guide expert selection in MoE architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MoE model (e.g., Qwen3-30A3B with 128 experts, OLMoE-7A1B with 64 experts) and a small calibration set of 128 sequences (2,048 tokens each from C4 and CodeAlpaca). It produces a statically pruned MoE model where removed experts are replaced by lightweight bias vectors (<0.1% of original parameter count), with all remaining expert weights frozen. The entire pipeline—token selection, expert scoring, pruning, and bias refinement—completes in approximately 10 minutes on a single A100 GPU with negligible peak memory overhead.

## Method

STEP (Selective Token-guided Expert Pruning) operates in three stages. First, attention-guided token selection identifies the top-τ fraction of tokens per layer using attention weights as a forward-only proxy for gradient-based importance, filtering out noise-contributing tokens before expert evaluation. Second, a dual-factor scoring mechanism computes each expert's importance as the product of its activation frequency (fraction of selected tokens routing to it) and its gating-weighted output norm, concentrating evaluation exclusively on selected tokens to improve signal-to-noise ratio. Third, pruned experts are converted into bias vectors initialized as the mean output over tokens where the expert was highly activated, then refined via a single-epoch gradient-based optimization (128 steps, AdamW) that updates only the bias vectors while freezing all expert parameters. The theoretical motivation formalizes the SNR improvement from token selection, and empirical IoU analysis confirms that token selection fundamentally reshapes which experts are retained.

## Results

On OLMoE-7A1B at 25% expert sparsity (48 experts), STEP achieves 58.42% average zero-shot accuracy across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the best baseline HC-SMoE (54.88%) by 3.54 points and MoNE (53.26%) by 5.16 points. On Qwen3-30A3B at 50% sparsity (64 experts), STEP reaches 65.78% average accuracy, exceeding MoNE (64.15%) by 1.63 points. On generation tasks (HumanEval + GSM8K) at 50% sparsity, STEP achieves 59.61% average, surpassing MoNE by 7.2 points and HC-SMoE by 15.1 points. Practically, STEP reduces memory by ~48% and delivers 1.31× prefill speedup on Qwen3-30A3B at 50% sparsity, completing the full pruning pipeline in 10 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEP consistently outperforms all five baselines across every model and sparsity level tested. Against MoNE (Zhang et al., ICLR 2026), the closest competitor, STEP gains 5.16 points on OLMoE-7A1B at 25% sparsity, 1.63 points on Qwen3-30A3B at 50% sparsity (64 experts), and 7.2 points on generation tasks (HumanEval/GSM8K) at 50% sparsity. HC-SMoE (Chen et al., ICML 2025) is the second-strongest baseline but trails STEP by 3.54 points on OLMoE-7A1B at 25% and 15.1 points on generation tasks at 50% sparsity. Gate Score (He et al., TMLR 2025) and D²-MoE (Gu et al., ICML 2025) are substantially weaker, with Gate Score dropping to 35.80% on OLMoE-7A1B at 50% sparsity versus STEP's 50.05%. MC-SMoE (Li et al., ICLR 2024) degrades most severely at higher sparsity (34.41% at 50% on OLMoE-7A1B), confirming that expert merging is inferior to expert preservation under aggressive compression. The performance gap between STEP and baselines widens with increasing pruning ratio, reaching up to 5.7 points in average accuracy and 27% relative perplexity reduction versus MoNE at 50% sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEP repurposes attention-based token selection mechanisms from efficient inference literature (FastV, PyramidDrop) — originally designed to drop unimportant visual or text tokens during decoding — and applies them as a preprocessing stage for MoE expert importance evaluation. It departs from the dominant token-agnostic paradigm in MoE pruning (MoNE, Gate Score, MC-SMoE, HC-SMoE, D²-MoE), which uniformly aggregates gating signals across all calibration tokens, by arguing this causes signal dilution that obscures which experts truly matter. The dual-factor scoring (frequency × gated output norm) and expert-to-bias conversion are novel components, though the bias idea has precedent in MoNE's 'lightweight novice' replacement; STEP's distinction is the global gradient-based refinement of bias vectors rather than per-expert closed-form means.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 10-minute, single-GPU pruning pipeline that halves memory and yields 1.5× speedup on a 30B MoE model makes large MoE deployment accessible in resource-constrained settings without retraining. However, the authors acknowledge that gains are marginal at low pruning ratios (e.g., +0.23% over the best baseline at 48 experts on Qwen3-30A3B) and become meaningful primarily at aggressive sparsity (≥50%), limiting the method's utility for mild compression. Open questions include whether the attention-based proxy remains optimal as models grow, whether the approach extends to dynamic or learned routing, and whether the 1-epoch bias refinement could be replaced by a fully closed-form solution to eliminate the fine-tuning step entirely.
