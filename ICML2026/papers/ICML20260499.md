## Problem

Existing variable selection methods handle either group sparsity (Group LASSO, group knockoffs) or transformational sparsity (Split Knockoffs), but none address the combined setting where sparsity is defined over groups of linearly transformed parameters. This gap is critical for applications like LLM behavior auditing, where one must identify outlier models by detecting group-level deviations in a contrast-transformed parameter space, and for neuroscience, where abnormal brain connectivity is encoded through graph-difference operators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a design matrix X, a response vector (or matrix for multivariate responses), a pre-specified transformation matrix D ∈ R^{m×p}, and a partition of the m-dimensional transformed space into k disjoint groups. The data is randomly split into two subsets D1 and D2; D1 estimates the regression coefficients while D2 constructs knockoff copies. The output is a selected group set Ŝ with guaranteed group-level FDR control at a user-specified nominal level q, where each selected group corresponds to a non-null block in the transformed parameter space (e.g., an outlier LLM or an abnormal brain region pair).

## Method

SGK extends Split Knockoffs to grouped transformed variables by introducing a variable-splitting formulation that relaxes the hard constraint γ = Dβ with a quadratic penalty controlled by ν, converting the group penalty into an identity-structured form that enables valid knockoff construction. In Step I, β(λ) is estimated on D1 via the Split Group Lasso objective; in Step II, a knockoff copy Ã_γ is constructed on D2 satisfying specific covariance constraints, and group importance scores Z_l and Z̃_l are computed as the emergence times (largest λ at which the group is nonzero) along the respective solution paths. The contrast statistic W_l = Z_l · sign(Z_l − Z̃_l) is thresholded using a data-adaptive rule to select groups while controlling modified group-FDR (SGK) or standard group-FDR (SGK+). Theoretical guarantees include FDR control for all ν > 0 and model selection consistency under a ν-incoherence condition, where ν governs a trade-off between suppressing false positives (larger ν) and retaining sensitivity to weak non-null signals (smaller ν).

## Results

In synthetic experiments (n=1000, p=200), SGK consistently controls empirical FDR at the nominal level q=0.2 while maintaining high power, with the cross-validated estimator outperforming the solution-path estimator. On MMLU-Pro (13 tasks, 6 LLMs), SGK flags outliers in 6 of 13 subjects (Chemistry, Physics, Comp.Sci., Health, Psychology, Biology) while correctly identifying no outliers in 7 subjects, with detected deviations aligning with high-entropy domains. On ADNI (n=752), the top-selected brain regions—left middle temporal gyrus (frequency 0.90) and bilateral hippocampus (0.70)—match well-established early-degeneration markers in Alzheimer's disease literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against a direct competing method, as it addresses a previously unaddressed problem (group-level FDR control under transformational sparsity). It extends two prior frameworks: Split Knockoffs (Cao et al., 2024), which handles element-wise transformational sparsity but not group structure, and Group Knockoffs (Dai et al., 2016), which handles group sparsity but not linear transformations. In the LLM application, SGK's detections go beyond what raw accuracy or entropy alone would suggest: GPT-5 is flagged in Health despite competitive accuracy (~51%), reflecting differences in reasoning structure rather than surface performance, while Qwen-Instruct is flagged in Psychology with ~41% accuracy versus 48–64% for other models. In the ADNI application, selected regions and connectivity patterns align with established neuroimaging literature on early AD degeneration (hippocampus, temporal lobe, amygdala), with one potential false discovery (Putamen_L–Pallidum_L) noted. The method's FDR guarantee at q=0.2 provides a principled error bound absent from heuristic outlier-detection approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Split Knockoff framework (Cao et al., 2024), which provides element-wise FDR control for transformational sparsity, to the group-level setting, thereby unifying two previously separate lines of work: Group Knockoffs (Dai et al., 2016) for group-sparse variable selection and Split Knockoffs for sparsity in linearly transformed parameter spaces. The key technical departure is constructing valid knockoff copies under a group-lasso penalty in a lifted (split) model, where the variable-splitting parameter ν introduces an orthogonal structure that facilitates knockoff construction while preserving group-level FDR guarantees. The Primal-Dual Witness analysis for model selection consistency under the ν-incoherence condition is a new theoretical ingredient not present in either parent framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework fills a concrete methodological gap: prior knockoff procedures handle either group sparsity or transformational sparsity but not both simultaneously, yet this combined structure arises naturally in LLM behavior auditing (model deviations from a reference) and neuroimaging (connectivity differences). The LLM auditing application is timely, revealing that behavioral deviations concentrate in semantically complex domains and can reflect reasoning-style differences rather than raw accuracy gaps. Open questions include extending to random design settings, incorporating adaptive group weights (e.g., Adaptive Group LASSO), and whether the fixed-design FDR guarantees hold under the distributional shifts common in real LLM evaluation pipelines.
