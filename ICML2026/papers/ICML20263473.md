## Problem

Peptides exhibit pronounced conformational flexibility at binding interfaces, creating a strongly coupled sequence-structure relationship that existing fixed-geometry latent diffusion models cannot capture simultaneously with conformational diversity and physical feasibility. Standard equivariant encoders miss local flexibility, static sequence-structure fusion cannot represent their dynamic interplay, and diffusion without timely geometric feedback causes sampling trajectories to drift from physically reasonable energy landscapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For sequence-structure co-design, the input is a receptor binding interface represented as a channel-augmented residue graph (G_aa^B) with C=14 predefined atomic positions per residue; the output is a full-atom peptide sequence and its 3D conformation. For binding conformation generation, the input additionally includes the peptide sequence, and the output is the peptide's spatial binding pose relative to the receptor. The receptor graph is consumed as a conditioning signal throughout encoding and diffusion, while the peptide is represented as a full-atom geometric graph with all backbone and side-chain coordinates.

## Method

PepFGLD builds a Flex-VAE that encodes peptide-receptor complexes into latent point clouds using FlexEGNN, an E(3)-equivariant GNN that injects sequence-distance-dependent flexibility modulation (learnable scalar fields per discretized residue index gap) into edge messages and a channel-modulation mechanism over multi-channel geometric coordinates, preserving equivariance while adapting interaction strength to local conformational states. A Bidirectional Sequence-Structure Interaction Module (SSBIM) applies mirrored cross-modal attention to couple sequence semantics with geometric representations, and a kernel-based (RBF) nonlinear mapping parameterizes the VAE posterior to form a continuous conformational manifold. Diffusion operates in this latent space with a two-stage training strategy (unsupervised pretraining on protein fragments, then fine-tuning on protein-peptide complexes), and a Time-Dependent Energy-Guided (TDEG) mechanism augments the reverse update with the gradient of a differentiable geometric energy (consecutive distances, clashes, bond angles, dihedrals, spatial distribution) weighted by a monotonically increasing schedule λ(t), so that exploration is preserved at high noise while physical convergence is progressively enforced at low noise.

## Results

On the Sequence-Structure Co-Design task (PepBench), PepFGLD achieves the best binding energy (ΔG = −19.86 kcal/mol) and highest success rate (59.35%), outperforming the strongest baselines PepGLAD (−13.46, 47.12%) and PepMimic (−11.21, 52.28%). On PepBDB, PepFGLD attains the best success rate (49.67%) and diversity (0.8276), with ΔG of −18.74. For Binding Conformation Generation on PepBench, PepFGLD achieves the lowest RMSD_α (4.14 Å), lowest full-atom RMSD (5.41 Å), and highest DockQ (0.607), while on PepBDB it achieves the best full-atom RMSD (9.45 Å) despite second-best backbone RMSD (8.86 Å).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PepFGLD outperforms all five baselines on the primary Sequence-Structure Co-Design task. Against the closest competitor PepGLAD (NeurIPS 2024), PepFGLD improves ΔG by 6.4 kcal/mol on PepBench (−19.86 vs. −13.46) and by 0.83 kcal/mol on PepBDB (−18.74 vs. −19.57), while raising success rate by 12.2 and 2.5 percentage points respectively. PepMimic (Nature BME 2025) is competitive on PepBDB (ΔG −19.27, Con 0.9573) but trails PepFGLD on PepBench by 8.65 kcal/mol in ΔG and 7.07% in success. GeoLDM (ICML 2023) achieves reasonable ΔG (−17.22 on PepBench) but lags in success rate (50.45% vs. 59.35%) and consistency. The non-diffusion baselines HSRN and dyMEAN are substantially weaker, with success rates below 15% and ΔG near or above zero, confirming that diffusion-based latent generation is essential for flexible peptide design. On Binding Conformation Generation, PepFGLD wins all three metrics on PepBench (RMSD_α 4.14, RMSD_a 5.41, DockQ 0.607) but is narrowly outperformed by GeoLDM on PepBDB backbone RMSD (7.85 vs. 8.86) and by PepMimic on DockQ (0.371 vs. 0.345), though it still achieves the best full-atom RMSD (9.45 Å).

## Relation to Prior Work

PepFGLD extends the latent diffusion paradigm for full-atom peptide design established by PepGLAD and PepMimic, which encode variable-length structures into a fixed latent space via equivariant autoencoders and perform diffusion there. The key departures are threefold: (1) FlexEGNN replaces the static equivariant encoder with a flexibility-modulated variant that uses sequence-distance-dependent scalar gates and learnable channel weights to adapt geometric interactions to local conformational states; (2) SSBIM introduces bidirectional cross-modal attention between sequence and structure streams, replacing the static fusion used in prior latent peptide models; and (3) TDEG injects a time-scheduled differentiable geometric energy into the reverse diffusion trajectory, a mechanism absent from prior latent diffusion approaches for peptides. The theoretical analysis (Appendices A, D) formalizes that these modifications preserve E(3)-equivariance and that TDEG corresponds to principled energy-tilted score guidance rather than ad-hoc force injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flexibility-aware design addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in peptide generative models: standard equivariant encoders treat all local neighborhoods identically regardless of conformational flexibility, which is precisely where peptide-receptor binding is most challenging. The finding that PepFGLD's advantage over baselines is largest on high-flexibility (coil-rich) peptides (ΔG gaps of 7–11 vs. 5–6 on low-flex) validates the core design hypothesis. A stated limitation is that receptor-side conformational dynamics are not explicitly modeled, which will matter for systems with induced-fit binding. The framework's modular architecture (FlexEGNN, SSBIM, TDEG) and the two-stage pretraining/fine-tuning strategy make it a practical reference for future flexibility-aware molecular generative models beyond peptides.
