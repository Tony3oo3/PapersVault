## Problem

While self-evolution (SE) methods that use model-generated supervision signals show promising empirical results, it remains unclear how close closed-loop SE can get to oracle-supervised training under a minimal formulation where all signals must come from the model itself. This paper systematically quantifies the generalization gap between four representative SE strategies and oracle supervision in a controlled logical-reasoning setting, and probes whether the gap can be closed with more structured or computationally intensive internal feedback.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled prompt set (e.g., Knights-and-Knaves instances parameterized by number of people, or OpenThoughts3 math prompts) and a single instruction-tuned LLM, which is instantiated as both generator and verifier via different system prompts. The generator produces k candidate solutions per query, and the verifier is queried n times per candidate; thresholded majority voting (with tunable τ) filters ambiguous judgments to yield confident preference pairs (y_win, y_lose). These pairs are then used for offline DPO fine-tuning, producing a refined reasoning model evaluated by exact-match accuracy on held-out test sets of varying difficulty.

## Method

The paper unifies four SE strategies under a generator-verifier game framework: (1) SimpleSE, where a single verifier pass with thresholded voting extracts preference pairs; (2) RevisionSE, where the verifier provides free-form feedback and the generator revises its solution over multiple rounds, extracting a pair only when an incorrect solution is corrected to a correct one; (3) iterative SE, which repeats the GV loop over the same prompt set across training rounds; and (4) curriculum SE, which schedules prompts from easy to hard. All variants train via DPO with a fixed reference policy. The key finding is that a persistent 8–13% gap to oracle supervision remains across most configurations, but RevisionSE at the 12B scale nearly closes it (52.8% vs. 53.6% on KK), revealing that the gap is strongly scale-dependent and tied to the model's intrinsic verification and revision capability.

## Results

On the Knights-and-Knaves (KK) benchmark, Gemma 3 4B achieves 31.0% baseline accuracy, which rises to 40.7% with single-round SimpleSE (τ=0.6), 42.2% with multi-turn RevisionSE, 44.1% with iterative training, and 44.8% with curriculum learning, while oracle supervision reaches 46.6–53.3% depending on the protocol. A persistent 8–13% generalization gap to oracle supervision remains across all closed-loop SE variants at the 4B scale. At the 12B scale, RevisionSE narrows this gap substantially, reaching 52.8% average accuracy versus 53.6% for oracle (98.5% of oracle performance). On real-world reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, MATHHard, TabMWP), SimpleSE yields only modest gains of +1.6% to +2.9% over base models, far below the ~+10% gains observed on KK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its offline SimpleSE method against two online self-evolution baselines: INTUITOR (Zhao et al., 2025b), which uses the model's own self-certainty as an RL reward, and Absolute Zero / AZR (Zhao et al., 2025a), which uses a code executor for verifiable rewards. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, SimpleSE outperforms INTUITOR on GSM8K (90.6% vs. 87.3%) and MATH500 (76.0% vs. 75%), and substantially outperforms both AZR variants across all five benchmarks (e.g., 92.3% vs. 68.8% on TabMWP, 51.5% vs. 32.8% on MATHHard, 17.6% vs. 5.1% on KK). However, the authors caution that these are not fully controlled comparisons: SimpleSE starts from instruction-tuned models and uses offline DPO, while AZR and INTUITOR are trained from base models via online RL. The key finding is that despite these favorable comparisons, the absolute gains from SimpleSE on open-ended reasoning tasks remain small (+1.6% to +2.9%), in stark contrast to the ~+10% gains on the deterministic KK benchmark, highlighting that closed-loop SE is highly sensitive to the verifiability of the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the core preference-optimization loss and on iterative DPO (Pang et al., 2024) for its multi-round training variant, but its primary contribution is a unified empirical framework that places single-round verification, multi-turn revision (RevisionSE), iterative training, and curriculum learning under a single closed-loop generator-verifier formulation. It departs from prior self-evolution work (INTUITOR, Absolute Zero, R-Zero, LSP) by restricting all supervision to a single model's internal signals with no external tools, code executors, or verifiable rewards, and by explicitly quantifying the residual gap to oracle-supervised training on a deterministic benchmark (Knights-and-Knaves) that avoids data contamination. The paper also connects to the generation-verification gap literature (Song et al., 2024) and model-collapse results (Shumailov et al., 2024), but reframes the question as a controlled measurement of how much of the oracle gap can be closed by increasingly structured internal feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantification of the closed-loop self-evolution gap to oracle supervision across model scales, showing a persistent 8–13% deficit that narrows sharply only at 12B parameters with multi-turn revision. The finding that SE primarily sharpens existing solution modes (Pass@1 improves while Pass@32 is unchanged) reframes self-evolution as a calibration mechanism rather than a capability-expanding one, with direct implications for when practitioners should invest in self-generated data pipelines versus oracle-labeled training. Open questions include whether hybrid strategies (oracle bootstrap followed by SE calibration) can close the remaining gap, how SE interacts with inference-time scaling methods, and whether the 12B near-parity result extends to even larger models or to open-ended reasoning domains where verifiability is absent.
