## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning prevents catastrophic forgetting by freezing task-specific prompts after learning, but this isolation also blocks later tasks from improving earlier ones, leaving backward knowledge transfer unexplored. The paper addresses two critical sub-questions: when is backward refinement actually beneficial (i.e., which prior tasks are compatible with the current task's learning signal), and how to update prior prompts safely without overwriting directions critical to the earlier task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264461/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text inputs from 15 sequential NLP tasks (sentiment analysis, NLI, QA, summarization, etc.) paired with 10-token soft prompts prepended to the input, processed by a frozen pretrained backbone (T5-Large, LLaMA-2-7B, or Qwen-3-4B). Beyond producing standard task outputs (class labels or generated text), SABER additionally outputs refined versions of selected prior-task prompts, updating them in-place during current-task training. The protected subspace rank is fixed at r=3, and backward refinement uses a reduced learning rate (0.001) applied only during the final 2 epochs of each task's training.

## Method

SABER combines two complementary components: (1) a task-correlation criterion that selects which prior prompts to refine, using either projection-based geometry (projecting the current-task gradient onto the prior task's SVD-derived gradient subspace and checking directional alignment via cosine similarity) or loss-distribution-based response similarity (Wasserstein distance between per-batch loss distributions under the frozen backbone vs. under the prior prompt); and (2) a constrained backward update that projects the current-task gradient onto the orthogonal complement of a cumulative protected subspace, ensuring each refinement step only explores previously unused directions. The protected subspace is initialized from the prior task's gradient subspace and augmented after each refinement to prevent successive updates from revisiting or overwriting earlier directions. A theoretical guarantee (Proposition 4.2) shows that under L-smoothness and stepsize η≤1/L, K safe refinement steps yield monotonically non-increasing current-task loss while remaining orthogonal to all protected directions, making the backward transfer provably non-interfering.

## Results

On the Long Sequence benchmark with T5-Large (Order 1), FPP+SABER-P achieves an Average Performance (AP) of 80.46 and Backward Transfer (BWT) of +1.76, outperforming the best baseline SAPT (AP 78.14, BWT −0.45) by 2.32 AP points and 2.21 BWT points. On SuperNI (Order 1), FPP+SABER-P reaches AP 45.50 with BWT +1.86, versus SHLPT's AP 43.87 and BWT −0.11. Critically, SABER is the only method across all settings (both benchmarks, both task orders, T5-Large and LLaMA-2-7B) to achieve consistently positive BWT, while every baseline exhibits negative backward transfer ranging from −0.11 to −17.18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SABER outperforms all seven baselines on both AP and BWT across every benchmark and task order. On AP, the closest competitors are SHLPT and SAPT (e.g., 77.40 and 78.14 on Long Sequence O1 with T5-Large), but SABER-P (FPP) leads by 2.3–3.0 AP points. The most striking gap is in BWT: SABER achieves +1.76 to +3.12, while the best baseline (ProgPrompt) reaches only −0.12, meaning SABER improves earlier tasks by roughly 2 percentage points while all baselines degrade them. On LLaMA-2-7B, the same pattern holds with FPP+SABER-P reaching AP 82.87 and BWT +1.56 on Long Sequence O1, versus SHLPT's 79.40 and −0.27. The loss-distribution variant (SABER-L) sometimes yields even higher BWT (e.g., +3.12 on Long Sequence O2) at the cost of slightly higher variance, while the projection-based variant (SABER-P) offers more stable gains. Both variants confirm that the orthogonal constrained refinement mechanism, not the specific correlation criterion, is the primary driver of positive backward transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

SABER extends the prompt-based continual learning paradigm (ProgPrompt, CODA-Prompt, SHLPT, SAPT) by departing from the standard assumption that once a task-specific prompt is learned it must remain frozen. While prior backward-transfer work in CL (Lin et al. 2022; Wong et al. 2024; Li et al. 2026) relies on replay buffers or global parameter updates, SABER repurposes gradient-projection ideas from GPM (Saha et al. 2021) and orthogonal gradient descent (Farajtabar et al. 2020) to constrain backward refinement to the orthogonal complement of a task's protected gradient subspace, enabling replay-free positive transfer. The task-correlation criteria—projection-based gradient geometry and Wasserstein loss-distribution similarity—are novel combinations that jointly determine *when* to refine, complementing the *how* of constrained updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SABER is the first framework to achieve consistently positive backward transfer in prompt-based CL without replay, backed by theoretical guarantees of non-interference and monotone loss decrease. Its modularity (plug-in to FPP or SPA frameworks) and backbone-agnostic design (T5, LLaMA, Qwen) make it broadly applicable, though the 34–44% runtime overhead and the need to maintain per-task gradient subspaces (for SABER-P) limit immediate deployment at very large task counts. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of tasks, whether richer correlation signals (e.g., representation-level) can further improve selection, and how it interacts with other PEFT strategies like LoRA or adapters.
