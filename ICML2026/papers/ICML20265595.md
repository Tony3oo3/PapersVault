## Problem

The classical successor representation (SR) decouples predictive dynamics from rewards, enabling rapid re-evaluation under new reward functions, but suffers from two bottlenecks that limit its utility for transfer: (1) inherent policy dependence, where policy re-optimisation for a new task invalidates the established predictive representation, and (2) spectral diffusion, where SR features are globally supported and overlapping, scaling poorly in topologically complex (e.g., compartmentalised) environments. The paper asks whether incorporating temporal abstraction into the SR construction can yield a predictive representation that is simultaneously robust to task-induced policy changes and amenable to sparse, interpretable decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state-transition trajectories from a discrete MDP along with a set of temporally extended options (each defined by an initiation set, option-specific policy, and termination function, typically discovered as eigenoptions from the random-walk SR eigenvectors) and a high-level policy over the augmented action space. It produces an N×N HSR matrix capturing expected discounted state occupancy under the high-level policy, which is then factorised via NMF into an N×K non-negative basis matrix Φ (the sparse state features used for linear function approximation) and a K×N coefficient matrix. In the exploration setting, the HSR matrix additionally yields intrinsic reward signals (row-norm or SPIE variants) that drive SARSA-based agents in procedurally generated mazes.

## Method

HSR extends the SR Bellman recursion to operate at the option level: the HSR operator T^μ decomposes into an intra-option SR term B (expected occupancy within a single option execution) and a continuation kernel term G (discounted probability of terminating in each state, computed analytically as F^ā = M^ā · diag(β_ā)), yielding a contraction mapping with factor γ (Theorem 3.1) that supports online TD learning. For transfer, the authors average HSRs across optimal policies from a distribution of pre-training tasks to obtain the 'expected HSR' (eHSR), further relaxing policy dependence. The key representational insight is that HSR dynamics are piecewise-smooth—preserving intra-compartment dispersion while maintaining inter-compartment structure—making NMF (rather than SVD) the appropriate low-rank decomposition, as SVD's orthogonal basis smears the multi-scale manifold structure into oscillatory, globally supported components. The resulting HSR-NMF basis is sparse, localised, and over-represents bottleneck states, providing a policy-agnostic hierarchical map that supports sample-efficient transfer to novel reward configurations and scalable intrinsically motivated exploration in large mazes.

## Results

In the four-room transfer task, HSR-based agents achieve significantly higher transfer efficiency than SR-based agents (p=0.008, df=38), with the relative change in the predictive representation after policy adaptation being significantly lower for HSR than SR (p<0.001, df=38). For low-rank basis comparison, HSR-NMF outperforms all alternatives: RW-SR_SVD (p=7.91×10⁻¹⁷), ESR_SVD (p=2.93×10⁻⁹), HSR_SVD (p=6.73×10⁻¹⁰), and even full HSR row features (p=4.59×10⁻¹⁸) in episodes to reach optimal performance. In procedurally generated mazes, HSR-SPIE maintains high asymptotic state coverage as maze size increases, whereas SR-SPIE coverage degrades drastically; the interaction effect between algorithm and maze size is highly significant (p=8.7×10⁻¹⁸, F₄=35.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical random-walk SR (Dayan, 1993), HSR achieves significantly higher transfer efficiency (p=0.008) and substantially less representation drift after policy changes (p<0.001); HSR-NMF further dominates RW-SR_SVD by a wide margin (p=7.91×10⁻¹⁷ in episodes to optimal). Against SR-GPI (Barreto et al., 2017), the paper shows that SR-GPI with one-hot features performs comparably to HSR-NMF Q-learning (p=0.199), but combining SR-GPI with HSR features (row or NMF) yields significantly stronger transfer than HSR features alone (p=0.003 and p=0.007 respectively), confirming the two approaches are complementary. Against SR-SPIE intrinsic exploration (Yu et al., 2023), HSR-SPIE maintains high state coverage in large mazes while SR-SPIE degrades sharply, with a highly significant algorithm×size interaction (p=8.7×10⁻¹⁸). Against SR-norm exploration (Machado et al., 2020), HSR-norm is slower in pure exploration but surprisingly more effective in sparse-reward goal navigation, suggesting the temporally extended structure helps agents escape local diffusive barriers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Dayan's (1993) successor representation by embedding the options framework (Sutton et al., 1999) into the predictive occupancy construction, yielding a temporally abstracted SR that is less sensitive to task-induced policy shifts. For option discovery, it adopts the eigenoption framework (Machado et al., 2017), which derives task-agnostic options from eigenvectors of the random-walk SR. A key methodological departure is the use of non-negative matrix factorisation rather than spectral decomposition (as in PVFs or spectral successor features) to extract the low-rank basis, motivated by the piecewise-smooth, locally dispersed geometry that HSR introduces relative to the globally diffusive structure of classical SRs. The paper positions HSR as complementary to Generalised Policy Improvement (Barreto et al., 2017): HSR addresses the representational axis while GPI addresses the algorithmic axis of transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HSR provides a principled bridge between model-free efficiency (linear value updates) and model-based interpretability (hierarchical, topologically grounded state features), with the NMF basis yielding sparse codes that align with bottleneck states and tile the state space without the 'whack-a-mole' interference of globally supported spectral bases. The demonstration that HSR-augmented intrinsic rewards scale to large procedurally generated mazes where SR-based exploration degrades suggests a practical path to efficient exploration in complex topologies. However, all evaluations are confined to discrete tabular MDPs; the authors acknowledge that extension to continuous or high-dimensional settings will introduce confounding factors (non-stationary feature learning, parameter interference) that could obscure the structural advantages demonstrated here. Open questions include principled (rather than heuristic) online option discovery, the behaviour of HSR-NMF in the over-complete regime, and integration into deep RL pipelines.
