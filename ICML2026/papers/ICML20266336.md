## Problem

Graph neural networks require complete node attributes, yet real-world graphs frequently have partially or entirely missing features. Existing generative approaches (e.g., SAT) rely on adversarial training in a shared latent space, which suffers from training instability, mode collapse, and a unidirectional structure-to-attribute alignment that fails to ensure generated attributes remain topologically consistent with the underlying graph.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266336/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GLAD takes a graph G=(V,E,A,X) where the adjacency matrix A is fully known and the attribute matrix X is partially observed (rows for V_obs are given, rows for V_miss are unknown). It produces the completed attribute matrix X̂ by imputing the missing rows, operating in a decoupled latent space where structure latents (Z_struct ∈ R^{N×d_s}) and attribute latents (Z_attr ∈ R^{N×d_a}) are compressed to dimensions far smaller than the original feature dimension F, enabling efficient diffusion on high-dimensional sparse attributes.

## Method

GLAD is a three-phase latent diffusion framework: (1) a GNN encodes topology into structure latents while an MLP encodes observed attributes into attribute latents (missing entries initialized as Gaussian noise); (2) a structure-conditioned denoising network performs T-step reverse diffusion in the attribute latent space, injecting structural information via multi-head cross-attention at each step; (3) a bidirectional alignment mechanism enforces consistency in both directions — a backward topological reconstruction loss (L_align) forces generated attribute latents to reconstruct the adjacency matrix, while a structure-aware classifier-free guidance (CFG) strategy extrapolates conditional and unconditional noise estimates during sampling to steer generation toward structurally relevant regions. Theoretically, the diffusion objective maximizes a tighter ELBO (minimizing forward KL) compared to GAN-based JS divergence, guaranteeing full support coverage, and the backward alignment loss is shown to maximize a variational lower bound on the mutual information I(A; Z_attr), formally anchoring generated attributes to graph topology.

## Results

On node classification (the primary downstream task), GLAD achieves 86.05% on Cora, 69.58% on Citeseer, 88.72% on Amazon-Computer, and 92.41% on Amazon-Photo, setting new state-of-the-art on three of four benchmarks. For direct attribute recovery, GLAD reaches Recall@10 of 17.32 on Cora (vs. 15.21 for the best GAN baseline WGNN, a 13.9% relative gain) and NDCG@50 of 27.22 on Citeseer. On real-world traffic ETA prediction, GLAD reduces MAE to 80.01 on London (8.6% below DIFFSTG's 87.55) and achieves 94.12% congestion classification accuracy on Melbourne, outperforming all 10 traffic-specific baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLAD outperforms all seven attribute-complete (AC) baselines (VAE, GCN, GAT, NEIGHAGGR, GRAPHSAGE, GRAPHMAE, ARWMF) by large margins on every dataset, with the closest AC method (NEIGHAGGR at 90.10% on Amazon-Photo) still trailing GLAD by 2.3 points. Among attribute-incomplete (AI) methods, GLAD beats T2-GNN by 11.6 points on Cora and GINN by 21.4 points on Amazon-Computer. Against the three attribute-missing (AM) GAN-based baselines, GLAD wins on Cora (86.05 vs. SAT-GAT 85.35, WGNN 82.74), Citeseer (69.58 vs. SAT-GAT 67.32), and Amazon-Computer (88.72 vs. WGNN 88.18), but is marginally behind SAT-GAT on Amazon-Photo (92.41 vs. 92.50, a 0.09-point gap). On the traffic ETA task, GLAD surpasses all four diffusion/representation baselines (START, JGRM, DYFFUSION, DIFFSTG) by 2–9 points in MAE across London, Madrid, and Melbourne. For traffic congestion classification, GLAD leads by 5.1 points over the best baseline (DYFFUSION, 90.36%) on Melbourne and by 6.6 points over JGRM on London. The diversity evaluation (Table 5) further confirms GLAD's advantage: its Average Pairwise Distance (1.12) closely matches ground truth (1.18), while GAN baselines collapse to near-deterministic outputs (APD ≈ 0.18–0.24).

## Relation to Prior Work

GLAD builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), transplanting both into a graph attribute completion setting. It inherits the decoupled latent space architecture from SAT (Chen et al., 2022) but replaces SAT's adversarial alignment with a likelihood-based latent diffusion process, and extends the unidirectional Structure→Attribute mapping with a backward topological reconstruction constraint. The paper positions itself against a family of GAN-based attribute-missing methods (SAT, AMER, WGNN) and propagation-based approaches (GINN, T2-GNN), arguing that the shift from JS-divergence minimization to forward-KL/ELBO optimization is the key conceptual departure that resolves mode collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional alignment mechanism—combining a backward structure-reconstruction loss with structure-aware CFG—offers a principled way to ensure generated attributes remain topologically grounded, which is critical for downstream GNN tasks on incomplete graphs. The quantitative diversity evaluation (APD ≈ 1.12 vs. ~0.2 for GAN baselines) provides direct evidence that diffusion-based generation captures multi-modal attribute uncertainty that adversarial methods miss. However, the approach is limited to fixed-topology attribute completion (not de novo graph generation), scalability to truly web-scale graphs remains untested, and the theoretical results (forward KL guarantees mode coverage) are well-known in the generative modeling literature, applied here in a formal but not novel way. Open questions include extension to heterogeneous graphs and whether the bidirectional constraint remains beneficial when the graph structure itself is partially observed.
