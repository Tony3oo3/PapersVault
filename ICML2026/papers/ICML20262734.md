## Problem

Existing GRN inference methods—whether regression-based (GENIE3) or causal graphical models (NO-TEARS, DCDFG, DCDI)—capture only static statistical associations or linear causal structures, failing to model the non-linear regulatory dynamics that drive processes like cellular differentiation. They also cannot handle the scale of modern ORF overexpression datasets (thousands of perturbations, hundreds of genes) while simultaneously producing interpretable, module-structured networks that reflect known biology.

## Input / Output

The method takes control (unperturbed) scRNA-seq gene expression profiles as initial cell states, the identity of perturbed transcription factors (overexpression or knockout), and the observed post-perturbation gene expression distributions (e.g., 7 days after TF overexpression in the TF Atlas). It outputs a factorized GRN matrix G = A·diag(α)·B of size d×d decomposed through l latent gene modules (reducing parameters from d² to 2dl), predicted final gene expression distributions under each perturbation, and interpretable co-regulated gene modules whose biological meaning is assessed via GSEA against Gene Ontology pathways.

## Method

PerturbODE models cell state trajectories under perturbation as a neural ODE whose dynamics function is a single-hidden-layer MLP with sigmoid activation (equivalent to the Hill function under log-expression), where each hidden neuron represents a gene module. The ODE is dy/dt = A·σ(α∘(By − β)) + Σ s_j·δ_j − Wy, with a diagonal decay matrix W ensuring stability and a shift term encoding the specific TF overexpression. Training pushes control cells through the ODE flow map and minimizes the Wasserstein-2 distance (computed via differentiable Sinkhorn) between predicted and observed post-perturbation distributions, plus an L1 sparsity penalty on B. The GRN is read directly from the product G = A·diag(α)·B, and an optional diffusion-based regularization enforces local contraction to establish equivalence with a deterministic structural causal model. The framework supports both imperfect (shift) and perfect (masking) interventions, and all model parameters are shared across perturbations while only the intervention strength s_r varies.

## Results

On the TF Atlas (812 genes), PerturbODE* with imperfect intervention achieves the highest recall (0.498 ± 0.020) and lowest p-value (0.001) against three experimentally validated human GRNs, substantially outperforming GENIE3 (recall 0.263) and DCDFG (recall 0.135). For perturbation prediction on 10 held-out TFs, PerturbODE achieves a median W₂ distance of 84 ± 88 and cell type distance of 0.86 ± 0.39, outperforming all linear SCMs (NO-TEARS: W₂ = 396) and scGPT (W₂ = 91), though GEARS achieves a lower W₂ (65 ± 7.3). On the 400-gene yeast GRN simulation, PerturbODE surpasses DCDFG, GENIE3, NO-TEARS, and NO-TEARS-LR in AUPRC, while DCDI achieves the highest AUPRC but fails to scale beyond ~400 genes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerturbODE substantially outperforms NO-TEARS and NO-TEARS-LR on both GRN inference (recall 0.498 vs. 0.000 on TF Atlas) and perturbation prediction (W₂ 84 vs. 396 and 105), with the linear SCMs producing nearly empty graphs at scale. Against DCDFG, PerturbODE* achieves 3.7× higher recall (0.498 vs. 0.135) and a dramatically lower p-value (0.001 vs. 0.416) on the TF Atlas, while DCDI wins on simulated 400-gene data (AUPRC 0.0059 vs. 0.0045) but fails to run on the 812-gene TF Atlas due to numerical instability. GENIE3 is the closest competitor on GRN inference (recall 0.263 vs. 0.498) and marginally outperforms PerturbODE* at low sparsity thresholds, but PerturbODE maintains lower FDR at medium and high thresholds (52.5% vs. 54.1%). For perturbation prediction, GEARS achieves a lower median W₂ (65 vs. 84) but relies on an external Gene Ontology knowledge graph as a prior, while PerturbODE achieves a slightly better cell type distance (0.86 vs. 0.89) and higher variance reflecting its ability to model diverse cell fates. Bicycle performs comparably to PerturbODE on 100-gene random DAGs but cannot be evaluated on 400+ gene datasets due to memory constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerturbODE unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) differentiable causal discovery methods (NO-TEARS, DCDI, DCDFG, Bicycle) that learn static DAGs from interventional data but lack dynamical modeling, and (2) neural ODE-based trajectory inference (Chen et al., 2018; Hossain et al., 2024; Jackson et al., 2023) that captures non-linear gene dynamics but is limited to single experimental conditions. The key architectural departure is encoding the GRN as the product of two factorized matrices (A·diag(α)·B) within a single-hidden-layer MLP, which simultaneously reduces parameter count from d² to 2dl and implicitly groups genes into biologically interpretable modules, drawing on the gene module network literature (Segal et al., 2005). The training objective uses Sinkhorn-approximated Wasserstein-2 distance between ODE-predicted and observed cell distributions, connecting the framework to optimal transport and causal representation learning (Schölkopf et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PerturbODE is the first method to scale GRN inference to 800+ genes from real large-scale perturbation data (TF Atlas, 1,836 TFs), where competing causal discovery methods fail outright due to memory limits or numerical instability. The module-based parameterization provides biological interpretability (validated against GO pathways and ChIP-Atlas), and the ODE formulation enables prediction of unseen perturbations—a capability static DAG methods lack. However, the method's perturbation prediction performance is mixed: it outperforms linear SCMs and scGPT but trails GEARS on W2 distance (which benefits from GO knowledge graph priors), and it exhibits high variance across held-out TFs. Open questions include extending to time-series Perturb-seq data (preliminary results show high variability), integrating ATAC-seq to reduce false discoveries, and establishing identifiability guarantees in noisy regimes.
