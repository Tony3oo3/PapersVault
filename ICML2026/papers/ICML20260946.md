## Problem

Depth estimation for transparent objects fails with conventional RGB-based methods because refraction and light transmission destroy the texture and shading cues that learning-based predictors rely on. While polarization imaging provides physically grounded geometric signals that remain informative in transparent regions, prior approaches treat polarization as a generic visual input without explicitly modeling the deterministic Fresnel relationships between polarization measurements and surface geometry, leaving these constraints largely underutilized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-shot RGB image (612×512) along with corresponding DoLP and AoLP maps captured simultaneously by a polarizer-array camera (FLIR Blackfly-S), and produces a dense metric depth map plus a pixel-level segmentation mask isolating transparent regions. The depth output is metrically calibrated via alignment to an active stereo sensor, and the segmentation mask specifically targets dielectric transparent surfaces rather than instance-level object boundaries, enabling selective application of polarization-derived geometric priors.

## Method

PolarDepth builds on a frozen Depth Anything V2 (ViT-S) backbone for initial global depth, then adds lightweight task-specific heads that explicitly decode refractive index η, zenith angle θ, and azimuth angle φ from DoLP and AoLP via the Fresnel reflection model, converting them into a surface normal field. A Poisson surface integration solved in the spectral domain via FFT recovers a relative, shape-constrained depth map from these normals, enforcing global gradient consistency. A Gaussian attention mechanism over predicted refractive index produces a transparency-aware segmentation mask that gates the fusion of polarization-derived local geometry with the foundational depth, injecting physical constraints only in transparent regions. Training uses a joint loss combining scale-invariant depth supervision, segmentation losses, and a physical consistency term that compares Fresnel-predicted DoLP against measured polarization, ensuring physically plausible surface orientations.

## Results

On the PTOD test set, PolarDepth achieves δ₁ = 0.859 and AbsRel = 0.142 in transparent regions, outperforming the best RGB-based competitor MODEST (δ₁ = 0.735, AbsRel = 0.252) by 12.4% and 43.6% respectively. On full-scene depth, PolarDepth reaches δ₁ = 0.612 and SI-Log = 0.209, and on segmentation it attains IoU = 0.893 and F_β = 0.943, surpassing the polarization-based PGSNet (IoU = 0.882, F_β = 0.936). The model uses only 24M parameters (ViT-S backbone) with 25.1 ms inference latency, comparable to the frozen DA2 baseline (23.0 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarDepth achieves the best score on every reported metric across all 11 baselines. Against the strongest RGB-based multi-task method MODEST, PolarDepth improves transparent-region δ₁ from 0.735 to 0.859 (+12.4 points) and AbsRel from 0.252 to 0.142 (−43.6%), while also surpassing it on full-scene depth (δ₁ 0.612 vs 0.605) and segmentation (IoU 0.893 vs 0.544). Compared to GW-Depth, the prior best transparent-object specialist, PolarDepth gains +24.5 points in transparent δ₁ (0.859 vs 0.614) and +1.4 points in segmentation IoU (0.893 vs 0.879). Against the frozen DA2 backbone that PolarDepth builds upon, the polarization-guided refinement yields a +31.2 point δ₁ improvement in transparent regions (0.859 vs 0.547). For segmentation, PolarDepth narrowly edges out the polarization-based PGSNet (IoU 0.893 vs 0.882; F_β 0.943 vs 0.936) while substantially outperforming all RGB-only segmentation methods (Trans4Trans IoU 0.574, InvPT 0.591). The diffusion-based Marigold, despite strong full-scene depth (δ₁ 0.579), lags significantly in transparent regions (δ₁ 0.467), confirming that generative priors do not resolve the physics of transparent surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarDepth builds on Depth Anything V2 as a frozen foundational depth backbone, extending it with polarization-guided geometric decoding that explicitly recovers refractive index, zenith angle, and azimuth angle from DoLP/AoLP via the Fresnel model. This departs from prior polarization-depth methods (DPS-Net, PPFT) that treat polarization as a generic auxiliary modality, and from RGB-only transparent object depth approaches (MODEST, GW-Depth) that rely on unreliable appearance cues in transparent regions. The paper also contributes the PTOD dataset, filling a gap left by existing benchmarks like HAMMER, which provides instance-level masks rather than the transparent-region-only masks needed to enforce polarization-derived physical priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate transparent object depth is a prerequisite for robotic grasping, AR, and autonomous navigation, yet remains unsolved by conventional RGB or active-sensor methods. The demonstration that physically grounded polarization priors (rather than learned appearance features) are the critical ingredient for resolving depth ambiguity in transparent regions offers a principled design principle applicable to other physics-informed perception tasks. A key limitation is sensitivity to weakly polarizing materials, and the evaluation is primarily on the authors' own PTOD dataset (2,878 scenes), leaving open questions about generalization to diverse real-world transparent materials and uncontrolled environments.
