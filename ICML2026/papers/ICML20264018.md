## Problem

Existing activation decomposition methods for language models (e.g., sparse autoencoders) assume concepts are realized as isolated global directions, implicitly requiring linear separability. This overlooks growing evidence that many concepts exhibit nonlinear or multi-dimensional structure dispersed across many directions with no built-in relational structure, making them unrecoverable without post hoc assumptions. The paper asks whether a scalable, unsupervised method can model the local geometric structure of activation space to yield interpretable decompositions that support both localization and steering.

## Input / Output

The method consumes residual-stream activations (d-dimensional vectors) from fixed layers of Llama-3.1-8B (layers 8, 22) and Gemma-2-2B (layers 6, 18), trained on 100M samples from The Pile. Each activation x is decomposed into a responsibility vector R_k(x) over K Gaussian centroids and a set of within-component latent coordinates ẑ_k(x) in a rank-R subspace, yielding a reconstruction x ≈ A·b(x) as a single matrix product against a shared dictionary of all centroids and loadings. For downstream use, centroids serve as steering intervention targets (interpolated toward μ_k), while DBM learns sparse masks over the routed component identity plus local coordinates for causal localization.

## Method

The core method is a Mixture of Factor Analyzers (MFA), a generative model where each of K components defines a low-rank Gaussian with mean μ_k and covariance C_k = W_k W_k^T + Ψ, partitioning activation space into regions each with its own local subspace of variation. Training uses gradient descent on the negative log-likelihood of the mixture (rather than EM), initialized via K-Means on a 4M-activation sample, with a uniform rank R=10 across components. The key structural difference from SAEs is that each activation is explained by two compositional geometric objects—a region centroid (absolute location) and a within-region offset (directional displacement in the local subspace)—rather than a sparse sum of global directions. This yields a two-segment reconstruction trajectory (anchor to centroid, then refine locally) and makes the local subspace span(W_k), not any individual loading, the meaningful unit of analysis. The approach scales to K ∈ {1K, 8K, 32K} components and is evaluated on the MIB benchmark (RAVEL, MCQA) for localization and AxBench-style steering, where it outperforms SAEs and is competitive with supervised methods like DAS.

## Results

On the MIB localization benchmark, MFA achieves 85.7% accuracy on RAVEL-Continent (Gemma-2-2B) and 81.6% (Llama-3.1-8B), outperforming SAEs by 14 and 11 points respectively, and beating the supervised DAS baseline on Continent for both models. On MCQA, MFA reaches 80.2% (Gemma) and 70.5% (Llama), surpassing SAEs by 15.3 and 4.9 points. For steering, MFA centroids roughly double the median concept+fluency score over SAEs on Gemma-2-2B and improve it by ~33% on Llama-3.1-8B. MFA decompositions also achieve a mean interpretability fraction of 0.96 ± 0.2, compared to 0.29 ± 0.2 for SAEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFA outperforms PCA and SAEs by large margins (3–16 points) on all RAVEL tasks and by up to 15 points on MCQA, confirming that local region structure captures causal variables that global directions miss. Against the supervised DBM baseline, MFA wins on 5 of 6 RAVEL subtasks (e.g., 85.7 vs. 69.7 on Gemma Continent) but trails on MCQA (80.2 vs. 82.1 Gemma; 70.5 vs. 75.0 Llama), where fine-grained positional pointers require within-region variation that DBM's sparse masking exploits more effectively. MFA beats the state-of-the-art supervised DAS on RAVEL-Continent for both models (85.7 vs. 84.2; 81.6 vs. 81.1) and is within 2–5 points on the remaining RAVEL tasks, though DAS dominates MCQA (92.0/91.7). On steering, MFA centroids outperform SAE features and DiffMeans in the majority of layer/model settings, roughly doubling the median harmonic-mean score on Gemma-2-2B, yet still fall short of the supervised ReFT-R1 and prompting baselines. In interpretability, MFA's two-part decomposition (centroid + local offset) yields a 0.96 mean interpretability fraction versus 0.29 for SAEs, indicating that ~75% of active SAE features are not directly interpretable from context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical Mixture of Factor Analyzers (Ghahramani et al., 1996) — a generative model from statistics — as the first application of MFA to LM activation decomposition, positioning it as a local-geometry alternative to the dominant sparse autoencoder (SAE) paradigm (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023) that assumes a single global dictionary of directions. It builds directly on recent evidence that LM activations exhibit local low-dimensional structure (Cai et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2025; Saglam et al., 2025) and the emerging shift toward subspace-level analysis units (Sun et al., 2025; Huang & Hahn, 2025; Tiblias et al., 2025), but differs by learning a single shared, scalable model of local geometry rather than task- or concept-specific subspaces. The key conceptual departure is from 'directions' to 'regions': each activation is decomposed into a region assignment (centroid) plus a within-region offset, rather than a sparse sum of global features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unsupervised decomposition tool that captures nonlinear and multi-dimensional concept structure that isolated global directions miss, with the striking finding that 96% of MFA's high-contribution features are interpretable versus only 29% for SAEs. It narrows the gap between unsupervised and supervised localization (beating DBM on 5/8 tasks, competitive with DAS) and outperforms SAEs on steering, suggesting local subspace structure is a viable unit for scalable model control. Key limitations include higher reconstruction error than SAEs due to the fixed rank constraint, and the model's inability to handle out-of-distribution activations well. Open questions include whether the mixture structure is truly necessary beyond a single global FA (the paper shows it is for MCQA but not RAVEL), and whether the observed multi-Gaussian concept neighborhoods can be exploited for more principled concept-level interventions.
