## Problem

Conventional photo retouching and portrait enhancement tools adjust low-level attributes (tone, color, skin) but cannot correct structural flaws introduced at capture time—such as unbalanced framing, poor viewpoint, or stiff poses. Existing instruction-based image editors lack robust photographic aesthetic understanding, producing edits that are semantically plausible yet aesthetically weak. The paper formalizes Aesthetic Photo Reconstruction (APR) as improving a photo's aesthetic quality via structural reconstruction while preserving subject identity and scene semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB photo with structural flaws and a text instruction prompt as input. It produces two outputs: (1) a structured, ordered editing action plan spanning seven progressive photographic dimensions (aspect ratio → framing/composition → camera viewpoint → subject placement → pose/action → focus/depth-of-field → color/light), each delimited by dimension-specific special tokens, and (2) a reconstructed RGB photo where structural edits have been applied. The action plan is the intermediate artifact that bridges planning and execution, and the reconstructed photo must preserve the original subject identity and scene semantics.

## Method

AesFormer decouples aesthetic planning from image editing into two stages. Stage 1 trains AesThinker (Qwen3-VL-8B) to generate ordered, executable editing actions: it is cold-started via SFT on GPT-5.2-distilled action plans from the AesRecon dataset, then further optimized with GRPO-A, a fully on-policy GRPO variant that uses three MLLM-based rewards (format compliance, semantic alignment with ground-truth actions, and creativity for diverse yet valid plans) to encourage exploration beyond single-trajectory imitation. Stage 2 fine-tunes AesEditor (Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, MMDiT backbone) with a rectified-flow objective on strictly aligned (poor, good, action) triplets, learning an action-conditioned velocity field that grounds photography-specific terminology into consistent pixel-level structural transformations. The seven-dimension ordering acts as a structural inductive bias, enforcing a global-to-local planning hierarchy that ablations confirm is essential for performance.

## Results

On AesRecon, AesFormer achieves a 65.33% win rate against the input poor image and 26.25% against the paired good image (GPT-4o evaluation), substantially outperforming all open-source baselines whose win rates against the poor image range from 3.43% to 16.50%. AesFormer also attains the highest Q-ALIGN aesthetic score (3.51) and LAION-V2 score (5.60) among all methods, and is competitive with the proprietary Nano Banana Pro (54.44% vs. poor, 16.67% vs. good by GPT-4o). Ablations confirm that each training stage (SFT, GRPO-A, action-conditioned editor) contributes monotonically, with the full pipeline yielding a 48.83-point gain in GPT-4o win rate over the unmodified Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AesFormer decisively outperforms all four open-source editing baselines (FLUX.1 Kontext, Bagel, Step1X-Edit-v1.1, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511) across every metric: its 65.33% GPT-4o win rate vs. the poor image exceeds the best open-source result (16.50% for Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 without a planner) by nearly 50 points. Against the proprietary Nano Banana Pro, AesFormer wins on GPT-4o win rates (65.33% vs. 54.44% vs. poor; 26.25% vs. 16.67% vs. good) and on Q-ALIGN (3.51 vs. 3.24), but trails on human win rate vs. poor (68.63% vs. 72.55%) and ArtiMuse (47.76 vs. 50.90), indicating a small residual gap in perceived aesthetic quality. Notably, simply pairing an off-the-shelf Thinker (Qwen3-VL-8B or GPT-4o) with any of the open-source editors does not close the gap and often degrades performance (e.g., Bagel drops from 12.40% to 3.43% with Qwen3 as Thinker), confirming that APR requires a jointly trained, aesthetic-specialized planner and editor rather than a plug-and-play composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior lines: instruction-following image editors (FLUX.1 Kontext, Step1X-Edit, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511) that execute explicit textual commands but lack aesthetic diagnosis, and photo retouching/portrait enhancement methods that operate only in tonal/appearance space. The key architectural move is decoupling aesthetic planning (a VLM emitting structured actions over seven photographic dimensions) from execution (a flow-matching editor conditioned on those actions), and applying GRPO-A—a reinforcement-learning variant of GRPO—to encourage diverse action plans rather than single-trajectory SFT. The data pipeline (VCMP) repurposes photography tutorial videos as a scalable source of aligned (poor, good) pairs, a curation strategy not seen in prior editing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APR is framed as a genuinely new task that existing editors cannot address, and AesRecon (9,071 pairs) provides the first dedicated benchmark for it. The finding that simply composing an off-the-shelf planner with an off-the-shelf editor fails—and can degrade performance—challenges the assumption that instruction-following is sufficient for aesthetic goals, motivating jointly trained Thinker+Editor pairs. Open questions include whether the seven-dimension ordering generalizes beyond the training distribution, whether APR extends to multi-image or video settings, and whether the GPT-4o-based pairwise evaluation protocol captures the full range of structural aesthetic improvements.
