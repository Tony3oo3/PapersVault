## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from a growing KV cache that causes both increasing per-step latency and escalating GPU memory as generation progresses, limiting usable temporal context and harming long-range consistency. Existing KV-cache compression techniques from NLP do not transfer directly, and sparse-attention methods designed for offline video diffusion frequently degrade quality or fail to sustain throughput in the streaming autoregressive setting where the cache grows unboundedly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263946/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time acceleration layer that wraps existing autoregressive video diffusion backbones (Rolling-Forcing, LongVie2, MAGI-1, SkyReels-V2, HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1-14B). It consumes a long text prompt (describing objects, actions, and events spanning the full video) and produces streaming video frames sequentially, with experiments generating up to 3000 frames on a single H100 GPU. No retraining, fine-tuning, or architectural modification of the underlying model is required; the method only modifies the attention inputs (cached K, V) and restricts which query-key pairs are computed.

## Method

FAST-AR is a unified, training-free attention framework built on the observation that autoregressive video diffusion attention is highly sparse (~85% recall at 30% density) and exhibits three exploitable redundancies: near-duplicate cached keys across frames, slowly evolving semantic queries/keys, and frame-selective cross-attention over long prompts. TempCache compresses the KV cache by using ANN matching (LSH or product quantization) to find temporally corresponding keys across frames and merging near-duplicates, with a proven exactness guarantee (Lemma 5.1) showing that attention over identical keys can be computed exactly from group representatives with a log-multiplicity logit shift and mean values. AnnCA prunes cross-attention by projecting latent queries and prompt keys into a shared LSH/quantized space and excluding prompt tokens that share no bucket with any current-frame query. AnnSA sparsifies self-attention by restricting each query to keys within the same semantic bucket, computed efficiently via block-sparse FlashInfer kernels. All three components use lightweight ANN retrieval (FAISS) at inference time with no offline preprocessing, and a dense-attention fallback ensures quality preservation when sparsity is insufficient.

## Results

On Rolling-Forcing (LongVBench), the full FAST-AR system (All Ours-LSH/Quant) achieves ×10.7–×10.8 end-to-end speedup over Dense FlashAttention-3 while matching VBench quality (84.02/83.99 vs. 84.08). TempCache alone compresses the KV cache to ~16% density with 90–91% attention recall and ×3.0–×3.2 E2E speedup, while AnnSA sparsifies self-attention to ~28% density at 92.4–92.6% recall (×2.7–×2.8 E2E). On LongVie2 (LongVGenBench), the full system delivers ×6.3–×6.9 speedup with VBench 63.69–64.91 vs. 69.67 for dense FA3. Critically, peak GPU memory remains nearly constant over 3K-frame rollouts, whereas all baselines show progressive memory growth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense FlashAttention-3 baseline (Shah et al., 2024), FAST-AR matches VBench quality (84.02 vs. 84.08) while achieving ×10.7–×10.8 E2E speedup and keeping peak GPU memory flat over 3K frames, whereas FA3's latency and memory grow linearly with cache length. Among KV-compression baselines, TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025) and FlowCache (Anonymous, 2026) provide only ×1.1 and ×2.3 component speedups respectively while retaining 83–93% attention density; TempCache compresses to ~16% density at comparable or better VBench (84.17–84.19 vs. 84.11–84.15) with ×6.8–×6.9 component speedup. For sparse self-attention, the offline-designed SVG1 (Xi et al., 2025) and SVG2 (Yang et al., 2025) catastrophically degrade quality (VBench 33.15/34.66 vs. 84.08) and are actually slower than dense attention (×0.1/×0.2 component speedup) in the autoregressive setting due to per-block preprocessing overhead; RadialAttn (Li et al., 2025) is more stable (VBench 61.51, ×2.8 component) but still loses ~23 VBench points. The strongest baseline combination, FlowCache+RadialAttn, reaches only ×4.4 E2E speedup with VBench 45.15, roughly half the quality of FAST-AR's ×10.7 at VBench 84.02.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three lines of work—KV-cache compression (TeaCache, FlowCache), sparse attention for offline video diffusion (SVG, SVG2, RadialAttention), and approximate nearest neighbor search (LSH, product quantization)—into a single training-free framework tailored to autoregressive video diffusion. It builds most directly on DiffTrack (Nam et al., 2025), which showed that temporal correspondence is recoverable from attention in video DiTs, repurposing this signal for KV-cache merging rather than point tracking. The key departure from prior sparse-attention work is the explicit treatment of the autoregressive KV-cache growth problem: the paper demonstrates that offline-designed sparsification methods (SVG, SVG2) actually *degrade* to sub-dense speedups in the streaming setting due to per-block preprocessing overhead, motivating the lightweight ANN-based design that avoids such overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of 5–10× end-to-end speedup with nearly constant peak GPU memory over 3K-frame rollouts is practically significant for real-time streaming video, interactive world models, and neural game engines, where unbounded cache growth is a hard blocker. The training-free, plug-and-play nature means existing autoregressive diffusion backbones can adopt these techniques without retraining. Open questions include how the method behaves under highly dynamic or scene-changing content (where temporal redundancy weakens, as shown by the reduced speedups on high-dynamic prompts), whether the ANN-based bucketing generalizes to 3D world models with richer action conditioning, and whether the dense-attention fallback can be made more granular to avoid losing all speedup in the worst case.
