## Problem

Prior outlier synthesis methods like VOS model virtual outliers as samples from simple parametric distributions (e.g., class-conditional Gaussians) outside the support of in-distribution data, ignoring the complex low-dimensional manifold geometry of learned feature spaces. This leads to synthetic outliers that are either trivially separable or indistinguishable from real data, and there is no principled mechanism to control their difficulty. Additionally, the OOD detection literature overemphasizes far-OOD benchmarks while under-addressing near-OOD scenarios where outliers share the same semantic domain as training classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 random crops for most datasets, 32×32 for CIFAR) and operates entirely on 128-dimensional penultimate-layer features extracted by a WRN-40-2 backbone (or ResNet-34 for ImageNet-scale experiments). At inference, it produces a class label from the main classification head and an OOD detection score via the energy-based log-sum-exp of logits; an optional conformal hypothesis-testing head converts per-class Mahalanobis or energy scores into p-values with formal error-rate guarantees. The method is architecture-agnostic and requires no image-space generation at training or inference time.

## Method

GCOS generates virtual outliers in two geometric stages: (i) a per-class PCA on a rolling feature buffer identifies low-variance principal components (the 'small' subspace below a 90% variance threshold) that define off-manifold directions, and (ii) a conformal shell—bounded by the 95th and 99th percentile Mahalanobis quantiles computed from a held-out calibration set—determines the scalar deviation α along each direction via binary search, ensuring synthetic points land in a controlled hard-negative regime. A contrastive regularization loss then minimizes the energy strangeness score of in-distribution features while maximizing it for synthesized outliers, with an adaptive margin scaled to batch statistics. The training loop alternates between an epoch-level 'Judge' calibration (fitting class-conditional subspace models and quantile thresholds) and a batch-level 'Proposer' synthesis step, decoupling direction identification from boundary determination to maintain stability as the network updates.

## Results

On the four near-OOD benchmarks, GCOS achieves the highest average AUROC of 93.47%, outperforming the strongest competitor NCIS (91.97%) and Dream-OOD (85.76%). Headline per-dataset AUROC values are 99.50 (Colored MNIST), 99.55 (Stanford Dogs), 95.61 (MVTec), and 79.23 (Retinopathy). GCOS also attains the lowest FPR95 on three of four datasets (1.00%, 0.00%, and 73.00%), with the sole exception being MVTec where NCIS achieves 3.08% versus GCOS's 23.08%. On the far-OOD CIFAR-10/100 benchmarks and ImageNet-100, GCOS remains competitive, matching or exceeding VOS on most OOD splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCOS outperforms all 16 baselines on average AUROC (93.47%), with the closest competitor being NCIS (91.97%), a diffusion-based method that requires costly image-space generation during training. On Colored MNIST and Stanford Dogs, GCOS achieves near-perfect AUROC (99.50% and 99.55%) and dramatically lower FPR95 (1.00% and 0.00%) compared to NCIS (24.50% and 10.00%) and VOS (18.50% and 5.00%). On MVTec, NCIS edges out GCOS on AUROC (96.50 vs. 95.61) and FPR95 (3.08 vs. 23.08), but GCOS still substantially surpasses VOS (80.37 AUROC) and the no-regularization baseline (94.86 AUROC). On Retinopathy, GCOS leads all methods (79.23 AUROC, 73.00 FPR95) over NCIS (75.29, 85.50) and Dream-OOD (73.35, 89.00). Classical scoring baselines (MSP, MaxLogit, ReAct, GradNorm, KL-Matching, ViM) are consistently 15–30 AUROC points below GCOS, with the largest gaps on Stanford Dogs where they drop to 40–58 AUROC. On the far-OOD CIFAR-10/100 benchmarks, GCOS is competitive but not dominant: it beats VOS on Textures AUROC (90.19 vs. 88.34 on CIFAR-10) but trails ASH and Free Energy on some splits, confirming that its primary advantage lies in the near-OOD regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCOS extends the Virtual Outlier Synthesis (VOS) / Outlier Exposure training paradigm by replacing its class-conditional Gaussian sampling with a geometric synthesis procedure that probes low-variance PCA directions of the learned feature manifold. The conformal prediction framework, traditionally an inference-time uncertainty tool, is repurposed here as a training-time heuristic: quantiles of a Mahalanobis nonconformity score on a calibration set define a 'shell' that controls how far synthetic outliers are pushed from the data centroid, ensuring they land in a hard-negative regime. For inference, the paper retains the standard energy-based score from Liu et al. (2020) while exploring conformal hypothesis testing as a statistically guaranteed alternative. This positions GCOS between the lightweight Gaussian synthesis of VOS and the computationally expensive diffusion-based methods (Dream-OOD, NCIS), operating entirely in penultimate feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that near-OOD detection (same semantic domain, unseen subcategories) is the practically critical regime, and GCOS achieves strong, consistent improvements on such benchmarks with a method that avoids image-space generation entirely. The exploratory conformal hypothesis testing extension is promising but currently limited by calibration-set size: it collapses to near-random performance on small datasets (Stanford Dogs, MVTec) while performing well on Colored MNIST, leaving open the question of how to maintain statistical guarantees with limited calibration data. The finding that Mahalanobis geometric loss effectively regularizes energy-based confidence (but not vice versa) suggests a deeper coupling between feature-space geometry and the classifier's logit landscape that warrants further theoretical investigation.
