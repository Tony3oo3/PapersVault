## Problem

In One-Shot Federated Learning, clients interact with the server only once, so distributional heterogeneity from joint domain shift and label shift cannot be corrected through iterative optimization. Existing OSFL methods (distillation, data synthesis, ensembling) either assume client feature representations are already aligned or address domain and label shift separately, leaving cross-client feature misalignment uncorrected in the one-shot regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260259/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes local RGB images through a shared frozen pretrained encoder (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32) and transmits only compact statistics: the sample count n_k, the feature mean μ_k ∈ R^m, and a Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage-regularized covariance Σ_k ∈ R^{m×m} (≈264 KB per client at m=512 in float16). The server returns global reference statistics, and each client applies a closed-form affine transport map to its local features, producing aligned representations z' that are then fed unchanged into any downstream OSFL training pipeline.

## Method

SLOT-Align is a learning-free, single-round alignment framework built on the fact that Gaussian measures form a totally geodesic submanifold of the 2-Wasserstein space. Clients estimate first- and second-order feature statistics from a frozen encoder; the server aggregates them into a global reference via a Bures-Wasserstein barycenter (weighted mean for locations, fixed-point iteration for covariances); and each client then applies a closed-form affine optimal transport map T_k(z) = A_k z + b_k that exactly transports its local Gaussian proxy to the barycentric reference. An interpolation parameter τ ∈ [0,1] controls alignment strength through displacement interpolation T_k^(τ) = (1−τ)Id + τT_k, yielding a constant-speed Wasserstein geodesic so that W_2(G_k^(τ), G_b) = (1−τ)W_2(G_k, G_b). The entire procedure requires no gradient updates, no auxiliary data, and no modification to the downstream OSFL objective, making it a drop-in preprocessing layer for any frozen-encoder-based OSFL method.

## Results

Under joint domain and label shift (α=0.1) with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder, SLOT-Align consistently improves macro-averaged top-1 accuracy across all three OSFL baselines and all three benchmarks. The largest gains appear for O-FedAvg: +7.86 pp on Office-Home (64.19→72.05), +7.91 pp on Digits (64.70→72.61), and +2.57 pp on DomainNet (37.69→40.26). FedPFT gains +6.55 pp on Office-Home (74.19→80.74) and +3.53 pp on DomainNet (48.04→51.57), while FedCGS, already the strongest one-shot baseline, improves by +0.68 pp on Office-Home, +2.99 pp on Digits, and +5.23 pp on DomainNet. Cross-domain standard deviation also decreases in most cases, indicating more consistent performance across heterogeneous clients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLOT-Align is not a standalone OSFL method but a learning-free preprocessing layer applied on top of existing one-shot pipelines, so the primary comparison is each baseline with versus without the alignment step. Against O-FedAvg, SLOT-Align delivers the largest absolute improvements (+7.86 pp on Office-Home, +7.91 pp on Digits, +2.57 pp on DomainNet), closing much of the gap to the multi-round FedAvg reference (81.36 on Office-Home). Against FedPFT, gains are substantial on Office-Home (+6.55 pp) and DomainNet (+3.53 pp) but more modest on Digits (+2.14 pp), where FedPFT already performs competitively. Against FedCGS, the strongest one-shot baseline, improvements are smaller on Office-Home (+0.68 pp) but meaningful on Digits (+2.99 pp) and DomainNet (+5.23 pp), suggesting that alignment is most beneficial when the underlying aggregation is sensitive to cross-client distribution mismatch. Across all comparisons, SLOT-Align also reduces cross-domain standard deviation in most settings, indicating more uniform performance across heterogeneous clients rather than gains concentrated in a single domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLOT-Align repurposes Bures-Wasserstein barycenter and Gaussian geodesic optimal transport—tools well-established in domain adaptation and multi-source learning—into the one-shot federated learning setting, where prior OT-in-FL work (FedOT, FedDaDiL) is inherently multi-round and iterative. It positions itself as a learning-free preprocessing module orthogonal to existing OSFL paradigms (distillation, data synthesis, ensembling, statistics-based aggregation), explicitly targeting the joint interaction of domain shift and label shift that these methods address only separately or implicitly. Unlike statistics-based OSFL methods such as FedCGS and FedPFT, which assume client feature summaries are directly comparable, SLOT-Align corrects first- and second-order feature misalignment before any downstream aggregation, without modifying the training procedure of the host OSFL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design makes SLOT-Align immediately adoptable by any OSFL pipeline relying on frozen pretrained encoders, with negligible communication overhead (~264 KB per client for m=512). Key limitations include the Gaussian proxy assumption (real deep feature distributions are non-Gaussian), reliance on only first- and second-order statistics, and a single global τ hyperparameter that may not be optimal under extreme heterogeneity. Open questions include whether higher-order or non-Gaussian transport formulations would yield further gains, whether the approach extends to non-vision modalities or tasks without strong pretrained encoders, and whether formal privacy guarantees can be established beyond compatibility with secure aggregation.
