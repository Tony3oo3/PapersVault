## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for LLMs faces a fundamental exploration bottleneck: the policy can only learn from samples it can already generate, yet RL training itself narrows the exploration space through policy entropy collapse. This dilemma is especially acute for open-ended reasoning tasks (medical consultation, creative writing, instruction following) where no single ground-truth answer exists, making it difficult to construct reliable reward signals or guide the model toward genuinely novel, high-quality responses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text instruction paired with a checklist-style rubric of N criteria, each specifying a verifiable requirement and a point value (positive or negative, e.g., +6 for 'explain key elements: agent, environment, reward' or −7 for 'confuse environment and reward roles'). During rollout, a subset of rubric criteria is injected into the instruction prompt as scaffolding; during training, a Grader LLM performs binary true/false judgments per criterion, and the resulting score vector is element-wise multiplied by the point vector and normalized by the total positive-point score to yield a scalar reward in roughly [0,1]. The policy model outputs a free-form text response, and the training objective is to maximize the expected rubric-based reward under GRPO.

## Method

RuscaRL introduces instructional scaffolding into RLVR by leveraging rubrics in two complementary roles. First, during rollout generation, rubric criteria are injected into the instruction as explicit guidance, with two control mechanisms: intra-group scaffolding differentiation assigns linearly decreasing scaffolding ratios (λ_i = (G−i)/(G−1)) across the G samples in a GRPO group to ensure response diversity, and inter-step scaffolding decay uses a sigmoid schedule λ_step(t) = 1/(1+e^{α(t−t₀)}) to gradually withdraw guidance over training, encouraging the model to internalize reasoning patterns. Second, during training, the same rubrics serve as verifiable rewards: a Grader LLM evaluates each criterion independently (binary judgment), and the aggregated, normalized score becomes the reward signal for GRPO. Crucially, the log-probability computation in the policy gradient uses the scaffold-free conditioning π_θ(o_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t}) rather than the scaffolded one, so the model learns to produce high-quality responses without relying on external rubric prompts at inference time.

## Results

On HealthBench-500 with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, RuscaRL achieves 56.4 (+33.0 over initial), outperforming Rubric-based RL (52.0), RL-Plus (53.6), EA-RL (51.3), and MeRF (36.8). On LLMEval-Med, RuscaRL reaches 65.3 (+17.3), a substantial margin over all baselines (best competitor: RL-Plus at 58.2). With Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct, RuscaRL achieves 61.1 on HealthBench-500, surpassing OpenAI-o3 (59.8) and GPT-4.1 (47.9). Across writing and instruction-following benchmarks, RuscaRL consistently leads, e.g., 56.1 on WritingBench and 31.0 on IFBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RuscaRL outperforms all RL-based baselines on the primary medical benchmark: HealthBench-500 scores of 56.4 vs. Rubric-based RL (52.0), RL-Plus (53.6), EA-RL (51.3), and MeRF (36.8) with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. The margin is largest on LLMEval-Med (65.3 vs. 58.2 for RL-Plus, the closest competitor), where rubric scaffolding provides the most structured guidance. Against external closed-source models, RuscaRL-trained Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct reaches 61.1 on HealthBench-500, surpassing OpenAI-o3 (59.8), GPT-4.1 (47.9), Rubicon-Preview (50.8), and gpt-oss-20b (42.5). On instruction-following, RuscaRL leads IFEVAL (75.3) and IFBench (31.0) over all RL baselines, though the gap over Rubric-based RL is narrow (75.3 vs. 75.1 on IFEVAL). MeRF underperforms notably, with negative gains on MedQA (−3.9) and MedMCQA (−3.9), suggesting that static full-rubric injection without decay harms generalization. In the SFT-then-RL setting, RuscaRL narrows its advantage over Rubric-based RL (56.9 vs. 55.5 on HealthBench-500), which the authors attribute to overlapping exploration-facilitation mechanisms between SFT and scaffolding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and adopts the rubric-based evaluation framework from HealthBench (Arora et al., 2025) for scoring open-ended responses. It departs from prior exploration-enhancement methods (RL-Plus, EA-RL, MeRF) that either bias the policy toward already-supported high-reward regions or provide only coarse directional signals, by introducing structured rubric criteria as explicit instructional scaffolding during rollout generation. The key conceptual departure is borrowing Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development from educational psychology to motivate a two-dimensional scaffolding mechanism (intra-group differentiation + inter-step sigmoid decay) that gradually internalizes reasoning patterns, while simultaneously using the same rubrics as verifiable rewards for exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exploration bottleneck is a fundamental limitation of RLVR for LLMs, and RuscaRL provides a practical, well-ablated mechanism to break it without requiring ground-truth answers. The finding that a 30B model can surpass OpenAI-o3 on HealthBench-500 (61.1 vs. 59.8) after RuscaRL training suggests that structured external guidance can compensate for model scale on open-ended tasks. Key limitations include the dependency on high-quality rubric data (which the authors acknowledge requires community investment to scale) and roughly 2× training cost from LLM grader calls. Open questions include whether the scaffolding approach transfers to multi-modal and agentic settings, and whether automated rubric generation can match hand-crafted quality.
