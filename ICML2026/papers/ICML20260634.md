## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods for physiological time series face a fundamental trade-off: weakly constrained pretext tasks (contrastive learning, MAE) may incorrectly collapse clinically distinct states or exploit spurious non-causal relationships, while structurally constrained models (SVAEs) preserve causal dynamics but cannot selectively filter sample-specific noise, limiting transferability. No prior method simultaneously preserves the dynamical system structure essential to physiological signals and explicitly removes non-transferable, sample-specific information.

## Input / Output

The method takes fixed-length physiological time-series windows (W time steps × M channels) segmented from long continuous recordings across sensor domains (e.g., 6-channel accelerometer/gyroscope at 128 steps for HAR, 2-channel ECG at 500 steps, 1-channel PPG at 960 steps, 2-channel EEG at 3000 steps). It produces a dynamical system embedding Θ_i (the shared system parameters) via a dilated convolution encoder, alongside a local initial-condition estimate X_{i,t0} from a separate CNN encoder. The embedding is a pooled representation invariant to the specific starting state and noise realization, suitable for linear probing or fine-tuning on downstream classification.

## Method

PULSE formulates a dynamical systems generative model across time-series samples, identifying system parameters Θ as transferable (shared across samples from the same physiological process) and initial conditions plus process noise as non-transferable. It trains via a cross-reconstruction objective: a system encoder f_sys (dilated convolution) estimates Θ_i from one window, while an initial-condition encoder f_init (local 2-layer CNN) estimates X_{i,t0}, and a GRU-based state-space decoder reconstructs a randomly cropped pseudo-pair of the same sample. This decoupling forces f_sys to discard sample-specific factors since they cannot explain a different crop's evolution. Theorem 1 proves that full-sample masking (i.e., cross-reconstruction) uniquely recovers system parameters as the minimal shared latent set, whereas partial masking recovers confounded state variables, providing a theoretical guarantee for the design. Time-varying system parameters are regularized by reducing their dimensionality and sharing max-pooled values across consecutive timesteps to prevent trivial copying of local signal values.

## Results

On linear probe classification across four physiological datasets, PULSE achieves the highest scores on PPG (64.27% Acc, 80.29 AUROC), ECG (87.41% Acc, 94.93 AUROC), and EEG (85.56% Acc, 96.17 AUROC), while remaining competitive on HAR (93.27% Acc, 99.42 AUROC). In semi-supervised settings with only 1% labels, PULSE achieves 84.77% on ECG and 84.74% on HAR, substantially outperforming all SSL baselines. For in-domain transfer learning, PULSE achieves 95.82% ACC on EEG→Epilepsy and 83.67% ACC on HAR→Gesture, the best in both scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PULSE outperforms all eight baselines on PPG, ECG, and EEG across all three metrics (Accuracy, AUROC, AUPRC), with the largest margins on ECG where it achieves 87.41% accuracy versus 81.54% for REBAR (the next-best CL method) and 70.42% for DSVAE. On HAR, PULSE is competitive but slightly trails REBAR (93.27% vs 95.35% accuracy) and SimCLR (94.65%), though it matches or exceeds them on AUROC. Against the SVAE baselines (LFADS, DSVAE), PULSE shows the most dramatic gains on ECG (+17.0% over LFADS, +17.0% over DSVAE in accuracy) and PPG (+11.5% over LFADS), confirming that explicitly removing sample-specific noise is critical for these noisy signals. Compared to MAE baselines (PatchTST, TimeMAE), PULSE wins on all datasets except HAR accuracy, with the largest advantage on ECG (+17.6% over PatchTST, +17.6% over TimeMAE). In transfer learning (Table 4), PULSE achieves the best ACC and AUROC in both EEG→Epilepsy (95.82/98.51) and HAR→Gesture (83.67/97.20) scenarios, with a particularly large 5.5-point accuracy gain over the next-best method on HAR→Gesture. In 5-fold cross-validation (Table 6), PULSE becomes the top performer on HAR as well (95.30% accuracy), confirming robustness to data partitioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PULSE bridges two previously separate lines of work in physiological time-series SSL: weakly constrained pretext tasks (contrastive learning à la SimCLR/TS2Vec/REBAR, and masked autoencoding à la PatchTST/TimeMAE) that filter noise but lack structural guarantees, and structurally constrained sequential VAEs (LFADS, DSVAE) that preserve dynamical structure but cannot selectively discard sample-specific noise. The key conceptual departure is the cross-reconstruction objective, which decouples system estimation from the reconstruction target—inspired by the MAE theory of Kong and Zhang (2023) but extended to a two-sample generative setting where whole-sample masking provably isolates shared system parameters. The pseudo-pair strategy (random crops as system-preserving augmentations) is a practical innovation that avoids the need for explicit system labels, and the theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) builds on the minimal-shared-latent-variable framework from prior MAE identifiability work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PULSE provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for separating transferable dynamical-system information from non-transferable sample-specific noise in physiological signals, which is critical for clinical transferability across patients and recording sessions. The large ECG gap over SVAE baselines (~17 points) and the ablation showing that random cross-pairs (vs. same-sample pseudo-pairs) catastrophically degrade performance highlight that the specific pairing strategy is as important as the reconstruction objective itself. Open questions include how the framework extends to settings where system identity is not preserved by simple temporal crops (e.g., non-stationary pathologies), whether the invertibility assumption in Theorem 1 can be relaxed for truly chaotic systems, and how to handle multi-system mixtures where a single window may contain transitions between different dynamical regimes.
