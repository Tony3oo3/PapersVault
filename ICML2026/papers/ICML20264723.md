## Problem

In long-horizon LLM agent tasks, sparse outcome-based rewards make credit assignment to intermediate steps extremely difficult. The paper identifies a fundamental, previously unformalized issue: the magnitude of policy gradients is inherently coupled with policy entropy (via Rényi-2 entropy), causing confident correct actions to receive inefficiently small updates while uncertain exploratory steps produce large, destabilizing gradients. This dual pathology limits learning speed and training stability in a way that neither reward shaping nor Process Reward Models adequately address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes task instructions, environment observations, and a single sparse binary reward at trajectory end, operating at the 'reason-then-act' step granularity (one ReAct cycle) rather than the token level. Step-level entropy is computed as the mean of token-level Shannon entropies within each assistant response segment. The output is a per-step modulated advantage scalar (combining a scaled trajectory advantage and a future-clarity bonus) that replaces the uniform trajectory-level advantage in the policy gradient update, ultimately producing updated policy parameters.

## Method

EMPG is a plug-in advantage-modulation framework built on two components. First, Self-Calibrating Gradient Scaling multiplies the trajectory advantage by g(H_t) = exp(-k·H_norm,t) / batch_mean(exp(-k·H_norm)), which amplifies updates for confident (low-entropy) steps and attenuates them for uncertain (high-entropy) steps while preserving the batch-mean scale. Second, a Future Clarity Bonus adds ζ·exp(-k'·H_norm,t+1), an intrinsic reward encouraging actions that lead to lower-entropy subsequent states. The theoretical grounding is Proposition 3.1, which proves E[||∇_z log π||²] = 1 − exp(−H₂(π)), formally establishing the entropy–gradient coupling that motivates the recalibration. EMPG is applied orthogonally on top of existing backbones (GRPO, DAPO) without modifying their core objectives, and the full update is shown to be the gradient of a composite extrinsic-plus-intrinsic objective.

## Results

On ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, DAPO+EMPG achieves 80.8% overall success rate (vs. 73.5% for DAPO alone, +7.3 pts) and GRPO+EMPG reaches 65.6% (vs. 57.5% for GRPO, +8.1 pts). On WebShop with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, DAPO+EMPG attains 69.3% success rate (+3.7 pts over DAPO baseline). On Deep Search with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, DAPO+EMPG improves the overall average from 62.0 to 65.3 (+3.3), with a particularly strong +3.9 point gain on out-of-domain tasks (Musique, Bamboogle), demonstrating superior generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMPG consistently outperforms all compared baselines. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), DAPO+EMPG on 1.5B achieves 80.8% ALFWorld success vs. PPO's 54.4% (+26.4 pts) and 73.2% WebShop success vs. PPO's 51.5% (+21.7 pts). Against the GRPO backbone (Shao et al., 2024), EMPG adds +8.1 pts on ALFWorld (1.5B) and the method surpasses RLOO on WebShop (78.0 vs. 73.9 score). Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), EMPG yields +7.3 pts on ALFWorld (1.5B), +3.7 pts on WebShop (7B), and +3.3 overall on Deep Search (32B), with the OOD generalization gap (+3.9) exceeding the in-domain gain (+3.1). Compared to prompting-based ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), the RL-trained EMPG agents achieve dramatically higher performance (e.g., 80.8% vs. 12.8% ALFWorld on 1.5B), confirming that entropy-modulated RL training far surpasses zero-shot prompting for long-horizon agent tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMPG builds directly on value-free policy-gradient backbones (GRPO and DAPO) and positions itself as an orthogonal advantage-modulation plug-in rather than a competing algorithm. It departs from prior entropy-based approaches in two key ways: unlike entropy-minimization objectives (Gao et al., 2025; Agarwal et al., 2025) that risk 'hallucinated confidence,' and unlike EDGE-GRPO (Zhang et al., 2025c) which modulates gradients for single-turn mathematical reasoning, EMPG is explicitly designed for multi-step credit assignment in long-horizon agent tasks. The theoretical grounding (Proposition 3.1, linking expected gradient norm to Rényi-2 entropy) formalizes a dynamic that prior work treated informally, and the Future Clarity Bonus draws on information-theoretic and empowerment principles (Klyubin et al., 2005) rather than learned reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMPG offers a practical, annotation-free alternative to Process Reward Models for dense credit assignment in long-horizon LLM agents, with the plug-in design making it immediately applicable to existing GRPO/DAPO training pipelines. The empirical finding that step-level entropy dynamics fundamentally differ from token-level dynamics (low-entropy steps still undergo significant changes, and restricting updates to high-entropy tokens causes a decisive performance drop) challenges the emerging '80/20 rule' narrative and has implications for how the community thinks about where critical policy information resides in multi-step agents. Open questions include whether the Shannon-entropy proxy for Rényi-2 entropy remains adequate at larger scales, whether the method extends to embodied or multi-agent settings, and how it interacts with longer horizons where the Future Clarity Bonus's local signal may become less informative.
