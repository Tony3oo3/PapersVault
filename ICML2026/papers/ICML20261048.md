## Problem

Recursive retraining of generative models on curated synthetic data has been shown to provably collapse onto a narrow set of outputs when a single reward function drives selection, with prior work suggesting that mixing in real data is the only remedy. This paper challenges that conclusion by asking whether collapse can be prevented purely through the curation strategy itself—specifically, by alternating between multiple competing reward functions—and provides a formal characterization of the resulting long-run dynamics.

## Input / Output

At each retraining iteration, the method takes the current model distribution p_t, a candidate pool of K i.i.d. samples drawn from it, and a randomly selected reward (r_1 with probability q or r_2 with probability 1−q), then produces a single curated sample via Bradley-Terry discrete choice. The retrained model p_{t+1} is obtained by idealized maximum-likelihood estimation on that selected sample. In the limit, the output is a stable two-basin mixture distribution whose component weights are governed by the reward-sampling probability q and cross-basin leakage factors, rather than a degenerate point mass on a single high-reward region.

## Method

The core mechanism is a pluralistic curation loop: at each step, K candidates are drawn from the current model, a reward is sampled from a fixed mixture, and one candidate is selected via the Bradley-Terry model before an MLE retraining update. In the large-K limit, the BT selection reduces to an exponential tilt, yielding the closed-form update p_{t+1}(x) = p_t(x)·[q·e^{r_1(x)}/Z_1(t) + (1−q)·e^{r_2(x)}/Z_2(t)]. Under an outside-domination condition, probability mass outside the union of ε-optimal basins contracts geometrically, and the remaining mass partitions into a two-component mixture whose basin weight is bounded by explicit leakage factors κ_i = exp(−(Δ_i − 2ε)). In the hard-separation plateau regime, the limiting mixture weight converges exactly to q and the distribution coincides with the unique weighted Nash bargaining solution over the two-basin mixture family, providing a formal value-aggregation interpretation. The theory is validated empirically on synthetic Gaussian mixtures, CIFAR-10 normalizing-flow retraining (OT-CFM with VGG11 classifier rewards), and GPT-2 text generation under conflicting length preferences.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 flow retraining, balanced multi-preference curation with 5 rewards achieves FID 35.9, class entropy 1.687, and intra-class variance 0.85, compared to FID 100.0, entropy 0.032, and intra-class variance 0.05 under single-reward curation (q=0). Increasing the number of balanced preferences from 2 to 5 monotonically improves all diversity proxies (entropy rises from 1.098 to 1.687; KL-to-uniform drops from 1.204 to 0.616) while also improving FID (77.6 → 35.9). In the synthetic GMM experiment, pluralistic curation maintains positive reward variance for both objectives across all retraining steps, whereas single-reward curation drives variance to near zero. The GPT-2 text experiment confirms that larger preference separation (distance d between length targets) yields higher sustained length-distribution entropy under balanced pluralistic selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the single-reward curation pipeline of Ferbach et al. (2024), which corresponds to q=0 (or q=1) in the paper's framework. On CIFAR-10, single-reward retraining (q=0) yields FID 100.0, class entropy 0.032, and intra-class variance 0.05, indicating severe mode collapse. Pluralistic curation with two balanced preferences (q=0.5) improves FID to 77.6, entropy to 1.098, and intra-class variance to 0.72; scaling to five balanced preferences further reduces FID to 35.9 and raises intra-class variance to 0.85. The paper also shows that even modest polarization (q=0.4) already recovers most diversity (entropy 0.791, intra-class variance 0.60), while extreme imbalance (q=0.1–0.3) remains close to the collapsed single-reward regime. In the synthetic GMM setting, the single-reward baseline collapses to a narrow region with near-zero reward variance, while pluralistic curation sustains a stable two-mode mixture with positive variance for both rewards throughout training. Theoretically, the paper proves that the single-reward limit collapses onto the argmax set (Ferbach et al.'s result), whereas the pluralistic limit is a non-degenerate mixture satisfying a weighted Nash bargaining solution, with basin weights approaching the reward-sampling probability q as separation grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recursive retraining framework of Ferbach et al. (2024), who modeled single-reward curation via the Bradley-Terry model and proved inevitable mode collapse, by replacing the single reward with a probabilistic mixture of two (or more) distinct reward functions. It departs from the standard mitigation of mixing in real data (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024; Shumailov et al., 2023) and from the noise-around-a-single-reward heterogeneity model of Zhao et al. (2025), instead treating preferences as genuinely competing objectives with potentially disjoint optima. The Nash bargaining interpretation of the limiting mixture weight is a novel theoretical lens not present in prior self-consuming model analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that pluralistic curation provably prevents collapse without any real data challenges the prevailing narrative that synthetic retraining loops are inherently degenerative, offering a principled alternative for alignment pipelines where human data are scarce. The weighted Nash bargaining characterization provides a formal fairness guarantee for how probability mass is allocated among competing stakeholder preferences, directly relevant to multi-objective RLHF and constitutional AI. Key limitations include the requirement for disjoint ε-optimal basins, the outside-domination condition, and the plateau assumption needed for the explicit Nash result; open questions include how the theory behaves with overlapping basins, many preferences, and finite-capacity models, and whether the guarantees hold at the scale of modern LLMs.
