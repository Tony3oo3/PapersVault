## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of contrastive representation learning (CRL) suffer from three critical gaps: the statistical consistency of CRL with respect to downstream retrieval performance remains unproven, available generalization bounds deteriorate as the number of negative samples m increases (contradicting the well-known empirical benefit of large negative sets), and the retrieval quality of CRL-trained models has received limited theoretical treatment. This paper develops a unified statistical learning theory that addresses all three limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262607/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on data pairs (x, y) from two modalities (e.g., images and texts), where for each anchor x, positive examples are drawn from a conditional distribution p_x^+ and negative examples from p_x^-. The scoring function s_w(x,y) = f_w(x)^T f_w(y) is an inner product of deep ReLU network encoders with bounded spectral norms. A key structural detail is the compositional nature of the loss: the outer expectation is over n positive pairs while the inner expectation is over m negative samples, and the two settings differ in whether negatives are shared across anchors (self-supervised) or drawn independently per anchor (supervised).

## Method

The paper establishes Fisher consistency by characterizing minimizers of the log-sum-exp contrastive loss as s(x,y) = τ log(p_x^+(y)/p_x^-(y)) + g(x), which provably maximizes an AUC-type retrieval criterion, and derives a calibration inequality E* − E(s) ≤ √(2/τ · (L(s) − L*)) linking excess contrastive risk to retrieval suboptimality. For generalization, the key technical insight is decomposing the gap into an inner error (bias from finite negatives) and an outer error (variance from finite anchors). For supervised CRL, the inner error is bounded as O(1/m) by reformulating the log-sum-exp as a stochastic minimization problem and applying algorithmic stability theory; for self-supervised CRL, where negatives are shared across anchors, uniform convergence yields O(1/√m). Combined with O(1/√n) outer error via Rademacher complexity, this gives overall bounds of O(1/m + 1/√n) and O(1/√m + 1/√n) respectively, explaining why performance improves with more negatives until saturation at m ≈ n.

## Results

The paper establishes generalization bounds of Õ(1/m + 1/√n) for supervised CRL and Õ(1/√m + 1/√n) for self-supervised CRL, where m is the number of negative samples and n the number of anchor points. A calibration-style inequality E*(s) − E(s) ≤ √(2/τ · (L(s) − L*)) quantitatively links excess contrastive risk to excess retrieval suboptimality. Empirically, CLIP models trained on DFN (n ∈ {14M, 10M, 6M}) with varying m/n ratios show that zero-shot ImageNet top-1 accuracy and MSCOCO/Flickr retrieval recall improve with m but saturate at a critical negative size m*(n) that lies between √n and n, empirically closer to m* = n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; the `compares_to` set is empty. The empirical validation in Section 5 does not benchmark against competing CRL methods (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo) but instead tests the paper's own theoretical predictions: the predicted critical negative size m* = √n (from Theorem 4.5, supervised setting) versus m* = n (from Theorem 4.6, self-supervised setting with shared negatives). The observed saturation point in Figure 1b falls between these two theoretical scalings but is closer to m* = n, confirming that the self-supervised bound (Theorem 4.6) better matches practice. The paper also contrasts its O(1/m) inner-error bound (via algorithmic stability) with the O(1/√m) rate from uniform convergence (Lee et al., 2020) and the O(m/√n) or O(log m/√n) outer-error-only bounds of Saunshi et al. (2019) and Lei et al. (2023), showing that its decomposition yields bounds that improve with m rather than deteriorate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical consistency and calibration framework from binary classification (Bartlett et al., 2006; Zhang, 2004) to the contrastive representation learning setting, where the downstream objective is an AUC-type retrieval measure over triples (x, y, y') rather than a standard instance-label pair. For generalization bounds, it departs from the Rademacher-complexity-only analyses of Saunshi et al. (2019) and Lei et al. (2023) that yielded O(m/√n) or O(log m/√n) scaling, by decomposing the compositional contrastive loss into inner and outer errors and applying algorithmic stability (Bousquet & Elisseeff, 2002) to the inner component. The compositional structure is analogous to conditional stochastic optimization (Hu et al., 2020), but the log-sum-exp loss requires non-trivial reformulation as a stochastic minimization problem to exploit stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing contradiction between CRL theory (which predicted generalization worsens with more negatives) and empirical practice (where large negative sets clearly help, as in SimCLR and CLIP), by showing the inner error actually decreases as O(1/m) or O(1/√m). The calibration-style inequality provides the first quantitative link between upstream contrastive risk and downstream retrieval suboptimality, which is essential for understanding when and why contrastive pretraining transfers. Open questions include which OCE/pairwise loss variants are theoretically and practically optimal, whether the framework extends to LLM pretraining objectives, and closing the gap between the theoretical critical m* (n for SSCRL, √n for SCRL) and the empirically observed scaling that lies between the two.
