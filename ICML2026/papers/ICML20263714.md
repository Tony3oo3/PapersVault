## Problem

Standard survival models discard censoring-related terms from the likelihood, treating censoring as incidental. This simplification is only justified under non-informative censoring (no shared parameters between event and censoring models), yet in practice the two processes are often coupled, and discarding censoring contributions loses information useful for representation learning and event-time estimation. Additionally, no principled framework existed for decomposing latent factors into event-specific, censoring-specific, and confounding components to mitigate censoring-induced bias.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tabular survival data: a feature vector x_i ∈ R^d, an observed time t_i, and an event/censoring indicator δ_i. At inference, only the event-specific and confounding representations (h^ε, h^κ) are fed into the event distribution head, while the censoring-specific representation h^γ is discarded. The framework is survival-layer-agnostic: it plugs into DeepSurv (producing Cox risk scores), N-MTLR (producing discrete survival/density functions), and AFTNN-Weibull/LogLogistic (producing parametric survival/density functions), so the output format depends on the chosen base model.

## Method

SALaD learns three separate encoder networks (Φ^ε, Φ^κ, Φ^γ) that map features to event-specific, confounding, and censoring-specific latent representations, then feeds them into four distribution heads: event distributions parameterized by (h^ε) and (h^ε, h^κ), and censoring distributions parameterized by (h^γ) and (h^γ, h^κ). Training maximizes the full observed-data likelihood (including both event and censoring terms) across all four heads, establishing an asymptotic efficiency gain over censoring-mechanism-free training (Theorem 3.1). To enforce the desired factor separation, SALaD applies two weighted MMD invariance regularizers that penalize H^γ for encoding event-time information and H^ε for encoding censoring-time information (using early/late group splits derived from Kaplan-Meier marginal survival curves), plus an orthogonal regularizer that discourages overlapping input-direction usage across the three encoders. At inference, only the event-relevant representations (h^ε, h^κ) are used, making the framework a drop-in upgrade for any existing deep survival architecture.

## Results

Across 10 datasets (2 semi-synthetic, 8 real-world) × 4 base survival architectures × 4 metrics (CI, IBS, MAE, D-cal), SALaD achieves 109 wins, 28 ties, and 19 losses out of 156 total comparisons against its corresponding baselines. On HFCR, SALaD (DeepSurv) attains the highest concordance index among all methods, while SALaD (N-MTLR) achieves the lowest IBS and MAE. The 2B extension (joint event+censoring modeling without decomposition) already improves over event-only baselines, but SALaD's latent decomposition provides additional consistent gains in discrimination (CI) and calibration (IBS, MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALaD consistently outperforms its four standard deep survival baselines (DeepSurv, N-MTLR, AFTNN-Weibull, AFTNN-LogLogistic) across all 10 datasets, with higher CI and lower IBS/MAE in most settings and comparable or better D-cal. Against the 10 SOTA methods (Nnet-survival, RSF, GB, DeepHit, CoxTime, IWSG, SODEN, CQRNN, DCSurvival, SurvivalBoost), SALaD is competitive and often superior; on HFCR it achieves the best CI (via DeepSurv backbone) and best IBS/MAE (via N-MTLR backbone). The 2B extension (shared encoder + two distribution heads, no decomposition) already improves over event-only baselines, confirming the benefit of modeling censoring, but is generally inferior to full SALaD, indicating that explicit disentanglement of H^ε, H^κ, H^γ provides additional gains. In a few cases, 2B yields slightly better calibration (lower IBS, higher D-cal), plausibly due to its simpler hypothesis class reducing finite-sample overfitting. Ablations confirm that removing either the distance-based invariance regularizers (β₂=0) or the orthogonal decomposition penalty (β₁=0) leads to consistent performance drops across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard deep survival models (DeepSurv, N-MTLR, AFTNN) by introducing a two-branch (2B) architecture that jointly models event and censoring distributions, then further develops SALaD, which learns disentangled latent representations (event-specific, censoring-specific, confounding) inspired by causal representation learning (Johansson et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2022). It departs from IPCW-based approaches (Cole & Hernán, 2004; Han et al., 2021) that treat censoring as a separate nuisance to be corrected post-hoc, arguing instead that shared parameters between event and censoring processes make joint modeling statistically more efficient. The orthogonal regularizer for variable decomposition is borrowed directly from the treatment-effect estimation literature (Kuang et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2022) and adapted to the survival setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper clarifies a subtle but important distinction often conflated in the literature: independent censoring (E ⊥ C | X) does not imply non-informative censoring (no shared parameters), and the standard practice of discarding censoring terms in the likelihood is only justified under the stronger condition. SALaD provides a survival-layer-agnostic framework that can upgrade any existing deep survival model, with consistent (if moderate) gains across 10 datasets. Open questions include extending to dependent censoring and competing risks, and the reliance on Kaplan-Meier estimates for the invariance regularizers introduces a known bias under feature-dependent censoring that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
