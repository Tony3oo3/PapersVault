## Problem

EEG-based emotion recognition models achieve high accuracy but remain opaque black boxes that map neural signals to discrete labels without offering an intelligible trace of the underlying emotional state. Existing interpretability approaches—post-hoc saliency maps or domain-specific spectral constraints—point to where the model attends but fail to bridge the gap to externally observable, human-readable behavioral semantics. The paper reframes EEG explainability as a cross-modal generation task, asking 'what does this neural state look like?' rather than 'which features matter?'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261638/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model consumes only multichannel EEG segments (e.g., 30 channels × 20 s at 100 Hz on EAV, 18 channels × 20 s at 300 Hz on MMER) and produces both a discrete emotion label and a temporally aligned sequence of identity-anonymized facial emojis rendered as 56×56 binary images (one emoji per 1 s on EAV, per 0.5 s on MMER). During training, synchronized frontal facial videos are additionally required to extract 68-landmark rasterized emojis that serve as reconstruction targets; these are never needed at test time. The emoji representation deliberately discards texture and identity cues, retaining only expression geometry.

## Method

The framework comprises two components: (1) FMENet, an EEG backbone whose Expression-Relevant Spatial Merger encodes electrode coordinates via learnable Fourier position embeddings and applies soft attention to aggregate channels into O functionally coherent latent sources, while its Multi-Scale Temporal Capturer stacks dilated 1D convolutional blocks with exponentially expanding receptive fields to model oscillatory dynamics across frequency bands; and (2) the Facial Emoji Learning Branch (FELB), which pretrains a VAE autoencoder on the binary emoji corpus to obtain a frozen decoder mapping a low-dimensional latent code to plausible facial expressions. In the joint training stage, the shared FMENet encoder feeds a generative branch (projecting EEG features into the emoji latent space and decoding via the frozen VAE) and a discriminative branch (predicting emotion labels conditioned on both EEG features and the sampled emoji latent code), with the total loss balancing cross-entropy classification and VAE reconstruction (BCE + KL). The key design insight is that forcing the network to reconstruct facial dynamics from EEG acts as a structured semantic regularizer, aligning the neural latent space with the behavioral manifold and yielding interpretable, privacy-preserving visual explanations without sacrificing—and in fact slightly improving—recognition accuracy.

## Results

On the EAV dataset, FMENet (EEG-only) achieves 76.96% accuracy and 76.47% macro F1, setting a new state-of-the-art among EEG-only models. With the Facial Emoji Learning Branch (FELB), FMENet+FELB reaches 77.13% accuracy and 77.02% F1 using only EEG at inference, surpassing all multimodal baselines except Hyper-MML(EFA) (78.21%), which requires EEG+Face+Audio simultaneously. On MMER, FMENet+FELB achieves 69.73% accuracy and 64.28% F1. Zero-shot transfer to the EEG-only SEED dataset yields ~60% 3-way emoji-based accuracy (chance 33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among EEG-only baselines, FMENet outperforms EEGNet by +16.96% accuracy (76.96 vs. 60.00), EEGformer by +23.46%, and the strongest EEG-only competitor Hyper-MML(E) by +5.13%. Against multimodal baselines that require EEG+Face+Audio at inference, FMENet+FELB (EEG-only inference) beats M3NET (+1.99%), HAUCL (+1.22%), AGF-IB (+0.94%), DialogueRNN (+15.93%), and bc-LSTM (+19.92%), losing only to Hyper-MML(EFA) by −1.08%. In the backbone compatibility study (Table 2), attaching FELB to existing backbones (SyncNet, EEGNet, TSception, ATCNet, EEGConformer, LMDA, EEGMiner, TSLANet) consistently degrades accuracy by 1–3%, whereas FMENet+FELB actually improves over FMENet alone (76.96→77.13% on EAV; 69.11→69.73% on MMER), indicating that FMENet's architecture is uniquely suited to the dual classification-plus-reconstruction objective. On cross-subject evaluation (Table 8), FMENet+FELB again leads all baselines, achieving 39.05% on EAV and 69.23% on MMER, confirming that the emoji prior acts as a robust regularizer for subject-invariant representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant EEG emotion recognition paradigm (EEGNet, TSception, graph/attention models) that treats the task as a purely discriminative classification problem, and also from post-hoc explainability methods (Grad-CAM, channel saliency) that remain abstract. Conceptually, it repurposes the cross-modal generation framework from fMRI-to-image decoding (Nishimoto, Scotti) and image-to-report generation, but redirects it toward affective dynamics with a novel identity-anonymized emoji proxy. The FMENet backbone draws on standard components (Fourier position embeddings, dilated convolutions, GLU) but arranges them around an explicit neuro-facial coupling hypothesis, while the FELB branch introduces a VAE-based emoji manifold as a structured semantic regularizer rather than a generative objective in its own right.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, privacy-preserving mechanism for making EEG emotion models transparent to non-experts, which is a genuine gap in clinical neurofeedback and trustworthy BCI deployment. However, the coupling between the generative and discriminative objectives is fragile: adding FELB to eight other backbones consistently degrades accuracy (Table 2), suggesting the interpretability benefit is tightly bound to the specific FMENet architecture rather than being a general-purpose plug-in. The acknowledged mismatch between frame-level facial dynamics and video-level emotion labels raises open questions about whether the emoji reconstruction truly explains the classification decision or merely runs in parallel. Zero-shot transfer to SEED (~60% 3-way) is promising but limited to a single target domain with only three coarse emotion classes.
