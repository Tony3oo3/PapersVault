## Problem

Prior work empirically observed that singular vectors of attention head QK matrices align with feature representations in language models, but no study provided a theoretical explanation for why or when this alignment occurs. Without such justification, the use of singular vectors as a basis for feature identification in mechanistic interpretability lacked a sound foundation, and there was no principled way to test for alignment in real models where features are not directly observable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261150/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the QK matrix Ω = W_Q^T W_K of a single attention head and model activations (tokens represented as sums of feature vectors). In the toy model, both features and singular vectors are directly observable, enabling measurement of cosine similarity alignment. In real models (GPT-2, Pythia), features are not directly accessible, so the method instead computes relative attention decomposed in the SVD basis and derives the sparsity metric S(v) and N_recon (minimum number of singular vectors needed to reconstruct relative attention) as indirect evidence of alignment. The key constraint is that sparse attention decomposition is only expected when a limited number of features of interest are present in the token pair.

## Method

The paper combines a toy model, theoretical analysis, and real-model validation. The toy model extends Elhage et al.'s autoencoder (where tokens are sums of features) with an attention head trained via cross-entropy on target logits that depend on feature-pair presence, allowing simultaneous observation of features and singular vectors. Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves that the population cross-entropy minimizer is rank-1 with singular vectors proportional to covariance-whitened features (Σ_X^{-1}x_1, Σ_Y^{-1}y_1); Corollary 1 shows exact alignment under isotropic features; Theorem 2 bounds alignment deviation under anisotropy via Wedin's perturbation theorem; and Theorem 3 proves that reconstruction loss induces orthogonalization of non-aligned features. The key testable prediction is sparse attention decomposition: under SVF alignment, relative attention (logit minus mean of other logits) decomposes sparsely in the SVD basis, with only a few singular vector pairs contributing significantly. This prediction is confirmed in Pythia-160M across 130 training checkpoints on the IOI circuit and in GPT-2, where N_recon typically ranges from 1–4, and sparsity is shown to be absent under random rotations of the SVD basis.

## Results

In the toy model, singular vectors of the QK matrix align with features of interest with near-perfect cosine similarity (≈1.0), and this alignment is robust across feature probabilities (down to p=0.038), loss weights λ (two orders of magnitude), context lengths, head dimensions, and random seeds. Under controlled anisotropy matching the range observed in GPT-2 SAEs (‖E_X‖₂ from 0 to 50), mean cosine similarity of SVF alignment remains above 0.75. In real models, sparse attention decomposition (SAD) is confirmed: in Pythia-160M on the IOI task, typically only 1–4 singular vectors (N_recon) are needed to reconstruct relative attention, and this count declines during training. Randomly rotating the SVD basis eliminates the observed sparsity, confirming that alignment—not merely large singular values—drives the effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing feature-identification methods in a head-to-head benchmark; instead, it validates a theoretical prediction (sparse attention decomposition) across multiple settings. The key internal comparison is between the SVD basis and randomly rotated U,V matrices: after rotation, the S(v) sparsity metric no longer declines during Pythia training and the visual decomposition loses its sparse structure, ruling out the alternative explanation that a few large singular values alone cause sparsity. The toy model's predicted dynamics (alignment emerging in order of logit importance, features orthogonalizing to reduce reconstruction interference) are confirmed qualitatively in Pythia's 130 training checkpoints. The paper also evaluates two distinct architectures—Pythia (with RoPE) and GPT-2 (without RoPE)—to mitigate architectural dependency, finding SAD in both. Theorem 2's lower bound on alignment angle is shown to be quite loose in practice: the bound predicts poor alignment at high anisotropy, yet empirical cosine similarity remains above 0.75.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the toy autoencoder model from Elhage et al. (2022) by adding an attention head, creating a setting where both feature representations and singular vectors can be directly observed and compared. It builds most directly on Franco & Crovella (2024, 2025), which assumed SVF alignment for circuit tracing and introduced sparse attention decomposition, and on empirical observations from Merullo et al. (2024), Ahmad et al. (2025), and Pan et al. (2024) that singular vectors of attention heads align with features. The key departure is providing the first theoretical justification (Theorems 1–3) for why and when this alignment occurs, moving the phenomenon from an empirical curiosity to a provably expected outcome under stated conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing theoretical grounding for SVF alignment, this paper elevates SVD-based feature identification from a heuristic to a principled interpretability tool, with sparse attention decomposition serving as a falsifiable prediction confirmed in GPT-2 and Pythia. Open questions remain: how features are deconflicted when they exceed head capacity, how singular vectors are allocated across multiple heads, and whether alignment holds for 'cone directions' arising from strong anisotropy in larger models. The single-head, small-model scope limits immediate applicability, but the framework provides a clear roadmap for scaling the analysis to full transformer architectures.
