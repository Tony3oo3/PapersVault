## Problem

Existing LLM hallucination benchmarks (HaluEval, TruthfulQA, SimpleQA, HaluBench, DefAn, Shroom2024, FELM) rely on unstructured textual context such as web links or passages for factual grounding, ignoring the structured factual resources available in Knowledge Graphs. Additionally, these benchmarks are English-centric, despite evidence that factual quality degrades significantly in lower-resource languages. MultiHal bridges both gaps by providing KG-grounded, multilingual hallucination evaluation.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests 31k+ unique questions from 7 foundational QA benchmarks and mines up to 2-hop paths from Wikidata, yielding 140k candidate KG paths that are pruned to 25.9k high-quality data points. Each data point pairs a question with a ground-truth answer and one or more KG paths formatted as semicolon-separated entity-predicate-entity label sequences. The final dataset is translated into five European languages (German, Italian, French, Portuguese, Spanish) using Nllb-200 3.3B, and evaluation produces three complementary scores: semantic similarity (via Multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2), NLI entailment/neutral/contradiction labels (via DeBERTa multilingual), and hallucination/consistency classification (via HHEM-2.1, English-only).

## Method

The dataset construction pipeline proceeds in four stages: (1) entity extraction and linking from questions and answers using Falcon 2.0 (top-3 Wikidata candidates) augmented with DBpedia-to-Wikidata and Wikipedia-to-Wikidata mappings for redundancy; (2) SPARQL-based path mining on Wikidata up to 2 hops, with custom query templates for entity, date, and numerical answer types; (3) a two-step LLM-as-a-judge filtering with GPT-4o Mini — first selecting the top-10 most relevant paths from larger candidate pools (with two shuffled passes and intersection to reduce ordering bias), then scoring each path on a 1–5 Likert scale and retaining only paths rated 4–5; (4) translation of questions, answers, and KG path labels to five languages. For baseline evaluation, KG paths are injected as in-context knowledge in a prompt (KG-RAG) versus a vanilla QA prompt, and outputs are scored against ground-truth answers using semantic similarity, NLI, and hallucination detection to triangulate factual quality across languages and models.

## Results

Across all three tested LLMs (GPT-4o Mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Llama 3.3 70B) and six languages, KG-RAG consistently outperforms vanilla QA. Semantic similarity improves by 0.12–0.36 points (e.g., Gemini 2.0 Flash: 0.71 vs. 0.51; GPT-4o Mini: 0.52 vs. 0.40), NLI entailment increases by 0.16–0.36 (e.g., GPT-4o Mini: 71% vs. 39%), and hallucination rates drop by 0.29–0.42 (e.g., GPT-4o Mini: 16% vs. 53%). All differences are statistically significant (Cramér-von Mises p < 1e-6) across every language-model pair.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the primary comparison is between two experimental conditions: vanilla QA (no external knowledge) and KG-RAG (mined Wikidata paths injected as in-context knowledge). KG-RAG wins on every metric for every model and language: semantic similarity gains range from +0.12 (GPT-4o Mini, French) to +0.36 (Gemini 2.0 Flash, English); NLI entailment gains range from +0.15 (Gemini 2.0 Flash, French) to +0.32 (GPT-4o Mini, English); and hallucination rates drop from 42–53% (QA) to 11–17% (KG-RAG). The largest absolute improvements are seen with GPT-4o Mini (the weakest base model), suggesting KG paths are most beneficial when the model's internal knowledge is less reliable. No external method baselines are compared; the paper positions MultiHal as a resource for future KG-RAG and knowledge-injection research rather than proposing a new generation method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established line of LLM hallucination and factuality evaluation benchmarks (HaluEval, TruthfulQA, SimpleQA, HaluBench, DefAn, Shroom2024, FELM) by adding two dimensions they all lack: structured knowledge-graph grounding via Wikidata paths and multilingual coverage across five European languages. Methodologically, it draws on KG-RAG (Sanmartín, 2024; Peng et al., 2025) for knowledge injection, Falcon 2.0 for entity linking, and LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-4o Mini) for path quality filtering, positioning itself at the intersection of graph-retrieval-augmented generation and multilingual factuality evaluation. Unlike prior KG-based fact-checking work (FactKG, FLEEK, GraphEval) that extracts graph structures from text post-hoc, MultiHal pre-mines KG paths as explicit in-context knowledge for generative QA evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiHal fills a concrete gap: no prior hallucination benchmark combined structured KG grounding with multilingual support, limiting the ability to study how factuality degrades across languages and whether KG integration mitigates this. The consistent KG-RAG improvements across all models and languages validate the benchmark's utility, while the ~11% false-positive rate in LLM-judge path filtering and the poor performance on temporal, suggestive, and domain-specific (medical, finance) questions highlight open challenges in entity linking quality and KG coverage. Future work includes extending typological diversity beyond Romance/Germanic languages, integrating domain-specific KGs (PubMed, PrimeKG), and evaluating more sophisticated knowledge-encoding methods beyond simple prompt-based injection.
