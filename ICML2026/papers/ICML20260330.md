## Problem

Training deep state space models (DSSMs) at scale is hampered by a fundamental tension between two existing paradigms: VAE-based DSSMs train in parallel but yield loose variational bounds and cannot support supervised state-estimation losses, while differentiable sequential Monte Carlo (DSMC) methods support supervised objectives and tighter bounds but are inherently sequential due to resampling, scaling poorly on modern GPU hardware. No prior method simultaneously achieves parallel-in-time computation, unbiased gradient estimates, and a statistically consistent posterior approximation for both discriminative and generative tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full observation sequence y_{0:T} and a parameterised SSM (prior P, transition kernels M_t, observation kernels H_t) along with a learned factorised proposal V_t(·|y_{0:T}). It produces N weighted particles at every time step t (the time-marginal smoothing posterior Q_t(x_t|y_{0:T})), an unbiased likelihood estimate L̂^N, and—when used generatively—synthetic trajectories sampled from the learned model. Unlike particle filters that output a single trajectory, PVMC outputs a full N-particle ensemble per time step with importance weights computed over all N^{T+1} cross-time trajectory combinations, enabling both per-step state estimation and trajectory-level generative sampling from the same forward pass.

## Method

PVMC (Parallel Variational Monte Carlo) replaces the sequential resampling of particle filters with a fully factorised variational proposal that samples N i.i.d. particles independently at each time step, then constructs an importance-weighted approximation to the joint smoothing distribution over all N^{T+1} possible trajectories. The key technical insight is that the time-marginal importance weights—each requiring a sum over N^T products of local N×N kernel matrices—can be computed via associative prefix and suffix scans over tuples of N×N matrices under the operation (C₁,C₂)⊕(D₁,D₂)=(C₁, C₂D₁D₂), yielding O(log N × log T) span complexity for both forward and backward passes. A new ELBO, L_PVMC^N = E[log L̂^N], is proven to be tighter than both the IWAE and standard VAE bounds (Theorem 3.2), and the estimator is shown to be unbiased with O_P(N^{-1/2}) convergence. By eliminating resampling entirely, PVMC achieves end-to-end differentiability with unbiased gradients while supporting both supervised losses (e.g., MSE against ground-truth states) and unsupervised generative training via the ELBO.

## Results

On the prey-predator state estimation task (Table 2), PVMC achieves the best state estimation MSE of 0.32±0.04, outperforming the next-best P-VAE ablation (0.43±0.06) and all DSMC baselines (0.52–1.20), while also achieving the best filtering MSE (0.40±0.03) and 2-SWD (2.96±0.74). Training completes in 1 min 49 s with zero failures across 20 runs, compared to 15–267 min and 0–14 failures for competing methods. On the linear-Gaussian benchmark (Table 1), PVMC with a learned proposal achieves an L2 error of 0.052 against the exact RTS smoother, far below TFS (0.501) and d-SMC (0.44), while running in 1.50 s versus 25.9 s for TFS. On SPX financial time series, PVMC most faithfully reproduces autocorrelation structure, skewness, and kurtosis of real returns compared to DMM, TCVAE, and Soft DPF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVMC outperforms all DSMC baselines on the prey-predator task: it beats Stop-gradient (Scibior & Wood, 2021) by 0.51 MSE, Soft DPF (Karkus et al., 2018) by 0.30 MSE, Diffusion DPF (Andersson & Zhao, 2025) by 0.20 MSE, and MDPS (Younis & Sudderth, 2024) by 0.88 MSE, while also being 150× faster than Diffusion DPF and 15× faster than MDPS. On the linear-Gaussian benchmark, PVMC (learned proposal) achieves 0.052 L2 error versus 0.501 for TFS (Briers et al., 2010) and 0.44 for d-SMC (Corenflos et al., 2022), approaching the exact RTS smoother (Rauch et al., 1965) and Kalman filter (Kalman et al., 1960) while running 17× faster than TFS. The P-VAE ablation (same architecture, VAE ELBO objective) confirms that the PVMC ELBO is the key driver: it matches PVMC's speed but degrades MSE from 0.32 to 0.43 and filtering MSE from 0.40 to 1.21. On SPX financial time series, PVMC captures short-term autocorrelation and distributional shape (skewness, kurtosis) more faithfully than DMM (Krishnan et al., 2017), TCVAE (Acciaio et al., 2024), and Soft DPF, which fail to reproduce the correlation structure entirely. Training stability is a consistent advantage: PVMC has zero failures across 20 seeds, whereas Soft DPF fails 7/20, MDPS fails 14/20, and Stop-gradient fails 2/20.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVMC bridges two established paradigms for training deep state space models: VAE-based DSSMs (Krishnan et al., 2017; Rangapuram et al., 2018), which are fully parallel but yield loose variational bounds and cannot support supervised losses, and differentiable sequential Monte Carlo (Jonschkowski et al., 2018; Chen & Li, 2023), which supports supervised objectives but is inherently sequential due to resampling. The method's ELBO is derived from the IWAE framework (Burda et al., 2016) but tightens the bound by importance-weighting over all N^(T+1) trajectory combinations rather than N, and the parallel computation leverages associative prefix/suffix scans (Blelloch, 1990) in a way that departs from prior parallel smoothers like d-SMC (Corenflos et al., 2022), which still require resampling and are not end-to-end differentiable. The closest prior work is the mixture density particle smoother (Younis & Sudderth, 2024), but PVMC differs by targeting the true smoothing posterior with unbiased gradients rather than a learned fusion of forward/backward filters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PVMC provides the first end-to-end differentiable particle smoother with unbiased gradient estimates and a statistically consistent posterior approximation, resolving a key tension in the DSSM literature between parallel scalability and posterior fidelity. The 10× speedup over the fastest DSMC baseline and the marked improvement in training stability (0 failures vs. 7–14 across 20 seeds) make it practically attractive for supervised state estimation. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences (the N^(T+1) trajectory space grows combinatorially), the gap between the factorised proposal assumption used in the main results and the more general non-factorisable proposals discussed only in the appendix without convergence guarantees, and whether the method's advantages persist at substantially larger state dimensions or longer horizons than those tested here.
