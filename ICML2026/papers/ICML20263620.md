## Problem

Multi-agent LLM cooperation under partial observability is fragile: explicit online messaging is costly, scales poorly with team size, and may be infeasible under bandwidth or interface constraints, while removing communication causes agents to fall back on independent local planning that suffers from miscoordination. The paper asks how to transfer centralized team-level preferences into per-agent guidance without any explicit message actions and without updating model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263620/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At execution time each agent receives its local partial observation (egocentric RGB-D at 256×512 or 512×512, or symbolic room state), the task description, its robot-specific action API set, and a small set of retrieved natural-language coordination rules from the Tacit Rule Memory (TRM). The local progress is encoded into a retrieval key (task description + robot ID + observation snippet) and matched by cosine similarity against precomputed TRM embeddings to select top-k rules. The output is a per-agent, time-indexed action plan in JSON format (one primitive action per robot per step), with no message actions emitted; the centralized LLM critic is never queried online.

## Method

Tact (Tacit Alike Contextual Thinking) is a gradient-free, three-stage protocol. Offline, an LLM critic scores candidate joint plans via log-odds of success/fail tokens; an additive VDN-style decomposition then fits per-agent bias corrections b_i through a projection-style residual-banding procedure that enforces |δ(τ,u)| ≤ ε_res on a small structured candidate set, yielding action-grounded coordination signals. These biases are distilled into natural-language TRM rules (role, do, don't, ordering) stored alongside progress embeddings. At decentralized execution, each agent retrieves the most semantically similar TRM rules from its local progress and injects them as prompt context, implicitly aligning its greedy action choice with the centralized joint value. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) shows that bounded residual implies the decentralized greedy plan incurs at most ε_res loss in joint LLM value, and a direction-recovery decomposition (Proposition 4.1) characterizes when retrieved rules faithfully transfer the distilled bias direction.

## Results

On TDW-MAT (oracle perception), Tact achieves the highest average transport rate of 86.5% with GPT-4o and 81.6% with Qwen2.5-72B, outperforming all communication-based and decentralized baselines. On C-WAH (symbolic observations), Tact reduces average steps to 46.8 (GPT-4o) and 53.1 (Qwen2.5-72B), the best among all methods. On VIKI-L2, Tact with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-SFT reaches ACC_ID=89.2 and ACC_OOD=30.2, a large improvement over the decentralized baseline P-dec (5.2 / 2.0). Tact also reduces online inference cost by 39.7% (output characters) compared to the communication-based CoELA baseline in the 2-agent setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tact outperforms all LLM-based cooperative baselines on TDW-MAT: with GPT-4o it achieves 86.5% average transport rate vs. RoCo (85.3%), CoELA (84.7%), and ProAgent (81.1%); with Qwen2.5-72B it leads at 81.6% vs. CoELA (80.4%), RoCo (80.6%), and ProAgent (79.7%). On C-WAH under symbolic observations, Tact achieves the fewest average steps (46.8 with GPT-4o, 53.1 with Qwen2.5-72B), beating CoELA (50.7 / 60.2), RoCo (63.8 / 70.7), and ProAgent (65.3 / 65.0). Against the classical hierarchical planners, Tact substantially outperforms MHP (103.0 / 75.0 steps) and RHP (75.0 / 75.0 steps) on C-WAH, and RHP (75.0% avg transport) on TDW-MAT. On VIKI-L2, Tact (89.2 ACC_ID) approaches the supervised centralized planner P-cen (96.5) while far exceeding the decentralized P-dec (5.2), demonstrating that implicit contextual coordination recovers most of the centralized advantage without global observations. In terms of efficiency, Tact uses 39.7% fewer output characters than CoELA in the 2-agent C-WAH setting and exhibits more moderate cost growth as team size scales to 4 agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lineages: (1) the log-odds value scoring of LLMs as critics (Dong et al., 2025), which it adopts to define joint and per-agent value functions, and (2) additive value decomposition from cooperative MARL (VDN, QMIX, QTRAN), which it repurposes as a gradient-free, inference-time consistency criterion rather than a differentiable training objective. The key departure from CTDE is that no parameters are updated; instead, residual banding identifies action-level miscoordination and distills it into a retrieval-augmented Tacit Rule Memory, positioning the work between value-decomposition MARL and the experience-distillation family (Reflexion, ExpeL, AutoGuide) while grounding rules in numeric bias signals rather than free-form reflection. Against explicit-communication baselines (CoELA, RoCo, CaPo), the paper replaces online messaging with implicit contextual conditioning, and against belief-modeling approaches (ProAgent, LIET), it avoids per-agent teammate modeling entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: explicit natural-language messaging between LLM agents incurs multiplicative LLM-call overhead that scales poorly with team size, and may be infeasible under privacy or interface constraints. By amortizing coordination into a static, retrieval-triggered rule memory, Tact offers a non-parametric, reusable coordination mechanism that works with closed-source backbones (GPT-4o) without fine-tuning. However, the method requires an offline warm-up stage with centralized scoring, and the authors acknowledge it is suboptimal for tasks requiring fresh, high-bandwidth private information exchange. Open questions include how the approach scales beyond 4 agents, whether TRM rules remain effective in highly dynamic or adversarial settings, and whether the residual-banding criterion generalizes to continuous action spaces or longer horizons where the finite candidate set assumption weakens.
