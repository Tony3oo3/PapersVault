## Problem

Despite the widespread use of Q-learning, no finite-time robustness guarantees existed for the infinite-horizon discounted setting when rewards are adversarially corrupted and data arrives asynchronously along a single Markovian trajectory. The paper asks whether one can still recover the near-optimal Õ(1/√T) convergence rate of vanilla Q-learning under a Huber contamination model with unbounded adversarial signals, and what fundamental lower bounds on estimation accuracy are imposed by such corruption.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes online state-action-reward tuples (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, y_t(s_t,a_t)) collected sequentially from a single trajectory, where each reward y_t is a Huber mixture of the true (possibly heavy-tailed) reward distribution and an arbitrary adversarial distribution with corruption probability ε. It requires only the corruption fraction ε, step-size α, confidence level δ, and horizon T as hyperparameters; in the reward-agnostic variant, no prior bounds on reward means or variances are needed. The output is a Q-table estimate Q_T with a high-probability ℓ∞ error bound that matches the classical Q-learning rate up to an additive O(√ε) corruption term, from which an optimal greedy policy is extracted.

## Method

Robust Async-Q combines two mechanisms: (i) a trimmed-mean estimator (adapted from Lugosi & Mendelson, 2021 to the Huber contamination model) that maintains running robust estimates of mean rewards for each state-action pair using historical data, and (ii) an adaptive threshold G_t that shrinks with sample size and rejects any reward estimate falling outside a 'typical' region, thereby bounding the iterates against rare extreme events. For the reward-agnostic variant (Robust Async-RAQ), the unknown reward bound is replaced by a slowly growing proxy m(t)=t^p, which introduces a coarse O(T^p) deterministic bound on iterates alongside a finer O(1) high-probability bound. The key analytical novelty is exploiting a refined Azuma-Hoeffding inequality from Shamir & Spencer (1987) for martingales with dual coarse/fine concentration, since standard Azuma-Hoeffding, sub-Gaussian martingale, and discrete-space variants all yield vacuous bounds under the inflated threshold. The Markovian extension applies a blocking subsampling technique (every τ-th sample, where τ scales with mixing time) to decouple temporal correlations, recovering i.i.d.-like rates up to a √τ inflation factor.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that Robust Async-Q achieves an ℓ∞-error bound of Õ(σ̃ log T / (λ_min^{3/2}(1−γ)^{5/2}) · √(log(|S||A|T/δ)/T) + σ̄√ε / (λ_min(1−γ))), matching the optimal Õ(1/√T) Q-learning rate up to an additive O(√ε) corruption term. The information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 3) establishes that Ω(σ̄√ε/(1−γ)) error is unavoidable, confirming near-optimality. Under Markovian sampling (Theorem 6), the same bounds hold up to a √τ inflation factor from the mixing time. Simulations on Grid-world, FrozenLake, CliffWalking, and Taxi confirm that vanilla Q-learning incurs significant errors under corruption while Robust Async-Q converges to a neighborhood of Q*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Robust Async-Q (and its reward-agnostic variant Robust Async-RAQ) against vanilla Q-learning (Watkins & Dayan, 1992) under the Huber contamination model. In the theoretical analysis, vanilla Q-learning's guarantees break down under adversarial corruption since it uses raw reward observations directly, while Robust Async-Q's trimmed-mean filtering and adaptive thresholding yield a clean O(√ε) additive degradation. In the empirical simulations (Appendix G) across Grid-world, FrozenLake, CliffWalking, and Taxi, the paper reports that vanilla Q-learning 'incurs significant errors' under corruption, whereas the proposed robust variants 'converge to a neighborhood of Q*.' The comparison is qualitative in the provided text (no specific numerical error values are tabulated in the main body), but the theoretical guarantee is that Robust Async-Q recovers the minimax-optimal Õ(1/√T) rate of vanilla Q-learning in the absence of corruption (ε=0), and degrades gracefully by only O(√ε) when corruption is present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust RL literature from bandits and episodic/offline settings to the infinite-horizon discounted MDP with the most challenging data model: asynchronous, single-trajectory, Markovian sampling. It builds directly on the trimmed-mean estimator of Lugosi & Mendelson (2021) for reward estimation and on the authors' own prior work (Maity & Mitra, 2024) on synchronous i.i.d. robust Q-learning, but departs substantially by handling time-correlated data, unknown reward statistics, and a refined Azuma-Hoeffding inequality from Shamir & Spencer (1987) that had not previously appeared in RL analysis. The adaptive thresholding mechanism and the coupling/blocking technique (inspired by Dorfman & Levy, 2022) are new algorithmic and analytical ingredients not present in prior Q-learning or robust statistics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide finite-time robustness guarantees for asynchronous Q-learning, closing a gap the authors explicitly identify as 'completely unexplored' in the literature. The matching upper and lower bounds (both scaling as O(√ε)) establish that the corruption penalty is fundamental, giving the field a clear target for future algorithms. Open questions include extending to function approximation, handling corruption in transitions (not just rewards), and reducing the algorithm's memory footprint via online robust estimators. For practitioners, the practical impact is currently limited to tabular MDPs on simple grid-world environments, but the theoretical tools (particularly the refined concentration inequality) may transfer to more complex settings.
