## Problem

Linear attention models (Mamba2, GDN, KDA) interpret their recurrences as closed-form online SGD, but naive SGD updates suffer from rapid information decay and limited retrieval capability. While momentum-based optimizers naturally address this by accumulating historical gradients, stepwise momentum (block size 1) preserves strict causality yet is sequential and impractical for large-scale parallel training, whereas blockwise momentum sacrifices causality and causes training-inference mismatch. The paper tackles the open problem of how to parallelize stepwise momentum while retaining exact causal consistency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text token sequence (tokenized with GPT-2, trained on 100B tokens of SlimPajama at 4k context length) and autoregressively predicts the next token. During training, the sequence is split into chunks of size C and processed via a chunkwise parallel algorithm that maintains two recurrent states per head: a fast-weight matrix S ∈ R^{d_k×d_v} and a momentum matrix M ∈ R^{d_k×d_v}. During inference, the model switches to the recurrent form (Eqs. 4–5) for O(1)-per-step decoding, with data-dependent scalar gates (α, β, μ, η) computed per token per head via lightweight linear projections.

## Method

MDN introduces a chunkwise parallel algorithm for the stepwise momentum delta rule by geometrically reordering the nested summation over the lower-triangular index domain (converting row-wise to column-wise accumulation), which decouples scalar coefficients from outer products and enables O(log C) prefix-sum computation of all coefficients in the log-domain. From a dynamical systems perspective, the momentum recurrence is reformulated as a second-order system whose transition matrix admits complex conjugate eigenvalues, enabling damped oscillatory memory that first-order decay/delta rules cannot express. To prevent sign-flipping instability, a stability-aware gating parameterization constrains β_t ≤ 1−α_t and μ_t ∈ (e^{−1}, 1), confining eigenvalues to the right half-plane. The architecture follows the GDN/Comba backbone (ShortConv + SiLU projections, L2-normalized keys, head-wise RMSNorm with data-dependent gating) and is implemented in Triton with a memory-efficient strategy that materializes the correction value Ṽ during the forward pass to avoid recomputing both S and M in the backward pass.

## Results

At the 1.3B scale (100B tokens), MDN achieves the lowest LAMBADA perplexity (14.87) and WikiText perplexity (18.03) among all linear attention models, while also attaining the highest average commonsense reasoning score (58.82) and in-context retrieval score (36.14). At the 400M scale (15B tokens), MDN similarly leads with LAMBADA ppl 41.62, WikiText ppl 31.51, average reasoning 49.42, and average retrieval 26.76. On LongBench (16K context), MDN achieves the highest average score of 20.18, with particularly strong gains on Code (50.50 LCC) and Summarization tasks. On the RULER Needle-In-A-Haystack benchmark, MDN outperforms the strongest baseline by 13.40, 11.45, and 8.95 points on multi-needle tasks at 8K context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDN outperforms all four linear attention baselines (Mamba2, GDN, Comba, KDA) on both perplexity and in-context retrieval at both 400M and 1.3B scales. Against the strongest linear baseline KDA, MDN improves LAMBADA perplexity by 1.96 (400M) and 1.96 (1.3B) points, and average retrieval by 2.29 and 3.52 points respectively. Against Comba, the closest competitor on reasoning, MDN gains 0.41 and 0.33 points in average reasoning at 400M and 1.3B, while also leading on retrieval by 2.75 and 0.89 points. Against the Transformer baseline, MDN achieves substantially lower perplexity (14.87 vs 17.90 at 1.3B) and higher reasoning scores, but the Transformer retains an advantage on in-context retrieval (40.60 vs 36.14 at 1.3B), reflecting the inherent limitation of finite-state recurrent models on unbounded-context recall. On LongBench, MDN leads all baselines by 1.56 points over GDN (20.18 vs 19.28) and 2.07 over KDA (20.18 vs 18.62), with the largest gap on Code tasks (50.50 vs 44.74 for Comba).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gated DeltaNet (GDN) and Comba architectures by replacing their naive SGD-based recurrent updates with a stepwise momentum rule, while preserving the delta-rule correction and output-correction mechanisms. The chunkwise parallel algorithm generalizes the State Space Duality framework (Mamba2) and the WY/UT-transform-based delta-rule parallelization (Yang et al., 2024b) to handle an additional momentum state, using a geometric reordering of nested summations to decouple coefficients from outer products. Unlike prior momentum-based sequence models (TTT, Titans, Atlas) that sacrifice strict causality via blockwise or sliding-window momentum, MDN maintains a fully causal stepwise update while achieving parallel training through the derived chunkwise formulation. The eigenvalue analysis introducing a second-order dynamical system with complex conjugate eigenvalues departs from the first-order real-valued spectral constraints of all prior linear attention models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation of linear attention models—poor in-context retrieval attributed to the oversimplified SGD update—by showing that momentum accumulation over historical gradients substantially narrows the retrieval gap with Transformers (e.g., +6.6 points on retrieval at 400M scale) while maintaining competitive perplexity. The stability-aware spectral gating (quadrant constraint on eigenvalues) provides a principled, minimal-overhead mechanism to prevent the numerical divergences that unconstrained second-order dynamics would otherwise cause, making the approach practical for large-scale pretraining. Limitations include lower training throughput than GDN/Comba due to dual-state computation, and the approach has only been validated up to 1.3B parameters; whether the momentum benefit persists at much larger scales or in hybrid architectures with very sparse full-attention layers remains open.
