## Problem

Reconstruction-based time series anomaly detection methods universally optimize MSE loss, which implicitly assumes residuals are i.i.d. Gaussian white noise. In practice, deep backbones produce residuals with significant temporal autocorrelation and non-Gaussianity, yielding noisy, unstable anomaly scores that are statistically flawed for reliable detection. The paper addresses this fundamental misalignment between the optimization objective and the statistical requirements of the downstream scoring step.

## Input / Output

The framework is model-agnostic and wraps around any reconstruction-based backbone (autoencoders, RNNs, Transformers, KANs, etc.) trained on windowed multivariate or univariate time series segments. During inference, a Fixed-Buffer Aggregator averages predictions from all overlapping windows covering each timestamp before feeding the aggregated residual into the Kalman smoother, producing a single scalar anomaly score per timestamp equal to the squared norm of the estimated structural signal. The only user-facing hyperparameter is the Circuit Breaker confidence level (1−α), set uniformly to 0.90 across all experiments.

## Method

COGNOS couples two stages. During training, the GWNR Loss augments standard MSE with two auxiliary terms: a Spectral Whitening loss (KL divergence between the normalized PSD of active residuals and a uniform distribution) to enforce temporal independence, and a Wavelet MMD loss (multi-scale RBF kernel MMD between Haar detail coefficients and a standard Gaussian) to enforce Gaussianity of the noise component; a dynamic gradient-rescaling mechanism aligns auxiliary gradient norms to the primary MSE gradient at each step. During inference, the Adaptive Residual Kalman Smoother (ARKS) models the residual as a Linear Gaussian State Space Model with system matrices calibrated offline via Method of Moments (selecting between AR(1) leakage-tracking and Random Walk noise-suppression modes per channel). A hypothesis-driven Circuit Breaker performs a per-step chi-squared test on the Normalized Innovation Squared; when the statistic exceeds a threshold, the process noise covariance is inflated (Q* = βR), forcing the Kalman Gain toward 1 for zero-lag anomaly tracking, then the filter recovers to its low-gain smoothing regime once the structural break is absorbed.

## Results

COGNOS is a model-agnostic enhancement framework applied on top of 9 backbone models across 7 datasets. The headline metric is Affiliation-based F1 (Aff-F1), where COGNOS achieves dramatic average improvements: GECCO (0.5475→0.9388), SWaT (0.4026→0.9557), MSL (0.4794→0.9308), and SWAN (0.1997→0.4577). On Std-F1, gains are more moderate but consistent (e.g., Autoformer on MSL: 0.7724→0.9321; KAN-AD on PSM: 0.8946→0.9744). Range-based metrics (R-A-R, V-R) show smaller or occasionally negative deltas, which the authors attribute to ARKS's deliberate onset-sensitivity design that stabilizes scores during sustained anomalies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COGNOS is not a standalone model but a plug-in enhancement, so all comparisons are Vanilla-backbone vs. COGNOS-enhanced-backbone (same architecture, same hyperparameters). Across all 9 backbones (Autoformer, CrossAD, DLinear, KAN-AD, LSTM-AE, MICN, ModernTCN, TimeMixer++, TimesNet) and all 7 datasets, COGNOS wins on Aff-F1 in 63 out of 63 model-dataset pairs — a 100% win rate on this metric. The largest absolute gains appear on GECCO (e.g., CrossAD: 0.3115→0.9320, +0.62) and SWaT (e.g., ModernTCN: 0.2495→0.9643, +0.71), where the vanilla models produce extremely noisy scores. On Std-F1, COGNOS also wins in the vast majority of cases (e.g., Autoformer MSL: 0.7724→0.9321; KAN-AD PSM: 0.8946→0.9744), though a few exceptions exist (e.g., TimeMixer++ on SMAP drops from 0.4924 to 0.2206, and MICN on SMAP drops from 0.8151 to 0.6314). Range-based metrics (R-A-R, V-R) show mixed results: COGNOS typically improves them modestly but occasionally decreases them (e.g., TimesNet MSL R-A-R: 0.6968→0.6663), which the authors explain as a consequence of ARKS's onset-focused design that stabilizes scores during sustained anomalies rather than maintaining elevated scores throughout. The ablation confirms that neither GWNR nor ARKS alone is sufficient — their synergy is essential, with GWNR creating the Gaussian-white-noise precondition that allows ARKS to operate near its theoretical MMSE optimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

COGNOS departs from the dominant trend of designing increasingly complex reconstruction backbones (TimesNet, ModernTCN, TimeMixer++, CrossAD) by instead targeting the statistical quality of their output residuals. It builds on two classical tools in a decoupled, principled way: the Kalman filter (Kalman, 1960) is repurposed as an inference-time anomaly scorer (ARKS) rather than a training-time hybrid, and the MMD kernel two-sample test (Gretton et al., 2012) is repurposed as a training-time distributional regularizer (GWNR) rather than a post-hoc evaluation metric. The theoretical grounding in the Wold Decomposition Theorem positions the work as 'residual engineering'—an axis orthogonal to and complementary with architectural innovation—rather than yet another backbone variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that correcting the statistical misalignment of MSE-trained residuals (non-Gaussianity, temporal autocorrelation) yields larger and more consistent gains than architectural refinements alone, validated across nine backbones and seven datasets. The tight coupling between GWNR and ARKS—where GWNR alone can actually degrade performance (GECCO Std-F1: 0.47→0.34) while ARKS alone gives inconsistent results—highlights that the synergy, not either component in isolation, is the active ingredient. Key limitations include applicability only to MSE-trained backbones (excluding association-discrepancy methods like Anomaly Transformer), a fixed observation-noise covariance assumption that may fail under heteroscedastic sensing, and a deliberate onset-detection bias that sacrifices range-based coverage metrics. Extending the residual-engineering principle to non-MSE objectives and state-dependent noise models are natural next steps.
