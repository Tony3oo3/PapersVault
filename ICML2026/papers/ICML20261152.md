## Problem

Existing LLM leaderboards rely on the parametric Bradley-Terry model, which is biased under misspecification (e.g., cyclic preferences), incompatible with flexible ML estimators for valid confidence intervals, and difficult to combine with proxy labels from LLM-as-a-judge systems. No unified nonparametric framework existed that could handle multiple ranking functionals, provide statistically efficient inference with black-box ML, and guide cost-efficient data collection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference labels (categorical outcomes such as win/loss/tie/both-bad), context features (e.g., prompt representations), binary selection indicators marking which item pairs were actually labeled, and optionally black-box judge predictions as auxiliary features. It outputs per-LLM GARS ranking scores (generalizing BT, Borda, and Rank Centrality), simultaneous 95% confidence intervals via a confidence ellipsoid, and a closed-form A-optimal labeling policy specifying per-pair, per-context annotation probabilities under a cost budget.

## Method

DMLRANK defines the ranking target as θ = E[F(μ(X))], where μ are contextual preference probabilities and F is a known functional (e.g., L²-projection of symmetrized log-odds for BT, average win-rate for Borda, or stationary distribution for Rank Centrality). It derives the efficient influence function (EIF) for this target, yielding a one-step debiased estimator that corrects plug-in bias via inverse-propensity-weighted residual terms scaled by the Jacobian of F, guaranteeing asymptotic normality and semiparametric efficiency even when μ is estimated by arbitrary black-box ML (e.g., LightGBM) under cross-fitting. External judges are incorporated as augmented input features, letting the nuisance model learn to exploit or ignore them automatically. For data acquisition, the paper derives a closed-form A-optimal labeling policy that allocates annotation probability proportional to the square root of each pair's information content (Jacobian-weighted label variance) divided by its cost, subject to a budget constraint solved via one-dimensional bisection.

## Results

Across all three GARS estimands (Borda, BT, Rank Centrality) and sample sizes (n=1000–3000), debiased estimators reduce estimation error by roughly 50–60% compared to plug-in estimators (e.g., Borda error drops from 0.38 to 0.15 at n=1000). Critically, debiased estimators achieve near-nominal 95% CI coverage (0.90–0.97), while plug-in coverage is severely invalid (0.05–0.20). For optimal data acquisition, the A-optimal policy reduces ranking MSE versus random collection across all GARS types (e.g., Borda: 0.130 vs. 0.141; RC: 0.017 vs. 0.020 at budget β=2000). On real-world Chatbot Arena data (n=32,980 prompts, K=20 models), all GARS rankings are largely consistent with the baseline, but plug-in confidence intervals collapse to near-zero width, confirming invalidity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the plug-in estimator (Eq. 11) that uses the same LightGBM nuisance models but omits the EIF-based debiasing correction. The debiased estimator wins decisively on both estimation error and CI coverage: at n=1000, error is reduced by 61% for Borda (0.15 vs. 0.38), 60% for BT (0.25 vs. 0.62), and 48% for RC (0.27 vs. 0.52), while coverage jumps from 9–17% (invalid) to 90–94% (near-nominal). For data acquisition, the A-optimal policy from Theorem 6.2 outperforms uniform random collection on all three GARS types (e.g., RC MSE 0.017 vs. 0.020), confirming the theoretical optimality result. Under BT model misspecification (γ>0), the debiased projection estimator outperforms both the plug-in estimator and a restricted debiased estimator that incorrectly assumes BT holds, demonstrating robustness to parametric misspecification. The paper does not compare against other nonparametric ranking methods (e.g., HodgeRank or PageRank without debiasing) as standalone baselines, focusing instead on the debiased-vs-plug-in contrast within the same GARS framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the debiased machine learning (DML) framework from Chernozhukov et al. (2018) — originally developed for causal inference and missing data — into the setting of preference-based LLM evaluation, where the target is a ranking functional rather than a treatment effect. It generalizes the parametric Bradley-Terry model (Bradley & Terry, 1952) into a nonparametric estimand (GARS) that unifies BT scores, Borda/win-rate scores, and Rank Centrality under a single efficient-influence-function-based inference procedure. The work departs from recent covariate-adjusted BT inference (Li & Li, 2025; Spokoiny, 2025) and prediction-powered ranking (Chatzi et al., 2024) by remaining agnostic to the specific ranking functional, accommodating ties and multi-category labels, and adding an optimal data-acquisition layer that prior preference-ranking methods do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives LLM leaderboard practitioners (e.g., LM Arena, MT-Bench) a principled way to report ranking scores with statistically valid confidence intervals even when flexible ML models or LLM-as-a-judge proxies are used — a gap that current parametric BT pipelines leave open. The optimal labeling policy is practically valuable for costly human-annotation budgets, and the robustness to BT misspecification (shown empirically) addresses a known failure mode of existing leaderboards. Key open questions include: what happens when the missing-at-random assumption is violated (the authors suggest partial identification as a direction), how the framework extends to multi-agent or long-horizon evaluation settings, and whether the inverse-propensity-weighting instability under low overlap can be mitigated without sacrificing efficiency.
