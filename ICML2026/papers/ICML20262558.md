## Problem

Existing LLM-based code transformation approaches treat editing as an end-to-end task, leaving the semantic understanding needed for correct transformations implicit in model weights or dependent on rigid compiler-level abstractions that may not generalize. This makes it difficult for models to disentangle what must be preserved from what can be changed, leading to incorrect edits and missed optimization opportunities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source program C_src and a natural language editing instruction I as input, and produces an instruction-specific transformed program C_tgt that preserves the original semantics (verified by test set U_sem) while satisfying the instruction (verified by test set U_edit). Semantic equivalence is defined via Equivalence Modulo Inputs (EMI): two programs are equivalent if they produce identical outputs for all tests in U_sem. At inference, the model iteratively generates intermediate semantically equivalent code variants (C_rep) before producing the final transformation, with a beam of b programs maintained across T iterations and k parallel rollouts per program.

## Method

SEMREP introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning framework using GRPO with verifiable execution-based rewards. Stage 1 (generative code representation learning) trains the model to produce semantically equivalent program variants, rewarding compilability and test-based equivalence while encouraging diverse syntax/structure. Stage 2 (instruction-specific transformation) fine-tunes the model to apply the editing instruction while preserving semantics, using a joint reward over compilability, semantic invariance, and instruction adherence. The total training budget is strictly matched to finetuning-only baselines (B/2 steps per stage). At inference, SEMREP performs iterative test-time scaling by alternating between semantic exploration and instruction-specific transformation, selecting top-b programs from k·b candidates based on execution against both test sets, enabling evolutionary search over diverse intermediate representations.

## Results

On KernelBench, SEMREP (QwQ-32B) achieves 93% Correctness best@16 and 2.87× Speedup best@16, outperforming the state-of-the-art Kevin-32B by 43.1% in Correctness and 1.1× in Speedup. On EditBench, SEMREP matches GPT-o4-mini at 57.41% Pass@1 while significantly outperforming open-weight models (72B) by up to 6.9%. In the OpenEvolve evolutionary agent setting, SEMREP achieves a 7.28× speedup on the MST task, outperforming 685B DeepSeek-V3 models by 25.95%, and reaches 93.88% of AlphaEvolve's circle packing result (2.4737 vs. 2.6358) with substantially fewer resources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kevin-32B (Baronio et al., 2025), the state-of-the-art RL-finetuned CUDA kernel generator, SEMREP wins decisively on KernelBench: 93% vs. 65% Correctness best@16 (+43.1%), 2.87× vs. 1.36× Speedup best@16, and 72 vs. 21 fast₁ best@16, all under a strictly matched training budget. On EditBench, SEMREP (32B) ties GPT-o4-mini at 57.41% Pass@1 and outperforms the much larger Qwen3-Coder (405B) and GLM-4.6 (355B) by 1.85 points. In the OpenEvolve evolutionary agent setting, SEMREP outperforms both 685B DeepSeek-V3-Chat and DeepSeek-V3-Reasoner on the MST task by 25.95% in speedup (7.28× vs. ~5.8×), discovering an iterative Union-Find implementation that neither 685B model identifies. On circle packing, SEMREP reaches 2.4737 (93.88% of AlphaEvolve's 2.6358 SOTA) using a single 32B model with far fewer iterations than AlphaEvolve's thousands of Gemini calls, while still significantly outperforming OpenEvolve paired with the 685B DeepSeek models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMREP departs from the dominant paradigm of treating code transformation as an end-to-end learning task (Shypula et al. 2023; Wei et al. 2026) where semantic understanding is implicit in model weights, and from rigid compiler-level abstractions (AST, PDG) that are expensive to construct and may not align with LLM pre-training distributions. It builds on equivalence modulo inputs (Le et al. 2014) to define test-based semantic preservation, and shares the spirit of reinforcement pre-training (Dong et al. 2025; Hatamizadeh et al. 2025) in using verifiable rewards for a foundational capability before task-specific fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is formulating semantic understanding as a *generative* task—producing semantically equivalent source code—rather than a discriminative or structural one, making the representation directly interpretable and amenable to evolutionary search (AlphaEvolve, OpenEvolve).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEMREP demonstrates that explicitly training a model to generate semantically equivalent code as an intermediate representation, under a strictly matched training budget, yields consistent gains in correctness, generalization to unseen hardware, robustness to syntactic perturbations, and test-time scaling over finetuning-only baselines. The integration with evolutionary coding agents (OpenEvolve) showing that a 32B SEMREP model discovers optimizations (iterative Union-Find, ring-based circle packing) that 685B DeepSeek-V3 models miss is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include the dependence on test coverage for verifiable rewards (Rice's theorem limits), which categories of semantic-preserving transformations are most beneficial for downstream editing, and whether the two-stage approach scales to multi-step, repo-level tasks beyond the T=2 iterations tested here.
