## Problem

Existing generalization guarantees for data-driven hyperparameter tuning are restricted to one-dimensional (scalar) hyperparameters and single-level objectives where training and validation losses coincide. The practically important multi-dimensional setting (e.g., ElasticNet tuning both L1 and L2 weights) remained unresolved, and prior geometric approaches required strong regularity assumptions on boundary polynomials that fail in higher dimensions. This paper establishes the first general framework for sample-complexity guarantees in the multi-dimensional, bi-level validation setting.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a family of problem instances drawn from an unknown distribution D, a bounded hyperparameter box A = [α_min, α_max]^p, and training/validation objectives f and g that admit piecewise polynomial structure (or more generally, semi-algebraic structure) with instance-independent complexity parameters. The output is a concrete pseudo-dimension bound on the induced loss class {ℓ_α : X → [-H,H]}, which via Pollard's theorem translates directly into a sample complexity guarantee for ERM-based hyperparameter selection. Notably, the loss ℓ_α(x) is defined implicitly through a bi-level optimization (minimize f over θ, then evaluate g at the minimizer), so the analysis must handle the implicit, potentially nonsmooth dependence on α without requiring explicit solution paths.

## Method

The core mechanism is a model-theoretic pipeline: (1) the threshold predicate I(ℓ_α(x) ≥ t) is expressed as a polynomial first-order logic (FOL) formula by encoding the inner optimality conditions as quantified predicates over θ; (2) quantifier elimination (Basu et al., 2006) transforms this FOL into an equivalent quantifier-free formula (QFF) in the free variables α, with bounded predicate count and polynomial degree; (3) evaluating the QFF is described as a Goldberg-Jerrum (GJ) algorithm, whose degree and predicate complexity yield the pseudo-dimension bound via the GJ framework. This logic-based approach replaces the ad hoc geometric arguments (oscillations, monotonic curves) that confined prior work to p=1, and naturally handles multi-dimensional α, the f≠g validation setting, and even semi-algebraic objectives beyond piecewise polynomial (e.g., Group LASSO norms). When an explicit piecewise rational solution path θ*(x,α) is available, the quantifier elimination step is bypassed entirely, recovering tighter bounds that depend only on p rather than the parameter dimension d.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline results are pseudo-dimension (sample complexity) bounds for multi-dimensional hyperparameter tuning (α ∈ ℝᵖ, p > 1). For the training-loss setting, Theorem 5.1 gives Pdim(ℒ) = O(pd log(M_f + T_f + d) + p²d log Δ_f), and Theorem 5.2 establishes a matching lower bound Ω(pd log Δ_f). For the general validation-loss (bi-level) setting, Theorem 6.1 yields Pdim(ℒ) = O(pd² log M_tot + p²d² log Δ_tot). When an explicit piecewise-rational solution path is available (e.g., ElasticNet), Theorem 7.2 tightens the bound to O(p log(M_total Δ_total)), recovering the known tight O(d) bound for ElasticNet that matches the Ω(d) lower bound of Balcan et al. (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

No experimental baselines or datasets are evaluated; all contributions are theoretical. The paper's main comparison is against Balcan et al. (2025), whose framework was restricted to one-dimensional hyperparameters (p = 1) and the single-level case (f ≡ g), relying on ad hoc geometric arguments (oscillations, monotonic curves) and strong regularity assumptions (e.g., ELICQ conditions). This paper removes both restrictions via a model-theoretic (quantifier-elimination) approach, extending guarantees to arbitrary p > 1 and to the general bi-level validation setting (f ≠ g) without additional regularity assumptions. Compared to the Goldberg–Jerrum (1993) bound O(p(p+q)∏d_k(log M + log Δ)), Theorem 4.1 removes the dependence on the data dimension q and eliminates a factor of p in front of log M, yielding a strictly tighter bound. For the ElasticNet application, the refined Theorem 7.2 recovers Pdim(ℒ) = O(d), which is tight (matching the Ω(d) lower bound from Balcan et al., 2023), demonstrating that the general framework achieves optimal rates in structured settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven algorithm design framework of Balcan et al. (2025), which established the first generalization guarantees for hyperparameter tuning but was restricted to one-dimensional hyperparameters and the single-level setting (f≡g). The key methodological departure is replacing the ad hoc geometric arguments (oscillations, monotonic curves) that break down in higher dimensions with a model-theoretic approach: expressing the loss threshold predicate as a polynomial first-order logic formula and applying quantifier elimination (Basu et al., 2006) to reduce it to a quantifier-free formula evaluable by a Goldberg-Jerrum algorithm (Goldberg & Jerrum, 1993; Bartlett et al., 2022). This logical machinery naturally handles multi-dimensional hyperparameters, the general bi-level validation setting (f≠g), and even semi-algebraic objectives beyond piecewise polynomial structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the central open question from Balcan et al. (2025) by providing the first general sample-complexity guarantees for multi-dimensional hyperparameter tuning with validation loss, and establishes the first matching lower bound in the training-loss setting. The framework's scope extends beyond piecewise polynomial functions to all semi-algebraic objectives (e.g., Group LASSO), broadening the class of problems with provable learnability. Two important open questions remain: fundamental lower bounds for the general bi-level (f≠g) setting are still unknown, and the semi-algebraic restriction excludes transcendental functions (exp, log, tanh) common in deep learning, motivating extension to o-minimal structures.
