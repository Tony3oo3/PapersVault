## Problem

Existing robust safety alignment methods for LLMs primarily target uncertainty in preference supervision (noisy labels, distribution shift) but overlook optimization-induced fragility: sharp alignment landscapes amplify even minor noise, producing brittle safety behavior under domain shift. The paper argues that robustness failures cannot be addressed by data-centric methods alone and that the geometry of the optimization process itself is a complementary, underexplored source of alignment fragility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training input consists of preference triples (prompt, preferred safe response, rejected unsafe response) from PKU-SafeRLHF-30K, plus a linear probe trained on safety labels to identify the safety-critical parameter subspace (Top-1% of value-vector neurons by similarity to the probe direction). For reward-level ShaPO, a pretrained Beaver-7B reward model additionally provides scalar preference scores. The output is an aligned policy model that generates safer text responses, evaluated via Win Rate and Attack Success Rate under two independent safety judges (MD and NV) across in-distribution and out-of-distribution safety benchmarks.

## Method

ShaPO (Sharpness-aware Preference Optimization) enforces worst-case alignment stability only along alignment-critical directions rather than uniformly across all parameters. A linear probe trained on residual-stream representations identifies safety-critical neurons via inner-product similarity with the probe direction; empirical analysis shows that perturbing fewer than 0.5% of these neurons accounts for over 80% of worst-case alignment loss, justifying selective control. The optimization follows a SAM-style two-step procedure (gradient on the safety subspace → normalized perturbation → robust update) but restricts the adversarial perturbation ε_S to the identified Top-K neuron subset, performed every τ steps to limit overhead. Two instantiations are provided: token-level ShaPO applies selective geometry control directly to the DPO likelihood-based loss, while reward-level ShaPO applies it to a binary cross-entropy loss anchored to a pretrained reward model's preference scores, offering a semantically grounded alternative that is more robust to noisy token-level supervision.

## Results

On the domain-shift setting (trained on PKU-SafeRLHF-30K, evaluated on four OOD safety benchmarks), ShaPO-R achieves the lowest average Attack Success Rate of 0.70% on LLaMA-3-8B and 0.55% on Qwen2.5-7B, compared to 2.09% and 1.05% for the strongest baseline Dr.DPO, and 22.85% and 14.39% for standard DPO. On the in-distribution setting, ShaPO-T achieves 85.45% Win Rate on LLaMA-3-8B (vs. 69.19% for DPO and 82.50% for Dr.DPO) while reducing MD-judge ASR from 26.57% to 2.76%. Under 40% label-flip noise, ShaPO-R maintains the highest Win Rate across all noise levels, substantially outperforming both standard and data-robust baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShaPO consistently outperforms all six baselines across both IID and OOD settings. Against standard DPO, ShaPO-T reduces average OOD ASR from 22.85% to 1.37% on LLaMA-3-8B (a ~16× reduction) while also improving Win Rate from 69.19% to 85.45% in-distribution. The strongest data-centric baseline, Dr.DPO, already achieves low ASR (2.09% avg OOD), but ShaPO-R further reduces it to 0.70%, with particularly large gains on HH-RLHF (4.58%→1.86%) and SaladBench (3.52%→1.13%) where distributional differences from training data are greatest. rDPO and IPO show moderate improvements over DPO but remain 3–15× worse than ShaPO on OOD ASR. cDPO performs poorly under domain shift (30.70% avg ASR), worse than standard DPO, suggesting its conservative objective overfits to the training distribution. SFT degrades safety substantially under OOD evaluation (64.03% avg ASR), confirming that preference-based optimization is necessary but insufficient without geometry-aware robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO preference-optimization framework by incorporating a selective variant of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), restricting adversarial parameter perturbations to a safety-critical subspace identified via linear probes on residual-stream representations. It departs from the dominant line of robust alignment work (Dr.DPO, rDPO, IPO, cDPO), which addresses data-side uncertainty through distributionally robust or noise-aware objectives, by introducing parameter-side robustness as a complementary axis. The key conceptual departure is the 'selective' geometry control: rather than applying uniform sharpness penalties across all parameters (as in standard SAM), ShaPO confines worst-case perturbations to a small subset of neurons, motivated by mechanistic interpretability evidence that safety behavior is localized. A second departure is the reward-level instantiation, which anchors geometry control to an external pretrained reward model rather than token-level likelihoods, creating a structural bridge between offline preference optimization and online RLHF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear conceptual decomposition of robustness failures in safety alignment into data-side and parameter-side components, and demonstrates empirically that these are orthogonal: ShaPO composes additively with data-robust objectives like Dr.DPO. The finding that uniform geometry control underperforms selective control under distribution shift (Table 3) is practically important, as it warns against naively applying SAM-style regularization to all parameters. Limitations include the ~2× computational overhead, reliance on an approximate probe-based identification of safety-critical neurons, and evaluation restricted to models up to 8B parameters, leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier-scale models. The non-monotonic effect of neuron ratio (Figure 5) suggests the optimal subspace size may be model- and task-dependent, a question the paper does not fully resolve.
