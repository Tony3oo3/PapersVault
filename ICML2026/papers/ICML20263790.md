## Problem

Existing mobile GUI navigation datasets are small-scale and English-centric, leaving Chinese mobile app coverage and the effects of data scaling under both supervised and reinforcement fine-tuning largely unexplored. There is also no standardized toolkit for unified offline benchmarking of diverse VLM-based GUI agents, and the role of explicit reasoning in shaping decision behavior and generalization has not been systematically characterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263790/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a static GUI screenshot, a natural-language task instruction (high-level or low-level), and a reconstructed interaction history of prior action-observation pairs, and outputs a structured action from a unified space (CLICK, SCROLL, TYPE, OPEN, PRESS, etc.) with screen coordinates normalized to [0, 1000]. Evaluation produces step-level metrics (type match, exact match) and episode-level metrics (progress, success rate); the semi-online variant additionally injects the model's own reasoning traces into the history whenever its predicted action matches the reference, tightening the evaluation context toward on-policy behavior.

## Method

The paper introduces HyperTrack (16,080 tasks across 674 Chinese Android apps in 17 categories) and GUIEvalKit, a unified evaluation toolkit spanning five benchmarks and over 30 VLMs. For training, they fine-tune UI-TARS-1.5-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking under both SFT and DAPO-style RL (GRPO with Clip-Higher, Dynamic Sampling, and Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss), using a decomposed reward of binary action-type correctness plus parameter correctness (bounding-box containment for clicks, direction match for scrolls, exact string match for text). A key evaluation contribution is semi-online evaluation (SOEval), which conditionally replaces offline reference history with the model's own on-policy decision artifacts to reduce off-policy mismatch. For behavioral analysis, they propose a clustering-based execution-to-decision abstraction (DBSCAN with ε=70 for spatial actions, two-stage edit-distance clustering for text actions) and define decision diversity (entropy over the induced decision distribution) and decision stability (probability mass on the correct decision) to reveal a fundamental stability-diversity trade-off: reasoning mode broadens the viable decision space but reduces PASS@1 stability, while benefiting more tolerant evaluation regimes such as PASS@8.

## Results

Under offline evaluation, specialized GUI agents dominate: UI-TARS-72B-SFT achieves the highest step exact match on AndroidControl (95.05% low-level, 79.37% high-level) and GUI-Odyssey (72.27%), while AgentCPM-GUI-8B leads on CAGUI (91.32%) and MiMo-VL-7B-RL-2508 leads on HyperTrack (77.98%). Larger models consistently outperform smaller ones (e.g., UI-TARS scales from 90.23% to 95.05% exact match on AndroidControl low-level from 2B to 72B). Thinking mode does not help: Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (72.20% exact match) outperforms Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (71.10%) on AndroidControl, and the same pattern holds for MiMo-VL and GUI-Owl. Under semi-online evaluation (SOEval), average step exact match improves across all models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct: 60.39→62.84; UI-Venus-Navi-72B: 74.09→76.16), and SOEval metrics correlate more strongly with AndroidWorld online success (R²=0.62) than offline metrics (R²=0.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Specialized GUI agents consistently outperform general-purpose VLMs: UI-TARS-72B-SFT achieves 95.05% step exact match on AndroidControl (low-level) versus 85.05% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 72.20% for Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. AgentCPM-GUI-8B is the strongest on CAGUI (91.32% exact match), surpassing UI-TARS-72B-SFT (74.53%) by 16.8 points, reflecting its Chinese-language training advantage. MiMo-VL-7B-RL-2508 leads on HyperTrack (77.98% exact match), outperforming UI-TARS-72B-SFT (75.20%) and AgentCPM-GUI-8B (54.26%). Within the Qwen3-VL family, instruct variants beat thinking variants on every benchmark (e.g., 72.20% vs. 71.10% on AndroidControl low-level), indicating that explicit reasoning traces degrade precise action execution. Under SOEval, all models improve over offline evaluation (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct: 60.39→62.84; GUI-Owl-7B: 65.49→67.37), and SOEval step exact match correlates more strongly with AndroidWorld online success (Spearman ρ=0.77, R²=0.62) than offline metrics (ρ=0.66, R²=0.48), validating semi-online evaluation as a better proxy for interactive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO/DAPO reinforcement learning framework for policy optimization and positions itself within the rapidly growing GUI agent literature (UI-TARS, UI-Venus, GUI-Owl, AgentCPM-GUI, MagicGUI). Rather than proposing a new agent architecture, it carves out a systematic empirical study: a large-scale Chinese mobile GUI dataset (HyperTrack), a unified offline/semi-online evaluation toolkit (GUIEvalKit), and decision-level behavioral metrics (diversity and stability) that go beyond standard step/episode accuracy. It complements training-focused systems like UItron and UI-S1, and evaluation efforts like MobiBench, by jointly addressing data scaling, evaluation methodology, and reasoning behavior in a single controlled study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap by providing the first large-scale (16K+ tasks, 674 apps) Chinese mobile GUI dataset with hierarchical annotations and precise bounding boxes, alongside a reproducible evaluation toolkit that supports both offline and semi-online protocols. The finding that explicit reasoning degrades PASS@1 performance while improving PASS@8 reveals a fundamental stability-diversity trade-off that has practical implications for how GUI agents should be deployed (single-shot vs. best-of-n). Open questions include whether the semi-online evaluation can be extended to fully interactive settings, whether adaptive mechanisms can balance near-decision context sensitivity with stable behavior, and whether the Chinese-only dataset limits generalizability conclusions to multilingual GUI navigation.
