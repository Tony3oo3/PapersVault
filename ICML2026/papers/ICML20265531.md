## Problem

Despite the widespread use of multimodal contrastive learning (MCL) in vision-language models, two fundamental questions remain unanswered: what geometric mechanism causes the modality gap (the separation of image and text representations in embedding space), and what actually determines downstream performance? Prior explanations—cone effect, contrastive objective, insufficient training, information bias—were based on empirical observations over small data subsets and produced inconsistent conclusions, with no unified theoretical framework for the convergent optimal representation (COR) of MCL.

## Input / Output

The method operates on paired image-text data, where both modalities are encoded and L2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere S^{h-1} (dimension 768 for ViT-B/16). The SSA loss specifically consumes the normalized projections of these representations onto the shared subspace (the intersection of the two collapsed subspaces detected via SVD), rather than the raw embeddings. Downstream evaluation produces zero-shot class labels (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-10/100) and cross-modal retrieval rankings (MSCOCO I→T and T→I), with the analysis conducted across 78 pretrained VLMs spanning ResNet, ConvNeXt, and ViT backbones trained on datasets from 1.4M to 400M pairs.

## Method

The paper establishes the first theoretical framework for the COR of MCL by proving four theorems: (1) without constraints, the COR is a paired uniform distribution with zero modality gap; (2) the cone constraint (vMF marginals) does not prevent gap closure; (3) under a subspace constraint where X and Y collapse into distinct hyperplanes, the gap converges to the minimal angle between those hyperplanes, identifying dimension collapse as the structural source; and (4) at optimality, projections of all sample pairs onto the shared subspace align. Building on the empirical finding that shared space alignment (SAlign) correlates strongly with downstream performance (Kendall's τ ≈ 0.73) while the modality gap's effect is limited, the authors propose the SSA loss—a symmetric contrastive objective applied to the normalized projections (X**, Y**) within the shared subspace—added to the standard MCL loss during pretraining. This directly optimizes alignment in the shared space without explicitly targeting gap reduction, and is validated by training CLIP ViT-B/16 from scratch on CC12M, outperforming SharedCLIP, IMSep, and AlignCLIP baselines on both classification and retrieval.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification, CLIP+SSA achieves 71.3% Top-1 on CIFAR-10, 37.8% Top-1 on CIFAR-100, and 33.3% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K, consistently outperforming all baselines. On zero-shot cross-modal retrieval (MSCOCO), CLIP+SSA reaches 34.5% I→T R@1 and 22.8% T→I R@1, again the best among all compared methods. The paper also validates its theory across 78 pretrained VLMs, showing that shared space alignment (SAlign) correlates strongly with downstream performance (Kendall's τ = 0.733 on MSCOCO, 0.689 on ImageNet), while the modality gap's effect is limited.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CLIP+SSA against four baselines: the original CLIP, and three gap-reduction methods from Eslami & de Melo (2025) — SharedCLIP (parameter sharing), IMSep (intra-modal separation), and AlignCLIP (combined). CLIP+SSA wins on every metric across all three classification benchmarks: it improves CIFAR-10 Top-1 by 1.9 points over AlignCLIP (71.3 vs. 69.4), CIFAR-100 Top-1 by 1.3 points (37.8 vs. 36.5), and ImageNet-1K Top-1 by 0.5 points (33.3 vs. 32.8). Notably, SharedCLIP actually degrades CIFAR-10 performance (56.9 vs. CLIP's 61.6), confirming the paper's claim that explicitly reducing the modality gap can be harmful. On MSCOCO retrieval, CLIP+SSA also leads with 34.5% I→T R@1 and 22.8% T→I R@1, surpassing AlignCLIP (34.0 / 22.3) and the original CLIP (31.4 / 20.5) by 3.1 and 2.3 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Convergent Optimal Representation (COR) framework from unimodal contrastive learning (Wang & Isola, 2020; Graf et al., 2021) to the multimodal setting, providing the first theoretical characterization of the COR of MCL. It departs from the dominant cone-effect hypothesis (Liang et al., 2022) and the information-bias hypothesis (Schrodi et al., 2025) by proving that dimension collapse into distinct subspaces is the structural source of the modality gap. The proposed SSA loss builds directly on the CLIP MCL objective (Radford et al., 2021) and is positioned against post-hoc gap-reduction methods (SharedCLIP, IMSep, AlignCLIP from Eslami & de Melo, 2025) by targeting shared space alignment rather than gap minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a long-standing open question in the VLM community by identifying dimension collapse (not cone geometry or information bias) as the geometric source of the modality gap, and by showing that the contrastive objective actually tends to close the gap rather than preserve it. The practical implication—that reducing the modality gap post-hoc does not improve (and can degrade) downstream performance, while shared space alignment is the dominant factor—redirects research effort away from gap-reduction heuristics toward alignment-focused objectives. A key open question acknowledged by the authors is what causes dimension collapse itself, which remains a major unsolved problem. The SSA method, while giving modest empirical gains (~1–2 points on classification, ~1 point on retrieval), provides a principled training signal that could be more impactful at larger scale.
