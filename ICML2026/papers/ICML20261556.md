## Problem

Existing model-free neural decoders for error-correcting codes rely on magnitude-based preprocessing (|y|) that discards per-coordinate directional information, and diffusion-based decoders like DDECC require explicit time/SNR conditioning at inference. This paper asks whether a score-based decoder can learn a continuous denoising field directly on the signed channel observation without any noise-level side information, and whether this formulation yields consistent gains over strong neural and classical baselines across diverse code families.

## Input / Output

The decoder receives the raw signed BPSK channel observation y ∈ R^n (not the magnitude |y| used by prior model-free decoders) together with the parity-check matrix H, which defines the Tanner-graph masking and syndrome computation. It outputs decoded codeword bits x̂ ∈ {0,1}^n obtained by thresholding the final PF-ODE iterate. The number of ODE solver steps N_steps is a free inference-time hyperparameter that directly trades latency for accuracy without retraining, and early stopping terminates the trajectory as soon as the syndrome vanishes.

## Method

SB-ECC formulates soft decoding as a variance-exploding (VE) score-based denoising problem: the AWGN channel observation is treated as a forward VE perturbation at an unknown noise level, and decoding integrates the associated probability-flow ODE (PF-ODE) in σ-space from σ_max down to σ_min. The denoiser uses the CrossMPT cross-attention architecture (variable-node and check-node tokens coupled via Tanner-graph masking) but operates on the signed observation rather than |y|, and is trained with a standard noise-prediction loss L_ε = ‖ε̂_θ(y, s(y)) − ε‖² across a linear noise schedule without any time or SNR conditioning. At inference, the PF-ODE is discretized uniformly in σ-space (enabled by the linear schedule) and integrated with either Euler or the higher-order DPM-Solver; after each step a hard decision is taken and the syndrome checked, allowing early termination upon reaching a valid codeword. The key design choices—signed input, time-unconditional training, and uniform σ-discretization—collectively eliminate the need for SNR estimation and enable a direct latency-accuracy knob at test time.

## Results

SB-ECC is evaluated on 42 code/SNR settings across BCH, Polar, LDPC, MacKay, and CCSDS codes, reporting −ln(BER) (higher is better) at E_b/N_0 ∈ {4, 5, 6} dB. It achieves the best BER in 39/42 entries (with one tie), yielding an average SNR gain of 0.17 dB and a maximum gain of 0.46 dB over the strongest competing baseline (DDECC with the same CrossMPT backbone). Replacing the Euler ODE solver with DPM-Solver preserves −ln(BER) while reducing end-to-end decoding time by 8.86% on average (up to 12.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SB-ECC is compared against four baselines: classical BP (50 iterations), learned AR-BP (50 iterations), CrossMPT (magnitude-based model-free decoder), and DDECC (diffusion-based decoder retrained with the same CrossMPT backbone for a controlled comparison). SB-ECC outperforms all four baselines in 39 out of 42 code/SNR entries, with the closest competitor being DDECC (same backbone, magnitude input, time-conditioned). The average SNR gain over DDECC is 0.17 dB, peaking at 0.46 dB on BCH(63,45) at 5 dB. Against CrossMPT, SB-ECC gains are larger (e.g., 9.03 vs. 7.52 on Polar(128,64) at 4 dB), confirming that the signed-input score-based formulation adds substantial value beyond the architecture alone. Against BP and AR-BP, SB-ECC consistently leads by 1–4 in −ln(BER) across all code families, with the gap widening at lower SNR. The three entries where SB-ECC does not strictly win are within a small margin (≤0.1 in −ln(BER)) of the best competing method.

## Relation to Prior Work

SB-ECC sits at the intersection of two lines of work: score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2020b) and neural channel decoding. It adopts the CrossMPT transformer backbone (Park et al., 2024) for its architecture and the variance-exploding SDE / PF-ODE framework for its decoding dynamics, but departs from both the DDECC diffusion decoder (Choukroun & Wolf, 2022a) and the broader model-free decoder family (ECCT, CrossMPT) in two key ways: it operates on the raw signed channel observation rather than the standard magnitude-plus-syndrome preprocessing, and it removes time/SNR conditioning entirely in favor of a linear noise schedule with uniform σ-space discretization. The conceptual shift is from learning a discrete bit-prediction function to learning a continuous denoising vector field in R^n that is integrated by an ODE solver, with parity constraints injected as a conditioning signal at each step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically relevant contribution is the demonstration that a time-unconditional score-based decoder can match or exceed time-conditional baselines without requiring SNR estimation at inference, and that the solver step budget provides a clean latency-accuracy knob without retraining. The magnitude-only ablation (Table 4) is a strong negative result: replacing signed y with |y| causes the network to collapse to a near-zero predictor, suggesting the standard reliability-based preprocessing paradigm may be fundamentally incompatible with continuous denoising objectives. Open questions include whether the signed-input advantage persists at much longer block lengths (the paper shows some LDPC(529,440) results but the gap narrows), whether the approach extends to non-linear codes or non-AWGN channels beyond the Rayleigh robustness check, and whether the modest 0.17 dB average gain scales with model capacity or training data.
