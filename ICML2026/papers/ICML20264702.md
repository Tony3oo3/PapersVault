## Problem

In non-parametric meta-RL settings (e.g., Meta-World ML-10/ML-45), tasks differ in both reward structure and physical dynamics, causing vastly different return scales that induce severe gradient interference during joint optimization. Existing categorical approaches (e.g., AMAGO-2) attempt to normalize scales via discretization but suffer from rigid binning and quantization errors, while prior context-based methods largely ignore the resulting training imbalance, overemphasizing high-return tasks and under-learning difficult ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (Cartesian positions of end-effector, objects, and goals in a simulated Sawyer robot environment) and context trajectory segments of horizon H (sequences of (s, a, r, s') tuples) to produce a 5-dimensional task representation via a GRU-based VAE encoder. The meta-value is not a scalar but a distribution represented as N adaptive floating particles, and the policy outputs 3D end-effector position actions conditioned on both the current state and the inferred task representation. During meta-testing, the agent adapts over 10 rollout episodes (500 steps each) without any prior knowledge of the test task.

## Method

RTC combines two complementary modules: (1) an entropy-regularized Sinkhorn TD update that models meta-values as adaptive floating particles rather than fixed categorical bins, computing the optimal transport cost between the current value distribution and the bootstrapped TD target to yield a geometry-aware, return-scale-invariant learning signal; and (2) a recursive error correction mechanism where an auxiliary network δ_ψ learns the temporal accumulation of Bellman inconsistencies via a Bellman-style recursion (δ_k = C(v_k, Γ^π v_{k-1}) + γ P^{π_{k-1}} δ_{k-1}), producing adaptive importance weights w_k ∝ exp(−γ[P^{π_{k-1}} δ_{k-1}](s,z)/β) that reweight the Sinkhorn TD loss to prioritize transitions critical for estimation accuracy. The paper provides a theoretical error propagation bound (Lemma 4.1) and proves that reweighting by the estimated error reduces meta-value estimation error (Theorem 4.2). The policy is updated via PPO, and the context encoder is trained with a standard VAE objective (reconstruction + KL divergence), following the variBAD formulation.

## Results

On Meta-World ML-10, RTC achieves a test success rate of 30.42% and test return of 1394.75, outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging ML-45 protocol, RTC reaches 28.66% test success rate and 757.95 test return, again leading all competitors. RTC also exhibits the lowest variance across 4 random seeds (e.g., ±2.02% on ML-45 test success rate vs. ±5.16% for AMAGO-2), indicating more stable convergence. The method surpasses the strongest baseline (variBAD) by 2.03% on ML-10 test success rate and 5.39% on ML-45 test success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTC outperforms all four baselines on every test metric across both ML-10 and ML-45. Against the closest competitor variBAD, RTC gains +2.03% test success rate on ML-10 (30.42% vs. 28.39%) and +5.39% on ML-45 (28.66% vs. 23.27%), with correspondingly larger return improvements (+319 on ML-10, +102 on ML-45). AMAGO-2 achieves the highest training success rate (98.44% on ML-10) but suffers the worst test performance (10.89% test SR on ML-10), revealing a severe train-test gap attributed to its rigid categorical discretization of the value space. LDM performs best among baselines on ML-45 test success rate (26.57%) but still trails RTC by 2.09%, while SDVT leads on ML-10 test return among baselines (978.93) yet remains 416 points below RTC. The consistent margin across both protocols and both metrics (success rate and return) suggests RTC's Sinkhorn-based distributional alignment plus error correction provides a robust advantage over both subtask-decomposition (SDVT), virtual-task (LDM), Bayesian (variBAD), and categorical (AMAGO-2) approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTC sits at the intersection of three research threads: context-based meta-RL (variBAD's VAE-based task inference), distributional RL (Bellemare's C51 and its quantile extensions), and entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn divergence from Genevay/Feydy). Its closest predecessor is AMAGO-2, which first identified return-scale imbalance in non-parametric meta-RL and addressed it via categorical discretization of the value space; RTC departs from this by replacing fixed-support bins with floating-particle Sinkhorn distributions, avoiding quantization error while preserving geometry-aware alignment. The recursive error correction module draws on DISCOR (Kumar et al.) for importance-weighted distribution correction, but applies it to the temporal accumulation of Sinkhorn TD errors rather than state-visit distribution mismatch, yielding a meta-value-specific reweighting scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored optimization pathology in non-parametric meta-RL—gradient interference from heterogeneous return scales—that prior methods either ignore or handle with lossy discretization. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.2) that error-aware reweighting provably reduces meta-value estimation error gives the approach a principled foundation beyond heuristic tuning. However, evaluation is confined to Meta-World's robotic manipulation tasks with a 5-dimensional task representation, leaving open whether the Sinkhorn-based approach scales to larger task distributions, higher-dimensional contexts, or continuous control domains beyond manipulation. The added computational cost of iterative Sinkhorn updates plus a separate error-model network also raises practical deployment questions.
