## Problem

Auto-regressive mesh generation models produce topologically precise meshes but require thousands to tens of thousands of sequential next-token predictions per mesh, resulting in substantial inference latency. While speculative decoding has accelerated LLM inference, it relies on a separate draft model whose parameter count and distributional alignment are hard to satisfy; mesh generation's much smaller vocabulary (~5K vs. ~128K+ for LLMs) suggests a lighter-weight alternative is possible, but no prior work has explored this for 3D mesh synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (obtained from scanned data or sampled from diffusion-generated mesh surfaces) as the generation condition, which is injected via cross-attention throughout the backbone transformer. The output is a 3D triangle mesh (vertices and faces) reconstructed by detokenizing the generated sequence using BPT's irregular compressed serialization (block indices, offset indices, and patchify operations over a ~5120-token vocabulary). The decoding heads operate on the backbone's final hidden state at each step, predicting D+1 tokens in parallel rather than one at a time.

## Method

XSpecMesh attaches D lightweight cross-attention decoding heads to the backbone's final hidden layer, where the d-th head predicts the (d+1)-th next token in a single forward pass, replacing the sequential next-token loop. A verification-and-resampling step uses the backbone's causal-masked forward pass to check each candidate token against a probability threshold δ, accepting a valid prefix and resampling from the first rejected position. Training proceeds in two stages: first, decoding heads are trained via cross-entropy distillation from backbone-generated sequences (with a decaying weight w(d)=0.8^d to stabilize gradients for later heads); second, LoRA adapters on the backbone's linear layers are jointly trained with the heads under a heavily weighted backbone loss (λ=50) to align multi-token prediction distributions without drifting the backbone. Cross-attention to the generation condition (rather than a plain MLP) is critical for accurate multi-token prediction, and LoRA's low-rank updates prevent the severe parameter drift that full fine-tuning would cause, while merging at inference incurs zero extra cost.

## Results

On the 200-mesh test set, XSpecMesh (built on BPT) achieves CD 0.1168 and HD 0.2261, nearly matching the base model BPT (CD 0.1165, HD 0.2223) while reducing average generation latency from 257.6 s to 151.4 s — a 1.71× speedup. In the user study, participants could not distinguish XSpecMesh outputs from BPT (36% vs. 37% preference). Compared to DeepMesh (0.5B), XSpecMesh improves CD by 12% (0.1168 vs. 0.1323), HD by 14% (0.2261 vs. 0.2648), and is 6.5× faster (151.4 s vs. 979.6 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BPT (the backbone model), XSpecMesh matches generation quality almost exactly (CD 0.1168 vs. 0.1165; HD 0.2261 vs. 0.2223; user-study preference 36% vs. 37%) while cutting inference time by 41% (151.4 s vs. 257.6 s), confirming the acceleration is quality-preserving. Against DeepMesh (0.5B), XSpecMesh wins on every metric: lower CD (0.1168 vs. 0.1323), lower HD (0.2261 vs. 0.2648), higher user preference (36% vs. 27%), and 6.5× lower latency (151.4 s vs. 979.6 s), as DeepMesh's truncated-window training produces fragmented meshes. In the broader comparison (Table 5), XSpecMesh outperforms MeshAnythingV2 (CD 0.1168 vs. 0.1708; HD 0.2261 vs. 0.3574) and TreeMeshGPT (CD 0.1168 vs. 0.1871; HD 0.2261 vs. 0.3425) on both geometric fidelity and speed. Against MeshSilk, XSpecMesh has a slightly worse CD (0.1168 vs. 0.1111) but better HD (0.2261 vs. 0.2293) and is nearly 2× faster (151.4 s vs. 294.8 s). On topology metrics (Table 6), XSpecMesh matches BPT's watertight and two-manifold ratios (both 0.04) while achieving lower boundary-edge (0.0320 vs. 0.0362) and non-manifold-edge (0.0052 vs. 0.0076) ratios, confirming no topological degradation from acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the speculative decoding paradigm from LLM inference (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) and the multi-head decoding idea from Medusa (Cai et al., 2024) to the domain of auto-regressive 3D mesh generation. Rather than training a separate draft model, it attaches lightweight cross-attention decoding heads to the backbone transformer (BPT, Weng et al., 2025) and uses LoRA fine-tuning to enable multi-token prediction from the backbone's hidden states. The key conceptual departure from standard speculative decoding is the use of multiple heads sharing a single backbone forward pass, combined with a probability-threshold verification criterion, motivated by the much smaller vocabulary of mesh tokenization compared to language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Auto-regressive mesh generation requires thousands to tens of thousands of sequential forward passes, making it impractical for interactive or large-scale 3D content pipelines; a 1.7× speedup with no measurable quality loss is a practical win for this setting. However, the paper itself notes the speedup is well below the 2–3× gains seen in LLM speculative decoding, attributing this to smaller model sizes, stronger local token dependencies in mesh serialization, and compact representations that leave less redundancy to exploit. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger mesh backbones, whether the verification threshold can be made adaptive per position, and whether the fundamental topological validity issues (watertightness, manifoldness) that affect all current auto-regressive mesh generators can be addressed jointly with acceleration.
