## Problem

Continuous-depth neural networks (CDNNs) such as Neural ODEs and Liquid Time-Constant Networks require iterative integration of H nonlinear ODEs at every training and inference step, creating a fundamental computational bottleneck that limits their deployment in large-scale and edge settings. Existing acceleration techniques either sacrifice accuracy (model order reduction, quantization), are restricted to a single CDNN family (neural flows only address NODE), or fail to reduce training time (MOR only accelerates inference). No prior method provides an accuracy-preserving, training-time-reducing, architecture-level transformation that is universally applicable across NODE, LTC-NN, and general continuous-depth latent variable layers.

## Input / Output

CoDA is a drop-in layer replacement: it accepts the same N-dimensional input sequence i(t) as the original H-cell CDLV layer and produces an equivalent H-dimensional hidden state h^C(t), so downstream layers and loss functions remain unchanged. The key architectural constraint is P ≪ H (e.g., P=16 for H=1024 in LFM2-1.2B), where P is the number of GRU cells and the dimensionality of the linear ODE. The transformation is applied per CDLV layer and can be stacked across multiple layers with unified backpropagation, and the matrix exponential is computed once per forward pass over the full integration horizon N_s·τ rather than at each ODE step.

## Method

CoDA defines an H-P transformation that replaces each H-cell CDLV layer with three components: (1) a P+1=2R+1 GRU module with weight configurations derived from Theorem F.1, where Block 1 (R cells) computes the linear observable dynamics, Block 2 (R cells) computes the implicit-dynamics convolution via a time-tracking cell that encodes exponential decay into the reset gate, and a single cell tracks time; (2) a single P-dimensional linear ODE dh/dt = Ah solved by matrix exponentiation Φ = exp(AN_s τ) computed via Padé approximation, replacing H sequential nonlinear ODE integrations; and (3) a P×H dense layer that recovers the original H-dimensional outputs through Dynamic Mode Decomposition. The theoretical foundation rests on the Mori-Zwanzig/Koopman decomposition, which shows the CDLV forward pass splits into a linear observable part and a nonlinear convolution of observables with an exponential kernel; Theorem F.1 proves the GRU forward pass belongs to the same function class (nonlinear polynomial convolved with exponential), yielding identical Rademacher complexity and hence PAC learnability. This reduces per-token cost from O(N_s·H²) to O(HP + P² + PH) and eliminates the sequential dependency of iterative ODE solvers, enabling full parallelization and hardware-friendly kernel fusion.

## Results

On the Liquid Foundation Model (LFM2-1.2B, H=1024 LTC-NN layers), CoDA (P=16) achieves a 6.7× training speedup (567 s vs. 3816 s total) and 1.8× inference speedup (19.24 s vs. 34.65 s) with statistically equivalent test loss (4.02 vs. 4.13) and 4.5× lower energy per epoch (1.79 J vs. 8.0 J). Across six application datasets, CoDA consistently achieves the lowest or near-lowest inference time (≤0.31 s/epoch) and training time while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy. On MNIST, CoDA (H=512, P=16) reaches 92% test accuracy at 0.03 s test time versus NODE-MOR(500)'s 93% at 0.09 s, while also reducing training time per epoch from 17.4 s to 13.3 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LTC-NN, CoDA achieves dramatic training-time reductions (e.g., power: 4 s vs. 210 s/epoch; occupancy: 0.5 s vs. 18.4 s/epoch) and lower energy (340 J vs. 16,387 J on power), while matching or beating test loss in 5 of 6 tasks. Against NODE, CoDA is 1–2 orders of magnitude faster in both training and inference (e.g., occupancy inference: 0.31 s vs. 27.15 s) with equal or better accuracy. Against GRUFlow (GRUF), CoDA wins on training time in most settings and matches inference speed, but GRUF occasionally achieves slightly lower test loss (e.g., power: 0.0022 vs. 0.03). Against NODE-MOR on MNIST, CoDA trades 1% test accuracy (92% vs. 93%) for a 3× inference speedup (0.03 s vs. 0.09 s) and a 24% training-time reduction (13.3 s vs. 17.4 s/epoch)—an advantage NODE-MOR cannot offer since it only accelerates inference. Against NODE-VF on the VF-NODE-ICLR2025 dataset, CoDA(P=16) achieves both better accuracy (2.72×10⁻³ vs. 3.54×10⁻²) and 10× faster training (0.0178 s vs. 0.1894 s/epoch).

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDA extends the GRU-based ODE-bypass idea from Neural Flows (Biloš et al., 2021), which was restricted to NODE, to all continuous-depth latent variable (CDLV) layers including LTC-NN and CT-RNN, by grounding the equivalence in Koopman operator theory (Brunton et al., 2022) and its Mori-Zwanzig formalism (Lin et al., 2021). The H-P transformation decomposes the nonlinear ODE dynamics into a linear Koopman component (solved via matrix exponentiation) and an implicit interaction component (approximated by a constrained GRU), with Dynamic Mode Decomposition (Kaiser et al., 2018) recovering the full H-dimensional output. Unlike model order reduction (Lehtimäki et al., 2022) which sacrifices accuracy, or variational formulations (Zhao et al., 2025) which modify the loss function, CoDA is an architecture-level replacement with a PAC learnability guarantee, making it the first accuracy-preserving, training-time-reducing transformation applicable across all CDNN families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.7× training speedup on the 1.2B-parameter Liquid Foundation Model without accuracy loss is practically significant for teams deploying CDNN-based foundation models where iterative ODE solving is the dominant bottleneck. The PAC learnability proof via Rademacher complexity preservation provides a theoretical foundation that the H-P transformation is not merely an approximation but a learnable equivalent, which is important for safety-critical applications (e.g., the AID insulin pump use case). Open questions include whether CoDA composes cleanly with quantization or sparsification (the paper notes this but does not explore it), whether the P ≪ H regime holds for arbitrarily deep or high-dimensional CDNNs, and how the approach scales beyond the 1.2B model size tested here.
