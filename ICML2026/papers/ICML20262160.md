## Problem

Estimating the treatment effect function (TEF) for continuous treatments in observational data is confounded by covariate-dependent treatment assignment, making direct regression biased. Existing debiasing methods require estimating the conditional treatment density in strong norms (L⁴ or L∞), a major bottleneck in high-dimensional or weak-overlap settings. This paper seeks minimax-optimal rates that adapt to the typically simpler structure of the TEF while entirely bypassing conditional-density estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an i.i.d. observational dataset of (covariates x ∈ ℝ^q, treatment a ∈ A, outcome y) along with a user-specified reference distribution P_ref on the treatment domain (defining the MISE metric) and a sampling distribution P_samp for probing the TEF across A. The output is a function estimate ĥ_λ in a target RKHS H over the treatment domain, evaluated by integrated squared error under P_ref rather than pointwise predictions. The nuisance regularizer λ₀ is set by theory (≈ n⁻¹ log n), while the main regularizer λ is selected data-drivenly via a proxy-validation procedure.

## Method

The estimator is a two-stage kernel ridge regression that decouples the complex conditional response surface f*(x,a) from the simpler marginal TEF h*(a). Stage 1 fits KRR on the joint space Z = X × A to recover f*; Stage 2 constructs pseudo-outcomes by averaging the fitted nuisance over the empirical covariate distribution at n sampled treatment points, then regresses these pseudo-outcomes on treatment in a second, simpler RKHS H. The key insight is that h* is a marginal average and thus lower-dimensional, so the leading bias-variance rate is governed by the spectral decay of H's kernel integral operator, while the nuisance f* enters only through a parametric-order term ‖f*‖²_F/(γn). A fully data-driven model selection procedure (proxy validation via data splitting) automatically selects the optimal regularizer, adapting to unknown overlap degree γ and kernel spectral decay without manual tuning.

## Results

On the semi-real Job Corps benchmark (n=4024, 40 covariates), the proposed two-stage KRR estimator achieves a MISE of 1.25 (SE 0.12), outperforming all DML variants from Colangelo & Lee (2026) by 40–58% (DML-GRF: 2.42, DML-NN: 2.11, DML-LASSO: 2.87, DML-KNN: 2.97). On the synthetic confounded benchmark, the method reduces MISE from 9.87 (Plug-in KRR) and 14.52 (Direct Regression) to 6.22 at n=1000. The theoretical contribution establishes minimax-optimal rates of Õ((γn)^{-2β/(1+2β)}) for Sobolev-class TEFs, with the leading rate governed by the target space complexity rather than the nuisance space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the DML estimators of Colangelo & Lee (2026), which use orthogonalization with four different nuisance learners (GRF, NN, LASSO, KNN). The proposed two-stage KRR method achieves the lowest MISE (1.25) across all variants, with the largest margin against DML-KNN (2.97, a 58% reduction) and the smallest against DML-NN (2.11, a 41% reduction). Notably, the ground-truth curve in the semi-real benchmark is constructed from a GRF-fitted response surface, yet the kernel-based method still outperforms DML-GRF (1.25 vs. 2.42), suggesting the second-stage smoothing step recovers TEF structure even under nuisance model misspecification. The method also consistently beats the simpler Plug-in KRR baseline (1.62) and Direct Regression (1.70), confirming that the two-stage pseudo-outcome construction and second-stage smoothing are both necessary. On the synthetic benchmark, the advantage over Plug-in KRR is 37% (6.22 vs. 9.87) and over Direct Regression is 57% (6.22 vs. 14.52) at n=1000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DML/debiasing paradigm for continuous treatment effects (Kennedy et al., 2017; Colangelo & Lee, 2026; Bonvini & Kennedy, 2022), which requires estimating the conditional treatment density in strong norms (L4 or L∞) to control second-order remainders. It also distinguishes itself from the closest RKHS-based competitor, Singh et al. (2024), whose tensor-product kernel analysis ties convergence rates to the eigendecay of the full joint nuisance space rather than the simpler treatment-only target space. The two-stage pseudo-outcome construction draws a conceptual parallel to semi-supervised transfer regression (Liu et al., 2023; Xia & Wainwright, 2024), but the causal identification structure and the adaptation to overlap/spectral decay are specific to the treatment-effect setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that practitioners working with high-dimensional or poorly-behaved covariates no longer need to estimate a conditional treatment density—a step that is notoriously unstable under weak overlap and model misspecification. The minimax-optimal rates governed by the target TEF space (rather than the nuisance space) mean that even when the conditional response is extremely complex (e.g., an NTK), the dose-response curve can still be estimated at a fast rate. A significant limitation is the requirement of a correctly specified outcome model; the paper explicitly acknowledges that robustness to misspecification (e.g., via doubly-robust kernel estimators) remains an open direction, as does extending the decoupling strategy beyond RKHS to general function classes like deep networks.
