## Problem

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) used as a cold-start for reinforcement learning often causes catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge, degrading out-of-distribution generalization and limiting downstream RL performance. The paper identifies that this forgetting is driven by a small subset of low-probability (off-policy) tokens in expert data that produce disproportionately large gradient norms under the log-likelihood objective, and asks whether selectively constraining these updates can yield a more generalizable RL initialization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of expert demonstration pairs (x, y*) from agentic environments (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld) along with the parameters of a base LLM (Qwen2.5-7B/1.5B-Instruct or Llama3.2-3B-Instruct). It produces a fine-tuned cold-start policy that is then further trained with GRPO for reinforcement learning. A key operational detail is the periodic refresh of the old policy π_θ_old every 256 training samples by default, which adapts the trust region as the model learns rather than anchoring to the initial base model throughout training.

## Method

OPC-SFT (Off-Policy token Clipped SFT) adapts PPO's clipped objective to the supervised setting by computing a per-token probability ratio r(θ) = π_θ(y*|x) / π_θ_old(y*|x) and replacing the standard negative log-likelihood with min(r(θ), clip(r(θ), 1−ε, 1+ε)), with the advantage fixed at 1 so all target tokens receive equal weight. The key insight is that tokens the base model assigns very low probability (e.g., ~10⁻⁴) produce gradient norms ~16× larger than high-confidence tokens, and clipping zeros out the gradient whenever r(θ) > 1+ε, thereby suppressing the most destructive early updates. A theoretical margin analysis shows that while reaching high confidence on an off-policy token requires a total correction of Θ(log(1/δ)), OPC-SFT restricts per-interval correction to O(log(1+ε)), bounding representational drift. The approach is validated on agentic benchmarks where OPC-SFT reduces OOD forgetting by 11.54% and improves final RL success rate by 7.09% over standard SFT, with PCA shift analysis confirming smaller latent-space divergence.

## Results

OPC-SFT reduces out-of-distribution forgetting by 11.54% and improves final RL performance by 7.09% across agentic benchmarks compared to standard SFT. On the ultimate RL metric (Table 3), OPC-SFT+GRPO achieves 77.70% average success rate on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 79.55% on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, and 81.49% on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, outperforming SFT+GRPO (69.97%, 74.27%, 79.02%) respectively. For OOD knowledge retention (e.g., MBPP on Qwen2.5-7B after ALFWorld SFT), OPC-SFT preserves 78.84% accuracy versus 71.69% for SFT (base: 79.68%), indicating substantially less catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPC-SFT consistently outperforms both baselines across all three model backbones on the final RL metric. Against DFT (token-probability-rescaled SFT), OPC-SFT+GRPO achieves 77.70% vs 72.84% average on Qwen2.5-7B and 81.49% vs 72.57% on Llama3.2-3B, with the largest gap on ALFWorld unseen (91.04% vs 80.06% and 92.54% vs 76.87%). Against NEFTune (embedding noise regularization), the advantage is even more pronounced: 77.70% vs 63.85% on Qwen2.5-7B and 81.49% vs 59.28% on Llama3.2-3B, where NEFTune suffers severe ALFWorld unseen degradation (59.70% and 45.52%). On OOD retention (Table 2), OPC-SFT also leads: for Qwen2.5-7B after ALFWorld SFT, it preserves MBPP at 78.84% (vs DFT 75.13%, NEFT 72.22%, SFT 71.69%) and MMLU at 70.60% (vs DFT 70.20%, NEFT 70.10%, SFT 66.30%). On the cold-start metric before RL (Table 1), OPC-SFT achieves the highest average success rate across all backbones (68.61%, 67.38%, 71.24% vs SFT's 64.17%, 62.20%, 64.04%), confirming that the clipping strategy improves both in-domain adaptation and generalization simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the PPO clipping mechanism (Schulman et al., 2017) from reinforcement learning into the supervised fine-tuning objective, creating OPC-SFT as a trust-region constraint on per-token updates during SFT. It is positioned against two concurrent baselines for improving SFT robustness: DFT (Wu et al., 2025b), which rescales the SFT loss by token probability, and NEFTune (Jain et al., 2023), which adds embedding noise. The key conceptual departure from prior SFT work is the diagnosis that a small subset of low-probability (off-policy) tokens, identified via their disproportionately large gradient norms, is the primary driver of catastrophic forgetting, and that selectively bounding their updates—rather than uniformly reducing learning rate or adding regularization—preserves generalization while still fitting the expert data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPC-SFT offers a drop-in, hyperparameter-light modification to standard SFT that consistently reduces OOD forgetting by ~11.5% and improves downstream RL (GRPO) performance by ~7% across three model backbones and two agentic environments, making it a practical tool for the widely adopted SFT-then-RL pipeline. A key limitation is that benefits are most pronounced when the fine-tuning data is highly off-policy (e.g., novel action formats in agentic tasks); for math reasoning, where the base model already has strong priors, gains are modest. Open questions include whether the optimal clipping ratio ε can be adapted dynamically per token or per training phase, and whether the approach extends to multi-task or multi-domain SFT settings where off-policy tokens arise from heterogeneous sources.
