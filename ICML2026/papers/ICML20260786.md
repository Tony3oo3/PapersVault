## Problem

Bayesian binary neural networks with mean-field Bernoulli posteriors suffer from posterior saturation on long non-stationary streams: natural parameters grow unboundedly, epistemic uncertainty collapses, and weight sign changes become nearly impossible, effectively freezing plasticity. This failure mode is compounded on edge devices where weight updates are expensive and data streams are strongly imbalanced, yet no existing binary Bayesian continual-learning rule prevents saturation while preserving actionable uncertainty for selective querying.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single images (MNIST/Fashion-MNIST at 28×28, OpenLORIS-Object at standard VGG19 resolution) or pre-extracted frozen VGG19 features (25,088-dim, optionally subsampled to 8,192 or 1,024 dims) in a strict one-pass, batch-size-1 streaming protocol with no replay buffer. For each incoming sample it produces a class prediction, epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty scores via K Monte Carlo forward passes over sampled binary weights, and a binary query/skip decision based on a fixed variation-ratio threshold; only queried samples trigger backpropagation and a BiMU weight update.

## Method

BiMU derives a per-synapse update for Bernoulli natural parameters λ from a bounded-memory variational objective that balances a data-fitting term, a KL-to-previous-posterior stability term, and a KL-to-prior forgetting term over an effective window of N updates. The resulting update (Eq. 6) combines a gradient-driven plasticity term with an uncertainty-gated relaxation toward the prior, where the gate 1/cosh²(λ) equals the Bernoulli variance so the pull is strongest for uncertain synapses and vanishes as |λ|→∞, directly preventing saturation. A bounded metaplastic step size (Eq. 7) replaces the intractable second-order curvature with a first-order surrogate that guarantees 0 < η ≤ α_max while creating a consolidation/de-consolidation asymmetry: steps reinforcing the current weight sign take full size, while opposing steps are inhibited. For active continual learning, BiMU uses K Monte Carlo binary-weight samples to compute a variation-ratio disagreement score on-the-fly; an incoming example is labeled and triggers a BiMU update only if this score exceeds a fixed threshold, yielding buffer-free one-pass querying that concentrates updates on informative, under-represented samples. Gradients through the discrete Bernoulli weights are estimated via the Concrete (Gumbel-softmax) relaxation, averaged over K samples.

## Results

On 1000-tasks Permuted-MNIST (100-unit MLP, fully online, no replay), BiMU achieves 90.30% mean accuracy over the last 5 tasks, 0.99 OOD AUC (Permuted-MNIST vs. Fashion-MNIST), and MMRR of 139.47, while all other binary baselines collapse (BayesBiNN 41.12%, STE 29.35%, Synaptic Metaplasticity 10.27%). On OpenLORIS-Object with frozen VGG19 features subsampled to 8,192 dims, BiMU reaches 89.19% mean accuracy across 12 tasks and, under class imbalance with one-pass active querying, achieves 88.70% accuracy while updating on only 3.1% of the stream (a 32× reduction in labels and gradient updates vs. the 100%-update baseline of 87.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiMU decisively outperforms all binary-weight baselines on 1000-tasks Permuted-MNIST: it reaches 90.30% late-stream accuracy versus 41.12% for BayesBiNN, 29.35% for STE, and 10.27% for Synaptic Metaplasticity, with OOD AUC of 0.99 versus 0.57, 0.69, and uninformative uncertainty respectively. Against real-valued baselines, BiMU trails MESU by 1.4 points in accuracy (90.30% vs. 91.69%) but surpasses it in OOD AUC (0.99 vs. 0.95) while using a compact binary posterior; it clearly beats EWC Online (81.78%), Synaptic Intelligence (74.41%), and SGD (66.64%). On OpenLORIS-Object (8,192 frozen VGG19 features), BiMU leads all binary methods (89.19% vs. 86.93% BayesBiNN, 88.03% Syn. Meta., 79.12% STE) and slightly exceeds real-valued baselines (MESU 87.01%, EWC Online 87.18%, SI 86.44%, SGD 86.27%). Under active continual learning with class imbalance, BiMU's variation-ratio querying achieves 88.70% at 3.1% update budget, outperforming MESU and BayesBiNN at matched queried-label fractions, while BayesBiNN's posterior disagreement is poorly calibrated and often underperforms random querying.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiMU is a direct specialization of the bounded-memory Bayesian learning-and-forgetting framework of Bonnet et al. (2025, MESU) from real-valued Gaussian posteriors to mean-field Bernoulli synapses, addressing the specific failure mode of posterior saturation that arises when the same principle is naively applied to binary weights. It also extends the BayesBiNN training rule (Meng et al., 2020) by adding controlled forgetting and a state-dependent metaplastic step size, whereas BayesBiNN performs cumulative variational updates without any forgetting mechanism. The active-learning component repurposes the variation ratio (Freeman, 1965) as a one-pass, buffer-free querying criterion, departing from pool-based active learning that requires storing and ranking unlabeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiMU provides a practical route to sustained online adaptation on energy-constrained edge devices by combining binary-weight efficiency with calibrated epistemic uncertainty, enabling both OOD monitoring and selective supervision without replay buffers or task-boundary signals. The 32× reduction in label and gradient-update cost under class imbalance is directly relevant to always-on deployments where annotation and backpropagation are the dominant costs. Open questions include whether the bounded-memory window N and metaplastic step-size surrogate transfer to deeper binary architectures (experiments use a 100-unit MLP or linear heads on frozen features), whether the approach handles pure labeling-function shifts (where p(x) is unchanged but p(y|x) changes), and how the fixed query threshold τ interacts with non-stationary uncertainty calibration over very long streams.
