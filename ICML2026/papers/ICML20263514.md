## Problem

Geometric deep learning has produced strong empirical results by encoding symmetry priors into network architectures, but the field lacks non-asymptotic statistical learning guarantees for equivariant regression and symmetry-aware conditional probability estimation. This paper bridges spectral contrastive learning (operator-theoretic approximation of the conditional expectation operator) with geometric deep learning, asking how equivariant architectures should be trained so that their learned features reveal conditional distributions with provable sample-complexity bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired vector-valued observations (x, y) that are assumed to be G-invariant under a known finite symmetry group, and produces a disentangled equivariant representation decomposed into orthogonal isotypic components plus a block-diagonal equivariant operator matrix. From this learned model, it outputs G-equivariant regression estimates, G-invariant conditional probabilities, and calibrated confidence intervals (e.g., 90% CIs for ground reaction forces, work, and kinetic energy). A key structural constraint is that each isotypic block of the operator must have singular-space dimension at least d_k, the minimal dimension of the k-th irreducible representation of the quotient group G^(k).

## Method

The core insight is that G-invariance of the Pointwise Mutual Dependency kernel makes the conditional expectation operator E_{y|x} a G-equivariant linear operator, which by Shur's lemma decomposes into a direct sum of operators acting on isotypic subspaces. The eNCP architecture uses any G-equivariant backbone (MLP, CNN, GNN, Transformer) followed by a non-learnable orthogonal change-of-basis into isotypic coordinates, and parameterizes the truncated operator as a block-diagonal matrix where each block is a G^(k)-equivariant matrix approximating the restriction of E_{y|x} to the k-th isotypic subspace. Training uses a decomposed CLoRa contrastive loss that separates into n_iso independent sub-problems (one per isotypic component), with U-statistics for unbiased estimation and symmetry-aware orthonormality regularization exploiting isotypic orthogonality. This yields the first non-asymptotic learning guarantees for equivariant regression and symmetry-aware conditional probability estimation, where the effective sample size is boosted by d_iso (the sum of irreducible representation dimensions) relative to the symmetry-agnostic NCP baseline.

## Results

On the quadruped locomotion uncertainty quantification task (target 90% coverage), eNCP achieves 95.0±0.4% empirical coverage with a set-size of 4.3×10⁹, while NCP reaches only 56.9% coverage (set-size 2.6×10¹⁰), eCQR 6.7% (set-size 1.7×10⁷), and CQR 8.5% (set-size 1.4×10⁸). On the CoM momenta regression task, eNCP outperforms NCP, MLP, and eMLP in both test MSE and sample efficiency (Fig. 1). On synthetic cGMM benchmarks across four symmetry groups (C₂, C₆, D₆, I_h), eNCP consistently achieves lower PMD MSE than NCP, DRF, and iDRF at all sample sizes (Fig. 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

eNCP decisively outperforms all three baselines on the uncertainty quantification task. Against NCP (Kostic et al., 2024b), eNCP achieves 95.0% coverage versus NCP's 56.9%—a 38-point gap—while also reducing the set-size by roughly 6×, indicating far tighter and better-calibrated intervals. Against CQR (Feldman et al., 2023) and its equivariant variant eCQR, the gap is even more dramatic: eNCP's 95.0% coverage dwarfs CQR's 8.5% and eCQR's 6.7%, meaning the quantile-regression baselines produce intervals that cover almost none of the true values under transient disturbances. On the CoM momenta regression task (Fig. 1), eNCP beats both NCP and the equivariant MLP (eMLP) in test MSE at every training-set size, and also surpasses the standard MLP, demonstrating that the symmetry-aware contrastive objective mitigates NCP's known sample-efficiency disadvantage. On the cGMM PMD approximation benchmarks (Fig. 4), eNCP achieves lower MSE than DRF (Tsai et al., 2020), its invariant adaptation iDRF, and NCP across all tested symmetry groups and dimensionalities, confirming that architectural equivariance constraints yield both accuracy and sample-efficiency gains over symmetry-agnostic or loss-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Conditional Probability (NCP) framework (Kostic et al., 2024b), which uses contrastive learning to approximate the spectral decomposition of the conditional expectation operator, by incorporating symmetry priors from Geometric Deep Learning. The key conceptual move is showing that the isotypic decomposition of the conditional expectation operator (via Schur's lemma) naturally induces a block-diagonal, disentangled architecture where each block is equivariant to a quotient group. This bridges two fields rarely studied together—spectral contrastive representation learning and equivariant neural networks—departing from prior equivariant contrastive learning work (Dangovski et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024b) by targeting regression and uncertainty quantification rather than classification, and by providing non-asymptotic statistical guarantees rather than purely empirical results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first non-asymptotic statistical learning guarantees for equivariant regression and symmetry-aware conditional probability estimation, showing that equivariant disentangled representations increase the effective sample size by a factor of d_iso (the sum of irreducible representation dimensions). This provides a principled, quantitative explanation for why symmetry priors improve generalization in GDL, moving beyond empirical observation. A key limitation is the requirement for fully specified symmetry priors; accommodating partial or misspecified symmetries, and statistically testing for their presence in data, remain open. The robotics experiments (quadruped locomotion with ground reaction force estimation) demonstrate practical relevance, but the framework's applicability is inherently bounded by the availability of well-characterized group actions in the target domain.
