## Problem

Task-vector-based model merging suffers from performance degradation on individual tasks, yet prior explanations focus narrowly on parameter-level conflicts (redundancy, sign conflicts) and most methods require costly validation data for hyperparameter tuning. This paper identifies that the loss of in-context learning (ICL) capability in the merged model strongly correlates with downstream performance degradation, and asks whether ICL preservation can serve as a validation-free optimization objective for merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained base model weights, N fine-tuned model weights (one per task), and the REGBench synthetic dataset (generated via finite-state machines, requiring no real validation data). It outputs the final merged model weights and the learned hierarchical refinement operators: an inter-layer weight matrix A ∈ R^{M×N}, a task scaling vector B ∈ R^{1×N}, and a global scalar C. The REGBench evaluation uses greedy-decoding accuracy over ~960 tokens per sample (D=20 samples, S=48 sequence length), making each fitness evaluation lightweight (~6.4 s on A100).

## Method

OPIC formulates model merging as a constrained optimization problem that maximizes ICL accuracy on REGBench, with the hierarchical refinement operators (A, B, C) as the decision variables. The refinement operator applies a broadcasted Hadamard product W = B ⊙ A to weight each task vector per layer, then globally scales the aggregated result by C, yielding θ_fused = θ + C·(Σ_k Σ_i W_{i,k}·τ_k^{(i)}). A Strengthen Elitist Genetic Algorithm (SEGA) with 20 generations and population size 15 searches over the operator parameters, using REGBench accuracy as the fitness function and Gaussian-noise mutation to avoid local optima. The framework is composable: it can be layered on top of existing methods (e.g., DARE, TIES) by applying the hierarchical scaling after their task-vector modifications, thereby replacing their validation-set-based grid search with the evolutionary ICL-driven optimization.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-1.5B, OPIC achieves a mean normalized performance of 80.73% across ANLI, COQA, MC_TACO, and MATHQA, outperforming all validation-dependent baselines (Task Arithmetic 79.56%, TIES 77.63%, DARE 78.32%, CABS 73.59%) and the only other validation-free method DOGE (69.62%) by 11.11% on average. The method also shows the smallest best-to-worst score gap (32.5%) among all methods, indicating superior robustness across tasks. On Qwen2.5-3B and Llama3.2-3B, OPIC maintains the top mean score (86.29% and 84.22% respectively), confirming generalization across model scales and architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPIC outperforms all five baselines on mean normalized score. Against Task Arithmetic (79.56%), the closest validation-dependent competitor, OPIC gains 1.17% while requiring no validation data. TIES (77.63%) and DARE (78.32%) both underperform even the simple Task Arithmetic baseline, which the authors attribute to the inadequacy of parameter-redundancy and direction-conflict explanations for merging degradation. CABS (73.59%) shows the weakest validation-dependent performance, particularly on ANLI (68.1%) and COQA (58.5%). The most striking comparison is against DOGE (69.62%), the only other validation-free method: OPIC improves by 11.11% on average, with the largest single-task gain of 30.1% on ANLI (97.5% vs. 67.4%). On the larger Qwen2.5-3B model, OPIC (86.29%) edges out DARE (86.12%) and Task Arithmetic (86.02%), while on Llama3.2-3B it leads TIES (83.81%) and DARE (82.56%) by 0.4–1.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPIC builds directly on the task-vector merging paradigm introduced by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), where fine-tuned model differences are summed into the base model, and on REGBench (Akyürek et al., 2024) as a synthetic benchmark for measuring in-context learning. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant parameter-level explanations of merging degradation (redundancy elimination in TIES/DARE, directional conflict resolution) by reframing the problem in terms of behavioral capability preservation—specifically ICL retention—as the optimization objective. The hierarchical refinement operator draws inspiration from AdaMerging's layer-wise weighting, while the evolutionary search (SEGA) replaces the grid-search hyperparameter tuning that plagues validation-dependent baselines like TIES, DARE, and CABS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that ICL preservation serves as a reliable proxy for downstream task performance, OPIC offers a practical pathway to eliminate the validation-data bottleneck that limits real-world deployment of merged models, particularly in privacy-sensitive or data-scarce settings. However, the evaluation is restricted to small models (1.5B–3B) and only four fine-tuning tasks, leaving open whether the ICL–performance correlation holds at larger scales or with more diverse task distributions. The 2–3× computational overhead over grid search, while modest, may become non-trivial for very large models, and the framework has not been tested in multimodal or many-task regimes.
