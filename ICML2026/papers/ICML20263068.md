## Problem

Targeted instruction tuning requires selecting pertinent samples from massive mixed candidate datasets guided by a small reference set, but existing selection methods either rely on expensive gradient computations (LESS, Grad-Sim), indirect embedding similarities (Rep-Sim, RDS+), or heuristic lexical overlap (BM25), none of which leverage the model's intrinsic data-understanding capability to directly quantify per-token information utility. The core gap is the lack of an efficient, target-aware mechanism that can distinguish high-quality, task-relevant data from noise through the model's own internal representations rather than post-hoc external metrics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale mixed candidate instruction dataset (e.g., 200k samples from Tulu-3) and a small reference dataset (e.g., 373 GSM8K or 1,221 CommonsenseQA samples) that reflects the target capability, plus a small randomly sampled warmup subset from the candidate pool. It produces a scalar quality score per candidate sample — the mean Information Retention Ratio (IRR) averaged across all tokens and all transformer layers — computed via a single forward pass, from which the top-K subset is extracted for downstream fine-tuning. Notably, the selector model (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B) and the target fine-tuning model (e.g., Qwen3-14B or Llama3-8B) can be architecturally different, enabling cross-model transfer of the curated data.

## Method

TarGATE inserts a lightweight gating module — a single learnable linear projection followed by a sigmoid — as a bypass before each FFN layer in a frozen transformer, producing a per-token Information Retention Ratio (IRR) that multiplicatively attenuates the FFN output. The gates are trained with a dual-objective strategy: a warmup regularization (L_warmup) penalizes high IRR on randomly sampled candidate data to suppress general noise, while a contrasting reference regularization (L_ref) penalizes low IRR on the reference set to enforce retention of target-aligned patterns, both combined with standard language modeling loss under a frozen backbone. A balance hyperparameter β controls the relative strength of these opposing forces, allowing the filter to be tuned from strict (low β, for reasoning tasks) to permissive (high β, for general knowledge). At inference time, a single forward pass through the gated model yields the mean IRR across all tokens and layers as the quality score, enabling top-K selection without any backward pass or gradient computation, achieving ~1.8× lower FLOPs than gradient-based baselines.

## Results

On the Real-World Massive Scenario (top-5% selection from 200k Tulu-3 samples), TarGATE with a Qwen3-0.6B selector achieves an average of 68.2% across CommonsenseQA, GSM8K, and MBPP on three target models (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-14B, Llama3-8B), surpassing Full Data fine-tuning (64.9%) and the strongest baseline RDS+ (66.6%). On the Synthetic Noisy Scenario (32k mixed samples with random-word noise), TarGATE reaches 66.5% average accuracy, outperforming Random (60.6%) and the best gradient-based baseline ToV (63.3%). TarGATE also achieves ~1.8× lower computational cost (424.9 PF) than gradient-based methods (776 PF) by requiring only a single forward pass for selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TarGATE consistently outperforms all six baselines across both scenarios. In the Real-World Massive Scenario, TarGATE (68.2% avg) beats the strongest baseline RDS+ (66.6%) by 1.6 points, Grad-Sim (66.2%) by 2.0 points, and LESS (65.7%) by 2.5 points; it also surpasses Full Data fine-tuning (64.9%) by 3.3 points, validating that curated subsets can exceed the full corpus. In the Synthetic Noisy Scenario, TarGATE (66.5%) leads ToV (63.3%) by 3.2 points and Random (60.6%) by 5.9 points, demonstrating robust noise filtering. TarGATE shows particular strength on code generation (MBPP: 57.2% vs. 55.2% for RDS+ and 54.8% for LESS) and maintains stable performance across all three target model architectures, whereas gradient-based methods like Grad-Sim degrade significantly on Llama3-8B GSM8K (45.7% vs. TarGATE's 58.2% with the 1.7B selector). The smaller 0.6B selector slightly outperforms the 1.7B selector (68.2% vs. 67.6%), and TarGATE's advantage is most pronounced on reasoning-intensive tasks where β=0.1 enforces strict reference alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

TarGATE repurposes the gating mechanism—previously used in architectures for representation learning (GLU, SwiGLU) or conditional routing (MoE)—as a data-centric probe that quantifies intrinsic sample utility via forward-pass activation. It departs from gradient-based influence methods (LESS, Grad-Sim) and embedding-based similarity methods (Rep-Sim, RDS+) by avoiding post-hoc gradient computation or external similarity metrics, instead learning a token-level Information Retention Ratio directly within the model's FFN pathway. The dual-objective training strategy (contrastive regularization between reference and warmup data) is novel in the data-selection context, borrowing the contrastive spirit of distribution-alignment work but applying it to internal gate activations rather than external representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TarGATE offers a practically attractive alternative to gradient-based selection: a single forward pass for scoring (1.8× cheaper than Grad-Sim/LESS) combined with strong cross-model transferability (0.6B selector curating data for 14B targets). The validation that 5% curated data outperforms the full 200k pool reinforces the 'less is more' paradigm with a concrete, efficient mechanism. However, the method's dependence on reference set quality, the preliminary nature of token-level gating analysis, and its focus on targeted (rather than general-purpose) filtering leave open questions about broader applicability and interpretability of the IRR signal.
