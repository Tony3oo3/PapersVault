## Problem

The finite volume method is the gold-standard discretization for nonlinear conservation laws due to its built-in conservation property, but it produces only point estimates without uncertainty quantification. GP-based probabilistic PDE solvers can express numerical uncertainty coherently, but naively scale cubically in discretization resolution, and prior scalable GP approaches either use collocation (losing conservation) or are restricted to linear PDEs. Expressing FVM as a scalable probabilistic numerical scheme for nonlinear flux operators has remained an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes PDE parameters (flux operator, source term), initial and boundary conditions, and optionally sparse noisy sensor measurements of the solution, all defined on a rectangular FVM grid. It produces a full joint posterior distribution over the solution, flux, and source fields simultaneously, yielding posterior means, marginal standard deviations, and posterior samples at every grid cell. The same framework handles forward and inverse problems identically by simply designating which fields are observed versus inferred; for inverse problems the source or flux is never directly observed and must be recovered entirely through the nonlinear PDE coupling.

## Method

The core insight is that FVM cell-integral and face-integral constraints are linear functionals of the solution, so the entire FVM discretization can be expressed as conditioning a GP prior on linear observations. For nonlinear fluxes (e.g., Burgers F=u²/2), the flux is given its own GP prior and the deterministic flux-solution coupling is enforced via a Gauss-Newton step with a Laplace approximation, treating the constraint as a virtual zero-valued observation. Scalability is achieved through three mechanisms: (1) a Vecchia/sparse Cholesky approximation extended to integral functionals, using a functional ordering (integrals → evaluations → derivatives) that exploits screening effects to achieve O(N^{3/2}) spatial factorization; (2) a Kronecker factorization of the joint spacetime precision into temporal (block-tridiagonal from the IWP) and spatial (sparse) components; and (3) a marginal moment-matching time-stepping scheme that propagates the filtering distribution by matching only marginal means and variances while preserving the sparse precision structure, yielding linear-in-time complexity without the dense fill-in of standard Kalman filtering.

## Results

On the 2D advection-diffusion source identification problem, GP-FVM achieves a source RMSE of 0.44 in 2 seconds, compared to a PINN baseline RMSE of 0.76 requiring ~5 minutes (150× speedup), while additionally providing calibrated marginal variances and posterior samples. On the 2D viscous Burgers forward problem, GP-FVM (minimal) achieves L² error within a factor of 2–3 of classical Crank-Nicolson FVM across grid sizes N∈{8,…,24}, whereas the GP-Collocation baseline is ~10× worse. Scalability is confirmed empirically with an O(N_s^1.35) wall-clock scaling (theoretical O(N_s^{3/2})), solving 70,802 DOF in under 4 minutes on a single CPU core, with reconstruction error plateauing beyond N=31 in the observation-limited regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PINN baseline (Raissi et al., 2019), GP-FVM wins decisively on both accuracy and speed for the source identification inverse problem: source RMSE 0.44 vs. 0.76, and 2 s vs. ~5 min (150× faster), while additionally providing structured uncertainty quantification (marginal variances, posterior samples) that the PINN point estimate lacks. Against classical Crank-Nicolson FVM (Eymard et al., 2000), the proposed method loses on raw accuracy (within 2–3× in L² error) and runtime (orders of magnitude slower per timestep), but gains the ability to propagate discretization, measurement, and model uncertainty into a coherent posterior. Against the GP-Collocation approach of Chen et al. (2025), GP-FVM achieves roughly 10× lower L² error on the 2D Burgers benchmark at comparable computational cost, demonstrating that the conservative finite-volume discretization is critical for accuracy in the probabilistic setting. The paper also shows that kernel smoothness (Matérn ν) controls convergence order analogously to polynomial reconstruction order in classical FVM, with Matérn-3/2 preserving O(h²) convergence under sparse approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic numerical methods framework (Hennig et al., 2022; Cockayne, 2019) and physics-informed GP regression (Pförtner et al., 2024) to the finite volume method for nonlinear conservation laws. It builds directly on Weiland et al. (2024), who formulated probabilistic FVM but were restricted to linear PDEs, and on Schäfer et al. (2021)'s KL-minimizing sparse Cholesky (Vecchia) approximation, which the authors extend from point evaluations to integral functionals. The key departure from Chen et al. (2025), who achieved scalable sparse GP inference for nonlinear PDEs, is the use of conservative FVM discretization rather than collocation, combined with a novel moment-matching time-stepping scheme that preserves spatial sparsity across timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled path to uncertainty quantification for conservation law simulations at scales previously intractable for GP-based approaches, with O(N_s^{3/2}) scaling in 2D and native handling of both forward and inverse problems. The moment-matching temporal propagation is the main practical limitation: it induces fill-in that restricts current scalability to 2D, and the Laplace approximation for nonlinear flux constraints is only O(Δt²) per step, which may degrade for shock-dominated problems. Open questions include extending to 3D with sparsity-preserving filtering, handling non-Gaussian posteriors beyond the Laplace approximation, and developing non-stationary or sparsity-promoting priors to fix the locally overconfident source posteriors observed with saturating link functions.
