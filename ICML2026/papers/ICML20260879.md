## Problem

Current video generation benchmarks for robotics rely primarily on perceptual quality metrics, overlooking critical dimensions like task completion, physical plausibility, and action-goal alignment. Additionally, no large-scale dataset specifically curated for training video generation models on robotic content exists, leaving a data gap that hinders progress toward physically realistic embodied video synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260879/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RBench takes 650 image-text pairs (reference keyframe images at 720P paired with task-specific prompts) and evaluates generated videos via MLLM-based VQA protocols on temporally sampled frame grids, producing scores across five sub-metrics: physical-semantic plausibility, task-adherence consistency, motion amplitude, robot-subject stability, and motion smoothness. RoVid-X provides 4 million 720P video clips enriched with optical flow (AllTracker), relative depth maps (Video Depth Anything), and standardized task captions, with no action labels preserved since the focus is on enhancing physical performance of pretrained video generation models.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary contributions: RBench, a benchmark with 650 evaluation samples spanning five task categories (Common Manipulation, Long-horizon Planning, Multi-entity Collaboration, Spatial Relationship, Visual Reasoning) and four embodiments (dual-arm, humanoid, single-arm, quadruped), evaluated using MLLM-based metrics (Qwen3-VL, GPT-5) for task-level validity and computational metrics (GroundingDINO + GroundedSAM + CoTracker for motion amplitude, Q-Align for smoothness) for visual quality; and RoVid-X, a 4M-clip dataset built via a four-stage pipeline (robot video collection from 20+ sources, multi-dimensional quality filtering, MLLM-based task segmentation and captioning, and physical property annotation with super-resolution, optical flow, and depth estimation). The evaluation of 25 models reveals a 0.96 Spearman correlation with human judgment and exposes a persistent 'media-simulation gap' where consumer-oriented models underperform on physical fidelity despite strong visual quality.

## Results

On RBench (650 image-text pairs across 5 tasks and 4 embodiments), Wan 2.6 achieves the highest average score of 0.607, followed by Seedance 1.5 Pro (0.584) and Wan 2.5 (0.570). The best open-source model, Wan2.2.A14B, scores 0.507 (Rank 8), revealing a clear commercial-over-open-source gap. Notably, Sora v2 Pro underperforms at Rank 17 (0.362), and robotics-specific models like Cosmos 2.5 (0.464, Rank 9) and DreamGen (0.420, Rank 12) trail top commercial models. Finetuning Wan2.1-14B on 200k RoVid-X clips improves the average score from 0.399 to 0.446, with the largest gain in Visual Reasoning (0.205→0.298).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 25 models are evaluated on the same RBench protocol; the paper's contribution is the benchmark and dataset rather than a new generator. Commercial models dominate the top 7 (Wan 2.6 at 0.607, Seedance 1.5 Pro at 0.584), while the best open-source model (Wan2.2.A14B, 0.507) trails by ~0.10 in average score. Sora v2 Pro (0.362, Rank 17) and Sora v1 (0.266, Rank 22) perform far below expectations, suggesting a 'media-simulation gap' where cinematic quality does not transfer to physical fidelity. Among robotics-specific models, Cosmos 2.5 (0.464) outperforms DreamGen (0.420/0.405) and Vidar (0.206), but all lag behind top commercial models. The Wan series shows the largest iterative gain: Wan 2.1 (0.399) → Wan 2.6 (0.607), a +0.208 improvement. Finetuning on RoVid-X (200k clips) lifts Wan2.1-14B from 0.399 to 0.446 and Wan2.2-5B from 0.380 to 0.439, with the largest relative gains in Visual Reasoning (+46% and +21% respectively) and Multi-entity Collaboration (+5% and +56%). The benchmark achieves 0.96 Spearman correlation with human preference judgments across 10 models, validating its reliability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-as-evaluator paradigm established by T2V-CompBench and PhyWorldBench into the specifically robotic domain, while adapting the motion estimation pipeline from VMBench (GroundingDINO + GroundedSAM + CoTracker) to measure robot-specific motion amplitude with camera-motion compensation. It departs from general video generation benchmarks (VBench, Video-Bench) that focus on perceptual quality, and from physics-reasoning benchmarks that lack task-specific robotic criteria, by introducing fine-grained sub-metrics for task completion (physical-semantic plausibility, task-adherence consistency) and visual quality (robot-subject stability, motion smoothness) tailored to embodied scenarios. On the data side, it distinguishes itself from existing robotic datasets (Open X-Embodiment, DROID, Agibot World) that are curated for VLM/VLA training with action labels, by providing a 4M-clip dataset specifically processed for video generation pretraining with physical property annotations (optical flow, depth maps) but no action labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a persistent 'Media-Simulation Gap' where consumer-optimized models like Sora v2 Pro (Rank 17/25) underperform on physically grounded robotic tasks despite excelling at cinematic video generation, suggesting that proficiency in creative video does not transfer to embodied AI without physically-grounded training data. RoVid-X (4M clips, 1300+ skills, 720P with optical flow and depth) addresses the critical data bottleneck for training video models on robotic interactions, and finetuning experiments confirm consistent gains across all task and embodiment dimensions. Key open questions include bridging generated video to actionable robot policies via inverse dynamics models, developing more automated physically-grounded metrics beyond MLLM-based VQA, and whether the observed performance hierarchy (closed-source > open-source) will narrow as physical training data scales.
