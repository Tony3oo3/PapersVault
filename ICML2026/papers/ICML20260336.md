## Problem

Conformal prediction guarantees only marginal coverage, yet practitioners need to know how far a method is from achieving conditional coverage. Existing diagnostics (CovGap, WSC, FSC, HSIC) suffer from sample inefficiency, sensitivity to group definitions, pessimism in high dimensions, or inability to detect violations when prediction set sizes are uniform, leaving no robust general-purpose metric for comparing conditional coverage across conformal methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260336/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test dataset of (X_i, Y_i) pairs, an already-constructed predictive set rule C_α(X), and a target miscoverage level α, then computes the binary label Z_i = 1{Y_i ∈ C_α(X_i)} for each test point. It outputs a scalar ERT value that is a guaranteed lower bound on the expected distance between true conditional coverage and the target, plus the learned classifier h(X) which serves as a pointwise proxy for conditional coverage. The metric can be decomposed into over-coverage and under-coverage components, and the proper loss choice (L1, L2, KL, or any convex distance via Proposition 3.1) determines which natural miscoverage quantity is estimated.

## Method

The core insight reframes conditional coverage evaluation as a binary classification problem: under perfect conditional coverage, no classifier can beat the constant predictor 1−α on predicting Z = 1{Y ∈ C_α(X)}, so any classifier that does exposes a violation. The ERT metric is defined as the risk difference R_ℓ(1−α) − R_ℓ(p(X)) for a proper scoring rule ℓ, and in practice is estimated as R_ℓ(1−α) − R_ℓ(h) using a learned classifier h, which always yields a lower bound on the true ERT. Proposition 3.1 establishes that any convex distance f with f(1−α)=0 can be recovered by choosing ℓ(p,y) = −f(p) − (y−p)f′(p), unifying L1, L2, KL, and arbitrary Bregman divergences under one framework. The classifier h is trained via k-fold cross-validation (or out-of-bag for random forests) to avoid overfitting, and modern tabular classifiers (LightGBM, CatBoost, TabICL) are shown to recover far more of the true ERT than the partition-wise estimators underlying CovGap, especially in high dimensions where clustering degrades.

## Results

On a synthetic 8-dimensional experiment with known ground truth, L1-ERT correctly estimates a 9.1% average absolute coverage deviation (true value 0.098) for non-conditional sets and drops to ~0 for oracle conditional sets, while L2-ERT reports 0.009 vs. 0.000 respectively. In the classifier comparison across 8 TabArena regression datasets, LightGBM recovers 68.9% of the maximum L1-ERT at only 2.4 s per 1,000 samples, while TabICLv1.1 achieves 72.7% but requires 16 s on GPU. The partition-wise estimator underlying CovGap recovers only 33.7% of the maximum L1-ERT and 10.5% of L2-ERT, confirming that modern classifiers dramatically outperform histogram-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's ERT metrics are the only ones that correctly separate the non-conditional (standard CP) from the conditional (oracle) scenario in Table 3. CovGap and its weighted variant WCovGap (Ding et al., 2023) report nearly identical values (0.016 vs. 0.014) across the two settings, making them unable to detect the coverage violation. WSC (Cauchois et al., 2021) detects the non-conditional failure (0.740) but falsely reports a conditional coverage violation in the oracle case (0.790 vs. the true 0.90), a pessimism that worsens with dimension. EOC (Wang et al., 2023) similarly produces a false positive in the conditional case (0.186). SSC, HSIC, and Pearson correlation (Feldman et al., 2021; Angelopoulos et al., 2021) all report near-zero values in the non-conditional setting because the prediction set sizes are uniform, rendering them blind to the coverage failure. FSC (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023) reports 0.868 vs. 0.881, values too close to distinguish the two scenarios. In the classifier benchmark (Table 2), the partition-wise estimator underlying CovGap recovers only 33.7% of the maximum L1-ERT compared to 68.9–72.7% for gradient-boosted trees and tabular foundation models, confirming that the classification-based reformulation is the key source of the diagnostic power gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes conditional coverage evaluation in conformal prediction as a binary classification problem, building on the theory of proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007) and Bregman divergences to define the Excess Risk of the Target Coverage (ERT) metric family. It explicitly generalizes existing partition-based diagnostics (CovGap, FSC) and geometric scans (WSC) by replacing their implicit histogram/slab classifiers with arbitrary modern predictors, positioning itself as a functional-estimation upgrade over prior nonparametric group evaluations. The benchmarking component builds on the multi-output conformal regression framework of Dheur et al. (2025), extending it with conditional coverage diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ERT metric fills a critical gap: prior conformal prediction methods lack a reliable, sample-efficient way to diagnose conditional coverage violations, making it difficult to compare or improve conditional coverage strategies. The finding that modern tabular classifiers recover 2–7× more of the true ERT than partition-based CovGap, and that WSC systematically overestimates violations even under perfect conditional coverage, provides concrete guidance for practitioners. Open questions include the trade-off between conditional coverage quality and prediction set size (the paper notes improved conditional coverage often inflates volumes), extension to non-tabular modalities, and whether the classifier-dependent nature of ERT introduces new failure modes in adversarial or distribution-shift settings.
