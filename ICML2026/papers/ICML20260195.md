## Problem

For structured best-arm identification problems, it was unclear whether the fixed-budget (FB) setting is fundamentally harder than the fixed-confidence (FC) setting, or whether FC's certification requirement inherently limits its sample complexity. While standard K-armed bandits show only a logarithmic gap between optimal FB and FC sample complexities, for generic structured problems (linear bandits, unimodal bandits, heterogeneous noise) the gap could in principle be polynomial. This paper resolves the question by showing FB is no harder than FC up to logarithmic factors, via a constructive reduction.

## Input / Output

The meta-algorithm FC2FB takes as input a black-box FC algorithm (which need not reveal its internal constants A and C), a total sampling budget B, a base failure rate δ₀ (typically 1/e), and a minimum-meaningful-samples parameter Q (e.g., K for unstructured bandits, d for linear bandits). It outputs an estimated best arm Ĵ with an error probability bounded by 3·exp(−B/(4Q/ln(1/δ₀) + 4A·log₂(B/Q))), where A is the leading constant of the FC algorithm's sample complexity. The FC algorithm is consumed as a subroutine invoked repeatedly at different confidence levels; no knowledge of its stopping rule or internal state is required beyond the ability to run it with a specified δ and a hard budget cap.

## Method

FC2FB splits the total budget B into R = ⌊log₂(B/Q)⌋ stages of roughly B/R samples each. At stage r, it runs the input FC algorithm with failure rate δ₀^(2^(R−r)) — a doubly exponential schedule — and force-terminates if the algorithm does not self-terminate within the stage budget. The first stage where the FC algorithm self-terminates determines the output arm. The key insight is that the doubly exponential schedule guarantees a 'threshold stage' r* where the per-stage budget exceeds the FC algorithm's high-probability sample complexity, so self-termination occurs with high probability, and the error probability decays exponentially in B. A companion meta-algorithm FCW2S converts weak FC algorithms (with guarantees only at a constant δ₀) into strong FC algorithms by running L parallel instances in round-robin and taking a majority vote, enabling the full pipeline to apply to algorithms like UniTT that lack logarithmic δ-dependence in their stopping time.

## Results

The main theoretical result shows that FC2FB converts any strong FC algorithm with sample complexity T*_δ = A ln(1/δ) into an FB algorithm with sample complexity A ln(A/δ)·ln ln(1/δ), matching up to logarithmic factors. In the known heterogeneous noise setting, FC2FB(PE-KHN) achieves Õ(σ₁²/Δ₂² + Σᵢ σᵢ²/Δᵢ²), which on a constructed instance yields O(K⁷) versus O(K⁹) for SHVar. In linear bandits, FC2FB recovers the FC-optimal rate γ* + ρ* ln(1/δ) + d, removing the extra ln(1/δ) factor on γ* present in prior FB algorithms. Empirically, FC2FB(PE-KHN) outperforms both SH and SHVar in misidentification probability across budgets B ∈ [1000, 6000] and arm counts K ∈ [32, 128].

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SH (Karnin et al., 2013), FC2FB(PE-KHN) strictly improves the sample complexity by replacing the global gap Δ₂ in the denominator of each arm's term with the arm-specific gap Δᵢ, and by removing the σ₁² term from suboptimal arms. Against SHVar (Lalitha et al., 2023), the improvement is that the per-arm variance σᵢ² is divided by the arm-specific gap Δᵢ² rather than the worst-case gap Δ₂²; on a constructed instance (Δ₂ = 1/K⁵, Δᵢ = 1/K⁴ for i≥3, σ₁²=σ₂²=1/K⁵, σᵢ²=1/K² for i≥3), FC2FB achieves O(K⁷) while SHVar requires O(K⁹). Against VBR (Faella et al., 2020) in the unknown-variance setting, FC2FB(VD-BESTARMID) improves by a factor of K when σ₁ ≫ σᵢ for all suboptimal arms, since its leading term depends on individual per-arm quantities rather than a global max. In the linear bandit setting, FC2FB combined with Fixed Confidence Peace matches the FC-optimal rate and is no larger than OD-LinBAI (Yang & Tan, 2022), while the paper constructs instances where ρ* = o(H_{2,lin}), showing the FC-based rate can be strictly tighter. Empirically (Figures 1–2), FC2FB(PE-KHN) consistently achieves lower misidentification probability than both SH and SHVar across all tested budgets and arm counts, with the gap widening as K increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by establishing a black-box reduction from fixed-confidence (FC) to fixed-budget (FB) best-arm identification, a relationship that prior work had only partially explored. Gabillon et al. (2012) proposed a unified FB/FC framework but required knowledge of problem-dependent complexity parameters and did not provide a true black-box conversion; Jun & Nowak (2016) attempted an anytime FB construction from LUCB but suffered from soundness issues in the stopping-time analysis. The FC2FB meta-algorithm departs from both by requiring no knowledge of instance-dependent constants and by employing a forced-termination mechanism that avoids the heavy-tailed stopping-time problems that plagued earlier attempts. The FCW2S component repurposes boosting/amplification ideas (inspired by the Brakebooster framework of Balagopalan et al., 2025) to convert weak FC guarantees into strong ones, broadening the class of usable input algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that FB is no harder than FC up to logarithmic factors resolves a long-standing open question and provides a systematic recipe for improving FB sample complexities by importing superior FC guarantees, yielding order-wise improvements in heterogeneous-noise bandits, linear bandits, unimodal bandits, and cascading bandits. Key limitations include the inability to share samples across FC2FB stages (reducing practicality for small budgets), the inapplicability to FC algorithms with only asymptotic guarantees (e.g., Track-and-Stop), and the assumption of a unique best arm. Open directions include extending the reduction to ε-good arm guarantees, developing sample-sharing variants, and investigating whether similar relationships hold for other performance measures like simple regret.
