## Problem

In attribute graph clustering, noisy edges in the original adjacency matrix introduce redundant information during GNN feature aggregation, degrading cluster discriminability. Additionally, existing contrastive learning approaches rely on data augmentation (node dropping, edge perturbation, attribute masking), which adds complexity and risks altering graph semantics. The paper addresses both issues by reconstructing a cleaner topology and performing contrastive learning without any augmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} as input, where d ranges from 334 to 3703 across the four datasets. It produces node cluster assignments via K-means on the final learned representations. The topology reconstruction step requires a pre-trained baseline (DFCN) to obtain initial feature representations for K-means-based global semantic filtering, and the number of k-nearest neighbors (k=1–5) and K-means cluster centers (K=90–200) are dataset-specific hyperparameters.

## Method

The method has two core innovations. First, it reconstructs the graph topology by computing k-nearest neighbors via cosine similarity on the feature matrix, then intersecting this set with both the original adjacency neighbors (capturing local structural information) and K-means cluster members (capturing global semantic information); the union of these two intersections defines the reconstructed adjacency used in the GCN-based GAE. Second, it performs augmentation-free contrastive learning by treating AE and GAE embeddings as two natural views, applying a MINE-based mutual information minimization (using a Jensen-Shannon divergence estimator) to suppress inter-view redundancy, combined with a Barlow Twins-style decorrelation loss that fits the cross-correlation matrix between views to the identity matrix. The full objective combines AE reconstruction, GAE reconstruction (structure + features), the contrastive loss, and a self-supervised clustering loss using Student-t-distribution soft assignments from three representations (fused, AE, GAE) with a sharpened target distribution and KL divergence.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best or near-best performance across all four datasets (DBLP, CITE, ACM, AMAP) on all four clustering metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI, F1). On DBLP, it reaches 80.89% ACC, 50.78% NMI, 56.25% ARI, and 80.43% F1, improving over the strongest baseline DFCN by 4.89%, 7.08%, 9.25%, and 4.73% respectively. On ACM, it attains 91.94% ACC and 77.59% ARI, and on AMAP it reaches 79.31% ACC and 73.42% NMI. The method wins on 15 out of 16 dataset-metric combinations, with the sole exception being NMI on CITE (43.44 vs. DFCN's 43.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on 15 of 16 dataset-metric combinations. DFCN, the strongest baseline and the backbone network, is the closest competitor: the proposed method beats it on every metric except NMI on CITE (43.44 vs. 43.90), with the largest margins on DBLP (up to +9.25% ARI) and AMAP (up to +4.21% NMI). AGCN is the second-strongest baseline, trailing the proposed method by 1–5 points across datasets. SDCN and DAEGC occupy the mid-range, with SDCN performing well on ACM (90.45% ACC) but struggling on AMAP (53.44% ACC). MVGRL is highly inconsistent: competitive on CITE (68.60% ACC) but severely degraded on DBLP (42.73% ACC) and AMAP (45.19% ACC). GAE is the weakest baseline across all four datasets, confirming that a plain graph auto-encoder without structural or contrastive regularization is insufficient for discriminative clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual AE-GAE architecture of DFCN (Tu et al., 2021) by adding a topology reconstruction step that intersects k-nearest neighbors with both the original adjacency and K-means cluster assignments to filter noisy edges before graph convolution. For contrastive learning, it departs from the data-augmentation paradigm used by MVGRL and InfoGCL, instead treating the AE and GAE embeddings as two natural views and applying a MINE-based mutual information minimization (Belghazi et al., 2018) combined with a Barlow Twins-style (Bielak et al., 2022) cross-correlation decorrelation loss. The overall design sits at the intersection of the SDCN/DFCN line of structural deep clustering and augmentation-free self-supervised graph learning (Huang et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2022), but is the first to jointly integrate topology reconstruction with view-based decorrelation in a single clustering objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The augmentation-free contrastive formulation is practically appealing because it removes the sensitivity to hand-crafted graph perturbation strategies that plague methods like MVGRL and InfoGCL. However, the paper itself reports that the Nemenyi post-hoc test shows no statistically significant difference from DFCN (p > 0.05), and the gains are modest (e.g., 0.48% ACC on CITE), limiting the strength of the empirical claim. The authors acknowledge that mutual information minimization may discard useful information alongside redundancy, and propose pseudo-label generation as future work—an open question that remains unresolved. Broader applicability is also constrained by evaluation on only four small-scale citation and POI datasets (≤7,650 nodes), leaving scalability to larger graphs untested.
