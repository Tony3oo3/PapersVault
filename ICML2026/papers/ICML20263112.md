## Problem

Existing open-world object detection methods improve unknown recall but lack interpretability, offering no explanation for why a region is classified as unknown. More critically, they do not diagnose the root cause of known-unknown confusion—visually similar unknown objects being misclassified as known categories—leading to unreliable predictions. This paper identifies that confusion arises when unknown objects fall into the discriminative feature space learned for known classes and proposes an interpretable framework to resolve it.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and processes them through a Faster R-CNN pipeline with a ResNet-50 backbone. Beyond standard bounding boxes with known class labels and an 'unknown' label, the system produces concept-level activation vectors for every detection, explaining which discriminative, shared, or background concepts fired for each prediction. An LLM is invoked only at task-initialization time to generate semantic concept descriptions (encoded via CLIP text encoder); it does not participate in training or inference, incurring no runtime overhead.

## Method

IPOW decomposes each RoI feature into three orthogonal concept subspaces: discriminative concepts (LLM-identified pairwise attributes between known classes, optimized with a margin-based contrastive loss to enforce an Equiangular Tight Frame structure per Neural Collapse), shared concepts (LLM-summarized cross-class attributes plus residual concepts discovered via a sparse auto-encoder with a concept dictionary), and background concepts (PCA basis vectors over background RoI features, where reconstruction error signals foreground/unknown). A GMM-based RPN supplements the standard RPN with spatially informed proposals to reduce known-category bias. The key rectification mechanism, Concept-Guided Rectification (CGR), exploits the observation that known objects exhibit full activation of their class-specific shared concepts while unknown objects trigger only partial activation, multiplying the raw classification score by the geometric mean of shared-concept activations to suppress false known predictions and boost the unknown score accordingly.

## Results

On M-OWODB, IPOW achieves U-Recall of 50.1%, 41.9%, and 46.3% on Tasks 1–3, surpassing the previous best (CROWD) by 7.2, 10.5, and 11.6 points respectively, while also attaining the highest known-class mAP (Both) of 62.4, 52.7, 45.0, and 42.2 across Tasks 1–4. On the more challenging S-OWODB (superclass-separated), IPOW reaches U-Recall of 34.7%, 32.6%, and 44.3% and mAP (Both) of 73.6, 55.1, 50.5, and 47.4. On the remote sensing DIOR dataset, IPOW boosts U-Recall from 2.8% to 19.9% and reduces A-OSE from 1833 to 1143. On PASCAL VOC incremental detection, IPOW achieves mAP of 73.8, 75.0, and 74.5 in the 10+10, 15+5, and 19+1 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPOW outperforms all 11 baselines on every evaluated benchmark. Against the closest competitor CROWD (Majee et al., 2025), IPOW gains 7.2–11.6 points in U-Recall on M-OWODB and 4.3–7.1 points on S-OWODB, while also improving mAP (Both) by 0.7–4.9 points; on PASCAL VOC incremental detection, IPOW edges out CROWD by 0.3–0.6 mAP. Compared to OrthogonalDet (Sun et al., 2024), the prior best on known-class mAP for several tasks, IPOW improves both U-Recall (by 15.7–17.2 points on M-OWODB) and mAP (by 1.4–4.9 points). Earlier methods—ORE, OW-DETR, PROB, CAT, and RandBox—are substantially outperformed, with U-Recall gaps of 20–45 points on M-OWODB Task 1. On the remote sensing DIOR dataset, IPOW improves U-Recall by 17.1 points over the base model and reduces A-OSE by 37.6%, demonstrating cross-domain generalization. On PASCAL VOC incremental settings, IPOW surpasses all IOD baselines (ILOD, Faster ILOD, Meta-ILOD, ROSETTA) by 5–11 mAP, confirming that the concept decomposition framework also benefits incremental learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Faster R-CNN as its detection backbone and repurposes the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) paradigm from image classification to open-world object detection, proposing a Concept Decomposition Model that splits RoI features into discriminative, shared, and background subspaces. It departs from the dominant objectness-based paradigm in prior OWOD work (ORE, OW-DETR, RandBox, OrthogonalDet, CROWD), which relies on class-agnostic scoring and self-supervised mining limited to known-unknown classes in the training set. The paper further grounds its discriminative concept design in Neural Collapse theory (ETF structure) and introduces LLM-assisted concept construction, a departure from purely data-driven feature learning in prior OWOD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing concept-level explanations for both known and unknown predictions, IPOW addresses a critical gap in OWOD: prior methods offer no rationale for why a region is classified as unknown, undermining trust and hindering the annotation loop essential for incremental learning. The identification of known-unknown confusion as unknown objects falling into the discriminative space, combined with the CGR rectification mechanism, offers a principled and interpretable solution that could inform future OWOD architectures. Limitations include reliance on a two-stage Faster R-CNN backbone, LLM dependency for concept construction (though only at task initialization), and the shared concept space's potential incompleteness for highly novel unknown categories. Open questions include whether the concept decomposition generalizes to single-stage DETR-based detectors and how it scales to very large open-world class spaces.
