## Problem

Existing covariate-aware shape modeling methods either provide deterministic point estimates without uncertainty (e.g., NAISR) or model variability in a parameter space (e.g., LDDMM momenta) where propagating uncertainty to the anatomical domain requires computationally intensive Monte Carlo resampling. No prior framework offers a theoretically grounded, closed-form formulation that directly maps spatially heteroscedastic, covariate-conditioned aleatoric uncertainty onto the anatomy at arbitrary resolution.

## Input / Output

PRISM consumes dense template-to-shape displacement fields (obtained via a learned invertible registration module) paired with a scalar chronological covariate t, and produces a continuous heteroscedastic Gaussian field N(μ(p,t), Σ(p,t)) queryable at any template coordinate and time. Beyond the mean and covariance, it outputs an amortized per-point intrinsic time estimate τ̂ = g(p,d) in a single forward pass, a closed-form temporal uncertainty σ_τ(p,t) = I(p,t)⁻¹, and downstream quantities including personalized future shape predictions and spatially resolved OOD anomaly scores. The displacement formulation (rather than raw positions) is key: since y = p + d with fixed p, the covariance structure is translation-invariant, allowing geometric uncertainty on y to be read directly from the displacement model.

## Method

PRISM parameterizes the conditional displacement distribution d|p,t ~ N(μ(p,t), Σ(p,t)) with a coordinate-based neural network whose outputs decompose into a covariate-driven term f(p,t)−f(p,0) and a covariate-independent residual h(p), ensuring identifiability at t=0. The covariance is parameterized via its Cholesky factor with softplus-activated diagonals for guaranteed positive definiteness, and training uses separate L1 (for μ) and NLL (for Σ) losses with a two-stage curriculum to avoid the known bias of jointly optimizing mean and variance. The central theoretical contribution is a closed-form Fisher Information metric I(p,t) = (∂μ/∂t)ᵀΣ⁻¹(∂μ/∂t) derived from the score function of the heteroscedastic Gaussian, which yields temporal uncertainty σ_τ² ≈ I⁻¹ via automatic differentiation—no sampling required. The paper explicitly discards the covariance-derivative term I_Σ from the full Fisher Information, arguing (and empirically verifying) that only I_μ answers the clinical question of localizing an individual along the mean developmental trajectory. An amortized inverse encoder g(p,d)→τ̂, trained on synthetic triplets generated by the forward model, enables dense per-point intrinsic time estimation without test-time optimization.

## Results

On mean trajectory reconstruction (Table 1), PRISM achieves the best EMD on Starman (G) (0.195 vs. 0.215/0.216), best CD on Starman (L) (0.287 vs. 0.288/0.351), best HD on ANNY (4.450 vs. 4.586/8.158), and best CD/HD/EMD on Airway (0.064/9.614/1.308). On personalized shape prediction (Table 4), PRISM dramatically outperforms both baselines, e.g., CD of 0.018 on ANNY vs. 0.081 (A-SDF) and 0.044 (NAISR). For OOD detection on pediatric airways (Table 5), PRISM with local scoring achieves AUC 0.875, substantially exceeding NAISR (0.605) and A-SDF (0.270). The amortized inverse encoder is ~100× faster than test-time optimization baselines (0.040 s vs. 4–8 s per case) while matching or exceeding accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM is compared against two covariate-conditioned implicit representation baselines: A-SDF (direct SDF regression) and NAISR (neural additive deformations from a shared template). On mean trajectory reconstruction, PRISM matches or beats both on most metrics, with the clearest advantage on the smaller Airway dataset where A-SDF overfits (CD 0.114 vs. PRISM's 0.064). On personalized longitudinal prediction, PRISM's advantage is most dramatic: it achieves CD 0.018 on ANNY versus 0.081 (A-SDF) and 0.044 (NAISR), and CD 0.099 on Starman (L) versus 0.467 and 0.595, because its pointwise inverse encoder avoids the extrapolation failure of A-SDF and the global-time optimization of NAISR. For OOD detection, PRISM with local scoring (AUC 0.875) substantially outperforms NAISR (0.605) and A-SDF (0.270), while PRISM with global scoring (0.502) actually underperforms NAISR, confirming that spatially varying intrinsic time is essential for detecting localized pathology. On global intrinsic time estimation, amortized PRISM is ~100× faster than both baselines' test-time optimization while achieving comparable accuracy (r = 0.993 vs. 0.996/0.988 on ANNY); NAISR with TTO slightly edges out amortized PRISM on Airway (r = 0.908 vs. 0.893), but PRISM with TTO closes that gap (r = 0.923) at 3× lower cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM extends the template-based neural deformation paradigm of NAISR (Jiao et al., 2024) by replacing its deterministic point-estimate output with a heteroscedastic Gaussian field, and draws on the SIREN-based displacement networks from DIF-Net (Deng et al., 2021) for its registration module. Conceptually, it departs from classical diffeomorphic shape analysis (LDDMM/Deformetrica), which models variability in a tangent parameter space and requires Monte Carlo propagation to obtain pointwise uncertainty on the anatomy, by instead deriving a closed-form Fisher Information metric directly in the spatial domain. It also distinguishes itself from other covariate-conditioned implicit atlases (CINA, CINeMA, SINA) that produce only a mean atlas, by explicitly modeling spatially varying population dispersion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form Fisher Information metric (Eq. 13) provides a theoretically grounded, sampling-free way to quantify spatially heterogeneous temporal uncertainty directly on anatomy—a capability that prior diffeomorphic methods could only approximate via expensive resampling. The unified framework addressing shape evolution, intrinsic time inference, personalized prediction, and OOD detection in one model is practically valuable for clinical workflows. Key limitations include conditioning on a single scalar covariate (age), unvalidated long-term forecasting for degenerative conditions, and the assumption that population-average intrinsic time equals chronological time. Extending the information-geometric formulation to high-dimensional covariate spaces and multi-covariate interactions remains an open direction.
