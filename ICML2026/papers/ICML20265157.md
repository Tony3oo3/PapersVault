## Problem

Existing Aggregation-Free Federated Learning (AFFL) methods optimize synthetic data by matching the mean of hidden representations to approximate real data distributions. However, the mean inherently mixes the class-level principal component with uncontrollable residual components, introducing a systematic bias that causes information loss and degrades global model performance, particularly under high data heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265157/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model weights and its local real data chunk, then initializes a small synthetic set (10 IPC for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 5 IPC for CIFAR-100/TinyImageNet) by sampling from the real data. The client outputs an optimized synthetic dataset that is transmitted to the server, which aggregates synthetic data from the current and previous T−1 rounds (T=3) and trains the global model on the expanded synthetic set. The synthetic data is split into l×l sub-pieces and differentially expanded back to full image size before feature extraction, increasing extractable features without enlarging the transmitted payload.

## Method

FedUSD replaces the biased mean-based distribution matching with an unbiased optimization that separately captures the principal and residual components of class-conditional feature distributions. The principal component is extracted via the High-energy Orthogonal Base (HOB) — the first right singular vector from SVD of the hidden representation matrix — which provably yields a smaller rank-1 reconstruction error than the mean (Theorem 3.1). The residual component is captured by feature-wise variance. Both are matched between synthetic and real data using Earth Mover's Distance (Wasserstein-1) rather than coordinate-wise Euclidean distance, better handling coupled feature dimensions in deep representations. Split-and-Expand Augmentation (SEA) is applied to increase the information capacity of the small synthetic set. The paper further provides a unified convergence theory showing AFFL achieves tighter bounds than standard FL, with the distillation error ζ² as the critical factor that FedUSD minimizes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ConvNet, FedUSD achieves 67.67% top-1 accuracy under the most heterogeneous setting (Dirichlet α=0.01), outperforming the best aggregation-free baseline FedAF (63.85%) by 3.82% and the best aggregation-based method FedNova (45.02%) by 22.65%. On CIFAR-100, FedUSD reaches 44.58% (α=0.01), a 10.05% gain over FedAF (34.53%). On SVHN, FedUSD attains 88.23% (α=0.01), improving 6.74%–30.82% over other methods. Communication costs remain identical to FedDM (e.g., 35.2 MB for CIFAR-10 with ConvNet), confirming no extra overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedUSD consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against the closest aggregation-free competitor FedAF, FedUSD gains 3.82% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.01), 10.05% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.01), and 6.74% on SVHN (α=0.01), with the gap widening under higher heterogeneity (e.g., 7.35% on CIFAR-10 at α=0.1). Compared to FedDM, the improvement is even larger: 6.40% on CIFAR-10, 13.34% on CIFAR-100, and 7.74% on SVHN at α=0.01. Against aggregation-based methods (FedAvg, FedNova, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedConcat), FedUSD leads by 22–37 percentage points on CIFAR-10 and 20–26 points on CIFAR-100 under α=0.01, demonstrating that aggregation schemes fundamentally cannot match distribution-balanced synthetic data. On ViT-Tiny-16 (Table 2), FedUSD also dominates: 57.07% vs. 47.81% (FedAF) and 45.33% (FedDM) on CIFAR-10 at α=0.1, confirming architecture-agnostic superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedUSD extends the Aggregation-Free Federated Learning (AFFL) line initiated by FedDM (Xiong et al., 2023) and FedAF (Wang et al., 2024c), which optimize synthetic data via mean-variance distribution matching. The key departure is replacing the class-level mean with the High-energy Orthogonal Base (first right singular vector from SVD) as the principal component representation, arguing that the mean conflates principal and residual structure and thus introduces bias. The method also incorporates Split-and-Expand Augmentation (SEA) from dataset distillation (Zhao et al., 2023) and uses Wasserstein-1 (EMD) distance for matching, building on distribution-matching condensation (Zhao & Bilen, 2023). Theoretically, it provides the first unified convergence analysis covering both standard FL and AFFL under shared assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical resolution to a practical weakness in AFFL: the mean-based distillation bias that limits synthetic data quality. The unified convergence framework (showing AFFL's crossover round and that distillation error ζ² is the critical bottleneck) gives practitioners a principled way to evaluate and improve future AFFL methods. However, the work is limited to unimodal image classification with relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet) and 10 clients; its applicability to cross-modal tasks, larger-scale FL deployments, or non-convex deep network settings remains open. The differential privacy guarantee is stated but not empirically stress-tested against concrete (ε,δ) budgets.
