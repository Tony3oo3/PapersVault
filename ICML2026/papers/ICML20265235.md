## Problem

Time series foundation models (TSFMs) face a fundamental scalability bottleneck in long-term and ultra-long-term forecasting: as look-back and prediction lengths grow, computational costs increase quadratically while accuracy degrades due to the volatility and complex long-range dependencies in high-resolution sequences. No existing training-free, model-agnostic mechanism allows TSFMs to effectively extend their receptive field for long-horizon prediction without architectural modifications or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SPRINT takes a historical multivariate time series of length H (default H=30P) and the intrinsic period P of the data as input, and produces a future forecast of length L at the original sampling resolution. Internally, the trend component is downsampled by a factor k=P/4 before being fed to the base TSFM, so the model only processes sequences of length ⌊H/k⌋ and predicts ⌊L/k⌋ points; the seasonal component is never downsampled but is instead replicated from a weighted-average of historical cycles. The final output is the element-wise sum of the spline-interpolated trend and the replicated seasonal pattern, all in a zero-shot, training-free wrapper that treats the TSFM as a black box.

## Method

SPRINT (Seasonal Pattern Replication and Trend Resolution INTERpolation) is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time framework that decomposes the input series into trend and seasonal components via moving average with window P, then processes each separately. For the low-frequency trend, a Resolution Interpolation workflow downsamples by factor k, feeds the shortened series to the base TSFM for low-resolution forecasting, and reconstructs the high-resolution trend via spline interpolation—justified by the Nyquist-Shannon theorem since the moving-average filter acts as a low-pass filter with cutoff ≈1/P. For the high-frequency seasonal component, a Pattern Replication mechanism extracts the last ⌊H/P⌋ complete cycles, computes an exponentially weighted average representative pattern (decay factor w=0.9), and tiles it across the forecast horizon, with a theoretical guarantee of only O(L²ε_max) cumulative error growth. The two predictions are summed to produce the final forecast, and the entire pipeline requires no parameter learning, no architectural changes to the TSFM, and no fine-tuning, making it universally compatible with encoder-only, decoder-only, encoder-decoder, and VLM-based TSFMs.

## Results

On standard LTSF (L ∈ {96,192,336,720}), SPRINT achieves average MSE reductions of 6.60%–29.81% across seven TSFMs, with the largest gains on Chronos (29.81%), TimesFM (29.46%), and TimeMoE (23.10%). For ultra LTSF (L=1440), improvements widen to 18.45%–32.39% average MSE reduction. On efficiency, SPRINT reduces MACs by 343.62×, max GPU memory by 6.36×, and inference time by 16.87× on ETTm1 (H=2880, L=720, batch=12), while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPRINT is evaluated as a plug-and-play wrapper on seven TSFMs of different architectures (encoder-decoder, encoder-only, decoder-only, VLM-based), comparing each base model against its SPRINT-enhanced version. SPRINT wins on nearly every dataset for every backbone: Chronos improves by 29.81% avg MSE (e.g., Solar drops from 0.876→0.255), TimesFM by 29.46% (Solar 0.450→0.212), TimeMoE by 23.10% (Solar 0.513→0.204), VisionTS by 20.48% (ETTm1 0.709→0.452), Timer by 15.32% (Service 0.745→0.494), Moirai by 8.85%, and ToTo by 6.60%. The gains are most dramatic on high-frequency, strongly seasonal datasets (Solar, Service) where base TSFMs struggle most. Notably, Timer without SPRINT produces NaN predictions beyond L=720, while Timer+SPRINT functions robustly up to L=5760. On the non-periodic financial datasets (Exchange, NASDAQ, NYSE), SPRINT's adaptive downsampling still yields competitive or improved MSE compared to k=1 (no downsampling), confirming generality beyond seasonal data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPRINT repurposes classical signal-processing ideas—seasonal-trend decomposition via moving average, Nyquist-Shannon sampling, and spline interpolation—as an inference-time wrapper around existing TSFMs, rather than proposing a new architecture. It departs from the dominant LTSF strategies of patching (PatchTST) and multi-scale mixing (TimesNet, TimeMixer) by operating at the resolution level: the trend is downsampled before being fed to the TSFM, and the seasonal component bypasses the TSFM entirely via a theoretically grounded pattern-replication mechanism. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic and training-free, positioning itself as a universal adapter rather than a competing foundation model, and it draws on the AR(1) preservation property under downsampling to justify zero-shot transfer to the coarser resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SPRINT simultaneously improves accuracy (~19% average MSE reduction) and slashes inference cost (16.9× faster, 6.4× less memory), and it rescues models like Timer that produce NaN beyond L=720, enabling ultra-long horizons up to L=5760. Because it is training-free and plug-and-play, any future TSFM can benefit without retraining, making it a low-friction enhancement for the rapidly evolving TSFM landscape. Key limitations include the reliance on identifiable seasonality (mitigated but not fully solved for non-periodic data), the channel-independent treatment that ignores inter-variable correlations, and the unexplored potential of fine-tuning or learnable components within the SPRINT wrapper.
