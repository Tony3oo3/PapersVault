## Problem

Existing climate QA benchmarks are fundamentally limited: small expert-curated datasets (hundreds to a few thousand items) are text-only and too small for robust evaluation, while larger automated datasets suffer from noise due to weak filtering and lack of systematic human validation. Even multimodal resources that include figures often rely on textual descriptions or RAG setups rather than requiring direct pixel-level figure reasoning, leaving no benchmark that is simultaneously large-scale, truly multimodal, and rigorously validated.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes two input modalities: (1) textual questions in MCQ, cloze, and free-form formats grounded in Wikipedia articles and YouTube video transcripts, and (2) visual questions in MCQ, yes/no, and open-ended formats grounded in scientific figures from IPCC Assessment Reports and Our World in Data, each paired with its caption, legend, and surrounding text. Outputs are evaluated with task-appropriate metrics: accuracy for MCQ and yes/no, BERTScore for free-form, a composite weighted score (0.45·EM + 0.10·ROUGE-1 + 0.45·cosine similarity) for cloze, and LLM-as-a-judge (Llama-3.3-70B) for open-ended VQA. All items are single-evidence grounded to ensure attribution and auditability.

## Method

The pipeline proceeds through source retrieval (Wikipedia API + YouTube transcription), character-based sliding-window segmentation (3200/640 chars for articles, 500/50 for transcripts), atomic claim extraction via GPT-4.1-nano, semantic deduplication using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings with a 0.9 cosine-similarity threshold, and a FactGen module that cross-checks claims against authoritative sources via RAG. A key design choice decouples extraction and QA synthesis by using different LLMs (GPT-4.1-nano for claims, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo for question generation) to mitigate model-specific phrasing bias. The VQA branch combines manual annotation (4 annotators distilling 4–7 visual insights per figure) with automated template-based generation enforcing balanced distractors and unit-consistent answers. Five domain experts then perform iterative human-in-the-loop validation (210+ hours, κ=0.83 inter-annotator agreement), yielding 104,902 validated QA pairs. For the domain-adapted baseline, Llama 3.3 70B is fine-tuned with LoRA (r=64, α=128, all linear layers, 3 epochs, lr=1e-5) on the 66% training split, producing MMCLIMA-70B-TXT which achieves 78.24 overall weighted score, surpassing all closed- and open-source baselines.

## Results

On the textual QA benchmark, the fine-tuned MMCLIMA-70B-TXT achieves the best overall weighted score of 78.24, surpassing the strongest closed-source model (Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus, 70.54) and the strongest open-source model (DeepSeek-Chat, 75.03). On the visual QA benchmark, GPT-5 leads with an overall weighted score of 71.94, while the best open-source VLM, Pixtral-Large-2411, reaches 68.53. Cloze QA is the most discriminative task (all models score below 50), whereas free-form QA is comparatively easy (BERTScore >90 across most models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMCLIMA-70B-TXT (78.24 overall) outperforms all 27 compared LLMs on textual QA, beating the strongest open-source baseline DeepSeek-Chat (75.03) by 3.21 points and the strongest closed-source model Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus (70.54) by 7.70 points. The gap is most pronounced on MCQ (89.54 vs. 81.80 for DeepSeek-Chat) and free-form (95.47 vs. 93.78), while cloze remains the hardest task for all models (49.72 vs. 49.52). On VQA, GPT-5 (71.94) leads overall, edging out Claude-Sonnet-4 (69.16) primarily through superior free-form performance (58.99 vs. 51.18), while Claude-Sonnet-4 dominates yes/no accuracy (85.69 vs. 83.64). Among open-source VLMs, Pixtral-Large-2411 (68.53) is the strongest, surpassing Llama-4-Maverick (66.40) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (65.97), and comes within 3.4 points of the best closed-source system. The modality ablation (Table 8) further shows that transcript-derived questions are consistently easier than Wikipedia-derived ones across all models, with MMCLIMA-70B-TXT scoring 80.17 on transcripts vs. 78.08 on text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes and scales ideas from several prior climate QA efforts—automated claim extraction and QA synthesis (ClimaQA, Climate Crisis QA), expert validation (Pirá 2.0, ClimaQA-Gold), and figure-based QA (CPIQA)—into a single unified framework that simultaneously achieves large scale (104k+ pairs), true multimodality (text, video transcripts, and pixel-level figure reasoning), and systematic human validation. Methodologically, it draws on RAG-based fact verification (Lewis et al., 2020), decoupled LLM pipelines for extraction vs. synthesis to reduce phrasing bias, and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2022) for domain adaptation. Unlike CPIQA, which relies on textual descriptions of figures and RAG at inference time, MMCLIMA requires direct visual interpretation of scientific figures, and unlike ClimaQA or Climate Crisis QA, it extends beyond text to include video transcripts and a substantial VQA component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMCLIMA fills a clear gap: no prior climate QA resource combined scale, multimodality, and rigorous validation, leaving the field without a standardized benchmark for evaluating multimodal climate reasoning. The finding that a LoRA fine-tuned 70B model outperforms frontier closed-source APIs on domain textual QA underscores the value of high-quality domain data over raw model scale, a result likely to encourage similar domain-adaptation efforts in other scientific fields. Open questions include whether single-evidence grounding adequately captures the multi-source, uncertainty-laden reasoning that real climate analysis demands, whether the benchmark's English-only, Wikipedia/IPCC-centric sourcing introduces coverage biases, and whether the VQA component (smaller than the textual split) sufficiently stresses fine-grained visual skills like subpanel localization and unit fidelity.
