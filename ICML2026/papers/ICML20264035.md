## Problem

Text-guided editing with MMDiT-based diffusion models (e.g., FLUX.1, SD3) suffers from two intertwined failure modes: accumulated numerical drift from DDIM inversion and semantic leakage caused by the joint text-image attention stream, where prompt perturbations propagate globally and corrupt unrelated spatial regions. Unlike U-Net architectures where cross-attention provides a controlled gate, DiTs' all-to-all token interaction makes even small edits trigger structural collapse, a problem that compounds severely in multi-step editing scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (or video clip of 36–72 frames at 832×480) together with a source prompt describing the original content and a target prompt specifying the desired edit, and produces a structurally consistent edited image or video. It operates training-free on MMDiT backbones (FLUX.1, SD3, HunyuanVideo) at resolutions up to 1024×1024 for images, requiring only the pre-trained model weights with no fine-tuning or auxiliary networks.

## Method

T-Edit formalizes consistent editing as a trajectory anchoring process via a three-branch paradigm: an inversion branch records latent states and per-layer KV attention pairs during DDIM inversion, a reconstruction branch regenerates the original image to compute step-wise error signals, and an editing branch generates under the new prompt while being continuously corrected. Two key mechanisms address distinct failure modes: (1) Dynamic Vital Layer Localization (DVL) computes a Layer-wise Information Energy via SVD on the stacked Value tensors at each timestep, adaptively selecting the structurally most influential attention layers (using an Information Preservation Ratio τ=0.9) for direct KV replacement; (2) Frequency-aware t-SVD Anchoring (TA) decomposes K/V tensors in non-vital layers via FFT + per-frequency SVD, injecting only high-frequency structural components with a dynamic fusion factor λ_t = σ(‖δ_t^drift‖²) that scales protection strength with reconstruction error. Online error correction at each denoising step aligns the editing trajectory with the structural baseline, and the entire pipeline is training-free with no additional forward passes beyond the standard inversion and generation.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, T-Edit achieves the best FID (62.22) and highest CLIP score (31.55) among all compared methods, indicating superior generation quality and semantic alignment. On structural preservation, T-Edit attains LPIPS of 0.1088 and PSNR of 23.89, closely approaching the mask-based KV-Edit (LPIPS 0.0950, PSNR 27.17) while substantially outperforming all other DiT-based baselines. In multi-step editing (Table 2), T-Edit maintains the highest CLIP scores across all three steps (33.12, 32.91, 31.09) while keeping LPIPS competitive with KV-Edit. For video editing on Ditto-1M (Table 3), T-Edit achieves the best FID (65.87), CLIP (33.28), Temporal LPIPS (0.182), and Flow Error (0.131).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-Edit achieves the best FID (62.22) and CLIP score (31.55) across all eight baselines, outperforming the next-best RF-Edit (FID 65.16, CLIP 30.27) by a clear margin on both generation quality and semantic alignment. On structural preservation, KV-Edit leads with LPIPS 0.0950 and PSNR 27.17 due to its mask-guided region-constrained editing, while T-Edit is a close second on LPIPS (0.1088) but trails by ~3.3 dB on PSNR (23.89). Among non-mask DiT-based methods (Flow-Edit, RF-Edit, FreeFlux, Stable Flow), T-Edit dominates on all four metrics, with the largest gaps over Flow-Edit (FID 77.65, LPIPS 0.2627) and Stable Flow (FID 87.13, LPIPS 0.1527). U-Net-based methods P2P and MasaCtrl are substantially worse across all metrics (FID >120, LPIPS >0.47), confirming the architectural advantage of MMDiTs-based editing. In multi-step editing, T-Edit maintains the highest CLIP at every step while KV-Edit achieves the lowest LPIPS, and in video editing T-Edit outperforms FateZero, COVE, and RF-Edit on all four metrics including temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-Edit extends the inversion-based editing lineage (DDIM inversion, Null-Text Inversion) from U-Net backbones to MMDiTs architectures, where joint text-image attention fundamentally changes how prompt perturbations propagate. It builds directly on Stable Flow's identification of 'vital layers' for structural anchoring but departs by making layer selection dynamic and content-dependent rather than fixed via batch ablation. The frequency-aware t-SVD anchoring mechanism is a novel contribution that repurposes tensor decomposition for spectral-domain attention control, complementing the spatial-domain feature injection used by prior methods like MasaCtrl and KV-Edit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that structurally vital layers shift across both samples and denoising timesteps challenges the working assumption in prior work (e.g., Stable Flow) that a universal fixed layer set suffices, suggesting that static heuristics are inherently suboptimal for MMDiTs editing. The training-free three-branch paradigm offers a practical, plug-and-play solution for consistent editing that extends naturally to multi-step and video scenarios without retraining. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain perturbation analysis generalizes beyond FLUX.1/SD3 to other DiT variants, whether the approach scales to ultra-long video sequences, and whether spectral constraints during pretraining could make such post-hoc anchoring unnecessary.
