## Problem

Existing data augmentation methods for offline RL generate trajectories via a single rollout direction (forward-future or backward-history) conditioned on in-dataset states, which preserves the original trajectory-level connectivity and fails to bridge disconnected behavior patterns. This means synthesized data introduces only local variations around observed trajectories rather than recovering global connections between distinct behavioral modes absent from the dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263757/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline trajectory dataset D, samples anchor states s_t from D, and pairs them with manually selected target return signals R^f and R^b as conditional inputs. It produces completed state-action-reward trajectories of effective span 2H−2 transitions (H=5 by default), where the anchor state is held fixed during both training and denoising. A two-stage filter (Isolation Forest for OOD detection, then greedy selection by cumulative reward) retains the final augmented set, which is mixed with D at a 30% ratio for downstream offline RL training.

## Method

BiTrajDiff trains two independent diffusion models—a forward model generating future state sequences from anchor s_t and a backward model generating history sequences ending at s_t—both using a 1D DiT backbone (2 transformer blocks with adaptive layer normalization) under VP-SDE with 20 denoising steps and Classifier-Free Guidance on the cumulative return condition. The key architectural insight is that factorizing generation around a shared anchor into two conditional problems allows sampling candidate pairings from the full product space of locally supported incoming and outgoing patterns, rather than being restricted to the sparse observed coupling set. Generated forward and backward segments are stitched at the anchor, then completed with actions and rewards via a supervised Inverse Dynamics Model and Reward Model. A two-stage filter (OOD via Isolation Forest anomaly scoring, then greedy top-reward selection) discards low-confidence and suboptimal trajectories, yielding a high-fidelity augmented dataset that recovers trajectory-level connectivity absent from the original data.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks, BiTrajDiff achieves an average IQL score of 85.5 and TD3BC score of 83.5, outperforming the best single-direction DA baselines (Synther: 82.2 IQL, 78.6 TD3BC) by 3.3 and 4.9 points respectively. On sparse-reward maze tasks, BiTrajDiff reaches 69.7 (IQL) and 45.6 (TD3BC) average, surpassing Synther (64.0/37.9) and RTDiff (61.0/38.0). On Franka Kitchen tasks, BiTrajDiff achieves 63.9 (IQL) and 15.1 (TD3BC), with the TD3BC gain being particularly large (over 2× the base of 6.9). The method also extends to CQL and DT backbones, achieving 83.9 and 71.8 average locomotion scores respectively, and leads on OGBench stitching tasks with 63.0 (IQL) and 74.7 (FQL) averages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiTrajDiff consistently outperforms all three primary DA baselines (Synther, RTDiff, DiffStitch) across IQL, TD3BC, CQL, and DT backbones on D4RL locomotion, maze, and kitchen tasks. The largest gains appear on hopper-medium (IQL: 81.1 vs. 100.6 for Synther on med-replay, and 79.0 vs. 76.4 for Synther on TD3BC med-replay) and sparse-reward maze tasks where BiTrajDiff leads Synther by 5.7 points (IQL) and 7.7 points (TD3BC). On OGBench stitching tasks (Table S4), BiTrajDiff (63.0 IQL, 74.7 FQL) surpasses ASTRO (57.4/60.1), DiffStitch (47.4/48.4), SSD (56.0/70.3), and Synther (48.2/44.0), confirming its advantage over both model-based rollout and direct segment-stitching approaches. In goal-conditioned settings (Table S5), BiTrajDiff (21.7 GCIQL, 33.7 CRL) edges out SCoTS (20.0/33.0) and CD/CompDiffuser (19.3/29.7). Against the world-model-based OTTO and diffusion-based GTA (Figure S1), BiTrajDiff achieves more consistent and larger performance improvements across IQL and TD3BC, particularly on long-horizon tasks where single-direction methods struggle to bridge disconnected state regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiTrajDiff builds directly on the return-conditioned diffusion trajectory generation paradigm established by Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2022) and implemented via the CleanDiffuser library (Dong et al., 2024), but departs from all prior DA-for-offline-RL methods by decomposing trajectory synthesis into two independent diffusion processes (forward-future and backward-history) conditioned on shared anchor states, then stitching the segments. This contrasts with single-direction methods (Synther, RTDiff) that generate only one temporal direction, and with existing stitching methods (DiffStitch, ASTRO, SSD) that either autoregressively roll out or generate unconstrained middle segments between endpoints. The paper's key conceptual departure is the anchor-centered factorization, which samples candidate trajectory pairings from the product space of locally supported incoming and outgoing patterns rather than being restricted to the observed coupling structure in the dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional stitching mechanism addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation in offline RL data augmentation: single-direction generators preserve the trajectory-level connectivity of the dataset and cannot bridge disconnected behavior patterns. The approach is practical (moderate compute overhead, plug-and-play with existing offline RL backbones) and shows consistent gains across IQL, TD3BC, CQL, and DT on D4RL locomotion, maze, and kitchen tasks. However, the method still relies on an inverse dynamics model for action/reward completion, which introduces accumulated errors that degrade performance under longer n-step TD estimation, and the evaluation is limited to continuous-control D4RL benchmarks. Open questions include whether the bidirectional factorization extends to multi-task or discrete-action settings, and whether the 30% augmentation ratio generalizes across task complexity.
