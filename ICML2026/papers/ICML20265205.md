## Problem

Domain-specialized LLMs are efficient but fail to generalize beyond their training distributions, while general-purpose LLMs are prohibitively expensive. Prior token-level multi-LLM collaboration methods (e.g., Collab) are brittle: they depend heavily on expert quality and correct selection, and the paper proves that pure expert-only routing cannot recover the optimal policy without strong global coverage assumptions. The paper asks whether a token-level collaboration paradigm can be both robust to expert failures and efficient, without requiring joint training or per-token evaluation across all models.

## Input / Output

At inference, FUSIONROUTE takes a text prompt and a fixed set of off-the-shelf specialized LLMs (e.g., a math expert, a code expert, and an instruction-following expert, all from the same model family). At each decoding step, a lightweight router LLM (initialized from one of the experts) produces two outputs simultaneously: a vector of routing weights selecting one expert, and its own next-token logits. The final next-token distribution is the element-wise sum of the selected expert's logits and the router's complementary logits, from which the next token is sampled or greedily decoded. No gradient access to the experts is needed at inference time.

## Method

FUSIONROUTE introduces a router that unifies expert selection and knowledge complementation: a linear projection on the router's final hidden state yields routing weights for expert selection, while the router's own LM head produces complementary logits added to the selected expert's logits (Eq. 1). Training is decoupled into two phases: (1) SFT with a combined language-modeling loss and a routing loss restricted to 'informative' tokens where experts disagree, establishing stable routing; and (2) CDPO (Complemented Direct Preference Optimization), a modified DPO objective where the expert's log-ratio acts as a non-backpropagated bias, so the router learns to supply corrective logits precisely when the expert is weak. A mix-training schedule alternates SFT batches (updating all parameters including the routing layer) with CDPO batches (updating only the base LM parameters, excluding the routing projection) to prevent preference optimization from degrading routing. Theoretically, the paper shows via an identifiability argument (Theorem 4.3) that pure token-level expert selection is fundamentally insufficient under realistic coverage assumptions, while the complementary logit expansion allows the combined policy to approximate the optimal policy under a mild total-variation-distance condition, yielding a performance bound of T·Q_max·Δ.

## Results

On the Llama-3 family, FUSIONROUTE achieves an average accuracy of 0.566 across GSM8K, MATH500, MBPP, HumanEval, and IfEval, outperforming the best single expert (Instruct Expert, 0.512), the fine-tuned model (0.536), and all collaboration/merging baselines. On the Gemma-2 family, FUSIONROUTE reaches 0.426 average accuracy, surpassing the best expert (Math Expert, 0.398) and the fine-tuned model (0.394). FUSIONROUTE also matches or exceeds domain experts on their strongest benchmarks (e.g., 0.82 on GSM8K matching the Math Expert's 0.86, and 0.63 on HumanEval exceeding the Code Expert's 0.66 in the Llama-3 family).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSIONROUTE consistently outperforms all three primary baselines across both model families. Against Collab (Chakraborty et al., 2025), the prior token-level collaboration method, FUSIONROUTE achieves 0.566 vs. 0.502 average accuracy on Llama-3 (+6.4 points) and 0.426 vs. 0.360 on Gemma-2 (+6.6 points), with particularly large gains on HumanEval (0.63 vs. 0.54) and IfEval (0.69 vs. 0.55). Against DARE (Yu et al., 2024), the model merging approach, FUSIONROUTE leads by a wide margin: 0.566 vs. 0.368 on Llama-3 and 0.426 vs. 0.224 on Gemma-2, indicating that static parameter merging cannot replicate the dynamic, token-level expert selection. Against TaskArithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), FUSIONROUTE again dominates: 0.566 vs. 0.424 on Llama-3 and 0.426 vs. 0.268 on Gemma-2. FUSIONROUTE also surpasses the directly fine-tuned model (0.566 vs. 0.536 on Llama-3; 0.426 vs. 0.394 on Gemma-2), confirming that the complementary logit mechanism provides gains beyond what a single fine-tuned model can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSIONROUTE sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: mixture-of-experts routing (Zhou et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2024a), token-level multi-agent collaboration (Collab, Chakraborty et al., 2025; CO-LLM, Shen et al., 2024), and preference optimization (DPO, Rafailov et al., 2023). It departs from MoE by requiring no joint training or architectural compatibility among experts, from sequence-level MAS by operating at token granularity, and from prior token-level methods (Collab, CO-LLM) by adding a trainable complementary logit that corrects expert failures rather than merely selecting among fixed expert outputs. The CDPO training stage is a direct modification of DPO, repurposed to train the router's base model to produce corrective logits while keeping the routing head frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical impossibility result (Theorem 4.3) showing that pure token-level expert routing cannot recover the optimal policy even under single-policy and generalization coverage assumptions is a meaningful conceptual contribution that reframes why prior token-level collaboration methods plateau. Practically, FUSIONROUTE offers a lightweight, training-free-for-experts framework that outperforms model merging and fine-tuning across math, code, and instruction-following domains, making it a viable recipe for assembling general-purpose systems from specialized checkpoints. Open questions include scalability beyond three experts, applicability to larger model families (70B+), whether the complementary logit mechanism transfers to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based generation, and how the framework behaves when expert quality is highly imbalanced.
