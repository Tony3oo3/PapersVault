## Problem

Template-free retrosynthesis methods treat the task as a black-box graph-to-graph transformation, forcing the model to implicitly learn both reaction center identification and synthon completion simultaneously, which limits learning efficiency. Semi-template methods decompose the task but rely on rigid reaction libraries that constrain generalization. The paper asks whether the two-stage structure of chemical reactions can be encoded as a positional inductive bias within a fully template-free generative framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264908/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a product molecular graph whose atoms are reordered so that reaction center atoms occupy the sequence head, the molecular scaffold follows in the body, and K dummy placeholder nodes (K=10 for USPTO-50k, K=20 for USPTO-Full) are appended at the tail to accommodate leaving groups absent from the product. During training, ground-truth reaction centers from atom mapping determine the root; during inference, a lightweight R-GCN predictor supplies top-k candidate roots. The output is a reactant molecular graph generated by evolving the product through a continuous-time Markov chain in 20–50 Euler steps, with multiple candidate reactant sets sampled and ranked by model likelihood.

## Method

The core mechanism is reaction-center-rooted atom ordering: by rooting a canonical graph traversal at a reaction center atom, the two-stage decomposition (identify where the reaction occurs, then generate what the reactants look like) is encoded as a concrete positional pattern rather than an implicit learning objective. To exploit this ordering, the authors design RetroDiT, a graph transformer backbone that injects Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) into self-attention so that relative sequence positions—which directly reflect topological distance from the reaction center—modulate attention weights, enabling the model to distinguish head (reaction center), body (scaffold), and tail (dummy) regions. Training uses a simulation-free discrete flow matching objective (following DeFoG) that interpolates linearly between the product graph (t=0) and reactant graph (t=1) via a cross-entropy loss on intermediate states, decoupling training from sampling. At inference, the trained network's predicted target distribution is used to construct a rate matrix for CTMC-based Euler sampling, requiring only 20–50 steps versus 500 for prior diffusion-based methods. The framework is modular: the R-GCN reaction center predictor and the RetroDiT generative backbone are trained and optimized independently, so improvements in either component directly boost overall performance without retraining the other.

## Results

On USPTO-50k, the method achieves 61.2% Top-1 exact match accuracy with predicted reaction centers, and 71.1% with oracle reaction centers. On USPTO-Full, it reaches 51.3% Top-1 (predicted RC) and 63.4% (oracle RC). Generation requires only 20–50 CTMC sampling steps versus 500 for prior diffusion-based methods, and training converges 6× faster than baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all template-free baselines on USPTO-50k, beating the closest competitor EditRetro (60.8%) by 0.4 points and RetroSynFlow (60.0%) by 1.2 points in Top-1 accuracy, while surpassing the diffusion-based RetroBridge (50.8%) by a substantial 10.4-point margin. It also exceeds all semi-template methods (best: Graph2Edits at 55.1%) and template-based methods (best: RetroComposer at 54.5%). The only method that outperforms it in the predicted-RC setting is RSGPT (63.4% vs. 61.2%), a 3.2B-parameter LLM pretrained on 10 billion synthetic reactions, whereas this work uses only 50k training samples and ~8M parameters. On USPTO-Full, the oracle-RC setting (63.4%) surpasses RSGPT (59.2%) by 4.2 points, and the predicted-RC setting (51.3%) beats all non-LLM baselines including NAG2G (49.7%) and R-SMILES (48.9%). The 10-point gap between predicted (61.2%) and oracle (71.1%) settings on USPTO-50k identifies reaction center prediction as the primary bottleneck rather than the generative backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of template-free retrosynthesis and discrete flow matching for graph generation. It builds directly on DeFoG (Qin et al., 2024) for the CTMC-based sampling procedure and on the discrete flow matching framework (Gat et al., 2024; Campbell et al., 2024) for the training objective. The key departure from prior template-free methods (RetroBridge, RetroSynFlow, EditRetro) is the introduction of reaction-center-rooted atom ordering as a positional inductive bias, combined with RoPE in a graph transformer (RetroDiT) to exploit this ordering. This effectively imports the structural decomposition insight of semi-template methods (G2G, RetroXpert) into a fully template-free generative framework, while the modular RC-predictor + generator design contrasts with the end-to-end black-box approach of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a 280K-parameter model with reaction-center-rooted ordering matches a 65M-parameter model without it—challenges the prevailing scaling paradigm in molecular generative modeling and suggests that domain-specific structural priors can substitute for orders of magnitude more parameters. The modular design identifies reaction center prediction as the primary bottleneck (predicted RC: 61.2% vs. oracle RC: 71.1% on USPTO-50k), providing a clear roadmap for future improvement without retraining the generator. Open questions include whether this ordering-based inductive bias generalizes to multi-step retrosynthetic planning, to reaction types outside the USPTO distribution, and whether the crossover point (~35-40% RC accuracy below which ordering hurts) can be pushed lower with better predictors.
