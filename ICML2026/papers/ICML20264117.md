## Problem

Fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts architectures (e.g., PEER, PKM) achieve superior parameter efficiency by routing to millions of tiny experts, but suffer from three compounding system failures at scale: limited per-expert expressivity (reduced to linear vector aggregation), prohibitive O(N) routing cost, and memory-bound execution from scattered, non-contiguous HBM accesses. Coarse-grained MoEs avoid these issues but waste computation on irrelevant parameters within large expert blocks. The paper asks whether the parameter efficiency of fine-grained experts can be reconciled with the hardware efficiency of coarse-grained designs through holistic system-algorithm co-design.

## Input / Output

At the MoE layer level, the method consumes a token hidden-state vector x ∈ R^d and produces a transformed representation y ∈ R^d that is the sum of a routed fine-grained branch (top-K atomic expert composition) and a universally active shared dense MLP. The atomic experts are not stored as individual parameter blocks but as rows in two global matrices W, V ∈ R^{N×d} (N up to 102,400), so the 'expert' is a pair of d-dimensional vectors retrieved on the fly. At the model level, the full transformer takes a text token sequence and produces next-token predictions, trained self-supervisedly on 40B tokens from SmolLMCorpus.

## Method

OmniMoE introduces three tightly coupled components. First, Atomic Experts with Dynamic Expert Assembly (DEA) parameterize each expert as a minimal (w_in, w_out) vector pair stored in global matrices, and for each token dynamically retrieve and compose top-K rows into a token-specific nonlinear computation y = (g_x ⊙ σ(x w_x^T)) v_x + MLP(x), preserving per-expert expressivity (SWIGLU nonlinearity) while enabling million-scale expert pools. Second, the Cartesian Product Router decomposes the 1D expert index space of size N into a 2D grid (N_r × N_c ≈ √N × √N), replacing a single d×N routing projection with two d×√N projections whose log-softmax outputs are summed element-wise to score the implicit grid, reducing routing FLOPs from O(Nd) to O(√N·d) and enabling parallel tiled top-K selection without materializing the full score matrix. Third, Expert-Centric Scheduling inverts the execution loop from token-centric to expert-centric: it flattens all (token, expert, weight) tasks, groups physically adjacent active experts into contiguous blocks of size B, hierarchically sorts tasks by (group ID, token ID), and fuses each group's computation into a single Grouped GEMM kernel, converting scattered random HBM reads into coalesced streaming accesses that maximize Tensor Core utilization. Together these yield a 10.9× inference speedup over PEER (73 ms → 6.7 ms at 4096 tokens) while achieving 50.9% average zero-shot accuracy across seven benchmarks.

## Results

On seven downstream commonsense benchmarks (MMLU, TriviaQA, ARC, PIQA, HellaSwag, OBQA, Winogrande), OmniMoE (6.4B total / 1.7B active params) achieves 50.9% average zero-shot accuracy, outperforming the best coarse-grained baseline DeepSeekMoE (50.2%) and the best fine-grained baseline PEER (48.9%). On five extended reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, BBH, MBPP, HumanEval), OmniMoE scores 42.5% average, beating DeepSeekMoE (40.5%) by +2.0 and PEER (27.0%) by +15.5. In system efficiency, OmniMoE reduces inference latency from 73 ms (PEER) and 102 ms (DeepSeekMoE) to 6.7 ms at 4,096 tokens, yielding 10.9× and 15.2× speedups respectively, while achieving the highest throughput (~14.2k tok/s) among all MoE variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniMoE outperforms all four baselines on average downstream accuracy: +0.7 over DeepSeekMoE (50.9 vs. 50.2), +1.7 over Gshard (49.2), +2.0 over PEER (48.9), and +4.7 over PKM (46.2). Against the coarse-grained DeepSeekMoE, OmniMoE gains most on knowledge-intensive tasks (TriviaQA +1.1, OBQA +1.4) and achieves a 15.2× latency reduction (102 ms → 6.7 ms) by eliminating the padding/alignment overhead inherent to tiled coarse-grained kernels. Against the fine-grained PEER, OmniMoE's shared dense MLP branch closes a large reasoning gap (ARC +3.6, HellaSwag +4.6, GSM8K +38.4) that purely vector-aggregation experts cannot handle, while Expert-Centric Scheduling delivers a 10.9× speedup (73 ms → 6.7 ms) by converting scattered HBM reads into coalesced Grouped GEMM operations. PKM, the earliest fine-grained design, trails on both accuracy and throughput (~7.9k tok/s vs. OmniMoE's ~14.2k), confirming that without a shared dense backbone and efficient scheduling, million-scale atomic experts remain impractical. Gshard, the simplest coarse-grained baseline, is outperformed by OmniMoE on every individual benchmark and by a wide margin on average (49.2 vs. 50.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniMoE sits at the intersection of two MoE design lineages: coarse-grained FFN experts (GShard, DeepSeekMoE) that are hardware-efficient but activate redundant parameters, and fine-grained vector-level experts (PKM, PEER) that maximize routing precision but suffer from scattered memory access and limited per-expert expressivity. The Cartesian Product Router directly adapts PKM's product-structured indexing principle—decomposing a large 1D index space into two smaller projections—but applies it to a novel vector-level expert formulation rather than static memory lookups. The key departure from prior work is the system-algorithm co-design: by pairing atomic experts (a single input-output vector pair with SWIGLU) with a shared dense MLP and inverting execution order via Expert-Centric Scheduling, OmniMoE reconciles the parameter efficiency of fine-grained routing with the hardware efficiency of dense matrix operations, a combination not previously explored at this granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.9× inference speedup over PEER and the 15.2× over DeepSeekMoE demonstrate that million-scale fine-grained MoE can be both accurate and fast, removing a major practical barrier to deploying highly specialized expert pools. The ablation showing expert usage collapsing to 4% without the Cartesian Product Router highlights a previously underappreciated failure mode: standard dense routing simply cannot learn balanced specializations over massive expert spaces. However, the validation is limited to 6.4B total parameters and custom Triton kernels on NVIDIA A100s, leaving open whether the Cartesian Product Router's independence assumption (p(i,j|x) ≈ p_r(i|x)·p_c(j|x)) holds at larger scales, whether the approach ports to non-NVIDIA hardware, and whether the shared dense MLP's role shifts as model size grows.
