## Problem

Existing MoE compression methods (expert dropping and coarse-grained merging) suffer from severe accuracy degradation—over 20% on MMLU at 50% compression—because they operate at the level of entire experts or weight tensors, failing to distinguish shared parameters from expert-specific weights. Fine-grained element-wise merging could preserve both, but faces two practical barriers: the combinatorial complexity of per-element decisions and the metadata overhead of storing binary masks and sign bits, which can negate the memory savings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MoE model's expert weight matrices in BFloat16 format and a small calibration set (128 samples, sequence length 2048, from C4) to compute activation-based saliency scores. It produces a compressed model where each pair of experts is replaced by a single merged weight tensor in standard BFloat16 format, with mask and sign information embedded in the 3 underutilized exponent bits—requiring no auxiliary metadata storage. At inference, the compressed model accepts standard text input and produces text output, with a custom decode-GEMM kernel reconstructing per-expert weights on-the-fly during matrix multiplication.

## Method

PuzzleMoE introduces a dual-mask element-wise merging algorithm: a similarity mask (based on symmetric percent difference with threshold τ_sim=0.4) identifies weight elements with comparable magnitude between expert pairs for safe averaging, while a Wanda-style saliency mask (|W| ⊙ ‖X‖₂) selects which expert's weights to retain at dissimilar positions. The merged expert stores only magnitudes, with original signs and per-element selection masks re-applied at inference. To eliminate metadata overhead, the method exploits the narrow exponent range (112–128) observed in BFloat16 expert weights, shifting exponents into a 5-bit range and repurposing the freed 3 bits to pack mask and sign information directly into the weight tensor. A custom Triton decode-GEMM kernel performs on-the-fly bit-level decoding piggybacked on the data-loading path, achieving ~2× throughput over cuBLAS by computing both experts' outputs from a single memory access to the packed tensor. The entire compression is training-free, single-pass, and completes in minutes (2 min for Mixtral-8x7B), with random pairwise grouping performing comparably to search-based alternatives.

## Results

On Mixtral-8x7B at 50% expert sparsity, PuzzleMoE achieves 72.6% average accuracy across 7 reasoning benchmarks and 4.36 WikiText-2 perplexity, outperforming the dense model's 74.1% by only 1.5 points while halving expert memory. On MMLU specifically, PuzzleMoE scores 65.7% at 50% sparsity, a 16.7-point improvement over HC-SMoE (49.0%). Across all four evaluated MoE models (Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE, Qwen1.5-MoE, Qwen3-MoE), PuzzleMoE consistently achieves the best accuracy at both 25% and 50% sparsity, with degradation of only 0.1–1.4% from the dense baseline. The method also delivers up to 1.80× end-to-end inference throughput gain and compresses Mixtral-8x7B in just 2 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuzzleMoE outperforms all six baselines (NAEE, STUN, D2, HC-SMoE, Sub-MoE, Wanda) on every model and sparsity level tested. The largest margin is against HC-SMoE at 50% sparsity: on Mixtral-8x7B, PuzzleMoE achieves 72.6% avg accuracy vs. HC-SMoE's 63.8% (8.8-point gap), and on DeepSeek-MoE the gap widens to 19.5 points (62.1% vs. 42.6%), with HC-SMoE's perplexity exploding to 89.94. Against the expert-dropping baseline NAEE, PuzzleMoE leads by 6.2 points on Mixtral at 50% (72.6% vs. 66.4%) and by 23.1 points on GSM8K (51.7% vs. 28.6%). Compared to Wanda's 2:4 structured pruning, PuzzleMoE gains 3.9 points on Mixtral (72.6% vs. 68.7%) and 1.5 points on DeepSeek (62.1% vs. 58.9%). D2 and Sub-MoE report results only at 40% or 25% sparsity respectively, but where comparable, PuzzleMoE still leads (e.g., 72.6% vs. D2's partial results at 40% on Mixtral). On domain-specific math reasoning (Qwen3-MoE, Table 3), PuzzleMoE retains 96.2/71.1/61.5 on Math-500/AIME24/AIME25 at 25% sparsity, while HC-SMoE collapses to 24.6/0.0/0.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wanda saliency metric (Sun et al., 2024) from dense-model pruning to MoE expert merging, but its core departure is from all prior MoE compression methods (NAEE, STUN, D2, HC-SMoE, Sub-MoE) that operate at expert-level or tensor-level granularity. PuzzleMoE introduces element-wise merging guided by a dual-mask design (similarity + saliency), which is the first such fine-grained approach for MoE. The bit-packing scheme builds on observations of BFloat16 exponent underutilization (Zhang et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2025a) but repurposes those freed bits to embed masks and signs, eliminating the metadata overhead that would otherwise negate fine-grained compression savings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures become the dominant scaling paradigm (Mixtral, DeepSeek, Qwen), their memory footprint is the primary barrier to practical deployment; PuzzleMoE's 50% expert compression with <2% accuracy loss and 1.8× throughput gain makes single-GPU or reduced-GPU deployment feasible. The bit-packing + decode-GEMM co-design is a practical systems contribution that avoids the common pitfall where sparsity metadata erases memory savings. Open questions include whether the 2-to-1 bit-packing generalizes cleanly to higher compression ratios (the algorithm supports it but requires explicit mask storage), and whether the approach transfers to other floating-point formats or non-Gaussian weight distributions.
