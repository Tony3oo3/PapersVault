## Problem

State-based Predictive Coding (sPC), the canonical formulation of PC, suffers from exponential signal decay during digital simulation: training signals attenuate multiplicatively at each layer traversal, requiring exponentially many update steps to reach deep layers and causing deeper PC networks to perform worse than shallower ones. No general solution existed for standard feedforward architectures using exact PC weight gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261290/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (MNIST/FashionMNIST at 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32) along with their target class labels, and produces a class prediction from the network's output layer. Unlike backpropagation's single forward-backward pass, ePC iteratively refines a set of per-layer prediction errors until the energy function reaches equilibrium, at which point local weight gradients are computed. The errors are initialized to zero (rather than copying feedforward activations as in sPC), and states are derived recursively from errors via a sequential forward pass at each iteration.

## Method

ePC reparameterizes PC by making prediction errors ε the primary optimization variables instead of states s, restructuring the computational graph from locally-connected to globally-connected so that the output loss gradient reaches all layers simultaneously via reverse-mode automatic differentiation. This eliminates the exponential signal decay inherent in sPC's local-only update rule, where each layer-to-layer signal transfer is attenuated by the state learning rate λ. The energy function is mathematically identical to sPC's, and a bijective mapping between states and errors guarantees that both formulations converge to the same equilibrium, yielding exact PC weight gradients. The training loop alternates between iterative error updates (using global reverse-mode AD for gradient computation) and local weight updates (identical to sPC's formula), with the key practical difference being that ePC converges in a handful of steps rather than the exponentially many required by sPC for deep networks.

## Results

On the deepest architecture (ResNet-18, CIFAR-100, MSE loss), ePC achieves 68.52% top-1 accuracy, matching backprop (69.94%) while sPC collapses to 22.83%. On VGG-9 (CIFAR-100, MSE), ePC reaches 61.35% vs. sPC's 45.70% and backprop's 66.28%. On shallower models (4-layer MLP, MNIST), all three methods perform comparably (~98.3% for MSE). In the proof-of-concept 20-layer linear network, ePC converges to the analytical equilibrium over 100× faster than sPC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is state-based PC (sPC), the canonical formulation of predictive coding, with backpropagation serving as the neutral gold standard. ePC decisively outperforms sPC on all deep architectures: on ResNet-18 (CIFAR-100, MSE), ePC achieves 68.52% top-1 accuracy versus sPC's 22.83%—a 45.7-point gap—while matching backprop (69.94%) within 1.4 points. The gap widens with depth: VGG-5 shows a 14-point sPC deficit (50.41% vs. 64.37%), VGG-7 a 23.7-point deficit (42.90% vs. 66.55%), and VGG-9 a 15.7-point deficit (45.70% vs. 61.35%). On shallow 4-layer MLPs, all three methods perform comparably (within ~1% on MNIST), confirming that sPC's failure is specifically a depth-scaling issue. ePC matches or slightly trails backprop on most settings, with the largest remaining gap on VGG-9 CIFAR-100 (61.35% vs. 66.28%), while on ResNet-18 CIFAR-10 ePC (92.17%) is statistically indistinguishable from backprop (92.36%). The paper also notes that sPC was unstable for ResNet-18 in their implementation, with those results copied from Pinchetti et al. (2025).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the canonical state-based Predictive Coding (sPC) formulation of Whittington & Bogacz (2017) and the broader PC-as-learning-algorithm literature (Bogacz 2017; Millidge et al. 2022a), but departs from it by identifying a previously unrecognized exponential signal decay mechanism that fundamentally limits sPC's digital simulation. It positions itself against the depth-scaling failures documented by Pinchetti et al. (2025) and the partial remedies proposed by Qi et al. (2025), Ha et al. (2026), and Innocenti et al. (2025, μPC), arguing that all of these address symptoms rather than the core hardware-algorithm mismatch. The ePC reparameterization draws a conceptual parallel to the VAE reparameterization trick (Kingma & Welling 2013) and the Least-Control Principle (Meulemans et al. 2022), while using reverse-mode automatic differentiation as its computational backbone to achieve global signal propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving sPC's exponential signal decay, ePC makes deep PC training practically feasible in digital simulation (100×+ speedup, matching backprop on ResNet-18 where sPC is unstable), which is a prerequisite for the iterative verification needed to develop neuromorphic PC hardware. The finding that μPC's celebrated 128-layer training actually leaves 80+ layers as untrained pass-throughs reframes a positive result as a silent failure, with implications for how the community interprets prior PC depth-scaling claims. Key limitations remain: ePC is not bio-plausible (it requires reverse-mode AD), experiments are confined to supervised image classification where backprop is already strong, and whether PC's distinctive advantages in online/continual learning extend to deep architectures with ePC is still open.
