## Problem

Estimating f-divergences (KL, JS, Hellinger, χ², etc.) from samples typically requires density-ratio estimation, which suffers from severe statistical error in moderate-to-high dimensions, or variational/neural surrogates that demand iterative optimization and can inherit adversarial-training instabilities. This paper asks whether one can build a divergence estimator that depends only on order information (ranks) rather than densities, while retaining the full flexibility of the f-divergence family.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N i.i.d. samples from μ and M i.i.d. samples from ν (the reference), along with a user-chosen rank resolution K ∈ ℕ. It outputs a scalar lower-bound estimate of the f-divergence D_{f,ν}(μ) computed purely by sorting and counting operations—no density evaluation or optimization is needed. In the generative-modeling application, the same rank energy drives a particle-transport update that takes N Gaussian-initialized particles and M reference images (e.g., 64×64 CIFAR-10) and produces transported particle positions that progressively match the target distribution.

## Method

The core construction applies the probability integral transform: for a draw Y~μ and K i.i.d. draws from ν, the rank statistic A^{(K)} = #{j : Ỹ_j ≤ Y} ∈ {0,…,K} encodes where Y falls relative to the ν-sample. The resulting rank histogram Q_{μ|ν}^{(K)} (a PMF on K+1 bins) is compared to the uniform law U_K via a discrete f-divergence, yielding D_{f,ν}^{(K)}(μ). This estimator is convex, monotone nondecreasing in K, always a lower bound of the true divergence, and converges to D_{f,ν}(μ) at rates O(K^{-α/2}) or O(K^{-1}) depending on the regularity of the quantile-domain density ratio. For d-dimensional data, the sliced version averages the univariate construction over L random unit-vector projections. For generative transport, the rank energy is minimized in quantile space via a proximal step (approximated by SGD or Langevin sampling), and the refined rank coordinates are mapped back to data space through the empirical quantile of the reference slice, producing a bounded, optimization-free particle update.

## Results

In 1D benchmarks (Table 1), the rank-statistic estimator achieves approximation ratios (estimate/reference) of 0.999±0.035 for JS divergence on Gaussian mean-shift (Δ=1.0) at K=512, and 0.980±0.027 for KL on the same setting. For harder cases like Laplace-vs-Gaussian (heavy tails, JS), the ratio reaches only 0.933±0.046 at K=512, reflecting the slower convergence for non-smooth density ratios. In the sliced high-dimensional setting (Figure 3), the d-scaled ratio stays close to 1 across dimensions for all three divergences, with squared Hellinger being the most stable. Against the neural KL baseline of Sreekumar et al. (2021), the rank-statistic estimator tracks the analytic KL reference more closely with tighter uncertainty bands, particularly at smaller sample sizes and higher dimensions (d=5, 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct quantitative baseline is the neural f-divergence estimator of Sreekumar et al. (2021), compared on a synthetic Gaussian-vs-uniform KL estimation task across dimensions d∈{2,5,10} (Figure 2). The rank-statistic estimator consistently tracks the analytic KL reference more closely than the neural baseline, with the gap narrowing rapidly as sample size n increases; at d=5 and d=10, the rank estimator is already accurate in the small-n regime where the neural baseline still shows noticeable deviation. The neural estimator exhibits larger variability (wider ±1 std bands) and more persistent bias in higher dimensions, suggesting it requires a substantially larger sample budget to stabilize. The rank-statistic approach also has a practical advantage: it is fully determined by sorting and counting once samples are fixed, requiring no iterative optimization, architecture selection, or training schedule. No head-to-head comparison is provided for the generative transport experiments (CIFAR-10, MNIST), where the method is demonstrated qualitatively rather than benchmarked against competing transport objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the rank-statistic approximation of the total-variation divergence introduced by de Frutos et al. (2024a, 2024b, 2026) to arbitrary f-divergences, replacing the fixed TV generator with a general convex entropy function f. The high-dimensional extension follows the sliced-projection framework of Kolouri et al. (2019) and Nadjahi et al. (2020), while the particle transport dynamics for generative modeling adapt the sliced-Wasserstein flow construction of Liutkus et al. (2019). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant density-ratio and variational/optimization-based paradigms for f-divergence estimation (Nguyen et al., 2010; Nowozin et al., 2016; Sreekumar et al., 2021) by showing that pure order-statistic information (rank histograms via the probability integral transform) suffices to approximate the divergence with provable convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-statistic f-divergence provides an optimization-free, bounded, and monotone-in-resolution estimator that avoids the instability of density-ratio estimation and adversarial/variational training, making it attractive as a plug-in objective for generative modeling and distribution comparison. Key open questions include the joint (K, N) scaling regime for optimal convergence, whether the approach extends to integral probability metrics or Wasserstein distances (which lack the clean quantile-domain reformulation), and how to handle strongly anisotropic high-dimensional discrepancies where random 1D projections are insufficient. The generative transport experiments on CIFAR-10 are qualitative and preliminary, leaving open whether the rank-based objective can compete with modern diffusion or flow-matching methods at scale.
