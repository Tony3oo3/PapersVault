## Problem

Coherent Factuality (CF) extends conformal prediction to multi-step LLM reasoning by enforcing logical dependencies among subclaims, but its hand-crafted frequency-based scoring is not differentiable, preventing end-to-end optimization. At high reliability levels (α < 0.1), this forces CF to remove up to 60% of true claims to maintain coverage guarantees, severely limiting practical utility. The paper asks whether CF's discrete operations can be relaxed into differentiable surrogates that enable learned scoring while provably recovering the original algorithm's guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM reasoning outputs decomposed into atomic subclaims arranged as Approximate Deducibility Graphs (ADGs), where nodes are claims and edges encode logical dependencies. Each claim is represented by 30 features spanning self-consistency frequency, semantic coherence flags, domain indicators, and NetworkX-computed graph metrics (PageRank, betweenness, reachability). The output is a filtered subgraph of retained claims with per-claim soft retention probabilities; at deployment, the learned logistic-regression scorer plugs into the original discrete CF algorithm, so the final output is a hard binary retain/reject decision per claim with a calibrated threshold guaranteeing 1−α factuality coverage.

## Method

DCF replaces CF's three coupled discrete operations with differentiable surrogates: a sigmoid relaxes the threshold indicator, a weighted geometric mean over ancestor retention probabilities relaxes the conjunction (a node is kept only if all ancestors pass), and a softmax over a utility function (λ·τ − violation score) relaxes the supremum that defines the nonconformity score. A differentiable soft quantile (Grover et al., 2019) replaces the order-statistic calibration step. The key theoretical result (Theorems 3.1–3.2) proves that as temperature parameters approach their limits in a specific coupled order, the soft pipeline recovers the hard CF algorithm exactly. Training follows the conformal-training paradigm: each epoch simulates the full calibration-then-prediction pipeline on disjoint subsets, and gradients from a retention loss (maximizing expected true claims kept) flow back through the entire graph-structured pipeline to the scorer parameters. At test time, the frozen scorer deploys in the original discrete CF algorithm, preserving the distribution-free coverage guarantee.

## Results

On MATH (202 problems), DCF achieves up to 141% retention improvement over the Coherent Factuality (CF) baseline at α=0.03 (1.76 vs. 0.73 claims retained per problem) while maintaining coverage within 0.5pp of the 97% target. Across α∈[0.05, 0.08], DCF consistently meets the coverage target with 60–105% retention gains. On FELM (710 problems), DCF achieves up to 61% retention improvement at α=0.01 (17.9% vs. 11.1% claims retained) and meets the coverage target at 9 of 10 α values, though CF achieves higher retention at mid-range α (0.06–0.08) where DCF carries 0.9–1.6pp excess coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCF consistently outperforms all three conformal factuality baselines and the XGBoost+Conformal variant. Against CF (Rubin-Toles et al., 2025), DCF wins on retention at 6 of 8 α values on MATH (gains of 60–141%) and at 6 of 10 α values on FELM, while CF retains higher retention at mid-range α (0.06–0.08) on FELM where DCF carries excess coverage. Against Independent Factuality (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024), DCF's graph-structured modeling provides additional value beyond independent claim filtering. Against Boosted Independent (Cherian et al., 2024), which uses the same learned features but without graph structure, DCF clearly wins—confirming that graph dependencies are necessary, not just learning. The XGBoost+Conformal baseline achieves near-zero retention at tight coverage levels, demonstrating that a standard classifier trained independently cannot replace DCF's differentiable conformal training objective. The overall ordering (DCF > CF > Independent > Boosted Independent >> XGBoost) validates that both learning and graph structure are required, and that learning must occur through the conformal objective itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) conformal factuality for LLMs, which evolved from independent claim filtering (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024) to graph-structured Coherent Factuality with Approximate Deducibility Graphs (Rubin-Toles et al., 2025), and (2) differentiable conformal training, which made CP end-to-end optimizable for classification (Stutz et al., 2022) and independent LLM claims (Cherian et al., 2024). The key departure is that CF's graph structure introduces tightly coupled discrete operations—threshold filtering, ancestor coherence enforcement, and constrained argmax—that form a cascade requiring *joint* relaxations preserving algorithmic ordering, unlike the independent per-sample relaxations in prior differentiable CP work. The paper proves these joint relaxations recover the original CF algorithm in the limit, bridging the gap between the two lines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The retention-reliability trade-off is the central practical bottleneck for conformal LLM factuality: at 97% reliability, hand-crafted scoring removes over 60% of true claims, making the guarantee nearly useless. DCF's 141% retention improvement on MATH (while preserving coverage) demonstrates that learned, graph-aware scoring can substantially narrow this gap, and the finding that standard classifiers (XGBoost) fail catastrophically when plugged into CF shows that the conformal objective must be optimized directly. However, validation is limited to 202 MATH and 710 FELM problems with a linear scorer and a single LLM (GPT-5-mini); quantile instability at α ≤ 0.02, the near-zero orthogonal feature signal on FELM, and the absence of distribution-shift or multi-LLM evaluation leave open whether these gains persist at scale and in deployment settings.
