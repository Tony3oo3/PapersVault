## Problem

The Natarajan dimension characterizes PAC learnability of multiclass partial concept classes only when the label space is finite, leaving the general (possibly infinite) multiclass setting without a known characterization. This paper asks whether the Daniely-Shalev (DS) dimension, which already characterizes total multiclass learnability, extends to partial concept classes, and further investigates how disambiguation schemes that complete partial hypotheses off-support affect the induced complexity.

## Input / Output

The learner receives n i.i.d. labeled samples (x_i, y_i) drawn from a distribution D over X × Y, where the underlying partial concept class H ⊆ (Y ∪ {★})^X may have an infinite label space Y. The output is a total hypothesis h: X → Y (no abstentions allowed at test time) together with explicit sample complexity bounds expressed purely in terms of DS(H), independent of |Y|. A secondary output is a disambiguation scheme φ that maps each partial hypothesis to a total one, whose off-support label domain Y_φ controls the DS-dimension of the induced total class.

## Method

The paper proves that finiteness of the DS-dimension is necessary and sufficient for PAC learnability of multiclass partial concept classes by constructing a two-component sample compression scheme: (1) a list-compression step using the one-inclusion graph (OIG) algorithm to produce a p-menu that bounds the effective label space at each point, and (2) a menu-conditioned compression step leveraging the Natarajan dimension to recover a single correct label from the menu. The composition yields an improper learner with sample complexity O(DS(H)^{3/2} · log³(DS(H)/ε)/ε) in the realizable case. A key algorithmic insight is that ERM fails for partial classes because it implicitly commits to an uncontrolled disambiguation; the paper instead explicitly regulates off-support behavior. The Disambiguation Paradox is then shown: for a constructed partial class with DS(H)=1, every binary disambiguation yields an induced total class of infinite DS-dimension, while a hypothesis-specific disambiguation using infinitely many private labels preserves DS-dimension 1, revealing that label parsimony can be counterproductive.

## Results

The paper establishes that the DS-dimension fully characterizes PAC learnability of multiclass partial concept classes with possibly infinite label spaces. The realizable sample complexity upper bound is O(DS(H)^{3/2}/ε · log³(DS(H)^{3/2}/ε) + log(1/δ)/ε), matching the lower bound Ω(DS(H)/ε + log(1/δ)/ε) up to a DS(H)^{1/2} polylog factor. The Disambiguation Paradox (Theorem 5.3) shows a partial class with DS(H)=1 for which every binary disambiguation yields infinite DS-dimension, while a hypothesis-specific disambiguation over an infinite label set preserves DS-dimension 1. The disambiguation number of the class family H_n satisfies D_d(H_n) = n^{Ω(log n / d)}, quantifying the label-sample complexity trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not present experimental comparisons but establishes theoretical separations between complexity measures and learning paradigms. Against the Natarajan dimension (Kalavasis et al., 2022), the paper shows Ndim=1 does not imply learnability when the label space is infinite (Theorem 3.1), whereas DS-dimension provides a tight characterization. Against ERM as a learning algorithm, the paper demonstrates (Theorem 3.2, building on Alon et al., 2021b) that ERM fails even for binary partial classes with VC=1, because its implicit disambiguation can induce infinite DS-dimension. Against the finite-label-space characterization of Kalavasis et al. (2022), whose upper bound scales as log|Y| and is vacuous for infinite label spaces, the paper's DS-based bounds are independent of |Y|. In the disambiguation comparison (Theorem 5.3), binary disambiguation (|Y_φ|=2) destroys learnability (DS→∞) while hypothesis-specific multi-label disambiguation (|Y_φ|=∞) preserves it (DS=1), inverting the Occam's Razor intuition that fewer labels are simpler.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads in PAC learning theory: the partial concept class framework of Alon et al. (2021b), which characterized binary partial learnability via VC-dimension, and the DS-dimension characterization of total multiclass learnability by Brukhim et al. (2022) and Daniely & Shalev-Shwartz (2014). It extends Kalavasis et al. (2022), who showed Natarajan dimension characterizes multiclass partial learning, by demonstrating that this characterization breaks down for infinite label spaces and that DS-dimension is the correct generalization. The constructive proof adapts the one-inclusion graph algorithm (Haussler et al., 1994) and the list-compression framework (Charikar & Pabbaraju, 2023) to the partial setting, while the disambiguation analysis builds on the binary disambiguation impossibility of Alon et al. (2021b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a natural open question—whether DS-dimension characterizes multiclass partial learnability—and provides the first sample-complexity bounds that are independent of the label space cardinality, which is essential for settings like open-vocabulary or continuous-label problems. The Disambiguation Paradox (Theorem 5.3) and the quantitative label-sample trade-off (Theorem 5.5) reveal that disambiguation is not merely a representational convenience but a fundamental complexity-control mechanism, with implications for how practitioners design abstention-aware predictors. Open questions include whether computationally efficient disambiguation schemes exist that achieve the optimal label budget, and whether the log(n) dependence in the compression scheme is inherent to multiclass learning or an artifact of the current proof technique.
