## Problem

Personalizing the size of conformal prediction sets (via the significance level α) for heterogeneous human decision-makers requires solving a contextual bandit with a large arm space (fine-grained α calibration) and high-dimensional context (human + task features), a double curse of dimensionality that existing methods handle poorly: parametric models misspecify human behavior, nonparametric function approximators incur large complexity constants, and static partitioning suffers from the curse of dimensionality. Prior bandit-based work on prediction-set sizing assumes homogeneous users and ignores contextual heterogeneity, while contextual simple-regret methods are either computationally expensive or rely on restrictive structural assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a d-dimensional context vector (e.g., 21-dim combining VGG-19 image features, user identity, and task-difficulty indicators) and a pre-trained model with a calibration set to construct conformal prediction sets C_α(x) for K candidate significance levels (K=100 in experiments). It outputs an adaptive binary-tree partition of the context space, a per-group optimal α, and a group-wise policy π_T(x) that maps any new context to its group's learned α for immediate deployment. The reward is the human's decision quality (binary correctness or continuous utility) multiplied by a coverage indicator that zeros out the reward when the true outcome is excluded from the prediction set.

## Method

AGCB is a meta-algorithm that decomposes the contextual bandit into lightweight per-group instances connected by three structure-exploiting mechanisms. First, a data-driven Zooming Mechanism splits a context group only when its local statistical uncertainty (Cσ√(log(KT)/n_g^eff)) falls below the Hölder approximation bias (ηL·d_g^β), balancing intra-group estimation error against inter-group bias and yielding minimax-optimal T-exponents. Second, cross-group information sharing pools reward estimates via Gaussian-kernel-weighted averaging, converting Hölder continuity into an effective sample-size gain s_g ≥ 1 that accelerates uncertainty reduction. Third, counterfactual updates exploit conditional monotonicity under coverage: a single observation at α_t provides directional bounds for all other α values (larger α should have no lower reward, smaller α no higher reward when coverage is preserved), reducing arm-space dependence from K^{β/(2β+d)} to (log K)^{β/(2β+d)}. Within each group, a base learner (Sequential Halving for simple regret, Successive Elimination for cumulative regret) performs binary-search-style arm selection, pulling only the median arm and propagating information counterfactually to the rest.

## Results

On the ImageNet16H-PS real-human dataset (21-dim context, 100 arms, T=1000), AGCB-SH achieves the lowest simple regret of 0.103 (binary) and 0.101 (continuous), outperforming the next-best baselines (SH vanilla at 0.110/0.205, Contextual-Gap at 0.110/0.109). For cumulative regret, AGCB-UCB achieves 75.93 (binary) and AGCB-SE achieves 106.23 (continuous), beating the strongest counterfactual baselines UCB-with-CF (93.97/109.86) and GP-UCB (94.50/120.52). On synthetic 100-arm, 10-dim settings, AGCB-SH attains 0.307 (binary) and 0.113 (continuous) simple regret, with regret barely increasing from 5 to 10 context dimensions, confirming scalable adaptive grouping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGCB-SH achieves the lowest simple regret across all real-human and synthetic 100-arm settings, beating SH vanilla by 6–45% (e.g., 0.103 vs. 0.110 binary, 0.101 vs. 0.205 continuous on ImageNet16H-PS) and Contextual-Gap by 6–44%. On cumulative regret (Table 5), AGCB-UCB (75.93 binary) and AGCB-SE (106.23 continuous) outperform the strongest counterfactual baselines from Straitouri & Gomez Rodriguez (2024) — UCB-with-CF (93.97) and SE-with-CF (122.16) — by 19% and 13% respectively, and substantially outperform GP-UCB (94.50/120.52), NeuralUCB (170.90/107.60), and LinUCB (113.33/137.53). In the small-arm (10-arm) synthetic regime, BOED slightly edges AGCB-SH on continuous feedback (0.071 vs. 0.086), but AGCB remains competitive and is designed for the harder large-arm, high-dimensional setting. AGCB runs 3–20× faster than GP-UCB, NeuralUCB, NeuralLinear, and Linear TS while achieving lower regret, demonstrating that adaptive grouping with counterfactual updates avoids the computational overhead of global function approximators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual prediction-set bandit framework of Straitouri & Gomez Rodriguez (2024), which treated significance-level α selection as a non-contextual stochastic bandit, to the contextual setting where human and task heterogeneity determines the optimal α. It departs from standard contextual bandit approaches (LinUCB, GP-UCB, NeuralUCB) by avoiding global function approximation entirely, instead using an adaptive binary partitioning mechanism (Zooming) that exploits Hölder continuity and monotonicity specific to human-AI collaboration. The within-group base learners are counterfactual-aware variants of Sequential Halving and Successive Elimination, extending the binary-feedback counterfactual updates of prior work to continuous reward structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Personalizing AI decision support for heterogeneous humans is a practical bottleneck in high-stakes domains (medicine, finance, law) where one-size-fits-all prediction-set sizes degrade team performance. The AGCB framework provides interpretable context partitions that reveal where human decision-making heterogeneity is concentrated, enabling targeted deployment. Open questions include reducing the unavoidable curse-of-dimensionality dependence (which would require stronger structural assumptions like low-rank latent factors), handling unknown Hölder parameters without rate degradation, and validating the framework in truly interactive (non-replay) human-AI settings with non-stationary behavior.
