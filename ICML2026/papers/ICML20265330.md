## Problem

While empirical evidence shows that spurious RLVR (random or incorrect reward signals) paradoxically improves Qwen2.5-Math on contaminated benchmarks, the internal mechanism by which incorrect rewards activate latent memorization over genuine reasoning remains unexplained. Prior work identifies behavioral signatures of over-memorization but does not localize the specific layers or circuits that mediate the reasoning-to-memorization mode switch under spurious RLVR.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes paired model weights (base Qwen2.5-Math-7B and its spurious RLVR-tuned checkpoint) and contrasts internal activations on matched math problems partitioned into 'Leakage' (wrong→right) and 'Stable' (always-right) subsets. All mechanistic probes operate at inference time without retraining; the causal steering intervention applies a multiplicative scaling factor α to the top-k MLP neuron keys identified by a relevance score combining activation magnitude and semantic overlap with ground-truth answer tokens, applied uniformly across all autoregressive generation steps.

## Method

The paper identifies an Anchor-Adapter circuit through a multi-tool mechanistic analysis: Path Patching confirms MLPs (not attention) as the primary storage for memorized solutions; counterfactual JSD on MLP sub-components (W_up, W_gate, W_down) reveals a peak-and-decline pattern at L21-22 marking structural adaptation; Logit Lens tracks layer-wise token emergence showing a precursor signal at L19 followed by aggressive answer injection at L23; and Neural ODE modeling of the residual stream localizes the trajectory bifurcation (separation force peak) precisely at L18-20. This establishes L18-20 as the Functional Anchor (causal trigger for retrieval) and L21+ as Structural Adapters (representational transformation to accommodate the shortcut signal). The causal steering intervention scales specific MLP keys within these layers, enabling bidirectional control—amplification (α=3.0) inflates contamination-driven accuracy while suppression (α=0.2) reveals baseline performance—and a probe-gated variant selectively audits suspicious gains without affecting clean benchmarks.

## Results

On contaminated MATH-500, the Anchor Reset ablation drops Qwen2.5-Math-7B accuracy from 98% to 86% (MinervaMath: 88%→72%), while the Adapter Reset produces smaller drops (−6% and −10%), confirming L18–20 as the primary retrieval trigger. Causal steering via MLP key scaling at Layer 18 yields maximal sensitivity: −3.8% accuracy under suppression (α=0.2) and +4.4% under amplification (α=3.0) on leakage datasets, with no systematic effect on leakage-free LiveMathBench. The probe-gated shortcut audit removes 19 RLVR-only successes on MATH-500 wrong→right (100%→85.16%, McNemar p=3.81×10⁻⁶) while leaving AIME-2025 (30%), LiveMathBench (21%), and OOD benchmarks unchanged, demonstrating selective targeting of contamination-dependent circuits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses three control models to validate that the Anchor-Adapter circuit is specific to data-contaminated Qwen checkpoints rather than a general RLVR artifact. LLaMA-3.1-8B and OLMo-2-1124-7B show no Perplexity Paradox (both full-text and answer perplexity rise uniformly), no path-patching accuracy recovery plateau, and monotonically increasing JSD across all MLP sub-components—confirming the absence of a localized memorization circuit. Qwen3-8B, the closest in-family comparison, replicates the qualitative pattern (peak-and-decline JSD, Logit Lens peak-valley-rise trajectory) but with a weaker contamination signal: its JSD peak shifts to Layer 33, its partial-prompt ROUGE-L separation between pre- and post-RLVR is less pronounced, and its path-patching recovery lacks the sharp L21 drop seen in Qwen2.5-Math-7B. Across all three controls, the key distinguishing metric is the presence or absence of the middle-layer (L18–20) separation force peak in NDE analysis and the corresponding Anchor Reset sensitivity, which is uniquely strong in Qwen2.5-Math-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability of memorization (Slonski, 2024; Hong et al., 2025) from standard pre-training/fine-tuning contexts to the specific and previously unmapped setting of spurious RLVR. It builds directly on the behavioral observations of Shao et al. (2025) and Wu et al. (2025b) that Qwen2.5 gains under random/incorrect rewards are contamination-driven, and on the 'over-memorization' perplexity signatures of Ruan et al. (2025), by providing the first causal, layer-level explanation of *how* spurious rewards physically reconfigure the network. Methodologically, it combines Path Patching (Meng et al., 2022), Logit Lens (nostalgebraist, 2020), JSD-based counterfactual ablation, and Neural ODE trajectory analysis (Chen et al., 2018) in a unified pipeline, with the MLP key-value memory framework (Geva et al., 2021) underpinning the final neuron-level steering intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, actionable diagnostic (the probe-gated shortcut audit) that can selectively stress-test suspicious RLVR gains on contaminated benchmarks while leaving fresh-benchmark behavior untouched, which is practically valuable for model developers and evaluators. The Anchor-Adapter decoupling—where the retrieval decision is causally made in middle layers (L18–20) but the structural weight changes concentrate in later layers (L21+)—refines the common assumption that knowledge storage and retrieval trigger co-locate. Key limitations include the analysis being confined to the Qwen family (7B, 1.5B, Qwen3-8B) and the math domain; whether the same circuit topology emerges in other architectures or in non-deterministic reasoning tasks remains open. The bidirectional steering also raises the dual-use concern the authors acknowledge: the same technique that suppresses contamination could amplify it.
