## Problem

Electrostatic generative models such as PFGM++ achieve competitive image synthesis quality but, like diffusion models, require expensive ODE integration (35–79 network evaluations) to produce a single sample. No principled distillation framework existed for PFGM++ across its full range of auxiliary dimensions D, which interpolate between the original PFGM (D=1) and diffusion models (D→∞). This paper addresses that gap by introducing a distillation method that works uniformly across all D values.

## Input / Output

The distilled generator takes a single random sample from the PFGM++ prior (a heavy-tailed distribution over the augmented space, parameterized by D and a noise level σ_init=2.5) and optionally a class label, producing a synthetic RGB image in 1–4 function evaluations instead of the teacher's 35–79. For finite D, the input is not a standard Gaussian but is drawn from the PFGM++ perturbation kernel centered at the origin; in the D→∞ diffusion limit this reduces to the standard Gaussian input. Evaluated at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) and 64×64 (FFHQ) resolutions.

## Method

IPFM formulates distillation as an inverse problem: find a few-step generator G_θ whose output distribution p_θ induces an electrostatic field matching that of the PFGM++ teacher trained on real data. The authors prove this inverse problem is well-posed (Theorem 3.2) and derive a tractable minimax objective (Theorem 3.3, Proposition 3.4) that avoids backpropagating through the inner argmin by rewriting it as a difference of teacher and student denoising losses on perturbed generator samples. A key theoretical result (Proposition 3.5) shows that in the D→∞ limit, IPFM reduces to Score Identity Distillation (SiD) up to a regularization term, enabling direct transfer of SiD's hyperparameters and its α-weighted regularization to finite-D settings. Training alternates between updating a student denoising model on generator samples (standard PFGM++ denoising loss) and updating the generator via the minimax objective, with the PFGM++ heavy-tailed perturbation kernel used for finite D instead of the Gaussian kernel.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, IPFM with SiD-inspired regularization (α=1.0) achieves FID 1.75 with only 4 NFE (D=128), surpassing the 35-NFE PFGM++ teacher (FID 1.92). On FFHQ 64×64, the 2-step IPFM generator (D=128) reaches FID 1.72, outperforming the 79-NFE teacher (FID 2.43). In the diffusion limit (D→∞), IPFM with α=1.0 recovers SiD exactly, yielding FID 1.86 (4-step, CIFAR-10) and 1.70 (2-step, FFHQ). Finite-D settings (D=128, 2048) consistently converge faster than the diffusion limit during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PFGM++ teacher, IPFM clearly wins on few-step generation: the 4-step CIFAR-10 generator (FID 1.75, D=128) beats the 35-NFE teacher (FID 1.92), and the 2-step FFHQ generator (FID 1.72, D=128) beats the 79-NFE teacher (FID 2.43) by a wide margin. Against SiD, IPFM at D→∞ with α=1.0 is mathematically identical to SiD (Proposition 3.5), so they tie by construction; however, finite-D IPFM (D=128) converges faster than the SiD/diffusion-limit setting at all training checkpoints (Table 2: FID 3.22 vs 3.61 at 70h on CIFAR-10). Against CTM (Consistency Trajectory Models), IPFM outperforms CTM on CIFAR-10 in a no-GAN-loss setting at both D=128 and D=∞ (Figure 6), confirming that exploiting the electrostatic structure of PFGM++ yields better distillation than general-purpose consistency-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formulates distillation of the PFGM++ electrostatic generative model as an 'inverse Poisson flow matching' problem, which is the natural electrostatic analogue of score matching in diffusion models. The key conceptual bridge is the proven equivalence (Proposition 3.5) between IPFM in the D→∞ limit and Score Identity Distillation (SiD), allowing direct transfer of SiD's regularization and hyperparameters to the electrostatic setting. The paper also positions itself against general ODE distillation methods like Consistency Trajectory Models (CTM), showing that exploiting the specific electrostatic structure of PFGM++ yields advantages over architecture-agnostic consistency-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled distillation framework for electrostatic generative models, reducing PFGM++ sampling from 35–79 NFE to 1–4 NFE while matching or exceeding teacher quality. The finding that finite-D PFGM++ teachers are more amenable to distillation than their diffusion-limit counterparts is practically significant, suggesting the electrostatic framework offers advantages beyond the diffusion paradigm. However, the minimax training procedure roughly triples memory usage compared to teacher training, and the finite-D convergence advantage, while consistent, is modest in magnitude and diminishes with more sampling steps. Open questions include whether these results scale to higher-resolution datasets and whether the electrostatic structure provides benefits beyond the 64×64 regime tested here.
