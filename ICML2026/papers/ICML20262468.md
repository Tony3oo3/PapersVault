## Problem

Panoptic Video Scene Graph Generation (PVSG) suffers from two unresolved challenges: (1) temporal sparsity, where relations occupy only short segments while most frames contain irrelevant background that causes attention drift in full-sequence models, and (2) a severe long-tailed relation distribution where existing debiasing methods redistribute supervision over observed instances but never introduce new visual-temporal evidence for rare predicates, leaving them impoverished in appearance and motion diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip V ∈ R^{T×H×W×3} (average 77 seconds on OpenPVSG) and produces a dynamic scene graph with pixel-level entity mask tubes (m_i ∈ {0,1}^{T×H×W}), entity category labels, and temporal relation triples each annotated with a specific temporal interval [start, end]. Evaluation uses volume-IoU (vIoU) thresholds of 0.5 or 0.1 between predicted and ground-truth mask tubelets, requiring both correct subject/object/relation labels and spatial-temporal overlap. Segmentation is handled by a frozen Mask2Former (IPS+T or VPS setting) with UniTrack or Video K-Net for cross-frame association, so TFN operates on pre-extracted entity feature tubes F_i ∈ R^{T×D} rather than raw pixels.

## Method

SegPVSG comprises two components. TempFocusNet (TFN) adopts a localization-then-recognition paradigm: K learnable temporal queries interact with pairwise entity features via cross-attention to regress candidate intervals (center, width), which are encoded as Gaussian temporal masks g_k^t that impose soft frame-level weights. These masks guide a Temporal Focus Attention mechanism (row-wise multiplication on the attention map before softmax) so the classifier concentrates on salient frames while preserving global context, with a score-based merging strategy at inference to unify overlapping high-confidence intervals. The Relation-centric Generative Video Augmentation (RGVA) module addresses long-tail scarcity by mining stable anchor frames, using an MLLM (Seed1.6) with Set-of-Mark visual grounding and a rarity-ranked vocabulary to generate context-consistent choreography instructions for rare relations, then synthesizing 5-second closed-loop clips (first frame = last frame = anchor) via Seedance 1.5 Pro. These clips are annotated with SAM2 mask propagation and inserted in-place into original training videos, preserving continuous temporal context essential for interval-level localization learning.

## Results

On OpenPVSG (IPS+T, vIoU=0.5), SegPVSG achieves R/mR@20/50/100 of 5.45/9.13, 9.54/12.74, and 13.63/15.91, improving mR@100 by +10.05 over the strongest two-stage baseline (Motion-aware Convolution, 5.86) and +7.70 over UNO (8.21). On ImageNet-VidVRD (bounding-box vIoU=0.5), TFN achieves R/mR@100 of 29.18/22.66, surpassing the best prior method BIG-C (24.27/18.98) by +4.91/+3.68. The RGVA augmentation component contributes an additional +3.04 mR@100 over TFN alone, with the largest gains concentrated in tail relation categories (Tail split mR@100: 12.75 vs. 6.86 for TFN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original PVSG baselines (Yang et al., 2023), SegPVSG improves R/mR@100 from 6.71/5.36 (Convolution, the strongest variant) to 13.63/15.91, more than doubling mean recall. The motion-aware extension (Nguyen et al., 2025) narrows the gap slightly (7.76/5.86 for Motion-aware Convolution) but still trails SegPVSG by +5.87/+10.05 in R/mR@100. The one-stage UNO (Le et al., 2026) achieves a higher R@20 (6.23 vs. 5.45) due to concentrated high-confidence predictions, but SegPVSG surpasses it on all mean-recall metrics and on R@50/R@100, indicating broader and more balanced relation retrieval. On ImageNet-VidVRD, TFN outperforms BIG-C (Chen et al., 2021) by +4.91/+3.68 in R/mR@100 and VrdONE (Jiang et al., 2024) by +7.60/+9.63, confirming that the temporal localization mechanism generalizes beyond pixel-level supervision to bounding-box tubelets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PVSG task introduced by Yang et al. (CVPR 2023) by addressing two limitations that prior two-stage methods (full-sequence self-attention, sliding windows) and long-tail debiasing methods (loss reweighting, feature calibration) leave unresolved. Conceptually, TFN repurposes the query-based localization paradigm from temporal action detection (Gaussian masks, learnable queries) into a pair-conditioned relation interval setting, while RGVA repurposes MLLM-guided image-to-video generation as a data augmentation tool for long-tail relations rather than as a standalone generation task. The key departure from prior long-tail work (IMPARTAIL, TEMPURA, VISA) is that instead of redistributing supervision over existing instances, RGVA introduces entirely new visual-temporal evidence for rare predicates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative augmentation strategy for long-tail video relations opens a new direction beyond loss reweighting and feature calibration, potentially transferable to other imbalanced video understanding tasks. However, the oracle perception analysis (R/mR@100 jumping from ~13 to ~41 with ground-truth entity labels) reveals that upstream tracking and segmentation remain the dominant bottleneck, limiting the practical ceiling of any relation-modeling improvement. RGVA's coverage is inherently constrained by anchor scene availability—relations requiring specific object co-occurrences (e.g., 'watering') receive few generated clips—raising open questions about whether more controllable video generation or synthetic scene construction could close this gap. The method also inherits dual-use risks from its reliance on large generative models, as the authors note.
