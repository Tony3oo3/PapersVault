## Problem

Existing perplexity-based preference calibration methods (e.g., PerpCorrect) apply a uniform mapping from perplexity differentials to label confidence across all samples, implicitly assuming the signal is equally informative regardless of task type. The paper demonstrates that this assumption is fundamentally flawed: perplexity differentials strongly correlate with label correctness for factual tasks (r = −0.61) but provide only a weak signal for creative tasks (r = −0.15), where novelty and originality can increase perplexity of genuinely preferred responses. This task-dependent reliability means uniform calibration systematically misweights creative and open-ended preferences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference pairs (prompt, y_w, y_l) along with two precomputed signals: the log-perplexity differential Δ_PPL between the two responses under a frozen SFT anchor model, and mean-pooled hidden states from the reference LLM (4096-dim for Llama-3-8B). These are projected through a lightweight 4K-parameter MLP into a 32-dimensional task embedding. The output is an instance-specific confidence target p̂ ∈ [0,1] that modulates the cross-entropy policy loss, together with interpretable task-conditioned slope γ and bias μ values that reveal how much each prompt type trusts the perplexity signal. The final product is the aligned policy LLM, trained with these calibrated soft targets rather than hard 0/1 preference labels.

## Method

TAPC parameterizes the calibration function as p̂ = σ(γ(z(x)) · Δ_PPL + μ(z(x))), where γ and μ are linear functions of a learned task embedding z(x), making the mapping from perplexity to confidence instance-specific and interpretable. The calibration module is trained via a MAML-style meta-learning loop: (1) a virtual policy update uses current confidence targets on a noisy training batch, (2) the meta-gradient is computed by evaluating the virtual policy on a small trusted reference set (200 samples) with standard DPO loss, and (3) the actual policy is updated with refreshed targets. This decouples calibration learning from the noisy training signal, preventing the module from accommodating rather than correcting label errors. Theoretically, the paper proves that task-aware calibration strictly reduces expected calibration error over task-agnostic methods whenever optimal per-task calibrations differ, with the improvement scaling as O(Δγ² · Var(Δ_PPL)). Empirically, TAPC learns steep slopes (γ ≈ 2.35) for factual tasks and near-zero slopes (γ ≈ 0.42) for creative tasks, confirming the intended behavior.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B trained with UltraFeedback, TAPC achieves 97.8% win rate on clean data and degrades gracefully to 84.5% at 40% synthetic label noise, outperforming the strongest baseline (PerpCorrect) by 7.7 points at that noise level. On standard benchmarks, TAPC scores 41.8±0.9% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 7.85±0.11 on MT-Bench, both leading all baselines. The largest gains appear on Creative tasks, where TAPC reaches 92.5% win rate versus 86.5% for PerpCorrect (a 6.0-point margin at 20% noise), confirming that task-aware calibration is most valuable where perplexity signals are least reliable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPC outperforms all eight baselines across every noise level and task category. Against the strongest perplexity-based method, PerpCorrect, TAPC leads by 0.6 points on clean data but widens the gap to 7.7 points at 40% noise (84.5% vs 76.8%), with the advantage driven primarily by Creative tasks (92.5% vs 86.5%). Compared to SimPO (reference-free DPO), TAPC gains 5.0 points on Creative and 6.6 points on Open tasks at 20% noise. Dr. DPO (distributional robustness) is competitive on Factual tasks (93.2% vs 94.2%) but trails TAPC by 5.3 points on Creative. The Uniform-TAPC ablation (same meta-learning without task conditioning) confirms that task-awareness contributes an additional 1.0–4.0 points over meta-learning alone, with the largest gap on Creative tasks (92.5% vs 88.5%). On the Llama-3-13B scale, TAPC's advantage over PerpCorrect at 40% noise further widens to 9.0 points, suggesting larger models produce more differentiated perplexity signals that TAPC exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of robust preference optimization methods (cDPO, rDPO, PerpCorrect, Dr. DPO, LPC) that address label noise in DPO training. The key departure is from PerpCorrect (Li et al., 2024), which uses perplexity differentials as a uniform label-quality signal across all samples; TAPC instead learns task-conditioned calibration functions via meta-learning, drawing on the MAML-style virtual-update/meta-gradient paradigm from noisy-label classification (Meta-Weight-Net, MentorNet) and applying it to the preference optimization setting. The theoretical contribution builds on standard decomposition arguments (Jensen's inequality, Rademacher complexity) to formalize when task-aware calibration provably outperforms task-agnostic approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that perplexity differentials are reliable for factual tasks but nearly uninformative for creative tasks (r = -0.61 vs. -0.15) provides a concrete, actionable diagnosis for why uniform robust-DPO methods underperform on open-ended generation. The method is practically attractive: only 4K additional parameters, 15% training overhead, and 200 reference samples suffice. However, several limitations temper the impact: experiments use only synthetic random-flip noise (not systematic annotator biases like length preference), the four-category task taxonomy is coarse and keyword-based, and the reference set must be curated with high-confidence labels. Open questions include whether task-aware calibration generalizes to systematic noise patterns, whether end-to-end task encoding (rather than frozen hidden states) yields further gains, and whether the approach extends to multi-signal calibration (e.g., combining perplexity with reward-model margins) in a principled way.
