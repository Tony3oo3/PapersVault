## Problem

In offline-to-online RL, existing methods either apply a single conservative objective uniformly across all samples or partition them by data origin (offline vs. online). However, distribution shift during fine-tuning causes both buffers to contain a mixture of offline-like and online-like behaviors, making origin-based separation unreliable and leading to objective-data misalignment. The paper asks whether constraint relaxation can be decided at the sample level based on behavioral consistency with a reference model rather than collection origin.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset, a growing online replay buffer, and a pre-trained behavior model (typically an energy-guided diffusion model on offline data) that produces a reference action a_b(s) for each state. The key intermediate quantity is a scalar per-sample behavioral distance d(s,a) = ‖a − a_b(s)‖₂, from which buffer-level Gaussian statistics (μ_c, σ_c for c ∈ {0,1}) are estimated every 1000 steps. The output is a re-partitioned mini-batch: samples are swapped between the offline-like and online-like pools so that each receives a behavior-appropriate training objective (conservative vs. relaxed), yielding an updated policy.

## Method

DARE models the behavioral distance D as a two-class Gaussian mixture and computes the Bayes-optimal posterior P(C=0|D=d), whose log-likelihood ratio is quadratic in d. From this quadratic structure, two thresholds are derived: τ (the midpoint of class means) and τ_side (the reflection of τ about the stationary point d*), producing a sign-based exchange rule that identifies samples in the variance-dominated side region where the posterior deviates from simple mean-based separation. Exchanged samples are swapped in balanced pairs (K = min of the two candidate pool sizes) between the offline-like and online-like mini-batches, after which each batch is trained with its respective objective (e.g., CQL regularizer retained vs. removed in DARE-C; expectile value targets + PEX vs. TD max-Q + SAC in DARE-I). Theoretically, each exchange strictly increases the absolute mean gap between the two behavioral classes (Thm. 3) and does not decrease the HΔH divergence for the threshold hypothesis class (Thm. 4), providing a provable separability guarantee. The framework is algorithm-agnostic, requiring only a per-sample alignment distance and a behavior model, and is instantiated on Cal-QL and IQL with an energy-guided diffusion model as the default behavioral reference.

## Results

On D4RL benchmarks after 0.2M online fine-tuning steps, DARE-C (Cal-QL-based) achieves a total score of 1415.4 across 15 locomotion and AntMaze tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline EDIS-C (1338.2) by ~77 points and the base CQL (1219.5) by ~196 points. DARE-I (IQL-based) reaches 1253.0 total, surpassing EDIS-I (1118.5) by ~135 points and base IQL (1085.3) by ~168 points. DARE-C attains the highest score on 13/15 tasks in the CQL group, while DARE-I leads on 12/15 tasks in the IQL group, representing an overall ~11% improvement in total score over the best baseline in each group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARE-C (Cal-QL backbone) dominates the CQL group: it outperforms base CQL by 195.9 points (1415.4 vs 1219.5), Cal-QL by 142.7 points, and EDIS-C by 77.2 points in total score, winning 13 of 15 individual tasks. The largest single-task gain is on HC-MR, where DARE-C scores 77.6 versus 56.4 for EDIS-C and 50.9 for base CQL. In the IQL group, DARE-I surpasses base IQL by 167.7 points (1253.0 vs 1085.3), PEX by 87.4 points, and EDIS-I by 134.5 points, winning 12 of 15 tasks. The most dramatic IQL-group improvement is on H-M, where DARE-I reaches 103.2±1.5 compared to 77.5±22.2 for base IQL and 58.9±6.3 for EDIS-I, indicating substantially improved training stability. DARE-I does not win on AM-MD (81.1 vs 84.9 for EDIS-I) or W2D-MR (89.7 vs 92.0 for PEX), showing that the exchange mechanism's benefit is task-dependent in the IQL group.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARE builds on Cal-QL and IQL as offline backbones but departs fundamentally from prior O2O RL constraint-handling strategies. While methods like FamO2O and Uni-RL apply uniform objectives, and origin-based approaches (OPT, Zheng et al.) assign constraints by data collection source, DARE instead uses a Bayes-optimal posterior over behavioral deviation from a learned diffusion-based behavior model to make sample-level exchange decisions. This reframes constraint relaxation as a distributional inference problem rather than a phase- or origin-based rule, drawing on the H-divergence framework from domain adaptation theory to provide separation guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode in O2O RL: offline and online data are not behaviorally separable, undermining the origin-based constraint assignments used by most existing methods. The algorithm-agnostic design (requiring only a per-sample behavioral distance) makes DARE a practical plug-in for diverse offline algorithms. Key limitations include dependence on offline dataset coverage for behavior model quality, and the theoretical analysis covers the exchange rule in isolation rather than end-to-end policy improvement, leaving open whether the separation guarantees translate into tighter value-estimation bounds.
