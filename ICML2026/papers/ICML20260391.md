## Problem

While MLLMs have made strong progress on semantic-level visual understanding (object recognition, scene reasoning), their ability to assess perceptual-level attributes—how an image actually looks and feels in terms of aesthetics, technical quality, and structural/textural richness—remains largely unexplored and unevaluated. No prior work provides a unified, hierarchical definition system or a comprehensive benchmark that jointly covers Image Aesthetics Assessment (IAA), Image Quality Assessment (IQA), and Image Structure & Texture Assessment (ISTA) under a single evaluation protocol.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260391/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Visual Rating (VR), the model receives a single RGB image and outputs a continuous perceptual score in [0, 100] derived via a Token As Score strategy that reads the score from the predicted token distribution rather than free-form text generation. For Visual Question Answering (VQA), the model receives an RGB image paired with a question constructed along a three-level Domain-Category-Criterion hierarchy (e.g., Domain=IAA, Category=Composition, Criterion=Visual Balance) and produces a multiple-choice text answer. As a downstream reward model, UniPercept outputs three simultaneous scores (IAA, IQA, ISTA) that are fed into a Flow-GRPO fine-tuning loop for FLUX.1-dev text-to-image generation.

## Method

UniPercept is built on InternVL3-8B and trained in two stages. First, Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training on ~800K curated samples spanning IAA, IQA, and ISTA (including structured-output QA pairs for fine-grained structural reasoning) establishes foundational perceptual representations. Second, Task-Aligned RL using GRPO jointly optimizes both VR and VQA objectives with task-specific rewards: a binary correctness reward for VQA and a novel Adaptive Gaussian Soft Reward for VR, where the Gaussian width σ_dyn scales with the prediction error |p_i − g_i| to provide smoother gradients and avoid threshold-induced discontinuities. The benchmark itself is constructed via a three-stage pipeline (GPT-4o generation → Qwen-2.5-VL-78B reject sampling → human refinement) that produces high-quality perceptual QA pairs grounded in a consensus-driven Domain-Category-Criterion taxonomy, with ISTA scores computed deterministically from weighted counts of annotated structural and textural attributes.

## Results

On UniPercept-Bench-VR, UniPercept achieves average SRCC/PLCC of 0.590/0.586 on IAA, 0.824/0.827 on IQA, and 0.778/0.767 on ISTA, substantially outperforming all general-purpose MLLMs (best: GPT-4o at 0.431/0.410 IAA, 0.643/0.655 IQA) and specialized models (best: Q-Insight at 0.767/0.758 IQA). On UniPercept-Bench-VQA, UniPercept reaches 76.55% (IAA), 81.07% (IQA), and 84.23% (ISTA) overall accuracy, exceeding the best general models by 8–9 percentage points. As a reward model for FLUX.1-dev post-training, UniPercept lifts PickScore from 22.46 to 22.67, HPSv3 from 10.71 to 10.93, and LAION-Aes from 5.77 to 6.19.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPercept outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every perceptual domain. Against proprietary models, UniPercept leads GPT-4o (the strongest general model) by +0.159 SRCC on IAA (0.590 vs. 0.431) and +0.181 on IQA (0.824 vs. 0.643), while GPT-4o's ISTA score is essentially zero (-0.003). Among open-source models, UniPercept surpasses InternVL3-78B (the best open-source baseline) by +0.253 on IAA and +0.205 on IQA, and by a margin of +0.933 on ISTA (0.778 vs. -0.155). Against specialized IQA models, UniPercept achieves 0.824 average SRCC vs. Q-Insight's 0.767 and DeQA's 0.753, with the closest call on KonIQ-10K (0.940 vs. DeQA's 0.953 SRCC) and SPAQ (0.904 vs. Q-Insight's 0.907). On VQA, UniPercept's 76.55% IAA accuracy exceeds the best general model (InternVL3-78B at 68.28%) by 8.3 points, and its 84.23% ISTA accuracy surpasses LLaVA-OneVision (81.13%) by 3.1 points. No baseline matches UniPercept's cross-domain coverage: specialized models (ArtiMuse, DeQA, Q-Align, Q-Insight) are restricted to a single domain and cannot handle the other two.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate perceptual assessment tasks—Image Aesthetics Assessment (IAA, e.g., Q-Align, ArtiMuse), Image Quality Assessment (IQA, e.g., DeQA, Q-Insight), and the newly formalized Image Structure & Texture Assessment (ISTA)—under a single hierarchical Domain-Category-Criterion taxonomy. Methodologically, it builds on InternVL3-8B as the backbone, adopts the Token As Score strategy from Q-Align/ArtiMuse for continuous rating, and applies the GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm from DeepSeekMath with a novel Adaptive Gaussian Soft Reward for VR alignment. The key conceptual departure from prior MLLM benchmarks (MMMU, MMBench, MMStar) is the explicit separation of perceptual-level understanding (how an image looks and feels) from semantic-level understanding (what an image depicts), arguing that current MLLMs are optimized for the latter and systematically underperform on the former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By defining perceptual-level image understanding as a first-class problem distinct from semantic understanding, this work provides the field with a standardized evaluation protocol (UniPercept-Bench) and a strong open-source baseline that can also serve as a plug-and-play reward model for T2I post-training. The introduction of ISTA as a formally defined, independently scored dimension fills a genuine gap—prior literature treated structure and texture only in passing. Limitations include the relatively small benchmark size compared to semantic-level suites, the reliance on GPT-4o for data generation (mitigated by human refinement but still a potential bias source), and the modest magnitude of T2I quality improvements demonstrated. Open questions include whether the ISTA scoring methodology generalizes beyond the curated annotation schema, how cultural diversity in aesthetic judgment affects IAA evaluation, and whether the Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training + RL recipe transfers to other perceptual sub-tasks.
