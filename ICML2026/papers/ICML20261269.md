## Problem

Existing test-time guidance methods for diffusion models (DPS, LGD, DPG, NDTM, etc.) use structurally biased approximations of the diffused likelihood, meaning they converge to incorrect posteriors even with unlimited compute. Additionally, the standard practice of rescaling the guidance gradient by a factor γ to obtain tempered posteriors is provably wrong for non-constant likelihoods. No prior method provides a consistent (bias-vanishing) estimator that recovers the true Bayesian posterior p(x|y) ∝ p(x)p(y|x).

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion model (serving as prior p(x)), an observation y, a likelihood function p(y|x) (which may be differentiable or not), and initial noise x_T ~ N(0,I). At each of N outer denoising steps, it draws K samples from the diffusion posterior p(x|x_t) — either via a DDPM inner loop or the renoise trick with a one-step model — and evaluates the likelihood on each. The output is a calibrated sample from p(x|y) (or the tempered p(x|y,γ)), which in practice yields reconstructed black-hole images (64×64), 256×256 super-resolved images from 64×64 inputs, or 512×512 text-aligned images with exact object counts.

## Method

Calibrated Bayesian Guidance (CBG) replaces the biased posterior-mean or Gaussian-posterior approximations with two consistent Monte Carlo estimators for the diffused likelihood gradient ∇_{x_t} log p(y|x_t). The gradient-based estimator (Eq. 16) applies the reparameterization trick through a differentiable sampler g_t(x_t; ε) to average ∇_{x_t} p(y|x) over K samples. The gradient-free estimator (Eq. 20) uses a REINFORCE form that absorbs the prior score into the integral, yielding a weighted average of denoising directions (a_t x^{(i)} − x_t)/b_t² with weights w_i = p(y|x^{(i)}), requiring no likelihood gradients. Both estimators are consistent: bias vanishes as K→∞, unlike all prior methods. For efficiency, the paper introduces a renoise procedure (Eq. 21) that uses a deterministic one-step model f_t to generate stochastic posterior samples by re-noising the deterministic prediction, reducing per-step cost from O(KN) DDPM evaluations to O(K) model calls. The paper further proves that correct tempered inference requires applying γ inside the posterior integral (Eq. 13) rather than rescaling the gradient (Eq. 11), a distinction that also invalidates standard classifier-free guidance for calibrated sampling.

## Results

On the Bayesian Inference Benchmark (5 tasks), CBG gradient-free achieves an average C2ST of 0.527 (lower is better), outperforming all other test-time guidance methods (DPS: 0.803, LGD: 0.750, DPG: 0.872) and approaching the MCMC NUTS oracle (0.553). On the black hole imaging task (InverseBench), CBG with renoise and K=65536 sets a new state-of-the-art PSNR of 27.08 dB, surpassing PnP-DM (26.07) and DPS (25.86). On 4× super-resolution (ImageNet 256×256), CBG leads in PSNR (24.41) but trails NDTM and DPS on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBG gradient-free dominates all test-time diffusion guidance baselines on the Bayesian Inference Benchmark: it beats DPS by 0.276 C2ST on average (0.527 vs. 0.803), LGD by 0.223 (0.527 vs. 0.750), DPG by 0.345 (0.527 vs. 0.872), and SCG by 0.208 (0.527 vs. 0.735). It also outperforms the likelihood-free SNPE (0.545) and SNLE (0.560) methods, and approaches the MCMC NUTS oracle (0.553) which has access to the full analytic posterior. On black hole imaging, CBG with renoise (K=65536) achieves 27.08 PSNR, beating PnP-DM (26.07), DPS (25.86), DAPS (25.60), and DiffPIR (25.01); at K=512 it matches PnP-DM (26.10 vs. 26.07) at 48s vs. 45s runtime. On super-resolution, CBG leads in PSNR (24.41 vs. DPS 23.61, NDTM 23.12) but loses to NDTM (0.158) and DPS (0.195) on LPIPS and to NDTM (28.75) and DPS (30.67) on FID, which the authors attribute to differences in prior model and step count. The gradient-based CBG variant underperforms the gradient-free version on Tasks 3–5 (e.g., 0.789 vs. 0.584 on Task 3), consistent with the paper's analysis that REINFORCE-style self-normalization reduces variance for sharp likelihoods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion model framework (DDPM, score-based SDEs) and the test-time guidance paradigm for inverse problems, which has been developed through DPS, LGD, DPG, and NDTM. Its key departure is a theoretical correction: it proves that all existing guidance methods use inconsistent estimators for the diffused likelihood, meaning they converge to the wrong distribution regardless of compute. The proposed Calibrated Bayesian Guidance (CBG) framework repurposes the REINFORCE estimator and the reparameterization trick to construct consistent estimators, and leverages the renoise trick with one-step mean-flow models for tractable sampling from the diffusion posterior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For scientific inference tasks (black hole imaging, physical sciences), calibrated posteriors are essential for correct uncertainty quantification, and this paper provides the first training-free guidance framework that provably converges to the true Bayesian posterior. The finding that even classifier-free guidance's standard rescaling of the likelihood gradient is biased for tempered inference has broad implications for how practitioners interpret guidance scales. Key limitations include the requirement that the likelihood overlap with the prior in measure (failing on measure-zero manifolds) and the substantial compute cost of drawing many per-step samples, which limits immediate adoption in high-dimensional image tasks where perceptual quality metrics still favor over-optimizing methods.
