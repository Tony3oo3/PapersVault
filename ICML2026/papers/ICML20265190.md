## Problem

Classical Bayesian Flow Networks and many graph diffusion/flow-matching methods rely on factorized beliefs and independent noise channels, so the intrinsic coupling between node and edge variables is not encoded by the generative geometry and must be recovered implicitly by the core network. This independence geometry makes discrete decoding brittle and forces structural consistency to be learned rather than enforced by the inference mechanism itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265190/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, VBFN takes a target number of samples and a number of reverse sampling steps T, and produces graphs G=(X,A) with node and edge attributes decoded from a continuous parameterization. Discrete attributes (atom/bond types, binary adjacency) are mapped to class centers in [-1,1] and decoded via truncated Gaussian CDF over class intervals; continuous attributes (e.g., 2D coordinates on Planar) are regressed directly. Variable-size graphs are handled with padding and binary masks, and structural constraints (symmetry, diagonal zeroing, validity) are enforced by projection operators at the decoding layer.

## Method

VBFN lifts the factorized Gaussian belief of classical BFNs to a joint Gaussian variational belief family whose uncertainty is governed by structured precision operators Ω_prior and Ω_obs, both constructed from a representation-induced dependency graph H that encodes incidence and symmetry couplings without querying sample-specific adjacency (avoiding label leakage). The Bayesian update at each time step reduces to solving a single SPD linear system P_t θ_t = Ω_prior θ_0 + β(t)Ω_obs y, which performs a coupled equilibrium computation across all node and edge variables simultaneously rather than element-wise fusion. Training retains the classical BFN message-matching objective, which under tied channel covariance simplifies to a weighted quadratic ‖z − ẑ_φ(θ_t,t)‖²_{Ω_obs}, making the structural geometry influence learning through the precision-weighted loss. The SPD system is solved efficiently via Conjugate Gradient with Jacobi preconditioning (O(KD) per update under sparse operators), and the predictor ẑ_φ is a Graph Transformer that takes the posterior belief state and time as input.

## Results

On QM9, VBFN achieves 99.98% validity, 0.083 FCD, 0.0002 NSPDK, and 0.9519 scaffold similarity, setting new state-of-the-art on all four metrics. On the more challenging ZINC250k (up to 38 atoms), VBFN reaches 99.63% validity, 1.307 FCD, 0.0007 NSPDK, and 0.6057 scaffold similarity, again leading all baselines. On synthetic benchmarks, VBFN attains the best Ratio (1.5 on Planar, 1.4 on Tree, 1.5 on SBM) while maintaining top-tier V.U.N. scores (99.5, 97.3, 89.7 respectively), demonstrating that the coupled Bayesian update improves structural fidelity without sacrificing validity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VBFN outperforms all 19 baselines on every reported metric across both molecular datasets. The closest competitor is TopBF (the prior BFN work by the same group), which trails VBFN by 0.24% validity and 0.08 FCD on QM9, and by 0.26% validity, 0.085 FCD, and 0.0685 scaffold similarity on ZINC250k. Among diffusion baselines, GruM is the strongest on ZINC250k (98.65% valid, 2.257 FCD) but still lags VBFN by over 1% validity and nearly 1.0 FCD; DiGress and GDSS are substantially weaker, with FCD values of 3.482 and 14.656 respectively on ZINC250k. The flow-matching baseline DeFoG is competitive on FCD (1.425 vs. 1.307 on ZINC250k) but falls behind on validity (99.22% vs. 99.63%) and scaffold similarity (0.5903 vs. 0.6057). On synthetic graphs, VBFN matches DeFoG's V.U.N. on Planar (99.5) while achieving a better Ratio (1.5 vs. 1.6), and on Tree it achieves a substantially better Ratio (1.4 vs. 4.0 for HSpectre) despite slightly lower V.U.N. (97.3 vs. 100.0), indicating that the coupled update avoids generating valid but structurally trivial samples. On SBM, VBFN achieves the best Ratio (1.5) among all methods with competitive V.U.N. (89.7), whereas DeFoG achieves higher V.U.N. (90.0) but a much worse Ratio (4.9), highlighting VBFN's superior balance between validity and distributional fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

VBFN extends Bayesian Flow Networks (Graves et al., 2023) by performing a variational lifting from the factorized independent-Gaussian belief family to a joint Gaussian variational belief governed by structured Laplacian-based precisions. This departs from both classical BFNs (which decompose Bayesian update element-wise) and from diffusion/flow-matching graph generators (DiGress, GruM, DeFoG) that start from factorized isotropic noise and leave node-edge coupling to be recovered implicitly by the core network. The closest prior work is TopBF (Xiong et al., 2026), also a Bayesian Flow approach for molecular graphs, but VBFN introduces the coupled SPD linear system update and the representation-induced dependency graph as a no-leakage structural prior, which TopBF does not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By encoding node-edge coupling directly in the generative geometry through structured precisions, VBFN provides a principled mechanism for joint evidence fusion that is absent in both factorized diffusion and classical BFN approaches, potentially reducing the burden on model capacity for structural consistency. The practical impact is most relevant to molecular generation in drug discovery, where validity and scaffold diversity are critical. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs (the dense edge-space precision matrices remain a memory bottleneck, as shown in the ZINC250k profiling), whether the Laplacian-based coupling template generalizes to heterogeneous or directed graphs, and whether the tied-covariance assumption can be relaxed to allow informative channel covariances without losing the closed-form KL simplification.
