## Problem

MoE LLMs explicitly modularize knowledge into routed expert modules, yet prior work has treated experts primarily as routing units for efficiency and specialization rather than as carriers of reusable knowledge. It remains unexplored whether a subset of experts encodes cross-domain, generalizable knowledge that can be systematically extracted and transferred to improve training of new models at different scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262902/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MoE LLM (OLMoE-7B or DeepSeekMoE-16B), a set of multi-domain datasets used only for inference-time activation profiling, and the target model's FFN dimensions (hidden size, intermediate size, layer count). It produces a compact set of block-wise FFN parameter matrices—approximately 1.25% of the source model's parameters—that directly initialize the FFN layers of a dense Llama target model, with all remaining parameters using standard random initialization. The entire extraction pipeline is training-free, requiring only forward passes through the source model.

## Method

XPERT operates in three training-free stages. First, cross-domain expert selection performs forward passes on M domain-specific datasets, computing each expert's average activation A_i and consistency score C_i across domains, then selecting the top-n experts by the composite score A_i − C_i to identify those encoding shared, domain-agnostic knowledge. Second, knowledge consolidation stacks the selected expert weight matrices into a third-order tensor Z ∈ R^{d_i × d_o × n} and applies Tucker decomposition to extract a low-rank core tensor and factor matrices, isolating the dominant shared structure while suppressing expert-specific noise. Third, parameter-scale adaptation reconstructs a full-rank FFN matrix via mode-wise multiplication, then resizes it to arbitrary target dimensions using truncated SVD with row/column importance scoring—selecting top-scoring rows when shrinking or replicating them when expanding—preserving the dominant subspace of the expert-derived representation.

## Results

On language understanding benchmarks (Table 1), XPERT-OLMoE achieves an average score of 54.10 for the 570M model (16 layers, dim=2048), outperforming Scratch (51.81) and Distillation (52.44). On dialogue generation (Table 2), the same 570M XPERT-OLMoE model reaches 18.50 average Rouge-L versus 15.97 (Scratch) and 16.54 (Distillation). XPERT also accelerates fine-tuning convergence by ~2.4× in Rouge-L and ~1.7× in loss, and a model pre-trained with only 2B tokens under XPERT outperforms a Scratch model pre-trained with 10B tokens on UnNI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XPERT is compared against two baselines under identical architecture and training pipelines: (1) Scratch, which trains from standard random initialization, and (2) Distillation (MiniLLM-style, Gu et al. 2024), which pre-trains the target model via teacher-student supervision from OLMoE-7B. XPERT-OLMoE wins on every model scale and every benchmark in both Tables 1 and 2. On the 570M language understanding setting, XPERT-OLMoE achieves 54.10 average versus 52.44 for Distillation (+1.66) and 51.81 for Scratch (+2.29); on the 570M dialogue generation setting, it reaches 18.50 average Rouge-L versus 16.54 for Distillation (+1.96) and 15.97 for Scratch (+2.53). XPERT-DeepSeek (using DeepSeekMoE-16B as source) also consistently beats both baselines, though slightly below XPERT-OLMoE on most tasks. The paper argues that the gap over Distillation indicates MoE expert knowledge is difficult to transfer through supervision alone and benefits from parameter-level reuse. Additionally, XPERT requires up to 5× less pre-training data to match Scratch performance (2B vs. 10B tokens on UnNI) and converges ~2.4× faster during fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vision-domain line of work on inheriting condensed knowledge from ancestor models (LearnGene, Wang et al. 2023a; Shi et al. 2024) to the MoE LLM setting, where knowledge is explicitly modularized at the expert level. It departs from prior MoE research, which has focused on routing efficiency, load balancing, and expert specialization, by treating expert parameters as extractable, reusable knowledge units rather than merely computational modules. The knowledge consolidation step repurposes Tucker decomposition (Tucker, 1966) from its classical multivariate analysis role into a denoising mechanism that isolates shared structure across expert matrices, and the parameter-scale adaptation via importance-guided SVD resampling is a novel mechanism for transferring fixed-size tensor representations to arbitrary target dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes MoE LLMs as structured, reusable knowledge sources beyond their original deployment, offering a training-free pipeline that reduces pre-training data requirements by up to 5× while improving downstream performance. However, the evaluation is confined to small target models (270M–570M parameters) and two source MoE LLMs, leaving open whether the approach scales to billion-parameter targets or benefits from larger source models. A key open question is whether the identified 'common experts' correspond to a stable, architecturally meaningful subspace or are an artifact of load imbalance in the routing mechanism, and whether the technique generalizes to non-MoE dense architectures where no explicit expert boundaries exist.
