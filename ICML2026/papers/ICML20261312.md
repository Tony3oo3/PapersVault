## Problem

Existing face relighting methods either suppress the original illumination (causing foreground-background inconsistency) or modify the entire scene, overlooking the practical need to add a virtual fill light to an underexposed face while preserving the original background and its lighting. No large-scale paired dataset with physically consistent, parameterized fill-light supervision exists to support scalable learning for this specific task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261312/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB face image (typically underexposed or captured under suboptimal lighting) and a 6D fill-light parameter vector specifying color temperature (Kelvin), half-peak beam angle, light-to-subject distance, disk diameter, and 2D image-plane offset. It produces a single fill-light-enhanced RGB image where only the facial region receives additive illumination from a virtual disk-shaped area light, while the background pixels remain unchanged. After inference, a training-free dual-tree complex wavelet transform allows continuous post-hoc adjustment of fill-light strength by scaling only the low-frequency residual subband.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a Physics-Aware Lighting Prompt (PALP) module encodes the 6D parameters into diffusion-compatible conditioning tokens via FiLM-style modulation and a shallow Transformer, pretrained with an auxiliary planar-light reconstruction objective that predicts both an irradiance map and a direction field from the parameters. Second, FiLitDiff is a one-step diffusion model fine-tuned from SD2.1-base (via LoRA, rank 16) that starts directly from the input image latent without forward noising, applies a deterministic DDIM update (σ_t=0) to recover the clean latent in a single step, and is supervised with a carrier-based target (γ·I_orig + 0.6·ΔI_lamp) to work around VAE limitations on pure illumination residuals. The training loss combines MSE, DISTS, and MSSWD (color-aware). The 160K paired training set is generated by a physically consistent renderer that discretizes a disk area light via Fibonacci-spiral sampling, uses Sapiens for depth/normals and IntrinsicAnything for albedo/specular, and computes Lambertian diffuse plus Blinn-Phong specular contributions in linear RGB with screen-space ray-marching for soft occlusion.

## Results

On LYF-Val, FiLitDiff achieves a PSNR of 25.65 dB, SSIM of 0.8773, DISTS of 0.0756, and LPIPS of 0.0851, substantially outperforming all relighting baselines on full-reference metrics. It also attains the lowest MSSWD (0.6284), indicating the most accurate color-temperature control among all methods. On the harder LYF-EditVal set (inputs edited with underexposure or side-lighting prompts), FiLitDiff maintains a large lead with PSNR 24.01 and SSIM 0.8308, while remaining competitive on no-reference IQA scores (CLIPIQA 0.6216, LIQE 3.6649, TOPIQ 0.7364).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiLitDiff decisively outperforms all three relighting baselines on every full-reference metric. Against SMFR (Hou et al., 2021), the gap is largest: FiLitDiff scores 25.65 vs. 12.02 PSNR and 0.8773 vs. 0.4090 SSIM on LYF-Val, reflecting SMFR's tendency to produce high failure rates on underexposed inputs. Against IC-Light (Zhang et al., 2025b), FiLitDiff leads by 17.8 dB in PSNR (25.65 vs. 7.83) and by a factor of ~5 in MSSWD (0.6284 vs. 3.7624), indicating far superior color-temperature fidelity; IC-Light's text-prompt conditioning appears poorly aligned with the physically parameterized evaluation. Against the prompt-based editing reference Qwen-Image-Edit (Wu et al., 2025), FiLitDiff wins on all paired full-reference metrics (PSNR 25.65 vs. 13.48, DISTS 0.0756 vs. 0.2126) and on MSSWD (0.6284 vs. 2.8623), but Qwen-Image-Edit edges ahead on two no-reference scores on LYF-EditVal (CLIPIQA 0.6600 vs. 0.6216, TOPIQ 0.7659 vs. 0.7364), suggesting that prompt-based editing can produce slightly more 'natural-looking' results in the unpaired setting while sacrificing precise physical controllability. DPR (Zhou et al., 2019), included as an additional baseline, sits between Qwen-Image-Edit and SMFR on full-reference metrics but is still far behind FiLitDiff (PSNR 16.67 vs. 25.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out face fill-light enhancement (FFE) as a distinct sub-task of face relighting, explicitly departing from two dominant paradigms: methods that suppress or normalize the original illumination (Hou et al., 2021; 2022) and scene-level relighting that co-modifies background and subject (Zhang et al., 2025b; Kim et al., 2024). Methodologically, it builds on latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion) with LoRA fine-tuning and one-step DDIM inference, but introduces a novel physics-aware conditioning pathway (PALP) that maps 6D area-light parameters into diffusion-compatible tokens via FiLM modulation and an auxiliary planar-light reconstruction objective. The dataset pipeline composes existing tools (Sapiens for geometry, IntrinsicAnything for intrinsics) with a custom Fibonacci-spiral disk-light renderer, positioning the contribution at the intersection of physically based rendering and generative diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LYF-160K provides the first large-scale paired dataset purpose-built for the FFE setting, and the physically consistent renderer is a reusable asset for future controllable relighting research. The one-step FiLitDiff model offers a practical, low-latency path to user-controllable fill lighting, and the wavelet-based post-hoc strength control is a useful training-free trick. However, the current formulation is limited to a single disk-shaped source, the pipeline depends on external geometric/intrinsic predictors whose errors propagate in dark regions, and no demographic-stratified evaluation is provided. Open questions include extending to multi-light and back-lighting setups, reducing reliance on imperfect depth/normal estimates, and validating on diverse real-world capture conditions beyond the synthetic pipeline.
