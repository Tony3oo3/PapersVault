## Problem

A robot's morphology and its control objective are fundamentally coupled, yet existing LLM-based pipelines treat body design and reward engineering as separate problems, missing co-adaptation opportunities where a reward tuned for one morphology is suboptimal for another. Single-agent generation further narrows exploration by reusing familiar patterns, precisely where co-design demands diversity. The paper asks whether structured, multi-agent LLM debate grounded in physics simulation can jointly optimize morphology and reward more effectively than either component optimized in isolation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial MuJoCo XML morphology with a fixed kinematic topology and a bounded set of editable geometric parameters (e.g., limb segment lengths, torso radius, joint attachment offsets, capsule radii — 6 to 24 parameters depending on the environment), along with a natural-language task description. It produces an optimized XML morphology specification and a JAX/Brax-compatible reward function (a pure, jit/vmap-safe Python code snippet returning a scalar reward and a diagnostics dictionary). The reward is explicitly conditioned on the proposed morphology, and all candidates are scored by a reward-agnostic cumulative distance-from-origin metric S to prevent specification gaming.

## Method

DEBATE2CREATE (D2C) structures co-design as a role-separated thesis-antithesis-synthesis debate between a design agent (proposes morphology edits) and a control agent (critiques designs for controllability issues, then writes reward code tailored to the revised morphology). Each debate round generates N_m=4 candidate morphologies and N_r=4 reward variants per morphology (16 synthesis pairs per round, 80 over 5 rounds), all trained in Brax with PPO or SAC under identical RL budgets. A panel of four criterion-specific LLM judges (speed, stability, efficiency, novelty) analyzes simulator metrics for the round's top candidate and provides one-sentence textual critiques that steer the next round's proposals. Crucially, all selection and ranking decisions are grounded in the physics simulator's task score S rather than LLM preference: a hall-of-fame archive stores evaluated design-reward pairs ranked by S, and the final output is the highest-scoring pair. This separation ensures that debate rhetoric is governed by empirical performance, preventing persuasive but ungrounded LLM explanations from determining the final design.

## Results

Under a fixed-topology, per-candidate-RL protocol with 80 trained candidates per seed, D2C achieves the highest Default-normalized score on all five MuJoCo locomotion tasks: 3.18× on Ant, 1.36× on HalfCheetah, 1.51× on Hopper, 9.35× on Swimmer, and 1.36× on Walker2D (mean ± std over 3 search seeds). Iterative debate yields 18–35% gains (mean 24%) over a compute-matched zero-shot baseline generating the same 80 candidates without feedback. D2C-generated rewards transfer to unmodified default morphologies in 4/5 tasks, and the difference-in-differences interaction term is positive in 4/5 tasks, confirming super-additive morphology–reward synergy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2C outperforms all three baselines on every one of the five environments. Against Eureka (reward-only search on fixed morphology), D2C leads by 2.24× on Ant, 0.36× on HalfCheetah, 0.66× on Hopper, 8.35× on Swimmer, and 0.68× on Walker2D. Against Bayesian Optimization (morphology-only black-box search), D2C wins on all tasks; the gap is smallest on Ant (3.18 vs. 2.54) but BO produces kinematically non-functional morphologies on Hopper and Walker2D (score 0.00). Against RoboMoRe (LLM co-design with 125 candidates), D2C achieves higher scores on all five tasks while evaluating 36% fewer candidates (80 vs. 125); the margin is largest on HalfCheetah (1.36 vs. 0.14) and Walker2D (1.36 vs. 0.00). The closest overall comparison is D2C vs. BO on Ant, where BO's morphology-only search still captures much of the design-space gain but lacks the reward co-adaptation that D2C provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2C extends the LLM-for-robotics line of work by unifying two previously separate threads—morphology generation (RoboMorph, Text2Robot) and reward-code generation (Eureka)—into a single role-separated debate loop, departing from RoboMoRe's single-LLM coarse-to-fine approach by explicitly splitting design and control into distinct agents with cross-critique. The thesis-antithesis-synthesis structure is adapted from multi-agent LLM debate (Chan et al., 2023; Du et al., 2024), but is grounded in a physics simulator rather than LLM-learned judges, and the hall-of-fame archive borrows the quality-diversity idea (MAP-Elites) while seeking a single best solution rather than a behavior-space repertoire. The reward-generation pipeline directly follows Eureka's JAX/Brax code template, with the key modification that reward is conditioned on the current morphology rather than a fixed body.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that structured, simulator-grounded multi-agent debate yields super-additive co-design synergy (positive interaction term in 4/5 tasks) and that LLM-generated rewards transfer to unmodified morphologies, suggesting debate discovers reusable locomotion principles rather than morphology-specific hacks. However, the framework is compute-intensive (~40 GPU-hours per environment), validated only in simulation on five fixed-topology MuJoCo tasks with n=3 seeds, and the single-scalar archive selection limits multi-objective optimization. Open questions include sim-to-real transfer, topology changes beyond parameter editing, manipulation tasks, and whether the debate structure generalizes to domains without a clean external task score.
