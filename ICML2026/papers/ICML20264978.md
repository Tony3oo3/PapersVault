## Problem

Existing theoretical results for private and robust alignment of language models are unsatisfactory in three ways: prior work claimed the standard MLE log loss cannot achieve near-optimal rates under local differential privacy, the state-of-the-art SquareχPO algorithm was shown to have strictly suboptimal corruption-dependent bounds, and no theoretical guarantees existed for online private or robust alignment. This paper resolves all three gaps by establishing new uniform convergence results that sharpen the analysis of both log-loss and square-loss-based alignment algorithms.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a preference dataset of response-pair/label triples where labels may be privatized via a randomized-response mechanism (parameter ε) and/or corrupted under an α-Huber model, in either corruption-then-privacy (CTL) or privacy-then-corruption (LTC) order. The method is agnostic to the corruption order and does not require knowing α in advance. The output is an aligned policy π̂ accompanied by a suboptimality gap bound that scales with the single-policy concentrability coefficient (offline) or coverability coefficient (online), the privacy cost c(ε)=(e^ε+1)/(e^ε−1), and the corruption level α (linearly for CTL, as c(ε)α for LTC).

## Method

The core technical contribution is two new uniform convergence lemmas: one for log loss under local differential privacy (showing the MLE estimator achieves the optimal c(ε)²·log|Π| rate without any de-biased loss redesign) and one for square loss under joint privacy-and-Huber-corruption (improving the corruption term from nα to nα² for CTL and from nc(ε)α to nc(ε)²α² for LTC). These lemmas enable four algorithms via one-line loss modifications: PrivχPO and PrivXPO (log-loss MLE for privacy-only, offline and online) and SquareχPO (re-analyzed) and SquareXPO (new, for privacy+corruption, offline and online). The online algorithms retain the global-optimism exploration mechanism of XPO, replacing the log loss with the private/corruption-aware loss while preserving the coverability-based rate. Empirically, all three loss variants (MLE log, debiased log, square) achieve comparable win rates on GPT-2-large fine-tuned on synthetic finance and PKU-SafeRLHF datasets under ε=0.5.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it improves the corruption term in the offline SquareχPO bound from √α to α (CTL) and from √c(ε)·α to c(ε)·α (LTC), and establishes the first online private/robust alignment guarantees via SquareXPO and PrivXPO. Empirically, on a synthetic finance preference dataset (ε=0.5), the proposed MLE-type log loss achieves 65.2±4.3% win rate over the SFT baseline, comparable to the debiased log loss (65.8±5.6%) and square loss (64.5±1.2%). On the real-world PKU-SafeRLHF safety dataset, all three losses achieve closely comparable win rates (61.45%, 61.95%, and 61.64% respectively), confirming the theoretical equivalence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the debiased log loss of Zhou et al. (2025a), the paper's key theoretical claim is that the standard MLE-type log loss achieves the same near-optimal rate O(κ·c(ε)√(log(\|Π\|/δ)/n)) without requiring a custom de-biased loss, resolving an open question. Empirically the two are statistically indistinguishable: 65.2% vs. 65.8% on the synthetic finance dataset and 61.45% vs. 61.95% on PKU-SafeRLHF, both within one standard deviation. Against SquareχPO of Zhou et al. (2025b), the paper does not propose a new algorithm but re-analyzes the same square-loss objective to obtain strictly tighter corruption terms: α replaces √α in CTL and c(ε)α replaces √(c(ε)α) in LTC, which is a meaningful improvement for small corruption levels (e.g., α=0.05 gives 0.05 vs. 0.223). Empirically the square loss is slightly lower (64.5% vs. 65.2% synthetic; 61.64% vs. 61.45% PKU-SafeRLHF), consistent with the theoretical equivalence up to constants. The paper also introduces the first online private/robust alignment results (PrivXPO, SquareXPO), for which no prior baselines exist.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends three concurrent lines of work on alignment: χPO (Huang et al., 2024) for offline chi-squared-regularized preference optimization, XPO (Xie et al., 2024) for online exploratory alignment, and SquareχPO (Zhou et al., 2025b) for private and robust offline alignment. The key departure is twofold: it shows that the standard MLE log loss achieves near-optimal rates under local differential privacy—contradicting Chowdhury et al. (2024b), who argued a de-biased loss was necessary—and it re-analyzes SquareχPO to extract strictly tighter corruption-privacy bounds (improving √α to α for CTL and √(c(ε)α) to c(ε)α for LTC) via a new uniform convergence result for square loss. The online private/robust setting (PrivXPO, SquareXPO) is entirely new, built by a one-line loss modification to XPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uniform convergence lemmas for log and square loss under privacy and corruption are broadly applicable beyond alignment, potentially informing private and robust learning in bandits, regression, and other decision-making problems. The resolution of the MLE-under-privacy question simplifies the practical toolkit: practitioners no longer need to design specialized de-biased losses for private preference optimization. Open questions include whether log loss can be made to work under Huber corruption (currently only square loss is handled), extending to adaptive (non-oblivious) corruption models, and combining offline datasets with online feedback in a hybrid private-robust setting.
