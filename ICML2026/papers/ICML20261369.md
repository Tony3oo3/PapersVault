## Problem

At infinite width, the NTK freezes and becomes deterministic, but this limit misses critical training phenomena such as NTK evolution and feature learning. While finite-width corrections can be computed via a 1/n expansion, the algebraic derivations of the required layer-wise recursion relations for NTK statistics are prohibitively laborious, hindering adoption in machine-learning research. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a Feynman diagram formalism specifically for NTK statistics, extending the existing preactivation-only diagrammatic framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes an MLP specification (depth L, hidden width n, activation function σ, and per-layer initialization variance C_W) along with a set of input data points {x_α}. It produces coupled layer-wise recursion relations for ten rank-four tensors (D, F, A, B, P, Q, R, S, T, U) plus the mean corrections K^{1} and Θ^{1}, where each recursion involves Gaussian expectations over up to four-dimensional integrals of activation-function compositions. These recursions are solved numerically (via custom SymPy routines reducing 4D integrals to lower-dimensional sums) to yield finite-width corrected kernel values Θ + Θ^{1}/n and K + K^{1}/n at each layer, which are then compared against Monte-Carlo samples from independently initialized networks.

## Method

The core contribution is a complete set of Feynman rules—cubic vertices encoding NTK-preactivation interactions (decorated with σ, σ', and the combined quantity ΔΩ), quartic vertices representing the rank-four tensors, quadratic vertices for mean corrections, and a propagator implementing Gaussian expectation with NNGP covariance—along with six selection rules governing how internal lines connect. The key structural insight is that NTK external lines propagate exclusively through dashed internal lines attached to the propagator, which generates dotted NTK lines within diagrams and yields susceptibility factors tied to infinite-width criticality. Theorems prove that these rules uniquely determine all recursion relations at order 1/n (Theorem 4.1 for D, F, A, B; Theorem 4.2 for dNTK/ddNTK tensors) and extend to all orders by adding higher-valence generalizations of the quartic vertices (Theorem 4.3). As applications, the framework derives the previously unknown recursion for the NTK mean correction Θ^{1}, proves that NTK statistics inherit criticality from the NNGP (Theorem 5.1), and shows that diagonal NTK entries receive no finite-width corrections for scale-invariant activations like ReLU (Theorem 5.2).

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: Feynman rules that provably reproduce all known 1/n recursion relations for NTK tensors (Theorem 4.1), extend to dNTK/ddNTK tensors (Theorem 4.2), and generalize to all orders in 1/n (Theorem 4.3). Numerically, the first-order finite-width corrected NTK and NNGP for a GeLU-MLP at layer 4 match Monte-Carlo estimates (10^5 and 10^6 samples respectively) closely for hidden widths n > 20, while the uncorrected infinite-width result shows visible deviation. For ReLU MLPs at critical initialization C_W = 2, the NTK grows linearly with depth (up to 30 layers, width 200, 1000 samples) rather than exponentially, confirming Theorem 5.1. The diagonal NTK component Θ(x,x) for ReLU and LeakyReLU shows no finite-width correction (verified with 5×10^6 samples), while GeLU does receive corrections, confirming Theorem 5.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the infinite-width frozen NTK computed via the neural-tangents library (Novak et al., 2020). In Figure 1, the infinite-width NTK (gray) and NNGP (gray) deviate noticeably from the Monte-Carlo sample means (red) at finite widths n ≤ 50 for a GeLU-MLP, whereas the first-order finite-width corrected quantities (blue) track the MC estimates closely already at n > 20. This demonstrates that the Feynman-diagram-derived corrections capture the leading finite-width effects that the infinite-width approximation misses. The paper also implicitly compares against the preactivation-only Feynman diagram framework of Banta et al. (2024), which is restricted to forward-pass statistics; the present work extends the diagrammatic approach to NTK, dNTK, and ddNTK statistics needed for training dynamics, a capability not available in prior diagrammatic treatments. No direct head-to-head numerical benchmark against other finite-width correction methods (e.g., Yaida 2020 or Roberts et al. 2022) is provided, as the contribution is the diagrammatic derivation framework rather than a new numerical algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Feynman diagram framework for neural network statistics from preactivations (Banta et al., 2024) to the NTK and its higher-derivative tensors (dNTK, ddNTK). The 1/n expansion and tensor decompositions (D, F, A, B, V, P, Q, R, S, T, U) are inherited from Roberts et al. (2022), who derived the corresponding recursion relations through lengthy algebraic manipulations. The key methodological departure is that the NTK is quadratic in the weights, invalidating the Gaussian conditional distribution approach used for preactivations; instead, this paper introduces new cubic and quartic Feynman vertices with NTK (dotted) lines and selection rules that handle the non-Gaussian structure. The work also builds on the QFT–neural network correspondence established by Halverson et al. (2021) and the 1/n expansion initiated by Yaida (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NTK is the central object governing training dynamics, yet finite-width corrections to its statistics were previously accessible only through laborious algebra, limiting adoption in the ML community. The Feynman diagram formalism provides a systematic, visual, and extensible method that the authors prove complete to all orders in 1/n, lowering the barrier for deriving new recursion relations. The all-orders stability theorem (critical NNGP implies critical NTK) and the exactness of the infinite-width NTK diagonal for scale-invariant activations are clean results with direct implications for initialization theory. Limitations include restriction to MLPs, focus on order 1/n for most results, and the need for high-dimensional numerical integration to evaluate the recursions, which constrains practical deployment. Open questions include extension to convolutional or attention architectures and whether the diagrammatic approach can be automated for higher-order corrections.
