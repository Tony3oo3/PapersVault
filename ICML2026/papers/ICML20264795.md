## Problem

Most expressive TTS systems enforce a single, globally coherent emotion per utterance, collapsing the mixed and text-emotion-misaligned expressions that characterize natural human speech. It remains unclear whether emotion representations in hybrid TTS are linearly steerable, which module and layers within the architecture are most amenable to steering, and how to quantitatively evaluate compositional mixed-emotion and mismatched text-emotion synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes input text, a neutral reference speech (to fix speaker identity), and emotion steering parameters specifying target emotion(s) with optional proportional weights for mixed emotions and an intensity scalar α. It produces a full emotional speech waveform via the standard two-stage pipeline (SLM → flow-matching → vocoder). Steering vectors are pre-extracted offline from speaker-matched, transcript-aligned neutral-emotion pairs, so at inference only the steering parameters change while the model weights remain frozen.

## Method

CoCoEmo is a training-free activation steering framework that injects direction vectors into the SLM of hybrid TTS models at inference time. A cross-conditioning diagnostic first establishes that the SLM (not the flow-matching decoder) is the primary driver of emotional prosody, and linear-probe analysis identifies mid-to-late layers and attention outputs as the most linearly separable steering sites. Single-emotion steering vectors are extracted via a mean-difference between speaker-matched emotion and neutral last-token activations, isolating acoustic emotional variation from content. Mixed-emotion vectors are formed as convex combinations of single-emotion vectors weighted by target proportions, and at inference the selected vector is added to activations at the chosen layers with intensity α, followed by norm renormalization to preserve semantic coherence. A multi-rater consensus evaluation protocol provides soft ground-truth emotion distributions for quantifying mixed-emotion fidelity.

## Results

On CREMA-D (in-distribution) with CosyVoice2, CoCoEmo (α=5.0) achieves E-SIM 0.779, Spearman ρ 0.209, and H-Rate 0.724, substantially outperforming no-steering (ρ and H-Rate undefined) and instruction-based baselines (ρ 0.111, H-Rate 0.694) while preserving speaker similarity (S-SIM 0.870) and naturalness (N-MOS 3.96). On IEMOCAP (out-of-distribution), CoCoEmo (α=5.0) reaches E-SIM 0.915 and ρ 0.215 with S-SIM 0.890. For IndexTTS2, combining steering with the native emotion vector (CoCoEmo+Emo_V, α=5.0) yields the strongest mixed-emotion control: ρ 0.399 and H-Rate 0.789 on CREMA-D, surpassing the emotion-vector baseline (ρ 0.236, H-Rate 0.731).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CosyVoice2's native instruction-based control, CoCoEmo alone wins on proportional mixed-emotion alignment (ρ 0.209 vs. 0.111 for Instruction1; H-Rate 0.724 vs. 0.694) but trails on TEP (0.149 vs. 0.200); however, combining steering with instructions (CoCoEmo+Ins1, α=5.0) dominates on all emotion metrics (TEP 0.335, ρ 0.319, H-Rate 0.760) at the cost of slightly lower N-MOS. Against IndexTTS2's emotion-vector baseline, CoCoEmo+Emo_V (α=5.0) achieves substantially higher ρ (0.399 vs. 0.236) and H-Rate (0.789 vs. 0.731) on CREMA-D, confirming that activation steering adds complementary quantitative control beyond the model's built-in conditioning. Against EmoSteer-TTS (the only prior training-free steering method, which targets the flow-matching module), CoCoEmo achieves higher ρ (0.209 vs. 0.193) and H-Rate (0.724 vs. 0.717) on CREMA-D while maintaining far better speaker similarity (S-SIM 0.870 vs. 0.807 at EmoSteer's best α=2.0), demonstrating that SLM-level steering is both more effective and less destructive to speaker identity than flow-matching-level steering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the activation steering paradigm—well-established in text LLMs via contrastive activation addition (Rimsky et al., 2024) and activation engineering (Turner et al., 2024)—into the hybrid TTS setting, where it had not been systematically explored. It departs from prior emotional TTS work (instruction-based control in CosyVoice2, emotion-vector conditioning in IndexTTS2, and flow-matching steering in EmoSteer-TTS) by operating directly in the SLM's latent space rather than through external conditioning interfaces, enabling compositional mixed-emotion vectors and text-independent acoustic steering that global conditioning cannot achieve. The cross-conditioning diagnostic and layer-wise linear-probe analysis represent a new empirical methodology for identifying where emotion is encoded in modular TTS architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that emotional prosody is primarily synthesized by the SLM rather than the flow-matching decoder provides a concrete architectural insight that redirects where future emotion-control research should focus. The training-free, plug-and-play steering mechanism offers a practical path to compositional and mismatched emotional expression without retraining, with potential applications in assistive communication, affective computing, and human-computer interaction. Key open questions remain: whether emotion representations in TTS latent spaces are truly linear or exhibit more complex nonlinear geometry, whether segment-level (rather than uniform) steering can capture dynamic intra-utterance emotional shifts, and how to extend the framework to continuous valence-arousal dimensions and free-form text prompts.
