## Problem

The Minimum Action Distance (MAD) is a fundamental state-similarity metric for reinforcement learning, but prior methods approximate it using symmetric distance functions (e.g., Euclidean distance between embeddings) that cannot capture the inherent asymmetry of the true MAD in environments with irreversible dynamics. Additionally, existing MAD approximation methods have not been systematically evaluated on their accuracy, and many rely on locality constraints rather than leveraging global trajectory structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only sequences of state observations (trajectories) collected by an arbitrary behavior policy—no actions, rewards, or transition probabilities are required. States can be discrete or continuous vectors of arbitrary dimensionality (e.g., 4D noisy grid coordinates or 8-joint Ant robot states). The output is a neural embedding function φ_θ: S → R^k (k=512 in main experiments) paired with a quasimetric d_q, yielding an asymmetric distance d_θ(s,s') = d_q(φ_θ(s), φ_θ(s')) that estimates the minimum number of actions needed to transition from s to s'.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating MAD learning as a constrained optimization (maximize all pairwise distances subject to one-step and triangle-inequality constraints), then making it tractable by parameterizing distances through a learned embedding and a quasimetric that enforces the triangle inequality by construction. Two algorithms are proposed: MadDist, which minimizes a composite loss combining a scale-invariant trajectory distance term, a contrastive separation term, and an upper-bound constraint penalty; and TDMadDist, which additionally bootstraps targets via a slowly-updated target network following temporal-difference logic (d(s_i, s_j) ≈ 1 + d'(s_{i+1}, s_j)). A novel simple quasimetric d_simple, defined as a weighted combination of max and mean ReLU differences, is introduced and shown to outperform Wide Norm and IQE alternatives. The approach is policy-agnostic and robust to stochasticity because it depends only on the support of the transition kernel, not on transition probabilities.

## Results

On the downstream goal-reaching planning task (Table 1), MadDist achieves perfect 1.00 success rates on all four PointMaze Large/Medium environments and 0.99 on PointMaze Giant Stitch, while reaching 0.80 on AntMaze Medium Explore (the hardest setting, using purely random exploration data). In direct MAD accuracy evaluation (Pearson/Spearman correlation and Ratio CV across all environments), MadDist consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest gaps in highly asymmetric environments like CliffWalking. TDMadDist, the temporal-difference variant, underperforms MadDist in most settings (e.g., 0.39 vs. 0.80 on AntMaze Medium Explore) but still substantially exceeds the Hilbert baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MadDist outperforms all three baseline families across every environment in both the planning task and direct MAD accuracy metrics. Against QRL (Wang et al., 2023), MadDist achieves substantially higher planning success on AntMaze Medium Explore (0.80 vs. 0.49) and PointMaze Medium Stitch (1.00 vs. 0.81), and wins on 6 of 7 planning environments; QRL's only edge is PointMaze Giant Navigate (0.87 vs. 0.93, where TDMadDist actually leads at 0.99). Against PlanDist and PlanDist-Simple (Steccanella & Jonsson, 2022), MadDist wins on all environments, with the gap most pronounced in asymmetric settings (e.g., 0.80 vs. 0.19 on AntMaze Medium Explore), confirming that the gains come from the combination of the quasimetric formulation and the revised loss (contrastive term + scale-invariant trajectory loss), not the quasimetric alone. Against the Hilbert representation (Park et al., 2024), MadDist dominates by a wide margin in every environment (e.g., 0.80 vs. 0.01 on AntMaze Medium Explore), highlighting the cost of using a symmetric Euclidean distance to approximate an inherently asymmetric quantity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PlanDist framework of Steccanella & Jonsson (2022), which formulated MAD learning as a constrained optimization over state embeddings, by replacing the symmetric distance metric with a quasimetric to capture the inherent asymmetry of the true MAD in environments with irreversible dynamics. It departs from QRL (Wang et al., 2023b), which also uses quasimetrics (IQE) but relies primarily on locality constraints rather than trajectory-derived path distances, and from Hilbert representations (Park et al., 2024b), which approximate MAD via symmetric Euclidean distances in a learned space. The key methodological contributions are a scale-invariant trajectory loss, a contrastive separation term, a temporal-difference bootstrapping variant, and a deliberately simple ReLU-based quasimetric that outperforms more elaborate constructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate MAD estimates are a foundational building block for goal-conditioned RL, reward shaping, and option discovery, yet prior methods had not been systematically evaluated on distance accuracy and could not represent directional structure. The introduced benchmark suite with known ground-truth MAD across discrete/continuous, deterministic/stochastic, and directed/undirected environments provides a much-needed controlled evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether MAD estimates transfer to nonstationary environments, whether they can guide search in stochastic domains, and whether the stochastic shortest-path distance (which accounts for transition probabilities rather than just support) can be recovered with analogous quasimetric formulations.
