## Problem

Existing 1-bit LLM quantization methods either train from scratch at enormous cost (hundreds of billions to trillions of tokens) or rely on post-training quantization with hardware-unfriendly mixed-precision strategies. The paper identifies that naively applying the sign function to pre-trained weights causes catastrophic initial loss (exceeding 10, near from-scratch levels) because the large gap between full-precision and binary representations irreversibly destroys the converged parameter state, making direct inheritance of pre-trained knowledge infeasible without careful optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained full-precision LLM weights (e.g., LLaMA3-1B/3B, SmolLM-135M, LLaMA2-7B) and a text corpus sampled from RedPajama (20B tokens for main experiments, 50B for ablations). It produces a 1-bit model where all linear layers in transformer blocks use {-1, +1} weights with a single per-layer scaling factor (S_l × S_a merged at inference), while embedding and output layers remain in higher precision. The final model supports bitwise inference with ~10× memory compression and ~10× theoretical speedup over FP16.

## Method

BinaryLLM introduces a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 (binary-aware initialization) performs an end-to-end search over per-input-channel scaling factors S_t that jointly minimize autoregressive loss across all layers, transforming pre-trained weights into a binary-friendly distribution without disturbing the original parameters (unlike AWQ's layer-wise approach). Stage 2 (progressive training) applies a consistent progressive quantization function F(x,t) = tanh(tx)/tanh(t) in both forward and backward passes, starting as a near-linear transformation that preserves the pre-trained weight distribution and smoothly transitioning to the sign function as t increases via an exponential scheduler. A dual-scaling compensation scheme retains the analytical L2-optimal scaling S_a (which minimizes quantization error at each step) alongside an additional learnable scaling S_l (which compensates for accuracy), avoiding the instability observed when S_a is made fully learnable as in FBI-LLM. At inference, the two scalings merge into a single factor and the progressive function is replaced by the sign function, incurring negligible overhead.

## Results

BinaryLLM achieves state-of-the-art 1-bit performance across model scales. At 1.3B (LLaMA3-1B), it reaches an average perplexity of 20.0 and 48.7% average zero-shot accuracy, outperforming FBI-LLM (21.9 PPL, 46.3% acc) and BitNet-1.58bit (63.7 PPL, 45.4% acc). At 3B (LLaMA3-3B), BinaryLLM attains 16.6 average PPL and 57.0% zero-shot accuracy, compared to BitNet's 34.9 PPL and 50.2% accuracy. At 7B (LLaMA2-7B), it achieves 9.2 average PPL and 56.7% zero-shot accuracy, narrowing the gap to full-precision LLaMA2-7B (6.4 PPL, 66.7% acc) to just 10 points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BinaryLLM consistently outperforms all 1-bit QAT baselines. Against FBI-LLM (trained from scratch on 1.26T tokens), BinaryLLM (trained on only 20B tokens from pre-trained weights) achieves lower average PPL at every scale (35.3 vs 63.9 at 135M; 20.0 vs 21.9 at 1.3B; 9.2 vs 10.3 at 7B) and higher zero-shot accuracy (42.8% vs 40.4%; 48.7% vs 46.3%; 56.7% vs 52.9%). Against BitNet (1.58-bit, trained from scratch), BinaryLLM at true 1-bit achieves dramatically lower PPL (20.0 vs 63.7 at 1.3B; 16.6 vs 34.9 at 3B) and higher zero-shot accuracy (48.7% vs 45.4%; 57.0% vs 50.2%). Against OneBit (pre-trained initialization), BinaryLLM reduces Wiki2 PPL by 10.8 points at 1.3B (14.7 vs 25.4) and by 1.0 at 7B (8.7 vs 9.7). Against 1-bit PTQ methods BiLLM and ARB-LLM, BinaryLLM is orders of magnitude better in PPL (16.6 vs 93.6/52.9 at 3B), as PTQ methods produce nearly unusable models at 1-bit. Against MoS at 7B, BinaryLLM achieves lower PPL (9.2 vs 8.8 is actually MoS winning on PPL, but BinaryLLM wins on zero-shot: 56.7% vs unavailable for MoS). Against IR-Net's progressive training (ablation), BinaryLLM's consistent progressive training reduces average PPL by 2.3 (35.0 vs 37.3) and improves zero-shot accuracy by 0.9% (42.0% vs 41.1%) at 135M scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends progressive training for binary networks (IR-Net, RBNN) from small CNNs to LLMs, but with a key departure: prior methods only approximate the gradient in backward while still applying the Sign function in forward, creating an optimization gap. BinaryLLM instead uses a tanh-based progressive function consistently in both forward and backward, smoothly transitioning from linear to Sign over training. It also builds on AWQ-style per-channel scaling for initialization but replaces the layer-wise objective with an end-to-end autoregressive loss to account for cross-layer error accumulation, and extends XNORNet's single scaling factor into a dual-scaling scheme that preserves the analytical L2-minimizing solution while adding a learnable compensation term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption (from BitNet and FBI-LLM) that 1-bit LLMs must be trained from scratch for stability, showing that pre-trained weights can be effectively binarized with far less compute (20B vs. 1.26T tokens at 130M scale). However, the practical gap to full-precision remains substantial (~10% perplexity, 6–7% zero-shot accuracy drop), and the largest model tested is only 7B, leaving open whether the approach scales to 70B+ models where pre-trained knowledge is denser and harder to preserve. Future work on better progressive schedulers and mitigating degradation in stronger baselines (where the paper itself notes sharper drops) are important open directions.
