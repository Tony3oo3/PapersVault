## Problem

Standard LLM pre-training shuffles corpora, effectively treating the training data as a timeless pool, yet models exhibit a 'knowledge horizon' gap where accuracy on recent facts lags behind older ones even within the training window. The impact of data sampling order on the temporal distribution of factual knowledge encoded in model parameters remains poorly understood, with no controlled comparison between sequential and shuffled pre-training at production scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261044/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes filtered Common Crawl snapshots (2018–2025 for sequential, 2020–2024 for shuffled) processed through a multi-stage pipeline (dactory) yielding ~315B tokens per yearly segment and 2.5T tokens total, with a 4096-token context window. It produces a family of eight 6B-parameter Transformer decoder checkpoints (one per yearly boundary) plus matched-token-count shuffled baselines. Evaluation outputs cloze-formulation accuracy and generative F1 scores on KairosQA (7,167 Wikidata-derived subject-relation pairs with year-specific ground truth), OLMES general language scores, and TAQA temporal F1.

## Method

The paper pre-trains 6B-parameter Transformer decoders (32 layers, 32 heads, 4096 hidden dim, GQA with 4 KV heads, RoPE, SwiGLU) under two data curricula: strict chronological ordering of CC snapshots versus global shuffling, with all other hyperparameters held identical (AdamW, Warmup-Stable-Decay, peak LR 1e-3, 4.2M-token batch). A branching cooldown strategy (30k-step cosine decay to 1e-4) finalizes each checkpoint to ensure converged comparisons at matched token budgets. The key finding is that sequential ordering induces a 'recency peak'—each model achieves maximum accuracy on the year immediately preceding its training cutoff—while shuffled models exhibit 'temporal alignment inertia,' over-prioritizing historically repeated content and degrading toward random on recent years. The authors introduce KairosQA, a benchmark of 7,167 temporally grounded multiple-choice and generative questions extracted from Wikidata with popularity-based filtering and LLM-assisted quality control, to isolate temporal alignment from mere knowledge absence. They further show that the sequential model's initial training lag on OLMES stems from non-stationary data quality across snapshots rather than an inherent cost of chronological ordering, and that simple mitigations for the associated forgetting of older knowledge (model soup, data replay) remain insufficient.

## Results

On OLMES (general language understanding), the sequential 6B model converges to parity with the shuffled baseline at 2.5T tokens, confirming that temporal ordering does not degrade general capabilities. On KairosQA (temporal factual knowledge), the sequential model exhibits a 'recency peak,' achieving its highest accuracy in the year immediately preceding its training cutoff, while the shuffled baseline shows a precipitous drop toward random accuracy on 2024 questions. In the cloze formulation with 12 choices, the sequential model reaches 22% accuracy versus a 7.7% random baseline for 2024 knowledge. On TAQA, discriminative power is limited with F1 scores ranging only from 1.5% to 10% across all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its sequentially pre-trained 6B model against four families of open-source base models (Llama 3.1-8B, Gemma 3-4B, Olmo 3-7B, and Qwen 3 in 4B/8B/14B variants), all of which use standard shuffled pre-training. On KairosQA, all open-source models exhibit a consistent performance degradation as questions target more recent years, with 2024 performance often approaching the random baseline; model scale does not fundamentally alter this trajectory, merely shifting the curve vertically. The sequential model is the only one to reverse this trend, outperforming similarly sized open-source models on older knowledge and surpassing significantly larger models (e.g., Qwen 3-14B) on recent knowledge from 2023 onward. On OLMES, the sequential model matches the shuffled baseline at convergence, confirming no general-capability trade-off. The shuffled baseline itself shows two local maxima (2015 and 2020) followed by a sharp decline, suggesting it over-prioritizes historically repeated content over contemporary facts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of pre-training data composition research (Blakeney et al., Feng et al.) and temporal knowledge evaluation (Zhao et al.'s TAQA, Park et al.'s ChroKnowledge), but carves out a distinct experimental question: isolating data *ordering* as the sole variable rather than data mixing ratios or post-hoc fine-tuning. Unlike TiC-LM (Li et al., 2025), which addresses continual learning by incorporating new data after pre-training, this work shows that the temporal structure of the pre-training curriculum itself—sequential vs. shuffled—fundamentally shapes how factual knowledge is distributed across time in model parameters. The KairosQA benchmark extends prior temporal QA datasets by grounding questions in Wikidata's explicit temporal annotations and filtering for popular, temporally-varying subjects, addressing a gap where existing benchmarks (TAQA) proved insufficiently discriminative for smaller models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard shuffled pre-training induces a 'temporal alignment inertia'—where models systematically underperform on the most recent years of their training data despite having access to it—challenges a core assumption in LLM development and has direct practical implications for how pre-training corpora are organized. However, the sequential approach's trade-off of forgetting older knowledge remains unresolved: both model soup and data replay experiments fail to recover historical accuracy without sacrificing recency, pointing to a fundamental tension in sequential learning that current techniques cannot address. The 6B parameter scale limits confidence in whether these dynamics persist at the 70B+ scale where most production models operate, and the paper acknowledges that disentangling data quality variation across years from the intrinsic cost of sequential ordering remains open.
