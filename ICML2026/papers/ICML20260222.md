## Problem

Despite the strong zero-shot forecasting performance of Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs), their internal structure remains poorly understood compared to LLMs. It is unclear which architectural components (attention heads, MLPs, layers) are genuinely necessary versus redundant, and no principled, data-independent strategy exists for compressing these models. This paper asks: what universal redundancies exist across diverse TSFM architectures, and can they be identified and exploited without access to task-specific data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the frozen weights of six pre-trained TSFMs (Chronos, Chronos-Bolt, Chronos 2, TimesFM 2.5, Toto, Moirai 1.1) and consumes time series context windows (up to 2048–8192 timesteps depending on the model) from the GIFT-Eval benchmark (144K series across 7 domains and 10 frequencies). It produces three types of outputs: per-component importance scores via zero-ablation and direct logit attribution on the residual stream, pruned model checkpoints with specific heads and MLPs removed (up to 28–43% of heads), and zero-shot forecasts from the pruned models evaluated via MASE, CRPS, NRMSE, sMAPE, and MSIS. The head-pruning ordering is purely intrinsic—computed from the stable rank of each head's W_Q W_K^T matrix—requiring no forward pass on evaluation data.

## Method

The paper introduces a mechanistic interpretability toolbox for TSFMs built on zero-ablation of individual components (heads, MLPs, entire layers) and direct logit attribution (DLA) on the residual stream, revealing that middle layers across all six models are highly redundant while early and late layers are critical. To explain and exploit this redundancy, the authors develop a theoretical framework that recasts cross-attention as a Nadaraya-Watson kernel regressor, showing via SVD of the combined query-key projection matrix M = W_Q W_K^T / √d_head that attention sharpness is governed by the spectral modes of M: low stable rank heads act as narrow-bandwidth kernels (sharp, task-specific) while high stable rank heads produce diffuse global smoothing. This motivates an intrinsic head-pruning strategy that orders heads by stable rank and ablates from the diffuse end, formalized via a heads@1pp metric (minimum heads to keep for ≤1% error increase). The theory is backed by a formal proposition (Prop. 4.1) proving that softmax attention concentrates exponentially around the best-aligned key with a rate controlled by the top-r spectral modes, and the strategy is validated to preserve seasonality structure and break degenerate behaviors like context parroting when the correct sharp heads are targeted.

## Results

Across six leading TSFMs (Chronos, Chronos-Bolt, Chronos-2, TimesFM 2.5, Toto, Moirai 1.1), the paper demonstrates that 28% of all attention heads (and in some cases additional MLPs) can be zero-ablated with a ≤6% increase in MASE on GIFT-Eval. Specifically, Chronos-2 loses only 4.71% MASE after ablating 24% of heads plus 2 MLPs, TimesFM 2.5 loses 6.12% MASE after ablating 28% of heads plus 2 MLPs, Toto loses 3.54% MASE after ablating 29% of heads, and Chronos-Bolt loses just 4.24% MASE (and only 0.19% CRPS) after ablating 28% of heads plus 6 MLPs. The intrinsic stable-rank-based head pruning strategy preserves the essential seasonality structure of forecasts, whereas random head ablation of the same proportion destroys forecast structure entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability and compression study rather than a forecasting benchmark comparison, so the key 'baselines' are alternative ablation strategies. The intrinsic stable-rank head pruning strategy (ablating heads from high to low stable rank of W_Q W_K^T) consistently outperforms random head selection: ablating the same number of heads randomly destroys the seasonality structure of forecasts (Fig. 13–14), while the intrinsic strategy preserves it even when ablating 51% of all heads in TimesFM 2.5. Across architectures, encoder-decoder models (Chronos-Bolt) show extreme resilience to MLP ablation (maintaining performance after removing half of all MLPs), while decoder-only models (Toto, TimesFM 2.5) are far more sensitive to MLP removal. The paper also contrasts its intrinsic (data-independent) strategy with the extrinsic data-dependent truncated-SVD compression of Yu et al. (2025c), positioning its approach as requiring no access to test data. Middle layers (e.g., layers 3–10 in Chronos) are consistently the most ablateable across all six models, while first and last layers are the most critical, a finding that holds uniformly across encoder-decoder, decoder-only, and encoder-only architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability framework of Elhage et al. (2021) and the kernel-regression correspondence of attention (Tsai et al., 2019) from LLMs to Time Series Foundation Models, introducing a spectral characterization of attention sharpness via the SVD of the query-key projection product. It departs from prior TSFM compression work (Yu et al., 2025c; Zhao et al., 2026) by proposing a purely intrinsic, data-independent head-pruning criterion based on stable rank, rather than data-dependent SVD truncation or structured pruning with fine-tuning. The finding of redundant middle layers and specialized early/late layers parallels LLM results (Michel et al., 2019; Voita et al., 2019; Sanyal et al., 2025), but the paper establishes these as universal properties across encoder-decoder, decoder-only, and encoder-only TSFM architectures, and connects specific 'sharp' heads to known degenerate behaviors (context parroting, seasonality bias) that are unique to the time-series domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: 28% of attention heads (and some MLPs) can be pruned with ≤6% MASE degradation across leading TSFMs on GIFT-Eval, using a criterion that requires no validation data. The theoretical kernel-regression framework provides a principled explanation for why certain heads are critical (sharp, low-stable-rank heads encode local-shift/parroting structure) and why others are redundant (diffuse, high-stable-rank heads perform weak global smoothing). Open questions include how architecture (encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only) determines the division of labor between MLPs and attention heads, whether the stable-rank strategy transfers to larger or differently-tokenized TSFMs, and whether the identified sharp heads can be targeted for intervention to eliminate degenerate behaviors like mean regression without sacrificing forecasting accuracy.
