## Problem

Although LoRA is widely used for parameter-efficient adaptation, its fundamental properties as a modular knowledge memory—storage capacity, saturation behavior, parameter efficiency, and composability across multiple adapters—remain largely uncharacterized. Prior works incorporate LoRA as a pipeline component for specific tasks but do not systematically probe its intrinsic memory limits or failure modes, leaving practitioners without guidance on when and how LoRA-based memory can be reliably deployed alongside non-parametric methods like RAG and ICL.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a text query (e.g., a factual question) and optionally external context (full-document ICL or retrieved RAG snippets), combined with one or more pre-trained LoRA adapters that encode the target knowledge. In multi-LoRA configurations, a router (embedding-based or token-based) selects top-k candidate adapters, which are then composed via merging (e.g., TIES) before the base model generates a text answer. The LoRA adapters themselves are trained in a supervised manner on synthetic supervision (QA pairs, summaries, rewrites) derived from source documents, with rank, training steps, and data format serving as the primary design knobs.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical audit of LoRA as a parametric memory across four dimensions: (1) single-module capacity, sweeping rank from 2 to 1024 and knowledge load from 1K to 20K tokens on PhoneBook and CounterFact to map saturation curves and parameter efficiency; (2) knowledge internalization, comparing synthetic data formats (QA, Summary, Rewrite) and their combinations on a novel PaperQA benchmark built from 15 recent conference papers, while varying base model scale (0.6B–14B) and generator quality; (3) multi-LoRA scaling, partitioning knowledge across many small adapters and quantifying the performance gap between oracle routing and practical embedding-based routing, then evaluating five merging strategies (linear averaging, concatenation, TIES, DARE, scaled concatenation) under routing uncertainty; and (4) long-context case studies on NarrativeQA and QuALITY, testing hybrid LoRA+ICL/RAG systems and measuring end-to-end latency. Two new benchmarks—PhoneBook (programmatically scalable key-value pairs) and PaperQA (hierarchical 3-level QA with LLM-judge scoring)—are introduced to isolate specific memory properties. Key findings include: capacity scales with rank but saturates finitely; low ranks (e.g., r=4) achieve peak parameter efficiency; QA-format synthetic data outperforms raw text and scales monotonically; multi-LoRA with oracle routing substantially exceeds single-LoRA capacity, but practical routing errors can negate these gains; TIES merging is the most robust composition strategy; and hybrid LoRA+ICL systems outperform all standalone approaches on long multi-hop tasks.

## Results

On NarrativeQA (ROUGE-L) and QuALITY (accuracy) with Qwen3-8B, the best hybrid configuration (Multi-LoRA Top-3 + ICL) achieves 37.23 and 76.26 respectively, outperforming standalone ICL (35.22 / 72.89) and RAG (31.15 / 64.53). In the closed-book setting, Single LoRA reaches 44.10 on QuALITY (vs. 26.06 base model), while Multi-LoRA Top-3 reaches 46.26. On the efficiency front, parameter efficiency peaks at low ranks (r=4) and declines at higher ranks, and TIES merging substantially outperforms linear averaging, CAT, and DARE for multi-LoRA composition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standalone ICL (Brown et al., 2020), the hybrid Multi-LoRA Top-3 + ICL configuration wins on both benchmarks for Qwen3-8B (37.23 vs. 35.22 on NQA; 76.26 vs. 72.89 on QuALITY) and Llama-3.1-8B (38.78 vs. 33.81; 69.22 vs. 70.20), confirming LoRA as a complementary rather than replacement memory. Against RAG (Lewis et al., 2020), the hybrid systems achieve substantially larger gains, e.g., Multi-LoRA Top-3 + ICL reaches 76.26 on QuALITY vs. RAG's 64.53 on Qwen3-8B. Against KMSDCD (Caccia et al., 2025), both Single LoRA and Multi-LoRA Top-3 + ICL outperform it on both benchmarks across all four models (e.g., 44.10 vs. 33.67 on QuALITY for Qwen3-8B). Among merging strategies, TIES (Yadav et al., 2023) is the most robust, reaching performance comparable to single LoRA, while DARE (Yu et al., 2024) underperforms due to stochastic dropping discarding critical memorized information. For LoRA variants, DoRA (Liu et al., 2024) and PiSSA (Meng et al., 2024) show no consistent advantage over standard LoRA across models and settings. Token-based routers (Arrow, SpectR, LAG) fail to close the gap to oracle routing, confirming that routing remains the primary bottleneck for multi-LoRA systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as the underlying parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism, but repurposes it from a task-adaptation tool into a modular parametric knowledge memory. It departs from recent works that embed LoRA as a component in end-to-end pipelines (e.g., DCD by Caccia et al. 2025, SEAL by Zweiger et al. 2025, PRAG by Su et al. 2025) by treating LoRA as an object of systematic investigation rather than a proven building block. The multi-LoRA composition and routing literature (LoRAHub, MoLoRA, LAG, SPECTR) informs the system-level analysis, but the paper explicitly targets the memory-specific questions—capacity limits, parameter efficiency, and interference under merging—that prior system papers leave uncharacterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic empirical mapping of LoRA's operational boundaries as a knowledge memory, providing practitioners with concrete guidance on rank selection, synthetic data design, merging strategy, and when to pair LoRA with ICL/RAG. The finding that LoRA can catastrophically fail under routing errors or over-merging, and that it is best deployed as a complementary rather than standalone memory axis, reframes how the field should think about parametric vs. non-parametric memory. Key open questions remain: continual time-incremental updates over diverse topic streams, robust routing at scale, and practical adapter serving (caching, prefetching, kernel-level optimization) for multi-module composition.
