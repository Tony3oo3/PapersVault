## Problem

State-of-the-art block-wise diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) waste computation during their remasking mechanism: each denoising step commits only the most confident tokens and discards the rest, throwing away intermediate probability distributions that still carry structured contextual signals. This information loss means dLLMs expend significantly more compute per token than autoregressive models without reliably translating it into accuracy gains, particularly on complex reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, RCD takes a text prompt and an all-masked token block (b=32 or b=64 tokens) and iteratively denoises it into a fully decoded text sequence. Uniquely, each denoising step's input is not just the current mask/unmask pattern but also a residual context vector per masked position, computed from the previous step's discarded probability distributions and the model's own embedding codebook. The model is evaluated on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AIME24/25, MinervaMath) with sequence lengths up to 16,384 tokens for SDAR and 512–1,024 for LLaDA.

## Method

RCD recycles discarded token computation by converting each remasked position's full probability distribution into a residual vector via a weighted sum over the input embedding codebook (Eq. 1), then injecting it into the next denoising step through a highway connection whose gate α is set to the normalized Shannon entropy of that distribution (Eq. 2–3), so high-uncertainty tokens contribute more residual context. To avoid the prohibitively expensive backprop-through-time that the self-referential residual loop would require, training uses a decoupled two-stage pipeline: a frozen lightweight reference model generates stable proxy probability targets and entropy weights, while the target model is trained with standard single-step cross-entropy supervision on inputs augmented with those proxy residuals. At inference, a temperature-scaled softmax (T_res) calibrates the target model's own entropy weights to match the reference model's distribution seen during training, and the residual stream is initialized either cold (zero vector) or warm (one reference-model forward pass). The entire conversion from a standard dLLM to RCD requires only ~300M tokens of fine-tuning.

## Results

RCD consistently outperforms standard Sequential Denoising (SeqD) across all SDAR model scales (4B, 8B) and block sizes (b32, b64), yielding 4–11 percentage-point gains on GSM8K and MATH500. The most dramatic improvements appear on competition-level benchmarks: on AIME24, SDAR-8B-b64 accuracy more than doubles from 7.08% (SeqD) to 18.75% (RCD), and on AIME25 from 9.79% to 16.04%. For LLaDA, RCD achieves 78.09% on GSM8K and 37.00% on MinervaMath versus 75.74% and 31.10% for SeqD. Under throughput-matched conditions, RCD maintains near-identical tokens-per-second while improving accuracy by 2–9%, and achieves up to 4–5× fewer denoising steps at the baseline's peak accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Sequential Denoising (SeqD), the standard block-wise dLLM decoding that discards low-confidence tokens at each step. RCD wins on every benchmark and model configuration: gains range from +3.2 pp (GSM8K, SDAR-8B-b32) to +11.6 pp (MATH500, SDAR-8B-b64), with the largest relative improvements on AIME24/25 where accuracy roughly doubles. Under throughput-matched evaluation (Table 3), RCD still outperforms SeqD by 2–9 pp while sustaining comparable tokens-per-second, confirming the gains are not merely a speed-accuracy trade-off. The paper also compares against Loopholing (Jo et al., 2025), the state-of-the-art latent-state diffusion method, under a constrained 300M-token training budget on SDAR-4B-b64. RCD achieves 86.35% on GSM8K and 66.20% on MATH500 within a single epoch, whereas Loopholing fails to produce any valid, evaluable mathematical sequences (reported as NA), demonstrating that RCD's use of the model's own input embedding codebook avoids the numerical instability that plagues raw hidden-state injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCD builds directly on the masked denoising framework of block-wise dLLMs (SDAR, LLaDA) and repurposes the soft-token mechanism from Butt et al. (2025) and Zhang et al. (2025b) — the probability-weighted sum over vocabulary embeddings — as the carrier for residual context. Conceptually it draws on residual learning (He et al., 2015) and highway connections (Srivastava et al., 2015) to interpolate between mask embeddings and recycled token distributions, but departs from prior latent-reasoning methods (Coconut, Loopholing) by operating in the model's own input-embedding space rather than raw hidden states, avoiding the norm-mismatch instability that plagues those approaches. The two-stage decoupled training pipeline (frozen reference model generating proxy residuals) is a practical departure from end-to-end backpropagation-through-time, which would be prohibitively expensive for the recursive self-referential loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RCD addresses a concrete and previously overlooked inefficiency in dLLM inference — the systematic discarding of already-computed token distributions during remasking — and shows that recycling this information yields 4–11 percentage-point accuracy gains and up to 4–5× fewer denoising steps at equivalent accuracy, with only ~300M tokens of fine-tuning to convert a standard dLLM. The finding that a non-informative Dirichlet prior outperforms both 1.7B and 4B reference models for residual initialization is a useful caution: low-quality proxy signals can actively degrade generation, suggesting the reference-model component may be eliminable. Open questions include whether the gains extend beyond mathematical reasoning to broader instruction-following and code tasks, whether the method scales to 100B+ dLLMs (e.g., LLaDA 2.0), and whether the entropy-weighting scheme is optimal or whether learned gating could further improve the trade-off.
