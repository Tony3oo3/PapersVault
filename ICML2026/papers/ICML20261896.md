## Problem

Standard RLHF methods frame text generation as a discrete-time MDP where the state is merely a passive record of previously emitted tokens, discarding the continuous trajectory of underlying meaning between tokens. The model has no access to the path between discrete endpoints, limiting its ability to model smooth semantic evolution. This paper asks whether text generation can be reformulated as a continuous-time latent dynamical process, where the internal state flows smoothly through a hidden meaning space governed by differential equations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, encodes it into an initial latent state X_0^x ∈ R^d, and generates a token sequence by alternating discrete policy actions (token selection) with continuous ODE integration over each unit interval (t, t+1]. The latent state is a d-dimensional vector (d=1024–2048 across model variants) that evolves via a parameterized neural ODE, rather than being a sequence of token embeddings. The output is a standard generated text sequence, but the internal representation is a continuous flow in latent space, requiring only O(d) memory per step regardless of sequence length.

## Method

CT-LLM formulates text generation as an MDP whose state evolves via a neural ODE (dX_s = b_φ(s, X_s, a_t) ds) between discrete token-emission steps, jointly optimizing a token-selection policy π_θ and the ODE dynamics b_φ through reinforcement learning. The policy gradient is computed via a REINFORCE-style estimator, while gradients with respect to the ODE parameters are obtained through the continuous adjoint sensitivity method, which integrates an augmented backward-in-time ODE to avoid storing the full forward trajectory. A KL divergence regularization term against a frozen reference policy (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) is decomposed into per-step divergences (Theorem 4.4), yielding a tractable step-level penalty. The architecture pairs a scaled residual ODE network (SwiGLU FFN blocks conditioned on sinusoidal time and action embeddings) with a hierarchical policy network (GeGLU FFN blocks with learnable temperature), achieving O(d) memory and O(T·N·d²) compute per sample—avoiding the O(T²) self-attention cost of Transformers. The paper provides a convergence guarantee of Õ(1/√K) for the bilevel policy-dynamics optimization and a suboptimality bound for the KL-regularized objective.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2, CT-LLM (1.2B, RL+KL) achieves 36.5% ROUGE-L, 16.6% raw win rate (WR), and 19.2% length-controlled win rate (LC), outperforming the strongest discrete-time baseline (Transformer Dec-only with RL+KL at 32.5% R-L, 11.4% WR, 12.8% LC) by approximately 4% on R-L and 5–6% on WR/LC. Under a compute-matched budget of ~1.4×10²⁰ FLOPs, CT-LLM (1.2B) scores 36.8 R-L versus 28.5 for a 1.7B Transformer and 30.2 for an 8B Transformer. In the long-context regime (≥500 tokens), CT-LLM retains 17.5% LC win rate compared to 10.7% for discrete Transformer baselines, approaching the 8B reference model's 18.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CT-LLM (1.2B) consistently outperforms both Transformer baselines (1.7B) across all three metrics. Against the strongest baseline, Transformer Dec-only with RL+KL, CT-LLM gains +4.0% on ROUGE-L (36.5 vs 32.5), +5.2% on raw win rate (16.6 vs 11.4), and +6.4% on length-controlled win rate (19.2 vs 12.8). The gap is even larger against the Encoder-Decoder Transformer, where CT-LLM leads by +9.3% R-L and +10.3% LC. However, CT-LLM still trails the 8B Llama-3-8B-Instruct reference model by 4.2% R-L and 4.0% LC, though it uses only 1.2B parameters (roughly 1/7 the size). Under compute-matched conditions (~1.4×10²⁰ FLOPs), CT-LLM achieves 36.8 R-L versus 28.5 for a 1.7B Transformer and 30.2 for an 8B Transformer, demonstrating substantially higher compute efficiency per FLOP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-time state evolution, the RLHF/MDP formulation of text generation (Christiano et al., 2017), and REINFORCE-style policy gradients (Williams, 1992). It departs from prior continuous-time NLP models (e.g., latent ODEs for representation learning or sequence interpolation) by making the ODE the core generative mechanism that governs the policy's internal state between discrete token emissions, rather than a post-hoc representation tool. The adjoint sensitivity method is repurposed from its original role in training Neural ODEs to enable end-to-end gradient computation through the latent dynamics during RL training, coupling it with policy gradient updates in a bilevel optimization scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretically grounded alternative to the dominant discrete-time Transformer paradigm, with provable convergence guarantees and an architectural memory advantage (O(d) vs O(T·d)) that could matter for very long sequences. However, the empirical evidence is limited to a single benchmark (AlpacaEval 2) with modest absolute gains over the 8B reference model, and the compute-matched comparison is weakened by the 8B baseline being forced to underfit. Open questions include whether the continuous latent dynamics scale meaningfully beyond 2B parameters, whether the ODE state captures genuinely interpretable semantic flow versus serving as a flexible parameterization, and how the method compares to emerging state-space models (e.g., Mamba, S4) that also avoid quadratic attention.
