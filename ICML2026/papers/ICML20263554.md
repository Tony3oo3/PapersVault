## Problem

Existing LLM debiasing methods adopt a suppressive paradigm—modifying parameters, prompts, or neurons associated with biased behavior—which is brittle, data-hungry, weakly generalizable across bias types and demographics, and often degrades general model capability. The paper identifies a key disconnect: LLMs simultaneously encode knowledge of social bias (recognizing when a statement is biased) and produce biased content, suggesting that activating this internal bias knowledge could mitigate bias without the costs of suppression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes any standard text prompt and produces debiased text generation with no architectural or parameter changes to the model. The one-time setup requires only 45 simple yes/no bias-knowledge questions (e.g., 'Do you think that race influences problem-solving skills?') spanning three demographic dimensions and three question types, rather than the hundreds to tens of thousands of stereotype-specific samples used by fine-tuning or editing baselines. The intervention is a single multiplicative scaling of identified neuron activations during the forward pass, taking less than 30 minutes to extract neurons for a given model.

## Method

KnowBias identifies 'know-bias neurons'—intermediate FFN neurons whose activations causally contribute to the model's correct recognition of biased content—using integrated gradients attribution (Dai et al., 2022) over a small set of bias-knowledge questions. Neurons are selected if their attribution scores exceed a threshold τ% of the maximum and appear consistently across at least β% of questions, then aggregated via union across demographic dimensions into a unified set. At inference time, the activations of these know-bias neurons are multiplicatively scaled by a factor λ (e.g., 2–3.5), amplifying the model's internal bias-awareness signal without suppressing any bias-behavior neurons or modifying parameters. This design is model-agnostic, requires no retraining, and leverages the insight that bias knowledge is encoded in shared, abstract representations that generalize across bias types and demographics, unlike demographic-specific bias-behavior neurons.

## Results

KnowBias achieves the best average rank (AvgR↓) across all social bias benchmarks on all three LLM backbones: 1.6/2.2/1.8 (gender/race/religion) on Llama-3.2-3B, 1.4/2.4/2.6 on Qwen-3-4B, and 2.2/2.2/2.4 on Llama-3.1-8B, outperforming or tying with the next-best method (SD) in most settings. On general capability (OBQA, COPA, ARC-C, ARC-E), KnowBias shows no >5% accuracy drop on Llama-3.2-3B (e.g., OBQA 53.45% vs. base 51.02%), while PEFT, BiasEdit, SD, and ReGiFT all exhibit ≥5% drops on multiple tasks. The method requires only 45 simple yes/no bias-knowledge questions and a single inference-time scaling factor (λ=2–3.5), with neuron extraction completing in under 30 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KnowBias outperforms all seven baselines on average rank across every backbone and demographic dimension. The closest competitor is SD (Self-Debiasing), which achieves AvgR of 2.8–3.6, still 0.8–1.4 ranks worse than KnowBias; however, SD degrades general capability by >5% on all four reasoning tasks (e.g., OBQA drops from 51.02% to 44.18% on Llama-3.2-3B). Fine-tuning-based methods (LFTF, ReGiFT, PEFT) show mixed bias performance but severe utility loss: PEFT drops OBQA to 34.65–48.97% and BiasEdit to 25.22–43.55% on Llama-3.2-3B, while KnowBias maintains or improves accuracy. Activation steering (FairSteer) and neuron elimination (CRISPR) are consistently outperformed on bias metrics (AvgR 4.4–7.0 vs. 1.4–2.6) and CRISPR also shows >5% drops on several general tasks. Model editing (BiasEdit) is the weakest on general capability, with up to 25% accuracy drops, while KnowBias requires no parameter modification at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'suppressive paradigm' in LLM debiasing (prompt steering, fine-tuning, model editing, activation steering, and neuron elimination) by reframing the problem: rather than weakening neurons that produce biased outputs, it strengthens neurons that encode the model's existing knowledge *about* bias. The technical mechanism builds directly on the knowledge-neuron attribution framework of Dai et al. (2022), repurposing integrated-gradients-based neuron identification from factual knowledge to normative bias-awareness knowledge. The conceptual motivation draws on cognitive science findings that awareness of one's own biases reduces reliance on stereotyped judgments, creating a bridge between human debiasing psychology and mechanistic interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KnowBias offers a practically attractive debiasing recipe: inference-time only, no retraining, 45 simple yes/no questions, and cross-demographic generalization, making it easy to deploy as a lightweight post-hoc intervention on existing LLMs. However, the approach is limited to three demographic dimensions (gender, race, religion) and relies on normative judgments that may not transfer across cultures or to underrepresented bias axes (disability, age, socioeconomic status). Open questions include whether the know-bias neuron set remains stable across model scales and architectures, whether the enhancement scale λ can be adapted per-prompt, and whether the paradigm extends to other normative objectives beyond social bias (safety, ethical alignment).
