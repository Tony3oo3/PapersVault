## Problem

Standard inverse folding models (e.g., ProteinMPNN) are trained to maximize sequence recovery rate, making them function-agnostic and unable to satisfy task-specific constraints like epitope geometry or binding affinity. More critically, the MLE training objective optimizes the expected reward across all samples, while practical protein design uses a Best-of-N (BoN) inference protocol that selects only the top candidate—creating a fundamental training-inference misalignment that no prior RL-based protein design method has addressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D protein backbone (Cα coordinates), a binary mask indicating which residues belong to a fixed functional motif (or catalytic/binding site), and optionally a ligand structure for enzyme tasks. It outputs a full amino acid sequence over the 20 standard residues, generated autoregressively in a random decoding order inherited from ProteinMPNN. At inference, N=8 candidate sequences are sampled per backbone and the best is selected by motif RMSD after ESMFold refolding; for enzyme tasks, AutoDock Vina scores guide selection.

## Method

FIDIA reformulates inverse folding as an MDP and derives an analytical policy gradient for the BoN objective (expected maximum reward over N samples) rather than the standard expected-return objective. The gradient decomposes into a score term for the best sample plus (N−1) times the expected score over the sub-level set of lower-reward samples, revealing a collaborative mechanism that elevates both the winner and its supporting distribution. A U-statistic-based estimator (Prop. 3.2) provides an unbiased, lower-variance Monte Carlo approximation by leveraging combinatorial weights p_i = C(i−1, N−1)/C(K, N) over a larger batch of K > N samples. The final optimization uses a GRPO-style PPO clipping objective with rank-based advantages A_i = (λ_i − λ̄)/σ_λ, where λ_i encodes each sample's dual role as a potential BoN winner and as a contrastive baseline. A composite reward integrates motif RMSD (Cauchy kernel), global RMSD (flat-top Cauchy), Vina binding energy, and batch diversity, with task-specific weighting.

## Results

On the FrameF motif scaffolding benchmark, FIDIA achieves a Success Rate of 37.50% (vs. 26.67% for ProteinMPNN) and the lowest mRMSD* of 1.43 Å, outperforming all RL and BoN baselines. On the RFD benchmark, FIDIA reaches 65.83% SR and 0.94 Å mRMSD*, a 10.83-point improvement over ProteinMPNN (55.00%). In the affinity-enhancing enzyme design task, FIDIA improves the Vina binding score from −6.53 kcal/mol (LigandMPNN) to −7.21 kcal/mol while maintaining competitive structural fidelity (mRMSD* 0.64 Å). FIDIA also achieves the highest sample diversity (0.35/0.43 on FrameF/RFD) among all methods, indicating it avoids mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIDIA outperforms all six baselines on the primary SR metric across both FrameF and RFD benchmarks, with the largest margin over ProteinMPNN (+10.83 pts on RFD) and ESM-IF (+31.67 pts on RFD). Among RL-optimized baselines, FIDIA surpasses GRPO by 4.17 pts (FrameF) and 7.50 pts (RFD) in SR, and MultiDPO by 3.33 and 8.33 pts respectively. The BoN top1 baseline, which trains on the single best candidate per batch, is the closest competitor on mRMSD* (1.44 vs. 1.43 on FrameF) but trails FIDIA by 3.33 pts in SR on FrameF and 10.83 pts on RFD. On the enzyme affinity task (Table 4), FIDIA achieves the best Vina score (−7.21 vs. −7.06 for GRPO and −6.53 for LigandMPNN) but sacrifices some structural success rate (83.33% vs. 90.74% for LigandMPNN), reflecting the trade-off between binding affinity and fold preservation. On the vaccine epitope scaffolding task (Table 3), FIDIA wins on every metric including SR (27.09%), mRMSD* (2.65 Å), and solved targets (7/11), confirming consistent gains in a realistic therapeutic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based fine-tuning for inverse folding models (GRPO from Wang et al. 2025, multi-round DPO from Xu et al. 2025) by identifying and resolving a fundamental training-inference misalignment: standard RL objectives maximize expected return, while practical protein design pipelines rely on Best-of-N (BoN) selection of the top candidate. The theoretical core—a closed-form BoN policy gradient derived via the induced distribution's score function and a U-statistic-based variance-reduced estimator—borrows structural ideas from the LLM inference-aware fine-tuning literature (Chow et al. 2024, Verdun et al. 2025) but instantiates them in the discrete, autoregressive protein sequence setting with a novel rank-based advantage that encodes each sample's combinatorial role as both potential winner and contrastive baseline. The base architectures (ProteinMPNN, LigandMPNN) and evaluation oracles (ESMFold, AutoDock Vina) are unchanged from prior work; the contribution is entirely in the optimization objective and its gradient estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: protein engineers already use BoN at inference (generate many candidates, keep the best), yet all prior training objectives optimize the wrong quantity, leaving the high-fitness tail underexploited. The demonstrated 6× computational efficiency gain (FIDIA at N=8 matching ProteinMPNN at N=64) is directly relevant to expensive evaluation settings like Vina docking, and the theoretical framework generalizes to any BoN-based pipeline (small molecule design, code generation). Key open questions include the computational overhead of iterative batch sampling during training, the sensitivity to surrogate oracle fidelity (ESMFold/Vina are imperfect proxies for wet-lab validation), and whether the approach transfers to end-to-end joint structure-sequence generation without a separate redesign step.
