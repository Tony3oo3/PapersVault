## Problem

Existing single-pass uncertainty quantification methods for classification, particularly evidential deep learning (EDL) variants, focus on enhancing the expressiveness of the output distribution family but leave the structural mechanism by which predictive uncertainty is formed and aggregated largely implicit. This results in uncertainty representations with weakly interpretable semantics, making it difficult to diagnose whether uncertainty stems from insufficient evidence, conflicting interpretations, or both.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265563/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MoDEX takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 for DMNIST) and produces, in a single forward pass, a class label, a full second-order predictive distribution over the class-probability simplex parameterized as a structured mixture of K Dirichlet components (equivalently, an Extended Flexible Dirichlet distribution), aleatoric uncertainty as the entropy of the induced first-order label distribution, and epistemic uncertainty as the trace of the second-order covariance. The structured parameters—shared evidence α(x), class-specific advocacy strengths τ(x), and plausibility weights ω(x)—are all explicitly predicted and carry courtroom-interpretable semantics.

## Method

MoDEX (Mixture of Dirichlet EXperts) instantiates a courtroom analogy in which K class-specific advocates each form a Dirichlet opinion whose concentration vector decomposes into a shared base evidence α(x) common to all advocates and a class-specific advocacy term τ_k(x)·e_k that modulates emphasis toward class k. These opinions are aggregated via input-dependent plausibility weights ω(x) to yield a structured mixture of Dirichlets, equivalent to an Extended Flexible Dirichlet (EFD) distribution. The neural architecture comprises a spectrally-normalized feature extractor and three lightweight prediction heads (concentration, gating, advocacy-strength) with exponential and softmax activations. Training uses a composite objective: MSE on the predictive mean, Brier-score regularization on ω, and KL-divergence regularization on τ with label smoothing. Theoretically, MoDEX is proven to generalize both standard EDL and F-EDL, admits two equivalent interpretations (a weighted ensemble of K EDL experts, and an input-adaptive mixture of a base EDL predictor and a softmax predictor), and decomposes epistemic uncertainty into interpretable inter-expert disagreement and intra-expert imprecision components.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, MoDEX achieves 92.46% test accuracy, 99.18% misclassification AUPR, and 91.58/89.28 AUPR for OOD detection (SVHN/CIFAR-100), outperforming all EDL-based baselines. On CIFAR-100, MoDEX reaches 75.91% test accuracy and 96.17% misclassification AUPR, a substantial improvement over the next-best F-EDL (69.40% / 94.01%). For distribution shift detection on CIFAR-10-C, MoDEX achieves 58.72/70.24/80.63 AUPR at corruption levels 1/3/5, surpassing F-EDL (58.16/68.44/78.52) and MSP (56.39/65.86/75.01). Under severe class imbalance (CIFAR-10-LT, ρ=0.01), MoDEX attains 71.53% accuracy and 72.05/76.52 OOD AUPR, exceeding F-EDL by 7.8% in accuracy and ~10 points in OOD AUPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDEX outperforms all seven EDL-family baselines (EDL, I-EDL, R-EDL, DAEDL, Re-EDL, F-EDL) and MC Dropout across every task and dataset. On CIFAR-10, MoDEX improves test accuracy by 1.27 points over the strongest baseline F-EDL (92.46% vs. 91.19%) and by 3.98 points over DAEDL (91.11%). On CIFAR-100, the gap widens dramatically: MoDEX gains 6.51 points in accuracy over F-EDL (75.91% vs. 69.40%) and 30 points over standard EDL (45.91%). For OOD detection, MoDEX leads F-EDL by 0.38/0.91 AUPR on CIFAR-10 and by 2.55/1.18 AUPR on CIFAR-100. Under severe class imbalance (CIFAR-10-LT, ρ=0.01), MoDEX's advantage is most pronounced, beating F-EDL by 7.8 points in accuracy (71.53% vs. 63.73%) and by 9.49/6.34 AUPR in OOD detection, while also surpassing MSP by 2.36 points in distribution shift detection at corruption level 5 (80.63 vs. 78.52 on CIFAR-10-C).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evidential deep learning (EDL) lineage, which models predictive uncertainty via a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities, by introducing a structured mixture-of-Dirichlet-experts formulation grounded in a courtroom analogy. While prior EDL variants (I-EDL, R-EDL, Re-EDL, DAEDL, F-EDL) focus on enhancing the expressiveness of the output distribution family, MoDEX instead elevates structural inductive bias as the primary mechanism: each class-specific expert's Dirichlet concentration is decomposed into shared evidence and class-specific advocacy, and experts are aggregated via input-dependent plausibility weights. The formulation is shown to be equivalent to the Extended Flexible Dirichlet (EFD) distribution and to reduce to both F-EDL and standard EDL under specific parameter settings, positioning MoDEX as a principled generalization rather than an unrelated alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The courtroom analogy provides a semantically interpretable decomposition of uncertainty that prior EDL methods lack, enabling practitioners to diagnose whether uncertainty stems from insufficient evidence (intra-expert) or conflicting class hypotheses (inter-expert). This diagnostic capability has practical value for selecting appropriate interventions in safety-critical deployments. Key limitations include restriction to classification, absence of explicit supervision on epistemic uncertainty (making it model-induced rather than ground-truth-recovered), and dependence on the Dirichlet mixture assumption. Open directions include extension to regression, integration with rebalancing strategies for long-tailed settings, and combining with Bayesian or ensemble-based guidance to strengthen epistemic signals.
