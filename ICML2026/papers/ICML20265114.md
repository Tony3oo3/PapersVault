## Problem

Existing speculative decoding methods enforce a binary accept-or-reject rule: each drafted token is either accepted from the small model or fully recomputed by the large verifier. The paper identifies a substantial 'middle zone' of tokens that are close but not fully reliable, requiring moderate verification resources that the full model over-provisions. No prior method handles this zone without loading an additional intermediate model or modifying training procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265114/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a small drafter model (e.g., Gemma2-2B, LLaMA2-7B), and a large verifier model (e.g., Gemma2-27B, LLaMA2-70B) as inputs, and produces a decoded text sequence. The slim-verifier is not a separate model but a routed submodel of the large verifier obtained by skipping a fixed set of Transformer layers (45% skip ratio by default), so no additional model weights need to be loaded. The routing mask is determined once offline via DIMR (18–68 minutes per model pair) and remains fixed during all subsequent inference, making the online pipeline memory-neutral relative to the two-tier baseline.

## Method

VIA-SD reformulates speculative decoding as a three-tier hierarchical verification process grounded in KL-divergence information geometry: the generalized Pythagorean theorem shows that inserting an intermediate distribution u between drafter p and verifier q can strictly reduce the cumulative verification cost. The intermediate verifier is instantiated as a slim-verifier q′ obtained by intra-model routing (layer skipping) from the full verifier q, selected via Dynamic Intra-Model Routing (DIMR) that alternates random search and periodic Bayesian optimization over the combinatorial mask space to minimize a KL-style log-margin cost. At inference, drafted tokens pass through two confidence gates: a stricter early gate (p→q′, threshold α₁=0.5) that accepts high-confidence tokens or rewrites them with q′, and a looser later gate (q′→q, threshold α₂=0.3) that escalates only genuinely uncertain tokens to the full verifier. Because q′ shares embeddings and the output head with q, the routed path p→q′→q maintains distributional consistency, yielding lower rejection rates (0.10–0.22) and 10–20% speedups over strong cascade baselines without any retraining.

## Results

Across six model pairs (Gemma2 2B→9B/27B, LLaMA2 7B→13B/70B, Qwen 7B→14B/72B) and three QA benchmarks, VIA-SD reduces rejection rates by 0.10–0.22 relative to standard speculative decoding (e.g., from 0.27 to 0.14 on WebQuestions with Gemma2 2B→27B) and delivers 10–20% speedup over the strongest cascade baselines (Faster Cascades), reaching up to 2.70× speedup on NaturalQA. On encoder-decoder tasks (XSum, CNN/DailyMail, WMT14), VIA-SD achieves 2.50–3.35× speedup on WMT14 En-De with T5-S→XL while maintaining or slightly improving ROUGE/BLEU scores. The one-time offline DIMR search cost is modest (18–68 min, 0.30–1.13 GPU-hours) and is amortized across all downstream tasks for a given model pair.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIA-SD consistently outperforms all three main baselines (Speculative Decoding, Cascade SD, Faster Cascades) on rejection rate and speedup across every model pair and task, while matching or slightly exceeding their accuracy. Against the strongest baseline (Faster Cascades), VIA-SD reduces rejection rates by an additional 0.03–0.10 (e.g., 0.10 vs. 0.13 on WebQuestions/Gemma2 2B→9B; 0.15 vs. 0.22 on WebQuestions/Qwen 7B→72B) and gains 10–20% relative speedup (e.g., 2.32× vs. 1.81× on WebQuestions/Gemma2 2B→27B). The advantage scales with the drafter–verifier capacity gap: on large-gap pairs (2B→27B, 7B→70B, 7B→72B), rejection rates drop from ~0.22–0.30 to 0.14–0.16, enabling speedups above 2.3×–2.7×. VIA-SD is also shown to be plug-and-play compatible with EAGLE-1/2/3 and PEARL, adding 8–26% further speedup and 20–31% rejection reduction on top of those frameworks without retraining. Ablations confirm that the intra-model routed slim-verifier (DIMR) outperforms both an independent 13B intermediate model (which requires 1.38× peak memory) and random skip-layer selection (which degrades accuracy), achieving 2.32× speedup at only 1.04× peak memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding (SD) paradigm by replacing the binary accept/reject decision with a multi-tier hierarchical verification scheme. It builds on lossy SD (Tran-Thien, 2023), cascade-based approaches (Faster Cascades, Narasimhan et al. 2025), and learned verification methods (EAGLE, PEARL), but departs from them by constructing the intermediate verifier not as an independent model or a learned policy, but as a dynamically routed submodel (via layer-skipping) of the full verifier itself. The theoretical motivation draws on information geometry—specifically the generalized Pythagorean theorem for KL divergence—to argue that intermediate distributions can reduce effective verification cost, a framing absent from prior SD work that typically relies on TV distance or empirical heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIA-SD offers a memory-efficient path to faster LLM inference: the slim-verifier requires no additional model loading (only 1.04× peak memory vs. 1.38× for an independent intermediate model), and it plugs into existing SD frameworks (EAGLE, PEARL) without retraining. The finding that three tiers is optimal while four or five degrade performance (Table 1) suggests a practical ceiling on hierarchical depth, and the non-monotonic benefit of additional tiers is an open question the paper does not fully resolve theoretically. Limitations include hardware-dependent absolute speedups (A100 only), a one-time offline DIMR search cost (up to ~1.1 GPU-hours), and the fact that the KL-based theoretical guarantee is an inequality rather than a tight bound, leaving the exact conditions under which the routed path dominates the direct path only partially characterized.
