## Problem

Existing MLLM-based 3D generation methods are limited to producing either low-resolution meshes constrained by token budgets or coarse structural proxies from low-resolution voxel/lego representations, none of which can natively generate high-fidelity 3D geometry end-to-end within the LLM framework. The core difficulty is that 3D geometry forms long, highly interdependent sequences that are severely inefficient under purely token-level autoregressive modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261743/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts rendered RGB images, free-form text prompts, and point clouds sampled from 3D object surfaces as input, and produces watertight 3D meshes with PBR materials (decoded via Hunyuan3D-2.1 VAE and its material generator) or detailed text captions as output. The 3D latent space operates at resolutions from 512 to 4096 tokens (downsampling factor 20, latent dimension 64), with inference using 50 Rectified Flow sampling steps at CFG scale 7.5. Training follows a progressive resolution schedule, ramping from 512 to 4096 tokens while reducing conditional-input dropout from 90% to 10%.

## Method

CG-MLLM employs a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture built on Qwen3-VL, with two specialized transformers bound to generation modes rather than tasks: a TokenAR transformer for sequential next-token prediction (text, image tokens) and a BlockAR transformer for parallel block-level 3D synthesis via Rectified Flow. A hybrid causal-parallel masking scheme lets each token attend to preceding tokens (causal) or all tokens within its block (parallel), enabling long-context interaction between linguistic intent and spatial construction in a single decoder-only stack. The 3D VAE from Hunyuan3D-2.1 is frozen and connected via a lightweight Connector, while a 3D-aware positional encoding strategy omits intra-block embeddings to preserve permutation invariance of point features. Training proceeds in two stages—unconditional alignment at 512 tokens followed by progressive resolution scaling to 4096 tokens—on 16 H20 GPUs, with the block-level approach yielding a 3× speedup over token-level processing at 4096-token resolution.

## Results

On the Trellis-500K toys4k test set, CG-MLLM achieves a p-FID of 12.55, p-KID of 0.27, MUSIQ of 71.65, CLIP score of 84.47, and a user-study preference score of 3.32/5, establishing it as the best-performing MLLM-based 3D generation method across all six evaluated metrics. For 3D captioning from image inputs, CG-MLLM (2B MoT) reaches BLEU-1 of 13.51, ROUGE-L of 19.13, and METEOR of 14.28, substantially outperforming the Qwen3-VL-2B backbone (BLEU-1 3.13) and other general-purpose VLMs. The block-level autoregressive design yields a 3× speedup over token-level processing at 4096-token resolution, with inference time of only 35 s and 11 GB memory on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLM-based methods, CG-MLLM decisively outperforms both SAR3D and ShapeLLM-Omni on every metric: p-FID drops from 30.07 (SAR3D) and 13.11 (ShapeLLM-Omni) to 12.55, MUSIQ rises from 66.01/55.71 to 71.65, and user preference improves from 2.93/2.30 to 3.32. Against non-MLLM baselines, CG-MLLM is competitive but not uniformly superior: TRELLIS achieves a substantially lower p-FID (7.36 vs. 12.55) and p-KID (0.12 vs. 0.27), while SAM3D edges out CG-MLLM on user preference (3.45 vs. 3.32); however, CG-MLLM surpasses Michelangelo, CraftsMan, and Hunyuan3D-2.1 on p-FID, p-KID, and MUSIQ simultaneously. For 3D captioning from rendered images, CG-MLLM (2B) dramatically outperforms general-purpose VLMs—Qwen3-VL-2B (BLEU-1 3.13), LLaVA-13B (4.02), and InstructBLIP-13B (4.65)—but still trails dedicated 3D captioning models such as ShapeLLM-Omni-7B (BLEU-1 18.51), PointLLM-13B (17.09), and 3D-LLM (16.91), which the authors attribute to their larger 3D captioning datasets and model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

CG-MLLM builds on the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture but departs from prior MoT methods (Liang et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025) by binding transformers to generation modes (serial TokenAR vs. parallel BlockAR) rather than to tasks (understanding vs. generation), enabling flexible encoder integration. It leverages the vision-centric Qwen3-VL backbone (with DeepStack, Interleaved MRoPE, GQA) and the Hunyuan3D-2.1 Spatial-VAE for 3D latent encoding/decoding, positioning itself between autoregressive 3D methods (SAR3D, ShapeLLM-Omni) that produce only coarse geometry and non-MLLM diffusion-based generators (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D-2.1) that lack native language modeling. The Rectified Flow formulation for the BlockAR branch follows the standard velocity-field modeling paradigm, while the hybrid causal-parallel masking is a novel combination not seen in prior AR-diffusion hybrids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MLLM to perform end-to-end high-resolution 3D generation natively without external diffusion modules, demonstrating that the LLM paradigm can absorb 3D content creation and potentially benefit from LLM scaling trends. The observation that 3D generation training transfers back to improve 3D captioning (outperforming Qwen3-VL on image-based 3D understanding) suggests a productive coupling between generation and perception. However, the method still trails non-MLLM SOTA (TRELLIS achieves p-FID 7.36 vs. 12.55), and the gap is attributed to limited 3D token budget (<4k vs. ~40k in leading methods) and VAE reconstruction fidelity. Open questions include whether more efficient 3D VAEs, larger token budgets, and multi-encoder conditioning can close this gap, and whether the mode-based MoT binding generalizes to other modalities (video, audio).
