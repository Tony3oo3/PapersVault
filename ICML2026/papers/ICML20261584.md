## Problem

Modality expansion into a unified embedding space has relied on large-scale paired datasets (e.g., text-image, text-audio), which are prohibitively expensive or infeasible in specialized domains like medical imaging and molecular analysis. While prior work identified a consistent modality gap in contrastive encoders that enables cross-modal transfer via geometric operations, whether this property can eliminate the need for paired supervision entirely—bridging independently pretrained encoders using only text descriptions—remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired text descriptions (100K per modality, sourced from domain-specific corpora like AudioCaps, Objaverse, PubChem, CheXpert, and COCO) plus 5K unpaired samples per modality for centroid estimation, with no instance-level pairing or target modality data required during training. At inference, any modality input (image, video clip, audio clip, 3D point cloud, X-ray, or molecular structure) is encoded by its pretrained encoder, centered by the precomputed offset, and projected into a 2560-dimensional Qwen3-Embedding anchor space, yielding a unified embedding vector suitable for cross-modal retrieval ranking or zero-shot classification.

## Method

TextME exploits the interchangeable space property of contrastive encoders: subtracting modality-specific centroids from text and modal embeddings makes them functionally equivalent for semantically matched pairs, so a projection trained on centered text generalizes to centered modal inputs. The framework operates in two phases—(1) precomputing modality-specific offsets from 5K unpaired samples, and (2) training a lightweight 2-layer MLP (≈10M parameters) per modality to map centered text embeddings into LLM anchor space via an InfoNCE loss with hard negative mining. At inference, modal embeddings are centered with the precomputed offset before passing through the text-trained projector, enabling zero-shot cross-modal transfer without any paired supervision. LLM embedding space (Qwen3-Embedding) is adopted as the unified anchor over multimodal encoders based on superior semantic textual similarity (Spearman ρ 85–90 vs. 67–68), and the paper further shows that gap-content orthogonality variance (r = −0.67, p < 0.001) predicts which encoders benefit most from this text-only expansion.

## Results

TextME achieves an average Performance Preservation Ratio (PPR) of 74.5% relative to pretrained encoders across all tasks, with zero-shot classification at 89.2% and retrieval at 65.3%. The best preservation is 104.6% on 3D ModelNet40 classification (surpassing the pretrained Uni3D encoder), while the worst is 43.9% on Molecule DrugBank retrieval. Notably, TextME enables emergent cross-modal retrieval between modality pairs never seen during training, achieving 10.27 R@1 on 3D→Image (Objaverse) and 1.06 R@1 on Audio→Image (FlickrNet), using only 100K unpaired text descriptions per modality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LanguageBind (10M paired samples), TextME trails on most retrieval benchmarks (e.g., COCO R@1: 28.63 vs. 44.53; MSVD R@1: 45.82 vs. 65.22) but uses over 100× less data and requires no paired supervision. Against Ex-MCR (1M paired samples), TextME wins on 3D ModelNet40 classification (70.86 vs. 66.53) and 3D→Image emergent retrieval (10.27 vs. 5.67), while losing slightly on AudioCaps R@1 (15.35 vs. 19.07) and Audio→Image (1.06 vs. 1.57). Against the unpaired COX baseline (10K labeled target samples), TextME dominates by a wide margin on every benchmark (e.g., ModelNet40 Top-1: 70.86 vs. 4.05; ESC-50: 77.25 vs. 2.00; COCO R@1: 28.63 vs. 0.02), while also requiring no target-modality data or classification labels. The Naïve PCA baseline is effectively non-functional across all tasks, confirming that learned projection is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modality gap centering analysis of Zhang et al. (2024a), which demonstrated that subtracting encoder-specific centroids creates an interchangeable space within a single contrastive encoder, to the cross-encoder setting where independently pretrained encoders for six different modalities must be aligned. Unlike prior modality expansion methods (LanguageBind, Ex-MCR, C-MCR) that require instance-level paired supervision or overlapping modality bridges, TextME eliminates paired data entirely by training lightweight projection networks on text descriptions alone. The choice of LLM embedding space as the unified anchor connects to recent LLM-anchored multimodal work (UniBind, E5-V, LCO-Emb) but differs fundamentally in operating on architecturally heterogeneous, independently trained contrastive encoders rather than a shared MLLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95%+ reduction in data requirements (100K text descriptions vs. 1–10M paired samples) makes modality expansion practically feasible for specialized domains like medical imaging and molecular analysis where expert-annotated pairs are prohibitively expensive or ethically constrained. The emergent cross-modal retrieval between never-seen modality pairs (audio-to-3D, molecule-to-image) suggests text-anchored alignment can establish implicit semantic bridges without direct cross-modal supervision, opening a pathway to integrating arbitrary new modalities into existing multimodal systems. Key open questions include: developing adaptive strategies for encoders with unfavorable geometry (e.g., MoleculeSTM's weak gap consistency of 0.78), formal theoretical guarantees for the cross-encoder generalization of the modality gap property, and closing the retrieval-classification PPR gap (65.3% vs. 89.2%) that reflects sensitivity to fine-grained similarity distortion.
