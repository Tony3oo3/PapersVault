## Problem

Multimodal generative models must handle both discrete data (text, code) and continuous data (image, audio, video) in a unified way, but existing approaches force a trade-off: vector-quantized tokenizers create a lossy bottleneck, image-level diffusion models require bidirectional attention incompatible with causal LLMs and KV cache, and masked autoregressive models share the same bidirectional limitation. No single causal, autoregressive architecture previously supported high-fidelity continuous generation while remaining fully compatible with the efficient LLM inference stack.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of discrete tokens (text, code) and continuous data (images at 256×256 or 384×384, speech waveforms at 24 kHz). Continuous inputs are compressed by σ-VAE into latent vectors (16×16 patches for images; convolutional encoder with 1600×–6400× token reduction for speech), then packed alongside discrete token embeddings into a single sequence fed to the causal Transformer. Outputs are produced autoregressively: discrete tokens via softmax next-token prediction, continuous tokens via a lightweight diffusion head that denoises one latent vector per position, with special tokens (<BOD>/<EOD>) switching between the two decoding heads. The VAE decoder then reconstructs raw pixels or waveforms from the predicted latents.

## Method

LatentLM uses a single causal Transformer (LLaMA-style with RMSNorm and SwiGLU) where each position is decoded by either a softmax classifier (discrete) or a next-token diffusion head (continuous). The diffusion head is a small residual network with AdaLN-Zero conditioning on both the diffusion timestep and the Transformer hidden state, trained with a DDPM or flow-matching noise-prediction loss; at inference it starts from pure Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises using DPM-Solver in ~5–20 steps. The key enabling component is σ-VAE, which enforces a fixed (or Gaussian-sampled) variance σ across all latent channels, preventing the variance collapse that makes conventional VAEs behave like autoencoders and makes autoregressive inference brittle to exposure bias. Training minimizes a weighted sum of standard cross-entropy (for discrete tokens) and diffusion noise-prediction loss (for continuous tokens), sampling multiple diffusion timesteps per forward pass to amortize the lightweight head's cost. This design preserves full KV-cache compatibility, allows direct reuse of LLM distributed-training infrastructure, and unifies generation and understanding in a single forward pass without the noisy-input interference that plagues Transfusion-style approaches.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, LatentLM-L (479M params) achieves FID 2.24 and IS 253.8, outperforming the larger GIVT-Causal-L+A (1.67B params, FID 2.59) and matching DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID 2.27) with fewer parameters. In multimodal LLM evaluation, LatentLM achieves the best PPL (2.73), text-to-image FID (14.54), MS-COCO CIDEr (54.5), and VQAv2 accuracy (38.72) among all compared methods. For zero-shot TTS on LibriSpeech, LatentLM at frame rate 15 achieves SIM 0.697 and WER-C 1.2, surpassing VALL-E 2 (SIM 0.643, WER-C 1.5) while requiring 10× fewer autoregressive decoding steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among causal-masking image generation models, LatentLM-L (479M) outperforms GIVT-Causal-L+A (1.67B, FID 2.59) by 0.35 FID with 3.5× fewer parameters, and beats LlamaGen-XXL (1.4B, FID 2.34) and LlamaGen-XL (775M, FID 2.62) on both FID and IS. Against non-causal baselines, LatentLM matches DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.24 vs 2.27) and outperforms U-ViT-H/2 (FID 2.29) and LDM-4 (FID 3.60), though it trails MAR-L (FID 1.78) which uses bidirectional attention incompatible with KV-cache-based inference. In multimodal LLM evaluation, LatentLM dominates Transfusion across all metrics (PPL 2.73 vs 2.74, FID 14.54 vs 16.10, MS-COCO 54.5 vs 43.4, VQAv2 38.72 vs 35.36) and VQ-MLLM (PPL 2.73 vs 2.79, FID 14.54 vs 16.92, MS-COCO 54.5 vs 37.4, VQAv2 38.72 vs 30.19), with the gap widening as training tokens scale up. For TTS, LatentLM at frame rate 15 beats VALL-E 2 (SIM 0.697 vs 0.643, WER-C 1.2 vs 1.5, WER-H 1.8 vs 2.4), Voicebox (SIM 0.697 vs 0.662), and MELLE (SIM 0.697 vs 0.625) while using 5–10× fewer autoregressive steps. The σ-VAE tokenizer achieves better reconstruction (Mel Dist 0.813) than DAC (0.916), WavTokenizer (0.871), and Mimi (0.987) at a 1600× compression ratio, versus 160×, 320×, and 240× respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

LatentLM builds on the VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014) and DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), but departs from both in critical ways: it repurposes the VAE as a continuous tokenizer with enforced variance (σ-VAE) rather than a standard autoencoder-like VAE, and it applies diffusion not to the entire image but per-token as a lightweight head atop a causal Transformer. This positions the work against three existing paradigms for multimodal generation—discrete tokenization (VQ-VAE/LlamaGen), image-level diffusion (DiT/Transfusion), and masked autoregressive modeling (MAR)—by showing that a purely causal, autoregressive architecture with next-token diffusion can match or exceed all three while retaining KV-cache compatibility and unified understanding-generation training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The σ-VAE insight—that variance collapse in conventional VAEs is a previously underappreciated cause of autoregressive error accumulation for continuous tokens—provides a concrete, minimal fix that makes continuous autoregressive generation stable over long sequences. Practically, the method reuses existing LLM distributed training and inference infrastructure, lowering the barrier to deploying unified multimodal models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to long video generation, robot action spaces, and whether the fixed-variance constraint generalizes beyond the tested modalities; the paper also notes that the raster-order factorization used in image experiments is suboptimal and that alternative factorizations may further improve quality.
