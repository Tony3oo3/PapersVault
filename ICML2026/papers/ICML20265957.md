## Problem

Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models achieve state-of-the-art real-world image super-resolution but are too heavy for practical deployment. Existing post-training quantization methods are either tailored for U-Net-based SR or generic text-to-image DiTs, and directly applying them to DiT-based Real-ISR causes severe degradation of fine-grained local textures under aggressive bit-widths (e.g., W4A4). The paper identifies three specific gaps: matrix decomposition approaches lose high-frequency detail, no calibration-free mechanism exists for cross-layer weight bit allocation, and static activation precision ignores temporal sensitivity across diffusion timesteps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image degraded by complex real-world processes and produces a ×4 upscaled high-resolution image via the quantized DiT4SR backbone. All MM-DiT linear layers are quantized (weights and activations at 4–6 bits) while softmax layers remain at 8-bit for numerical stability. The quantized model requires only 32 low-resolution calibration crops (128×128) from RealSR for activation statistics, and VaSMP operates entirely data-free using offline weight variance statistics.

## Method

Q-DiT4SR builds on a Hadamard-transform + truncated SVD + quantized-residual backbone and introduces three key components. First, H-SVD decomposes each weight matrix into a global low-rank branch (SVD-G, rank r) capturing dominant low-frequency structure and a local block-wise rank-1 branch (SVD-L) capturing fine-grained residual information, with block sizes chosen so that SVD-L's parameter budget matches SVD-G's. Second, VaSMP allocates per-layer weight bit-widths in a data-free manner by solving a rate-distortion optimization where layer distortion is proportional to the mean output-channel variance times 2^(−2b), yielding a closed-form continuous solution followed by greedy integer discretization. Third, VaTMP schedules per-timestep activation bit-widths within each layer via dynamic programming over piecewise-constant segments, using timestep-wise activation variance as a sensitivity indicator to assign higher precision to high-variance (more sensitive) timesteps under a fixed average activation-bit budget.

## Results

Under W4A6, Q-DiT4SR achieves near-FP performance across all four real-world benchmarks: on RealSR it reaches MUSIQ 67.72 (FP: 67.89), ClipIQA 0.5671 (FP: 0.5482), and LIQE 3.980 (FP: 3.988); on RealLR200 it even slightly exceeds FP on MUSIQ (70.36 vs. 70.33) and LIQE (4.312 vs. 4.303). Under the more aggressive W4A4 setting, Q-DiT4SR still leads all baselines, e.g., achieving MUSIQ 66.36 on RealSR (next best: SVDQuant at 63.14) and LIQE 3.179 (next best: SVDQuant at 3.115). The W4A4 configuration reduces model size by 5.8× and FLOPs by 6.14×, with a measured ~4.5× end-to-end speedup and peak memory dropping from 15,086 MiB to 3,975 MiB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-DiT4SR outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric under both W4A6 and W4A4. The closest competitor is SVDQuant (ICLR 2025), which achieves competitive LPIPS (0.3829 vs. 0.3880 on DrealSR W4A6) but trails Q-DiT4SR by 2.8–3.4 points on MUSIQ and by 0.05–0.07 on ClipIQA across datasets; under W4A4 the gap widens (e.g., RealSR MUSIQ: 66.36 vs. 63.14). QuaRot (NeurIPS 2024) is the second-closest baseline, reaching within ~1.5 MUSIQ points on RealSR W4A6 (64.47 vs. 67.72) but degrading more sharply under W4A4. PTQ4DiT (NeurIPS 2024) and FlatQuant (ICML 2025) show moderate performance but lose by 5–10 MUSIQ points on most datasets. Q-DiT (CVPR 2025), PassionSR (CVPR 2025), Q-Diffusion (ICCV 2023), EfficientDM (ICLR 2024), and QueST (ICCV 2025) all suffer severe degradation, with QueST and Q-Diffusion dropping below 45 MUSIQ on RealSR W4A4, indicating that generic diffusion or LLM quantization strategies fail to preserve the high-frequency textures critical for Real-ISR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVDQuant quantization backbone (Hadamard transform + global truncated SVD + quantized residual) by introducing H-SVD, which adds a local block-wise rank-1 branch under a matched parameter budget to better preserve high-frequency details critical for super-resolution. It also builds on RobuQ's per-token Gaussian activation quantization framework, but departs from prior DiT quantization work (SVDQuant, Q-DiT, PTQ4DiT) that targets text-to-image generation, and from prior SR quantization work (PassionSR, QuantSR) that targets U-Net/CNN backbones. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of variance-aware spatio-temporal mixed precision: VaSMP allocates weight bits across layers in a fully data-free manner via rate-distortion theory, while VaTMP schedules activation bits across diffusion timesteps using dynamic programming—neither of which has a direct analogue in prior DiT or SR quantization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiT-based Real-ISR models (DiT4SR, DreamClear) achieve state-of-the-art restoration quality but are impractical for deployment due to extreme parameter counts and compute; this paper demonstrates that aggressive W4A4 quantization can reduce model size by 5.8× and operations by 6.14× while maintaining near-FP perceptual quality, making DiT-based SR viable on resource-constrained hardware. The data-free nature of VaSMP (no calibration data needed for weight precision) is a practical advantage over Hessian-based or MSE-based mixed-precision methods. Open questions include: integrating the precision schedules with native arbitrary-precision kernels (W3A3, W5A5) to close the gap between theoretical 6.14× and measured 4.5× speedup, developing quantization-aware IQA metrics that better reflect perceptual quality under aggressive quantization, and whether the W4A6-over-FP improvements observed on DreamClear indicate a genuine regularizer effect or a metric artifact.
