## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have achieved strong results on ground-based manipulation, but their transfer to Aerial Manipulation Systems (AMS) remains unexplored due to the absence of dedicated benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation metrics. AMS introduces unique challenges—floating-base dynamics, tight UAV-manipulator coupling, 3D volumetric workspaces, and long-horizon multi-step tasks—that existing ground-based benchmarks (LIBERO, OVMM, Mobile ALOHA) cannot capture.

## Input / Output

VLA models consume synchronized multi-view RGB-D images (front-down chassis, wrist, and third-person cameras, each resized to 224×224) alongside full proprioceptive state (UAV 4D pose with velocity/angular velocity, 7 joint positions, end-effector 6-DoF pose, gripper state) and a natural language instruction, producing a hybrid action vector of 4-DoF relative UAV displacement, 7-DoF joint position targets, and gripper state at 20 Hz. For π-series models, only the first 5 actions of each predicted chunk are executed per inference step. VLMs instead receive four initial scene images plus a structured system prompt (skill library, format spec, few-shot examples) and output a JSON-formatted high-level plan specifying sub-task sequences with agent assignment (UAV vs. ARM), target objects, and atomic skills.

## Method

AIR-VLA is a full-stack benchmark rather than a new architecture: it constructs a physics-based simulation on NVIDIA Isaac Sim (PhysX 5, ray-tracing) with a quadrotor carrying a 7-DoF Franka Panda, then collects 3,000 human-teleoperated episodes across six scene types using gamepad-controlled teleoperation to capture diverse coordination strategies. The task taxonomy spans four suites (Base Manipulation, Object & Spatial, Semantic Understanding, Long-Horizon) with an average episode length of ~475 steps, and LLM-generated instruction augmentation introduces synonym substitution, implicit intents, and negation constraints. Evaluation uses a weighted four-metric scheme for VLA (positioning accuracy via Gaussian decay, manipulator efficacy combining orientation and interaction precision, environmental safety via object-displacement penalty, and task progression) and a parallel four-metric scheme for VLM planning (process logic, spatial navigation direction scoring, object grounding with hallucination penalty, and skill coverage). The paper fine-tunes five pre-trained VLA models (π0, π0.5, π0-FAST, ACT, Diffusion Policy) with only 30–50 trajectories per task and evaluates six open-source VLMs in a non-interactive planning protocol, revealing that spatial navigation understanding is the dominant bottleneck across all models.

## Results

On the AIR-VLA benchmark, π0.5 achieves the highest overall VLA score of 42.0 (Pos: 71.3, Arm: 45.9, Safe: 84.7, Task: 10.6), substantially outperforming π0 (34.5), π0-FAST (18.8), and both ACT and Diffusion Policy (13.9 each). For VLM high-level planning, Qwen3-VL-8B leads with an overall score of 82.4 and 23.3% planning success rate, followed by Qwen2.5-VL (73.7, 13.3%), GLM-4V (56.8, 6.7%), InternVL3.5 (45.2, 0%), Molmo-7B (38.5, 0%), and LLaVA-OV (37.5, 0%). Under UAV disturbance, π0.5's total score drops only 1.0 points (42.0→41.0), but removing the fixed third-person view causes a 7.5-point drop (42.0→34.5), revealing strong dependency on global perspective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among VLA models, π0.5 dominates all baselines: it outperforms π0 by 7.5 points overall (42.0 vs. 34.5) and achieves 10.6% task progression versus π0's 3.5%, while π0-FAST (18.8) suffers a 15.7-point drop from π0 due to distillation-induced degradation in aerial settings. ACT and Diffusion Policy are effectively non-functional on aerial tasks, scoring 0.0 on both positioning and task progression across all four suites (13.9 overall), indicating that traditional imitation learning cannot handle the 12-DoF coupled dynamics. Among VLMs for planning, Qwen3-VL (82.4, 23.3% success) and Qwen2.5-VL (73.7, 13.3%) form a clear first tier, outperforming GLM-4V (56.8, 6.7%) by ~17–26 points; InternVL3.5 (45.2), Molmo-7B (38.5), and LLaVA-OV (37.5) all achieve 0% planning success, with InternVL3.5's weakness concentrated in spatial navigation (Loc: 3.2) despite strong skill selection (89.4). A consistent finding across all VLMs is the severe spatial navigation bottleneck (Loc scores range from 3.2 to 43.5), which is the primary limiter of end-to-end planning success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by extending the VLA paradigm—previously confined to ground-based and tabletop manipulation (RT-1, OpenVLA, π0, π0.5)—to the aerial manipulation domain, where no standardized benchmark existed. It repurposes existing VLA architectures (flow matching, diffusion policy, action chunking) and VLMs (Qwen3-VL, GLM-4V, InternVL3.5) for a 12-DoF quadrotor-plus-manipulator system, while prior aerial VLA work (UAV-VLA, DroneVLA, RaceVLA) was limited to navigation or single-DoF gripping. The simulation environment builds on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, and the dataset construction follows the human-teleoperation paradigm established by Mobile ALOHA and LIBERO, but applied to a fundamentally different physical regime (floating base, 3D workspace, long-horizon coupling).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIR-VLA fills a critical evaluation gap: no prior benchmark could assess omnidirectional 3D mobility, floating-base manipulation robustness, and long-horizon temporal reasoning simultaneously. The findings reveal that VLA models transfer to aerial platforms but face a sharp asymmetry—UAV navigation is handled well while fine-grained end-effector manipulation remains a bottleneck—and that spatial grounding failure (correct category, wrong instance) is a pervasive weakness. Key open questions include integrating payload-aware multi-body dynamics into VLA training, reducing dependence on fixed third-person views, and scaling spatial reasoning to unconstrained 3D environments. The benchmark will likely become a standard reference for aerial embodied AI research.
