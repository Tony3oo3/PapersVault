## Problem

Heat diffusion via the Laplacian is the principled smoothing operator on structured domains like meshes, but unstructured representations (point clouds, voxel grids, Gaussian splats) lack a natural Laplacian. Practitioners fall back on raw convolution or message-passing kernels that are biased at boundaries, fail to preserve mass, and are sensitive to sampling density. The paper asks whether arbitrary smoothing operators on such unstructured data can be corrected into well-behaved diffusion operators without access to an underlying Laplacian.

## Input / Output

The method takes a symmetric smoothing matrix S (e.g., a Gaussian kernel weighted by a mass matrix M for point clouds, a separable convolution for voxel grids, or a regularized adjacency matrix for graphs) and produces a normalized diffusion operator Q = ΛSMΛ. S is consumed purely as a black-box matrix-vector product—no factorization, eigendecomposition, or explicit connectivity is required. The output Q is a dense or implicitly represented N×N matrix whose rows sum to one, whose spectrum lies in [0,1], and which is symmetric under the mass-weighted inner product ⟨f,g⟩_M = fᵀMg. In practice Q is applied to signals via a softmax-weighted sum (Eq. 37), making it directly compatible with attention-style GPU kernels.

## Method

The core mechanism is a symmetric variant of the Sinkhorn scaling algorithm (Knight et al., 2014) that finds a unique positive diagonal matrix Λ such that Q = ΛSMΛ simultaneously satisfies M-symmetry, exact mass preservation (Q1 = 1), entrywise positivity, and spectral damping (σ(Q) ⊆ [0,1]). The iteration Λ_ii ← √(Λ_ii / d_i) with d_i = Σ_j S_ij Λ_jj converges in 5–10 iterations and is equivalent to standard symmetric Sinkhorn on the matrix MKM. The theoretical proof (Theorem 4.4) recasts the fixed-point equation as a symmetric entropy-regularized optimal transport problem, establishing existence and uniqueness of Λ, while Theorem 4.5 proves stability under increasing sampling density at fixed kernel bandwidth σ. A key practical insight is that Qf can be written as an attention operation over (d+2)-dimensional embeddings of the input points, enabling FlashAttention-compatible GPU implementation that scales to millions of points without ever materializing the kernel matrix.

## Results

On shape correspondence (mean geodesic error, lower is better), Q-DiffNet achieves 2.1 on FAUST, 2.4 on SCAPE, and 3.5 on SHREC19, outperforming point-cloud baselines DiffNet(PC) (3.0/2.5/7.5) and ULRSSM(PC) (2.3/2.4/5.1). Notably, Q-DiffNet surpasses mesh-based upper bounds on SHREC19 (3.5 vs. 4.5 for DiffNet and 4.6 for ULRSSM), where missing parts bias spectral diffusion. The fully mesh-free variant Q-DiffNet(QFM), which replaces the truncated Laplacian spectrum with estimated eigenvalues, still achieves 4.1 on SHREC19, beating both mesh baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Q-DiffNet against two state-of-the-art shape correspondence pipelines: DiffusionNet (Sharp et al., 2022) and ULRSSM (Cao et al., 2023), each evaluated in both mesh and point-cloud variants. Q-DiffNet clearly wins against both point-cloud baselines across all three benchmarks, with the largest margin on SHREC19 (3.5 vs. 7.5 for DiffNet(PC) and 5.1 for ULRSSM(PC)), where incomplete shapes bias spectral diffusion. Against the mesh-based upper bounds, Q-DiffNet is competitive on FAUST (2.1 vs. 1.6) and SCAPE (2.4 vs. 2.1–2.2) but actually outperforms them on SHREC19 (3.5 vs. 4.5–4.6). The Q-DiffNet(QFM) variant, which removes all mesh-dependent components from the pipeline, still beats both mesh baselines on SHREC19 (4.1 vs. 4.5/4.6), demonstrating that the Sinkhorn-normalized diffusion operator can replace the truncated Laplacian spectrum without significant quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the symmetric Sinkhorn matrix scaling algorithm (Knight et al., 2014) with the entropy-regularized optimal transport framework (Feydy et al., 2019) to produce a principled normalization of arbitrary smoothing kernels into diffusion operators. It departs from the manifold-learning literature (Wormell & Reich, 2021; Cheng & Landa, 2024), which studies the joint asymptotic limit σ→0, N→∞ to recover a continuous Laplace-Beltrami operator, by instead targeting stable, mass-preserving diffusion at a fixed practical bandwidth on unstructured data. In the geometry-processing lineage, it replaces the expensive Laplacian exponential (used in DiffusionNet, Sharp et al. 2022, and ULRSSM, Cao et al. 2023) with a cheap diagonal rescaling that requires no factorization, while simultaneously satisfying both M-symmetry and constant preservation—properties that row normalization and symmetric normalization each achieve only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is likely to become a practical default for diffusion-based smoothing on unstructured geometric data (point clouds, Gaussian splats, sparse voxels) because it is GPU-friendly, requires only 5–10 iterations, and needs no explicit connectivity or sparse factorization. The main open limitation is the dependence on the mass matrix M: with highly irregular sampling, errors in M propagate (albeit damped by a factor of 2) into the normalization, and the theoretical link to a 'true' continuous Laplacian is weaker than in mesh settings. Strict mass preservation, while physically natural, may limit expressivity in tasks where signal re-amplification at high-degree nodes is desirable. Future work on sparse/low-rank attention variants and connections to Maas (2011) entropy-gradient flows could broaden applicability to very large-scale and probabilistic settings.
