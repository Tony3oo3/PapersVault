## Problem

Modern Hopfield Networks perform associative memory retrieval in Euclidean or Hilbert spaces, where arbitrary tree structures cannot be embedded with low distortion, causing hierarchical relationships to be distorted during retrieval. No prior framework had moved the Hopfield energy landscape and iterative retrieval dynamics onto a negatively curved manifold to exploit the exponential volume growth that naturally matches hierarchical data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261476/cropped_page13_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes Euclidean feature vectors (e.g., ResNet-18 embeddings for images, SBERT encodings for synsets, or GNN outputs for molecules) as both queries and stored memories, optionally applying tangent-space norm clipping before the exponential map to prevent boundary instability. Retrieval produces on-manifold state vectors that are mapped back to the tangent space via the logarithmic map and fed into task-specific heads (multi-level classifiers, property predictors, or link-prediction decoders). The module is plug-and-play: it slots between any upstream encoder and downstream head without modifying either.

## Method

HAMNs encode queries and memories onto a constant-negative-curvature manifold (instantiated as the Poincaré ball) via exponential maps at a fixed reference point, then define a retrieval energy combining a log-sum-exp of hyperbolic similarities (−cosh of geodesic distance) with a squared-geodesic regularizer. Retrieval proceeds by a Riemannian CCCP step: the energy is decomposed into a geodesically convex term (squared distance to the base point) and a locally concave log-sum-exp term; the Riemannian gradient of the concave term is computed as a softmax-weighted sum of unit tangent directions scaled by sinh of geodesic distances, parallel-transported to the base point, and exponentiated back onto the manifold with a damping factor. Three interchangeable modules (HypHopfield, HypPooling, HypLayer) share this retrieval core but differ in how queries and memories are supplied. Theoretically, the paper proves that the number of well-separated recall wells grows exponentially with the effective hyperbolic radius (hierarchy depth), in contrast to the polynomial packing bound in Euclidean space, and validates a hierarchy-sensitivity hypothesis showing gains concentrate on deep hierarchies while flat/shallow tasks see parity with Euclidean MHNs.

## Results

On the deepest 4-layer CIFAR-100 hierarchy, HAMNs achieve the best top-level accuracy (90.98%) and the highest cross-level coherence (coph_corr = 0.7184), outperforming all Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines simultaneously. On WordNet hypernym prediction, HAMNs reach 47.25% accuracy with an average hierarchical distance of 2.66, nearly doubling the 24.98% of Euclidean MHNs; with an ontology-embedding front-end, HAMNs achieve 96.97% accuracy and 0.037 average hierarchical distance (best overall). On weak-hierarchy tasks (MIL, MoleculeNet), HAMNs perform on par with Euclidean MHNs, setting SOTA on Fox (MIL) and on BBBP/SIDER (molecular property prediction), consistent with the hierarchy-sensitivity hypothesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Euclidean MHNs (Ramsauer et al., 2021), HAMNs win decisively on deep hierarchies: on 4-layer CIFAR-100, HAMNs improve top accuracy by 1.59 pp (90.98 vs 89.39) and coph_corr by 0.128 (0.7184 vs 0.5902); on WordNet hypernym prediction the gap is dramatic (47.25% vs 24.98% accuracy, 2.66 vs 4.40 avg. hierarchical distance). Against the kernelized U-Hop (Wu et al., 2024), HAMNs lead on 4-layer CIFAR-100 (90.98 vs 88.62 top, 68.51 vs 62.46 coarse) and on WordNet (47.25% vs 20.03%), though U-Hop matches HAMNs on coph_corr at the 4-layer depth (0.7154 vs 0.7184). Against hyperbolic attention (Gülçehre et al., 2019), HAMNs achieve higher top-level accuracy (90.98 vs 90.13) and coph_corr (0.7184 vs 0.6795) on 4-layer CIFAR-100, but HypAttn edges ahead on fine-grained accuracy (54.50 vs 53.49) and on plain WordNet (48.09% vs 47.25%). Against hyperbolic NNs (Ganea et al., 2018), HAMNs dominate on cross-level coherence (0.7184 vs 0.6046) while HypNN retains a fine-grained advantage (55.93 vs 53.49). On weak-hierarchy MIL and MoleculeNet tasks, HAMNs are competitive with attention-MIL (Ilse et al., 2018) and mi-Net (Carbonneau et al., 2018), achieving SOTA on Fox and BBBP/SIDER with only modest margins, confirming the hypothesis that hyperbolic geometry helps primarily when hierarchy is deep.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: modern Hopfield networks (Ramsauer et al., 2021), which reformulate associative memory as energy minimization with softmax-weighted retrieval, and hyperbolic representation learning (Nickel & Kiela, 2017; Ganea et al., 2018; Gülçehre et al., 2019), which exploits negative curvature to encode hierarchical structure. Prior MHN extensions—kernelized (Wu et al., 2024), sparse (Hu et al., 2023), latent-structured (Li et al., 2026)—all remain in Euclidean or Hilbert geometry, while prior hyperbolic modules (hyperbolic attention, hyperbolic neural networks) perform one-shot geometric aggregation without defining an explicit global energy or iterative retrieval dynamics. HAMNs are the first to transplant the full Hopfield energy landscape and CCCP-based iterative retrieval onto a constant-negative-curvature manifold, using the Minkowski inner product for similarity and Riemannian optimization with parallel transport for on-manifold updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchy-sensitivity hypothesis—hyperbolic retrieval helps on deep hierarchies but is comparable to Euclidean on flat data—gives practitioners a clear decision rule for when to adopt the added geometric complexity. The sphere-packing capacity bound (exponential in hyperbolic radius vs. polynomial in Euclidean radius) provides a clean theoretical justification for the empirical gains. However, the practical adoption is currently limited by significant GPU overhead from hyperbolic operations (Möbius addition, exp/log maps, Riemannian gradient transforms), which increase wall-clock time and peak memory despite lower theoretical FLOPs. Open questions include scalability to larger models and modalities, extension to variable or non-constant curvature, and whether the CCCP retrieval dynamics can be replaced by cheaper one-shot approximations that retain the hierarchical advantage.
