## Problem

Existing decentralized stochastic bi-level optimization algorithms require the lower-level loss to be strongly convex, limiting their applicability to practical machine learning models where this condition typically fails. Prior single-agent methods that handle nonconvex lower-level losses (e.g., Kwon et al., 2024b) introduce a regularization term to artificially enforce strong convexity, adding computational overhead, and rely on time-dependent two-timescale learning rates that are difficult to tune and may leak training-stage information in decentralized settings. This paper asks whether a single-loop, Hessian-/Jacobian-free decentralized algorithm can converge under the pure PL condition without any artificial strong convexity.

## Input / Output

Each of K workers receives stochastic gradient samples of the upper-level loss f^(k)(x,y) and lower-level loss g^(k)(x,y), and communicates over a doubly stochastic symmetric graph W. The algorithm requires hyperparameters η_x, η_y, η_z (all constant and of the same order in ε), momentum coefficients γ_x, γ_y, γ_z, and a penalty parameter ρ = O(ε/κ³). The output is a stationary point of the original bi-level problem with ε-accuracy, measured as (1/T)Σ E[‖∇L(x̄_t)‖²] ≤ O(ε²), where L(x) = min_{y∈Y*(x)} f(x,y). Notably, all three variables (x, y, z) are updated with constant single-timescale learning rates, unlike the two-timescale decaying schedules in prior work.

## Method

The algorithm (FO-DSVRBGD) first reformulates the bi-level problem into a single-level minimax problem via a penalty framework: min_{x,y} max_z (1/K)Σ_k [f^(k)(x,y) + (1/ρ)(g^(k)(x,y) − g^(k)(x,z))]. It then applies a single-loop decentralized gradient descent-ascent procedure where each worker computes STORM variance-reduced gradient estimators for all six component gradients (∇₁f, ∇₁g at y, ∇₁g at z, ∇₂f, ∇₂g at y, ∇₂g at z), combines them into per-variable estimators, and performs gradient tracking communication (U_{t+1} = U_t W + M_{t+1} − M_t) to handle data heterogeneity. Crucially, no regularization term is added to the lower-level subproblems, and all variables use constant single-timescale learning rates. The convergence analysis introduces a novel potential function that measures lower-level optimality via PL-based metrics (h_ρ(x̄_t, ȳ_t) − ĥ_ρ(x̄_t)) and (g(x̄_t, z̄_t) − ĝ(x̄_t)) rather than distance-to-optimum, yielding an iteration complexity of O(κ⁹/(K(1−λ)²ε⁵)) with linear speedup in K.

## Results

The paper's main results are presented as convergence plots (upper-level loss vs. iterations or wall-clock time) rather than numerical tables. On the nonconvex lower-level hyperparameter optimization task (ε=0.1, 8 workers, ring graph), FO-DSVRBGD converges substantially faster than all F²BSA variants (F²BSA-1, F²BSA-5, F²BSA-10, F²BSA-VR) across a9a, w8a, covtype, and MNIST. In the strongly convex setting (Figure 3), the algorithm reaches convergence in significantly less wall-clock time than all second-order baselines (DSBO, gossip-DSBO, MA-DSBO, VRDBO, DSVRBGD-S) and also outperforms the first-order DSFDA-GT. The theoretical iteration complexity is O(κ⁹/(K(1−λ)²ε⁵)), with linear speedup in K and O(1/(1−λ)²) dependence on the spectral gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against F²BSA (Kwon et al., ICLR 2024), the closest single-agent nonconvex bilevel method, FO-DSVRBGD converges faster in iterations on all four datasets (a9a, w8a, covtype, MNIST) for the nonconvex lower-level problem, and also on the toy model in the appendix. The advantage is attributed to the single-timescale constant learning rate and the absence of the regularization term that F²BSA requires. In the strongly convex setting (Figure 3), FO-DSVRBGD converges in substantially less wall-clock time than all five second-order decentralized methods (DSBO, gossip-DSBO, MA-DSBO, VRDBO, DSVRBGD-S), confirming the efficiency gain of avoiding Hessian/Jacobian computations. It also outperforms DSFDA-GT (Wang et al., 2025), the only other fully first-order decentralized method, demonstrating the additional benefit of the STORM variance-reduction mechanism. The paper does not report any setting where a baseline outperforms the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the penalty-based reformulation of nonconvex bilevel optimization (Shen & Chen, 2023; Kwon et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2024) to the decentralized setting, while deliberately removing the artificial strong convexity regularization that prior single-agent methods (notably Kwon et al., 2024b) required to make the lower-level subproblem tractable. It combines the STORM variance-reduction estimator (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019) with gradient tracking for decentralized communication, and introduces a novel potential-function analysis framework that operates under the pure Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition rather than relying on strongly convex optimization techniques. The key departure from prior work is twofold: no auxiliary variables or regularization terms are introduced, and a single-timescale constant learning rate replaces the two-timescale time-dependent schedule used in Kwon et al. (2024b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decentralized first-order algorithm for nonconvex bilevel optimization with rigorous convergence guarantees, removing a practical barrier (Hessian/Jacobian computation) that limited prior decentralized bilevel methods to strongly convex lower-level problems. The result that artificial strong convexity is unnecessary under the PL condition challenges a working assumption in the field and opens the door to applying decentralized bilevel optimization to overparameterized neural networks where PL holds but strong convexity does not. Open questions include whether the κ⁹ condition-number dependence can be tightened, whether the approach extends to heterogeneous data distributions or non-symmetric communication graphs, and whether the constant single-timescale learning rate generalizes to more complex bilevel structures (e.g., multi-level or constrained settings).
