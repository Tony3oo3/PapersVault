## Problem

Kronecker adapters enable high-rank weight updates with minimal parameters, yet existing work treats the component structure (dimensions r1, r2 and number of components r) as a fixed or heuristic choice, leaving it underexplored. Despite their theoretical rank advantage, Kronecker adapters empirically underperform even LoRA, which is explicitly constrained to a low-rank subspace. The paper asks whether component design is the key factor governing Kronecker adapter performance and whether principled guidelines exist for selecting r1, r2, and r.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen pretrained linear layers in transformer architectures (LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-3, T5-Base, CLIP-ViT-B/16), taking text prompts or RGB images as input and producing generated text (for mathematical reasoning and code generation) or class labels (for image classification). The Kronecker product B⊗A is never explicitly materialized; instead, an equivalent reformulation (B⊗A)x = vec(A·X·Bᵀ) is used during forward and backward passes, keeping computational cost nearly identical to standard LoRA at the same parameter budget.

## Method

CDKA generalizes Kronecker adapters to a sum of r Kronecker components W = W₀ + Σᵢ B⁽ⁱ⁾⊗A⁽ⁱ⁾ and provides a theoretical analysis via Kronecker singular value decomposition showing that subspace alignment with the first-step gradient of full fine-tuning is fully governed by r1, r2, and r. Three design principles emerge: keep r1 small (2–4), make r2 as large as the budget allows, and set r just above the gradient rank r* (typically 2–8). A stabilization scaling factor λ = α/√(r·r₂) is introduced to make gradient norms invariant to component configuration, preventing training instability. Components A⁽ⁱ⁾ use Kaiming initialization while B⁽ⁱ⁾ is zero-initialized, and parameter-budget-aware guidelines specify how to allocate the fixed budget across r1, r2, and r for practical deployment.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (GSM8k), CDKA achieves state-of-the-art performance: 56.71% EM with LLaMA-2-7B (beating LoRA-One by 1.31 points) and 73.74% with LLaMA-3.1-8B (matching full fine-tuning at 73.69%). On image classification (7 datasets with CLIP-ViT-B/16), CDKA attains a 90.08% average accuracy, surpassing LoRA-One (89.57%) and KronA (84.94%). On NLU (GLUE with T5-Base), CDKA reaches 87.30% average using only 0.41M trainable parameters (12.5% of LoRA-One's 3.24M), approaching full fine-tuning (87.91%). On code generation (HumanEval), CDKA achieves 24.59% PASS@1, the second-best result behind GoRA (24.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDKA substantially outperforms KronA across all tasks: +7.71 points on GSM8k (56.71 vs. 49.00), +4.94 on HumanEval (24.59 vs. 17.21), and +5.14 average on image classification (90.08 vs. 84.94), demonstrating that principled component design is the key missing ingredient. Against the strongest LoRA variant, LoRA-One, CDKA wins on GSM8k (56.71 vs. 55.40) and image classification (90.08 vs. 89.57), while trailing slightly on GLUE (87.30 vs. 88.73) though using only 12.5% of the trainable parameters. On HumanEval, CDKA (24.59) is marginally behind GoRA (24.80) but far ahead of all other LoRA-based methods (next best: LoRA-One at 20.73). On the GLUE benchmark, CDKA with 0.41M parameters approaches GoRA (87.96) and LoRA-GA (87.77) which use 3.05–3.24M parameters, and clearly surpasses LoRA (82.08), DoRA (82.57), and AdaLoRA (81.62). Across backbone models (LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-3-0.6B/8B), CDKA consistently achieves SOTA or near-SOTA on mathematical reasoning, confirming robustness beyond a single architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kronecker adapter line initiated by KronA (Edalati et al., 2022), which replaced LoRA's standard matrix product with a single Kronecker product, and the subsequent MoKA/MoKA-MoE extensions that used mixtures of Kronecker components. The key departure is treating the component structure (dimensions r₁, r₂ and number of components r) as the central design variable rather than a fixed or heuristic choice, and grounding the design in a Kronecker singular value decomposition alignment analysis adapted from the LoRA-One subspace alignment framework (Zhang et al., 2025). Unlike prior Kronecker adapter work that aimed to approximate full-rank updates, this paper shows that the *configuration* of how rank is realized matters more than the total attainable rank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, theoretically grounded guidelines for Kronecker adapter component design under a fixed parameter budget, closing a gap where prior work relied on manual tuning. The finding that Kronecker adapters can match or exceed the strongest LoRA variants (LoRA-One, GoRA) on math reasoning and image classification—despite using far fewer parameters—suggests the Kronecker product formulation was previously underutilized due to poor component choices rather than fundamental limitations. The main limitation is that the theoretical analysis is restricted to linear settings, leaving nonlinear dynamics uncharacterized. Open questions include whether the component design principles transfer to non-adapter PEFT methods and whether the alignment theory extends to multi-layer or non-linear training regimes.
