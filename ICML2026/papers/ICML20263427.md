## Problem

One-class anomaly detection on tabular data faces a fundamental trade-off: compactness-focused methods (e.g., KNFST) amplify pointwise deviations but destroy cluster and cross-feature geometry, while structure-preserving methods (e.g., PCA) retain that geometry but miss low-variance local anomalies. Existing detectors capture only one side of this trade-off or balance them via hyperparameters, which is infeasible in the one-class setting where validation labels are unavailable. No baseline ranks at the top across all four anomaly types (local, global, cluster, dependency) simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only normal training samples as d-dimensional vectors (no anomaly labels required) and synthesizes a single deterministic proxy point via feature-wise maximum to anchor the discriminant geometry. At inference, each test sample is projected into the learned m-dimensional discriminant space and scored by its minimum Euclidean distance to the projected training base points, then normalized by the maximum leave-one-out nearest-neighbor gap within the training set to yield a threshold-ready score in [0,1]. An optional binary prediction is produced by applying a user-specified deployment threshold to this normalized score.

## Method

DVM-AD is a closed-form one-class discriminant learning method that resolves the compactness-structure trade-off by selecting projection directions from both tails of a provably bounded spectrum. It constructs an augmented two-class geometry using a deterministic proxy point, then solves the generalized eigenproblem on the inverse-scatter ratio κ(θ) = θᵀS_wθ / θᵀS_sθ via a Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse formulation that remains well-posed under rank deficiency. Theorem 4.1 proves all eigenvalues of M = Σ†(QᵀS_wQ) lie in [0,1], where the lower tail (λ≈0) yields compressive directions that collapse the normal class and the upper tail (λ≈1) yields structure-preserving directions aligned with principal components; selecting from both extremes simultaneously captures complementary anomaly-detection behaviors without any dataset-specific tuning. Scoring uses FAISS-accelerated nearest-neighbor distance in the discriminant space, and the entire pipeline requires one scatter-matrix accumulation pass and one O(d³) eigen-decomposition with no iterative training.

## Results

On 47 ADBench tabular datasets, DVM-AD achieves the best average AUROC of 89.65% with the lowest average rank of 2.98 among 28 baselines. On 10 NLP/CV embedding benchmarks (74 class-novelty tasks), DVM-AD attains an average rank of 1.60 and average AUROC of 72.68%. Across all four controlled anomaly types (local, global, cluster, dependency), DVM-AD achieves the best average rank, with the highest mean AUROC in three of four types (within 0.09% of LUNAR on global anomalies). At extreme scale (N=10⁶, d=10³), DVM-AD is the fastest method overall at 32.5 s training time, and at N=10⁵, d=10⁴ it remains operable (496 s) where 7 of 13 baselines run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVM-AD outperforms all 28 PyOD baselines (shallow ML and deep learning) on average across the 47 ADBench tabular datasets, achieving 89.65% mean AUROC versus the next-best methods, and extends this lead to 10 NLP/CV embedding sources (rank 1.60, AUROC 72.68%). Against the three most directly related discriminant baselines—KNFST (rank 12.68 on dependency anomalies), PMKFN, and PCA (rank 12.06 on local anomalies)—DVM-AD shows the largest margins precisely where those methods' single-sided representations fail. Even under an oracle best-of-grid tuning advantage for KNN, IForest, and LUNAR, DVM-AD retains the best mean AUROC (89.01%), with Friedman and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests confirming statistical significance over every baseline. On the two most competitive recent deep tabular detectors, MCM and DRL-AD, DVM-AD wins on anomaly-type coverage (all four types vs. primarily global/dependency), requires zero tuned hyperparameters (vs. multiple deep training hyperparameters), and extends to CV/NLP embeddings under the same recipe. At extreme scale, DVM-AD is the fastest method at N=10⁶/d=10³ (32.5 s) and the only method that completes at N=10⁵/d=10⁴ (496 s) where 7 of 13 baselines exhaust 64 GB RAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVM-AD extends the Fisher discriminant / Foley-Sammon Transform lineage (Fisher 1936; Foley & Sammon 1975) into the one-class anomaly detection setting by introducing a deterministic proxy point that makes the two-class scatter decomposition well-defined without anomaly labels. The key departure from prior one-class discriminant methods (KNFST, PMKFN, PCA) is the two-tailed selection from a provably bounded [0,1] inverse-scatter spectrum: existing methods select only compressive (null-space) or only structure-preserving directions, whereas DVM-AD retains vectors from both extremes simultaneously, unifying the compactness and structure-preservation behaviors that prior work treats as mutually exclusive. The Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse formulation replaces the tuned regularization common in high-dimensional discriminant analysis, and the nearest-neighbor scoring with training-geometry normalization provides a threshold-free [0,1] score without iterative training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tuning-free property is practically significant for one-class deployment where validation labels are scarce or unavailable, and the bounded-spectrum theorem gives a clean theoretical guarantee that the two-tailed selection is well-posed without dataset-specific hyperparameters. The method's dominance over 28 baselines (including recent deep detectors like MCM and DRL-AD) on 47 tabular datasets reinforces the finding that simple linear methods remain competitive on tabular AD, but the approach is inherently linear and limited to moderate dimensions (d < ~2000 in practice), leaving open whether kernel or nonlinear extensions can preserve the tuning-free guarantee. The proxy-point construction (feature-wise max) is a heuristic whose sensitivity is limitedly explored, and the method has not been stress-tested on truly high-dimensional or non-numeric tabular data beyond the embedding benchmarks.
