## Problem

Medical LLMs trained with extended Chain-of-Thought reasoning apply uniformly long outputs to all queries, wasting inference compute on simple factoid questions while potentially under-reasoning on complex diagnostic cases. Existing length-calibration methods rely solely on correctness signals or static offline difficulty estimates, ignoring the model's own predictive uncertainty, and suffer from reward hacking that causes accuracy collapse. The paper addresses how to make a single medical LLM adaptively allocate reasoning length based on per-question difficulty without external routers or manually annotated difficulty classifiers.

## Input / Output

The model accepts medical questions in multiple-choice or open-ended text format, as well as free-text clinical records (history, examination, imaging notes) for open-ended diagnosis and treatment planning. It produces a single text output whose length is dynamically determined by the model's internal uncertainty: straightforward queries yield direct answers of ~35–80 tokens (a 'non-thinking' mode), while complex or ambiguous cases trigger extended reasoning chains of ~100–200+ tokens (a 'thinking' mode). No external difficulty classifier or router is invoked at inference time; the mode selection is internalized in the policy weights.

## Method

AdaThink-Med introduces an uncertainty-guided difficulty estimator that fuses rollout correctness with Top-K token Shannon entropy (default K = 20% of sequence length) to compute a per-question difficulty score, normalized via intra-batch min-max scaling. This score modulates a piecewise length reward within GRPO: for easy+correct samples the reward penalizes verbosity relative to a running average, for hard+incorrect samples it rewards extended reasoning, and the remaining two quadrants (easy+incorrect, hard+correct) receive zero length signal to prevent premature compression of fragile reasoning or reward for hallucinated padding. A dynamic EMA-smoothed quantile threshold separates easy from hard questions, and a staged training protocol (300 steps of standard GRPO followed by 200 steps of adaptive RFT) prevents early-training instability. The asymmetric reward design naturally induces a bimodal output-length distribution—distinct 'non-thinking' and 'thinking' modes—within a single model, achieving 4.7×–6.4× token compression with minimal accuracy loss across six medical benchmarks.

## Results

AdaThink-Med achieves a 4.7× compression on the Qwen backbone (average output length reduced from 497 to 106 tokens) and a 6.4× compression on the Llama backbone (410 to 64 tokens), with only a −1.13% accuracy drop on Llama and a +0.25% gain on Qwen relative to the GRPO baseline. It attains the highest Accuracy-Efficiency Score (AES) of +0.93 (Qwen) and +0.92 (Llama) across six medical benchmarks, outperforming all length-calibration baselines by at least +0.14 AES. The model also exhibits clear adaptive behavior, generating 36-token answers on MedQA but 100–175 tokens on GPQA-M and MMLU-ProM, confirming difficulty-aware length allocation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four length-calibration baselines (Kimi1.5, ShortBetter, DAST, CosFn), AdaThink-Med wins on AES across both backbones: on Llama it scores +0.92 vs. Kimi1.5 (+0.78), DAST (+0.74), and ShortBetter (+0.70), while on Qwen it reaches +0.93 vs. Kimi1.5 (+0.55), DAST (+0.54), and ShortBetter (+0.41). CosFn catastrophically fails on both backbones (AES −2.78 Llama, −2.38 Qwen), producing ~974-token outputs with 38% accuracy. In absolute accuracy, AdaThink-Med is within 0.35% of ShortBetter on Llama (55.59% vs. 55.94%) and within 0.42% on Qwen (54.99% vs. 54.28%), while using 2.0–3.2× fewer tokens. Against medical LLMs (HuatuoGPT-o1, UltraMedical, m1), AdaThink-Med trades a modest 1–3% accuracy drop for a 5–24× reduction in output length (e.g., 64 vs. 527 tokens vs. HuatuoGPT-o1 on Llama; 106 vs. 2451 tokens vs. m1 on Qwen). The compute-matched control (GRPO extended to 500 steps) confirms that gains come from the reward design, not extra training: GRPO(500) yields 56.73%/408 tokens vs. AdaThink-Med's 55.59%/64 tokens on Llama.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Guo et al., 2025) as its RL backbone and on the top-K Shannon entropy formulation from Wang et al. (2025) for sequence-level uncertainty estimation, but departs sharply from prior length-calibration methods (Kimi1.5, ShortBetter, DAST, CosFn) that impose uniform or correctness-only length penalties. Where SynapseRoute (Zhang et al., 2025) and Selective CoT (Zhan et al., 2026) rely on externally trained difficulty classifiers or routers, AdaThink-Med internalizes difficulty estimation into the RL reward itself, fusing rollout correctness with token-level entropy to produce a single-model, end-to-end adaptive reasoning policy. The staged training design (vanilla GRPO first, then adaptive length calibration) also distinguishes it from one-shot length-penalized RL approaches that the authors show collapse into degenerate shortcuts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.7×–6.4× token compression with ≤1.1% accuracy loss is practically significant for clinical deployment where latency and cost constrain real-time use, and the natural emergence of bimodal 'thinking/non-thinking' modes from a single reward function eliminates the need for external routers that prior hybrid architectures (Qwen3, DeepSeekV3.1) require. Key open questions include whether the entropy-based difficulty estimator remains reliable under miscalibration or in multi-turn clinical workflows, whether the Llama backbone's fragility (requiring an n-gram repetition penalty) generalizes to other architectures, and whether the difficulty threshold transfers across medical specialties without re-tuning. The dataset-selection application (40% retention exceeding full-dataset performance) suggests broader utility beyond inference efficiency.
