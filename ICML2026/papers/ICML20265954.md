## Problem

Latent-based watermarks for diffusion models are highly susceptible to black-box forgery attacks, where an attacker with only black-box access and a single watermarked image can transfer the provider's watermark to arbitrary illicit content. No effective defense existed within the latent-based paradigm, since the watermark lives purely in the initial latent noise, making forged and natural perturbations indistinguishable at verification time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265954/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At embedding, SemBind takes a text prompt, a watermark message, and a watermark key, and additionally generates an auxiliary clean image from the same prompt to extract a 1024-bit semantic code; the code is expanded into a spatial sign mask over the 4×64×64 latent (or 1×16×64×64 for FLUX) and multiplicatively modulates the watermarked initial latent before denoising. At verification, the watermarked image (512×512 RGB) is encoded to latent, inverted to recover the initial noise, unbound by the recomputed semantic mask, and decoded to yield a detection decision and bit-accurate watermark message. A single mask-ratio parameter σ∈[0,1] controls the fraction of latent coordinates modulated, trading anti-forgery strength against robustness to benign distortions.

## Method

SemBind introduces a contrastively trained semantic masker f_θ (frozen DINOv2-Giant backbone + residual MLP hashing network) that maps images to 1024-bit binary codes, producing near-invariant codes for same-prompt images and near-orthogonal codes across prompts. Training follows a two-stage cluster-then-quantize procedure: stage 1 optimizes an encoder and projection head with supervised contrastive loss to form a compact hyperspherical feature space; stage 2 freezes those modules and trains a hash head with a code-space contrastive loss plus quantization, bit-balance, decorrelation, and bit-consistency regularizers. During embedding, the semantic code is tiled, ratio-truncated, and permuted under a secret key to form a ±1 spatial mask that multiplicatively modulates the watermarked initial latent produced by any standard latent-based scheme (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading, PRC, GS++). At verification, the mask is recomputed from the received image and used to unbind the latent before standard watermark decoding; if the image semantics have been altered by a forgery attack, the mask mismatch randomizes the recovered payload and causes verification to fail. The paper proves that for schemes admitting provable undetectability, the SemBind variant preserves the same undetectability guarantee, since multiplying a Gaussian latent by an independent ±1 mask leaves the distribution invariant.

## Results

Under the imprinting attack with a matched SD 2.1 attacker on SDP, SemBind reduces detection rate from ~100% to 1–7% for Tree-Ring and Gaussian Shading, and to 6–25% for PRC and Gaussian Shading++, while pushing bit accuracy from near-perfect to ~0.50 (random). Under reprompting, SemBind cuts detection by more than 40% across all four schemes, with COCO detection often dropping below 20%. Image quality is preserved: FID and CLIP t-values remain below 1.1 for all SemBind variants, confirming no statistically significant degradation. Robustness to common distortions (JPEG, blur, noise, resize) degrades by only 3–5% on average for PRC-S and GS++-S, while TR-S and GS-S remain near the original baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemBind is evaluated as a plug-in defense on four latent-based watermarking baselines: Tree-Ring (TR), Gaussian Shading (GS), PRC, and Gaussian Shading++ (GS++). Against the strongest matched attacker (SD 2.1), SemBind achieves the most dramatic gains on TR and GS: imprinting detection drops from 100% to 1–7% and bit accuracy falls to ~0.49 (random), effectively nullifying the attack. For PRC and GS++, the gains are more moderate (detection reduced to 6–49% at 150 steps), reflecting their inherently stronger multi-bit robustness. Under reprompting, SemBind consistently reduces detection by 40–90% across all four schemes, with the largest relative gains on GS++ (from 0.91 to 0.43 on SDP, and from 0.84 to 0.25 on COCO with SD 2.1). In the mismatched-attacker setting (SD 1.5), SemBind is even more effective, driving detection to near-zero for TR and GS. On image quality, all SemBind variants match their baselines: FID t-values are ≤1.14 and CLIP t-values ≤1.14, confirming no perceptible degradation. Robustness to common distortions shows only a mild 3–5% average drop for PRC-S and GS++-S, while TR-S and GS-S remain within 1% of their baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent-based watermarking paradigm (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading, PRC, Gaussian Shading++), which encodes watermarks in the initial diffusion noise and recovers them via inverse DDIM. It departs from prior defenses that merely tighten detection thresholds by introducing a semantic binding mechanism: a contrastively trained DINOv2-based hasher produces a binary code that, after expansion and secret permutation, multiplicatively modulates the watermarked latent. The contrastive training recipe (cluster-then-quantize) is borrowed from the image hashing literature, while the threat model (imprinting and reprompting black-box forgery) is taken from Müller et al. (2025). The key conceptual departure is that anti-forgery is achieved not by making the watermark harder to extract, but by making extraction fail when the image semantics deviate from the original prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SemBind is the first defense that makes latent-based watermarks resistant to black-box forgery while preserving provable undetectability, addressing a gap that undermines the provenance guarantees being piloted by governments and major AI providers. The mask-ratio parameter offers a practical knob for operators to trade off anti-forgery strength against robustness to benign distortions. Key limitations include the need for an extra generation pass (for the auxiliary semantic image), the assumption that the semantic masker remains private, and the unresolved challenge of aligning prompt semantics with generated-image semantics without an image. Open questions include whether key-dependent semantic masking can protect against white-box attackers who obtain the deployed masker, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
