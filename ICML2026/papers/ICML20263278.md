## Problem

Removing specific knowledge from a trained LLM (machine unlearning) without degrading its general utility remains an open challenge: existing weight-modification methods (gradient ascent, negative preference optimization, task arithmetic) are computationally expensive, fragile to complex queries, and typically cause catastrophic forgetting. The paper asks whether unlearning can be achieved at inference time—without touching the target model's weights—by leveraging small auxiliary models as lightweight proxies for the ideal retrained model.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a text prompt (or, in the image domain, a class label) and produces generated text or images from the steered distribution. The target model P (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) is run alongside two small auxiliary models p and q (1B–3B), whose logits are combined per-token; auxiliaries may come from different model families, bridged via a one-time cross-tokenizer mapping. For the distillation variant, the frozen DD system acts as a teacher and the target model is fine-tuned on the forget set to yield a single standalone checkpoint that requires no auxiliary models at serving time.

## Method

Divergence Decoding (DD) steers the target model's logits by adding a scaled difference between two auxiliary models: p is fine-tuned on the forget+retain data while q is fine-tuned on retain only, so the logit gap ℓ_q − ℓ_p encodes the direction toward the ideal retrained distribution. Two variants are offered—Linear DD (additive combination with a scalar α) and Rank DD (masking the top-k most divergent tokens)—and the resulting steered distribution is shown to be a Product of Experts between the base model and a domain-expert ratio (q/p)^α, linking the method to importance sampling. The auxiliary models require only standard pre-training or fine-tuning with no specialized objectives or architectural changes. When inference-time overhead is unacceptable, the frozen DD system is distilled into the target model via a KL-divergence objective on the forget set, converting the inference-time controller into a single parametric network. The approach generalizes beyond text: the same logit-steering mechanism is applied in the latent space of a class-conditional diffusion model (Esser et al., 2021) to suppress specific visual semantics.

## Results

On TOFU, Rank DD (k=20) achieves the highest aggregate score of 0.85±0.02, surpassing the Retrain gold standard (0.78±0.01), while Linear DD (α=1.5) matches Retrain at 0.78±0.01 with 0.95 privacy and 1.00 utility. On MUSE Knowledge Memorization, DD marginally improves over the strongest baselines, though it is less effective on MUSE Verbatim Memorization where simpler filtering methods excel. Distill DD recovers most of the Linear DD improvement (0.73 aggregate) in a single-model checkpoint, outperforming all prior gradient-based unlearning methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TOFU, Divergence Decoding decisively outperforms all four compared baselines. Linear DD (α=1.5) achieves 0.78 aggregate, beating GradAscent/GradDiff (Eldan & Russinovich, 0.63–0.64) by ~14–15 points, NPO (Zhang et al., 0.67) by 11 points, UNDIAL (Dong et al., 0.60) by 18 points, and GUARD (Deng et al., 0.02) by 76 points. Rank DD (k=20) further extends the lead to 0.85 aggregate, exceeding even the Retrain gold standard (0.78) on memorization (0.80 vs. 0.53) while maintaining competitive privacy (0.81 vs. 0.98). On MUSE, DD marginally improves over the strongest baselines on Knowledge Memorization but is less effective on Verbatim Memorization, where n-gram-based filtering methods are particularly strong. The distilled single-model variant (Distill DD, 0.73) still outperforms all gradient-based baselines, confirming that the unlearning signal transfers effectively into weights without the utility degradation typical of prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Product of Experts and Importance Sampling frameworks to formulate machine unlearning as a logit-steering problem, building on prior logit-steering methods such as DExperts (Liu et al., 2021), gradient-ascent unlearning (Eldan & Russinovich, 2023), and contrastive decoding (Suriyakumar et al., 2025). The key departures are: (1) using two small auxiliary models trained on forget and retain sets to form a logit difference that approximates the target-to-retrain gap, (2) introducing a rank-based steering variant that masks the top-k most divergent tokens, and (3) bridging inference-time steering to a single-model checkpoint via standard knowledge distillation. The authors position this as the first rank-based steering mechanism and the first to close the loop from inference-time control back into weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: the method requires only standard fine-tuning of small auxiliary models, introduces less than 0.1% latency overhead in realistic distributed deployments, and substantially outperforms gradient-based and preference-optimization unlearning baselines on TOFU while matching retrain on MUSE knowledge memorization. The conceptual contribution—that weight modification is often unnecessary and can be harmful to utility—challenges the dominant 'forced forgetting' paradigm in machine unlearning. Open questions include the quality trade-off in image generation (requiring ~2× sampling to meet baseline perceptual quality), the behavior under very large or sequential forget sets at frontier scale, and whether the over-unlearning sensitivity (critical in financial backtesting) can be further controlled.
