## Problem

Neural retrievers in RAG systems exhibit systematic blind spots: certain entities are consistently missed under practical top-k budgets because they occupy geometrically inaccessible regions of the embedding space, yet existing evaluation is query-centric and post-hoc, offering no way to identify or predict which entities will be intrinsically hard to retrieve before deployment. This paper asks whether such blind-spot risk can be quantified at the entity level, predicted directly from embedding geometry without expensive retrieval simulations, and remedied at indexing time without retraining the retriever.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes corpus documents, a target neural retriever encoder (with its specific pooling operator), a Reference KB of Wikipedia first paragraphs, and NER-extracted entity spans (via dslim/bert-base-NER). RPS is computed by ranking each entity against per-query neutral pools of N=800 KG-disjoint entities sampled from Wikidata 1-hop relations, with mention-aware span pooling applied to Wikipedia first paragraphs. The output is a per-entity predicted RPS score, a set of flagged high-risk entities (RPS < τ=0.3), and augmented document views (either concatenated KB passages or a single LLM-synthesized view) indexed alongside the originals, producing an expanded retrieval index that preserves the original corpus.

## Method

ARGUS is a two-stage pre-index pipeline. In the Diagnosis stage, the Retrieval Probability Score (RPS) is defined as the expected top-k hit rate of an entity across its Wikidata-derived related-entity query set, evaluated against controlled neutral pools with KG-disjointness constraints; lightweight probes (XGBoost, Ridge, or MLP) are then trained per retriever to regress RPS directly from mention-pooled entity embeddings, achieving Pearson r ≈ 0.65–0.80 and enabling pre-index flagging of high-risk entities via a fixed threshold. In the Remedy stage, each flagged entity's surface form is used to retrieve top-2 Wikipedia first-paragraph passages via BM25S, which are then either concatenated to the original document (Document Expansion, yielding 1 + k_Aug·N_d indexed views) or fed to an LLM (Qwen3-30B) to produce a single unified augmented view with minimal entity-focused clarifications (LLM Synthesis, yielding exactly 2 views per document). The key insight is that blind spots form structured, separable regions in embedding space (confirmed via LDA projections across RPS terciles), so retrievability risk is an intrinsic geometric property detectable from the embedding alone, and targeted augmentation of only the lowest-RPS entities outperforms untargeted or random augmentation strategies.

## Results

ARGUS achieves consistent nDCG improvements across all eight evaluated neural retrievers on BRIGHT, IMPLIRET, and RAR-B. Averaged over nDCG@5 and nDCG@10, Document Expansion yields +3.44 (BRIGHT), +6.76 (IMPLIRET), and +1.68 (RAR-B) absolute points over the original indexing baseline, while LLM Synthesis gives +2.44, +2.21, and +1.81 respectively. The largest single-benchmark gain is NV-EMBED-V2 on IMPLIRET (nDCG@5: 33.9→50.2 with Doc-Exp, +16.3), and the most consistent improvement across all three benchmarks is CONTRIEVER (e.g., RAR-B nDCG@10: 23.7→29.2 with LLM-Synth, +5.5). Gains are substantially larger on challenging subsets (e.g., IMPLIRET) than on already-easier ones, confirming that ARGUS targets the retriever's weakest geometric regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare ARGUS against competing augmentation or retrieval methods; instead, it evaluates whether ARGUS improves each of eight neural retrievers over its own un-augmented baseline. ARGUS (both Doc-Exp. and LLM-Synth.) outperforms the baseline on every retriever across all three benchmarks, with no regressions in the Full Benchmark Avg. columns. The largest relative gains appear on weaker retrievers: CONTRIEVER improves by +2.6/+5.1 nDCG@5/10 on BRIGHT (Doc-Exp.) and +3.7/+5.5 on RAR-B (LLM-Synth.), while GRITLM-7B gains +1.4/+2.0 on BRIGHT (Doc-Exp.). Stronger retrievers show smaller but still consistent gains: REASONIR-8B improves by +3.7/+6.4 on BRIGHT (Doc-Exp.) and +8.1/+14.3 on IMPLIRET, while NV-EMBED-V2 (already the strongest on IMPLIRET at 33.9/38.5) jumps to 50.2/53.2 with Doc-Exp. LLM Synthesis occasionally outperforms Doc-Exp. on specific subsets (e.g., CONTRIEVER on RAR-B nDCG@10: 29.2 vs. 27.9) but shows higher variance across retrievers, degrading JINA-V3 on IMPLIRET (nDCG@10: 17.9→16.4) where Doc-Exp. improves it (→20.8). Overall, Document Expansion is the more stable intervention, while LLM Synthesis offers a more index-efficient alternative with comparable suite-level gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant query-centric, post-hoc paradigm of retrieval evaluation (MS MARCO, BEIR) by introducing an entity-centric, pre-index auditing framework. It builds on the dense retrieval literature (DPR, CONTRIEVER, REASONIR) but reframes retrieval failures as a geometric property of the embedding space rather than a query-distribution artifact. The diagnostic-probe approach—predicting retrievability risk from raw entity embeddings via lightweight models like XGBoost—repurposes standard supervised learning tools for a novel IR auditing purpose, while the remedy (targeted KB augmentation at index time) is distinct from prior query-rewriting, reranking, or retriever-retraining strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that retrieval blind spots are geometrically structured and predictable pre-index (Pearson r ≈ 0.65–0.80) provides RAG practitioners with a practical, low-cost auditing tool that avoids expensive retrieval simulations. ARGUS's consistent nDCG gains across eight retrievers and three benchmarks, without requiring retraining or query rewriting, make it a deployable intervention for production RAG systems. Key limitations include dependence on external KB coverage (Wikipedia first paragraphs), focus on entity-centric scenarios, and the somewhat artificial controlled-competition setup of RPS; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to hybrid retrieval, non-entity-centric documents, and whether adaptive retriever-aware remedies can further close the gap.
