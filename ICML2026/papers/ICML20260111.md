## Problem

LLMs can be systematically influenced by input attributes that never appear in their chain-of-thought reasoning, making CoT-based monitoring unreliable for detecting such 'unverbalized biases.' Existing bias evaluations require predefined categories and hand-crafted datasets, limiting coverage to biases humans think to test for. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a fully automated, black-box pipeline that discovers task-specific unverbalized biases without any predefined bias taxonomy or manual hypothesis generation.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a task dataset of structured binary-decision inputs (e.g., 1,336 resumes, 2,500 loan applications, 1,500 university applications) and a target LLM, and produces a ranked set of detected unverbalized biases, each annotated with an effect size (Δ in acceptance-rate difference), a Bonferroni-corrected McNemar p-value, and a verbalization rate on discordant pairs. A concept is reported only if it passes two simultaneous criteria: statistical significance at α=0.05 after Bonferroni correction, and a verbalization rate below τ=0.3 on the subset of inputs where the model's decision actually flipped between positive and negative variations. The pipeline is fully black-box, requiring only API access to the target model and a set of LLM autoraters for concept generation, variation creation, and verbalization detection.

## Method

The pipeline operates in stages: (1) inputs are embedded and k-means clustered, and an LLM autorater (o3) hypothesizes candidate bias concepts from representative samples per cluster; (2) for each concept, an LLM generates paired positive/negative input variations that isolate the target attribute, with a separate LLM judge filtering confounded variations; (3) a baseline verbalization filter removes concepts already routinely cited as justification in the model's CoT; (4) surviving concepts are tested on progressively larger input samples using McNemar's test on paired binary outcomes, with Bonferroni correction for family-wise error rate and O'Brien-Fleming alpha spending for staged early stopping, plus a futility analysis that drops concepts whose conditional power falls below γ=0.01. The key distinction from prior counterfactual faithfulness work is that verbalization is checked semantically via an LLM judge (GPT-5-mini) specifically for whether the concept is cited as a *decision justification* rather than merely mentioned, and the entire hypothesis-generation-to-testing loop is automated without per-task trained editors or predefined bias categories. This design reduces API calls by roughly one-third relative to exhaustive evaluation while preserving FWER control.

## Results

Across three decision tasks (hiring, loan approval, university admissions) and seven LLMs, the pipeline detects 52 significant unverbalized biases with mean effect size 3.4 percentage points (median 3.1pp, range 1.5–6.0pp); 67% have 95% CIs excluding zero. Gender bias is detected in 5/7 models on hiring (all pro-female: 22 pro-female vs. 0 pro-male across all tasks), and race/ethnicity bias in 5/7 models (21 pro-minority vs. 0 pro-majority). The pipeline also uncovers novel non-demographic biases (Spanish fluency, English proficiency, writing formality) not covered by prior manual analysis, and achieves 92.5% accuracy on an intentional bias-injection validation (85% secret-bias detection, 100% overt-bias filtering).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Karvonen & Marks (2025), who manually identified gender and race biases on the same resume dataset, the pipeline automatically rediscovers both bias types but with smaller effect sizes (3–5% vs. their 6–12%), consistent with their finding that CoT prompting reduces bias magnitude; the pipeline additionally surfaces a novel Spanish-fluency bias they did not report. On the multilingual setup of Demidova et al. (2024), the pipeline confirms 6 significant biases in English (4 unverbalized, 2 verbalized) and 1 in Arabic, adding verbalization-rate insights absent from the original. For Hemmatian et al. (2023)'s anti-Muslim bias study, 5 of 7 significant biases are unverbalized, a distinction the original work did not make. On Motoki et al. (2024)'s political bias setup, the pipeline reveals that Republican impersonation produces 1.6–3× larger agreement-rate shifts than Democrat impersonation, suggesting a pre-existing Democrat-leaning default. On the SALT benchmark (Arif et al., 2024), the pipeline finds no significant demographic biases on Gemma-2-9B-it in a decision-task context, contrasting with the original's findings in debate and career-advice tasks and suggesting bias expression is task-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual faithfulness testing framework of Atanasova et al. (2023) by replacing per-task trained input editors with LLM-generated variations and adding a verbalization criterion that distinguishes biases the model actually cites from those it silently uses. It departs from prior bias-detection work (Karvonen & Marks 2025, Nadeem et al. 2021, Parrish et al. 2022) that requires manually predefined bias categories and hand-crafted datasets, instead automating the entire hypothesis-generation-to-statistical-testing pipeline via LLM autoraters and k-means clustering. The statistical machinery (O'Brien-Fleming alpha spending, futility-based early stopping, Bonferroni FWER control) is borrowed from clinical trial monitoring and adapted to the LLM evaluation setting, where the 'treatment' is a concept variation and the 'outcome' is a binary model decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a practical, scalable auditing tool for high-stakes LLM deployments: it discovers task-specific biases without requiring domain experts to enumerate categories in advance, and it specifically targets the harder-to-detect case where the model's CoT does not reveal the influencing factor. The uniform directionality across all models and tasks (22 pro-female vs. 0 pro-male; 21 pro-minority vs. 0 pro-majority) is a striking pattern, though the authors caution it may reflect confounders in variation generation. Key open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to open-ended (non-binary) tasks, whether the conservative statistical design suppresses smaller genuine biases, and whether the pro-female/pro-minority direction is a true model preference or an artifact of how LLMs generate counterfactual variations.
