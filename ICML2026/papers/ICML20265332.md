## Problem

Fine-tuning CLIP for AI-generated image detection (e.g., via LoRA) induces catastrophic forgetting that erodes the transferable geometric structure of the pre-trained embedding space, causing the model to overfit to dataset-specific shortcuts rather than learning artifact-level cues that generalize across unseen generators. Existing approaches either discard all semantic information or enforce global feature alignment, failing to selectively preserve beneficial pre-trained priors while suppressing task-irrelevant components.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 224×224 via Patch Selection) and produces a binary real/fake prediction. During training, a text description auto-generated by BLIP is fed through a frozen CLIP text encoder to estimate harmful gradient directions, but at inference only the LoRA-adapted image encoder and a linear classification head are used, making the text branch a training-time-only auxiliary signal.

## Method

DGS-Net introduces a gradient-space decomposition that splits each gradient into a positive half-space (harmful directions that increase loss) and a negative half-space (beneficial directions that decrease loss). The Orthogonal Suppression component projects the image task gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the normalized positive half-space of the text gradient, removing task-irrelevant semantic components while retaining beneficial cues. The Prior Alignment component injects the negative half-space of a frozen CLIP image encoder's gradient as a lightweight feature-level alignment term (inner product with the image feature), preserving transferable pre-trained priors. Training uses LoRA adapters on CLIP ViT-L/14 with BCEWithLogits loss for 1 epoch; the final gradient to the image feature is the sum of the orthogonally suppressed task gradient and the scaled beneficial prior, jointly enforcing semantic suppression and prior preservation in a single optimization step.

## Results

On AIGCDetectBench (17 test subsets spanning GANs, diffusion models, and other generators), DGS-Net achieves 97.6% mean accuracy (mAcc.) and 99.8% mean average precision (mA.P.), outperforming the strongest baseline NS-Net (93.2% mAcc.) by 4.4%. On the more challenging AIGIBench (25 generators including face-swap and personalization models), DGS-Net reaches 81.6% average accuracy, a 14.2% gain over NS-Net (67.4%) and 10.1% over UnivFD (71.5%). On the UniversalFakeDetect diffusion test set, the method attains 99.0% mAcc. and 100.0% mA.P., approaching saturation across all diffusion-based generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGS-Net consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on both primary benchmarks. On AIGCDetectBench, it leads NS-Net (the strongest CLIP-based baseline) by 4.4% mAcc. (97.6% vs. 93.2%) and C2P-CLIP by 15.2% (97.6% vs. 82.4%); the gap is especially pronounced on the Deepfake subset, where DGS-Net reaches 96.7% versus 60.6% for NS-Net and 76.6% for Effort. On AIGIBench, the advantage widens: DGS-Net achieves 81.6% average accuracy versus 67.4% for NS-Net and 71.5% for UnivFD, with roughly 50% absolute gains on face-swap generators (BlendFace, E4S, FaceSwap, InSwap, SimSwap) where most baselines score below 25%. The method also surpasses non-CLIP baselines such as AIDE (85.6% mAcc. on AIGCDetectBench) and DFFreq (85.2%) by 12+ points. The only exceptions are DALLE-3, SocialRF, and CommunityAI on AIGIBench, where all methods—including DGS-Net—perform poorly (2.8%, 19.2%, 9.2% respectively), which the authors attribute to unknown post-processing operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP fine-tuning lineage for AI-generated image detection (UnivFD, C2P-CLIP, Effort, NS-Net) by reframing the catastrophic-forgetting problem in gradient space rather than feature space. Where prior methods either discard all semantic information (NS-Net's null-space) or decompose features into orthogonal subspaces (Effort), DGS-Net decomposes gradients into positive (harmful) and negative (beneficial) half-spaces and applies orthogonal projection to suppress only the harmful component while injecting beneficial descent directions from a frozen CLIP encoder. The knowledge distillation framing from Hinton et al. is repurposed: instead of enforcing global feature-level alignment, the paper performs selective gradient-space alignment, retaining only the coordinate-wise negative components of the teacher's gradients as a lightweight prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a principled, gradient-level mechanism for balancing task adaptation against prior preservation during CLIP fine-tuning, which is a recurring challenge across many vision-language model adaptation scenarios. The linear probing results (CLIP-LoRA scoring 52.4% vs. frozen CLIP's 71.5% on AIGIBench) offer concrete evidence that standard LoRA fine-tuning actively destroys transferable geometry, validating the need for the proposed surgery. However, performance remains poor on certain generators (DALLE-3, SocialRF, CommunityAI), which the authors attribute to unknown post-processing operations—an open challenge that limits real-world deployment. Whether the gradient-sign decomposition principle generalizes beyond CLIP-based detection to other fine-tuning settings (e.g., segmentation, retrieval) remains unexplored.
