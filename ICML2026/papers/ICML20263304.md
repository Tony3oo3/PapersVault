## Problem

State-of-the-art gradient-based data selection methods like LESS approximate optimization geometry using diagonal preconditioners derived from Adam optimizer states, implicitly assuming coordinate-wise parameter independence. The paper shows this assumption structurally breaks down under LoRA, where the bilinear parameterization W = W₀ + BA induces genuine cross-block curvature between factors A and B that no diagonal matrix can represent, leading to distorted influence estimates and suboptimal selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large candidate pool (~270K instruction examples from FLAN V2, COT, DOLLY, and Open Assistant 1), a small target validation set (few-shot examples from MMLU, TYDIQA, or BBH subtasks, ranging from 9 to 285 examples), and a base LLM with LoRA adapters. It produces a ranked list of candidate examples scored by projected gradient alignment, from which the top-k (5% budget) are selected for fine-tuning. Gradients are computed in the LoRA parameter space (34M–135M trainable parameters depending on model), and the target subspace dimension is determined by capturing 95% of explained variance in the validation gradient spectrum (e.g., rank 150 for MMLU, rank 9 for TYDIQA).

## Method

GIST (Gradient Isometric Subspace Transformation) replaces diagonal optimizer-state rescaling with a three-step spectral subspace procedure: (1) a lightweight one-epoch LoRA warmup on a 5% random subset stabilizes the optimization landscape; (2) an SVD on the stacked validation gradient matrix G_val ∈ R^{d×|D_val|} extracts the top-r left singular vectors forming a non-diagonal target projector Π = U_r^T that captures the coupled, rotated task-relevant directions; (3) each candidate's gradient is projected onto this subspace and scored by cosine similarity to each target example, with a maximum-relevance aggregation across tasks. Theoretically, Theorem 3.2 proves LoRA's bilinear structure creates irreducible cross-block Hessian entries, while Theorem 3.3 (via Davis-Kahan) bounds the eigenspace error of the gradient-covariance proxy relative to the true validation Hessian, justifying the warmup requirement. This yields a selection rule that respects parameter coupling without full second-order information, requiring only 0.29% of LESS's storage and ~25% of its compute time.

## Results

GIST selects only 5% of the training pool and achieves the best average improvement (Avg. Δ) across all three base models: +6.2 on Llama2-7B (matching the Full fine-tuning upper bound of +6.2), +4.5 on Llama3.2-3B (vs. +3.3 for Full), and +1.5 on Qwen2.5-1.5B (vs. +0.9 for Full). On Llama3.2-3B, GIST reaches 69.2% on TYDIQA and 48.0% on BBH, both exceeding the 100%-data fine-tuning results. In terms of efficiency, GIST requires only 0.29% of LESS's storage (217 MB vs. 75 GB) and ~25% of its wall-clock runtime (13.5 h vs. 54 h on a single A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIST outperforms the state-of-the-art optimizer-based method LESS (Xia et al., 2024) on average across all three models: +6.2 vs. +5.9 (Llama2-7B), +4.5 vs. +3.3 (Llama3.2-3B), and +1.5 vs. +1.1 (Qwen2.5-1.5B). On individual tasks, GIST wins on 7 out of 9 dataset–model combinations, with the largest margins on TYDIQA with Llama3.2-3B (69.2 vs. 67.1) and BBH with Llama3.2-3B (48.0 vs. 46.1); LESS retains a narrow edge on TYDIQA with Llama2-7B (56.2 vs. 55.8) and TYDIQA with Qwen2.5-1.5B (61.4 vs. 61.2). Against similarity-based baselines, GIST substantially outperforms RDS+ (Ivison et al., 2025) and GTR-Base embedding retrieval on every model–dataset pair, with gaps of 3–10 points. Heuristic baselines (Length, PPL) are consistently weaker: Length occasionally matches GIST on MMLU with Llama3.2-3B (57.6 vs. 56.1) but degrades sharply on other tasks, while PPL produces negative average gains on Qwen2.5-1.5B (−0.1). Notably, GIST with only 5% of the data matches or exceeds Full fine-tuning (100%) on all three models, whereas LESS matches Full only on Llama2-7B and underperforms it on the other two.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the influence-function framework of Koh & Liang (2017) and the targeted data selection formulation of LESS (Xia et al., 2024), but departs from the latter's reliance on diagonal Adam-state preconditioners. It unifies hard-example mining, similarity-based retrieval, and optimizer-based selection as different structural surrogates for the same Hessian-preconditioned alignment objective (Eq. 7), then proves that under LoRA's bilinear parameterization the true curvature is inherently non-diagonal (Theorem 3.2), making diagonal surrogates provably insufficient. The method draws on the broader spectral-optimization literature (GaLore, SOAP, Muon) by applying SVD-based subspace recovery to the data-selection problem rather than to the optimizer itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: GIST achieves comparable or better accuracy than LESS with ~4× less wall-clock time and ~350× less storage, making gradient-based data selection far more accessible. The theoretical result that diagonal preconditioners have an irreducible error floor under PEFT coupling provides a principled justification for moving beyond optimizer-state heuristics. Open questions remain around token-level granularity, incorporating reliable magnitude/scale information alongside direction, and characterizing the transient warmup dynamics before the Gauss-Newton approximation becomes valid. The finding that 5% selected data can outperform 100% full fine-tuning also raises broader questions about data quality vs. quantity in instruction tuning.
