## Problem

Existing video reasoning models—whether RL-based or chain-of-thought—tend to anchor predictions on visually salient but semantically irrelevant cues, failing to ground reasoning on subtle yet task-critical objects across frames. This leads to inconsistent, biased conclusions when the discriminative evidence is not the most prominent visual element. The paper addresses this by formulating video reasoning as a progressive, object-grounded decision process that explicitly selects and verifies evidence at the object level across time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T sampled video frames and a text instruction as input, with per-frame object instances extracted via SA2VA generic detection. It produces a final answer (multiple-choice or free-form) accompanied by an explicit object-grounded reasoning trajectory—a sequence of (frame_index, object_index) selections that form a spatio-temporal evidence path. The trajectory enforces a monotonic temporal constraint (frame indices never decrease), and the model operates on 32 frames with a fixed, shallow reasoning depth to bound the search space.

## Method

Chain-of-Glimpse combines two tightly coupled components: (1) an Object-Grounded Video Reasoning Formulation (OGVRF) that uses MCTS to explore multiple reasoning trajectories where each action selects a specific object instance from a specific frame, with states updated by the visual features of the chosen object; top-scoring trajectories are distilled into the model via supervised fine-tuning. (2) A Multi-turn Decision Policy (MTDP) that optimizes the reasoning policy using GRPO with a composite reward combining answer correctness and step-level evidence-grounding quality, explicitly incentivizing the model to attend to task-relevant but low-saliency objects rather than visually dominant ones. The key distinction from prior CoT or RL approaches is that every reasoning step is anchored to a concrete detected object in a specific frame, and the RL reward directly penalizes trajectories that ground on irrelevant objects, producing interpretable and self-correcting multi-step decisions.

## Results

On NExTQA, Chain-of-Glimpse-7B achieves 83.3% average multiple-choice accuracy, improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (79.7%) by +3.6 points, with the largest gains on Causal (+3.9) and Temporal (+2.8) sub-categories. On Video-Holmes, Chain-of-Glimpse-7B reaches 42.5% average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (42.0%) and the backbone (38.8%). On the grounded NExT-GQA benchmark, the method achieves 32.1 Acc@GQA and 77.9 Acc@QA with only 0.08 s/sample additional inference cost over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chain-of-Glimpse-7B outperforms all open-source baselines on NExTQA: it beats DyCoke (79.4%), Video-LLaVA (60.5%), and LangRepo (60.9%) by large margins, and improves over its own Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone by +3.6 points. Against proprietary systems, it surpasses GPT-4-based VideoAgent (71.3%) and GPT-3.5-based LLoVi (66.3%) by 12 and 17 points respectively. On Video-Holmes, Chain-of-Glimpse-7B (42.5%) edges out GPT-4o (42.0%) and clearly beats Gemini-2.0-Flash (30.6%), InternVL3-8B (32.3%), VideoChat-R1 (33.0%), and SEED-Bench-R1 (33.5%). On NExT-GQA, it leads VideoMind-7B (28.2 Acc@GQA), VideoChat-TPO-7B (25.5), and LangRepo (17.1) by 3.9, 6.6, and 15.0 points respectively. In computational efficiency, Chain-of-Glimpse-7B requires only 6.47 TFLOPs versus DyCoke's 14.13 and FastVID's 10.73, while achieving higher accuracy than both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: MCTS-based planning (James et al., 2017), RL-enhanced video reasoning (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, SEED-Bench-R1), and object-grounded multimodal understanding (SA2VA, VideoRefer Suite). It departs from prior RL-based video reasoning methods that operate under single-step perception and from CoT-based approaches that remain language-centric without explicit visual grounding. The key conceptual departure is formulating video reasoning as a multi-turn, object-grounded decision process where MCTS explores candidate trajectories over detected object instances across frames, and GRPO with a composite answer-plus-evidence reward refines the policy—rather than treating reasoning as a single autoregressive pass or a purely textual chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in video MLLMs: over-reliance on visually salient but semantically irrelevant cues, which produces inconsistent predictions especially on long videos. The object-grounded formulation offers an interpretable alternative where each reasoning step is anchored to a specific visual region, and the ablations confirm that long-video performance benefits most from this structured approach. Limitations include dependence on GPT-4o for trajectory generation during data construction, reliance on relatively small backbones (3B/7B), and a fixed shallow search depth that may constrain scalability to hour-scale videos or more complex compositional queries. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger models without the GPT-4o data-generation crutch and whether the MCTS search budget can be adapted dynamically per query complexity.
