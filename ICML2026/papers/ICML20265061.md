## Problem

Vision-Language Models exhibit rich semantic representations but insufficient 3D spatial reasoning, yet existing remedies either require explicit 3D prior inputs (depth maps, point clouds, camera parameters) or introduce external spatial encoders, both of which add pipeline complexity and degrade the VLM's general-purpose capabilities through invasive fine-tuning. The open question is how to elicit latent spatial structure from pretrained VLMs end-to-end on demand without sacrificing their general intelligence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265061/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images or multi-image sequences (resized to 448×448) together with a text instruction, requiring no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth sensors at inference. It produces a textual spatial reasoning response (e.g., metric depth at a marked location, relative direction, route plan) via standard autoregressive decoding. During training, a dual DPT head additionally outputs a dense depth map and a per-pixel camera ray map as auxiliary geometric supervision; for embodied manipulation, the same architecture regresses or discretizes robot action sequences.

## Method

SpatioLM freezes the entire pretrained VLM backbone and attaches a plug-and-play Spatio-Vision Module (SV-Module) of 6 stacked Spatio-Vision Blocks (~0.3B parameters total) in a ControlNet-inspired side-tuning fashion. Each SV-Block uses an Alternating-Attention mechanism (inspired by VGGT) that separately models intra-frame geometric detail and inter-frame geometric consistency, operating on intermediate ViT layer tokens (16th of 24) rather than final-layer features. Geometry-aware tokens are injected back into the frozen LM blocks at regular intervals via zero-initialized projection layers, preserving the pretrained feature distribution. Training combines a language modeling loss with two geometric objectives: Vision Token Supervision (Gram-matrix distillation toward Depth Anything V3 latent features) and Dense Geometric Supervision (depth and camera ray map losses from a dual DPT head), weighted at 0.6/0.2/0.2. At inference the SV-Module is forwarded once during prefilling with no additional per-token overhead, and the Dual DPT Head can be removed entirely.

## Results

SpatioLM_SenseNovaSI-8B achieves the best overall performance across all spatial perception benchmarks: 83.5 on MD-S (δ<1.25), 69.0 on MD-M, 83.8 on DA-2K, and 41.8 on DR, substantially outperforming proprietary models (e.g., Doubao-1.5 at 52.6/41.1/80.7/13.6) and specialized spatial models. On spatial understanding, it scores 71.6 on VSI-Bench (the first model to surpass 70), 101.8 CIDEr on ScanQA, and 58.6 ER1 on SQA3D. On embodied manipulation (LIBERO), SpatioLM-OFT reaches 96.3% average success with only 2B parameters and no large-scale robot pretraining, outperforming its base SenseNovaSI-OFT (95.3%) and competitive with much larger VLA models. General-purpose capability degradation is limited to ~3–4%, compared to 44% for Spatial-MLLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatioLM_SenseNovaSI-8B dominates all proprietary API models: it outperforms Doubao-1.5 (the strongest proprietary baseline) by +30.9 on MD-S, +27.9 on MD-M, +3.1 on DA-2K, and +28.2 on DR. Among open-source models, it surpasses Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (the largest at 235B parameters) by +35.1 on MD-S and +24.1 on DR, while using only 8B parameters. Against specialized spatial models, it beats VST-7B-SFT (the closest competitor on DA-2K at 83.8) by a large margin on MD-S (+32.5) and DR (+30.9), and outperforms its own base SenseNovaSI-8B by +30.9 on MD-S and +24.3 on DR. On spatial understanding, SpatioLM achieves 71.6 on VSI-Bench, exceeding the previous best (Spatial-MLLM-4B at ~70) and all proprietary models; on SQA3D it improves over SenseNovaSI-8B from 50.1 to 58.6 ER1 (+8.5). On LIBERO, SpatioLM-OFT (96.3%) edges out SenseNovaSI-OFT (95.3%) and approaches GR00T-N1 (93.9%) and π0 (94.2%) without any large-scale robot pretraining, while SpatioLM-VLA0 (91.0%) substantially improves over SenseNovaSI-VLA0 (79.2%) in the discrete-action setting. Crucially, SpatioLM preserves general capabilities with only 3–4% average degradation, whereas Spatial-MLLM suffers a 44% drop, confirming the non-invasive design advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatioLM departs from the three dominant paradigms for enhancing VLM spatial reasoning—explicit 3D geometric augmentation (DepthLM, SpatialBot), external spatial encoder injection (SpatialLLM, Spatial-MLLM), and tool-use agentic frameworks (ViperGPT, VADAR)—by instead eliciting latent spatial knowledge already present in intermediate ViT layers through a lightweight, ControlNet-inspired side-tuning module. The Spatio-Vision Block's alternating attention mechanism is directly inspired by VGGT, and the dual DPT head and Vision Token Supervision distillation are adopted from Depth Anything V3, but the key departure is that all 3D supervision is used only during training via pseudo-labels, while inference requires no geometric inputs whatsoever. The design also builds on the recent finding (Bolya et al., 2026) that intermediate ViT layers encode richer geometric structure than final-layer outputs, which motivates the choice of the 16th of 24 layers as the SV-Module's input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpatioLM demonstrates that a purely 2D, parameter-efficient (~0.3B trainable) side module can push VLM spatial reasoning to SOTA (first model above 70 on VSI-Bench) while preserving general-purpose capabilities within ~3–4%, in stark contrast to the 40%+ degradation seen with invasive spatial-encoder fine-tuning. This opens a practical path for deploying spatially-aware VLMs in open-world, RGB-only settings without sensor dependencies or multi-stage pipelines. Key open questions include whether the pseudo-label ceiling (inherited from Depth Anything V3) can be broken with real metric supervision, whether the motion-dynamics trade-off observed on MVBench can be resolved with explicit temporal signals, and whether the approach scales beyond 8B backbones or to safety-critical deployment.
