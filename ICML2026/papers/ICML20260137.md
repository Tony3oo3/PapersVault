## Problem

Bayes-assisted conformal prediction preserves finite-sample coverage even under model misspecification, but its efficiency degrades sharply when the Bayesian working model's prior is poorly aligned with the data, producing unnecessarily wide prediction sets. Existing Bayes-assisted methods lack a mechanism to detect and protect against this prior-data conflict, losing the efficiency gains that motivate their use in the first place.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed (non-Bayesian) predictive model f, a calibration set of covariate-response pairs, and a new covariate x_{n+1}, and produces a prediction interval for the unknown response. Unusually, the Bayesian working model is placed only on the scalar residuals r_i = y_i - f(x_i) rather than on the full conditional Y|X, reducing the BWM to low-dimensional parameters (mean and variance) and eliminating the need for priors over high-dimensional model weights. Prediction intervals are computed via a grid-free bracketed root-search rather than the standard grid-based p-value evaluation, avoiding discretisation error and grid-tuning challenges.

## Method

RoBAS (Robust Bayes-Assisted Shrinkage) places a hierarchical BWM with a heavy-tailed (horseshoe) prior on the residual mean, inducing a nonconformity score that provably interpolates between the Distance-To-Origin score (efficient when residuals are centred near zero) and the Distance-To-Average score (robust when the residual mean drifts far from zero). Two instantiations are provided: RoBAS-Full, which marginalises over the horseshoe prior and yields a score involving a confluent hypergeometric function, and RoBAS-EB, a closed-form empirical Bayes shrinkage score s(r_{n+1}, r_{1:n}) = |r_{n+1} - â(r_{1:n})·r̄_n| where the shrinkage factor â adaptively weights the sample mean. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that as |r̄_n| → ∞, the Bayes-assisted score converges to a monotone transformation of DTA, guaranteeing stable interval widths under prior-data conflict. A grid-free Algorithm 1 computes prediction interval endpoints via bracketed search and root-finding, and Theorem 3.5 proves the RoBAS-EB conformal set is always an interval for n ≥ 4.

## Results

On the UTKFaces out-of-distribution (OUT) subset (ages >50, where the predictor is biased), RoBAS-EB achieves interval widths of 2.670 (n_cal=5) to 2.457 (n_cal=50), matching the robust DTA baseline (2.674–2.463) while DTO, CQR, and LOCAL all produce widths of 7.2–7.9. On the in-distribution (IN) subset, RoBAS-EB is competitive with the best methods (e.g., 3.140 vs. LOCAL's 3.091 at n_cal=50), with only a small gap at very small calibration sizes. On tabular datasets with induced covariate shift (Figure 2), RoBAS maintains near-constant width as shift increases, while DTO, NNG, CQR, and LOCAL all show substantial width inflation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DTO and LOCAL, RoBAS-EB wins decisively in the out-of-distribution setting: at n_cal=50 on UTKFaces OUT, RoBAS-EB achieves width 2.457 versus 7.717 (DTO) and 7.728 (LOCAL), a ~3× reduction. Against CQR, the gap is similar (2.457 vs. 7.241 at n_cal=50 OUT). Against NNG (the standard Bayes-assisted Normal-Normal-Gamma score), RoBAS-EB reduces width from 4.677 to 2.457 at n_cal=50 OUT, showing that the heavy-tailed/shrinkage design prevents the efficiency collapse that NNG suffers under prior-data conflict. Against DTA (the robust non-informative baseline), RoBAS-EB essentially ties in the OUT setting (2.457 vs. 2.463 at n_cal=50) while being slightly worse in the IN setting at small n_cal (3.069 vs. 3.282 at n_cal=5), confirming that RoBAS recovers DTA's robustness without sacrificing it in-distribution. On the tabular datasets (facebook_1, airfoil, concrete) with increasing covariate shift parameter c, RoBAS maintains near-constant width while all other baselines (DTO, NNG, CQR, LOCAL) show monotonic width growth, with RoBAS matching or beating DTA at every shift level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayes-assisted conformal prediction framework of Hoff (2023) and Bersson & Hoff (2024) by relocating the Bayesian working model from the full conditional data-generating process Y|X to the scalar residuals of a fixed predictor f. This shift avoids the need for priors over high-dimensional parameter spaces and enables a key robustness property: a heavy-tailed (horseshoe) prior on the residual mean provably reverts the nonconformity score to the Distance-To-Average baseline under prior-data conflict (Theorem 3.1), while a closed-form empirical Bayes variant (drawing on Efron & Morris, 1973) preserves this shrinkage behaviour without MCMC. The paper also establishes a previously unarticulated equivalence between the DTA score and a Bayes-assisted score under a flat prior (Proposition 3.2), unifying two seemingly unrelated design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underappreciated failure mode of Bayes-assisted conformal prediction: while coverage validity is preserved under misspecification, interval widths can balloon arbitrarily when the prior conflicts with the data, undermining the practical utility of the method. The adaptive DTO-to-DTA interpolation is a clean solution, and the grid-free interval computation (Algorithm 1) removes a practical tuning burden. However, the robustness guarantee is specifically tied to mean misalignment of residuals; the authors acknowledge that variance or tail misspecification is not addressed, and extending the heavy-tailed adaptivity to scale parameters is an open direction. The method is most beneficial in small-calibration, high-noise, or distribution-shifted settings, which limits its impact in large-data regimes where standard scores already perform well.
