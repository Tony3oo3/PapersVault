## Problem

Divide-and-conquer QAOA frameworks like QAOA² suffer from two fundamental limitations: heuristic partitioning metrics (modularity, boundary size, cut size) are decoupled from quantum optimization objectives, showing negligible correlation with solution quality, and topology-blind random parameter initialization causes optimization cold starts that cannot be overcome even by doubling the optimization budget. No existing method jointly learns partitions and parameters in an end-to-end differentiable manner aligned with quantum performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted graph G=(V,E) representing a QUBO/MaxCut/Ising instance (21–1000 nodes) with five standardized structural node features (degree, weighted degree, clustering coefficient, PageRank, betweenness centrality) and produces a discrete partition matrix S∈{0,1}^{N×k} assigning nodes to subgraphs of at most max_nodes=10 qubits, plus continuous QAOA variational parameters P∈[0,2π)^{2p×k} (γ,β per subgraph). The predicted performance ratio ρ is architecturally bounded to [0.5, 1.0] via a scaled sigmoid, and the final output is a solution bitstring obtained by running the QAOA² recursion with the generated configuration.

## Method

Neural QAOA² introduces a Generative Evaluative Network (GEN) comprising a quantum evaluator f_φ (a multi-view GNN with parallel GAT/GCN encoders for global topology, partition-induced subgraph, and sine-cosine-embedded quantum parameters) that predicts the QAOA² performance ratio as a differentiable surrogate, and a joint generator g_θ that sequentially produces the partition and parameters. The generator uses an Orthogonal Complement Head (OCH) that enforces cluster centers to be mutually orthogonal and orthogonal to the global graph embedding via QR decomposition, yielding discriminative soft partitions; a Greedy Capacity Discretization (GCD) with Straight-Through Estimator (STE) that strictly enforces the qubit capacity constraint while enabling gradient backpropagation; and a stop-gradient operator to decouple partition and parameter generation. Training is two-stage: f_φ is first trained via supervised MSE on 52,587 offline (G,S,P,ρ) triplets, then g_θ is trained via gradient ascent guided by the frozen f_φ. At inference, test-time adaptation fine-tunes g_θ for 64–1000 steps against the frozen evaluator to specialize to each instance.

## Results

On 50 in-distribution test instances (B, BE, W), Neural QAOA² achieves a mean performance ratio ρ̄ of 0.8930 and average rank of 1.74, winning 28/50 instances. On 93 out-of-distribution instances (GKA QUBO, L Ising spin glass), it achieves ρ̄ = 0.8451 and rank 1.46, winning 60/93. Across all 183 instances (N = 21–1000), Neural QAOA² ranks first on 101 instances, nearly doubling the 53 wins of the strongest runner-up (Boundary on L). At the default 64-step test-time adaptation budget, total wall-clock time is 11.78 s, within the same order as the best heuristic (Modularity, 7.27 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural QAOA² outperforms all four heuristic partitioning baselines (Random, Modularity, Boundary, KL) on the overall 50-instance benchmark, achieving ρ̄ = 0.8930 vs. 0.8869 (Modularity, the strongest heuristic) and rank 1.74 vs. 2.54. The gap is most pronounced on QUBO instances (BE dataset: rank 1.06 vs. 3.13 for Modularity), while on MaxCut (W) Neural QAOA² ties Modularity at rank 2.23, indicating the learned partition captures community structure without handcrafted rules. Against advanced parameter initialization strategies (TQA, INTERP, FOURIER, QIBPI) combined with the best heuristics at p=2 and p=3 (Table 9), Neural QAOA² still leads overall (ρ̄ = 0.8927 at p=2, 0.8936 at p=3 vs. best baseline 0.8873), confirming that joint partition-parameter generation surpasses decoupled initialization. On out-of-distribution Ising spin glass (L), Boundary is competitive in mean ρ̄ (0.8205 vs. 0.8160) but Neural QAOA² wins on consistency (rank 1.42 vs. 1.60, 28 vs. 20 wins), demonstrating more stable generalization across topologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the QAOA² divide-and-conquer framework (Zhou et al., 2023), which partitions large graphs into subgraphs solvable on small quantum devices and merges solutions via Z₂ symmetry. The key departure is replacing handcrafted partitioning heuristics (modularity, boundary, KL) and random parameter initialization with a unified differentiable generative-evaluative network (GEN) that jointly produces both. The quantum evaluator (a multi-view GNN) serves as a differentiable performance surrogate, while the joint generator (GAT/GCN with an orthogonal complement head and greedy capacity discretization via STE) learns topology-aware mappings end-to-end. This connects threads from differentiable graph partitioning, QAOA parameter initialization (TQA, INTERP, QIBPI), and straight-through estimation for discrete optimization into a single trainable pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete, empirically validated limitations of D&C QAOA frameworks: the near-zero correlation (r≈0.29) between standard graph metrics and quantum solution quality, and the cold-start problem of random initialization that persists even when doubling optimization steps. The zero-shot generalization to unseen topologies (Ising spin glass) and scales (N up to 1000) is practically significant for deploying quantum optimization on near-term hardware. However, the framework is constrained to unconstrained binary problems with Z₂ symmetry (ruling out TSP, MIS), relies on noiseless simulation, and requires an offline dataset for evaluator pre-training—open questions that limit immediate hardware deployment.
