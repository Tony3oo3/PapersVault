## Problem

Existing federated learning frameworks treat clients as independent contributors and focus on mitigating statistical heterogeneity, leaving workflow dependencies unmodeled. In multi-agent LLM systems where data is siloed across organizations due to privacy, IP, and compliance constraints, this means upstream-stage outputs that downstream stages must follow are not captured during aggregation, producing incompatible updates and incoherent multi-stage collaboration. The paper asks how to enable LLM-based experts to solve sequential, workflow-structured tasks under strict data-locality constraints while preserving the directed stage-to-stage dependencies that make multi-agent pipelines coherent.

## Input / Output

FedWave consumes a single workflow-structured text prompt (formatted via the Alpaca template, max sequence length 2048) that encodes the full workflow context including cross-stage dependencies, and produces a single final answer in one forward pass rather than multi-turn agent dialogue. At inference, the model uses 8-bit quantization for efficiency, and the MoE router performs top-k (k=2) expert selection to fuse role-specific knowledge dynamically. This single-pass design contrasts with centralized multi-agent baselines that require multiple rounds of inter-agent communication, yielding substantially lower latency and token cost.

## Method

FedWave introduces three coupled mechanisms for federated workflow collaboration. First, a Value Chain Layer (VCLayer) applies stage-specific multi-head attention to LoRA-adapted hidden states, trained with three collaborative losses—positional (cosine similarity of stage embeddings), continuity (cosine similarity of adjacent query projections), and consistency (alignment of mean-pooled outputs on shared anchor prompts using previous-round snapshots)—that explicitly encode the workflow graph's edge structure. Second, a lightweight MoE router (MLP over mean-pooled hidden states) is co-trained with each expert's LoRA adapter and VCLayer, with the local SFT loss gated by the router's own weight for that expert, providing weak supervision that role-i prompts should favor expert i; the router is aggregated server-side via standard FL while expert modules remain role-specific. Third, a DPO alignment stage constructs preference pairs automatically from router-induced rankings (top-ranked expert response as preferred, lower-ranked as dispreferred) and fine-tunes the policy against a frozen SFT reference, improving end-to-end coherence without sacrificing stage specialization. The full local loss combines the router-gated SFT loss, VCLayer collaborative loss, and MoE regularizers (load-balancing and entropy), all optimized jointly per client.

## Results

On the MSCoRe workflow benchmarks with Qwen2-7B, FedWave achieves METEOR scores of 40.35 (Automotive), 48.28 (E-commerce), and 29.82 (Pharmaceutical), substantially outperforming the strongest federated baseline (FedYogi at 24.18/40.31/24.59 METEOR). The largest relative gain is on Automotive METEOR, where FedWave improves by +17.00 over FedAvg (40.35 vs. 23.35). Against centralized multi-agent baselines on Qwen2-7B, FedWave surpasses all centralized methods on E-commerce across every metric (e.g., BS-F 80.17 vs. CoA's 79.20) and achieves the best BERTScore-F, BLEU-4, ROUGE-1, and ROUGE-2 on Automotive and Pharmaceutical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedWave outperforms all six federated baselines (FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedProx, FedAdam, FedYogi, Scaffold) on every metric across all three MSCoRe datasets and all three backbones. The most striking gap is on METEOR: on Automotive with Qwen2-7B, FedWave scores 40.35 versus the best federated baseline FedYogi at 24.18 (+16.17), and on Llama3-8B the gap widens to 24.83 vs. 11.31 (Scaffold). Against centralized multi-agent baselines (PMC, MedAgents, Debate, CoA) on Qwen2-7B, FedWave surpasses all methods on E-commerce across every metric (BS-F 80.17 vs. CoA 79.20; METEOR 42.60 vs. CoA 38.08) and achieves the best BERTScore-F, BLEU-4, ROUGE-1, and ROUGE-2 on Automotive and Pharmaceutical, though CoA retains a slight edge on GLEU (22.30 vs. 20.42 on Automotive) and ROUGE-L (24.01 vs. 23.46). On the non-linear Automotive-Energy workflow, FedWave achieves BERTScore-F of 76.08 versus the best FL baseline SCAFFOLD at 57.07, a +19.01 gap. On MuSiQue, FedWave more than doubles EM (7.20 vs. 3.39 for FedAdam) and F1 (19.27 vs. 11.38 for FedProx), though on FinQA its F1 (3.33) trails FedAvg (5.58) despite a 6× EM improvement (3.00 vs. 0.50).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedWave sits at the intersection of federated learning and LLM-based multi-agent systems, drawing on LoRA for parameter-efficient client adaptation, MoE routing (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) repurposed as an input-conditioned expert fusion mechanism, and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for preference alignment. It departs from standard FL (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD) by explicitly modeling directed workflow dependencies through the Value Chain Layer rather than treating clients as statistically independent, and departs from centralized MAS (PMC, MedAgents, CoA, Debate) by operating under strict data-locality constraints while still producing a single deployable model. The novelty is architectural and loss-design: the VCLayer's three relational losses (positional, continuity, consistency) and the router-induced DPO preference construction are new, but each component is grounded in established techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedWave demonstrates that workflow-structured multi-agent collaboration can be achieved under federated privacy constraints without sacrificing competitiveness against centralized multi-agent systems, and with a dramatic inference-time efficiency gain (single forward pass vs. multi-turn dialogue). This is practically important for domains where data silos are legally or operationally unavoidable (healthcare, manufacturing, finance). Key limitations include the absence of formal privacy guarantees (the paper explicitly notes it inherits gradient-inference risks), the main experiments using only 4 experts, and the reliance on a single-pass generation paradigm at inference that may not capture truly iterative cross-stage feedback. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large workflow graphs, whether formal differential privacy can be integrated without degrading the router's input-conditioned specialization, and whether the router-induced preference signal remains informative as expert count grows.
