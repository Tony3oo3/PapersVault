## Problem

Existing gradient inversion attacks in federated learning assume the attacker can directly observe raw gradients, which holds only under vanilla SGD. In practice, FL clients increasingly use adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSProp, AdaGrad) that share optimizer-produced parameter updates rather than raw gradients, rendering all prior gradient-matching objectives and analytical label-inference derivations infeasible. This paper addresses the underexplored setting where only adaptive-optimizer updates are available to the adversary.

## Input / Output

The attacker (an honest-but-curious FL server) receives per-client model updates computed by an adaptive optimizer along with the initial optimizer state (e.g., first- and second-order momentum) but not the post-training optimizer state. From these, the method reconstructs 224×224 RGB images (batch size up to 16) and recovers the corresponding class labels. The key constraint is that raw gradients are never exposed; all reconstruction must proceed from the nonlinear, momentum-dependent update signal.

## Method

The method has two coupled components. First, an update-matching objective (Eq. 3) replaces gradient matching by minimizing the negative cosine similarity between dummy updates U(∇θ̂, s) and observed updates U(∇θ, s), plus a total-variation regularization, where U(·) is the optimizer-specific update rule (e.g., Adam). Second, an analytical label-recovery procedure inverts the Adam update equation element-wise to obtain candidate final-layer gradient values (a quadratic per element), then resolves ambiguity using structural constraints: column-sum-to-zero properties of cross-entropy gradients (post-initial phase) and inter-class low-entanglement plus non-negative-activation assumptions (initial phase). The recovered labels initialize the image optimization, and random dropping of 30% of update entries per layer per step regularizes the search. The approach generalizes directly to RMSProp and AdaGrad, and the update-matching loss also yields stronger reconstructions than conventional gradient matching even in the standard SGD setting.

## Results

In the adaptive-optimizer (update-only) setting on ImageNet, the proposed method achieves PSNR 20.60, SSIM 0.5026, LPIPS-AlexNet 0.4230, and LPIPS-VGG 0.5324, surpassing the best baseline (HFGI) by approximately 6.5 dB in PSNR, 0.20 in SSIM, 0.35 in LPIPS-AlexNet, and 0.23 in LPIPS-VGG. On PACS, the method reaches PSNR 19.01 and SSIM 0.5192, again outperforming the best baseline by a wide margin. Under the traditional SGD setting where raw gradients are accessible, the method still improves PSNR by roughly 3.0 dB over the strongest baseline (HFGI: 20.80 vs. 23.92 on ImageNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines (IG, GIAS, GIFD, HFGI) are substantially outperformed in the update-only adaptive-optimizer setting. HFGI (Ye et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline, achieving PSNR 13.35 on ImageNet and 12.47 on PACS, yet the proposed method exceeds it by ~7.2 dB and ~6.5 dB respectively. IG (Geiping et al., 2020) and GIAS (Jeon et al., 2021) produce barely recognizable reconstructions (PSNR ≈ 11–13), while GIFD (Fang et al., 2023) performs similarly to GIAS. Notably, even in the traditional SGD setting where all baselines have direct gradient access, the proposed update-matching objective still yields ~3.0 dB higher PSNR than HFGI (23.92 vs. 20.80 on ImageNet), demonstrating that the new loss function is intrinsically stronger than conventional gradient matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient inversion attack (GIA) lineage—originating from DLG (Zhu et al., 2019) and refined by IG (Geiping et al., 2020) and iDLG (Zhao et al., 2020)—from the SGD-only setting to adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSProp, AdaGrad), where attackers observe optimizer-produced parameter updates rather than raw gradients. The key methodological departure is twofold: (1) an update-matching objective (negative cosine similarity between optimizer updates) replaces the conventional gradient-matching loss, and (2) an analytical label-recovery procedure that inverts the Adam update rule element-wise into a quadratic equation and resolves ambiguity via column-sum and sign constraints on final-layer gradients. The paper also repurposes the update-matching objective as a stronger loss even in the standard SGD setting, where it outperforms native gradient matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because adaptive optimizers are now standard in federated learning (e.g., MIME, client-adaptive FL), this work closes a practical gap: practitioners may have assumed that sharing optimizer updates rather than raw gradients inherently reduces privacy leakage, but the paper shows that high-fidelity reconstruction remains feasible. Key limitations include dependence on knowing the initial optimizer state, degradation with larger local batch sizes and deeper training, and reduced effectiveness under multiple local update steps. Open questions include whether MPC-based secure aggregation (which the authors note can be evaded) or sufficiently large batches can provide robust defense, and whether the analytical label-recovery generalizes to architectures without a final fully-connected layer or to non-convex loss landscapes.
