## Problem

Standard drift-diffusion models become analytically intractable when drift rates vary continuously with time, as induced by asynchronous external signals (notifications, nudges, feed updates). Existing workarounds—numerical simulation, neural likelihood estimators, or piecewise-linear boundary approximations—are computationally expensive and lack the closed-form structure needed for likelihood-based inference and analytical optimization of intervention timing. This paper derives a closed-form first-passage-time approximation for single-boundary DDMs with general time-dependent drift, valid in the high-threshold regime that characterizes many real-world user decisions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes tabular user and video features (demographics, viewing history, BERT subtitle embeddings for MOOCCubeX) along with elapsed time since video start, which are expanded into polynomial, exponential, and trigonometric basis functions and interacted with a learned latent vector to produce a time-varying drift. Output is a full probability distribution over response (watch) times via a closed-form CDF integral, rather than a single point estimate, enabling both prediction and analytical derivation of optimal nudge placement. On KuaiRec, a hybrid two-step design routes 'quick-skip' samples (below the 66th quantile) to an EGMN classifier and longer watches to the ExtDDM component.

## Method

The core contribution is a closed-form approximation of the first-passage-time density for a single-boundary DDM with time-dependent drift, derived by adapting Cuzick's boundary-crossing theorem for Brownian motion to the high-threshold asymptotic regime. The resulting CDF is an integral of a function f(s,u,ξ_s) = (u − ∫μ)/√(s−τ_t) weighted by the standard normal density, with a normalizing constant that provably equals one under mild drift conditions. The drift μ(X(t),t) is parameterized by a neural network over time-expanded features, and the threshold u is learned per-sample via a separate network, with training performed by maximum likelihood (negative log-likelihood) under Adam. For nudge timing, a kernel structure X(t)=Σh(t−x_j) with a monotone transformation Ψ yields a stochastic-dominance theorem: for sufficiently high thresholds, comparing the cumulative drift integral near the end of the window suffices to rank signal sequences, reducing optimal nudge placement to a tractable analytical problem.

## Results

On KuaiRec 2.0, the 2-step ExtDDM+EGMN model achieves MAE 4.283s, XAUC 0.6053, and KL-divergence 0.2063, outperforming the second-best baseline (CREAD) by 2.3% on MAE, 16.1% on XAUC, and 42.1% on KL. On MOOCCubeX (long-form educational videos), ExtDDM alone achieves MAE 32.34s, XAUC 0.6547, and KL 1.3453, beating CREAD by 8.1% on MAE and 17.1% on XAUC. In simulated Go/No-Go environments, ExtDDM attains the best log-likelihood at high thresholds (u=8, u=12) across all four cognitive scenarios, while the Intensity-Free model is the only baseline that remains competitive at the low threshold u=4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExtDDM+EGMN dominates all five KuaiRec baselines on every metric: it beats CREAD (the strongest single model) by 2.3% MAE and 16.1% XAUC, and substantially outperforms CREAD+EGMN on KL-divergence (0.206 vs 0.360), confirming that the gains come from the ExtDDM formulation rather than the hybrid architecture alone. On MOOCCubeX, ExtDDM outperforms both EGMN (MAE 37.18, XAUC 0.5102) and CREAD (MAE 35.20, XAUC 0.6321) across all three metrics. In the simulated Go/No-Go evaluation (Table 3), RMTPP and NHP suffer severe log-likelihood degradation at high thresholds (e.g., RMTPP drops to −530 at u=12 in the Reflected scenario vs. ExtDDM's −9.80), while the Intensity-Free model remains the closest competitor, achieving log-likelihoods within ~1–2 nats of ExtDDM at u=4 but falling behind at u=8 and u=12. The VR (Wide & Deep) and TPM baselines are consistently the weakest, with TPM showing the worst MAE (5.81s) and KL (2.49) on KuaiRec.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Go/No-Go Drift-Diffusion Model (Gomez et al., 2007) by deriving a closed-form first-passage-time approximation for time-varying drift, building on Cuzick's (1981) boundary-crossing asymptotics for Brownian motion. It departs from both the classical DDM literature (which assumes constant drift and controlled settings) and the neural temporal point process literature (Hawkes, RMTPP, NHP), which treats event timing as a black-box intensity estimation problem without cognitive grounding. The key methodological departure is replacing simulation-based or neural-likelihood estimation of generalized DDMs (Shinn et al., 2020; Fengler et al., 2021) with a tractable parametric likelihood valid in the high-threshold regime, enabling both efficient MLE and analytical optimization of intervention timing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form approximation bridges a long-standing gap between interpretable cognitive decision models and scalable deployment on real-world event streams, and the derived nudge-timing theorems offer the first analytical (rather than purely empirical) guidance for notification scheduling. However, the high-threshold assumption limits applicability to decisions requiring prolonged evidence accumulation, the single-boundary/single-action formulation excludes multi-choice competition, and the nudge-timing theory is validated only in simulation rather than on real notification data. Open questions include extending the approximation to lower thresholds, handling correlated multi-action processes, and obtaining privacy-preserving real-world validation of the timing optimization results.
