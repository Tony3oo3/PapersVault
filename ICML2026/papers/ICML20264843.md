## Problem

Current multimodal unlearning methods forget at the sample level by removing multimodal knowledge (MK) while preserving unimodal knowledge (UK), but the causal dependency UK→MK means this inadvertently damages UK, degrading both reliability (e.g., failing to recognize a forgotten person as a bearded male) and locality (e.g., low-confidence predictions on remaining samples like identifying Tim Cook as CEO). The paper formalizes this failure through a Structural Causal Model and proposes moving beyond sample-level forgetting to modality-decoupled semantic editing that preserves cross-modal comprehension while precisely removing targeted knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained vision-language model (ALBEF or BLIP) and a set of image-text pairs to be forgotten (5–500 samples in privacy scenarios, 1K–5K in copyright scenarios), producing an unlearned model evaluated via membership inference vulnerability, retrieval recall, and task accuracy. The MVI generator uses frozen Stable Diffusion as text encoder/image decoder and frozen BLIP-2 as image encoder/text decoder to synthesize cross-modal anchor samples offline during training; these anchors are never exposed at inference. Unlearning is measured by the absolute gap (Δ) to a gold-standard retrained model across effectiveness, reliability, and locality metrics.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Multimodal Variational Inference (MVI) decouples each input sample into modal-specific semantics U'_i (learnable encoder supervised by a modality classifier θ_M) and modal-consistent semantics K'_i (frozen pretrained encoder), trained by maximizing an ELBO that combines modality-prediction log-likelihood, cross-modal reconstruction, and a KL penalty to a Gaussian prior. Second, Contrastive Semantic Editing generates anchor samples R_j from the decoupled factors [U'_i, K'_i], then defines a new decision boundary via positive pairs (forgetting sample + anchor, pulled together) and negative pairs (cross-modal forgetting samples, pushed apart), with a gradient-based optimization that computes token-wise cosine similarity α between textual embeddings to selectively shrink the boundary—routing K'_i gradients through positive pairs to preserve reliability and U'_i gradients through negative pairs to preserve locality. The key insight is that editing fine-grained, decoupled semantic components rather than overwriting sample-level representations mimics a retrained model's decision boundary without collateral damage to unrelated unimodal or multimodal knowledge.

## Results

On the privacy-sensitive scenario (50-sample unlearning, Flickr30k+MLLMU retrieval), the proposed method achieves MI=13.98% (Δ=0.46 vs. Retrain), R@Df=16.82% (Δ=0.67), and R@Dtest=81.72% (Δ=13.91), yielding the smallest average gap (Avg.G=5.01) to the Retrain gold standard among all baselines. On the VQA+MLLMU multiple-choice task at the same scale, it records MI=13.73% (Δ=1.24) and A@Dtest=59.22% (Δ=3.92) with Avg.G=2.07. In the copyright-sensitive scenario (1K samples, Flickr30k retrieval), the method achieves MI=17.76% (Δ=0.48), R@Df=92.84% (Δ=1.73), and R@Dtest=94.47% (Δ=1.35) with Avg.G=1.19, again the closest to Retrain. Training time is 0.22 h for 50-sample unlearning, comparable to other fast methods (0.01–0.24 h) and far below Finetune (13.91 h) or Retrain (69.50 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the smallest average gap (Avg.G) to the Retrain gold standard across all six baselines and all unlearning scales in both privacy- and copyright-sensitive scenarios. The closest competitor is SLUG (single-layer unlearning gradient), which reaches Avg.G=10.30 on 50-sample retrieval versus 5.01 for the proposed method; SLUG's weakness is insufficient forgetting (R@Df gap of 13.24% vs. 0.67%). Finetune attains strong locality (R@Dtest gap only 3.60%) but catastrophically fails on reliability (R@Df gap of 32.65%), confirming that naive fine-tuning retains multimodal knowledge from forgotten samples. GA (gradient ascent) is the worst overall, with a 64.14% R@Dtest gap indicating severe collateral damage to the model's general capability. Multidelete and BadT both show high MI values (23–24% vs. Retrain's 13.5%), meaning they fail to effectively remove membership signals, while Adv achieves low MI but at the cost of poor reliability (R@Df gap 25.64%) and locality (R@Dtest gap 19.29%). In the copyright-sensitive scenario (1K samples), the proposed method narrows the gap to Retrain to just 1.19 Avg.G, compared to 4.68 for Adv and 18.90 for Multidelete, demonstrating that the causal decoupling strategy is particularly effective when samples share dense semantic overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal unlearning line initiated by Multidelete (Cheng & Amiri, 2024) and SLUG (Cai et al., 2025), which perform sample-level modality decoupling to erase cross-modal associations. It departs from these methods by introducing a Structural Causal Model that formalizes the dependency between unimodal knowledge (UK) and multimodal knowledge (MK), revealing that sample-level MK removal inadvertently corrupts UK and degrades reliability and locality. The technical machinery draws on VAE-style variational inference (Kingma & Welling, 2014) for the MVI decoupling component and contrastive learning (Chen et al., 2020) for defining the new decision boundary, but recombines them in a novel gradient-modulated editing pipeline that has no direct precedent in the unlearning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal diagnosis of why sample-level forgetting causes collateral damage to unimodal semantics provides a principled explanation that can guide future unlearning designs beyond this specific method. Practically, the near-parity with full retraining (e.g., 0.25% MI gap vs. Retrain at 5-sample scale) at roughly 1000× lower compute cost makes multimodal unlearning more feasible for GDPR-style compliance. However, the method's reliance on external pretrained generators (Stable Diffusion, BLIP-2) for MVI introduces deployment complexity and unexamined privacy risks from those anchors, and scalability to large or closed-source MLLMs remains unvalidated. Robustness against sophisticated recovery attacks and formal privacy auditing of the generative components are explicitly flagged as open questions.
