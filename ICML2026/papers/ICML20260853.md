## Problem

While first-order asymptotics (expected stopping time scaling) are well-characterized for optimal sequential mean tests, the distributional fluctuations of the stopping time remain unknown in the nonparametric bounded-support setting. The KL_inf statistic is defined through an optimization over distributions rather than as a sum of i.i.d. increments, so classical first-passage CLTs for random walks do not directly apply, and no stopping-time CLT was previously known for asymptotically optimal nonparametric power-one sequential tests.

## Input / Output

The procedure consumes a streaming sequence of i.i.d. scalar observations bounded in [0,1] along with a fixed target mean m_o ∈ (0,1) and error level α ∈ (0,1). It outputs a stopping time τ_α (the first n at which n·KL_inf(q̂_n, m_o) crosses a boundary β(n,α)), a Gaussian limit distribution N(0, σ²_bd) with explicit variance σ²_bd = Var_q(ℓ(λ*,X)) / KL_inf(q,m_o)³, and an asymptotically valid (1−γ) confidence interval for the normalized center 1/KL_inf(q,m_o) constructed from a single sample path without requiring independent replications.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step CLT. First, the paper establishes a CLT for the empirical KL_inf statistic (Theorem 4.2) by exploiting the dual representation of KL_inf as a one-dimensional convex optimization: the empirical dual maximizer λ*_n converges to the true λ* at O_p(1/√n) rate, and a Taylor expansion decomposes the statistic into a leading partial-sum term with i.i.d. increments (yielding the Gaussian limit) plus a negligible remainder whose vanishing follows from the first-order optimality conditions of the dual problem. Second, to transfer this fixed-n CLT to the random stopping time τ_α, the paper verifies an Anscombe-type uniform continuity-in-probability condition (Lemma A.9) that overcomes the absence of i.i.d. increments, then applies a delta-method boundary-crossing inversion to convert fluctuations of the accumulated statistic into fluctuations of the crossing time, yielding the explicit Gaussian limit for √(log(1/α))·(τ_α/log(1/α) − 1/KL_inf(q,m_o)) (Theorem 4.4). A plug-in variance estimator evaluated along the same sample path enables single-run confidence interval construction (Proposition 4.5).

## Results

The paper establishes two main CLTs: (i) √n(KL_inf(q̂_n, m_o) − KL_inf(q, m_o)) → N(0, σ²(q, m_o)) where σ²(q, m_o) = Var_q(ℓ(λ*, X)), and (ii) √(log(1/α))(τ_α/log(1/α) − 1/KL_inf(q, m_o)) → N(0, σ²_bd(q, m_o)) where σ²_bd(q, m_o) = Var_q(ℓ(λ*, X)) / (KL_inf(q, m_o))³. Numerical validation on Bernoulli(0.6) with m_o = 0.2 shows the Gaussian approximation improves markedly as α decreases from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁸. The single-path confidence intervals from Proposition 4.5 yield [2.0, 41.0] and [11.9, 49.0] for α = 10⁻⁴ and α = 10⁻⁸ respectively, compared to empirical 95% CIs of [6.0, 50.0] and [22.0, 84.0] from 5000 independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no competing methods to benchmark against; the 'comparison' is between the predicted Gaussian limit and the empirical distribution of the stopping time across simulated runs. The simulations confirm that the CLT prediction (Theorem 4.4) matches the empirical histogram well for small α (10⁻⁸) with the practical boundary β(n,α) = log(1/α), while for α = 10⁻⁴ with the theoretically-supported boundary β(n,α) = 1 + log(2(1+n)/α), the distribution is noticeably right-skewed due to finite-sample effects. On the real-world DSSAT crop-yield dataset (m_o = 0.5, α = 10⁻⁴, 3000 bootstrap paths), the empirical distribution of τ_α/log(1/α) closely matches the N(1/KL_inf(q̂, m_o), σ̂²_bd/log(1/α)) overlay, providing empirical support in a non-synthetic setting. The single-path confidence interval from Proposition 4.5 is notably tighter than the multi-run empirical interval, demonstrating the practical utility of the CLT for predictability guarantees without requiring independent replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the first-order asymptotic optimality results of Agrawal & Ramdas (2025) for KL_inf-based power-one sequential tests by establishing a second-order, distributional characterization (CLT) of the stopping time. The KL_inf statistic and its one-dimensional dual representation are inherited from Honda & Takemura (2010), and the α-correct power-one framework follows Darling & Robbins (1967). The key departure from prior work is that classical first-passage CLTs for random walks (Gut, 2009; Asmussen, 2003) do not apply here because the KL_inf statistic is defined through a distributional optimization rather than additive i.i.d. increments, necessitating a novel Taylor-expansion-based argument to verify Anscombe's condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first stopping-time CLT for an asymptotically optimal nonparametric power-one sequential test, providing explicit Gaussian fluctuation bounds and a single-run confidence interval for the normalized stopping time—useful for predictability guarantees in safety-critical monitoring. A key limitation is the bounded-support assumption; extending to unbounded or heavy-tailed distributions requires new ideas. The complementary asymptotic regime (KL_inf → 0 at fixed α) remains largely unresolved in the nonparametric setting, and the authors note that moderate/large-deviation analyses of the KL_inf process could illuminate heavy regret tails in bandit algorithms.
