## Problem

Prior theoretical results showed that gradient-based deep learning algorithms can only provably learn models expressible by log-depth circuits, far short of the polynomial-depth computation that real deep networks perform. This paper closes that gap by proving that layerwise SGD on residual networks can efficiently learn hierarchical models that require polynomial depth to express, reaching the depth limit of efficient learnability.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a multi-label supervised setting where each input x ∈ [-1,1]^d is accompanied by the complete set of its positive labels (not just one), and the goal is to learn a predictor f̂: X → R^n. The labels are assumed to follow an unknown hierarchy L₁ ⊆ L₂ ⊆ … ⊆ L_r = [n], where first-level labels are polynomial threshold functions (PTFs) of the input and higher-level labels are PTFs of lower-level labels. An ensemble extension generalizes this to spatial locations G with a proximity mapping, so each label at location g depends only on nearby locations.

## Method

The architecture is a D-layer residual network where each block computes Ψ_k(x) = x + W₂^k σ(W₁^k E(x) + b^k), with W₂^k initialized to zero and (W₁^k, b^k) drawn from a β-Xavier distribution. Training proceeds layerwise: at step k, only the readout W₂^k is optimized (via SGD on a strongly-convex objective) while all other layers are frozen, making each step equivalent to learning a linear classifier on top of a random-features embedding σ(W₁^k E(x) + b^k). The key technical insight is that the Hermite polynomial expansion of σ ensures the random-features kernel can approximate any degree-K polynomial, so the PTF defining the next hierarchy level lies in the RKHS of the block with bounded norm. A robustness lemma guarantees that approximation errors from previously learned levels do not break the PTF structure, enabling an inductive argument: once level i−1 is learned to sufficient accuracy, O(B + 1/ξ) additional layers suffice to learn level i. The proof uses a custom margin-based loss ℓ = ℓ_{1/(2B)} + (1/(4m|G|))ℓ_{1−ξ/2} that simultaneously enforces correct classification and a quantitative margin, yielding a final error bound of Õ(D²(M+1)⁴(wn)^{2K+1} / (γ^{4+2K} m)).

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.2) proves that layerwise SGD on a residual network of depth D > r·(⌈ln(8m|G|/ξ)/γ⌉+1) and width q = Õ((M+1)⁴(wn)^(2K)/γ^(4+2K)) learns any (r,K,M,B,ξ)-hierarchical labeling function with expected error Õ(D²(M+1)⁴(wn)^(2K+1)/(γ^(4+2K)m)), where γ = (1/32)·min(1/B, ξ). Crucially, the depth r is arbitrary (polynomial in n), surpassing all prior theoretical results that were limited to log-depth circuits. The 'Brain Dump' hierarchy (Theorem 3.4) further shows that q = Õ(k²d·log|X|) random majority labels from a depth-r circuit suffice to guarantee an (r, K, O(kd^K), 2k+1)-hierarchy with high probability, establishing that granular human-provided labels naturally induce learnable structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons. The comparison is entirely in terms of what function classes are provably learnable by gradient-based algorithms. Prior work (Abbe et al. 2021, Allen-Zhu & Li 2020, Daniely et al. 2024, and most others cited) could only establish learnability for models realizable by log-depth (or even depth-2) circuits. This paper extends the provably learnable class to models requiring polynomial depth, reaching the depth limit of efficient learnability: for any polynomial-sized circuit, a corresponding hierarchical model is constructible and learnable by SGD on a ResNet. The 'Brain Dump' result (Theorem 3.4) provides a formal justification for why hierarchical structure should exist in practice, modeling human teachers as circuits whose random majority projections yield low-complexity hierarchies. No method is shown to 'win' or 'lose' in an empirical sense; the contribution is strictly an expansion of the theoretical landscape of what deep learning algorithms can provably learn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on provable learnability of hierarchical models by SGD on neural networks (Abbe et al. 2021; Allen-Zhu & Li 2020; Daniely et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025), which were previously limited to models realizable by logarithmic-depth circuits. The key advance is reaching polynomial depth—the depth limit of efficient learnability—by combining layerwise SGD on residual networks with a random-features/Hermite-polynomial analysis of each block. The proof technique builds directly on the conjugate-kernel SGD convergence framework (Daniely 2017) and the random features approximation machinery (Rahimi & Recht 2007), applied iteratively across hierarchy levels with a robustness argument for polynomial threshold functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable result showing that a standard deep learning algorithm (layerwise SGD on a ResNet) can learn models requiring polynomial depth to express, closing a gap between what is theoretically learnable and what was previously provable. The 'brain dump' hierarchy (Theorem 3.4) offers a formal mechanism for why granular human labels make complex functions tractable, lending theoretical support to the intuition behind modern LLM training on internet-scale data. However, the result relies on strong assumptions—layerwise training, full multi-label supervision, an orthogonal output matrix, and a non-standard loss—that limit immediate practical applicability. Key open questions include whether joint (end-to-end) training preserves the guarantee, whether single-label supervision suffices, and whether the hierarchical structure can be empirically identified in real datasets.
