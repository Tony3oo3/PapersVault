## Problem

Diffusion-based generative image codecs achieve strong perceptual quality but rely on billion-parameter foundation models, incurring decoding latencies exceeding 10 seconds and failing to meet real-time requirements. The paper investigates whether perceptual quality and real-time speed can be reconciled by asking: does diffusion pre-training benefit lightweight codecs, and are transformers (with their O(N²) attention) essential for compression-oriented diffusion?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261897/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image as input and produces a compressed bitstream via a 4-bit vector-quantized codebook with fixed-length coding, covering a wide bitrate range from 0.0039 to 0.5 bpp. The encoder applies 16× spatial downsampling, and the decoder reconstructs the image directly in pixel space. At 1080p (1024×1920) on an A100 GPU, encoding runs at 60 FPS (16 ms) and decoding at 42 FPS (24 ms), with the full pipeline using a compact 28M encoder and 52M decoder.

## Method

The codec is built on two empirical findings: (1) compression-oriented pre-training (CoD) with high-entropy conditions (~1024 bits) substantially outperforms generation-oriented pre-training for lightweight models, and (2) attention in CoD collapses to local neighborhoods because compressed conditions already encode global structure, making depth-wise convolutions sufficient. The architecture replaces DiT self-attention with depth-wise convolution blocks augmented by channel attention, removes redundant AdaLN-Zero in the one-step setting, and adopts a DeCo-style decoupled pixel head. Training is two-stage: Stage I pre-trains the diffusion prior via flow matching at 0.0039 bpp; Stage II fine-tunes as a one-step deterministic generator using DMD distillation (with a 700M DiT-based CoD as teacher) plus a projected GAN loss (DINOv2 feature pyramids), L1, LPIPS, and codebook commitment losses.

## Results

On Kodak at ~0.03 bpp, the proposed CoD-Lite codec achieves an FID of 31.5 with a compact 28M encoder and 52M decoder, delivering 16 ms encoding and 24 ms decoding (≈60 FPS / 42 FPS) at 1080p on an A100 GPU. This corresponds to an 85% BD-rate reduction on FID relative to MS-ILLM while maintaining comparable perceptual quality. The codec also supports a wide bitrate range from 0.0039 to 0.5 bpp, outperforming GAN-based and multi-step diffusion codecs across most metrics on both Kodak and CLIC2020.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN-based MS-ILLM, CoD-Lite achieves an 85% BD-rate reduction on FID at comparable perceptual quality, while also decoding 12.6× faster (24 ms vs. 302 ms). Compared to one-step latent diffusion codecs, CoD-Lite is dramatically faster: 45× faster than StableCodec (24 ms vs. 1095 ms), 26× faster than OneDC (24 ms vs. 637 ms), and 21× faster than One-Step CoD (24 ms vs. 517 ms), while using far fewer decoder parameters (52M vs. 985M, 1058M, 617M respectively). Against multi-step latent diffusion methods, the speedup is extreme: 1439× faster decoding than PerCo (SD) and 80,000× faster encoding than DiffC (SD). On CLIC2020 at very high resolutions, CoD-Lite surpasses most baselines and rivals state-of-the-art one-step methods, though it shows a slight DISTS drop attributed to the 512×512 training resolution limit. Notably, CoD-Lite also outperforms all diffusion-based baselines at lower resolutions (128×128, 256×256 Kodak), where other methods degrade significantly, demonstrating robust generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CoD's compression-oriented diffusion pre-training framework and DeCo's pixel-space diffusion architecture, but departs from the prevailing scaling-up paradigm by demonstrating that lightweight convolutional backbones can replace Diffusion Transformers for compression. It repurposes DMD distillation (originally for text-to-image) to bridge the quality gap between convolutional and DiT decoders, and introduces a key conceptual shift: generation-oriented pre-training (unconditional or class-conditioned) is less effective than compression-oriented pre-training at small model scales due to a difficulty-capacity mismatch. The work positions itself against one-step diffusion codecs like StableCodec and OneDC, which achieve comparable quality but at 20×+ slower decoding speeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based image codec to achieve real-time 1080p decoding (42 FPS), bridging a critical gap between generative compression quality and practical deployment latency. The finding that global attention is largely redundant in compression-oriented diffusion (19/26 layers attend locally) challenges the assumption that DiT is essential for diffusion-based compression and opens the door to further efficiency gains. Key limitations include training capped at 512×512 resolution (causing performance drops at 4K) and the reliance on a 700M DiT teacher for distillation, which limits the approach's applicability in resource-constrained training settings. Open questions include whether these insights transfer to video compression and whether the convolutional backbone can be further compressed without quality loss.
