## Problem

Existing DocVQA evidence localization methods adopt a one-step paradigm that directly maps from the entire page to evidence regions, lacking any progressive coarse-to-fine refinement. When page semantics are densely distributed or evidence regions occupy only a small fraction of the page, this leads to ambiguous boundaries, localization drift, and missed critical evidence, undermining both interpretability and answer reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a document page image (encoded by a frozen visual encoder) and a question string (encoded by a frozen text encoder) as input, and produces a generated answer text together with a set of evidence region bounding boxes on the page. The evidence localization is internally represented as a probability density over the page space that evolves through T discrete gradient-flow steps; the final distribution's support defines the localized regions, while the answer is decoded autoregressively conditioned on the final evolved density and the question embedding.

## Method

The paper reformulates precise evidence localization as a Wasserstein gradient flow over probability distributions, where a candidate evidence distribution evolves from a uniform initial state toward a target concentrated on true evidence regions, governed by an energy functional encoding semantic sufficiency (potential term), spatial minimality (entropy term), and target-distribution alignment (transport term). The intractable continuous flow is discretized via the JKO scheme, yielding an end-to-end trainable loss with differentiable Sinkhorn-approximated Wasserstein distances that can be directly optimized by neural networks. At each of T evolution steps, a semantic density updater decomposes region semantics into an intrinsic component (self-attention over candidate regions) and a conditional component (cross-attention with the question), enabling question-guided progressive refinement from coarse to fine. A multi-expert collaborative decoder with Top-K gating then generates the answer by adaptively selecting and fusing the most relevant expert representations conditioned on the final evolved evidence density.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best performance across all metrics on all three benchmarks. On DocVQA, it reaches 43.2% EM, 55.8% F1, 62.7% ANLS, and 79.6% mAP@IoU, outperforming the strongest baseline DocVXQA (40.5/52.6/59.8/72.1) by 2.7/3.2/2.9/7.5 points respectively. The gains are most pronounced on NiM-Benchmark (where evidence regions occupy <5% of the page), with mAP@IoU improving from 46.5% (DocVXQA) to 53.4% (+6.9 points). On DUDE, the method achieves 42.1% EM, 57.6% F1, 66.5% ANLS, and 62.8% mAP@IoU, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning attention-prior (GAP), multimodal LLM (DLaVA), embedding-similarity (EaGERS), agentic retrieval (ARIAL), and learnable-heatmap (DocVXQA) approaches. The proposed method wins on every metric across all three benchmarks, with the largest margins over DocVXQA (the strongest baseline): +7.5 mAP@IoU on DocVQA, +6.9 on NiM-Benchmark, and +5.4 on DUDE. The advantage is most pronounced under strict IoU thresholds (e.g., on NiM-Benchmark at IoU≥0.9, the method achieves 26.4% AP vs. 21.7% for DocVXQA), confirming that the progressive evolution mechanism improves boundary precision rather than merely coarse region matching. GAP and EaGERS, which rely on static similarity or voting without iterative refinement, trail the proposed method by 20+ mAP@IoU points on NiM-Benchmark, highlighting the difficulty of one-step localization for small evidence regions. DLaVA and ARIAL occupy the middle range, with DLaVA slightly ahead of ARIAL on DocVQA but behind on NiM-Benchmark, suggesting that multimodal LLM-based localization degrades more sharply when evidence occupies a tiny fraction of the page.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the mathematical theory of Wasserstein gradient flows (Jordan et al., 1998; Ambrosio et al., 2005) and practical document VQA evidence localization (EaGERS, DocVXQA, ARIAL, DLaVA, GAP). All prior localization methods adopt a one-step mapping from the full page to evidence regions, whereas this work reformulates the problem as a progressive optimal transport problem over probability distributions, discretized via the JKO scheme and made end-to-end trainable through a derived loss function. The semantic density updater, which decomposes region relevance into intrinsic and question-conditional components, draws on the information-theoretic chain rule to justify the two-branch attention design. The multi-expert Top-K gated decoder is a practical component that has no direct analogue in the prior DocVQA localization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for progressive evidence localization with explicit convergence and error-bound guarantees, which is absent from prior heuristic or attention-based approaches. The consistent gains across three benchmarks—particularly the large relative improvement on NiM-Benchmark where evidence regions occupy <5% of the page—suggest the progressive transport mechanism genuinely addresses the boundary-ambiguity failure mode of one-step methods. However, the method's complexity (T=6 evolution steps, multiple attention modules per step, Sinkhorn-based Wasserstein approximations) raises practical adoption questions, and the theoretical guarantees rely on displacement convexity assumptions that may only hold locally in real document settings. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-page documents, whether the number of evolution steps can be reduced without sacrificing the coarse-to-fine benefit, and whether the theoretical error bounds translate to meaningful improvements under stronger neural approximations.
