## Problem

Wasserstein VI (WVI) in Bures-Wasserstein space previously enjoyed strictly better non-asymptotic convergence guarantees than black-box VI (BBVI) in parameter space, suggesting the measure-space geometry was inherently superior. This paper shows the gap is not geometric but stems from the specific gradient estimator (Price's gradient, which uses Hessians) that WVI happened to employ, and closes the gap by proving identical state-of-the-art iteration complexities for both algorithms when the same estimator is used.

## Input / Output

The algorithms require access to the potential U = -log π̃ and, for Price's gradient, its Hessian ∇²U (or an unbiased estimator thereof); the reparametrization gradient variant needs only ∇U. The variational family is restricted to full-rank Gaussians parametrized via a Cholesky factor C ∈ L_{≻0}^d, so the output is a pair (m, C) defining q = Normal(m, CCᵀ). Convergence is measured in expected squared Wasserstein-2 distance μ·E[W₂(q_T, q_*)²] ≤ ε, and the free energy F(q) is estimated with 2¹² Monte Carlo samples for evaluation.

## Method

The key insight is that under the Cholesky parametrization, the Bures-Wasserstein gradient ∇_BW E and the parameter gradient ∇_λ E are related by a coordinate transformation, making the Bonnet-Price estimator (∇U(Z) for location, Cᵀ∇²U(Z) for scale) interchangeable between SPBWGD and SPGD. The authors establish matching iteration complexity O(dκ/ε + √d·κ^{3/2}·log(κΔ²)/√ε + κ²·log(Δ²/ε)) for both algorithms by proving gradient variance bounds in terms of the Bregman divergence D_U rather than W₂², which avoids an extra κ factor, combined with a generalized coercivity lemma that controls the Bregman growth term. They also demonstrate that WVI can be made more widely applicable by substituting the reparametrization gradient (requiring only ∇U) for Price's gradient, and empirically confirm on 12 PosteriorDB benchmarks that the Hessian-based estimator is the dominant source of performance improvement over the reparametrization gradient.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result (Table 1) shows that both SPGD and SPBWGD with the Bonnet-Price gradient estimator achieve an identical iteration complexity of O(dκ/ε + √d·κ^{3/2}·log(κΔ²)/√ε + κ²·log(Δ²/ε)) to reach μ·E[W₂(q_T, q_*)²] ≤ ε, closing the previously existing gap between the two algorithms. This improves over the prior SPBWGD bound of O(dκ/ε·log(1/ε) + κ³·log(Δ²/ε)) (Diao et al., 2023) and the prior SPGD bound of O(dκ²·tr(μΣ_*)/ε) (Kim et al., 2023; Domke et al., 2023). Empirically, across 12 PosteriorDB benchmark problems (dimensions 3–237), SPGD and SPBWGD with Price's gradient achieve comparable variational free energy, while both substantially outperform their reparametrization-gradient counterparts, which fail to converge on the Rats problem (d=65) for all step sizes between 10⁻⁸ and 10⁰.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diao et al. (2023) SPBWGD, the paper improves the maximum step size from 1/(Lκ²) to 1/(Lκ) (a factor of κ larger) and reduces the non-asymptotic κ-dependence from κ³ to κ²·log(Δ²/ε), while also removing the log(1/ε) factor from the leading dκ/ε term. Against Domke et al. (2023) and Kim et al. (2023) SPGD with the reparametrization gradient, the paper improves the asymptotic complexity from O(dκ²·tr(μΣ_*)/ε) to O(dκ/ε + √d·κ^{3/2}/√ε + κ²·log(1/ε)), a factor of κ·tr(μΣ_*) improvement in the leading term. Empirically, SPGD and SPBWGD with Price's gradient perform nearly identically across all 12 benchmark problems, confirming the theoretical equivalence. In contrast, both algorithms with the reparametrization gradient require significantly smaller step sizes and fail to converge on the Rats problem (d=65) for any step size tested. On some problems (e.g., Rats with Price gradient), SPBWGD requires step sizes orders of magnitude smaller than SPGD to avoid divergence, likely due to the extra C^{-⊤} scaling in the ∇_Σ estimator versus the ∇_C estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of variational inference: Wasserstein VI (SPBWGD) operating in Bures-Wasserstein space (Diao et al., 2023; Lambert et al., 2022) and black-box VI (SPGD) operating in Euclidean parameter space (Domke et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is showing that the previously observed convergence gap between these two approaches was entirely attributable to the gradient estimator (Price's Hessian-based estimator vs. the reparametrization gradient) rather than to the underlying geometry. By leveraging Stein's identity and Price's theorem, the paper demonstrates that both algorithms can use either estimator, yielding identical state-of-the-art iteration complexities when the same estimator is employed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result resolves a long-standing question in the VI community about whether measure-space optimization (using Wasserstein geometry) provides inherent advantages over parameter-space optimization, concluding that the gradient estimator is the dominant factor. Practically, it means BBVI can match WVI's theoretical guarantees by adopting Price's gradient (at the cost of requiring Hessians, O(d³) per step), while WVI can be made more widely applicable by using the reparametrization gradient (only O(d²)). Open questions remain about natural gradient VI with the Fisher metric, which may offer genuine geometric advantages, and about whether the theoretical equivalence extends to non-Gaussian variational families or to lower-bound matching.
