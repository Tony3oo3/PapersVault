## Problem

Neural operators for PDE solution and scientific super-resolution rely on a single forward pass, which systematically under-resolves high-frequency details (spectral bias). Existing mitigations require retraining, increased model capacity, or architectural changes, coupling accuracy gains to training-time scaling. This paper asks whether a lightweight, architecture-agnostic refinement module can be applied iteratively at inference time to progressively correct residual error without touching the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262897/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The refinement operator Φ_θ receives the concatenation of the original input field x (e.g., 4 consecutive PDE frames at 384×128 or 256×256, or a 45×90 low-resolution climate field) and the current iterate h_k (the base operator's prediction or a previously refined estimate), producing a residual correction of the same spatial shape as the solution. The base operator is frozen and provides h_0; IRNO then applies K shared-weight refinement steps (K=4 or 6 during training, extrapolated up to 2K at inference). The framework is grid-agnostic, operating on structured grids (FNO, TFNO, WDSR) and irregular unstructured meshes (RIGNO on 16,384-node graphs).

## Method

IRNO decomposes inference into a coarse initialization from a frozen base operator followed by successive residual corrections via the fixed-point iteration h_{k+1} = h_k + α·Φ_θ(x, h_k), where Φ_θ is a lightweight U-Net with circular padding and Xavier-0.1 initialization. Training uses a composite objective: (i) multi-step spatial supervision averaging L² error over all K unrolled steps to enforce trajectory stability, (ii) a progressive spectral loss whose frequency exponent λ_k increases linearly from 1.0 to 2.0 across refinement steps, pivoting emphasis from coarse to fine scales, and (iii) a fixed-point regularization ‖Φ_θ(x, y)‖² that drives the learned bias toward zero. A contraction-mapping analysis in function space establishes quadratic-linear convergence with a bias-controlled error floor, and the iteration-invariant update rule enables stable extrapolation beyond the training horizon and zero-shot transfer across different base operator architectures.

## Results

IRNO achieves consistent error reduction across all four physical systems: 45.32–56.05% VRMSE reduction on TR-2D, 50.73–80.46% on Active Matter, and 18.56% ACC improvement (34.09% RFNE reduction) on ERA5 with FNO base. On ERA5 16× super-resolution, IRNO(WDSR) attains ACC 0.9104 and RFNE 0.1953, outperforming both HiNOTE (ACC 0.9055, RFNE 0.2222) and ResUNet-HFS (ACC 0.8915, RFNE 0.2253). On the CE-Gauss irregular mesh, IRNO reduces relative L² error by 12.5–21.3% over a 7-step autoregressive rollout with RIGNO as base. Spectral analysis on Active Matter shows normalized error ratios dropping to 27.72–36.10% (low-freq), 5.07–6.68% (mid-freq), and 1.48–2.04% (high-freq) at the final refinement step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRNO is evaluated against its own base operators (FNO, TFNO, WDSR, RIGNO) as single-pass baselines, consistently outperforming them: the largest gains appear on TFNO-based Active Matter (80.46% VRMSE reduction) and TR-2D (56.05%), while WDSR on ERA5 shows only marginal improvement (0.14% ACC) due to its already strong super-resolution performance. Against dedicated spectral-bias-mitigation methods on ERA5 16×, IRNO(WDSR) achieves the best ACC (0.9104) and RFNE (0.1953), beating HiNOTE (0.9055 / 0.2222) by 0.5% ACC and 11.7% RFNE, and ResUNet-HFS (0.8915 / 0.2253) by 2.1% ACC and 13.3% RFNE. On the CE-Gauss irregular mesh, IRNO(RIGNO) reduces relative L² error from 16.617% to 13.080% at t=7 in autoregressive rollout, a 21.3% relative improvement over the RIGNO base. Cross-operator transfer experiments show IRNO trained on TFNO improves FNO's TR-2D VRMSE by 58.53%, exceeding the same-operator FNO+IRNO_FNO configuration (45.32%), suggesting the refinement dynamics generalize across architectures. The paper also notes IRNO is complementary to HFS: combining IRNO with HFS on Active Matter further reduces VRMSE from 0.0631 to 0.0486.

## Relation to Prior Work

IRNO repurposes the classical fixed-point iteration paradigm from numerical analysis as a learned inference-time mechanism for neural operators, departing from the dominant single-pass paradigm of FNO, DeepONet, and TFNO. While prior spectral-bias mitigations (HFS, HiNOTE, SpecBoost, F-Adapter) modify architectures or training objectives to recover high-frequency content within a single forward pass, IRNO instead introduces a lightweight, architecture-agnostic refinement module applied iteratively, drawing conceptual parallels to residual-based solvers and multigrid methods. The connection to implicit layers (DEQs, Neural ODEs) is acknowledged but distinguished: IRNO preserves an explicit, frozen base operator and learns only the residual update dynamics, retaining modularity. The progressive spectral loss and fixed-point regularization are new training objectives specifically designed to enforce the contraction conditions that the theoretical analysis requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in, architecture-agnostic design means IRNO can improve any pre-trained neural operator without retraining the base model, and the demonstrated cross-operator transferability (e.g., IRNO trained on TFNO improving FNO predictions by 58.53%) suggests a reusable refinement component across the operator ecosystem. The contraction-based analysis provides the first formal convergence guarantees for a learned iterative refinement scheme in function space, linking the empirical error plateau to a quantifiable bias term. Key open questions include extending the local-neighborhood convergence analysis to global behavior, developing adaptive step-size or stopping criteria, and validating cross-resolution and distributional generalization. The absence of uncertainty quantification and the fixed step-size choice limit deployment in safety-critical scientific workflows.
