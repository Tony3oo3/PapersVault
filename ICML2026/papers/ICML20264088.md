## Problem

Multimodal spatial reasoning frameworks like MVoT generate and append dense visual tokens (O(10³) per hop) to the autoregressive context, causing monotonic growth in sequence length, KV-cache memory, and attention cost that quickly exhausts the context window during long-horizon inference. Existing mitigations—token pruning and continuous latent reasoning—either discard global spatial structure needed across hops or lack explicit supervision and controllability for complex spatial tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial visual observation (image or map, encoded via VAE at 336×336 resolution) and a textual instruction/query as input. At each reasoning hop, it produces an autoregressive textual thought and a fixed-size visual workspace represented as block cosine coefficients (8×8 blocks by default) that is overwritten rather than appended, so the context grows only with the textual trace. The final output is a spatial answer or concatenated action plan (e.g., a sequence of L/R/U/D moves), emitted in a machine-parseable format.

## Method

SpecFlow replaces pixel-space visual thought generation with deterministic flow matching performed directly in a discrete cosine coefficient space, where a time-dependent spectral mask M(t) progressively activates frequency bands from low to high, creating a coarse-to-fine curriculum that first establishes global layout before refining spatial detail. A 20B MMDiT velocity field (initialized from Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) is trained with a standard flow-matching objective (linear interpolation in cosine space, MSE velocity loss) plus condition dropout for classifier-free guidance, which steers the visual workspace update using the accumulated textual trace as a control signal without expanding the context. The visual workspace is a fixed-size state overwritten at each hop via a 5-step Euler ODE solver, so memory and computation remain bounded regardless of reasoning depth. The autoregressive text module (Qwen3-VL-8B, LoRA rank 32) conditions on the current visual state to produce the next textual thought, closing a text→visual→text loop per hop. A theoretical analysis shows that restricting dynamics to low-frequency cosine subspaces cannot increase the Lipschitz constant of the velocity field, permitting larger solver steps and fewer integration iterations.

## Results

On four compositional spatial reasoning benchmarks, SpecFlow achieves the highest accuracy on all four: 70.14% on VSR, 61.28% on V-Star, 67.79% on EmbSpatial, and 70.47% on Winoground, while maintaining the lowest or near-lowest memory footprint (39.5–46.9 GB) among top-accuracy methods. On spatial decision-making tasks (Maze, MiniBehavior, FrozenLake), SpecFlow attains a 79.15% average success rate, outperforming MVoT (74.10%) and DiffThinker (75.59%), with the largest gap at larger grid sizes (e.g., Maze-16: 64.85% vs. 57.81% for MVoT). KV-cache memory is reduced by 1.6×–2.1× relative to token-accumulating baselines, and latency stays within 0.41–0.49 s per sample on compositional tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecFlow outperforms all ten baselines on every compositional benchmark. Against the strongest token-accumulating baseline VoCoT, SpecFlow gains +1.26 on VSR, +1.41 on V-Star, +8.03 on EmbSpatial, and +0.38 on Winoground, while using roughly 30% less memory (42.6 vs. 59.9 GB average). Among lightweight latent-reasoning methods, SpecFlow leads Heima by +18.45 on VSR and +19.58 on V-Star, and surpasses PCCoT by +15.43 on V-Star and +11.66 on EmbSpatial, confirming that explicit spectral structure in cosine space is more effective than unstructured continuous latents for spatial reasoning. Against CODI (self-distilled continuous CoT), SpecFlow wins on all four benchmarks, most notably on V-Star (+12.85) and EmbSpatial (+12.05). On spatial decision-making, SpecFlow (79.15% avg) beats MVoT (74.10%) by +5.05 and DiffThinker (75.59%) by +3.56, with the advantage widening at larger grid sizes where context-length pressure is greatest; it also exceeds the much larger Qwen3-VL-32B SFT baseline (63.27%) by +15.88, demonstrating that the spectral flow paradigm compensates for the smaller AR backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecFlow departs from the MVoT/VoCoT paradigm of autoregressively generating and appending dense visual tokens to the context, which causes monotonic KV-cache growth. It also moves beyond heuristic token-pruning methods (FastV, SparseVLM) and unsupervised latent-reasoning approaches (Heima, CODI, PCCoT) by formulating intermediate visual thoughts as a fixed-size, frequency-limited state in the discrete cosine domain, updated via deterministic flow matching with classifier-free guidance. The core conceptual shift is treating the visual workspace as an overwritten spectral buffer rather than an accumulating token sequence, with progressive low-to-high frequency activation providing a principled coarse-to-fine curriculum grounded in the energy compaction property of the DCT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling memory and computation from reasoning depth, SpecFlow makes long-horizon multimodal spatial reasoning practically feasible under tight latency and memory budgets, which is critical for embodied and interactive applications. The compound spectral+VAE ablation (4.9× speedup over VAE-only at higher accuracy) suggests that frequency-aware compute allocation is a broadly useful principle beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include whether the cosine domain is truly optimal versus other learned or structured transforms, whether the fixed 5-step Euler solver generalizes to higher-fidelity visual thoughts, and whether the approach extends to 3D spatial reasoning or video-based temporal reasoning where the spectral budget must also account for temporal frequencies.
