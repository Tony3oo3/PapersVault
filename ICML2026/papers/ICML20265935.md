## Problem

Existing VLM-based driving world models rely on autoregressive codebook-based image reconstruction that prioritizes texture over semantics, predict only short-term (≈0.5 s) future observations insufficient for safe trajectory planning, and focus on forward-view prediction while neglecting surrounding agents. These limitations hinder long-horizon spatial reasoning and robust decision-making in complex, interactive driving scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265935/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes N surround-view RGB images at the current timestep, 4 historical frames, ego-vehicle state, a target point, and a set of 5 learnable BEV world queries (each h_bev × w_bev × d_bev). It jointly produces 5 future BEV latent feature maps (one per 0.5 s step, covering 2 s), an adaptive chain-of-thought text (or a placeholder token when reasoning is unnecessary), and a sequence of (x, y) trajectory waypoints quantized into discrete grid-cell token indices for cross-entropy supervision. All three outputs are generated in a single unified forward pass without any external generative model.

## Method

DeepSight builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and introduces a driving world model that predicts DINOv3-ViT-L/16-extracted latent semantic features for 5 consecutive future frames in BEV space via parallel decoding with learnable world queries, enabling multi-frame prediction in one forward pass rather than autoregressive token-by-token image generation. An adaptive Chain-of-Thought module conditionally activates the LLM's social-commonsense and logical-reasoning capabilities only when scene complexity warrants it (triggered in <30% of frames), injecting external knowledge for long-tail cases such as emergency-vehicle yielding or OOD construction zones. Training minimizes a composite loss combining cross-entropy over quantized trajectory tokens, cross-entropy over CoT text tokens, and MSE between predicted and DINOv3 ground-truth BEV features, with the vision encoder frozen and the LLM fully fine-tuned. The key architectural insight is replacing pixel-level or codebook-based reconstruction with semantic latent feature prediction in a spatially complete BEV representation, which simultaneously captures agent motion dynamics and environmental structure at a fraction of the inference cost of autoregressive image generation (+3.57% latency vs. +60.71% for FSDrive).

## Results

On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark (220 routes, Think2Drive expert protocol), DeepSight achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 86.23 and Success Rate (SR) of 71.36%, surpassing the previous SOTA AutoVLA (DS 78.84, SR 57.73%) by +7.39 DS and +13.63% SR. Even without the adaptive CoT module, the world-modeling component alone yields DS 84.52 and SR 65.91%, already exceeding AutoVLA. On the open-loop nuScenes dataset, DeepSight achieves the best average L2 error of 0.33 m and lowest collision rate of 0.12%, outperforming FSDrive (0.35 m, 0.14%) and World4Drive (0.50 m, 0.16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepSight outperforms all Think2Drive-protocol baselines on both DS and SR. Against the closest SOTA, AutoVLA (DS 78.84, SR 57.73%), DeepSight gains +7.39 DS and +13.63% SR; against ORION (DS 77.74, SR 54.62%), the margin is +8.49 DS and +16.74% SR. Among E2E (non-VLM) methods, the best is DriveTrans (DS 63.46, SR 35.01%), which DeepSight exceeds by +22.77 DS and +36.35% SR. Against SimLingo (DS 85.94, SR 66.82%), which uses a different expert distribution (PDM-Lite), DeepSight still leads by +0.29 DS and +4.54% SR while achieving higher efficiency (201.71 vs. 244.18 is SimLingo's advantage, but DeepSight's comfort is comparable). On nuScenes open-loop, DeepSight achieves the best average L2 (0.33 m) and collision rate (0.12%), beating FSDrive (0.35 m, 0.14%) and World4Drive (0.50 m, 0.16%). In inference efficiency, DeepSight adds only 3.57% latency for world modeling and 4.12% for adaptive CoT, versus +60.71% for FSDrive's autoregressive image generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of VLM-based end-to-end driving (EMMA, SimLingo, ORION, AutoVLA) and driving world models (FSDrive, ReasonPlan, HERMES), but departs from both in a key way: rather than autoregressively generating pixel-level or codebook-token future frames, it predicts DINOv3 latent semantic features for five consecutive future BEV frames in a single parallel forward pass. The adaptive CoT mechanism extends the always-on textual reasoning of SimLingo and ORION by conditionally activating the language model's social-knowledge capabilities only when scene complexity warrants it, reducing inference overhead. The base architecture (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and the unified generative-understanding framing follow the broader MLLM-for-driving trend, but the BEV latent prediction objective and the parallel multi-frame world query design are the paper's core architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency gap: DeepSight adds only 3.57% inference latency for world modeling versus 60.71% for FSDrive's autoregressive image generation, making long-horizon world modeling potentially deployable in real-time driving stacks. The finding that latent semantic BEV prediction alone (without CoT) already surpasses the prior SOTA by +5.68 DS suggests that the representation choice (DINOv3 features over codebook tokens) is the dominant factor, which could redirect future work away from pixel-level reconstruction. However, all results are confined to the CARLA-based Bench2Drive simulation, and the authors acknowledge that scaling VLMs to real-time driving remains an open challenge; generalization to real-world sensor noise, diverse geographies, and edge-case safety certification are unaddressed.
