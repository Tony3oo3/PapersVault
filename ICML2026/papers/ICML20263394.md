## Problem

Covariance alignment under the exact Bures-Wasserstein (BW) objective is central to domain adaptation and Gaussian embedding pipelines, but no existing method provides a cheap, closed-form update for a low-rank covariance factor that is guaranteed to decrease the BW objective at every step. Gradient-based and Riemannian BW optimizers require repeated dense matrix square roots and inverses, while methods based on other SPD geometries (Euclidean, log-Euclidean, AIRM) or entropic sample OT (Sinkhorn) optimize different objectives and do not descend on the exact Gaussian Wasserstein energy.

## Input / Output

ITSPACE takes a full-rank target covariance Σ⋆ ∈ S++^d, an initial square-root factor Y₀ ∈ R^{d×r} (r ≪ d, e.g., r=16, d=2048), a proximal weight λ > 0, and a small iteration budget K (e.g., K=20). It produces a sequence of factors {Y_k} such that every covariance iterate X_k = Y_k Y_k^⊤ is positive semidefinite and rank-constrained by construction—no post-hoc rank truncation is needed. The target square root S = Σ⋆^{1/2} is computed once in O(d³) preprocessing and reused across all iterations, so the per-step cost is O(d²r + dr² + r³) rather than O(d³).

## Method

The key reduction rewrites the BW objective in the factor variable as F(Y) = ‖Y‖²_F + tr(Σ⋆) − 2‖SY‖_*, a quadratic term minus a nuclear norm. At each iteration, ITSPACE computes a polar/Procrustes certificate Q_k = polar(SY_k) via a thin SVD or Gram-based route, which gives a tight linear minorization of the nuclear norm and hence a tight quadratic majorizer of F. Adding a proximal term around Y_k and minimizing the resulting quadratic surrogate in closed form yields the update Y_{k+1} = α·S·polar(SY_k) + (1−α)·Y_k, where α = 2λ/(1+2λ) is the damping weight induced by the proximal penalty. The paper proves a sufficient-descent guarantee F(Y_k) − F(Y_{k+1}) ≥ (1/α)‖Y_{k+1} − Y_k‖²_F under exact polar computation, and an explicit certificate-gap bound F(Y_{k+1}) ≤ F(Y_k) + 2ε_k under inexact computation, making the method a certified few-step inner-loop primitive for rank-budgeted covariance alignment.

## Results

Under a rank-16 budget with K=20 steps, ITSPACE reaches the low-gap threshold (gap_r ≤ 0.02) in 0.037–0.058 s across Camelyon17, VisDA-2017, and Terra/CCT-20, compared to 11.5–13.8 s for BW-GD (a 200–310× speedup). In the downstream CovDrift-MR protocol, two ITSPACE updates (K=2) recover +3.8 AUROC on Camelyon17 (74.01 vs. 70.22 no-adapt), +8.6 accuracy on VisDA-2017 (84.26 vs. 75.68), and +5.1 accuracy on Terra (63.94 vs. 58.89), with adaptation times of 0.024–0.035 s at K=5. Sinkhorn variants fail to reach gap_r ≤ 0.02 within 20 steps on all three datasets under the exact BW evaluator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITSPACE dominates all baselines on the primary time-to-gap metric: it reaches gap_r ≤ 0.02 in under 0.06 s, while BW-GD (Fan et al., 2024) requires 11.5–13.8 s (≈200–310× slower), and Euclidean, Log-Euclidean (Arsigny et al., 2006), and AIRM (Moakher, 2005) updates take 7.5–19 s. The BW geodesic (Bhatia et al., 2019) fails to reach gap_r ≤ 0.02 within 20 steps on Camelyon17 and VisDA-2017, and Sinkhorn (Cuturi, 2013) and Sinkhorn-Gaussian fail on all three datasets, as expected since they optimize a regularized sample-OT objective rather than the exact BW distance. In the downstream CovDrift-MR task, ITSPACE matches or exceeds BW-GD within K=2 steps on all datasets (e.g., 74.01 vs. 73.97 AUROC on Camelyon17 at K=5), while Log-Euclidean and AIRM lag by 2–3 points at K=5. The one exception is Terra, where Sinkhorn achieves 65.00% accuracy (vs. ITSPACE's 63.94%) but at 7× the adaptation time (0.179 s vs. 0.024 s at K=5), and Sinkhorn's advantage stems from its sample-level transport rather than the exact BW objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the closed-form Bures-Wasserstein distance between Gaussian covariances (Gelbrich, Takatsu, Bhatia et al.) and the covariance-alignment tradition in domain adaptation (CORAL, Sun & Saenko 2016), but departs from both Riemannian/SPD-geometry optimizers (Han et al. 2021, Fan et al. 2024) and entropic sample-OT solvers (Cuturi 2013) by working directly in a low-rank square-root factorization. The key conceptual move is a proximal majorization-minimization step where a polar/Procrustes certificate linearizes the nuclear-norm term, yielding a closed-form factor update that preserves PSD and rank constraints by construction—something neither full-matrix Riemannian methods nor Sinkhorn iterations provide simultaneously. The paper positions itself as filling the gap between the BW geodesic (exact but full-rank, non-iterative) and gradient-based BW optimizers (correct objective but expensive per step and lacking inherent monotonicity certificates).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ITSPACE provides a theoretically grounded, lightweight inner-loop primitive for settings where only one or two covariance updates are affordable (test-time adaptation, minibatch domain correction), with the practical consequence that downstream recovery is achieved in 1–2 steps rather than 20. The scope is deliberately covariance-level and Gaussian: it does not address full distributional W2 for non-Gaussian data, nonlinear shifts, or barycenters, which the authors flag as natural extensions. The large speedups (200–300× over BW-GD) are largely explained by the O(d²r) vs. O(d³) per-step complexity, so the practical value is in the combination of exact-objective descent, structural preservation, and low per-step cost rather than in a fundamental algorithmic breakthrough.
