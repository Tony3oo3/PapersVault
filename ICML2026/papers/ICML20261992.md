## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models (DLMs) rely on heuristic random masking during pretraining, which treats all tokens equally and wastes compute on redundant or trivial positions rather than targeting the most informative gaps. No existing DLM pretraining framework adaptively selects masks based on measurable information gain or the model's evolving maturity, leaving a significant efficiency gap compared to how human reasoning selectively prioritizes informative uncertainties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261992/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Mask Policy Network (MPN, 1.2M params) consumes three inputs: token embeddings from the DLM's embedding layer, a low-frequency phase signal g = (g_H, g_R) combining exponentially smoothed predictive entropy and aggregated TIG reward, and a scalar target mask ratio ρ. It outputs per-token Bernoulli mask probabilities, from which a binary mask is drawn via bisection-enforced ratio matching. The DLM then takes the corrupted sequence (masked tokens replaced by [MASK]) and produces reconstructed token distributions over the vocabulary. The TIG reward is extracted from the same forward pass as the DLM's prediction, requiring no additional backpropagation.

## Method

InfoDLM introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a Trainable Information-Gain (TIG) reward scores each mask by the last-layer gradient energy ∑ᵢ‖p⁽ⁱ⁾−e_{xᵢ}‖²·‖h⁽ⁱ⁾‖², a tractable proxy derived from the conditional Fisher information trace under a Laplace approximation, computed in the same forward pass as the DLM's cross-entropy. Second, a maturity-aware phase signal g = (EMA(entropy), −β·log Σ r_step) is injected into the MPN via FiLM modulation (γ(g)⊙v + β(g)), enabling the policy to shift masking focus from basic structures to complex reasoning tokens as the DLM evolves. Third, an interleaved hybrid training loop alternates DLM updates (standard masked-token CE with a fixed policy) and MPN updates (REINFORCE with TIG reward, entropy regularization, and soft budget loss, with a fixed DLM), freezing one component per block to stabilize co-evolution. The full system adds only ~14.1% wall-clock and ~7.6% FLOPs overhead per 5:1 DLM/MPN cycle.

## Results

At 472M scale, InfoDLM achieves up to 13.8 pp improvement over SMDM on ANLI (49.30 vs. 35.50) and 11.5 pp on GSM8K (52.03 vs. 40.48) under matched pretraining token budgets (~35B tokens). At 1B scale, gains persist on SFT benchmarks (ANLI +6.1 pp, SNLI +1.1 pp) and zero-shot reasoning (ARC-c +2.8 pp, HellaSwag +5.9 pp). The per-cycle overhead is only ~14.1% wall-clock and ~7.6% FLOPs relative to the SMDM baseline, with the Mask Policy Network contributing just 1.2M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline SMDM (Nie et al., 2025a), InfoDLM wins on 6 of 8 benchmarks at 472M (GSM8K +11.5 pp, ANLI +13.8 pp, SNLI +2.1 pp, ARC-e +3.8 pp, ARC-c +3.6 pp) but loses slightly on HellaSwag (−2.1 pp) and WinoGrande (−0.6 pp) at 472M; at 1B the trend reverses with InfoDLM winning all 8 benchmarks. In the masking-strategy ablation (Table 3), InfoDLM outperforms Span masking (Joshi et al., 2020) by ~10 pp on GSM8K-35B (52.04 vs. 41.42), Frequency masking (Kosmopoulou et al., 2025) by ~10 pp (52.04 vs. 42.27), and PMI masking (Levine et al., 2021) by ~9.5 pp (52.04 vs. 42.54), and reaches comparable accuracy with roughly 3× fewer tokens (10B vs. 35B). The gains are most pronounced on reasoning-heavy tasks (GSM8K, ANLI) and diminish on already-saturated benchmarks (SNLI), consistent with the information-gain-driven masking reallocating budget toward reasoning-relevant tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SMDM/LLaDA discrete diffusion language model framework (Nie et al., 2025a,b), adopting its mask-and-denoise pretraining objective and transformer architecture. Its key conceptual departure is from the heuristic random masking that governs all prior DLM pretraining: rather than sampling masks stochastically, InfoDLM formulates mask selection as an active information-gain-maximization problem, drawing on Bayesian experimental design (Lindley, 1956; MacKay, 1992) and connecting it to a tractable last-layer gradient-energy proxy. The RL-based policy optimization (REINFORCE) and FiLM phase conditioning are borrowed from standard RL and conditional-generation toolkits, but their application to DLM mask construction is new. The paper positions itself against a spectrum of static masking strategies—random, span (SpanBERT), frequency-informed, and PMI-based—arguing that none adapt to the model's evolving competence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~13% GSM8K improvement at 472M with only ~14% wall-clock overhead suggests that a large fraction of DLM pretraining compute is currently wasted on low-information tokens, and that a learned masking policy can reallocate that budget toward reasoning-relevant gaps. The TIG proxy's strong rank correlation (ρ ≥ 0.72) with full-model Fisher information validates a practical design choice that avoids expensive full-gradient computation. However, all results are at ≤1B parameters on a single corpus, and the authors acknowledge that scaling behavior, theoretical guarantees beyond local Laplace approximations, and extension to autoregressive or multimodal settings remain open. The HellaSwag regression at 472M (30.04 → 27.99) also hints that the gains are not uniform across all task types.
