## Problem

Existing human-AI collaboration benchmarks operate in discrete or symbolic state-action spaces, failing to capture the continuous, physics-driven complexity of real-world teamwork (e.g., moving heavy objects through narrow passages). This paper asks how embodied agents can adapt to both diverse human behaviors and unseen physical constraints (mass, shape, size) in a continuous rigid-body environment where minor variations in force or rotation angle significantly alter outcomes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262034/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives an ego-centric 1-D state vector encoding its own position/angle, the partner's position/angle/holding status, and each item's position, angle, size, category, and shape (one-hot), plus wall and goal-region coordinates when training across maps. The output is a 4-dimensional continuous action (signed movement distance, target angle as [cos θ, sin θ], and a binary grasp/release flag) executed at 10 Hz. For the dynamics model, states are additionally encoded into a 32-dimensional VAE latent space, and the partner action predictor reuses the same diffusion policy architecture with the agent's and partner's states swapped.

## Method

BASS (Behavior Augmentation, Simulation, and Selection) augments training data in two ways: perturbing partner poses with small Gaussian noise, and recombining sub-trajectories by swapping a partner's segment between two demonstrations only when the fixed agent's start and end poses match within a 48×48 grid tolerance, preserving multi-agent coherence (99%+ physical validity). At inference, a VAE-based latent dynamics model (two autoencoders + a 2-layer MLP) predicts the next latent state given the current latent state and both agents' actions, with a partner action predictor (the same diffusion policy with swapped states) anticipating the partner's move. The diffusion policy samples 4 candidate actions independently; the dynamics model simulates each candidate's outcome, and the action yielding the best Normalized Final Distance is selected, enabling real-time (69 ms) action selection at 10 Hz without access to a physics simulator.

## Results

On Challenge 1 (seen behaviors), DP/BASS achieves the best Task Completion Rate (TCR) of 0.3503 and Normalized Final Distance (NFD) of 0.5724, outperforming DP (0.3233/0.5367), MAPPO (0.1929/0.3182), GRU (0.2369/0.3011), and MLP (0.2126/0.2987). Under unseen human behaviors, BASS shows the smallest performance drop (TCR −14.07%, NFD −9.22%) compared to DP (−20.72%/−14.50%) and MLP (−32.61%/−19.22%). When playing with real human partners, BASS dramatically improves over DP, achieving TCR 0.6512 vs. 0.3855 and NFD 0.7053 vs. 0.5547, while also reducing Waiting Time from 0.4886 to 0.3364.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BASS (built on Diffusion Policy) outperforms all four baselines—MLP, GRU, MAPPO, and standalone DP—across every evaluation protocol in Challenge 1. Against the closest competitor DP, BASS gains +2.7 TCR and +3.6 NFD on seen behaviors, and the gap widens to +26.6 TCR and +15.1 NFD when playing with real human partners, indicating BASS's simulation-and-selection module is critical for adapting to diverse human strategies. On unseen behaviors, BASS degrades least (TCR −14.07% vs. DP's −20.72% and MLP's −32.61%), confirming that behavior augmentation improves robustness. In the zero-shot coordination comparison (Table 8), BASS also beats CoMeDi [Sarkar et al., 2023] on both seen and unseen proxies (e.g., Map 1 unseen: 0.7080 vs. 0.3768 TCR) and MAPPO (0.7080 vs. 0.3287), suggesting data-driven behavior modeling is more effective than pure RL for continuous physical collaboration. On Challenge 2 (unseen physical constraints), BASS consistently leads in TCR and NFD, with the advantage attributed to the learned dynamics model enabling better action selection under novel object properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human-AI collaboration line of work (Overcooked-AI, Hanabi, Watch-and-Help) from discrete, symbolic action spaces to a continuous, physics-based setting with rigid-body dynamics. Methodologically, it builds on Diffusion Policy as a multi-modal action generator and on behavior cloning from human demonstrations, but introduces a novel BASS framework combining sub-trajectory recombination (for behavioral diversity) with a latent dynamics model (for outcome prediction and action selection). It departs from prior RL-based zero-shot coordination approaches (MAPPO, CoMeDi) by arguing that data-driven behavior modeling is essential in continuous physical spaces where dense rewards cause trajectory-level convergence that reduces behavioral diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Moving Out is the first benchmark providing continuous, low-level motion control for physically-grounded human-AI collaboration with human-collected datasets, filling a clear gap between discrete collaboration benchmarks and real-world embodied teamwork. The finding that RL methods with dense rewards paradoxically reduce the behavioral diversity needed for human adaptation in continuous spaces is a useful conceptual insight for the field. However, the method is limited to 2D physics, the performance gains over strong baselines (DP) are moderate in AI-AI settings, and significant failure cases (grasping unseen objects, not responding to assistance requests) remain unresolved, indicating substantial room for future work.
