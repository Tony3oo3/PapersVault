## Problem

Detection Transformers (DETR) frame object detection as set prediction via bipartite matching, but this paradigm introduces a preservation-specific optimization fragility in incremental learning that existing knowledge distillation and exemplar replay methods—designed for CNN-based detectors with static anchors—do not address. The paper identifies this fragility as Gradient Dilution, where preservation gradients are progressively weakened in magnitude by background-dominated signal dispersion, destabilized in direction by stochastic query-target matching drift, and reduced in support coverage by feature-space attrition of old classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265956/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces a set of N predictions, each consisting of a class probability vector and a 4-dimensional bounding box, via DETR's set-prediction formulation. A critical constraint is the missing-annotation issue: during incremental phase t, images may contain objects from previously learned classes that are unannotated and thus supervised as background, which the method must handle without explicit labels. The exemplar replay buffer is constrained to 10% of the training set size, and 10% of new-category data is mixed in during the replay fine-tuning stage to maintain plasticity.

## Method

FAS is a unified framework built on Deformable DETR with ResNet-50 that counteracts Gradient Dilution through three mechanism-aligned components. (1) Prior-Injected Query Formulation (PIQ) computes a semantic saliency map via cosine similarity between encoder features and a bank of class prototypes, then uses Top-P selection to initialize object queries exclusively from high-confidence foreground regions, physically filtering background gradients before they enter the decoder and yielding a theoretical SNR boost of HW/N. (2) Deterministic Anchor Distillation (DAD) selects invariant spatial anchors from the teacher's PIQ module and performs index-to-index KL distillation on both intermediate feature similarities and output logits, replacing stochastic bipartite matching during distillation with a fixed query-to-instance binding that restores gradient trajectory coherence from O(√T) to O(T) accumulation. (3) Manifold-Support Replay (MSR) decomposes each old class's decoder-output features into K=3 sub-distributions via K-Means and selects the medoid of each cluster for the replay buffer, explicitly preserving manifold boundaries and local modes rather than collapsing to a single centroid, thereby sustaining the geometric variance needed for robust decision boundaries under plasticity-driven interference.

## Results

On MS COCO two-phase protocol, FAS achieves 44.8 AP (40+40) and 45.5 AP (70+10), outperforming the strongest prior baselines SDDGR (43.0/40.9) and DyQ-DETR (42.4/42.4) by 1.8 and 3.1 AP respectively. Under the more demanding multi-phase 40+10×4 protocol, FAS maintains 45.2→42.9 AP across four incremental sessions, surpassing DCA and SDDGR by 5.7 and 6.1 AP at the final phase. The absolute gap to the joint-training upper bound is reduced to just 2.2 AP (40+40) and 1.5 AP (70+10), the smallest among all compared methods. On PASCAL VOC, FAS achieves 76.3/74.1/74.3/71.3 mAP across 19+1, 15+5, 10+10, and 5+15 splits, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAS outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On COCO 40+40, FAS (44.8 AP) surpasses the previous best SDDGR (43.0) by 1.8 AP and DyQ-DETR (42.4) by 2.4 AP; on the harder 70+10 split, the margin widens to 3.1 AP over DyQ-DETR and 4.6 AP over SDDGR. In the multi-phase 40+10×4 protocol, where forgetting compounds across sessions, FAS leads DCA by 5.7 AP and SDDGR by 6.1 AP at the final (70–80) phase, while older methods like RILOD collapse to 8.4 AP. On PASCAL VOC, FAS beats DCA by 1.8/2.9/0.9 mAP on 19+1/15+5/10+10 and is the only method reporting results on the 5+15 split (71.3 mAP). GFL-based baselines (LwF, RILOD, SID, ERD) trail D-DETR-based methods by 5–10 AP, confirming the advantage of set-based query prediction for IOD. FAS also achieves the lowest absolute gap to the joint-training upper bound (2.2 and 1.5 AP), indicating the most effective preservation of old-class knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Deformable DETR as its base architecture and positions itself at the intersection of DETR-based detection and incremental learning. It departs from prior IOD work (ERD, DMD, CL-DETR, SDDGR, DCA) that applies knowledge distillation or exemplar replay to DETR without diagnosing *why* these techniques underperform relative to their CNN-based counterparts. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'Gradient Dilution'—decomposed into Signal Dispersion, Assignment Drift, and Support Attrition—as a unified optimization-level explanation for DETR-specific forgetting, rather than treating forgetting as a purely representational or distributional problem. Each of the three proposed components (PIQ, DAD, MSR) is explicitly designed to counter one of these three failure modes, creating a mechanism-aligned framework rather than a collection of heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-centric diagnostic framework provides a reusable lens for understanding why set-based architectures (DETR, and potentially other bipartite-matching detectors) are structurally vulnerable in incremental settings, which could inform future work beyond this specific method. The 5+ AP gain in the 40+10×4 multi-phase setting is practically significant for long-horizon deployment scenarios. However, the analysis remains somewhat theoretical (the SNR and GTC metrics are derived but not extensively validated empirically), the method is tied to DETR's specific query-matching mechanism, and failure cases with small objects and heavy occlusions remain unresolved. An open question is whether the Gradient Dilution decomposition generalizes to other set-prediction architectures (e.g., query-based segmentation or tracking) or to non-DETR transformer detectors.
