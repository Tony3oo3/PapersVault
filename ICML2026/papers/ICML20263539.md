## Problem

While Talagrand's extensive work characterizes bounds on E[sup X_t] for Gaussian processes, no prior work addresses the optimization problem of choosing how to allocate a fixed total variance budget across Gaussian variables to maximize the expected maximum (or sum of maxima over subsets). This paper is the first to design efficient approximation algorithms for this non-convex stochastic optimization problem, covering independent and correlated Gaussians with non-zero means and arbitrary set structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263539/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the means μ_1,...,μ_n of n Gaussian variables and m subsets S_j ⊆ [n] defining which variables interact (the graph structure), with a fixed total variance budget of 1. For the independent case, the output is a variance vector (σ_1,...,σ_n) with Σσ_i² = 1; for the correlated case, the output is a full PSD covariance matrix Σ with tr(Σ) = 1. The objective is E[max_i X_i] for a single set or E[Σ_j max_{i∈S_j} X_i] for multiple sets, and the problem is non-convex in the variance/covariance parameters.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a modified chaining argument (Lemma 2.1) showing that variables with variance ≤ ε² contribute only O(ε√(ln(1/ε))) to the expected maximum, replacing Talagrand's O(ε√(log n)) bound by exploiting the total variance budget. For the single-set case, this yields a PTAS: since at most O(1/ε²) variables need non-negligible variance and the objective is Lipschitz in the variances (via coupling for independent variables, or via Earth Mover's Distance and the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality for correlated variables), a grid search over O(1/ε²) variables at poly(ε) resolution achieves additive ε approximation. For the multi-set case, the PTAS breaks down because the optimal allocation may spread variance uniformly across all n variables; instead, the paper groups variables by variance (powers of 4) into O(log n) groups, shows one group contributes Ω(1/log n) of OPT, and reduces the within-group selection to submodular maximization (proven via a discrete concavity argument on the expected maximum), solved greedily with a 1−1/e factor. The correlated multi-set case inherits the same O(log n) guarantee via a constant correlation gap lemma bounding E[max X_i] for correlated Gaussians by (2e/(e−1))·E[max Y_i] for independent Gaussians with the same marginals.

## Results

The paper establishes four main algorithmic guarantees: (1) a PTAS (additive ε) for the single-set variance allocation problem with independent Gaussians (Theorem 1.1), (2) a PTAS for the correlated single-set case (Theorem 1.2), and (3) an O(log n) multiplicative approximation for the multi-set GraphVarAlloc and CorrGraphVarAlloc problems (Theorems 1.3–1.4). The key structural lemma (Lemma 2.1) bounds the expected maximum of m zero-mean Gaussians with individual variances ≤ ε² and total variance ≤ 1 by O(ε√(ln(1/ε))), which is the critical ingredient enabling the PTAS. Monte-Carlo simulations on Erdős-Rényi graphs confirm that the optimal objective is concave in set density p and that variance concentrates on Θ(1/p) variables (Theorem 1.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or head-to-head algorithm comparisons. The most relevant comparison is against a direct application of Talagrand's (2021) chaining argument, which yields a weaker bound of O(ε√(log n)) in Lemma 2.1; the paper's modified chaining replaces the log n factor with log(1/ε), which is the difference between a quasi-polynomial-time approximation scheme (QPTAS) and a true PTAS. For the multi-set case, the O(log n) approximation factor arises from the log n variance groups, and within each group the problem reduces to submodular maximization solved by the classical Nemhauser–Wolsey–Fisher (1978) greedy algorithm achieving a (1−1/e) factor. The paper explicitly notes that tightness of the O(log n) bound is left open, and no competing approximation algorithms for these specific variance-allocation formulations are identified in the literature. The constant correlation gap in Lemma 2.8 (factor 2e/(e−1) ≈ 3.16) bridges the independent and correlated multi-set cases without additional algorithmic changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Talagrand's (2021) chaining arguments for bounding expectations of Gaussian suprema, but modifies the argument to exploit a total variance budget constraint, replacing a log n factor with log(1/ε) — a critical improvement that upgrades a quasi-polynomial-time approximation to a true PTAS. For the multi-set case, the O(log n) approximation reduces to submodular maximization, leveraging the classical greedy algorithm of Nemhauser et al. (1978). The authors explicitly note that Talagrand's extensive body of work does not address the variance budget constraint or non-zero means, positioning their four problem formulations (VarAlloc, CorrVarAlloc, GraphVarAlloc, CorrGraphVarAlloc) as new optimization problems at the intersection of stochastic process theory and resource allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first efficient approximation algorithms for a natural class of variance allocation problems with clear structural characterizations (concavity of OPT in graph density, concentration of variance on O(1/p) variables). The ML/auction motivation, while suggestive, remains largely hand-wavy — the SGD-to-Gaussian reduction is simplified and the auction connection is illustrative rather than empirically validated. Key open questions include whether the O(log n) approximation for the multi-set case is tight, whether the PTAS constants are practical, and whether the concentration phenomenon holds beyond the small-scale Erdős-Rényi simulations (n=8) presented.
