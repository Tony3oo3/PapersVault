## Problem

Empirical neurobiology has revealed hyperbolic structure in hippocampal place-cell populations, but no theoretical framework explains how such geometry is induced by neural tuning curves, how it affects downstream decoding, or how it can be exploited in machine learning architectures. This paper provides the first unified theoretical account linking exponential place-field distributions to Gromov hyperbolicity, connects the resulting geometry to associative memory capacity, and derives a hyperbolic memory model with provably larger storage than Euclidean Hopfield networks.

## Input / Output

For pattern completion, the input is a noisy query point in the hyperboloid model H^d, generated by taking a stored pattern's tangent-space anchor x_μ, adding Gaussian noise σz, and mapping back via the exponential map; the output is the recalled pattern in H^d, with success measured by geodesic distance below a tolerance ε. For downstream ML tasks, the model consumes standard RGB images (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32) or multiple-instance bags (Elephant, Fox, Tiger) and produces class labels or instance-level scores. A notable design choice is that the hyperbolic memory layers require no hyperbolic parameters in their trainable weights, so they can be optimized with standard Euclidean optimizers like AdamW.

## Method

The paper introduces the Karcher-flow model (KFM), a hyperbolic associative memory whose update rule replaces the Euclidean inner product in Modern Hopfield Networks with the Lorentz (Minkowski) inner product and replaces the Euclidean weighted average with the weighted Fréchet mean computed via Karcher flow (iterative exponential-map updates in the tangent space). The key theoretical insight is that the Lorentz inner product naturally encodes the quantity e^{d_g(a,b)} at the same O(d) computational cost as a Euclidean dot product, enabling the model to separate patterns with similar angular directions but different norms. Theorem 4.8 proves that under a Chernoff separation assumption, the recall success probability approaches 1 when log M = Θ(d/|κ| · e^{2αr_min}/r_min²), yielding a double-exponential capacity gain in r_max over Euclidean MHN and DAM. The paper further shows (Theorem 4.2) that Gaussian tuning curves with exponentially distributed place-field widths induce a statistically δ-hyperbolic semi-metric on stimuli, providing a biologically grounded construction for the hyperbolic geometry the memory model exploits.

## Results

On MNIST classification, the Karcher-flow model (KFAttention) achieves 85.52% accuracy at d=4, outperforming Hopfield attention (83.70%), Hyperbolic Attention Networks (84.85%), and Hyperbolic NN++ (67.35%), with gaps narrowing at higher dimensions (96.89% vs 96.71% at d=32). On Multiple Instance Learning, KFPooling achieves the best AUC on Fox (66.00) and is competitive on Tiger (87.34) and Elephant (91.20), though Gulcehre et al. (2019) edges ahead on Tiger (89.20) and Elephant (93.04). In pattern completion, the Karcher-flow model dramatically outperforms both MHN and DAM in low-dimensional settings (d=3), with recall success rates that scale with r_max while MHN shows only marginal improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Modern Hopfield Networks (Ramsauer et al., 2020), the Karcher-flow model wins on MNIST at d=4 (85.52% vs 83.70%) and on Fox MIL AUC (66.00 vs 60.54), but loses on Tiger (87.34 vs 83.52 is a win, but Gulcehre wins) and Elephant (91.20 vs 91.65, a narrow loss). Against Dense Associative Memory (Krotov & Hopfield, 2021), the Karcher-flow model substantially outperforms DAM in pattern completion, particularly in low-dimensional settings where DAM fails to store even a small number of patterns. Against Hyperbolic Attention Networks (Gulcehre et al., 2019), the Karcher-flow model wins on MNIST at d=4 (85.52% vs 84.85%) and Fox MIL (66.00 vs 62.92), but loses on Tiger (87.34 vs 89.20) and Elephant (91.20 vs 93.04). Against Hyperbolic NN++ (Shimizu et al., 2021), the Karcher-flow model dominates across all metrics, with the largest gap on MNIST at d=4 (85.52% vs 67.35%) and Elephant MIL (91.20 vs 85.32). The key finding is that the Karcher-flow model's advantage is most pronounced in low-dimensional regimes, consistent with the theoretical double-exponential capacity scaling in r_max.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Modern Hopfield Networks (Ramsauer et al., 2020) by formalizing the connection between MHN recall dynamics and the Bayes-optimal MMSE decoder, then transplanting the entire framework into hyperbolic space via the Karcher flow algorithm (Karcher, 1977) for computing Fréchet means on Riemannian manifolds. It departs from prior empirical work on hyperbolic geometry in the hippocampus (Zhang et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2018), which used topological data analysis to detect hyperbolic structure, by providing a constructive theoretical model showing that exponentially-distributed Gaussian place-field widths provably induce a statistically Gromov-hyperbolic semi-metric. The resulting Karcher-flow model is positioned as a non-Euclidean generalization of both MHN and Dense Associative Memory (Krotov & Hopfield, 2021), with the Lorentz inner product replacing the Euclidean inner product to encode geodesic distance at the same computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The double-exponential capacity scaling in r_max (Theorem 4.8) offers a principled mechanism for storing many patterns in low-dimensional spaces, which is directly relevant to biological constraints on neuron count and to efficient ML architectures. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10, three small MIL datasets), and the single-field, uniform-rate tuning curve assumption is acknowledged as restrictive for large environments. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic structure generalizes beyond hippocampal place cells to other brain regions, how synaptic plasticity constrains or promotes this geometry, and whether the capacity advantage persists at the scale of modern transformer architectures.
