## Problem

Data-driven weather forecasting models suffer from stepwise error accumulation during auto-regressive multi-step prediction, and existing extended finetuning strategies to mitigate this introduce catastrophic forgetting of short-term accuracy alongside prohibitive computational overhead. The paper addresses the dual challenge of maintaining temporal consistency over long horizons while keeping training and inference costs tractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260508/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single timestep of ERA5 reanalysis data comprising 70 variables (5 upper-air fields across 13 pressure levels plus 5 surface fields) on a global grid at 1.4° (128×256) or 0.25° (721×1440) resolution, which is patch-embedded and spatially pruned to HW/16 before processing. It produces iterative 6-hour-step forecasts up to 10 days (40 steps) of the same 70 variables, and additionally derives typhoon track predictions from the forecasted MSL and 10-m wind fields. For vision generalization, the same backbone accepts 224×224 RGB images (ImageNet-1K) or 512×512 crops (ADE20K).

## Method

EMFormer replaces standard multi-scale convolutional branches (1×1, 3×3, 5×5) with a single fused 5×5 convolution whose composite kernel is the center-aligned sum of the three sub-kernels; a custom CUDA kernel decouples the backward pass so each sub-kernel retains an independent gradient path during training, avoiding the collapse to a single effective kernel that plagues re-parameterization methods and yielding a 5.69× forward/backward speedup. For long-horizon consistency, an accumulative context finetuning strategy injects historical KV pairs from prior autoregressive steps into the current attention computation, with a memory module that scores cached tokens via attention-weighted importance (blended with historical scores at λ=0.9) and prunes to a fixed cache length of 5, bounding memory while preserving critical temporal dependencies. A sinusoidal-weighted composite loss dynamically interpolates between a latitude-area-weighted MSE and a per-variable adaptive loss (with learnable scaling w) using a learnable parameter θ that monotonically shifts emphasis from coarse geographic correction to fine-grained variable-specific refinement as training progresses.

## Results

On 1.4° ERA5 weather forecasting, EMFormer with accumulative finetuning achieves a 10-day normalized RMSE of 0.5094 and ACC of 0.5389, outperforming OneForecast (0.5918/0.4457), GraphCast (0.6009/0.4275), Pangu-Weather (0.6215/0.3542), and Fuxi (0.6981/0.2391). For typhoon track prediction, the method achieves a mean 96-hour error of 88.49 km across 10 typhoons, substantially below the next-best AIFS (119.17 km) and GraphCast (120.78 km). On ImageNet-1K, EMFormer-B reaches 84.4% Top-1 accuracy with 80.6M parameters and 12.3 GFLOPs, surpassing MambaOut-Base (84.2%) and MambaVision-B (84.2%). On ADE20K segmentation, EMFormer-B achieves 49.6 mIoU using only ~25% of the FLOPs of comparable methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 1.4° ERA5 weather forecasting, EMFormer with accumulative finetuning dominates all four baselines (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, Fuxi, OneForecast) from 1-day onward, with the largest gap at 10-day (0.5094 RMSE vs. 0.5918 for OneForecast, a 14% reduction). OneForecast retains a slight edge at 6-hour (0.0549 vs. 0.0599 RMSE), but EMFormer surpasses it on ACC at every horizon. For typhoon track prediction, EMFormer achieves 88.49 km mean error, beating AIFS (119.17 km), GraphCast (120.78 km), Pangu (123.94 km), Fengwu (125.23 km), ECMF (139.44 km), and FourCastNet (218.11 km) by 25–60% respectively. On ImageNet-1K, EMFormer wins at all three parameter scales: 83.2% (tiny, vs. MambaOut-Tiny 82.7%), 84.1% (small, tied with MambaOut-Small), and 84.4% (base, vs. MambaOut-Base and MambaVision-B at 84.2%), while using fewer FLOPs than most competitors. On ADE20K, EMFormer-B (49.6 mIoU) trails VMamba-S (50.6 mIoU) by 1.0 point but requires only 251 vs. 1028 GFLOPs (~75% reduction), and EMFormer-S (46.7 mIoU) is competitive with Focal-S (48.0) at a fraction of the compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) data-driven weather forecasting models (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, FourCastNet, FengWu) that learn atmospheric dynamics from ERA5 reanalysis; (2) multi-scale vision transformers (EfficientViT, CrossViT) and structural re-parameterization (RepVGG, DyRep), from which the multi-convs layer departs by preserving independent per-kernel gradients during training via a custom CUDA kernel rather than collapsing to a single fused kernel; and (3) long-context KV-cache compression techniques from LLMs and video generation, which are repurposed here as an accumulative context mechanism for autoregressive multi-step forecasting. The sinusoidal weighted loss is a novel combination of variable-adaptive and latitude-weighted terms, but each component draws on standard meteorological evaluation practices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in operational weather forecasting: extending prediction horizons without the catastrophic forgetting and computational overhead that plague standard multi-step finetuning. The 5.69× training speedup from the multi-convs layer and the KV-cache pruning for bounded memory make long-context forecasting more accessible, while the strong typhoon track results (88.49 km mean error vs. 119.17 km next-best) suggest real value for extreme-event prediction. Open questions include whether the accumulative context approach scales beyond the tested 10-day horizon, whether the sinusoidal loss generalizes to other physical domains with heterogeneous variables, and whether the CUDA-level gradient decoupling is necessary at larger model scales where standard re-parameterization might suffice. The competitive ImageNet and ADE20K results with fewer FLOPs hint at broader architectural utility, though the weather-specific design choices (latitude weighting, pressure-level structure) limit direct transfer.
