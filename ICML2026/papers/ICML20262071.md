## Problem

Existing pretraining data detection methods for LLMs rely on local token statistics (e.g., aggregating the lowest-k% token probabilities), which treat tokens as isolated data points and neglect the probability dynamics across the full generation sequence. This local perspective is highly sensitive to the hyperparameter k and can misclassify samples when a non-training text happens to have higher average log-probability on its worst tokens than a training text. The paper identifies that memorized and reasoning-generated content exhibit fundamentally different volatility patterns in the probability flow, motivating a shift from local token statistics to a global sequence perspective.

## Input / Output

The method operates under a gray-box setting, requiring access to the target LLM's per-token output probabilities (not weights or gradients) and a reference corpus (e.g., a C4 subset) for estimating token frequency distributions. The input is a token sequence of arbitrary length; the output is a scalar volatility divergence score S_AECA that can be thresholded for a binary membership decision, though in practice AUC is used as the evaluation metric, making the threshold unnecessary. The reference corpus only needs to approximate the natural language background distribution—experiments show robustness to corpus size (1 GB–10 GB) and domain (general vs. legal).

## Method

AECA reformulates pretraining data detection as a signal-processing problem by treating the per-token probability sequence as a dynamic signal. It first calibrates raw probabilities with Inherent Self-Information (derived from a Laplace-smoothed reference corpus frequency) to form an Entropic Potential Φ(t) = p_θ(x_t) · I_self(x_t), which amplifies anomalous model confidence on inherently rare tokens. A 1D convolution with kernel [1, −1] then extracts the Entropic Difference Spectrum S, acting as a high-pass filter that suppresses smooth semantic generation while amplifying overfitting-induced oscillations (Proposition 4.3). The final detection score is the Volatility Divergence S_AECA = σ(S) − λ·σ(L), where σ(L) is the standard deviation of the NLL sequence; the key insight (formalized in Lemma 4.4) is that for memorized text the loss volatility collapses while the entropic difference volatility is preserved, breaking the coupling that holds for generalized text. This yields a theoretically grounded, reference-corpus-calibrated, and computationally lightweight detector that outperforms local-statistics baselines by up to 1.5% AUC, with the largest gains on long texts.

## Results

On the WikiMIA benchmark, AECA achieves an average AUC of 0.712 (Len=128) and 0.714 (Len=256), surpassing the strongest baselines (DC-PDD at 0.699 and Min-K% at 0.692) by 1.3% and 1.7% respectively. On the more challenging MIMIR benchmark, AECA attains an average AUC of 0.546, outperforming the runner-up Min-K% (0.540) by 0.6% and PPL (0.537) by 0.9%. The advantage is most pronounced in long-text, large-model settings: on GPT-NeoX-20B at Len=256, AECA (0.758 Ori / 0.748 Para) exceeds the best baseline by 1.5% and PPL by 4.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AECA consistently outperforms all five baseline families across both WikiMIA and MIMIR. Against PPL (Carlini et al., 2021), AECA gains 5.2–6.3% AUC on WikiMIA (0.712–0.714 vs. 0.649–0.662) and 0.9% on MIMIR (0.546 vs. 0.537), with the gap widening substantially for longer texts and larger models. Against Min-K% (Shi et al., 2024), AECA leads by 1.5–2.0% on WikiMIA and 0.6% on MIMIR; notably, Min-K%++ (Zhang et al., 2025) degrades sharply on Len=256 WikiMIA (0.619 avg) while AECA remains stable at 0.714. Against DC-PDD (Zhang et al., 2024), AECA improves by 1.3% (Len=128) and 5.4% (Len=256) on WikiMIA, and by 0.7% on MIMIR. Against PAC (Ye et al., 2024), AECA leads by 2.2% on WikiMIA Len=128 and 2.0% on Len=256; on MIMIR, PAC is the closest competitor (0.533) but still trails AECA by 1.3%, with the gap reaching ~1.0% on PubMed Central with Pythia-2.8B. AECA also maintains its lead on paraphrased texts and non-Transformer architectures (Mamba), indicating robustness beyond the standard Transformer setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gray-box pretraining data detection line initiated by Carlini et al. (2021) and refined by Min-K% Prob (Shi et al., 2024), DC-PDD (Zhang et al., 2024), and PAC (Ye et al., 2024), all of which aggregate statistics over local token subsets. Its key conceptual departure is reframing the probability sequence as a dynamic signal amenable to signal-processing tools (high-pass convolution, spectral analysis) rather than a collection of independent points. The self-information calibration component is directly inherited from DC-PDD's reference-corpus frequency estimation, while the convolutional filtering and volatility divergence framework are novel additions that shift the detection paradigm from local extrema to global sequence dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The global-sequence perspective offers a principled explanation for why local methods fail on long texts and provides a detection signal that is robust to paraphrasing and non-Transformer architectures. However, the practical gains are modest (≤1.5% AUC), the method still requires full probability access (gray-box), and performance on short texts remains limited. Open questions include whether the volatility divergence signal scales to 70B+ models, whether λ can be estimated adaptively rather than via grid search, and whether the framework extends to corpus-level contamination detection rather than sample-level membership.
