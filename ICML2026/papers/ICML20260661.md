## Problem

Existing QAT scaling laws model quantization error as a function of model size alone, ignoring the number of training tokens and quantization granularity. This paper shows that both factors significantly affect W4A4 quantization error—error increases with more training tokens and coarser groups—yet no unified framework captures their interplay, limiting practical guidance for designing quantized LLMs.

## Input / Output

The scaling law takes three scalar inputs: model parameter count N (74M–973M in experiments), total training tokens D (10B–100B), and quantization group size G (32, 64, 128, 256, or per-channel/token). It outputs a single scalar quantization error δ_p (the loss gap between the QAT model and its BF16 counterpart), which is added to the Chinchilla loss to predict the full QAT training loss. The formula uses a logarithmic term log₂(G) so that G=1 (no quantization) yields zero error, and all exponents γ_N, γ_D, γ_G are positive fitted constants.

## Method

The paper proposes δ_p(N,D,G) = k·D^{γ_D}·(log₂ G)^{γ_G} / N^{γ_N}, a closed-form quantization error model fitted to 80 W4A4 QAT runs (R²=0.944) and validated by extrapolating to 973M/200B-token models. To identify error sources, they decompose W4A4 error into weight-only (W4A16) and activation-only (W16A4) components via a linear relationship (coefficient 0.906), revealing that activation error in the FC2 projection input—driven by persistent SwiGLU-induced outliers with kurtosis ~89 even after QAT regularization—is the dominant bottleneck. Applying 8-bit quantization to the FC2 input eliminates this bottleneck, after which weight and activation errors converge to similar magnitudes (ratio R ≈ 0.85–1.10), and at high data-to-parameter ratios (D/N > 1000) weight error begins to surpass activation error, indicating both components require joint optimization in future QAT algorithms.

## Results

The proposed unified QAT scaling law (Eq. 5) achieves an R² of 0.944 when fitting W4A4 quantization error across 80 QAT runs, and generalizes accurately to 973M-parameter models trained on 100B and 200B tokens. The law captures three key trends: quantization error decreases ~34% as model size grows from 74M to 594M, increases ~22% as training tokens grow from 10B to 100B, and decreases with finer quantization granularity (0.037 gap between coarsest and finest G). On a 7B MoE model (0.7B active) trained for 1T tokens, the W4A4 quantization error monotonically widens from 0.0153 at 200B tokens to 0.0212 at 1T tokens, confirming the D-dependence at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior QAT scaling laws: Frantar et al. (2025) and Kumar et al. (2024), both of which model quantization error as a function of model size N only (via an Effective Parameter Multiplier), neglecting training data volume D and quantization granularity G. The proposed method substantially outperforms both baselines: relative error drops from 19.3% to 5.2% for W4A16 (weight-only 4-bit) and from 8.5% to 4.7% for W4A4 (full 4-bit). The larger improvement on W4A16 is explained by the finding that weight quantization error (δ_W4A16) is far more sensitive to D (γ_D = 0.1610) than activation quantization error (γ_D = 0.0331), so omitting D from the model is especially costly for weight-only quantization. Additionally, the prior methods require 5 separate fitted curves (one per granularity G), whereas the proposed law uses a single curve that captures all granularities via the log₂(G) term, improving both accuracy and practicality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla scaling law framework to quantization-aware training by proposing a unified quantization error model δ_p(N,D,G) that explicitly incorporates training data volume and quantization granularity, factors that prior QAT scaling laws (Frantar et al., 2025; Kumar et al., 2024) neglected in favor of a model-size-only EPM formulation. It also builds on the PTQ scaling law literature (Kumar et al., 2024; Ouyang et al., 2024), which already recognized the role of D and G for post-training quantization, and brings that insight to the QAT setting for the first time. The decomposition of W4A4 error into weight and activation components, and the identification of the FC2/SwiGLU outlier bottleneck, connects to the broader LLM outlier literature (Xiao et al., 2023; Nrusimha et al., 2024) but applies it specifically to the QAT training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that QAT quantization error *increases* with more training tokens is a counterintuitive result with direct practical consequences: practitioners cannot simply scale up data to close the W4A4 gap, and the optimal data-to-parameter ratio for quantized training differs from full-precision Chinchilla-optimal ratios. The identification of FC2 activation quantization as the dominant bottleneck, and the demonstration that 8-bit FC2 inputs bring weight and activation errors to parity, provides a concrete engineering recipe (mixed-precision on FC2) and reframes future QAT research toward balanced weight-and-activation optimization rather than activation-only outlier suppression. Open questions include whether the law and the FC2 bottleneck generalize to ternary/1-bit QAT, to architectures without SwiGLU, and to MoE models where the weight-to-activation parameter ratio differs substantially from dense models.
