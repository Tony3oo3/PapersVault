## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees under exchangeability, but spatial data violate this assumption due to multidimensional dependence and the absence of a natural ordering. Existing spatial conformal methods either apply uniform weights (GSCP) or fixed distance-based kernels (SLSCP, LCP), requiring strong assumptions like infill sampling or i.i.d. data, and cannot adapt to complex local heterogeneity. This paper closes the gap by establishing valid coverage under weaker stationarity and spatial mixing conditions while producing tighter, data-adaptive intervals.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained regression model f̂, a calibration set of spatial observations, and a test location s_{n+1} with feature vector X(s_{n+1}), the method constructs an asymmetric prediction interval [f̂(X) + Q̂(β*), f̂(X) + Q̂(1−α+β*)] where β* is chosen to minimize interval width. The key input detail is that the k-nearest-neighbor residuals around s_{n+1}, ordered by increasing spatial distance, serve as features for the quantile regression model, making the interval width a learned function of local residual patterns rather than a fixed function of distance.

## Method

LSCP couples localized quantile regression with conformal calibration by training a single global Quantile Random Forest (QRF) on pairs of (neighborhood residual vector, target residual) across all calibration points. At inference, the k-nearest neighbors of the test location are retrieved, their residuals are ordered by spatial distance to preserve local dependence structure, and the QRF produces a weighted empirical quantile whose weights are data-adaptive rather than user-specified. This eliminates the need for hand-tuned kernel bandwidths and captures richer local dependencies than pairwise distance or feature-similarity kernels. Theoretically, the paper proves asymptotic conditional coverage under stationarity and strong spatial mixing of the noise process, with a coverage gap bounded by terms involving the estimation error δ_n, maximum weight M_n, and the mixing coefficient decay rate.

## Results

On five real-world spatial datasets (New Mexico, Georgia, Farming, California Housing, King County), LSCP achieves near-nominal 90% coverage (ranging 0.898–0.926) while producing the narrowest prediction intervals across all datasets. For example, on California Housing, LSCP achieves 0.898 coverage with a mean interval width of 159,370, compared to 196,959 for GSCP and 197,505 for LCP. On synthetic data with three noise scenarios (additive, signal-dependent, spatially adaptive), LSCP maintains 90.2–91.4% coverage with the tightest intervals (e.g., width 1.08 vs. 1.55 for GSCP in Scenario 1). The method is also robust to neighborhood size k, with coverage varying only 0.923–0.926 (NM) and 0.905–0.909 (GA) across k ∈ {5, …, 150}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSCP outperforms all five baselines on interval width across every real-world dataset while maintaining valid or near-nominal coverage. Against GSCP (uniform-weight global conformal), LSCP narrows intervals by 28–34% (e.g., 211.3 vs. 295.9 on New Mexico; 159,370 vs. 196,959 on California Housing) while matching or exceeding coverage. Against SLSCP (distance-kernel localized conformal), LSCP achieves 12–20% narrower intervals (e.g., 130.8 vs. 167.8 on Georgia) with comparable coverage, demonstrating that learned data-adaptive weights from residual features capture richer local structure than fixed spatial-distance kernels. Against LCP (feature-similarity kernel), LSCP narrows intervals by 19–24% on the two housing datasets (159,370 vs. 197,505; 383,316 vs. 420,069), showing that quantile regression on neighborhood residuals is more expressive than pairwise Gaussian-kernel similarity. Against EnbPI (time-series conformal with leave-one-out residuals), LSCP achieves higher coverage (0.926 vs. 0.884 on NM; 0.906 vs. 0.886 on GA) with 22–28% narrower intervals. Against SLCP (split localized conformal), LSCP matches coverage while producing 12–22% tighter intervals, particularly on the spatially heterogeneous New Mexico and California Housing datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework from exchangeable or time-series settings to spatial data by replacing fixed kernel-based weighting (as in SLSCP and LCP) with data-adaptive weights learned via Quantile Random Forests on neighborhood residuals. Theoretically, it departs from the exchangeability assumption of GSCP and the infill-sampling assumption of SLSCP, instead establishing asymptotic conditional coverage under stationarity and spatial mixing—a condition that naturally generalizes time-series mixing to multidimensional spatial fields. The method is positioned as a unification of localized conformal prediction (Guan, 2023) and spatial conformal prediction (Mao et al., 2024), with the key conceptual shift being that localization is learned from residual structure rather than prescribed by a user-chosen kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap in spatial conformal prediction theory: prior methods either required exchangeability (limiting applicability to homogeneous data) or infill sampling (requiring arbitrarily dense observations), both of which fail for many real-world spatial datasets. The data-adaptive weighting eliminates the kernel-tuning burden that plagues SLSCP and LCP, making the method more practical. Open questions include whether the spatial mixing assumption is satisfied in practice for highly non-stationary fields, how the method behaves under extreme sparsity (as seen in New Mexico where all methods under-cover), and whether the framework extends cleanly to spatio-temporal settings with time-varying mixing coefficients.
