## Problem

Effective deployment of outlier detection (OD) is hindered by the need for algorithm and hyperparameter selection, which is notoriously difficult without labeled outliers. While FOMO-0D introduced the first foundation model for zero-shot tabular OD, it was trained solely on Gaussian Mixture Model priors, limiting its distributional coverage. This paper addresses how to extend zero-shot tabular OD foundation models to state-of-the-art performance by incorporating diverse synthetic data priors and an effective training strategy that handles the resulting heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

At inference, OUTFORMER ingests unlabeled tabular inliers (up to 5K context points, up to 100 dimensions) and test query points (up to 10K) in a single forward pass, producing a continuous outlier score per test point that can be thresholded into a binary label. No labeled outliers, gradient updates, or hyperparameter tuning are required at test time. To handle datasets exceeding its context window, the model ensembles scores over 50 randomly subsampled context points and feature subsets, keeping inference latency low since each ensemble member is just a forward pass.

## Method

OUTFORMER is a 45.1M-parameter 10-layer Transformer (512 hidden dims, 8 heads) built on the Prior-data Fitted Network framework, pretrained in a supervised manner on synthetic tabular datasets with labeled inliers and outliers. Two key innovations drive its design: (1) a mixture of three synthetic data priors—Gaussian Mixture Models, Structural Causal Models, and Copulas—that generate five distinct outlier archetypes (contextual subspace, measurement, structural, probabilistic, and dependence outliers), and (2) a self-evolving curriculum (SEC) formulated as a non-stationary multi-armed bandit where each arm corresponds to a (prior, dimensionality-bin) category, with a reward function based on the variance of centered cross-entropy losses that prioritizes datasets with high learning utility (dispersed, confident predictions) over overly easy, overly hard, or uncertain ones. The curriculum adaptively shifts sampling toward productive task categories without any manual difficulty ordering, and a pace scheduler progressively relaxes a loss threshold to incorporate harder points as training progresses. At inference, the frozen model performs in-context learning via cross-attention from queries to labeled context, with ensembling over subsampled contexts and feature bagging to improve robustness and accommodate larger datasets.

## Results

On ADBench (57 datasets), OUTFORMER achieves the best average rank (4.02±2.6), highest ELO (1235), highest winrate (0.71), and best rescaled AUROC (rAUC 0.956±0.06), outperforming the prior SOTA DTE-NP (rAUC 0.939) and the previous foundation model FoMo-0D (rAUC 0.928). Across all 1,500+ datasets from three real-world benchmarks (ADBench, OddBench, OvRBench), OUTFORMER reaches SOTA with rAUC 0.903±0.12 and ELO 1209, tying with DTE-NP and TabPFN-OD at the top rank while significantly outperforming FoMo-0D (p≤0.00). On AUPRC across all benchmarks, OUTFORMER significantly outperforms every baseline including DTE-NP and TabPFN-OD (p≤0.00). Inference is fast (forward pass only), enabling plug-and-play zero-shot deployment without any model training or hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OUTFORMER significantly outperforms all deep-learning baselines (LOF, IForest, DTE-C, ICL, DDPM, GOAD, DeepSVDD) on ADBench with p≤0.02, winning on 54–84% of datasets. Against the prior foundation model FoMo-0D, OUTFORMER wins on 54% of datasets and loses on only 26% (p=0.01), with a rAUC improvement from 0.928 to 0.956. The closest competitors are DTE-NP (rAUC 0.939, p=0.06) and TabPFN-OD (rAUC 0.945, p=0.12), where OUTFORMER leads but the differences are not statistically significant at α=0.05 on ADBench; however, on the full 1,500+ dataset evaluation, OUTFORMER significantly outperforms all baselines on AUPRC (p≤0.00). Classical shallow methods (kNN, LOF, IForest) are consistently outperformed, with IForest showing the largest gap (rAUC 0.879 vs. 0.956). The repurposed TabPFN-OD ranks second on ADBench, confirming the promise of FMs for OD, but requires iterating over feature predictions, making it slower than OUTFORMER's single forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends FOMO-0D (Shen et al., 2025), the first foundation model for zero-shot tabular outlier detection, which was pretrained solely on Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) synthetic data. The two key departures are: (1) replacing the single GMM prior with a mixture of GMMs, Structural Causal Models, and Copulas to capture a broader spectrum of inlier/outlier archetypes, and (2) introducing a self-evolving curriculum based on multi-armed bandits to handle the resulting training heterogeneity. The broader PFN/TabPFN lineage (Müller et al., 2022; Hollmann et al., 2023) provides the architectural and in-context learning foundation, while the curriculum design draws on multi-task learning and bandit literature (Besbes et al., 2014; Pentina et al., 2015).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OUTFORMER establishes the first OD foundation model to reach state-of-the-art performance on ADBench while maintaining fast zero-shot inference, and the two new benchmarks (OddBench, OvRBench; 1446 datasets total) provide much-needed statistical power for OD evaluation beyond the 57-dataset ADBench. The counterintuitive finding that naive mixed-prior training underperforms GMM-only training—and that a bandit-based curriculum is essential to unlock the benefits of data diversity—offers a generalizable lesson for multi-task pretraining beyond OD. Open questions include scaling to larger context sizes, incorporating categorical priors, exploring dual (2D) attention or mixture-of-experts architectures, and addressing fairness and bias in sensitive deployment settings.
