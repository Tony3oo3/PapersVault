## Problem

MLLMs used to infer relative spatial relations for 3D layout generation suffer from accumulated errors during multi-hop reasoning, where repeated reference-frame transformations cause semantic drift (directional misinterpretation) and metric drift (compounding displacement errors). Prior pipelines patch these unreliable relations with post-hoc heuristics like grid discretization or conflict pruning, sacrificing semantic fidelity. The paper asks how to make MLLM spatial reasoning itself produce relations that are both semantically consistent and physically feasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language instruction, a room with known planar dimensions (L, W), and up to 40 floor-standing 3D assets from Objaverse, each annotated with a textual description, front-facing orientation, and axis-aligned bounding box size. Objects are constrained to be upright and rotate only around the +z axis, so the output is a planar pose (x, y, θ) per asset with height fixed at z = h/2. The generated layout must satisfy the instruction while remaining collision-free and within room boundaries.

## Method

R³L is a two-stage framework (reasoning + solving) built around three modules. Invariant spatial decomposition partitions assets into frame-invariant units (anchor + members) so that intra-unit relations are reasoned in a local anchor frame, breaking long multi-hop chains and provably reducing the number of reference-frame transformations (Proposition A.1). Consistent spatial imagination forces the MLLM to externalize global-local cognitive maps with explicit bounding boxes and rotated footprints, then runs an imagine-and-revise loop that detects axis-aligned collisions and iteratively edits the responsible intra- or inter-unit relations. Supportive spatial optimization re-parameterizes member poses into unit-local coordinates while keeping unit poses global, which by Proposition B.1 makes intra-unit penalty gradients vanish with respect to the unit pose, decoupling local and global optimization and yielding a 1.8× convergence speedup over standard global optimization.

## Results

On the average across nine scene types, R³L achieves 0.0% collision ratio and 0.0% out-of-bound ratio (perfect physical feasibility), while also attaining the highest semantic scores: 7.9 realism, 7.5 functionality, and 9.1 instruction following (all on a 1–10 scale). In pairwise comparisons, R³L is preferred over LayoutGPT in 23/27 scenes, over Holodeck in 27/27, and over LayoutVLM in 24/27, achieving the highest Elo rating of 1866. The supportive spatial optimization yields a 1.8× convergence speedup over standard global optimization under identical hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R³L outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against LayoutGPT (the strongest semantic baseline), R³L improves realism by +2.0, functionality by +2.0, and instruction following by +1.0, while eliminating all physical violations (reducing %CR from 4.3% to 0% and %OR from 8.1% to 0%). Against Holodeck, R³L matches and slightly exceeds its physical feasibility (0.0% vs. 1.3% %CR) while substantially improving instruction following by +6.1 points, reflecting Holodeck's reliance on coarse grid-based solving that sacrifices semantic fidelity. Against LayoutVLM, R³L achieves a large margin on out-of-bound ratio (0.0% vs. 10.9%) and instruction following (9.1 vs. 6.9), indicating that LayoutVLM's differentiable optimization remains sensitive to upstream relation-inference errors. In pairwise human and LLM evaluations, R³L is preferred in over 85% of comparisons against each baseline, with a perfect 27/27 win rate against Holodeck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constraint-driven 3D layout optimization paradigm pioneered by HOLODECK and refined by LayoutVLM, where MLLMs infer relative spatial relations that are then instantiated via differentiable optimization. It departs from prior work by identifying the root cause of unreliable relations as accumulated reference-frame transformation errors in multi-hop reasoning, rather than treating them as a post-hoc feasibility problem. The invariant spatial decomposition (frame-invariant units) and consistent spatial imagination (imagine-and-revise loop) are novel mechanisms that address semantic and metric drift respectively, while the global-to-local reparameterization for optimization builds on LayoutVLM's differentiable constraint framework but adds a structural gradient-decoupling property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why MLLM-based spatial reasoning fails on multi-hop chains (reference-frame transformation accumulation) and offers targeted, complementary fixes for each failure mode (semantic drift vs. metric drift). This reframing moves the field away from ad-hoc post-hoc heuristics (grid discretization, relation pruning) toward making the reasoning process itself reliable. Limitations include restriction to floor-standing objects with 2D planar poses, dependence on GPT-5 for all experiments, and evaluation on only 27 test cases. Open questions include whether the frame-invariant decomposition generalizes to full 3D poses with wall-mounted objects, and whether the imagine-and-revise loop scales to scenes with hundreds of assets.
