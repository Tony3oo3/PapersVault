## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented LLM methods for time series forecasting restrict learning to the original scale, failing to capture complex multi-scale periodic dynamics. Two obstacles prevent straightforward multi-scale augmentation: (1) simplistic decomposition produces entangled representations where each scale leaks periodic patterns from others, and (2) treating all scales equally introduces redundant interference from irrelevant scales, degrading the quality of LLM-generated semantic representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×N} with look-back window T (default 96) and N variables, and produces H future steps (H ∈ {96,192,336,720} for long-term; H ∈ {12,24,36,48} for short-term). Unusually, the FFT is computed once on the entire training sequence (length L) to identify global dominant periodicities, which are then spectrally projected down to the input window length T before decomposition. The frozen LLM (default GPT-2 small) consumes text prompts constructed from multi-scale subsequences and outputs only the final token embedding per scale as the augmented representation.

## Method

TimeMRA decomposes the input into S scales via FFT-based dominant periodicity detection on the full training set, followed by learnable 1D-convolution aggregation at each scale to produce subsequences and corresponding text prompts. A frozen LLM encodes each prompt, retaining only the final token as the augmented semantic representation. A CLIP-style cross-scale disentanglement constraint (CSDC) jointly enforces temporal-to-semantic and semantic-to-temporal contrastive alignment across scales, pulling same-scale pairs together while pushing cross-scale pairs apart. A router network computes per-variable, per-scale routing scores via softmax over concatenated temporal and semantic features, then applies a TopM hard mask (with a load-balancing loss) to suppress irrelevant scales. Cross-modality retrieval uses standard Q/K/V attention where queries derive from the temporal encoder and keys/values derive from the masked augmented representations, followed by a multi-head cross-attention decoder and linear projection to produce the forecast. Training minimizes MSE plus weighted CSDC and load-balancing losses, with the LLM kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (input length 96, horizons {96,192,336,720}), TimeMRA achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 7 datasets, reducing error by an average of 4.04% (MSE) and 3.69% (MAE) over the second-best baseline. On short-term forecasting (horizons {12,24,36,48}), TimeMRA achieves up to 6.64% MSE and 5.85% MAE improvements over the second-best method on traffic datasets. In few-shot settings (5% and 10% training data on ETT), TimeMRA delivers average error reductions of 10.05% (MSE) and 8.72% (MAE) compared to the second-best baseline, demonstrating strong robustness under data scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeMRA consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across all 10 datasets and three forecasting tasks. Against the closest retrieval-augmented LLM baseline TimeCMA, TimeMRA achieves average MSE/MAE reductions of 4.04%/3.69% on long-term forecasting and up to 6.64%/5.85% on short-term traffic forecasting. Compared to alignment-generative LLMs (S²IP-LLM, FPT), the margins are substantially larger, with TimeMRA reducing MSE by 9.44% over S²IP-LLM on average long-term results. Against non-LLM deep learning baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, TimesNet, DLinear), TimeMRA wins on every dataset, with the largest gaps observed on ETTm2 (e.g., 0.262 vs. 0.350 MSE vs. DLinear) and ECL (0.166 vs. 0.212 MSE vs. DLinear). In few-shot settings (5% training data), TimeMRA's advantage over the second-best method (UniTime) widens to 10.05% MSE and 8.72% MAE, confirming that multi-scale retrieval-augmented representations are particularly beneficial when training data is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeMRA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: multi-scale decomposition for time series (Autoformer, FEDformer, TimesNet, MSGNet, MSHyper) and retrieval-augmented LLMs for forecasting (TimeCMA, UniTime, TEMPO). It departs from prior retrieval-augmented LLMs, which operate at a single scale, by introducing FFT-based multi-scale decomposition to generate scale-specific prompts fed into a frozen LLM. The CSDC mechanism (a CLIP-style contrastive loss) and the router network for adaptive scale selection are novel architectural components that address entanglement and redundancy problems specific to multi-scale LLM-augmented representations, rather than being direct extensions of any single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to systematically combine multi-scale decomposition with LLM-augmented retrieval, offering a principled way to inject semantically rich, scale-specific context into time series forecasting. The router network's ability to adaptively select relevant scales per variable is a practical design choice that could transfer to other multi-scale or multi-expert settings. Limitations include reliance on FFT-based decomposition (assumes stationarity in training data), frozen LLM inference overhead, and evaluation limited to tabular/sensor time series with moderate sequence lengths. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-stationary or irregularly sampled series, and whether the multi-scale LLM retrieval paradigm scales to very long horizons or high-frequency financial data.
