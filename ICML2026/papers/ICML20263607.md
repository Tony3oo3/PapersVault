## Problem

Existing constrained generation methods face a fundamental tradeoff: exact methods like rejection sampling preserve the LM's conditional distribution but waste computation discarding invalid outputs (often <1% acceptance in structured domains), while efficient methods like greedy constrained decoding provably distort the distribution. No prior approach achieves both exact distributional fidelity and amortized efficiency when generating many diverse samples from the same LM context, as required in tasks like program fuzzing and molecular discovery.

## Input / Output

CARS takes an autoregressive LM, a prefix-checkable constraint (e.g., a context-free grammar for valid programs, SMILES strings, or SQL), and a text prompt, and yields an infinite stream of constraint-satisfying sequences sampled exactly from the conditional distribution P^L. The constraint must support incremental evaluation: given any prefix, the algorithm determines in O(|Σ|) time which next tokens keep the prefix extendable to a valid sequence. Unlike GCD which produces one (biased) sample per forward pass, CARS may require multiple LM calls per valid output but guarantees each output follows the true constrained distribution with no approximation error.

## Method

CARS extends Adaptive Rejection Sampling by maintaining a trie of invalid prefixes discovered during sampling. When any sequence is generated (valid or rejected), CARS queries the constraint oracle at every position to identify all continuations of each prefix that would inevitably violate the constraint, adding them to the trie. The trie defines a reweighted distribution R^W that subtracts the probability mass of known-invalid prefixes from future draws, steering the LM away from previously discovered dead ends without altering relative probabilities among valid sequences. This prefix-level pruning is the key efficiency gain over ARS: a single rejected sample eliminates an entire subtree of invalid continuations rather than just one sequence, yielding 2–10× fewer LM forward passes per valid sample. The algorithm is proven to produce unbiased samples from P^L with monotonically increasing acceptance rates, and the expected probability mass removed per sample is non-increasing, providing a principled criterion for freezing the trie.

## Results

On molecular generation (SMILES), CARS requires only 183±28 LM forward passes to obtain 100 valid molecules, compared to 793±127 for RS and 220±34 for ARS, while achieving the best diversity (0.85) and tied-best validity (0.87). On text-to-SQL (Spider), CARS achieves the highest execution accuracy among exact methods (0.578) with the fewest samples per query (1.11), versus 2.06 for RS and 10.00 for MCMC/AWRS. In grammar-based fuzzing (XML), CARS produces 100 valid seeds in ~440 generations while RS and ARS exceed the 2,000-sample budget entirely, and CARS-generated seeds yield ~7,985 lines of coverage versus ~7,115 for GCD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARS dominates all exact baselines on sample efficiency: it requires 4.3× fewer LM calls than RS and 1.3× fewer than ARS on molecular generation, and 1.85× fewer than RS and 1.28× fewer than ARS on text-to-SQL. Against the approximate GCD baseline, CARS achieves 5.3 percentage points higher execution accuracy on Spider (0.578 vs. 0.525) and 3.2× lower KL divergence, while requiring only 1.1× more samples. Compared to MCMC (Anaya Gonzalez et al., 2025), CARS achieves 1.5–2× lower KL divergence and needs roughly 4–9× fewer LM forward passes per valid sample (2.15 vs. 10 on text-to-SQL). Against AWRS (Lipkin et al., 2025), CARS shows 1.8–2× lower KL divergence and substantially better sample efficiency (183 vs. 1000 on molecular generation). In fuzzing, CARS is the only exact method that completes within budget on most benchmarks, while RS and ARS consistently time out and AWRS is computationally infeasible on the complex grammars tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARS extends Adaptive Rejection Sampling (ARS; Gilks & Wild, 2018; Mansinghka et al., 2009) by leveraging constraint structure to prune not just the single rejected sample but all constraint-violating continuations of its partial prefixes, tracked in a trie. This departs from prior exact methods (RS, ARS) that only avoid previously observed invalid outputs, and from approximate methods (GCD, MCMC, AWRS, ASAp) that sacrifice distributional fidelity for efficiency. The key conceptual move is that each sample—valid or rejected—yields a set of provably invalid prefixes whose probability mass can be permanently subtracted, yielding a monotonically improving proposal distribution while preserving exactness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARS resolves the long-standing exactness-versus-efficiency tradeoff in constrained LM generation: it is provably unbiased from the true constrained distribution yet achieves 1.3–4.3× fewer LM forward passes than prior exact methods, and in some domains (fuzzing) is the only exact method that completes within practical budgets. The main limitation is LM–constraint misalignment: when the model assigns negligible probability to the valid set (e.g., rare syntactic constructs absent from training data), even aggressive prefix pruning cannot compensate, and approximate methods remain preferable. Open questions include whether the trie-based approach extends to non-prefix-checkable (e.g., global or semantic) constraints, and how CARS behaves under adversarial constraint structures where invalid inputs share few common prefixes.
