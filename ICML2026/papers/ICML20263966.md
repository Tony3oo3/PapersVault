## Problem

Histopathology annotations are typically assigned to large tissue regions containing hundreds to thousands of cells, making it impossible to resolve individual cellular identities from routine H&E images. While single-cell transcriptomics repositories contain rich cell-level annotations, and spatial transcriptomics provides paired image–expression data at near-single-cell resolution, no prior method has formally combined these disjoint paired datasets to transfer cell-type semantics into histopathology embeddings. The paper asks how to align image, gene expression, and text modalities through their shared gene-expression bridge so that zero-shot cell-type annotation becomes possible directly from H&E patches.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a 224×224 H&E image patch (typically containing ~10–50 cells) and a set of free-text cell-type queries (e.g., 'T cells', 'smooth muscle'), encoding each into a shared 2048-dimensional embedding space via UNI2 (image) and BioBERT (text) backbones. It outputs per-class cosine similarity scores between the patch embedding and each text prompt embedding, which serve as continuous AUROC inputs for presence-based multi-positive evaluation. The same shared space also supports cross-modal retrieval (e.g., retrieving gene-expression profiles or captions most compatible with an image patch) across all three modality pairs.

## Method

SPATIALWHISPERER is a trimodal contrastive model that jointly aligns histopathology images (I), gene expression profiles (G), and natural-language annotations (T) in a shared embedding space using an InfoNCE-based composite objective over the observed modality pairs I↔G (HEST-1K) and G↔T (CellWhisperer), with an optional third I↔T edge (QUILT-1M). The key mechanism is transitive representation learning: by shrinking positive-vs-negative angular gaps on the two observed pairs, the model induces alignment on the unobserved I↔T pair without ever seeing directly paired image-text data. The paper formalizes this with Lemma 3.1, which derives an upper bound on the per-sample I→T InfoNCE loss in terms of the separation margins (ε, η) achieved on the observed pairs, showing the bound tightens as ε↓ and η↓, and degrades smoothly (O(√δ) additive slack) under imperfect overlap in the shared gene-expression modality. Architecturally, modality-specific pretrained backbones (UNI2, BioBERT, Geneformer) are paired with two-layer MLP projection heads; following the LiT convention, the image and gene-expression backbones are frozen while the text encoder and all projection heads are trained with a learnable temperature and cosine learning schedule.

## Results

SPATIALWHISPERER achieves mean AUROC of 0.621±0.008 on PathoCell (13 cell types, colorectal cancer), 0.749±0.016 on Lizard (3 classes, colon adenocarcinoma), and 0.687±0.001 on PanNuke (4 classes, 19 organs), all evaluated zero-shot via cosine similarity between H&E patch embeddings and text-embedded cell-type labels. These represent relative AUROC gains of 13.9%, 15.9%, and 13.9% over the strongest published baselines (CONCH, OmiCLIP-extended, and CONCH respectively) on each benchmark. In low-data regimes (1/512 subsampling of task-matched pairs), the trimodal transitive configuration outperforms the bimodal baseline by up to ~15% absolute AUROC, with gains diminishing as data volume increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPATIALWHISPERER outperforms CONCH (Lu et al., 2024) on all three benchmarks: PathoCell 0.621 vs 0.546, Lizard 0.749 vs 0.615, PanNuke 0.687 vs 0.604, with the largest per-class gains on immune and vasculature populations well captured by gene expression. Against PLIP (Huang et al., 2023), gains are even larger (e.g., PathoCell 0.621 vs 0.488; Lizard 0.749 vs 0.528), reflecting PLIP's smaller training corpus (208K vs 1.17M pairs). OmiCLIP (Chen et al., 2025a), which bridges images to gene expression via marker-gene sentences, is the weakest published baseline, recognizing only B cells and fibroblasts at meaningful AUROC while other classes remain near random (e.g., PanNuke 0.520–0.525 vs SPATIALWHISPERER's 0.687). Notably, SPATIALWHISPERER does not beat the self-trained bimodal I↔T baseline (QUILT-1M) on PanNuke (0.687 vs 0.713), suggesting that direct image-text supervision remains advantageous for coarse tissue-of-origin tasks where the transitive gene-expression bridge adds limited value. The two-stage UNI2→Geneformer pipeline, trained on identical paired data, is consistently outperformed (e.g., Lizard 0.749 vs 0.662), confirming that joint trimodal alignment avoids the noise amplification inherent in sequential image→expression→cell-type prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transitive representation learning paradigm demonstrated empirically by ImageBind (Girdhar et al., 2023) from a vision-centric hub (images) to a biomedical hub (gene expression), and provides the first theoretical analysis—via an InfoNCE upper bound—formalizing when and how alignment on observed modality pairs induces alignment on unobserved pairs. The training architecture follows the LiT (Zhai et al., 2022) frozen-backbone-plus-projection-head convention and the CellWhisperer (Schaefer et al., 2025b) gene-expression-to-text contrastive objective, while the image-to-expression pairing draws on spatial transcriptomics datasets (HEST-1K). The key departure from prior two-stage pipelines (image → predicted expression → cell type) is the joint contrastive alignment that avoids propagating prediction noise through an intermediate step, and the explicit treatment of imperfect bridge-modality overlap as a first-class design consideration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical route to fine-grained, zero-shot cell-type annotation from routine H&E images by coupling the single-cell transcriptomics ecosystem to histopathology through a shared gene-expression modality, with actionable diagnostics (margin estimates, hub-overlap scores) for when transitive transfer will succeed. The finding that raw cross-dataset text annotations actively degrade performance while LLM-harmonized captions restore and exceed the bimodal baseline is a concrete, reproducible lever for data curation in multimodal biomedical training. Key limitations include patch-level (not single-cell) resolution, the bridge modality upper-bounding recoverable information (tissue-architectural features absent from transcriptomics remain unreachable), and untested generalization across scanners, assays, and under-represented tissues—open questions that will shape the next generation of multimodal pathology foundation models.
