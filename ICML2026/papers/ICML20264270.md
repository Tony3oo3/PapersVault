## Problem

LLM confidence estimates (e.g., token probability products) are poorly calibrated, and standard calibration metrics like ECE and smECE cannot distinguish a perfect oracle from a useless base-rate estimator, nor do they account for the risk level of the task. This makes it impossible to reliably adjudicate whether to trust a model output in high-stakes versus low-stakes settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264270/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM hidden-state activations captured during generation (mean-pooled across the output sequence at each layer, or reduced via layer-wise cosine similarity with the final layer, or PCA-projected to 10–20 components per layer) as input features. It produces a single calibrated probability of correctness that is compared against a Minimum Bayes Risk threshold τ = u_ca, where u_ca encodes the task's risk level, yielding a binary trust-or-abstain decision. The random forest inference adds only ~0.00007 s of overhead per example, making it negligible relative to generation time.

## Method

ACUTE extracts activation-based features from LLM internals—mean-pooled activations at early/middle/late layers, layer-wise cosine similarities with the final-layer activation, or PCA-transformed activations aggregated across layers—and trains a 500-tree random forest classifier to predict generation correctness. The classifier's positive-class probability serves as the recalibrated confidence, replacing the poorly calibrated raw token-probability product. The paper also introduces EURO (Expected Utility Renormalized by the Oracle), a decision-theoretic metric that incorporates task risk via the Minimum Bayes Risk threshold and normalizes utility between an oracle and anti-oracle policy, resolving ECE's inability to reward informativeness or penalize risk-blindness. ACUTE is highly sample-efficient, outperforming kernel-regression baselines with as few as 25 training examples, and generalizes across 6 LLMs from 4 families on MCQA, tool-calling, and summarization tasks.

## Results

Across 6 LLMs on MMLU, APIGen, and SCITLDR, the best ACUTE variants (late activations, PCA-20) achieve AUC-EURO of 0.83, 0.88, and 0.78 respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline NWKR (0.79, 0.78, 0.77) while maintaining comparable smECE (0.07–0.09 vs. 0.07–0.08). On APIGen, ACUTE pca20 improves AUC-EURO by ~0.10 over NWKR (0.88 vs. 0.78) with only a marginal smECE increase (0.06 vs. 0.02). Improvements over NWKR are statistically significant (p < 0.05) on MMLU and APIGen across all models, and on SCITLDR for 6 of 7 models; smECE differences favoring NWKR are not significant (p > 0.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACUTE consistently outperforms NWKR (the strongest baseline) on AUC-EURO across all three tasks: on MMLU, ACUTE late act reaches 0.83 vs. NWKR's 0.79; on APIGen, ACUTE pca20 reaches 0.88 vs. NWKR's 0.78; on SCITLDR, ACUTE pca20 reaches 0.78 vs. NWKR's 0.77. On smECE, NWKR and HRE often achieve slightly lower values (e.g., 0.02 on APIGen vs. ACUTE's 0.06–0.07), but these differences are not statistically significant (p > 0.2), while the AUC-EURO gains for ACUTE are significant (p < 0.05). HRE performs nearly identically to NWKR on both metrics, confirming that kernel smoothing and histogram binning are comparable recalibration strategies. The posthoc calibration baselines (Platt scaling, isotonic regression, beta calibration) perform comparably to NWKR/HRE and do not close the AUC-EURO gap with ACUTE. On the larger MoE model Qwen3-30B-A3B, ACUTE pca20 achieves AUC-EURO of 0.868 on APIGen vs. NWKR's 0.797, suggesting the advantage may grow with model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MICE for CATs (Subramani et al., 2025a), which first demonstrated that model-internal activations can serve as features for confidence estimation in tool-calling agents, but required expensive logit-lens decoding and an auxiliary BERTScore model. ACUTE replaces that pipeline with lightweight activation-derived features (mean-pooled hidden states, layer-wise cosine similarities, PCA projections) fed into a random forest, reducing inference overhead by ~40×. On the evaluation side, the paper departs from the standard ECE/smECE calibration-error framing by introducing EURO, a decision-theoretic metric grounded in Minimum Bayes Risk that incorporates task risk level and distinguishes informative from uninformative estimators—addressing a known but under-addressed limitation of calibration metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that LLM developers can deploy a near-zero-overhead confidence estimator (0.00007s per inference) that improves decision-making utility across risk levels without sacrificing calibration, which is critical as LLMs are embedded in high-stakes agentic workflows. The sample-efficiency finding—that 25 labeled examples suffice to beat baselines trained on 1000—lowers the barrier to adoption for new tasks. Open questions include whether activation-based confidence transfers to open-ended generation (beyond MCQA, tool-calling, and short summarization), whether the approach generalizes to models without accessible internals (API-only deployments), and whether EURO's single-parameter risk framing adequately captures multi-dimensional cost structures in real deployment.
