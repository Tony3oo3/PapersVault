## Problem

Generative backdoors manifest as manifold-level distributional shortcuts that are statistically indistinguishable from benign samples under standard metrics like FID and ASR, rendering traditional pattern-based defenses ineffective. These backdoors persist through parameter-efficient fine-tuning and propagate recursively through synthetic data pipelines (Generative Heredity), creating a systemic supply-chain vulnerability. This position paper argues the field must shift from instance-level anomaly detection to manifold-level distributional integrity verification.

## Input / Output

The framework audits the model's internal structure rather than its per-sample outputs: it consumes parameter weights, Jacobian matrices, SVD decompositions of attention projections, and text prompts/conditioning contexts for geometric verification. It produces a Distributional Integrity Score (DIS) measuring local manifold continuity, certified bounds on the probability of transitioning to a malicious distribution, spectral anomaly flags for parameter-level backdoor detection, and latent collision results for cross-modal embedding spaces. Unlike FID or ASR, these outputs are structural certifications about the generative manifold's topological stability rather than aggregate quality or binary success metrics.

## Method

The DI framework formalizes security as a Lipschitz-like stability constraint requiring the KL divergence between output distributions under infinitesimal prompt perturbations to be bounded by a constant times the perturbation distance (Eq. 11). Three synergistic layers enforce this: (1) Parameter-Level Auditing (PLA) decomposes cross-attention projection matrices via SVD to detect high-frequency spectral anomalies in the null-space that indicate model-editing backdoors (e.g., EvilEdit-style injections); (2) Certified Weight Smoothing (CWS) shifts randomized smoothing from input space to parameter space by optimizing under a Gaussian parameter-noise distribution, creating a basin of distributional stability that disrupts the brittle weight configurations backdoor triggers depend on; (3) Cross-Modal Alignment Verification (CMAV) applies interval bound propagation to verify that semantic volumes in prompt space map to convex, bounded regions in the joint latent embedding space, preventing latent collisions where benign prompts with triggers align with malicious concept embeddings.

## Results

This is a position paper that does not report experimental results or quantitative benchmarks. Its primary contributions are formal: a mathematical definition of Distributional Integrity (Eq. 11) bounding the KL-divergence of the output distribution under prompt perturbations, a formalization of generative backdoors as manifold grafting (Eq. 1), and a three-layer defense framework (PLA, CWS, CMAV). The paper demonstrates conceptually that FID remains approximately zero between benign and poisoned distributions (Eq. 8–9) and that the cumulative Lipschitz constant of a T-step diffusion process scales as (1+δ)^T, rendering classical randomized smoothing infeasible for high-resolution AIGC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No empirical baselines are compared; the paper is a position statement rather than an experimental study. Conceptually, it contrasts classical discriminative defenses (Neural Cleanse, Spectral Signatures, Fine-Pruning) against the generative threat landscape, arguing they are structurally inadequate because they assume discrete label spaces and pixel-level triggers. It further contrasts traditional data poisoning with the proposed Data-Chain Backdoor (Table 2), showing that DCB shifts the victim's role from passive ingestion to active synthesis and makes detection significantly harder. The paper positions its Distributional Integrity framework as a successor to input-level Randomized Smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019) and parameter-space smoothing (Sun et al., 2024; Mu et al., 2023), but does not provide head-to-head quantitative validation of these claims.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper extends the backdoor defense literature from the discriminative era (BadNets, Spectral Signatures, Fine-Pruning) to generative models, arguing that the task fundamentally shifts from detecting discrete label flips to verifying the topological stability of high-dimensional output manifolds. It repurposes Randomized Smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019) and Certified Policy Smoothing (Mu et al., 2023) by transposing the smoothing operation from input space to parameter space (Certified Weight Smoothing), and draws on model-editing attacks (EvilEdit, ROME) to motivate parameter-level provenance auditing. The paper also synthesizes the model-collapse literature (Shumailov et al., 2024; Alemohammad et al., 2023) into the 'Generative Heredity' framing, connecting recursive synthetic-data training to backdoor amplification across model generations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful unified vocabulary and three-pillar research agenda (Parameter-Level Auditing, Certified Weight Smoothing, Cross-Modal Alignment Verification) for a security problem that is rapidly becoming critical as generative models enter medical, automotive, and creative pipelines. However, as a position paper with no empirical validation, the central claims—particularly that CWS bypasses the curse of dimensionality and that the Distributional Integrity Score (Eq. 11) is practically computable—remain untested. Key open questions include the computational cost of Jacobian-based spectral auditing at foundation-model scale, the utility-diversity trade-off under strict Lipschitz constraints, and whether 'Model DNA' provenance standards can be enforced across open-source ecosystems without centralizing development.
