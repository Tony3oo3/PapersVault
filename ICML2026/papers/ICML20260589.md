## Problem

Existing LLM monitoring approaches rely on external modules (Sparse Autoencoders, Transcoders) to decode unaltered internal representations, making monitoring passive and fundamentally constrained by the entanglement of the original representation space. If internal features are inherently entangled, no amount of external decoding can reliably separate them. The paper asks whether the LLM itself can be made easier to monitor by actively reorganizing its representation space, rather than building ever-more-complex external decoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260589/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TELLME takes a frozen LLM base model, a Disentangle Set of QA pairs grouped into C behavior categories (e.g., safe vs. harmful, or five specific safety risks), and a Retain Set of general-capability QA pairs (UltraChat). It produces LoRA adapter weights (~41M parameters) that, when applied to the frozen model, yield a disentangled representation space at a chosen target layer (default 80% depth) and token position (last token of input/output). The method is weakly supervised: it only requires behavior-category labels for grouping, not preference or reward signals, and operates on intermediate hidden states rather than final outputs.

## Method

TELLME fine-tunes the LLM via LoRA with a joint objective combining (1) an InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls representations of same-behavior examples together and pushes different-behavior examples apart at a target layer, and (2) a retain loss comprising an ℓ₂-norm constraint on representations from the Retain Set plus a KL penalty on output probabilities to preserve general capabilities. The key distinction from prior work is that TELLME directly modifies the LLM's weights so the disentangled representations propagate through all subsequent Transformer layers during inference, rather than relying on a separate external decoder. This active reorganization of the representation space simultaneously improves monitorability (Self-Sim and Linear Probe accuracy gain ~10% on average) and, as a hidden bonus, improves safety performance by ~8 points on detoxification benchmarks without any directional signal about which behavior is preferred. The paper grounds the generalization benefit in optimal transport theory, showing that reducing k-variance within each behavior cluster and increasing inter-cluster margin lowers the generalization bound for behavior-level classification.

## Results

TELLME achieves an average 10.2% improvement over the original LLM on representation-based monitors (Self-Sim and Linear Probe) across three monitoring scenarios (Multi-Risk, Safe-or-Harmful, Misalignment) and three model families (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Qwen2.5-7B). On the detoxification task, TELLME yields an 8.1-point absolute average improvement over Origin on BeaverTails and SaladBench safety benchmarks while maintaining nearly unchanged general capabilities (GSM8K, MMLU, AGIEval). The method scales consistently from 7B to 235B parameters across dense and MoE architectures, with safety gains remaining robust or even increasing at larger scales (e.g., +5.8 multimodal safety on Qwen3-VL-235B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TELLME consistently outperforms all SAE and Transcoder baselines on representation-based monitors (Self-Sim and Linear Probe) across all three scenarios and all three model families. On Self-Sim, TELLME achieves the largest gains on the Misalignment task (e.g., 99.0% vs. 67.5% Origin and 69.3% Transcoder on Mistral-7B; 91.3% vs. 80.5% Origin and 78.5% Transcoder on Qwen2.5-7B), where external modules struggle most. On Linear Probe, TELLME improves over Origin by 5.6% and over the strongest SAE/Transcoder baseline by 2.5% on average. On the Judge-based monitor (output-level), TELLME yields a modest 1.1% average improvement over Origin, and notably underperforms Origin on Mistral-7B Multi-Risk (22.8% vs. 36.7%), indicating that representation-level disentanglement does not uniformly transfer to generation-based judgment. The SAE baselines (Templeton et al., Karvonen, Gao et al.) and Transcoder baselines (Dunefsky et al., Paulo et al.) generally perform at or below Origin on Self-Sim, confirming that external decoding modules do not meaningfully enhance monitorability without modifying the model's own representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

TELLME departs from the dominant paradigm of building external interpretability modules (SAEs, Transcoders) that decode unaltered LLM representations, instead directly reorganizing the representation space via LoRA fine-tuning with a contrastive (InfoNCE) objective. The contrastive learning machinery is borrowed from self-supervised vision (MoCo, SimCLR, Barlow Twins) and repurposed for disentangling behavior-level representations in LLMs, while the retain loss draws on representation-editing techniques (l2-norm constraints from Zou et al. 2024, KL penalties from InstructGPT). The theoretical analysis connects the disentanglement to optimal transport generalization bounds (k-variance, Wasserstein distance) from Chuang et al. 2021 and Solomon et al. 2022, providing a principled explanation for why tighter intra-class clusters and wider inter-class gaps improve monitor generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that TELLME achieves a 'hidden bonus': simply separating safe from harmful representations in the space—without any directional signal about which behavior is preferred—yields ~8% absolute safety improvement, suggesting that representation geometry itself encodes actionable safety structure. This opens a path toward scalable oversight where monitoring and safety improvement are unified in a single lightweight intervention (~41M LoRA parameters vs. ~1.09B for SAEs), validated across dense/MoE architectures and 7B–235B scales. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more subtle or novel misalignment modes not captured by the training behavior categories, whether adversarial monitors could exploit the reorganized geometry, and whether the gains persist as base models become significantly more capable.
