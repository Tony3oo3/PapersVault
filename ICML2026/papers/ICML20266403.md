## Problem

The dominant gradient-matching paradigm in graph condensation requires training on the full dataset to compute target trajectories, making the condensation process more expensive than the very training it aims to accelerate. Compounding this, these methods are tightly coupled to specific GNN architectures, fail catastrophically under architecture transfer, and are evaluated on small datasets where condensation is unnecessary, using misleading node-count metrics that obscure true resource costs.

## Input / Output

The proposed reformulation takes an original graph G (nodes, edges, features), a byte budget B, a GNN model class M, and a confidence level δ, and produces a condensed graph G_c whose byte-size—defined as d·|V|·s_f + 2·|E|·s_i, accounting for both feature storage and edge indices—stays within B. The output is characterized by a performance gap ε with a probabilistic guarantee: with probability at least 1−δ over models drawn from M, the distance between model outputs on G and G_c is bounded by ε. Unlike prior formulations that fix a single model and report node-count ratios, this formulation explicitly decouples condensation from any specific architecture and uses a byte-size metric shown to be proportional to message-passing forward-pass computational cost.

## Method

This position paper proposes a principled reformulation of graph condensation as a size-constrained optimization problem (Definition 2.2) that minimizes the worst-case performance gap ε under a byte-size budget B with a probabilistic guarantee over a model class M, replacing the prior bi-level gradient-matching formulation. The paper establishes four necessary conditions (ε-task preservation, byte-size compactness, cross-architecture generalization, and compute efficiency) and five desirable properties (faithful representation, memory efficiency, democratization, fairness, hyperparameter simplicity) that any valid condensation method must satisfy. It provides a systematic taxonomy of existing methods along two axes—model-dependence and full-dataset training requirement—identifying four buckets, with the ideal being model-agnostic methods that avoid full-graph training. Empirical evidence includes scalability tests on MAG240M (where only BONSAI succeeds while gradient-based methods OOM/OOT), architecture transfer experiments showing catastrophic accuracy drops for gradient-matching methods (e.g., GCOND drops from 77.30% to 13.21% on Cora when switching from GCN to GAT), byte-size comparisons revealing 5.7× storage differences among methods claiming identical '1% compression,' and CO₂ emission analyses showing gradient-based methods produce up to 12.4× higher emissions. The paper concludes with a call to action: benchmark on large-scale datasets where condensation is necessary, adopt byte-level compression metrics with disaggregated statistics and total computational cost reporting, pivot toward model-agnostic methods, and invest in theoretical foundations including complexity guarantees and lower bounds.

## Results

On MAG240M (hundreds of millions of nodes), only BONSAI completes condensation (52.33–53.39% accuracy at 0.5–3% sampling), while GCOND, GDEM, GCSR, EXGC, GC-SNTK, and GEOM all fail with out-of-memory or out-of-time errors. On architecture transfer (Cora, 1% sampling), GCOND collapses from 77.30% (GCN) to 35.24% (GAT) and 33.91% (GIN), while BONSAI degrades only from 85.76% to 78.67% (GAT) and 84.80% (GIN). CO₂ emissions for condensation at 0.5% on Reddit reach 903 g for GCOND versus 17 g for BONSAI (≈52× difference). Byte-size of '1%' condensed ogbn-arxiv graphs ranges from 644 KB (GC-SNTK) to 3.66 MB (GDEM/GCSR), a 5.7× spread that invalidates direct performance comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven condensation methods (GCOND, GDEM, GCSR, EXGC, GC-SNTK, GEOM, and BONSAI) against Random and Herding baselines. On MAG240M, all gradient- and kernel-based methods (GCOND, GDEM, GCSR, EXGC, GC-SNTK, GEOM) fail entirely (OOM or OOT), while BONSAI achieves 52–53% accuracy, outperforming Random (34–43%) and Herding (36–38%) by 10–15 points. On architecture transfer, GCOND suffers catastrophic drops (e.g., 77.30→13.21% on Cora GCN→GAT at 0.5%; 79.94→32.01% on PubMed GCN→GIN at 3%), and GCSR similarly collapses (79.94→32.01% on PubMed GIN), whereas BONSAI degrades by only 3–10 points across architectures. GDEM is the most robust gradient-free baseline among model-agnostic methods but still drops 18–26 points on Flickr GAT/GIN. In terms of CO₂ cost, GCOND emits 903 g on Reddit versus 17 g for BONSAI, and GEOM exceeds the 540 g reporting threshold on three of six datasets. The paper's central empirical claim—that model-agnostic, gradient-free methods (BONSAI, MIRAGE, TMD) dominate on both scalability and cross-architecture generalization—is strongly supported across all reported experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper builds directly on GCOND (Jin et al., 2022), the seminal formulation of graph condensation as a bi-level gradient-matching optimization, but argues that this formulation is fundamentally self-contradictory because it requires full-dataset training as a subroutine. The authors reformulate the problem (Definition 2.2) to impose byte-budget constraints, probabilistic cross-architecture generalization, and a compute-efficiency inequality, drawing on their own prior model-agnostic methods (BONSAI, MIRAGE, TMD) as exemplars of the advocated direction. The paper also situates graph condensation relative to vision dataset distillation, noting the field's divergence from VLM/diffusion-based approaches, and categorizes ~30 existing methods into four buckets defined by model-dependence and full-training requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a systemic contradiction in the field: the dominant gradient-matching paradigm is guaranteed to be more expensive than the full-graph training it aims to replace, yet this has gone largely unchallenged in benchmarking efforts. Concrete evidence—catastrophic architecture-transfer failures (GCOND: 77.3%→13.2% on GCN→GAT), a 5.7× byte-size discrepancy among methods claiming identical '1% compression,' and universal OOM/OOT on MAG240M for all gradient-based methods—makes a compelling case for reorienting research. Open questions include whether formal guarantees (worst-case ε under byte-budget B with confidence 1−δ) can be established, how to eliminate even the soft L-hop depth dependence in computation-tree methods, and how to extend efficient model-agnostic approaches to task-agnostic settings for broader reuse.
