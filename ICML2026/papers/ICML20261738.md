## Problem

Most existing graph alignment theory and algorithms rely solely on edge topology, yet real-world networks (gene co-expression, social platforms, academic databases) carry informative node features that are correlated under the same latent vertex permutation. Prior attributed alignment results are limited to binary/unweighted edges (Erdős-Rényi or stochastic block models), leaving the weighted-and-attributed regime without sharp information-theoretic characterizations or efficient algorithms with provable guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two weighted graphs with n vertices each (adjacency matrices with continuous, variance-normalized edge weights) and d-dimensional node feature vectors for each graph, where edge weights are correlated standard normals (correlation ρ) and features are correlated multivariate normals (correlation r) under a hidden bijection π*. The output is a vertex correspondence: a permutation π̂ mapping V(G₁) to V(G₂), evaluated by the fraction of correctly matched vertices (overlap). The algorithm operates on dense n×n matrices, with a stated time complexity of O((d + T(K+n))n²), demonstrated on graphs up to 3,000 nodes.

## Method

The paper first derives optimal information-theoretic thresholds via a maximum-likelihood estimator that scores each candidate permutation π by S_π = φ(ρ)Σ_e β_e(G₁)β_{π(e)}(G₂) + φ(r)Σ_v x_v·y_{π(v)}, where φ(x)=x/(1−x²) balances the edge and feature contributions. The sharp threshold for exact recovery is n·log(1/(1−ρ²)) + d·log(1/(1−r²)) ≥ (4+ε)log n, showing that structural and feature information combine additively. For the algorithmic side, QPAlign relaxes the NP-hard quadratic assignment problem over the Birkhoff polytope of doubly stochastic matrices, adding a regularization term μΣΠ_kj(1−Π_kj) that pushes the solution toward permutation vertices. The relaxed QP is solved by projected gradient descent with approximate Sinkhorn normalization (replacing exact Euclidean projections), followed by rounding via the Hungarian algorithm. A convergence guarantee of O(1/K) is established, and a uniform recovery guarantee holds for any λ ∈ (δ, 1−δ) under a mild balance assumption on the edge-to-vertex information ratio.

## Results

On the ACM-DBLP dataset (2,224 matched pairs), QPAlign with optimal λ selection achieves an alignment overlap of 0.3445, substantially outperforming the best edge-only baseline Grampa (0.0746) and the best joint baseline PARROT (0.0441). On the Douban Online-Offline dataset (1,118 matched pairs), QPAlign achieves 0.8370 overlap, competitive with PARROT (0.8462) and far exceeding FGW (0.2773) and REGAL (0.1118). Ablated versions confirm that combining edge and feature information is critical: QPAlign(λ=0) (feature-only) scores only 0.0004 on ACM-DBLP, while QPAlign(λ=1) (edge-only) reaches 0.2896, showing that the joint formulation yields a 19% relative improvement over the best single-source variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPAlign dominates all edge-only baselines (Grampa, IsoRank, Umeyama, GW) on both datasets, with the largest gap on ACM-DBLP where QPAlign(λ=1) scores 0.2896 versus Grampa's 0.0746. Against feature-only methods (MAP, kNN), QPAlign(λ=0) matches them exactly on ACM-DBLP (0.0004) but slightly exceeds them on Douban (0.0767 vs. 0.0725 for kNN), confirming that the QP relaxation does not degrade feature-only performance. The key advantage emerges in the joint setting: QPAlign(max) achieves 0.3445 on ACM-DBLP, a 7.8× improvement over the best joint baseline PARROT (0.0441) and a 191× improvement over FGW (0.0018). On Douban, PARROT narrowly edges out QPAlign(max) (0.8462 vs. 0.8370), likely because PARROT's anchor-based semi-supervised design benefits from the dataset's structure, though the paper notes PARROT was run in an ablated variant without anchors. REGAL and FGW are consistently outperformed by QPAlign across both datasets and both information settings, with FGW particularly weak on ACM-DBLP (0.0018) where the weighted edge structure is more informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—correlated Gaussian Wigner graph matching (Wu et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2023a) and Gaussian database alignment (Dai et al., 2019a)—into a single model where both weighted edges and node features are jointly observed under a shared latent permutation. Prior attributed graph alignment results (Zhang et al., 2024; Yang & Chung, 2024) were restricted to binary Erdős-Rényi or stochastic block model edges, whereas here the continuous Gaussian edge weights allow a clean MLE with a natural φ(ρ)/φ(r) weighting. Algorithmically, QPAlign extends the Birkhoff polytope QP relaxation of Fan et al. (2023a) by incorporating a feature-distance term and a boundary-pushing regularizer, replacing the need for an explicit L2 penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that structural and feature information are genuinely complementary: exact recovery is achievable when their combined information exceeds the 4 log n threshold even though neither source alone suffices, a synergy that is not captured by simply running edge-only or feature-only methods. The sharp partial-vs-exact recovery gap (2d vs. d in the feature term) clarifies why exact alignment is fundamentally harder than partial alignment in the attributed setting. Open questions include the statistical-computational gap (whether polynomial-time algorithms can match the information-theoretic threshold), the tightness of the Birkhoff relaxation for the attributed case, and extension to partially overlapping or non-Gaussian settings.
