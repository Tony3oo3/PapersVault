## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP are vulnerable to spurious correlations where frequent contextual cues (e.g., background, gender) dominate over semantically meaningful content, degrading worst-group accuracy. Existing mitigations either require fine-tuning or prompt engineering that undermines zero-shot generalisation, or rely on dataset-specific scaling calibrated on training data. The paper identifies that CLIP embeddings occupy an anisotropic ellipsoidal shell—common patterns cluster near the mean while rare but informative patterns are pushed to the periphery—creating a geometric bias that cosine similarity systematically ignores.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen image encoder's embedding of a test RGB image, three categories of text prompts (class-only, spurious-attribute, and concatenated group prompts), and small per-group reference sets of n image embeddings constructed once via deterministic feature-space herding from a training or validation split. It outputs a predicted class label and rescaled similarity scores for each group. No model parameters are updated; the only tunable hyperparameters are the neighbourhood size k for density estimation, the reference set size n, and the scaling exponent λ. A label-free variant (DAT*) infers spurious attribute membership at test time via zero-shot attribute-prompt classification, removing the need for explicit group annotations.

## Method

DAT rescales raw image-text cosine similarities by a local geometric density term computed per group. For each group (y,a), a compact reference set is built via greedy feature-space exemplar herding, and the local density of a query embedding is estimated using the Simplified Local Outlier Factor (SLOF) relative to that reference set. The corrected score is s̃_{y,a}(x) = s_{y,a}(x) / (D_{y,a}(z) + ε)^λ, which suppresses overconfident similarities in sparse regions while preserving dense, semantically consistent matches. Final prediction aggregates group-specific corrected scores with a class-marginal (attribute-averaged) score. Theoretically, under a Kent (Fisher-Bingham) distribution model for group embeddings, the paper proves that pure cosine scoring ignores the quadratic anisotropy term β[(γ₂ᵀz)² − (γ₃ᵀz)²], and that DAT's log-density correction reinstates this missing term, aligning the discriminant with Bayes-optimal log-likelihood ranking up to a bounded remainder.

## Results

On Waterbirds (the primary benchmark), DAT achieves the highest Worst-Group accuracy across all three CLIP backbones: 75.08% (ViT-B/32), 83.33% (ViT-L/14), and 75.08% (ResNet-50), while also attaining the lowest Gap (5.28, 6.42, and 8.75 respectively). On the medical COVID-19 dataset, DAT reaches 65.22% WG and 75.69% Avg, surpassing the strongest baseline (TIE) by over 13% in WG. On the multi-label FMoW dataset (62 classes, 5 spurious attributes), DAT achieves 27.75% WG and 31.19% Avg with a Gap of only 3.44, the best among all methods. The label-free variant DAT* remains competitive, particularly on ViT-based backbones, without requiring explicit spurious attribute annotations at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAT outperforms the strongest prior method, TIE (Lu et al., 2025), on Waterbirds by +3.7% WG (ViT-B/32), +4.5% WG (ViT-L/14), and +22.1% WG (ResNet-50), while also achieving the lowest Gap on two of three backbones. On CelebA, DAT matches or exceeds TIE on ViT-L/14 (+0.34% WG) and ResNet-50 (+5.47% WG), though TIE retains a slight edge on ViT-B/32 (82.63 vs 78.53 WG). Against TIE* (the label-free variant of TIE), DAT* achieves comparable or superior WG across all datasets, with the largest margin on ResNet-50 Waterbirds (+29.6% WG). DAT also clearly surpasses text-side methods (Ideal Words, Orth-Cali) and LLM-based methods (Perception CLIP, ROBOSHOT) on Waterbirds, COVID-19, and FMoW, with the gap most pronounced on the medical dataset where DAT gains +13% WG over TIE and +22% over Perception CLIP. The only notable exception is CelebA with ViT-B/32, where ROBOSHOT (80.52 WG) and TIE (82.63 WG) slightly exceed DAT (78.53 WG), though DAT still achieves the best Avg (87.09%) and DAT* matches on WG.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAT builds directly on the geometric analysis of CLIP embeddings by Levi & Gilboa (2025), who showed that frequent concepts cluster near the modality mean while rare concepts occupy peripheral regions of an anisotropic shell. It departs from prior zero-shot debiasing methods (TIE, PerceptionCLIP, Orth-Cali, ROBOSHOT) that rely on prompt engineering, linear projections, or LLM-generated cues, instead introducing a nonparametric local density correction (SLOF) computed from small group reference sets. The theoretical framing via the Kent (Fisher-Bingham) distribution connects the practical density rescaling to a principled correction of the anisotropy term that cosine similarity provably ignores, positioning DAT as a calibration mechanism rather than a representation-learning or prompt-design intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAT offers a simple, interpretable, and computationally efficient (~6× faster than TIE) calibration for zero-shot VLMs that requires no fine-tuning, no prompt engineering, and no spurious labels at test time (DAT*), making it broadly applicable across domains including medical imaging. The theoretical guarantee that DAT reinstates the anisotropy-sensitive log-likelihood term missing from cosine scoring provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why the correction works, beyond empirical tuning. Open questions remain around the sensitivity of density estimation to reference-set selection in high-dimensional spaces, the scalability of SLOF to very large group counts (e.g., FMoW's 62×5 groups), and whether adaptive or learned density proxies could reduce the need for hand-tuned hyperparameters (λ, k, n) across datasets.
