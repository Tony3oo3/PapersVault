## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video subtitle removal methods require explicit mask sequences during both training and inference, relying on external text detection or segmentation modules that introduce runtime overhead, fragility to detection failures, and temporal inconsistency. Additionally, auxiliary priors are typically applied with uniform spatial-temporal weighting, failing to adapt to the varying reliability of subtitle visibility across frames and regions. This paper addresses the gap of achieving truly end-to-end, mask-free inference for video subtitle removal without any external modules or per-frame annotations.

## Input / Output

At inference, CLEAR accepts only a subtitled video sequence (resized to 1280×720 or 720×1280) and a fixed text prompt, producing a clean subtitle-removed video via 5-step DDIM denoising in the Wan-VAE latent space (8× spatial compression, 16 channels). No binary masks, optical flow, or detection outputs are required at test time. During training, the method uses paired subtitled/clean video frames to generate pseudo-labels via per-frame statistical thresholding on pixel differences, but these are never needed at inference.

## Method

CLEAR employs a two-stage design: Stage I trains dual ResNet-50 encoders with orthogonality constraints and adversarial purification to extract disentangled subtitle and content features, from which a lightweight UNet decoder predicts coarse occlusion priors using only pixel-difference pseudo-labels. Stage II freezes the base Wan2.1-Fun-V1.1-1.3B diffusion transformer and injects LoRA adapters (rank 64, ~0.77% trainable parameters) plus a 2.1M-parameter occlusion head on the DiT encoder's middle layer. The head produces adaptive weights that modulate the diffusion loss via a product of spatial emphasis (scaled by a triangularly oscillating α) and focal-style difficulty weighting based on reconstruction error, with gradients flowing back through the predicted weights to enable self-correction without ground-truth masks. Joint optimization unifies context distillation (SmoothL1 toward Stage I priors), the weighted diffusion objective, and sparsity/KL regularization. At inference, the occlusion head operates internally to shape attention but never appears in the output—the adaptive strategy is fully absorbed into the LoRA-augmented representations, yielding a single-pass mask-free pipeline that generalizes zero-shot across six unseen languages.

## Results

On the Chinese subtitle test set (400 samples), CLEAR achieves 26.80 dB PSNR, 0.894 SSIM, and 20.37 VFID, outperforming the best mask-dependent baseline (MiniMax-Remover) by +6.77 dB PSNR and reducing VFID by 74.7%. Flow Variance drops to 0.029 (−93.0% vs. MiniMax-Remover's 0.415), indicating exceptional temporal stability. Inference runs at 4.86 s/frame with only 5 DDIM steps, comparable to MiniMax-Remover (4.90 s/frame) while requiring no external mask input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEAR dominates all three baselines across every metric. Against MiniMax-Remover (the strongest baseline at 20.03 dB PSNR), CLEAR gains +6.77 dB PSNR, +0.121 SSIM, and reduces VFID from 95.39 to 20.37 (−74.7%), while also achieving far better temporal consistency (TWE 1.227 vs. 4.222, TC 1.049 vs. 3.016). Against DiffuEraser (17.85 dB PSNR, 72.51 VFID), CLEAR improves PSNR by +8.95 dB and VFID by −71.9%, with LPIPS dropping from 0.458 to 0.101. Against ProPainter (17.24 dB PSNR, 98.46 VFID), the gap is even larger: +9.56 dB PSNR and −79.3% VFID. Notably, CLEAR is the only method that operates fully mask-free at inference; all three baselines require externally provided binary masks, which introduces detection/tracking dependencies that CLEAR eliminates entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEAR builds on LoRA-based parameter-efficient adaptation (Hu et al., 2022) applied to the Wan2.1 video diffusion transformer (Wan et al., 2025), but departs fundamentally from the mask-guided paradigm shared by DiffuEraser, MiniMax-Remover, and ProPainter, which all require explicit binary masks at both training and inference. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage decoupling: Stage I learns self-supervised occlusion priors via disentangled dual-encoder features with orthogonality constraints (borrowing from representation learning literature), while Stage II internalizes adaptive weighting into LoRA-augmented attention so that no external mask, detector, or segmentation module is needed at inference. This positions CLEAR as the first method to frame video subtitle removal as a fully end-to-end, mask-free generation problem rather than a conditional inpainting task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mask-free inference paradigm removes a critical deployment bottleneck: existing pipelines depend on fragile text-detection/tracking modules whose failures produce flicker and residual artifacts, and CLEAR eliminates this engineering dependency entirely. The zero-shot cross-lingual generalization (trained on Chinese, tested on six other languages) suggests the learned occlusion representations are language-agnostic, which could reduce the cost of multilingual content localization. However, the evaluation rests on a single self-collected dataset with no public benchmark, cross-lingual results are primarily qualitative, and the 4.86 s/frame inference time precludes real-time use; whether the approach extends to other occlusion types (watermarks, logos, human removal) or scales to longer videos remains open.
