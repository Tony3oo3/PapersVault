## Problem

Omni-modal LLMs that natively process streaming video, audio, and text suffer from prohibitive quadratic attention costs due to massively extended input sequences, making real-time deployment infeasible. Existing token compression strategies are suboptimal for this setting because they either impose static modality-centric priors (e.g., audio-dominant compression) or enforce uniform retention across all layers, ignoring the depth-dependent redundancy and layer-varying modality preferences that are intrinsic to pre-trained OmniLLM backbones.

## Input / Output

OmniFit operates as a training-free plug-in on top of any OmniLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen3-Omni, OmniVinci). During a one-time offline calibration phase (~1024 samples, <30 min on 8 GPUs), it profiles per-layer SVD effective ranks and attention-based modality preference scores. At inference, it takes the standard encoder outputs (visual tokens from video frames, audio tokens from waveforms, and text tokens) and progressively prunes them layer-by-layer according to the pre-computed budgets, ultimately producing a text response from the LLM backbone with only 20–40% of the original tokens retained.

## Method

OmniFit comprises two components. First, Layer-Adaptive Heterogeneity Profiling (LAHP) quantifies per-layer token redundancy via SVD effective rank (lower rank → higher redundancy → more aggressive pruning) and per-layer modality preference via normalized attention mass, then formulates a progressive retention schedule as a cumulative product of normalized effective ranks, scaled by a closed-form penalty factor ξ that guarantees total computational cost does not exceed the uniform-pruning baseline. Second, Alignment-Rectified Token Selection (ARTS) uses DPC-KNN to extract a small set of global anchors per modality immediately after the encoder, then scores each token by fusing its L2 norm (intra-modal significance) with its maximum cosine similarity to the opposing modality's anchors (cross-modal alignment), weighted by the layer's modality preference score. This reduces the selection complexity from O(N²) to O(ND), and the scores are computed once globally rather than re-evaluated per layer, yielding up to 42× speedup in the scoring step itself.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, OmniFit retains 98.68% of full-model average performance at a 20% token retention ratio, 99.32% at 30%, and 99.94% at 40%, across 10 benchmarks (7 audio-video + 3 video-only). Under extreme 5% compression, OmniFit surpasses the training-based EchoingPixels on VideoMME (55.9 vs. 55.7), WorldSense (41.1 vs. 40.9), and Daily-Omni (54.1 vs. 52.8). End-to-end efficiency gains include up to 2.31× prefilling speedup, 1.39× decoding speedup, and 2.5× VRAM reduction (35.7G → 14.5G on the 7B model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniFit consistently outperforms the SOTA omni-modal baseline OmniZip at every retention ratio: by 1.07% at 40%, 2.20% at 30%, and 4.27% at 20% average performance retention. Against unimodal methods extended to the omni-modal setting (FastVid and DyCoke), OmniFit leads by 15.12% and 15.73% respectively at 20% retention, confirming that modality-agnostic compression causes severe cross-modal information loss. At the 20% ratio, OmniFit (98.68%) is marginally below the training-based EchoingPixels (98.74%) on average, but at the extreme 5% compression regime OmniFit surpasses EchoingPixels on VideoMME (55.9 vs. 55.7), WorldSense (41.1 vs. 40.9), and Daily-Omni (54.1 vs. 52.8) while remaining training-free. Across model scales, OmniFit retains 97.28% on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, 95.87% on OmniVinci, and 93.46% on Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct (vs. OmniZip's 90.28%), demonstrating consistent superiority over all baselines regardless of architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token compression paradigm from vision-language models (FastVid, DyCoke) to the omni-modal (audio+video+text) setting, departing from two key limitations of prior omni-modal methods: OmniZip's audio-centric compression that discards visually critical but audio-weak tokens, and EchoingPixels' reliance on training-based dense cross-modal attention. Conceptually, it repurposes SVD-based effective rank estimation (from matrix analysis) and DPC-KNN clustering (from density peak detection) into a unified layer-adaptive profiling framework, while introducing a lightweight cross-modal alignment score that replaces the quadratic attention computation. The layer-wise heterogeneity observations build on prior findings in vision transformers (PyramidDrop, ZipVL) but are the first to systematically characterize them in the omni-modal context with modality-specific attention preferences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: enabling 30B omni-modal models to fit on a single 80GB GPU (previously OOM) and achieving 2.31× prefilling speedup with 98% performance retention at 20% token usage makes real-time omni-modal interaction feasible on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that cross-modal alignment is sparse and anchor-driven—requiring only linear-complexity scoring rather than dense attention—challenges the assumption that exhaustive global computation is necessary for cross-modal reasoning, potentially informing broader efficient inference strategies. Open questions include whether the SVD-based redundancy profiling captures non-linear redundancy patterns, how the method generalizes to future modalities (e.g., tactile, proprioceptive), and whether the calibration profiles remain valid under distribution shift in deployment.
