## Problem

Existing LLM watermark detectors are validated in offline, balanced-class settings, but real-world deployment involves sequential text streams where watermarked content is sparse (e.g., 5% of traffic) and mixed with human edits. Applying a fixed significance threshold in such sparse streams triggers the Base Rate Fallacy, causing false positives to accumulate linearly and driving the empirical FDR to ~0.50 despite a nominal 5% per-test error rate. No prior method provides provable online FDR control tailored to the streaming, sparse, and partially-edited nature of watermark monitoring.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of text documents D_1, D_2, … (each of length N tokens, default N=400) together with a secret watermark key ξ used to reconstruct per-token pseudo-random seeds via a hash function. For each arriving document it produces a calibrated document-level GoF p-value P_t (via Monte Carlo calibration with 2×10⁴ null resamples), a dynamically adjusted significance threshold α_t, and an irreversible binary decision (reject H₀ = flag as watermarked, or accept H₀ = flag as human-written). Decisions are made on arrival with no opportunity for revision, and the threshold α_t depends only on past rejection history, not on future documents.

## Method

LORD-GoF couples two components: a robust Goodness-of-Fit (GoF) detection statistic and the LORD alpha-investing wealth process for online error control. For each document, token-level pivotal statistics Y_{t,i} are computed from the watermark scheme (Gumbel-Max or Inverse Transform), standardized to uniform p-values via the probability integral transform, and aggregated into a single document-level p-value P_t using one of eight GoF tests (Anderson-Darling, Kuiper, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Cramér-von Mises, Watson, Neyman, Chi-squared, or Tr-GoF). The LORD procedure then maintains an α-wealth W(t) that is spent by α_t on non-rejections and replenished by (α − W₀) on rejections, with the threshold set as α_t = γ_{t−τ_last}·W(t−1) where {γ_j} is a non-increasing decay sequence (γ_j ∝ j^{−1.2}). This dynamic thresholding automatically suppresses rejections during long null runs (preventing FDR inflation in sparse streams) and recharges after discoveries (restoring power in denser regimes). The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that under conditions of null super-uniformity, stream independence, and monotone spending, the procedure guarantees oFDR(T) ≤ α for all stopping times T, while GoF tests retain detection power even when a fraction of tokens have been human-edited.

## Results

In long-term streaming (M=10,000, OPT-1.3B, Gumbel-Max, τ=0.5, π=0.05, ρ=0.7), all Naive-Fixed baselines converge to FDR ≈ 0.50, while LORD-GoF (Anderson-Darling or Chi-Squared) maintains FDR ≤ 0.05 with Power > 0.92. In the extreme sparsity regime (π=0.01, Qwen-2.5-3B, Inverse Transform), Naive methods inflate FDR to 0.85, whereas LORD-GoF suppresses FDR to 0.00 while retaining Power ≈ 0.89. Across all six model–watermark configurations and five temperatures, LORD-GoF consistently keeps FDR at or below the target α=0.05, while Naive-Fixed methods exceed FDR 0.40 in every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Naive-Fixed (static threshold α=0.05), which LORD-GoF decisively outperforms on FDR control: across all 30 model–watermark–temperature configurations, Naive-Fixed FDR ranges from 0.38 to 0.85, while LORD-GoF stays at or below 0.10 (typically ≤ 0.05). On power, LORD-GoF is competitive: at τ=0.5 on Sheared-LLaMA-2.7B, LORD-Chi achieves Power 0.805 with FDR 0.029 versus Naive-Chi's Power 0.927 with FDR 0.542, a modest power cost for a 17× reduction in FDR. Among the online FDR procedures compared in Appendix E (LORD++, SAFFRON, ADDIS, and e-value variants), the paper reports that LORD and LORD++ attain nearly identical FDR/Power profiles with an absolute power gap of at most 0.006, while SAFFRON and ADDIS offer marginal gains only when the non-null fraction is non-negligible (π > 0.3). The e-value route (e-LORD/e-SAFFRON via the calibrator e_t = 0.5/√p_t) provides a robustness extension under arbitrary dependence but does not substantially change the FDR/Power trade-off in the i.i.d. streaming setting tested here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected communities: online multiple hypothesis testing (LORD, SAFFRON, ADDIS from the alpha-investing literature) and LLM watermark detection (GoF-based pivotal statistics from He et al., 2025). The key conceptual move is recognizing that the standard offline, balanced-class evaluation protocol used in watermarking papers is fundamentally mismatched with real deployment, where watermarked content is sparse in a stream of mostly human text. By combining GoF document-level p-values with LORD's dynamic alpha-wealth thresholding, the paper is the first to bring provable online FDR control into the watermarking detection pipeline, departing from the static-threshold paradigm that dominates prior watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical practical gap: standard watermark detectors evaluated with fixed α=0.05 thresholds will produce FDR of 0.50+ in realistic sparse streams, rendering them unreliable for API monitoring or content moderation. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) and extensive empirical validation across three models, two watermark schemes, five temperatures, and three attack types make this a deployable solution. Open questions include whether the independence assumption (Condition A3) holds under correlated document streams, how the framework extends to multi-scheme or key-unknown settings, and whether the setting-dependent GoF statistic choice can be automated. The shifting optimality of different GoF tests across temperature regimes also suggests a practical need for adaptive statistic selection.
