## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for the SAG, SAGA, and IAG variance-reduced algorithms are entirely disparate: SAG's proof is notoriously complex and requires computer-aided verification, SAGA relies on a different Lyapunov function, and IAG uses a fundamentally different deterministic analysis yielding a much slower rate. No unified framework has previously explained the dynamics of both biased (SAG) and unbiased (SAGA) estimators, nor have high-probability bounds been established for these algorithms.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a finite-sum objective f(x) = (1/N)Σf_i(x) where each f_i is L-smooth and f is μ-strongly convex, along with a sampling scheme (I.I.D. uniform or a time-homogeneous ergodic Markov chain). It produces a high-probability linear convergence bound of the form f(x_K) − f(x*) ≤ 6LB²(1 − 1/(64τκ))^K holding with probability at least 1−δ, where the staleness parameter τ = ⌈(8N/3)log(NK/δ)⌉ under I.I.D. sampling, or τ = ⌈(88t_mix/π_min)log(NK/δ)⌉ under Markov sampling. The step-size is set to α = 1/(16Lτ), and the bound requires K > τ iterations.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two modular steps. First, Bernstein's inequality (or its Markov-chain variant from Paulin 2015) is used to show that with probability at least 1−δ, every component gradient is at most τ = Õ(N) iterations stale, defining a 'good event' G on which SAG and SAGA behave as delayed gradient descent. Second, a novel Lyapunov function V_k = f(x_k) − f(x*) + Lα²W_k is constructed, where W_k = Σ_{j=1}^{τ}(τ−j+1)‖g_{k−j}‖² is a weighted window of past gradient norms with linearly decaying weights. The weight τ−j+1 is chosen because the stale gradient ‖g_{k−j}‖² appears in exactly τ−j+1 future descent inequalities over the window, and this precise accounting yields a one-step contractive recursion V_{k+1} ≤ (1−αμ/4)V_k. A burn-in analysis bounds V_τ ≤ 3LB², completing the proof. The same argument applies verbatim to IAG since its deterministic cyclic sampling satisfies the bounded-staleness condition with probability 1, immediately improving IAG's rate from O(exp(−K/(κ²N²))) to O(exp(−K/(κN))).

## Results

The paper establishes the first high-probability linear convergence bound for both SAG and SAGA: f(x_K) − f(x*) ≤ 6LB²(1 − 1/(64τκ))^K with probability ≥ 1−δ, where τ = ⌈(8N/3)log(NK/δ)⌉. As a byproduct, the IAG rate is improved from the prior O(exp(−K/(κ²N²))) to O(exp(−K/(κN))), removing the quadratic dependence on both the condition number κ and the number of components N. For non-convex objectives, the paper obtains a high-probability O(τ/K) rate on the average squared gradient norm, and extends all results to Markov sampling with τ scaling as (t_mix/π_min)·log(NK/δ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are against prior convergence guarantees. For SAG, the prior in-expectation rate from Schmidt et al. (2017) was O((1 − min{1/(16κ), 1/(8N)})^K), but its proof required computer-aided verification; the new high-probability bound matches the rate up to constants while being significantly simpler. For SAGA, Defazio et al. (2014a) gave a similar in-expectation rate, but no high-probability bound existed before this work. The most concrete improvement is for IAG: Gurbuzbalaban et al. (2017) established O(exp(−K/(κ²N²))), while the new unified analysis yields O(exp(−K/(κN))), a quadratic improvement in both κ and N. The paper also provides the first high-probability bounds under Markov sampling for SAG/SAGA, where the staleness parameter τ inflates by a factor of t_mix/π_min relative to the I.I.D. case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the convergence analyses of SAG (Roux et al., 2012; Schmidt et al., 2017), SAGA (Defazio et al., 2014), and IAG (Blatt et al., 2007; Gurbuzbalaban et al., 2017), which were previously analyzed using fundamentally different proof techniques—SAG required computer-aided polynomial verification, SAGA used a distinct Lyapunov function, and IAG relied on a separate deterministic analysis. The key conceptual departure is reframing all three algorithms as delayed versions of gradient descent with bounded staleness, then applying a single concentration argument (Bernstein's inequality) and a novel windowed Lyapunov function to all of them simultaneously. The paper also extends the analysis to Markov sampling via Paulin's (2015) concentration bound for ergodic chains, a setting where no prior high-probability bounds existed for these algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first high-probability convergence bounds for SAG and SAGA fill a notable gap, as prior guarantees held only in expectation and could not capture tail behavior. The dramatic improvement in the IAG rate—from O(exp(−K/(κ²N²))) to O(exp(−K/(κN)))—corrects a pessimistic picture that had motivated development of more complex deterministic methods like DIAG. The modular proof structure (concentration → bounded staleness → windowed Lyapunov) is explicitly designed to extend to other single-loop VR methods, though the authors note the rates are slightly worse than existing in-expectation bounds, and the dependence on the horizon K in the step-size choice remains an open practical limitation.
