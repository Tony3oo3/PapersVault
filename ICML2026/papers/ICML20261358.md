## Problem

Existing long-video understanding paradigms face a fundamental efficiency-accuracy trade-off: tokenized VLMs suffer from quadratic attention cost and limited context windows that degrade with video length, while agentic RAG-based systems introduce multi-minute latency per query through iterative planning and retrieval loops. Neither approach achieves the synergy of rapid, interactive response times and persistent, stable access to a video's full semantic content that real-world multi-turn applications demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system consumes only a text query Q and a pre-computed NKR (a LoRA adapter of rank 256 attached to the attention layers of a frozen Qwen3-14B backbone); no video frames or external database are loaded, yielding zero additional memory overhead beyond the base model. The NKR is produced offline via a one-time Agentic Knowledge Distillation pass over the full video, which for a 1-hour video takes 2–3 hours of data synthesis and ~2 hours of training on 4×A100 GPUs. The output is a free-form text answer generated directly by the VLM with the NKR mounted, with an end-to-end latency of ~0.33 s per query on a single A100.

## Method

The paper introduces a Neural Knowledge Representation (NKR) that compresses an entire video's semantic content into a compact, swappable set of LoRA weights, analogous to how NeRFs encode a 3D scene into network parameters. The weights are optimized through Agentic Knowledge Distillation (AKD), an automated pipeline that (1) segments the video into multi-scale clips and uses GPT-4.1 to synthesize dense multi-granularity descriptions plus entity summaries, (2) deploys a ReAct-style agent powered by o3 over a RAG-indexed clip database to generate ~1000 complex video-level QA pairs and o4-mini for ~10 clip-level QA pairs per clip, and (3) trains the LoRA adapter with a mixed objective combining next-token prediction on descriptions (memorization) and supervised fine-tuning on QA pairs (query-aware reasoning) at a 2:8 ratio. At inference, the NKR is simply merged into the frozen VLM, so the model answers queries in a single forward pass without any video token processing or iterative retrieval, decoupling inference cost from video duration entirely.

## Results

On LVBench (103 videos, 117 hours total, 1549 questions), Qwen3-NKR-14B-r256 achieves 48.8% overall accuracy with an inference time of 0.33 s/query and zero additional memory overhead. This matches GPT-4o (48.9%) and VideoRAG (49.2%) in accuracy while reducing end-to-end latency by over two orders of magnitude (0.33 s vs. 31.7–180 s). On videos longer than one hour, NKR's accuracy drops by only 0.3% (48.9% → 48.6%), compared to 2.9–6.7% drops for all other baselines, confirming its duration-independent scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against tokenized baselines, NKR (48.8%) matches GPT-4o (48.9%) and outperforms GPT-4.1 (45.8%), VideoLLaMA3-7B (45.3%), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (43.8%), and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (47.6%) in overall accuracy, while being 91×–195× faster (0.33 s vs. 30–64.5 s) and requiring zero extra memory. Against AdaReTaKe-7B, NKR trails by 2.4 points in accuracy (48.8% vs. 51.2%) but is 136× faster and uses no KV-cache. Among agentic baselines, NKR slightly underperforms VideoRAG (48.8% vs. 49.2%) yet is 273× faster (0.33 s vs. 90 s) with no external database; it substantially outperforms VCA (48.8% vs. 41.3%). Deep Video Discovery-o3 achieves the highest accuracy (74.2%) but at 545× the latency (180 s), positioning it for offline analysis rather than interactive use. NKR's unique advantage is its near-constant inference cost regardless of video duration, with only a 0.3% accuracy drop on >1-hour videos versus 2.9–6.7% for all other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms for long-video understanding—tokenized VLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, AdaReTaKe) and agentic retrieval systems (VideoRAG, Deep Video Discovery)—by proposing a third paradigm: encoding a video's knowledge into a compact set of LoRA weights (the NKR) that are mounted onto a frozen VLM at inference. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), where a 3D scene is compressed into network weights, but applied to semantic video content rather than geometric signals. The Agentic Knowledge Distillation process repurposes Deep Video Discovery's ReAct-style agent as a data-synthesis tool, inverting its role from an inference-time reasoner to an offline teacher that generates the supervision signals used to train the NKR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NKR paradigm decouples inference cost from video duration, offering a path to truly interactive long-video understanding (0.33s per query, zero extra memory) that neither tokenized nor agentic methods can match. However, the accuracy gap to the strongest agentic baseline (48.8% vs. 74.2% for DVD-o3) and the reliance on textual-only descriptions (a 'textual bottleneck' the authors acknowledge) limit its applicability to scenarios where latency and memory are the binding constraints rather than peak accuracy. Open questions include whether multi-modal distillation (beyond text) can close the accuracy gap, whether NKR can be incrementally updated as new content is appended, and whether the paradigm extends to other media types beyond video.
