## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction policies for long-context LLM inference rely on empirical heuristics—attention scores, L2 norms, or pairwise key dissimilarity—without a unified theoretical objective that explains why they work or how to design better ones. This paper asks whether a single information-theoretic principle can rationalize these disparate strategies and guide the construction of a principled eviction mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265424/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full KV cache (key and value vectors for all cached tokens), the sequence of historical query vectors observed during decoding, and a fixed cache budget B specifying the maximum number of entries to retain. It produces a retained index set C of size B, from which the compressed cache Z_C is formed for subsequent attention computation. The value vectors serve directly as output-direction proxies (u_i = v_i), and the mean of historical queries provides a lightweight importance prior, so no additional model parameters or forward passes are required beyond what standard inference already computes.

## Method

The core contribution is a linear-Gaussian surrogate that models attention as a communication channel from future queries to outputs, yielding a closed-form mutual information objective (Theorem 3.2): I(q;Y|Z_C) = ½ log det(I + Σ_noise⁻¹ U_C K_C Λ_Q K_Cᵀ U_Cᵀ). This unifies existing heuristics as different approximations of the same capacity-maximization principle. CAPKV then approximates the log-determinant objective via statistical leverage scores: it constructs a capacity matrix A = I + Σ w_i v_i v_iᵀ in value space, where w_i = exp(k_iᵀ μ_q · τ) encodes query-alignment importance, and assigns each KV pair an eviction score s_i = w_i v_iᵀ A⁻¹ v_i. The top-B entries by score are retained, giving a deterministic, greedy approximation to the optimal information-preservation subset. The full procedure runs in O(Nd² + d³) time, avoiding higher-order dependence on context length while remaining negligible relative to attention computation.

## Results

On LongBench (16 tasks) with Qwen3-8B, CAPKV achieves an average score of 49.51 at 25% compression and 36.57 at 90% compression, outperforming all baselines at every compression ratio. On Qwen3-14B, CAPKV reaches 50.80 (25% CR) and 39.53 (90% CR), again leading all methods. Under ultra-long contexts (32K–128K) on LongBench-v2, CAPKV scores 47.9 at 75% compression versus 45.4 for EA and 38.7 for SnapKV, approaching the uncompressed baseline of 44.5. On the AIME25 reasoning benchmark with Nemotron-7B, CAPKV exhibits the most graceful accuracy degradation as cache budget shrinks, maintaining a smoother accuracy–budget curve than all heuristic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPKV consistently outperforms all five baselines (EA, KeyDiff, KNorm, SnapKV, Sink) across every compression ratio on both Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B. The largest margins are against structure-aware methods: at 90% compression on Qwen3-8B, CAPKV scores 36.57 versus 20.95 for KeyDiff and 20.09 for KNorm, a gap of roughly 15–16 points. Against the strongest baseline EA, CAPKV leads by 2.26 points at 75% compression (44.88 vs. 42.62) and by 3.64 points on Qwen3-14B at 90% compression (39.53 vs. 35.89). Against SnapKV, the closest competitor, CAPKV gains 2.82 points at 75% compression on Qwen3-8B (44.88 vs. 42.06) and 2.40 points at 90% on Qwen3-14B (39.53 vs. 37.13). On ultra-long contexts (LongBench-v2, 32K–128K), CAPKV's advantage widens under aggressive compression: at 75% CR it scores 47.9 versus 45.4 (EA) and 38.7 (SnapKV), and at 90% CR it reaches 44.8 versus 43.7 (EA) and 42.9 (SnapKV). Sink is not included in the primary Qwen3 tables but appears in supplementary Llama3.1-8B and Mistral-7B results, where CAPKV also leads at all compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the heuristic-driven lineage of KV cache eviction (SnapKV, KeyDiff, KNorm, Expected Attention, H2O) by importing the Information Bottleneck principle and linear-Gaussian channel models from information theory to derive a unified log-determinant capacity objective. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes classical D-optimal experimental design (statistical leverage scores) as a practical approximation to the derived objective, positioning all existing eviction strategies as implicit approximations of the same mutual-information-maximization goal. The theoretical framing is novel in this domain, though each individual component (IB, leverage scores, Gaussian channel capacity) is well-established in its home field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical lens for KV cache eviction, explaining *why* diverse heuristics work and where they fail, which should guide future method design beyond ad-hoc scoring rules. The strong capacity-performance correlation (Spearman ρ 0.75–0.85) empirically validates the framework, and the method's compatibility with quantization and ultra-long contexts (32K–128K) suggests practical deployability. Key limitations include the linear-Gaussian surrogate, which cannot capture softmax nonlinearities or cross-head interactions, and the reliance on a mean-query prior that may be suboptimal under highly non-stationary query distributions. Open questions include whether richer surrogate models (controlled nonlinearity, multi-head coupling) can tighten the gap between the theoretical objective and actual attention dynamics.
