## Problem

Existing time series forecasting methods require sufficient training data and often rely on both source and target data for cross-domain transfer, raising privacy and efficiency concerns. This paper introduces source-free time series forecasting (SF-TSF), where a pretrained source model must adapt to a sparse target domain using only its parameters—no source data access—while also addressing the challenge of leveraging LLMs as proxy forecasters without their hallucinations distorting predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265336/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse target time series window (lookback length 336), the frozen parameters of a source model pretrained on a different domain (e.g., ETTh1), and a frozen LLM backbone (OFA) as a proxy forecaster. It produces future time series values at prediction horizons of 96, 192, or 336 steps. The target model is a lightweight Time-Series Feature Extractor (TSFE) with patching (patch length 16, stride 8), stacked self-attention and feed-forward blocks, and a linear output layer, totaling only 1.842M parameters.

## Method

TimeID comprises three coupled modules. First, Invariant Disentangled Feature Learning (IDFL) decomposes input series into seasonal and trend components via moving average, applies stochastic dropout to create two decompositions, and enforces invariance at both the representation level (gradient-based percentile masking to retain component-invariant features) and the gradient level (minimizing Euclidean distance between inter-branch gradients), with a Fourier transform module providing frequency-domain consistency supervision. Second, Proxy Denoising treats the frozen LLM as a noisy proxy and dynamically corrects its systematic bias by subtracting α·(θ_s(x) − θ_t(x)) from the LLM output, where the source-target model disagreement serves as an empirical noise estimate—requiring no extra parameters. Third, Knowledge Distillation establishes a bidirectional loop: the denoised LLM predictions supervise the target model via MSE, while the target model's own predictions feed back to stabilize the proxy correction, preventing distribution drift. The overall objective combines six loss terms (forecasting, invariant prediction, cross-domain prediction consistency, representation invariance, gradient invariance, and distillation) with tunable trade-off weights.

## Results

On the primary source-free forecasting benchmark (ETTh1 → 6 target datasets, PL ∈ {96, 192, 336}), TimeID achieves the lowest average MSE and MAE across all 18 tasks, outperforming the strongest baselines Time-LLM and OFA by 4.98%/4.39% (MSE) and 2.64%/3.21% (MAE) on average. The largest gains appear on ETTh1→Weather (MSE 0.169 at PL=96, beating Time-LLM's 0.176 by 4.0%) and ETTh1→ETTh2 (MSE 0.280, beating Time-LLM's 0.324 by 13.6%). Against time-series foundation models (Chronos, MOIRAI, Moment, UniTS) in a 30% few-shot setting, TimeID wins on 4 of 6 datasets, with the best margin on Weather (MSE 0.169 vs. 0.190 for Chronos, an 11.1% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeID outperforms all 12 classical/LLM baselines on 5 of 6 target datasets at PL=96, with the most decisive wins on Weather (MSE 0.169 vs. 0.176 for Time-LLM and 0.195 for OFA) and ETTh2 (0.280 vs. 0.324 for Time-LLM). On Electricity, TimeID (0.170) narrowly beats Time-LLM (0.171) and OFA (0.173); on Traffic it ties iTransformer and Time-LLM at 0.452. Against the four time-series foundation models in the 30% few-shot setting (Table 2), TimeID wins on ETTh2 (0.280 vs. 0.290 Moment), ETTm1 (0.359 vs. 0.405 Chronos), ETTm2 (0.177 vs. 0.178 UniTS), and Weather (0.169 vs. 0.190 Chronos), but loses on Electricity (0.170 vs. 0.175 MOIRAI) and Traffic (0.452 vs. 0.368 MOIRAI), where the massive multi-domain pretraining of foundation models captures complex spatial-temporal dependencies that a single-source adapted model struggles to match. Notably, several baselines (TimeKAN, SimpleTM, TimesNet, TimeMixer, WPMixer, FEDformer) suffer catastrophic degradation on Weather and Electricity (MSE > 0.7), while TimeID and the LLM-based methods (OFA, Time-LLM) remain stable, highlighting the importance of cross-domain generalization in source-free settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends source-free domain adaptation (SFDA), previously studied primarily in computer vision and NLP (SHOT, NRC), to the time series forecasting domain, where it had been explored only for imputation tasks. It builds directly on the proxy denoising mechanism from Tang et al. (ICLR 2025) for SFDA, repurposing it to correct LLM hallucinations in time series prediction. The invariant disentangled feature learning module draws on seasonal-trend decomposition (DLinear, FEDformer) and invariant representation learning (Parascandolo et al., 2021), but introduces a novel dual-branch architecture that enforces invariance at both representation and gradient levels via stochastic decomposition. The use of OFA as a frozen LLM proxy forecaster, calibrated by source-target model disagreement, departs from prior LLM-for-time-series work (Time-LLM, TimeCMA) that typically fine-tunes or reprograms the LLM directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of source-free time series forecasting, addressing a practically important scenario where source data is inaccessible due to privacy or regulatory constraints. The proxy denoising mechanism offers a general recipe for leveraging LLMs as noisy oracles in domain-adaptive settings without being misled by hallucinations, which could transfer to other LLM-assisted prediction tasks. However, the zero-shot ablation (Table 6) reveals that TimeID degrades substantially without adaptation data, and it still trails foundation models on high-dimensional datasets like Traffic, suggesting the approach is most effective in few-shot (≥5% target data) regimes. Open questions include whether the invariant feature learning generalizes to other time series tasks (classification, anomaly detection) and whether the proxy denoising strategy scales to truly large LLMs beyond OFA.
