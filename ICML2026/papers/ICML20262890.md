## Problem

MLIPs can achieve low energy and force regression errors while still producing non-smooth potential energy surfaces (PES) with artificial minima, discontinuities, or spurious forces, leading to unstable molecular dynamics trajectories that standard benchmarks miss. Existing evaluations rely on computationally expensive MD simulations or test sets limited to near-equilibrium states, leaving far-from-equilibrium smoothness largely unassessed. The paper asks whether a low-cost, targeted probe of PES smoothness can both validate MLIP reliability and serve as an actionable signal for iterative architectural design.

## Input / Output

For BSCT evaluation, the input is a set of 485 molecules (from SPICE test structures) with a selected bridge bond systematically displaced along its axis by parameter α, spanning 0.5× to 2× the sum of covalent radii, with 100 DFT reference points per scan. The MLIP produces per-atom energies and forces at each displaced geometry; the FSD metric is then computed as the maximum absolute difference of the log-derivative of the MLIP-to-DFT force-norm ratio along α. For the MinDScAIP model itself, input is a 3D point cloud of atomic positions (with element types), and output is a scalar system energy (sum of per-atom predictions) plus forces obtained either by direct regression or by automatic differentiation of the energy.

## Method

The paper introduces two coupled contributions: (1) the Bond Smoothness Characterization Test (BSCT) with the Force Smoothness Deviation (FSD) metric, which probes 1D bond deformations where the true PES is known to be smooth, making MLIP artifacts (artificial extrema, inflection points) directly detectable via the log-derivative of the force-norm ratio; and (2) the MinDScAIP architecture, a Transformer-like backbone that generalizes Swin-Transformer's shifted-window attention to directed graphs by alternating in-neighborhood and out-neighborhood attention over a kNN graph. A key architectural innovation is the Differentiable k-Nearest Neighbors (Diff-kNN) algorithm, which replaces the hard ranking in standard kNN with a sigmoid-based soft ranking and a smooth envelope function for edge selection, ensuring the potential remains fully differentiable so forces are conservative. BSCT is then used in-the-loop to identify and regularize three sources of nonlinearity—Gaussian smearing width, weight decay, and attention temperature—systematically reducing FSD from 97.4 to 43.2 while maintaining competitive energy/force MAE and energy conservation in NVE simulations.

## Results

On the SPICE test set, the best MinDScAIP configuration (Smear. & Temp., 60M params) achieves 0.12 meV/atom energy MAE and 2.94 meV/Å force MAE, with an FSD of 43.2 1/Å on BSCT and NVE energy drift of 0.708 meV/atom. On Matbench Discovery, MinDScAIP-60M reaches F1 = 0.833, κ_SRME = 0.691, and RMSD = 0.086. The FSD metric strongly correlates with MD stability: reducing FSD from 97.4 to 43.2 decreases the maximum 10 fs kinetic temperature jump at 2000 K from 9734 K to 490 K, while FSD computation takes ~40 min vs. ~40 hours for MD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MACE (4.7M), MinDScAIP with smoothness designs (Smear. & Temp.) substantially outperforms on both energy MAE (0.12 vs. 0.79 meV/atom) and force MAE (2.94 vs. 14.3 meV/Å), while also achieving a lower FSD (43.2 vs. 62.1 1/Å), indicating better far-from-equilibrium smoothness; NVE energy drift is comparable (0.708 vs. 0.691 meV/atom). Against GemNet-T, MinDScAIP wins decisively on force MAE (2.94 vs. 7.11 meV/Å) and NVE energy drift (0.708 vs. 110.6 meV/atom, reflecting GemNet-T's non-conservative behavior), but GemNet-T achieves a lower FSD (33.8 vs. 43.2 1/Å), suggesting it has fewer bond-scan artifacts despite its poor energy conservation. On Matbench Discovery, MinDScAIP-60M (F1 = 0.833, κ_SRME = 0.691) is competitive with top models like EquiformerV3+DeNS-MP (F1 = 0.863, κ_SRME = 0.275) and MatRIS-10M-MP (F1 = 0.847, κ_SRME = 0.489), though it trails on κ_SRME. In inference efficiency, MinDScAIP-60M is only slightly slower than MACE-MP-16M (1.15 vs. 1.49 M steps/day at 216 atoms) while being order-of-magnitude faster and more memory-efficient than eSEN-30M-OAM (which OOMs beyond 216 atoms on an 80 GB A100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Swin-Transformer's shifted window attention to directed molecular graphs via alternating in/out-neighborhood attention, and builds on the kNN graph construction of Qu & Krishnapriyan (2024) by making it differentiable (Diff-kNN). Conceptually, it departs from the MLIP community's recent use of 'smoothness' as bounded PES derivatives (evaluated via costly MD) by adopting the quantum chemistry definition of chemical smoothness—absence of spurious extrema and inflection points—and targeting the far-from-equilibrium regime specifically. It positions BSCT as complementary to existing benchmarks (OMol25, Matbench Discovery, MDR phonon) rather than a replacement, filling the gap of efficient far-from-equilibrium smoothness evaluation that neither MD simulations nor near-equilibrium test sets address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSCT and FSD provide a ~40-minute diagnostic that predicts what ~40 hours of MD would reveal, making physical reliability assessment tractable during iterative model development. The in-the-loop design paradigm—using BSCT to identify specific architectural nonlinearities (attention spikes, radial feature sharpness) and targeting them with temperature-controlled attention or wider smearing—offers a practical workflow for MLIP developers beyond post-hoc evaluation. Key limitations remain: 1D bond scans are necessary but not sufficient for full high-dimensional PES smoothness, sensitivity to DFT functional/basis mismatches is untested, and the approach has only been validated on one backbone architecture. Future work on multi-directional perturbations and BSCT for OMol25 would strengthen the framework.
