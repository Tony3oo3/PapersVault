## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models frequently hallucinate objects because visual features become suppressed by dominant language priors in deeper network layers. Existing mitigation strategies either require expensive retraining (RLHF/DPO) or act as post-hoc logit filters (contrastive decoding), failing to resolve the underlying representational conflict. Moreover, prior latent steering methods (e.g., VTI) use entangled vectors that amplify language priors alongside visual signals, causing model collapse at high intervention intensities.

## Input / Output

REVIS takes a standard RGB image and a text query as input to an LVLM and produces grounded text descriptions or VQA answers. The method requires a one-time calibration phase using 100 paired image-caption examples from Nullu (for vector extraction) and 100 COCO images with binary existence queries (for layer selection), after which inference proceeds with negligible overhead. The intervention is sparse: a risk score computed from the cosine similarity between the current hidden state and the purified visual vector gates whether steering is applied at each decoding step.

## Method

REVIS is a training-free sparse latent steering framework with three stages. First, it constructs a purified visual vector by applying Gram-Schmidt orthogonal projection to remove the language-prior component from the raw visual direction (difference between image-conditioned and no-image hidden states), ensuring intervention enhances visual grounding without amplifying hallucination-inducing priors. Second, it identifies the optimal intervention layer via a backward search over a calibration set, selecting the deepest layer where the purified vector geometrically separates factual from hallucinated states. Third, during inference, a dynamic risk-aware gate monitors the instantaneous hallucination risk score and injects the steering vector only when the representation drifts beyond a percentile-based safety threshold, preserving model capability on well-grounded tokens while surgically correcting drift at the precise depth where visual suppression occurs.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL, REVIS reduces CHAIR_S from 31.00% (Regular) to 25.00%, a 19.35% relative reduction in hallucination rate, while simultaneously improving POPE-Adversarial F1 from 86.09% to 87.63%. Critically, REVIS also improves general reasoning: MME Overall rises from 2327.52 to 2345.30 and MM-Vet from 70.18 to 72.16, whereas the closest latent-steering baseline VTI degrades MM-Vet to 56.38. Inference latency remains negligible at ~0.025s per token, matching Regular decoding and halving the cost of contrastive methods like VCD (~0.05s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVIS outperforms all five baselines on both hallucination and discriminative metrics. Against VCD (contrastive decoding), REVIS achieves 25.00% CHAIR_S vs. 34.00% and 87.63% POPE-Adversarial F1 vs. 86.19%, while VCD actually worsens hallucination relative to Regular decoding. Against M3ID (mutual-information grounding), REVIS improves CHAIR_S by 6 points (25% vs. 31%) and POPE-Adversarial F1 by 1.54 points. Against ONLY (one-layer attention intervention), REVIS reduces CHAIR_S by 8 points (25% vs. 33%) and gains 1.65 F1 on POPE-Adversarial. Against AGLA (global-local attention assembly), REVIS achieves 4 points lower CHAIR_S (25% vs. 29%) and 1.96 F1 higher on POPE-Adversarial. Most notably, against VTI (the closest latent-steering method), REVIS reduces CHAIR_S by 10 points (25% vs. 35%) and preserves MM-Vet at 72.16 versus VTI's catastrophic 56.38, confirming that orthogonal projection eliminates the reasoning degradation inherent in entangled steering vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVIS extends the latent steering paradigm introduced by VTI (Liu et al., 2024c) and the broader activation steering / representation engineering literature (Zou et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023a), but departs critically by identifying that prior steering vectors are entangled with language priors, causing model collapse at high intervention intensity. The key methodological advance is a Gram-Schmidt orthogonal projection that decouples the pure visual direction from the hallucination-inducing language prior, combined with a calibration-based sparse intervention at a single optimal layer rather than dense multi-layer injection. The counterfactual input construction protocol for isolating mechanistic states is adapted from Nullu (Yang et al., 2025b), and the risk-aware dynamic gating distinguishes REVIS from static steering approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REVIS offers a training-free, near-zero-overhead solution to LVLM hallucination that works across seven models spanning Qwen, LLaVA, and InternVL families, making it immediately deployable without retraining or auxiliary models. The orthogonal projection insight provides a clean geometric explanation for why naive steering fails (entanglement with language priors causes collapse) and how to fix it, which may generalize to other representation-engineering tasks. Limitations include reliance on the base model's representational capacity (cannot fix absolute perceptual blindness) and evaluation only up to 32B parameters; scalability to 70B+ models and whether the 'image.jpg' phenomenon at extreme intensities reveals a deeper structure of visual modality encoding remain open questions.
