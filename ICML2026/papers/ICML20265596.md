## Problem

Existing representation intervention methods for LLM alignment (e.g., activation steering, ReFT) apply a single fixed low-rank edit uniformly to all inputs, implicitly assuming the linear representation hypothesis holds globally. The paper demonstrates empirically that optimal intervention directions and magnitudes are highly heterogeneous across samples, and that indiscriminate always-on editing degrades general capabilities on benign inputs that do not require correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265596/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a frozen LLM, intervening at a single layer-position pair (l*, p*) on the hidden state of the last prompt token. Before generation, a short prompt-only probe pass computes per-layer propagation-response energy to decide whether to intervene and which expert to route to; no additional decoding pass is introduced. For non-applicable inputs (energy below threshold), the model falls back entirely to the frozen base model, producing an unmodified response; for applicable inputs, the selected adapter's low-rank update is injected and the model generates a steered response.

## Method

MARI combines two mechanisms at a single injection site. First, a Competitive Multi-Adapter module trains K lightweight rank-r low-rank adapters with winner-take-gradient routing (each training example is assigned to the adapter with the lowest supervised loss, and gradients flow only to that adapter), encouraging specialization; at inference, a parameter-free entropy-based router selects the most confident adapter, inducing a piecewise-affine actuation over the input space. Second, an Energy-Based Gate uses a separate small-rank probe module (Energy Calibrator) trained with an off-subspace PCA regularizer to produce a label-free propagation-response energy score E(x); a threshold τ_E calibrated on a held-out control set triggers full intervention only when E(x) ≥ τ_E, otherwise suppressing actuation entirely (α_safe = 0). The off-subspace penalty constrains the probe update to lie within an in-field PCA subspace, empirically improving separability between applicable and non-applicable inputs.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-32B, MARI achieves 81.94% on TruthfulQA MC1 and 70.59% on MC2, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ReFT (55.60% / 48.90%) and NL-ITI (47.92% / 66.23%). On the bias benchmark BBQ, MARI reaches 0.876 accuracy versus 0.821 for ReFT and 0.772 for NL-ITI. Crucially, MARI preserves or improves general capabilities: MMLU rises from 83.5 (vanilla) to 84.2, ARC-E from 77.8 to 78.5, and ARC-C from 59.3 to 60.0, whereas baselines like ReFT show no such gains. On Llama-3-8B, MARI similarly leads with 61.81% TQA MC1 (vs. 52.51% ReFT) and 0.792 BBQ (vs. 0.637 ReFT) while maintaining MMLU at 66.6 (vs. 65.9 vanilla).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARI decisively outperforms all four baselines on TruthfulQA MC1 across every model family and scale tested. On Qwen2.5-32B, MARI (81.94%) surpasses ReFT (55.60%) by 26.3 points, NL-ITI (47.92%) by 34.0 points, ITI (46.78%) by 35.2 points, and CAA (44.77%) by 37.2 points. On BBQ, MARI (0.876) leads ReFT (0.821) by 5.5 points and NL-ITI (0.772) by 10.4 points, while CAA and ITI offer only marginal gains over vanilla. A key differentiator is general-capability preservation: MARI improves MMLU and ARC scores over vanilla, whereas ReFT and NL-ITI show slight regressions (e.g., ReFT drops MMLU from 83.5 to 83.4 and ARC-E from 77.8 to 77.7 on Qwen2.5-32B). The ablation confirms that removing the energy gate (always-on intervention) yields marginally higher alignment scores but causes severe general-capability drops (e.g., MMLU falls from 84.2 to 76.2 on Qwen2.5-32B), validating the gating mechanism's role in the alignment-utility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation intervention paradigm established by ReFT (Wu et al., 2024) and activation steering methods (CAA, ITI, NL-ITI), which all assume a single input-invariant low-rank edit suffices for alignment. MARI departs from this by introducing a competitive multi-adapter (mixture-of-experts) mechanism that learns complementary low-rank corrections and routes inputs at inference via predictive entropy, directly challenging the linear representation hypothesis's universality. The energy-based gating module, which uses propagation-response dynamics under a small probe update to decide whether to intervene, draws on recent work showing internal model signals can selectively trigger interventions (Hedström et al., 2025; Ferrando et al., 2025), but adds an off-subspace PCA regularizer to sharpen the applicability signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARI addresses the practical 'alignment tax' problem by showing that selective, input-adaptive intervention can substantially improve truthfulness, fairness, and safety while preserving (or even improving) general capabilities—a trade-off that static methods fail to achieve. The energy gate's clear separability (where PC1 of hidden states shows heavy entanglement) provides a label-free, calibration-free mechanism for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the single-injection-layer assumption generalizes to multi-layer or trajectory-level control, whether the fixed threshold remains reliable under more extreme distribution shifts, and whether the competitive training dynamics scale gracefully to very large adapter counts or diverse attribute combinations simultaneously.
