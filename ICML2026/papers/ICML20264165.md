## Problem

Existing LLM safety defenses treat jailbreak outcomes as binary (refusal or compliance), yet successful jailbreaks vary widely in real-world operational usefulness—from vague, misleading text to highly actionable step-by-step instructions. The paper asks whether model failure modes can be explicitly reshaped so that, when upstream safeguards are bypassed, the resulting outputs are semantically aligned with harmful requests yet lack actionable content, thereby reducing real-world misuse risk without merely increasing refusal rates.

## Input / Output

Training consumes paired harmful–honeypot response sets (5,000 harmful prompt–response pairs from the Circuit Breaking dataset plus a constructed honeypot dataset generated by an uncensored Qwen-3 8B model) alongside benign UltraChat conversations. At inference, the defended model takes standard text prompts and produces text responses; under adversarial attack it preferentially emits honeypot-like completions that are flagged as harmful by automated judges but contain no executable or operationally useful guidance, as verified by a separate GPT-OSS-Safeguard-20B actionability judge.

## Method

The paper constructs honeypot responses—non-actionable replies that are semantically aligned with harmful requests and flagged as harmful by standard automated judges—and uses them as hard negatives in the safety training pipeline. A modified DPO objective (omitting the reference-model term to avoid inheriting base-model biases toward nonsensical text) enforces a relative preference ordering where honeypot completions are ranked as less severe failures than truly harmful ones, while both remain penalized by a shared harmfulness proxy. This preference signal is integrated into two defense frameworks: a thresholded likelihood suppression baseline and Circuit Breakers (a representation-engineering defense), with an additional thresholded likelihood constraint added to the latter to prevent reward hacking on internal representations. The key insight is that honeypots occupy a distinct region of output space where automated judges disagree (35–40% lower cross-judge correlation than genuinely harmful responses), so training toward this region reduces the probability that an attack simultaneously satisfies multiple judges while shifting residual failures toward non-operational content.

## Results

Under GRPO adaptive attacks, honeypot regularization reduces ensemble ASR by 10–21% across both Llama-3 8B and Qwen-3 8B for threshold-based defenses (e.g., Qwen-3 8B: 74.0% → 53.0%, −21.0%). The largest gains appear under the GPT-OSS actionability judge, where ASR drops by up to 36% (Qwen-3 8B Circuit Breaking+Honeypot: 44.4% → 8.3%). Under embedding-space attacks, ensemble ASR reductions of 5–21% are observed, with the strongest effect for threshold defenses on Llama-3 8B (51.6% → 30.6%). Benign capabilities (ARC-C, HellaSwag, GSM8K, MMLU, TruthfulQA) remain stable or slightly improve, confirming no capability collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Circuit Breakers (Zou et al., 2024), a representation-engineering defense that disrupts harmful internal representations. Adding honeypot preference shaping to Circuit Breaking yields a 17.7% ensemble ASR reduction on Qwen-3 8B under GRPO attacks (72.8% → 55.1%) and a 13.2% reduction under embedding attacks (45.8% → 32.6%), with the GPT-OSS actionability score dropping from 44.4% to 8.3%. However, on Llama-3 8B under GRPO, CB+Honeypot slightly degrades ensemble ASR (+7.2%) relative to CB alone, attributed to the embedding-space hyperparameter selection choosing an overly strong DPO setting that is suboptimal under adaptive attacks. The simpler threshold-based defense shows more consistent gains: honeypot regularization improves ensemble ASR by 18.7% (Llama-3) and 21.0% (Qwen-3) under GRPO, and by 21.0% and 7.2% under embedding attacks respectively. Across all comparisons, the most consistent and largest improvement is under the GPT-OSS actionability judge (up to 36% reduction), indicating that honeypot regularization primarily reshapes the *type* of residual harm rather than uniformly reducing all judge scores. WildGuard, which detects refusal patterns rather than semantic content, shows minimal change (≤5%), confirming the effect is not merely increased refusals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the honeypot concept from computer security and prior AI-safety work (which used honeypots as inference-time attractors or detection probes) into a training-time signal: honeypot responses serve as hard negatives in a modified DPO objective that ranks them as less severe than truly harmful completions. It builds directly on Circuit Breakers (Zou et al., 2024) as a strong representation-engineering baseline and on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the preference-optimization backbone, but deliberately drops the reference-model term from DPO to avoid inheriting the base model's strong bias against nonsensical honeypot text. The key conceptual departure from prior jailbreak defenses is shifting the optimization target from reducing the frequency of harmful outputs to reshaping the conditional distribution of failures toward non-actionable, judge-flagged-but-operationally-useless responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical complement to ASR-based safety evaluation by showing that the quality of residual failures (actionable vs. non-actionable) is a meaningful axis of defense performance that standard metrics conflate. However, the gains are moderate (10–20% ensemble ASR reduction), Circuit Breaking + Honeypot degrades under adaptive GRPO on Llama-3, and the strongest attack (harmful prefix tuning) does not consistently benefit—limiting the claim of broad robustness. Open questions include whether the approach scales to frontier models, whether the honeypot construction generalizes beyond the specific prompt-based generation pipeline, and whether deliberately shaping failure modes introduces unpredictable emergent behaviors in internal representations, a risk the authors themselves flag.
