## Problem

Existing activation-steering methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation extract a single steering direction by averaging image-versus-no-image activation differences over the entire sequence and apply it with a fixed strength at every decoding step. The paper shows that visual conditioning affects token prediction sparsely (most tokens have near-zero visual gain, with a small heavy-tailed fraction carrying most of the signal), so global averaging dilutes critical signals and fixed-strength steering over-perturbs non-critical tokens, misallocating the intervention budget.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inputs (an RGB image plus a text prompt) and produces a generated text response (caption or VQA answer). At inference time, each decoding step requires a dual-route forward pass—one with the image and one with the image ablated—to compute the per-step visual-sensitivity signal that modulates steering strength. The calibration phase uses a small set of RLHF-V image-question-correction triples to extract and optionally refine per-layer steering vectors, after which the method is plug-and-play with no additional parameters at test time.

## Method

TLVS has three components. First, it extracts per-layer steering directions by computing teacher-forced log-probability differences Δ_t between image-conditioned and image-ablated routes, selecting the top and bottom ρ% of tokens by |Δ_t| as visually sensitive and insensitive pseudo-labels, and taking the PCA-1 of per-sample difference-of-means vectors over L2-normalized hidden states. Second, it optionally refines these directions by optimizing only the steering vectors on RLHF-V correction data with a combined NLL + trust-region KL + proximal-anchoring objective, denoising the direction while limiting distribution drift. Third, at inference it computes a per-step visual-sensitivity score as the KL divergence between image-conditioned and image-ablated softmax logits, maps it through a standardized sigmoid gate to an adaptive steering strength λ_t ∈ [λ_min, λ_max], and applies optional exponential smoothing and a confidence-based per-step cap to prevent over-perturbation at high-confidence steps—thereby concentrating the intervention budget on visually critical, hallucination-prone tokens while leaving image-insensitive tokens largely undisturbed.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, TLVS improves POPE average accuracy from 85.09 to 89.78 (+4.69), reduces CHAIRs (COCO) from 46.0 to 31.2 (−14.8), and cuts AMBER hallucination rate from 36.4 to 19.7 (−16.7) while raising AMBER F1 from 74.7 to 82.2 (+7.5). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, TLVS reduces CHAIRs from 24.0 to 12.0 and AMBER Hal from 29.0 to 16.7, with POPE Avg improving from 86.90 to 87.33. The method introduces a predictable ~2× inference-time overhead (two forward passes per token) with negligible VRAM increase (1.01×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLVS achieves the best POPE average accuracy on LLaVA-1.5-7B (89.78), outperforming the strongest steering baseline ASD (87.87) by +1.91 and the training-based DPO baseline (86.36) by +3.42. On CHAIRs (COCO), TLVS reduces hallucinated objects to 31.2, beating SHARP (34.8), VTI (35.8), and all other baselines by at least 3.6 points. For AMBER hallucination rate, TLVS (19.7) substantially outperforms SPARC (23.1), VTI (27.0), VISTA (27.1), and DPO (27.3), while also achieving the highest AMBER F1 (82.2 vs. DPO 80.9). The main trade-off is CHAIRi, where TLVS (14.8) is slightly worse than SHARP (10.6) and VTI (11.1), reflecting a modest increase in incorrect object mentions. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, TLVS dominates VCD across all reported metrics and matches or exceeds DPO on POPE Avg (87.33 vs. 87.17) and AMBER Hal (16.7 vs. 21.9), though DPO retains an edge on CHAIRi (3.5 vs. 12.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm for LVLM hallucination mitigation (Li et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2025), which contrasts image-conditioned and image-ablated hidden states to extract a global 'more-visual' direction applied with fixed strength at every decoding step. TLVS departs from this in three ways: it extracts token-level steering vectors via pseudo-labels derived from per-token visual sensitivity (|Δlog p|), optionally refines them with a lightweight supervised objective on RLHF-V correction data, and adapts the steering strength per token using an online KL-based visual-sensitivity signal. The dual-route inference design (image vs. no-image forward pass) is shared with contrastive decoding methods like VCD and SPARC, inheriting a similar ~2× compute overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical diagnosis that visual conditioning is sparse and heavy-tailed across decoding steps provides a concrete explanation for why global averaging dilutes steering signals, and the token-level adaptive mechanism offers a principled way to allocate intervention budget. The plug-and-play design with minimal calibration training makes it practical for existing LVLM deployments. However, the ~2× inference overhead (from the auxiliary no-image route) limits real-time applicability, and the gains on the stronger Qwen2.5-VL backbone are more modest than on LLaVA-1.5-7B. Open questions include whether the dual-route overhead can be eliminated via a learned sensitivity proxy, whether the approach transfers to video or multi-image settings, and how it interacts with other inference-time interventions.
