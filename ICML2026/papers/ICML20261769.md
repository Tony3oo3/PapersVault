## Problem

Learning continuous-variable (CV) Hamiltonians at the Heisenberg limit remains unresolved for generic multi-mode, higher-order bosonic systems. Existing Heisenberg-limited protocols are restricted to low-order approximations, lack provable robustness against state preparation and measurement (SPAM) errors, or become experimentally infeasible when extended to arbitrary finite-order terms. No prior work addresses Hamiltonian learning in the first-quantized (position-momentum) regime.

## Input / Output

The protocol consumes unitary access to an unknown Hamiltonian e^{-iĤt} as a black-box oracle, with the experimenter choosing displacement parameters β and random rotation angles θ as designed queries; the only required physical resources are the vacuum state and displacement operators, ensuring high experimental accessibility. The output is the full coefficient vector — bosonic coefficients g_{p,q} in second quantization or physical coefficients G_{j,k} in first quantization — recovered to RMSE ε with total evolution time T ~ O(1/ε). The intermediate measurable quantity is a scalar polynomial response C(β) = Σ g_{p,q}(β*)^p β^q, estimated via ancilla-projective measurements in X/Y bases.

## Method

D-RUT maps the infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian learning problem to polynomial coefficient recovery through a two-step quantum transformation: (1) a displacement D(β) shifts the creation/annihilation operators, and (2) averaging over random phase rotations U(θ)=e^{-iθN} projects the displaced Hamiltonian onto a number-conserving effective operator whose vacuum eigenvalue C(β) encodes all target coefficients as a polynomial in β. C(β) is estimated at Heisenberg-limited precision via Robust Phase Estimation (RPE) implemented as a Trotterized sequence of D-RUT gates controlled by an ancilla qubit. Coefficients are then recovered through a two-stage classical inversion: Chebyshev interpolation over radial nodes extracts intermediate angular functions g_l(θ), followed by inverse discrete Fourier transform to resolve individual g_{p,q}. For multi-mode systems, a hierarchical divide-and-conquer strategy selectively zeros displacement parameters to decouple interaction clusters, yielding a provably tighter error bound (scaling with local cluster size |S| rather than total mode count N). For first-quantized Hamiltonians, the protocol reformulates the problem in a tunable squeezed bosonic basis {B', B'†}, where D-RUT operations reduce to Gaussian (linear-optical + squeezing) circuits, and physical coefficients are recovered by inverting a known linear mapping matrix M.

## Results

The D-RUT protocol achieves Heisenberg-limited scaling (T ~ O(1/ε)) for learning generic finite-order bosonic Hamiltonians in both second and first quantization, verified numerically on a Kerr oscillator (ω=1.5, χ=0.5), a Bose-Hubbard dimer with cross-Kerr coupling (g=[1.5, 0.5, 1.2, 0.4, 0.1, 0.05]), and first-quantized harmonic/anharmonic oscillators. SPAM robustness is confirmed with RMSE scaling linearly in displacement noise σ_β over the range [3×10⁻³, 0.15]. The hierarchical multi-mode recovery strategy provably yields a strictly lower worst-case error bound than simultaneous recovery, with the error ratio growing as O((1/(1−ρ_ν))^(N−|S|)) relative to the Möbus et al. (2025) baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior Heisenberg-limited CV Hamiltonian learning protocols. Li et al. (2024) achieves the Heisenberg limit and is SPAM-robust but is restricted to low-order operator approximations and does not support first-quantized Hamiltonians. Möbus et al. (2025) handles higher-order terms and achieves the Heisenberg limit but is sensitive to SPAM errors and also lacks first-quantization support. D-RUT matches both on the Heisenberg limit and higher-order capability, while additionally providing provable SPAM robustness (linear error bound in displacement deviation, Eq. 43) and extending to first-quantized physical coefficients. In Appendix D, the paper rigorously proves that the hierarchical recovery strategy yields a strictly lower worst-case coefficient error than the simultaneous recovery of Möbus et al. (2025), with the advantage growing exponentially in the number of bystander modes (N−|S|).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two prior Heisenberg-limited CV Hamiltonian learning approaches: the Random Unitary Transformation (RUT) averaging from Li et al. (2024) and Zhang et al. (2025), and the Chebyshev/Fourier coefficient recovery with Robust Phase Estimation from Möbus et al. (2025). The key departure is the addition of a displacement step (forming D-RUT), which converts the infinite-dimensional dynamics into a finite polynomial response C(β) amenable to structured interpolation, and the extension to first-quantized position-momentum Hamiltonians via a squeezing-based basis change—neither of which appears in prior work. The hierarchical multi-mode recovery (zeroing displacements to isolate interaction clusters) is proven in Appendix D to yield strictly tighter error bounds than the simultaneous N-dimensional recovery of Möbus et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CV Hamiltonian characterization is essential for calibrating bosonic quantum platforms (communication, networking, metrology), yet the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and higher-order nonlinearities have made generic, noise-robust learning protocols elusive. D-RUT's requirement of only vacuum states and displacement operators keeps it experimentally accessible, and the SPAM robustness guarantee (linear error scaling) addresses a practical gap in prior Heisenberg-limited protocols. Open questions include the practical cost of the RPE Trotter depth L needed to suppress systematic error below the statistical tolerance, the scaling of the hierarchical strategy when interaction clusters overlap heavily, and whether the first-quantized extension remains well-conditioned under large reference-frame mismatches.
