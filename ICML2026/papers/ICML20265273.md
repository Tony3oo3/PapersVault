## Problem

Existing Incomplete Multimodal Learning methods rely on the unrealistic assumption that complete modalities are available during training to provide reconstruction supervision or cross-modal distillation priors. In practice, training data often suffers from systemic missingness due to collection costs or privacy constraints, making this 'full-modal training' assumption infeasible. This paper addresses the more challenging setting where modalities are missing during both training and testing, without any 'God's eye view' of complete data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted segment-level features (T×d_m matrices) from frozen backbones (VST/TimeSformer for RGB, AST for audio, I3D for optical flow), which are projected into the LLM's embedding space via learnable 1×1 convolutional layers. Missing modalities are not zero-padded but replaced with repeated learnable missing tokens, and the entire input is structured as a token sequence with modality boundary tokens, a task-specific text prompt describing the current modality availability, and K fusion tokens appended at the end. The output is a scalar action quality score produced by a dual-path regression head that blends high-level semantic reasoning with low-level cross-modal pattern recovery.

## Method

LIMSSR reformulates incomplete multimodal AQA as a conditional sequence-to-score reasoning task, using a sub-1B LLM (Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA) as a reasoning engine to infer latent semantics of missing modalities from available context rather than performing pixel-level reconstruction. The architecture has three phases: (1) Prompt-Guided Context-Aware Modality Imputation structures the input with boundary tokens and missing tokens, prompting the LLM to fill in latent representations; (2) LLM-Driven Multidimensional Representation Fusion uses K special fusion tokens as information slots with role-aware weighted aggregation to compress diverse evaluation dimensions; (3) Mask-Aware Dual-Path Aggregation combines an uncertainty-calibrated reasoning path (gated residual correction conditioned on the missingness mask) with a cross-modal pattern recovery path (self-attention over modality-pooled features with adaptive per-modality trust weights), arbitrated by a learnable synergy coefficient. Training uses a composite loss of MSE, dual-path consistency regularization, and token-level metric regularization to prevent fusion-token collapse, all under the constraint that no complete-modality supervision is available.

## Results

On the incomplete multimodal AQA benchmark (Table 1), LIMSSR achieves average Spearman ρ / MSE of 0.789/14.08 (FS1000), 0.743/15.09 (Fis-V), and 0.710/7.29 (RG), outperforming the strongest baseline MCMoE by +0.9%/−8.4%, +1.2%/−11.3%, and +1.9%/−7.6% respectively. Under the full-modality setting (Table 2), LIMSSR sets new state-of-the-art on FS1000 (0.891/10.44) and Fis-V (0.841/10.19), improving over MCMoE by +1.1%/−9.5% and +1.4%/−16.1%. Crucially, LIMSSR is the only method trained under training-time incomplete observations (T-Miss), while all baselines assume complete training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIMSSR outperforms all 13 baselines on average metrics across all three datasets, with the closest competitor being MCMoE (Xu et al., AAAI'26), which also targets incomplete multimodal AQA but still assumes complete training data; LIMSSR beats MCMoE by +0.9% ρ / −8.4% MSE on FS1000, +1.2% / −11.3% on Fis-V, and +1.9% / −7.6% on RG. Among reconstruction-based methods, ActionMAE (Woo, AAAI'23) and IMDer (Wang, NeurIPS'23) trail LIMSSR by 13–24% in average ρ, while distillation/prior-based methods GCNet (Lian, TPAMI'23) and MoMKE (Xu, ACM MM'24) show similar gaps. SDR-GNN (Fu, KBS'25), the most recent incomplete multimodal baseline, is surpassed by LIMSSR by 12–13% ρ on average. In the full-modality comparison (Table 2), LIMSSR also beats unimodal AQA methods T2CR (Ke, Inf.Sci.'24), CoFInAl (Zhou, IJCAI'24), and ASGTN (Liu, TCSVT'25) by 6–11% ρ, as well as multimodal methods SGN (Du, TMM'24) and VATP-Net (Gedamu, TCSVT'25). The consistent advantage is attributed to LIMSSR's training under incomplete observations, which forces the LLM to develop robust cross-modal reasoning rather than relying on full-modal supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant IML paradigm (reconstruction-based methods like ActionMAE/IMDer and distillation/prior-based methods like GCNet/MoMKE/MCMoE) by rejecting their shared assumption of full-modal availability during training. Rather than generating missing data or distilling from a complete-modality teacher, LIMSSR repurposes a small LLM (Qwen3-0.6B) as a conditional sequence reasoning engine that infers latent semantics for missing modalities via prompt-guided token imputation, drawing on the LLM's world knowledge rather than pixel-level reconstruction targets. The specific mechanism—encoding missingness as learnable placeholder tokens and fusion as special 'information slot' tokens within a single LLM forward pass—repurposes the LLM's next-token prediction architecture for a structured imputation-and-fusion task without requiring any complete-modality supervision signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic and underexplored gap: training data in deployed multimodal systems often suffers from systemic missingness, yet nearly all IML methods assume complete training data for supervision. Demonstrating that a sub-1B LLM trained only on incomplete observations can match or exceed methods with full-modal supervision validates the 'reasoning over reconstruction' paradigm and opens a path toward data-efficient multimodal learning. Limitations include validation only on AQA (a narrow task), substantially higher computational cost (~600M params vs. ~5M for MCMoE), and reliance on a single small LLM backbone; open questions include generalization to other multimodal tasks, scaling to larger LLMs, and whether explicit physical or domain constraints can further reduce hallucination risk.
