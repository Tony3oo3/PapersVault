## Problem

Small language models (SLMs) underperform larger LLMs on multi-step reasoning, yet existing remedies are limited: collaborative inference incurs high latency from synchronous LLM calls, while standard distillation is constrained by the capacity gap, forcing the SLM to mimic the LLM's full generative distribution over an open-ended vocabulary. The paper identifies that at reasoning divergence points, the LLM's preferred token typically already resides within the SLM's top-K candidate set, suggesting the bottleneck is not generation capacity but candidate ranking.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and the SLM's model weights; in collaborative S2T mode it additionally requires the LLM's weights for scoring. At each triggered decoding step, the SLM produces a bounded candidate set of K=8 tokens from its next-token distribution, and the scoring mechanism (LLM in S2T, or the internalized reserved-token readout in S2T-LOCAL) outputs a scalar preference score per candidate to select the next token. The final output is a single generated text trajectory, with the selection scores computed in a single batched forward pass over the K augmented states, keeping inference cost close to standard greedy decoding.

## Method

S2T reframes LLM guidance from open-ended generation to discrete selection over the SLM's top-K proposals, reducing the supervision signal from full distribution matching to a simple ranking task. S2T-LOCAL internalizes this selection logic by repurposing a small set of reserved vocabulary tokens (|R|=16 for 1.5B) via the ZIP framework: the softmax over reserved-token logits, projected by a fixed bin vector, yields a continuous preference score for each candidate. Training uses a cross-entropy selection loss over the candidate scores (with an adaptive pairwise margin term) plus a KL regularizer against the frozen base model to preserve linguistic behavior, applied via LoRA adapters with unfrozen reserved-token embeddings. A lightweight MLP trigger head, distilled from KL-divergence labels, determines when to activate selection, and the entire pipeline requires only ~6% trainable parameters, achieving a 24.1% relative gain over greedy decoding on math benchmarks while matching 8-path self-consistency with single-trajectory efficiency.

## Results

On the 1.5B Qwen2.5-Instruct model, S2T-LOCAL improves Math Avg. from 36.9% (greedy) to 45.8%, a 24.1% relative gain, while matching 8-path self-consistency (Maj@8: 43.7%) with single-trajectory efficiency. At the 0.5B scale, the gain is even larger: Math Avg. rises from 20.2% to 29.2% (44.6% relative gain). In the collaborative setting, S2T (selection-based) achieves 55.8% Math Avg. on 1.5B, matching the generative Takeover baseline (55.7%) while reducing inference latency by ~75% compared to LLM-involved methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2T-LOCAL (teacher-free) outperforms all SLM-only baselines: it beats Maj@8 self-consistency (43.7% → 45.8% Math Avg. on 1.5B) while using only 1/8 the sampling budget, and surpasses TSD-KD (38.3%), Multiplex-Thinking (39.9%), Think-at-Hard (39.7%), and DistilQwen2.5 (40.2%) by 5.6–7.5 points. In the collaborative (LLM-involved) setting, S2T's selection-based guidance (55.8% Math Avg.) matches the generative Takeover baseline (55.7%) that allows the LLM to freely generate tokens, confirming that bounded candidate selection is sufficient to recover LLM-level reasoning. S2T also edges out R2R (56.5%) and SpecReason (52.3%) on Math Avg. at 1.5B, though R2R leads slightly on GSM8K (96.4 vs. 95.6). Critically, S2T-LOCAL achieves ~75% lower latency than any LLM-involved method (10.2s vs. 40.5s per MATH instance), making it the only approach that combines near-collaborative accuracy with standard SLM inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms for improving SLM reasoning: collaborative inference (where an LLM generates tokens at divergence points, as in R2R and RelayLLM) and standard knowledge distillation (where the SLM matches the LLM's full-vocabulary distribution, as in TSD-KD and DistilQwen2.5). It builds on the ZIP framework's reserved-token mechanism for encoding auxiliary signals and on the observation that reasoning errors are localized to a few critical tokens, but reframes the LLM's role from open-ended generation to discrete selection over the SLM's top-K candidates. This shift from generative imitation to discriminative ranking is the core conceptual departure, reducing the distillation objective from high-dimensional distribution matching to a bounded candidate-scoring task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local sufficiency finding (95% hit rate at K=8) challenges the prevailing assumption that SLMs lack the representational capacity for correct reasoning, suggesting the bottleneck is ranking rather than generation. Practically, S2T-LOCAL's 24.1% relative gain matching 8-path self-consistency at single-trajectory cost offers a compelling efficiency story for edge deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger SLMs where the capacity gap narrows, whether the trigger mechanism (currently KL-divergence-based) can be further simplified, and whether the selection signal generalizes to domains with less structured reasoning (e.g., creative writing, multi-turn dialogue).
