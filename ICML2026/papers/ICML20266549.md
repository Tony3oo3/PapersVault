## Problem

Genomic deep learning models are increasingly relied upon to predict molecular phenotypes from DNA sequence, yet their interpretability outputs are evaluated anecdotally—typically through a single IML method, subjective visual inspection, and cherry-picked 'successful' examples. This paper demonstrates that such practices mask fundamental failures: IML methods produce contradictory explanations for the same prediction, fail to reflect the model's actual decision process, and frequently misalign with known biological mechanisms, yet these failures remain invisible under current reporting norms.

## Input / Output

The benchmark operates on fixed-length DNA sequences centered on ENCODE narrowPeak summits, split strictly by chromosome (Chromosome 9 held out entirely for testing) to prevent information leakage, yielding ~289K training and ~11.5K test sequences across five transcription factors. For each test sequence, per-nucleotide importance scores from five IML methods are compared via Spearman rank correlation and top-20 Jaccard similarity (consistency), assessed for faithfulness through sequential deletion/insertion AUC, and scored against UniBind motif annotations using a Perception overlap metric. All outputs are reported as full distributions across all annotated sequences rather than individual exemplars, enabling quantitative assessment of typical explanation quality.

## Method

The paper combines a large-scale bibliometric mapping of 3,575 genomics papers (2010–2025) extracted via a two-stage LLM pipeline (few-shot ICL plus self-reflection, verified at 98% agreement on 100 manually annotated samples) with a controlled empirical audit on TF binding prediction. The audit uses five TFs as targeted stress tests—CTCF and MAX as high-signal positive controls, SP1 and TBP as compositional bias probes (GC-rich vs. AT-rich), and GATA1 as a short-motif resolution test—and evaluates five IML methods (DeepLIFT, Integrated Gradients, ISM, LIME, Categorical Jacobian) across three architecturally diverse foundation models (DNABERT-2 transformer, HyenaDNA SSM, NTv3 hybrid). Faithfulness is quantified through MoRF-style sequential deletion and RISE-style sequential insertion, while biological validity is measured by the overlap between the highest-attribution window and UniBind-annotated motif coordinates. The findings are synthesized into a tiered evaluation framework with seven concrete guidelines spanning low-effort (multi-method agreement, negative controls, quantitative metrics), moderate-effort (perturbation-based faithfulness tests, diverse-condition probing), and high-effort (pre-registered wet-lab validation with stratified sampling) tiers.

## Results

Across 3,575 genomics papers (2010–2025), 86% employed only a single IML method, ~50% validated via subjective visual inspection, and only 2.81% performed wet-lab validation. In the empirical audit on five TFs and three foundation models, inter-method Spearman rank correlation is consistently below 0.4 (highest: ISM–LIME at ρ=0.373; lowest: IG–CJ at ρ=−0.021), and top-20 Jaccard similarity never exceeds 0.5 (best: DeepLIFT–IG at 0.494). On the compositional-bias stress tests (SP1, TBP), all five IML methods yield near-zero Perception scores (biological alignment), while on the positive control CTCF, median Perception exceeds 0.5. For faithfulness, deletion-AUC clusters near 1.0 for all methods on SP1 and TBP, indicating no method reliably identifies the features driving predictions on these tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five IML methods (DeepLIFT, Integrated Gradients, ISM, LIME, Categorical Jacobian) across three genomic foundation models (DNABERT-2, HyenaDNA, NTv3) on five TF binding tasks. No single IML method dominates: IG achieves the best (lowest) deletion-AUC on most TFs for faithfulness, but all methods collapse to near-random performance on SP1 and TBP where compositional bias confounds attribution. For biological alignment (Perception score), all methods succeed on the high-signal CTCF task (median > 0.5) but fail on GATA1 (median 0.2–0.4) and catastrophically on SP1/TBP (near-zero). The Categorical Jacobian is particularly weak on GATA1, collapsing to a near-zero median Perception. Among foundation models, NTv3 achieves the highest predictive accuracy (e.g., 0.950 on CTCF) and serves as the primary platform for the interpretability audit, while DNABERT-2 shows the weakest performance on MAX (0.587 accuracy). The key takeaway is that no IML method consistently outperforms the others across all three evaluation axes (consistency, faithfulness, biological validity), undermining the common practice of relying on a single method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established perturbation-based faithfulness evaluation framework (MoRF from Samek et al., 2016; RISE from Petsiuk et al., 2018) and applies it systematically to genomic foundation models (DNABERT-2, HyenaDNA, NTv3) across five IML methods. It departs from the dominant practice in genomic IML—applying a single attribution method and reporting a handful of visually plausible examples—by conducting both a large-scale mapping study (3,575 papers) quantifying evaluation norms and a controlled empirical audit with biological ground truth from UniBind. The tiered evaluation framework it proposes draws an explicit analogy to clinical trial design, positioning IML validation as requiring the same rigor as experimental medicine rather than relying on post-hoc plausibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all five IML methods achieve near-zero motif recovery on compositional-bias stress tests (SP1, TBP) despite models attaining 0.77–0.87 F1 is a strong warning that high predictive accuracy does not guarantee biologically meaningful explanations, with direct implications for experimental prioritization and variant interpretation in genomics. The mapping study's quantification that 86% of studies use a single IML method and only 2.81% perform wet-lab validation provides the first large-scale evidence of the field's evaluation gap. Open questions include whether the proposed tiered framework will be adopted as a community standard, whether new IML methods can overcome compositional-bias confounding, and whether the findings generalize beyond TF binding to other genomic tasks like gene expression or chromatin accessibility.
