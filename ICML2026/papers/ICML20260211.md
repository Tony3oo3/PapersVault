## Problem

Multi-hop KGQA suffers from a semantic gap where intermediate bridge entities (e.g., a film director in an awards query) share no lexical overlap with the question, causing similarity-based traversal to fail. Existing differentiable retrievers break end-to-end differentiability at discrete selection steps, while LLM-centric agentic approaches incur prohibitive computational costs and remain prone to hallucination. The paper addresses both issues by decoupling a lightweight differentiable graph reasoner from a grounded LLM generator.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language question and a Freebase knowledge graph as input. A bi-encoder performs dense anchor retrieval to identify the topic entity, from which a K-hop BFS subgraph (K=2 for WebQSP, K=4 for CWQ) is extracted. The output is a final answer string generated by a fine-tuned LLM (Qwen3-8B or LLaMA-2-7B) conditioned on the original question plus up to K textualized reasoning paths (sequences of entity-relation-entity triplets linearized into natural language), rather than on the raw graph structure.

## Method

RSF-GLLM operates in two decoupled stages. Stage 1 trains a Recurrent Soft-Flow (RSF) module that propagates continuous probability mass over subgraph nodes via relation-guided structural flow, a GRU-based query updater that performs differentiable instruction subtraction across hops, and a dynamic gating mechanism λ^(t) that adaptively modulates the influence of content-based semantic matching versus pure structural propagation—suppressing content bias at hops where bridge nodes are semantically disjoint from the query. An entropy-based flow sparsity regularization forces the soft distributions to converge toward discrete, interpretable paths (proven via Theorem 2.1). Stage 2 freezes the RSF module, extracts top-K reasoning paths via greedy backtracking, textualizes them into natural language strings, and fine-tunes the LLM with standard causal language modeling loss on (question + paths → answer) pairs. Three theorems provide guarantees: sparsity convergence to simplex vertices, semantic gap bridging via structural propagation alone, and guaranteed structural faithfulness of extracted paths.

## Results

RSF-GLLM (Qwen3-8B) achieves 90.45% Hit@1 and 79.15% F1 on WebQSP, and 67.39% Hit@1 and 61.87% F1 on CWQ, setting new state-of-the-art on WebQSP Hit@1 among all non-agentic methods. The LLaMA-2-7B variant scores 89.50% Hit@1 on WebQSP and 66.44% on CWQ. Inference latency is approximately 0.25 seconds per question on a single A100 GPU, with the RSF retrieval module using only 176M parameters (38× smaller than RoG's 6.7B) and requiring zero LLM calls during the retrieval phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WebQSP, RSF-GLLM (Qwen3-8B) achieves 90.45% Hit@1, outperforming the strongest LLM+KG baselines RoG (85.7%), GNN-RAG+RA (82.8%), and DECAF (82.1%) by 4.8–8.4 points, and also surpassing the agentic FD-PORT (89.2%) by 1.25 points. On CWQ, RSF-GLLM (67.39% Hit@1) beats RoG (62.6%), GNN-RAG (61.7%), and GNN-RAG+RA (62.8%) by 4.6–5.7 points, but trails the agentic FD-PORT (74.5%) by 7.1 points and EffiQA (69.5%) by 2.1 points. On F1, RSF-GLLM leads all non-agentic methods on both datasets (79.15% WebQSP, 61.87% CWQ), exceeding DECAF's 78.8% F1 on WebQSP and GNN-RAG+RA's 60.4% on CWQ. All traditional graph-retrieval baselines (GraftNet, PullNet, NSM, ReaRev, UniKGQA) are outperformed by 12–24 points on WebQSP Hit@1. The paper notes that 19.3% of CWQ test questions have their gold answer outside the K-hop subgraph, imposing a theoretical Hit@1 ceiling of ~80.7%, which partially explains the CWQ gap versus agentic methods that can dynamically expand search.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSF-GLLM synthesizes two distinct lineages: the differentiable flow-propagation mechanism of GraftNet (Sun et al., 2018) for structural traversal, and the GRU-based state-transition architecture of the Neural State Machine (Hudson & Manning, 2019) for query intent evolution. It departs from both by introducing a dynamic gating mechanism that adaptively decouples structural propagation from semantic matching at each hop, and by imposing entropy-based flow sparsity regularization to recover discrete paths from continuous distributions. The decoupled Graph-to-LLM pipeline contrasts with the monolithic end-to-end designs of RoG and GNN-RAG, positioning the LLM purely as a conditional generator over extracted paths rather than as the reasoning engine itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of the semantic gap as a distinct failure mode, coupled with theoretical guarantees on sparsity convergence and structural faithfulness, provides a principled framework for diagnosing and addressing a long-recognized but under-theorized limitation in differentiable KGQA. The 21× inference speedup over agentic baselines while maintaining competitive accuracy makes grounded multi-hop QA practically deployable, and the text-path interface ensures compatibility with any LLM including proprietary black-box models. Key open questions include the K-hop subgraph coverage bottleneck (19.3% of CWQ answers fall outside the extracted neighborhood), the LLM's residual popularity bias when multiple valid candidates appear in paths, and whether the decoupled architecture can be extended to temporal or multi-modal knowledge graphs.
