## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from overthinking, generating unnecessarily long chain-of-thought sequences that inflate latency without improving accuracy. Existing inference-time early-exit methods rely on probing mechanisms (e.g., inserting 'Final Answer is' prompts) that introduce detection overhead, often negating the latency savings from shorter outputs and failing to generalize across diverse task domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265351/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and produces a truncated reasoning chain followed by a final answer, where truncation occurs at natural paragraph boundaries (\n\n delimiters) when smoothed reasoning signals exceed calibrated thresholds. The draft model's single forward pass simultaneously outputs token logits and three scalar reasoning signals (confidence, progress, remaining length), so no separate probing forward pass is needed. The target model's recover token is replaced with a  marker at the exit point, ensuring the output remains a well-formed reasoning trace.

## Method

SpecExit extends the Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) layer of an EAGLE3-style single-layer draft model by augmenting its linear projection to output, in addition to token logits, three auxiliary scalars: confidence (geometric mean of logit probabilities), reasoning progress (normalized 0-to-1 completion fraction), and remaining reasoning length (tokens to the earliest valid  insertion point). Training uses multi-task learning with a gradient-based dynamic weighting scheme that balances the token classification loss against MSE/MSLE regression losses for the three signals, keeping the backbone target model frozen. At inference, the three signals are smoothed via Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA, α=0.1) and jointly evaluated at step-split token boundaries; when all smoothed signals cross their respective thresholds, the accepted draft sequence is truncated at that boundary and the recover token is replaced with , terminating the thinking phase within the standard EAGLE3 speculative decoding loop without any additional forward pass.

## Results

SpecExit achieves up to 66% generation length reduction and 2.5× end-to-end latency speedup compared to the EAGLE3 speculative decoding baseline, while maintaining comparable accuracy across mathematical, coding, science, and logic benchmarks. On average across all six benchmarks, SpecExit reduces token count by ~20–57% and latency by ~25–44% relative to EAGLE3, with accuracy differences within ±1.5 points. For example, on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, SpecExit cuts average tokens from 7,308 (EAGLE3) to 5,852 and average latency from 200.0s to 149.6s while improving average accuracy from 87.0% to 88.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EAGLE3 (the speculative decoding baseline), SpecExit achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy (e.g., 88.9% vs 87.0% average on Qwen3-4B; 61.8% vs 62.0% on DeepSeek-R1) while reducing average token count by 20–57% and average latency by 25–44%, confirming that early-exit signals add efficiency without sacrificing output quality. Against DEER (probing-based early exit), SpecExit dramatically outperforms on latency: DEER actually *increases* end-to-end latency over vanilla on Qwen3-4B (319.4s vs 264.4s) due to probing overhead, whereas SpecExit reduces it to 149.6s; DEER also suffers larger accuracy drops (84.5% vs 88.9% average on Qwen3-4B). Against NoThink (skipping reasoning entirely), SpecExit maintains accuracy far more reliably—NoThink collapses to 34.3% average accuracy on DeepSeek-R1 (vs 61.8% for SpecExit) and fails to suppress reasoning on Qwen3-4B and Phi-4, yielding token counts similar to vanilla. Overall, SpecExit is the only method that simultaneously reduces both token count and end-to-end latency below the vanilla baseline while preserving accuracy across all three models and all six benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecExit extends the EAGLE3 speculative decoding framework by augmenting the multi-token prediction (MTP) head with auxiliary regression heads that predict confidence, reasoning progress, and remaining reasoning length from the draft model's hidden states. It departs from probing-based early-exit methods (DEER, NoThink) that require explicit intermediate-answer detection or logit inspection, which introduce additional forward passes and task-specific prompt sensitivity. Conceptually, the paper builds on recent evidence that hidden states encode predictive signals beyond next-token probabilities (Lin et al. 2025; Dong et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025a), repurposing these latent cues for inference control rather than for token prediction alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5× end-to-end speedup over the EAGLE3 speculative decoding baseline (not just vanilla) with up to 66% token reduction and minimal accuracy loss is practically significant for deploying reasoning models in latency-sensitive settings, and the vLLM integration lowers the adoption barrier. However, the method requires verifiable outputs with deterministic correctness criteria for training data construction, limiting direct applicability to open-ended generation or dialogue. Open questions include whether the signal heads generalize to out-of-distribution tasks, whether the approach extends to models without explicit think tokens, and whether the multi-signal fusion can be replaced by a single learned scalar without the current threshold-tuning burden.
