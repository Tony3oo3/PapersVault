## Problem

Existing next-best-view selection methods for active 3D reconstruction rely on surrogate signals such as parameter-space uncertainty or geometric heuristics, which are misaligned with the quantity that ultimately matters: the fidelity of rendered novel views. This misalignment can lead to inefficient view selection where parameter variance is reduced while rendering artifacts persist. The paper asks whether view selection can be driven directly by information gain in the prediction (rendering) space, and whether such an objective can be made both principled and computationally tractable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of already-captured RGB images (used to fit a 3DGS model), a discrete pool of candidate camera poses, and a user-specified target view manifold defined as a spatial sector (e.g., a 120° rotation around the scene centroid) in camera pose space. From this manifold, K probe poses are sampled to construct the Goal Hessian. The output is the single next-best-view pose to acquire, an incrementally updated 3DGS model, and a per-pixel predictive rendering uncertainty map for any arbitrary query viewpoint. Per-candidate scoring takes approximately 120 ms on an RTX 3090 with 20 views on Mip-NeRF360, enabling real-time interactive use.

## Method

GO-PRE formulates next-best-view selection as minimizing the expected average marginal predictive entropy over a target view manifold, grounded in Bayesian experimental design. Under a Laplace approximation to the 3DGS posterior and a first-order linearization of the differentiable renderer, the predictive distribution at any target pose becomes Gaussian, and its entropy reduces to a log-determinant of the predictive covariance. Applying the Matrix Determinant Lemma shifts this computation into parameter space, and Jensen's inequality yields a tractable upper bound—the GO-PRE Score—expressed as log det(I + Σ_x^{1/2} M Σ_x^{1/2}), where M is the Goal Hessian Matrix aggregating Fisher information (J_p^T J_p) across K Monte Carlo probes drawn from the target view distribution. A diagonal approximation of both the posterior covariance and M reduces the log-determinant to a sum of scalar logarithms, enabling real-time evaluation. The key distinction from prior work (FisherRF, POp-GS, GauSS-MI) is that the objective operates directly on rendering-space uncertainty weighted by the user's target manifold, rather than on parameter-space information or per-Gaussian mutual information, and the aggregation of Fisher terms into M before the log-determinant evaluation provides a variance-damping effect that outperforms direct Monte Carlo estimation.

## Results

On the global reconstruction protocol (20 views), GO-PRE achieves 21.23 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360, 17.96 dB on Tanks & Temples, and 25.57 dB on Blender, outperforming the strongest baseline POp-GS by 0.61, 0.55, and 0.05 dB respectively. In the goal-oriented protocol, the advantage widens substantially: GO-PRE reaches 23.43 dB on Mip-NeRF360 and 20.50 dB on Tanks & Temples, surpassing POp-GS by 2.81 and 3.75 dB. The method also achieves the best LPIPS (0.3370 on Mip-NeRF360, 0.3045 on Tanks & Temples, 0.0800 on Blender) and SSIM across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GO-PRE outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and metric in both the global and goal-oriented protocols. Against POp-GS (the closest competitor), GO-PRE gains 0.61 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and 0.55 dB on Tanks & Temples in the global setting, with the gap widening to 2.81 dB and 3.75 dB respectively in the goal-oriented setting where the target manifold is explicitly specified. FisherRF, which operates in parameter space via Fisher information, trails GO-PRE by 0.88 dB on Mip-NeRF360 and 0.95 dB on Tanks & Temples, confirming the paper's claim that parameter-space uncertainty is misaligned with rendering fidelity. GauSS-MI, which computes Shannon mutual information over per-Gaussian visual uncertainty, is beaten by 1.37 dB on Mip-NeRF360 and 1.22 dB on Tanks & Temples. ActiveNeRF, the earliest uncertainty-based baseline, performs comparably to or worse than random selection on Mip-NeRF360 (17.89 vs. 17.91 dB), highlighting the limitations of variance-based proxies in the 3DGS backend.

## Relation to Prior Work

GO-PRE extends the Bayesian experimental design lineage of active view selection (FisherRF, POp-GS, GauSS-MI) by shifting the information-gain objective from parameter space to prediction space. Where FisherRF maximizes expected Fisher information over model parameters and POp-GS recasts this via P-optimality with block-diagonal covariance, GO-PRE instead minimizes the expected marginal predictive entropy of rendered images over a user-specified target view manifold, using a Goal Hessian Matrix to weight parameter directions by their relevance to that manifold. The Laplace-approximated linearized renderer and the Jensen-inequality upper bound are the key technical departures that make this prediction-space objective tractable for high-dimensional 3DGS parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By tying view selection directly to rendering fidelity rather than surrogate parameter-space signals, GO-PRE addresses a fundamental misalignment that has limited prior active reconstruction methods, and the goal-oriented formulation enables practical controllability (e.g., focusing a robot's capture budget on a specific region of interest). The large goal-oriented performance gap (~2.8 dB over POp-GS on Mip-NeRF360) and the finding that the Jensen-based surrogate outperforms direct Monte Carlo estimation are both practically useful. The main open limitation is the reliance on a discrete candidate pool; the authors identify the need for mixed second-order derivatives of the 3DGS renderer as a structural barrier to continuous gradient-based pose optimization, which remains an important direction for truly autonomous deployment.
