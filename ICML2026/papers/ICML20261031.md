## Problem

InfoNCE's softmax link implicitly assumes a Gumbel (translation) tail geometry for top-1 selection, but in normalized embedding spaces where cosine similarities are bounded to [-1,1], the hardest negatives cluster near the finite score cap where Weibull endpoint-shortfall behavior dominates. This tail-geometry mismatch makes the softmax link statistically misspecified for hard negatives, causing suboptimal gradient allocation during contrastive training.

## Input / Output

WEINCE takes a batch of 2N ℓ₂-normalized embeddings and their positive-pair indices, computes the full cosine similarity matrix, and for each anchor extracts negative shortfalls δ_ij = 1 − s_ij. From these shortfalls it estimates two stop-gradient statistics per anchor — a cap-proximity signal ρ_i and a Weibull tail index β̂_i — without any additional forward passes or trainable parameters. The output is a single scalar cross-entropy loss computed over the interpolated logits, making it a direct drop-in replacement for the standard InfoNCE loss term.

## Method

WEINCE replaces the fixed softmax logit s_ij/τ with an anchor-wise interpolation ℓ_ij = (1−λ_i)·s_ij/τ + λ_i·(−β̂_i·log(1−s_ij)), where the second term is the Weibull endpoint-shortfall logit derived from extreme value theory for bounded scores. The mixing weight λ_i is a product of two sigmoid gates: one increasing in cap-proximity (small ρ_i = min_j δ_ij) and one increasing in a Weibull-vs-Gumbel AIC comparison fitted on the K_tail smallest shortfalls, so anchors with weak endpoint evidence stay near vanilla InfoNCE while near-cap Weibull-like anchors receive stronger shortfall correction. The tail index β̂_i is obtained by linear regression of log F̂ on log δ over the smallest shortfalls. The entire correction is grounded in the Fisher-Tippett-Gnedenko trichotomy: for bounded cosine similarities the relevant tail geometry is Weibull (ξ<0), whose canonical top-1 rule p_i ∝ q(s_i)^(−β) cannot be recovered by any single temperature retuning of the raw-score softmax (Lemma C.2), motivating the coordinate-level interpolation rather than a global link replacement.

## Results

WEINCE consistently improves over vanilla InfoNCE across five vision benchmarks, with the largest linear-probing gains on CIFAR-100 (+3.27% for ResNet-18, +4.82% for ResNet-50) and moderate gains on STL-10 (+1.40%/+1.58%), CIFAR-10 (+0.34%/+1.61%), and ImageNet-32 (+0.97%). On Tiny-ImageNet, linear accuracy is roughly flat (−0.10%) but kNN retrieval improves substantially (R@1 +1.55, R@2 +1.35 for ResNet-18; R@1 +4.23, R@20 +4.34 for ViT-Small). In the NLP setting, WEINCE raises SimCSE STS-B Spearman correlation from 71.74 to 76.36 (+4.63 points, p < 0.05) with no architecture or schedule changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla InfoNCE (the standard softmax contrastive loss), and WEINCE is evaluated as a drop-in replacement with no additional trainable parameters. WEINCE wins on 7 of 8 vision linear-probing configurations, with the most substantial margins on CIFAR-100 where the harder negative distribution (100 classes vs. 10) amplifies the endpoint-shortfall effect: +3.27% (R18) and +4.82% (R50). Gains on CIFAR-10 are smaller (+0.34% R18, +1.61% R50), consistent with fewer hard negatives in a 10-class setting. The only linear-accuracy loss is Tiny-ImageNet R18 (−0.10%), though kNN retrieval on the same dataset improves markedly, suggesting the correction helps retrieval more than linear separability at that scale. In the cross-domain SimCSE experiment, WEINCE outperforms InfoNCE by +4.63 Spearman points on STS-B (76.36 vs. 71.74), confirming the bounded-endpoint correction generalizes beyond vision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2018) through the lens of discrete choice theory and extreme value theory, showing that the softmax link implicitly assumes a Gumbel/translation tail geometry that is misaligned with the bounded cosine-similarity regime used in modern contrastive learning (SimCLR, Chen et al. 2020; SimCSE, Gao et al. 2022). It builds on prior observations that hard negatives receive disproportionate weight under contrastive losses (Wang & Liu, 2021; Kalantidis et al., 2020; Robinson et al., 2021) and on information-theoretic justifications of InfoNCE (Lu et al., 2023; Poole et al., 2019), but departs by identifying the specific statistical assumption behind the softmax and proposing an adaptive Weibull endpoint-shortfall correction (WEINCE) that interpolates anchor-by-anchor rather than replacing softmax globally. The Fisher-Tippett-Gnedenko trichotomy provides the theoretical backbone, connecting the three tail geometries (Fréchet, Gumbel, Weibull) to three canonical top-1 link functions, with the Weibull branch motivating the shortfall logit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WEINCE offers a parameter-free, negligible-overhead drop-in replacement for InfoNCE that consistently improves frozen-feature evaluation across five vision benchmarks and SimCSE sentence embeddings, suggesting the bounded-endpoint correction is not domain-specific. The EVT-based diagnostic framework (POT analysis, likelihood-based link selection, gradient misallocation plots) provides a reusable toolkit for auditing whether a contrastive objective's link function is well-specified for a given embedding geometry. Open questions include whether the anchor-wise adaptation is optimal versus learned mixing, whether the approach extends to unbounded or learned-metric similarity spaces, and whether the gains persist under fine-tuning rather than frozen-feature evaluation. The theoretical connection between EVT domains of attraction and contrastive loss design could inform future objectives beyond the softmax family.
