## Problem

Masked diffusion language models generate text by iteratively unmasking tokens, and the unmasking order acts as an implicit 'order of thought' that strongly influences generation quality. Existing schedules are fixed greedy heuristics (confidence, margin, entropy) that optimize only the next step rather than the full decoding trajectory, leading to myopic choices that underperform on complex reasoning tasks. The paper asks whether the unmasking order can be learned in a principled, model-aware way without retraining the diffusion backbone.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the order policy receives a compact per-position feature vector from the frozen denoiser (top-1 token probability, distribution entropy, and normalized position index) shaped as [B, L, 3], along with a binary mask indicating which positions are still masked. The policy outputs a scalar score per masked position, from which the next position to reveal is selected (greedy or sampled). The final output is a full position permutation σ = (i₁, …, iₙ) that dictates the unmasking sequence, which in turn determines the generated text. The prompt tokens are always visible and excluded from the action space; only completion positions are scheduled.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is showing that the KL divergence between the data distribution and the induced sequential decoding distribution is upper-bounded by a joint KL over (sequence, order) pairs, which equals the negative expected pathwise log-likelihood of the frozen denoiser under teacher forcing minus the data entropy. This yields a dense, self-aware reward R_θ(x, σ) = Σ_t log p_θ^{i_t}(x_{i_t} | x̃^{(t-1)}(σ)) that assigns credit to each individual reveal decision rather than only the final sample. The order policy is a lightweight Transformer encoder (a few self-attention layers plus a linear head) trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): for each prompt, G candidate orders are sampled, their pathwise returns are standardized within the group, and a clipped surrogate objective updates the policy. The diffusion model θ remains frozen throughout order learning, making the approach plug-and-play; an optional second stage then fine-tunes θ along the learned fixed trajectory using the same self-aware objective. The framework applies identically to both any-order sequential decoding and semi-autoregressive block decoding, with the parallelization gap characterized by within-block conditional total correlation.

## Results

On Sudoku with a 1B masked diffusion model (SMDM-1B), SAS improves puzzle accuracy from 82.0% (best heuristic: Confidence) to 91.8%, and cell accuracy from 98.56% to 99.21%. On mathematical reasoning with LLaDA-8B, SAS improves pass@1 on GSM8K from 64% to 76% and on MBPP from 39.5% to 41%. A second-stage fine-tuning along learned trajectories further boosts Sudoku accuracy to 97.5%, matching the 97.8% achieved by fine-tuning under a human expert order.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAS outperforms all heuristic baselines on Sudoku by a wide margin: it beats Confidence (Zheng et al., 2023) by 9.8 points (91.8% vs. 82.0%), Margin (Kim et al., 2025) by 11.8 points (91.8% vs. 80.0%), Entropy (Zheng et al., 2023) by 10.8 points, and Random (Zheng et al., 2024) by 29.1 points. Notably, the human expert order (Shah et al., 2024) underperforms SAS by 15.2 points (76.6% vs. 91.8%), demonstrating that the model's optimal decoding path diverges from human logical progression. On GSM8K and MBPP with LLaDA-8B, SAS consistently matches or exceeds all heuristic schedules across generation lengths (128–512) and block sizes (16–256), with the largest gains at longer sequences where left-to-right decoding becomes competitive. The paper also reports comparisons with concurrent order-learning methods (Hong et al., 2025; Jazbec et al., 2025) in Appendix D.4, where SAS's dense self-aware reward is shown to be comparable or superior to sparse 0/1 terminal rewards (Figure 3). Cross-domain transfer experiments confirm that a policy trained on math generalizes to code (and vice versa) with positive gains over the best heuristic in all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked diffusion language model framework (Austin et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2024) and the observation that unmasking schedules act as an implicit 'order of thought.' It departs from prior heuristic schedules (confidence, margin, entropy) and concurrent order-learning methods that rely on task-specific verifiable rewards (Hong et al., 2025; Jazbec et al., 2025) or jointly train the policy with the diffusion backbone (Huang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025a). The key conceptual departure is a KL-divergence-based theoretical bound (Theorem 3.2) that yields a dense, model-self-aware reward—the frozen denoiser's pathwise log-likelihood—eliminating the need for external verifiers. For optimization, it repurposes GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from LLM reasoning to the sequential unmasking MDP, keeping the diffusion backbone frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing decoding order as a learnable, optimizable reasoning primitive, the paper offers a practical plug-and-play recipe (no backbone retraining) that consistently improves structured and open-ended reasoning across scales (1B–8B) and decoding regimes (full diffusion, semi-autoregressive). The finding that a human expert Sudoku-solving order is ~15% worse than the learned policy challenges the assumption that logically natural ordering is optimal for a pretrained model, suggesting model-specific 'easiest paths' differ from human reasoning. Open questions include the teacher-forcing gap between training and free-running inference, whether cross-domain transfer generalizes beyond math/code, and how to combine the self-aware objective with dependency-aware parallel decoding to reduce the within-block conditional total correlation identified in Theorem B.1.
