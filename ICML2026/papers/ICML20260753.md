## Problem

Prior continual RL benchmarks introduce non-stationarity through abrupt task switches, whereas real-world environments evolve gradually via continual drift. This distinction matters because abrupt changes may overemphasize plasticity, masking the fact that stability is the primary bottleneck under naturalistic, continuous environmental change. The paper asks whether predictive representations (Successor Features) are better consolidation targets than Q-values for maintaining stability while preserving adaptability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260753/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

In the Slippery Four Rooms environment, the agent receives 84×84 egocentric RGB pixel observations and selects from 4 discrete actions; in MuJoCo, it receives state vectors and outputs continuous actions. The SF network produces a 10-dimensional successor feature vector ψ and a 10-dimensional reward weight vector w, with Q-values computed as their dot product. The consolidation chain (up to 9 variables) is updated analytically outside the computational graph, and a cross-attention readout uses w as query and the slower SF variables (excluding the most plastic u₁) as keys and values to produce a weighted recall signal.

## Method

The paper applies the neuro-inspired Synaptic Consolidation (SC) mechanism—a chain of K variables with increasing capacities C_k and decreasing flow strengths g_{k,k+1}—to the parameters of Successor Features rather than Q-values. The most plastic variable ψ_{u₁} is trained via SGD on the Q-SF-TD loss, while downstream variables ψ_{u₂}, …, ψ_{u_K} are updated analytically via Euler discretization of the SC dynamics, creating multi-timescale learning where fast variables track recent dynamics and slow variables preserve stable predictive structure. A proof sketch shows that SGD (not Adam) is required to preserve the relative timescale ratios, since Adam's adaptive learning rate inverts the intended κ scaling. The approach is evaluated under periodic noisy sine, non-periodic sine, and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift processes, with cross-attention over consolidation variables used as a diagnostic to quantify the relative contributions of fast versus slow timescales to behavior.

## Results

Across both the Slippery 3D Four Rooms and MuJoCo environments (Half-Cheetah, Walker, Quadruped, Humanoid), stability-preserving methods (EWC, SC) consistently outperform plasticity-injection methods (P-last, CBP) under naturalistic continual non-stationarity. SF+SC (synaptic consolidation applied to Successor Features) achieves the best AUC of episode returns and fewest steps to reach a good policy, particularly under moderate (50%) and severe (100%) mass perturbations. Ablations show that 6–9 consolidation variables outperform 3, and cross-attention analysis reveals that faster timescale variables (SF_u2, SF_u3) drive most learning while slower ones provide complementary stability. SF+SC also outperforms enlarged TD3 variants with substantially more parameters, confirming gains come from consolidation dynamics rather than model capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SF+SC against eight baselines across two environments. Against plasticity-injection methods, SF+SC clearly wins: TD3+P-last (Nikishin et al., 2023) and TD3+CBP (Dohare et al., 2024) both underperform, with CBP showing complete collapse on Humanoid. Against stability-preserving baselines, SF+SC outperforms both online EWC (Schwarz et al., 2018) and SC applied to Q-values (Kaplanis et al., 2018), with the gap widening under stronger non-stationarity. Simple SFs without consolidation (Chua et al., 2024) are outperformed by SF+SC, confirming that multi-timescale consolidation is the key ingredient. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), which relies on trust-region policy updates, SF+SC exhibits substantially greater robustness under continuous non-stationarity (Appendix S). The one setting where SF+SC does not dominate is mild (25%) non-stationarity, where SC on Q-values is competitive, and non-periodic/OU drifts on complex embodiments (Quadruped, Humanoid), where the predictive advantage of SFs is reduced due to lower predictability of the underlying dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Successor Features (SFs) for transferable predictive representations (Barreto et al., 2017; Chua et al., 2024) and neuro-inspired synaptic consolidation (SC) for multi-timescale learning in RL (Benna & Fusi, 2016; Kaplanis et al., 2018, 2019). It departs from prior continual RL work in two key ways: (1) it replaces the standard abrupt task-boundary formulation with naturalistic, continuous non-stationarity (noisy sinusoidal or OU drift) where no discrete task boundaries exist, and (2) it applies SC to the parameters of SFs rather than to Q-values or policies, which prior consolidation work targeted. The plasticity-injection baselines (parameter resets, Continual Backprop) and stability baselines (EWC) are drawn directly from the recent continual RL literature, but are evaluated under the new gradual-drift protocol rather than the sequential-task or single-task settings in which they were originally studied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that stability, not plasticity, is the primary bottleneck under naturalistic continual drift—reframes a debate in continual RL that has been dominated by plasticity-loss analyses (Nikishin et al., 2023; Dohare et al., 2024). The new benchmark (smooth, boundary-free non-stationarity via periodic/non-periodic sine and OU processes) is a practical contribution that better mirrors real-world deployment than abrupt task switches. Limitations include the restriction to SGD (Adam breaks the timescale structure), computational overhead from sequential analytical updates, and evaluation limited to navigation and low-dimensional control tasks; whether the SF+SC approach transfers to high-dimensional perception tasks or multi-agent settings remains open. The cross-attention interpretability tool for quantifying timescale contributions is a useful diagnostic that could inform future multi-timescale architectures.
