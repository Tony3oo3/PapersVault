## Problem

Existing identifiable latent dynamics models for locally-stationary systems F(z_{<t}; c) condition on predefined environment labels, preventing generalization to out-of-distribution environments and failing when labels are unavailable. Additionally, while identifiability of the latent state z_t has been studied, the identifiability of the dynamics function F and environment variable c themselves—critical for prediction beyond reconstruction—remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an n-dimensional observed time series x_{0:T} (the query sample) along with k-shot context samples D^s drawn from the same dynamics environment but distinct from the query. It produces m-dimensional latent dynamics states z_t, a d-dimensional latent environment variable c, and future trajectory predictions x_t for t > l. The key structural constraint is that c is inferred exclusively from the context samples (not the query), and the dynamics are governed by a Neural ODE whose parameters are modulated by c, making the environment variable a continuous, general-purpose modulator rather than a discrete label.

## Method

Meta-iLaD introduces a conditional prior p_ζ(c | D^s) implemented as a feedforward meta-learner that extracts the latent environment variable c from k-shot context samples, replacing predefined labels with data-driven inference. The latent dynamics are parameterized as a Neural ODE f_θ(z_t; c) where c modulates the vector field in a general, unconstrained fashion. Training proceeds via variational inference maximizing an ELBO over the conditional likelihood, augmented with a total-correlation loss (following FactorVAE) to enforce the factorized structure of c and a warm-up strategy that first trains the z_0 encoder via reconstruction before transitioning to the full forecasting objective. Theoretically, the paper establishes simultaneous identifiability of z_t (up to permutation and component-wise invertible transformation), c (up to affine transformation and permutation), and F (up to compositions of such transformations) for a general formulation of F(z_{<t}; c) without restricting the dimensionality or modulation form of c.

## Results

On the Pendulum system, Meta-iLaD achieves near-perfect identifiability with MCC(z)=1.00 and MCC(c)=0.98 across reconstruction, prediction, and OOD generalization, while also delivering the lowest prediction MSE (3.56e-1) and OOD MSE (5.89e-1). On CartPole, Meta-iLaD similarly achieves MCC(z)=0.99 and MCC(c)=0.99 in all settings with the best prediction MSE (2.69e-1) and OOD MSE (4.92e-1). On the real-world CMU MoCAP dataset, Meta-iLaD achieves the lowest reconstruction MSE (2.80e0) and prediction MSE (1.95e1) in-distribution, and remains the most stable under OOD (7.87e1 prediction MSE vs. 1.18e2 for IDF and 1.08e2 for MoNODE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-iLaD decisively outperforms all baselines in prediction and OOD settings. Against IDF (the closest identifiable baseline), Meta-iLaD reduces prediction MSE by 35× on Pendulum (3.56e-1 vs. 1.26e1) and 5.6× on CartPole (2.69e-1 vs. 1.52e0), driven by its superior MCC(c) (0.98 vs. 0.02 on Pendulum; 0.99 vs. 0.16 on CartPole), showing that IDF's process-noise assumption fails to capture general environment parameters. Against MoNODE, Meta-iLaD achieves dramatically higher MCC(c) (0.98 vs. 0.30 on Pendulum prediction) and lower MSE, confirming that inferring c from context samples (conditional prior) is fundamentally different from inferring it from the query sample (unconditional prior). Against ODE2VAE, Meta-iLaD reduces OOD prediction MSE by 13.5× on Pendulum (5.89e-1 vs. 7.97e0) and 13.8× on CartPole (4.92e-1 vs. 6.81e0), demonstrating the critical role of environment-adaptive dynamics. TDRL matches Meta-iLaD on reconstruction MCC(z) but cannot be applied to prediction or OOD due to its reliance on predefined environment labels. On CMU MoCAP, Meta-iLaD achieves the best ID prediction MSE (1.95e1 vs. 3.21e1 for IDF) and the most stable OOD performance (7.87e1 vs. 1.18e2 for IDF), further validating that identifiability of c and F is critical for long-horizon forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiable latent dynamics line built on nonlinear ICA (iVAE, Khemakhem et al. 2020) and the NODE parameterization from ODE2VAE (Yildiz et al. 2019). Prior identifiable models (TDRL, IDF) condition the dynamics function on predefined discrete environment labels, while MoNODE infers the environment from the query sample itself (an unconditional prior). Meta-iLaD departs from both by conditioning on few-shot context samples from the same environment, which the authors show is the key structural difference enabling a conditional prior p(c|D^s) that yields simultaneous identifiability of the latent state z_t, environment variable c, and dynamics function F — a triple not jointly established in any prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that identifiability of the environment variable c and dynamics function F, while nearly invisible in reconstruction metrics, is critical for accurate prediction beyond observed trajectories — a finding that reframes how the field should evaluate latent dynamics models. The meta-learning formulation also enables OOD generalization to unseen environments, which is impossible with predefined labels. Key limitations include the requirement for multiple context samples from the same environment (challenging in clinical or single-patient settings), NODE computational overhead, and the need for sufficient environment diversity in training data. Open questions include whether the few-shot context requirement can be relaxed (e.g., to a single trajectory) and whether the identifiability guarantees hold under weaker assumptions on the mixing function.
