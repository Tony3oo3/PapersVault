## Problem

Reducing the number of crops per image to cut soft-label storage in dataset distillation introduces local semantic drift (LVSD): visually ambiguous crops produce teacher soft labels that deviate from the image-level ground-truth class, causing train-test distribution mismatch and persistent errors. This paper shows that hard labels, which are content-invariant and immune to crop-level ambiguity, can serve as a corrective semantic anchor to calibrate this drift, yet their role has been largely overlooked in favor of soft-only supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small distilled dataset (e.g., 10–50 images per class on ImageNet-1K at 224×224 or Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64), a limited pool of pre-computed teacher soft labels (as few as 1 per image, measured by the SLC budget), and ground-truth class indices. During the hard-label calibration stage, it additionally uses heavily-flattened (label-smoothed, α=0.8) one-hot targets combined with CutMix geometry resampled at every step. The output is a trained student classifier (ResNet, ViT, MobileNet, etc.) evaluated by Top-1 accuracy on the full test set, with the key practical benefit being up to 100× reduction in soft-label storage (e.g., 285 MB vs. 28.33 GB on ImageNet-1K).

## Method

HALD (Hard Label for Alleviating Local Semantic Drift) is a three-stage Soft→Hard→Soft training schedule. Stage A trains the student on the limited soft-label pool until convergence; Stage B switches to heavily-flattened hard labels (label-smoothed one-hot with α=0.8) applied via CutMix over resampled crops, enforcing class-consistent constraints that suppress crop-induced prediction variance; Stage C resumes soft-label training on the variance-reduced representation to restore fine-grained teacher semantics. Theoretically, the paper proves that finite-s soft supervision incurs a strictly positive objective mismatch lower bound of order Θ(s^{-1/2}) (Theorem 3.5) and an excess generalization risk of Ω(1/s) (Theorem 3.6), while showing that soft-hard gradient alignment (Theorem 3.7) increases the effective sample size to s/(1−ρ²) (Corollary 3.8), formally justifying why the mid-training hard-label phase reduces variance without introducing gradient inconsistency.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with SLI=5 (the storage-efficient setting central to this paper), HALD achieves 49.5% Top-1 accuracy at IPC=50 (SLC=250, 475 MB storage), surpassing the prior SOTA LPLD by +10.1%. At the tighter SLC=150 budget (285 MB), HALD reaches 42.7%, outperforming LPLD by +9.0%. On Tiny-ImageNet with SLI=1, IPC=10, HALD achieves 18.6% vs. LPLD's 8.2% (+10.4%). The method also generalizes to CIFAR-100 (21.0% vs. 10.6% Soft-Only at SLC=10) and ImageWoof (27.4% vs. 23.5% at SLC=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALD consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. Against LPLD (the prior SOTA for storage-efficient distillation), HALD gains +10.1% at IPC=50/SLI=5 on ImageNet-1K (49.5% vs. 39.4%) and +10.4% at IPC=10/SLI=1 on Tiny-ImageNet (18.6% vs. 8.2%), with the gap widening under tighter storage budgets (e.g., +22.6% at 95 MB). Against SRe2L, RDED, and FADRM (generation-method baselines using Soft-Only training), HALD leads by 10–15 points on ImageNet-1K at IPC=50/SLI=5. Against GIFT (which fuses hard info into soft targets), HALD achieves 50.7% vs. 46.7% at SLC=300 on ImageNet-1K, while GIFT is roughly on par with Soft-Only. Against LPQLD (soft-label pruning/quantization), HALD wins by +3.4% at IPC=10 and +1.6% at IPC=20. The Joint Objective baseline (simultaneous soft+hard loss) degrades sharply with increasing hard-label weight, confirming that HALD's staged Soft-Hard-Soft schedule is critical to avoiding gradient conflict.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the soft-label distillation pipeline established by SRe2L and the storage-reduction strategy of LPLD, which limits per-image soft labels to cut storage costs. It identifies a previously unaddressed failure mode—Local-View Semantic Drift (LVSD)—that emerges when soft-label coverage is sparse, and positions hard labels (which prior work largely discards as 'too coarse') as a content-invariant corrective anchor. The method departs from GIFT's approach of fusing hard information into soft targets and from naive joint-objective training, instead proposing a staged Soft-Hard-Soft schedule with theoretical justification for why the ordering matters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 100× reduction in soft-label storage (28.33 GB → 285 MB on ImageNet-1K) while simultaneously improving accuracy by 9–10% over the prior SOTA, making large-scale dataset distillation far more deployable. The theoretical framework (lower bounds on drift, gradient-alignment guarantees, effective sample size amplification) provides a principled foundation that could guide future label-budget allocation strategies. Open questions include whether the three-stage schedule generalizes to non-crop augmentations or multi-task settings, whether the label-smoothing hyperparameter (α=0.8) transfers across domains without re-tuning, and whether the LVSD phenomenon extends to generative or retrieval-based distillation paradigms.
