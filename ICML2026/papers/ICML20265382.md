## Problem

Hypervolume-based multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) suffers from two fundamental limitations: exact HV computation scales exponentially with the number of objectives (making it intractable beyond ~4 objectives), and HV maximization introduces a geometric bias that concentrates solutions in steep knee regions while undersampling flat trade-off areas. Existing diversity-guided methods either retain HV computation costs or measure diversity in input space rather than objective space, failing to guarantee uniform Pareto front coverage.

## Input / Output

The method takes m black-box objective functions defined over a bounded d-dimensional input domain, an evaluation budget T, and optionally q constraint functions or user-specified preference region bounds (hyperrectangle [a_i, b_i] per objective). It produces a set of Pareto-optimal input points and their corresponding objective values forming a uniformly distributed approximation of the Pareto front. The GP surrogates use Matérn 5/2 kernels with ARD length-scales, and the inner MOO solver (NSGA-II) uses a population of 300 over 50 generations per iteration.

## Method

STAGE-BO decomposes the MOO problem into a sequence of constrained single-objective subproblems via adaptive ε-constraints, where the constraint thresholds are set by geometric gap-filling rather than fixed grids or random weights. At each iteration, it draws Thompson-sampled GP posterior paths (via Matheron construction), solves a cheap MOO problem with NSGA-II to obtain a sampled Pareto front, then identifies the point on that front with the maximum minimum Euclidean distance to all existing observations (fill distance). The target's coordinates become the ε-constraint thresholds (with a clipping rule for numerical stability in early iterations), and the primary objective is selected via round-robin rotation. The resulting constrained subproblem is solved using Constrained Expected Improvement (cEI), which multiplies standard EI by the probability of feasibility. This design ensures uniform objective-space coverage without any hypervolume computation, and extends naturally to constrained and preference-aware settings by simply appending external constraints or ROI bounds to the ε-constraint set.

## Results

Across six unconstrained MOO benchmarks (2–6 objectives), STAGE-BO consistently achieves the lowest IGD among all baselines while maintaining hypervolume (HV) comparable to the best HV-optimizing methods (qEHVI, qParEGO). The paper highlights an order-of-magnitude reduction in IGD relative to qEHVI on ZDT1/ZDT2 (Figure 1), demonstrating far more uniform Pareto front coverage. In constrained MOO (4 benchmarks) and preference-aware MOO (4 benchmarks), STAGE-BO again leads in IGD and matches or exceeds baselines in HV. On the real-world DP-SGD hyperparameter optimization task, STAGE-BO achieves the highest observed HV throughout the optimization process.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against qEHVI (Daulton et al., 2020), STAGE-BO matches or slightly trails in HV but dramatically outperforms in IGD (by roughly an order of magnitude on ZDT1/ZDT2), and qEHVI becomes computationally intractable for m≥4 objectives (DTLZ7, Water Planning). Against qParEGO (Knowles, 2006) and the scalarization-based TSTCH (Paria et al., 2020), STAGE-BO achieves superior IGD and, in the preference-aware setting, also superior HV, because random weight scalarization fails to produce uniform front coverage. Against information-theoretic methods JESMO (Tu et al., 2022) and MESMO (Belakaria et al., 2019), STAGE-BO outperforms in IGD while matching HV, and avoids the heavy approximations those methods require. Against diversity-focused baselines qPOTS (Renganathan & Carlson, 2025) and MOBO-OSD (Ngo et al., 2025), STAGE-BO achieves lower IGD and IGD+ because qPOTS measures diversity in input space (not guaranteeing output-space coverage) and MOBO-OSD still relies on HV for final selection. Against constrained-MOO baselines COMBOO (Li et al., 2025) and qPOTS, STAGE-BO leads in IGD and maintains high feasible-ratio, while COMBOO terminates early on MW7 due to its UCB-based feasibility estimates. Against decomposition-based EGO methods MOEA/D-EGO (Zhang et al., 2009) and DirHV-EGO (Zhao & Zhang, 2023), STAGE-BO achieves lower IGD with comparable HV, confirming that adaptive gap-filling via fill distance outperforms fixed or HV-guided decomposition strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAGE-BO builds on the classical ε-constraint decomposition (Haimes, 1971; Mavrotas, 2009) and the Fill Distance metric from Zhang et al. (2024), but departs fundamentally from the dominant HV-maximization paradigm (qEHVI, TSEMO) and scalarization approaches (ParEGO, TS-TCH) that either exhibit geometric bias toward steep knee regions or fail to guarantee uniform objective-space coverage. Unlike recent diversity-guided methods (DGEMO, PDBO, MOBO-OSD) that still retain HV computation or rely on input-space diversity, STAGE-BO replaces the acquisition objective entirely with a geometric gap-filling criterion, solving each subproblem via constrained expected improvement. The method also unifies standard, constrained, and preference-aware MOBO under a single constraint-augmentation framework, extending the ε-constraint machinery beyond its traditional role as a passive solver into a proactive, adaptive targeting strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating hypervolume computation, STAGE-BO removes the exponential-in-objectives bottleneck that makes HV-based methods intractable beyond four objectives, while simultaneously fixing the well-documented geometric bias that causes undersampling of flat trade-off regions. The unified constraint-augmentation framework for handling physical constraints and user preferences without structural modifications is practically valuable for engineering and scientific optimization. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the NSGA-II-sampled surrogate front (limiting scalability to m > 6 objectives) and sensitivity of gap detection to measurement noise, both flagged as open directions. The paper also positions STAGE-BO as a natural substrate for future interactive MOO where preferences are encoded as soft/hard bounds rather than fixed weights.
