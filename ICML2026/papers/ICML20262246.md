## Problem

Constitutional AI aligns single models with fixed human-written principles, but multi-agent LLM systems face emergent coordination failures that static ethical rules cannot address. Hand-crafted constitutions like 'be helpful, harmless, honest' provide insufficient operational guidance when agents must trade off self-preservation against collective welfare under competitive pressure. This paper asks whether effective multi-agent behavioral norms can be automatically discovered through evolutionary optimization of natural-language rules rather than prescribed by hand.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial constitution (a set of named, priority-ordered natural-language rules), a 6×6 grid-world environment with 6 LLM agents under partial observability (3×3 local view), and per-iteration simulation feedback including the scalar Stability Score and detailed action logs. It outputs an evolved constitution as 7 priority-ordered natural-language rules (e.g., 'Deposit First', 'Dynamic Targeting') that are applied as prompts to GPT-OSS-120B agents at inference time—no model weights are updated. The fitness signal is the expected Stability Score S = max(0, 0.5·P + 0.3·V − 0.2·C) averaged over K sampled trajectories, where P, V, and C capture productivity, survival rate, and conflict frequency respectively.

## Method

The framework treats the constitution as an optimizable symbolic object and searches over it using LLM-driven genetic programming with a multi-island architecture (3 populations × 10 constitutions, 30 iterations, 20% ring-topology migration every 5 iterations, MAP-Elites diversity in an 8×8 complexity-vs-score grid). At each mutation step, GPT-OSS-120B receives the parent constitution's code plus its simulation metrics (stability score, productivity %, conflict %) and proposes targeted rule modifications; the child is then evaluated by running the full 40-turn multi-agent simulation twice. The key differentiator from one-shot LLM design or hand-crafting is the closed-loop evolutionary pressure: the search discovers operationally specific, counter-intuitive rules—most notably 'Deposit First' (immediately deposit carried resources) and communication minimization (broadcast only for 2+ resource clusters, reducing social actions from 62.2% to 0.9%)—that neither human designers nor a single Claude 4.5 Opus prompt could identify, yielding S = 0.556 ± 0.008 versus 0.249 (HHH) and 0.332 (LLM-Generated).

## Results

The evolved constitution C* achieves a Societal Stability Score of S=0.556±0.008 (N=10), a 123% improvement over the human-designed HHH baseline (S=0.249) and 67% over the one-shot LLM-Generated baseline (S=0.332). Productivity reaches 91% under C* versus 30% (HHH) and 51% (LLM-Gen.), while conflict is eliminated (0% aggressive actions). C* also reduces social/communication actions from 62.2% (HHH) to 0.9%, a 98.6% reduction, while maintaining the maximum achievable survival rate of 33% (2/6 agents).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the HHH baseline (Bai et al., 2022; Askell et al., 2021), C* wins decisively: S=0.556 vs. 0.249 (123% higher), with productivity jumping from 30% to 91% and social actions dropping from 62.2% to 0.9%. The HHH constitution's vague directives ('Be Helpful,' 'Be Honest') cause agents to spend most turns broadcasting rather than depositing, and its equal-priority rules create decision ambiguity. Against the LLM-Generated baseline (Claude 4.5 Opus, Anthropic 2025), C* also wins: S=0.556 vs. 0.332 (67% higher), with productivity at 91% vs. 51%. Notably, the LLM-Generated constitution was designed with full knowledge of the game mechanics and optimization objective, yet still suffers from excessive communication (54.7% social actions) and lacks the critical 'Deposit First' insight that evolution discovers at iteration 23. Both baselines achieve the same 33% survival rate as C*, confirming that the performance gap is driven entirely by productivity and conflict management rather than survival.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Constitutional AI paradigm (Bai et al., 2022), which aligns single LLMs via hand-written natural-language principles, to the multi-agent setting by treating constitutions as evolvable objects rather than fixed prescriptions. The optimization engine is OpenEvolve (Sharma, 2025), a multi-island evolutionary coding agent that the authors repurpose from program synthesis (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve) to mutate natural-language behavioral rules guided by simulation feedback. The multi-agent coordination problem itself draws on classical game-theoretic and MARL literature (Axelrod & Hamilton, Leibo et al.), but the specific formulation—evolving interpretable governance rules for LLM agents under partial observability and survival pressure—is a synthesis not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that cooperative norms for multi-agent LLM systems can be discovered through evolutionary search rather than prescribed by hand, producing interpretable rules that outperform both human-written (HHH) and one-shot LLM-generated constitutions by 123% and 67% respectively. The counter-intuitive finding that minimizing communication (0.9% vs. 62.2% social actions) dramatically improves coordination challenges the default assumption that explicit messaging is necessary for effective multi-agent alignment. However, the evidence is confined to a 6×6 grid with six agents and a single LLM (GPT-OSS-120B), leaving open whether evolved constitutions transfer to larger populations, heterogeneous agents, or real-world domains; robustness to non-compliant agents and specification gaming remain unaddressed.
