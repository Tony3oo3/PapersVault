## Problem

Residual RL freezes a pretrained base policy and learns only a bounded correction, yet even state-of-the-art methods require over a million online interactions. The paper identifies that this sample inefficiency stems from two overlooked value-learning bottlenecks: a cold start pathology where the randomly initialized critic lacks any knowledge of the value landscape around the base policy, and a structural scale mismatch where the small residual (scaled by λ≈0.1) is dwarfed by the dominant base action, making credit assignment to the residual nearly impossible.

## Input / Output

The system takes robot state vectors (39–131 dimensions depending on task) and optionally 64×64 RGBD images from base and hand-mounted cameras as input. The output is a combined action a = a_base + λ·a_res, where a_base comes from a frozen Diffusion Policy or Behavior Transformer and a_res is produced by a lightweight 2-layer MLP (256 units, ReLU) with near-zero initialization. The critic receives the summed action as input, which is the structural source of the scale mismatch the paper addresses.

## Method

DAWN (Data-Anchored Warmup and Normalization) addresses the two bottlenecks with minimal interventions on top of standard SAC. First, 20K transitions are collected from the base policy alone before training begins, seeding the replay buffer to serve as a value anchor that grounds the critic to the relevant value landscape; the paper shows this is both necessary and sufficient, and that explicit critic pre-training actually fails because freezing the residual policy traps it in a near-deterministic regime where the entropy term dominates the TD target. Second, Layer Normalization is applied to the critic network to decouple internal representations from input magnitudes, restoring sensitivity to the λ-scaled residual; the paper demonstrates via gradient-norm and value-difference diagnostics that normalization enables the critic to detect residual variations and attribute increasing value to them, while distributional objectives (C51, QR, TQC) offer no benefit because the residual's effect on Q-values manifests as a clear mean shift rather than a distributional shape change.

## Results

DAWN achieves comparable final success rates to prior methods while converging approximately 5× faster, as measured by aggregated success rates across ManiSkill (3 tasks) and Adroit (3 tasks) with Diffusion Policy as the base policy. On the most challenging task (PegInsertionSide), DAWN reaches 90% success approximately 6× faster than Policy Decorator. Across all 8 tasks (including 2 with visual observations), DAWN consistently achieves superior sample efficiency and exhibits notably low variance across 8 random seeds, indicating improved training stability. The gains hold when switching the base policy from Diffusion Policy to Behavior Transformer (BeT) on Adroit tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Policy Decorator (Yuan et al., 2025), the state-of-the-art residual RL method, DAWN wins decisively on sample efficiency across all 8 tasks, reaching target success rates roughly 5–6× faster while achieving comparable or slightly higher final performance; the gap is largest on precision-critical tasks like PegInsertionSide. Against Vanilla Residual RL (Johannink et al., 2019), DAWN's advantage is even more pronounced, as the minimal baseline lacks both bounded residuals and progressive exploration. Against ResFiT (Ankile et al., 2025a), DAWN matches or exceeds performance while requiring only base-policy rollouts rather than a reward-annotated offline dataset, making it strictly more practical. Against diffusion-policy fine-tuning methods DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025) and DPPO (Ren et al., 2024), DAWN remains competitive, confirming that the value-learning bottleneck is orthogonal to the choice of base policy architecture. All comparisons use 8 random seeds with 95% confidence intervals, and DAWN's low inter-seed variance is a consistent differentiator across every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions residual RL (Johannink et al., 2019; Yuan et al., 2025) by shifting the analytical lens from policy-side stabilization (bounded residuals, progressive exploration) to the value-learning side, identifying cold start pathology and structural scale mismatch as the root causes of sample inefficiency. It builds on SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) as the underlying actor-critic algorithm and on Policy Decorator (Yuan et al., 2025) as the primary residual RL framework, but deliberately strips away progressive exploration to expose raw value-learning dynamics. The individual techniques proposed—base-policy warmup data and Layer Normalization—are well-established in off-policy RL and deep learning respectively; the contribution is the mechanistic diagnosis of why and where they become essential in the residual setting, a question prior policy-centric work left unexamined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5× sample-efficiency gain from two minimal interventions (20K base-policy rollouts + critic LN) is practically significant for real-world robot deployment where interaction budgets are tight, and the mechanistic insights (entropy dominance under frozen policies, mean-shift structure of residual value signals) save practitioners from pursuing dead-end approaches like explicit critic pre-training or distributional objectives. Key limitations include evaluation entirely in simulation, reliance on relatively compact base policies (~4M params), and moderate task difficulty; whether the findings transfer to large VLA models, real hardware with costly resets, and substantially harder or longer-horizon tasks remains open. The work also highlights a broader research philosophy: tracing problems to root causes in value learning can yield simpler and more effective solutions than layering defensive policy-side mechanisms.
