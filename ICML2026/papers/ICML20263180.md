## Problem

Existing SpMV methods perform poorly under the low, unstructured sparsity (30–90%) commonly produced by LLM pruning, because they either require specialized hardware units, extensive precomputation, or target only high sparsity levels. As a result, unstructured pruning of LLMs yields only limited practical memory reduction and speedup, making it less attractive than structured 2:4 sparsity despite its superior model quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse matrix of pruned LLM weights (fp16, b_val=16) in the MACKO format—delta-encoded column indices (b_delta=4 bits, packed 2 per byte) with strategically inserted zero-padding values—and a dense input activation vector, producing a dense output activation vector. The format is parameter-free and works out-of-the-box across matrix sizes from ~4096 to ~36864 dimensions and sparsity from 30% to 90%, with no per-row or per-matrix tuning required.

## Method

MACKO compresses column indices via delta encoding bounded to a fixed range [1, 2^b_delta], inserting zero-padding values into the matrix to keep all deltas within range; this mutual alignment between coordinates and values is the key design insight that enables a simple, hardware-friendly GPU kernel. The SpMV kernel adapts the SplitK GEMM algorithm: each warp of 32 threads processes one row, loading 128 bytes of packed deltas and 512 bytes of values per step in fully coalesced 128-byte transactions. Column indices are reconstructed on-the-fly using a warp-shuffling exclusive prefix sum (Algorithm 1), adding only computational overhead with zero additional memory traffic—critical because SpMV is memory-bandwidth-bound. The effective density of MACKO at 50% sparsity is ~0.625 (vs. 1.0 for dense), yielding 1.5× memory reduction and 1.2–1.5× speedup over cuBLAS, while outperforming cuSPARSE (2.8–13.0×), Sputnik (1.9–2.6×), and DASP (2.2–2.5×) across all tested GPUs and sparsity levels.

## Results

At 50% sparsity on Llama2-7b (FP16, RTX 4090), MACKO achieves 1.53× memory reduction (13.59 GB → 8.87 GB) and 1.4× inference speedup (66.53 → 98.60 toks/sec) over the dense cuBLAS baseline. In pure SpMV benchmarks on a 12288×12288 matrix, MACKO reaches 1.3× speedup over cuBLAS at 50% sparsity, scaling to 5.62× at 95% sparsity. MACKO matches or exceeds dense cuBLAS performance at sparsity as low as 20–30%, a threshold where existing sparse libraries require 2.6× fewer non-zeros to reach parity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACKO outperforms the dense cuBLAS baseline at all sparsity levels above 30%, reaching 1.3× speedup at 50% and 5.62× at 95% on a 12288×12288 matrix (RTX 4090). Against sparse baselines, MACKO achieves 2.8–13.0× speedup over cuSPARSE, 2.2–2.5× over DASP, and 1.9–2.6× over Sputnik across the 30–90% sparsity range; however, Sputnik surpasses MACKO at the extreme 95% sparsity level. In the end-to-end Llama2-7b evaluation, MACKO delivers 1.53× memory reduction and 1.4× speedup at 50% sparsity, scaling to 5.1× memory reduction and 3.8× speedup at 90% sparsity. Compared to 2:4 structured sparsity on Llama3-8B/70B, unstructured pruning with MACKO achieves significantly better perplexity at the same effective density (6.43 vs. 8.15 for 70B; 11.28 vs. 23.58 for 8B) and a higher speedup (1.8× vs. 1.6×) at matched perplexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACKO builds on the SplitK GEMM algorithm from NVIDIA's CUTLASS library, adapting it from matrix-matrix to matrix-vector multiplication, and draws conceptual inspiration from the CSR sparse format. However, it departs from prior SpMV work (Sputnik, DASP, cuSPARSE) by eliminating dependencies on specialized hardware units, extensive precomputation, or high-sparsity assumptions. The key conceptual departure is the 'mutual alignment' principle: by delta-encoding column indices and strategically padding values to keep deltas within a fixed bit-width, MACKO achieves a storage format whose access patterns are naturally aligned with GPU warp-level parallelism, something that CSR-based and tiling-based approaches cannot guarantee at low sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MACKO makes unstructured pruning at 50% sparsity practically viable on commodity consumer GPUs (RTX 2080/3090/4090) without any hardware modifications, closing the efficiency gap with structured 2:4 sparsity while preserving the superior model quality of unstructured pruning. The open-source PyTorch integration and parameter-free design lower the adoption barrier significantly. Remaining limitations include degraded performance above 95% sparsity, lack of support for quantized values (8-bit, 4-bit), and evaluation limited to consumer GPUs and matrix-vector (decode-phase) workloads; extending to matrix-matrix multiplication with small batch dimensions and combining with quantization are natural next steps.
