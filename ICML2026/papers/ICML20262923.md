## Problem

Existing wireless signal recognition (WSR) models operate directly on received signals that are distorted by non-Gaussian physical channel effects (multipath fading, carrier frequency offset, sampling rate offset), yet current denoising diffusion models only handle Gaussian noise and lack de-channeling capability. This mismatch between the physical degradation process and the assumed corruption model leads to degraded recognition accuracy, particularly in low-SNR and complex channel conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262923/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a received IQ signal of length L (represented as a 2×L real matrix for in-phase and quadrature components) and its Fourier-transformed spectrum, both corrupted by unknown channel effects and additive noise. During pretraining, the model learns to reconstruct the received signal from progressively channel-corrupted and noise-added versions; at inference, it produces a de-channelized representation closer to the transmitted signal, which is then fed to a lightweight classifier (global average pooling + MLP) to output a WSR label (modulation type, wireless technology type, or anomaly type).

## Method

PWC-Diff reformulates the diffusion forward process in the spectral domain, where physical channel convolution reduces to element-wise multiplication, and superimposes a learned channel distortion (estimated by a lightweight CPE-Net that predicts parameters of predefined candidate channel models such as Rician fading, CFO, SRO, and multipath) together with Gaussian noise. A key theoretical contribution proves that an unconstrained neural network for channel estimation degenerates to the identity mapping (H_φ ≡ 1), collapsing the model to standard DDPM; the candidate-based CPE-Net design provably prevents this degenerate solution. The reverse process is implemented by FusedFormer, which jointly encodes temporal IQ and spectral features via parallel Conv1D branches, concatenation, multi-head self-attention, and GLU-enhanced depth-wise convolutions to capture both time-domain and frequency-domain structure. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: self-supervised pretraining with a spectral-domain reconstruction loss (jointly optimizing channel parameters φ and denoiser θ), followed by supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy for the target WSR task.

## Results

PWC-Diff achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across all four datasets and three WSR tasks (AMC, WTC, AD), with an average rank of 1.0. On RML2016.10A it reaches 63.60% average / 93.91% best accuracy, on RML2022 69.97% / 98.64%, on TechRec 91.02% / 99.23%, and on GNSS 84.75% average. This represents approximately 0.89% average improvement over the strongest baseline (IQFormer) and about 3.33% improvement over IQFormer specifically on the AMC task, with the largest gains observed on TechRec (91.02% vs. 85.00% for IQFormer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PWC-Diff outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset, achieving the best average and best-SNR accuracy in all four settings. Against the strongest supervised baselines (AMC_Net, MSNet, MCNet), PWC-Diff leads by 2.7–3.6% average accuracy on RML2016.10A and by 1.5–3.8% on RML2022. The most notable margin is on TechRec, where PWC-Diff reaches 91.02% average versus 88.79% (MSNet) and 86.62% (AMC_Net), a gap of 2.2–4.4%. Against the two pretraining-based baselines, PWC-Diff improves over IQFormer (the prior SOTA AMC model) by 0.06% average on RML2016.10A, 0.75% on RML2022, 6.02% on TechRec, and 6.49% on GNSS; over SpectrumFM the gains are 3.75%, 1.10%, 11.39%, and 1.11% respectively. GRU2 and ResNet are clear outliers, with GRU2 collapsing to 37.79% on TechRec and ResNet to 36.18% on RML2022, highlighting the fragility of architectures that do not jointly model temporal and spectral structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

PWC-Diff extends the DDPM diffusion framework by replacing the standard Gaussian-noise forward process with physically motivated channel distortions, reframing the reverse process as de-channeling rather than mere denoising. The FusedFormer architecture is directly inspired by IQFormer's temporal-spectral fusion and borrows the GLU-based local feature enhancement from SpectrumFM, while the pretrain-then-finetune representation learning paradigm follows DDAE. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based WSR work (e.g., TS-DDAE) is the explicit incorporation of parameterized physical channel models (Rician, multipath, CFO, SRO) into the corruption schedule, supported by a theoretical result showing that unconstrained neural networks for channel estimation degenerate to the identity mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in WSR: existing models extract features from channel-distorted received signals without explicitly inverting the physical degradation, and this work shows that physics-informed diffusion pretraining yields more robust representations. However, the candidate-channel approach requires dataset-specific knowledge of which channel models to include, limiting generalizability to truly unknown propagation environments. The modest empirical gains (~0.9% over the best baseline) and the reliance on predefined channel families leave open the question of whether fully blind channel adaptation within a diffusion framework is feasible. Future work on more principled blind channel estimation and broader channel model coverage would strengthen the practical impact.
