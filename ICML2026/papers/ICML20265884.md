## Problem

Multi-task bilevel learning (MTBL) under lower-level general convexity (LLGC) has remained unexplored: existing bilevel methods either require the restrictive lower-level strong convexity assumption or are confined to single-task settings, and the multi-objective optimality criterion under LLGC is ill-defined. Compounding this, the equality-constrained multi-objective optimization (ECMO) problem that naturally arises from reformulating MTBL has no theoretical foundations—no Pareto stationarity characterization, no convergence metric, and no finite-time convergence guarantees exist in the literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the S upper-level objective functions f_s(x,y), the q lower-level gradient constraint functions h_i(z) = ∇_{y_i}g(x,y), a preference vector λ in the strictly positive S-simplex, and penalty hyperparameters u, v. The output is a Pareto stationary solution z = [x, y] whose quality is measured by the KKT-system norm ‖K(ρ, z, ω, ν, λ)‖², and by sweeping λ across the simplex the algorithm traces out the full Pareto stationary front. The convergence rate is O(ST^{-1/2}) in both deterministic and stochastic settings, with iteration complexity O(S²ε^{-2}) gradient evaluations per objective and constraint.

## Method

The core idea is a two-stage reformulation: first, the MTBL problem under LLGC is converted to an ECMO problem by replacing the lower-level argmin with its first-order stationarity condition ∇_y g(x,y) = 0 (valid under convexity); second, a Weighted-Chebyshev (WC) scalarization is applied to the ECMO problem, establishing a one-to-one correspondence between locally weak Pareto optimal solutions of ECMO and locally optimal solutions of the scalarized problem. This correspondence allows the KKT conditions of the WC-scalarized problem to serve as a necessary-and-sufficient characterization of Pareto stationarity for ECMO, yielding a concrete convergence metric. The proposed WC-Penalty algorithm then solves a penalized reformulation of the WC-scalarized problem (with slack variables for the min-max structure) via projected gradient descent, where the penalty coefficients u and v are tuned as Θ(T^{1/4}) and the step-size as Θ(T^{-1/4}) to balance constraint satisfaction against objective descent. The resulting O(ST^{-1/2}) convergence rate is, to the authors' knowledge, the first finite-time guarantee for any ECMO algorithm.

## Results

On the multi-objective LLM alignment task (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, HelpSteer dataset), the proposed WC-Penalty method achieves a hypervolume (HV) of 2.47e-2, substantially outperforming MOML (7.02e-7) and MoCo (1.66e-5) by roughly 3,500× and 1,500× respectively. The method also achieves a finite-time convergence rate of O(ST^{-1/2}) to KKT-based Pareto stationarity, matching the best existing rate for MTBL but under the weaker lower-level general convexity (LLGC) assumption rather than the standard lower-level strong convexity (LLSC) assumption. By varying the preference vector λ over the S-dimensional simplex, the algorithm systematically recovers a large portion of the Pareto front across both the RLHF reward model training and LLM alignment experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines across two MTBL tasks. On the LLM alignment task, WC-Penalty dominates MOML (Ye et al., 2021) and MoCo (Fernando et al., 2023) in hypervolume by orders of magnitude (2.47e-2 vs. 7.02e-7 and 1.66e-5), and also outperforms them on the ε-metric across all preference vectors. On the RLHF reward model training task, WC-Penalty outperforms MOML, MoCo, and FORUM (Ye et al., 2024) in recovering the Pareto front (Fig. 6b). For the LLM alignment task, the method also beats two bilevel baselines adapted from Ji et al. (2021) and Dagréou et al. (2022) that were extended with linear scalarization (Fig. 7b). A critical distinction is that all five baselines require the lower-level strong convexity (LLSC) assumption, whereas WC-Penalty operates under the weaker LLGC assumption, making it applicable to a broader class of real-world problems where the LLSC condition is typically violated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research lines: multi-task bilevel learning (MTBL), which has been studied exclusively under the restrictive lower-level strong convexity (LLSC) assumption (Ye et al. 2021; Fernando et al. 2023; Ye et al. 2024), and equality constrained multi-objective optimization (ECMO), for which only heuristic evolutionary algorithms exist without convergence guarantees (Cuate et al. 2020; García et al. 2021). The key conceptual move is reformulating MTBL under the relaxed lower-level general convexity (LLGC) assumption into an ECMO problem, then leveraging the Weighted-Chebyshev scalarization framework from Ehrgott (2005) to establish a KKT-based Pareto stationarity characterization. This departs from prior MTBL work that relies on hyper-gradients (ill-defined without LLSC) by eliminating the need for second-order information entirely through the penalty-based single-level reformulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time convergence guarantee for ECMO and the first MTBL algorithm under LLGC, removing a key assumption that limited prior bilevel methods to strongly convex lower-level problems — a condition frequently violated in modern ML (e.g., reward model training for RLHF). The reformulation elegantly closes the loop between MTBL and ECMO, providing a unified theoretical lens. Open questions include scalability to very large numbers of objectives S, extension to nonconvex lower-level problems, and whether the O(ST^{-1/2}) rate is tight. The experimental validation, while showing large hypervolume gains, is limited to a single dataset (HelpSteer) and a small model (Llama-3.2-1B), leaving open whether the advantage persists at scale.
