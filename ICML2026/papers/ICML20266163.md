## Problem

Self-supervised learning for Spiking Neural Networks remains unsolved at scale because the non-differentiable spike function breaks the bidirectional, cross-view gradient flow required by contrastive and redundancy-reduction objectives. Prior SNN 'SSL' methods either depend on supervised fine-tuning after pretraining, are restricted to low-resolution benchmarks like MNIST or CIFAR, or adapt ANN-style pretext tasks without exploiting the distinctive temporal dynamics of spiking computation. No existing work demonstrates a fully self-supervised, scalable SNN framework operating at ImageNet-1K scale or transferring to dense prediction tasks.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either static RGB images (replicated across T time steps via direct encoding, 224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) or event streams (128×128, with temporal augmentations including time-reversal, frame dropout, and polarity jitter). Two independently augmented views of the same input are processed through a shared-weight dual-path encoder. At inference, only the spiking path is retained, producing sparse, event-driven visual representations that can be frozen for linear probing or fine-tuned for classification, object detection, and instance segmentation.

## Method

The core contribution is the MixedLIF neuron, a dual-path activation where Path A uses standard LIF spiking dynamics with a clipped-rectangular surrogate gradient, while Path B uses the antiderivative of that surrogate (a clipped ReLU) to produce smooth, range-continuous activations that stabilize gradient flow for SSL objectives. Both paths share identical trainable weights; during backpropagation, gradients are computed separately by detaching the complementary path, then summed for the weight update. Two temporal alignment losses extend the Barlow Twins cross-correlation objective across time: Cross Temporal Loss (CTL) aligns all T×T pairs of time steps between the two views (O(T²) cost, used for event-driven data), and Boundary Temporal Loss (BTL) aligns only the first and last time steps (O(1) cost, used for static data where LIF leaky integration makes intermediate states well-approximated by boundary frames). The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both Spiking-ResNet (CNN) and Spikformer (ViT) backbones, and at inference discards the continuous Path B entirely, preserving the energy-efficient, accumulate-only spiking computation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K linear evaluation, Spiking-ResNet50 with Boundary Temporal Loss achieves 69.5% Top-1 accuracy (89.3% Top-5), while Spikformer-8-512 reaches 70.1% Top-1 (89.9% Top-5). On CIFAR-10, Spiking-ResNet34 attains 85.6% Top-1, surpassing the non-spiking Barlow Twins ResNet34 baseline (84.2%). On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, Spiking-ResNet34 with Cross Temporal Loss achieves 63.9% Top-1 under linear evaluation, and reaches 72.31% under full fine-tuning, substantially outperforming the prior SNN SSL baseline (64.1% with hybrid inputs and fine-tuning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K, Spiking-ResNet50 (69.5%) marginally beats SimCLR (69.3%) but trails Barlow Twins (73.2%) by 3.7% and MoCo v2 (71.1%) by 1.6%; Spikformer-8-512 (70.1%) narrows the gap to Barlow Twins to 3.1%. On CIFAR-10, the method clearly wins: Spiking-ResNet34 (85.6%) outperforms Barlow Twins ResNet34 (84.2%) by 1.4% and ViT-4-384 (83.9%) by 1.7%, while also surpassing the prior SNN SSL baseline of Bahariasl & Kheradpisheh (62.2%) by a large 23.4% margin. On CIFAR10-DVS, under the linear evaluation protocol, Spiking-ResNet34 (63.9%) is within 0.2% of Singhal et al. (64.1%), but Singhal's result relies on hybrid event-frame inputs and full fine-tuning; under an equivalent fine-tuning protocol, the paper's Spiking-ResNet18 reaches 70.21% and Spiking-ResNet34 reaches 72.31%, surpassing Singhal by 6.1% and 8.2% respectively using only clean event inputs. In semi-supervised settings (1% labels on CIFAR-10), Spiking-ResNet34 (75.1%) exceeds Barlow Twins (73.6%) by 1.5%, and at 10% labels the gap widens to 2.0% (87.6% vs. 85.6%). For COCO object detection, Spiking-ResNet50 achieves 37.8 AP, slightly below Barlow Twins (39.2 AP) by 1.4 points, indicating a small but consistent gap on dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Barlow Twins redundancy-reduction objective to the spiking domain, introducing the MixedLIF dual-path neuron (spiking path + antiderivative surrogate path) to resolve the gradient mismatch that prevents standard surrogate-gradient training from supporting cross-view contrastive flow. It departs from prior SNN SSL attempts (Qiu et al. 2023, Bahariasl & Kheradpisheh 2024, Singhal et al. 2024), which were limited to small datasets, required supervised fine-tuning, or adapted ANN pretext tasks without exploiting spike-timing dynamics. Architecturally, it builds on Spikformer for the ViT-based SNN backbone and on DIET-style direct encoding for static inputs, while the temporal alignment losses (Cross Temporal and Boundary Temporal) are novel objectives that treat all or boundary time-step pairs as contrastive anchors rather than aligning only matched timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a fully self-supervised SNN can be pretrained at ImageNet-1K scale and transferred to dense prediction tasks (COCO detection/segmentation) without any labeled fine-tuning, closing a gap that prior SNN SSL work left open. The 136× inference energy reduction over equivalent ANN SSL models, validated on Loihi via Lava-DL with <1.2% accuracy loss, makes the approach practically relevant for edge and neuromorphic deployment. Key limitations include the still-substantial GPU cost of pretraining, the non-spiking projection head (converting it to spiking costs ~0.8% accuracy), and the fact that on ImageNet the SNN SSL still trails Barlow Twins by ~3–4 points in linear evaluation. Open questions include whether the dual-path design generalizes to video or multi-modal SNN SSL, and whether fully spiking end-to-end architectures can close the remaining gap to ANN SSL at scale.
