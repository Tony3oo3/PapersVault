## Problem

Understanding how specific data characteristics drive LLM behavior remains an open problem because current approaches rely on expensive empirical heuristics with large real-world datasets whose underlying distributions are unknown. This paper advocates for 'data probes'—synthetic sequences generated from fully specified random processes—as a systematic, resource-efficient tool to isolate and study the causal relationship between data properties and model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266521/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The probe generation process takes a target entropy rate H (in bits/token) and a state-space size M, then searches for a Markov chain transition matrix whose entropy rate is closest to H. The probe-LLM (GPT-2 small with embedding layer resized to M) is trained on sequences of length n=128 drawn from this chain, and at inference time receives a single prompt token sampled from the stationary distribution π. Outputs are generated token sequences scored against the known Markov law, yielding per-sequence average NLL values and regime labels (over-conservative, typical, uncertain) relative to an ε-band around H; a pre-trained GPT-2 (text-LLM) provides a qualitative real-side comparator under matched temperature settings.

## Method

The data-probe methodology defines a formal object Π = (P, M, H, F) comprising a known generative process with controllable knobs, measurable diagnostics, testable claims, and pre-declared falsification rules, validated through four criteria (C1–C4) and a two-layer evaluation protocol (internal validity in probe space, external validity in matched real settings). In the worked example, a Markov chain with H=1 bit/token and M=128 states generates training data for a GPT-2 small model whose embedding layer is tailored to the chain's vocabulary; after training, the model's outputs are scored by computing average NLL under the known Markov distribution and assigning typical-set regime labels using an ε=0.2 band. Varying the decoding temperature T from greedy (T=0) through T=1, 1.3, and 1.5 reveals a directional shift from over-conservative to typical to uncertain regimes, mirroring qualitative degeneration patterns in the pre-trained text-LLM. The key differentiator from prior theoretical analyses is that the known ground-truth distribution makes likelihood computation and information-theoretic interpretation (typical sets, entropy bounds) directly applicable to practical transformer models, bridging theory and practice without requiring massive real datasets.

## Results

This is a position paper with a preliminary illustrative experiment rather than a full empirical study. The key quantitative finding comes from training a GPT-2 small model (probe-LLM) on Markov chain data probes with target entropy rate H=1 bit/token and state space M=128, then measuring average NLL of generated sequences under the known Markov distribution. With ε=0.2, greedy decoding yields average NLL of 0.694 (over-conservative regime), T=1 gives 0.866 and T=1.3 gives 0.979 (both within the typical set), and T=1.5 gives 1.406 (uncertain regime), confirming the predicted directional shift from over-conservative to uncertain as temperature increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's only direct comparison is between the probe-LLM (GPT-2 small trained from scratch on Markov chain data probes) and the pre-trained openai-community/gpt2 (text-LLM) as a matched real-side comparator. The comparison is explicitly qualitative rather than quantitative: both models exhibit the same directional decoding behavior—greedy decoding produces repetitive/over-conservative outputs, moderate temperatures (T=1, T=1.3) produce typical/coherent outputs, and high temperature (T=1.5) produces uncertain/off-topic outputs. The authors note this is a 'preliminary controlled validation step with qualitative alignment, not a formal transfer verdict,' and the real-side comparator is marked as 'Qualitative only' in their reduction record (Table 1, Step 4). No numerical performance gap is reported between the two models; the value of the comparison lies in demonstrating that the typical-set regime framework, validated quantitatively on the probe-LLM via known-distribution NLL computation, qualitatively matches observed behavior in a real pre-trained LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper carves new methodological ground by formalizing 'data probes'—synthetic sequences from fully specified random processes—as a systematic tool for studying data-LLM relationships, going beyond the simplified Markov-chain analyses of Makkuva et al. (2025) and Rajaraman et al. (2024) which lack pre-declared falsification criteria and transfer protocols. It repurposes information-theoretic concepts (typical sets, entropy rate) as practical diagnostics for LLM behavior, bridging the gap between theoretical transformer analyses and empirical data-curation heuristics (Wettig et al., 2024; Penedo et al., 2024). The paper explicitly departs from the 'physics of LLMs' approach (Allen-Zhu, 2024) by emphasizing that the known statistical distribution of probe data, not just hand-crafted structure, is what enables principled analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: current understanding of how data characteristics drive LLM behavior relies on expensive, case-by-case empirical heuristics with no principled framework for controlled experimentation. The formal validation protocol (C1–C4 criteria, IV/EV transfer logic, reduction records) provides a reusable structure that could standardize how the community tests data-related hypotheses. However, the experimental evidence is extremely preliminary—a single GPT-2 small model trained on a 128-state Markov chain with only qualitative real-side alignment—so the practical impact remains speculative. Key open questions include whether probe-based insights transfer to richer linguistic phenomena (compositionality, pragmatics, factual knowledge), how to design probe families for multi-stage LLM workflows, and whether the framework scales beyond toy generative processes.
