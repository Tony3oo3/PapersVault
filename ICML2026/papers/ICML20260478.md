## Problem

Existing unsupervised RLIF methods for LLM fine-tuning rely on handcrafted intrinsic rewards (self-certainty, entropy, majority voting) that lack a principled distributional target, making them prone to degenerative pathologies such as length collapse, overconfidence, and mode collapse. Moreover, prior analyses attributing RL gains to 'distribution sharpening' remain observational and overlook the structural length bias inherent in autoregressive generation, which causes naive distribution-matching objectives to be dominated by sequence length rather than semantic density.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text queries formatted in official chat templates—math problems appended with a 'think step by step' instruction requiring a \boxed{} final answer, or creative writing prompts (poem continuation, story generation, joke writing) with a ~200-word length constraint. It produces either a full reasoning trajectory ending in a boxed answer or a creative text passage. Crucially, no external reward signal, ground-truth label, or verifier is required at any point; the sole information source is the base model's own token-level probability distribution, making the entire pipeline self-supervised.

## Method

PowerFlow reformulates unsupervised fine-tuning as matching the fine-tuned policy to the α-power (escort) distribution p_base(y|q)^α of the base model, where α is a single control knob: α>1 sharpens the distribution to concentrate mass on latent reasoning paths, while α<1 flattens it to release creative diversity suppressed by alignment. To optimize this intractable target, the paper casts GFlowNet as an amortized variational sampler and derives a length-aware Trajectory Balance (LA-TB) objective that reparameterizes the partition function as (Z′(q))^|y|, projecting optimization onto a length-normalized energy surface and decoupling gradients from sequence length. Theoretical analysis proves LA-TB is the exact I-projection (minimum-distortion length correction) of the α-power target onto the length-calibrated family, with global KL distortion bounded to second order in the learned multiplier λ_q. Training uses an importance-sampled clipped objective for off-policy stability, a lightweight 3-layer MLP to amortize log Z′(q), and a format penalty ψ(y) to enforce answer extractability, requiring only ~10% wall-clock overhead over standard RL training.

## Results

On reasoning benchmarks (avg@16), PowerFlow achieves 19.85 on Qwen2.5-1.5B, 34.30 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, 22.88 on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and 42.17 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, outperforming supervised GRPO on three of four configurations (e.g., 34.30 vs. 32.75 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B) and matching it on the fourth (42.17 vs. 42.38 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B). For creative writing, PowerFlow (α=0.5) achieves a Pareto-dominant shift over Instruct models across all four model families, simultaneously improving both semantic diversity and LLM-judged quality (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B: quality 0.6537 vs. 0.6149, diversity 0.2170 vs. 0.1898). PowerFlow also preserves solution-path diversity, scoring 4.05/5 on AIME24/25 versus 3.80 for EMPO and 3.93 for GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PowerFlow consistently outperforms all unsupervised RLIF baselines: it beats Intuitor (Zhao et al.) by +0.90 avg on Qwen2.5-1.5B and +0.51 on Llama-3.2-3B, surpasses EMPO (Zhang et al.) by +1.85 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and +1.29 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and exceeds TTRL (Zuo et al.) by +0.99 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B. Against the inference-time baselines, PowerFlow (α=4) outperforms both Low-temp (Karan & Du) by +9.05 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and PowerSampling by +2.37 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, while One-shot EM (Gao et al.) trails by +9.30 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B. Against the supervised GRPO baseline, PowerFlow wins on three of four model configurations (Qwen2.5-1.5B: 19.85 vs. 18.13; Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: 34.30 vs. 32.75; Llama-3.2-3B: 22.88 vs. 22.33) and is within 0.21 points on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (42.17 vs. 42.38). For creative writing, PowerFlow (α=0.5) Pareto-dominates VS-Standard (Zhang et al.) on all four model families, achieving higher quality (e.g., 0.6537 vs. 0.5144 on Qwen2.5-7B) while also improving semantic diversity (0.2170 vs. 0.2700 on Qwen2.5-7B, though VS-Standard edges out on diversity for that specific model; PowerFlow wins on quality across the board).

## Relation to Prior Work

PowerFlow sits at the intersection of GFlowNet-based distribution matching (Malkin et al., 2022; Zimmermann et al., 2023) and the RLIF paradigm for unsupervised LLM fine-tuning (Zhao et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025c; Zuo et al., 2025). It directly extends Karan & Du's (2025) observation that α-power distribution sharpening improves reasoning by amortizing the MCMC sampling cost into training via a GFlowNet policy, and adopts the partition-function estimation architecture and Clip-Higher mechanism from FlowRL (Zhu et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure from prior RLIF work is replacing heuristic intrinsic rewards (self-certainty, semantic entropy, majority voting) with an explicit distribution-matching objective, and the critical technical departure from standard GFlowNet TB is the length-aware reparameterization that neutralizes the exponential length bias of autoregressive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PowerFlow provides a theoretically grounded alternative to the ad-hoc reward engineering that dominates current RLIF methods, with the α parameter offering a single interpretable knob for directional capability elicitation (sharpening for reasoning, flattening for creativity). The I-projection characterization and second-order KL distortion bound (Propositions 3.2–3.3) give practitioners quantitative guarantees about how much the length correction distorts the ideal target, which is absent in prior heuristic approaches. Open questions include whether automated α-scheduling can replace the current manual tuning, whether the framework generalizes beyond α-power to other distribution families, and the safety implications of deliberately flattening aligned distributions (potentially resurfacing suppressed harmful content). The framework also does not introduce new knowledge—it only reshapes the base model's existing probability landscape—so its ceiling is bounded by the base model's latent capability.
