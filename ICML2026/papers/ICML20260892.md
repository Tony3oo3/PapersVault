## Problem

Randomized smoothing provides scalable ℓ2 certified robustness but suffers from a persistent gap: certified guarantees do not translate into strong empirical robustness against modern ℓ∞ attacks (e.g., AutoAttack, PGD), while empirical randomized defenses (CTRW, RPF, PNI) improve ℓ∞ robustness without formal certificates. No prior method simultaneously tightens certified ℓ2 radii and resists strong ℓ∞ attacks across both natural-image and medical-imaging benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB or medical images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, variable for medical datasets) augmented at inference with isotropic Gaussian smoothing noise ε~N(0,σ²I). Because HySCAN is randomized at test time—both RWAN weight noise and SANI feature noise are independently resampled on every forward pass—the smoothed classifier F_Θ(x) = argmax_c P_{ε,ω,ψ}[f_Θ(x+ε;ω,ψ)=c] is deterministic at the population level, yielding a class label and a certified ℓ2 radius r₂(x) = (σ/2)(Φ⁻¹(p_A)−Φ⁻¹(p_B)) that is valid for any internal HySCAN randomness.

## Method

HySCAN replaces each convolutional block in a CNN backbone (e.g., ResNet) with a hybrid stochastic block containing two coupled modules. RWAN (Random Weights with Attention Noise) injects Gaussian noise into convolutional kernels, gated per input-channel by a SANI-derived attention response, producing input-conditioned heteroscedastic weight-space stochasticity whose magnitude is bounded by a learnable SoftPlus-scaled parameter ρ. SANI (Stochastic Attention Noise Injection) adds an attention-modulated perturbation to intermediate features at the end of each block, using a dual-branch design: RecRAN applies progressive recursive stochastic smoothing while NoRAN contributes a single-pass channel-aware heteroscedastic injection, fused via a normalized nonnegative mixture. Crucially, the same SANI attention-noise learner serves two roles—gating RWAN's per-layer weight perturbations and driving per-block feature injection—so weight-space and feature-space stochasticity are explicitly shared and coordinated rather than independently added. Training minimizes a hybrid objective combining the RS-compatible expected loss under joint randomness (ε,ω,ψ) with an adversarial ℓ∞ term (κ·max_δ E[ℓ]), approximated via multi-step PGD with fresh randomness per attack step, preserving the standard RS ℓ2 certificate while strengthening empirical ℓ∞ robustness.

## Results

On ImageNet (σ=0.25), HySCAN achieves 56.7% certified accuracy at r=0.50 and 42.3% at r=1.00, outperforming the strongest prior certified baseline ARS (52.7% and 39.1%) by up to ~4 percentage points. On CIFAR-10 (σ=0.50), HySCAN reaches 26.2% certified accuracy at r=2.0 versus 22.4% for ARS and 21.3% for DRS. For empirical ℓ∞ robustness, HySCAN attains 90.7% robust accuracy on NCT-CRC-HE-100K (APGD-20, ε=8/255) and 73.9% on EyePACS, improving over the strongest empirical baseline CTRW by ~2–5 percentage points while maintaining clean accuracy within 0.5% of the best.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HySCAN outperforms all certified baselines (RS, ARS, DRS, IRS) across every nonzero radius on both ImageNet and CIFAR-10, with the largest gap over ARS reaching ~9.6 percentage points (e.g., CIFAR-10 σ=0.50, r=2.0: 13.9% vs 8.47%). Against the closest prior hybrid defense HyCAS, HySCAN gains up to ~1.9 points on certified accuracy (e.g., ImageNet σ=0.25, r=0.50: 56.7% vs 55.6%) and consistently leads at larger radii. For empirical ℓ∞ robustness, HySCAN beats CTRW (the strongest empirical baseline) by ~2–5 points across NCT-CRC-HE-100K, NIH-CXR, EyePACS, and HAM10000 under both APGD-20 and AA-20 at ε∈{8,16}/255, and surpasses DCS by up to ~7.8 points (EyePACS, AA-20, ε=8/255: 73.1% vs 65.3%). HySCAN also outperforms diffusion-based defenses (FreqPure, AGDM) under PGD, AA, EOT-PGD, and adaptive EOT-BPDA attacks on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Clean accuracy is preserved within ~0.5% of the best baseline across all eight benchmarks, confirming that the hybrid stochasticity does not sacrifice standard performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

HySCAN extends the randomized smoothing (RS) certification framework of Cohen et al. (2019) by incorporating internal model randomness into the certified predictor, building directly on the authors' prior HyCAS architecture (Dhar et al., 2026a) which used separately controlled Lipschitz-bounded filter- and feature-space stochastic streams. The key departure from HyCAS and from prior RS variants (ARS, DRS, IRS) is a shared attention-noise learner that simultaneously gates weight-space perturbations (RWAN) and drives feature-space injection (SANI) within a single convolutional block, coupling the two stochastic spaces rather than treating them independently. On the empirical side, HySCAN subsumes the design philosophy of weight-sampling defenses (CTRW) and feature-perturbation defenses (PNI, Learn2Perturb) but adds attention conditioning and formal ℓ2 certification that those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important gap: certified ℓ2 guarantees from randomized smoothing do not translate well into empirical ℓ∞ robustness against modern attack suites (AutoAttack, EOT-BPDA), and this mismatch is arguably worse in medical imaging where sample sizes are smaller and feature statistics differ. By evaluating across eight benchmarks including four medical datasets (NIH-CXR, HAM10000, EyePACS, NCT-CRC-HE-100K), the paper provides evidence that the hybrid dual-space stochasticity generalizes beyond natural images, a setting where most certified defenses have not been tested. Open questions include deriving tighter ℓ∞ certificates (the current guarantee is ℓ2-only), reducing the computational overhead of per-forward-pass resampling during certification, and whether the attention-gated coupling remains beneficial in transformer-based backbones rather than ResNets.
