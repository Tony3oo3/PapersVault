## Problem

Sampling from the power distribution p^α of a pretrained LLM can match or surpass RL post-training performance without external rewards, but existing methods rely on iterative MCMC (Metropolis-Hastings) that incurs ~8.8× inference overhead and suffers from exponentially slow mixing in high-dimensional token spaces. The paper asks whether the global power distribution can be approximated by a tractable autoregressive procedure that eliminates MCMC while preserving its reasoning benefits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261883/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen pretrained LLM (no parameter updates, no reward model or verifier) and produces a single generated reasoning trajectory sampled from the sharpened power distribution. At each autoregressive step, the algorithm restricts attention to a Top-K_t candidate set from the base model's logits and performs M_t short lookahead rollouts per candidate (truncated to H_t tokens), so the total inference cost is bounded by O(Σ K_t·M_t) generated sequences rather than N full trajectories as in Best-of-N.

## Method

The central result (Theorem 3.1) proves a token-level decomposition: the global power distribution p_α^(pow) factors into the low-temperature distribution p_α^(low.temp) multiplied by a token-specific scaling factor ζ_t(x_t') that encodes the expected powered likelihood of all future completions. This scaling factor is estimated via M_t Monte Carlo rollouts from the base model conditioned on each candidate token, and a jackknife bias correction (leave-one-out recombination) reduces the estimator bias from O(1/M_t) to O(1/M_t²), cutting the required sample budget from O(1/ε) to O(1/√ε). The resulting algorithm (Algorithm 1) operates purely autoregressively: at each step it selects Top-K_t candidates, computes jackknife-corrected power probabilities via short lookahead rollouts, and samples the next token—eliminating all iterative MCMC loops and achieving >10× lower inference latency while matching or exceeding MCMC power sampling and one-shot GRPO on MATH500, HumanEval, and GPQA.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B, the proposed method achieves 70.8% pass@1 on MATH500, 75.6% on HumanEval, and 34.9% on GPQA, matching or exceeding MCMC power sampling (70.6%, 62.2%, 31.8%) while reducing inference latency by up to 10×. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, it reaches 75.8% (MATH500), 60.4% (HumanEval), and 40.9% (GPQA), surpassing MCMC on all three benchmarks. The method is training-free and verifier-free, requiring no parameter updates or external reward signals, and matches one-shot GRPO on several in-domain and out-of-domain settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against low-temperature sampling (Wang et al., 2020), the proposed method consistently wins across all models and benchmarks, with the largest gap on Qwen2.5-7B HumanEval (75.6% vs. 52.4%). Best-of-N (Stiennon et al., 2020) is also uniformly outperformed, e.g., 75.6% vs. 60.9% on Qwen2.5-7B HumanEval. Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022) is the strongest reward-free baseline and is competitive on MATH500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B (78.9% vs. 75.8%), but the proposed method wins on HumanEval (60.4% vs. 59.1%) and GPQA (40.9% vs. 38.4%) for that model, and dominates on Qwen2.5-7B across all three tasks. Compared to MCMC power sampling (Karan & Du, 2025), the method matches or exceeds performance in every setting (e.g., 75.8% vs. 74.8% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B MATH500; 75.6% vs. 62.2% on Qwen2.5-7B HumanEval) while being up to 10× faster at inference. Against GRPO post-training (Shao et al., 2024/2025), the method is competitive or superior on out-of-domain tasks (e.g., 75.6% vs. 56.1% on Qwen2.5-7B HumanEval; 40.9% vs. 39.9% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B GPQA) but trails on in-domain MATH500 for the strongest GRPO checkpoints (75.8% vs. 78.5% for Qwen2.5-Math-7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Karan & Du (2025), who showed that sampling from the global power distribution p^α(x) can match or exceed RL post-training performance without external rewards, but whose implementation required computationally expensive MCMC (Metropolis-Hastings) sampling. The key departure is Theorem 3.1, which decomposes the global power distribution into a token-level scaled low-temperature distribution, bridging the gap between trajectory-level reweighting and standard autoregressive generation. The jackknife bias-correction technique is borrowed from classical statistics and adapted to the ratio-of-Monte-Carlo-estimates structure inherent in the power distribution. The paper also positions itself within the broader 'distribution sharpening' narrative (He et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2025) that RL post-training primarily reshapes probability mass rather than introducing new capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating iterative MCMC while preserving the reasoning gains of power-distribution sampling, this work makes training-free, verifier-free inference-time sharpening practically deployable (10× lower latency), lowering the barrier for organizations without large-scale RL training infrastructure. The diversity-preservation results (pass@K tracking base model at K≥8 while GRPO collapses) suggest that inference-time sharpening may be a more sustainable path to reasoning gains than parameter-level RL, which risks mode collapse. Open questions include variance reduction via control variates, adaptive compute allocation across tokens, and whether the approach extends to agentic multi-step settings where the 'future' is not a fixed-length completion. A noted safety concern is that sharpening amplifies all latent model behaviors, including potentially harmful ones, without an external reward filter.
