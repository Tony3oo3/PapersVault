## Problem

Continual VQA is typically handled by training one expert per task and hard-routing each query via task-ID supervision, but this assumes tasks are well-separated. In practice, continual VQA tasks overlap heavily in both input and answer space: on VQA-v2, a non-native expert outperforms the oracle task expert on 49.9% of queries, so hard routing wastes transferable knowledge and can produce confidently wrong predictions when the selected expert is miscalibrated on the query distribution. The paper addresses both the routing objective (task identification vs. capability selection) and the inference-time aggregation (single-expert commitment vs. calibrated multi-expert evidence pooling).

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language question as input, processed by a frozen ViLT or FLAVA vision-language backbone. At test time the task identity is unknown (task-agnostic setting), and the router operates on text-only question embeddings (BERT CLS). The output is a predicted answer selected from a predefined answer vocabulary, accompanied by a full confidence distribution over the unified answer space (the union of all task-local answer sets). Each expert's local distribution is mapped into this shared space via a sparse alignment matrix before aggregation, so overlapping answers across tasks receive pooled probability mass.

## Method

The framework has three components. (1) Per-task experts are lightweight LoRA adapters (rank 8, injected into FFN modules) plus a task-specific answer head, trained on each incoming task and then frozen to prevent forgetting. (2) A utility-driven router is trained to maximize the expected VQA soft-score of the induced expert mixture rather than to predict task ID; because this linear-over-simplex objective naturally collapses to one-hot routing, an entropy-regularization term (λ=0.1) is added to maintain non-degenerate mixture weights. (3) At inference, the top-k=3 experts under the router are combined via Bayesian model averaging in the unified answer space: p(a|x) = Σ_t w(t|x)·p_t(a|x), which pools evidence when experts agree and spreads mass when they disagree, reducing overconfident errors. The router is retrained at each stage on current-task data plus a 5000-sample replay buffer to retain sensitivity to earlier experts.

## Results

On VQA-v2 CL-LS (ViLT backbone), the method achieves 64.16 Micro / 55.82 Macro accuracy with only 0.63 average forgetting, outperforming the strongest replay baseline (ER: 58.42 Micro, 3.62 forgetting) by +5.74 accuracy and −2.99 forgetting. On TDIUC CL-LS it reaches 78.81 Micro / 68.48 Macro with 0.40 forgetting (+3.10 accuracy, −1.74 forgetting vs. GEM), and on TDIUC CL-VS it attains 83.41 Micro / 83.74 Macro with 3.21 forgetting (+1.58 accuracy, −0.82 forgetting vs. GEM). Calibration improves substantially: ECE drops from 0.15 (task-ID router baseline) to 0.07 on VQA-v2, and the method matches or exceeds Oracle-MoE ECE on TDIUC CL-VS and VQA-v2 while surpassing Oracle-MoE accuracy on VQA-v2 (64.16 vs. 62.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven learned baselines (Naive, EWC, InfLoRA, DualPrompt, ER, DER, GEM, VQACL) on Micro accuracy across all three benchmarks. On VQA-v2 CL-LS it beats the strongest replay method ER (58.42) by +5.74 Micro and reduces forgetting from 3.62 to 0.63; on TDIUC CL-LS it surpasses GEM (75.71) by +3.10 with forgetting of 0.40 vs. 2.14; on TDIUC CL-VS it edges out GEM (81.83) by +1.58 with forgetting 3.21 vs. 4.03. Notably, the method exceeds the Oracle-MoE upper bound (which uses ground-truth task-ID for hard routing) on VQA-v2 (64.16 vs. 62.20 Micro) and TDIUC CL-LS (78.81 vs. 79.57, within 0.76), demonstrating that utility-driven soft routing with Bayesian aggregation can exploit cross-task competence that oracle task-ID routing misses. On TDIUC CL-VS a gap to Oracle-MoE remains (83.41 vs. 86.96), attributed to the more separable visual-domain tasks where hard routing is closer to optimal. For calibration (ECE), the method reduces ECE from 0.15 to 0.07 on VQA-v2 and from 0.15 to 0.06 on TDIUC CL-VS, matching or beating Oracle-MoE ECE on both, while DER achieves slightly lower ECE on TDIUC CL-LS (0.13 vs. 0.15) but at substantially lower accuracy (73.40 vs. 78.81).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA-based Mixture-of-Experts continual learning framework (Yu et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2022) by replacing the standard task-ID supervised router with a utility-driven objective that directly maximizes expected VQA score, and by replacing hard top-1 expert selection with Bayesian model averaging over experts in a unified answer space. The entropy-regularized routing objective is a targeted fix for the well-known simplex-vertex collapse of linear objectives over probability simplices, applied here to preserve multi-expert evidence pooling. The work departs from prior continual VQA methods (VQACL, TRIPLET) that treat routing as task identification, reframing it as capability selection under substantial cross-task overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that non-native experts outperform the oracle task expert on ~50% of VQA-v2 queries challenges the task-separability assumption underlying most modular CL designs, suggesting that routing should target competence rather than identity. The practical impact is a parameter-efficient continual VQA pipeline that simultaneously improves accuracy, reduces forgetting, and halves ECE, which matters for deployed vision-language systems where overconfident wrong answers are costly. Limitations include reliance on task boundaries during training, evaluation on relatively small encoder-only benchmarks (VQA-v2, TDIUC), and a fixed answer vocabulary that may not generalize to open-ended or generative VQA settings. Open questions include whether utility routing and calibrated aggregation transfer to decoder-based or instruction-tuned VLMs, and how the approach behaves under truly task-free (no task-ID at training time) continual settings.
