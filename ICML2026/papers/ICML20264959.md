## Problem

Existing text-to-image models (diffusion, GANs) cannot generate geometric diagrams with the spatial precision required for mathematical validity, as their stochastic pixel-level generation is fundamentally mismatched with geometry's exact placement of points, lines, and angles. This gap is compounded by the absence of high-quality paired datasets that align natural language geometric descriptions with precise diagrammatic specifications, and by the lack of evaluation metrics that capture structural geometric correctness rather than perceptual similarity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language geometric description (e.g., 'In triangle ABC, AB=AC, make AD perpendicular to BC at point D, ∠BAC=70°') and produces a rendered 2D geometric diagram via Matplotlib with exact point coordinates. The intermediate representation is a structured GeoLingua formal expression organized into four typed lists (shapes, dependence, length constraints, angle constraints) in JSON format, which the coordinate solver then resolves into a concrete coordinate assignment before rendering. The output is a vector-precise raster image where all geometric constraints (parallelism, perpendicularity, length ratios, angle values) are satisfied within a configurable tolerance.

## Method

GeoLoom is a two-stage framework that decouples logical reasoning from spatial realization. Stage 1 (autoformalization) translates natural language into GeoLingua, a generation-oriented formal language that explicitly encodes hierarchical construction dependencies (free vs. dependent points) and typed geometric constraints; this is achieved either via prompt-engineered LLM inference (DeepSeek-V3) or supervised fine-tuning (Qwen2.5, LLaMA) on the 4,730-pair GeoNF dataset. Stage 2 (coordinate solver) formulates the diagram as S = (P_f, P_d, C) and applies a Monte Carlo optimization that iteratively perturbs free-point coordinates, propagates updates to dependent points through constraint functions, and minimizes a max-deviation loss over five constraint families (length ratio, length relation, angle value, angle ratio, angle relation). The solver uses a nested-loop structure (inner loop Q=1000 local perturbations, outer loop T=1000 global restarts) with a convergence threshold α=0.05, balancing local refinement against global exploration to escape local minima in the nonconvex constraint landscape.

## Results

On the GeoNF test set (430 pairs), the best fine-tuned model (Qwen2.5-7B) achieves 85.34% manual accuracy, LCI of 0.747, and ADI of 1.818 (average 1.283), outperforming all other configurations. The training-free DeepSeek-V3 baseline reaches 81.16% manual accuracy with LCI 0.926 and ADI 1.747 (average 1.337). In the user study on 100 diagrams, the fine-tuned GeoLoom is preferred by 55.1% of participants for quality and 53.9% for alignment, while AutomaTikZ and Seedream 3.0 receive 0% and 0.2% respectively. Nearly 70% of diagrams are generated within 10 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLoom decisively outperforms both pixel-based and code-based baselines. In the user study, AutomaTikZ (Belouadi et al., 2024) and Seedream 3.0 (Gao et al., 2025) receive 0% preference for quality and near-0% for alignment, while GeoLoom (fine-tuned) earns 55.1% quality and 53.9% alignment votes. Qualitatively (Figure 4), AutomaTikZ and Seedream produce diagram-like visuals that frequently violate geometric constraints (e.g., incorrect angles, wrong parallelism), whereas GeoLoom's constraint-aware solver ensures mathematical validity. For complex IMO-style problems (Figure 5), GeoLoom automates generation in ~60 seconds per diagram, compared to Penrose (Katherine et al., 2020) which requires users to manually author symbolic scripts, and GeoGebra (Hohenwarter & Preiner, 2007) which demands ~300 seconds of expert manual construction. The paper also demonstrates that CLIP scores are misleading for geometric diagrams: baselines with visibly incorrect geometry achieve higher CLIP scores than GeoLoom's correct outputs, motivating the use of the proposed LCI and ADI metrics instead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of formal geometry solvers (Penrose, Inter-GPS) and LLM-based autoformalization, but departs from both: unlike problem-solving formal languages, GeoLingua explicitly encodes constructive dependencies and sequential construction orders needed for diagram synthesis. It also diverges from code-guided diagram generators (AutomaTikZ, DiagramAgent) by replacing free-form code generation with a constrained formal language plus a Monte Carlo coordinate solver, and from pixel-based text-to-image models (Seedream, Stable Diffusion) by operating entirely in a symbolic-geometric space. The vector graphics generation line (DeepSVG, SVGFusion) is acknowledged but positioned as lacking explicit geometric constraint modeling, which GeoLoom addresses directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, mathematically grounded pipeline for geometric diagram generation that pixel-based methods fundamentally cannot replicate, and the GeoNF dataset (4,730 aligned NL–formal pairs) plus the LCI/ADI constraint-based metrics offer reusable evaluation infrastructure for the community. Key limitations include strict 2D Euclidean scope, sensitivity to ambiguous natural-language input, and Monte Carlo local-minima failures (~8% of test set). Open questions include extending the solver to 3D or non-Euclidean geometry, improving robustness to diverse phrasing, and whether the formal-language bottleneck can be further reduced as LLMs improve at structured output.
