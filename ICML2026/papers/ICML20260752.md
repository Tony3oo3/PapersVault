## Problem

Conventional autoregressive image generation uses a two-stage pipeline where the tokenizer is trained only for reconstruction and then frozen during AR model training, preventing the tokenizer from learning representations optimized for sequential generation. Additionally, 2D grid-structured tokenizers impose bidirectional spatial dependencies that are fundamentally misaligned with unidirectional raster-order autoregressive factorization, and the next-token-prediction loss on discrete tokens does not reflect final pixel-space generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260752/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ImageNet-1K class label as input and generates a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image. Internally, the 1D ViT tokenizer compresses each image into a fixed-length sequence of 256 discrete tokens (latent dimension 64, codebook size 4096 via IBQ), which the AR model predicts sequentially; the decoder then reconstructs the image from these tokens concatenated with 256 mask tokens in a 2D grid. Sampling uses temperature 1.0 with KV cache, and AutoGuidance replaces classifier-free guidance for the larger models.

## Method

EOSTok jointly trains a 1D ViT tokenizer (with learnable query tokens that compress 2D patches into a sequential latent space without spatial prior) and a LlamaGen-based AR model in a single end-to-end stage. The key training innovation is the Autoregressive Prediction Reconstruction (APR) loss, which decodes the AR model's teacher-forcing token predictions back to pixel space via the decoder and computes an L2+LPIPS reconstruction loss, providing direct generative feedback to the tokenizer and preventing the latent-space collapse that pure NTP loss induces. For semantic enrichment, the paper proposes implicit alignment: rather than aligning the 1D latent tokens to DINOv2 features (which would leak 2D spatial structure), it aligns the encoder's hidden patch embeddings to VFM representations, distilling global semantics into the sequential space without enforcing spatial order. A decoder alignment loss similarly aligns intermediate decoder hidden states to VFM features, accelerating convergence of the 1D reconstruction task.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256 generation, EOSTok-H (644M AR model + 388M tokenizer) achieves a state-of-the-art gFID of 1.48 without classifier guidance, outperforming all prior AR, masked, and diffusion-based methods. EOSTok-L (312M AR model) reaches gFID 1.74 without guidance using only 312M generator parameters, surpassing even guided baselines like AliTok-L (1.98 w/o guidance) and Lightning-DiT-XL (2.17 w/o guidance). The tokenizer also achieves strong reconstruction quality with rFID 0.71–0.73 across model sizes, and EOSTok-L extends to ImageNet 512 with gFID 1.98 without guidance, beating TiTok-L-64 (3.99) and DiT-XL/2 (12.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EOSTok-H achieves the best gFID without guidance (1.48) among all compared methods, beating the strongest 2D continuous-latent diffusion baseline Lightning-DiT-XL (2.17) by 0.69 FID, and the best 2D discrete AR baseline AliTok-L (1.98) by 0.50 FID. Among 1D tokenizer methods, EOSTok-H outperforms VFMTok (2.11), TiTok-L-32 (3.15), and GigaTok (3.26 w/ guidance only) substantially. EOSTok-L (1.74 w/o guidance) already surpasses AliTok-L (1.98) and Lightning-DiT-XL (2.17) without any guidance, and matches Lightning-DiT-XL's guided score (1.35) while using a 1D tokenizer. With guidance, EOSTok-L (1.35) ties Lightning-DiT-XL (1.35) and slightly beats REPA-XL/2 (1.42), while EOSTok-H (1.38) ties AliTok-L (1.38). On ImageNet 512, EOSTok-L (1.98 w/o guidance) outperforms TiTok-L-64 (3.99) and DiT-XL/2 (12.03) by large margins, confirming the scalability of the 1D end-to-end approach to higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 1D ViT tokenizer architecture from TiTok (Yu et al., 2025b), which uses learnable query tokens to compress images into 1D sequences without 2D spatial priors, and on LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024) for the autoregressive transformer backbone. The key departure is the training paradigm: rather than the conventional two-stage pipeline (train tokenizer for reconstruction, then freeze it and train the AR model), EOSTok jointly optimizes both from scratch, introducing an APR loss that decodes AR predictions back to pixel space to provide end-to-end generative supervision. The semantic alignment strategy draws on REPA and VA-VAE for diffusion models but adapts them to 1D tokenizers via 'implicit alignment' of hidden patch embeddings rather than direct latent-to-VFM matching, which the authors show would force a degenerate 2D spatial structure onto the 1D sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that next-token-prediction loss alone causes latent space collapse (codebook usage dropping from 99.8% to 51.8%) while simultaneously *worsening* generation quality is a practical warning for anyone training AR image models, and the APR loss provides a simple, effective remedy. The end-to-end training paradigm is likely to be widely adopted since it requires no architectural changes—only a modified embedding layer for gradient flow—and the implicit alignment strategy offers a general recipe for injecting VFM semantics into 1D tokenizers without spatial constraints. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image generation, whether the 1D vs. 2D tokenizer distinction remains important at much larger scales, and whether the reconstruction-generation tradeoff observed in sequence length and codebook size can be further mitigated.
