## Problem

VLA models face two critical bottlenecks: (1) the entanglement between high-level chain-of-thought reasoning and low-level control representations, which degrades action precision and causes catastrophic forgetting of the underlying VLM's vision-language capabilities; and (2) the instability of reinforcement learning at VLA scale, where high-dimensional continuous action spaces and multi-billion parameter counts make policy updates prone to drift, gradient explosion, and capability collapse. These issues prevent VLA models from simultaneously achieving dexterous manipulation, robust out-of-distribution generalization, and preservation of general visual-language understanding.

## Input / Output

The model consumes tri-view RGB images (center camera plus left and right wrist cameras), a natural-language task instruction, and the robot arm's proprioceptive joint positions. The VLM first autoregressively generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, after which 20 learnable Feature Query Tokens (dimensionality 2048) are appended to produce Feature Query States. The Action Expert then outputs a continuous action chunk of length 50 (joint-position commands), which is executed sequentially on the Cobot Magic robotic arm. During inference, the VLM and Action Expert can operate asynchronously to reduce latency.

## Method

SyVLA adopts a dual-system architecture connecting a Qwen2.5-VL-3B VLM to a 0.69B Flow Matching transformer Action Expert via 20 learnable Feature Query Tokens (rather than Pi0's KV cache), with the tokens' last hidden states passed through an MLP adapter as cross-attention conditioning. The key algorithmic contribution is an annotation-free Intention Decoupling step: during training, the action loss gradient is computed with respect to each Feature Query State, and tokens whose gradient L2-norm falls below the 5th percentile are masked to zero before a second forward pass, thereby suppressing reasoning information that leaks into the control representation (formally justified via a single-layer attention gradient decomposition showing small-gradient tokens carry redundant or attention-irrelevant information). For stabilization, a Similar-Sample Guided RL pipeline retrieves semantically similar expert trajectories from the IL dataset using CLIP-encoded multi-view cosine similarity, merges them with rollout data into a single PPO batch, and assigns a fixed advantage of 1.0 to expert samples (instead of standard GAE, which causes gradient explosion within 1k steps), effectively anchoring policy updates to expert behavior while allowing the rollout data to refine fine-grained action boundaries.

## Results

On three real-world robotic tasks (Folding Shirt, Calculating & Wrapping, Bagging Snacks), SyVLA achieves an average in-domain success rate of 0.73 and an OoD success rate of 0.64, outperforming all baselines. On multi-modal benchmarks, SyVLA scores 1795 on MME and 42.53 on HallBench (both best among VLA models), while remaining competitive on DocVQA (80.01) and AI2D (67.70). On the LIBERO simulation benchmark, SyVLA achieves 87.7/84.0/87.3/65.7 success rates on Spatial/Object/Goal/Long suites respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyVLA outperforms Pi0(pretrained) on Tasks 2 and 3 in both in-domain and OoD settings (e.g., 0.68 vs 0.39 on Task 2 in-domain; 0.57 vs 0.29 on Task 2 OoD), while trailing slightly on Task 1 in-domain (0.86 vs 0.93). Against Pi0 trained from scratch on the same data, SyVLA wins on every task in both settings, confirming the gain comes from architecture and training strategy rather than data scale. ChatVLA, despite strong multi-modal scores, collapses on real-world dexterous tasks (0.21 avg in-domain), while SyVLA achieves 0.73. On multi-modal benchmarks, SyVLA leads on MME (1795 vs 1435 for ChatVLA) and HallBench (42.53 vs 39.90), but trails ChatVLA on DocVQA (80.01 vs 83.30) and MMMU (35.78 vs 37.40). In RL comparisons on Fold Shirt, SyVLA (0.86) substantially outperforms both Pi-RL (0.714) and SimpleVLA-RL (0.357), with the latter suffering gradient explosion.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyVLA sits at the intersection of two active VLA research threads: (1) dual-system architectures pairing a VLM with a flow-matching Action Expert (Pi0, GR00T, DvLA), and (2) 'Think-Before-Act' training that mixes multi-modal data with robotic data to preserve VLM reasoning (ChatVLA, Wall-Oss, Pi0.5). It departs from Pi0's KV-Cache interface in favor of lightweight Feature Query Tokens, and from ChatVLA's MoE routing and Wall-Oss's Fast Tokens by proposing a gradient-based Intention Decoupling mechanism that masks control-irrelevant hidden states. For RL, it diverges from world-model-based simulators (WMPO, World-Env) and RECAP-style diffusion guidance by retrieving semantically similar IL samples as update anchors, a simpler but more transferable stabilization strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles two bottlenecks that block practical VLA deployment—catastrophic forgetting of VLM capabilities and RL instability at billion-parameter scale—with concrete, ablated solutions. The Intention Decoupling algorithm provides a theoretically motivated (albeit single-layer) explanation for why CoT reasoning leaks into control representations, and the Similar-Sample Guided RL pipeline offers a practical recipe for stable real-robot PPO that avoids the safety risks of world-model rollouts. Key open questions include whether the gradient-masking threshold and the constant-advantage trick generalize beyond the three tested tasks and a single 3B VLM, whether the simplified single-layer attention analysis extends to the full 15-layer Action Expert, and whether the approach scales to deformable-object manipulation beyond shirt folding. The authors acknowledge the lack of rigorous theoretical guarantees as a limitation.
