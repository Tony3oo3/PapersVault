## Problem

Calibration—the alignment of predicted probabilities with true outcome frequencies—has been extensively formalized for classification and regression but remains unexplored for probabilistic label ranking, where models predict distributions over orderings of items. Naively treating each ranking as a class ignores the metric structure of the ranking space, is computationally infeasible due to the factorial number of rankings, and fails to capture practically important modalities such as pairwise and top-k predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264434/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a context feature vector x and a set of m items, and the model outputs a full probability distribution over all m! rankings. Calibration is then assessed by projecting this distribution onto sub-rankings (all rankings containing a given partial ordering over k items) or top-k rankings (only the first k positions), computing expected calibration error (ECE) at each granularity. For the RLHF application, reward models predict utilities for four candidate responses, yielding top-1 probabilities via the Plackett-Luce model, which are then evaluated for calibration against RewardBench2 ground-truth rankings.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchy of six calibration notions for ProLR: full-rank calibration (conditioning on the entire predicted distribution), rankwise calibration (conditioning on a single ranking's probability), sub-k calibration (conditioning on the full marginal distribution over k-item subsets), top-k calibration (conditioning on the top-k marginal), and their respective rankwise weakenings. The key theoretical contribution is establishing the implication structure: full-rank calibration implies all weaker notions, but sub-k and top-k calibration are mutually incomparable, and neither implies full-rank calibration—proven via explicit counterexamples and general constructions valid for Plackett-Luce and Mallows models. Empirically, the paper evaluates PL, Mallows, RPC, and a neural RankClassifier on real-world label ranking datasets (movies, political), finding that popular models are often poorly calibrated with substantial gaps between sub-ranking and top-k metrics, and examines RLHF reward models on RewardBench2, showing that top-1 ECE correlates strongly but imperfectly with benchmark accuracy.

## Results

The paper's empirical findings are primarily diagnostic rather than proposing a new method that outperforms baselines. On the movies and political datasets (Figure 2), RPC achieves the lowest ECE for pairwise (sub-2) calibration, while Plackett-Luce and Mallows models show substantially higher ECE across all granularities (sub-2, sub-3, top-2, top-3). The PL-RPC variant, which recovers Plackett-Luce parameters from RPC pairwise probabilities, exhibits poor calibration despite RPC itself being well calibrated at k=2, illustrating the rank-breaking effect. On RewardBench2 (Figure 3), the ten most calibrated reward models also tend to achieve high leaderboard scores, but the correlation between top-1 ECE and benchmark accuracy is imperfect, with Focus and Safety categories showing the strongest correlation and Math and Precise If the weakest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five label ranking models (PL, MM, RPC, RankClassifier, PL-RPC) on calibration rather than proposing a new model to beat them. RPC achieves the strongest pairwise (sub-2) calibration on both movies and political datasets, attributable to its pairwise learning paradigm that directly optimizes binary classifiers for each item pair. Plackett-Luce and Mallows models are generally poorly calibrated across all considered granularities, with ECE values substantially higher than RPC for k=2. The PL-RPC variant, which aggregates RPC pairwise probabilities into Plackett-Luce weights via the Zermelo algorithm, shows degraded calibration relative to RPC alone, demonstrating that the rank-breaking step introduces miscalibration. RankClassifier (treating each full ranking as a class) displays mixed behavior: well calibrated in some settings but showing substantial miscalibration on sub-2 rankings for both datasets. For RLHF reward models on RewardBench2, calibration (top-1 ECE) correlates with leaderboard accuracy but not perfectly, suggesting calibration captures a quality dimension beyond top-1 accuracy, with the strongest correlation observed for Focus and Safety categories and the weakest for Math and Precise If.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-class calibration hierarchy (strong/weak calibration from Widmann et al. 2019 and Guo et al. 2017) to the probabilistic label ranking setting, where the outcome space is the set of all permutations of items rather than a flat class set. It departs from the naive reduction of label ranking to multi-class classification by introducing structurally-aware calibration notions (sub-k and top-k) that respect the metric geometry of ranking space. The work also connects to calibration in recommender systems (Sculley 2010; da Silva & Jannach 2026), but reframes calibration as aligning a predicted distribution over rankings with the true data-generating distribution rather than aligning scalar scores with latent user preferences. In the RLHF context, it complements prior work on reward model calibration (Kaufmann et al. 2025a; Halpern et al. 2025) by providing a formal framework for measuring calibration across multiple prediction granularities rather than proposing new training methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that calibration for probabilistic label ranking was entirely unaddressed and provides the first formal hierarchy of notions with clear theoretical relationships (full-rank implies sub-k and top-k, but the latter two are incomparable). Empirically, it reveals that widely used models (Plackett-Luce, Mallows) are often poorly calibrated, and that in RLHF reward models, calibration captures a quality dimension beyond top-1 accuracy—though the correlation is imperfect, leaving open whether miscalibration actually degrades downstream fine-tuning. Key open questions include developing calibration-correction methods tailored to ranking distributions and understanding the practical impact of miscalibration on policy optimization in RLHF. The factorial growth of the ranking space also raises fundamental questions about which calibration notion is most relevant for a given downstream task.
