## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning teaches models what to output without explaining why, leading to shallow imitation and poor generalization to harder problems. Critique-based training (CFT) addresses this by teaching models to generate critiques, but introduces output-format drift and catastrophic degradation of general capabilities (e.g., −21.3% on IFEval). The paper asks whether critiques can be leveraged as a training signal for reasoning improvement without forcing the model to produce them at inference.

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives a triple (prompt, student's own initial answer, teacher-generated critique) and is trained to produce a refined answer. At inference, only the prompt is provided in a single forward pass with no special tokens or format changes, and the model directly outputs a refined reasoning answer. The unusual design constraint is that critiques are a purely training-time supervision signal: they shape learning but are entirely absent during deployment, so the model must internalize the error-correction transformation rather than rely on external feedback at test time.

## Method

CGD decouples critique consumption from critique generation through a three-stage data pipeline: (1) the student model generates an initial (potentially flawed) answer for each prompt, (2) a teacher model produces a textual critique identifying specific errors in that answer, and (3) the teacher generates a refined gold answer conditioned on the full context. The student is then fine-tuned with a standard negative log-likelihood objective to map (prompt, student answer, critique) → refined answer, so the training objective remains answer generation rather than critique generation, avoiding CFT's format drift. Because the student answer is sampled fresh per prompt, critiques address the current checkpoint's actual failure modes, creating a natural curriculum. At inference the model receives only the prompt and produces refined answers in one pass; empirically it generates 4.4× longer reasoning chains on AIME without explicit prompting, indicating internalized self-correction. Ablations confirm that critique specificity and relevance drive the gains: specific-relevant critiques outperform generic placeholders, and irrelevant critiques actively harm training, showing the model reads and internalizes critique content rather than treating it as inert context.

## Results

On LLaMA3.1-8B Instruct, CGD achieves 46.9% average on math reasoning (Group 1) and 36.7% on general reasoning (Group 2), outperforming CFT by +5.4 and +4.3 points respectively, with standout gains of +15.0% on AMC23 and +8.0% on OlympiadBench. On S1.1-3B, gains are even larger: +7.2% on math (including +12.2% on MATH500) and +3.5% on general reasoning. On AIME 2024, CGD improves greedy Pass@1 from 3.3% to 16.7% (5×) with 4.4× longer reasoning chains, and on Qwen2.5-Math-7B achieves a +22.6% gain over the base model (27.8→50.4 avg) in only ~8 A100 GPU-hours. Critically, CGD preserves instruction-following (IFEval: 76.1% vs 76.9% base) where CFT degrades by 21.3% (76.9%→55.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT, Wang et al. 2025b), CGD wins consistently across both model families: +5.4% average on math reasoning and +4.3% on general reasoning for LLaMA3.1-8B, and +7.2% and +3.5% respectively for S1.1-3B. The largest individual gaps appear on competition-level benchmarks (AMC23: +15.0%, OlympiadBench: +8.0% on LLaMA; MATH500: +12.2% on S1.1-3B). Crucially, CGD preserves general capabilities (IFEval 76.1% vs. base 76.9%) while CFT suffers catastrophic degradation (IFEval drops to 55.6%, a −21.3% loss), and CGD even improves out-of-distribution code generation (HumanEval: 64.6% vs. 59.7% base). Against SimpleRL-Zero (Zeng et al. 2025), CGD achieves competitive aggregate scores (11.9 vs. 10.6 avg on LLaMA3.1-8B; 50.4 vs. 48.9 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B) at roughly 1/24th the compute (~48 A100 GPU-hours vs. 1,152 H100 GPU-hours), though RL retains an edge on the hardest competition problems (AIME24: 33.3% vs. 20.0%), suggesting the two paradigms are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific niche within the critique-based learning and knowledge distillation literature by decoupling critique consumption from critique generation. Prior work either trains models to produce critiques (CFT, causing format drift and capability degradation) or requires critique-and-revise loops at inference time (Self-Refine, Reflexion, incurring multi-pass latency). CGD repurposes teacher critiques as a purely training-time semantic scaffold: the student is conditioned on (prompt, flawed answer, critique) → refined answer during fine-tuning, but at inference only the prompt is provided. This positions CGD as a supervised, on-policy variant of distillation that provides explicit error-localizing supervision (as opposed to the implicit token-probability or scalar-reward signals in GKD, SKD, or RL-based methods), while avoiding the format-shift pathology of critique-generation objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CGD offers a practical, compute-efficient (~48 A100 GPU-hours) alternative to both RL-based reasoning training (1152+ H100 GPU-hours) and inference-time self-refinement, with the notable advantage of preserving general instruction-following capabilities where CFT degrades catastrophically (−21.3% on IFEval). The finding that self-correction skills transfer to out-of-distribution tasks (code generation, +4.9% HumanEval) despite no code in training data suggests CGD learns generalizable reasoning patterns rather than domain-specific answer mapping. Open questions include whether combining CGD with RL yields complementary gains (CGD excels at efficient knowledge transfer; RL at exploration on competition-level problems), how the method extends to open-ended domains where correctness is subjective, and what the optimal critique-correctness mixture ratio is across different task distributions.
