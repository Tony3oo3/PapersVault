## Problem

Neural operators trained on PDE families struggle to generalize when test dynamics fall outside the training distribution, such as novel PDE coefficient values or combinations of physical phenomena seen only in isolation. Existing remedies—large-scale pretraining followed by fine-tuning, or meta-learning—still require target-domain examples and lack compositionality, falling short of true zero-shot generalization. This paper asks whether test-time computation alone, with all pretrained weights frozen, can recover accurate predictions for unseen dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes L=16 consecutive spatiotemporal snapshots of an OOD test trajectory (e.g., 256-point 1D fields or 256×256 2D vorticity fields) and a frozen dictionary of N trajectory-conditioned neural operators (N ranges from 17 to 256 depending on benchmark). It produces H future snapshots via autoregressive rollout, an interpretable operator composition (which training operators were selected), and PDE parameter estimates recovered by mapping each selected operator back to its source trajectory's known coefficients. Sensitivity analysis shows the fitting window can shrink to L=2 without accuracy loss, and the dictionary can be as small as N=16.

## Method

The approach builds on DISCO, where a transformer hypernetwork generates weights for a U-Net neural-ODE operator from trajectory context; after pretraining, each training trajectory yields one operator, forming a dictionary. At test time, a beam search (or uniform sampling baseline) greedily selects a subset of dictionary operators whose composition best fits the observed OOD trajectory, minimizing a next-step NRMSE objective over the fitting window. The sum of selected operators is realized through neural operator splitting—Lie or Strang splitting of the learned operator flows—which the paper coins as the first application of classical splitting schemes to neural PDE surrogates. Strang splitting provides second-order accuracy (O(Δt³)) and a palindromic composition pattern generalized to m operators. The entire procedure operates with fixed weights: no gradient updates, no fine-tuning, making it complementary to adaptive methods and naturally scaling with test-time compute budget.

## Results

On the operator composition benchmark (Table 1), the beam-search variant achieves the best NRMSE on 6 of 7 unseen PDE combination tasks, with particularly dramatic gains over the original DISCO encoder: 0.015 vs. 0.170 on Advection+Diffusion (11×), 0.066 vs. 0.572 on Euler+Diffusion (8.7×), and 0.036 vs. 0.164 on the three-physics AllThree task (4.6×). On parameter extrapolation (Table 2), beam search improves over DISCO by 10× on advection-speed extrapolation (0.052 vs. 0.768) and over 200× on diffusion-coefficient extrapolation (0.002 vs. 0.159). The method also enables zero-shot PDE parameter recovery, with estimation accuracy improving monotonically as the search budget increases (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DISCO encoder (no test-time adaptation), the proposed beam search wins on all 7 composition tasks and all 4 extrapolation tasks, with improvements ranging from 1.5× (Nonlinear Adv.+Diffusion: 0.056 vs. 0.085) to over 200× (diffusion extrapolation: 0.002 vs. 0.159). Against MPP, the method wins on 6 of 7 composition tasks; MPP is slightly better only on Nonlinear Adv.+Diffusion (0.050 vs. 0.056). Against FNO, the method wins on 6 of 7 composition tasks; FNO edges ahead on Nonlinear Adv.+Diffusion (0.031 vs. 0.056). Against Zebra, the method wins on 6 of 7 composition tasks; Zebra is better on Nonlinear Adv.+Diffusion (0.022 vs. 0.056) due to its autoregressive flexibility on that specific regime. Against GEPS (gradient-based fine-tuning), the method wins on all 7 composition tasks and all 4 extrapolation tasks; notably, GEPS diverges entirely on advection-speed and diffusion-coefficient extrapolation after 2000 fine-tuning steps, while beam search remains stable and achieves NRMSE of 0.052 and 0.002 respectively at comparable wall-clock cost (~26 s vs. ~57 s on a single A100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DISCO (Morel et al., 2025), which learns a dictionary of trajectory-conditioned neural operators via a hypernetwork, and on classical Strang splitting from numerical analysis, repurposing it to compose learned neural operators at test time. It departs from the dominant pretrain-then-finetune paradigm (MPP, WALRUS, POSEIDON) and meta-learning approaches (CODA, GEPS) that require gradient-based weight updates at test time, instead performing a discrete beam search over operator compositions with all weights frozen. Unlike in-context learning methods (ICON, Zebra, VICON) that implicitly reuse physical operators through conditioning, this work makes the composition explicit and interpretable, and is the first to introduce test-time compute scaling laws for neural PDE solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that zero-shot generalization to unseen PDE combinations and parameter extrapolation is achievable without any weight modification, opening a complementary axis to fine-tuning for test-time adaptation in physics simulation. The operator decomposition provides a natural system-identification capability, recovering PDE coefficients from the selected operators. Key limitations include the requirement for compatible input/output domains across composed operators, the O(B·N·L) search overhead that may become prohibitive in 3D, and the dependence on a sufficiently diverse pretrained dictionary. Open questions include scaling the search to 3D, extending to broader forms of compositionality across spatial dimensions and discretizations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the DISCO operator-dictionary paradigm.
