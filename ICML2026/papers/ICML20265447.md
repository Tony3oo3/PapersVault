## Problem

Continual learning with large pre-trained models suffers from catastrophic forgetting and parameter interference, especially when historical data is unavailable. Existing model merging approaches treat fine-tuning and merging as independent stages, overlooking the impact of loss landscape sharpness and dominant singular value directions, which leads to subspace misalignment and severe knowledge degradation as the number of tasks grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained backbone (CLIP-ViT for vision, Llama for language) and processes a sequential stream of tasks, each with its own labeled dataset. For vision tasks, RGB images are mapped to class labels across 20 diverse classification datasets; for language tasks, text prompts produce text responses evaluated on benchmarks like MergeBench and TRACEBench. Critically, no historical training data is accessible during the merging stage—all knowledge integration occurs purely in parameter space, and the model must be evaluated on all previously seen tasks using only the final merged weights.

## Method

SAIM unifies fine-tuning and merging into a synergistic framework with two modules. First, the SA-BCD optimizer selectively updates only the top 30% of parameters ranked by first-order momentum magnitude, while applying a SAM-style worst-case perturbation (scaled by ρ) to the selected subset before computing the final gradient, thereby guiding convergence toward flat minima that remain valid after future parameter shifts from merging. Second, the adaptive isotropic merging algorithm performs SVD on the combined cumulative-plus-new-task update matrix at each layer, then rebalances the singular value spectrum by interpolating each singular value toward the layer mean with a 1/√t decay factor—preserving task-specific spectral structure early in the sequence while progressively enforcing isotropy as the subspace stabilizes. This dual mechanism simultaneously reduces parameter interference (via selective, flat-minima fine-tuning) and prevents spectral dominance by any single task (via adaptive singular value equalization), eliminating the need to store historical task vectors.

## Results

On vision continual learning (FusionBench protocol), SAIM achieves 87.5% average accuracy on ViT-L/14 with 20 tasks, outperforming the strongest merging baseline OPCM (76.0%) by 11.5 points and EWC (82.9%) by 4.6 points. On TRACEBench (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), SAIM reaches 0.480 average score versus 0.406 for MagMAX-IND and 0.459 for PASs-MoE, with a backward transfer of −0.022 (vs. −0.086 for LoRAMoE). On MergeBench (Llama-3.2-3B), SAIM attains 0.454 overall score, a 4.7-point gain over Task Arithmetic (0.407). SAIM also shows the best backward transfer among all methods at 20 tasks (−4.3% on ViT-L/14 vs. −11.8% for EWC and −13.4% for ER).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIM consistently outperforms all model-merging baselines (SWA, Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, MagMAX-IND, OPCM) across every backbone and task count, with the largest gap at 20 tasks (e.g., +7.9% over OPCM on ViT-B/32, +11.5% on ViT-L/14). Against regularization/replay methods (EWC, ER), SAIM achieves higher accuracy at 14 and 20 tasks while also showing substantially better backward transfer (−4.3% vs. −11.8% for EWC on ViT-L/14 20-task), indicating that EWC/ER suffer severe forgetting as tasks accumulate. On TRACEBench, SAIM beats the strongest PEFT baseline PASs-MoE by 2.1 points (0.480 vs. 0.459) and MagMAX-IND by 7.4 points, while also achieving the best BWT (−0.022 vs. −0.063 for PASs-MoE). On MergeBench (Llama-3.2-3B), SAIM leads Task Arithmetic by 4.7 points (0.454 vs. 0.407) and DARE/MagMAX-IND by 5.6 points. In the PEFT comparison on ViT-B/32 (Table 4), SAIM surpasses InfLoRA by 9.3% and SD-LoRA by 7.2% at 20 tasks, with BWT of −11.5% versus −22.5% and −18.6% respectively, confirming that weight-space merging with SA-BCD scales better than parameter-isolation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAIM sits at the intersection of three research threads: sharpness-aware optimization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021), model merging via task vectors (Task Arithmetic, Ilharco et al. 2022), and isotropic spectral balancing (ISO-C, Marczak et al. 2025). Its key conceptual departure is treating fine-tuning and merging as a *synergistic* pipeline rather than independent stages: the SA-BCD optimizer pre-emptively defends against the parameter drift that merging will later introduce, while the adaptive SVD-based isotropic merging preserves the flat-basin structure that SA-BCD establishes. This contrasts with prior continual merging methods (MagMAX, OPCM, TIES) that optimize only the merging step, and with sharpness-aware fine-tuning work (SAFT, Lee et al. 2025) that addresses flatness in isolated multi-task settings rather than sequential continual learning without data replay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, data-free continual learning recipe that is plug-and-play: SA-BCD can replace the fine-tuning optimizer in any existing merging pipeline (demonstrated with TIES, Task Arithmetic, and SWA), and the isotropic merging step requires no stored historical task vectors. The ~35× reduction in loss-landscape drift (Table 6) and consistent 5–10% accuracy gains across three ViT scales and three LLM scales make it a credible default for parameter-space continual learning. Open questions include whether the 1/√t isotropic schedule and the 30% momentum-based selection ratio generalize to multi-modal or generative tasks, and whether the approach remains stable beyond 20 tasks where the cumulative update matrix becomes increasingly ill-conditioned.
