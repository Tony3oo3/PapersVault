## Problem

Modern classifiers trade explainability for accuracy, while existing symbolic regression methods rely on expensive heuristic search over vast expression spaces without exploiting the signomial structure that dominates standard benchmarks (45 of 100 AI Feynman equations). No prior method directly targets this prevalent power-law form as a supervised classification model with built-in interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a positive-valued feature vector (all features are affinely shifted and rescaled to [1,10] to satisfy the positivity constraint of real-valued exponents) and produces, for each class, a closed-form signomial score function whose softmax yields class probabilities. The learned equation itself is the explanation: it exposes per-feature exponents that encode direction and strength of influence, and all attributions (elasticities, counterfactuals, local contributions) are computed in closed form at zero additional cost.

## Method

ECSEL parameterizes each class score as a sum of K power-law terms z_c(x) = Σ_k α_{c,k} ∏_j x_j^{β_{c,k,j}} with learnable coefficients and exponents, trained end-to-end via cross-entropy plus L1 regularization on exponents to induce sparsity. The key insight is that signomials are universal approximators on compact subsets of the positive orthant (proven via Stone-Weierstrass in log-space), so this constrained family is theoretically sufficient while remaining human-readable. The signomial structure yields closed-form interpretability properties: constant elasticities for K=1, exact counterfactual score transformations under proportional perturbations, analytic decision-boundary sensitivities, and log-space additive decompositions for local attributions. For symbolic regression, the same architecture is trained with MSE and a multi-start staged optimizer (L-BFGS-B for single-term, Adam-then-L-BFGS for multi-term), recovering target equations at 95.86% rate in ~86s versus 59% at ~613s for the prior state-of-the-art DGSR.

## Results

On the symbolic regression benchmark (58 signomial equations across AI Feynman, Livermore, Jin, and Korns), ECSEL achieves a 95.86% exact recovery rate, substantially outperforming DGSR (59.10%), NGGP (58.54%), and NeSymRes (56%), while averaging only 86.4 s per equation versus 612.9 s, 468.7 s, and 126.3 s respectively. On 11 classification benchmarks, ECSEL attains the highest F1-score on 4 of 11 datasets (SEEDS, HEARTS, ILPD, COMPAS) and ranks within one percentage point of the best method on 9 of 11, with particularly strong minority-class recall (e.g., 42.42% vs. 6.06% for XGBoost on ILPD). On the PaySim fraud detection case study, ECSEL achieves 79.08% F1 (68.10% recall, 94.27% precision), exceeding the prior symbolic-regression-based DSC baseline (78.0% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECSEL decisively outperforms all three neural symbolic regression baselines on the signomial benchmark: it recovers 95.86% of target equations versus 59.10% (DGSR), 58.54% (NGGP), and 56% (NeSymRes), while being 7× faster than DGSR and 5× faster than NGGP. The gap is most pronounced on multi-term and rational-exponent equations where DGSR and NGGP frequently time out (marked 'dnf'), while ECSEL solves most single-term signomials in under one second. On classification, ECSEL is competitive but not uniformly dominant: it leads on 4/11 datasets by F1 (notably +11.36% over XGBoost on ILPD) and trails the best baseline by <1% on 5 more, with ensemble methods (XGBoost, RF) retaining an edge on the two most imbalanced datasets (SKINNONSKIN, MAMMOGRAPHY). Against DSC (Visbeek et al., 2024) on PaySim fraud detection, ECSEL achieves a modest F1 improvement (79.08% vs. 78.0%) while producing a more interpretable two-term signomial. Compared to post-hoc explainers (SHAP, LIME), ECSEL's attributions are derived in closed form at zero additional cost (0.1 s vs. up to 28.5 s for KernelSHAP) and its global feature rankings align closely with tree-based SHAP (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.80, p < 0.001), validating the analytical properties as faithful explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECSEL repurposes signomial functions—originally introduced in geometric programming (Duffin & Peterson, 1973) as optimization objectives—as a supervised learning model class, a framing the authors note has not been explored in prior work. It departs from general-purpose symbolic regression methods (GP, PySR, DGSR, NeSymRes) by targeting the specific signomial structure prevalent in benchmarks rather than searching over arbitrary expression spaces, and from post-hoc explanation methods (SHAP, LIME) by following Rudin's (2018) tradition of inherently interpretable models. The combination of signomials with gradient-based optimization and L1 sparsity regularization into a classifier with closed-form interpretability properties (elasticities, counterfactuals, attributions) is the paper's core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECSEL offers practitioners in high-stakes domains (fraud detection, e-commerce, medical diagnosis) a transparent alternative to black-box ensembles that achieves competitive accuracy while providing zero-cost closed-form explanations, shifting computational cost from inference-time explanation to training. Key open questions include adaptive selection of the number of terms K, formal faithfulness guarantees beyond the K=1 case, behavior under correlated features, and extension to regression. The method's advantage is structural: it excels when the underlying data-generating process has power-law character, but offers no guarantee for arbitrary functional forms, and its classification gains over strong baselines are modest (top on 4 of 11 datasets).
