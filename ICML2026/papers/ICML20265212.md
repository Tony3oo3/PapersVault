## Problem

Emotional support dialogue systems struggle to efficiently acquire and generalize emotionally relevant knowledge because user needs are weakly signaled, implicit, and only disambiguated through multi-turn interaction. Existing methods either produce generic supportive responses that mismatch the user's actual need, or passively accumulate knowledge without actively selecting responses to elicit informative diagnostic feedback, resulting in redundant signals and limited coverage of the emotional-intelligence knowledge space.

## Input / Output

At each turn the system consumes the multi-turn dialogue history, the latest user reply, and an external vector-indexed EQ knowledge memory (natural-language state-action entries embedded with EmbeddingGemma-300M). It outputs a single selected assistant response from a pool of N candidates, an updated belief distribution over M natural-language user-need hypotheses, and—during training only—a new EQ knowledge entry appended to the memory. The framework is entirely gradient-free: no model parameters are updated, and at test time the knowledge base is frozen with only belief-state updates.

## Method

UKA is a three-stage, gradient-free active dialogue pipeline. Stage 1 maintains an explicit hypothesis set of user-need descriptions and updates a belief distribution via teacher-forced log-likelihood scoring of the observed user reply under each hypothesis, with hypothesis expansion when uncertainty persists. Stage 2 constructs a belief-aware summary anchor from the dialogue history and current belief vector, then retrieves top-K EQ knowledge entries by cosine similarity. Stage 3 generates N candidate responses and scores each by (i) knowledge support (average cosine similarity to retrieved KB entries) and (ii) ToM uncertainty (average cross-hypothesis disagreement in simulated user-reaction embeddings). The key design is a train/test separation: training selects the candidate maximizing S_u − S_k to probe beyond the current knowledge boundary and write diverse new entries, while testing selects the candidate maximizing S_k + γS_u to ground responses in acquired knowledge while retaining mild diagnostic value for rapid need disambiguation.

## Results

UKA achieves the best Success Rate (SR) on both ESConv and ExTES across all four LLM backbones, e.g., 0.525 SR on ESConv and 0.746 SR on ExTES with Seed-1.6-36B, and 0.483 / 0.620 with Qwen3-235B-A22B. On Sentient Eval, UKA obtains the highest average emotion score in most settings, reaching 82.9 (Qwen3-235B-A22B) and 73.0 (Seed-1.6-36B), with the largest gains on the challenging Negative persona (69.4 vs. 57.2 for PRINCIPLES on Qwen3-235B). UKA also maintains competitive Average Turns (AT), indicating that the improvements are not achieved at the cost of longer dialogues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PRINCIPLES (Kim et al., 2025), a passive strategy-memory baseline that synthesizes reusable dialogue principles from offline self-play, UKA wins on SR for every backbone on both ESConv (e.g., 0.525 vs. 0.450 on Seed-36B; 0.483 vs. 0.452 on Qwen3-235B) and ExTES (e.g., 0.746 vs. 0.732 on Seed-36B; 0.620 vs. 0.591 on Qwen3-235B), and achieves higher Sentient Eval averages in 3 of 4 backbone settings (82.9 vs. 74.2 on Qwen3-235B; 73.0 vs. 67.9 on Seed-36B). Against MetaMind (Zhang et al., 2026), a multi-agent Theory-of-Mind deliberation framework, UKA similarly dominates on SR across all backbones and benchmarks (e.g., 0.525 vs. 0.243 on ESConv/Seed-36B; 0.620 vs. 0.555 on ExTES/Qwen3-235B) and on Sentient Eval averages (82.9 vs. 69.9 on Qwen3-235B; 73.0 vs. 70.2 on Seed-36B). The largest relative gains appear on the Negative persona of Sentient Eval, where UKA's uncertainty-driven response selection is most beneficial: 69.4 vs. 57.2 (PRINCIPLES) and 69.4 vs. 54.1 (MetaMind) on Qwen3-235B. Notably, MetaMind underperforms the simple Prompting baseline on several Sentient Eval sub-scores (e.g., 21.3 vs. 28.5 average on Qwen3-32B), suggesting that multi-agent deliberation without explicit belief tracking can be counterproductive under ambiguous user needs.

## Relation to Prior Work

UKA synthesizes three research threads—retrieval-augmented generation, active learning, and Theory-of-Mind user modeling—into a unified gradient-free framework for emotional support dialogue. It departs from passive strategy-memory methods like PRINCIPLES (which distill reusable principles from offline self-play) by actively selecting responses to elicit maximally informative user feedback, and from per-turn multi-agent ToM deliberation like MetaMind by maintaining a persistent, explicitly tracked belief state over user-need hypotheses across turns. The active-learning component is repurposed from its traditional role of selecting annotation instances to selecting the assistant's reply that maximizes diagnostic information gain about the user's latent need, a framing closer to active prompting than to classical AL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-free, memory-based design makes UKA practically deployable across heterogeneous LLM backbones without fine-tuning, and the consistent gains across four architectures and three benchmarks (including a Chinese-language one) suggest the approach generalizes beyond a single model family. However, the reliance on LLM-based user simulators and critics introduces potential distribution bias, and the small hypothesis budget (M=3) may not capture the mixed, evolving motivations of real users. Open questions include whether the acquired EQ knowledge transfers to clinical or long-horizon settings, how to govern memory growth and prune simulator-specific entries, and whether the extra inference cost of ToM rollouts (M×N×R per turn) is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployments.
