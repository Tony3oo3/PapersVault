## Problem

LLMs exhibit systematic, model-specific brittleness under meaning-preserving question rewrites (paraphrases, format shifts, benign distractors), where small surface changes reliably trigger the same error pattern. Existing augmentation pipelines either treat all perturbations uniformly, diluting signal with uninformative rewrites, or search for idiosyncratic adversarial examples that do not transfer. The paper addresses the gap of identifying *which* rewrite-induced transitions systematically break a given model and converting that localized failure knowledge into compact, transferable training data.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a base question, a small set of meaning-preserving rewrites (generated via fixed templates and filtered by a semantic-equivalence judge), and K=2 stochastic solution traces per input from the target LLM. These traces are discretized into step-level spans, annotated with operation labels (RETRIEVE/INFER/GENERATED/OTHER) and slot/variable tags, then embedded with BGE-Large-EN. The output is a small set of contrastive alternation pairs—two meaning-preserving rewrites sharing a common trace prefix but diverging at a boundary-crossing edge, one reaching a correct terminal and the other an incorrect one—applied either as DPO preference pairs for fine-tuning or as side-by-side in-context demonstrations for closed models.

## Method

For each training question, the method samples multiple solution traces under controlled rewrites, merges recurring intermediate steps into a per-question directed reasoning graph (nodes = canonicalized steps, edges = observed transitions), and computes a Solution Boundary Cut (SBC) via reverse BFS from correct terminal nodes to partition states into recoverable and irrecoverable regions. A GraphSAGE-based edge classifier is then trained across questions to predict which transitions cross the SBC, surfacing recurring, model-specific failure signatures (e.g., low-probability continuations from branching nodes). High-confidence predicted crossings guide the construction of targeted alternation pairs: for each boundary node, the method finds a success-inducing and a failure-inducing rewrite that share the same prefix up to that node but diverge immediately after. These pairs are applied via DPO fine-tuning for open-weight models or as 200 in-context demonstrations for closed models, with a leave-one-benchmark-out protocol ensuring cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

Across four model families (DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Phi-4, LLaMA-3.3-70B, GPT-4.1-mini) and three benchmarks (Big-MATH, MMLU-PRO, DROP), targeted alternation training yields the most consistent gains over all baselines under a leave-one-benchmark-out protocol. The largest absolute improvements appear on GPT-4.1-mini (e.g., +7.7 pp on Big-MATH from 67.1% to 74.8%, +6.0 pp on DROP from 85.9% to 91.9%) and LLaMA-3.3-70B (+9.4 pp on DROP from 67.1% to 76.5%). On the closed-weight frontier evaluation (Humanity's Last Exam, text-only), 200 in-context targeted alternation examples raise GPT-5.2 (xhigh) solve rate from 35.4% to 38.1% (p<0.05, McNemar's test), competitive with the best publicly reported text-only score of 37.72%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Targeted alternation training outperforms both listed baselines across all four models and all three benchmarks. Against hard-example mining (Yang et al., 2025; logit-margin uncertainty selection), the gap is largest on GPT-4.1-mini (+4.8 pp on Big-MATH: 74.8 vs. 70.0; +5.4 pp on DROP: 91.9 vs. 86.5) and LLaMA-3.3-70B (+3.1 pp on Big-MATH: 65.3 vs. 62.2; +5.0 pp on DROP: 76.5 vs. 71.5). Against inference-time rewrite ensembling / self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023b), targeted alternation training wins on Big-MATH for every model (e.g., +5.7 pp on GPT-4.1-mini: 74.8 vs. 69.1) and on DROP for GPT-4.1-mini (+6.9 pp: 91.9 vs. 85.0) and LLaMA-3.3-70B (+2.4 pp: 76.5 vs. 74.1), though self-consistency is competitive or slightly ahead on MMLU-PRO for LLaMA-3.3-70B (73.0 vs. 72.8) and Phi-4 (71.5 vs. 72.3, within CI). The paper also shows that the contrastive pairing is essential: the positive-only trajectory ablation (training only on correct traces without matched failure pairs) consistently underperforms the full method, confirming that the SBC-guided contrastive structure—not merely additional correct examples—drives the robustness gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of rewrite-robustness training and graph-based reasoning analysis. Prior robustness work either augments uniformly (Wei et al., 2019; Feng et al., 2021), searches adversarially for idiosyncratic failures (Wang et al., 2024b), or applies step-level preference signals (Step-DPO, Xu et al., 2025), but none localizes *where* in the reasoning trajectory a model becomes irrecoverable. The key conceptual departure is the Solution Boundary Cut: aggregating multiple stochastic traces into a per-question reasoning graph and identifying the precise transition from recoverable to irrecoverable states, then using that boundary to construct minimal contrastive pairs. The training mechanism (DPO) and the rewrite generation are standard; the novelty lies in the graph-derived data selection criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a small set of SBC-guided contrastive examples can improve even a closed, API-only frontier model (GPT-5.2) via in-context learning, suggesting the approach is accessible without fine-tuning infrastructure. The cross-dataset transfer (train on two benchmarks, evaluate on a third) indicates the learned failure patterns are model-specific rather than task-specific. Open questions include whether the observational (non-causal) nature of the reasoning graph limits the SBC's reliability under distribution shift, whether the K=2 sampling budget is sufficient for harder reasoning tasks with longer trajectories, and how the method extends to tool-augmented or multi-turn settings where the 'reasoning graph' structure is less clear. The finding that simplified phrasing can be more failure-inducing than complex phrasing for some models is a useful diagnostic but remains a descriptive hypothesis without causal validation.
