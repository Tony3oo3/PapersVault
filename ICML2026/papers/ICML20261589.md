## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods are designed for autoregressive (left-to-right) generation and embed signals by biasing token probabilities, which degrades text quality and relies on a fixed causal context that is unavailable in discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs). While dLLMs can generate tokens in arbitrary order, prior work has not exploited the decoding order itself as a watermarking channel, instead still resorting to probability shaping or controlled sampling. This paper asks whether the practical order-sensitivity of dLLMs (arising from imperfect training) can be leveraged to embed a detectable watermark without modifying the model's learned conditional probabilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a secret watermark key ξ (used to seed a binary hash function over the vocabulary), and a dLLM predictor p_θ, and produces a watermarked text sequence of fixed length (e.g., 256 tokens) generated via block-wise decoding. Detection takes only the generated text and the key, computing a global z-score on the parity-matching rate or a sliding-window aggregated statistic (z_win) for robustness to insertions, deletions, and substitutions. The method operates as a plug-and-play wrapper around any standard dLLM decoding strategy (confidence, entropy, or margin-based), requiring no access to model internals or the original prompt at detection time.

## Method

dgMARK embeds a watermark by steering the unmasking order rather than reweighting token probabilities. A deterministic binary hash f(v, ξ) partitions the vocabulary at each position i into a parity-matching set G_i = {v : f(v,ξ) ≡ i mod 2} and a residual set R_i. During decoding, the method restricts the candidate set to positions whose sampled or greedy token falls in G_j, then selects the highest-reward position among them (falling back to all positions if none match). A one-step lookahead beam search variant (top-k) further improves embedding by estimating, for each top-k candidate, how many subsequent positions would remain parity-matching after committing to that token, and selecting the candidate that maximizes this future alignment count. Detection measures whether the observed parity-matching rate significantly exceeds the 0.5 random baseline via a one-sided z-test, with a sliding-window two-sided variant (aggregating z_s² over overlapping windows) to handle index shifts from post-editing. The key distinction from prior watermarking is that p_θ(y_j | y_I, x) is never modified—no logit biasing, green/red boosting, or distribution renormalization is applied—so the watermark signal arises purely from the order in which positions are committed.

## Results

On the C4 dataset with LLaDA 1.5 under multinomial sampling, dgMARK with 3-beam search achieves TPR = 1.000 at FPR = 0.0 with PPL 5.40, compared to KGW(δ=3) at TPR 0.984 / PPL 7.87 and PATTERN-MARK(δ=3) at TPR 0.586 / PPL 7.69. Under greedy sampling, dgMARK+3-beam reaches TPR 0.963 at PPL 4.75, while KGW(δ=3) achieves TPR 0.970 at PPL 5.83 and PATTERN-MARK(δ=3) only TPR 0.584 at PPL 5.86. On benchmark tasks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval), dgMARK incurs the smallest quality degradation among all watermarking methods, with MMLU accuracy dropping by only 0.001 (greedy) or 0.005 (multinomial) relative to the non-watermarked baseline, versus drops of 0.083–0.084 for KGW and 0.071–0.061 for PATTERN-MARK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), dgMARK with 3-beam search achieves a higher TPR (1.000 vs. 0.984) at a substantially lower PPL (5.40 vs. 7.87) under multinomial sampling, indicating better quality preservation at equal or superior detectability. Against PATTERN-MARK (Chen et al., 2025), dgMARK dominates on both axes: TPR 1.000 vs. 0.586 and PPL 5.40 vs. 7.69, showing that order-agnostic probability-biasing is both less detectable and more quality-degrading than decoding-order steering. Against the concurrent dLLM watermarking baseline of Gloaguen et al. (2026), dgMARK (multinomial + 3-beam) achieves TPR 1.000 at PPL 5.40 versus TPR 0.917 at PPL 6.04 for Gloaguen(δ=2), and also preserves downstream benchmark accuracy better (e.g., GSM8K 0.723 vs. 0.644, HumanEval 0.134 vs. 0.128). Across all three baselines, dgMARK consistently wins on perplexity while matching or exceeding detectability, confirming that the decoding-order channel is a less intrusive watermarking mechanism than explicit probability reweighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

dgMARK repurposes the green/red partition idea from KGW (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) but applies it to an entirely different channel: instead of biasing token probabilities, it steers the unmasking order in discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) so that positions whose candidate tokens satisfy a parity constraint are decoded first. This departs from both autoregressive watermarking (KGW, PATTERN-MARK) and concurrent dLLM watermarking efforts (Bagchi et al., 2025; Gloaguen et al., 2026; Raban et al., 2026), which all embed signals by altering token selection probabilities. The method exploits a practical property of dLLMs identified by Kim et al. (2025)—that imperfect training makes generation sensitive to decoding order—turning what is typically a nuisance into a watermarking degree of freedom.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs (LLaDA, Dream, Mercury, Gemini Diffusion) gain traction as alternatives to autoregressive models, reliable watermarking for them becomes practically urgent, and dgMARK offers a plug-and-play solution compatible with standard decoding strategies without modifying learned probabilities. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the watermark relies on the current imperfection of dLLMs: a perfectly trained, truly order-invariant model would render the signal undetectable, making this an empirical rather than a theoretically guaranteed guarantee. Open questions include security against well-resourced adversaries who might infer the hash mapping, and whether the approach remains effective as dLLM training improves and order-sensitivity diminishes.
