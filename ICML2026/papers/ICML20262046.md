## Problem

When intermediate empirical marginals are available between two endpoints, standard piecewise conditional flow matching (CFM) produces trajectories with unphysical velocity discontinuities at each marginal boundary. Existing multimarginal smoothing methods (MMFM, 3MSBM) impose prescriptive interpolation strategies (cubic splines, phase-space lifting) that need not reflect the true physical dynamics. The paper asks whether a principled, data-driven relaxation of the hard marginal constraints can yield smooth, flexible trajectories without ad-hoc design choices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of K+2 empirical marginals {μ_{t_k}} at times t_k ∈ [0,1] (two endpoints plus K intermediate snapshots) along with a precomputed joint coupling π_all across all marginals (either product of consecutive OT couplings or independent sampling). During training, it conditions on endpoint pairs (x_0, x_1) and one sample per intermediate marginal, solving a K×K linear fixed-point system in closed form for W2² potentials. The output is a parametric velocity field u_t^θ trained via a consistency model (iMF/MeanFlow), enabling 1- or 2-step inference of the flow map Ψ_{t1,t2} between arbitrary time points without iterative ODE integration.

## Method

The key insight is that piecewise CFM is equivalent to conditional dynamic OT with singular (Dirac-delta) potential penalties enforcing exact intermediate marginal constraints; relaxing these to smooth, finite-strength potential energy terms w_k·λ_k(t)·D[ρ_t, μ_{t_k}] in the Benamou-Brenier action yields the OTP variational problem. The resulting Euler-Lagrange equations give sample trajectories with acceleration ∑_k w_k λ_k(t) ∇g_k(X_t, t), where ∇g_k is the functional-derivative force from the k-th marginal (e.g., x − ψ*(x) for W2²). Temporal localization of λ_k(t) allows evaluating forces only at discrete marginal times, making the conditional solution a CFM straight-line base plus potential-driven corrections with a closed-form linear fixed point for W2². Training regresses a MeanFlow consistency model onto these conditional velocities using a homotopy curriculum (sigmoid α schedule from 0 to 1) that gradually introduces corrections, and the paper proves Wasserstein alignment bounds W2(ρ_{t_k}, μ_{t_k}) ≤ C_k/w² + O(w^{-3}) controlled by potential strength and training loss.

## Results

On the EB 100D L2O protocol (holding out two intermediate marginals), OTP-FM(W₂²) achieves a mean MMD of 0.068±0.001, outperforming the next-best methods OT-MFM (0.105) and MMFM (0.101) by ~35%. On CITE 5D L1O, OTP-FM(W₂²) reaches W₁ = 0.435±0.003, beating MMFM (0.553) and all simulation-based baselines. On the non-biological Beijing air quality dataset, OTP-FM(W₂²) achieves a held-out W₂ of 17.28±0.56, roughly halving the error of 3MSBM (38.09) and MMFM (38.89). Training completes in 3–5 minutes on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTP-FM(W₂²) achieves the best or second-best result on nearly all metrics across both scRNA-seq datasets. On the key interpolation benchmark (EB 100D L2O MMD), it outperforms OT-MFM (0.105) and MMFM (0.101) by ~35%, and 3MSBM (0.135) by ~50%. On CITE 5D L1O W₁, it beats MMFM (0.553) by 21% and all simulation-based methods (NLSB 0.767, MIOFlow 0.494, DMSB 1.705). The only setting where OTP-FM is not best is EB 100D L0O (no holdouts), where plain I-CFM (0.028) and [SF]²M (0.023) win—precisely the setting that does not test interpolation. On the non-biological datasets (Table 4), OTP-FM(W₂²) dominates: on Gulf of Mexico held-out W₂ it scores 0.107 vs. 3MSBM's 0.135 and MMFM's 0.150, and on Beijing held-out W₂ it scores 17.28 vs. 38.09 (3MSBM) and 38.89 (MMFM), a >2× improvement. Among simulation-free baselines, OT-CFM and [SF]²M (piecewise CFM variants) are consistently outperformed on interpolation metrics, confirming that the soft potential formulation captures dynamics that rigid stitching cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conditional flow matching (CFM) to the multimarginal setting by reframing piecewise CFM as dynamic optimal transport (Benamou-Brenier) with hard constraints on intermediate marginals, then relaxing these to smooth, finite-strength potential energy terms in the OT action. Prior multimarginal methods (MMFM, 3MSBM) smooth the resulting kinks with prescriptive strategies (cubic splines, phase-space lifting), whereas OTP-FM derives the interpolated dynamics from a variational principle, letting the data determine trajectories through optimization over a broad design space (potential type, strength, temporal kernel). The training algorithm leverages improved MeanFlow (iMF) for simulation-free few-step inference and builds on the consistency-model framework of Boffi et al. for theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OTP-FM provides the first principled variational framework for multimarginal trajectory inference that is both simulation-free and theoretically grounded, with Wasserstein alignment bounds controlled by potential strength and training loss. The 3–5 minute training time on a single GPU and SOTA interpolation accuracy across scRNA-seq, oceanographic, and meteorological datasets make it practically accessible for scientific domains with sequential snapshot data. Key limitations include the tuning burden of the design space (potential strength is the most impactful parameter), the fact that marginal matching does not guarantee physically meaningful interpolated states for sparsely-sampled data, and the instability of MMD/KLD potentials relative to the recommended W2²/∞ choices. Open directions include combining with data-manifold learning (MFM), incorporating domain-specific inductive biases (cell growth, conditional inference), and moving toward self-consistent dynamics-coupling optimization.
