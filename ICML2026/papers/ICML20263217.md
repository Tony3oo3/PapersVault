## Problem

Existing text-rich image datasets (Marion10M, AnyWords3M) predominantly contain short, simple text with average token lengths of 10–26, failing to capture the long-form, layout-complex, and semantically dense text found in real-world scenarios such as infographics, documents, and advertisements. This data gap leaves both diffusion and autoregressive models poorly equipped for dense-text rendering, and no large-scale benchmark exists to rigorously evaluate this capability.

## Input / Output

The unified input is a long-form prompt combining a scene description (S) and rendered text content (T), with an average token length of 148.82 across the dataset. Adaptive prompting strategies are used per subset: LLMs merge S and T into diverse natural-language formulations for filtered real-image subsets, while placeholder tokens (e.g., "<>") are inserted into VLM-generated scene captions for synthetic subsets to enable controllable text placement. Outputs are dense-text RGB images at 512×512 (Lumina-mGPT, PixArt-α) or 1024×1024 (Janus Pro 1B), evaluated via CLIP similarity, PaddleOCR word-level accuracy/F1/CER, and a proposed IOU Count layout metric.

## Method

TextAtlas5M is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline spanning synthetic and real splits. The synthetic split progresses through three complexity levels: CleanTextSynth (2M pure-text images rendered with 8,700 fonts via OCR Rendering), TextVisionBlend (interleaved image-text PDFs generated with PyMuPDF from Obelics/WIT data, annotated with bounding boxes and Qwen2-VL captions), and StyledTextSynth (GPT-4o simulates 18 real-world text-integration scenes, SD3.5 renders text-free backgrounds, and LLMs generate contextually relevant text placed into detected regions). The real split aggregates filtered long-text samples from AnyWords3M/Marion10M, PowerPoint slides annotated by Qwen2-VL, book covers, arXiv PDFs, and OCR-filtered web images from CommonCrawl/LAION-5B with human-verified annotations. For evaluation, both autoregressive (Lumina-mGPT 7B, Janus Pro 1B) and diffusion (PixArt-α 0.6B) models are fine-tuned from scratch on TextAtlas5M and benchmarked against 13 SOTA systems on the 4,000-sample TextAtlasEval, revealing that even GPT-4o struggles with long-text rendering while fine-tuning on the dataset yields consistent gains across all architectures.

## Results

On TextAtlasEval, GPT-4o achieves the best overall text rendering among closed-source models, with CER of 0.36 (CleanTextSynth), 0.15 (TextVisionBlend), 0.21 (StyledTextSynth), and 0.32 (TextScenesHQ). Fine-tuning Lumina-mgpt 7B at 1024×1024 on TextAtlas5M reduces CER from 0.97 to 0.31 on CleanTextSynth (beating GPT-4o's 0.36) and from 0.95 to 0.22 on TextVisionBlend. On the hardest subset (TextScenesHQ), even GPT-4o only reaches 82.88% OCR accuracy, while fine-tuned models achieve 3.83–6.44%, highlighting the persistent difficulty of dense real-world text rendering. On LongText-Bench, PixArt-α fine-tuned on TextAtlas5M improves Text Score from 0.14 to 24.28, and on CVTG-2K, Word Accuracy jumps from 0.02 to 31.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, GPT-4o leads on TextVisionBlend (CER 0.15) and StyledTextSynth (CER 0.21), while Nano Banana is competitive and sometimes superior (e.g., CleanTextSynth CER 0.23 vs. GPT-4o's 0.36; TextScenesHQ Acc 75.15% vs. 82.88%). SD-3.5 Large and Qwen Image trail significantly on CER (0.73–0.88 on most subsets). All open-source baselines—AnyText, TextDiffuser-2, PixArt-σ, and Infinity-2B—perform far below closed-source models, with CER consistently above 0.83 and OCR accuracy below 3%. Fine-tuning on TextAtlas5M yields dramatic gains: Lumina-mgpt 7B (1024) drops CER from 0.97 to 0.31 on CleanTextSynth (beating GPT-4o) and from 0.95 to 0.22 on TextVisionBlend (approaching GPT-4o's 0.15). PixArt-α 0.6B (512) similarly improves from CER 0.98 to 0.33 on CleanTextSynth, surpassing GPT-4o. On the most challenging TextScenesHQ subset, even the best fine-tuned model (Lumina-mgpt 1024, CER 0.79) remains far behind GPT-4o (CER 0.32), indicating that real-world dense-text generation remains an open problem. On the short-text LAIONEval4000 benchmark, PixArt-α fine-tuned on TextAtlas5M (Acc 67.53, CER 0.36) outperforms both the Marion10M-trained baseline (Acc 52.37) and TextDiffuser (Acc 53.25), confirming that long-text training does not degrade short-text capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the text-to-image generation literature by reframing the text rendering problem around 'long text' defined not merely by token count but by layout complexity and semantic richness. Prior text-rich datasets (Marion10M, AnyWords3M, TextCaps) average 10–26 tokens per image and focus on short, isolated words, leaving a substantial gap for dense, multi-region, interleaved text scenarios. TextAtlas5M addresses this gap by combining a three-tier synthetic pipeline (clean text → interleaved documents → styled natural scenes) with diverse real-world sources (PowerPoint slides, book covers, arXiv PDFs, CommonCrawl images), and by introducing TextAtlasEval, a 4,000-sample human-verified benchmark. Methodologically, the dataset construction repurposes existing tools (PyMuPDF, SAM2, YOLOv11, RT-DETR, Qwen2-VL, GPT-4o) in a novel multi-stage pipeline, while evaluation spans both diffusion (PixArt-α) and autoregressive (Lumina-mGPT, Janus-Pro) architectures to demonstrate broad applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: existing resources average ~10–26 tokens while TextAtlas5M averages 148.82, and the human-verified TextAtlasEval benchmark reveals that even GPT-4o achieves only ~77–83% OCR accuracy on the hardest subsets, exposing a genuine capability gap in current models. A notable practical finding is that models fine-tuned on the 5M-sample TextAtlas5M outperform TextDiffuser2 trained on the larger Marion10M (10M), suggesting that for text rendering, data diversity and layout complexity matter more than raw scale. Limitations include the absence of specialized domains (legal documents, formula-heavy scientific papers, multilingual posters) and the reliance on English/Chinese (99.7% of content). Open questions include whether iterative dataset bootstrapping and multi-caption generation can further close the gap between open-source and proprietary models, and whether the VQ-VAE tokenizer bottleneck identified for AR models can be resolved without sacrificing generation quality.
