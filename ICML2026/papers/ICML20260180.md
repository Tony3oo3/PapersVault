## Problem

Most test-time scaling (TTS) methods are built around autoregressive left-to-right decoding and are ill-suited to discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs), which generate sequences via parallel iterative denoising over the entire sequence. Naive best-of-N width scaling for dLLMs is computationally prohibitive at O(NT) function evaluations, and standard external verifiers introduce substantial memory overhead. The paper addresses how to allocate test-time compute effectively under multi-step denoising dynamics without relying on external reward models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (a math word problem or a code task description) and produces a single text completion (a numerical answer or a Python function). Internally, the dLLM operates on partially masked token sequences of fixed length (256 for math, 512 for code), partitioned into contiguous blocks of size M=32 for block-diffusion decoding. At any denoising step, the z0-prediction head yields a full unmasked completion that serves as the hypothesis for verification, while the actual denoising state retains masked positions within the current and future blocks.

## Method

PRISM integrates three mechanisms into a unified inference pipeline. First, Hierarchical Trajectory Search (HTS) divides denoising into three stages—stochastic exploration at high noise, progressive thinning with geometric decay of the active trajectory pool from N to K, and final refinement—reducing complexity from O(NT) to approximately O(N+KT). Second, local branching via partial remasking preserves high-confidence tokens as a stable 'logic skeleton' while selectively re-masking low-confidence positions to generate diverse children around top-ranked survivors, providing targeted diversity without restarting from scratch. Third, Self-Verified Feedback (SVF) reuses the same dLLM as a lightweight binary verifier by prompting it with a Yes/No judgment on intermediate completions, replacing external reward models with a single-token decoding call that adds negligible overhead relative to denoising steps.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, PRISM (K=8) achieves 85.30% accuracy on GSM8K and 42.80% on MATH-500, improving over the N=1 baseline (67.58% and 26.40%) by 26.2% and 62.1% respectively, while using only 1,048 NFE versus 4,096 NFE for Best-of-16 (87.50%). On code generation, PRISM (K=8) reaches 79.27% Pass@1 on HumanEval and 38.20% on MBPP, surpassing Best-of-16 (82.32% and 35.20%) on MBPP while using roughly 3× fewer function evaluations. Across all three dLLMs (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B, LLaDA-2.0-mini), PRISM consistently delivers 26–45% relative accuracy gains over single-trajectory decoding at a cost comparable to Best-of-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Best-of-N width-scaling baseline (Brown et al., 2024), PRISM (K=8) matches or approaches Best-of-16 accuracy with roughly 4× fewer NFE: on GSM8K it reaches 85.30% at 1,048 NFE versus 87.50% at 4,096 NFE, and on MATH-500 it actually surpasses Best-of-16 (42.80% vs. 38.00%) at less than one-third the compute. Against ReMDM (Wang et al., 2025) on TruthfulQA, PRISM achieves higher ROUGE-1 (31.8 vs. 29.5) with shorter inference time (1,048 s vs. 1,355 s on a single H100). For external verifier comparisons on GSM8K, PRISM's self-verified feedback (SVF, 85.30%) outperforms Qwen-7B as verifier (84.39%) but is slightly below Qwen2-7B (85.98%) and Qwen3-8B (87.35%); however, SVF avoids loading a separate 15–16B model, keeping deployment memory at a single 8B model. Overall, PRISM's key advantage is the performance-efficiency trade-off: it recovers most of the Best-of-16 accuracy gain at a fraction of the denoising cost, and its self-verification signal is competitive with external verifiers at zero additional model memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time scaling (TTS) paradigm—dominated by autoregressive decoding methods like best-of-N, self-consistency, and tree-of-thoughts—to discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs), whose parallel denoising dynamics make standard left-to-right search ill-suited. It departs from SMC-style inference (PG-DLM) by replacing importance-weighted resampling with sparse top-S pruning and heuristic SVF scoring, and from block-schedule ensembling (HEX) by introducing adaptive hierarchical pruning rather than exhaustive parallel completion. The self-verification mechanism repurposes the dLLM itself as a binary judge, avoiding the memory and deployment overhead of external reward models that prior TTS work typically requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs (LLaDA, Dream, Mercury, Gemini Diffusion) gain traction as alternatives to AR models, an efficient TTS framework is needed to unlock their reasoning potential without the O(NT) cost of naive width scaling. PRISM's near-linear NFE scaling and self-verification design make test-time scaling deployable on a single GPU without loading a second model, which is practically significant for inference cost. Open questions include whether the pruning window and decay schedule generalize to larger dLLMs (100B+), whether SVF reliability holds on open-ended or multi-turn tasks beyond verifiable math/code, and whether the block-diffusion assumption limits applicability to fully parallel dLLMs.
