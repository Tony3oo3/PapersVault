## Problem

Existing single-cell perturbation prediction methods assume a single fixed response distribution conditioned on observed context (cell type, perturbation type), implicitly marginalizing over unobserved latent factors such as microenvironmental fluctuations and batch effects. In reality, these latent factors induce a family of plausible response distributions for the same observed conditions, and collapsing this variability into one static distribution limits generalization to unseen perturbations and cellular contexts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of m control cells (each a |G|-dimensional gene expression vector, typically 2,000 HVGs for downstream tasks), a perturbation type τ, and a cellular context label c as input, and produces a batch of m predicted perturbed cell expression profiles as output. Crucially, the unit of modeling is the entire cell batch (an empirical distribution) rather than individual cells: the control batch is consumed as a collective distributional object, and the output is a population-level sample from the distribution of possible perturbed responses. For pretraining, the gene space expands to 12,626 genes to unify across datasets.

## Method

PerturbDiff shifts the random variable from individual cells to cell distributions by treating the perturbed population as a distribution-valued random variable D_{c,τ} supported on the space of probability measures. Each empirical cell distribution is embedded into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) via its kernel mean embedding, reducing the diffusion state to a single point in a function space. A DDPM-style forward process adds Gaussian random elements in the RKHS, and the reverse denoising objective—expressed as the squared RKHS norm between the target and predicted embeddings—is exactly equivalent to the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between the true and predicted cell populations, providing a distribution-aware loss naturally suited to sparse single-cell data. The denoising network is a Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) maintaining parallel control and perturbed token streams with joint attention, conditioned via AdaLN-Zero on timestep and covariates, trained with a hybrid MMD + MSE loss and classifier-free guidance. A marginal pretraining stage on 61M unperturbed CellxGene cells learns a broad prior over the cell manifold before fine-tuning on perturbation data, substantially improving low-data generalization.

## Results

Across 12 evaluation metrics on three benchmarks (PBMC, Tahoe100M, Replogle), PerturbDiff (From Scratch) achieves state-of-the-art performance on the two large-scale datasets (PBMC and Tahoe100M), with particularly strong gains on differential-expression metrics (AUPRC, AUROC, DEPrec) that capture biologically meaningful population-level response shifts. On the smaller Replogle dataset, PerturbDiff (From Scratch) is second-best, but the pretrained-and-finetuned variant (PerturbDiff Finetuned) closes the gap and matches the strongest baseline. Per-perturbation analysis on PBMC and Tahoe100M shows PerturbDiff outperforming STATE on DE-related metrics with win rates above 96–100% across held-out perturbation conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STATE (the strongest baseline), PerturbDiff (From Scratch) wins on nearly all 12 metrics for PBMC and Tahoe100M, with per-perturbation win rates exceeding 96% on DE metrics (AUROC, AUPRC, DEPrec); on Replogle, PerturbDiff (From Scratch) trails slightly, but PerturbDiff (Finetuned) matches STATE across all metrics. The Linear model baseline is competitive on Tahoe100M but collapses on Replogle, with all three PDS metrics dropping to ~0.5 (random level). The Mean baseline performs reasonably on PBMC but degrades substantially on Tahoe100M, with AUPRC and AUROC approaching random. Squidiff and CellFlow exhibit the weakest overall performance, ranking last on multiple metrics across datasets. CPA is consistently outperformed by PerturbDiff on both large-scale datasets. The paper also shows that removing the MMD loss (ablation) consistently degrades performance, especially on DE metrics, confirming that the distribution-aware objective is critical rather than the MSE term.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerturbDiff extends the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) from finite-dimensional cell vectors to the infinite-dimensional RKHS of kernel mean embeddings, where each cell distribution is a point. This departs fundamentally from prior perturbation models (STATE, CellFlow, Squidiff, CellOT) that treat individual cells as the random variable and learn a single conditional distribution P_{c,τ}; instead, PerturbDiff models a distribution-valued random variable D_{c,τ} capturing variability from unobserved latent factors. The MMD training objective arises naturally from the squared RKHS norm between kernel mean embeddings, rather than being externally imposed as in STATE. The MM-DiT architecture and classifier-free guidance are borrowed from image-generation diffusion transformers (Esser et al., 2024; Peebles & Xie, 2023), and the marginal pretraining strategy parallels foundation-model pretraining on large-scale atlases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By modeling a family of plausible response distributions rather than collapsing to a single one, PerturbDiff addresses a structural limitation of all prior perturbation methods that marginalize over unobserved batch and microenvironmental factors, yielding substantially better generalization to unseen perturbations and cell lines. The marginal pretraining on 61M CellxGene cells provides a practical recipe for data-efficient perturbation modeling when only a few hundred cells per perturbation are available (as in Replogle). Open questions include whether the RKHS formulation scales to multi-omics or spatial data, whether the non-monotonic scaling behavior (medium model outperforming larger ones) reflects a fundamental capacity limit for perturbation tasks, and whether the distribution-level framing can be extended to model sequential or combinatorial perturbations.
