## Problem

Standard policy gradient methods require O(ε^{-2}) trajectories to reach an ε-approximate stationary point, and while gradient reuse has been extensively studied (improving rates to O(ε^{-3/2})), the reuse of past off-policy trajectories—despite being the most natural strategy for improving sample efficiency—remains largely unexplored from a theoretical perspective. The paper asks whether reusing past trajectories can provably accelerate PG convergence and, if so, at what rate.

## Input / Output

At each iteration k, the method consumes N fresh on-policy trajectories collected under the current policy π_{θ_k} plus N·ω_k trajectories from the most recent ω_k iterations (each collected under its respective past policy). The output is an updated policy parameter vector θ_{k+1} ∈ ℝ^{d_Θ} via gradient ascent. A key practical detail is that RT-PG does not require storing past policy parameterizations—only the trajectories themselves—making it memory-efficient for large-parameter policies, though it does require O(ε^{-1}) trajectories in memory at peak.

## Method

The core contribution is the MPM (Multiple Power Mean) estimator, which combines trajectories from the most recent ω iterations using a power-mean-corrected multiple importance weighting scheme. The vanilla IW p_{θ_k}(τ)/p_{θ_i}(τ) is replaced by the harmonic mean ((1−λ_{i,k})·p_{θ_i}(τ)/p_{θ_k}(τ) + λ_{i,k})^{-1}, which is bounded by 1/λ_{i,k} (defensive property) while introducing a controllable PM bias. Deterministic coefficients α_{i,k}=1/ω_k eliminate the cross-time bias that plagues the balance heuristic, and a covering argument over a d_Θ-dimensional ball handles the target bias arising from the history-dependent parameter θ_k. The resulting concentration bound scales as O((Nω_k)^{-1/2}), yielding a sample complexity of Õ(ε^{-2}ω^{-1}) for partial reuse and Õ(ε^{-1}) for full reuse—the best known rate for PG methods with stochastic gradients.

## Results

Theoretically, RT-PG achieves a sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-1})$ for full trajectory reuse (the best known rate for PG methods), improving over the $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ of standard on-policy PGs and the $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3/2})$ of gradient-reuse methods. In Cart Pole, RT-PG achieves a sample efficiency ratio of up to 101.52× over GPOMDP (ω=8, Nω=128), meaning GPOMDP requires over 100× more trajectories to match RT-PG's performance. In Half Cheetah-v4, RT-PG (ω=8, N=40) attains an average return of 7.04 (95% CI: 6.51–7.63), outperforming all variance-reduced baselines (SVRPG, SRVRPG, STORM-PG, DEF-PG) and the naive MIW-PG trajectory-reuse method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-PG decisively outperforms the standard on-policy baseline GPOMDP: in Cart Pole, the sample efficiency ratio reaches 101.52× (ω=8, Nω=128), and even at the smallest window (ω=2, Nω=32) it still achieves 2.11×. Against the four gradient-reuse variance-reduced baselines (SVRPG, SRVRPG, STORM-PG, DEF-PG) in Half Cheetah-v4, RT-PG achieves the highest average return (7.04 at ω=8) while using the same amount of fresh data per iteration. RT-PG also outperforms MIW-PG (Lin et al., 2025), a trajectory-reuse method using uniform-weight MIW, empirically validating the benefit of the power-mean correction over naive importance weighting. Compared to BH-PG (balance heuristic), RT-PG achieves similar performance but without the need to store all ωₖ most recent policies or evaluate every trajectory under every policy, reducing per-iteration likelihood evaluations from 2Nωₖ to Nωₖ. The key theoretical advantage is that RT-PG is the first trajectory-reuse method to achieve Õ(ε⁻¹) sample complexity without requiring storage of old policies, whereas BPO (the only prior trajectory-reuse method with guarantees) requires O(ε⁻⁴/³) memory and stricter assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multiple importance weighting (MIW) framework from Veach & Guibas (1995) for combining data from multiple behavioral policies, and the power mean (PM) correction from Metelli et al. (2021) for bounding importance weights. It departs from the dominant line of variance-reduced PG work (SVRPG, SRVRPG, STORM-PG, DEF-PG), which reuses past *gradients*, by instead reusing past *trajectories*—a more natural but theoretically underexplored strategy. The key conceptual departure is the identification of two distinct biases (target bias and cross-time bias) that arise specifically from the sequential dependency in trajectory reuse, and the design of the MPM estimator with deterministic coefficients to eliminate cross-time bias while retaining boundedness via PM correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Õ(ε⁻¹) sample complexity in the full-reuse regime is the best known rate for PG methods with stochastic gradients, general policy classes, and continuous state-action spaces, and the adaptive Õ(ε⁻²ω⁻¹) partial-reuse rate gives practitioners a tunable trade-off between memory and sample efficiency. Key limitations include the bounded χ²-divergence assumption (standard but restrictive), the Õ(ε⁻¹) trajectory memory requirement, and the d_Θ dependence in the rate. Open questions include removing the bounded IW variance assumption entirely (as DEF-PG did for gradient reuse), eliminating the dimensionality dependence, and developing adaptive MPM coefficients that do not require knowing the divergence bound D in advance.
