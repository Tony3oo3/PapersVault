## Problem

Diffusion language models (dLMs) promise higher throughput via parallel generation, but their learning efficiency lags behind autoregressive (AR) models when trained from scratch, and existing AR-to-dLM conversion methods suffer from accuracy drops due to fully bidirectional attention patterns that diverge from AR causality and a training-test gap in token masking distributions. The paper asks: how can pretrained AR models be converted into dLMs that preserve task accuracy while unlocking the speed benefits of parallel decoding?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the model receives a sequence partitioned into contiguous blocks where each block is partially corrupted (mask tokens sampled via position-dependent probabilities at noise level t) and concatenated with the clean tokens of all preceding blocks, with a special attention mask restricting noisy tokens to attend to clean context. At inference, the model performs iterative block-wise denoising using confidence-based sampling: at each denoising step, all tokens exceeding a confidence threshold are finalized simultaneously, yielding variable tokens-per-forward (TPF) that controls the accuracy-throughput trade-off. The final output is a fully generated text sequence, with throughput measured in tokens per second on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is a continuous pretraining scheme that converts pretrained AR models into dLMs using a block-wise attention pattern where each corrupted block is conditioned on clean preceding context (enabling native KV caching and preserving AR weight distributions), combined with a position-dependent token masking strategy that assigns exponentially increasing masking probabilities to later token positions within a block (w_i(t) = exp(β(1−t)·i)) to mimic the left-to-right denoising tendency observed at test time. The training objective is a masked denoising loss over blocks with no token shift (predicting mask tokens directly rather than next tokens), initialized from AR weights and trained with a cosine-decayed learning rate (1e-5) over 25B–500B tokens. The paper systematically ablates attention patterns (bidirectional vs. block-wise, with/without clean context, with/without token shift), block sizes, and masking schemes on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen3-4B/8B, showing that block-wise attention with clean context and no token shift yields the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off, and that longer training steadily improves likelihood estimation enabling more aggressive parallel decoding.

## Results

On 12 tasks spanning coding, math, factual knowledge, and commonsense reasoning, Efficient-DLM-8B achieves 70.93% average accuracy at 109.78 tok/sec (TPF=2.74), matching Qwen3-8B (71.58%) while delivering 2.6× higher throughput. Efficient-DLM-4B reaches 67.39% average accuracy at 119.33 tok/sec, surpassing Qwen3-1.7B (59.39%) by +8.0% with 1.7× higher throughput. On text embedding (MTEB, 15 datasets), Efficient-DLM-1.5B/4B outperforms same-size AR Qwen models by +7.71%/+9.91% on average, demonstrating the bidirectional advantage of dLMs for representation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest dLM baseline Dream-7B (65.30% avg, 28.11 tok/s), Efficient-DLM-8B achieves +5.6% higher accuracy with ~4× higher throughput (109.78 tok/s), a direct benefit of block-wise attention enabling native KV caching versus Dream's fully bidirectional design. Compared to LLaDA-8B (54.92% avg, 25.04 tok/s), Efficient-DLM-8B leads by +16.0% accuracy and 4.4× throughput. Against AR baselines, Efficient-DLM-8B (70.93%) is within 0.65% of Qwen3-8B (71.58%) while running 2.6× faster, and outperforms Qwen3-4B (67.97%) by +2.96% at 2.3× throughput. Efficient-DLM-4B (67.39%) beats Qwen3-1.7B (59.39%) by +8.0% accuracy with 1.7× higher throughput, and matches Qwen3-4B within 0.6% at 2.5× speed. At the 1.5B scale, Efficient-DLM-1.5B (52.04%) trails Qwen2.5-1.5B (54.47%) by 2.4% but delivers 2.2× throughput, and substantially outperforms Llama-3.2-1B (36.82%) by +15.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AR-to-dLM conversion line initiated by Gong et al. (2025a) and the SOTA fully-bidirectional dLMs (Dream-7B, LLaDA), but departs from them by replacing full bidirectional attention with block-wise attention conditioned on clean context, following the Block Diffusion architecture of Arriola et al. (2025) yet repurposing it for continuous pretraining from AR checkpoints rather than training from scratch. It also introduces position-dependent token masking to address a training-test gap in mask token distributions that prior dLM work (uniform masking) overlooked, and leverages Fast-dLLM's confidence-based sampling for parallel decoding. The paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study providing actionable design guidelines for the AR-to-dLM conversion pipeline rather than proposing a single new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated recipe for converting existing pretrained AR models into dLMs that achieve 2–5× higher throughput while matching or exceeding AR accuracy, dramatically reducing the training cost barrier that has limited dLM scaling. The one-for-all flexibility (a single model serving different speed/accuracy points via confidence thresholds) is practically valuable for deployment. Key limitations include diminishing efficiency gains at large batch sizes (≥32) and the fact that the approach still requires 250–500B tokens of continuous pretraining. Open questions include whether adaptive block sizes, linear attention hybrids, or more sophisticated masking schedules can further close the gap with AR models in memory-bound serving scenarios.
