## Problem

Functional similarity (assessed via model stitching) and representational similarity (assessed via geometric metrics like CKA, RSA, Procrustes) have been studied largely in isolation, with no unified theoretical framework connecting them. The paper addresses this gap by grounding both notions in usable information theory, showing they are hierarchically related concepts governed by task granularity and the computational capacity of the predictive family used for comparison.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260073/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes two layer activation matrices Z₁ ∈ ℝ^{n×d₁} and Z₂ ∈ ℝ^{n×d₂} extracted from the same architecture family (intra-architecture only), along with task labels Y and input data X. It produces bounded [0,1] similarity scores: a directional functional similarity S_func^V (accuracy ratio of stitched vs. native head) and a directional representational similarity S_rep^V (fraction of variance explained, i.e., R²), each symmetrized by taking the minimum over both directions. The predictive family V (affine, orthogonal+isotropic scaling, or orthogonal) is an explicit design choice that determines what transformations are allowed, making the scores relative to the observer's capacity.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified information-theoretic framework where similarity is defined relative to a predictive family V. Proposition 4.2 formally shows that minimizing cross-entropy during stitcher training is equivalent to minimizing usable conditional mutual information I_V(Z₂→Y|Z₁), grounding model stitching in information theory and revealing its inherent asymmetry (a single stitcher only confirms a one-way Markov blanket). For representational similarity, under a Gaussian likelihood assumption, the usable conditional information reduces to normalized MSE (Eq. 14), showing that Procrustes, linear regression, and CKA/RSA are all estimators of usable information under specific transformation groups. The key bridging result (Corollary 4.8) establishes that representational similarity (input reconstruction, the maximum-granularity task) is sufficient but not necessary for functional similarity on any coarser deterministic task, with the hierarchy validated empirically across 12,459 layer-to-layer comparisons spanning 58 models on six vision datasets.

## Results

Across 12,459 layer-to-layer comparisons spanning 58 models (14 architectures) on six datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet), the paper validates its theoretical hierarchy. The key quantitative finding is the metric alignment: CKA achieves R²=0.937 and RSA achieves R²=0.912 correlation with the proposed usable-information-based representational similarity under Orthogonal+Scaling constraints, while SVCCA shows a weaker R²=0.650. The representational similarity hierarchy (Affine > Orthogonal+Scale > Orthogonal) is confirmed across all pairs, and coarse-task functional similarity consistently upperbounds fine-task similarity on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions CKA, RSA, and SVCCA not as competing methods but as empirical proxies for the same underlying quantity (usable conditional information) under different implicit predictive families. CKA (R²=0.937) and RSA (R²=0.912) show near-perfect alignment with the proposed Orthogonal+Scaling representational similarity, confirming they are valid estimators of usable information under unitary transformation invariance. SVCCA (R²=0.650) diverges substantially, which the authors attribute to its hard thresholding that retains only the top-k singular vectors, implicitly assuming a low-rank predictive family and discarding distributed information that the full stitcher exploits. The paper does not claim to outperform these metrics; rather, it provides a unifying theoretical interpretation that explains *why* they work and *when* they should differ, reframing them as specific instances of a general usable-information framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously disconnected threads in representation comparison: model stitching (Lenc & Vedaldi, 2015; Bansal et al., 2021) and geometric similarity metrics (CKA, RSA, SVCCA, Procrustes). It grounds both in the theory of usable information under computational constraints (Xu et al., 2020), showing that stitching loss is formally equivalent to usable conditional mutual information and that standard metrics are specific estimators of this quantity under particular predictive families. The Markov blanket formalism provides a precise definition of functional and representational similarity that prior work treated only informally, and the task-granularity hierarchy (Proposition 3.8, Corollary 3.10) is a new theoretical bridge connecting the two notions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework resolves a long-standing ambiguity in the representation comparison literature by showing that 'similarity' is inherently relative to the observer's computational capacity—representations that appear distinct under rigid (orthogonal) constraints may be identical under more expressive (affine) ones. The empirical finding that stitching is frequently asymmetric across 12,459 layer-pair comparisons has direct practical implications: the common practice of one-directional stitching is insufficient for establishing functional similarity. Key limitations include restriction to supervised vision models, intra-architecture comparisons only, deterministic encoder assumptions, and no analysis of transformer architectures; extending the framework to attention-based models and cross-architecture settings remains an open question.
