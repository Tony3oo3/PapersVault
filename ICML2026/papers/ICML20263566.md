## Problem

Existing continual learning research studies class-incremental shifts, domain drift, and label scarcity in isolation, yet in practical dense prediction systems these nonstationarities arise simultaneously. This paper formalizes continual semantic segmentation under coupled class, domain, and supervision shifts (joint nonstationarity) and exposes that prior methods—including foundation models like SAM—suffer catastrophic performance collapse under this realistic setting, with baselines dropping to near-zero Dice scores after just two incremental sessions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263566/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of sessions, each providing a small set of labeled samples (5–30 per class) drawn from a new or shifted distribution, plus abundant unlabeled data from the current session. Inputs are 3D CT/MRI volumes (medical benchmarks) or RGB driving images (natural scene benchmarks), and the model outputs pixel-wise segmentation masks over all classes seen so far. A key structural constraint is that previously observed class-domain pairs never recur, and the base session provides abundant supervision while all subsequent sessions are few-shot.

## Method

JASCL combines two mechanisms: (1) Gradient-Adaptive Stabilization (GAS), a parameter-wise regularization that injects stochastic perturbations into classifier weights scaled inversely to squared gradients—parameters actively contributing to learning receive minimal noise while potentially overfitting parameters receive higher noise—formally grounded in Bayesian posterior approximation and PAC-Bayes generalization bounds; and (2) Prototype Anchored Supervision (PAS), which computes per-class feature-space prototypes from labeled data and validates pseudo-labels from a mean-teacher framework using a joint criterion of prediction confidence and cosine similarity to the prototype, suppressing error propagation under domain shift. Training minimizes cross-entropy on labeled data plus a consistency loss restricted to PAS-validated pseudo-labels, with GAS applied to the decoder's pixel classifier. The approach is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated across U-Net, DeepLabv3+, MedFormer, SwinUNetr, and SAM, and adds negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On the 3-session Med JASCL-Disjoint benchmark (Dice score), JASCL (U-Net) achieves 0.736 → 0.460 → 0.398, while the U-Net Vanilla baseline collapses to 0.700 → 0.076 → 0.057 and the best prior method (SoftNet) reaches only 0.820 → 0.305 → 0.146. On the 6-session extension, JASCL sustains 0.324 at Session 5 versus 0.042 for Vanilla. On Natural-JASCL (mIoU), JASCL(SAM) improves over SAM Vanilla from 32.6→30.81 to 33.2→31.22, and on Med Semi-Supervised-JASCL, JASCL(U-Net) reaches 0.554 at Session 1 versus 0.082 for UaD-CE and 0.048 for NNCSL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JASCL (U-Net) decisively outperforms all 28 baselines on Med JASCL-Disjoint, achieving 0.398 Dice at Session 2 versus the next-best C-FSCIL (0.297) and GAPS (0.253), with most methods collapsing below 0.15. The gap is most dramatic against replay-based (Gen-Replay: 0.102), prototype-based (PIFS: 0.078, FeCAM: 0.042), and meta-learning (MAML: 0.059) methods, which suffer near-total forgetting. On Natural-JASCL, JASCL(SAM) marginally improves over SAM Vanilla (31.22 vs 30.81 mIoU at Session 2), while GAPS achieves 16.68 and MiB only 2.37. On Med JASCL-Mixed, JASCL(MedFormer) reaches 0.228 at Session 4 versus 0.067 for MedFormer Vanilla and 0.089 for CLIP-driven. In the semi-supervised setting, JASCL(U-Net) on Med SS-JASCL achieves 0.554 at Session 1, far exceeding UaD-CE (0.082) and NNCSL (0.048), and JASCL+GAPS on SS-Natural-JASCL reaches 25.47 mIoU at Session 3 versus 14.45 for GAPS alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant continual learning paradigm that studies class-incremental, domain-incremental, and few-shot shifts in isolation, instead formalizing their simultaneous occurrence as 'joint nonstationarity' in dense prediction. Methodologically, it builds on the mean-teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) for its pseudo-labeling pipeline, but introduces two novel mechanisms: gradient-adaptive stabilization (GAS), a parameter-wise regularization via gradient-scaled stochastic perturbations grounded in Fisher information and PAC-Bayes theory, and prototype-anchored supervision (PAS), which validates pseudo-labels through joint confidence and feature-space prototype consistency. The five self-constructed benchmarks (3D medical and 2D driving) are positioned as the first to stress-test models under coupled class, domain, and supervision shifts simultaneously, filling a gap the authors identify in prior continual segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental failure mode: nearly all existing continual segmentation methods, including the billion-mask-pretrained SAM, collapse to near-zero performance within two sessions under joint nonstationarity, revealing that prior methods were never validated under realistic coupled shifts. However, JASCL itself still suffers severe drops (e.g., 0.329→0.025 at the CT-to-MRI transition), and the authors acknowledge that extreme domain shifts, small-structure segmentation, and noisy unlabeled data remain largely unsolved. The self-constructed benchmarks are a useful community resource, but the problem is far from resolved, and the strong theoretical assumptions (gradient-Fisher correspondence, bounded Fisher ratios) limit the practical generality of the guarantees. Open questions include whether the joint-shift formulation generalizes beyond the specific medical/driving domains tested and whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or video segmentation settings.
