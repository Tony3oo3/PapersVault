## Problem

Evaluating generative AI models is increasingly unsustainable: full benchmark runs cost thousands of dollars and days of compute, while naive downsampling yields inaccurate estimates and misses rare but critical failure cases. Existing efficient evaluation methods (benchmark pruning, surrogate-guided active testing) lack model-specific adaptability, and failure discovery approaches (automated red teaming) are sample-inefficient or collapse into single failure modes. No unified framework simultaneously achieves sample-efficient performance estimation and diverse, proactive failure discovery under a tight evaluation budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical per-question scores from N previously evaluated models on shared benchmarks (or, for new benchmarks with no historical scores, raw text/image prompts processed through a frozen text-embedding-3-large encoder) to construct a GP prior, then actively queries the target model on a small set of selected inputs. It produces a scalar performance estimate with an analytical posterior variance via Bayesian quadrature, a set of actively selected test inputs, and LLM-synthesized failure cases organized by semantic topic. A 'rounded' BQ variant exploits the binary {0,1} nature of correctness scores by thresholding posterior means before summation, and a GMM-based abstention rule halts estimation when fewer than three source models share the target's behavioral cluster.

## Method

PROEVAL places a pre-trained Gaussian Process prior over the unknown performance score function f(x), constructed either from empirical score covariance across historical models (yielding a linear kernel that exactly reconstructs the sample covariance) or from learned embeddings via an MLP on frozen text/image encoders with a Matérn kernel. Performance estimation is formulated as Bayesian quadrature: the integral ∫f(x)p(x)dx inherits a posterior distribution from the GP, enabling analytical computation of estimator variance and greedy active selection of inputs that maximize posterior variance reduction. Failure discovery uses superlevel set sampling with an acquisition function balancing exploitation (high predicted failure probability) and exploration (high posterior uncertainty), extended by LLM-based synthesis that transposes failure patterns from identified 'hard' anchor examples into diverse semantic topics selected via UCB1 multi-armed bandit over BERTopic clusters. A GMM clustering step over PCA-projected model score profiles selects relevant source models to prevent negative transfer. Theoretically, the pre-trained GP-based BQ estimator is proven unbiased and bounded without assuming a known GP prior, with error scaling as O(1/√N) in the number of historical models.

## Results

On performance estimation (MAE at 1% budget, Gemini 2.5 Flash), BQ-SF achieves the lowest error across all 9 benchmarks, ranging from 0.002 (ToxicChat, DIVE) to 0.067 (DICES), compared to 0.034–0.108 for the best active-testing baseline (RF+LURE) and 0.040–0.108 for random sampling. The paper reports that PROEVAL requires 8–65× fewer samples than baselines to reach ±1% MAE (Figure 5). For failure discovery, TSS-RPF achieves 26–31% failure rates with 99% topic entropy and 0.73–0.74 overall diversity, outperforming random generation (5–7% failure rate) by 2–5× in cumulative failures discovered over 100 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROEVAL is compared against four active-testing baselines derived from Kossen et al. (2021, ICML) and Kossen et al. (2022, NeurIPS), which use Logistic Regression (LR) or Random Forest (RF) surrogates with Importance Sampling (IS) or LURE weighting to correct for selection bias. BQ-SF (the score-feature variant with active selection) dominates all four baselines on every one of the 9 benchmarks, achieving 2–30× lower MAE. For example, on MMLU, BQ-SF reaches 0.006 MAE versus 0.072–0.127 for the Kossen baselines; on SVAMP, 0.005 versus 0.041–0.094. The LURE-weighted variants (Kossen 2022) are generally stronger than the IS variants (Kossen 2021) but still fall well short of BQ-SF. Notably, BQ-SF also outperforms random sampling by 3–32×, confirming that the transfer-learned GP prior plus variance-minimizing acquisition is the primary driver of efficiency rather than active selection alone. In the stricter New Model / New Bench scenarios where score features are unavailable, BQ-TPF and BQ-RPF still beat random sampling on most datasets, though with larger margins than in the Default setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROEVAL unifies two previously disjoint lines of work—Bayesian quadrature for performance estimation (Osborne et al., 2012; adapted from scientific computing) and Bayesian level set estimation for failure discovery (Gotovos et al., 2013)—under a single Gaussian process framework enhanced by transfer learning from pre-trained GPs (Wang et al., 2024). It departs from active testing baselines (Kossen et al., 2021, 2022) that rely on importance sampling with surrogate models by instead minimizing posterior variance of the integral directly, and from static benchmark pruning (TinyBenchmarks, MetaBench) by being target-adaptive and capable of synthesizing novel test cases. The topic-aware synthesis strategy (TSS) borrows UCB1 bandit logic (Auer et al., 2002) to decouple semantic topic from failure pattern, addressing a mode-collapse limitation of prior LLM-based red teaming (PAIR, TAP, Rainbow Teaming).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8–65× sample reduction for reaching ±1% estimation error is practically transformative for teams running frequent evaluations across many models and benchmarks, where even a 10× saving translates to thousands of dollars and days of compute. The first theoretical guarantee that BQ with pre-trained GPs is unbiased and bounded without assuming a known GP prior provides a foundation for future work on certified evaluation. Key open questions include reducing reliance on high-quality pre-trained embeddings (which are a bottleneck for non-text modalities), handling heterogeneous rater costs, and extending the framework to multi-turn or agentic evaluation settings where the input space is sequential rather than static.
