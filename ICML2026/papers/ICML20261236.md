## Problem

While knowledge editing is well-studied for text-only language models, it remains unclear whether parameter edits that successfully modify textual completions in unified multimodal models (UMMs) also propagate to image generation within the same shared backbone. This paper addresses that gap by asking: if a UMM is edited to assert a new fact in text, will it generate images consistent with that updated knowledge?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261236/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a statement-completion edit triple (q, y, y′) with an answer-neutral image prompt that depicts the subject without revealing either the original or edited value, plus a VQA query for automated visual verification. Under the reasoning-augmented protocol, the edited model first produces a category-conditioned textual rationale that is concatenated with the image prompt as additional conditioning before generation. The benchmark comprises 2,971 edit subjects (964 attribute edits across four progressive compositional stages and 2,007 relation edits) yielding 5,535 evaluation instances, with visual correctness judged as a binary decision by Qwen3-VL-235B.

## Method

The paper introduces UNIKE, the first cross-modality knowledge editing benchmark for UMMs, and evaluates three parameter-editing methods (MEMIT, PMET, AlphaEdit) on three architecturally distinct UMMs (Ovis-U1, BLIP3o-4B, OmniGen2) under both direct and reasoning-augmented generation protocols. The central finding is a large modality gap: text-side efficacy reaches ~92% while the best direct VQA accuracy is only 18.5%, revealing that edit-induced perturbations sufficient to alter next-token distributions are not reliably preserved in the representations that condition the diffusion transformer. To mitigate this, the paper proposes Reasoning-augmented Parameter Editing, which elicits an intermediate textual rationale from the edited model before image generation, converting a latent parameter change into an explicit textual constraint that produces a stronger and qualitatively different conditioning shift at the DiT input (up to 8.56× amplification for Ovis-U1). Mechanistic SVD analysis of Ovis-U1's frozen 4096→1536 projection shows that MLP-based edit perturbations are broadly distributed across the input space, retaining only ~35% of signal energy through the projection, establishing a fixed architectural bottleneck independent of the editing algorithm. For models with shared visual backbones, a softened AlphaEdit null-space projector (interpolating with identity at α=0.6–0.7) prevents the standard projector from overly constraining updates in layers that serve both text and visual conditioning.

## Results

Under the DIRECT protocol, text-side efficacy reaches up to 91.9% (OmniGen2 + AlphaEdit* on relation edits), yet the best overall VQA accuracy is only 18.5% (BLIP3o-4B + PMET), revealing a stark modality gap where visual verification retains roughly one-eighth to one-quarter of textual success. Reasoning-augmented generation improves overall VQA accuracy for every model–editor pair, with the largest absolute gain of +18.6 pp on Ovis-U1 (PMET: 9.7% → 28.3%). Across all settings, VQA accuracy remains far below reasoning accuracy, confirming that even successfully verbalized edited knowledge is only partially converted into visually verifiable generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three parameter-editing baselines—MEMIT, PMET, and AlphaEdit—across three UMMs under both DIRECT and REASONING-AUGMENTED protocols. PMET consistently achieves the highest text-side efficacy and VQA accuracy among the three editors on Ovis-U1 (72.2% eff., 28.3% VQA under RA) and BLIP3o-4B (76.3% eff., 19.3% VQA under RA), while AlphaEdit* (with a softened null-space projector) edges out PMET on OmniGen2 (17.9% vs. 16.0% VQA under RA). MEMIT is the weakest editor across all models, particularly on attribute edits where its text-side efficacy drops to 34–52%. The key comparison is not between editors but between protocols: reasoning augmentation improves VQA accuracy for every model–editor pair, with gains ranging from +1.2 pp (BLIP3o-4B + MEMIT) to +18.6 pp (Ovis-U1 + PMET), confirming that the bottleneck is not the editing algorithm per se but the conditioning pathway through which edited knowledge reaches the image generator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge editing literature (ROME, MEMIT, PMET, AlphaEdit) from text-only evaluation to a cross-modality setting in unified multimodal models. Prior editing benchmarks (ZsRE, CounterFact, MQuAKE) measure text-side efficacy, and TMKE tests cross-modal consistency via image-conditioned text answering (I2T), but none test whether a text-side edit propagates to image generation (T2I) in a monolithic UMM. The paper also departs from T2I editing methods (TIME, ReFACT, DiffQuickFix) that target modular architectures with separate text encoders, instead studying edits applied to the shared backbone of unified systems. The reasoning-augmented protocol is a novel intervention that leverages the model's own verbalization to bridge the text-to-visual conditioning gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-side edit efficacy (~92%) collapses to ~18.5% VQA accuracy in image generation reveals a fundamental limitation of current editing methods for deployed UMMs: a model can verbally assert updated knowledge while generating images that contradict it. The conditioning-pathway bottleneck (frozen projections, learned query compression) provides a concrete architectural explanation, suggesting that future editing methods must explicitly target modality-relevant generation pathways rather than relying on shared-backbone propagation. Open questions include whether editing the DiT or projection layers directly could close the gap, whether the modality gap persists as UMMs scale, and whether the reasoning-augmented protocol generalizes to more complex multi-fact edits.
