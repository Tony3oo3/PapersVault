## Problem

Current chemical LLMs force continuous physicochemical reasoning (e.g., electron delocalization, steric hindrance, property optimization) into discrete natural-language tokens via explicit Chain-of-Thought, creating a fundamental 'modality mismatch' that imposes an artificial bottleneck. It remains unclear whether this linguistic discretization is optimal, or whether chemical logic is more naturally realized in a continuous latent space that mirrors the smooth, high-dimensional structure of the chemical property landscape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265521/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular SMILES string (encoded by a frozen SMI-TED encoder into variable-length dense features) and a text instruction as input. The molecular features are compressed via a Perceiver Resampler into 128 fixed-length 'ChemTokens' prepended to the instruction. During reasoning, the model generates a sequence of continuous latent thought vectors (not text tokens) interleaved with dynamic re-queries of the molecular representation, and ultimately decodes an explicit answer (optimized SMILES, reaction product, molecular description, or structural analysis) in a structured XML format. A key behavioral detail is that under RL optimization the model spontaneously collapses most reasoning into the latent phase, outputting only a trivial transition token before the final answer.

## Method

LatentChem introduces a latent thinking loop between perception and generation on a Qwen-3-8B backbone: at each step the LLM emits a hidden state that is simultaneously (1) projected by a residual FFN 'latent projector' to form the next input vector, and (2) used as a query in a 'ChemUpdater' cross-attention layer that dynamically re-weights the molecular ChemTokens based on accumulated reasoning history. This dual-pathway design distinguishes LatentChem from prior latent-reasoning work (e.g., Coconut) by making molecular perception active rather than static. Training follows a four-stage progressive protocol: answer-only alignment with a counterfactual hinge loss, full CoT SFT, latent-module activation under a frozen backbone, and finally GRPO with outcome-only rewards (format, validity, correctness) that triggers the emergent 'spontaneous internalization' where the model voluntarily discards verbose textual reasoning in favor of compact latent computation, achieving a 59.88% non-tie win rate over the explicit-CoT baseline and a 10.84× reduction in reasoning-step overhead.

## Results

LatentChem achieves a 59.88% non-tie win rate against the explicit CoT baseline (Stage 1+2+4) on ChemCoTBench, 85.26% on ChEBI-20, and 55.58% on ChemLLMBench, while maintaining competitive parity (49.88%) on Mol-Instructions. On open-ended molecule optimization, LatentChem reaches 96% success rate on LogP (Δ=1.37) and 82% on GSK3-β (Δ=0.17), substantially outperforming the CoT baseline (77% and 67% respectively). The method also delivers a 10.84× average reduction in reasoning step overhead (up to 29.9× on reaction tasks) with a measured 5.96× wall-clock speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the text-only Qwen-3-8B baseline (Yang et al., 2025), LatentChem dominates across all benchmarks: 59.88% vs. 8.61% non-tie win rate on ChemCoTBench, 49.88% vs. 23.97% on Mol-Instructions, and 85.26% vs. 74.86% on ChEBI-20, confirming that molecular-aware latent reasoning far exceeds pure text generation. Against Coconut-Chem (Hao et al., 2025), which adapts the generic Coconut latent-reasoning paradigm to the same SMI-TED encoder and Chemical Adapter, LatentChem wins on every benchmark: 59.88% vs. 32.11% on ChemCoTBench, 49.88% vs. 40.57% on Mol-Instructions, 55.58% vs. 44.82% on ChemLLMBench, and 85.26% vs. 65.58% on ChEBI-20. The gap is largest on ChemCoTBench (27.77 percentage points), where the dynamic ChemUpdater's ability to re-query molecular features during latent reasoning provides a clear advantage over Coconut's static molecular context. On Mol-Instructions, the margin narrows (9.31 pp), consistent with the paper's observation that closed-ended deterministic tasks saturate the benefits of creative latent exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Coconut paradigm (Hao et al., 2025c) of continuous latent-space reasoning to chemistry, but departs from it in a critical architectural way: whereas Coconut treats molecular embeddings as a static context, LatentChem introduces the ChemUpdater mechanism that allows latent thoughts to dynamically re-query and refine the molecular representation at each reasoning step. The training protocol builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for outcome-only reinforcement learning and on the SMI-TED molecular encoder with a Perceiver Resampler adapter for modality alignment. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant explicit-CoT paradigm in chemical LLMs, arguing that forcing continuous physicochemical logic into discrete tokens creates a 'modality mismatch' that a latent interface can resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in scientific LLMs—that natural language CoT is the necessary computational substrate for domain reasoning—by showing that a model, when granted the option, spontaneously abandons textual derivations for latent computation while *improving* accuracy and achieving ~11× step-overhead reduction. The interpretability trade-off is a genuine open problem: internalized reasoning is opaque to human auditors, complicating scientific verification and trust. The authors propose hybrid System 1/System 2 architectures as a path forward, but the broader question of when and how to expose latent computations to human inspection remains unresolved. Generalizability beyond chemistry and beyond the Qwen-3 backbone family is also untested.
