## Problem

Expressive offline RL methods that combine flow-matching policies with distributional critics achieve strong performance but at prohibitive computational cost: flow policies require iterative ODE solving to produce a single action, and distributional critics must process multiple quantile samples per update. The paper asks whether these expressivity mechanisms can be retained while reducing both training and inference to near-single-step computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy π_ω(s, ε) takes a state observation (state vector or RGB image) and a d-dimensional Gaussian noise vector ε ~ N(0, I_d) to produce a single action in one forward pass. The noise-conditioned critic Q_φ(s, a, ε) is evaluated at the same noise dimension, so a single ε sample simultaneously parameterizes the action and conditions the return-distribution estimate. The upper expectile network Z_ψ(s, a) is noise-free, collapsing the distribution to a scalar for the Bellman target.

## Method

FAN introduces two mechanisms that eliminate iterative computation. First, Flow Anchoring replaces ODE-based behavior sampling with a velocity-matching loss: the one-step policy's implicit velocity (π_ω(s,ε) − ε) is regressed onto the flow field v_θ(s, t, a_t) at a random time t, requiring only one flow evaluation per update and applying to both actor and critic. Second, the noise-conditioned operator T_n^π defines the Bellman backup as r + γ ess sup_ε Q(s', π(s',ε'), ε), collapsing the next-step return distribution to a single scalar via the essential supremum, which is approximated by an upper expectile Z_ψ trained with κ=0.9. This removes the need for multiple quantile samples, reduces bootstrap variance, and preserves the max-based policy improvement principle. Theoretical guarantees establish γ-contraction of T_n^π under the supremum metric, convergence of the upper expectile to the essential supremum as κ→1, and a Wasserstein-2 bound linking the Flow Anchoring loss to distributional deviation.

## Results

FAN achieves state-of-the-art performance (≥95% of best) on 7 out of 9 task environments across D4RL and OGBench. On OGBench puzzle-3x3-play, FAN reaches 100±1% success rate (vs. 87±13% for the next-best Value Flows), and on puzzle-4x4-play it achieves 42±10% (vs. 27±4% for IQN). On D4RL antmaze, FAN scores 76±4 normalized return, competitive with FQL (79±8). In terms of computational efficiency, FAN is 5–14× faster in training wall-clock time than distributional baselines (CODAC, IQN, Value Flows) and achieves the lowest inference FLOPs and runtime among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-distributional baselines, FAN substantially outperforms ReBRAC and IDQL on most OGBench manipulation tasks (e.g., puzzle-3×3: 100 vs. 22/19; puzzle-4×4: 42 vs. 14/25; antsoccer: 60 vs. 16/33), and matches or exceeds FQL on puzzle-3×3 (100 vs. 30) and puzzle-4×4 (42 vs. 17), though FQL retains a slight edge on D4RL antmaze (79 vs. 76) and Adroit (52 vs. 53, within noise). Against distributional baselines, FAN dominates on puzzle-3×3 (100 vs. 87 for Value Flows, 20 for CODAC, 15 for IQN) and puzzle-4×4 (42 vs. 27 for IQN/VF, 20 for CODAC), and wins on visual manipulation (33 vs. 30 for VF, 2 for CODAC). FAN trails Value Flows on cube-double-play (46 vs. 69) and is marginally behind on scene-play (58 vs. 59), but compensates with 5–14× lower training runtime and the fastest inference among all methods. In the offline-to-online setting (Table 2), FAN achieves SOTA on 4/5 OGBench tasks after 1M online fine-tuning steps, including 100±1% on puzzle-3×3 and 98±2% on cube-double-play.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAN sits at the intersection of two recent trends in offline RL: flow-matching-based behavior policies (FQL, Park et al. 2025) and distributional critics (CODAC, IQN, Value Flows). It departs from both by replacing their computationally expensive mechanisms—iterative ODE solving for flow sampling and multi-quantile updates—with single-iteration alternatives (Flow Anchoring and a noise-conditioned critic with upper expectile regression). The theoretical grounding draws on the BRAC framework (Wu et al. 2019; Tarasov et al. 2023) for behavior regularization and on the Wasserstein contraction properties of distributional Bellman operators (Bellemare et al. 2017), extending them to the noise-conditioned setting via a supremum-metric contraction proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FAN achieves 5–14× faster training than distributional baselines and the fastest inference among all tested methods while matching or exceeding SOTA on D4RL and OGBench, making expressive offline RL feasible on resource-constrained hardware. The conceptual takeaway—that a single flow iteration and a single noise sample suffice to capture the benefits of full ODE solving and multi-quantile distributional critics—challenges the prevailing assumption that more sampling steps or more quantiles are necessary. Open questions include whether Flow Anchoring transfers to online or model-based settings, whether the upper-expectile approximation (κ=0.9) can be tightened for risk-sensitive tasks, and whether the ess sup operator generalizes beyond deterministic transitions.
