## Problem

Falck et al. (2024) demonstrated that LLMs violate the martingale property of predictive beliefs on synthetic in-context learning tasks, casting doubt on the Bayesian interpretation of LLMs. However, it remained unclear whether this violation persists in the most common practical usage regime—repeatedly answering a fixed query—where the discrete answer space allows exact computation of predictive distributions. This paper asks whether the observed drift is a permanent structural failure or a transient phenomenon that can be mitigated.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multiple-choice question (4–5 options) as input, optionally augmented with a short prefix of i.i.d. seed answers sampled from the model's own distribution. The output is a sequence of single answer tokens (one per line, newline-delimited) generated autoregressively, from which the exact predictive distribution over the answer simplex is read off directly from model logits at each step. The final deliverable is a martingale posterior—a probability distribution over the simplex representing the model's stabilized belief—estimated either via long-rollout empirical frequencies (PPR) or the immediate next-token distribution (AM-PPR).

## Method

The paper introduces Prompted Predictive Resampling (PPR), a prompting protocol that instructs the LLM to generate a structured sequence of answers to the same question, enabling direct observation of belief dynamics over the discrete answer simplex. Empirically, PPR reveals an initial drift phase (≈5 steps) violating the martingale property, followed by spontaneous self-stabilization analogous to MCMC burn-in. To accelerate stabilization, the authors propose answer seeding, which prepends a few i.i.d. samples from the model's own distribution to the prompt, providing a 'mean-field' preview of future answers. To eliminate the need for long rollouts at inference time, they introduce a Self-Consistency (SC) loss that fine-tunes the model (via LoRA) to match its early-horizon predictive distributions to its late-horizon ones, effectively amortizing the burn-in phase into the weights. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that minimizing the SC loss bounds the martingale residual by O(√ε), guaranteeing approximate c.i.d. behavior and hence an approximate martingale posterior for both seen and unseen queries.

## Results

On LLAMA-3.1-8B, AM-PPR+SEED5 achieves 62.2% accuracy and 85.1% AUC on TinyMMLU, 80.5% accuracy and 94.2% AUC on AI2-ARC, and 77.0% accuracy and 94.2% AUC on CSQA, matching or exceeding Direct-Query accuracy (61.2/80.5/78.0%) while providing a proper martingale posterior. Compared to unseeded PPR (46.0/62.6/50.0% accuracy), the amortization + seeding combination yields a 16–27 point accuracy gain. On calibration, AM-PPR+SEED5 achieves the lowest ECE on AI2-ARC (0.0845) and CSQA (0.0674) and the lowest Brier Score on AI2-ARC (0.2872), substantially outperforming PPR+NoSEED (ECE 0.1623/0.1273, BS 0.5222/0.6430) and Direct-Query (ECE 0.1251/0.1072, BS 0.2978/0.3453).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four PPR variants (no seed, 1 seed, 5 seeds) and two AM-PPR variants against a Direct-Query baseline. AM-PPR+SEED5 matches Direct-Query accuracy on AI2-ARC (80.5%) and is within 1 point on CSQA (77.0 vs 78.0%), while exceeding it on TinyMMLU (62.2 vs 61.2) and achieving higher AUC across all three benchmarks (85.1/94.2/94.2 vs 81.6/93.1/91.9). The unseeded PPR baseline suffers severe accuracy degradation (46.0% on TinyMMLU, 50.0% on CSQA) due to early-stage belief drift, which both seeding and amortization correct. On calibration metrics, AM-PPR+SEED5 achieves the best ECE on AI2-ARC (0.0845 vs 0.1251 for Direct-Query) and CSQA (0.0674 vs 0.1072), and the best Brier Score on AI2-ARC (0.2872 vs 0.2978), confirming that the martingale-amortized posterior yields better-calibrated uncertainty without sacrificing accuracy. The paper notes that the martingale property is a necessary but not sufficient condition for full Bayesian calibration, as PPR variants show that reducing drift does not monotonically improve all calibration metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the martingale-coherence framework of Falck et al. (2024), who demonstrated permanent martingale violations in LLMs on synthetic Bernoulli/Normal ICL tasks, to the practical regime of discrete multiple-choice question answering where exact predictive distributions are directly accessible from logits. It also builds on the martingale posterior theory of Fong et al. (2023), using the c.i.d./martingale equivalence as the formal target for a new self-consistency (SC) fine-tuning loss. The key conceptual departure is reframing martingale violations as *transient* (self-stabilizing after a burn-in phase) rather than structural, and proposing both an inference-time seeding trick and a training-time amortization objective to collapse the convergence horizon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLM belief drift is transient in practical MCQA settings reframes the martingale-violation literature: the violation is not a fundamental defect but a short-lived initialization artifact, which opens the door to lightweight fixes (seeding, SC fine-tuning) rather than architectural changes. However, the paper itself cautions that martingale coherence is only a *necessary* condition for Bayesian calibration, and indeed the PPR variants show non-monotonic calibration improvements, leaving open whether additional structure is needed to align stabilized beliefs with ground truth. The extension to continuous embedding spaces (GSM8K, GPQA) is only preliminary, and the practical scope remains limited to discrete-answer tasks with 7–8B models, so generalization to open-ended reasoning and larger architectures is a significant open question.
