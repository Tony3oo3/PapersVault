## Problem

Existing model-based safe RL methods handle the reward-cost balance through linear penalties or Lagrangian multipliers, which impose a single global exchange rate and provide limited guidance when safety and performance interact in complex, state-dependent nonlinear ways. The paper asks whether a structured, self-supervised preference ordering over imagined trajectories can be learned directly from experience—without human preference labels—and embedded into the world model to enable flexible multi-step planning under safety constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264786/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB observations from forward and rear cameras (as in SafeDreamer's Safety-Gymnasium setup) and outputs a continuous control action at each step. Internally, the world model encodes observations into an RSSM latent state (deterministic hidden state plus 48-class discrete stochastic latent), and the planner evaluates N=500 candidate action sequences over a 15-step horizon, selecting the first action of the score-best trajectory that satisfies a cost budget B=2.0. The learned score is a scalar per-step preference signal distilled into the world model, not a separate reward or penalty term.

## Method

USB-RL introduces a three-stage score pipeline on top of a DreamerV3/SafeDreamer RSSM world model. First, a structurally monotone ScoreNet (teacher) is trained via self-supervised pairwise dominance and tie losses constructed from long-horizon TD(λ) reward/cost returns, with within-root pair construction among stochastic continuations sharing the same imagination root; monotonicity is enforced architecturally through non-negative-weight branches for reward and cost inputs. Second, the teacher's outputs are distilled into a native world-model score head (heteroscedastic Gaussian) trained on stop-gradient latents with an EMA teacher, so the score is available at zero extra forward-pass cost during imagination rollouts. Third, a ScoreCritic temporalizes per-step scores via TD(λ) into a long-horizon value signal, which shapes the actor with a small advantage-based term and ranks cost-feasible candidates in an OSRP-style planner that enforces strict cost-budget gating before score-based selection.

## Results

On Safety-Gymnasium (PointGoal2, PointButton1, PointPush1, CarGoal1), USB-RL consistently achieves higher asymptotic episode reward while driving episode cost substantially below all baselines, often approaching near-zero violations. On MetaDrive (4×10⁶ env steps), USB-RL attains the highest arrival rate and the lowest episode cost return among all methods. The score manifold analysis (Fig. 6) confirms the learned score forms a curved, non-planar surface in (score, reward, cost) space, indicating non-linear state-dependent trade-offs beyond a fixed linear penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USB-RL outperforms all three baselines on both reward and cost across all four Safety-Gymnasium tasks. Against SafeDreamer (the closest model-based competitor using OSRP planning with Lagrangian cost penalties), USB-RL achieves higher reward on PointGoal2, PointButton1, and CarGoal1 while reducing cost to near-zero; on PointPush1 it matches SafeDreamer's reward at lower cost. Against LAMBDA (Bayesian world-model + Lagrangian), USB-RL achieves comparable or higher reward but substantially lower cost, particularly on PointPush1 where LAMBDA reaches high reward at noticeably higher violation rates. Against PPO-Lag (model-free CMDP baseline), USB-RL dominates on both metrics across all tasks, reflecting the advantage of imagination-based planning with a structured score. On MetaDrive, USB-RL also outperforms PPO-RS (reward-shaping variant) in both arrival rate and episode cost, confirming that the learned score generalizes to realistic driving scenes with sparse binary safety costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

USB-RL builds directly on the DreamerV3 RSSM world model and the SafeDreamer framework (OSRP planning with cost critics and Lagrangian updates), positioning itself as a drop-in augmentation that replaces the hand-tuned linear reward-cost penalty with a learned, structurally monotone preference signal. The key departure is the self-supervised pairwise dominance/tie learning of a monotone ScoreNet, which is then distilled into the world model as a native score head and temporalized by a ScoreCritic—components absent from prior model-based safe RL methods that rely on fixed Lagrangian multipliers or PID controllers for reward-cost balancing. Conceptually, the work bridges preference learning (typically requiring human labels) and model-based safe RL by constructing the preference signal entirely from the agent's own long-horizon reward-cost outcomes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in model-based safe RL: the sensitivity of performance to manually tuned cost weights, and offers a principled alternative that adapts to state-dependent trade-offs. However, it provides no formal safety guarantees—safety depends entirely on world-model accuracy and finite-horizon planning—and the distilled student head is only an empirical surrogate of the teacher's monotonicity, not a formally monotone function over latent coordinates. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-constraint CMDPs, how it behaves under significant model misspecification or distribution shift, and whether the within-root pair construction generalizes to settings with very sparse cost signals. The ~2× wall-clock speedup from distillation (vs. querying the external ScoreNet at planning time) is a practical bonus that makes the method more deployable.
