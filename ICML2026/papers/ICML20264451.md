## Problem

Feature-map knowledge distillation reliably transfers representations between comparably-sized Vision Transformers but often fails or even hurts accuracy when compressing a wide teacher into a narrow student. Prior work has observed this brittleness empirically but lacks a mechanistic explanation for why the naive recipe breaks, and no simple, general feature-map-based distillation scheme exists for the wide-to-narrow ViT compression regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264451/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K (1,000 classes) and produces class labels via a student ViT (e.g., DeiT-Tiny, 192 channels, 12 layers). During training, the student's last-layer token feature matrix (N×D_S, where N=196 patch tokens) is aligned to the teacher's (N×D_T, e.g., 384 channels) through either a fixed linear projector (Lift) or a widened final Transformer block (WideLast). At inference, the Lift projector is retained so the classification head operates on the lifted D_T-dimensional representation, while WideLast feeds the widened block output directly to the head.

## Method

The paper diagnoses the failure of naive feature-map KD as an 'encoding mismatch' with two coupled facets: (1) dataset-level subspace rotation, where per-image token matrices are low-rank (e.g., 61 dims for 95% energy in CaiT-S24) but a shared PCA subspace must be nearly full-width (302/384 dims) to cover 99% of images, and (2) high per-token channel bandwidth, revealed by a new Spectral Energy Pattern (SEP) diagnostic showing tokens spread energy across ~90% of channel modes to capture 90% of their energy, an architecture-invariant law. Two minimal remedies address this: Lift attaches a lightweight D_S→D_T linear projector after the student's last layer and retains it at inference, supplying near-teacher endpoint width with a fixed interface; WideLast replaces only the student's final Transformer block with one at teacher width D_T, providing both near-teacher bandwidth and an input-dependent mapping that can adapt to rotating subspaces. Both are trained with a standard objective combining cross-entropy, optional logit KD, and a feature loss (MSE or SpectralKD), and both reactivate simple feature alignment, improving DeiT-Tiny distilled from CaiT-S24 from 74.86% to 77.53%/78.23% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, distilling a CaiT-S24 teacher (384-dim) into a DeiT-Tiny student (192-dim), naive feature KD (SpectralKD) yields only +0.21% top-1 accuracy (75.07% vs. 74.86% baseline). The proposed Lift strategy (post-hoc 192→384 projector retained at inference) improves top-1 to 77.53% with SoftKD+SpecKD (+2.67%), while WideLast (widening only the final Transformer block to 384-dim) reaches 78.23% with SoftKD+MSE (+3.37%). Both strategies also improve the standalone student without any teacher: Lift gives +0.55% and WideLast gives +0.68% over the 74.86% baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is naive feature-map KD (SpectralKD, Tian et al. 2025) applied directly to the plain DeiT-Tiny student, which achieves only 75.07% top-1 (+0.21% over the 74.86% no-KD baseline), confirming the well-known failure of straightforward feature distillation in wide-to-narrow ViT compression. The proposed Lift strategy, which adds a lightweight 192→384 linear projector retained at inference, turns the same SpectralKD signal into a reliable +1.54% gain (76.40%) and, combined with logit KD, reaches 77.53% (+2.67%). WideLast, which widens only the final Transformer block to 384-dim, further improves to 78.23% (+3.37%) with SoftKD+MSE, outperforming Lift by 0.70% because the input-dependent widened block can adapt to rotating subspaces. The generality check with a DeiT-Small teacher (Table 6) shows the same pattern: naive SpectralKD drops below baseline (74.52%), while Lift+MSE recovers to 76.22% (+1.36%) and WideLast+SpecKD reaches 75.73% (+0.87%). A parameter-matched control (Uniform219dim, widening all layers to 219-dim) underperforms WideLast both standalone (75.15% vs. 75.54%) and under SpectralKD (75.61% vs. 76.53%), confirming that endpoint-specific capacity placement matters more than total parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FitNet-style feature-map distillation lineage (Romero et al., 2015) into the ViT compression regime, where prior work (ViTKD, SpectralKD, ScaleKD, MiniViT) has largely abandoned raw feature alignment in favor of alternative signals (attention, contrastive, spectral) or heavy alignment modules. Rather than proposing yet another distillation objective, the paper provides a diagnostic framework—combining sample-wise SVD, dataset-level PCA, and a new token-level Spectral Energy Pattern (SEP) analysis—that identifies a specific 'encoding mismatch' (subspace rotation plus high per-token bandwidth) as the root cause of failure. The proposed fixes (Lift: a retained linear projector; WideLast: widening only the last block) are deliberately minimal, positioning the work as a mechanistic explanation that reactivates the simplest possible feature loss (MSE) rather than a new distillation recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing empirical puzzle—why per-image low-rank compressibility does not translate into successful narrow-student distillation—by separating three conflated notions (per-image rank, shared-subspace width, per-token bandwidth). The finding that both fixes also improve the student without any teacher (+0.55–0.68% standalone) suggests the endpoint bottleneck is a genuine architectural limitation of compact ViTs, offering a design cue beyond distillation. Open questions include whether the same dual-view analysis applies to intermediate layers, whether adaptive or input-dependent lifting can improve the accuracy-efficiency trade-off, and whether analogous mismatches persist in detection/segmentation or multimodal transformer settings. The ImageNet-21K teacher experiments also reveal that dataset/pretraining mismatch in feature-level KD remains an unsolved complication.
