## Problem

Existing search and browsing benchmarks either specify explicit search targets, are predominantly text-centric, or treat images as auxiliary rather than primary evidence. What remains missing is a systematic evaluation of whether a model can decide *whether* external search is warranted from visual cues, revise search directions as cross-image evidence accumulates, and integrate evidence across images and domains — particularly when multiple images provide overlapping or conflicting signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance provides one or more RGB images (averaging 4.62 per question) alongside an intentionally lightweight text prompt that cannot be solved by language priors or parametric recall alone. The model must produce a short answer (fewer than four words) in one of four formats — single-choice, multi-choice, open-ended, or sorting — while implicitly deciding whether to invoke web search at all. The unusual constraint is that single-image 'search-avoidance' instances penalize unnecessary browsing, making the when-to-search decision an explicit part of the evaluation signal rather than a given.

## Method

NAVIGATE is constructed via a failure-driven paradigm: search interaction logs are collected where initial VLM predictions are incorrect but correctable through human-guided query refinement, preserving real search dynamics such as path corrections and strategy variations. Multi-domain questions use a complementary fact-first strategy, starting from a predefined fact with a unique answer and collecting supporting evidence from multiple web sources and images. A three-stage filtering pipeline (difficulty gate, quality gate, final filter) reduces 1,800 candidates to 500 balanced instances across 20 domains and 3 difficulty tiers (single-image, multi-image single-domain, multi-image multi-domain). Beyond binary accuracy, the paper introduces structure-aware diagnostic metrics: set coverage (|P∩G|/|G|) for multi-choice questions to separate missing-evidence failures (high FN) from distractor-susceptibility failures (high FP), and pairwise ordering consistency for sorting questions to quantify partial structural recovery when exact ranking is not achieved.

## Results

On NAVIGATE (500 questions, 20 domains, 3 difficulty tiers), the best-performing model Gemini-3-Pro-Preview-Search achieves only 36.4% overall accuracy, with GPT-5.1-Search (34.1%) and GPT-5-Search (33.3%) close behind. Search-enabled models consistently outperform their non-search counterparts within the same family (e.g., GPT-5-Search 33.3% vs. GPT-5 27.2%; GPT-5.1-Search 34.1% vs. GPT-5.1 28.4%), confirming the critical role of external retrieval. Performance drops sharply on multi-domain composition (MDQ) for all models, with even the top model falling to ~29%, and single-image (SIQ) tasks—where search is unnecessary—reveal that several search-enabled models underperform non-search baselines due to over-searching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among search-enabled systems, Gemini-3-Pro-Preview-Search leads at 36.4% overall, followed by GPT-5.1-Search (34.1%) and GPT-5-Search (33.3%), while Perplexity-Sonar trails at 9.5%—a 27-point gap that highlights how poorly uncontrolled search degrades performance (its FP average of 5.32 vs. ~0.6–1.3 for top models confirms over-trust in irrelevant retrieved content). Within the same model family, search consistently helps: GPT-5-Search gains +6.1 pp over GPT-5, and GPT-5.1-Search gains +5.7 pp over GPT-5.1, confirming that retrieval is necessary but not sufficient. On single-image (SIQ) tasks where search is unnecessary, non-search models like GPT-5 (42.1%) and Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct (47.4%) actually outperform several search-enabled models (e.g., GPT-5.1-Search at 26.3%, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Search at 33.3%), exposing a critical over-searching failure mode. On the hardest multi-domain (MDQ) tier, all models drop substantially—Gemini-3-Pro-Preview-Search falls from 55.6% (SIQ) to 29.2% (MDQ)—and search-enabled systems maintain a consistent 5–10 pp edge over their non-search counterparts, underscoring that cross-domain evidence integration remains the dominant bottleneck. Open-source VLMs without search (Qwen2.5-VL series, InternVL2.5) cluster in the 11–16% range, well below even mid-tier search-enabled systems, indicating that parametric knowledge alone is insufficient for the long-tail, multi-image, search-grounded reasoning NAVIGATE demands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of visual question answering and web-search evaluation. Prior search benchmarks (BrowseComp, MM-BrowseComp, LiveVQA, MMSearch) are largely text-centric or treat images as auxiliary cues, and they specify explicit search targets; NAVIGATE departs by making images the primary evidence, rendering search targets implicit, and introducing the 'elective' search decision (whether to invoke search at all). Methodologically, it also departs from knowledge-centric dataset construction by adopting a failure-driven collection paradigm grounded in real search-interaction error trajectories, and it introduces structure-aware scoring metrics (set coverage, pairwise ordering consistency) that go beyond binary accuracy to diagnose search behaviors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and practically important failure mode: search-enabled models can underperform their non-search counterparts on single-image tasks because unnecessary retrieval introduces distracting evidence, meaning that 'when not to search' is as critical as 'how to search.' With the best model reaching only 36.4% and multi-domain accuracy dropping sharply across all systems, the results suggest that cross-image grounding, search triggering, and multi-source evidence integration remain substantially unsolved. Open questions include whether the 500-question scale is sufficient for stable cross-model comparisons, whether the failure-driven collection introduces distributional bias toward particular failure types, and how the benchmark's findings transfer to agentic multi-turn search settings beyond the single-turn QA formulation used here.
