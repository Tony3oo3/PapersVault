## Problem

Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) methods claim to perform visual imagination through hidden states of MLLMs, yet the causal role of these latent tokens in the reasoning pipeline remains unverified. This paper asks whether latent tokens genuinely mediate the input-to-answer process or merely act as inert placeholders, and if not, what alternative mechanism can effectively support visual imagination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a text question as input, and produces a text answer preceded by an explicit text-form imagination chain that verbalizes visual manipulations (e.g., 'zoom into the highlighted region showing a red cart with four wheels'). Unlike latent-space methods that emit continuous hidden-state tokens between text segments, CapImagine keeps the entire reasoning trajectory in discrete text tokens, making the imagination process fully inspectable and intervenable.

## Method

The paper first applies Causal Mediation Analysis (X→Z→Y) to three LVR baselines (Monet, LVR, Mirage), showing that latent tokens are highly homogeneous across instances, collapse progressively during autoregressive generation, and exert negligible causal effect on the final answer when perturbed. To replace this ineffective mechanism, CapImagine reformulates the Monet-SFT-125K interleaved multimodal training data: for each intermediate reasoning image, Qwen3-VL-4B generates a concise textual caption describing the visual manipulation (zoom, highlight, draw), and an MLLM globally refines the reasoning chain for coherence. After quality filtering (retaining 17k instances), the model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is trained via standard CoT-SFT on this text-only imagination data, achieving 4.0% and 4.9% gains over Monet on HR-Bench-8K and MME-RealWorld-Lite while maintaining comparable inference speed.

## Results

CapImagine achieves 85.9% overall on V*, 74.1% on HR-Bench-4K, 70.7% on HR-Bench-8K, and 54.8% on MME-RealWorld-Lite, outperforming the strongest latent-space baseline Monet (83.3%, 71.0%, 68.0%, 46.9%) by 2.6%, 3.1%, 2.7%, and 7.9% respectively. On TableVQA, CapImagine reaches 70.7% overall accuracy, surpassing Monet (64.8%) by 5.9 points. On BLINK, CapImagine scores 64.7 on Jigsaw and 49.6 on Multi-View, exceeding Monet by 14.7 and 2.2 points respectively. CapImagine remains slightly behind the tool-augmented DeepEyes on V* (85.9 vs. 90.0) and HR-Bench (74.1/70.7 vs. 75.1/72.6), but matches or exceeds it on MME-RealWorld-Lite (54.8 vs. 53.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary latent-space baseline Monet, CapImagine wins on every benchmark: +2.6% on V*, +3.1% on HR-Bench-4K, +2.7% on HR-Bench-8K, +7.9% on MME-RealWorld-Lite, and +14.7 on BLINK Jigsaw. Against LVR (another latent-space method), gains are larger: +4.2% on V*, +7.7% on HR-Bench-8K, and +4.2% on MME-RealWorld-Lite. CapImagine substantially outperforms the tool-based PixelReasoner (+5.3% on V*, +5.1% on MME-RealWorld-Lite) but trails the stronger tool-based DeepEyes by 4.1% on V* and 1.9% on HR-Bench-8K, while slightly exceeding it on MME-RealWorld-Lite (+1.6%). Compared to the proprietary GPT-4o, CapImagine leads by 18.4% on V* and 15.2% on HR-Bench-8K. Among text-space reasoning baselines (PAPO, Vision-R1, R1-OneVision, LLaVA-OneVision), CapImagine achieves the highest scores on V* (85.9), HR-Bench (72.4), MME-RealWorld-Lite (54.8), and BLINK (54.8), confirming that explicit text-form imagination outperforms both generic text CoT and latent-space approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical diagnostic study of the Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) paradigm, which includes methods like Monet, LVR, and Mirage that reason through hidden states of MLLMs. It builds directly on Monet's training pipeline and the Monet-SFT-125K dataset, using them as the primary subject of causal mediation analysis rather than as a baseline to beat. The paper departs from prior LVR work by applying Pearl-style causal intervention (perturbing inputs and latent tokens) and probing analysis to test whether latent tokens genuinely mediate reasoning, and it contrasts its findings against tool-augmented methods (DeepEyes, PixelReasoner) and text-space reasoning methods (PAPO, Vision-R1, R1-OneVision) to triangulate where the actual reasoning signal resides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous causal diagnostic framework that can be applied to any latent-reasoning method, and its findings—that latent tokens are highly homogeneous, minimally input-sensitive, and causally negligible for the final answer—challenge the core mechanistic assumption of the LVR paradigm and could redirect substantial research effort. The practical alternative (CapImagine) demonstrates that explicit text-space imagination outperforms latent approaches under controlled data conditions, offering a simpler and more interpretable path forward. Key open questions remain: whether entirely new training paradigms (e.g., latent-space regularization) could make latent tokens causally effective, and whether the granularity limitations of natural language ultimately cap the ceiling of text-space imagination compared to the theoretical capacity of high-dimensional latent representations.
