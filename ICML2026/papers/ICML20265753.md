## Problem

Existing symbolic regression methods learn direct input-output mappings f: X → y, neglecting relational structures among data instances that are crucial for capturing local non-parametric patterns. No prior work integrates contrastive learning with feature-construction-based symbolic regression, and standard contrastive objectives rely on binary or ordinal positive/negative relations that discard the continuous magnitude information essential for regression targets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset X ∈ R^{n×d} with continuous target y ∈ R^n, where each individual in the GP population consists of m=10 symbolic trees that construct m new features from the original d inputs. The output is a regression prediction ŷ obtained by combining a ridge regression global component with a contrastively-transformed KNN residual component, plus an ensemble of E=30 diverse symbolic feature sets. For large datasets, a subsample of S=100 instances is used to optimize the transformation matrix W, while the full dataset is used for final KNN regression.

## Method

CSR evolves m GP trees per individual to construct a feature space Φ(X), then learns a linear transformation W ∈ R^{m×m} via a closed-form ridge-regularized least-squares solution that minimizes the squared difference between pairwise feature distances and pairwise target distances, avoiding iterative gradient-based optimization. The transformed features feed a two-stage boosting learner: ridge regression captures the global linear trend, and KNN models the local residual patterns in the contrastively-aligned space. During evolution, feature quality is assessed with linear-rank weighted KNN (fixed K) to provide comparable fitness signals, while an efficient single-pass vectorized LOOCV selects the optimal K at the end of evolution. A DPP-based ensemble selection mechanism uses a kernel L_{ij} = q_i q_j cos(R_i, R_j) combining normalized fitness and cosine similarity of residual semantics to greedily select E diverse, high-quality individuals for the final ensemble.

## Results

On 58 real-world regression datasets from PMLB/SRBench, CSR (SR^All) achieves a normalized median R² of 0.8884 (95% CI: [0.7787, 0.9547]), the highest among all 12 symbolic regression variants tested. The full CSR framework outperforms the strongest prior SR variant (SR^LR+DT at 0.7628) by over 12 percentage points in normalized median R². In the broader SRBench comparison against 27 algorithms (including XGBoost, LightGBM, RealMLP, and ModernNCA), CSR achieves the highest overall R² score with comparable training time and a smaller model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSR (SR^All) outperforms all symbolic regression baselines: it beats SR^LR (0.6602) by 22.8 points, SR^LR+DT (0.7628) by 12.6 points, and SR^KNN (0.4613) by 42.7 points in normalized median R². Against modern ML baselines in the SRBench comparison (Figure 4), CSR achieves higher R² than RealMLP, MLP-PLR, ModernNCA, XGBoost, and LightGBM while maintaining comparable training time and a smaller model size. Compared with the closest SR competitor RAG-SR, CSR attains higher R² with comparable training time, indicating that contrastive representation learning yields stronger generalization than error-driven feature construction. The paper reports that CSR's superiority over all other algorithms is statistically significant under Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction (Figure 11). Ensemble selection via DPP further improves robustness over top-T selection and RBF-based DPP, with gains observed on both small and large datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-construction-based symbolic regression paradigm (La Cava & Moore, 2020; Cava et al., 2019), where GP evolves composite features for a downstream learner, by introducing a contrastive alignment step that reshapes the feature geometry to reflect continuous target-space distances. Unlike prior contrastive learning formulations that rely on binary or ordinal positive/negative pairs (Chen et al., 2020; Baek et al., 2024), CSR directly matches pairwise target distances and derives a closed-form ridge-regularized solution for the transformation matrix, avoiding iterative gradient optimization within the evolutionary loop. The DPP-based ensemble selection (Chen et al., 2018) and EQL-based initialization (Zhang et al., 2023c) are repurposed from recommendation diversity and neural symbolic regression, respectively, into the GP feature-construction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing symbolic regression as a representation-learning problem rather than a direct f: X→y mapping, CSR opens a path toward instance-level interpretability (KNN retrieval over symbolic features) while matching or exceeding the accuracy of gradient-boosted trees and deep tabular models on 58 real-world datasets. Key limitations include the restriction to single-modality tabular regression, reliance on Euclidean distance (closed-form solutions for other metrics are non-trivial), and quadratic KNN cost that limits scalability. Open questions include extending to multi-target or multi-modal settings, exploring non-Euclidean contrastive objectives, and understanding when the contrastive alignment provides the largest gains over simpler linear projections.
