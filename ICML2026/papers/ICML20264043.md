## Problem

Existing time-series dataset condensation methods are either vision-centric, failing to capture temporal dynamics, or tightly coupled to a single downstream task (e.g., forecasting or classification), causing feature mismatch that prevents generalization across diverse time-series analysis tasks. This paper asks whether a single unified condensation framework can preserve the underlying generative mechanisms of time series—temporal evolution, spectral periodicity, and multivariate coupling—without bias toward any specific task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale multivariate time series dataset (e.g., ETT with 7 channels, Electricity with 321 channels, Traffic with 862 channels) and produces a compact synthetic dataset of 96–100 samples for sequence tasks or 5 prototypes per class for classification, aligned with standard input window lengths. For sequence tasks, synthetic data is parameterized as a single continuous latent tensor from which overlapping segments are extracted via sliding windows, enforcing temporal coupling; for classification, it uses discrete class-balanced prototypes with fixed labels. The condensed budget corresponds to roughly 0.01%–1.14% of the original training set depending on the dataset.

## Method

UniTSC employs a Multi-View Hybrid Encoder that extracts task-invariant representations through three parallel branches: multi-scale patching with a shared Transformer (temporal view), an adaptive Fourier Mix Block that applies learnable complex modulation to selected low- and high-frequency rFFT components (spectral view), and a self-attention-based Topological Relation Branch that projects variable-level embeddings into discrete interaction regime logits (topological view). Optimization proceeds via a Tri-Space Alignment objective that jointly minimizes (i) normalized Euclidean distance between student and expert parameter trajectories over T steps, (ii) Frobenius-norm divergence of real and imaginary rFFT coefficients of model outputs, and (iii) KL divergence between softmax-normalized topological regime distributions. A theoretical proposition shows that parameter-space alignment alone cannot reduce high-frequency or topological discrepancies under NTK dynamics, justifying the auxiliary spectral and topological constraints, while a generalization error bound demonstrates that minimizing the tri-space loss tightens the worst-case gap between student and expert models.

## Results

On forecasting (96 samples, ~0.6% of training data), UniTSC achieves an average MSE reduction of 7% over the second-best method and retains 92.4% of full-data performance on average (up to 98.8% on ETTh1). On anomaly detection (100 samples), UniTSC retains 96.7% of full-data F1-score on average, reaching 97.9% on SMD using merely 0.01% of the original training set. For imputation, UniTSC retains up to 90.6% of full-data performance, and for 5-shot classification, it improves accuracy by up to 21.3% over coreset selection baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTSC consistently outperforms all nine baselines across all four tasks. Against coreset selection methods (Random, K-Center, Herding), UniTSC achieves 20–30% MSE reduction on forecasting, confirming that synthesis-based condensation substantially surpasses selection. Among general image-domain condensation methods adapted to time series (DC, MTT, DM), UniTSC leads by 5–15% MSE, with the gap most pronounced on high-dimensional datasets like Traffic and Electricity where temporal and spectral structure matter most. Against time-series-specific baselines, UniTSC outperforms CondTSF (forecasting-oriented) by ~7% average MSE and TimeDC by ~5%, while also surpassing CondTSC on classification and anomaly detection—demonstrating that task-specific bias in these methods causes feature mismatch when transferred across tasks. UniTSC approaches but does not fully match the full-data upper bounds of TimesNet and GPT4TS (e.g., 0.442 vs. 0.437 MSE on ETTh1 forecasting), with the residual gap most visible on Electricity forecasting (0.348 vs. 0.304 for TimesNet) and Traffic (0.470 vs. 0.382), where extreme dimensionality (321 and 862 channels) makes full distributional coverage harder under a 96-sample budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset condensation paradigm from the vision domain (DC, MTT, DM) and early task-specific time-series methods (CondTSF for forecasting, CondTSC for classification, TimeDC for both) into a unified, task-agnostic framework. The key conceptual departure is shifting from task-fitting objectives (which implicitly act as low-pass filters or class-separability optimizers) to mechanism-preserving condensation via a multi-view hybrid encoder (temporal, spectral, topological) and a tri-space alignment loss. The theoretical contribution—formalizing via NTK dynamics why parameter-space alignment alone provably fails to preserve high-frequency and topological structure—provides a principled justification for the multi-space design that prior time-series condensation work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniTSC offers a practical path toward data-efficient time-series modeling: a single batch-equivalent budget (<128 samples) retains 92–98% of full-data performance across four diverse tasks and 22 datasets, with cross-architecture generalization to MLPs, CNNs, and Transformers. The main open questions are scalability to very long sequences or high-dimensional multivariate systems where the topological branch may become expensive, and whether the fixed condensation budget (96–100 samples) remains sufficient as sequence length or channel count grows substantially. The acknowledged limitation that classification with high intra-class variance still requires a scaled budget suggests the 'one batch is enough' claim is task-dependent rather than universal.
