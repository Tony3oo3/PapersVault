## Problem

Multi-modal in-context learning (ICL) is severely limited by the quadratic growth of KV cache memory in long interleaved image-text sequences. Existing compression methods for MLLMs rely on sample-specific attention heuristics and hard token pruning, which introduce bias toward demonstration answers, destroy the 2D spatial topology of visual tokens, and produce static memories that cannot recover discarded information for new queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260480/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TASM consumes the KV pairs extracted from a frozen MLLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B) processing an interleaved image-text context of few-shot demonstrations plus a test query, operating entirely in latent KV-space rather than pixel space. It produces a two-tier structured memory: a compact GPU-resident Core Memory (20% budget) holding merged tokens, and a CPU-offloaded Latent Bank (40% budget) retaining unmerged high-resolution tokens. At inference, the system dynamically expands the active cache by retrieving top-k tokens from the Latent Bank when a JS-divergence trigger fires, then generates the text response via standard multi-head attention over the expanded cache.

## Method

TASM is a training-free, three-stage compression pipeline. First, it extracts a global task vector τ per layer as the normalized difference between question and answer centroid embeddings from the few-shot demonstrations, then scores each cached token by projecting its key onto τ (with a ReLU gate and value-norm term), blended with standard attention scores via a layer-adaptive sigmoid gate that shifts from attention-dominant in shallow layers to task-dominant in deep layers. Second, it replaces hard Top-K pruning with semantics-aware token merging: tokens are partitioned into high-scoring 'sinks' and low-scoring 'sources,' and a bipartite graph matching with a spatial locality constraint (L1 distance ≤ Δ_win) assigns each source to at most one spatially adjacent sink, updating the sink's key as a weighted centroid to preserve the 2D visual manifold. Third, it structures the compressed cache hierarchically and triggers sparse retrieval from the CPU-resident Latent Bank using a Jensen-Shannon divergence between consecutive attention distributions, fetching top-k relevant tokens only when the query attends to a novel subspace, thereby maintaining O(1) average retrieval cost while recovering fine-grained details on demand.

## Results

On the primary 20-example (200-shot for ImageNet-100) setting, TASM achieves 65.0 on ImageNet-100, 19.5 on ScreenSpot, 43.5 on MME-RealWorld, 42.0 on IllusionVQA, 60.1 on OK-VQA, and 109.5 on YouCook2, while reducing average context length by 78.6% on ImageNet-100 (16,264 → 3,485 tokens) compared to full-context MLoC. TASM surpasses the full-context baseline on YouCook2 (109.5 vs. 108.8) and ScreenSpot (19.5 vs. 18.2), and outperforms the compression baseline EMLoC on all six benchmarks. At 32k context length, TASM sustains 43.8 tok/s decoding with 11.5 GB GPU memory (85% reduction), whereas full-context inference triggers OOM on A800 80GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-context MLoC baseline (Jiang et al., 2024), TASM matches or exceeds accuracy on all six benchmarks while using 78–85% fewer tokens, most notably surpassing MLoC on YouCook2 (109.5 vs. 108.8) and ScreenSpot (19.5 vs. 18.2). Against the primary compression baseline EMLoC (Ma et al., 2025), TASM wins on every benchmark: +1.4 on ImageNet-100, +1.2 on ScreenSpot, +1.3 on MME-RW, +1.1 on IllusionVQA, +1.4 on OK-VQA, and a decisive +7.5 on YouCook2 (109.5 vs. 102.0), where EMLoC's hard pruning discards temporal cues. On the V-NIAH retrieval stress test, TASM achieves 45.5% at 200 images versus EMLoC's 18.9%, FastV's 7.1%, and SparseVLM's 8.4%, more than doubling the best prior method. On the 10% retention ratio compression ablation, TASM leads EMLoC by +3.1 on ImageNet-100 (60.8 vs. 57.7) and +3.4 on ScreenSpot (15.9 vs. 12.5), while SnapKV and FastV collapse to 42.1 and 45.8 respectively on ImageNet-100. Across architectures (LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL3), TASM achieves near-lossless compression (−0.3 to −0.4 points) at 20% memory, whereas SnapKV degrades by −8.3 to −12.3 points on InternVL3's dynamic tiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

TASM repurposes the task vector concept from Huang et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which originally steered model weights during inference, into a KV-cache importance scoring mechanism for memory compression. It departs from the hard-pruning paradigm dominant in text-domain KV compression (H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV) and the answer-aware attention of EMLoC (ICML 2025) by replacing token deletion with bipartite graph-matching-based soft merging that preserves visual spatial topology. The dynamic retrieval component draws on information-geometric ideas (JS divergence as a surprise signal) to make the compressed memory query-adaptive rather than static, addressing a limitation the authors identify across all prior compression methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a concrete and growing bottleneck: many-shot multi-modal ICL generates KV caches that exceed consumer and even datacenter GPU memory, and existing compression methods degrade spatial and temporal reasoning. The training-free, architecture-agnostic design (validated on Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL3) makes it immediately deployable without retraining. Open questions include whether the graph-matching overhead becomes prohibitive at extreme context lengths (>100k tokens), whether task vectors remain stable under distribution shift in streaming settings, and whether the JS-divergence trigger generalizes to tasks where attention distributions are inherently unstable. The finding that compression can *exceed* full-context performance on temporal tasks (YouCook2: 109.5 vs. 108.8) suggests structured denoising may be a broader principle worth exploring beyond ICL.
