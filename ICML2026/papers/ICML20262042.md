## Problem

Standard top-k routing in Mixture-of-Experts models causes representation collapse: load-balancing objectives force disparate experts to process overlapping token distributions, trapping them in an 'echo chamber' where they fail to develop distinct specializations. Existing remedies either freeze routers (making them non-learnable), require full expert activation (incurring prohibitive overhead), or introduce auxiliary regularization terms that conflict with the primary training objective.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a token hidden state h ∈ R^D and a globally maintained E×E expert co-occurrence matrix C (updated every 10 iterations) to produce a diversity-aware subset of k experts via greedy Mahalanobis routing. At inference, the model reverts to standard softmax top-k routing, so the output format and latency are identical to the baseline OLMoE architecture. The covariance matrix Σ is derived from C via a closed-form estimator (Eq. 5) and is shared across all tokens within a layer, requiring only O(E²) fixed memory.

## Method

MP-MoE reformulates expert selection as a diversity-aware subset maximization problem: for each token, it selects k experts that maximize the squared Mahalanobis norm μ_Σ^{-1}μ, where Σ is an inter-expert covariance matrix constructed from accumulated co-occurrence statistics rather than expert parameters. The key empirical insight is that the co-occurrence matrix faithfully mirrors output-based expert similarity (validated via CKA ≈ 0.6 and t-SNE alignment), enabling efficient correlation modeling without activating all experts. An incremental Cholesky-based greedy algorithm solves the NP-hard selection in O(Ek²) per token, with theoretical approximation guarantees bounded by the submodularity ratio and the restricted minimum eigenvalue of Σ. The method is plug-and-play: it adds ~3% wall-clock training overhead, requires no auxiliary losses, and introduces zero inference-time latency since deployment routing remains standard top-k.

## Results

On the OLMoE-1B-7B model pre-trained for 1B tokens, MP-MoE achieves an average benchmark accuracy of 0.312 versus 0.282 for the standard MoE baseline (+3.0 pp), with gains across all six benchmarks (e.g., MMLU 0.255 vs 0.245, BBH 0.169 vs 0.121). At 5B tokens, the advantage persists (0.319 vs 0.294 avg). On the smaller OLMoE-0.2B-1B model trained for 50B tokens, MP-MoE still outperforms its baseline (0.291 vs 0.279 avg). In pre-training PPL comparisons on Flame-MoE, MP-MoE achieves a 4.71% PPL reduction over standard MoE, while in SFT it achieves a 9.31% PPL reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-MoE is compared against two diversity-enhancing routing methods, SimSMoE and HyperRouter, on the Flame-MoE architecture (38M active parameters). In pre-training, MP-MoE achieves a 4.71% PPL reduction over standard MoE, substantially outperforming SimSMoE (1.31% reduction) and HyperRouter, which actually degrades performance by 20.62% in PPL. In supervised fine-tuning on Alpaca, MP-MoE achieves a 9.31% PPL reduction versus SimSMoE's 4.90%. On SFT benchmarks (COPA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, RTE, WSC), MP-MoE wins on all five: e.g., COPA 0.620 vs 0.570 (SimSMoE) and 0.570 (MoE), WSC 0.529 vs 0.346 (SimSMoE) and 0.356 (MoE), and RTE 0.549 vs 0.505 (SimSMoE) and 0.526 (MoE). MP-MoE also requires no auxiliary loss and no full expert activation, unlike SimSMoE which needs expert embeddings, and unlike HyperRouter which freezes the router entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes MoE expert selection as an ensemble pruning problem, directly generalizing the quadratic subset-selection objective of Zhang et al. (2006) to the MoE setting via a Mahalanobis-distance formulation. It departs from the standard top-k routing inherited from Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022) and from prior diversity-promoting approaches (StableMoE, HyperRouter, SimSMoE, GatePro) that either freeze routers, require full expert activation, or introduce auxiliary losses. The key conceptual departure is using the expert co-occurrence matrix — a byproduct of routing statistics — as a zero-cost proxy for inter-expert representational similarity, eliminating the need for expert embeddings or additional architectural components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MP-MoE offers a theoretically grounded, plug-and-play routing modification with ~3% training overhead and zero inference cost, making it attractive for practitioners who want to improve MoE pre-training without architectural changes. However, validation is limited to small-scale models (≤7B total params) on a single 100B-token dataset, leaving open whether the gains persist at frontier scale or with grouped-expert variants. The paper itself identifies extensions to Mixture of Grouped Experts and MoE-LoRA as future directions, and the SFT-stage evaluation remains preliminary.
