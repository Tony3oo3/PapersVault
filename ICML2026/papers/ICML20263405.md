## Problem

Most graph embeddings encode similarity geometrically but lack intrinsic interpretability: distances and directions carry no semantics beyond post-hoc analysis. Role-based methods (e.g., MMSBM, struc2vec) assume discrete, axis-aligned latent roles, which is restrictive when node roles are continuous, overlapping, and defined by relative trade-offs among multiple latent factors rather than absolute membership in a single role.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263405/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected, unweighted graph adjacency matrix (featureless setting) and produces node embeddings as ILR coordinates in R^(K-1), where K is the number of latent archetypal components. Edge probabilities are computed directly from a Bernoulli log-odds function of ILR distance plus node-specific degree-heterogeneity biases, requiring no auxiliary classifier. A distinctive output is the subcompositional restriction: any subset of simplex components can be removed and renormalized to yield a valid lower-dimensional embedding without retraining.

## Method

AICoG represents each node as a composition z_i on the (K-1)-simplex over K latent archetypal factors, parameterized via softmax of unconstrained logits. The key geometric step is the isometric log-ratio (ILR) transformation, which maps compositions to R^(K-1) while exactly preserving Aitchison distances, enabling standard gradient-based optimization in Euclidean space. Edges are modeled with a Bernoulli likelihood whose log-odds equal the negative ILR distance plus per-node biases, making AICoG a latent distance model under Aitchison geometry. A central theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) proves that this compositional formulation is expressively equivalent to an unconstrained Euclidean latent distance model, so the inductive bias costs no representational power. Subcompositional coherence (Lemma 3.1) guarantees that restricting to a subset of components and reclosing corresponds to an orthogonal projection of ILR embeddings, enabling principled component removal and analysis of archetype-group influence.

## Results

On link prediction (AUC-ROC, D=64), AICoG achieves 0.976 on AstroPh, 0.961 on GrQc, 0.929 on HepTh, 0.851 on Cora, and 0.962 on DBLP, matching or exceeding the strongest baselines (SLIM-RAA, NODE2VEC) across all five networks. On node classification (Micro-F1, D=64), AICoG reaches 0.831 on Cora, 0.736 on Citeseer, and 0.870 on LastFM, remaining competitive with Euclidean embeddings (NODE2VEC: 0.814, 0.695, 0.865) while substantially outperforming simplex-based baselines. The subcompositional variant (AICoG HB SUBCOMP) retains most predictive utility after aggressive component removal (e.g., 0.976→0.974 on AstroPh at D=32), demonstrating geometric stability under principled restriction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AICoG outperforms or ties all seven baselines on link prediction across the five benchmark networks. Against the strongest competitors SLIM-RAA and HM-LDM (simplex-based latent distance models), AICoG gains 0.7–6.2 AUC points (e.g., 0.976 vs. 0.969 on AstroPh, 0.851 vs. 0.789 on Cora), with the gap largest on Cora and HepTh. Against Euclidean embeddings (NODE2VEC, ROLE2VEC), AICoG leads by 1–8 AUC points on link prediction (e.g., 0.976 vs. 0.962 on AstroPh, 0.851 vs. 0.777 on Cora) but trails slightly on node classification Micro-F1 (0.831 vs. 0.814 on Cora at D=64). Against mixed-membership models (MMSBM, MNMF), AICoG achieves substantially higher scores across all datasets (e.g., 0.976 vs. 0.913 on AstroPh for MMSBM; 0.851 vs. 0.680 on Cora for MNMF). The SIMPLEX-EUCLIDEAN ablation (Euclidean geometry on the simplex without ILR) drops 10–15 AUC points below AICoG, confirming that the Aitchison geometry—not merely the simplex constraint—is responsible for the performance gain. On node classification, AICoG is competitive with NODE2VEC and ROLE2VEC (within 1–2 Micro-F1 points) while clearly surpassing all simplex-based and mixed-membership baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AICoG sits at the intersection of three research threads: (i) the latent distance model framework of Hoff et al. (2002), which parameterizes edge probabilities as a function of pairwise latent distances; (ii) Aitchison's (1982) compositional data geometry and the isometric log-ratio (ILR) transformation of Egozcue et al. (2003), which provide a canonical Euclidean structure on the simplex; and (iii) mixed-membership and role-based graph models (MMSBM, RolX, struc2vec) that assign nodes to latent roles. The key departure is replacing the Euclidean latent space of standard latent distance models with the Aitchison simplex, so that node representations are compositions over archetypal factors and similarity is governed by log-ratio trade-offs rather than absolute coordinate differences. Unlike MMSBM, which assumes discrete, axis-aligned roles, AICoG treats roles as continuous regions of a compositional space, and unlike prior simplex-based methods (SLIM-RAA, HM-LDM) that project compositions into higher-dimensional Euclidean space via linear maps, AICoG works natively in the (K−1)-dimensional ILR space with exact distance preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is interpretability by construction: distances in the learned embedding directly quantify differences in relative archetypal mixtures, and subcompositional coherence enables principled component restriction without retraining—operations unavailable to standard Euclidean embeddings. The expressive equivalence theorem (Theorem 3.2) is a clean guarantee that this geometric reparameterization costs nothing in representational power, making the interpretability argument compelling. However, the method's advantage is most pronounced when node roles are genuinely compositional; on datasets where this assumption is weak, it may not outperform unconstrained Euclidean embeddings. Open questions include extending AICoG to disconnected graphs, integrating compositional node states into message-passing GNNs, and determining when the compositional inductive bias is actually warranted in practice.
