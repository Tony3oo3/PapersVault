## Problem

Existing Salient Object Ranking (SOR) methods model human attention as a single deterministic sequence, penalizing equally plausible fixation orders as errors. This ignores the inherent stochasticity of human visual attention, where the next attended object follows a conditional probability distribution rather than a fixed choice, leading to overfitting to specific annotation orders and unfair evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 448×448 RGB image and a text prompt as input to Qwen3-VL, which generates a structured textual output in the canonical format {rank_t | category_t | bbox_t} for t=1..T, encoding the ordered sequence of salient objects with their bounding boxes. A frozen SAM 2 decoder then converts each predicted bounding box into a pixel-level instance mask, decoupling ranking/localization (handled by the VLM) from mask refinement (handled by SAM 2).

## Method

ProbSOR reformulates SOR as a probabilistic task using an Attention-Shift Probability Tree (ASPT), where nodes are salient objects, edges encode conditional transition probabilities P(s_t | I, s_{1:t-1}), and each root-to-leaf path represents a plausible attention sequence with an associated probability and cumulative reward. The backbone is Qwen3-VL (8B) with LoRA adapters, trained in two stages: Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on the highest-probability ASPT path to establish basic ranking capability, and Stage 2 applies an adapted Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that samples candidate paths offline from the ASPT (rather than online from the policy) to form balanced groups, computing group-normalized advantages and optimizing with a clipped policy gradient plus KL regularization. This offline ASPT sampling avoids the instability of standard GRPO where early-policy samples yield mostly low-reward sequences. The paper also introduces the PA-SOR metric (cumulative path reward) and PA-SOR_Norm (normalized by the best path) for probabilistic evaluation, and constructs ProbSOR-Bench with 15,000 samples derived from SALICON fixation trajectories.

## Results

On ASSR, the full ProbSOR model (Two Stage) achieves PA-SOR of 0.932 and PA-SOR_Norm of 0.710, outperforming the best prior method DSGNN (0.891 / 0.675) by 4.1% and 3.5% respectively. On IRSR, it reaches PA-SOR of 1.075 and PA-SOR_Norm of 0.743, surpassing DSGNN (0.842 / 0.650) by 27.7% and 9.3%. On the new ProbSOR-Bench, ProbSOR attains PA-SOR of 0.955 and PA-SOR_Norm of 0.711, leading the strongest baseline DSGNN (0.857 / 0.667) by 11.4% and 4.4%. On the traditional deterministic SA-SOR metric, the method is competitive on ASSR (0.676 vs. 0.772 for QAGNet) but drops on IRSR (0.431 vs. 0.618) due to ground-truth misalignment between benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbSOR (Two Stage) achieves the best PA-SOR and PA-SOR_Norm on both ASSR and IRSR, beating the strongest deterministic baselines DSGNN and QAGNet by 4–28% on PA-SOR. On the traditional SA-SOR metric, however, the method trails QAGNet (0.772) and LG-SOR (0.787) on ASSR and QAGNet (0.618) on IRSR, which the authors attribute to ground-truth misalignment: their probabilistic annotations bias the model toward a distribution that does not perfectly match the single-path labels of ASSR/IRSR. On ProbSOR-Bench (Table 3), ProbSOR leads all four baselines (QAGNet, DSGNN, PoseSOR, LG-SOR) on PA-SOR (0.955 vs. 0.857 best) and PA-SOR_Norm (0.711 vs. 0.667 best), while DSGNN retains a slight edge on IoU_mask (0.781 vs. 0.766). The ablation confirms that the two-stage SFT+GRPO pipeline is essential: SFT alone yields PA-SOR 0.934, GRPO alone barely improves over the frozen VLM (0.671), and the full pipeline reaches 0.945. A box-only validation shows the ranking gains come from the probabilistic attention modeling rather than the SAM 2 mask decoder, as ProbSOR outperforms QAGNet on PA-SOR_Norm (0.710 vs. 0.652) while maintaining competitive box IoU (0.774 vs. 0.827).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire deterministic SOR lineage (ASRNet, IRSR, OCOR, QAGNet, DSGNN, LG-SOR, etc.) by arguing that the single-ground-truth-ranking assumption is fundamentally misaligned with human attention behavior, and reformulates the task as learning a distribution over plausible attention-shift sequences via an Attention-Shift Probability Tree. Methodologically, it repurposes GRPO—a technique developed for aligning LLM policies—into a visual attention modeling context, and leverages a VLM (Qwen3-VL) with LoRA as the generation backbone, a pattern borrowed from recent language-guided SOR work (LG-SOR) but extended with reinforcement-learning-based distributional training. The SALICON fixation dataset, previously used only to derive single consensus rankings, is restructured here into multi-path probabilistic annotations, creating a new data representation for the task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-benchmark ground-truth misalignment (ASSR vs. IRSR achieving only ~0.5 SA-SOR with each other) exposes a previously underappreciated fragility in how the SOR community has been evaluating models, and the proposed PA-SOR metric offers a more principled alternative that rewards plausible attention paths rather than penalizing them. The ProbSOR-Bench (15K samples with multi-path annotations) and the ASPT formulation provide a reusable foundation for future probabilistic attention research. Open questions include whether the probabilistic framing generalizes beyond natural-scene fixation data to domains like autonomous driving or medical imaging, and whether the modest GRPO gains over SFT alone (0.934→0.945 PA-SOR) will grow with richer or more diverse fixation data. The method's difficulty with crowded, overlapping scenes remains a practical limitation for real-world deployment.
