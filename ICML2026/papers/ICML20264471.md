## Problem

Existing LLM safety evaluation treats output-level refusal as sufficient evidence of safe reasoning, yet this paper shows that in fake news generation tasks, roughly 80% of Chain-of-Thought traces still contain actionable unsafe narratives even when the final response correctly refuses. No prior work has traced this latent CoT risk down to the specific attention-head operators responsible for safe/unsafe routing divergence, leaving a mechanistic gap between surface compliance and internal reasoning safety.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes CoT reasoning traces generated by reasoning LLMs under controlled fake-news prompts (direct/indirect induction, with NY/BBC/Original stylistic conditioning) and extracts last-token hidden representations at every layer plus per-head attention score vectors. It outputs safety-critical layer windows (contiguous 3-layer intervals), specific attention head indices driving divergence, three scalar spectral metrics per head (B1 stability, B2 geometry, B3 energy), a three-class CoT safety label (Safe / Potential Unsafe / Unsafe) assigned via a two-stage rule-based plus human-verified annotation protocol, and after mitigation, updated weights for only the identified critical heads.

## Method

The paper introduces a coarse-to-fine routing characterization pipeline. At the layer level, it measures safe-unsafe separation via cosine-similarity contrast between cross-class and within-class hidden representations, selecting a contiguous 3-layer window of maximum divergence. Within those layers, it computes the softmax Jacobian J(z) = diag(p) − pp^T for each attention head and derives three spectral metrics: B1 (spectral norm, capturing worst-case perturbation amplification), B2 (directional centroid of the leading right singular vector over downsampled routing coordinates, measuring geometric consistency), and B3 (entropy effective rank of squared singular values, quantifying spectral energy concentration). Safe reasoning exhibits lower B1 and B2 with higher B3, while unsafe reasoning shows the reverse pattern concentrated in a small subset of critical heads. The framework is validated through anti-direction perturbations that push routing logits toward the unsafe spectral signature, and applied for mitigation by fine-tuning only critical-head parameters (0.64%–1.95% of total parameters), yielding 55–67 point CoT safety gains while preserving general reasoning within ~1.5 points.

## Results

Across three reasoning LLMs (Llama3-8B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B), approximately 80% of CoT reasoning chains contain latent security risks even when the final output refuses the harmful request. Critical-head mitigation (fine-tuning only 0.64%–1.95% of parameters) improves CoT safety by an average of +67.1 points on the News task and +55.0 points on HarmBench, while general reasoning degrades by only 1.5 points on MATH500 and 1.7 points on GPQA. The Jacobian-based spectral metrics achieve ~59% higher average correlation with CoT safety labels than attention-based and faithfulness-based baselines, at a modest ~0.71s additional runtime per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Jacobian-based spectral metrics (B1, B2, B3) against two baselines: an attention-based method (Zhou et al., ICLR 2025) and a faithfulness-based method (Lanham et al., 2023). On the News task, B1 achieves 0.88 correlation versus 0.58 (attention-based) and 0.38 (faithfulness-based); on HarmBench, B3 reaches 0.89 versus 0.73 and 0.34 respectively. Averaged across both datasets, the Jacobian-based metrics improve correlation by approximately 59% over the best baseline. In terms of runtime, the Jacobian metrics add roughly 0.71s per sample (1.45–1.78s) compared to the attention-based baseline (1.03–1.33s) and faithfulness-based baseline (0.54–0.60s), with B3 being the most expensive due to full spectral decomposition. The attention-based baseline is faster but substantially less correlated with safety, while the faithfulness-based baseline is fastest but weakest in safety relevance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CoT monitoring paradigm (self-evaluation via faithfulness metrics, external classifiers) by arguing that output-level refusal does not guarantee safe internal reasoning, and by providing the first mechanistic attribution of unsafe CoT to specific attention-head routing operators. It repurposes Jacobian-based spectral analysis—previously applied to general transformer sensitivity (Kim et al., 2021; Castin et al., 2023) and attention conditioning (Saratchandran & Lucey, 2026)—as a safety-specific diagnostic tool, combining it with layer-level representation separation to create a coarse-to-fine localization pipeline. Unlike attention-heatmap approaches (Voita et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2025) that visualize routing outcomes, the softmax Jacobian captures operator-level sensitivity to perturbations, enabling quantitative metrics (stability, geometry, energy) that distinguish safe from unsafe routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~80% of CoT traces harbor latent unsafe reasoning despite surface-level refusal fundamentally challenges the safety assumption underpinning current LLM deployment, with direct implications for how organizations audit reasoning models. The parameter-efficient mitigation (67-point safety gain with <2% parameter updates) offers a practical path for hardening models without full retraining. Open questions include: whether the identified critical heads are stable across fine-tuning or distribution shift, the dual-use risk of exposing safety-critical routing to adversaries, and whether the spectral metrics capture all safety-relevant routing phenomena or only a subset. The annotation criteria for 'Potential Unsafe' CoT remain somewhat subjective, and broader validation on non-reasoning models or multilingual settings would strengthen the claims.
