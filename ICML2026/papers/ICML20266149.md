## Problem

Existing online video narration models (e.g., VideoLLM-online) accumulate visual KV context linearly with video duration, causing VRAM to exceed typical GPU limits and throughput to degrade on long-form videos. Additionally, prior evaluations rely on teacher-forcing with ground-truth narration history, masking the compounding errors that arise when models condition on their own imperfect predictions in realistic deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an untrimmed, arbitrarily long video stream at 2 FPS, where each frame is encoded by a frozen SigLIP-L/16 into 10 tokens (1 CLS + 9 spatially averaged 3×3 patch tokens) and projected to the LLM's hidden dimension via a 2-layer MLP. Output is a sequence of timestamped narrations Ψ = {(t_n, y_n)}, where the model autonomously decides both when to narrate (via a [SKIP] token probability threshold) and what to generate, without any external query or pre-segmentation.

## Method

FLOWNAR combines two key mechanisms: (1) Dynamic Context Management (DCM), which explicitly prunes the visual KV cache after each narration segment to prevent unbounded growth, and (2) CLAM (Cross Linear Attentive Memory), which reformulates linear attention as a streaming visual compressor that maintains a fixed D×D recurrent state updated via a gated recurrence over frame tokens, with M learnable query vectors reading out constant-size memory tokens prepended at each new segment start. Training uses a specialized attention mask that blocks attention to raw frames and memories from distant past segments, forcing the model to learn from compressed memory tokens and current frames only. A dynamic two-threshold trigger (with a refractory period after each narration) controls narration cadence, and the FLOWNAR-C variant additionally bounds narration context via a last-k sliding window, achieving constant total memory for arbitrarily long videos.

## Results

Under the self-conditioned protocol on Ego4D, FLOWNAR achieves a temporal alignment F1 of 24.85 (vs. 16.29 for Videollm-online) and CIDEr of 35.64 (vs. 28.04), while reducing peak cache from 737.6M to 59.2M parameters. On EK100, the gap widens dramatically: FLOWNAR reaches F1=29.12 and CIDEr=46.63 versus 12.98 and 29.00 for Videollm-online, with FLOWNAR-C cutting cache by 48.3× (22.7M vs. 1096.0M). FLOWNAR supports 10× longer videos and achieves ~3× higher throughput (FPS) than Videollm-online on an H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOWNAR decisively outperforms both directly comparable streaming baselines: Videollm-online (Chen et al., 2024) and Videollm-mod (Wu et al., 2024). On Ego4D, FLOWNAR improves F1 by +8.56 points and CIDEr by +7.60 over Videollm-online, and by +10.10 F1 and +8.63 CIDEr over Videollm-mod; on EK100 the F1 gap over Videollm-online is even larger (+16.14 points). Under the teacher-forcing protocol (Table 2), FLOWNAR also beats LION-FS (Li et al., 2025b) on PPL (1.995 vs. 2.090) and LM-Correctness (53.9% vs. 52.4%), though the margin narrows compared to the self-conditioned setting. In the downstream video summary task (Table 17), FLOWNAR's non-finetuned narrations fed to Llama-3-8B achieve METEOR 17.94 and ROUGE-L 32.63, surpassing non-finetuned baselines (LaViLa+GPT3.5: METEOR 13.45; BLIP2+GPT3.5: METEOR 13.47) and approaching finetuned methods like Video ReCap (METEOR 18.55). The paper also reports an 8% relative improvement in object-level accuracy over Videollm-online on EgoExo4D (36.6% vs. 33.8%), confirming reduced hallucination.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOWNAR extends the streaming video narration paradigm introduced by VideoLLM-online (Chen et al., 2024), which established the [SKIP]-token trigger mechanism and frame-aligned online generation. Its core architectural departure is reformulating linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) as a cross-attention-based visual compressor (CLAM) that maintains a fixed-size recurrent state, combined with an explicit dynamic context management policy that prunes the visual KV cache after each narration segment. This positions FLOWNAR between the unbounded-cache online LMMs (VideoLLM-online, VideoLLM-mod) and offline long-video summarizers, targeting the specific gap of bounded-memory streaming narration. The self-conditioned evaluation protocol also departs from the teacher-forced evaluation used in prior streaming narration work, introducing a first-align-then-evaluate pipeline with IoU-based segment matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical bottleneck: prior streaming LMMs scale linearly in memory with video duration, making them infeasible for long-form content. FLOWNAR's 10× longer video support and 3× throughput improvement are directly relevant to deployment scenarios like real-time accessibility narration or autonomous driving. The self-conditioned evaluation protocol is a methodological contribution that reveals error compounding effects invisible under teacher-forcing, likely to influence how the community benchmarks streaming models. Open questions include whether the DCM+CLAM paradigm transfers to other streaming tasks (QA, detection), whether CLAM's O(D) associative capacity suffices for even longer or more semantically diverse videos, and whether the training cost overhead (1.9×) can be eliminated with custom sparse-attention kernels.
