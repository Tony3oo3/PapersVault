## Problem

While contrastive learning objectives like Sampled Softmax (SSM) are widely adopted for optimizing graph collaborative filtering (GCF) models, it remains unclear how these losses interact with GCF's actual prediction mechanism. The paper reveals that GCF prediction scores are computed by aggregating learnable weights over multi-hop neighbor pairs, yet SSM's gradient dynamics only consider item-side structural similarity and apply a uniform upweighting rule across all neighbor pair types, misaligning with the selective, type-aware upweighting that empirically yields the best recommendations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a user-item bipartite interaction graph with learnable ID embeddings, propagating them through L layers of linear message passing (LightGCN-style). The prediction score for a user-item pair is decomposed into four sub-scores corresponding to neighbor pair types (User-User, Item-Item, User-Item, Item-User), each computed as a weighted sum of inner products between initial embeddings of multi-hop neighbors. The training loss operates on these type-decomposed similarity components with per-type scaling coefficients, while inference remains unchanged from standard GCF.

## Method

The paper first unfolds LightGCN's closed-form prediction to show that user-item scores aggregate learnable weights over all pairs of multi-hop neighbors, then empirically demonstrates that selectively upweighting only neighbor pairs whose constituents are structurally similar to the target user and item (with type-dependent optimal retention ratios) yields the best recommendation performance. Building on this, the authors derive the SSM gradient with respect to individual neighbor pair weights, revealing that SSM upweights pairs based solely on item-side structural similarity and applies the same criterion uniformly across all four neighbor pair types. To address both limitations, NT-SSM introduces a bidirectional contrastive objective (user-to-item plus item-to-user) where negative similarities are decomposed by neighbor type and scaled by type-specific coefficients α, so that the gradient jointly accounts for user-side and item-side structural similarity while adaptively modulating upweighting strength per neighbor pair type. Setting all α=1 and dropping the symmetric term recovers standard SSM, making NT-SSM a strict generalization.

## Results

NT-SSM consistently outperforms SSM across all four datasets (LastFM, MovieLens-1M, Yelp, Amazon-Book) and three GCF backbones (LightGCN, SimGCL, NCL). The largest gains appear on MovieLens with LightGCN, where NT-SSM achieves NDCG@20 of 0.3216 versus SSM's 0.2648 (21.45% improvement), and on Amazon-Book with NCL, where NT-SSM reaches 0.0414 versus SSM's 0.0322 (28.57% improvement). NT-BPR similarly improves over BPR by 5–10% in NDCG@20 across datasets. Training overhead is modest: NT-SSM adds roughly 30–55% per-epoch time over SSM with no additional learnable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NT-SSM outperforms SSM (Wu et al., 2024) on every dataset–model combination, with NDCG@20 improvements ranging from 1.69% (LightGCN on Amazon-Book) to 28.57% (NCL on Amazon-Book); the most consistent large gains appear on MovieLens (19–21% across backbones). NT-BPR similarly beats BPR across all settings, though by smaller margins (5–10% NDCG@20), confirming that the neighbor-type-aware mechanism is loss-agnostic. When applied to SimGCL (Yu et al., 2022) and NCL (Lin et al., 2022), NT-SSM still improves over their SSM counterparts, but the relative gains are smaller than on LightGCN, likely because these methods already introduce auxiliary contrastive signals that partially reshape neighbor-pair interactions. LightGCN (He et al., 2020) serves as the primary backbone where the full benefit of type-aware upweighting is most visible, since its purely linear message passing makes the neighbor-pair decomposition exact. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the proposed neighbor-type-aware loss is a drop-in replacement that improves any GCF backbone without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LightGCN's linear message-passing architecture and the Sampled Softmax (SSM) loss, which has become the dominant contrastive objective in graph collaborative filtering. It departs from prior CL-in-GCF analyses that explain effectiveness through embedding geometry (alignment and uniformity) by instead unfolding the closed-form prediction score to reveal that GCF computes scores by aggregating learnable weights over multi-hop neighbor pairs. This reframing positions the contribution as a mechanistic, optimization-level analysis of how contrastive losses shape neighbor pair weights, rather than a representation-level study. The proposed NT-SSM extends SSM (and BPR) by introducing bidirectional contrastive terms and neighbor-type-specific coefficients, generalizing both while reducing to them under specific parameter settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a much-needed mechanistic bridge between contrastive loss design and GCF's actual prediction mechanism, revealing that SSM's widely-adopted upweighting dynamics are asymmetric (item-side only) and type-agnostic, which misaligns with empirically desirable behavior. The finding that effective recommendation requires upweighting only a tiny fraction of structurally similar neighbor pairs, with type-dependent optimal ratios, opens a new axis for designing GCF objectives beyond embedding geometry. Limitations include reliance on LightGCN's linear aggregation (the analysis may not transfer to MLP-based scorers) and the moderate, dataset-dependent magnitude of gains. Future directions include extending NT-SSM to inference-time refinement, heterogeneous/multimodal graphs, and training distribution design.
