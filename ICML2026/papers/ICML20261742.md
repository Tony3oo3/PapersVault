## Problem

Existing LLM inference optimization treats model routing (selecting among models of different scales) and test-time scaling (adjusting inference-time compute within a fixed model) as independent, decoupled mechanisms. This separation yields coarse-grained, discrete quality jumps from routing and capacity-ceiling-limited diminishing returns from single-model TTS, while also preventing adaptive co-optimization under dynamic, non-stationary deployment environments.

## Input / Output

The system receives a text query (e.g., a math problem or coding task) at each online iteration and produces two outputs: a selected UIS configuration (a tuple of base model M, question parallelism QP, candidate parallelism CP, and beam size BS) and the final generated answer. The query is mapped to a 1024-dim semantic embedding via a pretrained text encoder, which is concatenated with a pre-computed 1024-dim action embedding to form a 2048-dim joint feature vector consumed by the LinUCB bandit. The composite reward combines binary correctness, a continuous verifier path score, and a log-normalized eFLOPs cost term, weighted by user-specified coefficients.

## Method

UNISCALE unifies model routing and TTS into a single configuration space parameterized by (M, QP, CP, BS) and formulates adaptive selection as a contextual multi-armed bandit solved with LinUCB. A shared Transformer encoder maps both the incoming query and each candidate configuration into a common 1024-dim semantic space, enabling cross-action knowledge transfer and principled uncertainty estimation via the Mahalanobis norm of the Gram matrix. Three efficiency mechanisms are tightly integrated: (1) Path-Aware Early Exiting uses verifier scores to compute a theoretical upper bound on each incomplete path's final score and prunes branches that cannot beat the current best, reducing compute by ~87% with <1pp accuracy loss; (2) Dense Verification Feedback replaces sparse binary correctness with continuous PRM path scores to provide denser supervision for the reward estimator; (3) a UIS Cost Model based on equivalent FLOPs (eFLOPs) unifies compute and memory overhead into a single hardware-aware cost metric. The reward estimator is updated online via Sherman-Morrison rank-1 updates (O(d²) per step), and the exploration factor α balances exploitation of high-reward configurations against principled exploration of uncertain regions of the high-dimensional action space.

## Results

In the full Unified Inference Scaling (UIS) scenario, UNISCALE achieves a reward of 0.7079 with 46.88% accuracy at only 49.4 TeFLOPs cost under Cost-Sensitive mode, and 0.6306 reward with 57.37% accuracy under Quality-Priority mode, consistently outperforming all baselines on the composite reward metric. The Oracle upper bound sits at 0.8337 reward (Cost-Sensitive) and 0.7924 (Quality-Priority), indicating UNISCALE closes a substantial portion of the optimality gap. Across the three evaluation scenarios (TTS, Routing, UIS), UNISCALE secures the highest reward in 5 out of 6 settings, with the sole exception being the Routing scenario under Quality-Priority where it is marginally below the Oracle but still competitive with Greedy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNISCALE outperforms all baselines on the composite reward metric in the UIS scenario under both reward modes. Against the k-NN predictive router (from RouterBench), UNISCALE achieves +0.049 reward and reduces cost from 326.0 to 49.4 TeFLOPs (−85%) in Cost-Sensitive mode, while gaining +0.050 reward and +10.62pp accuracy in Quality-Priority mode. Against the MLP router, UNISCALE leads by +0.078 reward in Cost-Sensitive mode with 92% lower cost (49.4 vs. 644.0 TeFLOPs), and by +0.025 reward with +9.87pp accuracy in Quality-Priority mode. Against NeuralUCB, UNISCALE achieves +0.120 reward in Cost-Sensitive mode (0.7079 vs. 0.5880) with 97% lower cost, and +0.038 reward in Quality-Priority mode. Against Thompson Sampling, UNISCALE leads by +0.153 reward in Cost-Sensitive mode (0.7079 vs. 0.5549) with 97% lower cost, and by +0.006 reward with +3.37pp accuracy in Quality-Priority mode. The only setting where UNISCALE does not clearly dominate is the low-dimensional Routing scenario, where its performance is comparable to the Greedy baseline due to the small action space limiting the benefit of exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent lines of work—model routing (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, GraphRouter, BEST-Route) and test-time scaling (Best-of-N, self-consistency, PRM-guided tree search)—into a single joint decision space called Unified Inference Scaling (UIS). It builds on the contextual multi-armed bandit framework (LinUCB) for online decision-making and adopts the eFLOPs cost model from Kinetics to unify compute and memory overhead into a single metric. The key departure from prior routing work is the shift from offline-trained, model-centric predictors to an online, jointly-optimized policy that treats TTS parameters (QP, CP, BS) as first-class action dimensions alongside model selection, and from prior TTS work is the removal of the fixed-model assumption by allowing the base model to be part of the action.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UIS paradigm addresses a practical bottleneck in LLM deployment: neither coarse model routing nor single-model TTS alone can achieve fine-grained quality-cost trade-offs, and the paper demonstrates that their joint optimization yields a strictly superior Pareto frontier. The online bandit formulation with semantic action representations and path-aware early exiting makes the approach deployable in production settings with negligible orchestration overhead (0.91% of total latency). Key limitations include dependence on PRM quality for both search guidance and reward signal, evaluation primarily on mathematical reasoning (AIME, MATH-500) with a single model family (Qwen3), and a relatively small instance count (210). Open questions include generalization to open-ended generation tasks, scaling to larger and more heterogeneous model pools, and whether more generalizable verification mechanisms can reduce sensitivity to PRM accuracy.
