## Problem

Large language models exhibit brittle failure modes under even minor distributional shifts (typos, adversarial prompts), yet existing OOD detection methods (softmax confidence, Mahalanobis distance) operate at the input/output level and cannot capture how these shifts manifest inside the model's representational geometry. There is no mechanistic, inference-time diagnostic that links internal representation drift to downstream performance degradation, nor a principled way to use such a signal for targeted fine-tuning or safety alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (clean or perturbed with typos, jailbreak phrasing, or ASR transcription noise) and extracts token-level residual stream activations at a chosen intermediate transformer layer. A pre-trained SAE projects each activation into a sparse concept space, from which a per-sample energy score is computed by combining the normalized reconstruction error with a log-odds-weighted count of activated concepts. The system outputs continuous energy scores for sample ranking, and in the corrective setting, lightweight LoRA adapters that reshape the LLM's attention projections to align OOD feature activations toward an in-distribution centroid.

## Method

The core mechanism repurposes sparse autoencoders trained on residual stream activations as a surrogate model for the transformer's learned representational manifold, exploiting the fact that OOD inputs force the model to recruit excess spurious concepts and incur higher reconstruction error. The proposed energy score F(x) = ||x − Dz||²/s + Σᵢ zᵢ log((1−pᵢ)/pᵢ) fuses normalized MSE reconstruction error with a surprisal-weighted L₀ norm, where pᵢ is each feature's training-set activation frequency, yielding a single scalar that is stable across random SAE initializations. For robustness fine-tuning, OOD samples are sorted into equal-sized deciles by energy score, and the paper shows that training on the highest-energy decile achieves lower validation loss in two-thirds the steps compared to the lowest decile. For jailbreak correction, LoRA adapters are applied to attention projection matrices in layers preceding the SAE probe layer, minimizing MSE between the SAE feature activations of successful jailbreaks and the mean activation centroid of unsuccessful ones, collapsing jailbreak success from 46% to 7% while preserving MMLU accuracy within 0.09%.

## Results

On the MMLU benchmark, introducing typos to just 5% of prompt words drops Llama 3.1 8B accuracy from 66.7% to 51.01%, GPT-4o mini from 82.10% to 74.85%, and GPT-5-thinking-nano from 91.26% to 86.45%. For jailbreak defense, SAE-informed LoRA fine-tuning collapses Llama 3.1 8B's jailbreak compliance from 46% to 7% on 1,988 held-out WildJailbreak prompts (93% converted to rejections), while MMLU accuracy drops by only 0.09%. On Spoken-SQuAD (ASR-noisy OOD), the SAE-aligned model improves exact-match from 49.45% to 58.33%, and surprisingly also boosts clean SQuAD (ID) from 59.97% to 67.88%. For selective fine-tuning, training on the top energy-score decile achieves >3% lower validation loss than the bottom decile and reaches comparable loss in two-thirds of the training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against entropy-based OOD detection (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2018) and Mahalanobis distance (Lee et al., 2018b), the SAE energy score outperforms both for selective fine-tuning sample selection, achieving lower final validation loss at every energy decile (Table 11, Appendix A.11), with near-zero Pearson correlation (|r| < 0.09) between the energy score and either baseline metric, confirming it captures a distinct axis of OOD-ness. Against Meta's Llama Prompt Guard 2 (an input-side classifier), the SAE-informed LoRA approach is substantially more effective: Prompt Guard 2 flags only 48.3% of the adversarial prompts as harmful, whereas the SAE alignment converts 93% of originally successful jailbreaks into rejections. The SAE approach also avoids the over-refusal pitfall, with OR-BENCH over-refusal at 3.0% (slightly better than the base model's 3.5%), confirming that the robustness gain is surgical rather than indiscriminate refusal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes sparse autoencoders (SAEs), originally developed for mechanistic interpretability (Cunningham et al., 2023; Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024), as an out-of-distribution detection and correction tool operating on transformer residual stream activations. It departs from prior OOD detection methods (softmax-based scores, Mahalanobis distance, energy functions on logits) by grounding the OOD signal in the model's internal representational geometry rather than output distributions, leveraging the linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2024; Elhage et al., 2022) to treat SAE concept activations as a compact code whose expansion signals off-manifold processing. The fine-tuning mechanism builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), but the novelty lies in using SAE-derived feature alignment as the training objective rather than standard cross-entropy or classification losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, inference-time diagnostic for quantifying distributional shift inside LLMs and demonstrates that this signal can drive targeted, sample-efficient fine-tuning and adversarial defense without degrading general capabilities. The finding that even 5% character-level typos drop frontier models by 5–10 points on MMLU underscores a robustness gap that current alignment pipelines do not address. Open questions include whether the energy score generalizes to multimodal or non-text OOD shifts, how sensitive the approach is to SAE training quality and layer selection at scale, and whether the SAE-informed alignment strategy can be extended to more complex adversarial taxonomies beyond single-turn jailbreaks.
