## Problem

Kearns et al. (2026) showed that sequential logit-passing over a DAG can aggregate information for linear regression under squared loss, with network depth as the key resource. Extending these guarantees to binary classification is nontrivial because the squared-loss variance decomposition and Pythagorean orthogonality do not carry over to Binary Cross-Entropy with a logistic link. This paper asks whether the same sequential protocol achieves small excess loss for classification, and characterizes the fundamental role of network depth.

## Input / Output

Each agent in the DAG receives two types of input: its own local feature sub-vector x_{S_i} (a subset of the d-dimensional feature vector) and the scalar logits z_j from its parent agents. The agent outputs a binary classification probability σ(z_i) and forwards its logit z_i (not the probability) to its children, preserving linearity of the transmitted signal. The protocol runs in a fixed topological order, so each agent's input is fully determined before it acts, and the final output is the prediction of a sink agent.

## Method

The paper proves an upper bound of O(M/√D) on excess BCE loss for paths of depth D satisfying the M-coverage condition (every contiguous block of M agents collectively observes all features). The proof replaces the squared-loss variance decomposition with a KL/Bregman-type loss decomposition (L(q) = L(p*) + D(p*||q)), a Pinsker-style link from KL progress to squared prediction error, and an orthogonality condition for BCE residuals; a stability argument then identifies a low-loss-reduction segment that forces small residuals. Complementary lower bounds of Ω(k/D) are established via a hard instance where features are prefix sums of i.i.d. Gaussian latent variables, so that each feature is individually uninformative but the full set recovers the target logit, and the sequential protocol can only cancel one noise term per pass.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical bounds for the sequential logit-passing protocol on a DAG. The upper bound (Theorem 3.8) shows that under the M-coverage condition, the excess loss of the final agent on a path of depth D is O(M/√D), where M is the block size ensuring every contiguous group of M agents collectively observes all features. The lower bound (Theorem 4.5) constructs a hard instance where the excess loss is Ω(k/D), with k being the feature dimension, demonstrating that depth is a fundamental bottleneck. Together, these results bracket the achievable aggregation quality and leave a gap between O(M/√D) and Ω(k/D) as an open problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The primary 'comparison' is between the paper's upper bound O(M/√D) and lower bound Ω(k/D), which together characterize the depth bottleneck for information aggregation in networked logistic regression. The paper extends the framework of Kearns et al. (2026), who studied the analogous problem for linear regression under squared loss, by adapting the analysis from Pythagorean variance decompositions to KL/Bregman-type progress measures suitable for Binary Cross-Entropy. The key technical difference is that the squared-loss orthogonality and variance decomposition tools do not directly transfer to the logistic link, requiring new lemmas (residual orthogonality for BCE, Pinsker-style KL-to-L2 links, and a stability argument over path segments). No empirical baselines or competing methods are benchmarked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the networked information aggregation framework of Kearns et al. (2026) from linear regression under squared loss to binary classification under Binary Cross-Entropy. The key technical departure is that the squared-loss analysis relies on an exact variance (Pythagorean) decomposition that has no direct analogue for BCE; instead, the authors develop a KL/Bregman-type loss decomposition (Lemma 3.3) combined with a Pinsker-style inequality (Lemma 3.4) to certify progress. The work also positions itself relative to the broader social learning literature (DeGroot, Banerjee, Bikhchandani) and Vertical Federated Learning (SecureBoost, split learning), distinguishing its non-interactive, single-pass logit-forwarding protocol from the multi-round interactive protocols typical of VFL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that network depth is a fundamental bottleneck for information aggregation in networked logistic regression, with matching (up to a gap) upper and lower bounds showing depth is both sufficient and necessary. The gap between the O(M/√D) upper bound and Ω(k/D) lower bound remains open, and closing it would sharpen our understanding of how much sequential communication is truly needed. More broadly, the regression-to-classification gap highlighted here mirrors a recurring theme across ML theory (sketching, conformal prediction, Newton methods), suggesting that KL/Bregman-flavored progress measures are the natural replacement for Euclidean residual decompositions whenever the information flowing through a system is a probabilistic prediction.
