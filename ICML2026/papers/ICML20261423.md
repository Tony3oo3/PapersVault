## Problem

Class-level machine unlearning suffers from two overlooked blind spots: over-unlearning, where the unlearning process inadvertently degrades retained samples near the forget-class decision boundary, and post-hoc relearning attacks that exploit residual feature clustering to resurrect forgotten classes from just a handful of samples. Existing evaluation protocols rely on global retained-accuracy summaries that obscure where collateral damage concentrates, and the relearning threat in vision classification had not been systematically studied.

## Input / Output

Spotter takes a trained classifier (ResNet-18, ViT-S, DeiT-S, or CLIP ViT-B/32), the forget-set images, and PGD-generated perturbations of those images (ℓ∞ budget ε=0.03, step size 0.01, 3 iterations) as its only data inputs—no retained data is required. It outputs an unlearned classifier whose feature extractor and head weights are updated so that forget-class logits are suppressed, boundary-proximal retained predictions are preserved, and forget-class embeddings are dispersed. The method is plug-and-play: the base unlearning loss L_u can be swapped with any existing MU objective (SalUn, DELETE, UNSC, etc.) while L_o and L_sim are appended unchanged.

## Method

Spotter's objective combines three terms: (i) a masked knowledge-distillation loss L_u that trains the unlearned model to match the original model's softmax over retained classes only (forget-class probabilities masked to zero) on the forget set; (ii) an over-unlearning penalty L_o that applies the same masked-distillation divergence on an ε-perturbed neighborhood A_ε(D_f) of the forget set, regularizing the local decision boundary to prevent collateral drift on nearby retained samples; and (iii) an intra-class dispersion loss L_sim that minimizes the average pairwise cosine similarity among forget-class feature embeddings, scattering them so that a prototypical relearning attack (which computes a per-class prototype from a few samples and replaces the classifier head) cannot reconstruct a coherent forget-class direction. The final loss is L = λ₁L_u + (1−λ₁)L_o + λ₂L_sim, where λ₁ balances forgetting versus boundary preservation and λ₂ controls dispersion strength. The paper also introduces OU@ε, a retain-data-free metric that measures KL/JS divergence between the original model's masked softmax and the unlearned model's softmax on PGD-perturbed forget samples, quantifying boundary-proximal over-unlearning without access to retained data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (1 forget class), Spotter (λ₂=1) achieves near-perfect forgetting (Acc_f = 0.00%), full retention (Acc_r = 99.98%), the lowest over-unlearning metric (OU@ε = 0.0228, ~4× lower than the best baseline Boundary Expand at 0.0958), and near-immunity to the Prototypical Relearning Attack (Proto-Acc_f = 0.24% vs. 99.98% for Boundary Expand). On CIFAR-100 (10 forget classes), Spotter (λ₂=1) maintains Acc_r = 99.96% with OU@ε = 0.0314 and Proto-Acc_f = 3.00%, while baselines like DELETE (OU@ε = 0.2405, Proto-Acc_f = 31.72%) and UNSC (OU@ε = 0.1789, Proto-Acc_f = 73.62%) suffer substantially higher collateral damage and relearning vulnerability. When combined with existing methods (Table 2), Spotter reduces OU@ε by 5–20× (e.g., UNSC: 0.1789 → 0.0089) and drives Proto-Acc_f to near zero (e.g., SalUn: 95.34% → 0.34%) while preserving or recovering retained accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spotter dominates on the two novel metrics it introduces. On OU@ε (over-unlearning), Spotter (λ₂=0.1) achieves 0.0139 on CIFAR-10, roughly 4× lower than the best baseline Boundary Expand (0.0958) and 12× lower than DELETE (0.1216); on CIFAR-100, Spotter (λ₂=0.1) reaches 0.0168 vs. Boundary Expand's 0.0043 (the only baseline competitive on this metric, but at the cost of complete PRA vulnerability). On Proto-Acc_f (relearning robustness), Spotter (λ₂=1) is nearly invulnerable at 0.24% (CIFAR-10) and 3.00% (CIFAR-100), whereas Boundary Expand is fully reversed (99.98%/100.00%), SalUn leaks 95.34%/11.70%, DELETE leaks 83.84%/31.72%, and UNSC leaks 71.10%/73.62%. On standard retention (Acc_r), Spotter matches or exceeds all baselines (99.98–100.00% on CIFAR-10, 99.96–99.98% on CIFAR-100), while NegGrad (87.73%), Boundary Shrink (93.79%), and Fisher (93.99%) suffer significant utility loss. When Spotter is attached to existing methods (Table 2), it consistently reduces OU@ε by 5–20× and Proto-Acc_f to <1% (e.g., SalUn+Spotter: 95.34→0.34; DELETE+Spotter: 83.84→0.04; UNSC+Spotter: 71.10→0.82) while preserving or recovering retained accuracy, confirming its plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the class-level machine unlearning literature (Cao & Yang, Ginart et al., Bourtoule et al., and subsequent methods like SalUn, DELETE, UNSC) by identifying two under-addressed failure modes: boundary-proximal over-unlearning and prototypical relearning. It draws on prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017) to formalize a new attack vector in vision classification, analogous to fine-tuning-based relearning attacks previously studied only for LLMs (Lynch et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2025). The proposed Spotter framework repurposes knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) with a masked-softmax teacher and adds an intra-class dispersion objective, positioning itself as a plug-and-play add-on to existing unlearning losses rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that near-perfect unlearning metrics (Acc_f ≈ 0, low OU@ε) coexist with exploitable residual feature structure—enabling a 5-sample prototype to restore ~90%+ forget accuracy on several baselines—challenges the assumption that decision-boundary removal equates to knowledge erasure, with direct implications for GDPR-compliant deployments in face recognition and content moderation. Key limitations include the class-level scope (no attribute or arbitrary-subset unlearning), the white-box assumption for PRA, and the absence of formal removal guarantees. Open questions include whether gray-box or black-box relearning variants exist, whether dispersion-based defenses generalize to generative models, and whether OU@ε can be certified rather than merely measured empirically.
