## Problem

Adam's optimizer states (first- and second-moment matrices) consume twice the model's parameter memory, making LLM training severely memory-bound even at small batch sizes. Existing memory-efficient alternatives—SVD-based projections (GaLore), random projections (APOLLO), weight freezing (LoRA), or block-shared learning rates (Adam-Mini)—introduce significant computational overhead, require extra memory for projection matrices, cap achievable compression at ~50%, or degrade model quality. The paper asks whether optimizer states can be compressed to a fraction of their size while preserving Adam's convergence guarantees and matching or exceeding its empirical performance.

## Input / Output

At each step, FOAM consumes the full 2D gradient matrix G_t ∈ R^{m×n} and a fold level l (requiring n mod 2^l = 0, enforced via padding), and produces updated weights W_{t+1} plus compressed moment states of size m × n/2^l for each moment. The residual R_t = G_t − G_t A^{(l)} E^{(l)} is computed on-the-fly from the current gradient and immediately discarded, so it adds no persistent memory. FOAM is applied selectively to MLP and Attention weight matrices, while Embedding and other modules retain standard Adam, with a scaling coefficient α (typically 0.25) balancing their effective learning rates.

## Method

FOAM compresses both Adam moments by replacing each group of 2^l consecutive gradient entries with their block mean via a fold operator A^{(l)}, tracking compressed moments (M̃_t, Ṽ_t) at the reduced dimension, then unfolding back to full size via E^{(l)}. To prevent all parameters within a block from receiving identical updates, FOAM injects a per-step residual R_t = G_t − G_t A^{(l)} E^{(l)} into both the unfolded first moment (M_t = M̃_t E^{(l)} + R_t) and second moment (V_t = Ṽ_t E^{(l)} + R_t²), recovering the full gradient information without storing it. This yields optimizer state memory of 1/2^{l−1} of Full-Adam (e.g., l=2 gives 50%, l=3 gives 25%, FOAM-Mini with l=⌊log₂ d_hidden⌋ gives ~10%). Under standard L-smoothness, bounded-variance, and bounded-gradient assumptions, the paper proves FOAM achieves the same O(log T / √T) convergence rate as vanilla Adam, with the fold-level-dependent term δ_l² bounded by 1. The method is plug-and-play: it can be combined with Adam-Mini, Muon, or int8 state quantization without architectural changes.

## Results

On LLaMA-1.3B pre-training (C4), FOAM-2 achieves 13.13 validation PPL with 4.45 GB memory, outperforming Full-Adam (14.51 PPL, 8.03 GB) by 1.38 PPL while using 45% less memory. FOAM-3 further reduces memory to 3.97 GB (50% reduction) with only 0.06 PPL degradation, and FOAM-Mini reaches 13.43 PPL at 3.20 GB (60% memory reduction). On fine-tuning, FOAM matches or exceeds Full-Adam on GLUE (87.64 vs 87.66 avg) and surpasses all baselines on MMLU with Qwen2.5-7B (73.04 avg accuracy vs 72.65 for APOLLO, 72.41 for LoRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOAM-2 outperforms Full-Adam on PPL at every model size (e.g., 13.13 vs 14.51 on 1.3B) while using 45% less memory, and also beats Muon (14.28) and Adam-Mini (15.10) at the same scale. Against low-rank projection methods, FOAM-2 achieves 13.13 PPL versus GaLore-1/4 (15.66) and APOLLO-1/4 (14.20) on 1.3B, a 1.07–2.53 PPL advantage at comparable or lower memory. At the most aggressive compression (rank-1 equivalent), FOAM-Mini (13.43 PPL, 3.20 GB) substantially outperforms APOLLO-Mini (14.18) and GWT-Mini (14.99) while matching their memory footprint. On fine-tuning, FOAM matches Full-Adam on GLUE (87.64 vs 87.66 avg) and beats LoRA (87.04), GaLore (86.54), and APOLLO (86.58); on MMLU with Qwen2.5-7B, FOAM achieves 73.04 avg accuracy, surpassing APOLLO (72.65), GaLore (72.55), and LoRA (72.41), while Full-Adam runs out of memory. Under 8-bit quantization, FOAM-2 (28.86 PPL on 60M) beats 8-bit Adam (30.78) and 8-bit GaLore (34.88) at the same 0.18 GB memory budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOAM sits within the memory-efficient optimizer lineage that began with Adam and was extended by methods reducing optimizer state memory through weight freezing (LoRA), low-rank or random projections (GaLore, APOLLO, GWT), or block-shared second moments (Adam-Mini). Its key conceptual departure is replacing learned or random projection matrices with a simple blocked-averaging (fold) operator plus a per-step residual correction, eliminating the SVD overhead of GaLore and the projection storage of APOLLO while going beyond Adam-Mini's coarser block-sharing by simultaneously compressing both first and second moments at finer granularity. The residual term, which is computed on-the-fly and discarded, is the critical addition that prevents intra-block parameter homogenization and restores full gradient information to the update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOAM offers a practically attractive middle ground: a 90% reduction in optimizer-state memory with a convergence guarantee matching Adam, and a method simple enough to implement without SVD or random-projection infrastructure. Its demonstrated compatibility with Muon and Adam-Mini suggests it can serve as a plug-and-play compression layer across the optimizer ecosystem. Open questions include scaling beyond 7B parameters (the authors note >100 GPUs would be needed), extension to non-2D gradients in diffusion models and ViTs, and interaction with distributed strategies like ZeRO. The finding that module-wise learning-rate scaling (common in memory-efficient optimizers) partly explains FOAM's advantage over vanilla Adam is an important caveat for interpreting the perplexity gains.
