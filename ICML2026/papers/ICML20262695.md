## Problem

Handwritten text recognition wastes computation on background pixels and blank-dominated CTC frames, yet Spiking Neural Networks face an additional mismatch: spiking dynamics unfold over timesteps while handwriting is observed as a single static image. Existing static-to-spike conversions (frame repetition, Poisson sampling) are either redundant or introduce noise that perturbs thin strokes under short temporal budgets. The paper asks how to allocate both a small temporal budget T and a reduced width-axis sequence budget ℓ_b so that each spiking step carries distinct, progressively refined stroke evidence and the deep mixer processes only informative positions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB text-line image normalized to height 64 and right-padded to width ≤512, and produces a recognized transcription via greedy CTC decoding over a dataset-specific vocabulary (79–89 characters plus blank). Internally, the static image is converted by InkCoder into T timestep-indexed spatial gates that modulate a shared stem feature, and the width-axis token sequence is dynamically shortened from L₀≈W/4 to ℓ_b≤L₀ before the deep 1D mixer, with variable lengths padded per batch and valid lengths carried for CTC masking.

## Method

Spike-HTR formulates offline HTR as a two-budget problem and introduces two complementary mechanisms. InkCoder deterministically converts one static image into a coarse-to-fine temporal stream by constructing an intensity proxy (smoothed, quantile-normalized ink mass) and an edge proxy (multi-scale Sobel magnitude filtered by cross-modal consistency and spatial coherence), fusing them with a step-dependent convex weight w_t, and applying scheduled soft-thresholding gates g_t whose threshold θ_t rises monotonically over timesteps. A CTC-guided length reducer uses a stop-gradient blank-posterior preview from the shared classifier head to identify likely character and uncertain positions, then performs an order-preserving keep-and-merge pass that compresses long blank runs into bounded spans (cap k) while enforcing a minimum keep ratio γ, reducing the effective mixing length by ~32% with negligible accuracy loss. The spiking backbone uses multi-step LIF neurons with membrane shortcuts (analog bypass) and dual-resolution fusion to preserve fine stroke topology, followed by a hybrid 1D mixer (linear attention + QK mixing + large-kernel convolution) and learned convex temporal fusion across T steps, all trained end-to-end with CTC loss plus auxiliary cross-depth consistency regularization.

## Results

With T=2 spiking timesteps, Spike-HTR achieves test CERs of 5.4% on IAM, 2.5% on LAM, and 3.9% on READ2016, trained entirely from scratch on target data without external pretraining, synthetic data, language models, or lexicons. The model uses 76.7M parameters and decodes with greedy CTC. Sweeping T from 1 to 4 shows CER largely saturates by T=2, while WER continues to improve modestly (e.g., IAM test WER drops from 19.4 to 18.8), indicating extra timesteps mainly refine word-boundary errors. The CTC-guided length reducer shortens the effective sequence by ~32% (r=0.68) before deep 1D mixing with negligible accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spike-HTR (T=2) clearly outperforms all CNN-RNN and FCN baselines: it beats CNN+BLSTM by 2.9/3.3/4.4 CER points on IAM/LAM/READ2016, Gated-CRNN by 2.4/1.3/3.9, GFCN by 2.6/2.7, and OrigamiNet by 0.6/0.6. Against the strongest ANN competitors, Spike-HTR wins on LAM (2.5 vs. 2.8 for HTR-VT, 4.1 for VAN, 10.2 for PAR) and READ2016 (3.9 vs. 4.1 for VAN/DAN, 4.6 for SPAN, 4.7 for PAR), but trails HTR-VT on IAM (5.4 vs. 4.7) and SPAN on IAM (5.4 vs. 4.8). The paper cautions that prior ANN results may benefit from external pretraining, synthetic data, or language-model decoding, whereas Spike-HTR is trained strictly from scratch on target data with greedy CTC only. Notably, Spike-HTR matches HTR-VT exactly on READ2016 (3.9) while using a spiking architecture with T=2 timesteps and a ~32% shorter effective sequence for the deep mixer.

## Relation to Prior Work

Spike-HTR extends the spiking transformer lineage (Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer, QKFormer) to offline handwritten text recognition, where the central difficulty is that the input is a single static image while spiking dynamics unfold over timesteps. Rather than relying on frame repetition or stochastic Poisson coding, the paper introduces InkCoder, a deterministic coarse-to-fine gating module that allocates distinct stroke evidence to each timestep. The CTC-guided length reducer adapts the token-merging idea from ViT efficiency work to the CTC blank-posterior setting, compressing blank-dominated width positions while preserving order and short separators. The paper positions itself as a from-scratch, no-external-pretraining study, contrasting with prior HTR systems that benefit from large-scale pretraining or synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-budget framing (temporal T and sequence length ℓ_b) gives practitioners a clear knob for trading off spiking depth against sequence-mixing cost, and the CTC-guided reducer is explicitly reusable in ANN-CTC recognizers, broadening its impact beyond SNNs. The finding that T=2 largely saturates CER while additional steps mainly reduce WER (word-boundary errors) is practically useful for deployment. Key limitations include the hybrid early visual pathway (dense stem, analog bypass) that limits how spike-native the system truly is, the restriction to line-level Latin scripts, and the fact that padding in batched execution can mask the algorithmic benefit of variable-length reduction. Open questions include whether the intensity/edge/coherence priors in InkCoder transfer to non-Latin scripts or severely degraded documents, and whether more spike-native early layers can close the gap between measured sparsity and actual energy savings on neuromorphic hardware.
