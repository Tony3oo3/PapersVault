## Problem

Existing LLM collaboration studies operate at the conversation level with weak environmental grounding, making it difficult to evaluate or train agents for proactive, context-aware cooperation. Three specific gaps remain: simulating diverse player personalities with grounded in-game behaviors is unsolved, collaborative awareness within LLMs (as opposed to architecture-level coordination) is largely unexplored, and evaluation criteria are restricted to efficiency metrics like game scores, overlooking affective qualities such as empathy and trust that are essential for real human-agent collaboration.

## Input / Output

At each interaction step, the target agent receives a partial environment observation (game state, task progress, dialogue history, available action sets) and produces a single-pass structured output containing internal reasoning (<think>), a natural-language message (<message>), and an executable action (<action>); only the action is executed in the environment. The simulated partner is driven by a separate LLM conditioned on a Big Five personality profile, and the target agent's trajectory is scored on both efficiency (completion steps or game score with standard deviation across profiles) and three affective dimensions (Helpfulness, Trustfulness, Empathy) via a penalty-based LLM judge operating on 3-step windows.

## Method

CollabBench introduces a Diverse Players Profiles Simulation pipeline that generates heterogeneous player behaviors from Big Five personality descriptors using multiple LLMs in ReAct-style game play, then constructs personality-behavior mappings via embedding clustering and applies interaction-based filtering (personality-reasoning and reasoning-action consistency scores) to retain high-fidelity profiles. For training, the paper proposes Collaborative Agentic Training built on GIGPO, a hierarchical GRPO variant that combines a sparse trajectory-level efficiency reward (game score) with a dense step-level affective reward decomposed into format validity, communication frequency, and LLM-judged interactivity conditioned on the partner's profile. The key architectural choice is a unified single-pass rollout where reasoning, communication, and action are generated simultaneously rather than in separate modules, reducing latency and encouraging joint reasoning over communication and action. The hybrid advantage (Eq. 5) balances global task efficiency against per-step affective alignment, and the clipped policy objective (Eq. 6) is optimized via full-parameter RL without supervised fine-tuning, yielding 19.5% efficiency and 24.4% affective improvements over the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model.

## Results

On CWAH-MultiPlayer, the trained Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reduces completion steps from 84.51 (Agent 1) / 90.03 (Agent 2) to 71.64 / 63.65, a 15.2%–29.3% efficiency gain, while improving Helpfulness by 17.2%–39.4%, Trustfulness by 17.4%–37.6%, and Empathy by 33.5%–31.5% over the base model. On Cook-MultiPlayer, the trained model raises game scores from 86.93 / 85.87 to 99.20 / 102.40 (+14.1% / +19.3%) and boosts Helpfulness by 62.7% (Agent 1). Overall, the paper reports 19.5% higher efficiency and 24.4% improved affective performance averaged across both environments. GPT-5.2 remains the strongest base model on affective dimensions (e.g., Empathy 3.07–3.32 in CWAH), while the Oracle setting (GPT-5.2 with ground-truth partner profile) sets the upper bound at ~60 steps and ~3.5–4.0 affective scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The trained Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct substantially outperforms its own base model on both efficiency and affective metrics: 15.2%–29.3% fewer steps in CWAH and 14.1%–19.3% higher scores in Cook, with the largest affective gains in Helpfulness (+62.7% on Cook Agent 1) and Empathy (+33.5% on CWAH Agent 1). Among base models, GPT-5.2 leads on affective dimensions (e.g., CWAH Empathy 3.07–3.32 vs. 2.50–2.30 for Qwen2.5-7B), while DeepSeek-V3.1 and Qwen2.5-72B cluster closely on efficiency (68–69 steps in CWAH) but trail GPT-5.2 on empathy. The Oracle setting (GPT-5.2 with ground-truth partner profile) remains the upper bound, achieving ~60 steps and ~3.5–4.0 affective scores, indicating a persistent gap even for the strongest model. On Cook-MultiPlayer, non-LLM baselines such as COLE (121.9–148.6 score) and MEP (97.3–125.9) surpass all LLM-based agents, highlighting that LLMs still struggle with high-frequency coordination tasks. The ablation without affective reward shows that efficiency gains are preserved but affective scores drop sharply, confirming that the hybrid reward is necessary for balanced collaboration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cooperative game environments (CWAH, Overcooked-AI) from homogeneous multi-agent settings to heterogeneous collaboration with Big Five personality-driven player simulation, and departs from the architecture-centric collaborative agent paradigm (CoELA, ProAgent) by instead training the LLM itself via agentic RL (GIGPO) with a hybrid efficiency-plus-affective reward. It builds on the LLM-as-a-judge evaluation tradition and persona simulation literature but grounds both in embodied game actions rather than conversation-level interaction. The key conceptual departure is treating affective alignment (helpfulness, trustfulness, empathy) as a first-class training objective alongside task efficiency, rather than a post-hoc evaluation criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CollabBench provides the first systematic benchmark that jointly evaluates and trains collaborative agents on both task efficiency and affective quality across diverse personality profiles, filling a gap where prior gamified evaluations relied solely on scores. The ablation finding that efficiency-only RL actively degrades communication (models treat the game as single-player) highlights a real tension in current agentic RL that the field has not adequately addressed. Open questions include robustness of affective reward design against reward hacking, generalization beyond two game environments, and the reliability of LLM-based affective scoring versus human judgment at scale.
