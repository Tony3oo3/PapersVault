## Problem

Pretrained generative BC policies (diffusion or flow-matching) produce physically plausible but imperfect robot behaviors, and existing RL finetuning methods either require costly backpropagation through iterative denoising chains or lack controllable exploration under tight online interaction budgets. The paper asks how to use RL as a 'distribution contraction' operator that selectively sharpens the pretrained action distribution around high-success behaviors while keeping exploration within the prior's support, enabling stable and sample-efficient mastery of long-horizon manipulation skills.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (96×96 or 240×240 depending on task) and proprioceptive state, processed by a frozen ResNet-18 or MLP observation encoder inherited from BC pretraining. The output is an h-step action chunk (h=4–16, spanning ~400–800 ms of robot execution) representing end-effector actions, structured as the sum of the frozen generative policy's base chunk and a learned residual correction. The residual is explicitly conditioned on the same latent noise z drawn from the generative policy, making the correction aware of which specific base action chunk it is modifying.

## Method

DICE-RL freezes a pretrained flow-matching (or diffusion) BC policy π_pre(s,z) and learns a lightweight residual s_θ(s,z) via a TD3+BC-style actor-critic objective over action chunks, avoiding any backpropagation through the generative model's ODE solver. Three mechanisms enable stable, sample-efficient contraction: (1) a selective BC-loss filter that disables the residual penalty only when the critic predicts a value improvement consistent with a Monte-Carlo return estimate, preventing exploitation of critic overestimation; (2) multi-sample expectation training that averages actor and critic objectives over K latent-induced action candidates per state, improving the entire distribution rather than a single draw; and (3) best-of-N action selection during interaction, where K candidate chunks are scored by the critic and the highest-valued one is executed. An RLPD-style linear decay schedule mixes offline demonstrations with online replay, anchoring early learning to demonstrations before shifting to online experience.

## Results

On Robomimic, DICE-RL achieves the highest final success rate across all four tasks (Can, Square, Transport, Tool Hang) for both state and pixel observations, while being more stable and sample-efficient than all baselines. Most notably, DICE-RL is the first RL finetuning method to reach ≥90% success on Tool Hang from either state or pixel inputs using only 50 demonstrations, surpassing 90% within roughly 2000 online episodes starting from a 45% BC baseline. On real-robot NIST tasks (GearInsertion, LightBulbInsertion, BeltAssembly), DICE-RL achieves reliable success on all three contact-rich manipulation tasks after RL finetuning. On LIBERO-10, DICE-RL outperforms DSRL in sample efficiency across all 10 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DICE-RL outperforms all five baselines on the most challenging long-horizon tasks (Transport and Tool Hang) in both state and pixel settings. ResFit and EXPO are competitive on the easier Can and Square tasks but collapse on Transport and Tool Hang, likely due to unbounded exploration and compounding errors without strong BC regularization. DSRL preserves the pretrained policy's initial performance (avoiding early unlearning) but is substantially less sample-efficient than DICE-RL, requiring more online episodes to reach comparable success rates. DPPO (which finetunes the diffusion policy directly via PPO over the denoising chain) and IBRL (which selects between BC and RL actions by Q-value) both underperform DICE-RL across all tasks. On LIBERO-10, DICE-RL also outperforms DSRL in sample efficiency, confirming the advantage generalizes to multitask settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICE-RL sits at the intersection of three threads in robot learning: (1) RL post-training of pretrained BC policies, inspired by RLVR in LLMs; (2) diffusion/flow-based generative policies as expressive BC backbones; and (3) residual correction and steering methods that avoid backpropagating through the generative model. It departs from direct finetuning approaches (DPPO, flow-matching policy gradients) by freezing the generative policy entirely and learning only a lightweight residual, and from pure steering methods (DSRL) by adding a learnable action-space correction. The selective BC-loss filter and multi-sample expectation training are novel mechanisms that, combined with the residual parameterization, yield a TD3+BC-style objective tailored to stochastic generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a practical, stable RL finetuning recipe (validated on real robots with sparse rewards) and a conceptual lens—'distribution contraction'—that clarifies what RL post-training does to a generative policy: it sharpens the action distribution around high-value modes and induces trajectory-level contraction that improves robustness. The finetunability metrics (GOODCOV, BADCOV, BADENT) offer actionable guidance for practitioners choosing pretraining data and checkpoint timing. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large multi-task VLA policies with richer behavioral diversity, and whether theoretical guarantees on stability and sample efficiency can be established for this contraction-based finetuning paradigm.
