## Problem

Existing MoE load-balancing losses (e.g., Switch-style LBL) are heuristic: they minimize a per-mini-batch dot product of realized top-k frequencies and routing probabilities, which introduces systematic bias relative to the true population-level uniformity objective. Because the potential is nonlinear, the expectation of the batch-level loss does not equal the loss of the expected routing distribution, so small batches force the router to balance every individual batch rather than the global distribution. No prior method provides a principled, population-level balancing objective with a tractable online solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-top-k softmax routing probabilities p_{i,e} for each token in a mini-batch and an EMA state vector m ∈ Δ^E maintained across iterations. It produces a scalar auxiliary loss L_aux = Σ_e p_{t,e} · ∇φ(m_{t+1})_e per MoE layer, where the gradient ∇φ(m_{t+1}) is stop-gradiented so that backpropagation flows only through the current batch's routing probabilities. The EMA update m_{t+1} = (1−η)m_t + η·p_t is the sole state carried between steps, requiring O(E) memory per layer and negligible compute overhead.

## Method

φ-balancing formulates load balancing as minimizing a strictly convex, symmetric, differentiable potential φ( p̄(θ) ) over the population mean routing distribution p̄(θ) = E_x[p(x;θ)], whose unique minimizer over the simplex is the uniform distribution. Via the Fenchel conjugate identity, this becomes a min-max problem in a dual price vector q, and a single mirror ascent step with mirror map φ* yields the closed-form update m_{t+1} ← (1−η)m_t + η·p_t followed by q_{t+1} ← ∇φ(m_{t+1}). The resulting auxiliary loss replaces the discrete top-k frequency f_e in ST-MoE with the smooth dual price ∇φ(m_{t+1})_e, operating entirely in the continuous probability space and avoiding the non-differentiable assignment noise of hard routing. The choice of φ controls the pricing elasticity: the paper ablates Euclidean, ℓ_p, negative Shannon/Tsallis/Rényi entropy, pseudo-Huber, log-cosh, and softplus potentials, finding that negative Shannon entropy (link function q = log(m)+1) gives the best trade-off between routing stability and downstream accuracy.

## Results

On downstream fine-tuning across three MoE backbones (DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Chat, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Moonlight-16B-A3B-Instruct) and seven benchmarks, φ-balancing (negative entropy) achieves average accuracies of 53.92, 51.86, and 76.07 respectively, outperforming ST-MoE (51.95, 49.29, 73.37) in over 90% of the 21 model–benchmark setups. In pretraining ablations (Table 2), the negative Shannon entropy potential yields the lowest validation loss (3.084) and the smallest global load-balance violation (MaxVio_global = 0.104), far below all norm-based and robust alternatives. In the batch-size ablation (Table 3), φ-balancing at batch size 32 already matches or exceeds ST-MoE at batch size 512 on HellaSwag (63.46 vs. 63.34), indicating more effective population-level balancing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares φ-balancing against two baselines: ST-MoE (Switch-style auxiliary load-balancing loss, Fedus et al. 2022) and loss-free balancing (Wang et al. 2024). Against ST-MoE, φ-balancing wins in over 90% of the 21 downstream fine-tuning setups, with average accuracy gains of +1.97 (DeepSeek-MoE-Chat), +2.57 (DeepSeek-V2-Lite), and +2.70 (Moonlight-16B-A3B); the largest single-benchmark gap is +4.62 on LiveBench (Moonlight) and +4.24 on GPQA (DeepSeek-V2-Lite). Against loss-free balancing, φ-balancing consistently achieves lower validation loss and higher downstream accuracy in pretraining (e.g., at batch size 32 on the 986M model: 63.46 vs. 62.38 HellaSwag, 42.88 vs. 41.58 MMLU). In the mirror-map ablation (Table 2), the negative Shannon entropy potential used by φ-balancing achieves MaxVio_global of 0.104, roughly 6× lower than the best norm-based alternative (0.610 for ℓ₂), confirming that the population-level convex formulation yields substantially more uniform expert utilization than either heuristic baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Switch-Transformer (ST-MoE) auxiliary load-balancing loss and the mirror descent framework of Beck & Teboulle (2003). The key departure is conceptual: rather than treating per-mini-batch routing frequencies as the balancing signal (as ST-MoE and loss-free methods do), the authors formulate load balancing as minimizing a convex potential of the population-level routing distribution, then derive the EMA update as an exact mirror ascent step on the convex dual. The only practical change from ST-MoE is replacing the discrete frequency f_e with the smooth dual variable ∇φ(m_{t+1})_e, which eliminates the non-differentiable assignment noise while preserving the same computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, drop-in replacement for the heuristic load-balancing losses used in virtually all large-scale MoE training (DeepSeek, OLMoE, Gemma), with negligible overhead and consistent gains across scales and architectures. The ablation showing that the choice of φ matters enormously (MaxVio_global of 0.104 for negative entropy vs. 0.610 for L2) opens a design space that is currently unexplored: which potential is optimal in different regimes, and how do these choices interact with expert count, granularity, and data distribution. Open questions include extension to heterogeneous expert capacities, multimodal routing, and whether the population-level framing changes optimal routing at inference time.
