## Problem

Negative-label-guided OOD detection (NegLabel) has shown strong empirical performance with pre-trained vision-language models, yet its effectiveness in open-world scenarios—where negative labels can arbitrarily diverge from real OOD labels—lacks rigorous theoretical explanation. Moreover, the paper identifies that NegLabel's implicit use of KL divergence induces an overly pessimistic worst-case distribution with exponentially weighted outliers, leading to high sensitivity to false negative labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (typically 224×224 or 336×336) and processes it through a pre-trained CLIP image encoder, while ID class labels and 8000 pre-mined negative labels from WordNet are encoded by the CLIP text encoder. The output is a scalar OOD score S_ours(x;θ) that, compared against a threshold, classifies the input as ID or OOD. Notably, the method is entirely training-free: it requires no fine-tuning of the CLIP model, only 15 steps of per-input SGD to optimize a single scalar β_x (adding ~1.5 ms per sample).

## Method

The paper formulates CLIP-based OOD detection as distributionally-augmented density estimation within an energy-based framework, where the energy function is built on the margin between an input's similarity to ID labels and to negative labels (inspired by triplet metric learning). The key theoretical result proves that NegLabel's scoring function implicitly estimates the worst-case energy over a KL-divergence ball centered on the negative label distribution, explaining its tolerance to sampling bias but also revealing its over-pessimism: the KL-based worst-case reweighting is exponential, making it dominated by outlier negative labels. To fix this, the authors replace KL divergence with the Cressie-Read family of Rényi divergence, which introduces an order parameter γ that shapes the worst-case reweighting into a polynomial function (proportional to (h(x,ŷ)−β*)_+^{1/(γ−1)}), yielding a milder, outlier-robust distribution. The resulting scoring function S_ours(x;θ) = T log Σᵢ exp[h(x,yᵢ)/T] / exp[ζ̂(x,Ŷ,β*_x)/T] uses an input-specific β*_x optimized via 15-step SGD, with hyperparameters T=0.01, γ=1.05, and c_γ(η)=1.2.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1K benchmark with CLIP ViT-B/16, the proposed method achieves 94.51% AUROC and 23.17% FPR95 on average across four OOD datasets (iNaturalist, SUN, Places365, Textures), establishing a new state-of-the-art among zero-shot training-free methods. It outperforms NegLabel (94.21% AUROC, 25.40% FPR95) by 0.30 AUROC and 2.23 FPR95 points, and substantially outperforms MCM (90.82% AUROC, 43.93% FPR95). The method also surpasses most training-based baselines such as LoCoOp (93.52% AUROC, 28.66% FPR95) and CLIPN (93.10% AUROC, 31.10% FPR95), while remaining competitive with the strongest training-based method LAPT (94.68% AUROC, 23.40% FPR95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (zero-shot, training-free) outperforms all zero-shot baselines: NegLabel (94.21→94.51 AUROC, 25.40→23.17 FPR95), MCM (90.82→94.51 AUROC, 43.93→23.17 FPR95), ZOC (81.79→94.51 AUROC), and Energy zero-shot (79.57→94.51 AUROC). Among training-based methods, it surpasses MSP (81.63 AUROC), ODIN (88.80), Energy (91.54), ViM (87.82), KNN (90.97), VOS (91.19), CLIPN (93.10), LoCoOp (93.52), and LSN (92.96) in average AUROC, while remaining within 0.17 AUROC of the strongest training-based method LAPT (94.68) despite requiring no fine-tuning. The advantage over NegLabel is most pronounced on iNaturalist (99.64 vs. 99.49 AUROC, 1.29 vs. 1.91 FPR95) and SUN (95.71 vs. 95.49 AUROC, 17.94 vs. 20.53 FPR95), while the gap narrows on Textures (90.79 vs. 90.22 AUROC). The method also generalizes across architectures (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, ResNet-50) and input resolutions, consistently outperforming NegLabel in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NegLabel (Jiang et al., 2024), which introduced negative labels mined from WordNet to improve CLIP-based zero-shot OOD detection, and on the energy-based OOD detection framework of Liu et al. (2020). The key conceptual departure is providing the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why NegLabel works: it implicitly performs distributionally robust optimization by estimating a worst-case energy over a KL-divergence ball centered on the negative label distribution. The paper then departs from NegLabel's implicit KL formulation by explicitly replacing it with the Cressie-Read family of Rényi divergences, drawing on distributionally robust optimization literature (Duchi & Namkoong, 2021) to obtain a polynomial-bounded worst-case distribution that is less sensitive to outlier negative labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification of NegLabel with distributionally robust optimization provides a principled lens for understanding and improving negative-label-based OOD detection, and the identification of KL divergence's over-pessimism (exponential weighting amplifying false negative labels) as a concrete failure mode is actionable. The Rényi-divergence refinement is a minimal, tunable modification that consistently improves over NegLabel across architectures, resolutions, and domain-shift settings without requiring retraining. However, the empirical gains are modest (~0.3 AUROC, ~2 FPR95 on average), and the authors acknowledge the framework only explains the negative-label paradigm, leaving other OOD detection directions (e.g., prompt-based, test-time adaptation) theoretically unaddressed. Open questions include whether other f-divergences or Wasserstein-based uncertainty sets could further close the gap between mined negative labels and truly arbitrary OOD data.
