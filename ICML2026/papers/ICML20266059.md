## Problem

Pesme & Flammarion (2023) characterized saddle-to-saddle dynamics and ℓ1 implicit bias for two-layer diagonal linear networks with symmetric growth times, and Woodworth et al. (2020) extended this to deep networks only under specific initialization directions. The generalization to deep networks under generic initialization and to a broader class of two-layer networks with asymmetric per-neuron growth rates remained open, leaving the implicit bias in these settings uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{m×n}, a target vector y ∈ R^m, and a fixed initialization direction θ* ∈ R^{nL} (or R^{nd} for general two-layer networks). It produces the full saddle-to-saddle training trajectory as a sequence of vectors {k^(p)} generated by Algorithm 1, which converges to k* solving the modified ℓ1 minimization min Σ(t_i^+ k_i^+ + t_i^- k_i^-) s.t. Xk = y. The asymmetric weights t_i^+, t_i^- are determined by the Hessian eigenvalues at the origin (two-layer case) or by explicit integrals of the conserved-quantity geometry (deep case), making the implicit bias initialization-direction-dependent.

## Method

The core technique is the Structural Invariant Manifold (SIM), a data-independent geometric structure that constrains gradient flow trajectories to simple curves per neuron. SIMs induce a natural two-phase separation: a feature selection phase lasting O(1/s) where parameters remain near zero, followed by a finite-time learning phase. During the learning phase, the sign of each activated feature is locked because flipping would require traversing back through the origin along the SIM curve, taking O(1/s) time. This yields Algorithm 1, a recursive procedure where each iteration selects one new feature by minimizing a 'distance-to-speed' ratio δ_i = t_i^±/|u_i|, then solves a sign-constrained least-squares subproblem over the active set. The algorithm terminates in finite steps and its output satisfies the KKT conditions of the modified ℓ1 norm minimization, establishing the implicit bias.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 4.6) establishes that the implicit bias of both deep diagonal linear networks and general two-layer diagonal linear networks under infinitesimal initialization is a modified ℓ₁ norm R(k) = Σᵢ(tᵢ⁺kᵢ⁺ + tᵢ⁻kᵢ⁻), where the growth-time coefficients tᵢ⁺ and tᵢ⁻ depend on the architecture and initialization direction. Theorem 4.5 proves that the full gradient flow trajectory is equivalently characterized by Algorithm 1, a recursive saddle-to-saddle procedure. In the small-scale experiment (Section 7), a 4-neuron model with mixed activations (quadratic and tanh/exponential) trained at initialization scale 10⁻⁶⁰ and learning rate 0.1 exhibits the predicted saddle-to-saddle transitions, with the dashed-line values of kᵢ matching Algorithm 1's iterates k⁽ᵖ⁾ for p = 1, 2, 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing optimization methods; its contribution is a theoretical generalization. The closest comparison is with Pesme & Flammarion (2023), which proved saddle-to-saddle dynamics and ℓ₁ implicit bias only for two-layer diagonal linear networks with symmetric growth times (tᵢ⁻ = tᵢ⁺ = 1) and for deep networks restricted to a specific initialization direction. The present work removes both restrictions: it handles general two-layer networks (Definition 4.1) with asymmetric tᵢ⁺ ≠ tᵢ⁻ and deep networks under generic initialization directions. The practical consequence is illustrated in Section 8.2: on the 2×4 data matrix of Equation (7), standard ℓ₁ minimization selects feature support {3, 4} (k₃ = 1.43, k₄ = −0.20), while the modified ℓ₁ with a 0.5 penalty weight on |k₁| shifts the support to {1, 4} (k₁ = 1.0, k₄ = −0.71), demonstrating that the architecture-dependent growth times can alter which features the network learns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Theorem 1 of Pesme & Flammarion (2023), which characterized saddle-to-saddle dynamics for two-layer diagonal linear networks under infinitesimal initialization, by generalizing to both deep networks with generic initialization directions and a broader class of two-layer networks (Definition 4.1) where the per-neuron activation k_i(θ_i) need not be a simple product. The key technical departure is replacing the explicit mirror-flow computation used by Pesme & Flammarion with the Structural Invariant Manifold (SIM) framework from Zhao et al. (2026), which provides a data-independent geometric mechanism explaining why the dynamics reduce to a recursive algorithm. The analysis also builds on the limit-trajectory machinery of Li et al. (2021) (Lemma A.2) to handle the infinitesimal-initialization asymptotics for general analytic activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical implication is that the implicit bias is a *modified* ℓ1 norm with asymmetric weights (t_i^+, t_i^-) determined by the initialization direction and network depth, which can shift the selected feature support compared to standard ℓ1 minimization—a distinction that matters for understanding which features a network actually learns. The SIM framework offers a potentially transferable geometric lens for analyzing training dynamics in other overparameterized models (matrix sensing, ReLU networks), though the paper itself only validates it on diagonal linear networks. Key limitations include confinement to infinitesimal initialization and continuous-time gradient flow; the authors note that curved SIMs lose exact invariance under discrete GD, and extending the analysis to finite initialization or stochastic optimization remains open.
