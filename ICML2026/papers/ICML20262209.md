## Problem

Existing diffusion-bridge approaches for modality translation rely exclusively on paired correspondences as the sole alignment signal, implicitly conflating semantic correspondence, distributional validity, and geometric consistency into a single data-driven constraint. This makes them inapplicable in unpaired or semi-paired regimes and potentially suboptimal even with abundant data, since the model must infer transport geometry from scratch without explicit structural enforcement. The paper asks whether the space of admissible bridges can be restricted via composable inductive biases that operate independently of pairing.

## Input / Output

For super-resolution, the method takes 16×16 low-resolution images and produces 128×128 high-resolution images; for multi-view-to-3D, it takes four 224×224 rendered views and produces 32×32×32 binary voxel occupancy grids. Both endpoints are first mapped into a shared latent space via modality-specific autoencoders (LDM autoencoder for images, 3D convolutional encoder for voxels, 2D convolutional encoder for multi-view), and the diffusion bridge operates entirely in this latent space rather than raw pixel or voxel space. The method supports a continuous supervision spectrum from fully unpaired (ρ=0) to fully paired (ρ=1), with the total number of endpoint samples held fixed across regimes.

## Method

SDB augments the Latent Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (LDDBM) with three composable structural constraints that restrict the space of admissible stochastic couplings. First, Marginal Matching (MM) trains the bridge score via denoising score matching on target samples, using a winner-takes-all (WTA) assignment with a per-condition capacity constraint (C_y=2) to select the most compatible conditioning endpoint and prevent condition dominance. Second, Endpoint Cycle Consistency enforces approximate invertibility by running the forward bridge X→Y and reverse bridge Y→X and penalizing the latent endpoint mismatch. Third, Trajectory-level Cycle Consistency extends this to intermediate diffusion states, penalizing the weighted mismatch between forward and reverse latent paths at matching times with a noise-scaled weight w(t)=1/(σ_t²+ε) to account for scale variation. Paired supervision is incorporated as an optional additional term gated by an indicator on the paired subset, so the unified objective gracefully degrades from fully paired to fully unpaired training while maintaining the same architectural components (DTDiT denoiser, pretrained autoencoders, T=40 diffusion steps).

## Results

On zero-shot super-resolution (FFHQ→CelebA-HQ, 16×16→128×128), SDB achieves 25.9±0.3 PSNR at full pairing (ρ=1.0), surpassing LDDBM (25.6±0.4) and DiWa (23.3). At ρ=0.5, SDB maintains 25.2±0.3 PSNR, still ahead of both baselines. In the fully unpaired regime (ρ=0) where paired baselines cannot be trained, SDB achieves 19.0±0.6 PSNR. On multi-view-to-3D (ShapeNet), SDB reaches 0.677±0.002 IoU at ρ=1.0, outperforming LDDBM (0.664) and EDM (0.631), though trailing the task-specific LRGT+ (0.762).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDB outperforms LDDBM (Berman et al., 2026), the closest general latent diffusion-bridge baseline, on all metrics at every supervision level where both are applicable: +0.3 PSNR at ρ=1.0 and +0.3 PSNR at ρ=0.5 on SR, and +0.013 IoU at ρ=1.0 and +0.126 IoU at ρ=0.5 on ShapeNet. Against DiWa (Moser et al., 2024), a task-specific diffusion-wavelet SR method, SDB gains +2.6 PSNR at ρ=1.0 and +2.6 PSNR at ρ=0.5, indicating that general structural bridge constraints can surpass a purpose-built SR inductive bias. On multi-view-to-3D, SDB beats EDM (Karras et al., 2022) by +0.046 IoU at ρ=1.0 and +0.172 IoU at ρ=0.5, with the gap widening as pairing decreases, suggesting EDM's purely data-driven coupling degrades more sharply. SDB trails the task-specific LRGT+ (Yang et al., 2023) by 0.085 IoU at ρ=1.0 on ShapeNet but surpasses it by +0.126 IoU at ρ=0.5, showing SDB's structural constraints provide greater robustness under reduced supervision. LADB (Wang et al., 2025) is discussed as a closely related semi-supervised bridge approach in the related work; the Appendix E table shows SDB achieving comparable or slightly better PSNR (35.2 vs 34.78 at ρ=1.0) while remaining applicable at ρ=0 where LADB's paired-latent coupling is not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Latent Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (LDDBM, Berman et al., 2026) and its underlying DDBM formulation (Zhou et al., 2024) by reframing paired supervision as one data-driven heuristic among several, rather than the sole alignment signal. It departs from classical unpaired translation methods (CycleGAN, contrastive objectives) by enforcing cycle consistency at the level of stochastic diffusion trajectories rather than deterministic reconstructions, and from Schrödinger-bridge unpaired approaches (UNSB, simulation-free SB) by avoiding adversarial training and ground-cost selection. The closest related work, LADB (Wang et al., 2025), also reduces pairing demands but through a different mechanism (reusing a pretrained source latent model); SDB instead composes explicit structural constraints—marginal matching with WTA assignment and multi-level cycle consistency—directly into the bridge objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that diffusion-bridge translation need not collapse when paired data is scarce or absent, offering a graceful degradation path from fully paired to unpaired regimes that is not available to standard DDBM/LDDBM training. The finding that structural constraints still improve fully-paired performance (e.g., 25.6→25.9 PSNR on SR) suggests that paired loss alone does not fully capture transport geometry, pointing toward richer composite objectives. Key limitations include reliance on strong pretrained autoencoders to expose cross-modal content in latent space, the added complexity of WTA assignment, and the fact that individual heuristics remain complementary rather than individually sufficient—leaving open how to systematically discover and compose additional inductive biases within the bridge framework.
