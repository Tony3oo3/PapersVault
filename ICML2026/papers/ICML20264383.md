## Problem

Existing federated learning research treats data selection and feature selection as isolated subproblems, ignoring their coupling in high-dimensional, noisy, heterogeneous settings where optimizing one axis alone yields suboptimal or contradictory results. Moreover, prior federated matrix decomposition methods (e.g., FedQR) are non-pivoted and therefore cannot support rank-revealing feature selection, leaving the federated CUR problem largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes s locally held data matrices A_c ∈ R^{n_c×d} (rows partitioned across silos, columns shared), a target column rank k ≤ d, and per-silo row quotas r_c ≤ n_c. It outputs a single global column-selection matrix D ∈ R^{d×k} shared by all silos, per-silo row-selection matrices S_c ∈ {0,1}^{n_c×r_c}, and optionally the CUR factors (C_c, M_c, T_c) for reconstruction. All inter-silo communication consists solely of scalar secure-aggregation calls (squared norms and inner products); no raw data or local matrices are ever transmitted.

## Method

FedGCUR is built on a new subroutine, FedCPQR, which performs column-pivoted QR decomposition in a federated setting by exploiting the additive decomposition of pivot scores and projection coefficients across silos: each site computes local squared norms ‖U_c(:,j)‖² and local inner products Q_c(:,i)ᵀU_c(:,j), and the server aggregates them via additive secure aggregation to make the same pivot and deflation decisions as centralized modified Gram-Schmidt CPQR. The paper proves exact equivalence between FedCPQR and centralized CPQR under exact arithmetic and deterministic tie-breaking. On top of FedCPQR, FedGCUR uses the global pivots to form C_c = A_c D at each silo, then each silo runs a local exact CPQR on C_cᵀ to select r_c informative rows T_c, and optionally computes the middle factor M_c = C_c† A_c T_c†. A per-silo reconstruction error bound is established: ‖A_c − Ĉ_c‖_ξ ≤ η_c‖A_c(I−P_k)‖_ξ + ρ_{c,ξ}, where η_c quantifies how poorly the global feature set aligns with silo c's data, making cross-silo heterogeneity explicit in the guarantee.

## Results

On four vision benchmarks (2048-dim ResNet-50 features, k=1500, 10 silos), FedCPQR achieves 90.2% on Fashion-MNIST, 90.1% on CIFAR-10, 70.6% on CIFAR-100, and 73.9% on Tiny ImageNet (i.i.d. downstream accuracy), while FedGCUR reaches 89.4%, 89.2%, 67.7%, and 72.4% respectively. For CUR reconstruction quality (whole-matrix Frobenius error, ×10², lower is better), FedCPQR/FedGCUR achieve 29.2/29.1 on FMNIST, 30.9/30.7 on C10, 30.9/30.8 on C100, and 44.6/44.5 on Tiny-Img, substantially outperforming all baselines (35–54). On the six OpenML tabular datasets, FedGCUR reaches 46.9% on USPS (k=100) and 37.9% on isolet (k=100), matching or exceeding the best baseline (R.Variance at 46.2% and 24.8% respectively). FedCPQR is verified to produce pivot columns exactly matching centralized SciPy CPQR (pivot exact match = True for all datasets and ranks, with principal-angle deviations ≤ 2.6×10⁻¹⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Coreset (Sener & Savarese, 2018), FedCPQR wins on downstream accuracy for FMNIST (90.2 vs 89.7/89.9), CIFAR-100 (70.6 vs 69.8/70.0), and Tiny ImageNet (73.9 vs 72.7/72.5), but loses narrowly on CIFAR-10 (90.1 vs 90.3/90.7). On reconstruction error, FedCPQR/FedGCUR dominate Coreset by 7–10 units (×10²) across all four vision datasets. Against Leverage Score sampling (Larsen & Kolda, 2022), FedCPQR again leads on FMNIST, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet accuracy, ties or trails slightly on CIFAR-10, and achieves the lowest reconstruction error in all cases (e.g., 29.2 vs 38.7 on FMNIST WRE). Against FedQR (Hartebrodt & Röttger, 2023), the comparison is primarily on runtime: FedCPQR and FedGCUR run faster than FedQR at all tested party counts and ranks (Figure 1), while additionally providing the column-pivoting capability that FedQR lacks, enabling feature selection. Overall, FedCPQR is the best or statistically comparable selected method on 3 of 4 vision datasets for accuracy, and FedGCUR achieves the best reconstruction in 12 of 16 whole-matrix and per-silo cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated QR decomposition line (Hartebrodt & Röttger, 2023) by adding column pivoting via secure aggregation of squared norms and inner products, yielding FedCPQR — a federated analogue of the classical column-pivoted QR (Quintana-Ortí et al., 1998). It then builds a unified data-and-feature-selection framework (FedGCUR) on top of the generalized CUR decomposition (Mahoney & Drineas, 2009; Gidisu & Hochstenbach, 2022), which the authors generalize from matrix pairs to s silos sharing a common column-selection matrix. The key departure from prior federated selection work (Fed-mRMR, FEAST, FAST) is that those methods treat data selection and feature selection as isolated subproblems, whereas FedGCUR couples them through a single decomposition whose column pivots are globally coordinated and whose row pivots are locally chosen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedGCUR is the first framework to jointly select data and features in a cross-silo FL setting with provable equivalence to centralized CPQR and explicit per-silo reconstruction bounds that make heterogeneity effects visible. The practical impact is reduced communication (O(kd) scalar aggregations independent of n) and a label-free selection routine that can be plugged into any downstream FL pipeline. Limitations include the large accuracy gap to the oracle on several tabular datasets (e.g., ~17% vs 59% on gina_prior2), the fact that FedCPQR uses all rows while FedGCUR uses half (making direct comparison asymmetric), and the absence of label-aware extensions. Open questions include whether the global pivot strategy can be adapted to vertical FL, how the method behaves under tighter DP budgets in practice, and whether the reconstruction-error bound can be tightened to predict downstream accuracy more directly.
