## Problem

Existing diffusion-based RL methods are confined to offline settings and do not address safety constraints during online exploration, while primal-dual safe RL methods suffer from severe training instability due to the nonconvex Lagrangian landscape. The paper asks whether diffusion models can resolve these bottlenecks and, if so, what structural modifications are needed to make Lagrangian-guided diffusion stable in online safe RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260696/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous environment state s as input and produces a continuous action a sampled from a multimodal policy distribution via a K=5-step reverse diffusion process (VE SDE). The policy is parameterized as a Boltzmann distribution over the augmented Lagrangian energy, with the score network amortizing the denoising computation at test time. An ensemble of M cost critics provides the constraint value estimates that shape the energy landscape during training.

## Method

ALGD reformulates safe RL as guided diffusion by interpreting the Lagrangian as the energy function governing the reverse-time SDE, then shows that directly using the standard Lagrangian induces a highly nonconvex energy landscape that destabilizes both the score field and dual variable updates. The key fix is replacing the standard Lagrangian with an augmented Lagrangian whose quadratic penalty term locally convexifies the energy landscape around constraint boundaries, adding a positive-semidefinite curvature correction ρ∇ₐQ_c∇ₐQ_cᵀ that smooths gradients and biases sampling toward the feasible set. Theoretically, this convexification preserves the optimal policy distribution and objective value at the KKT point while stabilizing training. Practically, the algorithm combines ensemble cost critics for accurate constraint estimation with a weighted Monte Carlo score estimator that approximates the exact augmented-Lagrangian-guided score, yielding a finite-sample KL discrepancy bound of O(log(1/δ)/N) between the generated policy and the target Boltzmann distribution.

## Results

ALGD achieves competitive rewards while consistently reducing constraint violations compared to all baselines across Safety-Gymnasium and MuJoCo benchmarks. The method demonstrates markedly more stable training dynamics, with the dual variable λ converging to zero (indicating constraint satisfaction) significantly faster than standard Lagrangian-based methods. On tasks like PointButton1&2 and HalfCheetah, primal-dual baselines attain relatively high rewards but incur substantial constraint violations, whereas ALGD maintains lower cost while preserving reward. ALGD also shows higher sample efficiency than on-policy methods (e.g., PPO+Lag) due to its off-policy training paradigm, achieving comparable or better performance with fewer environment interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against off-policy primal-dual CAL (Wu et al., 2024), ALGD achieves comparable or higher rewards with substantially lower constraint violations and more stable dual variable updates, as CAL suffers from oscillatory dual updates that lead to repeated sampling of high-reward/high-risk actions. Compared to on-policy SAC+Lag and PPO+Lag (Ji et al., 2023), ALGD demonstrates higher sample efficiency due to its off-policy paradigm, reaching competitive performance with significantly fewer environment interactions. Against the hard-constrained HJ reachability baseline (Yu et al., 2022), ALGD avoids the overly conservative behavior inherent to restricting policies to estimated safe sets, achieving higher average returns while still satisfying all prescribed safety thresholds. Notably, naively adding augmented Lagrangian penalties to SAC (SAC+AugLag) does not yield consistent improvements, confirming that the Gaussian policy parameterization cannot resolve the underlying non-convex optimization geometry that ALGD's diffusion-based multimodal policy addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALGD builds on Q-score matching (Psenka et al., 2023), which established the connection between diffusion score functions and Q-function gradients, and extends it to the constrained safe RL setting. It departs from standard primal-dual safe RL (SAC+Lag, PPO+Lag, CAL) by replacing the Gaussian policy with a diffusion-based multimodal policy, and from prior diffusion-based RL (largely offline) by enabling online off-policy training. The key conceptual departure is importing the augmented Lagrangian from classical constrained optimization (Hestenes, 1969; Rockafellar, 1976) into the diffusion framework, showing that the quadratic penalty term locally convexifies the energy landscape to stabilize the score field—a connection not previously explored in the RL or diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap: diffusion-based safe RL in online, off-policy settings was essentially unexplored, and the paper provides both a theoretical explanation for why naive Lagrangian-guided diffusion fails and a principled fix with convergence guarantees (Theorem 3.3). The practical impact is that ALGD achieves competitive rewards with consistently lower constraint violations than both primal-dual and hard-constrained baselines across Safety-Gym and MuJoCo benchmarks. However, the authors acknowledge the absence of formal sample complexity bounds for the coupled diffusion-primal-dual dynamics, and all experiments are confined to simulated environments, leaving open the critical question of whether the approach transfers to real-world safety-critical systems with partial observability and model mismatch.
