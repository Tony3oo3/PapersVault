## Problem

Unified multimodal models need a single visual representation that simultaneously supports semantic understanding, high-fidelity reconstruction, and generation-friendly predictability, but existing continuous approaches rely on two mismatched spaces (ViT features for understanding, VAE latents for generation) that break representational consistency. Even when these features are naively concatenated, a Diffusion Transformer exhibits severe optimization imbalance—fitting the ViT component well while underfitting the VAE component—due to spectral, dimensional, and conditional heterogeneity between the two spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a class label as condition and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 image by predicting the unified Twins token via flow matching, then decoding the VAE component through the Flux.2 VAE decoder. For understanding, an RGB image is encoded into the unified token (SigLIP2 ViT features concatenated with Flux.2 VAE latents on the same spatial grid, preserving sequence length) and fed into a Qwen2.5-7B LLM via a projector in an LLaVA-style pipeline. The key structural constraint is that both encoders share the same patch size, so the concatenated token has the same sequence length as either component alone, avoiding any increase in quadratic attention cost.

## Method

Twins constructs a unified continuous representation by channel-wise concatenating SigLIP2 ViT features (768-dim) with Flux.2 VAE latents (128-dim) on the same token grid, yielding a 896-dim token per spatial position without changing sequence length. A Diffusion Transformer trained with rectified flow matching predicts this unified token, but the authors identify three sources of optimization imbalance—spectral bias (ViT is low-frequency, VAE is broadband), intrinsic dimensionality gap (ViT ID≈15 vs. VAE ID≈35), and conditional alignment (ViT features are largely deterministic given a class label, while VAE features retain condition-independent uncertainty). To remedy this, they replace the standard MSE loss on VAE channels with a focal reweighting w_i = |v_i − v_θ(z,t)_i|^(2γ) (γ=0.5) that upweights large-error dimensions, steering optimization away from the semantic-only local optimum. This focal flow-matching loss yields up to 10.57 gFID improvement over naive MSE on ImageNet-256 without classifier-free guidance, while also slightly improving SigLIP2 component prediction through gradient balancing.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Twins with Focal Loss achieves a gFID of 2.50 (w/o guidance) and 1.47 (w/ guidance) at 80 epochs, compared to 14.41 for the MSE-loss baseline — a 10.57 gFID improvement. On ImageNet 512×512, Twins with Focal Loss reaches gFID 1.79 (w/ guidance) at 80 epochs. For reconstruction, Twins achieves PSNR 31.46, SSIM 0.90, and rFID 0.11, matching dedicated generative VAEs (e.g., FLUX.1-VAE: PSNR 32.74) while far exceeding semantic-only RAE (PSNR 18.83). On multimodal understanding, Twins (SigLIP2 + Flux.2 VAE) scores 87.82 on POPE, 64.93 on GQA, and 1971.0 on MME-S, outperforming the SigLIP2-only baseline on fine-grained tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most critical comparison is Twins Focal Loss vs. Twins MSE Loss: at 80 epochs, Focal Loss reduces gFID from 14.41 to 3.84 (VAE-decoded) or 2.50 (SigLIP-decoded) w/o guidance, a >10 gFID gain, confirming that standard MSE fails to jointly optimize heterogeneous ViT and VAE channels. Against the Flux.2 VAE-only baseline (gFID 3.99 w/o guidance at 80 epochs), Twins Focal Loss (3.84) matches or slightly exceeds it while providing a unified representation that also supports understanding. Against RAE (Zheng et al., 2025b), Twins achieves a much higher PSNR (31.46 vs. 18.83) but a slightly worse gFID with guidance (1.59 vs. 1.13 at 800 epochs), trading a small generation gap for dramatically better reconstruction fidelity. On multimodal understanding, Twins (SigLIP2 + Flux.2 VAE) outperforms the SigLIP2-only baseline on GQA (64.93 vs. 64.54), TQA (58.89 vs. 56.92), and MME-S (1971.0 vs. 1826.8), while matching on POPE and MMB. For reconstruction, Twins (PSNR 31.46, rFID 0.11) substantially outperforms RAE (PSNR 18.83, rFID 0.57) and UniLIP (PSNR 22.99), approaching the dedicated FLUX.1-VAE (PSNR 32.74, rFID 0.18). Among unified MLLMs in Table 2, Twins achieves the highest MME-S (1971.0) and MME-P (1588.8) scores, surpassing Show-o2 (1620.5 MME-S) and TokenFlow-XL (1922.2 MME-S) on the MME suite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of unified multimodal modeling and flow-matching-based generation. It departs from prior unified-tokenizer efforts (UniFlow, UniLIP, RAE) that attempt to repurpose or fine-tune a single encoder for both understanding and generation, instead proposing a training-free channel-wise concatenation of two off-the-shelf encoders (SigLIP2 ViT + Flux.2 VAE). The method builds on Rectified Flow for the generative objective and adapts Focal Loss (originally for classification) into a regression reweighting scheme for flow matching, while adopting the DDT head design for the DiT architecture. The empirical analysis of optimization imbalance—attributing it to spectral bias, intrinsic dimensionality, and conditional dependency—draws on tools from signal processing (FFT), manifold geometry (Two-NN), and PCA, but applies them jointly to diagnose a specific failure mode in heterogeneous feature prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The channel-wise concatenation is an elegantly simple design that avoids the information bottleneck of dimensionality reduction (as in UniFlow/UniLIP) while preserving the reconstruction fidelity that purely semantic encoders (RAE/DINOv2) sacrifice. The focal-loss-for-flow-matching technique is a practical, low-cost calibration that could generalize to any setting where a model must jointly predict features with heterogeneous difficulty. However, the evaluation is limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation and a single VLM pipeline (Qwen2.5-7B with LLaVA-style training); open questions include whether the approach scales to video, open-ended text-to-image, or other encoder pairs, and whether the focal reweighting on VAE channels alone is the optimal strategy versus more principled per-dimension scheduling.
