## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability methods identify 'circuits' as point estimates without quantifying their statistical stability, yet the underlying causal mediation analysis (CMA) scores are highly variable random variables rather than fixed component properties. This paper asks: how much of the instability in discovered circuits is intrinsic to the CMA estimand, how much is introduced by gradient-based approximations, and how much is amplified by aggregation and selection heuristics?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a frozen language model (gpt2-small, Llama-3.2-1B, or Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), a task-specific dataset of clean/corrupted input pairs (IOI, SVA, Greater-Than), and a set of circuit-discovery hyperparameters (estimator type, aggregation function, patching strategy, noise amplitude). Rather than producing a single circuit, the pipeline generates a distribution over circuits under controlled perturbations, from which the paper extracts pairwise Jaccard indices, circuit error rates, KL divergences, and coefficients of variation as the primary outputs. The key structural detail is that stability is measured across N=100 bootstrap resamples or across hyperparameter sweeps, not on a single run.

## Method

The paper reframes circuit discovery as a statistical estimation problem where the target parameter μ_e is the population-level expected CMA score for each edge, and the discovered circuit is a function of the finite-sample estimate, the approximation method, and the selection hyperparameters. To decompose instability, they first compute exact CMA scores (Eq. 1) per input-edge pair to establish the intrinsic variance of the estimand, then compare against four approximate estimators (EAP, EAP-IG on inputs, EAP-IG on activations, clean-corrupted) to isolate approximation noise. Variability is then propagated through the full pipeline via three data-level perturbations (bootstrap resampling, meta-dataset shifts, prompt paraphrasing) and method-level perturbations (aggregation function, patching type, estimator choice, noise amplitude). Structural stability is quantified via pairwise Jaccard indices over edge sets, and functional stability via circuit error and KL divergence, revealing that instability compounds at every stage: exact CMA already shows CV≈0.5, EAP pushes CV>1, and the final circuits exhibit Jaccard means as low as 0.33 under bootstrap resampling.

## Results

Exact CMA edge scores exhibit high intrinsic variability across inputs, with a coefficient of variation (CV) often near 0.5, while EAP approximations amplify this to CV > 1, indicating the signal-to-noise ratio for any given edge is below 1. Under bootstrap resampling (n=100), discovered circuits show the lowest structural stability (mean pairwise Jaccard = 0.561, CV = 0.335) and highest circuit error (0.440), compared to meta-dataset (Jaccard = 0.790) and prompt paraphrasing (Jaccard = 0.799). Changing hyperparameters (aggregation, patching type, estimator variant) can reduce Jaccard similarity to the median circuit as low as 0.086, yielding nearly disjoint subgraphs. Stability degrades for larger models (Llama-3.2-1B) relative to gpt2-small, and instruction tuning does not significantly alter the stability profile.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not propose a new circuit-discovery method to benchmark against baselines. Instead, it compares the stability of circuits under different sources of variability. Exact CMA (the gold-standard estimator) already shows high per-input variance (CV ≈ 0.5), and EAP/EAP-IG approximations worsen this (CV > 1), meaning gradient-based estimators add substantial noise on top of the intrinsic causal-effect variability. Among data perturbations, bootstrap resampling produces the most unstable circuits (Jaccard μ = 0.561) while meta-dataset and prompt paraphrasing are more stable (Jaccard ≈ 0.79), suggesting the general mechanism is somewhat robust to semantic shifts but fragile to sampling noise. Among methodological choices, different EAP variants (EAP, EAP-IG-inputs, EAP-IG-activations, clean-corrupted) and aggregation strategies (sum vs. median) yield circuits with Jaccard overlap as low as 0.071–0.086, indicating they isolate different artifacts of the high-variance edge distribution rather than converging on a shared mechanism. Varying the intervention noise amplitude (0.01 to 5.0) also shifts the identified circuit, with a critical regime around amplitude ≈ 0.2 where Jaccard CV peaks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes circuit discovery in Mechanistic Interpretability as a statistical estimation problem built upon causal mediation analysis (CMA), departing from the prevailing practice of reporting a single point-estimate circuit without uncertainty quantification. It builds directly on the EAP/EAP-IG attribution-patching pipeline (Syed et al., 2023; Hanna et al., 2024) and the CMA formalism (Pearl, 2001; VanderWeele, 2016), but introduces a systematic variance decomposition that prior MI work has not performed: separating intrinsic CMA variability, approximation noise from gradient-based estimators, and sensitivity to aggregation hyperparameters. The non-identifiability argument from the same authors (Méloux et al., 2025) provides the theoretical motivation, while this paper supplies the first empirical quantification of how that non-identifiability manifests as estimator instability across the full discovery pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that exact CMA scores already exhibit CV ≈ 0.5 across inputs, and that EAP pushes this above 1, fundamentally undermines the assumption that a stable, identifiable circuit exists to be recovered — a concern that has been raised anecdotally (dead-salmon artifacts, non-transferability) but never quantified at this level of granularity. The practical recommendation to report bootstrap stability metrics (e.g., mean pairwise Jaccard ≥ 0.8) is immediately actionable and could reshape MI reporting norms. Key limitations include restriction to the EAP family, three discrete linguistic tasks, and exact CMA only on gpt2-small; open questions include whether probabilistic circuit discovery (a posterior over graphs rather than a single subgraph) can resolve the instability, and whether stability-aware optimization objectives can recover a reliable functional core.
