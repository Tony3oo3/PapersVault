## Problem

Dynamic graph anomaly detection (DGAD) faces a fundamental tension: unsupervised methods produce ambiguous, weakly discriminative decision boundaries where anomaly scores collapse into a narrow range, while semi-supervised methods overfit to the few available labeled anomalies and generalize poorly to unseen anomaly patterns. The paper tackles the largely underexplored problem of learning an explicit, discriminative boundary from normal/unlabeled data while leveraging limited labeled anomalies without sacrificing open-set generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous-time dynamic graph as an ordered stream of timestamped events ξ(t_k) = (v_i, v_j, t_k, e_{i,j}^{t_k}), where each event is a directed interaction with optional edge features, and additionally samples L historical interactions per endpoint to construct the residual signal. It outputs a per-interaction anomaly score s ∈ [0,1] computed as 1 − exp(log p(x)), where log p(x) is the normalizing-flow log-likelihood of the projected residual representation. The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into any CTDG encoder (TCL, CAWN, DyGFormer, etc.), operating under three supervision settings: fully unsupervised, limited supervision (all available anomaly labels), and few-shot (k ∈ {1,2,3} labels).

## Method

SDGAD combines three components. First, residual representation encoding computes each node's embedding with and without the current event and takes the difference ΔE_i^t = Enc(S_i^t) − Enc(S_i^{t−}), then concatenates the residuals of both endpoints to form an event-level representation that explicitly highlights deviations from recent graph evolution. Second, a representation restriction loss projects the residual through a linear layer, measures distance via a pseudo-Huber norm, and constrains normal samples into a compact annular region between two co-centered hyperspheres (radii r_min = γ·r_max and r_max = 0.4) while pushing anomalies outside an expanded radius r′ = r_max + Δr, using log-sigmoid-weighted exponential penalties. Third, a normalizing flow (6 coupling blocks) models the normal log-likelihood distribution, and a bi-boundary optimization strategy splits the decision boundary into B_n and B_a = B_n − τ with a softplus-based margin loss that enforces separation in likelihood space, mitigating the known failure mode where normalizing flows assign high likelihood to out-of-distribution inputs. The overall training objective is L = L_ML + λ₁·L_BO + λ₂·L_RR, and the framework is supported by error-bound propositions for both the bi-boundary and restriction losses.

## Results

On real-world anomaly datasets under the limited-supervision (S1) setting, SDGAD(TCL) achieves 80.36 AUROC / 5.41 AP / 8.34 F1 on Wikipedia and 72.87 AUROC / 7.89 AP / 7.15 F1 on MOOC, substantially outperforming the TCL backbone alone (77.69 AUROC / 2.41 F1 on Wikipedia). SDGAD(DyGFormer) attains the best AUROC of 86.60 on Wikipedia. On synthetic-anomaly datasets under the fully unsupervised (S3) setting, SDGAD(TCL) reaches near-perfect AUROC (≥99.86%) across UCI, Enron, and LastFM, while most baselines remain well below 90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDGAD consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across the three real-anomaly datasets. The most dramatic gain is in F1: SDGAD(TCL) achieves 8.34 F1 on Wikipedia versus 2.41 for the TCL backbone alone and 4.25 for the strongest DGAD-specific method SAD, reflecting a much sharper decision boundary. On MOOC, SDGAD(TCL) improves F1 from 0 (TCL) to 7.15 and AUROC from 72.51 to 72.87, while also surpassing SAD (71.25 AUROC, 3.12 F1). DTDG-based methods (Netwalk, AddGraph, StrGNN, TADDY) perform worst across all datasets, confirming that snapshot-level modeling discards critical temporal signals. On synthetic-anomaly datasets under the fully unsupervised S3 setting, SDGAD(TCL) reaches ≥99.86% AUROC on UCI, Enron, and LastFM, whereas the best CTDG baselines (TGAT, GraphMixer) remain at 85–87% AUROC on UCI and 78–82% on Enron. The framework's gains are encoder-agnostic: swapping the backbone from TCL to CAWN or DyGFormer preserves the F1 advantage, with SDGAD(DyGFormer) achieving the highest AUROC (86.60) on Wikipedia.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDGAD carves new ground at the intersection of continuous-time dynamic graph (CTDG) anomaly detection and one-class classification under limited supervision. It departs from unsupervised DGAD methods (Netwalk, TADDY, SAD, GeneralDyG) that produce ambiguous, weakly discriminative boundaries, and from semi-supervised approaches that overfit to a narrow subset of labeled anomaly patterns. Conceptually, it repurposes the bi-hypersphere constraint from Zhang et al. (2024) for the non-i.i.d. dynamic graph setting and combines it with normalizing-flow density estimation (building on Kirichenko et al., 2020; Kumar et al., 2021) to construct an explicit, margin-enforced decision boundary. The residual representation encoding is a novel mechanism specific to this paper, designed to counteract history-dominated aggregation in standard CTDG encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in high-stakes DGAD applications (e.g., financial fraud detection) where stable, well-calibrated decision thresholds are required but labeled anomalies are extremely scarce. Its model-agnostic design means it can be plugged into any CTDG encoder, broadening its applicability. The paper also highlights that AUROC is overly optimistic under extreme class imbalance and advocates for AP and F1, which is important for fair evaluation in the field. Open questions include extending from binary detection to anomaly typing, and whether current synthetic anomaly injection protocols are too simplistic (as evidenced by near-perfect AUROC on LastFM).
