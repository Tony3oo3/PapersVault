## Problem

While the implicit bias of gradient descent toward ℓ₂ margin maximization in homogeneous neural networks is well understood, the analogous question for momentum-based optimizers (Adam, Muon, Signum) had only been resolved for linear predictors. This paper closes that gap by proving margin-maximization bias for these optimizers on the substantially broader class of smooth homogeneous models with non-linear activations.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a parameterized model f(x;θ) that is L-homogeneous (L≥1), a log-concave exponentially-tailed loss (logistic or exponential), and a decaying learning rate schedule η(t) satisfying specific integrability and decay-rate conditions. The output is a characterization of the limiting parameter direction θ_t/‖θ_t‖ as a KKT point of the norm-dependent margin maximization problem, where the norm depends on the optimizer (spectral/max-spectral for Muon, ℓ∞ for Adam, ℓ₂ for MomentumGD). Empirically, the method is validated on 2048 MNIST digits (even/odd binary classification) with two-layer networks using squared-ReLU or ReLU activations, and on CIFAR-10 with 4-layer networks.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the Approximate Steepest Descent framework, which shows that KKT stationarity of limit points holds even when the trajectory only approximately satisfies the steepest descent alignment condition ⟨dθ/dt/‖dθ/dt‖, −g/‖g‖_⋆⟩ = 1. For momentum steepest descent (covering Muon, Signum, MomentumGD), the key lemma shows that the momentum estimate m_t[j] equals the instantaneous gradient g_t[j] up to o(1) for every coordinate that is momentarily significant, making the trajectory an approximate steepest descent path. For Adam, the analysis leverages the fact that the ratio m̂_t[j]/√v̂_t[j] approximates sign(g_t[j]) for significant coordinates, reducing Adam to approximate sign gradient descent (i.e., ℓ∞ steepest descent), with a crucial bound (adapted from Zhang et al.) ensuring the update magnitude does not exceed the learning rate on average. Composite optimizers like Muon-Signum and Muon-Adam are handled by showing that running multiple normalized steepest descent algorithms in parallel on different parameter blocks is equivalent to a single run under the maximum of the component norms.

## Results

The paper proves that under a decaying learning rate schedule, Muon (and any normalized Momentum Steepest Descent algorithm) converges to a KKT point of the max-margin problem with respect to the max-spectral norm ‖·‖_msp (Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3), while Adam (without stability constant) converges to a KKT point of the ℓ∞ max-margin problem (Theorem 3.5). Muon-Adam is shown to maximize a hybrid norm max{(η₀ᴬ/η₀ᴹ)‖(W₁,…,W_K)‖_msp, ‖u‖∞} (Theorem 3.6). Empirically, on 2048 MNIST digits (even/odd) with squared-ReLU and ReLU activations trained to loss 10⁻⁸, each optimizer indeed achieves the highest margin in its predicted norm: NGD maximizes ℓ₂, Signum and Adam maximize ℓ∞, and Muon maximizes ‖·‖_msp, with directional convergence (cosine similarity > 0.99 in the second half of training) confirmed across all algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five optimizer families on two-layer homogeneous networks (MNIST) and a 4-layer network (CIFAR-10, Appendix D). NGD (with and without momentum) achieves the highest ℓ₂ margin, consistent with its identity as normalized steepest descent under the ℓ₂ norm. Signum (momentum sign gradient descent) outperforms Adam on the ℓ∞ margin, which the authors attribute to Signum being a closer approximation to exact sign gradient descent (the ℓ∞ steepest descent direction). Muon achieves the highest ‖·‖_msp margin, with NGD ranking second — a phenomenon the authors explain by noting that the spectral norm of the single-row output layer equals its ℓ₂ norm. Muon-Adam is shown to maximize the predicted hybrid norm, sitting between pure Muon and pure Adam in its margin profile. All results hold for both squared-ReLU and ReLU activations, though squared-ReLU tends to yield higher absolute margin values for ℓ∞-maximizing algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias analysis of steepest descent in homogeneous models (Tsilivis et al. 2025; Gunasekar et al. 2018a) from exact gradient-based methods to momentum-based optimizers (Adam, Muon, Signum), and extends the linear-model results for Adam (Zhang et al. 2024) and Muon/spectral descent (Fan et al. 2025) to the broader class of smooth homogeneous networks. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an 'approximate steepest descent' framework: rather than requiring the trajectory to exactly follow steepest descent, the paper shows that KKT stationarity of the limiting direction is preserved even when the optimizer only approximately tracks the steepest descent direction, which is the regime in which momentum-based optimizers operate. This also departs from prior Adam analyses (Wang et al. 2021) that relied on a stability constant ε dominating the denominator asymptotically, which the authors argue is unfaithful to practical behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since Adam and Muon are used near-universally for training large language models and vision transformers, understanding which margin each optimizer implicitly maximizes provides a principled lens for understanding their generalization behavior and potential vulnerabilities (e.g., adversarial robustness, privacy attacks via KKT satisfaction). The paper identifies several open questions: whether directional convergence can be proved for Adam and Muon (as was done for gradient descent by Ji and Telgarsky 2020), whether the results extend to non-smooth models like ReLU networks without additional trajectory assumptions, and whether the margin-maximization bias holds for non-homogeneous architectures. The practical implication is that optimizer choice is not neutral with respect to the geometry of the solution found, which could inform deliberate optimizer selection for targeted generalization properties.
