## Problem

Standard RL assumes instantaneous observation, action selection, and execution, but real-world cyber-physical systems experience stochastic, time-varying delays that are unobservable at decision time. Existing approaches either enforce a fixed worst-case delay (conservative and wasteful when actual delays are typically much lower) or, in the case of DCAC, offer no control over when actions are applied. No prior method enables an agent to make informed, adaptive decisions under random unobservable delays while handling packet loss and out-of-order delivery.

## Input / Output

The agent receives observation packets o_t = (t, s_t, b_t, δ_t, c_t) containing the current state, action buffer contents, the delay of the last received packet, and a shift counter, along with memorized previously-sent action packets. It produces an action packet a_t = (t, M) where M is an L×h matrix: each row i encodes the sequence of h actions to execute if the packet arrives at time t+i, and each column j specifies the fallback action if no new packet arrives at step j. The interaction layer's action buffer selects the appropriate row upon packet arrival and shifts columns forward when no new packet arrives, so the agent never knows which of its L candidate rows will actually be executed.

## Method

ACDA combines the interaction layer framework with a model-based distribution agent. A heuristic (Algorithm 1) assumes temporal correlation in delays to estimate which previously-sent actions will be applied before the current packet arrives, selecting the first-column action from the k-th row of each memorized packet. A three-component model (EMBED as MLP, STEP as GRU, EMIT as MLP) embeds the distribution p(s_{t+k} | s_t, â_1, ..., â_k) into a fixed-size vector z, solving the variable-length input problem for the policy MLP. The policy π_θ(a|z) generates actions conditioned on this distribution embedding, allowing it to reason about state uncertainty at the execution time. Training follows a SAC-based actor-critic setup with a BPQL-style critic operating on undelayed MDP states, while the model is trained online via KL-divergence minimization over multi-step state predictions from a replay buffer.

## Results

ACDA achieves the best average return in 24 out of 25 benchmark configurations (5 MuJoCo locomotion environments × 3 delay processes), with the sole exception being Ant-v4 under M/M/1 queue delays where BPQL edges ahead (3074.17 vs. 2898.46). The margins are substantial: on HalfCheetah-v4 with real-world WiFi delays (DLib), ACDA reaches 8368.57 versus the next-best VDPO at 4159.15 (2.0×), and on Walker2d-v4/DLib it scores 4424.82 versus BPQL's 994.69 (4.4×). Across the simulated Gilbert-Elliot process GE₁,₂₃, ACDA outperforms the strongest constant-delay baseline (BPQL) by 50–220% depending on the environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACDA outperforms all five baselines in 24 of 25 benchmark configurations, with the only loss being Ant-v4 under M/M/1 queue delays where BPQL (3074.17) narrowly beats ACDA (2898.46). Against the strongest constant-delay baseline BPQL, ACDA gains 52% on Ant-v4/GE₁,₂₃ (4112.78 vs. 2691.88), 790% on Humanoid-v4/GE₁,₂₃ (4608.76 vs. 585.19), and 344% on Walker2d-v4/DLib (4424.82 vs. 994.69). Against VDPO, the advantage is even larger in most settings, e.g., 90% on HalfCheetah-v4/DLib (8368.57 vs. 4159.15) and 353% on Walker2d-v4/GE₁,₂₃ (3863.59 vs. 846.88). Dreamer (world-model baseline) is consistently outperformed by 2–8×, while DCAC (the only prior method handling unobservable random delays) and vanilla SAC perform worst, often scoring below 1000 on most environments. The paper notes that even when constant-delay baselines are given an optimistic (lower) assumed worst-case delay, ACDA still wins 15 of 25 benchmarks, confirming that the adaptiveness of the interaction layer—not just the policy architecture—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAC actor-critic training framework and the BPQL critic simplification for delayed settings, but departs fundamentally from the fixed-delay paradigm that underpins BPQL, VDPO, and most prior delayed-RL work. The key conceptual departure is the interaction layer, which reframes the problem as a POMDP with an action buffer and action packets, enabling the agent to handle random unobservable delays while retaining control over which action is applied. The model-based distribution agent (GRU-based STEP over EMBED/EMIT) is a novel architecture that embeds state distributions rather than single states, distinguishing it from RSSM-based approaches like Dreamer and from the direct MLP policies used in constant-delay methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interaction layer provides a general, deployable framework for any RL agent operating over a network with stochastic latency, making it practically relevant for cyber-physical systems and remote robotics. The real-world WiFi delay data confirming temporal correlation (resembling Gilbert-Elliot dynamics) validates the core heuristic assumption and suggests the approach will transfer well to real deployments. However, convergence and optimality guarantees remain open, the heuristic degrades under i.i.d. delays, and evaluation is limited to MuJoCo locomotion with artificial 5% action noise, leaving open whether the gains persist in more complex or truly stochastic physical environments.
