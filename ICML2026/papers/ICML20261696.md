## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization solvers depend on expensive near-optimal labels from classical solvers and handcrafted feasibility heuristics, limiting their applicability to general mixed integer linear programs (MILPs) where such heuristics are unavailable. Moreover, most neural solvers are designed for purely integer variables, while real-world optimization problems naturally couple discrete and continuous decisions. This paper asks whether general MILPs can be solved in a fully unsupervised manner without heuristic repair or explicit neural modeling of continuous variables.

## Input / Output

The method takes a MILP instance (matrices A, B, vectors c, d, b) and encodes it as a bipartite graph with three node types—integer variables (6-dim features including the current predicted solution), continuous variables (5-dim features), and constraints (1-dim RHS features)—connected by edges encoding constraint coefficients. The neural network outputs only a binary assignment for the integer variables; the continuous variables are then recovered by solving the resulting LP with Gurobi, so the final output is a complete feasible MILP solution. This decomposition means the network never directly predicts continuous values, isolating the combinatorial hardness to the discrete backbone.

## Method

The solver reformulates the MILP via Benders decomposition so that fixing integer variables reduces the continuous subproblem to an LP, then applies a learned Langevin dynamics sampler exclusively over the integer space. Gradients of the LP value function with respect to integer variables are obtained in closed form via the envelope theorem (−Aᵀu*, where u* is the dual solution), so a single LP solve yields both the objective evaluation and the subgradient needed for the energy-based update. Infeasible integer assignments are handled by solving an auxiliary LP that produces a direction pushing the solution back into the feasible region. The transition distribution is parameterized by a GNN on the bipartite graph and trained unsupervised using one of three losses—REINFORCE (policy gradient), Erdős (expected energy with a Gauss-Newton second-order correction), or Gumbel-Softmax (differentiable sampling)—all minimizing the expected energy after a single Langevin step without any labeled solutions.

## Results

On CFLP-(100,100), the best unsupervised variant (Erdős loss) achieves a 6.24% objective gap with 100% feasibility in 4.73s, while the supervised SL+LP baseline achieves 4.78% gap. On CFLP-(200,200), the gap widens to 11.96% (28.12s). On the harder IP and LB benchmarks with downstream solvers, the Gumbel variant achieves the best gaps of −22.05% (IP) and 0.01% (LB), and is the only method producing feasible solutions without any post-processing (all other methods yield NaN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four neural MILP solvers (SL, DIFUSCO, FMIP, DiffILO) and the Gurobi reference solver. FMIP and DiffILO fail catastrophically on CFLP, producing 0% feasible solutions even after LP post-processing, while the proposed method consistently achieves 100% feasibility. Among methods that do produce feasible solutions, the supervised SL+LP baseline achieves a smaller gap (4.78% vs. 6.24% on CFLP-100), and DIFUSCO+LP is also slightly better (3.50% gap) but at 2.2× the inference time. On the harder IP and LB benchmarks with downstream solvers (Neural Diving, Predict-and-Search, Apollo), the proposed Gumbel variant achieves the best gaps (−22.05% on IP, 0.01% on LB), outperforming FMIP (−21.09%, 0.04%) and DIFUSCO (13.08%, 1.66%). Without any post-processing, the proposed method is the only approach that yields feasible solutions on IP and LB, whereas all baselines produce infeasible outputs (NaN). Overall, the method trades a modest quality gap versus supervised baselines for complete elimination of label dependence and guaranteed feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Langevin dynamics framework for discrete combinatorial optimization (Feng & Yang, 2025; Zhang et al., 2022) to the mixed-integer setting by exploiting the Benders decomposition structure: integer variables are sampled by a learned Langevin neural network while continuous variables are recovered via an exact LP solver. The unsupervised training objective draws on the Erdős loss (Karalias & Loukas, 2020) and is augmented with a Gauss-Newton second-order correction and a Gumbel-Softmax variant. The key departure from prior neural CO solvers (DIFUSCO, FMIP, DiffILO) is the explicit variable-type decomposition and the use of the envelope theorem to obtain subgradients through the LP subproblem, rather than modeling all variables homogeneously within the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first unsupervised neural solvers that handles general MILPs (with continuous variables) and achieves 100% feasibility without post-processing, addressing a gap the authors identify in the literature. The clean decomposition insight—that NP-hardness resides solely in the integer space—provides a principled rationale for hybrid neural/classical architectures. However, the method is currently restricted to binary integer variables, relies on a commercial LP solver (Gurobi) as a black box at every sampling step, and still trails supervised baselines in solution quality on CFLP. Open questions include extension to general integer variables, scalability to very large MILPs where per-step LP solves become prohibitive, and whether the approach generalizes to non-linear or stochastic MILPs.
