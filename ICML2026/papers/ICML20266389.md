## Problem

The concept-based model (CM) literature treats information leakage—where concept representations encode information beyond their intended semantics—as a purely negative property to be eliminated. This paper argues that view is ill-posed and counterproductive: in real-world settings where concept sets are inherently incomplete, strictly non-leaky representations preclude both high task accuracy and effective test-time interventions, rendering CMs impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266389/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CMs consume RGB images (e.g., 299×299×3 for CUB-200) and, optionally, test-time concept interventions that override specific concept representations with ground-truth values. They output a class label distribution over L categories and a vector of k concept scores in [0,1]. A key experimental detail is that the incomplete CUB task uses only 22 concepts (7 of 28 groups) rather than the full 112, and interventions are applied to all concepts within a group simultaneously.

## Method

The paper introduces a formal definition of benign leakage requiring two conditions: sufficiency (the residual component R captures I(Y;X|C) when concepts are incomplete) and localization (concept-specific information is confined to the concept-aligned component C̄ᵢ). The core technical contribution is an architecture-agnostic sufficiency regularizer L_int that minimizes the task loss when all concepts are intervened on, requiring only one additional forward pass of the label predictor. A proof shows that under well-specification, minimizing L_int is equivalent to maximizing I(Y;R|C), thereby achieving sufficiency. The paper further argues that DNN simplicity bias causes L_int to implicitly encourage localization, and demonstrates empirically that adding L_int to previously unintervenable leaky models (Hybrid CBMs, CEMs without RandInt, Joint Logit CBMs) recovers intervenability without sacrificing task or concept fidelity.

## Results

On the incomplete CUB task (k=22 concepts), the well-conditioned Hybrid CBM (M_H, λ_int=5) achieves 73.31±0.26% task accuracy and 93.65±0.22% concept ROC-AUC, substantially outperforming the non-leaky Independent CBM (M_I) which reaches only 47.61±1.01% task accuracy while maintaining comparable concept fidelity (93.90±0.11%). The top-5 weight overlap between M_H's and M_I's label predictors is 4.31±0.86, statistically indistinguishable from the 4.45±0.75 overlap between two differently-seeded Independent CBMs, indicating that the leaky model reasons about concepts in a manner similar to the non-leaky baseline. A DNN trained with L_int but no concept alignment loss still achieves 80.79±0.11% mean concept ROC-AUC when intervened on selected neurons, demonstrating that sufficiency optimization implicitly encourages localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five CM architectures on incomplete CUB: Independent CBM (minimal leakage), Hybrid CBM (bypass channel), CEM (dynamic embeddings), ProbCBM (probabilistic embeddings), and Post-hoc/Residual Post-hoc CBM. The central finding is that the Hybrid CBM with the L_int regularizer (λ_int=5) achieves 73.31% task accuracy versus 47.61% for the Independent CBM—a 25.7-point gap—while maintaining nearly identical concept fidelity (93.65% vs. 93.90% ROC-AUC). CEMs and ProbCBMs, which use dynamic embedding representations, remain intervenable in incompleteness even without explicit L_int (due to their RandInt training implicitly approximating it), but their intervention gains are smaller than the L_int-regularized Hybrid CBM. Without L_int, large Hybrid CBMs and CEMs without RandInt become almost entirely unintervenable under incompleteness (non-increasing intervention curves), but adding L_int recovers intervenability across all tested architectures. The label-predictor weight analysis shows the well-conditioned Hybrid CBM's concept-to-class importance rankings are statistically indistinguishable from the Independent CBM's (overlap 4.31 vs. 4.45 for two seeded baselines), whereas the ill-conditioned Hybrid CBM drops to 2.01, confirming that proper conditioning preserves interpretable reasoning structure despite the presence of leakage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper directly challenges the dominant anti-leakage narrative in the concept bottleneck model (CBM) literature, which has motivated methods for preventing (Marconato et al., 2022), mitigating (Havasi et al., 2022), and quantifying leakage (Parisini et al., 2025). It builds on the CBM framework of Koh et al. (2020) and its leakage-enabling variants (Hybrid CBMs, CEMs, ProbCBMs, PCBMs) to argue that in concept-incomplete settings, some leakage is necessary for task fidelity. The paper's key technical contribution is the sufficiency regularizer L_int, which it shows unifies several existing inductive biases (independent CBM training, RandInt in CEMs, intervention-aware losses) as special cases, and whose minimization provably maximizes I(Y;R|C) under well-specification assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the paper's central claim holds—that well-conditioned leaky CMs retain intervenability and concept-fidelity properties comparable to non-leaky ones—then the field's current push toward leakage elimination may be counterproductive, particularly for label-free and foundation-model-based CMs where concept sets are inherently incomplete. The practical takeaway (add L_int to training) is simple and architecture-agnostic, but the simplicity-bias explanation for why L_int induces localization remains a hypothesis rather than a proven mechanism, and the empirical evidence is concentrated on CUB-200 with ResNet-18 and linear predictors. Open questions include principled, architecture-agnostic metrics for benign leakage, whether the results hold for non-linear label predictors and larger backbones, and how to extend the framework to concept-based foundation models at scale.
