## Problem

Latent action models trained on unlabeled video learn per-clip inverse dynamics that are non-identifiable across contexts: the same semantic action (e.g., 'move forward') maps to different latent directions in different scenes because local reconstruction objectives provide no shared coordinate system. This cross-context non-identifiability, compounded by shortcut learning of context-dependent visual cues, prevents the learned latent actions from serving as a transferable control interface for video world models.

## Input / Output

The LAM consumes 16-frame video clips at 272×480 and outputs a 32-dimensional latent action per frame transition. The world model takes a single initial frame (540p) plus a sequence of latent actions (grouped in blocks of 4 to match the 3D VAE temporal compression) and generates a 97-frame action-conditioned video. After adaptation, explicit environment actions (e.g., 8 discrete navigation/camera controls) are mapped to the latent action space via a learned adapter, so the same DiT backbone can be driven by either inferred or ground-truth controls.

## Method

Olaf-World is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a β-VAE latent action model (causal spatio-temporal Transformer encoder + spatial decoder) augmented with SeqΔ-REPA, a sequence-level alignment loss that computes a target effect direction τ* as the mean temporal feature difference from a frozen V-JEPA2 video encoder, then aligns the clip-averaged latent action (projected via an MLP) to τ* via cosine similarity, enforcing a shared action-effect coordinate system across contexts. Stage 2 pretrains a SkyReels-V2 1.3B DiT with flow-matching, injecting per-step latent actions through a linear projection into the timestep embedding and per-block AdaLN-Zero modulation. For adaptation to a new action space, a lightweight embedding-table adapter (initialized with class-wise latent prototypes) plus rank-16 LoRA on the DiT is fine-tuned on as few as one labeled video (~1 minute), preserving the globally aligned latent control semantics learned from passive video.

## Results

On the MIND benchmark, Olaf-World achieves the lowest Relative Pose Error (RPE) across all adaptation budgets and both camera perspectives (1st-P and 3rd-P), indicating the most faithful action following. With 50 adaptation videos on 3rd-P, Olaf-World reaches RPE-Trans of 0.0222 and RPE-Rot of 0.2082, compared to AdaWorld's 0.0393 and 0.3353. In cross-domain linear probing, Olaf-World achieves 0.6250 Macro-F1 (1st→3rd) versus AdaWorld's 0.4820, and 0.8256 in-domain (3rd→3rd) versus 0.4827. On unseen visual contexts, Olaf-World attains RPE-Rot of 1.2221 versus AdaWorld's 1.7063, confirming better generalization under appearance shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: AdaWorld (latent-action pretraining with vanilla β-VAE) and DirectAct (direct conditioning on ground-truth actions without latent-action pretraining). Olaf-World consistently outperforms AdaWorld on action accuracy (RPE) across all adaptation budgets and both camera perspectives, with the largest gap at 50 videos on 3rd-P (RPE-Rot: 0.2082 vs. 0.3353, a 38% reduction). On cross-domain linear probing, Olaf-World improves Macro-F1 by 14–34 points over AdaWorld (e.g., 0.6250 vs. 0.4820 for 1st→3rd), confirming a more transferable latent action space. DirectAct with 0 adaptation videos reduces to standard image-to-video generation and shows poor controllability (RPE-Rot > 0.79), while with 50 videos it still lags behind both latent-action methods on RPE, suggesting that latent-action pretraining provides a more effective control interface than direct action conditioning under matched LoRA capacity. On unseen visual contexts (Table 3), Olaf-World achieves the lowest RPE-Rot (1.2221) versus AdaWorld (1.7063) and DirectAct (1.2343), indicating superior OOD robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent action model (LAM) paradigm—where an inverse-dynamics encoder infers per-step latents from observed transitions and a forward model predicts future frames—by identifying and fixing a fundamental identifiability gap: step-wise β-VAE objectives leave the latent coordinate system unconstrained across contexts. The key methodological departure is SeqΔ-REPA, which repurposes the representation-alignment idea (REPA, VideoREPA, MoAlign) from feature-to-feature matching to a control-to-effect alignment, anchoring integrated latent actions to temporal feature differences from a frozen self-supervised video encoder (V-JEPA2). The world-model backbone (SkyReels-V2 DiT with flow-matching) and the adaptation protocol (LoRA + action adapter) follow standard practice, so the contribution is concentrated in the latent-action learning objective and its theoretical justification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-context non-identifiability formalization (Proposition A.1) clarifies why prior LAMs fail at transfer even with strong reconstruction, and the β-VAE remark showing the KL term only reduces symmetry to signed permutations is a useful theoretical clarification for the field. Practically, the demonstrated data efficiency—adapting to a new action space with ~1 minute of labeled video—could significantly lower the barrier to deploying action-controllable world models in new domains. Open questions include whether the single aggregated effect signal can be factorized (ego vs. agent vs. environment), whether hierarchical latent actions improve long-horizon rollouts, and whether physics-grounded constraints can be layered on top of effect-aligned latents for contact-rich manipulation.
