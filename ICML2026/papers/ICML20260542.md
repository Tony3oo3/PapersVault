## Problem

Existing receptor-based molecular generation methods capture only pairwise amino acid interactions in protein pockets, neglecting higher-order spatial dependencies among multiple residues. Additionally, they lack a mechanism to dynamically integrate pocket and molecular information during generation, limiting the target specificity and quality of produced molecules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260542/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket 3D structure (PDB format) as input, which is decomposed into a residue-level hypergraph (amino acid nodes with hyperedges grouping Cα atoms within 5 Å) and an atomic-level graph (atom nodes with bond-type edges). The output is a generated molecule in canonical SMILES string form, produced autoregressively. During training, the corresponding ligand SMILES is also encoded as a sequence to provide molecular features for the bidirectional fusion module.

## Method

TSMGen represents the protein pocket at two scales: a residue-level hypergraph processed by a 2-layer Hypergraph Neural Network (node→hyperedge→node message passing) followed by Set2Set pooling, and an atomic-level graph processed by 5-layer message passing with a Jumping Knowledge Network and Set2Set pooling. A Context-Aware Bidirectional Fusion (CABF) module then performs dual-layer gated cross-attention: molecular features query pocket features in the first layer, and pocket features query molecular features in the second layer, with results combined via element-wise addition after sequence-length replication. The fused feature conditions an autoregressive SMILES generator trained with cross-entropy loss, enabling the model to produce molecules whose topology is steered by both local atomic geometry and global residue-level spatial organization of the pocket.

## Results

On the CrossDocked test set (100 held-out protein-ligand pairs), TSMGen achieves the best Vina Score of −7.642, the highest High Affinity of 0.631, the top QED of 0.659, the best SA score of 0.762, and the highest Diversity of 0.753 among all compared methods. In the case study on β-secretase (PDB 7D5U), the top generated candidate reaches a binding energy of −10.2 kcal/mol, surpassing the reference ligand (−9.0 kcal/mol), and 90% of 50 generated molecules are predicted synthesizable in ≤6 steps by AiZynthFinder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSMGen outperforms all seven baselines on Vina Score, with the closest competitor PMDM (−7.572) trailing by 0.070. On High Affinity, TSMGen (0.631) edges out PMDM (0.628) and substantially surpasses DrugGPS (0.565) and FLAG (0.580). For drug-likeness, TSMGen leads in QED (0.659 vs. DrugGPS's 0.613) and SA (0.762 vs. FLAG's 0.745), while its Lipinski score (4.966) is marginally below PMDM (4.975) but well above LiGAN (4.002) and DTF-diffusion (4.403). TSMGen also achieves the highest Diversity (0.753), exceeding DTF-diffusion (0.740) and PMDM (0.709). The weakest baseline, LiGAN, lags significantly on nearly every metric (Vina −6.032, QED 0.365), highlighting the advantage of explicit 3D pocket modeling over voxelized density approaches. Overall, TSMGen wins or ties on 6 of 7 reported metrics, with only Lipinski slightly behind PMDM.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSMGen sits within the receptor-based molecular generation lineage, where prior methods (LiGAN, DrugGPS, FLAG, PMDM) represent protein pockets via Coulomb matrices, voxel grids, or pairwise graphs. Its key architectural departure is the use of residue-level hypergraphs to capture many-body spatial dependencies among amino acids—going beyond the pairwise edges used by standard GNNs—combined with an atomic-level GNN for fine-grained geometric detail. The Context-Aware Bidirectional Fusion module repurposes gated cross-attention (common in multimodal NLP) to perform a two-way, sequence-length-aware fusion between pocket and molecule embeddings, a design not previously applied in this specific pocket-conditioned generation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete gaps in pocket-conditioned generation—higher-order residue interactions and bidirectional pocket-molecule information flow—and shows consistent (if moderate) gains across binding affinity, drug-likeness, and synthetic accessibility on CrossDocked. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset and a single case-study target (β-secretase, PDB 7D5U), and the model treats the pocket as a static structure, ignoring conformational flexibility that is known to affect binding. Open questions include whether the hypergraph formulation generalizes to larger or multi-site pockets, whether the bidirectional fusion adds value beyond a simpler unidirectional conditioning, and how the approach performs on targets with sparse or no known ligands.
