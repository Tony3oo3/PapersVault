## Problem

Persistent LLM failures—sycophancy, incomplete logical generalization, and confident incorrect responses—are typically treated as isolated shortcomings addressed by bespoke remedies like curated datasets or prompt engineering. The paper argues these failures share a common root cause: the modeling assumption that behavior can be specified and evaluated independently on single input-output pairs, making relational inconsistencies invisible to pointwise objectives. No unified framework existed for specifying, optimizing, or evaluating the structured relationships between a model's outputs across related inputs.

## Input / Output

Rather than a single prompt, the framework consumes structured sets of related inputs (paraphrases, counterfactuals, temporal variants, different user framings) paired with their corresponding model outputs, and evaluates them jointly through a consistency function φ. At the meta-level, the input additionally includes a self-referential query (e.g., 'explain your own computation' or 'state your confidence') whose response is checked for consistency against the instance-level behaviors. The output is either a scalar consistency score for evaluation, or—when used as a training signal—updated model parameters that satisfy the relational constraint across the input set.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified formal framework in which a consistency function φ scores sets of input-output pairs, and shows that a wide variety of existing alignment techniques (sycophancy reduction, factual consistency, calibration, CoT faithfulness, self-red-teaming, RLAIF) are special cases of a single objective. It provides three optimization formulations—hard constraint, soft regularizer, and posterior regularization—that decouple the semantics of the constraint from the optimization procedure. The framework is instantiated at two levels: instance-level (invariances and equivariances over input-output pairs) and meta-level (consistency between a model's self-descriptions and its actual behaviors). This reframing enables both diagnosing existing failures as specific consistency violations and specifying emerging capabilities like introspection and self-improvement under a common set of algorithmic tools.

## Results

This is a position paper that proposes a unifying theoretical framework rather than reporting quantitative experimental results. The paper formalizes self-consistency as a general objective (Equations 3–8) and demonstrates that a wide range of existing alignment techniques—sycophancy reduction, factual consistency, chain-of-thought faithfulness, calibration, RLAIF, and self-red-teaming—can be recovered as special cases of a single consistency function φ over sets of input-output pairs. Three optimization strategies (hard constraint, soft regularizer, posterior regularization) are specified for enforcing these objectives, and seven case studies illustrate the framework's coverage. No new models are trained or benchmarked; the contribution is conceptual unification and a proposed training paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare its method against baselines with quantitative metrics, as it is a position paper proposing a framework rather than a new algorithm. Instead, it demonstrates that existing methods from the literature—consistency training for sycophancy (Irpan et al., 2025), deductive closure training for factual consistency (Akyurek et al., 2024), generator-validator consistency (Li et al., 2024b), RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024), and self-distillation (Zhang et al., 2019)—are all recoverable as instantiations of the same general self-consistency objective with different choices of the relational constraint φ. The paper's key claim is that this unification enables shared data-generation, optimization, and evaluation pipelines across previously disparate alignment tasks, rather than requiring bespoke interventions for each failure mode. The authors acknowledge that consistency training can sometimes amplify sycophancy in controlled settings (Africa & Mani, 2026) and that degenerate fixed points may satisfy φ while producing unhelpful outputs, motivating joint optimization with standard SFT/RLHF objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper does not build on a single prior method but rather unifies a wide range of existing alignment and interpretability techniques—sycophancy invariance (Sharma et al., 2024), factual equivariance (Akyurek et al., 2024), CoT faithfulness (Chen et al., 2025), calibration (Damani et al., 2026), RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024), and self-distillation—under a single relational consistency objective φ. It departs from the standard pointwise training paradigm (SFT, RLHF) by arguing that the correct level of specification for many LM desiderata is relational, across sets of related inputs, rather than per-datapoint. The framework draws conceptual inspiration from invariance/equivariance in geometric deep learning (Bronstein et al., 2021) and posterior regularization (Ganchev et al., 2010), but applies these ideas at the output/behavior level rather than the architectural level, and extends them to a novel meta-level where the model's self-descriptions must be consistent with its own behaviors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a common language and optimization toolkit for a fragmented landscape of alignment techniques, potentially enabling shared data-generation, training, and evaluation pipelines across sycophancy, bias, factual consistency, and introspection. The meta-level extension (self-description and self-alignment as consistency constraints) opens genuinely new capability directions—automated self-red-teaming and scientific hypothesis generation—that are not well-addressed by existing methods. Key open questions the paper raises include: whether consistency training can entrench misalignment (citing Africa & Mani, 2026), when enforcing consistency induces harmful rigidity versus beneficial robustness, and whether strong consistency pressure could inadvertently encourage the formation of a latent self-representation with agentic implications. As a position paper without experimental validation, its practical impact depends on whether the community adopts the framing and whether the proposed optimization procedures (hard/soft constraints, posterior regularization) scale effectively.
