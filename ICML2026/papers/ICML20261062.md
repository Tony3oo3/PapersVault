## Problem

Existing device-cloud LLM collaboration frameworks rely on external binary classifiers as routers to decide whether to process a query locally or offload it to the cloud. These routers judge difficulty solely from surface-level prompt features, which fails for complex reasoning tasks where structurally similar problems vary greatly in difficulty. Moreover, treating routing and on-device model optimization as decoupled two-stage processes ignores the potential for the model to jointly optimize its problem-solving ability and its collaboration strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math problem or arithmetic puzzle) and produces a reasoning response that is either entirely generated by the on-device LLM or jointly produced with a cloud LLM. The routing decision is embedded in the response itself: the on-device model either emits a final answer in <answer> tags or an <unknown> token to trigger cloud invocation, with the cloud response appended to form the complete output. Cloud usage is constrained to a budget ratio ρ (e.g., 30% of queries may invoke the cloud), and the on-device model is trained offline before deployment.

## Method

The paper proposes GAPG (Group-Adaptive Policy Gradient), an RL-based post-training algorithm that integrates routing optimization directly into the on-device LLM's training. It uses a collaboration-aware hierarchical reward that assigns a higher accuracy reward α_a for correct local answers and a lower coordination reward α_c for correct cloud-assisted answers, making the two mutually exclusive and incentivizing independent problem-solving. The group-level policy gradient samples G responses per prompt and computes an unbiased, variance-reduced gradient estimator (variance scales as O(1/G)) using the group mean as a baseline. Adaptive prompt filtering splits each training batch into locally-solvable prompts (D_b^1) and cloud-required prompts (D_b^2, capped at ρ|D_b^1|) to provide complementary learning signals and prevent policy collapse toward exclusive local execution or exclusive cloud offloading. Unlike GRPO, which normalizes advantages and treats all correct responses equally, GAPG preserves the reward hierarchy and explicitly enforces the cloud-usage constraint through the filtering mechanism.

## Results

On MATH-lighteval with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, GAPG achieves 80.4% overall accuracy (66.7% average across five benchmarks), compared to 43.7% for GRPO-only tuning and 93.8% for the full cloud LLM (DeepSeek-R1). With Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, GAPG reaches 79.5% on MATH-lighteval (61.7% average), again substantially outperforming all baselines while operating under a 30% cloud-call budget. On the Countdown symbolic reasoning task with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, GAPG improves accuracy by approximately 30% over task-tuning-only, nearly matching the cloud offloading ratio, and approaches the cloud LLM's performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAPG consistently outperforms all six baselines across both on-device models and all five math benchmarks. Against the strongest non-cloud baseline (Task-Tuning & Router with DeBERTa), GAPG gains +5.9% average accuracy on Qwen2.5-1.5B (66.7% vs. 60.8%) and +7.6% on Llama-3.2-3B (61.7% vs. 54.1%). The improvement over GRPO-only task tuning is even larger: +23.0% (Qwen) and +24.4% (Llama), demonstrating that the unified routing-and-reasoning training substantially exceeds what separate routing can achieve. Compared to the reasoning-based Router-R1 baseline (Table 7), GAPG leads by +4.8% (Qwen) and +4.9% (Llama) in average accuracy, while eliminating the need for a separately deployed router model. Against the full cloud LLM (DeepSeek-R1 at 93.8% average), GAPG narrows the gap from ~50 points (GRPO-only) to ~27 points (Qwen) or ~32 points (Llama) under a strict 30% cloud-call budget. The Collaboration-Aware Tuning baseline (Dr. GRPO + hierarchical rewards) underperforms GAPG by 13.6% (Qwen) and 8.8% (Llama), confirming that the group-level policy gradient and adaptive prompt filtering are critical beyond simply adding collaboration-aware rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) framework from DeepSeekMath by repurposing its group-sampling mechanism for a fundamentally different objective: jointly optimizing on-device reasoning and cloud-invocation routing within a single RL post-training loop. It departs from the dominant two-stage pipeline (tune model → train external router) by embedding routing as an internal policy capability, and from prior device-cloud collaboration work (AutoMix, Division-of-Thoughts, Route-and-Reason) by eliminating the separate router entirely. The hierarchical reward design and adaptive prompt filtering are novel algorithmic components that address the specific challenge of preventing policy collapse when two competitive behaviors (local solving vs. cloud offloading) are optimized simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified formulation eliminates the engineering overhead of training and maintaining a separate router, and the co-evolution of reasoning and routing means the model's offloading decisions are grounded in its actual problem-solving attempts rather than surface-level prompt features. A key limitation is the reliance on verifiable correctness rewards, restricting applicability to math and symbolic reasoning; extending to open-ended tasks would require quality-aware reward functions (e.g., LLM-as-a-Judge). The finding that GRPO's normalized advantages cause degenerate policy collapse in this hierarchical-reward setting provides a useful caution for applying standard RL post-training algorithms to multi-behavior objectives. Open questions include whether the learned routing generalizes to substantially different cloud models or to multi-turn interactive settings.
