## Problem

Pre-trained vision language models lack robust intuitive physics, and while supervised fine-tuning can improve within-task performance, it is unclear whether learning through active interaction with a physical environment (as humans do) yields more generalizable physical intuitions. This paper tests whether reinforcement learning from interaction produces models that transfer to new physical tasks better than supervised fine-tuning on the same tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263982/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Models receive 256×256 RGB images of 2–4 colored block towers rendered from a fixed camera angle in ThreeDWorld, paired with a text prompt specifying the task. Outputs are constrained to a binary Yes/No stability judgment or one or two integers (x-displacement in [−600, 600], y-displacement in [0, 1000]) wrapped in <answer> tags. A triplet variant additionally presents two intermediate frames showing the consequences of prior actions before requesting a final action, and evaluation includes real photographic block towers from Lerer et al. (2016).

## Method

The paper fine-tunes 4-bit quantized Qwen3-VL-8B (and ablates with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-32B, Llama-3.2-11B, Gemma-3-12B) using QLoRA adapters (rank 16, α=16) under two regimes: GRPO (16 sampled completions per prompt, group-relative advantage, Gaussian reward functions based on distance to the optimal block position) and standard SFT on optimal action sequences. Four task–dataset combinations are trained (binary-stability and x-only on top-block data; x-only and x-y on side-block data), each with 10,000 training images and 10,000 steps on a single A100. Generalization is assessed across all task pairs, to real images, and via linear-probe decodability of physical quantities (stability, x-offset) from intermediate-layer activations. Ablations include a multi-step triplet formulation, GSPO as an alternative RL algorithm, longer training horizons (up to 48K steps), varied adapter ranks, and blocked vs. interleaved multi-task training.

## Results

Both GRPO and SFT post-training bring Qwen3-VL-8B to near-ceiling performance on their respective training tasks after 10,000 steps: binary-stability top block reaches 0.943 (GRPO) / 0.969 (SFT) accuracy, x-only top block reaches 19.999 / 20 reward, x-only side block reaches 19.998 / 20, and x-y side block reaches 17.313 / 19.86. However, cross-task generalization is minimal: the best off-diagonal transfer (x-y side block → x-only side block) yields only 5.396 (GRPO) vs. 19.316 (SFT) reward, and all other off-diagonal entries remain near or below baseline. On real wooden block tower images from Lerer et al. (2016), the best model (GRPO binary-stability top block) achieves only 0.60 accuracy, well below human performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between interactive training (GRPO, a form of online RL) and non-interactive training (SFT, analogous to offline RL / behavioral cloning). The headline finding is that interaction confers no reliable advantage: on within-task performance both methods reach near-ceiling, and on cross-task generalization neither method consistently outperforms the other. In fact, SFT slightly outperforms GRPO on the x-y side block training task (19.86 vs. 17.313 reward) and on the x-y → x-only side block transfer (19.316 vs. 5.396), while GRPO shows a marginal edge on x-only top block → binary-stability top block (10.626 vs. 12.170 is actually SFT winning; GRPO wins on binary-stability → x-only top block at 0.788 vs. 0.624). Ablations with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-32B, Llama-3.2-11B, Gemma-3-12B, and an alternative RL algorithm (GSPO) all reproduce the same pattern: no method produces models that reliably generalize beyond their training task, and longer training (up to 48K steps) only worsens overfitting to the specific reward function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes a long-standing hypothesis from cognitive science—that interaction with an environment is essential for learning robust physical intuitions (Gibson, 1979; Piaget, 1952; Gopnik et al., 1999)—in the context of VLM post-training. It builds on prior work showing VLMs fail at intuitive physics tasks (Schulze Buschoff et al., 2025a; Balazadeh et al., 2025) and that SFT improves within-task performance without yielding generalizable intuitions (Schulze Buschoff et al., 2025b), but departs from Chu et al. (2025), who found RL generalizes better than SFT for arithmetic and navigation, by testing this specifically for physical reasoning. The block tower stability task itself follows Lerer et al. (2016) and Han et al. (2025), but this paper is the first to systematically compare interactive (GRPO) versus non-interactive (SFT) training for cross-task generalization in intuitive physics, augmented with a decodability analysis to disentangle competence from performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central negative finding—that single-step RL interaction confers no generalization advantage over SFT for intuitive physics—challenges a natural expectation grounded in developmental psychology and suggests that current post-training paradigms (SFT, GRPO, GSPO) are insufficient for instilling robust physical intuitions in VLMs. The decodability analysis revealing that relevant physical quantities (stability, x-offset) are already linearly decodable in the base model yet not recruited for out-of-distribution tasks points to a competence-performance gap that may require fundamentally different training objectives or longer-horizon interaction. Key open questions include whether multi-step, stateful interaction (where the model sees consequences of its actions) would change the picture, whether full fine-tuning rather than PEFT would help, and whether the multi-task training signal (GRPO retaining both tasks sequentially) can be scaled into a curriculum that produces genuinely generalizable physics.
