## Problem

Optimal experimental design for multinomial logit (MNL) models is computationally intractable because the action space is combinatorial (all subsets of size up to K from N items), and the Fisher information is matrix-valued and depends nonlinearly on the entire subset rather than decomposing additively across arms. Consequently, classical G-optimal design theory does not apply, and no prior work provided sample complexity guarantees for best assortment identification in MNL bandits with linear utilities and non-uniform revenues.

## Input / Output

The method takes N arms each represented by a d-dimensional feature vector, per-item revenue parameters r_i ∈ [0,1], a maximum assortment size K, and a failure probability δ. It produces a locally G-optimal design π̂ (a distribution over assortments computed at a nominal parameter θ₀) and, in the BSI application, the optimal assortment S* together with a data-dependent stopping time τ. A key structural detail is that each 'experiment' is a subset of arms (not a single arm), and the feedback is a single MNL choice from that subset, so the information matrix is determined by the full subset's choice probabilities rather than being a sum of per-arm contributions.

## Method

The paper develops a Frank-Wolfe algorithm for G-optimal design under MNL, where the linear maximization oracle (LMO) over the combinatorial assortment space is addressed via two complementary routes: (i) an exact reformulation as a 0-1 MILP through a quadratic-fractional-to-linear-fractional reduction followed by the Charnes-Cooper transformation, enabling solver-certified early stopping with a user-specified optimality gap, and (ii) a polynomial-time Schur-complement lifting that replaces the centered Fisher information with an uncentered (d+1)×(d+1) surrogate, yielding a ratio-of-sums objective solvable as a linear program. The Kiefer-Wolfowitz equivalence theorem is extended to the matrix-valued MNL Fisher information, establishing G-optimality ↔ D-optimality and a finite-support guarantee. For best assortment identification, the BSI-MNL algorithm combines a burn-in exploration phase, design-based sampling at an independently estimated nominal parameter, and a stopping rule comparing pessimistic best-assortment revenue against optimistic best-alternative revenue, yielding an instance-dependent sample complexity of Õ(d log N / Δ_min²).

## Results

The paper establishes the first instance-dependent sample complexity bound for best assortment identification in MNL bandits with linear utilities: $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\log N / \Delta_{\min}^2)$, matching the known lower bound up to a log factor. Empirically, BSI-MNL-Lift achieves nearly constant LMO runtime (0.15–0.18 s) across all tested (N, K) configurations, while BSI-MNL-MILP scales from ~2 s (N=30, K=3) to ~326 s (N=200, K=5). The brute-force oracle becomes infeasible at (N, K) = (200, 5) where $\binom{N}{K} \approx 2.5 \times 10^9$. All three variants produce comparable stopping times, confirming that the proposed methods retain statistical efficiency while drastically reducing computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines, as it is the first to study best assortment identification in MNL bandits with linear utilities and non-uniform revenues. Instead, it compares three internal variants: BSI-MNL-MILP (exact MILP reformulation with Gurobi), BSI-MNL-Lift (polynomial-time Schur-complement surrogate), and BSI-MNL-Oracle (brute-force enumeration over all $\binom{N}{K}$ assortments). In terms of statistical efficiency (stopping time $\tau$), all three variants perform comparably, as shown in Figure 1, confirming that neither the MILP early-stopping nor the lifted relaxation introduces meaningful statistical degradation. Computationally, BSI-MNL-Lift dominates: it maintains a near-constant ~0.16 s LMO runtime regardless of N or K, while BSI-MNL-Oracle's runtime grows combinatorially and becomes infeasible at (N, K) = (200, 5). BSI-MNL-MILP offers a middle ground, providing solver-certified optimality gaps at a cost that grows sub-exponentially (up to ~326 s at the largest tested instance). The lifted variant incurs a theoretical multiplicative factor of $(1 + \epsilon_{\text{lift}})$ in sample complexity, bounded by $\mathcal{O}(Ke^B)$, but this degradation is not observed empirically in the tested regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical G-optimal design theory (Kiefer & Wolfowitz, 1960) to the MNL model, where the Fisher information is matrix-valued, depends nonlinearly on the parameter, and is determined by the entire subset rather than decomposing additively across arms. It builds on the matrix-valued KW equivalence theorem of Mukherjee et al. (2024) and the confidence bound of Han et al. (2026), while addressing a computational bottleneck absent in prior linear-bandit pure exploration work (Soare et al., 2014; Jedra & Proutiere, 2020): the combinatorial action space of size O(N^K) makes naive enumeration infeasible. The two proposed LMO solutions—a 0-1 MILP reformulation via Charnes-Cooper transformation and a Schur-complement lifted surrogate reducible to a ratio-of-sums LP (Rusmevichientong et al., 2010)—are the key methodological departures from prior design-based bandit algorithms that assume individual-arm design spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sample complexity guarantee for best assortment identification in MNL bandits with linear utilities and non-uniform revenues, filling a clear gap between context-free MNL BSI and linear-bandit BAI literature. The lifted design's polynomial-time computation with a bounded approximation error (ε_lift = O(Ke^B)) offers a practical path to scalability that naive V_t-based designs cannot match, improving worst-case statistical efficiency by a factor of K^3 e^{5B}. Open questions include tightening the lifted design's approximation error, extending to non-linear utility models, and understanding whether the MILP's NP-hardness can be further mitigated for very large K. The empirical validation uses only d=5 and moderate N, leaving scalability to truly large-scale settings untested.
