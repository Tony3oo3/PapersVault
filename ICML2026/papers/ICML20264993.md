## Problem

Despite extensive empirical work on neural algorithmic reasoning, it remains unclear when MPNNs trained on finite data provably generalize to arbitrarily large graph inputs with worst-case guarantees. Prior theoretical analyses either focus on expressivity (what can be represented in principle) or provide only in-distribution/PAC-style bounds, leaving the size-generalization question open. The paper also extends the limited Bellman-Ford-specific result of Nerem et al. (2025), which relied on a non-differentiable regularizer.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on edge-weighted graphs with a designated source node (for SSSP) or plain edge-weighted graphs (for MST, knapsack), producing either node-level k-tuple invariants (e.g., shortest-path distance from source to each vertex) or graph-level invariants (e.g., MST cost, optimal knapsack value). A key structural requirement is that the source/root node must be explicitly marked in the input to provide the dynamic program's initial condition. Training uses small, explicitly constructed datasets (e.g., K+1 path instances for K-step Bellman-Ford) rather than large sampled sets, and the learned model must extrapolate to graphs of arbitrary size beyond the training range.

## Method

The paper introduces a finite Lipschitz class framework: it equips the graph input space V₁(𝒢) with a pseudo-metric, proves that MPNNs with normalized-sum, mean, or max/min aggregation satisfy finite Lipschitzness (Theorem 3), and then shows that minimizing a regularized empirical loss L_X(f_θ) = (1/N)Σ|f_θ(x_i)−y_i| + η·ReLU(B_θ − B_{f*}) on a finite ε-cover of the input space guarantees uniform approximation of the target algorithm to arbitrary precision (Theorem 2). For SSSP specifically, the authors construct an explicit K+1-instance training set of path graphs and replace the non-differentiable ℓ₀ penalty of Nerem et al. with a differentiable weighted ℓ₁ regularizer whose layer-specific weights enforce the correct sparsity pattern in the learned update functions. The framework also yields impossibility results: standard 1-WL MPNNs cannot approximate SSSP or MST without node individualization, and unnormalized-sum MPNNs fail the finite covering number condition on unbounded-degree graph spaces. Empirically, the authors validate size generalization on Erdős–Rényi and path graphs, confirming that the 1-iWL architecture and proposed ℓ₁-based regularizer outperform standard MPNNs and ℓ₂ regularization.

## Results

On the SSSP task, the standard 1-WL MPNN fails to generalize to unseen graphs, with test scores of 0.97–0.99 across all graph sizes (64 to 1024 nodes) and datasets, while the 1-iWL-equivalent MPNN achieves test scores of 0.0030–0.0038, demonstrating that the additional expressivity is essential for learning. For regularization (Table 2), the proposed differentiable ℓ_reg term yields test scores of 0.0032–0.0033 uniformly across 64–1024 nodes, outperforming standard ℓ₁ (0.0053–0.0061) and ℓ₂ (0.0133–0.0369) regularization, with ℓ₂ showing notably higher variance (±0.05). Size generalization is confirmed: test error does not increase as graph size grows from 64 to 1024 nodes for the 1-iWL MPNN with ℓ_reg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external SOTA methods but instead contrasts architectural and regularization choices to validate its theoretical predictions. The standard 1-WL MPNN (the weakest architecture) completely fails to learn SSSP, scoring ~0.97–0.99 (near-random) on all datasets and sizes, while the 1-iWL-equivalent MPNN achieves ~0.003, a gap of over 300× in test error. This empirically confirms Proposition 5's expressivity limitation: without node individualization, no MPNN can approximate SSSP. For regularization, the proposed layer-weighted ℓ₁-based ℓ_reg outperforms standard ℓ₁ by ~45% (0.0033 vs. 0.0056 at 1024 nodes) and ℓ₂ by ~10× (0.0033 vs. 0.0369), while also showing far lower variance (±0.0002 vs. ±0.0557 for ℓ₂). The ℓ_reg advantage grows with graph size, supporting the theoretical claim that the differentiable regularization enables better size generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural algorithmic reasoning (NAR) literature, which has been dominated by either empirical demonstrations or expressivity analyses (e.g., 1-WL characterizations by Morris et al. 2019, Xu et al. 2019), by introducing the first general learnability framework with worst-case size-generalization guarantees for MPNNs. It directly builds on and extends Nerem et al. (2025), which provided the only prior theoretical guarantee for learning a single graph algorithm (Bellman-Ford) with MPNNs but relied on a non-differentiable ℓ₀ regularization and a larger training set. The paper also connects to the broader metric-based generalization literature (Levie 2023, Rauchwerger et al. 2024, Vasileiou et al. 2024a) by establishing finite Lipschitzness for max/min-aggregation MPNNs—a gap not previously addressed—and to the 1-WL expressivity hierarchy by showing that individualization (1-iWL, (1,1)-WL) is necessary for learning SSSP and MST.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled characterization of which graph algorithms MPNNs can provably learn from finite data and extrapolate to arbitrary-size inputs, moving the NAR field beyond empirical benchmarks and expressivity-only analyses. The differentiable ℓ₁ regularization with layer-specific weights is a practical contribution that enables standard gradient-based training pipelines, removing the non-differentiability barrier in prior work. Key limitations include the non-constructive nature of the general training set requirement (Theorem 2), the lack of convergence guarantees for gradient descent, and the restriction to polynomial-time algorithms. Open questions include developing sampling-based methods for informative training set construction, bridging the learning-theoretic analysis with optimization convergence, and extending the framework to approximation algorithms for computationally hard problems.
