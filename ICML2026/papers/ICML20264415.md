## Problem

Existing uncertainty proxies for LLMs (token entropy, self-consistency, semantic entropy) operate at the single-shot or sequence level and miss trajectory-level breakdown signals in multi-turn, tool-using agentic interaction. In dual-control settings where both agent and user are stochastic, failures are triggered by sparse critical episodes—looping, incoherent tool use, user-agent miscoordination—that occur even when local generation appears confident, causing global averages of token uncertainty to systematically underweight the decisive segments.

## Input / Output

TRACER consumes a full multi-turn dialogue trajectory in a dual-control Dec-POMDP setting, where each step records the acting entity (agent or user), the emitted event (natural-language utterance or structured tool call), and the resulting environment observation (tool output, simulator response, or counterparty message). Token log-probabilities from the generating model are required for the surprisal component (omitted gracefully when unavailable, e.g., for black-box user simulators), and sentence embeddings are computed for all utterances and observations to drive similarity-based indicators. The output is a single non-negative scalar trajectory-level uncertainty score, computed by aggregating per-step risk values; higher scores indicate greater propensity for uncertainty-driven breakdown.

## Method

TRACER computes three families of per-step signals and aggregates them with a tail-focused risk functional. Stage I produces a content-aware normalized surprisal U_t by averaging −log p over only content-bearing tokens (filtered by stop-word exclusion, numeric-token exclusion, and a predictability threshold π₀), yielding an unbiased estimator of content-filtered cross-entropy that decomposes into aleatoric entropy plus epistemic KL mismatch. Stage II introduces situational-awareness indicators: a hybrid local repetition score D_a(t) (product of semantic cosine similarity and lexical Jaccard overlap over a sliding window of prior agent turns), an agent coherence gap D_o^A(t) (semantic distance between a tool action and its observation), and a user coordination gap D_o^U(t) (semantic distance between the agent's last message and the user's reply). Stage III forms a MAX-composite step risk r_t = max(U_t, αD_a, βD_o^A, γD_o^U) to preserve the dominant failure signal without dilution, then aggregates across the trajectory via a convex combination of the top-K tail mean (empirical CVaR) and the ℓ∞ norm, controlled by a weight w that interpolates between chronic and acute breakdown patterns. The paper provides theoretical guarantees: coherence of the tail functional (monotonicity, subadditivity, positive homogeneity), 1-Lipschitz stability under ℓ∞ perturbations, and a union-bound upper bound on breakdown probability under sparse-hazard and risk-dominates-hazard assumptions.

## Results

On τ²-bench, TRACER achieves the highest AUROC and AUARC across all model–domain combinations, with AUROC ranging from 0.673 (gemini-2.5-pro, Retail) to 0.809 (gemini-2.5-flash, Telecom) and AUARC from 0.517 (gemini-2.5-pro, Telecom) to 0.725 (gemini-2.5-pro, Retail). Relative to the strongest baseline, TRACER improves AUROC by 4.7%–37.1% and AUARC by 6%–55%. On ToolHop and ComplexFuncBench, TRACER similarly dominates, reaching AUROC 0.889 (gpt-5.4-mini, Computing) and AUARC 0.813 (gpt-5.4-mini, Genealogy). Early-warning analysis shows TRACER detects 68–97% of failures within the first 20% of trajectory progress, versus 54–82% for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACER outperforms all four baselines (Normalized Entropy, Self-Confidence, Semantic Entropy, and SAUP) on every model–domain pair across all three benchmarks. Against Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature 2024), the closest competitor, TRACER gains up to 18.8 AUROC points (gpt-4.1-mini, Telecom: 0.765 vs. 0.686) and up to 22.5 AUARC points (gemini-2.5-pro, Telecom: 0.517 vs. 0.395). Against SAUP (Zhao et al., ACL 2025), which propagates uncertainty through tool-call chains, TRACER achieves up to 37.1% relative AUROC improvement (gpt-4.1-mini, Airline: 0.742 vs. 0.531) and up to 55% relative AUARC improvement (gpt-4.1-mini, Airline: 0.615 vs. 0.403). The gap is largest for smaller models (gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-5.4-mini), where likelihood- and confidence-based proxies fail to separate easy from hard episodes, while TRACER's trajectory-level situational-awareness signals remain discriminative. No baseline matches or exceeds TRACER in any single cell of the main results tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACER extends the line of work on uncertainty estimation for LLM agents (e.g., SAUP by Zhao et al., 2025) and semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., 2024) by shifting the unit of analysis from single-shot token/sequence confidence to full multi-turn, dual-control trajectories. It draws on coherent risk measures from the CVaR literature (Chow et al., 2017) to define a tail-focused aggregation, and on situation-awareness concepts from agentic evaluation (Liu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024) to introduce explicit repetition and coherence-gap indicators. The key conceptual departure is treating failure as arising from sparse critical episodes rather than uniformly elevated token uncertainty, and modeling both agent and user as stochastic actors in a Dec-POMDP rather than scoring a single generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACER addresses a practical gap: deployed agentic systems (customer support, troubleshooting) need to detect when an interaction is derailing before it's too late, and standard confidence/entropy signals are blind to looping and coordination collapse. The early-warning results (detecting ~97% of failures within the first 20% of retail/telecom trajectories) suggest the metric could enable real-time intervention or graceful abstention. Limitations include reliance on token log-probabilities (unavailable for black-box user simulators), conservative theoretical bounds based on union bounds, and evaluation limited to three benchmarks with a small set of LLM backends. Open questions include whether the parameter grid search generalizes across domains without re-tuning, how the method behaves at much longer horizons, and whether the dual-control attribution can be made end-to-end differentiable.
