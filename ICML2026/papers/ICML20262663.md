## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) improves LLM reasoning accuracy but systematically reduces outcome diversity, causing the policy to collapse onto a narrow set of solutions. Existing diversity-preserving mechanisms—token-level entropy regularization and pairwise separation objectives—suffer from length bias, fail to capture semantic-level trajectory diversity, or optimize Pass@K at the expense of Pass@1. The paper asks whether an explicit set-level diversity objective can be integrated into group-based policy optimization to preserve multi-modal outcome distributions without sacrificing single-sample correctness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (e.g., a GSM8K math word problem or a Countdown arithmetic constraint) and produces a group of G sampled reasoning trajectories, each ending in a boxed final answer. During training, a pretrained embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) maps each trajectory to a fixed-dimensional vector, and pairwise cosine similarities (clamped to [0,1]) form a G×G kernel matrix used to compute per-trajectory diversity credits. At inference, the trained policy generates a single reasoning chain (temperature 0.5, top-p 0.9) whose final answer is scored for correctness; Pass@K is estimated unbiasedly from a pool of 64 samples.

## Method

SetPO defines a kernelized diversity functional F(P) = E_{y~P}[g(m_P(y))] where m_P(y) is the expected semantic similarity of y to the rest of the distribution and g(x) = −log(1+x) encodes diminishing marginal returns. For a finite rollout group of G trajectories, each trajectory's leave-one-out marginal contribution s_i = D(Ω) − D(Ω\{o_i}) is computed by re-evaluating the group diversity score with and without that trajectory, yielding an exact (not heuristic) measure of how much o_i adds to the set's semantic coverage. This s_i is then added as a weighted plug-in term to the standard group-normalized advantage (Â_i = Ā_i + λ·s_i) inside the GRPO, GSPO, or DAPO surrogate objective, requiring no architectural changes or additional critic. Theoretical analysis via Gâteaux derivatives and finite-set decompositions proves that s_i is strictly decreasing in every pairwise similarity k(o_i, o_t), so rarer trajectories provably receive higher diversity credit, and the overall objective follows a diminishing-returns principle that penalizes redundancy while rewarding novel semantic modes.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, SetPO+DAPO achieves the best average Pass@1 of 53.8% across six mathematical benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, College Math, AMC23, AIME24, AIME25), outperforming the strongest baseline DAPO (51.7%) by +2.1 points. The largest single gain is SetPO+GRPO over GRPO, improving average accuracy from 47.2% to 52.8% (+5.6), with a >40% relative improvement on AIME24 (15.4% → 21.6%). On the 1.5B scale, SetPO+GRPO and SetPO+DAPO both reach 46.7% average, surpassing GRPO (43.4%) by +3.3 points. On the Countdown task, SetPO+GRPO increases diversity width from 351 to 415 and average mode from 2.3 to 3.5, while consistently outperforming GRPO on Pass@K across all sampling budgets up to k=64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SetPO consistently outperforms all three group-based RL baselines (GRPO, GSPO, DAPO) across both 7B and 1.5B model scales on every benchmark. The most pronounced gain is over GRPO: SetPO+GRPO improves average Pass@1 by +5.6 points on 7B (47.2→52.8) and +3.3 on 1.5B (43.4→46.7), with the largest relative jump on AIME24 (+8.2 absolute, >40% relative). Against the strongest baseline DAPO, SetPO+DAPO still achieves +2.1 average on 7B and +2.0 on 1.5B, with the clearest per-benchmark wins on AIME24 (+3.6) and AIME25 (+2.5). Against GSPO, SetPO+GSPO gains +2.7 average on 7B, with the largest single-benchmark improvement on AIME24 (+3.7). The R1-zero-Div baseline (Yao et al., 2025) is only partially reported (GSM8K, MATH500, College Math) and is outperformed by SetPO+DAPO on all three available benchmarks (93.0 vs. 90.6 on GSM8K, 79.2 vs. 76.9 on MATH500, 49.5 vs. 47.5 on College Math). On the Countdown task, SetPO+GRPO surpasses GRPO on Pass@K for all k∈{1,…,64} under both 3-rollout and 7-rollout settings, with the gap widening as k increases, confirming improved coverage of distinct solution modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SetPO builds directly on the GRPO/DAPO/GSPO family of group-based policy optimization methods for RLVR, positioning itself as a modular advantage-shaping plug-in rather than a new optimization algorithm. It departs from prior diversity-preserving approaches that rely on token-level entropy regularization (which introduces length bias) or pairwise trajectory separation, instead introducing a set-level kernelized diversity objective with a leave-one-out marginal contribution mechanism. The theoretical framework draws on Gâteaux derivative analysis of distributional perturbations and connects to the diminishing-returns principle, providing a principled anti-redundancy guarantee that prior entropy-based or MMD-based regularizers lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented failure mode of RLVR training—mode collapse and reduced outcome diversity—with a practical, low-overhead (<10%) solution that is compatible with existing group-based RL pipelines. The consistent gains across 1.5B to 32B scales and multiple benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AIME, AMC, Countdown) strengthen confidence in generalizability. Open questions include whether the reliance on an external embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) for semantic similarity introduces domain-specific biases, whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical reasoning tasks (code, science), and whether the leave-one-out mechanism scales gracefully to much larger group sizes beyond the typical G=6–7 used in practice.
