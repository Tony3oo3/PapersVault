## Problem

Existing test-time debiasing methods for VLMs define a single ideal embedding point to correct bias, which fails to capture the structured cluster geometry of sensitive groups and leaves sample-level disparities unaddressed. Moreover, most such methods are designed for binary attributes and lack a unified treatment of multi-class sensitive attributes, where group structure cannot be adequately represented by a point estimate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264695/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text query encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, a set of labeled training images with sensitive-attribute and concept annotations (used to build empirical reference distributions in the embedding space), and a learned probabilistic prompt distribution represented as M learnable offset vectors. At inference, K Monte Carlo samples are drawn from the prompt distribution to approximate the expected embedding shift, producing a single debiased query embedding that is then used for zero-shot classification or top-m image retrieval against the frozen image encoder.

## Method

W4D reframes debiasing as aligning the query-embedding distribution to per-group reference distributions under the 2-Wasserstein distance, using a variance-of-distances objective that enforces equidistance across all sensitive groups in a single permutation-invariant term. Performance preservation is enforced via a contrastive Wasserstein loss that pulls the debiased distribution toward concept-consistent group distributions and pushes it away from concept-inconsistent ones, while a bias-subspace orthogonality loss constrains the mean shift to lie outside sensitive directions. The distributional perturbation is realized through probabilistic prompts: a finite set of M learnable offsets forms an empirical distribution over embedding shifts, optimized end-to-end via Sinkhorn-regularized OT (L iterations per Wasserstein call) without updating any VLM parameters. The overall objective combines the debiasing variance term, the contrastive preservation loss, and the orthogonality penalty with tunable weights, solved over T gradient steps at test time.

## Results

On stereotype-query retrieval, W4D achieves the lowest KL divergence and MaxSkew across all four dataset–attribute–backbone combinations. For example, on UTKFace gender (CLIP-ViT-B-P16), W4D reduces KL from 0.1575 (baseline) to 0.0048 and MaxSkew from 0.8204 to 0.0833; on FairFace race (CLIP-ViT-L-P14), KL drops from 0.1865 to 0.0466 and MaxSkew from 1.2872 to 0.6639. In zero-shot classification (CelebA hair-color, Facet job), W4D maintains Worst-Group AUC-ROC within ~1–2% of baseline CLIP while cutting MaxSkew by 30–40% relative to the strongest prior method (BendVLM). The advantage is most pronounced for multi-class attributes (race, skin tone), where W4D's distributional alignment yields 2–5× lower KL than the next-best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W4D outperforms all five baselines on both KL divergence and MaxSkew across every dataset, attribute, and CLIP backbone tested. Against the strongest prior method BendVLM, W4D achieves roughly 2× lower KL on UTKFace gender (0.0048 vs. 0.0096, CLIP-B) and 2.3× lower KL on FairFace race (0.0383 vs. 0.0879, CLIP-B), with the gap widening for multi-class attributes. Orth-Proj. and Orth-Cal. (Chuang et al.) actually worsen bias on FairFace gender (KL 0.3155 and 0.4043 vs. baseline 0.1179), confirming their instability in multi-class settings, while W4D remains consistently below baseline. DeAR (Seth et al.) provides only marginal improvement over the unmodified CLIP baseline (e.g., UTKFace gender KL 0.1315 vs. 0.1575), whereas W4D reduces it by over 97%. SFID (Jung et al.) is the second-best baseline overall but still trails W4D by 3–8× on KL in the multi-class race setting. In zero-shot classification (CelebA, Facet), W4D matches or slightly exceeds baseline Worst-Group AUC-ROC (e.g., 0.7518 vs. 0.6995 on CelebA CLIP-L) while achieving the lowest MaxSkew, demonstrating a favorable fairness–utility trade-off that no single baseline replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

W4D extends the test-time debiasing paradigm for VLMs (BendVLM, Orth-Proj, Orth-Cal, SFID) by replacing the point-based 'ideal embedding' formulation with a distributional alignment objective under the Wasserstein distance. The probabilistic prompt mechanism draws from the prompt-learning literature (Kwon et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023), but is repurposed here to induce controlled distributional perturbations rather than fixed text-side adjustments. The optimal-transport machinery (Sinkhorn solver, entropy-regularized Kantorovich formulation) is borrowed from the OT literature and applied to fairness for the first time in this VLM debiasing context, providing a geometry-aware distance that captures intra-group spread and inter-group structure beyond mean shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distributional reframing offers a principled path to multi-class fairness that prior point-based methods handle poorly, and the test-time-only design (no parameter updates) makes it deployable in settings where retraining is infeasible. Key limitations include the need for labeled reference data to construct group distributions, the Monte Carlo sampling overhead at inference, and the assumption that a low-dimensional bias subspace U can be reliably identified. Open questions include whether the approach extends to intersectional attributes, non-CLIP VLMs with different embedding geometries, and whether the Wasserstein-ball constraint can be relaxed for open-world queries where the query distribution is unknown.
