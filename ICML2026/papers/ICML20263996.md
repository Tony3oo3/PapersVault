## Problem

Diffusion policies generate actions through iterative denoising in a noisy latent space, yet reinforcement learning value functions are defined only over clean executed actions. This structural mismatch means existing methods either treat diffusion models as mere samplers, backpropagate through long stochastic denoising graphs, or naively apply action-value functions to noisy latents that have no MDP semantics. The paper identifies the absence of a principled value formulation that bridges noisy latent-space optimization and action-space returns as a fundamental gap in value-based training of diffusion policies for offline RL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (low-dimensional state vectors for D4RL tasks or RGB images for OGBench visual tasks) and a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions. It outputs continuous actions generated by a 5-step DDPM diffusion policy; at evaluation time, 10 candidate actions are decoded from independent noise samples and the one with the highest critic score is selected. A key design constraint is that the action-space critic is never evaluated on noisy latent variables—only on clean executable actions—preserving a strict separation between latent-space policy optimization and action-space value estimation.

## Method

The paper defines a noisy-space action-value function Q_noisy(s, x^(k), k) as the expected action-space return induced by the diffusion policy when conditioned on a noisy latent, and introduces a surrogate joint distribution built from the forward diffusion kernel to make conditioning on noisy latents well-defined. From this, it derives the Noisy-Space Policy Gradient (NSPG), a closed-form expression that weights the score of the forward diffusion kernel ∇_{x^(k)} log q_k(x^(k)|a) by a noisy-space advantage, yielding a true value-function gradient without backpropagating through the denoising trajectory. This gradient is embedded into a diffusion-compatible regression objective (Eq. 13) that combines standard denoising loss with a soft value-guidance term, enabling KL-regularized policy improvement directly in noisy latent space. The full NSAC algorithm alternates between updating a pessimistic LCB critic ensemble on offline data and updating the diffusion actor via the noisy-space objective, with the critic queried only on clean actions sampled from the policy.

## Results

On D4RL benchmarks, NSAC achieves the highest aggregate normalized scores across all three task families: 861.7 on locomotion (vs. 836.4 for DAC, 798.3 for DTQL), 337.2 on AntMaze (vs. 300.4 for IDQL-A, 275.4 for DAC), and 168.5 on Adroit (vs. 145.4 for DTQL, 145.2 for DAC). On OGBench visual tasks, NSAC obtains the best aggregate success rate of 271.5, outperforming DAC (255.9) and FQL (229). The most pronounced gains appear on AntMaze-large tasks, where NSAC improves over DAC by more than 50% (77.8 vs. 50.3 on large-play; 78 vs. 55.3 on large-diverse), and on Pen-Cloned-v1 where NSAC reaches 84.6 versus 81.3 for DTQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSAC outperforms all 15 baselines on the majority of D4RL tasks, with the largest margins over diffusion- and flow-based methods. Against DAC (the closest score-based diffusion actor-critic baseline that applies clean Q-values directly to noisy latents), NSAC wins on 13 of 15 D4RL tasks, with the most dramatic gaps on AntMaze-large-play (+27.5) and AntMaze-large-diverse (+22.7), where the noisy-space value formulation avoids the semantic mismatch of evaluating critics on non-executable variables. Against DTQL, NSAC leads on all locomotion and AntMaze tasks and on both Adroit tasks, while against flow-based FQL it dominates on AntMaze (337.2 vs. 230) and Adroit (168.5 vs. 127) but is more competitive on locomotion where FQL's one-step design is less disadvantaged. On OGBench visual tasks, NSAC achieves the best aggregate success rate (271.5), beating DAC by 15.6 points and FQL by 42.5 points, with the clearest advantage on the 4×4 puzzle task (47.5 vs. 40 for DAC, 33 for FQL). NSAC also surpasses classical Gaussian-policy baselines (IQL, CQL, IVR, XQL) across nearly all tasks, confirming that the expressive diffusion policy class combined with principled noisy-space value guidance yields consistent gains over simpler policy parameterizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the score-matching framework for diffusion actor-critic methods (Fang et al., 2024; Vincent, 2011), which formulates KL-regularized policy improvement as a diffusion noise regression objective. The key departure is the introduction of a noisy-space action-value function Q_noisy that assigns value to diffusion latents through the expected clean-action return under the diffusion-induced conditional distribution, rather than applying the action-space Q-function directly to noisy variables as in DAC. This is supported by a 'surrogate semantic model' that makes conditioning on intermediate latents well-defined via the forward diffusion kernel, yielding a principled noisy-space policy gradient (NSPG) that avoids backpropagation through the denoising trajectory while keeping all critic evaluations on executable actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental semantic gap in diffusion-based offline RL: the mismatch between the noisy latent space where optimization occurs and the clean action space where value is defined. The resulting NSAC algorithm is computationally efficient (comparable to DAC with 4 Monte Carlo samples) and achieves consistent gains across D4RL and OGBench benchmarks, with particularly large improvements (>50%) on sparse-reward AntMaze-large tasks. Open questions include whether the noisy-space formulation extends naturally to online and offline-to-online settings, whether it generalizes to flow-based policies beyond DDPM, and whether the surrogate semantic model can be replaced by a tighter coupling to the actual reverse-time dynamics.
