## Problem

Existing deep learning weather models operate in pixel space, which suffers from three coupled limitations: smooth outputs due to uncertainty in reconstructing small-scale extreme values, restriction to a single pressure-variable subset (PVS) per model, and prohibitive storage costs (hundreds of TB for full ERA5). No prior framework simultaneously addresses all three by providing a unified, compressed latent representation that decouples reconstruction from downstream task modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes weather data as a tensor X ∈ R^{C1×H×W} at 721×1440 spatial resolution (0.25° ERA5) along with pressure-variable metadata M ∈ R^{C1} specifying which variables and pressure levels are present. It produces binary latent tokens B ∈ {−1,1}^{C'×H'×W'} in a unified latent space with fixed channel dimensionality C2, regardless of the input PVS configuration. The full ERA5 dataset (164 variables, 244.34 TB) is compressed to 0.43 TB (566.3× ratio), and decoding the tokens reconstructs the original pixel-space weather fields.

## Method

The Weather Latent Autoencoder (WLA) comprises three cascaded modules: (1) a Pressure-Variable Unified Module (PVUM) that uses a hypernetwork to generate adaptive linear-layer weights from pressure-variable metadata, mapping any PVS to a fixed-dimensionality unified feature; (2) a VAEformer encoder-decoder pair (16-layer encoder, 32-layer decoder, from CRA5) that compresses unified features into low-dimensional latents; and (3) a Binary Quantization Module that L2-normalizes latents onto a sphere and applies sign-based quantization to produce ±1 bitwise tokens. Training uses a combined entropy + MSE + GAN loss over 500K steps on 4 A100 GPUs. The key architectural insight is decoupling: the pretrained WLA handles all weather reconstruction (preserving small-scale extremes via GAN loss), while downstream task models operate solely in the compact latent space with standard losses (e.g., BCE), eliminating the smoothness problem and enabling a single model to accept multiple PVS configurations without structural modification.

## Results

WLA achieves the lowest weighted RMSE across all evaluated variables, e.g., 0.076 for w500 and 0.083 for w700 (vs. 0.117/0.134 for the strongest baseline VAEformer), while maintaining a 625.9× compression ratio and 0.051 BPSP. The smaller WLA_small variant pushes compression to 1251.7× with the lowest BPSP of 0.025, at a modest reconstruction cost. Applied to the full ERA5 dataset (164 variables, 244.34 TB), WLA compresses the data to 0.43 TB (566.3× reduction). In downstream weather forecasting, the latent-space model (WTL) outperforms its pixel-space counterpart (WT) on extreme-event metrics (SEDI, RQE) while remaining competitive on 7-day RMSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On compression quality, WLA dominates all baselines: it achieves the lowest weighted RMSE on every variable (e.g., 0.076 vs. 0.117 for VAEformer on w500, a 35% reduction), while VQVAE and VQGAN show the worst reconstruction (0.382/0.367 on w500). On compression ratio, WLA (625.9×) is competitive with ELIC (648.3×) and far exceeds VAEformer (323.1×) and IEN (202.5×), though VQVAE/VQGAN reach 1100× at the cost of substantially higher reconstruction error. WLA_small achieves the highest compression (1251.7×) and lowest BPSP (0.025) among all methods. For downstream weather forecasting, WTL (latent) surpasses WT (pixel) on SEDI and RQE for extreme events, and both outperform Pangu-Weather and FourCastNet on overall 7-day RMSE, while Operational IFS remains the strongest baseline. In precipitation forecasting, WTLP consistently outperforms both WTP and FourCastNet on RQE, though it still trails Operational IFS.

## Relation to Prior Work

WLA composes three existing building blocks into a new pipeline for weather data: the VAEformer transformer encoder-decoder from CRA5 (Han et al., 2024a) for latent compression, the binary spherical quantization (BSQ, Zhao et al., 2024) for bitwise tokenization, and a novel Pressure-Variable Unified Module (PVUM) that uses a hypernetwork to map arbitrary pressure-variable subsets into a shared feature space. The downstream forecasting model (Window-ViT) reuses FengWu's (Chen et al., 2023a) multimodal encoder-decoder architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior compression work (ELIC, IEN, VQVAE, VAEformer) is the Latent Space Framework: rather than compressing and then decompressing before running a task, WLA keeps the entire training/inference loop in the compressed latent domain, and the PVUM removes the need for task-specific input projections that plague pixel-space models restricted to a single PVS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 566× compression of ERA5 (244.34 TB → 0.43 TB) and the release of the ERA5-Latent dataset make full-variable, multi-pressure-level weather research accessible on modest storage, which is a significant practical unlock for the community. The finding that latent-space forecasting matches or exceeds pixel-space models on extreme-event metrics (SEDI, RQE) while using a fraction of the data challenges the default assumption that pixel-space access is necessary for sharp predictions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution datasets (e.g., 0.09° HRES), whether separate autoencoders per variable group can be unified, and whether the latent-space framework generalizes beyond the Window-ViT architecture to other model families.
