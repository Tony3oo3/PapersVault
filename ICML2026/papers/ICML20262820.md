## Problem

Vision-Language Models face a perceptual bandwidth bottleneck: fixed visual-token budgets force a field-of-view versus resolution trade-off, causing fine-grained details (small objects, text, spatial relations) to vanish before reasoning begins. Prior active-vision and tool-use methods select crops heuristically without a decision-theoretic objective for which observation is most informative, and they do not account for the super-additive 'information cliff' where individual views yield near-zero value until a critical coverage-resolution threshold is reached.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution (up to gigapixel) RGB image, a natural-language query, and an initial crop proposal generated by the VLM as a noisy spatial prior. Crop parameters are continuous 4-tuples (u, v, w, h) in [0,1]⁴ specifying a rectangular region. The output is a refined crop region selected by the S-BOED-guided procedure, which is then executed through a ReAct agent equipped with vision tools (Grounding DINO, SAM 2, Depth Anything, MinerU) to produce a final text answer. The entire procedure is training-free and operates purely at inference time, intercepting the VLM's crop tool calls before execution.

## Method

The paper formalises active visual reasoning as sequential Bayesian optimal experimental design (S-BOED), where a foveation action allocates a fixed perceptual bandwidth budget B over a crop area A(d), inducing an information density ρ(d) = B/A(d) and a resolution probability φ(d) via a saturation function. Under a factorised belief approximation, calibrated visibility, and an ideal-observer assumption, the task-relevant expected information gain reduces to a closed-form coverage-resolution objective J(d) = (∫_{x∈d} p_t(x) dx) × φ(d), explicitly trading spatial posterior coverage against perceptual resolution. FOVEA instantiates this as a training-free inference-time module that treats the VLM's initial crop proposal as a noisy prior, generates a small candidate pool of local perturbations (scaling factors {0.8, 1.0, 1.5}), scores each candidate with a binary resolvability probe (Monte Carlo estimation of the VLM's Yes/No response to whether the crop contains sufficient query-relevant evidence), and selects the highest-scoring crop. Three optimisation strategies are supported: greedy argmax selection, MCMC-style Metropolis-Hastings refinement with adaptive step sizes, and one-step look-ahead planning that simulates the VLM's next action to score candidates by estimated future resolvability, addressing the information cliff that renders myopic strategies insufficient.

## Results

On the 30B backbone (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct), FOVEA achieves a mean score of 77.7% across five high-resolution benchmarks (MME-RealWorld, CV-Bench, V*Bench, HR-Bench 4K, HR-Bench 8K), improving over the ReAct baseline (75.1%) and the direct-encoding baseline (73.3%). On the 8B backbone, FOVEA lifts the mean from 70.9% (Direct) / 72.5% (ReAct) to 74.9%. In the search-dominated Remote Sensing subset of MME-RealWorld-Lite, FOVEA-Lookahead reaches 54.7% versus 45.1% for ReAct, closing part of the gap to the oracle-crop baseline (68.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ReAct agent (same 30B backbone, same tool interface, but no S-BOED-guided crop refinement), FOVEA gains +2.6% mean accuracy (77.7% vs. 75.1%), with the largest per-benchmark improvement on MME-RealWorld (+3.5 pp) and HR-Bench 4K (+3.7 pp). Against RAP (Wang et al., 2025), a retrieval-augmented perception baseline on the same backbone, FOVEA leads by +5.8% mean (77.7% vs. 71.9%), particularly on CV-Bench (+12.6 pp) and MME-RealWorld (+13.8 pp) where RAP underperforms even the direct baseline. Against Thyme (Zhang et al., 2025b), an RL-trained visual agent on a smaller 7B backbone, FOVEA achieves +4.7% mean (77.7% vs. 73.0%), indicating that principled inference-time crop refinement can outperform learned tool-use policies. FOVEA's 77.7% mean is within 0.3 pp of Gemini 2.5 Flash (78.0%), a proprietary frontier model, while surpassing GPT-5 (74.1%) by 3.6 pp. On the 8B backbone, the same trend holds: FOVEA improves over ReAct by +2.4% mean, confirming the strategy is not specific to a single model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian optimal experimental design (BOED) from its recent applications to discrete question selection in LLMs (Kobalczyk et al., 2025; Choudhury et al., 2025) to the continuous spatial domain of high-resolution visual foraging. It departs from prior visual agents (Thyme, LATTE, RAP) that treat crop selection as a heuristic or RL-learned policy by reframing it as a decision-theoretic problem with an explicit information-theoretic objective (the coverage-resolution utility). The 'perceptual bandwidth bottleneck' and 'information cliff' (super-additivity) concepts draw on classical active vision (Bajcsy, 1988) and information foraging (Pirolli & Card, 1999) but are formalised here for the first time in the context of fixed-token-budget VLMs, distinguishing the problem from both passive VLM inference and discrete active disambiguation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, training-free framework that decouples the decision-theoretic objective from the optimiser, allowing practitioners to plug in greedy, MCMC, or look-ahead strategies based on compute budget. The 'information cliff' insight—that individual observations can have near-zero value until a coverage-resolution threshold is crossed, violating the submodularity assumption underlying standard greedy active learning—challenges a common working assumption and motivates non-myopic planning. Key open questions include the proposal-limited cold-start bottleneck (where the target never enters the candidate pool), the ideal observer assumption that may not hold for weaker backbones, and whether amortised inference can reduce the substantial inference-time overhead (up to 55× output tokens for look-ahead).
