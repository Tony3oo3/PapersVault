## Problem

Adapting large-scale pre-trained video Diffusion Transformers for Video Super-Resolution in novel domains is computationally prohibitive: LQ-to-HQ mapping methods require full fine-tuning (100% parameters, tens of GPUs), while ControlNet-style adapters lose their efficiency under DiT because the absence of an encoder-decoder hierarchy forces duplication of the entire backbone. The paper asks whether a frozen DiT backbone can be effectively conditioned for VSR with a lightweight adapter, and identifies flow matching's constant velocity field as the key property that makes this feasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality video degraded by a composite operator (downsampling, blur, noise, compression) and produces a high-quality super-resolved video. It operates entirely in the latent space of a 3D VAE (Wan2.2-5B), with training on 37×512×512 crops and inference supporting arbitrary spatial-temporal resolutions via tiled query prototypes. Sampling uses the UniPC scheduler with 5 steps by default but supports down to a single step without distillation, adding only ~50 ms per step of adapter overhead.

## Method

LiteVSR keeps all DiT blocks of a frozen Wan2.2-5B generator entirely fixed and injects conditioning via a lightweight State-Aware Adapter (11.25% trainable parameters, 634M). The adapter uses a dual-stream design: a structural stream extracts static layout features from the LQ latent, and a refinement stream extracts dynamic details from the current clean estimate (ẑ₀,ₜ), both projected into the DiT hidden space. A time-modulated cross-attention mechanism with a learnable query prototype (tiled for resolution-agnostic inference) fuses the two streams, shifting focus from structural alignment at high noise to texture refinement at low noise. The adapter output is injected into each frozen DiT block through zero-initialized linear layers, and the LQ and noisy latents share the same DiT blocks via batched forward pass, eliminating parameter duplication. Training uses a single-stage flow matching objective in latent space with an Adaptive Unrolling Strategy that unrolls M(t) refinement steps (time-dependent, up to 3) to expose the adapter to realistic intermediate states, requiring only 266 REDS clips and ~12 GPU-hours on a single A100.

## Results

LiteVSR achieves the best perceptual quality across all five evaluation benchmarks (REDS4, UDM10, SPMCS, YouHQ40, VideoLQ), leading on CLIPIQA (e.g., 0.3748 on REDS4, 0.5741 on YouHQ40), DOVER (e.g., 0.3622 on REDS4, 0.5984 on YouHQ40), NIQE (e.g., 2.6938 on REDS4, 3.5094 on YouHQ40), and MUSIQ (e.g., 65.99 on REDS4, 70.42 on SPMCS). On PSNR, LiteVSR is competitive but trails DOVE (e.g., 21.10 vs. 23.08 on REDS4), reflecting the perception-distortion tradeoff. The method achieves these results with only 11.25% trainable parameters (634M out of 5.6B) and ~12 GPU-hours of training on a single A100, compared to 32×A100/H100 clusters for competing methods. A user study confirms 75.5% overall preference over DOVE and FlashVSR, rising to 91.2% under extreme degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiteVSR dominates on all perceptual/no-reference metrics (CLIPIQA, DOVER, NIQE, MUSIQ) across every benchmark, with the largest margins over ControlNet-style methods (STAR, Upscale-A-Video) that lack strong structural control. Against the strongest one-step baselines DOVE and FlashVSR, LiteVSR wins on perceptual quality (e.g., CLIPIQA 0.5741 vs. 0.4919/0.5278 on YouHQ40; DOVER 0.5984 vs. 0.5805/0.5766) but trails DOVE on PSNR (21.28 vs. 23.67 on YouHQ40), consistent with the perception-distortion tradeoff. MGLD-VSR is competitive on LPIPS/DISTS but falls well behind on perceptual scores (e.g., CLIPIQA 0.4413 vs. 0.5741 on YouHQ40). DiffVSR, despite full fine-tuning on 400K samples, is outperformed on nearly all perceptual metrics and requires 8×A100 GPUs versus LiteVSR's single A100. The user study further confirms the advantage: LiteVSR is preferred 75.5% overall and 91.2% under extreme degradation, versus 18.3% and 2.2% for FlashVSR respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteVSR departs from two dominant paradigms in generative VSR: (1) full fine-tuning of video generators (SeedVR, FlashVSR, DiffVSR), which requires 85–100% trainable parameters and tens of GPUs, and (2) ControlNet-style adapters (STAR, RealisVSR), which lose their efficiency advantage under Diffusion Transformers because the absence of an encoder-decoder hierarchy forces backbone duplication. The paper's key conceptual move is to exploit flow matching's constant velocity field property (Lipman et al., 2022) to argue that conditioning reduces to a fixed injection pattern rather than a time-varying transformation, thereby justifying a completely frozen DiT backbone (Wan2.2-5B) with only a lightweight State-Aware Adapter. This positions the work as a principled bridge between the efficiency of adapter-based control and the generative quality of large frozen video models, building directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and the flow matching formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing VSR adaptation from 32×A100 GPUs and millions of training samples to a single A100 and 266 clips (12 GPU-hours) makes domain-specific VSR deployment feasible on consumer hardware, and the frozen backbone means adapting to new degradation domains requires only retraining the 11.25% adapter. The method also preserves compatibility with fast samplers (down to 1 step) without distillation, which is important for real-time applications. However, PSNR remains competitive rather than dominant (DOVE and FlashVSR often lead on pixel-fidelity metrics), and the paper acknowledges a clear limitation on text reconstruction. Open questions include whether the frozen-backbone + flow-matching insight generalizes to other restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising) and to non-flow-matching diffusion formulations.
