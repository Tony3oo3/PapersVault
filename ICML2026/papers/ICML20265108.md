## Problem

Classical dataset distillation optimizes in pixel space with a specific prior architecture (e.g., ConvNet), causing distilled images to overfit to architecture-specific patterns that suppress high-level visual semantics and severely degrade cross-architecture generalization. Existing remedies either remain in pixel space (dual-time matching) or abandon the classical DD framework entirely in favor of coreset selection, leaving the question of whether classical distilled datasets retain recoverable semantic value unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a compact distilled dataset (unreadable images with class labels, e.g., IPC=1 or IPC=10) produced by any classical DD algorithm, along with a frozen pre-trained DiT-XL/2 diffusion model and VAE (vae-ft-mse) trained on ImageNet-1K. It outputs a synthetic dataset of identical size and label structure, with realistic 256×256 images that preserve the semantic content of the distilled set. No access to the original dataset or any training is required; the entire refinement runs in ~2.5 s per image on a single RTX-4090 with 4 GB GPU memory.

## Method

DIVER decouples dataset distillation into Stage I (any classical DD) and Stage II (Diving into Distilled Data, DDD), which refines the distilled set via a frozen guided diffusion model using three training-free semantic recovery strategies. Semantic Inheritance encodes each distilled image into the VAE latent space and adds moderate forward noise (t_f=25 steps) to initialize the reverse process, filtering architecture-specific high-frequency noise while retaining high-level semantics. Semantic Guidance applies a quadratic guidance function G_t = (ẑ_t − z_0)²·σ_t/2 during sampling to anchor the trajectory toward the inherited latent, preventing semantic drift. Semantic Fusion restricts the combined guidance and conditional-label injection exclusively to the Semantic Phase (t_h=40 to t_l=25) of the 50-step reverse process, using classifier-free guidance in the Chaotic and Refinement phases, thereby avoiding the blurring and artifacts that full-phase fusion introduces. The key insight is that the VAE acts as a low-pass filter for architecture-specific noise while the diffusion model pulls the latent back onto the real data manifold, and phase-restricted fusion preserves category clarity without overfitting to residual distilled patterns.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224) with ResNet-18, DIVER integrated with MGD3 achieves 46.9% top-1 accuracy at IPC=10 and 61.0% at IPC=50, outperforming the original MGD3 (45.8% and 60.2%) and all classical DD baselines. On ImageNet subsets (128×128) for cross-architecture generalization, DIVER applied to MTT yields 22.3% vs. 15.4% (IPC=1, ImageFruit) and 29.8% vs. 18.9% (IPC=10, ImageFruit) on unseen architectures. The method requires only 2.48s per image with 4.02 GB GPU memory on a single RTX-4090, comparable to raw DiT inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVER consistently and substantially outperforms all classical DD baselines on cross-architecture generalization: MTT+DIVER improves over MTT by 6.9–15.7 points across subsets (e.g., 29.8% vs. 18.9% on ImageFruit at IPC=10), and EDF+DIVER gains 5.8–11.3 points over EDF. Against the generative-prior method GLaD, DIVER achieves 2–5 point improvements on ImageNet subsets (Table 2), while also being training-free and not requiring gradient computation. On ImageNet-1K, DIVER integrated with dual-time matching (G-VBSM) improves ResNet-18 accuracy from 51.8% to 57.2% at IPC=50, and with MGD3 from 45.8% to 46.9% at IPC=10. The one consistent trade-off is a 5–15 point drop on the specific ConvNet used during distillation (Table 9), which the authors frame as an acceptable cost for the large cross-architecture gains. DIVER also slightly outperforms the diffusion-based D4M and MGD3 when applied to their prototype-learning paradigm (Table 5: 75.1% vs. 70.2% for D4M+Ours on ImageNette at IPC=50).

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVER extends the classical dataset distillation (DD) paradigm by introducing a second refinement stage—'Diving into Distilled Data' (DDD)—that leverages a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (DiT) to recover expressive semantics suppressed by architecture-specific patterns in already-distilled images. It departs from single-stage DD methods (gradient/distribution/trajectory matching) that optimize in pixel space and overfit to a prior ConvNet, and from generative-prior methods (GLaD) that still require training a GAN in latent space. Unlike diffusion-based DD approaches (D4M, MGD3, Minimax) that abandon the classical DD framework entirely in favor of prototype learning, DIVER preserves the classical DD output as its input and treats the diffusion model purely as a training-free semantic recovery plug-in, requiring no access to the original dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIVER offers a practical, training-free, raw-data-free plug-in that consistently improves cross-architecture generalization of existing DD methods by 5–10+ points while requiring only 4 GB GPU memory and ~2.5 s per image, making it immediately deployable on top of any classical DD pipeline. The conceptual insight that distilled images contain recoverable high-level semantics masked by architecture-specific 'noise'—and that a simple VAE encode-decode already partially addresses this—reframes the cross-architecture generalization problem as one of semantic recovery rather than re-optimization. Key limitations include heavy dependence on the quality of the input distilled images, evaluation restricted to ImageNet and its subsets, and a measurable (though acknowledged) trade-off in performance on the original ConvNet architecture. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-ImageNet domains, other modalities, or settings where the distilled dataset was produced by non-ConvNet priors.
