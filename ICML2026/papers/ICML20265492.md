## Problem

Existing differentiable causal discovery methods rely on MSE or log-likelihood losses, which provably fail to identify the true DAG in nonlinear additive noise models with heterogeneous (non-Gaussian, varying-scale) noise. While NOTIME established identifiability for the linear LiNGAM case, extending independence-based differentiable learning to general nonlinear ANMs with arbitrary noise distributions remained an open problem, compounded by the appearance of functionally trivial constant edges in the global minimizer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265492/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled observational data matrix of n continuous variables (d dimensions) and outputs a directed acyclic graph as a binary adjacency matrix. The structural functions are parameterized by two-layer MLPs (hidden dimension m₁=10 in experiments), and the DAG constraint is enforced via the trace-exponential condition on the first-layer weight matrix W^(1). The output DAG may initially contain extra constant edges that are pruned by the adaptive group lasso penalty, yielding the causal minimal graph.

## Method

NOTIME-CAM minimizes the d-variate Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (dHSIC) of the regression residuals under a trace-exponential DAG constraint, proving that the population-level global minimizer recovers the true causal minimal DAG up to edges with zero derivative (constant edges). To prune these uninformative edges, an adaptive group lasso penalty is applied to entire input columns of the first-layer weight matrix, with reweighting coefficients ω_ij = 1/(‖Ŵ_j^(1)[:,i]‖₂^β + ε) that assign lower penalties to strong signals. A CAM-based initialization scheme provides a warm start: CAM's causal ordering defines a mask M_order for MSE pretraining, the resulting first-layer norms set the adaptive penalty weights, and a second mask M_DAG (from CAM's predicted edges) constrains a final first-layer retraining step. This pipeline combines the identifiability of independence-based objectives with the scalability of continuous DAG optimization, with finite-sample guarantees showing P(Ĝ_n ≥ G₀) → 1.

## Results

On the Sachs real-world dataset (n=853, d=11, 17 known edges), NOTIME-CAM achieves SHD=15 with default hyperparameters, substantially outperforming CAM (SHD=30), NOTEARS-MLP (SHD=22), and DAGMA (SHD=17). With oracle hyperparameters, NOTIME-CAM further improves to SHD=14 with FDR=0 and TPR=0.18, while NOTEARS-MLP and DAGMA both reach SHD=16. On synthetic nonlinear ANMs (d=10–70, ER graphs with degree 4), NOTIME-CAM is consistently the best or near-best method across normal, lognormal, and t(3) noise distributions and three scaling schemes, while NOTEARS-MLP and DAGMA degrade significantly under non-raw scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOTIME-CAM clearly outperforms all four baselines on the Sachs dataset: it halves CAM's SHD (15 vs. 30) and beats NOTEARS-MLP (15 vs. 22) and DAGMA (15 vs. 17) under default settings. On synthetic data, NOTIME-CAM is robust across all three noise distributions and scaling schemes, whereas NOTEARS-MLP and DAGMA are highly sensitive to data scaling—under reverse scaling with oracle hyperparameters, both NOTEARS-MLP and DAGMA output zero graphs (SHD equal to the total number of true edges). CAM, while used for initialization, performs poorly on Sachs (SHD=30), yet NOTIME-CAM still substantially improves upon this weak initialization, demonstrating the value of the independence-based optimization. GraN-DAG was excluded from main comparisons due to its much higher computational cost and instability under non-Gaussian noise. Overall, NOTIME-CAM is the only method that maintains competitive SHD across all noise types and scaling conditions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NOTIME (Berrevoets et al., 2025), which proved identifiability of differentiable causal discovery via dHSIC minimization for LiNGAM, to general nonlinear ANMs with arbitrary noise distributions and variances. It departs from the dominant MSE/log-likelihood paradigm in differentiable DAG learning (NOTEARS-MLP, DAGMA, GraN-DAG) by showing these losses are provably inconsistent under heterogeneous noise, and instead grounds the objective in the independence structure of the ANM. The adaptive group lasso penalty (Wang & Leng, 2008) is repurposed to prune functionally constant edges at the column level of the first-layer MLP weights, and CAM (Bühlmann et al., 2014) is leveraged as a warm-start initialization rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap: prior differentiable causal discovery methods lacked identifiability guarantees beyond Gaussian or equal-variance noise, a limitation that is practically significant since real-world noise is rarely homoscedastic. The finite-sample convergence guarantee (Corollary 3.4) and the clean separation between the independence-based loss (for ordering) and the group lasso (for edge pruning) provide a principled recipe that other differentiable methods could adopt. Key limitations remain: the ANM assumption is restrictive, the method does not guarantee finding the global minimum without good initialization, and dHSIC loses statistical power in very high dimensions. Future extensions to latent confounding and partial observability are identified as open directions.
