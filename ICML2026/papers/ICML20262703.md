## Problem

In long-horizon offline goal-conditioned RL, success depends on crossing a few hard-to-cross bottlenecks between metastable regions, yet existing hierarchical methods select subgoals via time offsets or short-horizon value heuristics that do not explicitly localize these bottlenecks. This leaves the agent without clear high-level guidance about which chokepoint to pass next, causing costly backtracking and failure on distant goals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262703/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a current state s_t and a goal state g (state vectors for AntMaze/PointMaze/HumanoidMaze, or RGB observations for Visual Antmaze) from a fixed offline dataset with no online interaction. At the high level it outputs a keypoint route over a directed reachability graph; at the low level it produces an action a_t via a pluggable controller (Decision Diffuser with inverse dynamics, or a goal-conditioned MLP trained with IQL). Each keypoint is a partial state specification (I_Δ, v_Δ) that constrains only a subset of coordinates, leaving the rest free for routing flexibility.

## Method

BASS learns a Laplacian encoder φ_θ via the ALLO augmented-Lagrangian objective so that the embedding is flat within metastable regions and sharp across bottlenecks, then applies K-Means spectral clustering to expose region boundaries that align with hard-to-cross chokepoints. Trajectories in the offline dataset that cross these boundaries yield keypoint (KP) candidates, from which representative KPs are instantiated and a directed KP reachability graph G_KP is built by connecting KPs that have data-supported short-hop transitions. At deployment, a lightweight graph search (BFS or Dijkstra) routes over G_KP from the start anchor to the goal anchor, and a pluggable low-level controller executes each short transition into the next KP's acceptance region. Two theorems provide the theoretical grounding: the next bottleneck is a near-optimal one-step subgoal (additive gap ≤ 2D_B*), and Laplacian spectral clustering recovers bottleneck neighborhoods with error controlled by the operator-coupling and estimation ratios.

## Results

On D4RL, BASS achieves 96.5% average success on AntMaze (vs. 75.7% for HIQL and 82.3% for ESD) and 84.7% on FrankaKitchen (vs. 66.4% for HIQL and 68.5% for ESD). On OGBench, BASS reaches 97.3% on pointmaze-large-navigate, 99.3% on pointmaze-large-stitch, and 88.0% on pointmaze-giant-navigate, substantially outperforming all baselines including QRL (86±9) and HIQL (58±5) on the navigate tasks. BASS also achieves shorter evaluation trajectories than HIQL across all tested environments (e.g., 265 vs. 905 steps on pointmaze-large-stitch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BASS outperforms all 13 baselines on the majority of tasks. Against the strongest OGCRL baselines, BASS leads HIQL by 20.8 points on AntMaze (96.5% vs. 75.7%) and by 18.3 points on FrankaKitchen (84.7% vs. 66.4%), and beats ESD by 14.2 and 16.2 points respectively. Diffusion-based planners (Diffuser, DD, Diffusion-QL, IDQL, DIAR) collapse to 0% on AntMaze or remain well below BASS on Kitchen (e.g., DD 61.0% vs. BASS 84.7%), while sequence models (TT) and imitation (RvS-G) degrade sharply on large/ultra mazes (TT: 60.0% on large-play vs. BASS 96.0%). On OGBench, BASS dominates: it beats QRL (the strongest OGBench baseline) on pointmaze-large-navigate (97.3% vs. 86±9) and pointmaze-large-stitch (99.3% vs. 84±15), and surpasses CRL, GCBC, GCIVL, GCIQL, and HIQL by wide margins across all PointMaze, AntMaze, and HumanoidMaze variants. The one exception is maze2d-large-v1, where DIAR (200.3) slightly edges BASS (189.3) on the D4RL normalized score, though both far exceed other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

BASS sits at the intersection of Laplacian representation learning (building on the ALLO/Proper Laplacian objective of Gomez et al., 2024) and hierarchical offline goal-conditioned RL (building on HIQL's fixed-horizon subgoal interface and IQL-based training). It departs from prior subgoal-selection strategies—time-offset (HIQL), value-based (ESD), adjacency-constrained (Zhang et al., 2020), or graph-planned (Kim et al., 2023)—by explicitly identifying structural bottlenecks via spectral clustering and routing through them, with theoretical justification (Theorems 3.1–3.2) that the next bottleneck is a near-optimal one-step subgoal under metastable decomposition. The low-level controller is pluggable, borrowing from Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2022) and IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), but the high-level bottleneck-routing principle is the paper's core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bottleneck-guided principle offers a simple, theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic subgoal selection in long-horizon OGCRL, and the cross-domain KP-graph transfer (G1) and controller transfer (G2) results suggest practical reusability across environments sharing similar topology. However, the approach is most effective in metastable environments (mazes, corridors) where a small number of hard-to-cross bottlenecks dominate; performance degrades on teleport tasks where the metastable structure is less clear. Open questions include whether the bottleneck principle generalizes to non-metastable or high-dimensional continuous manipulation beyond Franka-Kitchen, and whether the spectral-clustering step can be made more robust to noisy or sparse offline data without the current over-segmentation heuristic.
