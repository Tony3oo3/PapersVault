## Problem

Current ASR systems frequently misidentify input language when confronted with diverse accents and dialectal variation, causing cascading failures in downstream dialogue tasks. Standard fine-tuning approaches are computationally expensive, prone to overfitting on high-dimensional speech data, and require large volumes of training data to generalize across dialects. The paper tackles the open challenge of robust language detection under dialectal variance in low-resource regimes where only tens to hundreds of samples per accent are available.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an audio waveform, which is first processed by a frozen ASR encoder (e.g., Whisper) to produce hidden representations that are masked-mean-pooled into a fixed-dimensional utterance vector. The CLD detection head predicts a language token from this pooled representation, which is then supplied as the initialization token to the ASR decoder to condition the final transcription. The entire detection step is a single lightweight forward pass on encoder features, designed to maintain sub-500ms end-to-end latency for natural conversational timing.

## Method

CLD recasts the language detection head as a convex reformulation of a two-layer ReLU network (cvxNN), where the non-convex training objective is equivalently expressed as a convex program over sampled activation patterns of the training data. The resulting convex program is solved to global optimality in polynomial time using a multi-GPU, batched ADMM solver implemented in JAX, eliminating dependence on brittle hyperparameters such as learning rate. A key theoretical contribution is deriving a data-dependent variation-norm certificate directly from the convex solution, which bounds the logit Lipschitz constant and yields a computable certified margin-stability radius against hidden-feature perturbations. This makes CLD both sample-efficient (achieving 97–98% detection accuracy with as few as 100 samples per language) and approximately 13× more compute-efficient than standard neural network baselines in training time and TFLOPs.

## Results

On the multiclass 5-language task (English, Chinese, Indonesian, Malaysian, Hindi; 24 accents, ~3,200 samples per language), CLD achieves 97.15% detection accuracy on Whisper-Small, 98.06% on Whisper-Large-V3, and 97.02% on MMS-1B, outperforming all baselines. CLD reduces WER from 139.37 (default Whisper-Small) to 31.74, and from 51.88 to 45.27 on MMS-1B. In the binary English–Chinese ablation at 500 training samples, CLD reaches 96.95% overall accuracy versus 70.77% (Whisper default) and 55.80% (vanilla NN). Training takes only 64.45 seconds and 14,075 TFLOPs—roughly 13× fewer TFLOPs than the vanilla NN baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Whisper's default language detector (Radford et al., 2023), CLD improves detection accuracy by 25.61 points on Whisper-Small (97.15% vs. 71.54%) and by 17.73 points on Whisper-Large-V3 (98.06% vs. 80.33%), while reducing WER from 139.37 to 31.74 on Whisper-Small. Against MMS-1B's default detector (Pratap et al., 2024), CLD raises detection accuracy from 67.01% to 97.02% (+44.78 points) and lowers WER from 51.88 to 45.27. CLD also outperforms the vanilla NN baseline (which requires hyperparameter tuning) on every metric: 97.15% vs. 77.37% accuracy on Whisper-Small, and 31.74 vs. 53.84 WER. The NN baseline shows strong English-centric bias—misclassifying 88.88% of Chinese samples as English—while CLD maintains uniformly high accuracy (>94%) across all 10 dialects. CLD is also the only method that scales well to MMS-1B, where SVM and KNN baselines degrade substantially (56–57% accuracy vs. CLD's 97.02%). Notably, CLD requires no hyperparameter tuning due to its convex formulation, whereas all NN and SVM baselines required extensive grid search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU networks (Pilanci & Ergen, 2020) and the CRONOS GPU-accelerated solver (Feng et al., 2024) to a new domain: multi-class spoken language detection on frozen ASR encoder features. Prior convex-NN work targeted image benchmarks or binary text classification at GPT-2 scale; here the authors extend the framework to high-dimensional, multi-class speech representations and add a variation-norm-based certified robustness analysis that is constructive (read off from the optimization variables) rather than a post-hoc black-box Lipschitz estimate. The method is positioned as a lightweight plug-in detection head that sits between the encoder and decoder of existing systems like Whisper and MMS-1B, rather than a full end-to-end retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete accessibility gap—accented and dialectal speech (e.g., Singlish, Min Dong) being misrouted to the wrong decoding language—and shows that a convex two-layer head trained on as few as 100 samples per language can outperform fine-tuned baselines by large margins while training in under a minute. The certified margin-stability radius is a practical differentiator: after training, one can read off a data-dependent robustness guarantee directly from the learned weights, something prior ASR language detectors do not offer. Key limitations include the restriction to a two-layer detection head (the encoder remains frozen and non-convex), the feature-space certificate being conservative for end-to-end audio perturbations, and evaluation limited to five languages and twenty-four dialects. Open questions include whether the ADMM solver scales to hundreds of languages, whether end-to-end differentiable CLD (via implicit differentiation through KKT conditions) can jointly optimize encoder and head, and whether the convex formulation generalizes beyond the two-layer setting.
