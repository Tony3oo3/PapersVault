## Problem

Existing LoRA initialization strategies select adaptation subspaces based solely on the spectral structure of pre-trained weights (e.g., principal or minor singular directions), implicitly assuming that weight geometry reflects task relevance. However, the most useful singular directions for one task can be uninformative for another, and weight-only criteria risk allocating scarce low-rank capacity to directions that are passive under the target data distribution. The paper asks whether data-aware parameter sensitivity, rather than singular values alone, should govern which subspace LoRA adapts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained weight matrix W_0 of a target linear layer together with a minibatch (default size 320) of downstream task data, from which it collects input activations X and output gradients ∇_Y L via one forward and one backward pass. It produces two LoRA factor matrices A ∈ R^{r×n} and B ∈ R^{m×r} initialized along Fisher-aligned directions, plus a residual weight W_res = W_0 − α(BA) that retains all components orthogonal to the selected subspace. Unlike SVD-based baselines that require casting weights to FP32 for numerical stability, FILet operates entirely in BF16 using only matrix multiplications on surrogate bases, avoiding full singular value decomposition.

## Method

FILet formulates LoRA initialization as selecting rank-1 directions (u,v) that minimize the expected second-order loss increase, quantified by the Fisher Energy E(Z) = vec(Z)ᵀ S_W vec(Z). To make this tractable, it applies the K-FAC approximation to factorize the Fisher information as S_W ≈ S_X ⊗ S_Y, where S_X = E[XXᵀ] and S_Y = E[(∇_Y L)(∇_Y L)ᵀ] are empirical second-moment matrices estimated from a single minibatch. Candidate directions are constructed from surrogate singular bases (column-normalized Gram matrices of W_0) rather than exact SVD, and each direction's Fisher Energy decomposes into the product (vᵀ S_X v)(uᵀ S_Y u). The top-r directions with minimal Fisher Energy are selected, and the LoRA factors are initialized with magnitudes scaled by their corresponding Fisher Energy values (not singular values), ensuring that the initial update lies in a subspace of low data sensitivity while preserving task-relevant information in the frozen residual.

## Results

On natural language reasoning (8 benchmarks), FILet achieves 83.1% average accuracy on Llama2-7B and 86.7% on Llama3-8B, outperforming the strongest baselines KaSA (81.7% / 85.7%) and LoRA-Dash (81.7% / 85.6%). On image classification (ViT-B/32, 7 datasets), FILet reaches 69.3% average accuracy versus 68.7% for DoRA and 68.6% for KaSA/MiLoRA. On generation tasks, FILet yields the largest gains on GSM8K (e.g., 63.5 vs. 58.7 for LoRA-Dash on Gemma-7B) and MBPP (e.g., 27.1 vs. 22.8 for LoRA-Dash on Llama3-8B), indicating particular strength on tasks requiring structured reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FILet consistently outperforms all seven baselines (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA, NEAT, KaSA, LoRA-Dash) on the majority of reasoning benchmarks for both Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B, with the largest margins over SVD-based methods: +9.3 points over PiSSA and +3.9 over MiLoRA on Llama2-7B average. Against the two strongest baselines, FILet gains +1.4 points over KaSA and +1.4 over LoRA-Dash on Llama2-7B, and +1.0 / +1.1 on Llama3-8B, respectively. On image classification (ViT-B/32), FILet leads by +0.6 to +1.0 points over all baselines, with the most notable gain on SVHN (+1.1 over KaSA). On generation tasks, FILet's advantage is most pronounced on GSM8K and MBPP (e.g., +4.8 over LoRA-Dash on GSM8K for Gemma-7B; +4.3 on MBPP for Llama3-8B), while gains on E2E and MT-bench are smaller but still positive across all four models tested. The only dataset where FILet does not achieve the top score is ARC-challenge on Llama3-8B (79.9 vs. KaSA's 80.3), a 0.4-point margin within reported standard deviations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FILet extends the LoRA initialization problem by replacing weight-geometry criteria (singular values from SVD, as in PiSSA, MiLoRA, KaSA) with a data-aware sensitivity measure: Fisher Energy computed via Kronecker-factored (K-FAC) approximation of the Hessian. While prior data-driven methods like LoRA-Dash and LoRA-GA also incorporate downstream data, they do so through training-time warm-up or gradient alignment rather than through second-order curvature statistics at initialization. The key conceptual departure is the observation that directions with *low* Fisher Energy (i.e., data-insensitive directions) are the optimal candidates for LoRA adaptation, inverting the intuition that 'important' weight directions should be the ones trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FILet offers a principled, task-dependent initialization criterion that is both more accurate than SVD-based heuristics and more computationally efficient (avoiding full SVD and FP32 casts), making it attractive for large-scale PEFT pipelines. The consistent 1–2 point gains across LLMs (Llama2/3, Gemma, Qwen2.5) and modalities (text reasoning, image classification, code/math generation) suggest the Fisher-guided signal is robust rather than task-specific. Open questions include whether the K-FAC independence assumption degrades in very deep or highly correlated architectures, how the method behaves under extreme rank settings or multi-task scenarios, and whether the surrogate basis construction (normalized Gram matrices) can be further refined to approach exact Fisher eigendecompositions at scale.
