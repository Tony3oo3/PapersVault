## Problem

Constraint-based causal discovery methods (e.g., PC) rely on conditional independence tests whose statistical reliability degrades sharply as conditioning sets grow, a problem that is acute in hub-dominated graphs where conditioning sets quickly become prohibitive. Existing approaches constrain adjacency or global structure but leave conditioning-set dimensionality uncontrolled, causing statistical errors to cascade. This paper argues that conditioning-set dimensionality should be a first-class constraint, addressed by restricting the directional search space globally before any CI test is performed.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. observational samples X ∈ R^{n×d} along with hyperparameters (CI threshold α, score threshold τ, and max-parents K) and produces an ordering-consistent sparse DAG. The output is constructed by intersecting the locally validated Markov blanket with the ordering-constrained parent candidate set, ensuring all directed edges respect the backbone ordering. The method is modular: Stage I can be swapped among ordering backbones (CaPS, SciNO, DiffAN) and Stage II among MB learners (IAMB, HITON-MB, MMPC), with the kNN-based CMI estimator of Kraskov et al. (2004) as the default CI test.

## Method

OCMB decouples causal discovery into two stages. Stage I runs an ordering backbone (e.g., CaPS) to obtain a topological ordering π and edge scores S, then constructs per-node candidate parent sets C_Pa(X_i) as the top-K predecessors with scores above threshold τ, along with candidate children and (optionally) spouse-closure sets, reducing the parent search from O(d) to O(K). Stage II performs a constrained IAMB (grow-prune) procedure restricted to the candidate Markov blanket C_MB(X_i), so all CMI evaluations operate on bounded conditioning sets. Edges are emitted only where the validated MB intersects C_Pa(X_i), yielding an acyclic DAG by construction. The key theoretical contribution is a consistency guarantee under a high-recall ordering assumption (true parents rank in the top-K predecessors with high probability), which does not require the ordering to be globally correct—only that it preserves sufficient parent recall, making the ordering a soft directional filter rather than a hard structural constraint.

## Results

On scale-free graphs (d=100, n=1000, nonlinear SEM, 10 seeds), OCMB-HITON achieves F1=0.639 and SHD=60.3, second only to DAGMA-linear (F1=0.736, SHD=45.0), while using ~4,850× fewer CI tests than PC (704 vs. 3.4M). On real-world benchmarks, OCMB-scino achieves the best SHD on Sachs (21 vs. PC's 32), reduces DREAM3 SHD by 43–56% over EEMBI, and achieves mean SHD 221 on DREAM4 (32% lower than PC's 327). On the Alarm network, OCMB-scino attains SHD=44, outperforming PC (53), NOTEARS (47), and DAGMA-linear (57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCMB-HITON outperforms PC (F1 0.639 vs. 0.427, SHD 60.3 vs. 158.7) on hub-dominated scale-free graphs while requiring ~4,850× fewer CI tests, confirming that high-dimensional conditioning around hub nodes degrades PC's reliability. Against the hybrid methods MMHC and H2PC (both F1=0.550), OCMB-HITON gains ~9 F1 points with 30–45× fewer CI tests. DAGMA-linear achieves the best accuracy on synthetic SF graphs (F1=0.736) despite assuming a linear SEM mismatched to the nonlinear data, but OCMB-caps surpasses it on DREAM4 (SHD 221 vs. 278) and Alarm (SHD 44 vs. 57). On DREAM3, OCMB-scino reduces SHD by 43–56% relative to EEMBI and substantially outperforms DiffAN (e.g., 70 vs. 204 on Ecoli1). OCMB also dramatically improves over its own ordering backbone used alone (CaPS-alone F1=0.249), demonstrating that local CI validation is essential for filtering false positives. On homogeneous Erdős-Rényi graphs, PC matches OCMB-HITON's skeleton F1 (0.716 vs. 0.717) with 11× more CI tests, and PC achieves higher directed F1 (0.663 vs. 0.355), delineating OCMB's primary advantage as concentrated in hub-heavy structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCMB sits at the intersection of ordering-based causal discovery (CaPS, SciNO, DiffAN) and constraint-based Markov blanket discovery (IAMB, HITON-MB, MMPC), but departs from both in a key way: it uses the ordering as a soft directional prior to bound candidate parent sets *before* any CI test, rather than treating the ordering as a definitive structural estimate or performing CI tests on unconstrained neighborhoods. This contrasts with hybrid methods like MMHC that discover undirected skeletons first (incurring high-dimensional conditioning around hubs) and with bounded-conditioning-set methods (Textor et al., Wienobst et al.) that cap conditioning order directly rather than constraining the search space through directional priors. The theoretical contribution formalizes when a *noisy* ordering suffices (high-recall assumption) for consistent parent recovery, clarifying the ordering's role as an inductive bias rather than a ground-truth requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-documented failure mode of constraint-based methods—CI test unreliability under high-dimensional conditioning in hub-dominated graphs—and provides both theoretical guarantees and empirical evidence that ordering-constrained candidate sets resolve it with orders-of-magnitude fewer CI queries. Key open questions include: (i) extending to latent confounders (FCI-style rules), (ii) adaptive K selection without circular dependency on CI testing, and (iii) scalability beyond d≈200, where the d=500 experiments remain preliminary (1/5 seeds). The ANM assumption inherited from the Stage-I backbone limits generality, and the method's advantage narrows on homogeneous (non-hub) graphs where PC remains competitive.
