## Problem

When LLM agents face underspecified instructions, standard Bayesian Experimental Design (BED) approaches treat each sampled solution as a discrete, orthogonal hypothesis, causing them to waste limited clarification turns distinguishing trivial syntactic variants (e.g., `cnt += 1` vs `cnt = cnt + 1`) rather than resolving substantive functional differences (e.g., recursive vs. iterative). The paper identifies this pathology as 'semantic blindness' and asks whether the geometric structure of the solution space can be exploited to separate semantic signal from syntactic noise, enabling efficient intent resolution.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ambiguous natural language instruction (e.g., a vague code function signature or an underspecified visualization request) and iteratively consumes binary or multi-choice user clarification responses (including an 'I don't know' option). It produces a sequence of contrastive clarifying questions targeting semantic distinctions between intent clusters, and ultimately a disambiguated solution (code, plot specification, or navigation plan) sampled from the resolved cluster. Internally, the method reduces the hypothesis space from N=20 raw candidates to K≈2–5 topological clusters, operating on a K-dimensional belief vector rather than an N-dimensional one, with a stopping threshold of 1−ρ=0.95 posterior concentration.

## Method

TAI is a training-free, two-phase framework. In the perception phase, it samples N candidate solutions from the LLM's posterior, maps them to a semantic embedding space (OpenAI text-embedding-3 or Qwen3-Embedding, PCA-reduced to d=256), and computes 0-th Vietoris-Rips persistence barcodes via a Union-Find algorithm; an adaptive spectral-gap threshold τ filters short-lived syntactic noise to recover K robust intent clusters (the robust Betti number β₀^robust). In the active inference loop, it generates contrastive 'scalpel' questions between the top competing clusters using a dedicated LLM prompt, selects the optimal question by maximizing Topological Expected Information Gain (TEIG)—the mutual information between the latent intent variable Z and the answer A—which under deterministic cluster-level answers reduces to maximizing binary entropy of the induced partition (favoring balanced splits). Beliefs are updated via Bayes' rule over the K clusters, and the loop terminates when one cluster's posterior exceeds 1−ρ. Under a γ-effective separability assumption, the paper proves O(log K) query complexity, an exponential improvement over the O(N) complexity of discrete sample-based BED.

## Results

On clean settings, TAI achieves 100% success rate across all three Ambi-Bench domains (Ambi-Code, Ambi-Plot, Ambi-Nav), while Standard BED plateaus at 75.0%, 94.2%, and 88.5% respectively. TAI resolves user intent in only 1–3 interaction turns to reach >95% success, validating the theoretical O(log K) query complexity. Under the strongest combined feedback noise (p_flip=0.20, p_unk=0.20), TAI degrades gracefully to 85.4%/91.2%/87.5% while BED collapses to 38.6%/63.7%/51.4%, yielding average robustness gaps of +38.8, +21.6, and +30.3 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Standard BED (Kobalczyk et al., ICLR 2025), which selects clarifying questions by maximizing expected information gain over a discrete set of N sampled candidates. TAI dominates Standard BED on every metric and domain: on clean settings, TAI reaches 100% success while BED caps at 75.0% (Ambi-Code), 94.2% (Ambi-Plot), and 88.5% (Ambi-Nav), with the largest gap on the hardest code domain. Under adversarial feedback noise, the gap widens dramatically—e.g., at p_flip=0.30 on Ambi-Code, TAI retains 79.0% while BED drops to 34.0% (a 45-point gap). A secondary baseline, Random-Ask (uniform random questions), performs at or near chance level, confirming that unstructured probing is ineffective. The paper also ablates against K-Means clustering (replacing persistent homology) and shows significant degradation on non-convex and high-noise tasks, confirming that the topological structure—not mere geometric proximity—is the critical mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Experimental Design (Lindley, 1956; Mackay, 1992) from discrete hypothesis spaces to continuous topological ones, building directly on the Vietoris-Rips filtration and persistent homology formalism of Edelsbrunner & Harer (2010). It departs from the closest prior work on active task disambiguation with LLMs (Kobalczyk et al., ICLR 2025) by identifying 'semantic blindness'—the pathology of treating each sampled string as an orthogonal hypothesis—and replacing per-candidate information gain with Topological Expected Information Gain over persistent intent clusters. The approach also repurposes TDA tools (previously used in NLP for post-hoc embedding analysis, e.g., Rathore et al. 2023) as an active, online decision-making component within an agent loop, which is a novel integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(log K) query complexity and the demonstrated robustness to noisy feedback (38.8% average success-rate gap over BED under combined noise) make interactive disambiguation substantially cheaper and more reliable for safety-critical code generation and planning. However, the framework's effectiveness is contingent on embedding quality and candidate coverage: missing intent modes cannot be recovered, and the evaluation remains small-scale (50 tasks, N=20 samples, single embedding family). Open questions include whether the topology can be made adaptive through online resampling, how TAI integrates with free-form multi-turn dialogue beyond binary yes/no clarification, and whether the scale-separation assumption holds for highly entangled or novel intent spaces.
