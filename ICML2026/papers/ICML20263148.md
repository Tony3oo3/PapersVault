## Problem

Contrastive activation-based methods are widely used to identify 'reasoning features' in SAEs by selecting features with large activation differences between chain-of-thought and non-reasoning text. However, CoT text differs from non-reasoning text not only in reasoning content but also in surface lexical and stylistic patterns, making it ambiguous whether large activation differences reflect genuine reasoning computations or shallow token-level confounds such as recurring discourse markers. The paper asks whether any contrastively selected SAE feature robustly tracks reasoning behavior under causal and adversarial testing.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes pre-trained SAE feature activations (16K–65K features) on residual stream activations at specific middle-to-late layers of open-weight LLMs, evaluated on 64-token chunks sampled from reasoning corpora (s1K-1.1, General Inquiry CoT) and a non-reasoning corpus (Pile Uncopyrighted). For each candidate feature, the method produces a multi-stage classification: a token-injection category (token-driven, partially token-driven, weakly token-driven, or context-dependent) based on Cohen's d and p-values from injected vs. baseline activations, followed by an LLM-guided falsification verdict (genuine reasoning feature or confound) supported by constructed false-positive and false-negative counterexamples with measured activation thresholds.

## Method

The paper combines a stylized theoretical analysis with a two-stage empirical falsification pipeline. Theoretically, it shows that under ℓ1-regularized decoding with an orthonormal dictionary, the recovered energy from a high-dimensional isotropic component decays exponentially in (k−1)λ²/b², while a stable low-dimensional cue coordinate is retained via soft-thresholding—formalizing why sparsity favors cue-like correlates over distributed reasoning variation. Empirically, the first stage performs causal token injection: top-activating unigrams, bigrams, and trigrams for each feature are inserted into non-reasoning text via prepending, interspersing, replacement, and context-preserving strategies, and features are classified by the magnitude of induced activation shifts. The second stage applies LLM-guided falsification to remaining context-dependent features: an LLM (Gemini 3 Pro) generates a hypothesis about the detected pattern, then constructs non-reasoning false positives that instantiate that pattern and meaning-preserving paraphrases of top-activating reasoning traces as false negatives, iterating up to 10 rounds until sufficient valid counterexamples are found. A small steering experiment on AIME and GPQA serves as a supplementary behavioral check.

## Results

Across 22 model-layer-dataset configurations, 45%–90% of the top-100 contrastively selected SAE features are classified as token-driven, partially token-driven, or weakly token-driven, meaning that inserting only a few associated tokens into non-reasoning text produces statistically significant activation increases (mean injection Cohen's d ranging from 0.521 to 1.471). Of the 248 remaining context-dependent features subjected to LLM-guided falsification, zero are classified as genuine reasoning features; the vast majority are identified as confounds with high confidence. A supplementary steering study on AIME 2024 and GPQA Diamond shows minimal or negative effects (e.g., AIME accuracy drops from 26.7% baseline to 10.0%–26.7% under steering, GPQA from 37.9% to 13.6%–33.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing interpretability methods; instead, it evaluates the standard contrastive activation-based selection pipeline (used by Venhoff et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b; Galichin et al., 2025) against a proposed falsification framework. The contrastive method reliably separates reasoning from non-reasoning text at the distributional level (mean Cohen's d of 0.675–1.043 for top-100 features), but the falsification pipeline shows that this separability does not imply monosemantic reasoning representation. Specifically, the token injection test eliminates 45%–90% of candidates as lexically driven, and the LLM-guided counterexample test eliminates all remaining context-dependent features (0/248 genuine). The paper also notes that alternative ranking metrics (ROC-AUC, frequency ratio) select highly overlapping feature sets (substantial Jaccard similarity) and yield identical falsification outcomes, confirming that the negative result is not an artifact of the Cohen's d selection criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SAE-based reasoning interpretability literature (Chen et al. 2025; Galichin et al. 2025; Venhoff et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025b), which uses contrastive activation differences between reasoning and non-reasoning corpora to identify 'reasoning features' and then steers them. The key departure is methodological: rather than accepting contrastive separability as evidence of a reasoning representation, the paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that ℓ₁ sparsity in SAE decoding preferentially retains stable low-dimensional lexical cues while suppressing high-dimensional within-behavior variation, and then operationalizes this insight through a falsification pipeline combining causal token injection with LLM-guided adversarial counterexample generation. It also distinguishes its dimensionality-driven mechanism from the related but distinct phenomenon of feature absorption (Chanin et al. 2025), where nested features compete under sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that zero features survive falsification across 248 context-dependent candidates and 22 configurations challenges the implicit assumption in the SAE reasoning literature that contrastive activation differences identify monosemantic reasoning representations, and the theoretical analysis provides a concrete, testable mechanism (sparsity-induced dimensionality asymmetry) for why this happens. The falsification framework itself is a reusable methodological tool applicable to other SAE feature interpretation claims beyond reasoning. Key open questions remain: whether reasoning-relevant information is distributed across many features or represented nonlinearly in ways that resist single-feature attribution, and whether the conclusions extend to non-monosemantic features that jointly encode cues and reasoning aspects. The authors explicitly scope their results to monosemantic individual features selected by activation-based criteria, leaving the broader representational geometry of reasoning in LLMs unresolved.
