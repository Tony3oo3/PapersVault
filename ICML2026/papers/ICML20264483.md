## Problem

Standard Bayesian neural networks require O(mn) variational parameters per weight matrix for mean-field Gaussian posteriors, doubling the parameter count of deterministic networks while imposing an independence assumption that discards structured weight correlations. This cost is often unnecessary when weight matrices exhibit fast singular value decay, yet no prior work trains low-rank BNNs end-to-end across diverse architectures with rigorous theoretical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264483/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised inputs—44 clinical features (MIMIC-III), 15 meteorological features in 24-hour sliding windows (Beijing PM2.5), and tokenized text sequences (SST-2)—and produces Monte Carlo-averaged class probabilities, regression point estimates, and prediction intervals. Uncertainty is quantified via mutual information across posterior weight samples, enabling OOD detection (AUROC-OOD, AUPR-OOD), selective prediction (AUPR-Succ), and calibration metrics (NLL, ECE, PICP). The low-rank constraint means each weight matrix is a sum of r rank-1 outer products, so uncertainty propagates through shared latent factors rather than independent per-weight noise.

## Method

Each weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} is factorized as W = AB^T with A ∈ R^{m×r} and B ∈ R^{n×r}, and mean-field Gaussian posteriors are placed on the factors rather than on W directly. This induces a posterior singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, concentrating entirely on the measure-zero rank-r manifold, which captures structured weight correlations (Cov(W_ij, W_i'j') ≠ 0 for weights sharing latent factors) while reducing variational parameters from O(mn) to O(r(m+n)). Training optimizes the ELBO via the reparameterization trick with a scale mixture prior (following Blundell et al., 2015), using Adam with KL annealing. Theoretically, the paper proves PAC-Bayes generalization bounds whose complexity term scales as √(r(m+n)) instead of √(mn), and loss approximation bounds via the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem that decompose error into optimization error and rank-induced bias controlled by tail singular values. The framework is implemented end-to-end for MLPs, LSTMs (with batch-level factor sampling across time steps), and Transformers (with position-wise factorization for embeddings), serving as drop-in replacements for standard layers.

## Results

On SST-2 (Transformer), Low-Rank BBB achieves 0.806 accuracy and 0.640 AUROC-OOD with only 1.5M parameters, compared to Deep Ensemble's 0.825 accuracy and 0.657 AUROC-OOD at 49.6M parameters (33× fewer). On MIMIC-III (MLP), Low-Rank achieves the best OOD detection (AUC-OOD = 0.802, AUPR-OOD = 0.788) with 13.6k parameters, outperforming Deep Ensemble (0.738, 0.754) and Full-Rank BBB (0.770, 0.759). On Beijing Air Quality (LSTM), Low-Rank attains the best prediction interval coverage (PICP = 0.790) and near-best calibration (ECE = 0.114) with 47k parameters versus 330k for Deep Ensemble. Training time on SST-2 is 8.2 min for Low-Rank versus 23.1 min for Full-Rank BBB and 64.7 min for Deep Ensemble.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Deep Ensemble (5 members)**, the proposed Low-Rank method trades a small in-distribution gap (0.806 vs 0.825 accuracy on SST-2; 0.895 vs 0.929 AUROC on MIMIC-III) for dramatically lower parameter cost (1.5M vs 49.6M; 13.6k vs 112k) and, on MIMIC-III, superior OOD detection (AUC-OOD 0.802 vs 0.738). Against **Full-Rank BBB**, Low-Rank consistently wins on OOD detection and selective prediction metrics while using 70–93% fewer parameters; Full-Rank BBB occasionally matches or slightly beats Low-Rank on NLL/ECE calibration (e.g., 0.111 vs 0.114 ECE on Beijing). Against **Rank-1 multiplicative perturbations** (Dusenberry et al., 2020a), Low-Rank outperforms on OOD detection (AUC-OOD 0.802 vs 0.705 on MIMIC-III; 0.710 vs 0.580 on Beijing) and calibration (PICP 0.790 vs 0.449 on Beijing), while Rank-1 sometimes achieves slightly better NLL. Against **SWAG** (Maddox et al., 2019), Low-Rank is competitive on point prediction but achieves stronger MI-based OOD separation (AUROC-OOD-MI 0.802 vs 0.634 on MIMIC-III) with far fewer stored parameters (1.5M vs 208M on SST-2). Overall, the method's clearest wins are in OOD detection and parameter efficiency, while Deep Ensembles retain an edge on in-distribution likelihood-based calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayes by Backpropagation framework (Blundell et al., 2015) by replacing the full-rank mean-field posterior over individual weights with a factorized parameterization W = AB^T, placing mean-field Gaussians on the factors instead. This departs from low-rank covariance methods (Tomczak et al., 2020; Swiatkowski et al., 2020) that retain O(mn) mean parameters, from rank-1 multiplicative perturbations (Dusenberry et al., 2020) that require a deterministic backbone, and from LoRA-based Bayesian fine-tuning (Yang et al., 2024; Meo et al., 2024) that depend on pretrained models. The theoretical analysis additionally adapts Gaussian complexity bounds from deterministic low-rank networks (Pinto et al., 2025) to the Bayesian predictive-mean setting, and the paper positions itself as the first to train low-rank BNNs end-to-end across MLPs, LSTMs, and Transformers with PAC-Bayes and EYM-based guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(r(m+n)) parameterization with up to 33× fewer parameters than 5-member Deep Ensembles makes Bayesian uncertainty quantification more accessible for resource-constrained deployment, and the PAC-Bayes complexity reduction from √(mn) to √(r(m+n)) provides a principled justification for the efficiency gain. However, the empirical gains are task-dependent: low-rank methods excel at OOD detection and selective prediction but can underperform Deep Ensembles on in-distribution NLL and calibration, revealing a calibration-sharpness tradeoff that limits applicability in likelihood-focused settings. Open questions include adaptive rank selection, scaling to large language models, the effect of prior choice (e.g., spike-and-slab vs. scale mixture), and whether the geometric singularity of the posterior provides benefits beyond what parameter-count arguments already capture.
