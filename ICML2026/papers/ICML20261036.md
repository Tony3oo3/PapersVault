## Problem

Standard optimal-transport trajectory inference for scRNA-seq relies exclusively on gene-expression distances, implicitly treating cells as independent particles and ignoring the structured cell-cell communication (CCI) mediated by ligand-receptor signaling. This leaves the snapshot-alignment problem underdetermined in a way that feature-only least-action priors cannot resolve, particularly when transcriptionally similar cells play distinct signaling roles. The paper asks whether directed, typed CCI structure can serve as a biologically grounded, interpretable prior orthogonal to expression smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple scRNA-seq population snapshots (gene-expression vectors per cell, projected to 20 PCA components) at distinct time points, together with a curated ligand-receptor (LR) catalog of P pairs selected per dataset via LIANA consensus scoring. Expression is mapped to [0,1] via per-gene Hill saturation functions before CCI scoring. The primary output is a full-snapshot coupling matrix Γ* ∈ R^{n₀×n₁} that simultaneously respects feature similarity and directed, multi-channel interaction geometry; this coupling is then passed as an endpoint distribution to downstream continuous-time learners (CFM, MFM, SF2M, UOT-FM) to produce velocity fields and interpolated trajectories. A secondary output is the ability to perform in silico perturbations by ablating specific LR pairs from the catalog and re-solving the coupling, yielding interpretable trajectory shifts.

## Method

CellBRIDGE generalizes Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) to multi-relation, vector-valued directed interaction structure: each directed edge between cells i and j is a P-dimensional vector encoding per-LR-pair interaction scores (z_{i→j}^{(p)} = s_{i,l_p} · s_{j,r_p}), rather than a scalar. The core optimization (Eq. 5) balances a feature cost (squared Euclidean in PCA space) against a structure-distortion term measuring squared Euclidean mismatch between LR-channel vectors of corresponding sender-receiver pairs, solved via a customized conditional-gradient (Frank-Wolfe) solver extended from POT to handle multi-channel tensors. Endpoint normalization rescales the feature and structure terms so that the trade-off parameter α meaningfully interpolates between pure OT and pure GW. Crucially, the interaction prior is injected only at the coupling level: the resulting Γ* defines an endpoint distribution Π that is agnostic to the downstream dynamics model, making CellBRIDGE a plug-and-play regularizer that improves CFM, MFM, SF2M, and UOT-FM without modifying their architectures or training objectives.

## Results

On three real scRNA-seq datasets (V1 Light, Dendritic Stimulus, Lung Tumor), CellBRIDGE consistently reduces held-out interpolation error (Wasserstein-1 and Wasserstein-2) when its interaction-aware coupling replaces the default coupling in four downstream dynamics models. The most pronounced gains appear for SF2M (stochastic Schrödinger bridges): on Lung Tumor, CellBRIDGE+SF2M (α=1) achieves W1=3.638 vs. 3.826 for vanilla SF2M (−4.9%), and on Dendritic Stimulus W1 drops from 4.333 to 4.289. For CFM, MFM, and UOT-FM, improvements are smaller but consistent (e.g., CellBRIDGE+CFM α=1: W1=2.362 vs. OT-CFM 2.392 on V1 Light; W1=3.639 vs. 3.696 on Dendritic Stimulus). Paired Wilcoxon tests confirm significance (adjusted p<0.05, Cohen's d_z = 0.27–0.63) across all model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CellBRIDGE is evaluated as a plug-and-play coupling prior: the same interaction-aware FGW coupling is injected into four downstream dynamics models (CFM, MFM, UOT-FM, SF2M) while keeping their architectures and training objectives unchanged. Against their own unmodified counterparts, CellBRIDGE consistently wins: CellBRIDGE+CFM (α=1) beats OT-CFM by 0.030 W1 on V1 Light and 0.057 on Dendritic Stimulus; CellBRIDGE+MFM (α=0.5) beats OT-MFM by 0.008 and 0.035 W1 respectively; CellBRIDGE+UOT-FM (α=1) beats UOT-FM by 0.051 and 0.077 W1; and CellBRIDGE+SF2M (α=1) beats SF2M by 0.028, 0.044, and 0.188 W1 on V1 Light, Dendritic Stimulus, and Lung Tumor. Against non-paired baselines, the picture is mixed: on V1 Light and Dendritic Stimulus, CellBRIDGE+CFM/MFM/UOT-FM outperform TrajectoryNet, DSB, SnapMMD, VGFM, MIOFlow, and Moscot; however, on Lung Tumor, VGFM (W1=2.175), MIOFlow (2.001), and Moscot (2.000) achieve slightly lower W1 than CellBRIDGE+CFM/MFM/UOT-FM (≈1.97–2.06), though CellBRIDGE+SF2M (α=1, W1=3.638) still trails these by a wider margin. The paper reports that all paired improvements remain significant after Holm correction (adjusted p<0.05) with small-to-moderate effect sizes (Cohen's d_z = 0.27–0.63), and that the gains are driven by coherent LR structure rather than arbitrary regularization, as confirmed by shuffle and random-catalog ablations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CellBRIDGE extends the Fused Gromov-Wasserstein framework (Vayer et al., 2020) from scalar relational structure to multi-typed, directed ligand-receptor communication tensors, and adapts the conditional-gradient solver (Braun et al., 2022; POT library) to handle this vector-valued structure. It departs from feature-only OT (Waddington-OT, TrajectoryNet) and spatial/geometric OT (Moscot, NicheFlow) by injecting a biologically grounded, interpretable prior—smoothness of directed CCI geometry—directly at the coupling level, making it orthogonal to and composable with downstream continuous-time learners (CFM, MFM, SF2M, UOT-FM). Unlike communication-aware models such as CytoBridge that learn interaction effects end-to-end, CellBRIDGE uses curated LR catalogs as an explicit, editable regularizer, enabling mechanism-specific counterfactuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play coupling-level design means CellBRIDGE can refine endpoint correspondences for any existing trajectory learner without architectural changes, and the editable LR catalog enables interpretable in silico perturbations (e.g., silencing EGFR/ALK/MET shifts lung-cancer trajectories consistent with known pharmacology). A key limitation is that the structural prior requires CCI geometry to be approximately persistent across the temporal gap; on a 6-day embryo interval the method provides no benefit and can degrade performance, suggesting practitioners must assess interaction persistence before applying the structural term. Open questions include scalability to atlas-scale datasets (1.7M cells), time-varying LR catalogs for non-stationary signaling, and tighter integration with spatial transcriptomics to validate proxy CCI networks.
