## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees but produces impractically large prediction sets in structured domains like route planning and trip planning, where the output space is a graph and the conformal set can contain exponentially many candidate paths. No prior work bridges conformal calibration with combinatorial compression to yield compact, interpretable subgraphs while preserving statistical validity. The paper formalizes this gap as the conformal subgraph problem and provides the first provably valid and efficient compression algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method takes a model-predicted route A*, a road network (or activity graph), a calibration set of (A_t, B_t) pairs split into two disjoint subsets, a target coverage level φ, and a nonconformity score f(A,B). It outputs a compact subgraph K_{τ*}(A*)—a set of road segments (vertices) whose induced hyperedges capture the required probability mass—along with a nested sequence {K_τ} that is monotone in τ. The subgraph is not an explicit path list but a vertex set whose induced hyperedges implicitly define all covered routes, making it tractable even when the underlying path space is exponential.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 performs standard split conformal calibration to obtain a distance threshold d* and an initial bag of plausible paths S_{d*}(A*); Stage 2 compresses this bag into a minimal subgraph via LP rounding on a weighted hypergraph formulation. The compression LP minimizes vertex count subject to covering (1−ε)W total hyperedge weight, and threshold rounding at ρ=κ/(1+κ) yields a (1+κ, 1+1/κ) bicriteria approximation. The key theoretical contribution is proving monotonicity of the rounded solutions by showing the LP's Lagrangian is identical to a parametric minimum s-t cut on a bipartite flow network, so the nested structure of parametric min-cuts (Gallo et al., 1989) directly implies nested subgraphs. This monotonicity enables valid distribution-free conformal calibration via a second quantile step on the compression scores, and the same nested family plugs into risk-controlling prediction sets for general loss functions.

## Results

On the synthetic 6×6 grid navigation task, the LP-based conformal subgraph algorithm is significantly more compressed than both greedy baselines for all coverage levels φ ≤ 0.8; at φ = 0.75 it selects 52 edges while correctly prioritizing the urban center over the bypass highway. On the real-world Porto Taxi dataset (~300 airport-to-center trips, 100 train / 100 test), LP and reverse greedy achieve comparable non-trivial compression for φ ≤ 0.4, both outperforming forward greedy, with LP selecting 797 edges at τ = 0.8. In the trip-planning simulation with a planted core (τ = 0.8, R = 5 activity types), the LP algorithm recovers the optimal core exactly (vertex ratio = 1.0) for all φ ≤ 0.75, with a sharp performance knee appearing at φ ≈ 0.8. Theoretically, the algorithm achieves a (1+κ, 1+1/κ) bicriteria approximation guarantee on both coverage loss and subgraph size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the LP rounding algorithm against two greedy baselines: Forward Greedy (selects edges in decreasing order of training-path frequency) and Reverse Greedy (iteratively deletes lowest-traffic edges, generalizing Charikar's peeling to hypergraphs). On the synthetic navigation task, LP clearly wins: it is significantly more compressed than both greedy methods across all φ ≤ 0.8, and at equal edge budgets (52 edges, φ = 0.75) LP correctly identifies the urban grid as necessary for coverage while omitting the bypass highway entirely, whereas reverse greedy wastes budget on highway edges. On the real-world Porto dataset, LP and reverse greedy achieve comparable compression for φ ≤ 0.4 (both beating forward greedy), suggesting the empirical route distribution lacks strong compressible structure beyond frequency-based heuristics in this sparse-sample regime. In the trip-planning experiment, LP perfectly recovers the planted core structure (ratio = 1.0) up to φ = 0.75, demonstrating that the algorithm exploits density structure that greedy methods would miss. The paper also notes that greedy baselines, while monotone and thus conformally valid, cannot provide a worst-case compression guarantee analogous to Theorem 4.1 (illustrated by Example G.1 where reverse greedy incurs edge cost a ≫ b versus the optimal b).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conformal prediction and combinatorial optimization, building on split conformal calibration (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021; Lei et al., 2013), the learn-then-test framework (Angelopoulos et al., 2022), and risk-controlling prediction sets (Bates et al., 2021) for the statistical side, while drawing on densest ratio subgraph and parametric min-cut machinery (Goldberg, 1984; Charikar, 2000; Gallo et al., 1989; Chekuri et al., 2022) for the algorithmic side. It departs from classical densest k-subgraph (DkS) hardness results by identifying that conformal compression operates in a high-coverage regime (linear fraction of edges retained) where efficient constant-factor approximations exist, and from Gao et al. (2025b) by replacing arbitrary greedy monotone constructions with an LP rounding scheme that provably achieves bicriteria compression guarantees. The key conceptual departure from Zhang et al. (2025) on structured conformal prediction is that this work starts from a calibrated (potentially large, unstructured) conformal set and compresses it, rather than constructing prediction sets from model probabilities, and achieves distribution-free validity via a monotonicity proof rather than requiring a learned distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled bridge between conformal prediction's validity guarantees and combinatorial optimization's efficiency guarantees for structured output spaces, with the monotonicity-via-parametric-min-cut result being the key technical enabler that makes distribution-free calibration possible for the compressed subgraph. Open questions include whether tighter approximations (PTAS or exact) exist for special graph classes (planar road networks, tree-structured labels), whether end-to-end training with differentiable compression regularizers is tractable, and whether the framework extends to conditional coverage beyond the marginal guarantees shown here. The empirical validation is proof-of-concept (synthetic grids, ~300 real-world taxi trips), so practical scalability to large-scale routing networks remains to be demonstrated.
