## Problem

Existing 3D world generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: panorama-based approaches produce fully immersive (360°×180°) environments but restrict camera motion to a small neighborhood, while iterative completion methods enable long-range navigation but cannot cover all viewing directions simultaneously. No prior method generates 3D scenes that are both fully omnidirectional and navigable over extended spatial extents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263976/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing an outdoor/natural scene and an integer N (number of panoramas to fuse, controlling world length) as input. It produces a colored 3D point cloud W = {(p_k, c_k)} where positions p_k ∈ R³ and colors c_k ∈ [0,1]³, representing a navigable world with full omnidirectional coverage at every spatial location. The spatial extent scales linearly with N; a world with N=3 panoramas takes approximately 40 minutes to generate on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

SphericalDreamer operates in three stages. Stage I generates N layered depth panoramas (LDPs) from text using a Flux-based panorama generator and 360MonoDepth, then lifts each into a 3D 'sphere' point cloud; LDPs resolve occlusion by using SAM to detect foreground masks, inpainting the background with a two-stage LaMA+Flux pipeline, and constructing a panoramic hull depth from row-wise maximum depth. Stage II performs pairwise generative fusion: consecutive spheres are opened on facing sides to form a capsule with a missing central region, an intermediate equirectangular view is rendered from the capsule center, missing regions are inpainted with FluxFill, and a novel Harmonic Blending technique—formulated as a graph-based Laplacian energy minimization with Dirichlet boundary constraints on a k-NN graph—smoothly deforms the estimated depth to align with existing geometry before 3D back-projection. Stage III assembles all spheres and filling blocks into the final world. The key insight is that panoramic representations, when fused via generative inpainting and harmonic depth alignment, can simultaneously preserve full omnidirectional coverage and enable long-range navigation.

## Results

SphericalDreamer achieves near-perfect scene coverage (0.999) across all three camera trajectory types (rotation, translation, and combined), making it the only method that is simultaneously immersive and navigable. It also attains the best BRISQUE scores on every trajectory: 44.96 (rotation), 36.57 (translation), and 41.73 (combined), outperforming the next-best baselines by 7–30 points. On supplementary metrics (Table 5), SphericalDreamer leads on CLIP-Score (0.3325), C-CLIP (0.8433), CLIP-IQA (0.7014), and Q-Align (2.3088), confirming state-of-the-art visual and semantic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SphericalDreamer is compared against four baselines with publicly available code: two panorama-based (LayerPano3D) and three iterative-completion methods (LucidDreamer, WonderJourney, SceneScape). LayerPano3D achieves full coverage under pure rotation (1.000) but collapses under translation (0.476) and combined motion (0.594), confirming its lack of navigability. Conversely, WonderJourney and SceneScape handle translation well (0.998 and 0.960 coverage) but fail under rotation (0.556 and 0.796) and especially under combined trajectories (0.404 and 0.724), reflecting their restricted field-of-view design. LucidDreamer performs moderately across all trajectories but never reaches full coverage (best 0.798) and has the worst BRISQUE scores (62.54–65.16). SphericalDreamer wins on every metric in every trajectory setting, with the largest margins on combined rotation+translation coverage (0.999 vs. 0.775 for LucidDreamer, the next best) and BRISQUE (41.73 vs. 55.91 for SceneScape). On the supplementary CLIP-based metrics, SphericalDreamer leads by 11–43% over the strongest baseline on each metric, further validating its superior visual fidelity and prompt alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SphericalDreamer sits at the intersection of two previously incompatible paradigms for text-to-3D world generation: panorama-based methods (LayerPano3D, HoloDreamer) that provide full omnidirectional coverage but limited spatial extent, and iterative completion methods (SceneScape, WonderJourney, LucidDreamer) that enable long-range navigation but sacrifice immersion. The paper builds directly on LayerPano3D's layered depth panorama construction and Shih et al.'s 3D Photography for the spherical building blocks, while borrowing the render-inpaint-backproject loop from iterative completion for the pairwise fusion stage. Its key technical departure is the Harmonic Blending step, which repurposes classical Laplacian mesh editing and harmonic surface deformation (Sorkine et al., 2004; Yu et al., 2004) to resolve geometric discontinuities when fusing monocular depth estimates with rendered geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously achieve full 360°×180° omnidirectional coverage and long-range navigability in generated 3D worlds, directly addressing a stated fundamental limitation of all prior approaches. The practical impact for VR/AR content creation is significant, as it removes the trade-off between immersion and spatial extent that has constrained the field. Key limitations include reduced effectiveness for planar geometry (urban/indoor scenes) due to panoramic depth estimation inaccuracies, and the current restriction to linear camera trajectories with a fixed number of panoramas. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-linear topologies (branching paths, loops), whether the ~40-minute generation time on a single A100 is acceptable for interactive use, and whether advances in panoramic depth estimation will close the remaining quality gap for structured environments.
