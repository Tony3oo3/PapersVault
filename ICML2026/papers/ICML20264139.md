## Problem

Existing autoregressive models for vector graphics and layout generation rely on discretizing continuous coordinates into finite token vocabularies, which imposes a hard precision ceiling that scales exponentially with resolution. In high-precision domains like semiconductor circuit design, even minor positioning errors cause functional failure, yet no prior autoregressive framework jointly models discrete and continuous values in variable-length sequences without lossy quantization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes variable-length sequences of atomic units, each pairing a discrete identifier (e.g., layer type, SVG command) with an M-dimensional continuous vector (e.g., x, y, w, h for layouts; four coordinate pairs for SVG paths). Inputs can be a partial layout (50 or 100 given layers for completion), a text description (for text-to-SVG), or nothing (unconditioned generation). Outputs are full variable-length hybrid sequences where continuous values are produced at infinite precision via diffusion sampling, with sequence length determined autoregressively by an EOS decision rather than fixed in advance.

## Method

IPAM uses a Transformer decoder that embeds each atomic unit by concatenating a learned discrete embedding with a linear projection of its continuous vector, then conditions two parallel generation branches on the resulting latent state: a categorical head (MLP + softmax) for discrete identifiers and a compact diffusion denoiser (MLP with AdaLN residual blocks) for continuous values. Two key components enable variable-length control: an EOS logit adjustment mechanism where a dedicated MLP dynamically modifies the EOS token logit based on the full sequence context, and a differentiable length regularization loss that penalizes the gap between expected sequence length (computed from adjusted EOS probabilities) and the ground-truth length. Training combines cross-entropy for discrete prediction, denoising score-matching for continuous prediction, and the length penalty, while generation alternates categorical sampling with reverse diffusion (Improved DDPM) at each autoregressive step.

## Results

On the novel ContLayNet benchmark (334K semiconductor layouts), IPAM achieves CLC=0.088, PDC=0.068, HSC=0.027, VSC=0.023 for 50-layer completion, outperforming LayoutTransformer (18-bit) by 4.5× on CLC and DLT by 2.6× on CLC. On PubLayNet completion, IPAM reaches FID=4.58 versus 186.61 for LT and 35.80 for DLT, with Overlap=4.59 and Alignment=0.17. For text-to-SVG on FIGR-8-SVG, IPAM (infinite precision) achieves FID=48.73 and CLIP=20.05, while IconShop degrades from FID=37.35 at 8-bit to complete failure at 10-bit precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPAM decisively outperforms LayoutTransformer (LT) across all four DRC metrics on ContLayNet: at 50-layer completion, IPAM's CLC of 0.088 is 4.5× lower than LT's 0.397, and its HSC/VSC are roughly halved. Against DLT, IPAM also wins on every metric (e.g., CLC 0.088 vs. 0.227, PDC 0.068 vs. 0.125), with the gap widening at 100 layers (CLC 0.046 vs. 0.144). On PubLayNet, IPAM's FID of 4.58 is 40× better than LT's 186.61 and 7.8× better than DLT's 35.80, while also achieving the lowest Overlap (4.59 vs. 64.16 and 9.65) and Alignment (0.17 vs. 1.06 and 0.84). For text-to-SVG, IPAM at infinite precision (FID=48.73, CLIP=20.05) is comparable to IconShop's best 8-bit setting (FID=37.35, CLIP=22.13) but robustly scales where IconShop fails entirely beyond 10-bit precision. The key differentiator is that IPAM maintains infinite precision without the exponential vocabulary growth that causes LT and IconShop to degrade or fail at higher precision levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPAM extends the autoregressive-diffusion paradigm introduced by MAR (Li et al., 2024), which modeled purely continuous sequences for fixed-size image generation, to the broader setting of hybrid discrete-continuous variable-length sequences. It departs from discretization-based autoregressive methods (LayoutTransformer, IconShop) that sacrifice precision through token quantization, and from non-autoregressive hybrid models (DLT, TabNAT) that are confined to fixed schemas. The key architectural departure is the unified atomic-unit representation where a single latent vector conditions both a categorical head for discrete tokens and a diffusion denoiser for continuous vectors, augmented by an MLP-based EOS logit adjustment and a differentiable length regularization loss for sequence-length control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ContLayNet benchmark (334K semiconductor layouts with DRC-based functional metrics) fills a critical gap: prior layout benchmarks used image-level metrics (FID, CLIP) that cannot capture whether a generated circuit is actually functional. The precision-scaling analysis (exponential vocabulary growth with discretization) provides a clear theoretical justification for why infinite-precision modeling is necessary in engineering domains. However, all methods including IPAM still show substantial gaps from real data (e.g., CLC of 0.088 vs. 0.0 for real), inference remains slow (~25 min for 100 samples at 100 diffusion steps), and error propagation in longer sequences remains an open challenge. Whether this framework scales to full-chip designs with millions of elements, and whether the diffusion component can be further accelerated without quality loss, are important open questions.
