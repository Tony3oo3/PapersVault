## Problem

Training Binary Neural Networks relies on the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) to approximate gradients through non-differentiable binarization, but STE introduces systematic gradient mismatch: its fixed-value surrogate is biased relative to the true sign-function gradient, and its fixed-range clipping (|x|≤1) discards informative gradient components outside that range. Existing remedies—piecewise polynomials, SignSwish, progressive quantization—are handcrafted heuristics that cannot guarantee optimal gradient approximation, and the accumulated mismatch degrades accuracy in deep networks.

## Input / Output

SURGE is architecture-agnostic and applies to any binarized linear operator (convolution, linear, attention projection). During training, each binarized layer receives the same input as a standard BNN layer but internally maintains a parallel full-precision auxiliary branch; the forward output is identical to a vanilla BNN (the auxiliary contribution cancels via a detach trick), so no inference-time overhead is introduced. At deployment, only the 1-bit weights and activations remain, yielding 32× memory reduction and ~4× latency speedup on edge hardware (e.g., ODROID C4) relative to the full-precision backbone.

## Method

SURGE introduces two coupled components. The Dual-Path Gradient Compensator (DPGC) builds a full-precision auxiliary branch with identical dimensions alongside each binarized (main) branch; the layer output is written as f_b(x) − λ·f_a(x)↓ + λ·f_a(x), where ↓ is a gradient-stop operator, so the forward value equals the standard binary output while backpropagation splits into a STE-based binary gradient g_b and a full-precision compensator gradient g_a that captures higher-order terms lost to clipping. The Adaptive Gradient Scaler (AGS) then combines them as g_b + λ_AGS·g_a with λ_AGS = η·‖g_b‖₂/(‖g_a‖₂+ε), a norm-ratio rule derived as a practical approximation of the theoretically optimal scaling factor (Theorem 5.3) under an isotropic-noise moment model, ensuring the binary gradient dominates while the auxiliary path injects magnitude-aware compensation without destabilizing convergence. After training the auxiliary branches are discarded, leaving a standard BNN with no extra parameters or FLOPs at inference.

## Results

On ImageNet with one-stage training (ResNet-18, 1/1 bits), SURGE achieves 62.0% top-1 and 83.7% top-5 accuracy, outperforming the previous best RBONN (61.4% top-1) by 0.6%. On CIFAR-10, SURGE attains 93.1% (ResNet-18), 88.0% (ResNet-20), and 92.5% (VGG-Small) top-1 accuracy, consistently leading all baselines. On PASCAL VOC (Faster-RCNN, ResNet-18), SURGE reaches 77.0 mAP, surpassing IDa-Det (76.5) and the real-valued Faster-RCNN (76.4). On GLUE (BERT-base, 1/1 bits), SURGE achieves a 72.0 average score, exceeding BiT (70.6) by 1.4 points and BiBERT (63.2) by 8.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURGE outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every benchmark. On ImageNet one-stage, SURGE leads RBONN (61.4%) by 0.6% and ReCU (61.0%) by 1.0% in top-1 accuracy, with a 3.9% margin over IR-Net (58.1%). On CIFAR-10, SURGE consistently edges out the strongest competitor ReCU by 0.3–0.6% across ResNet-18, ResNet-20, and VGG-Small, and surpasses older methods like XNOR-Net and DoReFa by 2–13%. On PASCAL VOC, SURGE (77.0 mAP) beats IDa-Det (76.5) by 0.5% and even exceeds the real-valued Faster-RCNN (76.4) while using 5.21× fewer FLOPs. On GLUE, SURGE (72.0 avg) improves over BiT (70.6) by 1.4 points and over BiBERT (63.2) by 8.8 points, with the largest gains on QQP (+1.7) and STS-B (+3.7). The two-stage ImageNet results show SURGE (66.7%) marginally ahead of RBONN (66.5%) and ReCU (66.4%), confirming consistent gains across training protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURGE builds directly on the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) framework for training binarized networks, which has been the standard gradient approximation since Bengio et al. (2013). Rather than modifying the surrogate function itself—as prior work does with piecewise polynomials (Bi-Real Net), parameterized sigmoids (DSQ), or learnable thresholds (ReActNet)—SURGE keeps STE intact and introduces a parallel full-precision auxiliary branch whose backpropagated gradients compensate for STE's first-order approximation error. The key conceptual departure is the gradient-decoupled dual-path design (using a detach operator to preserve forward outputs) combined with a theoretically derived norm-ratio scaling rule (Theorem 5.3), which replaces the ad-hoc fixed clipping ranges and handcrafted scale factors used in prior BNN training strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURGE addresses a persistent and well-identified limitation in BNN training—gradient mismatch from STE's fixed-range clipping—without altering the forward pass or introducing any inference overhead, making it a drop-in training improvement for existing BNN pipelines. The theoretical grounding for the adaptive scaling factor provides a principled alternative to the heuristic choices that have dominated the field. However, the method requires retaining full-precision auxiliary parameters throughout training, incurring ~25% additional training time and ~8% memory overhead, which may be prohibitive for very large models or resource-constrained training environments. Open questions include whether the auxiliary branch can be further compressed (e.g., low-rank or 1×1 kernels, as the SURGE* variant hints), whether the approach extends naturally to sub-1-bit or mixed-precision settings, and whether the theoretical assumptions (isotropic noise, stable cosine alignment) hold in practice for very deep or heterogeneous architectures.
