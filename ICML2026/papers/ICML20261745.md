## Problem

Existing anchor-based multi-view clustering methods perform cross-view alignment at the sample-anchor bipartite graph level rather than on more discriminative sample-level similarity structures, yielding inadequate consensus representations. Additionally, they follow a unidirectional optimization pipeline where spectral embedding and discrete label assignment are decoupled, preventing clustering results from feeding back to refine the graph structure and limiting both performance and stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view feature matrices X^(v) ∈ R^{n×dv} (with potentially different feature dimensions per view) and a target number of clusters k. It outputs a discrete label matrix Y ∈ {0,1}^{n×k} and a purified consensus similarity graph W̄ ∈ R^{n×n}. The number of anchors m is set as a multiple of k (typically 3k–5k), and the method applies top-q sparsification to each view's similarity graph to control computational cost while preserving discriminative structure.

## Method

DRMC first learns per-view anchor assignments A^(v) and anchor features U^(v) via non-negative matrix factorization with sparse regularization, then elevates each anchor graph to a full sample-level similarity matrix W^(v) = A^(v) D_v^{-1} A^(v)^T. These sample-level graphs are fused into a consensus graph using adaptive distance-inverse view weighting, where views closer to the current consensus receive higher weight. A discrete feedback module jointly optimizes the spectral embedding F and discrete labels Y by minimizing ||F − YR||_F^2 under orthogonality constraints on R (solved via SVD) and row-stochastic constraints on Y (solved by per-sample argmax). The resulting partition S = YY^T is then used to purify the consensus graph element-wise, suppressing inter-cluster edges via W̄_ij = C_ij/(1+μ(1−S_ij)), creating a bidirectional loop between label assignment and graph refinement. All variables are updated via alternating optimization until convergence.

## Results

DRMC achieves the best ACC on 7 out of 8 datasets, with particularly strong margins on 3sources (0.8343 vs. 0.7396 for RCAGL, a 9.47-point gap), Caltech101-20 (0.7800 vs. 0.6605 for OMSC), and Caltech101-all (0.4576 vs. 0.3206 for RCAGL). On BBC4, DRMC ties FDAGF at 0.8832 ACC. However, DRMC trails on F-score for Caltech101-20 (0.3938 vs. 0.6987 for OMSC) and Caltech101-all (0.1658 vs. 0.2927 for TLRLF4MVC), and on NMI for MSRC_v1 (0.8532 vs. 0.8986 for TLRLF4MVC), indicating that the method's advantage is most pronounced in accuracy and purity rather than in all four metrics uniformly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRMC outperforms all 11 baselines on ACC for 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin over RCAGL on 3sources (0.8343 vs. 0.7396) and over OMSC on Caltech101-20 (0.7800 vs. 0.6605). On BBC4, DRMC ties FDAGF at 0.8832 ACC, and on AwA it edges out TLRLF4MVC by only 1.01 points (0.1095 vs. 0.0994). The closest competitive threats are TLRLF4MVC (strong on MSRC_v1 NMI at 0.8986 vs. DRMC's 0.8532, and on Caltech101-all F-score at 0.2927 vs. 0.1658) and OMSC (which beats DRMC on Caltech101-20 F-score, 0.6987 vs. 0.3938). AWMVC and FDAGF show strong but inconsistent performance across datasets, while older methods like SMVSC, OPMC, and FPMVS are consistently outperformed by a wide margin on nearly all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRMC extends the anchor-based multi-view clustering (AMVC) lineage that began with unified anchor-graph optimization frameworks (Wang et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2022) and adaptive multi-view fusion strategies (Fang et al. 2023, Shu et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2023). Its key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) it elevates cross-view alignment from the sample-anchor bipartite graph level to full sample-to-sample similarity matrices, and (2) it replaces the standard unidirectional pipeline (graph → spectral embedding → k-means) with a bidirectional loop in which discrete cluster labels are fed back to purify the consensus graph via an orthogonal Procrustes alignment between the embedding and indicator matrices. This positions DRMC as a refinement of the AMVC paradigm rather than a departure from it, repurposing spectral clustering and matrix factorization tools within a new iterative feedback architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional feedback mechanism—where discrete labels iteratively suppress inter-cluster edges in the consensus graph—offers a principled way to close the gap between continuous representation learning and discrete assignment that has plagued spectral clustering pipelines. However, the O(n²) space and time complexity introduced by constructing full sample-level similarity graphs limits scalability to very large datasets (e.g., AwA with 30k samples shows only modest absolute accuracy), and the authors explicitly flag this as the primary open challenge. For practitioners, the method is most relevant at moderate scales (hundreds to a few thousand samples) where the quality gain from graph purification outweighs the computational overhead, but it does not yet address the large-scale regime that motivated anchor-based methods in the first place.
