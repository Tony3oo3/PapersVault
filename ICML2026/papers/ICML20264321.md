## Problem

Pretrained-model-based continual learning methods rely on offline multi-epoch training and disjoint task boundaries, making them sub-optimal for General Continual Learning (GCL) where data arrives in a single online pass and label spaces overlap across tasks. The paper identifies two specific failure modes: representation learning degrades under one-pass online updates, and output alignment suffers under blurry task boundaries with imbalanced classes, particularly under self-supervised pretrained models whose representations are underfitted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264321/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images as input, processed in small online batches (batch size 64) with imbalanced class distributions during the GCL phase, and produces class labels via a linear output layer. It operates as a plug-in on top of existing PTM-based CL/GCL methods (L2P, DualPrompt, MVP, MISA) using a ViT-B/16 backbone with prefix tuning (prompt length 5, layers 1–5), requiring no rehearsal of past samples. The one-time meta-refinement phase uses subsets of ImageNet-1K pretraining data (100 classes × 400 samples for meta-learning, 1000 classes × 200 samples for covariance initialization), processing only ~2M images (0.15% of full pretraining).

## Method

MePo consists of two components. First, Meta Rep constructs pseudo task sequences from pretraining data and applies a bi-level meta-learning paradigm: an inner loop sequentially trains the backbone and output layer on each pseudo task (simulating CL drift), while an outer loop performs joint training on a held-out validation set to counteract sequential bias, with a Reptile-style first-order meta-update accumulating parameter changes across K meta-epochs to yield a CL-tailored backbone initialization. Second, Meta Cov computes a meta covariance matrix from class-wise feature means of pretraining data as a reference geometry, then during GCL aligns each batch's feature covariance to this reference via Cholesky decomposition (A = L_cur⁻¹ L_pre), producing post-aligned features that are combined with pre-aligned features through a weighted interpolation (α·f̂ + (1−α)·f) to balance stability and plasticity before updating the model. The approach is inspired by meta-plasticity (positioning the backbone in a critical neurodynamic state for rapid adaptation) and reconstructive memory (continually encoding new memories into a shared representation space).

## Results

Under the most challenging Sup-21/1K self-supervised checkpoint, MePo as a plug-in yields AUC improvements of +15.10% (CIFAR-100), +13.36% (ImageNet-R), and +12.56% (CUB-200) over the state-of-the-art MISA baseline. Gains are even larger under the weakest iBOT-21K checkpoint, where MISA+MePo achieves 75.80% AUC on CIFAR-100 (+10.50%) and 49.33% on CUB-200 (+30.71%). The method adds negligible runtime overhead (identical batch time to baselines in Table 3) and only 0.67% extra storage for the covariance matrix, while the one-time meta-refinement phase processes just 0.15% of the original pretraining data budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MePo consistently outperforms all six baselines across every PTM and dataset combination. Against MISA (the strongest GCL baseline), the largest gains appear under self-supervised PTMs: +15.10% AUC on CIFAR-100 and +30.71% on CUB-200 under iBOT-21K, confirming that MePo's meta-refinement is most beneficial when pretrained representations are underfitted. Against Deep L2P, MePo delivers the most dramatic single improvement of +19.97% AUC on ImageNet-R under Sup-21/1K (42.74→62.71), suggesting the meta-learned backbone initialization compensates for L2P's limited prompt capacity under online datastreams. Against MVP, gains are moderate under Sup-21K (+3.34% on ImageNet-R) but substantial under iBOT-21K (+16.06% on ImageNet-R), indicating MVP's contrastive prompt loss is insufficient for weakly-supervised features without MePo's backbone refinement. Against DualPrompt, improvements are smaller (2–5% AUC) because DualPrompt's complementary prompts already provide some robustness, though MePo still helps most on ImageNet-R. Against CODA-P (evaluated only under Sup-21K), MePo+Deep L2P surpasses CODA-P by +3.52% AUC on CIFAR-100 (83.63 vs 78.81) and +8.60% on ImageNet-R (58.71 vs 50.11). Against Seq FT(SL), MePo+Deep L2P leads by +8.73% AUC on CIFAR-100 (83.63 vs 74.90) under Sup-21K, showing that meta-refinement of the backbone substantially outperforms simply reducing the backbone learning rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

MePo extends the PTMs-based continual learning line (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) and the GCL-specific methods (MVP, MISA) by introducing a meta-learning-based post-refinement phase that operates on pretraining data before any downstream GCL begins. Conceptually, it repurposes the MAML/Reptile bi-level optimization framework (Finn et al., 2017; Nichol & Schulman, 2018) not for few-shot adaptation but for reshaping the pretrained backbone into a CL-tailored initialization, and pairs this with a Cholesky-based covariance alignment inspired by reconstructive memory theory. Unlike prior PTMs-based GCL methods that rely solely on prompt initialization or contrastive regularization during the GCL phase, MePo decouples the adaptation problem into a one-time upstream refinement and a lightweight downstream alignment, positioning it as a method-agnostic plug-in rather than a standalone GCL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in, rehearsal-free design with negligible per-batch overhead makes MePo practically attractive for deploying continual learning systems on pretrained vision transformers, particularly under self-supervised checkpoints where prior methods degrade most severely. The observation that a re-implemented Deep L2P can match or exceed dedicated GCL methods (MVP, MISA) under certain settings raises questions about whether the GCL community has been over-engineering solutions to problems that better backbone initialization could resolve. Open questions include whether the meta-refinement phase scales to substantially longer task sequences or non-vision modalities, how sensitive the approach is to the quality and diversity of the pretraining data used for pseudo-task construction, and whether the covariance alignment generalizes to settings where the pretrained representation space is not well-conditioned.
