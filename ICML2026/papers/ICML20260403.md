## Problem

Existing robot manipulation policies predict actions directly in the time domain, treating all timesteps uniformly and failing to capture the hierarchical temporal structure where coarse task-level motion and fine-grained execution refinements play distinct causal roles. This uniform treatment causes policies to struggle on precision-sensitive manipulation tasks and to degrade sharply under noisy teleoperated demonstrations, where high-frequency corrective behavior is most affected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260403/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy receives multi-view RGB images (fixed and wrist-mounted cameras), a language instruction encoded by a frozen language model, and proprioceptive state (end-effector pose and gripper state), all concatenated into a shared observation tuple. It outputs a continuous action chunk of length K (16, 32, or 64 timesteps) consisting of 6D twist plus a 1D gripper channel; the twist dimensions are predicted in the DCT frequency domain and reconstructed via inverse DCT, while the gripper channel is predicted and concatenated unchanged in the time domain.

## Method

CSP (Causal Spectral Policy) represents action chunks in the spectral domain via the discrete cosine transform and factorizes the action distribution into a causal coarse-to-fine process: p(c|o,l) = p_low(c_low|o,l) · p_high(c_high|o,c_low). Two GPT-style transformer trunks (8 layers, 256 hidden dim, 4 heads) are trained separately — the first predicts low-frequency coefficients from observation and language, and the second predicts high-frequency coefficients conditioned on observation, language, and the stop-gradient of the realized low-frequency prediction, enforcing the intended causal dependency. The low/high frequency split is selected data-drivenly per task using a 90% cumulative spectral energy threshold (typically ~30% of coefficients). Training uses L2 regression on spectral coefficients for both stages, and the final action is reconstructed by concatenating predicted coefficients and applying the inverse DCT. The key insight, validated through frequency-domain interventions and counterfactual conditioning experiments, is that low-frequency components govern global reaching motion while high-frequency components are essential for precise alignment and contact, and that this low-to-high ordering is not interchangeable with a high-to-low alternative.

## Results

On LIBERO-90 and LIBERO-10 (chunk size 16), CSP achieves 91.9% and 80.8% success rate, outperforming all baselines. On MimicGen precision tasks (chunk size 16), CSP reaches a 65.5% mean success rate versus 58.5% (DP-CNN), 53.8% (DP-Transformer), 51.5% (ACT), and 49.8% (BAKU). The advantage is most dramatic at chunk size 64, where CSP maintains 50.0% MimicGen mean while ACT collapses to 9.0% and BAKU to 17.3%, demonstrating that the spectral coarse-to-fine structure preserves performance under longer prediction horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSP outperforms all three primary baselines across every evaluated setting. Against Diffusion Policy (DP-CNN/DP-Transformer), CSP gains 8.9–14.9 points on LIBERO-10 (K=16) and 15.4–22.4 points on MimicGen mean (K=16); at K=64 the gap widens to 22.4 points on MimicGen mean over DP-CNN. Against ACT, CSP leads by 10.3 points on LIBERO-10 (K=16) and by a dramatic 41.0 points on MimicGen mean (K=64: 50.0% vs. 9.0%), where ACT's time-domain autoregressive prediction collapses under longer horizons. Against BAKU, CSP achieves 23.4 points higher on LIBERO-10 (K=16) and 32.7 points higher on MimicGen mean (K=64). The key differentiator is robustness to chunk length: while all baselines degrade sharply from K=16 to K=64 on precision tasks, CSP degrades gracefully, suggesting the spectral coarse-to-fine factorization provides a structural inductive bias that standard time-domain policies lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FAST's DCT-based action tokenization (Pertsch et al., 2025) for the spectral reparameterization, but departs from both FAST and FreqPolicy by introducing an explicit *causal* coarse-to-fine factorization: low-frequency coefficients are predicted from observation and language, then high-frequency corrections are conditioned on the realized coarse trajectory. Prior coarse-to-fine manipulation work (CARP, DiSPo, Dense Policy) operates in the time domain via progressive temporal resolution, while FreqPolicy iteratively unmasks spectral coefficients without enforcing a directional dependency. CSP's key conceptual departure is treating the frequency split as a causal boundary rather than a mere representation choice, validated through counterfactual interventions that reverse or remove the conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong for real-world deployment: teleoperated demonstrations are inherently noisy, and the paper shows that spectral coarse-to-fine structure substantially improves robustness to human-like execution noise (AR(1) drift + corrective bursts) compared to time-domain baselines. Open questions include whether the data-driven frequency-split heuristic generalizes to whole-body or multi-robot control, whether the causal low-to-high ordering holds for tasks where fine motion precedes coarse planning (e.g., reactive avoidance), and whether the approach extends to flow-matching or diffusion-based decoders beyond the current GPT-style regression. The method is relatively lightweight (two 8-layer transformer trunks) and could be integrated into existing VLA pipelines.
