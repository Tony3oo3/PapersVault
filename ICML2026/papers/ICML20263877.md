## Problem

Prior work demonstrated that machine-text evasion attacks (paraphrasing, DPO optimization, adversarial prompting) can fool standard zero-shot and supervised detectors, but it remained unclear whether these attacks also erase the underlying stylistic fingerprints that distinguish machine from human authorship. This paper shows that style-based detectors remain robust to all prior attacks, then introduces a new style-aware paraphrasing attack that defeats even style-based detectors in the single-document regime, while revealing that multi-document analysis ultimately recovers detectability.

## Input / Output

The system takes a machine-generated text sample (32–128 tokens) and M in-context exemplar pairs (machine paraphrase → human original) from a target author as conditioning context, and produces a single style-matched paraphrase. At inference, it runs three iterative rounds, each generating 10 candidates and re-ranking with SBERT cosine similarity to preserve semantics, with the final output selected for maximum semantic fidelity. The training data consists of human texts from the Reddit Million Users Dataset paired with 5 Mistral-7B-generated paraphrases per text, stratified-sampled across 63,184 stylistically diverse authors.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage training recipe for a Mistral-7B paraphraser (LoRA, r=32) that jointly optimizes for author-specific style mimicry and detector evasion. Stage 1 is supervised fine-tuning on paraphrase-inversion pairs conditioned on same-author exemplars, teaching the model to map machine paraphrases back to human-style originals. Stage 2 applies DPO (β=5) against a detector specifically trained to distinguish the system's own outputs from human text, rather than a generic zero-shot detector, capturing the full feature set that makes the system identifiable. The key insight is that generic optimization against a 'machine' signal is ill-posed because human style is author-specific; the system must target a concrete human author's fingerprint. At inference, iterative application (3 rounds) bridges the distribution mismatch between training on paraphrases of human text and deployment on paraphrases of machine text, with SBERT re-ranking at each step to limit semantic drift.

## Results

StyleDetect (few-shot stylistic detector) maintains AUROC ≥ 95 across all evasion attacks, while FastDetectGPT drops to 35–66 and Binoculars to 34–92 (Table 1). The proposed style-aware paraphraser is the least detectable method: at N=1 document, the strongest detector achieves AUROC(1) ≈ 0.54 (near chance), whereas Detector-Guided DPO becomes detectable (AUROC(1) ≈ 1.0) by N=5, and the proposed method remains robust up to N=50 (AUROC(1) ≈ 0.88). On style transfer, the method improves CISR stylistic similarity by +0.12 (0.71→0.83) and semantic preservation by +0.09 (0.74→0.83) over TinyStyler, while achieving comparable edit distance to DIPPER (199 vs 227) with higher semantic fidelity (0.85 vs 0.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Detector-Guided DPO (Nicks et al., 2024), the proposed method is substantially harder to detect: Detector-Guided DPO becomes fully detectable (AUROC(1)≈1.0) by N=5 documents, while the proposed method remains near chance (AUROC(1)≈0.55) at N=1 and only reaches 0.88 at N=50. Against DIPPER (Krishna et al., 2023), the proposed method achieves comparable edit distance (199 vs 227) but preserves semantics better (SBERT 0.85 vs 0.84), and DIPPER remains detectable by StyleDetect (AUROC 89) whereas the proposed method evades it at small N. Against OUTFOX (Koike et al., 2024), the proposed method is far superior: OUTFOX actually *increases* detectability by SemDetect (73→91) and leaves StyleDetect at 99, while the proposed method suppresses all nine detectors near chance at N=1. Against TinyStyler (Horvitz et al., 2024b), the proposed method wins on both style transfer (CISR 0.83 vs 0.71) and semantic preservation (0.85 vs 0.78), and avoids the critical failure mode where TinyStyler's style disruption introduces artifacts that *increase* zero-shot detectability (Binoculars rises 79→92).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its core training mechanism, but departs from prior DPO-based evasion work (Nicks et al., 2024) in two key ways: it conditions on specific human author styles rather than optimizing against a generic 'humanness' signal, and it trains its preference detector on the system's own outputs rather than relying on a zero-shot detector. It also extends the style-transfer literature (TinyStyler, ParaGuide) by applying author-conditioned rewriting to machine-generated text rather than human-to-human transfer, and is the first to jointly optimize for author-specific style adherence and detector undetectability. The paper positions itself against the full spectrum of evasion attacks—optimization, paraphrasing (DIPPER), adversarial prompting (OUTFOX), and style transfer (TinyStyler)—showing that all fail to erase stylistic fingerprints while its proposed method succeeds in the single-sample regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that reliable machine-text detection must shift from per-document to multi-document analysis, a finding with direct consequences for academic integrity systems, content moderation platforms, and peer review processes where multiple samples per author are naturally available. The attack's practical limitations (requiring author exemplars, iterative inference at ~5.6s/sample, domain sensitivity) may constrain real-world deployment, but they also raise the cost of large-scale evasion. Open questions include whether the multi-document detection threshold can be pushed further by more sophisticated attacks, how detection performance scales with the diversity of the target author's corpus, and whether watermarking or other generation-time defenses can complement the multi-document paradigm.
