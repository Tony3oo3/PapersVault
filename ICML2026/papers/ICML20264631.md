## Problem

Existing AIGV evaluation benchmarks suffer from three critical limitations: they cover only one evaluation dimension (perception, text-video correspondence, or task-specific accuracy) rather than all three simultaneously; their annotation scale and granularity are insufficient (coarse MOS or limited model coverage); and they evaluate at either the instance level or the model level but not both. No prior work provides a unified, all-in-one metric that jointly assesses perceptual quality, prompt-video alignment, and task-specific correctness across a large, diverse set of T2V models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an AI-generated video (decomposed into N_v frames) and its corresponding text prompt as input. Each frame is split into an L×L grid, mini-patches are sampled from each grid cell and spliced into a single mini-patch map of size H×W×3 for temporal encoding. The model produces four types of output: a perceptual quality score (0–100, derived from z-score-normalized 1–5 Likert ratings by 15 annotators), a text-video correspondence score (same scale), a categorical quality level label (bad/poor/fair/good/excellent per ITU-R BT.500), and task-specific yes/no answers for 20 fine-grained challenges. Evaluation is reported at both instance level (per-video SRCC/PLCC) and model level (ranking 30 T2V models by average score).

## Method

LOVE employs a dual-encoder architecture: a pre-trained InternViT vision encoder extracts spatial features per frame, while a Swin-T temporal encoder processes a spliced mini-patch map constructed via grid-based sampling across all frames to capture temporal dynamics. Both feature streams are projected through 2-layer MLPs into the token space of an InternLM3-9B-instruct LLM backbone, which performs instruction-tuned generation of quality level labels and task-specific QA answers, followed by a quality regression head (L1 loss) on the LLM's last hidden states for precise numerical scores. Training is two-stage: first, instruction tuning with text-defined quality levels (language loss), then regression fine-tuning with LoRA adapters (rank 16) on both the vision encoder and LLM. LOVE-Reward extends this by using LOVE's combined perception and correspondence scores (equal-weighted) as a reward signal within a GRPO reinforcement learning objective to fine-tune T2V generation models, with group-normalized advantages computed over G sampled video trajectories per prompt.

## Results

On AIGVE-60K, LOVE achieves instance-level SRCC of 0.7932 (Perception), 0.7466 (T2V Correspondence), and 0.8115 (Overall-Averaged), with 78.69% QA accuracy, all the best among 28 compared methods. At the model level, LOVE reaches SRCC of 0.9324 (Perception), 0.9778 (Correspondence), and 0.9657 (Overall), confirming strong alignment with human rankings of 30 T2V models. In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation (Table 4), LOVE trained only on AIGVE-60K achieves SRCC 0.747 on its own test set and outperforms VQAScore (trained on GenAI-Bench) on all five external benchmarks, including GenAI-Bench itself (0.682 vs. 0.632).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOVE decisively outperforms all handcrafted metrics (BRISQUE 0.5843, QAC 0.5958) and deep VQA models (FAST-VQA 0.6391, DOVER 0.6414) on instance-level Perception SRCC, with a gap of 0.15–0.70. Among vision-language pre-training models, LOVE surpasses the strongest (HPSv2 at 0.5415) by 0.25 SRCC on Perception and 0.25 on Correspondence, while CLIPScore and VQAScore remain far below (≤0.17). Against zero-shot open-source LMMs, LOVE exceeds the best (InternVL3-72B at 0.5441 Perception, 0.6314 Correspondence) by 0.25 and 0.12 respectively. Among close-source LMMs, LOVE outperforms Grok3 Vision (0.5628/0.6659) and GPT-5.2 (0.5263/0.6639) on both dimensions. The closest competitor is the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL*(7B) at 0.7868/0.7354, which LOVE edges out by 0.006/0.011 SRCC; on QA accuracy, LOVE (78.69%) also slightly beats InternVL3*(9B) (78.36%). At the model level, LOVE achieves the highest SRCC across all three dimensions (0.9324/0.9778/0.9657), confirming superior T2V model ranking fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AIGV evaluation line from VBench, FETV, and GenAIBench by unifying three previously fragmented dimensions—perceptual quality, text-video correspondence, and task-specific accuracy—into a single LMM-based framework (LOVE) trained on the largest fine-grained MOS dataset to date (AIGVE-60K). For the generation-optimization side, it repurposes the GRPO/Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning objective (Liu et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024) by plugging LOVE's multi-dimensional scores as a composite reward, bridging the evaluation-to-generation gap that prior VQA metrics (DOVER, FAST-VQA) and alignment scores (PickScore, VQAScore) could not address. The architecture itself composes standard components (InternViT, Swin-T temporal encoder, InternLM3-9B backbone, LoRA adapters) in a two-stage instruction-tuning-plus-regression pipeline, departing from prior work that either used handcrafted features or single-purpose detection models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIGVE-60K (58,500 videos, 30 T2V models, 2.6M annotations) is likely to become a standard reference benchmark for AIGV evaluation, and the bidirectional design—simultaneously benchmarking T2V generators and V2T interpreters—fills a gap where prior datasets only addressed one direction. The LOVE-Reward loop, while showing only modest per-metric gains (~1 SRCC point), validates the conceptual pipeline of using a learned evaluator as an RL reward for video generation, opening a path toward self-improving T2V systems. Open questions include whether the 20 task dimensions and 1–5 Likert scale capture the full complexity of real-world AIGV failures, whether the reward signal generalizes to longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the modest RL gains scale with more training steps or larger generation models.
