## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of the implicit bias for steepest descent methods (SignSGD, Muon, Normalized-SGD) are restricted to full-batch gradients, yet practical training relies on mini-batch stochastic updates. It remains unclear how batch size, momentum, and variance reduction jointly determine whether stochastic steepest descent recovers the same norm-induced max-margin solution as its full-batch counterpart, or converges to a fundamentally different bias.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a labeled multi-class dataset with linearly separable features, a norm constraint (entry-wise p-norm or Schatten-p norm for p ≥ 1), a mini-batch size b, an optional momentum decay β₁ ∈ [0,1), and a decaying learning rate schedule η_t = c·t^{-a}. The output is a trained weight matrix W whose direction converges to a norm-induced max-margin solution, accompanied by explicit, dimension-free margin-gap convergence rates that quantify the gap γ − min_i,c≠y_i (e_{y_i}−e_c)ᵀW_t x_i / ‖W_t‖ as a function of t, b, β₁, and the learning rate exponent a.

## Method

The paper unifies SignSGD, Normalized-SGD, Muon, and their variants under a single stochastic steepest descent update W_{t+1} = W_t − η_t·φ_{‖·‖}(G_t), where φ selects the unit-norm direction maximally aligned with a stochastic signal G_t under a given norm geometry. The key technical contribution is a margin-gap analysis using a proxy function G(W) = (1/n)Σ(1−S_{y_i}(Wx_i)) that relates gradient magnitude to margin, combined with global ℓ₁-norm bounds on gradient differences to eliminate the dimension dependence present in prior work. Without momentum, the large-batch condition ρ = γ − 4(n/b−1)R > 0 necessarily forces b = n (full batch), and a counterexample at m = 2 proves failure is not merely a loose bound. Momentum stabilizes the descent direction via exponential moving average, enabling small-batch convergence to an approximate max-margin with gap vanishing as b→∞ or β₁→1, at the cost of slower rates scaling with m/(1−β₁). An SVRG-style variance-reduced estimator recovers the exact full-batch max-margin solution for any batch size and momentum setting, while batch-size-1 without momentum on an orthogonal scale-skewed dataset converges to a sample-averaging bias independent of max-margin geometry.

## Results

The paper establishes that without momentum, stochastic steepest descent can only guarantee convergence and successful classification in the full-batch regime (b=n), with a counterexample showing failure already at m=2 mini-batches. With momentum, the method converges to an approximate max-margin solution (gap γ−ρ) even with small batches, requiring only b=Ω(n((1−β₁)R/γ)^(1/3)), and achieves dimension-free convergence rates of O((log t + n)/t^(1/2)) for η_t ∝ t^(−1/2), improving upon Fan et al.'s O((d log t + nd)/t^(1/2)). Variance reduction recovers the exact full-batch max-margin solution γ for any batch size, at the cost of m^(2+a) factors in the rate. Empirically on synthetic 10-class data (n=200, d=5) and MNIST (n=1000, 2-layer NN), the theory is validated: mini-batch N-SGD without momentum fails to converge to the max-margin solution, while β₁=0.99 momentum or SVRG-style variance reduction restores convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Fan et al. (2025), which analyzes full-batch steepest descent with entry-wise and Schatten-p norms, this paper extends the analysis to the stochastic mini-batch setting and removes the explicit dimension d dependence from the convergence rate (e.g., O((log t + n)/t^(1/2)) vs. O((d log t + nd)/t^(1/2)) at a=1/2). Compared to Baek et al. (2025), which studies per-sample Adam and Signum under a fixed deterministic mini-batch cycle, this paper provides a unified framework covering random reshuffling, general norms, and both momentum and variance reduction, with explicit convergence rate guarantees rather than only asymptotic behavior. Relative to Nacson et al. (2019c) and Wang et al. (2022), which characterize ℓ₂ max-margin convergence for mini-batch SGD and SGD with momentum respectively, this paper generalizes to arbitrary entry-wise and Schatten-p norm geometries (covering SignSGD, Muon, Normalized-SGD, etc.) and systematically quantifies the batch-size–momentum tradeoff. The paper also identifies a regime (batch-size-1, no momentum) where the implicit bias is fundamentally different from all full-batch methods, a phenomenon not captured by prior stochastic margin-maximization results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the full-batch steepest descent implicit bias framework of Fan et al. (2025) to the stochastic mini-batch regime, unifying SignSGD, Normalized-SGD, Muon, and their variants under a single norm-induced steepest descent mapping. It builds on the SVRG-style variance reduction estimator of Johnson & Zhang (2013) and the margin-maximization geometry of Gunasekar et al. (2018a), but departs from prior implicit bias analyses (which are almost exclusively full-batch) by systematically characterizing how batch size, momentum, and variance reduction jointly shape the limiting solution. The paper also connects to Baek et al. (2025) on per-sample Adam, but provides a more general treatment covering multiple norms and algorithmic configurations rather than a single optimizer under a fixed mini-batch cycle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical account of when stochastic steepest descent recovers full-batch max-margin behavior, with sharp negative results (no momentum implies full-batch-only guarantees) and positive results (momentum and variance reduction enable small-batch convergence). This clarifies a practical question for large-scale training: why momentum and variance reduction are not merely convergence accelerators but are necessary for the correct implicit bias to emerge under mini-batch sampling. Key limitations include restriction to linear classifiers on separable data, and the batch-size-1 analysis is confined to a specially constructed orthogonal dataset, leaving the general small-batch dynamics without stabilization mechanisms open.
