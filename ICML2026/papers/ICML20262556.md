## Problem

Existing Latent Factorization-of-Tensors (LFT) models assume that the relationship between any two modes of a relation tensor is a bipartite graph, enabling graph isomorphism-based exploration of entity interactions. However, when modes contain entities with explicit internal structure (e.g., ordered time slots or spatial coordinates), the inter-mode relationship forms a stricter 'aligned bipartite network' whose topological constraints are violated by the bipartite-graph assumption, leading to erroneous structural inference and degraded reconstruction accuracy. This paper is the first to formally identify and analyze this misalignment issue.

## Input / Output

The model takes an N-mode high-dimensional incomplete (HDI) relation tensor with a known-entry set K, where each mode is pre-classified as either an 'entity set' (unordered) or an 'entity graph' (with explicit internal topology such as temporal ordering). It produces reconstructed values for all tensor entries via the Asymmetric Tensor Network. A notable design choice is the sample-aware weighted L2 regularization: per-entity regularization weights are proportional to each entity's observation frequency relative to the maximum in its mode, while diagonal constraint matrices receive a uniform average weight to prevent penalty fluctuations.

## Method

The core contribution is the Asymmetric Tensor Network (ATN), which extends the Fully-Connected Tensor Network (FCTN) by inserting a learnable diagonal matrix M_{p,q} between the latent feature tensors of every mode pair (p,q). Crucially, M_{p,q} is activated (i.e., learned) only when both linked modes are entity graphs, creating an SVD-like structure that fixes the interaction topology and eliminates spurious gauge degrees of freedom; when either mode is an entity set, M_{p,q} is set to identity. The theoretical motivation draws from the observation that a diagonal matrix with distinct entries reduces the gauge freedom of a low-rank factorization from GL(f) (f² parameters) to generalized permutation transformations (2f continuous degrees), thereby anchoring row/column identities. The full ALFT objective combines elementwise squared reconstruction error over known entries with the asymmetric weighted L2 regularization, optimized via mini-batch gradient descent with closed-form gradient updates for both latent tensors and diagonal matrices.

## Results

On the primary tensor completion benchmarks (Shanghai, Guangzhou, Aarhus traffic datasets with four train/val/test splits each), ALFT achieves the best RMSE across all six evaluated splits, with improvements of 0.1%–9.5% over the closest competitor FCTN (e.g., RMSE 3.423 vs. 3.613 on Guangzhou D2.1, and 2.731 vs. 2.733 on Aarhus D3.2). On MAE, ALFT is best on 5 of 6 splits, with a marginal loss to DAIN on Shanghai D1.1 (3.984 vs. 3.930). Against traditional tensor decomposition baselines (TUCKER, BCGP, STD, TCA), ALFT improves RMSE by 15%–35%, while the time-series model FreTS is 3–5× worse, confirming its unsuitability for high-dimensional incomplete tensor data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALFT outperforms FCTN (the closest tensor-network baseline) on RMSE across all six splits, with the largest gap on Guangzhou D2.1 (3.423 vs. 3.613, 5.3% improvement) and the smallest on Aarhus D3.2 (2.731 vs. 2.733, 0.1%). Against SVDinsTN, ALFT consistently leads by 4–8% on RMSE, confirming that the asymmetric diagonal constraint placement is more effective than SVDinsTN's uniform SVD-inspired structure. DAIN is the only baseline that beats ALFT on any metric (MAE on Shanghai D1.1: 3.930 vs. 3.984), but ALFT recovers the lead on all other splits. Traditional tensor decomposition methods (TUCKER, BCGP, STD, TCA) are substantially outperformed, with RMSE gaps of 15–35%, while FreTS (a frequency-domain time-series model) is 3–5× worse, highlighting its inability to handle high-dimensional incomplete relational tensors. In the supplementary matrix-oriented comparison (Experiment 1.2), ALFT also beats NCF, GLCPN, AFDGCF, and AEMC-NE by 9.5%–34.8% on RMSE, demonstrating that preserving the aligned bipartite network structure across separate temporal modes is superior to merging them into a single matrix dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FCTN (Fully-connected tensor network) architecture by inserting learnable diagonal matrices between mode-pair latent tensors, drawing an analogy to the diagonal structure in SVD that fixes gauge degrees of freedom. The key conceptual departure from prior LFT models (CP, Tucker, FCTN, SVDinsTN) is the recognition that tensor modes are not uniformly 'entity sets' but can also be 'entity graphs' with explicit internal structure (e.g., time slots, spatial coordinates), in which case the inter-mode relationship is an 'aligned bipartite network' rather than a plain bipartite graph. The proposed ATN activates these diagonal constraints selectively—only when both linked modes are entity graphs—thereby differentiating the latent feature space structure across mode pairs within a single tensor network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'erroneous graph isomorphism' as a systematic limitation of existing LFT models is a useful conceptual framing, but the practical impact is currently limited: the model requires manual classification of each mode as entity set vs. entity graph, the asymmetric structure introduces hyperparameter sensitivity, and the empirical gains over FCTN are modest (0.1%–8% depending on dataset and split). Open questions include whether the mode-type distinction can be learned automatically, whether the diagonal constraint generalizes beyond the SVD-inspired form (e.g., to tensor-ring or other decompositions), and whether the approach scales to higher-order tensors with many entity-graph modes. The narrow domain focus (traffic flow tensors) and small dataset sizes leave the broader applicability of the aligned-bipartite-network constraint underexplored.
