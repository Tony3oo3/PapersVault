## Problem

Existing AI-generated text detection methods either modify token-level distributions (watermarking) or rely on statistical classifiers that struggle to distinguish among multiple LLMs. This paper asks whether LLMs naturally encode model-specific fingerprints in their internal activations that can be detected without external watermarking, and whether such signals can be deliberately amplified to enable reliable multi-model attribution without degrading generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260113/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires white-box access to the target model: the text to be attributed is run as a forward pass through the model to extract intermediate activations at a fixed layer (near the network midpoint), taking roughly 21% of autoregressive generation time. In the prompt-agnostic setting, only the generated text is needed; in the prompt-conditioned setting, the original prompt is prepended. Outputs are per-token attribution scores (cosine similarity or MLP logits) aggregated via majority voting into a text-level decision: a binary AI-vs-human label for self-recognition, or a steering-vector index identifying which of K steered variants produced the text.

## Method

During generation, a random sparse steering vector (99.7% zero dimensions, sampled uniformly from [-1,1]) is added to the residual stream at a fixed intermediate layer at every token step, scaled by a coefficient α=5. This nudges the activation trajectory in a consistent direction, embedding a geometric fingerprint that survives the discretization of token sampling and re-embedding. At detection time, the generated text is fed through the unsteered base model to recover activations at the same layer; a linear LDA probe on length-averaged activations handles binary self-recognition, while a two-hidden-layer MLP (width 32, single-epoch training) classifies among K steering vectors at the token level. The key insight is that random sparse vectors are approximately orthogonal to semantic manifolds by concentration of measure, so the signal coexists with content without interference, and can even be retrieved training-free via cosine similarity between activations and candidate steering vectors. Sparse vectors outperform dense ones on the quality-detectability trade-off, and the signal remains robust to strong paraphrasing (DIPPER-XXL) while perplexity-based baselines collapse in the prompt-agnostic setting.

## Results

On the XL-Sum self-recognition task, the activation-based LDA classifier achieves 99.82–100% AUROC with prompt access and 98.58–99.99% without, across Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.2-1B, and Ministral-3-8B. For multi-model attribution (distinguishing two steered variants), text-level F1 reaches 99.1% for Llama-3.1-8B and 100% for Ministral-3-8B on both ELI5 and Fresh News. After strong paraphrasing via DIPPER-XXL, the trained MLP retains 89.3% text-level F1, while a training-free cosine-similarity approach still achieves 77.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the perplexity baseline (Jelinek et al., 2005), the activation-based classifier is near-parity when the prompt is available (e.g., 99.99% vs. 99.19% AUROC for Llama-3.1-8B) but pulls decisively ahead in the prompt-agnostic setting, where perplexity drops to 32–52% AUROC while the activation signal stays above 98.5%. Against the KGW green-list watermark (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), the proposed steering-based method shows greater robustness to DIPPER-XXL paraphrasing, particularly in freeform generation (Fresh News), where its F1 remains largely stable while the KGW watermark degrades more sharply. The paper also notes that for Ministral-3-8B the perplexity ordering is inverted in the prompt-agnostic setting (human text gets lower PPL than model text), further highlighting the limitations of perplexity as a self-recognition signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two interpretability threads: self-recognition in LLMs (Ackerman & Panickssery, 2025; Bowman et al., 2024) and activation steering (Panickssery et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023). It departs from both the token-level watermarking literature (KGW, SemStamp) and dense activation interventions (Li et al., 2023) by showing that a random, semantically meaningless sparse vector injected into the residual stream can serve as a recoverable fingerprint, building on the sparsity principles from Ardoin et al. (2025). The key conceptual shift is treating the model's own internal representation space as both the encoding medium and the detection substrate, rather than applying an external signal at the output token level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers model providers a lightweight, quality-preserving mechanism for multi-instance attribution that avoids the overhead and quality trade-offs of traditional watermarking, though it requires white-box access and does not transfer across architectures. Open questions include robustness to recursive translation and adversarial attacks, the security implications of steering-vector secrecy, whether multi-bit superposition of sparse vectors remains recoverable, and the semantic side-effects of random steering that remain uncharacterized. The cross-architecture boundary (detection collapses to chance across model families) mirrors subliminal learning and suggests a deeper architectural constraint on how activation-space signals propagate.
