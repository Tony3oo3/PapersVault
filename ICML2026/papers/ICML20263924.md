## Problem

Amortized symbolic regression learns to predict expressions from data but is bottlenecked by the computational cost of simplifying training expressions to normalized forms. General-purpose CAS systems like SymPy are too slow for real-time use in training loops, forcing compromises on data scale, diversity, or simplification quality. This paper identifies this simplification bottleneck as the key obstacle preventing amortized SR from scaling to realistic scientific complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263924/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-sized dataset of M input-output pairs (x_m ∈ R^D, y_m ∈ R) as input, encoded via a Set Transformer using 32-bit IEEE-754 multi-hot representations of each scalar value. It produces a symbolic expression in prefix-notation token sequence with free constant placeholders (◇), whose constants are then numerically optimized via Levenberg-Marquardt. The final expression is selected by a parsimony-regularized scoring function that trades off fit quality (log-FVU) against expression length with a per-symbol penalty γ.

## Method

The core contribution is SimpliPy, a rule-based simplification engine that amortizes symbolic simplification by pre-computing a size-decreasing term rewriting system offline (via stratified enumeration and numeric equivalence testing up to L_max=7 symbols), reducing runtime to O(1) hash lookups for explicit rules and bounded pattern matching for metavariable rules, achieving ~100× speedup over SymPy at comparable quality. This enables FLASH-ANSR, a Pre-RMSNorm encoder-decoder Transformer with a Set Transformer encoder (masked RMSSetNorm, RoPE, FlashAttention) that trains on 512M on-the-fly generated and simplified data-expression pairs with strict decontamination. At inference, the model uses softmax sampling rather than beam search to draw diverse candidate skeletons, which are simplified, deduplicated, and constant-optimized in parallel across CPU cores. A parsimony regularization selects the final expression, and the framework exhibits a 'parsimony inversion' where increasing inference budget yields more concise rather than more complex expressions, contrasting with GP methods that grow complexity over time.

## Results

On the Fast-SRB benchmark (115 expressions), FLASH-ANSR (120M) reaches a peak validation Numeric Recovery Rate (vNRR) of ~58%, surpassing PySR's 50.0% at higher inference budgets, while amortized baselines NeSymReS (~10%) and E2E (<2.5%) fail to generalize. SIMPLIPY achieves a ~100× speedup over SymPy (median ~100 ms → millisecond regime) at comparable simplification quality, with L_max≥5 even exceeding SymPy's conciseness. A key finding is the 'parsimony inversion': as inference budget grows, FLASH-ANSR's expression length ratio decreases (1.40→1.27 for 120M) while PySR's increases (0.94→1.85), indicating FLASH-ANSR converges to simpler canonical forms rather than appending corrective terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLASH-ANSR (120M) clearly dominates both amortized baselines: NeSymReS saturates at ~10% vNRR and E2E stays below 2.5% across all compute budgets, confirming that static corpora and unsimplified training are insufficient for realistic scientific domains. Against the GP baseline PySR, FLASH-ANSR 120M achieves parity at ~10 s and partially surpasses it at higher budgets (58% vs. 50% peak vNRR), while exhibiting the opposite complexity trend (decreasing vs. increasing length ratio). On sparse data (M>64 points, fixed ~10 s budget), FLASH-ANSR 120M outperforms PySR, but on noisy data (η≥10⁻²) PySR retains higher recovery rates due to FLASH-ANSR's noiseless training distribution. Against SymPy, SIMPLIPY delivers a ~100× wall-clock speedup with comparable or better simplification ratios, and SymPy paradoxically increases the length of 38–52% of expressions while timing out on 9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the amortized symbolic regression line initiated by Lample & Charton (2020) and scaled by NeSymReS (Biggio et al., 2021), adopting their encoder-decoder Transformer and Set Transformer encoder while replacing Post-Norm with Pre-RMSNorm, sinusoidal encodings with RoPE, and 16-bit with 32-bit IEEE-754 input encoding. The central departure is identifying the CAS simplification bottleneck (SymPy) as the key obstacle to scaling, and resolving it with SimpliPy, a purpose-built rule-based term rewriting system that amortizes rule discovery offline and reduces runtime to hash-indexed pattern matching. This enables on-the-fly generation of 512M simplified training pairs rather than the static 100M corpora or unsimplified streams used by prior methods, and introduces rigorous symbolic/numeric decontamination absent from most prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the simplification bottleneck, the paper enables amortized SR to scale to higher-dimensional inputs, broader operator sets, and much larger training distributions, closing the gap with state-of-the-art genetic programming (PySR) on the inference-time vs. recovery-rate Pareto frontier. The 'parsimony inversion'—where increasing the sampling budget yields simpler, more canonical expressions (opposite to PySR's trend toward complexity)—suggests amortized priors may be better suited for structural discovery than evolutionary search. Key limitations include sensitivity to noisy data (a distributional shift from noiseless training) and dependence on the fixed operator vocabulary; future work will incorporate noise into training and explore alternative encoding/decoding paradigms.
