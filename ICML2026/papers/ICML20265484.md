## Problem

Classical universality theory applies to models on fixed-size compact domains, but any-dimensional architectures (DeepSets, GNNs, PointNet) produce sequences of functions over growing input spaces, making it unclear in what sense they can be universal. The paper formalizes this question by embedding all finite-size inputs into a single infinite-dimensional limit space and shows that several widely used architectures are either discontinuous in the natural topology or fail to be universal across dimensions.

## Input / Output

Inputs are objects of arbitrary finite size: sets represented as n×k matrices (n any positive integer), graphs as n×n symmetric adjacency matrices, and point clouds as n points in R^k. Outputs are scalar real values. The key structural constraint is invariance: set and graph models must be permutation-invariant, while point cloud models must be invariant under both permutations and orthogonal rotations (O(k)). Universality is established on orbit spaces equipped with task-specific metrics—Wasserstein-p distance for sets/measures, cut metric δ_□ for graphs, and a symmetrized L²-based metric for point clouds—rather than on the raw input spaces.

## Method

The paper develops a three-step recipe: (1) characterize compact sets in the orbit space (quotient of the infinite-dimensional limit space by the symmetry group), (2) construct model classes that are continuous with respect to the symmetrized metric, and (3) prove universality via Stone-Weierstrass by showing the class forms a subalgebra that separates points. Concretely, for sets the authors introduce DeepSets_∞ with norm-weighted summation (‖X_i‖^p · ρ(X_i)) to ensure convergence, and a normalized variant σ(∫ρ dμ) for the Wasserstein setting; for graphs they propose a product-based architecture h_m^{a,b}(W) = ∫∏(a_ij·W(x_i,x_j)+b_ij) dx that expands to linear combinations of homomorphism densities of simple graphs only (avoiding discontinuous non-simple motifs); for point clouds they compose the Gram map X↦W_X with Invariant Graphon Networks. A central insight is that in infinite dimensions the norm choice dictates the topology and hence which activations and primitives are admissible, unlike finite dimensions where all norms are equivalent.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The main results are four universality theorems: (1) Theorem 4.3 shows a norm-weighted DeepSets variant is dense in C(K/G_∞) for any compact K ⊆ ℓ_p(ℝ^k) with a continuous non-polynomial activation; (2) Theorem 4.6 shows normalized DeepSets (integral form) is dense in C(Q) for any compact Q ⊆ P_p(ℝ^k) with a Lipschitz, asymptotically polynomial activation; (3) Theorem 4.10 shows a homomorphism-density-based graph model class F_W is dense in C(W_0, δ_□); and (4) Theorem 4.13 shows a Gram-matrix + Invariant Graphon Network architecture is dense in C(K_R/G_∞) for point clouds. A key negative result is that vanilla DeepSets and standard pointwise-nonlinear GNNs are discontinuous in the natural topologies, hence cannot be universal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons but establishes theoretical failures of existing architectures. Vanilla DeepSets (Zaheer et al., 2017) is shown to be discontinuous on ℓ_p(ℝ^k) because the infinite sum may diverge; the fix is multiplying each term by ‖X_i:‖^p (Theorem 4.2). Standard GNNs with pointwise nonlinearities (Maron et al., 2019b; Keriven & Peyré, 2019) are discontinuous in the cut metric δ_□ since pointwise maps W↦φ(W) are continuous in ‖·‖_□ only when φ is linear (Herbst & Jegelka, 2025); the proposed F_W class avoids this by using only simple-motif homomorphism densities. For point clouds, the paper builds on Villar et al. (2021) and Herbst & Jegelka (2025) but proves universality on the orbit space K_R/G_∞, going beyond prior fixed-dimension results. The graph model F_W uses only m(m−1) parameters to represent all O(2^{m choose 2}/m!) homomorphism densities of simple m-node graphs, a significant parameter efficiency gain over direct coefficient learning (Maehara & NT, 2019; Nguyen & Maehara, 2020).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the any-dimensional learning framework of Levin et al. (2025), which formalizes consistent sequences, limit spaces, and transferability, and applies it to the question of universality across all input dimensions simultaneously. For sets, it extends the DeepSets architecture (Zaheer et al., 2017) and the normalized DeepSets universality result of Bueno & Hylton (2021) to broader compact sets in the Wasserstein space; for graphs, it leverages the homomorphism-density density theorem from Lovász (2012) and the Invariant Graphon Networks of Herbst & Jegelka (2025); for point clouds, it composes the Gram map with IGNs. The key departure from prior work is the systematic identification of any-dimensional models with a single function on an infinite-dimensional orbit space, which reveals that standard architectures are often discontinuous in the natural topology—a failure mode invisible in fixed-dimensional analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general recipe (via Stone-Weierstrass on orbit spaces) for certifying any-dimensional universality, and demonstrates that widely used architectures (DeepSets, message-passing GNNs, PointNet-style models) fail basic continuity requirements in the appropriate infinite-dimensional topology, necessitating principled modifications. Key open questions include whether universality is achievable with fixed width or depth in the graph model (where tensor order grows with motif size), extending the framework to equivariant (non-scalar) outputs, and applying it to sparse graph limits. The finding that pointwise nonlinearities are discontinuous in the cut metric (only linear maps are continuous) has direct implications for the design of graph architectures intended to generalize across sizes.
