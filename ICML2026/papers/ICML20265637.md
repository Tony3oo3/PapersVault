## Problem

Scene text tracking (STT) in videos is an underexplored task with no dedicated methods or benchmarks. Generic visual object trackers fail on text due to severe perspective-induced distortions, high visual ambiguity among similar character structures, and sensitivity to fine-grained structural details, while Video Text Spotting (VTS) pipelines treat tracking as a by-product of per-frame detection, producing fragmented trajectories when instances are occluded or blurred.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265637/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a template image patch (the target text region from the first frame) and a search region patch (a larger crop from a subsequent frame) as input, both processed at 256×256 or 384×384 resolution. It produces a bounding box for the target text location and a spatial response score map indicating confidence at each position. During inference, the training-free Adaptive Inference Engine can trigger multi-scale re-evaluation (scale factors 0.95/1.05) when peak confidence drops below 0.98, and applies Kalman-based temporal smoothing to the final box coordinates.

## Method

SymTrack is a detection-free, dual-branch ViT-based tracker. The first branch (Predictive Token Rectification) distills a semantic query from template tokens via mean pooling, projects it to a channel-wise modulation field, and applies a depth-wise convolution gating mask to the search feature map, resolving the information bottleneck between the deep backbone and shallow prediction head. The second branch (Cross-Expert Calibration) uses a frozen 48-layer text expert (TokenFD/InternVL) to extract text-specific features, applies multi-head cross-attention between template and search text tokens, and generates a spatial calibration mask that suppresses visually similar distractors. The two masks are fused via element-wise multiplication before a lightweight prediction head. Training is two-stage: pre-training the visual backbone (ViT-B, MAE-initialized) and PTR on generic tracking datasets (LaSOT, GOT-10K, COCO, TrackingNet), then fine-tuning all trainable components on three curated STT benchmarks (ArTVideo_SOT, DSText_SOT, BOVText_SOT) while keeping the text expert frozen.

## Results

SymTrack achieves state-of-the-art AUC of 77.74% on ArTVideo_SOT, 70.66% on DSText_SOT, and 77.06% on BOVText_SOT, outperforming the strongest vision-only tracker ODTrack by +7.93%, +7.95%, and +12.32% AUC respectively, and the best vision-language tracker DUTrack384 by +5.65%, +7.03%, and +11.98% AUC. On the PNorm metric, SymTrack reaches 91.29%, 83.61%, and 90.05% across the three benchmarks. VTS-based methods (TransDETR, TransVTSpotter) suffer near-total failure under SOT evaluation, scoring only 9.18% and 5.08% AUC on ArTVideo_SOT and DSText_SOT, confirming the advantage of a detection-free tracking paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymTrack decisively outperforms all 12 baselines across all three benchmarks. Among vision-only trackers, the strongest competitor ODTrack (AAAI'24) is surpassed by +7.93% AUC on ArTVideo_SOT, +7.95% on DSText_SOT, and +12.32% on BOVText_SOT; even after fine-tuning ODTrack on the text datasets (Table 4), SymTrack retains a +9.83% AUC margin on BOVText_SOT, confirming an architectural rather than data-domain advantage. The best vision-language tracker DUTrack384 (CVPR'25) trails by +5.65%, +7.03%, and +11.98% AUC respectively. Earlier Siamese and Transformer trackers (SiamRPN++, STARK, OSTrack, AiATrack, SeqTrack, ARTrack, GRM, ROMTrack) all fall 7–21% AUC behind SymTrack. VTS methods TransDETR and TransVTSpotter collapse to ~9% and ~5% AUC under SOT evaluation due to per-frame detection failures breaking trajectory continuity; even under an ID-agnostic protocol (Table 11) they reach only 41.10% and 39.89% AUC on ArTVideo_SOT, still far below SymTrack's 77.74%. The training-free AIE module also generalizes as a plug-and-play boost, adding +1.85% AUC to OSTrack256 and +1.22% to ODTrack without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task (Scene Text Tracking) at the intersection of two existing paradigms: one-stream ViT-based visual object trackers (OSTrack, ODTrack) and Video Text Spotting (VTS) pipelines (TransDETR, TransVTSpotter). It departs from generic trackers by adding a frozen text-specific expert (TokenFD/InternVL) in a parallel branch and three purpose-built modules (PTR, CEC, AIE), and departs from VTS by eliminating per-frame detection entirely in favor of continuous, instance-oriented tracking. The AIE is explicitly designed as a training-free, plug-and-play inference module that can be attached to other trackers without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing STT and providing three converted benchmarks, this work gives the community a dedicated evaluation setting for continuous text tracking that was previously only a by-product of VTS. The finding that detection-free tracking outperforms end-to-end VTS by 30–65% AUC (even under ID-agnostic evaluation) challenges the assumption that VTS is the natural framework for video text manipulation. Open questions include whether the high computational cost (395M params, 1315G MACs from the 48-layer text expert) can be reduced, whether the benchmarks derived from VTS annotations fully capture real-world STT difficulty, and whether the approach extends to multi-instance or open-vocabulary text tracking.
