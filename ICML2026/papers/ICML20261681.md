## Problem

Existing post-training MoE pruning methods rely on scalar statistics (activation frequency, reconstruction loss, output similarity) that ignore the representational geometry of expert feature spaces, leading to sub-optimal inter-layer budget allocation and retention of linearly redundant experts. This geometric misalignment causes catastrophic functional collapse on complex reasoning tasks, particularly in fine-grained architectures like DeepSeek-V2-Lite.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MoE model, a small calibration set (32 C4 sequences of 2048 tokens, ~65K tokens total), and a global expert budget K_total as input, requiring only a single forward pass to collect activations. It outputs per-layer pruned expert sets S^(l) that form a high-energy orthogonal basis, yielding a structurally compressed MoE model with reduced static VRAM footprint (e.g., ~50% memory reduction at 50% retention) and 1.3–1.35× inference speedup, without any retraining or weight modification.

## Method

RaGEP is a retraining-free, two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. Stage 1 (Rank-aware Budget Allocation) computes each layer's effective rank via a Frobenius-norm approximation of the covariance matrix (O(d²) instead of O(d³) eigendecomposition) and allocates expert budgets proportionally to (r_l)^α, directing more capacity to information-dense high-rank layers. Stage 2 (Spectral-Salience Pruning) constructs a PCA subspace (top-r=32 singular vectors) for each expert, quantifies Spectral Novelty as 1 − ρ(i)/r where ρ(i) is the squared Frobenius norm of the expert's projection onto the complementary subspace (equivalent to average squared sine of principal angles on the Grassmann manifold), and quantifies Activation Salience as the total output energy ‖A_i‖²_F. The final selection score harmonizes both via rank-based normalization with a weighted sum (β=0.8), filtering out both linearly redundant high-activation experts and unique-direction low-energy noise experts. Theoretical analysis connects the metric to volume maximization (DPP) and derives a reconstruction error upper bound proportional to the product of salience and novelty of pruned experts.

## Results

At a 50% expert pruning ratio, RaGEP achieves the best average zero-shot accuracy across all three MoE architectures: 63.12 on Qwen3-30B-A3B (vs. 67.91 unpruned), 63.83 on Mixtral 8x7B (vs. 69.95 unpruned), and 47.33 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite (vs. 64.28 unpruned). The most striking gain is on MMLU for Qwen3-30B-A3B, where RaGEP scores 58.45, outperforming the strongest baseline EEP (55.67) by 2.78 points. At 75% retention, degradation is minimal: Qwen3-30B-A3B retains 67.72 (vs. 67.91 original) and Mixtral 8x7B retains 67.78 (vs. 69.95 original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaGEP consistently outperforms all three baselines (EEP, MoP, HC-SMoE) on average accuracy across all three model architectures at 50% pruning. On Qwen3-30B-A3B, RaGEP (63.12) beats MoP (62.18) by 0.94 points and EEP (61.60) by 1.52 points, with the largest margin on MMLU (58.45 vs. 55.67 for EEP, a 2.78-point gap). On Mixtral 8x7B, RaGEP (63.83) leads MoP (62.90) and EEP (62.86) by roughly 0.9–1.0 points, while HC-SMoE trails significantly at 61.16. On the fine-grained DeepSeek-V2-Lite, where baselines suffer severe collapse (EEP drops to 24.55 on MMLU), RaGEP (47.33) still outperforms MoP (45.96) and EEP (45.70) by 1.4 and 1.6 points respectively, and HC-SMoE (42.29) by a substantial 5.04 points. The advantage is most pronounced on knowledge-intensive tasks (MMLU, OBQA) and least on already-saturated benchmarks (BoolQ, RTE), suggesting the geometric selection criterion primarily preserves experts critical for complex semantic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant scalar-statistics paradigm in MoE expert pruning (reconstruction loss in EEP, clustering in MoP, hierarchical merging in HC-SMoE) by reframing expert redundancy as a geometric subspace problem. It repurposes effective rank from signal processing (Roy & Vetterli, 2007) for adaptive inter-layer budget allocation, and draws on Grassmannian geometry (principal angles, chordal distance) to define a spectral novelty metric for intra-layer expert selection. The observation of representation collapse in MoEs (Chi et al., 2022) and layer-wise anisotropy in dense LLMs (Ethayarajh, 2019) motivates the geometric framing, but no prior pruning method had explicitly leveraged subspace orthogonality or effective rank for expert selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RaGEP provides a principled, retraining-free compression path for deploying large MoE models on memory-constrained hardware, with demonstrated compatibility with quantization (GPTQ) that extends the viable compression range beyond pure numerical precision. The rank-preservation analysis (Figure 6) offers a mechanistic explanation for why scalar-based methods fail: they retain collinear experts and collapse feature-space dimensionality. Open questions include whether the geometric approach generalizes to even larger MoE models (e.g., 100B+), whether it transfers to other sparse architectures (e.g., conditional computation in vision transformers), and whether the PCA subspace dimension r=32 remains optimal as hidden dimensions grow. Evaluation is limited to zero-shot benchmarks on three models, leaving fine-tuning performance and long-context reasoning untested.
