## Problem

Existing multi-reward alignment methods for text-to-image generation rely on a unified global optimization target (a constant upper bound C in a weighted-sum loss), but different prompts inherently admit different maximum achievable rewards. The paper proves that this mismatch under heterogeneous prompt-wise reward bounds inevitably induces reward hacking for a subset of samples, and that weak reward models further exacerbate the problem by providing exploitable shortcut signals. Additionally, no principled metrics exist to detect multi-reward synergy or hidden collapse that mean-based scores mask.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts (5,000 non-repetitive prompts from the Pick-High dataset) and produces 512×512 RGB images via Stable Diffusion 3.5-Turbo with LoRA fine-tuning. Gradients are backpropagated through four reward models (CLIP, ICT, HPS, HP) using the DRaFT-K strategy, which restricts gradient updates to the final k=2 denoising steps. A precomputed Pareto frontier of M=50 candidate images per prompt serves as the optimization target, and a GPT-4o VLM agent is invoked every 100 steps to inspect generated images for early collapse signatures.

## Method

PG-OT replaces the global reward bound with a prompt-specific Pareto frontier constructed via dominance-matrix non-dominated sorting over precomputed candidate samples. During training, dominated reward vectors form a source distribution that is mapped toward the Pareto frontier (target distribution) via entropy-regularized optimal transport solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm, with the OT loss backpropagated through the reward models. A VLM-based decision agent (GPT-4o with chain-of-thought reasoning) periodically detects reward-model-specific collapse patterns and removes unstable weak rewards, reverting to a stable checkpoint. Once only strong rewards remain, an online strategy dynamically aggregates reward vectors across parallel generation processes and uses pairwise Pareto domination to continuously expand the frontier. The paper also introduces Joint Domination Rate (JDR) and Joint Collapse Rate (JCR) as evaluation metrics that require consistent improvement or degradation across all K rewards simultaneously, making them robust to single-reward inflation that masks reward hacking.

## Results

On the Parti-Prompts dataset, PG-OT achieves a Joint Domination Rate (JDR₂) of 47.98% on the two strong rewards (ICT, HP), an 11% absolute improvement over the best baseline (Weighted-Sum at 29.84%), and a JDR₄ of 17.10% across all four rewards, a 3.4% gain over the strongest competitor. The Joint Collapse Rate (JCR₄) drops to 2.39%, a 0.2% reduction over the best baseline, indicating effective suppression of reward hacking. In human evaluation on DiffusionDB, PG-OT secures a win rate of 76.34% against the SD3.5-Turbo base model and 79.57% against the strongest multi-reward baseline (Multi-2:3:2:3), confirming near-80% perceived quality superiority.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-OT substantially outperforms all baselines on the joint metrics that capture multi-reward synergy. Against the strongest heterogeneous-bounds baseline (Weighted-Sum), PG-OT gains 18.14 points on JDR₂ (47.98% vs. 29.84%) and 3.44 points on JDR₄ (17.10% vs. 13.66%), while reducing JCR₄ from 3.49% to 2.39%. Single-reward baselines achieve high individual win rates on their target reward (e.g., +HP reaches 90.26% on HP) but suffer severe degradation on other rewards and joint metrics (JDR₂ drops to 20–36%, JCR₄ rises to 3–10%), confirming the reward-hacking problem. Multi-reward weighted optimization and Reward Soup baselines improve joint metrics over single-reward but remain 10–20 points below PG-OT on JDR₂, and their performance is highly sensitive to weight selection. The Separate-Constraints baseline, which uses prompt-specific bounds without optimal transport, shows the worst JCR₄ (6.68%), demonstrating that simply matching per-reward bounds without geometric transport guidance is insufficient. In human evaluation, PG-OT wins 76–80% of pairwise comparisons against all baselines, with the largest margin (79.57%) against the best multi-reward weighted baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the differentiable reward optimization framework of DRaFT-K (Clark et al., 2024) for fine-tuning diffusion models, and extends the multi-reward alignment line of work (ReNO, Parrot, CaPO, BalancedPO) that has so far relied on weighted summation or paired-sample selection. Its key departure is a theoretical argument that the shared flaw across single- and multi-reward methods is the use of a unified global reward target under heterogeneous prompt-specific bounds, which provably induces reward hacking. The method repurposes optimal transport (Sinkhorn) not as a generative tool but as a geometric mapping from dominated reward vectors toward prompt-specific Pareto frontiers, and introduces a VLM-based agent for adaptive reward-model pruning—neither of which has a direct precedent in the T2I reward-optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent failure mode (reward hacking) in reward-based T2I alignment, and the JDR/JCR metrics fill a genuine gap in how multi-reward optimization is evaluated beyond misleading population averages. The VLM-based agent for detecting and removing collapsed weak rewards is a practical contribution that scales linearly with the number of rewards, unlike combinatorial weight-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SD3.5-Turbo and the specific four-reward setup, whether the offline Pareto frontier precomputation remains tight as the model improves substantially during training, and whether the VLM agent's collapse-signature knowledge base transfers to new reward models without re-annotation.
