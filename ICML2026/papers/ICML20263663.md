## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on raw invariants extracted from symbolic solvers like UAutomizer does not reliably improve invariant generation and can even degrade model performance, because solver outputs are structurally obfuscated, reflect only one valid choice among many, and carry solver-specific artifacts. The paper identifies data quality, rather than model scale, as the key bottleneck for neural invariant synthesizers and asks how to curate training invariants that are correct, useful, and compact enough to teach small models effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263663/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a C program instrumented with INVARIANT_MARKER_k() calls at each loop body entry, plus a designated target loop marker, and must output a single JSON object containing the marker name and a C boolean expression for the invariant. The invariant must be inductive (hold at loop entry and be preserved by each iteration) and strong enough to help prove the target assertion; outputs using side-effect operators (++, +=, =) or non-ASCII characters are rejected as syntactically invalid. The invariant is then checked by a formal verifier in two parallel queries—correctness (inductiveness) and sufficiency (implying the postcondition)—whose combined wall-clock time determines whether the invariant actually accelerates verification relative to a direct solver baseline.

## Method

WONDA is a two-stage data curation pipeline that transforms raw UAutomizer invariants (V0) into high-quality training samples (V2). Stage 1 applies AST-based normalization (V0→V1) using bottom-up rewrite rules to eliminate tautologies, contradictions, redundant parentheses, and integral type casts while preserving semantic equivalence. Stage 2 invokes an LLM (Kimi K2) as a non-semantics-preserving simplification function that generates N=4 compact candidates per verbose invariant, guided by transformation goals including range generalization, constraint factoring, closed-form arithmetic discovery, and redundancy removal. Each candidate is formally verified and assigned a quality grade G∈{0,1,2,3} based on correctness, sufficiency, and whether it yields a verification speedup over the baseline; only G≥2 candidates are retained as training pairs. This yields 7,284 curated samples from 4,000 raw invariants, which are used to fully fine-tune (or LoRA-adapt) small models (0.6B–14B) across Qwen3, Llama-3.1, and Mistral families, enabling a 4B model to match a 20× larger off-the-shelf model and a 14B model to match GPT-5.2 on end-to-end verification time without test-time compute overhead.

## Results

On the InvBench hard split (n=123), WONDA-curated fine-tuned models achieve substantial gains over their base counterparts: Qwen3-4B-V2 nearly doubles correctness (22.8%→44.4%) and speedup rate (11.1%→24.7%), while Llama-3.1-8B-V2 triples correctness (14.9%→45.5%). The best VBP among fine-tuned models is 162.1 s (Qwen3-14B-V2), compared to the 193 s verifier-only baseline. On end-to-end verification time (VBPE2E), Qwen3-14B-V2 achieves 162.9 s, matching GPT-5.2 (163.4 s), and Qwen3-4B-V2 achieves 165.7 s, matching GPT-OSS-120B (167.6 s), all without test-time compute overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-5.2 (the strongest baseline at 72.4% correctness and 163.4 s VBPE2E), Qwen3-14B-V2 matches on VBPE2E (162.9 s vs. 163.4 s) but trails on correctness (43.4% vs. 72.4%) and speedup rate (18.4% vs. 37.1%). Against GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-4B-V2 matches on VBPE2E (165.7 s vs. 167.6 s) while being ~30× smaller, and Qwen3-8B-V2 is slightly better (166.7 s vs. 167.6 s). Against the open-weight Qwen3-80B, all fine-tuned Qwen3 models at 4B parameters and above outperform it on both correctness (44.4% vs. 38.8% for 4B-V2) and VBPE2E (165.7 s vs. 169.7 s), with the 4B model being ~20× smaller. The most striking result is that a 4B fine-tuned model surpasses a 20× larger off-the-shelf model on both axes, demonstrating that data quality (WONDA curation) compensates for model scale in this domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fine-tuning-for-invariant-synthesis line initiated by Wei et al. (2025), who showed that training on raw UAutomizer outputs yields only marginal gains on hard benchmarks. WONDA departs from that work by arguing the bottleneck is data quality rather than model scale, and introduces a two-stage curation pipeline (AST normalization followed by LLM-driven semantic rewriting with verifier-graded filtering) that transforms obfuscated solver invariants into compact, learnable training signals. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the generate-and-verify LLM paradigm (Loopy, Lemur, ACInv) and classical invariant synthesis (UAutomizer, Craig interpolation), but its core contribution is a data-engineering methodology rather than a new verification algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 4B fine-tuned model matches a 20× larger off-the-shelf model on end-to-end verification time, and that raw solver outputs actively degrade fine-tuning performance, provides a practical recipe for integrating small language models into production verifiers without test-time compute overhead. Key limitations include reliance on UAutomizer as the sole invariant source (risking inherited solver biases), absence of chain-of-thought or verifier-feedback signals during training, and the fact that timeout resolution remains largely unsolved. Open directions the authors identify—extending to multiple backend verifiers, RL driven by verification outcomes, and embedding WONDA-trained models in iterative counterexample-guided loops—suggest the pipeline is a foundation rather than a final solution.
