## Problem

Standard scalar reward models fail to capture the multifaceted nature of response quality in non-verifiable domains like creative writing and open-ended instruction following. While rubric-based reward models offer interpretable, structured evaluation, existing approaches either rely on static human-authored rubrics, use frozen prompting-based pipelines, or train the rubric generator and judge as decoupled modules—preventing them from co-evolving to produce in-domain, preference-aligned evaluation signals.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt and two candidate responses, the rubric generator (greedy decoding, max 1024 tokens) produces a structured set of k criteria split into hard rules (binary pass/fail constraints) and principles (qualitative guidelines). The judge (sampling at temperature 1.0, max 4096 tokens) then generates a per-criterion explanation chain, an overall justification, and a binary preference label. A format reward enforces that the judge addresses every rubric criterion before issuing a final decision, and the system supports ensemble voting across multiple independent judge rollouts.

## Method

RUBRIC-ARM formulates rubric generation as a latent action in a shared preference-correctness objective and jointly optimizes the rubric generator and judge via alternating GRPO updates. Training proceeds in two stages: an SFT warmup on synthetic rubric-judge trajectories, followed by alternating RL where the judge is first optimized with cached rubrics from a frozen generator, then the generator is optimized against a frozen judge using single-rollout greedy judging. A gradient-variance analysis proves that this judge-first ordering is critical: fixing the rubric eliminates cross-rubric inconsistency variance (the C₁ exploration term), yielding a strictly lower-variance gradient estimator than updating the generator first. Both components are initialized from Qwen-3-8B, and the trained rubric-judge pair serves as a pairwise reward signal for downstream DPO/IterDPO or online GRPO policy optimization.

## Results

RUBRIC-ARM achieves the best average score (74.8) among all white-box reward models across 9 benchmarks, improving over the SFT-only RUBRIC-RM baseline (70.1) by +4.7 points and reaching 76.2 with voting@5. On the strictest instruction-following metric (RewardBench2-Precise IF), RUBRIC-ARM scores 41.9 vs. 34.4 for RUBRIC-RM and 36.9 for RM-R1-32B. For downstream policy training, IterDPO with RUBRIC-ARM yields the best IFEval average (80.8), InfoBench (85.0), Arena-Hard/AlpacaEval average (53.4), and WildBench macro score (55.7), while online GRPO with RUBRIC-ARM reaches 55.4 average vs. 52.3 for GRPO with RM-R1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUBRIC-ARM outperforms the closest same-scale rubric-based baseline RUBRIC-RM (Liu et al., 2025a) by +4.7 points on average (74.8 vs. 70.1), with the largest gap on RewardBench2-Precise IF (+7.5) and RM-Bench (+3.5), confirming that RL-based joint optimization of rubric and judge yields more discriminative evaluation than SFT-only training. Against reasoning-based reward models, RUBRIC-ARM surpasses RM-R1-7B (61.7) and JudgeLRM-7B (53.8) by wide margins, and even exceeds the larger RM-R1-32B (74.0) and RRM-32B (73.4) despite using only 8B parameters. For downstream policy training, IterDPO with RUBRIC-ARM beats IterDPO with RUBRIC-RM on IFEval (80.8 vs. 79.4), InfoBench (85.0 vs. 83.3), Arena-Hard/AlpacaEval (53.4 vs. 51.3), and WildBench (55.7 vs. 54.0), and outperforms the online RL baseline OnlineRubrics (Rezaei et al., 2025) on AlpacaEval (44.0 vs. 30.4). In the online GRPO setting, RUBRIC-ARM as reward signal achieves 55.4 average vs. 52.3 for RM-R1, a +3.1 gain. The only method that surpasses RUBRIC-ARM on the reward-modeling average is the black-box Gemini-2.5-Flash (78.5), which is included for reference only and is not a trainable white-box model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rubric-based reward modeling line initiated by OpenRubrics (Liu et al., 2025a), which introduced the rubric-generator + judge architecture but trained the two components independently via SFT. The key departure is treating rubric generation as a latent action and jointly optimizing both components through alternating GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), rather than the decoupled pipelines used by prior rubric-based methods (RUBRIC-RM, OnlineRubrics). The ReMax-style baseline construction (Li et al., 2024) is repurposed for the online RL policy training setting. The theoretical variance analysis providing a principled justification for the judge-first-then-generator alternating schedule is a new contribution not present in prior multi-component RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in non-verifiable domains where scalar reward models fail to capture multifaceted response quality, and shows that jointly learned rubrics yield more discriminative and position-bias-resistant evaluation signals than static or independently trained alternatives. The alternating RL paradigm for co-optimizing generator and judge could generalize to other multi-component reward systems beyond rubric-based evaluation. Limitations include evaluation only at 8B scale, reliance on a single backbone (Qwen-3-8B), and theoretical assumptions (Exploration-Gradient Sufficiency) that are argued to be mild but not empirically verified across architectures. Open questions include whether the alternating schedule's benefits persist at larger scales and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or agentic reward settings.
