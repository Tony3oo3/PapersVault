## Problem

Standard LLM safety alignment follows a reactive, disjoint loop: attackers exploit a static model, then defenders patch exposed vulnerabilities via retraining. This sequential setup causes attackers to overfit obsolete exploits while defenders perpetually lag behind emerging threats, leaving vulnerability coverage ad-hoc and incomplete. The paper asks whether attackers and defenders can instead co-evolve in a fully adaptive, mutually reinforcing manner within a single training procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262846/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes seed prompts (50% harmful, 50% benign) sampled from WildJailBreak, which the attacker rewrites into adversarial variants while the defender generates responses to them. Both roles produce structured outputs with a private chain-of-thought block (invisible to the opponent) followed by a public answer, and a guard model (WildGuard-7B or Qwen3Guard) scores the prompt–response pair on harmfulness and refusal. The final output is a full-parameter safety-aligned LLM policy, optionally co-trained with a self-distilled SFT dataset (30K examples from WildJailBreak benign prompts and HelpSteer3) to preserve conversational quality.

## Method

SELF-REDTEAM is the first fully online self-play multi-agent RL algorithm for LLM safety, in which a single shared policy π_θ alternates between attacker and defender roles within each training step. The attacker transforms seed prompts into stealthy adversarial queries (or deceptive benign-looking prompts), and the defender responds; both use hidden chain-of-thought reasoning that is concealed from the opponent, enabling strategic planning without information leakage. The game is formulated as a two-player zero-sum min-max objective, and the paper proves a Nash-equilibrium safety guarantee: at convergence, the defender produces a safe response to any adversarial input. Training uses the critic-free Re++ algorithm with role-specific advantage normalization (critical for a single model learning from opposing reward signals), a composite reward combining zero-sum game outcomes (response harmfulness and refusal) with shaping terms (CoT format adherence and revision faithfulness), and an optional auxiliary SFT loss to prevent conversational degradation. Empirically, the co-adaptive training uncovers 17.8% more diverse attacks than attacker-only baselines, reduces attack success rates by up to 95% on RLHF-aligned models across 14 benchmarks, and requires only ~44–48% more wall-clock time than standard defender-only RL fine-tuning.

## Results

SELF-REDTEAM reduces attack success rates (ASR) by an average of 33.4%–45.2% across four RLHF-aligned models (Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-IT, Llama3.1-8B-IT) on 9 harmful-refusal benchmarks, with up to 95% ASR reduction on the abliterated Llama3.1-8B-IT-AB variant. Against dynamic jailbreak methods (X-Teaming, PAIR, AutoDAN), SELF-REDTEAM achieves up to 50.7% ASR reduction (PAIR, Qwen2.5-14B-IT) and averages 31.3% improvement across all settings. Benign compliance is largely preserved (XSTest VB ≥ 0.936 for Qwen models; 0.806 for the abliterated Llama), and AlpacaEval-2 LC win rate improves by up to +4.5% on Qwen models, confirming minimal capability degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLM-LAT (Sheshadri et al., 2025), SELF-REDTEAM achieves a far more practical safety–utility trade-off: LLM-LAT attains near-perfect harmfulness reduction but causes severe over-refusal, collapsing XSTest benign compliance from 0.924 to 0.004 (Llama3.1-8B-IT) and from 0.948 to 0.552 (Qwen2.5-7B-IT), whereas SELF-REDTEAM maintains benign compliance at 0.948 and 0.964 respectively while still substantially improving safety. Against CircuitBreaker (Zou et al., 2024), SELF-REDTEAM delivers meaningfully larger safety gains: CircuitBreaker yields only a marginal WildGuardTest adversarial-harm improvement (0.297 vs. 0.303 base on Qwen2.5-7B-IT), while SELF-REDTEAM reduces the same metric to 0.179 (−41%). Internally, SELF-REDTEAM outperforms the Defender-Only + SFT baseline by an additional 17.33% average ASR reduction across 11 safety benchmarks while also achieving a higher AlpacaEval-2 LC win rate (38.09% vs. 35.50% on Qwen2.5-14B), confirming that co-evolution with an adaptive attacker produces more robust defenses than training against static attack data. The Attacker-Only baseline (training the attacker against a fixed defender) generates 17.8% fewer semantically diverse attacks (SBERT) and collapses onto a single dominant tactic (86.3% of attacks), whereas SELF-REDTEAM spreads across multiple tactics with none exceeding 30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Re++ critic-free RL algorithm (Hu et al., 2025a) as its training backbone and extends the self-play paradigm for LLMs from offline settings (SPIN, SPAG, SPPO) to fully online multi-agent reinforcement learning. It departs from the dominant reactive red-teaming pipeline (attack a static model, then patch via retraining) by formulating safety alignment as a two-player zero-sum game with a Nash-equilibrium safety guarantee, and from prior MARL-for-LLMs work by requiring no quantization, LoRA, or RNN architectures. The Hidden CoT mechanism, which conceals each agent's reasoning from its opponent, is a novel design element not present in prior self-play or red-teaming frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (zero-sum game → Nash equilibrium safety guarantee) and empirically validated (5 models, 14+ benchmarks, dynamic attacks, cross-judge validation) path toward proactive, continuous LLM safety alignment, reducing attack success rates by up to 95% while preserving conversational quality. Key open questions include whether the Nash-equilibrium guarantee holds under imperfect or adversarially-gamed reward models, how the approach scales to 70B+ parameter models, and whether the 44–48% compute overhead remains acceptable at production scale. The finding that a single model self-playing both roles outperforms separate attacker/defender pipelines suggests a simpler, more deployable safety training recipe than the current multi-stage RLHF + red-team + patch loop.
