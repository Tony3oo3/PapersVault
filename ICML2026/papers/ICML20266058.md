## Problem

Most deep hypergraph clustering methods rely on fixed-depth (typically 1-hop) propagation that implicitly assumes homophily, i.e., hyperedge-connected nodes share similar features. Under heterophilic conditions this assumption fails, causing static propagation to mix dissimilar features and degrade clustering quality. The paper addresses the lack of an adaptive mechanism that can flexibly balance local and long-range structural information across diverse homophily levels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and a binary incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{n×m} defining node-hyperedge membership. The propagation operator G = D^{-1/2}(HD_ε^{-1}H^T + I)D^{-1/2} is precomputed from H and reused across all K propagation steps. Output is a fused node embedding Z = (Z¹ + Z²)/2 of dimension d, on which standard k-means produces C cluster assignments; no labels are used during training.

## Method

Uni-DHC first applies SE-based channel recalibration to suppress uninformative feature channels, then performs learnable high-order propagation X_prop = Σ_{k=0}^{K} γ_k G^k X′, where the coefficients γ_k are learned end-to-end to weight contributions from different propagation orders. Two unshared MLP encoders project X_prop into dual-view embeddings, regularized by a node-level cross-view alignment loss (pulling diagonal entries of the inter-view similarity matrix toward 1) and a hyperedge-level correlation reduction loss (pushing off-diagonal hyperedge similarities toward 0 while preserving diagonal ones). From a spectral perspective, the paper proves that conventional HGNN-style stacking corresponds to a fixed low-pass filter g(λ)=λ^L, whereas the proposed scheme realizes a learnable polynomial spectral filter g_γ(λ)=Σ γ_k λ^k that can adaptively reweight frequency bands, preserving discriminative mid/high-frequency signals critical under heterophily.

## Results

On heterophilic datasets, Uni-DHC achieves the best ACC across all four benchmarks: 67.57% on Actor, 58.03% on Cornell, 66.50% on Texas, and 65.94% on Wisc, with the largest margin on Wisc (+16.26% over the runner-up HCN). On homophilic datasets, Uni-DHC also leads on all four (e.g., 77.82% ACC on Cora vs. 76.10% for SE-HSSL; 72.67% on Citeseer vs. 71.67% for HCN). The NMI gains on heterophilic data are particularly striking: 31.58 on Texas vs. 22.91 for HCN, and 41.21 on Wisc vs. 16.58 for HCN. Training time averages 9.96 s across all eight datasets, comparable to HCN (8.44 s) and far below MMACL (8987.54 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-DHC outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all four heterophilic datasets. The most dramatic gaps appear on Wisc, where Uni-DHC achieves 65.94% ACC versus 49.68% for HCN (the strongest baseline), a 16.26-point margin, and 41.21 NMI versus 16.58 for HCN. On Texas, Uni-DHC leads HCN by 6.77 points in ACC (66.50 vs. 59.73) and 8.67 in NMI (31.58 vs. 22.91). Among contrastive-learning baselines, HyFi and HCN are the closest competitors on homophilic data (within ~1–2% ACC on Cora/Citeseer), but both degrade sharply on heterophilic sets; TriCL and SE-HSSL fail with OOM on Actor. ED-HNN and SheafHyperGNN, designed for heterophily in supervised settings, perform poorly in the unsupervised clustering regime (ACC below 47% on Actor). CHGNN is competitive on Actor (61.21% ACC) but lags Uni-DHC by over 6 points and is far behind on the smaller heterophilic graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-DHC extends the deep hypergraph clustering line initiated by HCN (Wang et al., 2025b) and the broader family of contrastive hypergraph learning methods (HyperGCL, TriCL, MMACL, CHGNN, SE-HSSL, HyFi), all of which rely on fixed-depth (typically 1-hop) propagation that implicitly assumes homophily. The key departure is replacing this fixed propagation with a learnable polynomial spectral filter over multiple propagation orders, drawing on the spectral graph signal processing perspective to show that conventional HGNN-style propagation is a fixed low-pass filter. The SE-based channel recalibration is borrowed from vision (Hu et al., 2018), and the heterophily motivation connects to recent heterophily-aware GNN work (ED-HNN, SheafHyperGNN) but is the first to address it specifically in the hypergraph clustering setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: heterophilic hypergraph clustering was largely unaddressed, and the spectral reinterpretation (fixed low-pass vs. learnable polynomial filter) provides a clean theoretical justification for why adaptive propagation should help. However, the heterophilic evaluation relies heavily on very small graphs (Cornell: 183 nodes, Texas: 183, Wisc: 251), limiting confidence in scalability; the only large heterophilic dataset (Actor, 16K nodes) shows more moderate gains. Open questions include whether the optimal propagation depth K scales with graph size, how the framework extends to dynamic or attributed hyperedges, and whether the hyperedge-level correlation reduction generalizes to hypergraphs with very high edge overlap.
