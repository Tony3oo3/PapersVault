## Problem

Standard VAEs use Gaussian decoders that fundamentally cannot represent heavy-tailed conditional distributions, and existing heavy-tail-aware extensions (t3-VAE, xVAE) remain locked into a single predefined parametric tail family (Student-t or stable distributions). This rigidity prevents a single model from adapting to the diverse decay behaviors—Pareto, Weibull, lognormal-type—observed across real-world heavy-tailed data, leading to tail collapse and poor extreme-quantile estimation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes non-negative multivariate vectors x ∈ R_+^D (e.g., insurance claim sizes, word-frequency counts, absolute daily equity returns) and produces a d-dimensional latent code z along with generated samples drawn directly from the latent-conditioned PH likelihood rather than from the decoder mean. Each dimension's decoder outputs an initial probability vector α_j(z) ∈ R^m and an ordered rate vector λ_j(z) ∈ R^m that together define a series-canonical-form PH distribution; the data must be strictly positive, and the number of phases m (default 10) controls the decoder's expressiveness per dimension.

## Method

PH-VAE replaces the fixed-parametric decoder with a latent-conditioned Phase-Type distribution, defined as the absorption time of a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain, parameterized in series canonical (acyclic) form so that only 2m−1 parameters per dimension are needed. The decoder maps z through an MLP and produces α_j(z) via softmax and λ_j(z) via softplus followed by cumulative sum, guaranteeing a valid upper-bidiagonal sub-generator matrix with ordered rates. Training maximizes a PH-based ELBO where the reconstruction term is the exact sum of PH log-densities evaluated via the uniformization method (a Poisson-weighted matrix-power series), and the KL term is a standard Gaussian divergence with a β-VAE weight. Cross-dimensional dependence is induced entirely through the shared latent z, requiring no explicit copula, while the acyclic PH class is provably dense on (0,∞), ensuring the decoder can approximate any positive-valued distribution as m grows.

## Results

On four synthetic heavy-tailed distributions (Weibull, Pareto, Lognormal, Burr), PH-VAE achieves the lowest conditional tail KS distance (KStail) and 99th-percentile relative error (Q99) across nearly all settings, with KStail ranging from 0.020 (Lognormal) to 0.080 (Burr) and Q99 from 0.010 (Weibull) to 0.599 (Burr). On real-world data (Danish Fire Insurance, Google Web Trillion), PH-VAE reduces extreme quantile error (QErr at q=0.99) from 0.654 to 0.456 and CVaR error from 0.809 to 0.200 relative to Gaussian VAE. In multivariate settings, PH-VAE cuts Kendall's τ error from 0.1429 to 0.0672 (synthetic 5D) and from 0.2667 to 0.0293 (real financial returns), while tail co-exceedance error drops from 1.38×10⁻³ to 5.44×10⁻⁴ (synthetic) and 1.38×10⁻³ to 9.79×10⁻⁴ (financial).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PH-VAE outperforms the standard Gaussian VAE on every metric across all four synthetic distributions, with KStail improvements of 4–25× (e.g., 0.020 vs. 0.498 on Lognormal) and Q99 reductions of 5–36×. Against t3-VAE (Kim et al., 2023), PH-VAE is consistently superior; t3-VAE suffers catastrophic Q99 errors on Lognormal (100.783) and Burr (39.845), indicating its fixed Student-t tail cannot adapt to non-power-law decay. Against xVAE (Zhang et al., 2024), PH-VAE wins on Weibull (KStail 0.022 vs. 0.187), Pareto (0.051 vs. 0.161), and Burr (0.080 vs. N/A, where xVAE exhibits complete tail collapse with Q99=1.000); xVAE is competitive on Lognormal (KStail 0.032 vs. 0.020) but still trails on Q99 (0.209 vs. 0.016). In multivariate settings, PH-VAE reduces Kendall's τ error by 53% (synthetic) and 89% (financial) over Gaussian VAE, and tail co-exceedance error by 61% and 29% respectively, confirming that the shared latent representation captures realistic cross-dimensional dependence without explicit copula modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard VAE architecture (Kingma & Welling, 2013) by replacing the fixed Gaussian decoder with a latent-conditioned Phase-Type distribution, defined as the absorption time of a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain. It departs from prior heavy-tail-aware VAEs—t3-VAE (Kim et al., 2023) with its Student-t decoder and xVAE (Zhang et al., 2024) with its stable-distribution decoder—by not committing to any predefined tail family; instead, the PH decoder learns a composition of exponential time scales directly from data. The specific parameterization uses the series canonical form of acyclic PH distributions (Cumani, 1982), which reduces the generator matrix to O(m) parameters and eliminates non-identifiability, making the decoder both expressive and optimization-friendly. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first integration of Phase-Type distributions into a deep generative model, bridging applied probability (queueing theory, performance modeling) with variational representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PH-VAE addresses a practical gap: heavy-tailed data is ubiquitous in finance, insurance, NLP, and network traffic, yet standard VAE decoders systematically collapse tails, and existing heavy-tail-aware alternatives lock the model into a single parametric tail regime. The PH decoder's universality (dense on R+) means a single architecture can adapt to Weibull, Pareto, Lognormal, and Burr tails without re-specification, and the closed-form matrix-exponential likelihood keeps training cost comparable to a standard VAE. Key limitations remain: PH distributions are asymptotically light-tailed (so exact power-law recovery is out of scope), the model is restricted to non-negative data, and tail fidelity degrades when extreme observations are scarce in training. Open questions include extension to high-dimensional and non-positive data (images, signed returns), whether the shared-latent dependence mechanism scales beyond 5 dimensions, and how to handle truly unbounded tail extrapolation.
