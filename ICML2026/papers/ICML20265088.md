## Problem

In practical few-shot learning, support sets are quickly assembled and inevitably contain noisy samples (label errors, occlusions, irrelevant images), and even a single corrupted instance can distort class prototypes and degrade generalization. Existing noise-robust FSL methods either assume partial label correctness, rely on inter-sample discrepancies that break down under extreme scarcity, or treat noise as independent outliers while overlooking the geometric disruption it introduces into intra-class feature structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy N-way K-shot support set of 84×84 RGB images, a query image, and class text descriptions (used to obtain semantic center embeddings via a frozen text encoder). A frozen pretrained visual encoder (ResNet-12 or CLIP ViT-B/32) extracts high-dimensional feature vectors from all images; all subsequent processing—subspace estimation, noise detection, diffusion refinement, and prototype construction—operates entirely in this feature space rather than pixel space. The final output is a class label via nearest-prototype cosine matching, along with intermediate refined support features and semantic-weighted class prototypes.

## Method

The core insight is that clean samples from the same class occupy an approximately low-rank subspace in semantic feature space (especially CLIP, where the first principal component explains ~40% of variance), while noisy samples manifest as rank anomalies that flatten the singular-value spectrum. A differentiable Singular Value Embedding (SVE) module extracts each class's dominant subspace via power iteration with soft-thresholded singular values, enabling end-to-end gradient flow. Noisy samples are flagged when both a geometric criterion (projection residual onto the top-k subspace) and a semantic criterion (cosine distance to the class text embedding) are violated. Detected noisy features are then replaced by a rank-guided reverse diffusion process that augments the standard denoising step with a guidance term −η_t(I−P_{U(c)})z_t, which selectively suppresses only the off-subspace component while preserving in-subspace variation; a restricted contraction analysis proves the orthogonal residual decreases geometrically toward a bounded neighborhood of the class subspace. Finally, a semantic-weighted MAP prototype combines the refined support features with the text-embedding semantic center, using sample-level importance weights derived from both geometric and semantic consistency scores.

## Results

On the 5-way 5-shot label-swap noise benchmark (ResNet-12 backbone), CRDProto achieves 74.42% and 70.33% accuracy on MiniImageNet and TieredImageNet respectively at 40% noise, outperforming the next-best method (FGFL) by 7.97 and 8.93 points. At 60% noise, CRDProto reaches 55.12% and 57.53%, maintaining a 1.4–3.8 point lead over FGFL and a 5.8–3.8 point lead over SVAE-Proto. Under clean (0%) conditions, CRDProto scores 80.25% and 83.50%, slightly below FGFL (86.70%/87.20%) and SVAE-Proto (83.20%/85.88%), indicating the method trades a small clean-data margin for substantially improved noise robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRDProto is compared against five prior few-shot methods under increasing label-swap noise (0–60%). Against ProtoNet (the standard baseline), CRDProto improves 40%-noise accuracy from 57.07% to 74.42% on MiniImageNet (+17.35 pts) and from 57.07% to 70.33% on TieredImageNet (+13.26 pts). Against SVAE-Proto, CRDProto wins at every noise level ≥20% (e.g., +17.21 pts at 40% on MiniImageNet) but trails by ~3 pts at 0% noise. Against FGFL, the closest competitor, CRDProto is competitive at 20% noise (78.50 vs. 78.10 on MiniImageNet) but pulls ahead decisively at 40% (+7.97 pts) and 60% (+1.40 pts on MiniImageNet, +3.43 pts on TieredImageNet). Against AM3 and AM3-BERT, CRDProto dominates at all noise levels ≥20%, with margins of 6–17 points. Against TRAML (MiniImageNet only), CRDProto leads by 7.4–14.1 points across 20–60% noise. The consistent pattern is that CRDProto sacrifices a small clean-data margin (2–6 pts below FGFL/SVAE-Proto at 0% noise) in exchange for the largest accuracy retention as noise increases, with the gap widening from ~1 pt at 20% to ~8 pts at 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Prototypical Networks as the base classification framework and on diffusion models (Stable Diffusion/DDIM) as a generative mechanism, but departs from prior noisy-label FSL methods (e.g., CO3, DETa) that treat noisy samples as independent outliers or rely on inter-sample discrepancy. The key conceptual departure is framing noise detection through low-rank geometric structure in pretrained semantic feature spaces (especially CLIP), where clean intra-class features concentrate in a low-dimensional subspace and noisy samples manifest as rank anomalies. The differentiable Singular Value Embedding (SVE) module and the rank-guided reverse diffusion update are the main architectural contributions, bridging matrix decomposition and score-based generative modeling in a way not previously explored for FSL support-set purification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Noisy support sets are a persistent practical bottleneck in deployed few-shot systems, and this paper offers a geometrically principled (rather than purely heuristic) mechanism for both detecting and correcting such noise. The low-rank prior is particularly attractive because it is lightweight, differentiable, and does not require large-scale statistics. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality of the pretrained feature extractor's low-rank structure; the authors themselves note that domain-specific or out-of-distribution data where CLIP's priors conflict can degrade performance. Open questions include how the approach behaves when the support set is extremely small (e.g., 1–2 shots per class, making rank estimation ill-posed) and whether the low-rank assumption holds uniformly across all semantic categories or only for visually coherent classes.
