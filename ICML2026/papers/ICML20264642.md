## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) trained on transformer attention outputs suffer from a severe dead feature problem (up to 87% of features inactive at 1M scale), but the root cause has been unclear. This paper shows for the first time that multi-head self-attention outputs are confined to a low-dimensional subspace (~60% effective rank), and that this low-rank geometry creates a fundamental mismatch with randomly initialized SAE features, driving the dead feature prevalence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes mean-centered activation matrices (10M tokens, dimension d=4096 for Llama-3.1-8B) from attention outputs, MLP outputs, or residual streams, along with SAE hyperparameters (dictionary size h, sparsity k, and subspace dimension d_init). It produces SAE encoder and decoder weight matrices whose columns are projected onto the top d_init principal components of the activation covariance, yielding a sparse feature dictionary where dead features drop from 87% to below 1% at 1M features. The initialization is a one-time pre-training step requiring only an SVD of the activation matrix; no architectural changes to the SAE are made.

## Method

Active Subspace Initialization (ASI) computes the SVD of the mean-centered activation matrix, extracts the top d_init right singular vectors to define the active subspace, and projects the randomly initialized decoder weight matrix onto this subspace via W_D ← W_D V_active V_active^T before setting W_E = W_D^T. This aligns all SAE feature directions with the effective geometry of the data from the start, eliminating the random-init mismatch that causes features to never activate. The paper traces the low-rank structure to the anisotropy of the output projection W^O and overlap among per-head output subspaces, validated by decomposing Var(Oê) into contributions from Z and W^O. Combined with SparseAdam (which updates only non-zero-gradient parameters to prevent stale momentum), ASI achieves the best reconstruction at 1M features and generalizes to Lorsa by initializing per-head groups within the corresponding MHSA head's active subspace.

## Results

At the standard setting (Llama-3.1-8B, Layer 15, L₀=50, 32768 features), Active Subspace Initialization (ASI) reduces dead features from 16,144 (Base TopK) and 55 (AuxK) to just 4.00±1.73, while achieving the lowest normalized MSE (0.31680 vs. 0.33367 and 0.32241) and smallest Δ LM Loss (7.847×10⁻³ vs. 8.375×10⁻³ and 7.958×10⁻³). At 1M features with SparseAdam, ASI reduces dead features from 87% to below 1% in Attention Output SAEs. The method generalizes to Lorsa, where ASI-enhanced Lorsa achieves a better reconstruction-sparsity trade-off than both TopK Lorsa and AuxK Lorsa at 32768 heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TopK SAE and AuxK variants from Gao et al. (2024), ASI dominates on all three metrics (dead features, normalized MSE, Δ LM Loss) across every sparsity level tested (L₀ = 40–80). At L₀=50, ASI reduces dead features by ~4000× relative to Base TopK (4 vs. 16,144) and ~14× relative to AuxK (4 vs. 55), while also achieving the best reconstruction (MSE 0.31680 vs. 0.33367 and 0.32241) and the smallest LM loss degradation. Statistical significance is confirmed via Welch's t-test with p-values as low as 10⁻⁴⁰ across 15 independent seeds. Against the Lorsa sparse replacement model from He et al. (2025), applying ASI to Lorsa initialization yields a better reconstruction-sparsity trade-off and lower Δ LM Loss than both TopK Lorsa and AuxK Lorsa at 32768 heads, confirming that the subspace-constrained initialization generalizes beyond SAEs to other sparse dictionary learning architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: (1) the observation that neural representations concentrate in low-dimensional subspaces (representation collapse in self-supervised learning, low-rank bottlenecks in single-head attention), and (2) the persistent dead-feature problem in sparse autoencoders and related dictionary learning methods. It departs from prior low-rank attention work by showing that the *multi-head* attention output (not just individual heads) is confined to a subspace of only ~60% of the model dimension, and from prior SAE work by identifying this geometric property as the root cause of dead features. The proposed Active Subspace Initialization is directly inspired by PCsInit (Phan et al., 2025), which embeds PCA into the first layer of a network, but repurposes the idea to project SAE decoder weights into the activation's active subspace rather than transforming the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention outputs are universally low-rank across model families (GPT-2, Llama-3.1, Gemma-2, Qwen3) and datasets provides a new lens on transformer geometry with potential implications for model design (e.g., mitigating rank collapse to increase expressivity). Practically, ASI is a drop-in initialization that reduces dead features from 87% to below 1% at 1M-feature scale, making large-scale SAE training substantially more efficient and reliable. Key open questions remain: the causal mechanism linking low-rankness to dead features is correlational rather than proven, attempts to directly increase attention rank via auxiliary losses failed, and it is unclear whether architectural modifications (gated attention, removing GQA) can meaningfully alter the phenomenon without sacrificing efficiency.
