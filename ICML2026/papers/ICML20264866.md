## Problem

Prior work (Weller et al., 2026) argued that even the largest embedding dimensions with ideal test-set optimization are insufficient to model all top-k retrieval combinations, suggesting a fundamental geometric capacity limit. This paper challenges that interpretation by asking: what is the true minimal dimension (MED) in which every subset of size at most k can be exactly retrieved by score comparison, and does the observed empirical failure reflect a lack of geometric capacity or something else?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264866/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the method takes m object vectors in R^d, a scoring function (inner product, cosine similarity, or Euclidean distance), and a target answer-set size k, and produces the minimal dimension d for exact or robust k-shattering, along with explicit object and query vector configurations. Empirically, it consumes text documents and queries through a tokenizer (vanilla word, Qwen3 subword, or handcrafted phrase), assigns each observed token a fixed random unit Gaussian vector in R^d, embeds each text as the sum of its token vectors, and outputs Recall@2 scores. Notably, the empirical construction requires no training or label access—vectors are drawn once and reused for both documents and queries.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a cyclic-polytope construction: placing m objects on the moment curve (t, t², …, t^{2k}) in R^{2k} and, for each target subset S of size ≤ k, building a query vector from the coefficients of the squared polynomial P_S²(t) = ∏_{i∈S}(t−t_i)². This yields inner-product scores c₀ − P_S²(t_i) that achieve the maximum c₀ exactly on S and are strictly lower elsewhere, proving MED = Θ(k) independent of m for all three scoring rules. For the robust variant (unit-norm vectors with a required ε score gap), the paper derives a feasibility ceiling ε*(m,k) = m/√(k(m−1)(m−k)) via an averaging argument over k-subsets, and shows a Gaussian centroid construction in O(k² log m) dimensions achieves margin c/√k. Empirically, a label-unaware random token-sum embedding with a vanilla tokenizer surpasses the strongest reported single-vector LLM baseline (PromptRiever Llama3 8B at d=4096) on both LIMIT and LIMIT-small, and the cyclic-polytope construction exactly overfits both datasets in dimension 4, ruling out geometric capacity as the explanation for prior failures.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that exact MED is Θ(k) for inner product, Euclidean, and cosine similarity scoring, independent of the universe size m (lower bound k−1, upper bound 2k or 2k+1). In the robust (positive-margin) regime, a Gaussian centroid construction achieves RMED ≤ O(k² log m) at margin c/√k, while a feasibility ceiling ε*(m,k) = m/√(k(m−1)(m−k)) caps the achievable gap. Empirically, a label-unaware random token-sum construction with a vanilla tokenizer achieves Recall@2 of 0.7060 on LIMIT and 0.9545 on LIMIT-small at d=4096, far exceeding the PromptRiever Llama3 8B single-vector baseline (0.030 and 0.543 respectively). The cyclic-polytope construction exactly overfits both LIMIT and LIMIT-small top-2 tasks in just 4 dimensions with Recall@2 = 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole empirical baseline is the PromptRiever Llama3 8B single-vector embedding at d=4096 reported by Weller et al. (2026), achieving Recall@2 of 0.030 on LIMIT and 0.543 on LIMIT-small. The random token-sum construction (no training, no label supervision) surpasses this baseline on both datasets with all three tokenizers: the vanilla tokenizer (simplest, no LLM subwords) crosses the baseline by d=512 on both datasets and reaches 0.7060/0.9545 at d=4096, while the handmade tokenizer (using known retrieval phrases) reaches 0.9980/1.0000. The Qwen tokenizer performs weakest (0.2675 on LIMIT at d=4096), suggesting its subword geometry is less favorable for this additive construction. Additionally, the cyclic-polytope construction achieves perfect Recall@2 = 1.0 on both LIMIT (50,000 docs) and LIMIT-small (46 docs) in only 4 dimensions, demonstrating that the reported 'extremely hard' benchmark failures are not due to insufficient geometric capacity but rather to optimization, tokenization, or learning-protocol limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neighborliness property of cyclic polytopes (Ziegler, 2012; Matousek, 2013) to construct explicit low-dimensional witnesses for top-k retrieval, and on the positive-margin embedding framework and packing lower bounds of Weller et al. (2026) to define the robust MED regime. It departs conceptually from Weller et al. by showing that exact geometric capacity is Θ(k) and independent of m, directly contradicting the empirical suggestion that dimension must grow polynomially with universe size. The lower bounds draw on VC dimension theory (Mohri et al., 2018), while the robust upper bound uses Gaussian concentration and Johnson-Lindenstrauss compression, and a Radon-theorem argument sharpens the linear-score bound to min{2k, m−1}.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes a central question in embedding-based retrieval: failures of learned retrieval systems are not caused by insufficient geometric capacity but by optimization, margin conditioning, tokenization, and finite-precision effects. The gap between the Ω(k) lower bound and O(k² log m) upper bound for centroid-query robust witnesses remains open, and the cyclic-polytope construction, while certifying existence, has poor numerical conditioning and an infeasible number of subset-specific query maps for deployment. A key open question is whether the O(k² log m) centroid-query bound can be tightened toward the k log(m/k) regime known from sparse recovery, and whether practical learned encoders can approach the theoretical witnesses without explicit per-subset query optimization.
