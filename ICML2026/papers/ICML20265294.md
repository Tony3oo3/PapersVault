## Problem

Existing multimodal continual instruction tuning (MCIT) methods route test samples to task-specific experts by measuring sample-to-task-center distance within each modality and then naively averaging the visual and textual predictions. This is suboptimal because (1) center-based distance underexploits intra-task distributional diversity, and (2) modalities with high inter-task overlap produce unreliable scores that mislead the averaged routing decision.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system receives an RGB image and a natural-language instruction, extracts features via a frozen CLIP-L/14-336 visual encoder and the LLM's text pathway, and must first select the correct task-specific LoRA expert before generating a free-form text response. The routing decision is made entirely from the statistical geometry of pre-computed task distributions (centroids and variances) rather than from any stored exemplars, making the system exemplar-free at test time.

## Method

Hyper-LLaVA maintains a pool of task-specific LoRA experts (MoE) and introduces two routing mechanisms. First, it embeds each task's Euclidean statistics (centroid and aggregate variance) into a Poincaré ball via an adaptive radial mapping where low-variance specific tasks are pushed to the boundary and high-variance general tasks remain near the center; test samples are mapped to the boundary, and similarity is measured by Poincaré distance, which exponentially penalizes angular deviations near the boundary to resolve the 'gravity well' problem. Second, it computes pairwise Bhattacharyya Distances between all learned tasks in each modality to derive per-task discriminability scores, normalizes them, and passes them through a temperature-scaled Softmax to obtain adaptive modality weights that up-weight the more separable modality for each task. The final routing score is a weighted element-wise sum of the two modality's Poincaré-distance-based probability distributions, and the expert with the highest fused score is selected for response generation.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark (8 vision-language tasks), Hyper-LLaVA achieves 66.47% MFN, 68.79% MAA, 68.77% MFT, and -2.30 BWT, outperforming the strongest MoE-based baseline HiDe-LLaVA by 3.65% in MFN and SEFE by 7.90% in MFN. On the UCIT benchmark (6 tasks), Hyper-LLaVA reaches 72.81% MFN, 81.87% MAA, and 78.03% MFT, surpassing the best prior method (SEFE) by 6.27% in MFN and 3.11% in MAA. The method also achieves the best MFT on UCIT (78.03%), indicating superior plasticity in acquiring new tasks while maintaining low forgetting (BWT = -5.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-LLaVA outperforms all seven baselines on both benchmarks in MFN and MAA. Against the closest MoE-based competitor HiDe-LLaVA, it gains +3.65% MFN on CoIN and +10.52% MFN on UCIT, with the largest per-task improvements on ImageNet (+24.21% on CoIN) and CLEVR-Math (+24.23% on UCIT), tasks where routing ambiguity is most severe. Compared to SEFE (which merges LoRA experts rather than routing), Hyper-LLaVA improves MFN by +7.90% (CoIN) and +6.27% (UCIT), while also reducing forgetting from -10.45 to -2.30 BWT on CoIN. ModalPrompt achieves the best BWT (-1.33 on CoIN, -0.05 on UCIT) due to explicit anti-forgetting constraints, but sacrifices plasticity (MFT 56.39 vs. 68.77 on CoIN), resulting in substantially lower MFN (55.06 vs. 66.47). Classical regularization methods (LwF, EWC) and orthogonal-subspace methods (O-LoRA) trail by 15–25% in MFN, confirming that parameter-level isolation via expert routing is essential for MCIT. On UCIT, Hyper-LLaVA also sets the best MFT (78.03%), exceeding SEFE (75.98%) and O-LoRA (76.01%), demonstrating that its hyperbolic routing improves not just retention but also the ability to learn new tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE-LoRA paradigm for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT) established by MoE-LoRA, HiDe-LLaVA, and SMoLoRA, which route test inputs to task-specific LoRA experts via sample-to-task-center distance. It departs from these methods in two specific ways: (1) replacing Euclidean point-to-center distance with Poincaré-ball (hyperbolic) distribution-to-distribution similarity to better capture intra-task variance, and (2) replacing equal-weight dual-modality averaging with Bhattacharyya-Distance-driven adaptive modality weighting. The theoretical grounding connects the BC-based weighting to the classical optimal inverse-variance fusion result, providing a principled justification that prior MCIT routing work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation revealing that adaptive modality fusion contributes more (+9.67%) than the hyperbolic metric alone (+5.92%) identifies modality imbalance as the dominant bottleneck in MCIT routing, a finding that should influence how future methods design their cross-modal aggregation. The framework is validated on two benchmarks (CoIN, UCIT) with consistent gains over strong baselines, but remains limited to LLaVA-v1.5-7B and assumes approximately isotropic Gaussian task distributions, which may not hold for all MLLM feature spaces. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic routing generalizes to larger or architecturally different MLLMs, and whether the BWT trade-off (worse forgetting than ModalPrompt) can be mitigated without sacrificing the plasticity gains.
