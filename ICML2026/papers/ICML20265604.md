## Problem

Conventional anchor-learning multi-view clustering methods implicitly assume a uniform distribution of anchors across clusters, which fails to account for varying cluster sizes and internal structural complexity. Larger clusters with greater diversity require more anchors to capture their characteristics, while compact small clusters may be over-provisioned, leading to degraded representation quality and systematically unfair clustering outcomes where larger clusters are disadvantaged.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265604/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view data matrices {X_v ∈ R^{d_v × n}}, the number of clusters k, the number of anchors m, and four regularization hyperparameters (λ1–λ4) as input, and produces discrete clustering labels y ∈ {1,…,k}^n as output. A key design constraint is that the entire optimization pipeline operates with O(n) time and space complexity with respect to sample size, enabling scalability to datasets with over 100,000 samples where many competing methods fail to run. The method assumes full view completeness (no missing views).

## Method

FCFMVC introduces a dual-path cluster-composition-aware mechanism for anchor allocation. The first path (sample-cluster) transfers anchor distribution supervision through the bipartite graph Z, backpropagating cluster state—quantified by cardinality (F^T F) and dispersion (coefficient of variation σ/μ per cluster)—to inform anchor learning. The second path (anchor-cluster) treats anchors as pseudo-samples via per-view binary indicator matrices G_v ∈ {0,1}^{m×k} and explicitly bounds the proportion of anchors assigned to each cluster within a tolerance margin δ. The two paths are coupled through a consistency loss (‖F − Z^T G_v‖_F²) that enforces anchor-sample label alignment across views. Optimization proceeds via alternating updates: A_v is solved in closed form via SVD, Z is updated column-wise as a quadratic program, and G_v and F are updated via row-wise binary scanning with incremental loss-change computation (Theorems 4 and 5), yielding an overall O(n) complexity.

## Results

FCFMVC achieves the best ACC on FORETYP (82.79%), NUSWINE (38.62%), NUECTEN (25.13%), and YTFATEN (78.29%), and the best NMI on FORETYP (63.42%), NUSWINE (11.08%), YTFATEN (83.21%), and YTFSFLF (23.67%). On the large-scale YTFATEN dataset (38,654 samples), FCFMVC leads all metrics (ACC 78.29%, NMI 83.21%, PURITY 80.18%, FSCORE 70.83%), while several baselines (UOVSC, PFSC, SFMC, PGSC, LTBL) fail to run entirely. On YTFSFLF (101,499 samples), FCFMVC again achieves top NMI (23.67%) and PURITY (34.02%) with competitive ACC (26.32%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCFMVC outperforms all 13 baselines on NUSWINE across every metric (e.g., ACC 38.62% vs. next-best PGSC 37.05%; FSCORE 37.86% vs. PGSC 43.18% is the sole exception where PGSC leads on FSCORE). On the large-scale YTFATEN (38,654 samples) and YTFSFLF (101,499 samples) datasets, FCFMVC is the only method that runs successfully alongside MSGL, FMAC, DGF, MFSA, UDBG, and TTDM, while UOVSC, PFSC, SFMC, PGSC, and LTBL fail entirely. On FORETYP, FCFMVC leads in ACC (82.79% vs. TTDM 80.42%), NMI (63.42% vs. UDBG 55.43%), and FSCORE (72.17% vs. UDBG 64.73%), though UOVSC edges it on PURITY (84.51% vs. 81.23%). On CITSER, FCFMVC trails HOMGC on ACC (49.58% vs. 52.46%) and FMAC on NMI (24.93% vs. 27.07%), but still leads in FSCORE (40.24% vs. HOMGC 38.92%). On NUECTEN, FCFMVC achieves the best ACC (25.13%) and PURITY (34.16%) but is slightly behind DGF on NMI (11.12% vs. 11.16%) and SFMC on FSCORE (22.18% vs. 25.87%), which the authors attribute to over-smoothing of discriminative sample-anchor relationships by the shared bipartite graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

FCFMVC extends the anchor-learning paradigm for multi-view clustering (as in MSGL, UOVSC, FMAC, UDBG, TTDM, and others) by challenging their shared implicit assumption of uniform anchor distribution across clusters. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes the bipartite graph bridge between samples and anchors as a conduit for backpropagating cluster-level statistics (cardinality and coefficient-of-variation-based dispersion) into anchor assignment, while simultaneously introducing a per-view anchor-cluster indicator matrix with tolerance-bounded proportion constraints. The optimization combines SVD-based anchor updates, column-wise quadratic programming for the bipartite graph, and discrete row-wise scanning for both sample-cluster and anchor-cluster assignments, drawing on combinatorial optimization techniques rather than differentiable relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously underexplored fairness gap in anchor-based MVC: larger, more structurally complex clusters are systematically under-served by uniform anchor allocation, degrading both representation quality and clustering equity. The demonstrated linear time and space complexity, validated on datasets up to ~100K samples where several baselines simply fail to run, makes the method practically relevant for large-scale multi-view applications. However, the method requires tuning four hyper-parameters (λ₁–λ₄) plus the tolerance δ, and assumes full view completeness, limiting its applicability to incomplete multi-view scenarios that are common in real-world data collection. Open questions include whether the fairness-informed allocation principle transfers to other anchor-based paradigms (e.g., deep unfolding networks) and whether a parameter-free variant can be developed.
