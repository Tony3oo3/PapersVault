## Problem

Existing data valuation methods are either model-dependent (e.g., Influence Functions, TimeInf) or designed for i.i.d. data (e.g., LAVA, SAVA), leaving a critical gap for time series where value depends on temporal context, multi-scale patterns, and non-stationary dynamics. No learning-agnostic framework exists that can capture these sequential dependencies while remaining robust to regime shifts and outliers.

## Input / Output

The method takes an evaluated time series and a reference time series (e.g., validation set, normal segments, or verified labeled data), partitions both into overlapping segments via a sliding window of length L with stride S, and optionally incorporates segment labels for label-aware tasks. It outputs per-segment value scores (positive = helps distributional alignment, negative = hinders it) and point-wise scores obtained by averaging segment values across all windows containing each time point, enabling both coarse-grained curation and fine-grained anomaly localization.

## Method

TimeLAVA defines a Selective Wavelet-based Wasserstein (W_SW) discrepancy that combines two key mechanisms: (1) a Discrete Wavelet Transform (Daubechies-4, 2-level decomposition) as the ground metric to capture localized temporal patterns across multiple scales, and (2) Unbalanced Optimal Transport with KL-divergence marginal relaxation to enable selective matching, so that dissimilar segments or outliers can remain partially unmatched rather than being forced into high-cost alignments. The cost matrix optionally includes a label-consistency term (Wasserstein distance over conditional distributions) weighted by a hyperparameter c. Segment values are derived in closed form from the optimal dual potentials of the W_SW problem via sensitivity analysis—specifically, v(x_i) = −(ψ_κ(f_i*) − mean of ψ_κ(f_j*) for j≠i)—requiring no model training. Theoretical guarantees include bounded sensitivity to outlier contamination (Theorem 5.1) and a model-agnostic generalization bound linking W_SW to downstream performance (Theorem 5.3).

## Results

On anomaly detection (Table 1), TimeLAVA achieves the highest AUC and F1 across all four multivariate benchmarks: SMD (AUC 0.91, F1 0.52), SMAP (AUC 0.74, F1 0.54), MSL (AUC 0.81, F1 0.49), and PSM (AUC 0.77, F1 0.58), while running 10–100× faster than most deep-learning baselines (e.g., 0.72 s on SMAP vs. 245 s for LSTM). In data pruning on Exchange, TimeLAVA attains the best corruption-detection AUC of 0.782 (vs. 0.542 for TimeInf and 0.520 for KNN-Shapley) and maintains the highest R² until 45% of data is removed. For noisy-label detection on the Moving dataset with periodic noise at 15% rate, TimeLAVA reaches F1 = 0.78, outperforming TimeInf (0.65) and KNN-Shapley (0.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeLAVA outperforms the closest model-dependent baseline TimeInf on every anomaly-detection metric: AUC gains of +0.04 (SMD), +0.01 (SMAP), +0.09 (MSL), and +0.14 (PSM), with F1 improvements of +0.27 to +0.56, while also running 2–5× faster. Against deep anomaly detectors (Anomaly Transformer, ModernTCN, DCdetector, CATCH), TimeLAVA achieves substantially higher F1 (e.g., 0.52 vs. 0.05–0.35 on SMD) at a fraction of the runtime (0.25–14 s vs. 20–4258 s). Among learning-agnostic valuation baselines (LAVA, SAVA, KNN-Shapley, Data-OOB), TimeLAVA leads in corruption-detection AUC (0.782 vs. ≤0.542) and maintains the best forecasting R² during progressive data pruning. On noisy-label detection with periodic corruption, TimeLAVA's F1 of 0.78 at 15% noise exceeds TimeInf (0.65) and KNN-Shapley (0.52) by 13 and 26 points respectively; for random noise lacking temporal structure, TimeLAVA ties KNN-Shapley, confirming it exploits temporal patterns without overfitting. Isolation Forest remains competitive on AUC for PSM (0.71 vs. 0.77) but trails on F1 (0.51 vs. 0.58) and lacks the interpretability of a valuation score.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeLAVA extends the learning-agnostic data valuation paradigm of LAVA (Just et al., 2023) and SAVA (Kessler et al., 2025) from i.i.d. data to time series, where temporal dependencies and non-stationarity invalidate the independence assumptions underlying those methods. The key technical departure is the introduction of a wavelet-based ground metric (replacing Euclidean or learned distances) combined with unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat et al., 2018) to enable selective matching that naturally handles regime shifts and outliers. This positions the work between model-dependent time series valuation (TimeInf, which extends influence functions via temporal blocking) and model-agnostic i.i.d. valuation, filling the gap of a learning-agnostic framework that respects sequential structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-agnostic valuation framework specifically designed for time series, providing theoretical guarantees (bounded outlier sensitivity, model-agnostic generalization bounds) that prior heuristic or model-dependent approaches lack. The practical impact is significant for data curation in safety-critical domains (healthcare, finance, industrial monitoring) where identifying low-quality or anomalous segments without retraining models saves substantial computational and financial cost. Key limitations include dependence on reference set representativeness, per-channel processing that ignores cross-channel dependencies, and scalability constraints for very long sequences. Open directions include diversity-aware reference sampling, cross-channel modeling, and extension to active learning and classification tasks.
