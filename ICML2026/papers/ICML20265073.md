## Problem

Zero-shot inverse problems require recovering a clean signal from a single degraded observation without external training data, yet existing approaches either rely on complex over-parameterized networks (DIP), are restricted to denoising via patch-based autoencoders (ZS-NCD), or lack theoretical grounding for how compression acts as a regularizer. This paper challenges the assumption that high model complexity is necessary, showing that a lightweight randomized network with entropy and complexity constraints in a compression-based formulation suffices for state-of-the-art restoration across diverse inverse problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single degraded observation y (e.g., a noisy image under AWGN or Poisson noise, a bicubic-downsampled image at ×4 or ×8 with additive noise, or a partially masked image) together with the known degradation operator A, and produces a full-resolution restored image. No external training data, pretrained weights, or clean ground truth are required; all parameters are optimized per-instance from the single observation. The method handles both grayscale (Set11, Set13, Kodak24) and RGB (Set5, AFHQ) images at resolutions from 192×192 to 768×512, and extends to nonlinear degradations such as JPEG artifacts and demosaicing.

## Method

Lottery Prior formulates restoration as a rate-distortion optimization: a randomly initialized implicit neural network (with Fourier-feature positional encoding) maps pixel coordinates to signal values, but only a sparse subnetwork selected by a learned binary mask τ is active, and its layers are modulated by a lightweight network f_θ that processes a multi-scale entropy-constrained latent Z. The latent Z is an L-level pyramid of learnable vectors whose quantized values are encoded by a factorized auto-regressive model with Laplace conditionals, imposing a rate term R(Z) that acts as an implicit structural prior. The mask scores P are optimized via a straight-through estimator under measurement-consistency and entropy losses, while the random initialization seed and mask ratio together induce a family of lottery priors whose ensemble (via linear averaging or gated aggregation) further refines the reconstruction. The paper additionally derives non-asymptotic measurement- and signal-domain error bounds for compression-based ML inverse solvers, proving that the rate-distortion constraint provides provable regularization with a two-term bound consisting of codebook distortion and a rate-dependent noise term.

## Results

On denoising (AWGN σ=15), Lottery Prior achieves 32.30 dB PSNR / 0.8922 SSIM on Set11 and 34.29 dB / 0.9118 on Kodak24, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art ZS-NCD (31.35 / 33.18) by ~1 dB while using only 0.7–3.3K effective parameters versus ZS-NCD's 0.4M. At σ=25, it reaches 29.73 dB on Set11 and 31.48 dB on Kodak24. For noisy 4× super-resolution (σ=15), Lottery Prior achieves 25.87 dB average PSNR on Set5, surpassing the supervised BM3D+LapSRN pipeline (25.17 dB) and DIP (21.37 dB). For random-mask inpainting at ρ=0.5, it reaches 33.22 dB versus DIP's 31.27 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lottery Prior outperforms all nine baselines across every task and noise level. Against the closest competitor ZS-NCD (previous SOTA, 0.4M params), it gains ~1.0–1.1 dB PSNR on AWGN denoising (e.g., 34.29 vs 33.18 on Kodak24 at σ=15) and ~0.8 dB on Poisson, while using roughly 100× fewer effective parameters. Against DIP (2.2M params), the margin is larger: 3.2 dB on Set11 at σ=15 (32.30 vs 29.11) and 4.5 dB on noisy 4× SR (25.87 vs 21.37), with DIP also requiring sensitive early stopping. Pixel-masking methods (ZS-N2S, S2S) degrade severely on high-resolution images (e.g., ZS-N2S drops to 18.18 dB on Set13 at σ=15), while Lottery Prior's global structural prior maintains consistent performance. On noisy super-resolution, Lottery Prior (25.87 dB avg) surpasses even the supervised BM3D+LapSRN pipeline (25.17 dB) in a zero-shot setting. For inpainting, it beats DIP by 1.95 dB at ρ=0.5 (33.22 vs 31.27) and 1.37 dB at ρ=0.8. Against the pretrained foundation model RAM, Lottery Prior achieves 32.22 dB on demosaicing without any pretraining, compared to RAM's 31.38 dB pretrained (though RAM with 10 self-supervised fine-tuning measurements reaches 36.87 dB). On text removal, Lottery Prior preserves finer details without halo artifacts compared to Shepard-Net and DIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LotteryCodec implicit codec architecture (Wu et al., 2025) from image compression to zero-shot inverse problems, repurposing randomized subnetworks as architectural priors rather than compression codecs. It generalizes the compression-based denoising framework of Zafari et al. (2026) from the denoising-specific setting to general linear inverse problems (super-resolution, inpainting), while replacing their AE-based patch-driven approach with a global implicit codec. The Fourier reparameterized initialization from Shi et al. (2024) is adopted to mitigate spectral bias in the random network, and the auto-regressive entropy model provides spatial context that is particularly beneficial for inpainting. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of the lottery ticket hypothesis (subnetwork selection), rate-distortion theory (entropy as implicit regularizer), and deep image priors (architectural bias), unifying these into a single compression-based formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that zero-shot inverse problems do not require complex multi-scale models, challenging the prevailing assumption that architectural complexity is necessary for high-quality restoration. The non-asymptotic error bounds provide the first theoretical guarantees for compression-based solvers in general inverse problems, clarifying how rate-distortion constraints act as implicit regularizers with provable error control. However, the method's gains diminish for severely ill-conditioned operators (JPEG artifacts, MRI), where operator-induced ambiguity dominates over signal uncertainty, suggesting that entropy-based regularization alone is insufficient in those regimes. A promising direction highlighted by the authors is integrating Lottery Prior into plug-and-play frameworks that explicitly handle the forward operator, which could address the current limitation on highly ill-posed problems.
