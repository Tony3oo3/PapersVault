## Problem

LLMs frequently fail on multiple-choice questions even when their hidden representations linearly encode the correct answer, revealing a misalignment between internal knowledge and output behavior. Prior work identified this knowledge-prediction gap only in narrow settings (truthfulness, arithmetic) without a geometric account of its structure or a systematic inference-time mitigation. This paper asks whether the gap is pervasive across diverse MCQ domains, what subspace geometry underlies it, and whether it can be closed at inference time without additional training.

## Input / Output

KAPPA consumes an MCQ text prompt, extracts the residual stream activation at a selected intermediate layer (the final token of the answer prefix), and applies a closed-form affine transformation using pre-trained knowledge-probe and prediction-probe weight matrices. The adjusted hidden state is fed back into the model at every subsequent decoding step, so the intervention persists across all generated tokens. The output is the model's standard MCQ answer (option symbol), with the side effect that the model's prediction coordinates in the prediction subspace now match its knowledge coordinates.

## Method

The paper first trains two linear k-class probes on the residual stream: a knowledge probe predicting the ground-truth answer and a prediction probe predicting the model's own decoded option. Treating each probe's weight vectors as spanning a subspace, the authors show via mean principal angles and CKA that these subspaces become nearly orthogonal in deeper layers, and that this misalignment correlates strongly (Spearman ρ=0.976) with the behavioral gap across benchmarks. KAPPA then solves a constrained optimization—minimize ‖h′−h‖² subject to the prediction coordinates of h′ equaling the knowledge coordinates of h—yielding the closed-form update h′ = h + W_pred(W_predᵀW_pred)⁻¹(W_knowᵀh + b_know − W_predᵀh − b_pred), which minimally shifts the hidden state within the prediction subspace while leaving orthogonal components untouched. An extended variant introduces hyperparameters α and β to amplify or sharpen the knowledge signal. The intervention is applied at top-n consecutive intermediate layers selected by knowledge-probe validation accuracy, and unlike fixed-direction steering methods, KAPPA computes an input-specific adjustment at each decoding step.

## Results

KAPPA consistently reduces the knowledge-prediction gap (measured by AGR, the agreement rate between the knowledge probe and model predictions) across six benchmarks and two model families, with AGR improvements of up to ~22 points over the base model (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B on BBQ-Age: 59.2% → 81.1% with KAPPA(6)). These alignment gains translate into substantial accuracy improvements: on Llama 3.1 8B, KAPPA(6) lifts TruthfulQA accuracy from 56.7% to 73.5% (+16.8 pts) and BBQ-Age from 59.9% to 76.8% (+16.9 pts), while delivering consistent 2–5 point gains on reasoning benchmarks (GSM8k: 32.6% → 36.6%). KAPPA approaches the knowledge-probe ceiling (KP row) on AGR, confirming that the model's linearly accessible knowledge is largely recoverable through the intervention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAPPA outperforms both CAA (Contrastive Activation Addition) and DoLA across nearly all benchmarks and metrics. Against CAA, KAPPA(1) on Llama 3.1 8B improves AGR by 10–21 points (e.g., TruthfulQA: 78.8% vs. 67.2%; BBQ-Age: 75.0% vs. 64.8%) and accuracy by 5–14 points (TruthfulQA: 67.8% vs. 62.3%; BBQ-Age: 73.8% vs. 65.8%). Against DoLA, the advantage is even more pronounced: DoLA actually degrades accuracy on several Llama 3.1 8B benchmarks (BBH-Algo drops from 45.1% to 42.4%; BBQ-Religion from 67.6% to 64.9%), while KAPPA(1) improves all of them. On Qwen2.5 7B, where the base model is stronger, CAA and DoLA yield only marginal changes (±1–2 points), whereas KAPPA(6) still delivers consistent 1–5 point accuracy gains and 4–9 point AGR improvements. The knowledge probe (KP) row serves as an upper bound, showing that KAPPA recovers a large fraction of the gap without requiring the model to fully adopt the probe's predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'knowledge-prediction gap' (or 'cognitive dissonance') literature—where linear probes extract correct answers from hidden states despite incorrect model outputs (Marks & Tegmark, 2024; Liu et al., 2023; Orgad et al., 2025)—from its prior narrow scope (truthfulness detection, simple arithmetic) to diverse MCQ benchmarks spanning reasoning, knowledge, and bias. It also repurposes the activation-steering paradigm (Rimsky et al., 2024; Chuang et al., 2024) but departs from fixed-direction steering by deriving a closed-form, input-specific affine correction grounded in the geometric relationship between two probe-defined subspaces. The geometric framing via principal angles and CKA is novel relative to prior work that identified the gap behaviorally without explaining its representational structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that many LLM MCQ failures stem from a geometric misalignment between internally encoded knowledge and output predictions—rather than from missing knowledge—reframes a common failure mode and suggests a lightweight, training-free remedy. KAPPA's transfer to free-form generation (trained only on MCQ data) hints that the identified subspaces capture abstract, task-general features, though this remains preliminary. Open questions include whether the approach extends to nonlinearly encoded knowledge, whether the gap persists under different prompting strategies (e.g., chain-of-thought), and whether the n=8-benchmark correlation between subspace angle and behavioral gap generalizes to larger task distributions.
