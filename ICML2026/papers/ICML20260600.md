## Problem

Decoding-based regression reformulates numerical prediction as autoregressive token generation, but the standard cross-entropy loss treats tokens as independent categories, ignoring their ordinal value and the cumulative magnitude of the detokenized number. Existing token-level fixes (NTL, DIST²) still operate locally on individual digits and fail to capture global numerical error, leaving the potential of this paradigm largely unrealized.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—tabular feature vectors, raw source code snippets, serialized ONNX computational graphs, or free-form text prompts—and produces a continuous scalar prediction. Internally, the target value is tokenized into a discrete sequence (via normalized base-B expansion or scientific notation such as P10/IEEE), the model generates this sequence autoregressively over K steps, and multiple sampled sequences are detokenized and aggregated (median or mean) into the final scalar. The reward signal is computed only after full-sequence detokenization, making it inherently sequence-level rather than per-token.

## Method

GenRe² reformulates decoding-based regression as a Markov Decision Process and optimizes the policy via REINFORCE-style gradients with a sequence-level reward (negative MSE on the detokenized prediction). The paper proves that ReMax outperforms GRPO for regression because GRPO's standard-deviation normalization erases absolute error scale, disconnecting gradient magnitude from regression severity. To solve the sparse-reward credit-assignment problem, GenRe² augments the ReMax objective with On-Policy Distillation (OPD): an expert oracle, conditioned on the ground-truth target, identifies at each step the optimal next token that minimizes numerical distance given the model's self-generated prefix (rather than blindly forcing the target digit). Theoretical analysis via an entropy-increasing condition (π_k < exp(−H)) shows that OPD selectively targets low-probability recovery tokens, naturally injecting entropy to prevent the mode collapse observed in vanilla ReMax while simultaneously sharpening numerical precision.

## Results

On the TALENT benchmark (100 tabular regression tasks), GenRe² achieves a median RMSE of 0.4930 and R² of 0.6587, outperforming the Riemann head baseline (RMSE 0.5152, R² 0.6547) and all token-level methods (NTL-WAS: RMSE 0.5378; DIST²: RMSE 0.5392). On code metric regression (Table 2), GenRe² attains the best Spearman correlation on 8 of 9 datasets, including 0.977 on APPS Leetcode and 0.631 on Triton Kernel Latency. For Generative Reward Modeling, the 7B GenRe²-prometheus model achieves an average Spearman score of 0.6085, surpassing its 56B teacher (0.6060).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenRe² decisively outperforms the Riemann head (Imani & White, 2018) on TALENT, reducing median RMSE from 0.5152 to 0.4930 and improving R² from 0.6547 to 0.6587, while also achieving the best Rank Correlation (0.7826 vs. 0.7746). Against the token-level NTL variants (Zausinger et al., 2025), GenRe² improves median RMSE by ~0.04 (0.4930 vs. 0.5378 for NTL-WAS and 0.5356 for NTL-MSE), showing that sequence-level RL captures global numerical magnitude that local token penalties miss. DIST² (Chung et al., 2026) is the weakest decoding-based baseline (RMSE 0.5392, R² 0.6180), and GenRe² surpasses it by a wide margin on all three metrics. In the GRM setting, GenRe²-prometheus (7B, avg 0.6085) beats both TRACT (7B, 0.5994) and the 56B Prometheus-2 teacher (0.6060), while GenRe²-TRACT (0.5136) trails TRACT (0.5994), indicating that the quality of the OPD teacher matters. On code metric regression, GenRe² wins on 8/9 datasets, with the sole exception being NASNet where ReMax (0.553) narrowly edges out GenRe² (0.539).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends decoding-based regression (Song & Bahri, 2025), which reformulates continuous prediction as autoregressive token generation, by replacing token-level losses (CE, NTL, DIST²) with a hybrid sequence-level RL objective. It builds on ReMax (Li et al., 2024) as the policy-gradient backbone and integrates On-Policy Distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024) to address the temporal credit assignment problem inherent in sparse-reward RL. The key departure is from both purely local token-level supervision (which ignores global numerical magnitude) and purely global RL (which suffers from mode collapse and cannot localize errors), positioning GenRe² as a principled middle ground that leverages the strengths of each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GRPO's standard-deviation normalization induces a detrimental scale invariance for regression—disconnecting gradient magnitude from error severity—has implications beyond this paper for any RL application where absolute error magnitude should drive update strength. The OPD mechanism provides a general recipe for combining sparse sequence-level rewards with dense token-level expert guidance, potentially applicable to other structured generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long numerical sequences, whether the computational overhead of RL training (vs. simple SFT) is justified at scale, and whether the mode-collapse limitation of RL (sharpening without expanding capability, as shown by best@k analysis) can be further mitigated.
