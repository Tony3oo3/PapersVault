## Problem

Pixel-space video generation models (diffusion, autoregressive) produce visually compelling short clips but fail on the precise symbolic layouts, step-by-step reasoning, and long-form temporal coherence that educational videos require. Existing multi-agent pipelines decompose generation into subtasks but rarely address the dual challenge of maintaining both temporal coherence (concepts introduced, expanded, and reinforced in logical sequence) and spatial clarity (elements arranged legibly without occlusion), and no prior work provides an evaluation protocol that measures actual knowledge transfer rather than mere visual fidelity.

## Input / Output

The system takes a concise learning topic query (avg. 6.3 words, e.g., 'Fourier Transform'), optionally augmented by reference images retrieved via Google Images and reusable visual assets (icons, logos) sourced from Iconfinder/Freepik. It outputs section-wise executable Manim Community Edition v0.19.0 code that renders into a minute-level educational video (~2 min, 14–15× longer than pixel-based baselines), structured as synchronized left-side lecture lines and right-side animations on a black background. All spatial positioning is constrained to a 6×6 grid of fixed Manim coordinates, and the code is fully auditable and editable post-generation.

## Method

Code2Video is a three-agent pipeline that generates educational videos by writing executable Manim programs rather than synthesizing pixels. The Planner converts a topic query into a hierarchical storyboard (outline → per-section lecture lines and paired animations) and retrieves reference images and reusable assets from an external database to anchor complex concepts. The Coder synthesizes section-wise Manim code in parallel and applies ScopeRefine, a hierarchical debugging strategy that escalates context from line-level (±1 line) to block-level to full-section regeneration only on persistent failure, reducing token cost by 1.4–1.6× over naive retry. The Critic refines spatial layout using Visual Anchor Prompting, which discretizes the 2D canvas into a 6×6 grid of fixed-coordinate anchor points, converting continuous positioning into a discrete selection task; a VideoLLM then inspects rendered frames alongside an occupancy table (tracking each element's assigned anchors, scale, and code lines) to detect overlaps and occlusions, producing targeted grid-coordinate edits. This code-centric paradigm yields a 40% improvement in TeachQuiz (an unlearn-then-relearn knowledge-transfer metric) over direct code generation and produces videos competitive with professional 3Blue1Brown tutorials.

## Results

On the MMMC benchmark, Code2Video with Claude Opus 4.1 achieves the best overall performance: 87.9 average Aesthetics (AES) and 86.0 TeachQuiz, compared to 37.8 and 40.0 for the same backbone used as a direct CodeLLM baseline (+50.1 and +46.0 points). Pixel-based diffusion models (OpenSora-v2, Wan2.2, Veo3) score near-zero on Element Layout and Logic Flow, with AES of only 3.7–9.0 and TeachQuiz of 0.0–2.5. Human-crafted 3B1B videos remain the upper bound at 99.7 AES and 97.1 TeachQuiz, but the best Code2Video configuration narrows the gap to roughly 12 points on AES and 11 on TeachQuiz. On TheoremExplainBench, Code2Video (GPT-4o) reaches 0.88 overall, surpassing TEA (0.78) with particularly large gains in Element Layout (0.91 vs. 0.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixel-based diffusion models (OpenSora-v2, Wan2.2, Veo3), Code2Video wins decisively on every metric: AES improves from 3.7–9.0 to 57.4–87.9, and TeachQuiz from 0.0–2.5 to 44.0–86.0, confirming that pixel-space synthesis cannot maintain symbolic precision or coherent exposition needed for educational content. Against direct CodeLLM generation (same backbone, no agent pipeline), Code2Video yields consistent gains of roughly 40–50 points on AES and 35–46 points on TeachQuiz across all three tested backbones (GPT-5, GPT-4.1, Claude Opus 4.1), with the largest absolute improvement observed under Claude Opus 4.1 (AES 37.8→87.9, TQ 40.0→86.0). On TheoremExplainBench, Code2Video outperforms TheoremExplainAgent (TEA) by 0.06–0.10 in overall score, with the most striking gain in Element Layout (e.g., 0.91 vs. 0.59 for GPT-4o), validating that the Visual Anchor Prompting and occupancy tracking generalize beyond the MMMC setting. Human-crafted 3B1B videos still lead on both AES (99.7 vs. 87.9) and TeachQuiz (97.1 vs. 86.0), but the gap is substantially smaller than for any automated baseline. In the unlearning ablation (Table 7), the proposed in-context unlearning matches or slightly exceeds both fine-tuning-based and standard in-context unlearning on closed-source VLMs (e.g., 84 vs. 81 vs. N/A on Gemini-2.5 Pro for Claude Opus 4.1–generated videos), while enabling use of stronger evaluation models that fine-tuning cannot access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-agent LLM pipelines, code generation, and video creation, but departs from both pixel-space video synthesis (diffusion/autoregressive models) and prior code-centric video work (Kubrick, TheoremExplainAgent) by arguing that executable Manim programs are the correct intermediate representation specifically for educational content requiring symbolic precision and stepwise exposition. The multi-agent decomposition (Planner-Coder-Critic) follows the broader trend of agentic video pipelines (Mora, DreamFactory, GenMAC), but the Visual Anchor Prompting mechanism and ScopeRefine debugging strategy are novel contributions tailored to the spatial and executability constraints of code-based rendering. The evaluation protocol, particularly TeachQuiz with in-context unlearning, has no direct precedent in prior video generation benchmarks, which typically rely on perceptual or FID-style metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that code-centric generation is not merely an alternative but a fundamentally superior paradigm for structured educational video, with the 40% TeachQuiz gain over direct code generation and near-zero pixel-based scores on layout/logic providing strong evidence. The MMMC benchmark and TeachQuiz metric fill a genuine gap in evaluating whether generated videos actually transfer knowledge rather than just looking plausible. Key limitations include the substantial remaining gap to human-made quality (87.9 vs. 99.7 AES), dependence on Manim as the sole rendering backend, and the prompt-based unlearning in TeachQuiz which may not perfectly isolate video-induced learning from residual model knowledge. Open questions include whether hybrid code-pixel pipelines can close the remaining quality gap and whether the paradigm generalizes to 3D or interactive educational content.
