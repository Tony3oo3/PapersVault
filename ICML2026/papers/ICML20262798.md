## Problem

Existing evidential multi-view fusion rules (Dempster-Shafer, Sum, Mean, Weighted) operate directly on raw logit magnitudes, implicitly assuming that evidence scale is proportional to semantic reliability. The paper identifies and formally proves the Scale Mismatch Problem: arbitrary scale perturbations (e.g., sensor amplification/attenuation) cause these rules to conflate numerical magnitude with confidence, enabling a single inflated noisy view to hijack the fusion via false dominance while suppressing reliable low-magnitude views. No prior fusion mechanism guarantees invariance to such nuisance scale factors.

## Input / Output

Each view is processed independently by a ResNet-18 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet) or pre-extracted features (AVE), producing a K-dimensional logit vector per view. The SAEF module then standardizes these logits instance-wise and fuses them in Z-space, outputting a joint Dirichlet parameter vector from which both the class prediction and a calibrated evidential uncertainty score (vacuity u = K/S) are derived. The uncertainty output is provably invariant to any global or per-view temperature scaling of the input logits, which is the key structural guarantee distinguishing SAEF from baselines.

## Method

SAEF decouples semantic direction from magnitude in two stages with zero additional learnable parameters. First, Instance-wise Evidential Standardization computes per-view logit mean and standard deviation, then normalizes with a noise floor ε (preventing amplification of flat/noisy views) and a fixed canonical scale β (restoring the model's capacity to express low uncertainty in the Softplus linear regime). Second, Uncertainty-Weighted Stouffer Fusion maps the standardized Dirichlet probabilities to Z-scores via the inverse normal CDF, then combines them using a weighted average where weights are (1 − standardized uncertainty) and the denominator is the standard Stouffer variance stabilizer. This yields strict global scale invariance (Theorem 5.1), robustness against asymmetric dominance (Theorem 5.2), and automatic noise suppression (Theorem 5.3), all while maintaining O(VK) computational complexity identical to elementary fusion rules.

## Results

SAEF achieves the best classification accuracy across all four benchmarks: 82.84% on AVE, 48.17% on SUNRGBD, 44.97% on CheXpert, and 78.97% on MURA. The most notable gain is on SUNRGBD, where SAEF surpasses the strongest baseline (SAEML) by 2.14% in accuracy, attributed to its ability to handle inherent RGB–Depth scale discrepancies. SAEF also ranks first in failure-detection AUROC on AVE (98.28%), SUNRGBD (84.38%), and CheXpert (73.82%), while remaining competitive on MURA (83.99%). Inference overhead is negligible, with per-sample latency within ~0.1 ms of the fastest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAEF outperforms all seven baselines in classification accuracy across every dataset. The largest margin is on SUNRGBD, where SAEF (48.17%) beats the strongest baseline SAEML (46.03%) by 2.14%, and also surpasses TMDOA (45.93%) and TEF (45.15%) by over 2%. On AVE, SAEF leads RAR (82.63%) and SAEML (82.60%) by roughly 0.2–0.3% in accuracy while also achieving the highest AUROC (98.28%). On CheXpert, SAEF (44.97%) edges out TMDOA (44.45%) and TMCEK (44.40%) by ~0.5–0.6%. On MURA, SAEF achieves the top accuracy (78.97%) over RAR (78.44%), though its AUROC (83.99%) is marginally below RAR's (84.05%), indicating a near-tie on failure detection for this highly saturated binary task. Overall, SAEF consistently wins or ties on both accuracy and reliability metrics, with the clearest separation appearing on the multi-modal SUNRGBD benchmark where scale mismatches between RGB and Depth views are most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) framework of Sensoy et al. (2018) to multi-view classification, building directly on the Dirichlet-based evidence extraction and uncertainty quantification paradigm. It departs from all canonical evidential fusion strategies (Dempster-Shafer, Sum, Mean, Weighted) by identifying a shared structural flaw—operating on raw evidence magnitudes that conflate logit scale with semantic reliability—and replaces them with instance-wise standardization followed by Stouffer's Z-score fusion (Kim et al., 2013). The key conceptual departure is reframing multi-view evidential fusion as a hypothesis-testing problem in a standardized Gaussian domain rather than an algebraic combination of raw evidence vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the Scale Mismatch Problem exposes a previously unacknowledged vulnerability in safety-critical multi-view systems: a single corrupted or amplified view can hijack the fusion decision with high false confidence, a failure mode particularly dangerous in medical imaging or autonomous sensing. The zero-parameter solution with rigorous theoretical guarantees (global scale invariance, asymmetric dominance robustness) is practically attractive, though the accuracy gains over strong baselines are modest (1–2%). Open questions include whether the instance-wise standardization assumption holds under non-uniform per-class scaling, how the method behaves with more than 3 views or highly heterogeneous backbones, and whether the synthetic perturbation protocols (temperature scaling, Gaussian noise injection) faithfully capture real-world sensor degradation patterns.
