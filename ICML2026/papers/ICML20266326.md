## Problem

Prompt learning for vision-language models (e.g., CoOp on CLIP) suffers sharp performance degradation when downstream training labels are noisy, and existing robust-loss or sample-partitioning methods propagate estimation errors that worsen under high noise rates. The paper identifies that pre-trained CLIP embeddings remain largely unaffected by label noise and can serve as a stable reference, but no prior work adaptively leverages this noise-agnostic signal to balance trust between the prior and corrupted supervision at the sample level.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and outputs a class probability distribution over C classes, trained with 16-shot splits under symmetric or asymmetric label noise. Unusually, the training signal is not the raw noisy label but an evidence-conditioned soft target y_s that is a convex combination of the noisy one-hot label and the frozen CLIP prior distribution, with the mixing weight e_i computed per-sample from credibility estimates. The only trainable parameters are 16 learnable context tokens prepended to the class token in the text prompt; both the image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT) and text encoder remain frozen throughout.

## Method

Evidence-Prompt constructs a sample-wise evidence prior E_i = {S(i), p_i^t, q_i^t} entirely from frozen CLIP representations: a top-K cosine-similarity support pool S(i), the single-sample CLIP probability p_i^t, and a similarity-weighted soft-vote mixture q_i^t over the pool. It then estimates two credibility scores — supervision-agnostic credibility E_T,i (product of marginal entropy-based evidence and joint Jensen-Shannon-divergence evidence between p_i^t and q_i^t) and supervision-conditioned credibility E_L,i (conflict-adaptive mixture of noisy-label consistency evidence and semantic plausibility evidence) — to compute a per-sample weight e_i = E_T,i/(E_T,i + E_L,i + ε). The optimization target y_s = (1−e_i)·ỹ_i + e_i·p_i^t is a Bayesian-inspired posterior that shifts toward the stable prior when the label is unreliable and toward the label when it is trustworthy. Prompts are trained by minimizing a generalized cross-entropy loss (z=0.5) between the model's predicted distribution and y_s, requiring no label filtering, partitioning, or additional network parameters beyond the standard CoOp context tokens.

## Results

On OxfordPets at 75% symmetric noise, Evidence-Prompt achieves 88.83% top-1 accuracy (vs. 70.77% for NLPrompt, the strongest baseline), and 85.20% under 75% asymmetric noise (+36.6% over NLPrompt). The accuracy degradation metric Δ = Acc(12.5%) − Acc(75%) is 0.46% (Sym) and 4.06% (Asym) on OxfordPets, far below the next-best JoAPR (9.60% / 39.33%). On the real-world noisy dataset Food101N, Evidence-Prompt reaches 79.24%, outperforming NLPrompt (76.46%) by 2.78 points. Across all seven synthetic benchmarks, the method achieves the best or tied-best accuracy at every noise level, with the largest margins appearing at 62.5–75% noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Evidence-Prompt outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and noise setting. Against the primary SOTA competitor NLPrompt (Pan et al., CVPR 2025), the gap widens sharply at high noise: +18.06% on OxfordPets Sym-75, +36.60% on OxfordPets Asym-75, and +13.37% on Flowers102 Asym-75. JoAPR (Guo & Gu, CVPR 2024) is the second-strongest baseline but still trails by 14.43% (OxfordPets Sym-75) and 41.63% (OxfordPets Asym-75). The simpler CoOp+GCE baseline (Wu et al., ICCV 2023) degrades rapidly, reaching only 53.17% on OxfordPets Sym-75 versus 88.83% for Evidence-Prompt. Among classic noisy-label losses adapted to CoOp (GCE, JAL-CE, JAL-FL, OGC-CE), OGC-CE is the closest competitor but still lags by 6.75–11.38% at 50% Asym noise on OxfordPets, DTD, and EuroSAT. The distillation-based DHO (Kang et al., 2025) achieves 88.7% on Caltech101 Sym-75, slightly below Evidence-Prompt's 94.9%, and adding Evidence-Prompt to DHO further improves it to 94.4%. Zero-shot CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) serves as a noise-agnostic reference in Figure 6; while stable across noise rates, it lacks task adaptation and is consistently outperformed by Evidence-Prompt, which preserves prompt-learning gains while matching the prior's noise resilience.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CoOp's parameter-efficient prompt learning for CLIP-based VLMs and extends the GCE loss to distributional targets, but departs conceptually from prior noisy-label prompt learning methods (JoAPR, NLPrompt) that rely on loss-based sample partitioning or optimal transport. The key departure is introducing a noise-agnostic 'evidence prior' constructed from frozen pre-trained CLIP embeddings and framing the learning problem as Bayesian posterior reasoning with sample-wise credibility estimation, rather than attempting to correct or filter noisy labels. This positions the work at the intersection of Bayesian inference and vision-language model adaptation, where the pre-trained model serves not merely as an initialization but as a persistent, reliability-weighted reference throughout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world VLM adaptation datasets frequently contain noisy annotations, and existing prompt learning methods degrade sharply under high noise. The evidence-prior framework is modular and transferable (improving JoAPR and NLPrompt by up to 15.66%), suggesting it could be integrated into other VLM adaptation pipelines. Limitations include evaluation primarily on 16-shot classification with CLIP backbones; open questions include whether the approach extends to generative VLM tasks, larger-scale fine-tuning, or instance-dependent noise structures where the top-K support pool assumption may break down.
