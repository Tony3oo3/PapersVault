## Problem

Optimizing retrieval in agentic reasoning is hampered by the lack of dense, principled reward signals for intermediate search actions. Existing RL approaches rely on sparse final-answer correctness, which cannot distinguish between retrieval steps that are equally distant from the correct answer, while heuristic process-supervision methods lack a formal probabilistic framework guaranteeing that local retrieval decisions contribute to global uncertainty reduction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261631/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language question and, at each reasoning turn, optionally issues a <search> query to a local retrieval backend (E5/BGE-M3 over Wikipedia or cached web pages), receiving top-k documents as observations. The model iteratively produces <search> queries and <answer> tags within a multi-turn loop (up to T turns). During training, the reward computation additionally samples M=12 candidate responses under two conditioning contexts—a fact-free paraphrase of the question (prior) and the question concatenated with retrieved evidence (posterior)—and feeds them through a DeBERTa-large NLI model to construct semantic equivalence classes before computing the information gain signal.

## Method

InfoReasoner redefines information gain as uncertainty reduction over a belief state in a POMDP formulation, proving three key properties: non-negativity under ideal Bayesian updates, telescoping additivity (local gains sum to global uncertainty reduction), and monotonicity with respect to Blackwell-dominating information channels. Practically, the belief state is approximated by clustering M sampled model outputs into semantic equivalence classes via bidirectional textual entailment (DeBERTa-large fine-tuned on MNLI with a confidence threshold τ), and the uncertainty functional is instantiated as semantic entropy over these classes. The training reward r_t = log p(c*|C_t) − log p(c*|B) measures the log-probability increase on the gold semantic class after incorporating retrieved evidence, providing dense per-step credit without manual retrieval annotations. The policy is optimized via GRPO with a composite reward R_i = EM(y_i, y*) + λ·(1/T_i)Σ r_t (λ=0.6), balancing final-answer correctness with intermediate information-gain supervision, trained on Qwen2.5-3B/7B over NQ and HotpotQA training splits.

## Results

On seven QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, Bamboogle), InfoReasoner-3B achieves 34.6% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest 3B retrieval baseline Search-R1-3B-Instruct (30.1%) by 4.5 points and even surpassing 7B-scale baselines like Search-R1-7B-Instruct (33.7%). At 7B scale, InfoReasoner-7B reaches 39.1% average, beating AutoRefine-7B-Base (36.9%) and Search-R1-7B-Instruct (33.7%). On MATH500 (tool-integrated reasoning), InfoReasoner-7B scores 85.8% vs. SimpleTIR-7B's 84.6%, and on WebDetective (long-horizon deep search), InfoReasoner-3B achieves 30.0% Pass@1 vs. Search-R1-3B-Base's 26.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoReasoner-3B outperforms all 3B retrieval-augmented baselines: it beats Search-R1-3B-Instruct (30.1%) by +4.5 avg points, InForge-3B (27.8%) by +6.8, and AutoRefine-3B-Instruct (23.1%) by +11.5, with the largest gains on multi-hop benchmarks (MuSiQue: 32.4% vs. 5.6% for Search-R1). At 7B scale, InfoReasoner-7B (39.1%) surpasses AutoRefine-7B-Base (36.9%) by +2.2, Search-R1-7B-Instruct (33.7%) by +5.4, and ReasonRAG-7B (31.8%) by +7.3. On MATH500, InfoReasoner-7B (85.8%) edges out SimpleTIR-7B (84.6%) and substantially outperforms ZeroTIR-7B (80.2%). On WebDetective, InfoReasoner-3B (30.0% Pass@1) beats Search-R1-3B-Base (26.5%) and approaches GPT-5-Chat (29.5%) while using a 3B model. The ablation (λ=0.0, degenerating to Search-R1) drops to 29.9%, confirming the information gain reward contributes +4.7 points over pure outcome-based RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based agentic retrieval paradigm established by Search-R1 and Search-o1, which optimize retrieval policies using sparse outcome rewards (exact match) via GRPO. The key departure is replacing the sparse terminal reward with a dense, information-theoretic signal: a synthetic semantic information gain computed from the model's own output distributions over NLI-clustered semantic equivalence classes. Theoretically, it grounds this reward in a POMDP belief-state framework, proving non-negativity, telescoping additivity, and channel monotonicity properties that prior heuristic process-supervision approaches (e.g., ReasonRAG) lack. It also differs from concurrent work IGPO by operating in semantic class space rather than surface-form probability space, and from EMPO by being retrieval-specific with a gold-class anchor rather than general entropy minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, annotation-free dense reward for intermediate retrieval steps, addressing the credit-assignment bottleneck that limits RL-based retrieval optimization. The 66.7% reduction in retrieval turns at inference (matching Search-R1's peak in 2 turns vs. 6) has direct practical value for latency-sensitive deployments. However, the 1.9× training overhead from M=12 sampling plus pairwise NLI inference is a significant cost that limits scalability to larger models, and the reliance on a fine-tuned DeBERTa NLI model for semantic clustering introduces a quality bottleneck that may not generalize beyond short-answer QA. Open questions include whether the gold-class reward can be replaced with fully self-supervised alternatives and whether the framework extends to open-ended generation tasks where semantic equivalence is harder to define.
