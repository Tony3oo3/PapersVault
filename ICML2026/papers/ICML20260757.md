## Problem

Model-based offline RL can generate on-policy trajectories for value learning, but single-step dynamics models accumulate compounding errors through repeated inference on self-generated states. Geometric horizon models (GHM) mitigate this by directly predicting discounted future states, yet they are locked to a single geometric horizon distribution and cannot predict at a specified timestep, forcing them to model the difficult long-horizon tail. The paper asks whether a more flexible future-prediction framework can make model-based value expansion scalable to noisy datasets and long-horizon reasoning tasks.

## Input / Output

The UHM takes a continuous state observation (augmented with a terminal indicator), an action, and an integer horizon n sampled from a winsorized geometric distribution min(Geom(1−λγ), k_max), and outputs a sample from the n-step future state distribution via a flow-matching vector field. The critic consumes the predicted future state and a sampled action to produce a value estimate, while the actor (a deterministic Gaussian policy regularized by TD3+BC) outputs actions. The horizon n is not fixed but resampled at every gradient step, and the effective horizon grows linearly during training via λ scheduling.

## Method

The core contribution is the Universal Horizon Model (UHM), a generative model m^π(x|s,a,n) = Pr(s_n = x | s_0=s, a_0=a, π) that directly samples n-step future states for any n, unifying GHM (n ~ Geom(1−γ)) and single-step dynamics (n ~ δ(1)) under one framework. UHM is trained via recursive bootstrapping with flow-matching (coupled-CFM): the one-step case regresses to the dataset transition, while n>1 cases regress to a bootstrapped sample from the (n−1)-step model at the next state. For value learning, the paper introduces a ν-Bellman operator parameterized by a winsorized geometric measure that caps the maximum horizon at k_max, yielding importance-sampled TD(λ) targets that avoid the intractable long-tail while retaining convergence guarantees (Proposition 4.1). Practical stabilizations include λ scheduling (effective horizon grows linearly from 1 to 1/(1−λ_f γ)), behavior mixing (deploying a stochastically mixed policy π^mix = (1−β)π + βδ(a') with β=0.3 to limit distributional shift), and explicit terminal-state handling via absorbing augmented states. The full algorithm jointly trains the UHM vector field, a TD3+BC actor, a clipped-double critic, and a reward model in a single end-to-end update loop.

## Results

On 50 standard OGBench tasks, UHM achieves a 55±1% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline DTD(λ) (52±1%) by 3 percentage points and the best model-free baseline FQL (44%) by 11 points. On 25 noisy tasks with highly suboptimal datasets, UHM reaches 39±1% versus 38±1% for GHM and 32±1% for MBTD(λ). On 25 long-horizon reasoning tasks, UHM achieves 22±1%, a 69% relative improvement over DTD(λ) (13±3%) and 38% over GHM (16±2%). Overall, UHM delivers a 14% higher average success rate than the second-best method across all 100 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UHM (55% avg) outperforms all six external baselines on the 50 standard OGBench tasks. Among model-free methods, FQL (44%) is the strongest, followed by ReBRAC (31%) and IQL (23%); UHM exceeds FQL by 11 points, largely due to its ability to perform on-policy value learning via imagined rollouts. Among model-based methods, MAC (40%) is the best external baseline, benefiting from action chunking on manipulation tasks but failing on locomotion (e.g., 0% on antmaze-giant); UHM surpasses MAC by 15 points on average. MOPO (4%) and MOBILE (10%) perform poorly across nearly all tasks, as their uncertainty-based reward penalties severely hinder value learning in sparse-reward settings. In the controlled ablation comparisons (same protocol, only α tuned), UHM beats DTD(λ) (52%), GHM (48%), and MBTD(λ) (45%), confirming that direct n-step future prediction with winsorized horizons is more effective than both dataset-trajectory TD(λ) and single-step model rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes geometric horizon models (GHM; Janner et al., 2020), which predict states from a fixed geometrically-discounted future, into universal horizon models (UHM) that directly sample n-step future states for any arbitrary horizon n. The generative backbone follows coupled-CFM (Farebrother et al., 2025) for flow-matching-based state prediction, and the value learning framework unifies n-step TD and TD(λ) under a single ν-Bellman operator. The key departure from GHM is the introduction of a winsorized horizon distribution that caps the maximum prediction horizon, addressing the difficulty of accurately modeling the long geometric tail that plagues GHM in offline settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, theoretically grounded recipe for offline model-based value expansion that avoids the compounding errors of repeated single-step inference while remaining more flexible than GHM. The 14% average improvement over the strongest baseline across 100 OGBench tasks, particularly on noisy and long-horizon tasks, demonstrates that horizon flexibility and winsorization are meaningful levers for offline RL scalability. Open questions remain around data scarcity (UHM extrapolates beyond data support), model capacity for very long horizons, and extension to high-dimensional visual observations with action chunks, which the authors identify as key limitations.
