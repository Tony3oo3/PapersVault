## Problem

Long-horizon motion prediction under external commands is hindered by latent decision uncertainty: the internal cognitive states governing future behavior are unobservable and evolve stochastically over time. Existing discriminative and generative models treat commands as static conditioning variables rather than time-varying causal interventions on latent dynamics, and they lack explicit modeling of intrinsic stochasticity in decision processes, causing errors to compound and trajectories to drift from commanded behavior over extended horizons.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 30-frame historical 2D keypoint sequence (8 keypoints × 2D coordinates for rat; 22 joints × 3D for BABEL) together with a temporally aligned discrete command sequence spanning both history and the 60-frame prediction horizon. Commands are sparse, step-wise categorical signals (e.g., forward/left/right/none via BCI stimulation at 100–300 µA for ~100 ms each), encoded into 7-dim (rat) or 13-dim (BABEL) continuous control vectors. The output is a stochastic sample of future motion (K=20 trajectories), from which best-of-K and distribution-level metrics are computed.

## Method

CogSDE models unobserved decision states as a 64-dim latent process governed by a controlled SDE whose drift incorporates a dual-channel command modulation: a level channel (B_lvl(Z_t)·c_t) captures sustained command persistence and an event channel (B_evt(Z_t)·ċ_t) responds to abrupt command changes, both parameterized by a shared regime-based mixture (S=3 experts). The diffusion term is a state-dependent diagonal operator with regime-weighted scales, allowing uncertainty to adapt to the current latent state. The latent rollout is discretized via Euler-Maruyama (Δt=0.033) and conditions a MoFlow flow-matching decoder through FiLM modulation to generate future poses. A one-sided dissipativity assumption yields a mean-square boundedness theorem (E|Z_t|² ≤ C₁e^{-2αt} + C₂), and a soft boundedness regularizer (L_bnd) is added during training to encourage the learned dynamics to satisfy the dissipativity condition.

## Results

On the Rat Locomotion dataset, CogSDE achieves the lowest minimum ADE across all prediction horizons (10–60 frames), reaching 3.79 at horizon 60 compared to 3.89 (SLD-HMP), 6.34 (MoFlow), 9.57 (TimeXer), and 12.29 (DiffSTG). On BABEL, CogSDE similarly leads at every horizon, achieving 0.018 ADEmin at horizon 60 versus 0.023 (SLD-HMP), 0.025 (MoFlow), 0.054 (TimeXer), and 0.280 (DiffSTG). Performance gaps widen substantially as the horizon increases, with CogSDE's advantage over the nearest baseline (SLD-HMP) growing from ~0.03 at horizon 10 to ~0.10 at horizon 60 on Rat Locomotion. On the distribution-level metric (average ADE), CogSDE also achieves the lowest error at all horizons on both datasets (e.g., 10.42 vs. 11.18 for SLD-HMP at horizon 60 on Rat Locomotion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogSDE outperforms all four baselines on every horizon of both datasets. Against the closest competitor SLD-HMP (a command-aware latent-direction model), CogSDE reduces ADEmin from 3.89 to 3.79 at horizon 60 on Rat Locomotion and from 0.023 to 0.018 on BABEL, with the gap widening at longer horizons (e.g., 2.98 vs. 3.15 at horizon 50 on Rat). Against the flow-based MoFlow baseline, CogSDE achieves substantially lower errors at longer horizons (3.79 vs. 6.34 at horizon 60 on Rat; 0.018 vs. 0.025 on BABEL), indicating that explicit command-modulated drift is more effective than unconditioned flow dynamics for long-horizon prediction. The deterministic TimeXer and diffusion-based DiffSTG baselines are far behind, with DiffSTG particularly struggling (12.29 ADEmin at horizon 60 on Rat), likely because it lacks any command-conditioning mechanism. On the distribution-level average ADE metric, CogSDE also leads all baselines, suggesting that the controlled SDE produces more consistently accurate samples rather than merely a single good trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogSDE extends the latent dynamical systems line (Neural SDEs, controlled ODEs) by embedding external commands as time-varying drift modulators rather than static conditioning variables, and couples this with a flow-matching decoder (MoFlow) for motion generation. It departs from prior command-driven motion predictors (SLD-HMP, DiffSTG) that treat instructions as fixed context or token-level biases, instead using a dual-channel (level + event) regime-mixture drift to capture both sustained and abrupt command effects. The theoretical contribution draws on standard one-sided dissipativity conditions from control theory (Müller et al., 2014) to derive a mean-square boundedness guarantee for the controlled latent SDE, a result not previously applied to neural motion-generation SDEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled continuous-time mechanism for modeling how sparse, time-varying biological commands causally reshape latent decision dynamics over long horizons, with a provable stability bound that constrains latent energy growth. The rat locomotion dataset with invasive BCI commands provides a biologically grounded testbed that is difficult to replicate, though the small cohort (3 rats, 36 trials) limits generalizability claims. Key open questions include whether the dissipativity-based boundedness extends to fully discrete-time rollouts with finite step sizes, how the framework scales to higher-dimensional observations (e.g., video), and whether cross-subject generalization improves with larger animal cohorts.
