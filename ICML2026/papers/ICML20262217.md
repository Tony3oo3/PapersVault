## Problem

It is folklore that reusing training data improves statistical efficiency of gradient-based learning, but this benefit is poorly understood in nonlinear, non-convex settings. For single-index models with quadratic activation (information exponent 2), one-pass SGD provably requires n ≳ d log d samples for weak recovery, while the information-theoretic limit is n ≳ d. This paper asks whether standard full-batch gradient descent (reusing all data every step) can close this log d gap, and if so, at what iteration cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ~ N(0, I_d) and y_i = σ(⟨x_i, θ*⟩) where σ is a quadratic or truncated-quadratic link function and ||θ*|| = 1. The output is a direction estimate θ̂ whose quality is measured by normalized overlap |⟨θ̂, θ*⟩| (weak recovery: overlap bounded away from 0) or ℓ₂ distance min_{s∈{±1}} ||θ̂ − sθ*|| (strong recovery: distance → 0). All guarantees are stated in the proportional regime where n/d → δ > 0, with the key threshold being whether δ is a constant or must grow as log d.

## Method

The paper establishes a sample complexity separation between full-batch GD and one-pass SGD via three results. First, for the unbounded quadratic activation σ(z)=z², full-batch spherical gradient flow on the correlation loss is a power iteration on a fixed matrix A*, whose top eigenvector is asymptotically uncorrelated with θ* when n = o(d log d), so no statistical gain over one-pass SGD is possible. Second, by truncating the activation (σ(z) = z² for |z|² ≤ M, constant beyond), the ODE becomes a power iteration on a time-varying matrix A(θ(t)); the key technical contribution is proving a uniform-in-θ BBP spectral transition (top eigenvalue ≈ 6, second ≈ 2, top eigenvector aligned with θ*) along the entire trajectory, then invoking the stable manifold theorem to show the flow almost surely converges to the principal eigenvector, yielding weak recovery at n ≳ d. Third, for Euclidean GD on the squared loss from small initialization (r₀ = d^{-15}), a two-phase trajectory analysis—initial power-iteration-like search (T ≳ log d/η steps to align direction and grow norm) followed by geometric refinement via one-point strong convexity—gives the first strong recovery and convergence rate guarantee for plain full-batch GD in the proportional n/d regime.

## Results

The paper establishes a sample complexity separation between full-batch GD and one-pass SGD for single-index learning with information exponent 2. For the unbounded quadratic activation σ(z)=z², full-batch spherical gradient flow on the correlation loss still requires n ≳ d log d samples (Theorem 3.1), matching the one-pass SGD lower bound. With a truncated quadratic activation, full-batch spherical GD achieves weak recovery at n ≥ CM⁴d samples (Theorem 3.2), removing the log d factor. Furthermore, full-batch Euclidean GD on the squared loss from small initialization achieves strong (exact) recovery with n ≥ CM⁴d samples in T ≤ C log d / η gradient steps (Theorem 4.1), with geometric error decay ‖θ_t − θ*‖² ≤ C(1−ηα)^{t−t̄}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between full-batch GD (reusing all data every step) and one-pass SGD (using each data point once) in the Gaussian single-index model with information exponent 2. One-pass SGD provably requires n ≳ d log d samples for weak recovery (Ben Arous et al. 2021, Theorem 1.4), a lower bound that applies to all even activations including the quadratic. The paper's negative result (Theorem 3.1) shows that full-batch spherical GD with the unbounded quadratic activation σ(z)=z² also fails below n ≳ d log d, meaning data reuse alone does not help in this setting. The positive result (Theorem 3.2) demonstrates that a minimal modification—truncating the quadratic activation—enables full-batch spherical GD to achieve weak recovery at the information-theoretically optimal n ≳ d, while one-pass SGD with the same truncated link still requires n ≳ d log d. The strong recovery guarantee (Theorem 4.1) further shows that full-batch Euclidean GD on the squared loss achieves exact recovery in O(log d) steps at n ≳ d, a regime where prior full-batch GD analyses (Netrapalli et al. 2013; Candes et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2019) still require n ≳ d polylog d. Thus, the statistical separation from one-pass SGD is achieved not by data reuse per se, but by the combination of data reuse with a bounded (truncated) activation that creates a benign BBP-type spectral landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-index learning literature (Ben Arous et al., 2021; Damian et al., 2023) by asking whether full-batch GD can remove the log d sample complexity barrier that one-pass SGD faces for information-exponent-2 activations. It departs from the loss-modification mechanism identified by Dandi et al. (2024b) and Lee et al. (2024), showing that for even activations this mechanism is insufficient and that a fundamentally different analysis of multi-pass dynamics is needed. The proof techniques draw on the BBP phase transition framework from random matrix theory (Baik et al., 2005; Lu & Li, 2020), the stable manifold theorem approach of Panageas & Piliouras (2017), and the small-initialization power-iteration analysis of Stöger & Soltanolkotabi (2021), but apply them in a new uniform-in-θ setting for time-varying matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous demonstration that full-batch GD can achieve information-theoretically optimal linear-in-d sample complexity for a non-convex feature learning problem with information exponent 2, closing a gap that has persisted since the one-pass lower bound of Ben Arous et al. (2021). The negative result for the unbounded quadratic activation is equally important: it shows the benefit of data reuse is not automatic and requires a structural property (boundedness/truncation) of the activation. Open questions include computing sharp phase-transition constants via dynamical mean-field theory, extending the analysis to higher information exponents and multi-index models, and characterizing the runtime of the spherical dynamics (which the current landscape analysis cannot bound).
