## Problem

Existing training-free coverless image steganography (CIS) methods such as CRoSS and DiffStega are designed for single-image concealment and lack robust access control, making it impossible to selectively reveal different hidden contents to different authorized users. Naive concatenation-based extensions to multi-image hiding produce visually fragmented stego images with visible boundaries due to residual structural information in noisy latents, and prior multi-image CIS approaches either require expensive training or demand transmission of secret-related side information.

## Input / Output

The sender provides N secret RGB images (each downsampled by spatial factors N₁ and N₂ such that N₁·N₂ = N so that concatenated latents match the 4×64×64 base latent dimension of Stable Diffusion v1.5), N private keys (random seeds), a shared public key (random seed), and a public text prompt. The output is a single 512×512 stego image that is visually indistinguishable from a natural image; each authorized user holding their private key recovers only their designated secret image, while any other user's reconstruction attempt yields a meaningless, heavily degraded output. No auxiliary secret-related information (e.g., control images, private prompts) needs to be transmitted beyond the stego image itself.

## Method

MIDAS is a training-free pipeline built on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent diffusion model with DDIM sampling and EDICT exact inversion. The core encryption mechanism, Random Basis, constructs a seed-dependent orthonormal matrix Q_d(K, γ) via QR decomposition and row shuffling, where only a fraction γ of the latent elements are transformed—this modifies actual latent values rather than merely flipping signs (as in Noise Flip), more effectively destroying residual spatial structure. After individually encrypting each secret image's noisy latent with its private key, the sender concatenates them and applies a Latent Vector Fusion step: a public-key Random Basis transformation M_D shuffles the concatenated spatial components to eliminate concatenation boundaries, and the result is mixed with a deterministically generated reference image latent via z_pub = √α·M_D·z_prot + √(1−α)·z_ref (α = 0.95), which restores visual coherence. A theoretical information-leakage bound (Theorem 3.1) shows the per-pixel leakage rate scales as O((−log Δ + log m)/m + (1−γ)(−log Δ + 1)), confirming that even moderate γ (e.g., 0.4) provides strong security for typical image dimensions.

## Results

On Stego260 with N=2 (the default setting), MIDAS achieves the best stego image quality (MANIQA 0.434) and diversity (CLIP Score 30.129, PSNR 9.885 with secret images) among all training-free CIS methods, while maintaining a correct-key reconstruction PSNR of 23.903 dB. Access control is validated by a large gap between correct-key (PSNR 23.903, SSIM 0.771) and wrong-key (PSNR 9.964, SSIM 0.090) reconstruction, far exceeding the gap in DiffStega* (21.908 vs 18.137) and CRoSS* (17.606 vs 15.270). Under N=4, MIDAS scales well, maintaining MANIQA 0.479 and CLIP Score 30.169 while wrong-key PSNR drops further to 9.399 dB. Under practical degradations (Gaussian noise σ=5, JPEG Q=70, Gaussian blur), MIDAS retains ~20 dB reconstruction PSNR, whereas modification-based IIS and AIS drop to 10–14 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRoSS*, MIDAS wins on every stego diversity metric (PSNR 9.885 vs 15.550, SSIM 0.287 vs 0.521, LPIPS 0.752 vs 0.579, CLIP 30.129 vs 26.071), stego quality (MANIQA 0.434 vs 0.406), correct-key reconstruction (PSNR 23.903 vs 17.606), and access control (wrong-key PSNR 9.964 vs 15.270, SSIM 0.090 vs 0.470). Against DiffStega*, MIDAS similarly dominates on diversity (PSNR 9.885 vs 17.065, CLIP 30.129 vs 26.952), quality (MANIQA 0.434 vs 0.399), correct reconstruction (PSNR 23.903 vs 21.908), and access control (wrong-key PSNR 9.964 vs 18.137, SSIM 0.090 vs 0.587), indicating that DiffStega* fails to enforce access control since wrong-key reconstructions remain meaningful. Against modification-based IIS and AIS, MIDAS trades off correct reconstruction fidelity (PSNR 23.903 vs 41.360/30.724) for fundamental security advantages: steganalysis detection accuracy is ~20% lower for MIDAS, and robustness to channel noise is dramatically better (MIDAS ~20 dB vs IIS/AIS 10–14 dB under Gaussian noise). Against HIS* (a simplified training-based variant), MIDAS achieves substantially higher stego diversity (PSNR 9.885 vs 22.712 at N=2) and better stego quality (MANIQA 0.434 vs 0.394) while remaining training-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIDAS builds directly on the training-free coverless steganography line initiated by CRoSS (DDIM inversion with private/public prompts) and DiffStega (Noise Flip encryption on noisy latents), extending both from single-image to multi-image hiding. It departs from DiffStega's Noise Flip mechanism by introducing a Random Basis (seeded orthonormal transformation) that more effectively scrambles residual spatial structure, and from ControlNet-based conditioning by omitting control images entirely in favor of a Latent Vector Fusion step that blends the encrypted latent with a publicly reproducible reference. The access-control formulation inherits from modification-based multi-image steganography (Kweon et al., IIS, AIS) but relocates it into a coverless, training-free diffusion pipeline, eliminating the steganalysis vulnerability inherent in cover modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free coverless framework that simultaneously supports multi-image hiding and per-user access control without transmitting any secret-related side information, addressing a practical gap for scalable multi-user secure communication. The theoretical information-leakage bound for the Random Basis mechanism provides a principled security guarantee that prior empirical CIS methods lacked. Key limitations include the ~30 s inference latency of the diffusion pipeline (comparable to baselines but still slow for real-time use), reliance on a specific pre-trained checkpoint (Stable Diffusion v1.5), and the fact that the security analysis assumes a quantized latent model that may not fully capture adversarial side-channel attacks. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to video or 3D content, whether sampling efficiency can be improved without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity, and how the access-control guarantees hold under active adversarial manipulation of the stego image beyond simple noise/blur.
