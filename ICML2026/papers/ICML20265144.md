## Problem

Existing offline multi-agent skill discovery methods rely on fixed-size skill libraries, which cannot accommodate the exponentially growing coordination skill space when tasks arrive sequentially in open-ended environments. This leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills and plasticity loss for new ones, as agents cannot simultaneously memorize diverse coordination patterns and adapt to emerging task-specific behaviors. The paper formalizes this as a continual offline multi-agent skill discovery problem over an infinite stream of Dec-POMDPs and asks how to partition and selectively reuse skills to balance stability and plasticity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of offline multi-agent datasets (2000 trajectories each), where the agent team size and task semantics vary across tasks. Each agent's input is its local trajectory (a sequence of observations and actions), while the global encoder additionally receives the full state-action pair for skill discovery. The output is a set of task-specific skill-conditioned policies (one per multi-head module), continuous skill embeddings per agent, and per-state reusability scores that determine which previously learned skill heads to activate for a new task. The multi-head structure means the policy output is a geometric mixture over all active heads, weighted by reusability scores computed from NCE-based state density estimates.

## Method

COMAD trains in two stages per incoming task: first, a VAE-based skill encoder (global encoder q_m plus local encoder p_m) discovers task-specific coordination skills from the offline dataset via an advantage-weighted policy loss and KL distillation; second, a skill-augmented policy loss reuses previously acquired skills by augmenting the advantage function with reusability-weighted log-probabilities from all prior task heads. The core architectural innovation is a multi-head module with a shared transformer+GRU feature extractor and task-specific output heads for action decoding, skill prior, and state density estimation, which prevents modal collapse when the action distribution is multi-modal. Reusability scores Δ_m^k(s) are computed via a noise-contrastive-estimation state density estimator, and a new head is allocated only when the expected state density of all existing heads falls below a threshold d_0. Theoretically, Theorem 4.1 proves that the optimal continual policy is a geometric mixture of single-task optimal policies weighted by Lagrange multipliers and state densities, and COMAD's augmented advantage function approximates this optimum, with the multi-head architecture serving as a practical surrogate for the intractable Lagrange multiplier computation.

## Results

COMAD achieves an overall average performance of 64.34 across 9 task streams spanning 5 environments (LBF, CN, SMAC, SMACv2, MAMuJoCo), significantly outperforming the strongest baseline OWL (59.84) and all other methods. On expert datasets, COMAD reaches 99.26 on LBF, 94.05 on Marines, and 78.49 on CN, while skill-discovery baselines ODIS-FT (18.46) and HiSSD-FT (19.37) suffer severe plasticity loss and fail to learn feasible policies on most task streams. COMAD also demonstrates strong forward and backward transfer, with ablation studies confirming that skill partitioning via multi-head architecture and reusability estimation are the primary drivers of performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMAD outperforms all four dedicated baselines on the overall metric: it beats OWL (59.84) by 4.5 points, EWC (28.44) by 35.9 points, HiSSD-FT (19.37) by 44.9 points, and ODIS-FT (18.46) by 45.9 points. Against OWL, the closest competitor, COMAD wins on most task streams (LBF Expert: 99.26 vs 98.65; Marines Expert: 94.05 vs 92.52; CN Expert: 78.49 vs 64.59) but loses on Stalker-Zealot (72.20 vs 77.49) where OWL's parameter isolation is more effective. The skill-discovery baselines ODIS-FT and HiSSD-FT perform catastrophically on most streams (often below 20), confirming that fixed-size skill libraries cannot handle the expanding skill space in continual settings. EWC, relying on Fisher-information regularization, also fails to prevent interference in the high-dimensional multi-agent joint space, scoring below 30 on nearly all task streams. Notably, on MAMuJoCo Dynamics Expert, the non-continual multitask baseline MT (53.14) outperforms COMAD (44.54), suggesting that extreme physical dynamics shifts remain challenging even for the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMAD sits at the intersection of three research threads: offline MARL (building on OMIGA's behavior-regularized critic and IQL's implicit Q-learning), multi-agent skill discovery (extending ODIS and HiSSD's VAE-based skill extraction), and continual RL (adopting OWL's multi-head parameter isolation). The key departure is that prior skill discovery methods assume a fixed-size skill library, while prior continual MARL methods (MACPro, RPG) operate online or rely on pre-trained models. COMAD unifies these by formalizing continual offline multi-agent skill discovery as a constrained optimization over an infinite Dec-POMDP stream, where a multi-head architecture partitions skills and an NCE-based density estimator gates their reuse, with Theorem 4.1 showing the resulting augmented advantage approximates the optimal constrained policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying MARL: the exponential growth of the coordination skill space as tasks arrive sequentially, which fixed-library methods cannot handle. The theoretical result (geometric mixture of single-task optimal policies weighted by Lagrange multipliers and state densities) provides a principled justification for the reusability mechanism rather than a heuristic. However, the authors acknowledge that relying on state density to approximate skill gating is insufficient for complex real-world scenarios requiring fine-grained semantic reusability, and suggest integrating LLMs or human feedback as a future direction. The approach is also limited to task-bounded settings where task identity is known at inference time, which constrains applicability to truly open-ended deployment.
