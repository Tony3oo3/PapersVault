## Problem

The optimal accuracy-fairness Pareto frontier in multi-class fair classification has not been explicitly characterized, and existing bias-mitigation techniques are largely confined to binary settings or rely on surrogate fairness metrics that introduce an unavoidable gap. This paper addresses both the theoretical gap (no closed-form Bayes-optimal fair classifier for multi-class group fairness) and the algorithmic gap (no unified framework that approximates the frontier across in-processing and post-processing phases under multiple fairness criteria simultaneously).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265824/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature vectors (tabular datasets: Adult, ENEM, ACSIncome) or face images (CelebA via ResNet) together with discrete sensitive group attributes during training, but operates in an attribute-blind manner at inference time—no sensitive attribute is required at test time. The output is a probability vector on the m-dimensional simplex (a randomized classifier), which can be converted to a deterministic label via argmax. The fairness level ξ is a user-specified hyperparameter that directly controls the position on the accuracy-fairness Pareto frontier.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form characterization of the Bayes-optimal fair classifier via Lagrangian duality, showing that under an entropic regularizer the optimal classifier takes a softmax form h_i(x) = exp(β_i^λ(x)/τ) / Σ_j exp(β_j^λ(x)/τ), where β^λ(x) is a linear combination of group-conditioned Bayes scores weighted by dual parameters λ. For in-processing, the saddle-point problem is reduced to a sequence of cost-sensitive classification subproblems using a calibrated cross-entropy loss ℓ_cal, solved by alternating gradient updates on model parameters θ and proximal updates on λ. For post-processing, the problem is reformulated as a convex L-smooth optimization over λ solved by proximal gradient descent, with plug-in estimators for the conditional probabilities P(A|X,Y) and P(Y|X). Both algorithms are proven to converge to the optimal accuracy-fairness Pareto frontier with explicit generalization bounds depending on sample size, model complexity (pseudo-dimension), and auxiliary model error.

## Results

The paper presents its main results as accuracy–fairness Pareto frontier curves (Figure 1) across four datasets (Adult, ENEM, ACSIncome, CelebA) under three fairness criteria (DP, EOP, EO), rather than as a single numerical table. OptFair achieves a more favorable trade-off than all baselines, with the advantage being most pronounced in the in-processing setting where the method offers greater controllability over the fairness level ξ. The experiments confirm the intrinsic accuracy–fairness trade-off: imposing fairness constraints generally reduces accuracy, though in some cases (e.g., EO on Adult with in-processing) accuracy actually improves due to reduction of inherent bias. The ablation study (Figure 2) shows that sequentially applying in-processing followed by post-processing yields performance intermediate to the two individual approaches, without further gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the in-processing comparison, OptFair-in dominates all four baselines (AdvDebias, Weight-ERM, FairBatch, F-divergence) across all four datasets and fairness criteria, achieving a Pareto frontier closer to the upper-left corner (higher accuracy at the same fairness level, or lower disparity at the same accuracy). The advantage is described as 'substantial' and stems from the method's theoretical guarantee of approximating the optimal accuracy–fairness trade-off, whereas baselines rely on surrogate fairness metrics that introduce a gap. In the post-processing comparison, OptFair-post outperforms FairProjection, LinearPost, and FRAPPE, with the gap being smaller than in the in-processing setting. The paper notes that post-processing methods are generally less effective than in-processing on these datasets, and that combining both approaches (ablation) does not yield further improvements over either method alone, as in-processing targets representation-level bias while post-processing only calibrates output distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary reduction framework of Agarwal et al. (2018) to the multi-class setting by formulating the inner optimization as a cost-sensitive cross-entropy loss, and unifies it with a post-processing plug-in estimator under a single theoretical umbrella. It departs from prior multi-class fairness work (Alghamdi et al. 2022, Xian et al. 2023, Denis et al. 2024) by providing an analytically tractable characterization of the Bayes-optimal fair classifier via an entropic regularizer borrowed from optimal transport, rather than relying on continuity assumptions or single-criterion formulations. The in-processing and post-processing algorithms are developed in parallel from the same dual characterization, addressing a gap where prior methods treated these phases in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first explicit, provably attainable characterization of the multi-class accuracy-fairness Pareto frontier, giving practitioners a principled way to tune the trade-off via a single fairness-level parameter ξ across DP, EOP, and EO simultaneously. The attribute-blind design is practically important for deployment settings where sensitive attributes are unavailable at inference. Limitations include coverage of only group-level fairness (no individual or intersectional fairness), reliance on auxiliary models for post-processing that introduce estimation error, and the ablation showing that combining in- and post-processing yields no compounding benefit—suggesting the two phases address largely orthogonal sources of bias. Open questions include extending the framework to richer fairness notions (e.g., multi-group intersectional constraints) and to settings where the entropic regularizer's approximation gap becomes non-negligible.
