## Problem

Existing CLIP-based continual learning methods freeze the backbone to preserve pre-trained alignment, which limits the model's ability to learn discriminative features for new classes, especially under long task sequences. Additionally, standard Euclidean embedding space becomes progressively crowded as the class space expands across tasks, making it difficult to maintain separation between old and new classes. The paper addresses both the adaptation strategy and the geometry of the embedding space by enabling controlled hyperbolic adaptation of CLIP.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and class-name text prompts as input, operating in an exemplar-free setting where no historical samples are stored after each task. At inference, the model produces a class label by fusing two logit sources: hyperbolic visual-text distance logits (computed between adapted image features and frozen CLIP text embeddings in the Poincaré ball) and visual-prototype logits (obtained via Gaussian Discriminant Analysis over per-class mean features), combined with a learned fusion weight η. The visual prototype space is dataset-dependent: frozen backbone features for broad-distribution datasets and adapted backbone features for fine-grained datasets.

## Method

HypCL freezes both CLIP encoders and attaches lightweight banded adapters (bandwidth b) to the query and value projections of the visual encoder's attention blocks, with each task learning an independent adapter that is frozen after training. Instead of linearly summing task updates in Euclidean space, HypCL maps the frozen projection and each task's adapter update into the Poincaré ball and composes them sequentially via Möbius addition, yielding a nonlinear geometric composition that reduces interference between tasks. For classification, adapted image features and frozen text embeddings are mapped into a Poincaré ball and scored by negative geodesic distance, aligning the prediction objective with the hyperbolic geometry. At inference, these hyperbolic visual-text logits are fused with visual-prototype logits (GDA over per-class mean features) to provide complementary calibration, with the fusion weight η balancing the two sources.

## Results

HypCL achieves the best average and final accuracy in 17 out of 18 benchmark settings across nine datasets. On CIFAR-100 (B0 Inc10), it reaches 89.09% average and 82.95% final accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline Engine by +2.17% and +3.73% respectively. Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained and domain-shifted datasets: +1.65% average on Aircraft, +2.76% on ImageNet-R, and +3.20% on ObjectNet over Engine. The only setting where HypCL trails is UCF-101 (B0 Inc10), where Engine edges it by 0.36% in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HypCL outperforms all prompt-based baselines (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) and the lightweight adapter method RAPF by large margins across every dataset, with gaps exceeding 10% on fine-grained benchmarks like Aircraft and CUB. The closest competitor is Engine (Zhou et al., 2025a), a CLIP-based method that also freezes the backbone and adapts lightweight components; HypCL surpasses Engine in 17 of 18 settings, with the largest margins on ObjectNet (+3.20% avg, +6.88% final) and ImageNet-R (+2.76% avg, +3.30% final), while Engine narrowly wins on UCF-101 B0 Inc10 by 0.36% average accuracy. Against exemplar-based methods (iCaRL, MEMO, PROOF) that store 20 samples per class, HypCL achieves substantially higher accuracy without any stored exemplars—e.g., 71.34% vs. 61.00% average on Aircraft and 85.85% vs. 83.57% on SUN. SimpleCIL, which uses a simple fine-tuning strategy with pre-trained models, is competitive on broad datasets (Cars, Food) but falls well behind HypCL on fine-grained and domain-shifted benchmarks. Zero-shot CLIP serves as a strong reference on broad datasets (e.g., 81.81% on CIFAR-100) but degrades significantly on fine-grained categories (26.66% on Aircraft), highlighting the need for task-specific adaptation that HypCL provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

HypCL sits at the intersection of two research threads: CLIP-based continual learning (which typically freezes the backbone and adapts lightweight prompts or adapters, e.g., CoOp, RAPF, ENGINE) and hyperbolic representation learning (Poincaré embeddings, hyperbolic image-text alignment). The paper directly adapts the hyperbolic adapter formulation from HyperET (Peng et al., 2025a) to the continual learning setting, replacing Euclidean addition of task-specific adapter matrices with sequential Möbius composition in the Poincaré ball. Its key conceptual departure from prior CLIP-CL work is treating the frozen CLIP projection as a geometric anchor in hyperbolic space rather than a fixed Euclidean weight, and complementing hyperbolic visual-text distance logits with visual-prototype calibration via GDA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric rationale—exponential volume growth in hyperbolic space naturally accommodates an expanding class space without compressing prior representations—which is more principled than ad-hoc regularization or replay strategies. The exemplar-free setting and consistent gains across nine diverse benchmarks (including fine-grained and domain-shifted datasets) make the approach practically attractive for deployment scenarios where storing historical data is infeasible. Open questions include whether the curvature parameter c should be task- or dataset-adaptive, whether the approach extends to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H) or other VLMs, and how the banded adapter constraint interacts with the hyperbolic composition at scale. The paper does not explore open-set or domain-incremental variants, limiting the generality of the evaluation.
