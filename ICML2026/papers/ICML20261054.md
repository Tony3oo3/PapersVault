## Problem

Existing VLA models face a precision gap when mapping high-dimensional observations to low-level actions, and a data heterogeneity gap when transferring across diverse robot embodiments and actionless human demonstrations. Prior latent representation approaches encode either visual dynamics or robotic actions in isolation, failing to capture the cross-modal alignment needed for embodiment-agnostic control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261054/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each inference step, the policy receives K multi-view RGB images, a natural-language instruction, and proprioceptive states (joint positions and gripper commands), and outputs a 50-step action chunk of joint positions and gripper commands. The system operates at ~5 Hz action-chunk frequency with ~200 Hz action-level rate on a single RTX 4090. The main XR-1 model uses a PaliGemma backbone (SigLIP 400M visual encoder + Gemma 2.6B transformer) with a flow-matching action head, while the lightweight XR-1-Light variant uses Florence-2 (230M) within the SwitchVLA framework.

## Method

The core contribution is Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC), a discrete latent representation learned via a dual-branch VQ-VAE that jointly encodes visual dynamics (SigLIP + 4-layer ViT dynamic module predicting future frames) and robotic motion (1D causal strided convolutions + 8-layer transformer encoding action sequences) into a shared 256-entry, 256-dim codebook with 13 codes per modality. A cross-modal alignment loss (KL divergence between codebook posteriors) regularizes visual codes to stay consistent with their motion counterparts, suppressing task-irrelevant visual noise. Training follows three stages: (i) self-supervised UVMC pretraining on ~1.26M episodes from Open-X, RoboMIND, Ego4D, and XR-D; (ii) UVMC-guided VLA pretraining where learnable [ZMO] and [ZVIS] tokens in the VLM predict the quantized codes alongside the action head; (iii) task-specific post-training on target embodiment data. The framework is model-agnostic, demonstrated on both the full PaliGemma-based XR-1 and the compact Florence-2-based XR-1-Light.

## Results

Across six robot embodiments and 120+ manipulation tasks (14,000+ rollouts), XR-1 consistently outperforms all baselines. On Tien Kung 2.0 (an unseen embodiment during pretraining), XR-1 achieves 72.0% average success rate versus 41.0% for π₀.₅, 40.8% for π₀, 38.0% for GR00T-N1.5, 17.8% for UniVLA, and 17.0% for RDT. On Dual-Arm Franka, XR-1 reaches 73.5% vs. 41.0% (π₀.₅) and 37.3% (π₀); on AgileX Cobot Magic V2.0, it achieves 60.0% vs. 41.3% (π₀.₅) and 32.5% (π₀). In generalization tests (novel objects, distractors, illumination changes), XR-1 also surpasses π₀ by 20–50 percentage points across all OOD conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XR-1 wins against all five baselines on every evaluated embodiment, with the largest margins over UniVLA (30–50+ points) and RDT (30–40+ points), which struggle with multi-task optimization and collapse to 0% on many harder tasks. Against the strongest baselines π₀ and π₀.₅, XR-1 maintains a consistent 20–30 point advantage (e.g., 72.0% vs. 41.0% on Tien Kung 2.0; 73.5% vs. 41.0% on Dual-Arm Franka). GR00T-N1.5 is the closest competitor on some individual tasks (e.g., TK2-Press Button at 85% vs. 90%) but still trails XR-1 by ~34 points in average success on Tien Kung 2.0. Notably, Tien Kung 2.0 was entirely unseen during XR-1's pretraining, making the 72.0% result a strong cross-embodiment transfer benchmark. In the fast-adaptation setting (15 novel tasks, 20 demos each), XR-1 also outperforms per-task baselines ACT and Diffusion Policy, confirming that UVMC supervision enables effective few-shot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

XR-1 builds directly on the π₀ architecture (PaliGemma backbone with flow-matching action head) and the VQ-VAE framework for discrete representation learning, but departs from both unimodal latent-action approaches (BeT, QueST, Moto) and unimodal visual-dynamics approaches (C-BeT, VPP, GR-2, GO-1) by jointly encoding visual dynamics and robotic motion into a single shared discrete codebook via a dual-branch VQ-VAE. The three-stage training paradigm (self-supervised UVMC learning → UVMC-guided cross-embodiment pretraining → task-specific post-training) extends the standard two-stage VLA pipeline by inserting a representation-learning stage that can exploit actionless human video data (Ego4D). The lightweight variant XR-1-Light repurposes the SwitchVLA/Florence-2 architecture, demonstrating the framework's model-agnostic design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UVMC framework offers a practical path to leveraging the vast pool of unlabeled human demonstration video for robot learning, which is a significant data-efficiency gain over action-centric methods that require expensive labeled rollouts. The model-agnostic design and the demonstrated scaling law (monotonic improvement from 1% to 100% pretraining data) suggest the approach can be integrated into diverse VLA backbones as the field evolves. However, high-precision manipulation remains challenging, the compute cost is substantial (~77K GPU hours for Stages 1–2), and it is unclear whether the KL-based cross-modal alignment loss is the optimal mechanism for enforcing vision-motion consistency or whether the gains would hold on even more morphologically diverse embodiments (e.g., humanoids with full-body control).
