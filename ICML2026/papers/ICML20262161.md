## Problem

Global rotation-based PTQ methods (e.g., SpinQuant) achieve efficient inference by fusing a single shared rotation into weights, but their limited expressivity prevents handling layer-specific outlier patterns. Layer-wise transformation methods improve accuracy by assigning unique rotations per layer, but they cannot fuse activation rotations into preceding weights, forcing expensive online O(D²) computations. The paper addresses how to retain the full expressivity of layer-wise rotations while recovering the near-zero inference overhead of global rotation methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-2 7B/13B, LLaMA-3 8B, LLaMA-3.2 1B/3B) and 800 random WikiText-2 calibration segments. During the offline calibration phase, it optimizes full-size D×D orthogonal rotation matrices per layer (O(L·D²) parameters, ~1091M for LLaMA-3 8B) via the Cayley optimizer. At inference, the output is a quantized model (W4A4 or W3A3) where all rotations are absorbed into weight matrices, leaving only a rank-r (r=32) subspace residual correction online (~8.4M parameters, ~0.2% MAC overhead).

## Method

ReSpinQuant assigns distinct learnable orthogonal matrices (R₁, R₂, R₃) to each layer's MHSA and FFN blocks, initialized with Hadamard matrices and optimized on the orthogonal manifold via the Cayley optimizer. These rotations are mathematically fused into the corresponding weight matrices offline (e.g., W̃_v = R₁ᵀW_vR₃), so the main computation path remains standard linear layers with no additional online cost. The key insight is that optimized rotations remain close to their Hadamard initialization, making the residual transition matrix T = R_outᵀR_in nearly identity. The residual basis mismatch is then approximated by projecting T−I onto its top-r singular vectors (r=32), extracting the closest orthogonal matrix via polar decomposition in that subspace, and applying only a small r×r dense transform during inference. This reduces residual alignment from O(D²) to O(D) while preserving the full expressivity of unconstrained layer-wise rotations.

## Results

On W4A4 quantization, ReSpinQuant achieves the lowest perplexity and highest zero-shot accuracy across all five LLaMA-family models, e.g., PPL 7.24 and 64.65% avg. accuracy on LLaMA-3 8B (vs. SpinQuant's 7.50/64.53%). Under the more aggressive W3A3 setting, gains are substantially larger: on LLaMA-3.2 1B, ReSpinQuant reduces PPL from 69.70 (SpinQuant) to 49.90, and on LLaMA-3 8B from 15.07 to 13.09. The method also demonstrates that a 4-bit quantized 3B model (PPL 9.06, 58.84%) outperforms a full-precision 1B model (PPL 9.76, 55.75%), confirming that high-quality quantization shifts the Pareto frontier for deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSpinQuant outperforms all five baselines on both PPL and zero-shot accuracy across every model and bit-width setting. Against the primary baseline SpinQuant (global learned rotation), ReSpinQuant consistently improves: on W4A4 LLaMA-3 8B, PPL drops from 7.50 to 7.24 and accuracy rises from 64.53% to 64.65%; on W3A3 LLaMA-3.2 1B, PPL drops dramatically from 69.70 to 49.90. Compared to QuaRot (random Hadamard, no learning), ReSpinQuant's advantage is even larger, e.g., W3A3 LLaMA-3 8B PPL 13.09 vs. 98.04. Against the layer-wise baselines, ReSpinQuant beats OSTQuant (layer-wise scaling) on all metrics (e.g., W4A4 LLaMA-3 8B: PPL 7.24 vs. 7.42) and decisively outperforms FlatQuant (layer-wise affine), which suffers severe degradation under W3A3 (PPL 133.52 on LLaMA-3 8B vs. ReSpinQuant's 13.09). GPTQ/RTN without rotation are catastrophically outperformed, confirming that rotation-based outlier suppression is essential at 4-bit and 3-bit precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSpinQuant sits at the intersection of two competing paradigms in rotation-based PTQ: global rotation methods (QuaRot, SpinQuant) that fuse a single shared rotation into weights for zero online overhead but sacrifice layer-specific expressivity, and layer-wise transformation methods (OSTQuant, FlatQuant) that assign per-layer matrices for better outlier suppression but cannot pre-merge rotations, incurring O(D^1.5)–O(D²) online cost. The paper's key architectural departure is enabling full-size layer-wise rotations to be fused offline into preceding-layer weights (as in global methods) while resolving the resulting residual basis mismatch via a low-rank subspace rotation approximation. This is made possible by the empirical observation that Cayley-optimized rotations remain close to their Hadamard initialization, so the inter-layer transition matrix T = R_out·R_inᵀ is near-identity and compressible to rank r ≪ D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work resolves a concrete engineering trade-off that has constrained rotation-based quantization: you previously had to choose between the efficiency of global rotations and the accuracy of layer-wise ones. By showing that the residual mismatch is low-rank, ReSpinQuant achieves layer-wise accuracy with only ~0.2% additional MACs and 8.4M online parameters (0.1% of an 8B model), making it a practical drop-in for production deployment. Open questions include whether the low-rank structure of T holds under different initializations or optimization schedules, whether dedicated hardware kernels can further reduce the already-small overhead, and whether the approach scales beyond 13B parameters (the paper's compute limit). The reliance on a standard cross-entropy calibration objective (rather than specialized layer-wise losses) also leaves room for further gains.
