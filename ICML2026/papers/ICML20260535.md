## Problem

Flow Matching models achieve state-of-the-art image synthesis but rely on iterative ODE solving, creating a severe latency bottleneck: training-free solvers (Euler, DPM-Solver) need 20–100+ sequential NFEs for high fidelity, while training-based one/few-step methods (Consistency Models, MeanFlow, Shortcut) demand hundreds of thousands of training iterations and full-model fine-tuning, sacrificing plug-and-play compatibility with off-the-shelf backbones. The paper asks whether one can retain the versatility of a standard solver while achieving 5–10 NFE generation quality at negligible training cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Gaussian noise tensor and a class label, and outputs a generated RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The frozen backbone is a REPA-enhanced SiT (Transformer) that provides anchor velocities, while a lightweight convolutional SideNet (~1–2% backbone size, 6M parameters) predicts intermediate velocity corrections. At inference, the total cost is exactly N backbone evaluations (one per interval) plus a small number of parallelized SideNet calls per interval, yielding a ~6% wall-clock overhead over the backbone alone. The same pipeline supports image editing by reversing the FM ODE from a source image to a target class in 10 NFEs.

## Method

BA-solver introduces a lightweight SideNet S_φ that, given the current state x_t, backbone velocity v_t, and a bidirectional offset Δt, predicts the first-order velocity deviation at t+Δt, thereby granting the frozen backbone lookahead and lookback capabilities without retraining it. During sampling, each interval [t−h, t] is processed in three phases: (1) Forward Probe — SideNet predicts all intermediate velocities anchored on v_t in a single parallel batch; (2) Backward Refinement — the backbone evaluates v_{t−h} at the predicted terminal, and SideNet re-predicts velocities for nodes closer to the terminal, halving the maximum prediction offset; (3) Integration & State Reuse — a 4-point Gauss-Lobatto quadrature fuses the proximity-based forward and backward velocity estimates, and v_{t−h} is cached as the starting anchor for the next interval, guaranteeing exactly N NFEs. Training uses a K-interval chain strategy that unrolls the solver for K steps and minimizes a velocity-matching loss between SideNet's predicted terminal velocity and the backbone's ground-truth velocity (with stop-gradient), requiring only K+1 backbone evaluations per iteration and converging in ~250 iterations (batch size 4096).

## Results

On ImageNet-256², BA-solver achieves a FID of 1.96 at 7 NFEs and 1.72 at 10 NFEs, substantially outperforming all training-free solvers (e.g., Flow-DPM: 4.03 FID at 7 NFEs; Euler: 7.56 FID at 7 NFEs). On ImageNet-512², BA-solver reaches FID 2.88 at 7 NFEs and 1.83 at 15 NFEs. In terms of training efficiency, BA-solver converges in just 250 iterations (batch size 4096) with only 6M trainable parameters, compared to 24K–800K iterations and 675–676M parameters for competing one-/few-step methods, while achieving a competitive FID of 1.85 at 8 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all four training-free solvers (Euler, Heun, Flow-UniPC, Flow-DPM), BA-solver achieves the best FID at every NFE budget on both ImageNet-256² and 512². The gap is most dramatic in the few-step regime: at 7 NFEs on ImageNet-256², BA-solver reaches FID 1.96 versus Flow-DPM's 4.03 and Euler's 7.56 (a >2× improvement over the strongest baseline). At 5 NFEs, BA-solver's FID of 2.84 nearly halves Flow-DPM's 6.18. Against training-based one-/few-step methods, BA-solver achieves FID 1.85 at 8 NFEs with only 250 training iterations and 6M parameters, outperforming MeanFlow(SiT) (FID 5.58 at 8 NFEs, 41K iterations, 675M params), π-Flow (FID 1.97 at 2 NFEs, 24K iterations), α-flow (FID 2.15 at 2 NFEs, 95K iterations), Shortcut (FID 10.60 at 1 NFE, 78K iterations), and iCT (FID 20.30 at 2 NFEs, 800K iterations). BA-solver thus wins on both generation quality and training efficiency simultaneously, requiring 100–3000× fewer training iterations and ~100× fewer trainable parameters than all competing training-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

BA-solver sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: numerical ODE solvers for flow matching (Euler, Heun, DPM-Solver++, UniPC) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning via Side-Tuning (Zhang et al., 2020). It departs from training-free solvers by introducing a learned component, but unlike one/few-step generation methods (Consistency Models, MeanFlow, Shortcut, π-Flow, α-flow) that retrain or distill the entire backbone, it freezes the backbone and trains only a 1–2% SideNet. The key conceptual departure is reframing the sampling step from extrapolation (predicting forward from a single anchor) to bi-directional interpolation (bridging two anchors with a lightweight network), which is enabled by repurposing the Side-Tuning architecture for temporal velocity prediction rather than task adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 6M-parameter SideNet trained in 250 iterations (batch 4096) achieves FID 1.85 at 8 NFEs, matching or exceeding methods requiring 24K–800K iterations and 675M+ trainable parameters, while remaining plug-and-play with any frozen FM backbone. This opens a path to accelerating existing production models (Flux, SD3) without retraining. Limitations include evaluation only on class-conditional ImageNet with SiT backbones; generalization to text-to-image, video, or other modalities is untested. An open question is whether the bi-anchor mechanism and SideNet design transfer to architectures where the velocity field is less smooth or where the ODE structure differs (e.g., SDE-based samplers).
