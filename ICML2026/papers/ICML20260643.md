## Problem

Production LLM serving routers rely on heuristics (round-robin, shortest-queue, power-of-2) that were designed for classical parallel-server settings with homogeneous job sizes, offering no explicit control over the latency-throughput trade-offs demanded by heterogeneous SLOs. No prior work formulates LLM decode-side routing as an optimization problem that simultaneously models time-coupled batch and KV-cache constraints, decomposes aggregate SLOs (including tail latency) into per-request rewards, and derives a computationally tractable policy grounded in LP duality.

## Input / Output

At each decode iteration the router observes the waiting queue (each request carrying its known prefill KV footprint s_j and predicted decode length ô_j), the residual batch-occupancy and KV-cache capacity on every device, operator-chosen SLO weights (α, β, γ, σ, ζ₁, ζ₂), and a rolling history of previously admitted action columns. It outputs a binary admission vector assigning each waiting request to at most one device for the current period, plus a 2GT-dimensional dual price tensor (batch and memory shadow prices per device-time slot) that is carried forward as a warm start. The entire decision loop must complete in 1–2 ms to fit within the 10–30 ms wall-clock of a single A100 decode iteration.

## Method

The paper formulates decode-side routing as a per-step online linear program whose constraints couple batch-size and KV-cache capacity across future time slots via age-dependent kernels, and whose objective is a weighted sum of six SLO terms (throughput, average EEL, average TTFT, QPS, tail EEL, tail TTFT) each decomposed into a per-request reward. A key modeling step converts aggregate tail-latency metrics into indicator rewards (e.g., 1{TTFT < t′}) so that the tail weight ζ directly maps to the target quantile. Rather than solving the LP at every step, the authors derive the LP dual and implement a bid-price control rule: a request is admitted to the device where its SLO-weighted reward minus the imputed shadow-price resource cost (a_{(j,g,k)}ᵀp) is largest, subject to residual feasibility. To keep per-step cost at O(KGH) with predictable runtime, dual prices are updated via K warm-started projected mini-batch subgradient steps on a sample-average-approximation objective over a compact action history, with a rolling-window mechanism to adapt to non-stationary arrival rates.

## Results

Under noisy decode-length prediction (20% error) on the LMSYS-Chat-1M dataset with 4 A100 GPUs, the proposed bid-price router achieves 45.75% relative improvement in average end-to-end latency, 42.49% in P95 EEL, and 25.85% in P99 EEL over Round Robin, while also improving throughput by 0.90% and reducing SLO violations by 11.10%. Under a non-stationary rate-shift workload (λ switching from 0.4 to 0.6), the method maintains 44.81% Avg EEL improvement and 45.69% P95 EEL improvement, confirming robustness to distributional shifts. The routing computation takes approximately 1–2 ms per period on a single CPU core, well within the 10–30 ms decode-iteration budget on A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four Vidur-simulator baselines: Round Robin, Least Outstanding Request (LOR), Random, and Power-of-2 Choices. The proposed method dominates all baselines on every latency metric by a wide margin: it achieves 45.75% Avg EEL improvement versus only 0.67% (LOR) and 1.30% (Power-of-2), and 42.49% P95 EEL improvement versus 4.01% and 3.84% respectively. On tail latency (P99 EEL), the gap is 25.85% vs. 6.19% (LOR) and 6.06% (Power-of-2). On throughput, the proposed method (0.90%) edges out Power-of-2 (0.73%) and LOR (0.53%), though all improvements are modest relative to the latency gains. SLO violation reduction is most pronounced for the proposed method (11.10%) compared to LOR (0.98%) and Power-of-2 (1.84%). Under the non-stationary rate-shift stress test, the proposed method again leads on all metrics (44.81% Avg EEL, 45.69% P95 EEL, 31.15% P99 EEL), while LOR and Power-of-2 show only 1.47–1.80% and 1.39–1.80% improvements respectively, confirming that the optimization-based approach is substantially more robust to workload shifts than heuristic load-balancing rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the online linear programming and bid-price control machinery from operations research (Agrawal et al., 2014; Talluri & Van Ryzin, 1998; Li & Ye, 2022) into the LLM serving domain, where routing has been handled by heuristics (round-robin, power-of-2, shortest-queue) designed for classical parallel-server settings with known or homogeneous job sizes. The key departure is the explicit modeling of time-coupled KV-cache and batch-size constraints inherent to autoregressive decoding, combined with a reward decomposition that converts aggregate SLOs (including tail latency) into per-request incentives suitable for online optimization. Computationally, the warm-started projected mini-batch gradient updates for dual price tracking draw on online convex optimization (Zinkevich, 2003; Sun et al., 2020) to meet millisecond decision latency without solving an LP at each step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, interpretable mechanism for controlling multi-SLO trade-offs in LLM routing: adjusting objective weights traces the Pareto frontier, and shadow prices diagnose which resource (batch slots vs. KV-cache) is the bottleneck under a given workload—information entirely absent from heuristic routers. However, validation is limited to the Vidur simulator with four A100s and weak baselines (default Vidur heuristics), and the paper does not address production challenges such as preemption, KV-cache swapping, or coordination overhead in distributed deployments. Open questions include whether the framework extends to heterogeneous GPU fleets, fairness across request classes, and whether the 45%+ improvements over simple heuristics persist against stronger optimization-based or learned routing policies in real serving stacks.
