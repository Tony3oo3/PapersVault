## Problem

Risk-sensitive multi-objective RL lacks a practical, theoretically grounded framework: existing distributional MORL methods either use MMD-based critics that are not transport maps (making risk calculation invalid) or marginal-based critics that ignore inter-objective dependencies. No prior work provides a multivariate quantile formulation that simultaneously satisfies the axiomatic definition of vector-risk measures and guarantees critic convergence under the distributional Bellman operator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265560/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The critic receives the environment state, action, a preference vector ω sampled uniformly from the Dirichlet(1,...,1) distribution over the simplex, and a d-dimensional quantile level u ∈ [0,1]^d, and outputs d value-distribution quantiles via the autoregressive KR map. The actor consumes the state and preference to produce an action, optimizing a risk-sensitive objective defined as the weighted expectation of the KR-map evaluated at the risk-measure-transformed quantile levels Φ⁻¹(u). Unlike standard IQN, the critic's output is inherently ordered (objective 1 is unconditional, objective k is conditioned on objectives 1..k−1), and the preference vector modulates which risk measure is applied per objective.

## Method

KR-IQN approximates the Knothe-Rosenblatt multivariate quantile map using a transformer-based architecture without positional encoding, where task embeddings (not positional encodings) identify which objective is being estimated at each autoregressive step, and previously inferred objective values are fed back as input to relax the artificial ordering. The critic is trained with a KR quantile regression loss (an autoregressive extension of the univariate quantile loss) against TD-targets computed via the distributional MO-Bellman operator, with convergence guaranteed under the adapted Wasserstein distance. To stabilize training, MO-TQC applies two truncation steps over an ensemble of critics: marginal TQC removes the largest (and optionally smallest) quantile atoms per objective dimension, then projective TQC removes the largest atom from the preference-weighted sum ωᵀQ_target, addressing overestimation that persists after scalarization. The paper proves the KR-map-induced risk measure satisfies the vector-risk axioms (monotonicity, translation invariance, normalization) and introduces the simplex risk measure as a novel multivariate extension of CVaR.

## Results

KR-IQN achieves an average 29.77% improvement over the strongest baseline across MO-Gymnasium and Finance benchmarks, with a 41.18% gain on risk-sensitive metrics (EU_Risk, HV_Risk) versus 18.36% on standard metrics (EU, HV). On risk-sensitive CVaR₁₀% evaluation, KR-IQN(RS) leads on EU_Risk in Walker2d (1019.89 vs. 661.47 for DPMORL), Ant (1847.12 vs. 1579.72 for Marginal-IQN(N)), and Hopper (1163.43 vs. 1070.63 for KR-IQN(N)). In the Finance case study, KR-IQN achieves 25.40% earnings with a Sortino ratio of 4.50, outperforming all baselines and the S&P 500 benchmark (5.80% earnings, 0.70 Sortino).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KR-IQN(RS) dominates on risk-sensitive metrics across most environments: on Walker2d it achieves EU_Risk of 1019.89, nearly 1.5× the next-best DPMORL (661.47) and Marginal-IQN(RS) (661.23); on Ant it reaches 1847.12, surpassing Marginal-IQN(N) (1579.72) by ~17%; on Hopper it leads with 1163.43 vs. its own risk-neutral variant (1070.63) and DPMORL (642.83). The sole exception is Finance, where DPMORL achieves the highest HV_Risk (21.17 vs. KR-IQN's 12.62), though KR-IQN(N) still leads on EU_Risk (54.97 vs. DPMORL's −33.49). Among non-distributional baselines, PGMORL and CAPQL consistently underperform on risk-sensitive metrics, with PGMORL showing the weakest EU_Risk in every environment. EWP (MMD-based) is competitive on Ant HV_Risk (10.46) but lags significantly on EU_Risk in Walker2d (198.69) and Hopper (378.14), confirming the paper's theoretical argument that MMD-based methods lack the transport property needed for proper risk computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IQN framework (Dabney et al., 2018a) from single-objective to multi-objective risk-sensitive RL by replacing the univariate quantile function with the Knothe-Rosenblatt (KR) map, an autoregressive multivariate quantile. It builds on the preference-conditioned policy architecture of CAPQL (Lu et al., 2023) and the overestimation control of TQC (Kuznetsov et al., 2020a), while deliberately departing from MMD-based distributional MORL (Zhang et al., 2021; Wiltzer et al., 2024) and marginal-based approaches (Zhou et al., 2021) that lack the transport property needed for risk computation. The vector-risk measure definition is imported from financial mathematics (Jouini et al., 2004; Hamel et al., 2007), and the paper's key theoretical contribution is proving that the KR map induces a valid vector-risk measure and that the resulting Bellman operator is a contraction under the adapted Wasserstein distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that provides both a tractable risk-sensitive MORL algorithm and convergence guarantees, filling a gap the authors identify as largely unaddressed in the ML literature. The introduction of the simplex risk measure (Def. 4.4) is a genuinely new multivariate risk concept with no univariate analogue, and the financial case study demonstrates practical relevance beyond robotics benchmarks. The principal limitation is that the KR map's inherent ordering cannot be fully eliminated (only mitigated via shuffling and PE-free transformers); the authors acknowledge that optimal transport would be the ideal replacement but is computationally intractable for RL. Open questions include whether the ordering bias persists in high-dimensional objective spaces and whether the adapted Wasserstein contraction result extends to continuous state spaces.
