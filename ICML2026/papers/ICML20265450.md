## Problem

Existing self-supervised speech and audio models are optimised for either linguistic/paralinguistic understanding or acoustic event classification, leaving a persistent gap between the two domains. Directly applying a single SSL objective (e.g., data2vec or MAE) to joint speech-and-audio pre-training degrades performance, and prior distillation-based unified models (MT2KD, USAD) rely on feature-matching losses that merely mimic teacher representations without encouraging the student to discover its own semantic and acoustic structure.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw audio waveforms (speech or general audio) converted to 128-dimensional filter-bank features at 100 Hz, which the Zipformer encoder processes with dynamic temporal downsampling to produce 50 Hz frame-level contextualised representations. During pre-training, the student predicts two sets of discrete MVQ tokens per frame: 16-codebook speech tokens (from WavLM Large's 1024-d, 50 Hz features) and 8-codebook audio tokens (from Dasheng 1.2B's 1536-d, 25 Hz features upsampled to 50 Hz). The retained encoder outputs frame-level representations at 50 Hz for downstream fine-tuning or frozen evaluation.

## Method

SPEAR reformulates unified speech-and-audio representation learning as masked prediction over fine-grained discrete tokens derived from domain-specific SSL teachers via multi-codebook vector quantisation (MVQ), whose non-hierarchical parallel codebooks yield K^N representable states far exceeding k-means granularity. An asymmetric dual-domain loss treats speech MVQ tokens as universal prediction targets for all inputs while audio MVQ tokens serve only as targets for general-audio data, with a tunable weight λ balancing the two losses. A token mixing mechanism stochastically combines MVQ tokens from two mixed signals proportionally to their signal power, training the model to represent overlapping sources rather than suppressing secondary signals. The student encoder is a Zipformer (94M–600M parameters) trained with cross-entropy over N independent linear heads per codebook, with an adjustable α weighting masked versus unmasked frame losses to stabilise the multi-codebook prediction task.

## Results

On LibriSpeech ASR (LS-100), SPEAR_S Large achieves 2.6/4.7 WER (clean/other), outperforming WavLM Large (3.0/6.1) with comparable model size and pre-training data. On AudioSet tagging (AS-2M), SPEAR_a Large reaches 49.7 mAP, surpassing BEATs (48.6), EAT (48.6), and ATST Frame (48.0). The dual-domain SPEAR_{s+a} XLarge (600M, 197k hours) achieves 2.4/4.6 WER on LS-100 and 50.0 mAP on AS-2M, while establishing new SOTA on 12 of 15 SUPERB tasks and 83.41 average on HEAR (concatenated layers), surpassing Dasheng 1.2B (81.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WavLM Large (the prior SUPERB SOTA), SPEAR_S Large wins on 12 of 15 SUPERB tasks with the same pre-training corpora, including a 16.7% relative EER reduction on speaker verification (3.14 vs 3.77) and 1.5% absolute accuracy gain on emotion recognition (72.1 vs 70.6). On ASR, SPEAR_S Large reduces WER by 25.9% (relative) on LS-960 test-other compared to WavLM Base+. Against audio SSL baselines (BEATs, EAT, ATST Frame), SPEAR_a Large achieves the highest AS-2M mAP (49.7 vs 48.6/48.6/48.0) with only 5k hours of AudioSet. Against USAD (the closest unified speech+audio competitor), SPEAR_{s+a} Large achieves 2.6/4.8 WER on LS-100 vs USAD Large's 4.0/7.7, and 49.6 mAP on AS-2M vs USAD's 37.4 on AS-20k, while using less pre-training data (97k vs 126k hours). Against Dasheng 1.2B (1.2B params, 272k hours), SPEAR_{s+a} XLarge (600M, 197k hours) substantially outperforms on SUPERB (e.g., 3.15 vs 13.8 ASR WER) and matches or exceeds it on HEAR environment tasks (84.85 vs 83.20 for SPEAR_{s+a} XLarge with concatenated layers), though Dasheng retains an edge on overall HEAR due to its much larger general-audio data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPEAR builds on the masked-prediction-of-discrete-units paradigm from HuBERT and the multi-codebook vector quantisation (MVQ) mechanism from Guo et al. (2023), repurposing MVQ from a compression tool into a fine-grained SSL supervision interface. It departs from prior unified speech-audio distillation frameworks (USAD, MT2KD) that rely on layer-wise feature matching losses by reformulating domain fusion as a masked token prediction task over a discrete interface, and from EncodecMAE by using non-hierarchical MVQ tokens instead of hierarchical RVQ tokens, enabling generalisation to the speech domain. The asymmetric dual-domain loss and token mixing mechanism are novel contributions not present in any prior unified SSL framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPEAR addresses a persistent fragmentation in acoustic representation learning by providing a single model that achieves SOTA on SUPERB (12/15 tasks) while remaining competitive on the audio-centric HEAR benchmark, making it a practical unified frontend for diverse speech and audio applications. Key limitations include the computational overhead of running frozen teacher models during pre-training, dependence on teacher quality (though the student consistently exceeds the teacher), and an imbalanced pre-training data composition (only ~13k hours of general audio out of 197k) that constrains performance on environment and music tasks. Open questions include whether the framework scales effectively with more balanced audio data, how to integrate generative capabilities into the unified representation space, and whether the asymmetric loss design generalises to more than two domains.
