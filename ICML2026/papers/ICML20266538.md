## Problem

Despite impressive benchmark results from 'Thinking with Images' VLMs that generate interleaved images via tool-calling or code execution during chain-of-thought reasoning, it remains unclear whether these interleaved images are genuinely necessary for visual reasoning or whether performance gains stem from the finetuning process itself. This position paper argues that current VLMs seldom truly 'think' with interleaved images, challenging the prevailing assumption that visual information in the CoT is the primary driver of success.

## Input / Output

The 'Thinking with Images' paradigm takes an RGB image and a text query as input and produces a reasoning trajectory that interleaves textual thought steps with images generated mid-sequence by external tools (e.g., cropping, zooming) or code execution, ultimately yielding a text answer. The interleaved images increase context length substantially and require specialized agentic loops during both training and inference. In the ablation setting studied here, the interleaved image outputs are suppressed while the preceding textual reasoning is retained, isolating the contribution of the visual tokens themselves.

## Method

The paper provides empirical evidence from three complementary angles. First, an ablation study removes all interleaved images from the CoT of DeepEyes-7B, Pixel-Reasoner-7B, and Thyme-7B (all RL-finetuned from Qwen2.5-VL-7B via GRPO) while retaining textual reasoning, showing negligible or even positive performance changes across eight benchmarks. Second, attention rollout visualization reveals that answer tokens predominantly attend to the original input image rather than the interleaved crops. Third, a vanilla SFT experiment finetunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the DeepEyes dataset (which contains no CoT or interleaved images) for one epoch, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding the full 'Thinking with Images' models, and a masked-object experiment on V* confirms that finetuning aligns the language generation distribution with benchmark expectations independent of visual perception. The paper concludes by recommending lightweight ablation reporting, more informative visual tools beyond cropping, and fundamentally novel benchmarks that strictly require visual feedback.

## Results

The central finding is that ablating interleaved images from three RL-based 'Thinking with Images' models (DeepEyes-7B, Pixel-Reasoner-7B, Thyme-7B) causes negligible performance drops across eight benchmarks, with most deltas within ±1.5 points (e.g., DeepEyes on V*: 84.3 → 84.3, Thyme on HR-Bench-4K: 78.3 → 78.5). Vanilla SFT on the same DeepEyes training data (without any CoT or interleaved images) matches or exceeds the full 'Thinking with Images' model on 7 of 8 benchmarks (avg. 65.8 vs. 65.3), with the only notable gap on MME-RealWorld-Lite (49.1 vs. 53.9, −4.8). In a masked experiment on V*, all three 'Thinking with Images' models still outperform the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B even when target objects are occluded, confirming that finetuning shifts the language generation distribution rather than enabling genuine visual reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B, all three 'Thinking with Images' models show substantial gains (e.g., +8–10 points on V*, +4–10 on HR-Bench), but the paper demonstrates these gains come from finetuning rather than interleaved images. DeepEyes-7B, Pixel-Reasoner-7B, and Thyme-7B all retain nearly identical scores when their interleaved images are removed (max drop: 1.5 points on V* for Pixel-Reasoner), indicating the visual crops are not causally responsible for the improvement. The vanilla SFT variant (DeepEyes-SFT-7B, trained on the same data without CoT or images) achieves a macro average of 65.8, slightly exceeding DeepEyes-7B's 65.3, and matches or beats it on 7 of 8 individual benchmarks. On Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct, enabling 'Thinking with Images' mode yields only +0.1 overall on HR-Bench (84.0 → 83.9), further confirming the marginal contribution of interleaved images even in a much larger model. The masked V* experiment shows that all three 'Thinking with Images' models still outperform the base model (37.7–40.8 vs. 28.8) even with target objects occluded, proving the gains stem from a shifted language prior rather than active visual perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant 'Thinking with Images' paradigm (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, Thyme, Active-o3) by questioning its core assumption that interleaved images generated via tool-calling or code execution are essential to visual reasoning. It aligns with a growing 'rethinking reasoning' literature (Hassid et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2025b; Zhang et al. 2025c) that has challenged the necessity of long CoT in text-only LLMs, extending that skepticism to the multimodal setting. The analysis draws on attention rollout (Abnar & Zuidema 2020) and ablation methodology from interpretability work, while the bonus section repurposes HiDe (Liu et al. 2025a) and Vicrop (Zhang et al. 2025a) to demonstrate that simpler context management plus finetuning can match or exceed the full 'Thinking with Images' pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the findings generalize beyond the tested 7B Qwen2.5-VL-based models, the field may be investing substantial compute in expensive agentic RL loops and tool-calling infrastructure for gains that vanilla SFT on the same data can replicate, fundamentally reshaping how 'Thinking with Images' research is prioritized. The masked experiment—where models answer correctly even with the target object occluded—raises the open question of whether current benchmarks actually test visual perception or merely reward aligned language priors. The paper calls for benchmarks that strictly require visual feedback (e.g., BabyVision, InkSlop) and for tools that introduce genuinely novel information rather than duplicating existing patches, but whether such tasks exist at scale remains unresolved.
