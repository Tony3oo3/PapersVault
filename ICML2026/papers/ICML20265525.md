## Problem

Backpropagation enforces three fundamental limitations on deep network training: biological implausibility (each layer needs the next layer's weights), update locking (each layer must wait for gradients from all downstream layers), and structural fragility (removing intermediate layers causes catastrophic collapse). The paper asks whether a training paradigm can simultaneously eliminate all three constraints while matching or exceeding BP accuracy, and whether network depth can be dynamically adjusted during training to adapt to available compute.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet) and produces a terminal categorical belief p_L over class labels. The key structural difference from standard architectures is that every intermediate layer outputs a full probability distribution over the label space (via a lightweight projection head) rather than raw feature maps, creating a belief-centric interface between layers. For GNNs, node features plus graph adjacency are consumed and node class labels produced; for diffusion, a noisy image and timestep are consumed and per-layer noise estimates are produced, with the final layer's estimate serving as the denoising prediction.

## Method

DMoL assigns each layer a local cooperative objective L_i = α·D_KL(p_i‖p_y) + (1−α)·D_KL(p_i‖sg(p_{i−1})), where the first term aligns the layer's belief with the ground-truth label and the second enforces consistency with the predecessor's belief via a stop-gradient teacher signal. The critical design choice is the direction of the KL divergence: placing the prediction p_i as the first argument yields a geometric interpolation in probability space (equivalent to additive logit-space updates), which the paper proves is formally equivalent to Entropic Mirror Descent and Gradient Boosting, guaranteeing exponential error decay with depth. Training proceeds in two phases: a lightweight forward pass caches teacher beliefs without building a computation graph, then all layers compute their local gradients in parallel via module-restricted backpropagation, completely eliminating inter-layer backward dependencies. This decoupling enables dynamic addition or removal of layers during training, linear scaling of throughput with parallel workers, and per-device memory proportional to a single module rather than the full network depth.

## Results

On vision benchmarks, DMoL matches or exceeds standard backpropagation across all four datasets: 84.42% on CIFAR-10, 56.12% on CIFAR-100 (+4.31% over BP), 41.37% on Tiny-ImageNet (+6.86% over BP), and 63.74% on ImageNet. On deep 8-layer GCN node classification, DMoL achieves 81.40% on Cora versus 43.86% for BP (+37.54%), effectively eliminating over-smoothing. In diffusion model (DiT) training, DMoL reduces final MSE to 0.034 compared to 0.060 for BP, a 43% reduction in denoising error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMoL decisively outperforms both gradient-approximation baselines: Feedback Alignment (77.03% on CIFAR-10) and Direct Feedback Alignment (73.53%) collapse on harder datasets (1.32% and 15.48% on ImageNet respectively), while DMoL maintains 63.74%. Against NoProp (Li et al., 2025), DMoL wins on all four datasets, with the largest gap on Tiny-ImageNet (41.37% vs 2.80%) and ImageNet (63.74% vs 0.27%). Forward-Forward (Hinton, 2022) is far behind at 34.10% on CIFAR-10 and below 10% on CIFAR-100. Among the closest competitors, Decoupled Greedy Learning (Belilovsky et al., 2020) achieves 54.31% on CIFAR-100 and 38.13% on Tiny-ImageNet, both below DMoL's 56.12% and 41.37%, while HSIC Bottleneck (Ma et al., 2019) edges out DMoL on CIFAR-10 (85.21% vs 84.42%) but is surpassed on CIFAR-100 (55.18% vs 56.12%), Tiny-ImageNet (35.61% vs 41.37%), and ImageNet (33.62% vs 63.74%). Overall, DMoL achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all four vision benchmarks while being the only method that also matches BP on ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMoL extends the layer-wise training paradigm by replacing the single global cross-entropy objective with a set of cooperative local objectives built from KL divergences, drawing directly on the teacher-student distillation mechanism of Hinton et al. (2015) for its consistency term. It is most closely related to Stochastic Layer-wise Learning (Yin & Corradi, 2025), which also assigns probabilistic beliefs to intermediate layers, but DMoL departs by introducing task-adaptive semantic alignment (the task-alignment KL term) and, critically, structural robustness under dynamic depth changes. Unlike gradient-approximation methods (FA, DFA, NoProp) or local-supervision methods (Forward-Forward, DGL, HSIC), DMoL eliminates inter-module gradient transport entirely rather than approximating it, and its specific choice of KL direction (p_i || target) yields a geometric-interpolation structure that the authors formally connect to Entropic Mirror Descent and logit-space Gradient Boosting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic-depth capability—adding or removing layers mid-training without catastrophic collapse—addresses a practical pain point in resource-constrained and distributed training that no prior BP-based method handles. The theoretical guarantees (monotonic improvement with depth, exponential convergence, contraction under non-stationary teachers) provide a principled foundation that most alternative training schemes lack. However, the method requires a projection head at every layer and shows a notable performance drop on unmodified ResNet-18 (93.32% vs 95.68% on CIFAR-10), suggesting that architectural compatibility remains an open design question. Scaling to very large models (beyond ViT-Tiny on ImageNet) and to language or multi-modal tasks is untested, and the practical utility of dynamic depth at the scale of modern foundation models is unclear.
