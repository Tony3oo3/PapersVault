## Problem

Observability time series data—zero-inflated, highly stochastic, and dominated by abrupt transitions—remains underrepresented in public benchmarks due to proprietary restrictions. The few available datasets (e.g., BOOM) are anonymized and normalized, stripping away covariate identities and absolute scale information, which prevents multi-modal task construction and obscures magnitude-driven anomalies critical for real-world anomaly detection and root cause analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261116/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a 128-timestep window at 100 ms resolution across 18 heterogeneous KPI channels (10 float, 6 integer, 2 categorical), preserving absolute scale without normalization. Downstream tasks consume these samples alongside natural-language questions and structured metadata (zone, mobility state, congestion status), producing outputs that range from binary anomaly labels and timestamp intervals to 11-class root-cause predictions, per-channel statistical estimates (mean, variance, period, trend), and free-form Q&A answers with explicit reasoning traces.

## Method

TelecomTS is constructed from a lab-deployed 5G network (OpenAirInterface gNB + core, USRP N300 RU, n78 TDD band) where a mobile device generates real traffic (YouTube, Twitch, file downloads) across three spatial zones under static and mobile conditions, while a USRP X310 jammer introduces controlled over-the-air interference. Two independent KPI traces (PHY-layer and network-layer) are synchronized via histogram-matching that minimizes KL divergence between correlated signals. Synthetic anomalies are generated by applying eight literature-grounded transformation functions (constant/linear/exponential/logistic growth, sinusoidal fluctuation, etc.) to KPI subsets mapped from ten fault types, with durations and inter-arrival times drawn from exponential distributions calibrated to operational network data. The benchmark evaluates LLMs, reasoning models, time-series foundation models (Toto, Moment, Moirai2, Mantis), classical architectures, and a Toto+Qwen-3-4B multi-modal model across anomaly detection, duration localization, root cause analysis, forecasting, and Q&A, with a scale ablation (adding/removing an NME branch encoding per-channel mean and std) demonstrating that absolute magnitude carries diagnostic information that temporal patterns alone cannot recover.

## Results

On the primary anomaly detection task (1,000 samples, 80/20 normal/anomalous split), Mantis achieves the best F1 of 0.800, followed by Toto (F1=0.615) among foundation models and DeepSeek-R1 without context (F1=0.362) among reasoning models. For root cause analysis across 11 anomaly types, Toto leads with 84.8% accuracy, while the best LLM (o4-mini with context) reaches only 27.5%. On forecasting, Informer achieves the lowest MAE (0.143) and RMSE (0.358), though the paper notes these metrics obscure the core difficulty of predicting abrupt spikes in observability data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mantis (Feofanov et al., 2025) dominates anomaly detection with F1=0.800, outperforming Toto (0.615), Moment (0.397), Moirai2 (0.405), and Informer (0.453) by 10–40 F1 points; removing its NME scale encoders drops F1 to 0.692, confirming scale awareness is critical. Toto (Das et al., 2024) is the strongest foundation model overall, achieving the best root cause accuracy (84.8%) and the best anomaly duration F1 (0.921), but still trails Mantis on detection. Moment (Goswami et al., 2024) and Moirai2 (Woo et al., 2024) both underperform on anomaly detection (F1≈0.40) and root cause (55.0% and 22.5% accuracy respectively), revealing that large-scale pretraining on general time series does not transfer well to observability data. Informer (Zhou et al., 2021) achieves the best forecasting MAE (0.143) but ranks mid-pack on anomaly detection (F1=0.453) and root cause (65.4%), and the scale ablation (Table 7) shows it gains +40.1 F1 points on anomaly detection when NME scale information is added. Across all five baselines, LLMs and reasoning models (GPT-4.1, Claude, o4-mini, DeepSeek-R1) consistently underperform dedicated time series architectures, with the best LLM F1 (0.333) trailing the worst time series model (Autoformer, 0.308) by only a margin, highlighting a fundamental gap in handling zero-inflated, high-variance observability signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the observability time series dataset space by addressing three specific gaps left by the BOOM dataset (Datadog, 2024): anonymization that obscures covariate identity, normalization that strips absolute scale, and the absence of multi-modal task annotations. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of time series foundation model evaluation (Toto, Moment, Moirai2, Mantis) and multi-modal LLM reasoning (GPT-4.1, DeepSeek-R1, o4-mini), but its primary contribution is a de-anonymized, scale-preserving 5G KPI dataset with structured Q&A and reasoning traces rather than a new architecture. The empirical benchmarking repurposes existing model families—transformer-based time series models, decoder-only foundation models, and multi-modal early-fusion LLMs—under a unified observability evaluation protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scale ablation (Table 7) provides well-evidenced evidence across five architectures that absolute magnitude carries diagnostic information irrecoverable from normalized temporal patterns, with gains up to 41 points on traffic classification—this challenges the prevailing normalization-first pipeline in time series modeling. However, the dataset's scope is limited to a single lab-based 5G network with 32K samples, raising open questions about generalizability to larger, multi-operator telecom or cloud observability settings. The finding that even Toto, pretrained on observability data, achieves only 0.615 F1 on anomaly detection suggests that current foundation models lack the inductive biases needed for zero-inflated, bursty signals, pointing toward a need for scale-aware, domain-specific pretraining strategies.
