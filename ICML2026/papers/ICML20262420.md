## Problem

Single-loop stochastic algorithms for nonconvex constrained optimization with DC-regularized objectives remain largely unexplored. Existing DC methods are either double-loop (requiring inner subproblem solvers and extensive hyperparameter tuning), confined to unconstrained or convex-constrained settings, or assume access to the proximal operator of the full DC composite h−g, which is intractable for common regularizers like capped-ℓ₁ or ℓ₁−ℓ₂. The paper fills this gap by developing a single-loop stochastic penalty method that handles general nonconvex constraints and nonsmooth DC structure simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only stochastic gradient samples of the smooth loss f (not exact gradients), the individual proximal operators of h and g (not the intractable prox of h−g), evaluations of the constraint mapping c(x) and its Jacobian, and an initial point x⁰. It outputs a randomly selected iterate x^{R+1} that is a stochastic ε-KKT point (satisfying both criticality and feasibility residuals at level ε²) or a stochastic ε-stationary point (satisfying criticality and infeasible-stationarity residuals). An approximately feasible initialization with ‖c(x⁰)‖² = O(K^{-1/4}) or O(K^{-1/3}) is required to achieve the best complexity rates.

## Method

MoSSP applies a quadratic penalty to the nonconvex constraints, then smooths the resulting penalized DC objective via the Difference-of-Moreau-Envelopes (DME) technique, yielding a smooth surrogate F_{ρ,μ}(z) whose gradient equals μ⁻¹(prox_{μg}(z) − prox_{μψ_ρ}(z)). The key single-loop mechanism replaces the intractable prox_{μψ_ρ}(z) with a stochastic proximal-gradient step using only prox_{μh} and a stochastic gradient estimator of the penalty term, while computing prox_{μg}(z) in parallel; the z-update then uses the difference of these two proximal outputs as a gradient estimate. Two momentum variants are provided: MoSSP-P uses Polyak (heavy-ball) momentum for the stochastic gradient, achieving O(ε⁻⁴) oracle complexity, while MoSSP-R uses recursive momentum (STORM) with a moderate initial batch, achieving the improved O(ε⁻³) rate under mean-squared smoothness. A DC-aware potential function L_{ρ,μ}(w) = Q_ρ(x)+h(x)+(1/2μ)‖x−z‖²−M_{μg}(z) is constructed to handle the descent analysis despite the concave −g term, enabling coordinated calibration of penalty, smoothing, and momentum parameters.

## Results

On the large-scale a9a dataset (32,561 samples), MoSSP-P achieves a constraint violation of 1.53×10⁻³, roughly 20× lower than SPDC-P (3.23×10⁻²) and 8× lower than SALM-P (1.30×10⁻²), while attaining a competitive objective value of 0.5901. With recursive momentum, MoSSP-R further improves the a9a objective to 0.5809 (best among all methods) and reduces constraint violation to 1.03×10⁻³. On the smaller australian dataset, SPDC attains a lower final constraint violation (1.95×10⁻³) than MoSSP-P (7.14×10⁻³), though MoSSP-P still achieves the best objective (0.6239). The paper also establishes O(ε⁻⁴) oracle complexity for MoSSP-P and O(ε⁻³) for MoSSP-R, matching the unconstrained stochastic optimization lower bound under mean-squared smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two double-loop baselines: SPDC (Xu et al., 2019; Nitanda & Suzuki, 2017), a stochastic DC method for convex-constrained problems, and SALM (Sun & Sun, 2023), an augmented Lagrangian method for linearly constrained DC-regularized problems, each equipped with either Polyak or recursive momentum. On the large-scale a9a and phishing datasets, MoSSP variants dominate both baselines on constraint violation by 1–2 orders of magnitude (e.g., 1.53×10⁻³ vs. 3.23×10⁻² for SPDC-P on a9a) while matching or slightly improving the objective value. On the smaller australian dataset, SPDC achieves a lower final constraint violation (1.95×10⁻³ vs. 7.14×10⁻³ for MoSSP-P), though MoSSP-P still attains the best objective. With recursive momentum, MoSSP-R achieves the best objective on a9a (0.5809 vs. 0.5921 for SPDC-R and 0.6303 for SALM-R) and the best constraint violation on a9a and phishing. The paper attributes MoSSP's speed advantage to its single-loop design, which rapidly reduces early-iterate constraint violations from 10⁰ to 10⁻³ with few gradient evaluations, whereas the double-loop baselines remain at 10⁻² or higher for many iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct threads: the Difference-of-Moreau-Envelopes (DME) smoothing technique from Sun & Sun (2023) for handling nonsmooth DC structure, the quadratic penalty framework standard in constrained nonconvex optimization, and momentum-based variance reduction (Polyak momentum and STORM) from unconstrained stochastic optimization. The key departure is that prior DC methods were either unconstrained, convex-constrained, or double-loop, while prior single-loop stochastic penalty methods assumed prox-friendly objectives without DC structure. MoSSP is the first to combine all three in a single-loop design where the concave DC component is nonsmooth and the constraints are general nonconvex equalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the DC regularization structure does not increase the oracle complexity order relative to unconstrained stochastic optimization (O(ε⁻⁴) with Polyak momentum, O(ε⁻³) with recursive momentum matching the known lower bound), which is a clean theoretical guarantee for a practically important class of problems including DNN compression under energy budgets and sparse CCA. A notable limitation is that the best rates require an approximately feasible initialization, and the empirical validation is limited to small-scale LIBSVM classification datasets. Open questions include extending to stochastic concave components (e.g., PU learning, partial AUC) where prox_g is unavailable in closed form, and to inequality constraints with active-set dynamics.
