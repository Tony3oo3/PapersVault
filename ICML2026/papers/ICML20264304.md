## Problem

Compositional diffusion planners stitch short trajectory chunks into long-horizon plans using heuristic averaging or overwriting, but this produces a non-conservative update field that cannot correspond to any valid global trajectory log-density, causing mode inconsistency and slow convergence. Existing alternatives like CDGS fix consistency through expensive inference-time resampling, leaving a gap between fast-but-inconsistent and consistent-but-slow methods.

## Input / Output

The planner receives a start state x_s and goal state x_g and outputs a long-horizon state sequence x_{0:L} with L >> l (the training chunk length). States range from 2D (x-y center-of-mass position) to 17D (including all joint poses for AntSoccer). Actions are not directly predicted; instead, a separate MLP inverse-dynamics model maps consecutive planned states to executable actions. The local chunk denoiser is trained on short fragments of fixed length l and is frozen at inference time—ECD only changes how chunks are composed during the reverse-diffusion loop.

## Method

ECD inverts the standard stitching paradigm: instead of composing local noisy-mean predictions, it constructs a global scalar energy E_t(x) = Σ_k E_bridge^k(x,t) where each local bridge energy is the squared Mahalanobis distance between the current chunk state and the denoiser's conditional mean prediction. The negative gradient of this energy yields a conservative correction field by construction, and its chain-rule expansion reveals a boundary reaction term (a Jacobian-vector product J_{O,k}^T r_k) that propagates interior residual back to chunk boundaries—precisely the term that heuristic stitching drops, causing non-conservativity. To avoid expensive backpropagation through the denoiser at every step, ECD approximates this reaction via a first-order Markov surrogate energy whose Hessian is block-tridiagonal, reducing the solve to O(L) per chunk. In practice, the correction is applied as an additive update on top of CD's interleaved DDIM proposal, with time-dependent scales η_b(t) and η_r(t) absorbing the σ_t^{-2} energy-gradient factor and DDIM conversion.

## Results

On the OGBench stitching benchmark, ECD achieves 91% success on PointMaze Giant (vs. 78% for CDGS and 68% for CompDiffuser), 87% on AntMaze Giant (vs. 85% for CDGS and 70% for CD), and 47% on HumanoidMaze Giant (vs. 38% for CDGS and 42% for CD). In the 17D AntSoccer setting, ECD reaches 66% on Arena and 20% on Medium, outperforming all baselines. Inference runtime is approximately 8–9 seconds per episode, comparable to heuristic stitching (CD) and roughly 3× faster than the exact-reaction variant (~22 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECD consistently outperforms the primary diffusion baseline CompDiffuser (CD) across all environments and sizes: by 17 points on PointMaze Giant (91 vs. 68), 17 on AntMaze Giant (87 vs. 70), and 5 on HumanoidMaze Giant (47 vs. 42). Against the strongest baseline CDGS (which uses inference-time resampling), ECD wins on PointMaze Giant (91 vs. 78), AntMaze Giant (87 vs. 85), and HumanoidMaze Giant (47 vs. 38), while roughly matching on AntMaze Large (91 vs. 88). The older diffusion baseline GSC (Generative Skill Chaining) degrades severely on long horizons (29% on PointMaze Giant, 20% on AntMaze Giant, 5% on HumanoidMaze Giant), confirming that simple mode-averaging cannot handle out-of-span composition. Among offline RL baselines, QRL is the strongest on PointMaze (50% on Giant) but still trails ECD by 41 points; HIQL reaches 94% on AntMaze Medium but collapses to 0% on Giant. ECD also matches or exceeds CD on the 17D AntSoccer tasks (66% Arena, 20% Medium vs. CD's 61% and 17%), demonstrating that the energy-based correction extends to high-dimensional joint-state planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CompDiffuser (Luo et al., 2025) and Generative Skill Chaining (Mishra et al., 2023), which established the compositional diffusion planning paradigm of stitching short-horizon chunk predictions into long trajectories. The key departure is a theoretical diagnosis: the authors prove that CD's heuristic stitching (averaging or overwriting overlapping predictions) induces a non-conservative score field that cannot correspond to any valid global log-density, thereby invalidating the standard score-based reverse-diffusion interpretation. Rather than addressing this inconsistency through inference-time search as CDGS (Mishra et al., 2025) does, ECD restores conservativity by construction via a global energy whose negative gradient yields a boundary reaction term that stitching heuristics omit. The Markov-based block-tridiagonal approximation for this reaction term is a new technical contribution that keeps inference linear in horizon length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of non-conservativity in heuristic stitching is a fundamental diagnostic that explains why compositional diffusion planners struggle with multimodal long-horizon tasks, and the energy-based fix provides a principled alternative that achieves SOTA success rates (e.g., 91% vs. 68% on PointMaze Giant) at comparable inference speed. Open questions include whether the first-order Markov assumption for the reaction approximation holds in contact-rich manipulation or delayed-dynamics settings, whether the chunking/overlap hyperparameters can be learned rather than hand-tuned, and whether the bridge-energy formulation can be replaced by learned conditional energies that better approximate true short-segment likelihoods. The evaluation is confined to OGBench navigation-like domains, leaving real-robot deployment with perception noise and actuator limits untested.
