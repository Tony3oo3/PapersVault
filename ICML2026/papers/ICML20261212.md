## Problem

Standard active learning methods for CATE estimation suffer from an objective mismatch: they reduce uncertainty in observable outcomes or model parameters rather than in the unobservable causal quantities (potential outcomes and their difference) that define the CATE. This indirect targeting leads to inefficient data selection, a problem that is amplified under distribution shift between the sampling pool and the target population.

## Input / Output

The method operates on observational data where treatment assignments are fixed: it receives factual covariate-treatment pairs (x, t) from a finite pool, a target covariate set X_tar defining the population of interest, and a warm-start labeled set D_T. It produces acquisition scores ranking which pool outcomes to query next (batch selection under a fixed budget) and, after iterative labeling, a CATE estimator τ̂(x) evaluated via √PEHE on the target set. A key constraint is that only the pre-existing factual outcome y(t) can be queried for each unit—no counterfactual intervention is possible.

## Method

Causal-EPIG operationalizes the Causal Objective Alignment principle by adapting Expected Predictive Information Gain to target causal quantities directly in prediction space rather than model parameters. It yields two acquisition strategies: Causal-EPIG-μ, which measures expected information gain about the joint potential-outcome response pair (f(x*,0), f(x*,1)), and Causal-EPIG-τ, which measures expected information gain about the CATE τ(x*) = f(x*,1) − f(x*,0) directly. For GP-based estimators (CMGP, NSGP), mutual information is computed in closed form from the analytical posterior covariance; for sample-based estimators (BCF), a Gaussian approximation fitted to paired MCMC posterior draws provides a tractable covariance-based MI estimate. Theoretically, Prop. 4.1 shows that minimizing expected CATE estimation error is equivalent to minimizing integrated posterior CATE variance (which depends on both marginal outcome variances and their posterior covariance), and Thm. 4.3 establishes a finite-budget posterior uncertainty contraction bound under a submodularity condition for the global PO-based variant.

## Results

Across 180 dataset-seed combinations (4 datasets × 3 estimators × 10 seeds × 2 shift settings), Causal-EPIG-τ achieves the best average Nemenyi rank of 3.03 and significantly outperforms all 11 baselines (p < 0.05) with an 80.6% pairwise win rate. Causal-EPIG-μ follows with rank 3.98 and a 72.1% win rate over 8 baselines; neither variant significantly loses to any baseline. The primary metric is √PEHE (root mean squared CATE error) evaluated on the target set, with results reported as mean ± std over 10 runs. On semi-synthetic IHDP and ACTG-175, both Causal-EPIG variants achieve competitive or leading performance, with Causal-EPIG-μ particularly strong in the lower signal-to-noise ACTG setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-EPIG-τ dominates all five baseline families across the synthetic benchmarks (CausalBALD, Hahn) under both regular and distribution-shift settings, with the 80.6% win rate confirming consistent superiority in √PEHE. Against Causal-BALD variants (τ-BALD, μ-BALD, Propensity, μρ-BALD, μπ-BALD), Causal-EPIG-τ wins on the majority of dataset-estimator combinations, though on IHDP the Propensity baseline remains competitive due to the dataset's strong selection-bias structure. Causal-EPIG-μ is slightly attenuated relative to Causal-EPIG-τ when paired with BCF, likely because BCF's explicit separation of prognostic (μ) and treatment (τ) effects makes joint potential-outcome targeting less direct. Against Causal-EIG (parameter-space information gain about θ_τ) and factual EPIG (prediction-focused but targeting factual outcomes rather than causal quantities), both Causal-EPIG variants show clear gains, supporting the paper's core claim that prediction-space causal targeting outperforms parameter-space or factual-outcome targeting. The coreset/QHTE diversity-based baseline and the sign-ambiguity (γ-acquisition) baseline are consistently outperformed, as their objectives (geometric coverage or sign uncertainty) remain indirect proxies for CATE estimation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-oriented Bayesian active learning framework (EPIG, Smith et al. 2023) by redirecting its information target from factual outcomes to causal quantities—specifically the joint potential-outcome surfaces or the CATE estimand itself. It departs from the parameter-space causal acquisition line (Causal-BALD, Causal-EIG) by operating in prediction space rather than parameter space, and from standard EPIG by replacing factual prediction targets with causal ones. The theoretical analysis maps the global variant onto the transductive active learning (TAL) framework of Hübotter et al. 2024, while the Gaussian approximation for sample-based estimators follows Kirsch 2023 and Jesson et al. 2021.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, estimator-agnostic recipe for sample-efficient CATE estimation when outcome measurement is costly, with theoretical guarantees linking acquisition to posterior CATE variance contraction. The context-dependent trade-off between the comprehensive (μ) and focused (τ) strategies—governed by the base estimator and how treatment-effect signal is organized relative to the full outcome structure—offers practical guidance rather than a one-size-fits-all rule. Key limitations include the no-unobserved-confounding assumption, reliance on the base estimator's uncertainty quality (especially under misspecification), and the Gaussian approximation for tree-based models. Open questions include extending to hidden confounding, robust estimators like CausalPFN, and adaptive experimental design where treatment assignment is also a decision variable.
