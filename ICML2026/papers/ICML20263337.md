## Problem

Standard Transformers are provably limited to AC⁰ (hard attention) or TC⁰ (soft attention) circuit complexity, which prevents them from uniformly representing periodic and sequential logic such as parity, modular counting, and all regular languages. This 'Regular Gap' manifests empirically as catastrophic length generalization failure: models trained on short contexts collapse to random chance on longer sequences for algorithmic tasks. The paper asks whether a minimal architectural augmentation can close this gap while preserving the O(log T) parallel training efficiency that distinguishes Transformers from sequential RNNs.

## Input / Output

The architecture consumes a discrete token sequence x = (x₁, …, x_T) over a finite alphabet Σ. The rational head maintains a low-dimensional state vector h_t ∈ ℝ^d (typically d ∈ [4, 32]) that evolves via input-dependent matrix multiplication, while the Transformer backbone produces standard hidden representations z_t^(l) ∈ ℝ^{d_model}. Outputs are task-dependent: a scalar class label via a linear readout on the final rational state (for language recognition), an exact integer value (for arithmetic evaluation), or the augmented sequence representations at each position. A key design constraint is that the rational state is computed entirely from the input tokens via a parallel associative scan, independent of the Transformer's layer-wise computation, so no positional encodings are needed for the state-tracking stream.

## Method

Rational Transductors introduce a dual-stream architecture where a Transformer backbone handles semantic mixing and a 'Rational Head' — a linear matrix-valued recurrence h_t = M_{x_t} h_{t-1} grounded in Weighted Finite Automata — handles sequential state tracking. The critical integration mechanism is Deep Rational Injection: at every Transformer layer l, the rational state is projected via a layer-specific linear map W_proj^(l) and added to the hidden state, ensuring uncorrupted state information is available at all depths without signal attenuation from repeated layer normalizations. Transition matrices are parameterized via a Unified Scaled Cayley form M_t = g_t · (I + A_t)(I − A_t)⁻¹ where A_t is skew-symmetric, structurally guaranteeing unitary dynamics (g_t = 1) for infinite-horizon memory or contractive dynamics (g_t < 1) for forgetting, while preventing gradient explosion. Because the recurrence is strictly linear (no tanh/sigmoid in the state update), the entire state history is computed via parallel associative scans in O(log T) depth, and the architecture provably captures all regular languages, NC¹-complete problems, and exact modular arithmetic — capabilities strictly beyond AC⁰/TC⁰ Transformers — while remaining strictly less expressive than general non-linear RNNs.

## Results

On Modulo-5 Counting, the Rational Transductor achieves 100% accuracy while the Standard Transformer collapses to random chance (20%). On length generalization (trained at L=40, tested at L=1000, a 25× extrapolation), the Rational Transductor maintains >99% accuracy whereas the Transformer drops to random guessing. On 1000-digit Long-Integer Addition, the Universal Rational Transductor achieves 100% sequence-level accuracy while the Standard Transformer drops to 0% outside its training window (L=20). In latency benchmarks, the Rational Transductor's parallel scan outperforms the Sequential RNN for T>512 and avoids the quadratic wall of FlashAttention at T=32,768.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Rational Transductor against two primary baselines: the Standard Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the FlashAttention Transformer (Dao et al., 2022), plus a Sequential RNN for latency. On all algorithmic tasks (Modulo-5 Counting, Length Generalization, Long-Integer Addition), the Rational Transductor decisively wins: 100% vs. 20% on Modulo-5, >99% vs. random chance at 25× training length, and 100% vs. 0% on 1000-digit addition. The Standard Transformer fails uniformly because its AC⁰/TC⁰ expressivity cannot represent cyclic group operations or maintain state beyond its training horizon. On computational efficiency, the Rational Transductor's O(log T) parallel scan outperforms the Sequential RNN (O(T)) for sequences longer than T=512, while the FlashAttention Transformer (O(T²)) hits a quadratic latency wall at extreme lengths (T=32,768). The paper notes that SSMs like Mamba share the same time-varying linear recurrence but are not directly benchmarked, as the Rational Transductor serves as a minimal theoretical proxy to isolate automata-theoretic mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the State Space Model / linear RNN lineage (S4, Mamba, H3, RWKV) and the formal language theory of Weighted Finite Automata and Rational Power Series (Schützenberger, 1961; Fliess, 1974). It departs from interleaved SSM architectures (Mamba, H3) by adopting a 'sidecar' design where the linear recurrence is strictly input-driven and computed in parallel via associative scans, rather than cascaded through layers. The theoretical motivation draws directly on the expressivity lower bounds for Transformers (Hahn, 2020; Merrill & Sabharwal, 2023; Chiang, 2024), which show self-attention is limited to AC⁰/TC⁰, and provides the first rigorous automata-theoretic proof that a linear recurrence sidecar is sufficient to close the 'Regular Gap' and capture all regular languages and NC¹-complete problems. The Scaled Cayley parameterization and the Krohn-Rhodes decomposition argument are novel contributions that ground the architecture in algebraic automata theory rather than signal processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded answer to a well-known practical failure mode of Transformers (brittleness on sequential/state-tracking tasks) by showing that a minimal linear recurrence sidecar, not a more complex SSM, is sufficient for all regular languages. The 'sidecar vs. interleaved' design distinction is practically important: it preserves O(log T) parallel training without the sequential layer-wise bottleneck that stacked SSMs reintroduce. However, all empirical validation is on synthetic tasks (modulo counting, integer addition, base-2 evaluation) with small models; the paper explicitly defers large-scale NLP pretraining to future work, leaving open whether the theoretical guarantees (exact group-theoretic solutions, length-invariant error bounds) survive the noise and optimization challenges of real-world training. The inability to handle context-free languages (no unbounded stack) is a deliberate limitation that constrains applicability to hierarchical or nested-structure tasks.
