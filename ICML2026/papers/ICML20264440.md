## Problem

Error feedback (EF) and EF21 are two widely used mechanisms to compensate for gradient compression in distributed optimization, but their relative worst-case performance in the multi-agent setting remained poorly characterized. Prior tight convergence analyses were restricted to the single-agent regime (n=1), leaving open whether the number of agents, statistical heterogeneity (differing local minimizers), or heterogeneous regularity parameters (agent-specific smoothness/strong-convexity constants) alter the optimal step size and contraction rate. This paper closes that gap by providing method-specific tight guarantees for both algorithms in the multi-agent setting and by exhibiting counterexamples showing that classic EF provably fails under statistical heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a finite-sum objective with n local functions each in F_{μ,L} (or with agent-specific parameters F_{μ^(i),L^(i)}), a contractive compression operator C satisfying E‖x−C(x)‖²≤ε‖x‖², and the algorithm's step size η. The outputs are closed-form optimal step sizes, worst-case one-step contraction factors ρ⋆ (with multi-step tightness ρ⋆^k), and explicit quadratic Lyapunov functions over the algorithm state (iterate, local gradients, and error/estimator vectors). For general heterogeneous regularity parameters where no closed-form proof is available, the outputs are stated as empirical laws validated by numerical PEP solves, including a cubic polynomial whose largest real root gives the n=2 rate.

## Method

The core technique is the construction of worst-case-optimal Lyapunov functions within a structured candidate class for each algorithm, combined with sum-of-squares algebraic identities that certify the contraction inequality. For EF21 (Theorem 3.1), the Lyapunov function couples the squared norm of the summed local gradients with the per-agent estimator errors, and the proof decomposes the one-step change into nonnegative sum-of-squares terms with multipliers derived from the interpolation inequalities of F_{μ,L} and the contractive compression bound. For EF under statistical homogeneity (Theorem 3.4), a different Lyapunov structure involving the summed error-feedback state and shifted iterates yields the identical optimal rate, recovering the single-agent result for any n. The Lyapunov structures were discovered via SDP-based certificate search (log-det heuristic with sparsification) and then proved analytically; tightness is established by showing that identical-function instances replicate the single-agent worst case, and all formal statements carry CAS or PEP proof certificates. For heterogeneous regularity parameters, the paper provides a linear-compressor corollary (Corollary 3.2) via an averaged-parameter reduction, plus empirically validated laws for the general case including an explicit cubic rate formula for n=2.

## Results

The paper establishes that under homogeneous regularity parameters (shared L, μ across agents), the worst-case contraction factor for both EF21 and EF is ρ⋆ = √ε + ((1−√ε)/2)·((κ−1)/(κ+1))²·Ψ(κ,ε), with optimal step size η⋆ = (2/(L+μ))·((1−√ε)/(1+√ε)), and this rate is provably independent of the number of agents n. For EF21, this holds even under statistical heterogeneity (differing local minimizers), while classic EF requires the shared-minimizer (interpolation) assumption. For the n=2 heterogeneous-regularity case, the optimal rate is characterized as the largest real root of an explicit cubic polynomial whose coefficients depend on the per-agent (L⁽ⁱ⁾, μ⁽ⁱ⁾) parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is with the distributed EF21 analysis of Richtárik et al. (2021). Figure 3 shows that the paper's empirical-law rate (Empirical Law 4.3) requires strictly fewer iterations to reach a fixed target accuracy: the ratio log(ρ_EF21)/log(ρ⋆) is consistently below 1 across all tested heterogeneity levels (e.g., with L⁽¹⁾=1, L⁽²⁾∈{1,5,500}, the ratio drops well below 1 as heterogeneity increases), meaning the tight rate outperforms the Richtárik et al. bound by a non-trivial margin. The paper also recovers the single-agent tight rates of Berg Thomsen et al. (2025) exactly for any n≥1 under homogeneous parameters, confirming that the multi-agent worst case does not degrade beyond the single-agent bound in that regime. Under statistical heterogeneity, classic EF provably fails (2-cycle counterexample), whereas EF21 retains the same optimal rate as the homogeneous case, giving EF21 a clear structural advantage. For deterministic linear compressors with heterogeneous regularity, both methods achieve the same linear rate via an averaged-parameter reduction (Corollary 3.2), though in that specific regime plain compressed GD actually outperforms both error-feedback variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent tight convergence analysis of Berg Thomsen et al. (2025) for EF and EF21 to the multi-agent (n > 1) distributed setting, using the same PEP/SDP-based Lyapunov framework. It builds on Richtárik et al. (2021), who introduced EF21 and established its equivalence to EF under deterministic linear compressors, and on the broader literature of contractive-compression convergence guarantees (Stich et al., 2018; Karimireddy et al., 2019; Beznosikov et al., 2023). The key departure from prior work is the identification of worst-case *optimal* Lyapunov functions and step sizes in the multi-agent regime, rather than merely providing upper bounds, and the explicit demonstration that classic EF provably fails under statistical heterogeneity while EF21 does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the worst-case contraction factor is independent of the number of agents n (under homogeneous regularity parameters) provides a clean unification: the single-agent theory fully characterizes the multi-agent worst case, simplifying the theoretical landscape for practitioners. The counterexample showing EF's 2-cycle under statistical heterogeneity gives a concrete, minimal justification for preferring EF21 in heterogeneous federated settings. Open questions include rigorously proving the empirical laws for general heterogeneous regularity parameters (currently supported only by numerical PEP evidence), extending tight guarantees beyond deterministic linear compressors, and establishing method-agnostic communication-complexity lower bounds that apply across all compressed distributed algorithms rather than being method-specific.
