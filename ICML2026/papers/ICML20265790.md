## Problem

In query-based LLM-conditioned segmentation, language semantics are typically applied only as static prompts or post-hoc matching signals, leaving the iterative mask generation process dominated by visual cues. This decoupling causes a systematic failure mode—semantic misalignment—where high-quality mask candidates are generated but incorrectly selected, as evidenced by near-identical oracle upper bounds (~91%) across methods despite large performance gaps. The paper shows that the bottleneck lies in candidate selection rather than candidate generation, and that language and vision need to interact bidirectionally throughout decoding.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text instruction containing an explicit phrase span delimited by <p>...</p> tokens (e.g., 'segment <p>the red car</p>') plus a <SEG> token marking the output position. Each forward pass processes a single referring expression, producing one pixel-wise segmentation mask. The phrase-span markers are essential for extracting phrase-level condition embeddings from the LLM hidden states, which are then projected into the 256-dim decoder space via a two-layer MLP.

## Method

FlowSeg introduces Bidirectional Semantic Flow (BSF), a decoder-level mechanism where LLM-derived condition embeddings and query representations co-evolve across all Mask2Former decoder layers. At each layer, queries first attend to visual features, then attend to condition embeddings via semantic cross-attention; an adaptive fusion gate dynamically balances the visual and semantic contributions before self-attention. Crucially, condition embeddings are also updated by attending back to the refined queries (condition refinement), closing a vision-to-language feedback loop that has no counterpart in prior cross-modal attention designs where language remains fixed. A lightweight Boundary-Aware Refinement module then applies a bounded residual correction restricted to morphologically detected boundary pixels, improving local precision without perturbing confident interiors. The full system uses Qwen3-4B as the LLM, SigLIP2-so400m and SAM-ViT-Large as dual encoders, and is trained in three stages (segmentor pre-training, vision-language alignment, multi-task joint training) with Hungarian matching and deep supervision.

## Results

On referring expression segmentation, FlowSeg achieves 85.8/87.4/84.8 cIoU on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 80.2/84.5/76.9 on RefCOCO+, and 86.5/86.1 on RefCOCOg val/test, outperforming the prior SOTA X-SAM by 0.8%, 2.7%, and 2.4% average cIoU respectively. On the reasoning segmentation benchmark (ReasonSeg test), FlowSeg reaches 54.7% cIoU and 60.5% gIoU, surpassing X-SAM (41.0% cIoU) by 13.7 points. On COCO-Panoptic generic segmentation, FlowSeg achieves 56.1 PQ and 67.4 mIoU, matching or exceeding all MLLM-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowSeg outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary referring expression segmentation task. Against the closest competitor X-SAM (Wang et al., 2025), FlowSeg gains +0.7 to +1.4 cIoU on RefCOCO splits, +2.2 to +2.5 on RefCOCO+, and +2.7 to +2.2 on RefCOCOg, with the largest margins on the harder RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg benchmarks. On ReasonSeg test, FlowSeg (54.7 cIoU) surpasses X-SAM (41.0) by 13.7 points and LISA-7B finetuned (48.4) by 6.3 points; HyperSeg reports only validation results (56.7 cIoU) so a direct test-set comparison is not available. Among earlier MLLM-based methods, FlowSeg leads SAM4MLLM-7B by 6.2–11.9 cIoU, PSALM by 2.2–12.1, and GSVA-7B by 9.4–14.4 across the three RefCOCO variants. On COCO-Panoptic generic segmentation, FlowSeg (56.1 PQ, 47.2 AP, 67.4 mIoU) beats all MLLM-based baselines (X-SAM, PSALM, OMG-LLaVA, SEEM, X-Decoder) but trails the dedicated non-MLLM Mask2Former (57.8 PQ, 48.6 AP), which is specifically trained for close-set panoptic segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowSeg builds directly on the query-based LLM-segmentor scaffold established by X-SAM (dual-encoder design, three-stage training, Mask2Former-style decoder) and the special-token protocol from LISA, but departs fundamentally in how language semantics participate in decoding. Prior methods (PSALM, HyperSeg, X-SAM, Sa2VA) treat LLM-derived condition embeddings as static keys for post-hoc matching or as fixed cross-attention sources that never change during iterative refinement. FlowSeg's Bidirectional Semantic Flow inverts this: condition embeddings are updated layer-by-layer by emerging visual evidence while simultaneously guiding query refinement, creating a co-evolution loop rather than a one-way information flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle upper-bound analysis (~91% cIoU for both X-SAM and FlowSeg) provides compelling evidence that the bottleneck in query-based LLM-conditioned segmentation is candidate selection, not candidate generation—a reframing that could redirect future work toward better matching mechanisms rather than stronger mask decoders. The 44.6% recovery of prior-work failure cases (cIoU < 0.5) demonstrates practical impact on the hardest examples. Key limitations include reliance on explicit phrase markers (<p>, </p>) that reduce applicability to unconstrained conversational settings, and the open question of whether bidirectional flow introduces semantic contamination in dense multi-entity decoding scenarios where multiple condition embeddings coexist in a single decoder pass.
