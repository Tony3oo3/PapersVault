## Problem

Existing diffusion-based object insertion methods for remote sensing imagery suffer from three critical physical inconsistencies: semantically implausible placements (e.g., aircraft in forests), radiometric mismatches where shadow orientations and shading contradict the scene's solar geometry, and textural discontinuity producing a visible 'sticker effect.' These failures arise because general-purpose generative models lack explicit priors for the rigid opto-physical constraints of overhead imagery, limiting their utility for downstream detection despite achieving reasonable visual fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 remote sensing background image, a 512×512 target object image, and a category set defining semantically valid placement regions, and produces a composite image with the object inserted at a physically coherent pose. The Planner first outputs a geometric pose (center coordinates and orientation angle) that constrains where and how the object is placed; the generation stage then synthesizes the object content within the corresponding mask in the DiT latent space. The framework operates modality-agnostically, requiring only a swap of the upstream segmenter when transferring from optical RGB to SAR imagery.

## Method

PDA decomposes the insertion problem into three synergistic modules on top of an OminiControl DiT backbone fine-tuned with LoRA. The Planner (P) uses a SAM3-based open-vocabulary segmenter to extract valid semantic regions, then applies Euclidean Distance Transform to find the ridge anchor with maximal clearance and aligns orientation orthogonally to the local gradient. The Decoupling module (D) employs Asymmetric Spectral Adaptation: a dual-LoRA stream where the DWT low-frequency component adjusts global illumination while the full latent preserves structural geometry, and an implicit solar context vector extracted from the masked background is projected to affine (γ, β) modulation on the Value matrices in DiT attention layers. The Assimilation module (A) defines a local annular neighborhood via morphological dilation, computes a Gram-matrix texture loss in the latent space, and injects its gradient as an energy-based correction into the flow-matching ODE velocity field, steering the generation trajectory to match local micro-texture statistics without requiring backbone feature extraction.

## Results

On the optical remote sensing object insertion benchmark (iSAID/SAMRS), PDA achieves the best whole-image FID of 9.73 (15.7% lower than the strongest baseline OminiControl at 12.05) and the best insertion-region PSNR of 20.87 dB (15.5% higher than OminiControl's 17.90 dB). In downstream oriented object detection on the MAR20-11-FewShot benchmark (30 images/category), PDA-augmented training boosts average mAP50 by +17.07 points over the real-data-only baseline, reaching 80.26% with YOLO26 and 77.36% with S2ANet. Cross-modality experiments on SAR (HRSID, SSDD) confirm consistent gains, with zero-shot transfer to SSDD ship detection improving YOLO26 mAP50 from 79.62% to 84.28%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDA outperforms all eight generative baselines on every reported metric in Table 1. The closest competitor, OminiControl (the architectural backbone PDA fine-tunes), is surpassed by +3.19 dB in whole-image PSNR, −2.32 in FID, and +2.97 dB in insertion-region PSNR; its main weakness is failure to adapt illumination to scene-specific solar context. Insert Anything (FID 11.54) retains coarse geometry but exhibits global color deviation and unrealistic hue shifts. MimicBrush achieves the highest whole-image SSIM (0.9337) largely by copying the frozen background, yet its insertion-region SSIM collapses to 0.3676, indicating it cannot generate valid objects. General-purpose editors (AnyDoor, Qwen Image Edit, UniCombine, ACE++) all show FID values above 18, reflecting structural breakdowns from lacking orthographic imaging priors. In downstream detection (Table 3), PDA consistently outperforms Copy-Paste, CutMix, and OminiControl across all four OBB detectors; notably, OminiControl actually degrades Rotated R-CNN from 58.13% to 55.22%, while PDA lifts it to 77.88%. On the SAR cross-modality benchmark (SSDD, Table 10), PDA also beats OminiControl on average mAP50 (82.54% vs. 81.56%) and Copy-Paste (75.35%) without any SAR-specific architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDA builds directly on OminiControl's DiT backbone and its unified sequence processing for subject-driven condition injection, extending it with three domain-specific modules that enforce physics-aware constraints absent from general-purpose insertion methods. Conceptually, it departs from the black-box paradigm of prior remote sensing insertion work (AeroGen, CRS-Diff, Control Copy-Paste) by explicitly factorizing the conditional generation into geometric planning (EDT-based placement), spectral decoupling (DWT + implicit solar context via affine modulation), and texture assimilation (Gram-matrix guidance in flow-matching latent space). The Planner's use of Euclidean Distance Transform for topologically valid pose selection is a repurposing of classical computational geometry into a generative pipeline, whereas prior methods relied on manual or heuristic placement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physically inconsistent synthetic data can actively harm downstream detection (OminiControl degrades Rotated R-CNN from 58.13% to 55.22% mAP50), establishing that visual plausibility alone is insufficient for augmentation in remote sensing. The ablation revealing that spectral/radiometric consistency (ASA) is the dominant bottleneck—contributing ~3 dB PSNR and 19% FID reduction versus NATA's marginal gains—redirects future work toward illumination modeling rather than texture refinement. Open questions include handling bare backgrounds where no shadow-casting cues exist (7% failure rate), extending binary masks to soft boundaries for amorphous targets, and whether explicit solar azimuth conditioning could close the remaining gap.
