## Problem

Unlike supervised learning, where test error reliably approximates population error, generative model evaluation lacks a well-defined performance metric and it is unclear whether any chosen metric can be reliably estimated from finite held-out samples. This paper formalizes the notion of 'evaluability'—whether a metric can be reliably compared across models from finite data—and establishes which commonly used metrics (IPMs, Rényi divergences, KL divergence, perplexity) are or are not evaluable, and under what conditions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes two candidate models (probability distributions over a data space), IID evaluation data drawn from a ground-truth distribution, and a specified evaluation metric (e.g., an IPM with a given test function class, or an α-Rényi divergence). Rather than producing a single scalar score, the output is a guarantee: either the metric is strongly evaluable (exact ranking with arbitrary precision) or weakly evaluable (ranking up to a multiplicative factor c and additive error ε), accompanied by an explicit sample complexity bound m(ε, δ, c). For negative results, the output is a proof that no evaluation algorithm can achieve any finite multiplicative factor.

## Method

The paper introduces a formal definition of evaluability (strong and c-weak) and connects it to the classical notion of estimability. For IPMs, it establishes a strict dichotomy: binary test classes with finite VC dimension yield strong evaluability via uniform convergence, while unbounded VC dimension yields only 3-weak evaluability (optimal, via Bousquet et al.'s density estimation algorithm); real-valued test classes are characterized by the γ-fat-shattering dimension. For Rényi and KL divergences, it proves fundamental non-evaluability by constructing pairs of distributions whose metric values are determined by exponentially rare points invisible in any finite sample. For perplexity (nll score), it shows geometric misalignment with TV distance and β-restricted KL divergence, then recovers a positive TV result under a Δ-closeness-in-ratio assumption that ensures one candidate model has bounded likelihood ratios relative to the ground truth.

## Results

The paper establishes a strict dichotomy for IPMs with binary test classes: strong evaluability (arbitrary precision) holds if and only if the test class has finite VC dimension, with sample complexity O(d·log(1/ε)/ε²); otherwise only 3-weak evaluability is achievable, and the factor of 3 is provably optimal. For real-valued bounded test classes, strong evaluability requires finite γ-fat-shattering dimension for all γ ∈ (0, 1/2), with sample complexity O(ε⁻²(d·log²(4d/ε²) + log(1/δ))). In sharp contrast, α-Rényi divergences (α > 1), KL divergence, and the coverage profile are fundamentally not weakly evaluable from any finite sample, as their values can be determined by arbitrarily rare events. The nll/perplexity score also fails to weakly evaluate TV distance or β-restricted KL divergence without additional assumptions.

**Table 1: Summary of evaluability results for different evaluation metrics.**

| Evaluation Metric | Evaluability Guarantee |
|---|---|
| IPM w.r.t. binary-valued F | Estimable ⇒ strongly evaluable if VC(F) < ∞; 3-weakly evaluable if VC(F) = ∞ (Thm 3.2) |
| IPM w.r.t. real-valued F | Estimable ⇒ strongly evaluable if Pdim_γ(F) < ∞ for all γ ∈ (0, 1/2]; 3-weakly evaluable if Pdim_γ(F) unbounded for some γ (Prop 3.4) |
| Fixed Statistical Test w.r.t. g | Estimable ⇒ strongly evaluable when g bounded; not weakly evaluable when g sufficiently unbounded (Thm 3.8) |
| α-Rényi divergence (α > 1) | Not weakly evaluable (Thm 4.2) |
| Coverage Profile | Not weakly evaluable (Prop 4.5); strongly evaluable under margin + finite-support assumptions (Thm 4.8) |
| Total Variation Distance | Not weakly evaluable by perplexity (Thm 5.3); strongly evaluable under Δ-closeness (Prop 5.6) |
| β-Restricted KL Divergence | Not weakly evaluable by perplexity (Thm 5.3); not strongly evaluable (Thm 5.4) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or method comparisons. Instead, the paper draws a sharp theoretical boundary between two families of evaluation metrics. Test-based metrics (IPMs, fixed statistical tests) are evaluable under natural complexity conditions on the test class, with the key tradeoff being between the expressiveness of the test class and the sample complexity required. Direct distributional-similarity metrics (Rényi divergences, KL divergence, coverage profile) are fundamentally not evaluable from finite samples, regardless of sample size, because rare events with vanishing probability can dominate the metric value. The paper also positions perplexity/nll as a score function that is geometrically misaligned with both TV distance (bounded vs. unbounded) and β-restricted KL (sensitive to single points vs. tolerant of β-fraction mass), explaining why perplexity rankings can be unreliable in practice. The only positive result for perplexity requires a Δ-closeness-in-ratio assumption ensuring one candidate model is already close to the ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical uniform convergence theory (VC dimension, fat-shattering dimension) from the problem of estimating a single population-level quantity to the distinct problem of reliably *ranking* two models from finite samples, which the authors formalize as 'evaluability.' It builds directly on Bousquet et al. (2019), whose optimal 3-approximation algorithm for density estimation is repurposed to prove the 3-weak evaluability of IPMs with unbounded VC dimension, and on Alon et al. (1997) for fat-shattering-based uniform convergence guarantees. The paper also connects to the IPM literature (Müller, 1997) and information-theoretic divergences (Rényi, 1961), but departs from prior work by showing that the most natural distributional metrics (Rényi, KL) are fundamentally unevaluable, a question not previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative results for Rényi and KL divergences reveal a fundamental limitation: the most widely-used distributional similarity metrics cannot be reliably evaluated from finite samples because their values are determined by rare events. This has direct practical implications for the ubiquitous use of perplexity in language model evaluation, showing it is geometrically misaligned with TV distance and restricted KL without strong assumptions. Key open questions include whether strong evaluability implies estimability, whether the 3-approximation factor is optimal for real-valued IPMs, and whether any score function can evaluate β-restricted KL divergence. The framework provides a principled lens for future work on generative model evaluation metrics.
