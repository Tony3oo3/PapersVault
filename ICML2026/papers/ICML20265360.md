## Problem

Existing PPI prediction methods encode proteins in a flat manner (e.g., simple averaging of residue embeddings), ignoring the intrinsic hierarchical organization where meso-scale functional motifs mediate interactions. Additionally, while sequence, structure, and function modalities carry complementary information, prior multimodal approaches (e.g., MASSA) fuse them only at a coarse global level, diluting the synergistic potential of fine-grained motif-level interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265360/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per protein: amino acid sequences (encoded by ESM2-3b), 3D residue coordinates from AlphaFold (encoded by pre-trained MAPE-PPI), and Gene Ontology functional annotations (encoded by OntoProtein), all processed on a shared protein graph with sequential, radius, and K-nearest edges. Motif occurrences are identified via FIMO against the PROSITE database (p-value threshold 1e-4). The output is a set of partner-specific protein embeddings (each protein receives a different embedding depending on its interaction partner) that feed into a GIN for multi-label prediction across seven interaction types (Activation, Binding, Catalysis, Expression, Inhibition, Ptmod, Reaction).

## Method

MMM-PPI constructs protein embeddings bottom-up across three scales: (i) micro-scale, three modality-specific heterogeneous GNNs produce residue embeddings on a shared 3D-aware protein graph; (ii) meso-scale, FIMO-identified motifs are encoded by summing their constituent multimodal residue embeddings; (iii) macro-scale, a novel pairwise motif co-attention mechanism (inspired by VQA co-attention but avoiding dense motif-to-motif alignment) computes partner-specific protein embeddings by conditioning motif importance weights on a global interaction vector (element-wise product of naive protein embeddings). Training is decoupled into two stages: the encoder is pre-trained on labeled PPI pairs via cross-entropy, then frozen while a 2-layer GIN is trained on the PPI network graph. This compositional motif-level design enables generalization to unseen proteins, as novel proteins can be decomposed into familiar local substructures learned during training.

## Results

On the transductive setting, MMM-PPI achieves an average Micro-F1 of 85.60% across three PPI datasets (SHS27k, SHS148k, STRING), outperforming the best baseline MicroEnvPPI (84.30%) by 1.30 points. The advantage is most pronounced on challenging BFS and DFS partitions, where MMM-PPI scores 79.09% and 75.93% on SHS27k versus 76.22% and 74.57% for MicroEnvPPI. In the inductive setting (unseen PPIs), MMM-PPI achieves 82.06% average Micro-F1, surpassing the best baseline MASSA (79.99%) by 2.07 points, with the largest gap on SHS27k Random (87.27% vs. 83.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMM-PPI outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset, partition, and evaluation setting. Against the strongest multi-modality baseline MASSA (the only other method using all three modalities), MMM-PPI gains +1.56 points on average in the transductive setting and +2.07 points in the inductive setting, with the largest margins on BFS partitions (e.g., +2.42 on SHS27k BFS transductive: 79.09 vs. 76.67). Compared to the best two-modality baselines (MicroEnvPPI, SaProt), MMM-PPI leads by +1.30 to +1.47 points on average, indicating that the third modality (function) contributes meaningfully. Against single-modality baselines (ESM2-3b for sequence, MAPE-PPI for structure), the gap widens to +2.59 to +2.82 points on average, underscoring the benefit of multimodal integration. In the inductive setting, where generalization to unseen PPIs is tested, MMM-PPI's advantage over the best baseline (MASSA) grows to +2.07 points on average, and it remains the top method in nearly all individual partition settings, demonstrating robustness to partitioning strategy. Even without pre-trained modality models (Table 13), MMM-PPI still outperforms all non-pre-training baselines by +1.50 points on average, confirming that the hierarchical motif architecture itself provides substantial gains independent of pre-training quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the two-stage training paradigm and 3D residue coordinate framework of MAPE-PPI (Wu et al., 2024), and uses Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks (Zhang et al., 2019b) as the core residue-level encoder. It departs from the dominant flat-encoding paradigm in PPI prediction—where protein embeddings are obtained by simple averaging of residue representations—by introducing an explicit meso-scale motif layer between residues and the whole protein. It also moves beyond the coarse-grained global multimodal fusion used by MASSA (Hu et al., 2023) and similar methods, instead performing fine-grained multimodal integration at the motif level via a context-aware co-attention mechanism inspired by VQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical motif-based encoding addresses a biologically grounded gap: PPIs are often mediated by specific meso-scale motifs whose behavior is context-dependent, yet prior methods collapse this information into flat residue averages. The motif recurrence argument provides a principled explanation for why the model generalizes to unseen proteins and data-scarce settings, which is practically important for drug discovery and synthetic biology workflows. Limitations include reliance on sequence-based FIMO/PROSITE motif detection rather than 3D geometric motif mining, and the co-attention mechanism's effectiveness depends on the quality of upstream pre-trained modality encoders. Open questions include whether explicit 3D motif discovery could further improve performance and whether the approach extends to other hierarchical biological structures.
