## Problem

CLIP models inherit spurious correlations (e.g., background–object or gender–occupation associations) that severely degrade worst-group accuracy, yet existing debiasing methods either require costly fine-tuning or apply global transformations to the pooled embedding space without identifying which internal components encode the bias. No prior approach localizes the specific attention heads responsible for spurious versus target signals and corrects them independently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265527/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of a [class]') and outputs a class label or a ranked image list for retrieval. At inference time, the debiasing operates on intermediate attention-head activations inside the ViT encoder: a small set of labeled samples from the negatively-associated subgroup (G_N) is used once to identify which heads to modify, but the actual correction (mean ablation and knowledge injection) is sample-independent and applied identically to every test image. No model parameters are updated; only the residual-stream contributions of selected heads are altered before aggregation into the final image representation.

## Method

LTC (Locate-Then-Correct) exploits the linear decomposability of ViT representations into per-head contributions. First, Logit Lens computes each attention head's direct effect on the target-class logit; a contrastive comparison between correctly and incorrectly classified samples in the negatively-associated group G_N isolates spurious states (Z_S, whose contribution is larger on misclassified samples) from target states (Z_Y, whose contribution is larger on correct samples), using a thresholded mask to filter noise. Second, spurious states are corrected via mean ablation (replacing each head's activation with its dataset mean), while target states are enhanced via Knowledge Injection: LLM-generated discriminative text features are projected into the text embedding space, and the selected head activations are orthogonally projected onto these vectors before being added back. This 'diagnose-then-correct' pipeline requires no gradient updates, no group labels at test time, and leaves all other model components untouched, yielding over 50% worst-group accuracy gains on Waterbirds compared to non-training baselines.

## Results

On Waterbirds (binary background bias), LTC* achieves a worst-group accuracy of 73.3% (ViT-B/16), 75.5% (ViT-L/14), and 77.4% (ViT-H/14), reducing the group gap to 1.3, 8.5, and 3.0 respectively, compared to zero-shot gaps of 22.4, 27.8, and 19.2. On GenderBias-VL, LTC reduces the worst-occupation gender bias (B_T) from 74.0 to 10.0 on ViT-B/16 and from 67.7 to 27.4 on ViT-H/14, while also improving retrieval fairness (MaxSkew@10 drops from 38.2 to 17.2 on ViT-B/16). As a lightweight extension to fine-tuning, JTT-LTC reaches 86.4% WG accuracy on Waterbirds (ViT-B/16), surpassing Cont Adapter (83.2%) and JTT alone (72.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTC outperforms all four non-parameter-tuning baselines on Waterbirds worst-group accuracy: it beats TextSpan (62.3→73.3 on ViT-B/16), Ortho-Cali (68.1→73.3), and Roboshot (57.5→73.3), while also achieving the smallest group gap (1.3 vs. 14.4, 5.2, and 5.5 respectively). On GenderBias-VL, LTC dramatically reduces worst-occupation bias (B_T) to 10.0 on ViT-B/16, compared to Ortho-Cali (36.2), TextSpan (42.2), and Roboshot (65.2), with Roboshot actually worsening overall bias relative to zero-shot. As a fine-tuning extension, JTT-LTC surpasses Cont Adapter (86.4 vs. 83.2 WG on ViT-B/16) and JTT alone (86.4 vs. 72.3 WG) while using fewer hyperparameters. On the multi-class CounterAnimal dataset, LTC is the only non-tuning method that consistently improves over zero-shot CLIP across all three model sizes, whereas Roboshot and Ortho-Cali fail to close the easy-hard gap. The paper attributes Roboshot's underperformance to LLM safeguards that prevent reliable identification of spurious directions, and Ortho-Cali's failure on ViT-H/14 (WG drops to 18.1) to the difficulty of solving a single global projection across multiple spurious and target classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mechanistic interpretability framework of Elhage et al. (2021) and the Logit Lens technique (Nostalgebraist, 2020) to decompose CLIP's ViT representations into individual attention head contributions, extending Gandelsman et al.'s (2023) TextSpan approach by replacing its manual annotation step with an automated contrastive identification procedure. It departs from prior debiasing methods (Ortho-Cali, Roboshot) that operate on the global pooled embedding space by intervening on specific attention heads, and from Huang et al. (2025) by using a causally-motivated contrastive framework rather than simple per-head ablation. The knowledge injection component adapts the orthogonal projection strategy from Adila et al. (2023) but applies it at the head level rather than the full image representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that targeted, interpretable intervention on a small number of attention heads can outperform global projection methods for debiasing CLIP, offering practitioners a lightweight, training-free tool that also explains *why* specific components encode bias. The distinction between Z_S (spurious attribute alone) and Z_SY (spurious-target association) is a useful mechanistic finding, though its generalizability beyond the tested settings remains open. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for generating discriminative features, complications in multi-class settings where the counterfactual pairing is ambiguous, and the observation that LLM safety filters can prevent identification of gender as a spurious attribute. Whether the linear separability assumption and the contrastive identification procedure extend to deeper or non-ViT architectures is an open question.
