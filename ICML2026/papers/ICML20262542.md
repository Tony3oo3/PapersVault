## Problem

Semi-supervised 3D medical image segmentation suffers from a twofold overconfidence problem: algorithmically, mainstream pseudo-labeling frameworks conflate prediction confidence with uncertainty, causing miscalibrated high-confidence errors to be recycled as supervision in a confirmation-bias loop; strategically, the absence of dedicated validation sets in several benchmarks leads to test-set-based model selection, inflating reported performance and triggering an arms race of overfitting that obscures genuine progress.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262542/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical image volumes (CT or MRI) split into a small labeled set and a larger unlabeled set, processed via sliding windows of 112×112×80 (LA) or 96×96×96 (Pancreas-CT, BraTS2019) at isotropic 1 mm resolution. A shared VNet-style encoder feeds two parallel decoders with different upsampling operators, producing four prediction views (two student, two teacher) whose agreement and disagreement jointly drive reliability estimation. The output is a voxel-wise 3D segmentation mask, with the framework additionally producing per-voxel confidence and uncertainty scores used to gate pseudo-label supervision and guide targeted augmentation.

## Method

TCSeg introduces a dual-axis reliability engine that explicitly decouples confidence (the maximum of the ensemble-averaged prediction across four decoder views) from uncertainty (L1 divergence between paired decoders in both probability space and feature space, where feature-space disagreement is measured via cosine similarity to class prototypes computed from high-confidence voxels). Only voxels in the high-confidence, low-uncertainty quadrant receive pseudo-label supervision, while the other three regimes are handled by a tri-space calibration: probability-space confidence-band regularization bounds multi-branch variance, feature-space prototype alignment pulls same-class embeddings toward their centroid to filter semantically atypical predictions, and image-space reliability-driven CutMix targets cognitive blind spots (low-confidence/low-uncertainty and over-confident regions) with targeted structural perturbation. The two decoders operate in a reciprocal two-branch scheme where each alternately serves as supervisor and learner, and the overall loss combines supervised, pseudo-label, calibration, and mixing terms. The paper further advocates a multi-run evaluation protocol reporting median and extrema across five seeds for both best and final checkpoints to separate genuine gains from checkpoint cherry-picking.

## Results

Under the rigorous last-checkpoint protocol (5-run median DSC), TCSeg achieves 90.28% on LA-10%, 81.08% on Pancreas-CT-10%, and 86.47% on BraTS2019-20%, surpassing the nearest competitor (TraCoCo) by 1.0–1.8 percentage points. Under the best-checkpoint protocol, the median DSC reaches 90.73% (LA-10%), 82.22% (Pancreas-CT-10%), and 86.47% (BraTS2019-20%), rivaling or exceeding the single-run peaks of CC-Net and VerSemi. The multi-run standard deviation under the last protocol is tightly bounded (e.g., LA-10%: 89.86 ± 0.12; Pancreas-CT-10%: 80.86 ± 0.28), indicating stable convergence across seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the last-checkpoint protocol, TCSeg's median DSC of 81.08% on Pancreas-CT-10% exceeds TraCoCo (79.22%) by 1.86 points and CauSSL (72.89%) by 8.19 points; on LA-10% it leads TraCoCo (89.29%) by 0.99 points. On LA-20%, AUA (91.08%) slightly edges out TCSeg's median (90.83%), and on BraTS2019-20% TraCoCo (86.69%) marginally exceeds TCSeg's median (86.47%), though TCSeg's maximum (86.68%) is essentially tied. Under the best-checkpoint protocol, TCSeg's median on Pancreas-CT-10% (82.22%) surpasses CC-Net (81.93%) and VerSemi (78.62%), while on LA-10% CC-Net's single-run peak (90.82%) slightly exceeds TCSeg's median (90.73%) but falls below TCSeg's maximum (90.85%). The paper's central argument is that the last-protocol margins are more informative: TCSeg's consistent 1–2 point lead over TraCoCo across Pancreas-CT settings, with low run-to-run variance (±0.28–0.37), indicates the gains are not artifacts of favorable checkpoint selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCSeg departs from the dominant pseudo-labeling and consistency-regularization paradigm in semi-supervised 3D medical segmentation by challenging its core assumption that softmax confidence is a faithful proxy for pseudo-label correctness. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it reorganizes existing components (dual-decoder VNet, CutMix, class prototypes) around a novel dual-axis reliability principle that explicitly decouples confidence (ensemble consensus) from uncertainty (cross-view disagreement in both probability and feature spaces). The tri-space calibration (probability, feature, image) unifies these signals into a collaborative correction mechanism, and the paper additionally introduces a meta-level contribution: a multi-run, best-vs.-last checkpoint evaluation protocol that critiques the community's reliance on single-run, test-set-selected best-checkpoint reporting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The evaluation-methodology critique is arguably the paper's most impactful contribution: by showing that best-checkpoint, single-run reporting can inflate SOTA numbers and trigger an arms race of test-overfitting, it provides a concrete protocol (median and extrema over five seeds, both best and last checkpoints) that could reshape how the community benchmarks semi-supervised segmentation. The confidence-uncertainty decoupling addresses a real calibration failure mode in self-training, and the ablation showing feature-space prototypes are critical for low-contrast structures (Pancreas-CT drops from 81% to 59% without them) highlights a dataset-dependent importance that practitioners should consider. Open questions remain around adaptive threshold selection, out-of-distribution robustness under scanner/protocol shift, and whether the multi-run protocol will be widely adopted; the authors note they are preparing a systematic re-evaluation of 25+ methods under a unified protocol.
