## Problem

Existing world models (diffusion transformers, memory-augmented SSMs, recurrent models) fail to maintain consistent predictions over long horizons in partially observed dynamic environments: once objects leave the agent's field of view or the attention window, their state is lost and the model hallucinates new objects. The root cause is that no existing memory mechanism respects the time-parameterized symmetries (flows) governing both self-motion and external object dynamics, so information about out-of-view regions cannot be kept spatially aligned as the world evolves.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of partial first-person RGB observation frames (128×128 in 3D Block World; a fixed window smaller than the world canvas in 2D MNIST World) together with the agent's discrete action sequence (turn left/right, move forward, or do nothing in 3D; relative (x,y) offsets in 2D). It outputs predicted future RGB frames (20 frames at training length, extrapolated to 150–210 frames) and maintains a persistent structured latent memory map — a spatially organized 2D top-down token grid for the 3D setting or a set of stacked 'velocity channel' feature maps for the 2D setting — that is updated at every timestep by the agent's action and internal object flows.

## Method

FloWM introduces a flow-equivariant recurrent memory where the hidden state is organized into 'velocity channels,' each flowing according to its own vector field, so that external object motion is tracked even when unobserved. Self-motion equivariance is achieved by transforming the memory with the inverse of the agent's action (T_{a_t}^{-1}) before the update, keeping the memory in the agent's co-moving reference frame and making it spatially homomorphic to the world. The generalized recurrence h_{t+1}(ν) = T_{a_t}^{-1} ψ_1(ν) · U_θ[h_t(ν); E_θ[f_t, h_t](ν)] requires only that the encoder and update be group-equivariant and that the encoder perform a trivial lift to all velocity channels. Two instantiations are provided: a convolutional RNN for 2D and a ViT-based encoder/decoder for 3D, where the encoder learns equivariance empirically rather than relying on costly exact depth unprojection. Training is supervised next-frame prediction on fixed-length context, yet the structured memory enables stable rollouts far beyond the training horizon.

## Results

On the 3D Dynamic Block World benchmark (70 context frames), FloWM achieves MSE 0.000603, PSNR 32.19, and SSIM 0.9673 at the training horizon (70 frames), and maintains MSE 0.001539, PSNR 28.13, SSIM 0.9525 at 210 frames (3× length extrapolation). On the 2D MNIST World benchmark, FloWM achieves MSE 0.0005 at 20 frames and 0.0018 at 150 frames (7.5× extrapolation), while all baselines degrade to near-random performance. For downstream MPC planning on Block World, FloWM reaches a mean distance-to-optimal of 0.727, compared to 5.571 for DFoT and 6.449 for DreamerV3 RSSM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FloWM outperforms all three baselines by a wide margin on every metric. Against DFoT (History-Guided Diffusion Forcing), FloWM reduces MSE by ~19× at the training horizon (0.000603 vs. 0.011759) and by ~14× at 210-frame extrapolation, while DFoT's PSNR drops from 19.30 to 16.64 as it hallucinates new objects. Against DFoT-SSM (which adds a state-space memory module), FloWM's MSE is ~37× lower at 70 frames (0.000603 vs. 0.022616); the SSM memory fails to track out-of-view dynamics and its predictions plateau at the same error level regardless of horizon. Against DreamerV3's RSSM, FloWM achieves ~27× lower MSE and ~14 points higher PSNR, while the RSSM degrades to a blurry average of overlapping blocks. The most striking result is length extrapolation: FloWM's MSE increases only 2.5× from 70 to 210 frames, whereas DFoT's increases ~1.8× and DFoT-SSM's stays flat (indicating it has already saturated at its error floor). On the downstream planning task, FloWM's mean distance-to-optimal is 0.727 versus 5.571 (DFoT) and 6.449 (RSSM), confirming that accurate dynamic prediction translates directly into better action selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: flow equivariance (Keller, 2026), which extends group equivariance to time-parameterized sequence transformations, and generative world modeling with memory (DFoT, DFoT-SSM, WORLDMEM, Neural Map). Prior world models treat latent memory as unstructured—relying on sliding-window attention, SSM compression, or viewpoint-dependent storage—while prior equivariant world models (Neural Map, EgoMap) handle only static scenes with translation-only actions. FloWM departs by imposing flow equivariance as a hard architectural constraint on a recurrent latent map that simultaneously tracks self-motion and external object dynamics, formalizing what earlier 'neural map' approaches did heuristically within a general Lie-group framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why existing world models fail on long-horizon partially observed dynamics (information leaves the attention window and is irrecoverable) and shows that a flow-equivariant memory resolves this by keeping out-of-view state spatially aligned. The dramatic length generalization (trained on 20 prediction frames, stable at 150+) and the probe-verified learned equivariance (error dropping from 6.96 to 0.22) suggest that structural inductive biases can substitute for data scale in world modeling. Key limitations are the synthetic evaluation settings (MNIST digits, colored blocks), rigid-body actions with known parameterizations, and discrete velocity channels; extending to realistic open-world datasets, continuous action spaces, and stochastic dynamics remains open.
