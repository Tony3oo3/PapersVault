## Problem

Existing low-rank compression methods minimize weight-space error (e.g., Frobenius norm) without accounting for the input feature distribution, so small singular values may align with high-energy activation directions and cause disproportionate output distortion. In deep models this is compounded because each layer's approximation shifts the feature distribution seen by subsequent layers, yet prior methods (ASVD, SVD-LLM) use a fixed or pre-computed whitening that becomes stale as compression propagates. The paper asks how to establish a direct, layer-adaptive correspondence between rank truncation and actual output distortion.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weight matrices and a small calibration set (256 samples from WikiText-2) to obtain per-layer input activations. It processes layers sequentially: at each layer ℓ, it uses the actual (possibly already-compressed) output of layer ℓ−1 to compute a per-layer whitening matrix, so the input to the whitening step is the propagated compressed feature, not the original. The output is a pair of low-rank matrices (W_u, W_v) per layer whose product replaces the original weight, or equivalently a LoRA adapter initialization (A, B) for PEFT.

## Method

The core mechanism is a dynamic, distribution-aware whitening: for each layer ℓ, a Cholesky-based whitening matrix S_ℓ is computed from the empirical covariance of the actual compressed input X̂_{ℓ−1}, the weight is transformed to W̃_ℓ = W_ℓ S_ℓ^{-1}, standard truncated SVD is applied in this (approximately isotropic) space, and the result is mapped back as Ŵ_ℓ = W̃_ℓ^{(k)} S_ℓ. Because the whitening is recomputed from the propagated compressed features at every layer, the 'whitening drift' δ_ℓ that plagues static-whitening baselines is eliminated, and under ideal whitening the output reconstruction error reduces exactly to the Eckart–Young truncated-SVD error, giving a principled link between rank k and output distortion. Theoretically, the paper derives a global error bound showing that static whitening incurs an extra √(1+δ_ℓ) inflation factor per layer that compounds multiplicatively through the network, while distribution-aware whitening removes this factor. The same whitened-space SVD also yields a superior LoRA initialization, since the retained singular directions are aligned with the dominant signal under the current feature distribution rather than the raw weight geometry.

## Results

On WikiText-2 at 30% compression, the proposed method achieves perplexity of 23.70 on LLaMA3-8B (vs. 33.18 for SVD-LLM), 13.04 on Qwen2-7B (vs. 14.82), and 19.91 on Qwen2.5-32B (vs. 30.96). At 50% compression, the gap widens substantially: 83.95 vs. 3908.40 on LLaMA3-8B and 30.87 vs. 46.79 on Qwen2.5-32B. For LoRA-style PEFT initialization (rank 256, Vicuna-7B), the method achieves 0.384 GSM8K accuracy vs. 0.320 for PiSSA and 0.293 for vanilla LoRA, with consistent gains across MATH, HumanEval, and Object Counting tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVD-based compression baselines, the proposed method consistently outperforms SVD-LLM across all 11 models and all three datasets, with the largest gains at aggressive compression (e.g., 9.46 PPL lower on LLaMA3-8B WikiText-2 at 30%, and 3824 PPL lower at 50%). ASVD and FWSVD are substantially outperformed, often by orders of magnitude at higher compression ratios (e.g., ASVD reaches 37479 PPL on LLaMA-7B WikiText-2 at 50% vs. 22.40 for the proposed method). Against pruning-based methods (LLM-Pruner, FLAP), the proposed method achieves lower perplexity on WikiText-2 across all compression ratios, though pruning methods retain an edge on C4 at lower compression levels. For LoRA-style PEFT initialization, the method surpasses both vanilla Kaiming-initialized LoRA and PiSSA on all five evaluation tasks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, HumanEval+, Object Counting) at ranks 64, 128, and 256, with the largest margin on GSM8K (0.384 vs. 0.320 for PiSSA at rank 256 on Vicuna-7B). The method also demonstrates superior compatibility with post-compression quantization (GPTQ-4bit: 50.26 vs. 85.03 PPL on Vicuna-7B C4) and dynamic rank allocation, maintaining its advantage over SVD-LLM under both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based LLM compression line initiated by ASVD (activation-aware scaling) and SVD-LLM (static whitening with layerwise closed-form updates) by replacing the fixed, pre-compression whitening matrix with a dynamically re-estimated one at each layer, computed from the actual compressed features that propagate forward. It also builds on PiSSA's SVD-based LoRA initialization by performing the SVD in a distribution-aware whitened space rather than directly on the raw weight matrix. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of layerwise distribution drift as a first-order error source, formalized through a Lipschitz-based error propagation bound that shows static whitening incurs a multiplicative inflation factor √(1+δ_ℓ) per layer that compounds across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge between rank selection and output distortion (via the Eckart-Young theorem in the whitened space), which prior SVD-based methods lacked, and demonstrates that this principled link translates into consistent perplexity gains across 11 LLMs and multiple compression ratios. Practical limitations include additional per-layer whitening computation, sensitivity to calibration data distribution, and the absence of experiments on very large models (≥70B) due to compute constraints. Open questions include whether adaptive or distribution-free calibration strategies can eliminate the calibration-data dependency, and whether the dynamic whitening overhead becomes prohibitive at scale or in streaming inference settings.
