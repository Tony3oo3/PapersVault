## Problem

Static retrieval-augmented generation issues a single query from the question and keeps the retrieved context fixed throughout generation, which fails on multi-hop QA where later-hop evidence depends on bridge entities the question does not name. Diffusion language models hold a noisy draft of the entire response at every denoising step, yet no existing RAG framework for diffusion LMs exploits these intermediate states to guide retrieval. The paper asks whether the denoising trajectory can serve as a lookahead signal for dynamic retrieval and whether RAG grounding makes parallel decoding more reliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and an external passage corpus, and produces a step-by-step reasoning trace followed by a final answer delimited by a '###' marker. At each denoising iteration, the retrieval query is the concatenation of the original question and the detokenized partially-denoised sequence (with low-confidence positions dropped), so the query evolves as the response takes shape. The framework is training-free and retriever-agnostic, requiring only a discrete diffusion LM capable of producing reasoning traces and any sparse or dense retriever (BM25 or E5-base-v2 in experiments).

## Method

SARDI interleaves retrieval with denoising by maintaining two confidence thresholds: a lower query threshold τ_q that determines which tentative tokens enter the retrieval query, and a higher commit threshold τ_c that determines which tokens are finalized. At each denoising step, all masked positions receive a confidence score; tokens meeting τ_q are filled into a proxy sequence that, concatenated with the question, forms the retrieval query for a fresh set of K passages, while only tokens meeting τ_c are unmasked and committed. This separation lets speculative, low-confidence tokens inform retrieval long before they are stable enough to appear in the output, surfacing bridge entities for later-hop evidence early in the trajectory. The paper further shows empirically that grounding in retrieved evidence sharply reduces conditional mutual information between adjacent tokens (especially entity spans), making the RAG regime well suited to the parallel decoding that diffusion LMs enable. SARDI is training-free, retriever-agnostic, and applicable to any reasoning-capable discrete diffusion language model.

## Results

On five multi-hop QA benchmarks, SARDI (τc=0.9) achieves 57.8% EM on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 48.5% on HotpotQA, 45.3% on CofCA, 20.5% on MuSiQue, and 21.1% on SynthWorlds-SM, improving over static diffusion retrieval (DLM w/ RET@STATIC) by 14.1, 8.6, 1.9, 9.4, and 6.7 EM points respectively. SARDI runs at 0.39 s/example on 2WikiMultiHopQA, up to 8× faster than the strongest training-free AR iterative-retrieval baseline (AR w/ RET@1 at 1.26 s) while matching or exceeding its accuracy. Raising the commit threshold to τc=0.95 further lifts 2Wiki EM to 59.1% at a modest throughput cost (0.56 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLARE (Jiang et al., 2023), SARDI wins on all five benchmarks in EM (e.g., 57.8 vs 46.6 on 2Wiki, 48.5 vs 37.3 on HotpotQA) while also being 2–4× faster, because parallel diffusion decoding avoids the error compounding that plagues FLARE's left-to-right speculative lookahead. Against AdaptiveRAG (Jeong et al., 2024), SARDI dominates on every benchmark (57.8 vs 34.1 on 2Wiki, 48.5 vs 37.1 on HotpotQA) and runs 6–14× faster, since AdaptiveRAG's query-complexity router adds multiple LLM inference calls. Against ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), SARDI wins on 2Wiki (57.8 vs 42.7), HotpotQA (48.5 vs 40.1), and CofCA (45.3 vs 42.9) but trails slightly on MuSiQue (20.5 vs 20.9) and SynthWorlds-SM (21.1 vs 22.2), while being 3–5× faster. Against the RL-trained Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025), SARDI wins on 2Wiki (57.8 vs 52.4) and CofCA (45.3 vs 44.4) but loses on HotpotQA (48.5 vs 50.3), MuSiQue (20.5 vs 26.4), and SynthWorlds-SM (21.1 vs 26.9); however, SARDI runs 3–8× faster (0.39 s vs 3.36 s on 2Wiki), and the authors note the two approaches are complementary rather than directly competing since Search-R1 requires RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARDI extends the line of dynamic/iterative retrieval for autoregressive models (FLARE, IRCoT, AdaptiveRAG, ReAct, Search-R1) to the diffusion language model setting, but with a key structural departure: rather than deriving follow-up queries from a left-to-right committed prefix, it conditions retrieval on the full noisy draft that a DLM holds at every denoising step. This exploits a property unique to non-autoregressive decoders—speculative future tokens can inform retrieval before they are stable enough to commit—avoiding the error-compounding problem that makes AR lookahead (e.g., FLARE's tentative next sentence) fragile. The method also builds on threshold-based unmasking from Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2026) for confidence-gated commitment, and departs from prior diffusion RAG work (Yu et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2026), which is restricted to single-shot static retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that diffusion LMs offer retrieval-specific advantages beyond raw throughput, and the CMI analysis (entity-pair mutual information dropping ~10× with grounding) gives a concrete mechanistic explanation for why RAG is a regime well-suited to parallel decoding—a finding that could guide future DLM architecture and decoding design. A significant practical limitation is that current DLMs (e.g., DREAM-7B) do not reliably produce reasoning traces without fine-tuning, so the method's applicability is contingent on DLMs maturing in this regard. Open questions include whether self-augmenting retrieval extends to latent diffusion LMs, how to amortize per-step retrieval cost via document-level KV caching, and whether combining diffusion trajectories with light RL-based query supervision (à la Search-R1) yields further gains.
