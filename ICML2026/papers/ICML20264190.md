## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation requires thousands of sequential forward passes, making it impractically slow. While Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) offers a training-free, lossless acceleration path, its acceptance rate is severely limited by instability in the independent draft sampling process, capping speedup at only ~2× for images. The paper identifies that the collision probability between consecutive draft tokens is exponentially bounded by the Rényi-2 entropy of the (typically very flat) visual token distributions, leaving most tokens changed each iteration and preventing convergence.

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in decoding replacement for any pre-trained AR visual model (Lumina-mGPT at 768×768, Janus-Pro at 384×384, Cosmos-1-AR for 33-frame video, CosyVoice2 for TTS). It takes the same text prompt or context frames as the base model and produces bit-identical output distributions to standard AR decoding, but with fewer sequential NFEs. The only hyperparameter is the draft window length L (16–128), and the method requires no additional model weights, training, or architectural changes.

## Method

SCD replaces the independent multinomial sampling in SJD's drafting phase with a Coupling between the current and previous iteration's token distributions, increasing the probability of sampling identical tokens across iterations. Two couplings are provided: Maximal Coupling (π_MC), implemented by reusing the Modified Rejection Sampling (MRS) algorithm already used for verification, achieving the theoretical upper bound of 1 − TV(P,Q) on collision probability; and Gumbel Coupling (π_GS), which shares a single Gumbel noise vector across both distributions via the Gumbel-Max trick, offering better long-range stability with a lower bound of (1−TV)/(1+TV). The lossless guarantee is preserved because any valid coupling's marginals exactly match the original distributions, so the draft token remains a valid sample from p^(t). In practice, this is a single-line code change (or vectorized MRS call) with under 5% overhead, yielding up to 4.2× speedup on images and 13.6× on video.

## Results

On Lumina-mGPT (768×768, MS-COCO), SCD with π_MC at L=64 reduces NFE from 2390 (Vanilla AR) to 567.7, achieving a 4.21× NFE speedup and 4.18× latency speedup (24.41s vs 102.03s on A100), while maintaining identical FID (30.83 vs 30.79), IS, and CLIP-Score. On Cosmos-1-AR video generation (Real-Estate-10k), SCD with L=128 achieves a 13.6× latency speedup (13.60s vs 157.25s) with no FVD degradation. On Janus-Pro 7B, SCD achieves up to 3.7× step compression (154.42 NFE vs 576 NFE for AR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCD outperforms SJD (Teng et al., 2024) by approximately 1.8× in NFE reduction across all window sizes (e.g., L=64: 567.7 vs 1035.9 NFE), while SJD's speedup plateaus at ~2.3× regardless of L. Against the lossy GSD baseline (So et al., 2025), SCD achieves faster NFE (652.3 vs 701.4 at L=32) while maintaining lossless quality (FID 30.75 vs 33.21 for GSD G=10). In the training-based comparison (Table 7, LlamaGen-XL), SCD (515.72 NFE) surpasses both EAGLE (853.30 NFE, lossless but requires training) and LANTERN (585.14 NFE, lossy and requires training), confirming that the training-free, lossless SCD is strictly superior in speed. Notably, SCD's advantage grows with window size (L=16: 2.94× vs L=64: 4.21×), whereas SJD shows no meaningful improvement beyond L=16, indicating that coupling is essential for exploiting longer draft windows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD), a training-free lossless acceleration method for autoregressive visual generation that eliminates the need for a separate draft model by reusing its own previous verification output. The key departure is identifying that SJD's low acceptance rate stems from the independence of its draft sampling, and resolving this via the information-theoretic tool of Coupling (Lindvall, 2002) — specifically, showing that the Modified Rejection Sampling (MRS) algorithm already used for SD verification is itself a maximal coupling, requiring only a single-line code change. This positions the work at the intersection of speculative decoding theory, Jacobi iteration convergence, and optimal coupling theory, rather than introducing a new draft model or verification criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-line, training-free, lossless modification that delivers up to 4.2× speedup for image generation and 13.6× for video generation across multiple AR models (Lumina-mGPT, Janus-Pro, Cosmos-1-AR), making large-scale visual AR generation substantially more deployable. The coupling framework is general and applies to any VQ-based autoregressive model, with a proof-of-concept on TTS (CosyVoice2) suggesting broader modality applicability. Open questions include whether the parallel-computation overhead at very large window sizes limits further gains, and whether the long-range stability advantage of Gumbel coupling over maximal coupling can be exploited more systematically for tasks with strong temporal or spatial redundancy.
