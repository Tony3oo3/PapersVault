## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs predominantly rely on the attention intensity assumption, attributing hallucination to a quantitative deficit in global visual attention. This paper demonstrates that global visual attention magnitude shows no statistically significant difference between faithful and hallucinated outputs, revealing instead a dynamic structural misalignment: specific attention heads (risky mediators) decouple from visual evidence at decision-critical steps to lock onto language priors, establishing a pathological shortcut that bypasses visual grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265577/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

Fox operates as a training-free, inference-time intervention on standard LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Shikra, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3) that take an RGB image and a text prompt (including system instructions) and produce autoregressive text. At each decoding step, the method requires two forward passes — one observational and one interventional — and intervenes on pre-softmax attention logits at a sparse set of diagnosed heads in early-to-mid layers, without modifying model weights or the input/output interface.

## Method

Fox formulates LVLM decoding as a Structural Causal Model where attention heads at decision-critical steps (the multimodal handshake x_last and autoregressive anchor y_{t-1}) serve as dynamic mediators. It unsupervisedly identifies risky mediators via a joint risk score S = m_sys · H_vis, a multiplicative conjunctive filter that pinpoints heads simultaneously exhibiting high system-prior reliance and high visual attention entropy. The do-operator is implemented as numerical logit saturation: a large negative bias γ is applied to the pre-softmax logits of identified heads, exploiting finite-precision arithmetic to drive spurious prior-driven connections to near-zero while preserving robust structural signals. Finally, a conflict-gated cooperative decoding strategy fuses the observational and interventional distributions using a dynamic weight governed by truncated Jensen-Shannon Divergence, applying a fixed gain in consensus regimes and a conflict-proportional correction in divergence regimes to balance faithfulness with linguistic fluency.

## Results

On CHAIR (lower is better), Fox achieves the lowest hallucination rates across all three backbones: C_I = 12.90 on LLaVA-1.5, 11.98 on InstructBLIP, and 13.66 on Shikra, corresponding to a 29.1% relative reduction in C_I over SID on InstructBLIP. On POPE (higher is better), Fox reaches 81.93% accuracy on LLaVA-1.5 Adversarial and 84.97% on Shikra Random, outperforming all baselines in the high-bias setting. On MME, Fox attains a total score of 613.33 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 600.00 for SID), with the largest gains in Position (93.33→131.37) and Color (150.00→165.00) subsets. GPT-4V evaluation confirms simultaneous improvements in Correctness (5.89→7.04) and Detailedness (5.82→6.19) on LLaVA-1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fox consistently outperforms all five baselines on CHAIR across all three backbones, with the largest margin over SID (29.1% C_I reduction on InstructBLIP: 16.89→11.98) and a clear lead over OPERA and CausalMM (e.g., C_I 12.90 vs. 13.52/13.49 on LLaVA-1.5). On POPE, Fox achieves the best Adversarial accuracy on LLaVA-1.5 (81.93% vs. 81.27% CausalMM, 81.13% OPERA, 81.10% SID) and the best Random accuracy on Shikra (84.97% vs. 83.73% OPERA), though OPERA edges out Fox on InstructBLIP Adversarial (81.57% vs. 80.60%). VCD and ICD, which rely on the attention intensity assumption, show the weakest performance overall, confirming that global magnitude amplification is insufficient for structural hallucination. CausalMM, which uses input-level counterfactuals, is competitive on POPE but trails Fox on CHAIR by 0.5–1.0 C_I points, suggesting that holistic causal correction without targeting sparse risky mediators is less effective for localized shortcut suppression. The consistent CHAIR advantage across backbones (including generalization to Qwen3-VL-8B and InternVL3-8B) indicates that the causal intervention transfers beyond the primary evaluation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'attention intensity assumption' underlying most inference-time hallucination mitigation methods (VCD, ICD, OPERA, PAI), which treat hallucination as a quantitative deficit in visual attention and remedy it by amplifying visual signals or suppressing language priors. It also distinguishes itself from prior SCM-based analyses of LVLM hallucination (CausalMM, Huang et al. 2024a) that rely on input-level counterfactuals (masking image regions or tokens); instead, Fox reformulates internal attention heads as dynamic causal mediators and applies the do-operator directly to pre-softmax logits at decision-critical steps. The conflict-gated cooperative decoding strategy draws on the broader contrastive-decoding lineage but introduces a JSD-based adaptive fusion rule that reconciles interventional faithfulness with observational fluency, a mechanism absent from prior contrastive or reweighting approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing empirical counter-evidence that global visual attention magnitude does not discriminate hallucinated from faithful outputs, the paper challenges a foundational heuristic in the LVLM hallucination literature and redirects attention toward sparse, head-level structural diagnostics. The training-free, model-agnostic design (validated across five backbones including recent Qwen3-VL and InternVL3) makes it practically deployable without retraining. Open questions remain: the framework is validated only on object-centric hallucination, and the authors explicitly flag compositional failures (relation, attribute, counting errors), video-language settings, and distribution shift as unaddressed. The reliance on a fixed intervention window (early-to-mid layers) and a per-layer head budget k also raises questions about robustness under architectural changes or longer contexts.
