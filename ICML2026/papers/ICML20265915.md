## Problem

Prior work characterizes MLLM modality following using coarse dataset-level ratios (e.g., Text-Following Ratio) that treat all conflict instances indiscriminately, masking the case-specific reasoning confidence that actually drives individual decisions. This paper asks what governs the dynamic process of modality following and what internal mechanisms produce model hesitation when modalities provide contradictory information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265915/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a conflicting triplet (image I, text description T, question Q) where the model's unimodal predictions disagree, and computes per-modality output entropy over the answer token distribution to derive a normalized relative uncertainty score ΔH_rel = 2(H_t − H_v)/(H_t + H_v). Beyond the scalar uncertainty, the method extracts a per-model balance point (the ΔH_rel at 50% text-following probability), layer-wise top-1 prediction trajectories via LogitLens-style probing, and, in the SFT application, LoRA-adapted model weights. The synthetic dataset supports independently controllable visual difficulty tiers (d_v = 0–13, varying target size, distractor count, and occlusion) and textual reasoning tiers (d_t = 0–3, from direct statements to multi-hop relational descriptions).

## Method

The paper proposes a two-factor decomposition of modality following: a case-specific relative preference uncertainty (measured by the entropy gap between unimodal predictions) and a stable inherent modality preference (quantified as the balance point where following probability equals 50%). Across eight MLLMs and multiple benchmarks, the probability of following a modality monotonically decreases as its relative uncertainty increases, a trend verified via Spearman correlation (65/72 pairs below −0.8) and robust to alternative uncertainty indices (prediction margin, NLL). To explain internal hesitation near the balance point, the authors probe layer-wise predictions with LogitLens and identify 'concept oscillations'—repeated switches between modality-supported top-1 answers—strictly localized to middle-to-late layers (typically after layer 15), with causal validation via activation patching showing a 70% oscillation reduction and 95% following-rate recovery. For preference steering, the authors demonstrate that SFT with LoRA on boundary-case data (where relative uncertainty is near the balance point) is essential for robust alignment, while training on easy samples where one modality already dominates fails to generalize, revealing a 'failure of easy-to-hard generalization' governed by preference uncertainty.

## Results

Across 72 model-task pairs (8 MLLMs × 9 tasks), Spearman's rank correlation ρ between relative preference uncertainty (ΔH_rel) and text-following probability is uniformly negative, with 65/72 (~90%) below −0.8 and many reaching −0.99 or −1.00. On the DriveBench safety-critical benchmark, the untrained base model collapses under text-vision conflict (LLaVA-1.5-7B drops from 76.67% vision-only to 3.33% conflict accuracy; Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 83.33% to 6.67%), while SFT on the Text-easier (boundary) subset recovers LLaVA-1.5-7B to 50.00% and Qwen2.5-VL-7B to 63.33%. In long-form reasoning, Text-easier training lifts Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 32.81 to 45.35 overall score. Layer-wise probing reveals oscillation frequency nearly doubles in the ambiguous region versus the clear region, localized to middle-to-late layers (typically after layer 15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so the key contrasts are between data-selection strategies for preference steering and between the 8 evaluated MLLMs. On DriveBench, the Text-easier (boundary-case) training subset consistently outperforms both Tie and Vision-easier subsets for both LLaVA-1.5-7B (50.00% vs. 40.00% vs. 33.33%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (63.33% vs. 53.33% vs. 43.33%), confirming the boundary-case principle. On the synthetic SFT experiment, training exclusively on 'easy' data where the target modality is already dominant (Pos subset) produces the worst generalization in the challenging Neg region, with oscillation spikes of +164% for LLaVA, whereas Neg-trained models better suppress instability. Across the 8 models (LLaVA-1.5/1.6, Qwen2-VL/2.5-VL, GLM-4V, CogVLM2), the monotonic uncertainty-following law holds universally, but balance points differ: Qwen models show a negative balance point (inherent vision preference) while LLaVA models sit near or above zero (neutral/text-leaning), explaining their divergent macro-level TFR/VFR on identical datasets. The causal activation-patching experiment confirms that suppressing middle-to-late layer oscillations raises modality-following rates to 95%, validating oscillations as the direct mechanism rather than a mere correlate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior modality-bias studies (Deng et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025) that characterize MLLM behavior through aggregate dataset-level statistics (TFR/VFR), arguing these are confounded reflections of two separable factors: case-specific relative preference uncertainty and a stable inherent preference. It repurposes LogitLens-style layer-wise probing (nostalgebraist, 2020; Zhang et al., 2024) from general interpretability to specifically track modality-conflict resolution, identifying 'concept oscillations' as the internal signature of indecision. The SFT data-selection experiments build on standard LoRA fine-tuning but introduce a principled stratification criterion (relative uncertainty boundary) that prior alignment work has not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The balance point provides a capability-disentangled, quantitative measure of inherent modality preference that macro-level ratios cannot capture, and the finding that boundary-case data is essential for robust preference steering offers concrete, actionable guidance for MLLM alignment pipelines. Limitations include evaluation primarily on 7B–19B models, a synthetic dataset limited to color/attribute recognition, and a single real-world validation domain (autonomous driving); whether the framework extends to more than two modalities, to video, or to non-transformer architectures remains open. The layer-wise oscillation mechanism, while well-evidenced, has not yet been leveraged for intervention beyond the activation-patching demonstration.
