## Problem

Reinforcement learning has become the dominant paradigm for LLM fine-tuning, but it suffers from low sample efficiency with long-horizon outcome rewards, sensitivity to base model choice, susceptibility to reward hacking, and run-to-run instability. Evolution Strategies (ES), a population-based zeroth-order optimizer, were long assumed to be infeasible at LLM scale because prior applications were limited to models with at most a few million parameters. This paper challenges that assumption by demonstrating the first successful full-parameter ES fine-tuning of multi-billion-parameter LLMs without any dimensionality reduction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full pretrained LLM parameter tensor (no adapters or layer restrictions), a scalar reward function evaluated on model-generated responses (e.g., binary correctness for math, negative length difference for conciseness), and a set of training prompts. It outputs updated full-parameter LLM weights after T iterations. Notably, only response-level (outcome) rewards are required—no token-level process rewards or gradients—and evaluation uses greedy decoding so that all variance comes from parameter-space exploration rather than stochastic sampling. The population size is as small as 30, far below the 10,000+ used in prior ES work on much smaller models.

## Method

The paper implements a simplified variant of Natural Evolution Strategies (following OpenAI ES) that directly perturbs the entire LLM parameter vector with i.i.d. Gaussian noise scaled by σ, evaluates each perturbed model's reward, and updates parameters by aggregating noise vectors weighted by z-score-normalized rewards. Seven engineering modifications enable scalability: random seeds are stored instead of full noise tensors, perturbation and restoration are performed layer-by-layer in-place to minimize peak memory, evaluations are parallelized across GPUs, and the final parameter update is decomposed layer-by-layer and seed-by-seed. The key algorithmic insight is that parameter-space exploration injects noise only once per rollout (at the weight level), producing a single deterministic action trajectory per perturbed model, which yields far lower variance in reward estimation than RL's action-space exploration that injects noise at every token position. This lower variance makes gradient estimation more reliable, reduces reward hacking (since ES optimizes a solution distribution rather than a single point), and eliminates the need for KL-divergence penalties or other regularization terms that RL requires.

## Results

On the Countdown task, ES with a single fixed hyperparameter set (N=30, σ=0.001, α=5×10⁻⁴) outperforms all RL baselines across all seven models tested. For the largest models, ES achieves 66.8% accuracy on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (vs. 57.0% for the best RL variant GRPO-v) and 61.2% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (vs. 51.3% for GRPO-z(30)). On math reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, OlympiadBench, Minerva, AIME2024, AMC), ES fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-Math-7B is competitive with SOTA RL baselines (SimpleRL-Zero, OpenReasoner-Zero, Oat-Zero). On puzzle tasks, ES dramatically improves base model performance: ARC-AGI accuracy jumps from 0.2% to 29.5% (Qwen-2.5-14B) and Sudoku from 2.5% to 69.5% (Qwen-2.5-3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ES outperforms all five RL baselines on the Countdown task across every model tested, with margins ranging from ~1 point (Qwen-2.5-0.5B: 14.4% vs. 13.5% Dr.GRPO-v) to ~17 points (Qwen-2.5-3B: 60.5% vs. 43.8% Dr.GRPO-v). Critically, ES uses one fixed hyperparameter configuration for all models, while each RL baseline required a per-model grid search over KL penalty β and learning rate α to find its best setting, making the comparison conservative for ES. On math reasoning benchmarks, ES is competitive with but does not clearly surpass the SOTA RL implementations (SimpleRL-Zero/GRPO, OpenReasoner-Zero/PPO, Oat-Zero/Dr.GRPO), which the authors attribute to their vanilla ES setup versus the extensive algorithmic refinement in those RL systems. In the conciseness fine-tuning experiment, ES achieves a higher reward (0.919) with lower KL divergence (0.813) than GRPO's best non-hacking configuration (0.911 reward, 1.591 KL), and does so without any KL penalty, while GRPO without KL penalty (β=0) exhibits reward hacking. ES also shows substantially lower variance across independent runs (e.g., 37.73±1.14 vs. 26.77±1.98 for GRPO-v on Qwen-2.5-1.5B Countdown), confirming greater training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OpenAI ES / Natural Evolution Strategies lineage (Salimans et al., 2017; Wierstra et al., 2014) from its prior ceiling of a few million parameters (Zhang et al., 2017; Lehman et al., 2018; Lorenc & Neruda, 2025) to full-parameter fine-tuning of billion-parameter LLMs without any dimensionality reduction. It departs from the dominant RL fine-tuning paradigm (PPO, GRPO, Dr.GRPO) by operating entirely in parameter space with zeroth-order updates, and from prior zeroth-order LLM work like MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023) by using population-based exploration rather than single-sample SPSA-style perturbation. The implementation deliberately strips away common ES enhancements (rank transformation, mirrored sampling, Adam) to isolate the core algorithm's effectiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result overturns a long-standing assumption that population-based zeroth-order optimization is infeasible at LLM scale, opening a backpropagation-free post-training paradigm that is simpler to implement, more memory-efficient (inference-only), and more robust across base models and hyperparameter choices than RL. Open questions include why a population of 30 suffices for billions of parameters (possibly related to low intrinsic dimensionality of LLMs), whether ES can perform unsupervised fine-tuning on internal model behaviors, and how ES and RL might be combined in alternating exploration/exploitation phases. The current implementation is deliberately vanilla, so the practical ceiling of ES fine-tuning—especially on math reasoning where it is competitive but not dominant—remains to be explored.
