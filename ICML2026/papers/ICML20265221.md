## Problem

Online RL in non-episodic, finite-horizon MDPs is fundamentally harder than episodic or infinite-horizon settings because the agent must estimate full-horizon returns from a single trajectory without resets, leading to linear regret for all known algorithms. Existing model-based methods are exponentially sample-inefficient, and standard model-free methods face a high-variance estimation barrier when learning Q-functions targeting the terminal step. The paper asks whether a deliberately truncated planning objective can break the linear regret barrier in this regime.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm receives the current state s_t and maintains per-state-action visit counts, cumulative reward estimates, and LCB values. It selects an action a_t by checking which actions' K-step lookahead LCBs exceed a time-varying threshold γ_t; if no action qualifies, it falls back to a UCB-based exploration choice. The K-step LCB is decomposed into a 1-step component (updated every step) and a K-1-step component (updated only during dedicated exploration episodes triggered with probability ε_t), making the estimation of the truncated reward the central computational challenge.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the full-horizon Q-function with a K-step lookahead Q-function paired with a thresholding rule: actions are selected only when their estimated K-step reward (via LCB) exceeds a time-varying threshold γ_t, rather than greedily maximizing cumulative return. The LGKT algorithm uses a lower confidence bound with a deliberately small exploration function g(t)=3log(t) to rapidly eliminate sub-threshold actions, achieving minimax optimal constant regret for K=1 and O(max((K-1), C_{K-1})√(SAT log T)) regret for K≥2. For K≥2, the K-step reward is estimated via an ε-greedy framework that periodically runs a subroutine ALG_{K-1} to collect K-1-step trajectory samples, balancing exploration cost against estimation accuracy. An adaptive variant (LG1-2T) starts with K=1 for fast initial convergence and switches to K=2 after a cutoff to improve long-horizon reward, while a hybrid (LG1T-RL) transitions to a standard RL algorithm to recover asymptotic optimality.

## Results

Across 1,000 synthetic MDPs (S=10, A=5), the 1-step and 2-step lookahead greedy oracles achieve competitive ratios of 0.75 and 0.96 relative to the full optimal policy, yet LG1T and LG1-2T still surpass all six RL baselines in cumulative reward over 20,000 steps. For larger MDPs (S=100, A=25), competitive ratios drop to 0.80 and 0.94, and LG1-2T (which switches from 1-step to 2-step lookahead at step 100) achieves the best overall performance. In JumpRiverSwim (S=5,8,15), FrozenLake (4×4), and AnyTrading, LGKT variants consistently achieve the highest cumulative reward, with LG2T outperforming all baselines in the continuous-state AnyTrading environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGKT (LG1T, LG2T, LG1-2T) outperforms all six tabular RL baselines—UCRL2, KLUCRL, PMEVI-KLUCRL (model-based), MDP-OOMD, Optimistic Q Learning (model-free), and Q Learning (episodic)—on cumulative reward across every tested environment. Model-based methods (UCRL2, KLUCRL, PMEVI-KLUCRL) initially achieve higher reward due to optimistic exploration but are surpassed by LG1T and LG1-2T within fewer than 1,000 steps; PMEVI-KLUCRL is excluded from the 100-state experiments due to prohibitive runtime (>3 h/instance). Model-free average-reward methods (MDP-OOMD, Optimistic Q Learning) are consistently outperformed, confirming that their reliance on infinite-horizon structural assumptions (mixing time, diameter) fails in the non-episodic finite-horizon regime. In JumpRiverSwim, where no short-horizon K-step policy is optimal, the hybrid LG1T-RL (switching to PMEVI-KLUCRL after 10,000 steps) matches or exceeds standalone PMEVI-KLUCRL, demonstrating that LGKT can effectively warm-start a standard RL algorithm. In AnyTrading (continuous state), LG1T is surpassed after a short horizon due to 1-step suboptimality, but LG2T and LG1-2T maintain the lead over all baselines within the 20,000-step budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the thresholding/satisficing bandit framework (Michel et al., 2022; Feng et al., 2025) from multi-armed bandits to general finite-horizon MDPs, replacing the empirical-mean threshold test with an LCB-based test that yields faster elimination of sub-threshold actions. It departs from standard RL theory (episodic finite-horizon methods like UCRL2, KLUCRL, and infinite-horizon average-reward methods like MDP-OOMD) by deliberately targeting a truncated K-step lookahead Q-function rather than the full-horizon Q-function, thereby sidestepping the linear regret lower bound that applies to learning the optimal policy in non-episodic settings. The K-step lookahead idea connects to approximate dynamic programming (Bertsekas, 2022), but here it serves as a learning objective for online sample efficiency rather than a planning heuristic with a known model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically important but theoretically underexplored regime—non-episodic finite-horizon MDPs (e.g., single trading sessions, treatment regimens)—and shows that fast convergence to a deliberately suboptimal K-step thresholding oracle can beat slow convergence to the optimal policy, a counterintuitive result with clear implications for finite-horizon deployment. Key limitations include the tabular nature of the method (requiring state recurrence), the linear optimality gap of the K-step oracle for K < T in general MDPs, and the need to tune the threshold γ and lookahead depth K. Extending the approach to function-approximation or deep RL settings, and characterizing when the K-step oracle is near-optimal beyond the two-state stochastic dominance case, are natural open directions.
