## Problem

Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) trains a network against a single-sample conditional velocity target that is a high-variance Monte Carlo estimate of the true marginal velocity, destabilizing optimization and slowing convergence. While prior work has empirically noted variance-related inefficiencies in diffusion training, a principled variance-theoretic characterization within flow matching and stochastic interpolants has been missing, leaving both training and sampling to treat the entire generative trajectory uniformly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For training, the method consumes latent representations (32×32×4 from the Stable Diffusion VAE) of 256×256 ImageNet images along with class labels or text prompts, and produces a learned velocity field. For inference, StableVS operates on pretrained T2I models (SD3.5, Flux, Qwen-Image at 1024×1024) and T2V models (Wan2.2 at 640×480), taking Gaussian noise plus a text prompt and producing generated images or videos. A key structural detail is that StableVS only replaces the base solver in the low-variance regime [0, ξ] (e.g., ξ=0.85), leaving the high-variance regime [ξ, 1] to use the original solver, so the total step count is reduced without altering the high-noise integration path.

## Method

The paper identifies a two-regime variance structure in stochastic interpolants: a high-variance regime near the prior where the posterior over x₀ is multimodal, and a low-variance regime near the data where conditional and marginal velocities nearly coincide. StableVM reduces training variance by replacing the single-sample CFM target with a self-normalized importance-weighted average over n reference samples, with the noisy input x_t sampled from a composite GMM conditional path over the entire reference batch, yielding an unbiased estimator whose variance decays as O(1/n). VA-REPA applies representation alignment selectively in the low-variance regime via a weighting function w(t) (sigmoid or SNR-based), since semantic supervision is only well-posed when x_t retains information about x₀. StableVS exploits the fact that in the low-variance regime the PF-ODE trajectory reduces to a straight line with constant velocity, enabling closed-form large-step integration (Euler steps of arbitrary size) that achieves >2× sampling speedup without retraining or perceptible quality loss.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with SiT-XL/2 and CFG, StableVM + VA-REPA achieves FID 1.80 at 80 epochs and FID 1.44 at 480 epochs, outperforming REPA (FID 1.98 at 80 epochs) and REG (FID 1.86 at 80 epochs) at the same budget. With class-balanced sampling, the method reaches FID 1.33 at 480 epochs, approaching REPA-E's 1.12 at 800 epochs while keeping the VAE frozen. For sampling acceleration, StableVS with 9 low-variance steps matches or exceeds 30-step baselines on GenEval (e.g., SD3.5: Overall 0.723 vs. 0.723; Flux: 0.666 vs. 0.660) and T2V-CompBench (Wan2.2: Consist 0.843 vs. 0.842) using only 20 total steps, yielding >2× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against REPA (Yu et al., 2024), StableVM + VA-REPA achieves FID 1.80 vs. 1.98 at 80 epochs and FID 1.44 vs. 1.42 at 480/800 epochs respectively, winning at equal compute and matching at longer training. Against REG (Wu et al., 2025b), the method achieves FID 1.80 vs. 1.86 at 80 epochs and 1.44 vs. 1.40 at 480 epochs, with a smaller gap at longer training. Against iREPA (Singh et al., 2025), the method wins at 80 epochs (FID 1.80 vs. 1.93) and also improves when plugged into iREPA directly (FID 16.02 vs. 16.62 at 100k iterations, Table 3). Against REPA-E (Leng et al., 2025), which fine-tunes the VAE end-to-end, the method is slightly behind at 800 epochs (FID 1.33 vs. 1.12 with class-balanced sampling) but reaches comparable quality at 480 epochs (FID 1.33 vs. 1.67 at 80 epochs) without any VAE fine-tuning. For sampling acceleration, StableVS with 9 low-variance steps matches or exceeds 30-step baselines across SD3.5, Flux, Qwen-Image, and Wan2.2 (e.g., Flux GenEval Overall 0.666 vs. 0.660; Wan2.2 T2V-CompBench Consist 0.843 vs. 0.842), while naively reducing steps to 20 degrades quality (e.g., SD3.5 Overall drops to 0.710). On CIFAR-10, StableVM outperforms both CFM and the original STF implementation at all checkpoints (e.g., FID 6.87 vs. 7.50 vs. 7.07 at 50k iterations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching / stochastic interpolant framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Albergo et al., 2025) by introducing a variance-theoretic lens that reveals a two-regime structure in the conditional velocity targets of CFM (Tong et al., 2023). The StableVM objective adapts the multi-sample self-normalized aggregation idea from Stable Target Field (Xu et al., 2023) but crucially samples the noisy input from a composite GMM mixture rather than a single reference, yielding an unbiased estimator. VA-REPA builds directly on REPA (Yu et al., 2024) and its variants (REG, iREPA, REPA-E) by adding a variance-aware weighting that restricts alignment to the low-variance regime, while StableVS extends DDIM-style (Song et al., 2021) large-step integration to the flow matching setting by exploiting the near-deterministic dynamics in that regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-based two-regime decomposition provides a principled, unified explanation for both training instability (high-variance targets near the prior) and the effectiveness of representation alignment (well-conditioned only near the data), offering a single design principle for future objectives. StableVS is particularly impactful as a finetuning-free, solver-agnostic acceleration that achieves >2× speedup across multiple production models (SD3.5, Flux, Qwen-Image, Wan2.2) without quality degradation, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include: the split point ξ remains heuristic and data-dependent; the variance analysis assumes affine conditional velocities for the strict inequality; and it is unclear whether the two-regime structure persists under non-linear interpolants or in extremely high-dimensional settings where the low-variance regime may dominate the entire trajectory.
