## Problem

VLA models are bottlenecked by the scarcity of expensive expert-teleoperated demonstrations (observation–language–action triplets), creating a fundamental data wall that limits scalability. The paper observes that abundant, cheap task-agnostic interaction data—discarded off-task trajectories and autonomous robot play—is currently thrown away, and argues that the physical motor skills embedded in this data ('how to move') can be learned entirely without task labels, decoupled from semantic task understanding ('what to do').

## Input / Output

During Stage 1 pretraining, the model consumes two consecutive RGB frames (224×224, downsampled to 5 Hz) as a visual-only pair and predicts the 7-dimensional delta-pose action (Δx, Δy, Δz, 3-D axis-angle rotation, gripper command) that caused the transition. In Stage 2, the future frame is replaced by a natural-language instruction processed through the VLM's text encoder, while the SigLIP visual encoder and 2-layer MLP action head are reused. The delta-pose (relative) action representation ensures invariance to global workspace coordinates, a property critical for transferring physical priors across different robot configurations.

## Method

TAP is a two-stage framework on a Qwen2.5-VL (3 B) backbone with a SigLIP visual encoder and a lightweight 2-layer MLP action head. Stage 1 applies a self-supervised Inverse Dynamics objective—predicting action a_t from consecutive observations (o_t, o_{t+1})—on massive task-agnostic data (discarded Bridge trajectories or 30 hours of autonomous random play), forcing the visual encoder to attend to end-effector kinematics and object displacement while ignoring static background, thereby acquiring physical affordances with zero language supervision. Stage 2 performs standard behavior cloning on a minimal expert set (5 k trajectories in simulation, 200 in the real world), where the conditioning signal shifts from the future observation to a language instruction, learning a lightweight projection from semantic space into the pre-established dynamics space. The autonomous data-collection pipeline uses constrained procedural trajectory generation with voxel-grid downsampling, a contact-forcing heuristic that prevents hovering, and boundary-aware Gaussian noise to guarantee safe, contact-rich interactions. Ablations show that Stage 1 raises the performance ceiling rather than accelerating convergence: pretrained models continue improving during Stage 2 while the from-scratch baseline saturates early, and Grad-CAM visualizations confirm that the inverse-dynamics objective induces emergent attention on manipulable entities even without any text prompt.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark, TAP-20k (pretrained on 20k task-agnostic episodes) achieves 33.32% Avg-All success rate, a +10.2% absolute gain over the Standard BC baseline (23.15%) with identical architecture and Stage 2 data. This matches Octo (31.31%)—trained on ~800k expert trajectories—and surpasses OpenVLA (7.75%), RT-1-X (3.03%), and Nora (20.06%), though it trails π0 (40.08%). In real-world WidowX experiments, TAP achieves 61% average success on the pushing task (vs. NORA's 56%) and, critically, retains 15–25% success under severe camera perturbations where both NORA and Standard BC drop to 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Standard BC baseline (identical Qwen2.5-VL architecture, same 5k expert fine-tuning data, no pretraining), TAP-20k wins by +10.2% absolute on Avg-All (33.32% vs. 23.15%) and +14.0% on Avg-Partial (45.82% vs. 31.79%), confirming that task-agnostic pretraining specifically improves low-level physical grounding. TAP decisively outperforms RT-1-X (33.32% vs. 3.03%), OpenVLA (33.32% vs. 7.75%), and Nora (33.32% vs. 20.06%), all of which were pretrained on ~1M OXE trajectories. TAP slightly edges out Octo (33.32% vs. 31.31%) but trails π0 (33.32% vs. 40.08%), the strongest baseline, which benefits from a flow-matching action head and massive multi-embodiment pretraining. In real-world evaluation, TAP beats NORA on the dynamic pushing task (61% vs. 56% average) and dramatically outperforms both NORA and Standard BC under out-of-distribution perturbations: under viewpoint variation, TAP retains 15–25% success while both baselines collapse to 0%; under background texture shift, TAP achieves 25%/65% vs. NORA's 10%/55% on the two tasks respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the inverse dynamics pretraining line (Brandfonbrener et al., 2023; SMART; PACT) and video pretraining frameworks (VPT, GR-1), but departs from them by using inverse dynamics as a standalone, task-agnostic pretraining phase on robot interaction data rather than as an auxiliary loss or a pseudo-labeling tool for internet videos. It positions itself against the dominant VLA scaling paradigm (RT-1, OpenVLA, π0, Octo, NORA) that relies on millions of expert-teleoperated, language-annotated trajectories, arguing that the physical affordances these models learn can be extracted from far cheaper, unlabeled task-agnostic data. The conceptual lineage draws on developmental psychology (infant motor exploration) to motivate the decoupling of 'how to move' from 'what to do.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: if task-agnostic pretraining can substitute for large fractions of expensive expert data, it lowers the barrier to deploying VLA models in new environments and reduces the 'data wall' that currently limits generalization. However, the current evidence is limited to a single robot platform (WidowX), simple contact-level tasks (grasping, pushing), and modest absolute success rates (~33% in simulation). Open questions include whether the approach scales to dexterous long-horizon manipulation, whether integrating forward dynamics (predicting future states) would close the gap to true embodied world models, and whether active curiosity-driven exploration can replace the current procedural random-play pipeline.
