## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods either jointly optimize forgetting and retaining objectives (causing severe utility degradation) or fully decouple optimizer states (DualOptim), which yields only marginal gains for LLMs. The key issue is that unlike in computer vision, LLM unlearning gradients are not consistently conflicting—especially in later training phases—so a rigidly decoupled optimizer wastes the shared signal. The paper asks how to adaptively bridge shared and decoupled optimizer states based on the degree of directional conflict between the two objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (e.g., Phi-1.5-1.3B, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct), a forget set (QA pairs or instructions to be erased), and a retain set (data whose utility must be preserved), and produces unlearned model parameters via alternating fine-tuning with configurable forget/retain frequencies (F_f, F_r). It is plug-and-play with any optimizer storing states (demonstrated with AdamW and Muon), and the 8-bit variant quantizes all optimizer states via bitsandbytes to reduce peak memory from 3× to 3/4× that of a single Adam.

## Method

DualOptim+ decomposes each optimizer state (e.g., first- and second-order momentum) into a shared base state B updated by both forget and retain gradients, and decoupled delta states Δ_f, Δ_r updated by the residual between each objective's gradient and the bias-corrected base state. The parameter update uses the sum of the bias-corrected base and the respective delta state, so when gradients align the delta vanishes (behaving like Alternate) and when they conflict the base cancels out (behaving like DualOptim). The base state is updated after the parameter step to provide a stable lagged reference for the delta computation. A convergence theorem (Theorem 3.2) proves the asymptotic expectations of B, Δ_f, and Δ_r under alternating gradient dynamics, confirming the adaptive intermediate behavior. An 8-bit quantized variant (DO+ 8bit) stores all states in int8, cutting memory to near-parity with standard Adam while preserving peak performance.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark with IDK+GD (targeted unlearning), DualOptim+ achieves the best Overall Performance (OVR) across both Phi-1.5 and Llama-2: 59.57 vs. 57.96 (DualOptim) and 56.91 (Alternate) on Phi-1.5 at 5% forget, and 74.30 vs. 73.53 (DualOptim) and 73.48 (Alternate) on Llama-2. For untargeted unlearning (ME+GD), DO+ reaches OVR 71.97 on Phi-1.5 and 86.50 on Llama-2 at 5% forget, consistently outperforming all baselines. The 8-bit variant (DO+ 8bit) retains nearly identical performance (e.g., OVR 58.55 vs. 59.57 on Phi-1.5) while reducing memory from 39.04 GB/GPU to 33.68 GB/GPU. On real-world Llama-3 unlearning, DO+ 8bit achieves OVR 73.52 (ME+GD) with downstream task average of 50.70, surpassing DO (67.60 / 41.69) and Alternate (66.49 / 48.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualOptim+ consistently outperforms all baselines on Overall Performance (OVR). Against Joint updating, DO+ gains +8.72 OVR on Phi-1.5 (IDK+GD, 5% forget) and +9.26 on Llama-2, primarily by preserving Model Utility (51.52 vs. 36.26 on Phi-1.5) while maintaining comparable forget efficacy. Against Alternate, DO+ improves OVR by +2.66 on Phi-1.5 and +0.82 on Llama-2 (IDK+GD), with the advantage driven by better TFE (67.60 vs. 64.30) and MU. Against DualOptim (DO), DO+ gains +1.61 OVR on Phi-1.5 and +0.77 on Llama-2 (IDK+GD), confirming that the shared base state captures useful common representations that fully decoupled states miss. In the real-world Llama-3 setting (Table 2), DO+ 8bit achieves OVR 73.52 (ME+GD) versus DO's 67.60 and Alternate's 66.49, with a downstream task average of 50.70 vs. 41.69 (DO) and 48.00 (Alternate). Against federated-learning-inspired baselines (Table 14), DO+ achieves AVG 59.57 vs. SCAFFOLD 58.76, FedCM 58.59, and Local Adam 58.93, attributed to its more frequent base-state updates. The 8-bit variants (DO 8bit, DO+ 8bit) match their full-precision counterparts within ~1 OVR point while reducing memory to near-parity with Joint/Alternate (33.68 vs. 33.26 GB/GPU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DualOptim (Zhong et al., 2025), which decoupled optimizer states for forgetting and retaining objectives in machine unlearning, by introducing a shared base state alongside the decoupled delta states. The motivation departs from the CV-based assumption that forgetting and retaining gradients are persistently conflicting: the authors show empirically that in LLMs, gradient similarity is highly dynamic and often near zero in later training phases, making full decoupling suboptimal. The base+delta decomposition is conceptually analogous to SCAFFOLD/MIME in federated learning, but applied to adversarial multi-objective optimization rather than non-IID client drift, and the 8-bit variant leverages bitsandbytes quantization to offset the memory cost of additional states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a plug-and-play optimizer wrapper that improves the forget-utility trade-off across targeted/untargeted unlearning, safety alignment, and multi-task learning without requiring task-specific tuning, with the 8-bit variant keeping memory overhead near that of standard Adam. The finding that fully decoupled states (DualOptim) can underperform the simpler Alternate scheme on real-world LLM unlearning challenges the assumption that more aggressive gradient separation is always beneficial. Open questions include whether the theoretical convergence guarantees (which assume stationary gradient expectations) hold under the highly non-stationary dynamics of LLM training, whether the approach scales to more than two objectives in practice, and whether the modest 1–3 point gains persist at larger model scales (the largest model tested is 8B).
