## Problem

In the sparse spiked Wigner model, the information-theoretic threshold for recovering an s-sparse spike is β = Õ(√s), yet all known polynomial-time algorithms require β = Õ(s), leaving a substantial computational-statistical gap. This gap is conjectured to be fundamental for uniform spikes under the planted clique hypothesis, but the complexity landscape for non-uniform spikes remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether a concrete class of non-uniform spikes admits polynomial-time recovery at the information-theoretic rate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d×d noisy symmetric matrix Y = βuu^T + W (with W ~ GOE(d)) and the sparsity level s as input, and produces an estimated s-sparse unit vector û ∈ ℝ^d along with its support set T̂. The key structural assumption is ‖u‖∞ = Ω(1), which rules out the uniform spike case and is naturally satisfied by power-law decaying signals. The algorithm runs in O(d log d + s³) time, matching the cost of diagonal thresholding while requiring only O(√(s log d)) signal strength for constant-error recovery.

## Method

The core contribution is a column thresholding procedure that replaces the diagonal-entry statistic of classical thresholding with a column-entry statistic: it first identifies the index i₀ of the largest diagonal entry of Y, then selects the s largest entries in the i₀-th column to estimate the support, and reconstructs the spike via the leading eigenvector of the corresponding s×s submatrix. The key insight is that the column-based gap g_col = β|u_{i₀}|·min|u_i| dominates the diagonal gap g_diag = β·min|u_i|² whenever ‖u‖∞ = Ω(1), yielding the improved Õ(√s) signal requirement. A normalization variant (Algorithm 2) skips the eigendecomposition and directly normalizes the selected column, trading modest accuracy for O(d log d) runtime. Building on this initializer, a truncated power method (TPM) iteratively refines the estimate via sparse matrix-vector multiplication and hard thresholding, achieving geometric convergence to an irreducible statistical floor in O(log ζ⁻¹) iterations under the same optimal signal strength.

## Results

On the three-peak benchmark (p=5000, β=100), TPM achieves estimation error 0.10 and F-score 0.88, outperforming diagonal thresholding (DT: error 0.68, F-score 0.26), covariance thresholding (CT: error 0.14, F-score 0.83), and spectral projection (SP: error 0.17, F-score 0.80). Empirically, column thresholding achieves constant estimation error when β/√(s log d) ≥ 10 and exact support recovery when β/√(s log d) ≥ 7.5, confirming the theoretical Õ(√s) signal-strength scaling. In the growing-s experiment (ψ=0.2), support recovery success rate transitions from near-zero at s=d^0.4 (Impossible/Hard boundary) to 1.0 at s=d^0.6 (Hard region), matching the predicted phase diagram.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPM dominates all three baselines on the three-peak benchmark: it reduces estimation error by 6.8× over DT (0.10 vs 0.68), 1.4× over CT (0.10 vs 0.14), and 1.7× over SP (0.10 vs 0.17), while achieving the highest F-score (0.88 vs 0.26, 0.83, 0.80). In the signal-strength sweep (Figure 5, d=2000, s=10), TPM's advantage is most pronounced in the weak-signal regime where CT and SP degrade rapidly, and TPM consistently outperforms DT across all tested β values. On runtime, TPM scales comparably to DT (both O(d log d + s³)) while CT and SP are substantially more expensive (SP requires O(d³) for full eigendecomposition). The key theoretical distinction is that column thresholding (the initialization step of TPM) requires only β = Õ(√s) under ‖u‖∞ = Ω(1), whereas DT and CT require β = Õ(s) and SP requires β = Õ(√d), placing TPM in a regime inaccessible to the other methods for non-uniform spikes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparse spiked Wigner model framework (Deshpande & Montanari, 2014) and the computational-statistical gap characterization of Brennan et al. (2018), which established that polynomial-time algorithms require β = Ω̃(s) while the information-theoretic threshold is β = Ω̃(√s). The key methodological departure is replacing diagonal-entry thresholding (Johnstone & Lu, 2009) with column-entry thresholding, which yields a stronger in-support/out-of-support separation. The truncated power method refinement stage (Yuan & Zhang, 2013) is used as-is, but the paper's contribution is providing the missing concrete initialization that satisfies Yuan & Zhang's correlation condition at the optimal signal level, rather than developing new convergence theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the computational-statistical gap for a natural and practically relevant class of non-uniform spikes (power-law decaying signals with ‖u‖∞ = Ω(1)), showing that the planted-clique hardness barrier does not extend to this class. The paper is explicit that it does not resolve the hard regime for uniform spikes, leaving open whether the gap is truly fundamental there or whether other structural assumptions could also enable polynomial-time recovery at the √s scaling. A natural extension discussed is applying the column-thresholding initializer to the symmetric two-cluster sparse Gaussian mixture model, where the expected covariance has the same structural form.
