## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods produce overconfident yet erroneous predictions on unseen domains, lacking reliable uncertainty quantification. While ensemble learning combined with knowledge distillation could address this, naively applying standard uncertainty distillation (e.g., Dirichlet NLL) to DG tasks degrades classification accuracy, and the data allocation strategy for ensembling and distillation in the multi-domain setting has been largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple source domains (e.g., PACS with 4 domains, DomainNet with 6 domains) and produces both a class label (via softmax on student logits) and a Dirichlet concentration vector α = exp(z) that parameterizes a distribution over class probabilities, enabling epistemic uncertainty estimation at inference. The student is a single ResNet-50 model, so inference requires only one forward pass, unlike test-time ensembles that need M forward passes. Teachers are trained on individual domains with reduced batch sizes (1/M of standard), and distillation targets are generated on complementary domains the teacher has never seen.

## Method

DomED trains M domain-specific teacher models, each on a single source domain, then performs complementary distillation: for samples from domain m, the soft target is the average of predictions from all teachers except the one trained on domain m, which theoretically filters out domain-specific spurious correlations (proven via a linear structural equation model). To preserve uncertainty without sacrificing accuracy, the method decouples the distillation objective into two orthogonal losses: a standard temperature-scaled cross-entropy loss L_S that anchors the mean prediction, and a Dirichlet NLL loss L_Dir (weighted by a small β≈0.01) that shapes the predictive distribution's spread to match ensemble disagreement. This decoupling resolves the optimization conflict where a single Dirichlet NLL term simultaneously tries to match both the mean and variance of teacher predictions, which causes accuracy degradation in the DG setting. The complementary allocation also reduces training cost since each teacher sees only 1/M of the data and the student saturates before teachers fully converge.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark, DomED achieves an average accuracy of 66.8% across five datasets (PACS, OfficeHome, VLCS, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), outperforming the ERM baseline (64.4%) by +2.4 pp and surpassing strong standalone DG methods like DomainDrop (66.5%) and GMDG (66.3%). When combined with SWAD weight averaging, DomED reaches 67.8% average accuracy, competitive with test-time ensembling (EoA, 68.0%) and diverse weight averaging (DiWA, 67.1%) while requiring significantly less inference overhead. On uncertainty quantification (PACS), DomED achieves ECE of 0.044 and PRR of 0.787, closely matching a full 3-model ensemble (ECE 0.038, PRR 0.798) while using a single forward pass at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a standalone method, DomED (66.8% avg) outperforms ERM (+2.4 pp), MIRO (+0.9 pp), SAGM (+0.7 pp), DomainDrop (+0.3 pp), and GMDG (+0.5 pp), with the largest gain on OfficeHome (+4.1 pp over ERM) where inter-domain gaps are most pronounced. Among weight-averaging methods, DomED+SWAD (67.8% avg) surpasses DNA+SWAD (67.6%) and CORAL+SWAD (67.3%), and is within 0.2 pp of EoA (68.0%), which requires test-time ensembling of multiple models at inference. Compared to DiWA (67.1%), which averages weights from 60 training runs, DomED+SWAD achieves higher accuracy with far less training overhead. On uncertainty quantification (PACS), DomED (ECE 0.044, PRR 0.787) substantially outperforms ERM (ECE 0.089, PRR 0.776), CORAL (ECE 0.082, PRR 0.598), and MC Dropout (ECE 0.129, PRR 0.746), while closely matching a full 3-model ensemble (ECE 0.038, PRR 0.798) at a fraction of the inference cost. The only method that consistently outperforms DomED on accuracy is EoA on TerraIncognita (52.3 vs. 51.9) and DomainNet (47.4 vs. 46.7), but EoA requires running multiple models at test time, making DomED more practical for deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

DomED builds on the knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al. (2015) and the Dirichlet-based ensemble distribution distillation of Malinin et al. (2019), but departs from both in two key ways. First, rather than training all ensemble members on the full dataset (standard ensembling) or a single model (self-distillation à la Lee et al., 2022), it exploits domain labels to train each teacher on a single source domain and distill on complementary unseen domains, a data allocation strategy not previously studied for DG. Second, it decouples the Dirichlet NLL (uncertainty) from the cross-entropy (accuracy) loss, addressing an accuracy degradation that standard EnD²-style distillation exhibits in the DG setting. The theoretical analysis connects the complementary distillation target to spurious-correlation filtering in a linear structural equation model, extending the IRM/REX line of work with a distillation-specific mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing DG methods achieve reasonable accuracy but produce overconfident, poorly calibrated predictions on OOD data, and standard uncertainty distillation (EnD²) degrades accuracy when applied to DG. The decoupled distillation recipe (small β on Dirichlet NLL alongside standard CE) is a simple, broadly applicable fix that practitioners can adopt. Limitations include the ~50% training overhead over ERM, the restriction to classification with a fixed number of domains equal to ensemble size, and evaluation limited to ResNet-50 on mid-scale benchmarks. Open questions include whether the complementary allocation and decoupling strategy transfer to regression, dense prediction, or settings with many more domains than practical teacher models.
