## Problem

Camera pose estimation and camera-controlled video generation have been treated as separate tasks, with pose estimators (SfM, COLMAP, DUSt3R) producing fixed conditioning for video generators. This decoupling creates fragility under sparse views or ambiguous motion, since each task depends on the other's output. No prior model learns the joint distribution over video frames and camera trajectories, preventing a single framework from performing both pose prediction and trajectory-conditioned generation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts video clips (for pose estimation), one or more sparse source images at 480×832 (which can occupy any temporal position in the sequence), and optionally a predefined camera trajectory. Outputs include recovered camera trajectories (decoded from raxel images via Procrustes alignment and median-of-ratios focal estimation), generated video sequences, or both jointly. Camera parameters are internally represented as dense 3-channel raxel images at half resolution (H/2 × W/2 × 3), where each pixel encodes the sum of ray direction and origin in a canonicalized coordinate frame, making them directly compatible with the pretrained 3-channel spatio-temporal VAE.

## Method

The core contribution is the raxel representation: each camera is encoded as a dense per-pixel image where every pixel stores the world-space ray origin plus direction (Rd + T), a 3-channel format that shares the same VAE latent space as RGB video frames, eliminating the need for adapter modules or channel-mismatched Plücker embeddings. Built on the Wan 2.1 14B T2V backbone, the model introduces Decoupled Self-Cross Attention (DSCA), which replaces standard self-attention with separate intra-modal self-attention for video and ray latents followed by bidirectional inter-modal cross-attention, motivated by the chain-rule factorization of the joint log-likelihood. Training uses flow matching with a combined MSE and cosine-similarity velocity loss, jointly denoising video and ray latents from a shared Gaussian prior. At inference, asymmetric schedules exploit the fact that ray latents converge in ~2 steps while video latents require more, enabling efficient pose estimation. Pose recovery from decoded raxels uses closed-form Orthogonal Procrustes alignment against the reference frame bundle, followed by a robust median-of-ratios estimator for focal lengths.

## Results

On camera-controlled video generation (Table 4), Rays as Pixels achieves the best FID and FVD across all three benchmarks: FID 15.76 / FVD 98.72 on RealEstate10K, FID 9.73 / FVD 102.52 on DL3DV-140, and FID 13.02 / FVD 187.03 on Tanks and Temples, outperforming all five baselines on visual quality and temporal coherence. On camera pose estimation (Table 3), the model reaches its peak mRRA@30 at 2 diffusion steps (95.91% on Re10K, 88.37% on DL3DV-140, 93.51% on T&T), trailing VGGT by 2–4 points but without requiring explicit 3D pointmap supervision. The cycle self-consistency ablation (Table 2) confirms that the raxel representation is the primary load-bearing design choice, with the Plücker-embedding variant degrading FID from 7.33 to 21.97 and FVD from 68.17 to 333.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rays as Pixels achieves the best FID and FVD on all three benchmarks, beating the strongest visual-quality competitor Wonderland by 0.4 FID / 54.8 FVD on Re10K, 8.0 FID / 66.8 FVD on DL3DV-140, and 6.4 FID / 2.3 FVD on T&T. Kaleido, the closest FVD competitor on Re10K (103.03 vs 98.72), suffers a dramatic FID collapse on DL3DV-140 (41.18 vs 9.73), suggesting its camera positional embedding struggles with wide-baseline frames. On trajectory adherence (R_err, T_err), Kaleido is the clear leader (e.g., R_err 0.011 on DL3DV-140 vs 0.098 for Ours), while Wonderland, VD3D, and ViewCrafter cluster in the 0.05–0.12 range; Rays as Pixels trades some trajectory precision for substantially better visual fidelity. Against MotionCtrl, the gap is largest on T&T (FID 13.02 vs 30.17), where the cluttered large-scale scenes expose MotionCtrl's camera-matrix conditioning. For pose estimation, VGGT outperforms Rays as Pixels on all three benchmarks (e.g., 98.07 vs 95.91 mRRA@30 on Re10K), which the authors attribute to VGGT's explicit 3D pointmap output encoding geometry directly, whereas Rays as Pixels infers poses from a learned raxel modality without explicit 3D supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan 2.1 14B video diffusion backbone and Flow Matching as its generative framework, but departs fundamentally from prior camera-controlled VDMs (MotionCtrl, VD3D, CameraCtrl) that inject camera parameters as fixed conditioning via adapters or Plücker embeddings. The key conceptual departure is representing cameras as dense 3-channel 'raxel' images that share the same pretrained VAE latent space as video frames, enabling joint denoising of both modalities rather than treating poses as solved prerequisites. The Decoupled Self-Cross Attention mechanism is a new architectural component motivated by the structural asymmetry between high-frequency visual latents and smooth ray latents, and the joint distribution learning (p(z,r) vs. conditional p(z|r) or p(r|z)) is the paper's central framing that distinguishes it from all prior camera-conditioned or pose-estimation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The raxel representation establishes a general pattern for encoding non-visual modalities as image-compatible tensors that share a pretrained visual backbone's latent space, potentially applicable to segmentation, depth, or other spatially-aligned signals. The cycle self-consistency test is a novel evaluation that only joint-distribution models can pass, providing a principled way to verify that forward and inverse predictions agree. However, the model does not surpass dedicated pose estimators like VGGT, is limited to static scenes with smooth trajectories, and the 4× VAE temporal compression introduces information loss. Open questions include whether the raxel pattern generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether it extends to other modalities beyond cameras, and whether the joint distribution framing yields advantages at larger scale.
