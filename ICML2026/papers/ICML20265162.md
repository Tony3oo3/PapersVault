## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-video diffusion models assume that undesired concepts can be suppressed in a layer-agnostic manner, applying interventions at fixed or heuristically chosen depths. However, semantic representations in diffusion transformers are organized in a depth-dependent structure, so the same erasure operation at different layers produces markedly different outcomes—often suppressing coarse categories while missing fine-grained concepts. The paper identifies this as a representational bottleneck (concept-layer topological alignment) and asks: at which representational depth does concept-non-target separability naturally emerge for a given target?

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained T2V diffusion model (Wan2.2-5B or CogVideoX-2B), a target concept C, and LLM-synthesized positive/negative prompt pairs that share identical semantics except for the target concept. Intervention is applied exclusively within the T5-based text encoder blocks (not the diffusion backbone), and the entire training pipeline—topological search plus SAE learning—completes in approximately two hours on a single H100 GPU. At inference, the trained SAE is attached at the single selected layer and the target concept is erased by subtracting an aligned sparse feature vector from the hidden state, producing a video with the concept suppressed while preserving non-target semantics and visual fidelity.

## Method

CLEAR jointly optimizes where to intervene and how to erase through two coupled components. For depth selection, it models intervention location as a soft preference distribution over all text-encoder layers using learnable parameters α, sampled via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation with linearly annealed temperature, making the discrete layer choice differentiable. For the erasure mechanism, a Sparse Autoencoder decomposes dense activations into a high-dimensional sparse feature space, and contrastive masks (specificity vs. shared) separate concept-specific from non-target features using positive/negative prompt activations. Training alternates between preference-weighted SAE reconstruction and a separability-aware contrastive loss L_con that penalizes dominance of shared energy over concept-specific energy, steering depth preferences toward layers with maximal concept-non-target separation. At inference, the distribution collapses to a single layer l*, and erasure is performed by projecting the masked sparse features back to model space and subtracting them from the hidden state with a tunable strength γ, requiring no modification to the diffusion model weights.

## Results

On Wan2.2-5B, CLEAR reduces the average object-concept Generative Rate from 61.8% (origin) to 12.8%, while improving Imaging Quality from 0.6910 to 0.7025. On CogVideoX-2B, CLEAR achieves the lowest Generative Rate of 7.1% with substantially better Imaging Quality (0.4683) than T2VUnlearning (0.3907). For nudity erasure on Wan2.2-5B, CLEAR attains 11.1% Generative Rate versus 18.6% for T2VUnlearning and 48.7% for SAFREE, while maintaining Imaging Quality of 0.6928 (vs. 0.6913 origin). The full pipeline completes in ~2 hours on a single H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEAR outperforms all three primary baselines (NegPrompt, SAFREE, T2VUnlearning) on Generative Rate across both architectures and all four concept categories. On Wan2.2-5B objects, CLEAR achieves 12.8% vs. NegPrompt (54.3%), SAFREE (28.1%), and T2VUnlearning (24.5%); on nudity, CLEAR reaches 11.1% vs. T2VUnlearning (18.6%) and SAFREE (48.7%). Against T2VUnlearning specifically, CLEAR matches or beats erasure effectiveness while preserving substantially higher Imaging Quality (0.7025 vs. 0.6652 on Wan; 0.4683 vs. 0.3907 on CogVideoX), indicating that global weight modification causes catastrophic forgetting that CLEAR avoids. Against VideoEraser (compared on CogVideoX-2B), CLEAR achieves lower nudity Generative Rate (14.63% vs. 19.22%) and outperforms on several celebrity identities (Biden, Elizabeth), though VideoEraser attains a lower average identity similarity (0.1140 vs. 0.1802) at the cost of ~1.4× inference overhead from online gradient optimization. CLEAR's key advantage is that it operates as a cached, single-layer SAE intervention with negligible additional latency, making it more deployment-friendly than both training-time and online-optimization baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAE-based concept erasure in diffusion models (Huben et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2025; Cywiński & Deja, 2025), which established that sparse feature decomposition enables selective concept suppression, but those methods treat intervention depth as a fixed hyperparameter. CLEAR departs from the prevailing layer-agnostic assumption in T2V concept erasure (NegPrompt, SAFREE, T2VUnlearning, VideoEraser) by identifying a depth-dependent representational bottleneck—concept-layer topological alignment—and formulating layer selection as a differentiable optimization problem via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation. The method thus combines two previously separate threads: mechanistic interpretability (SAE feature decomposition) and differentiable architecture search, applied jointly to the 'where' and 'how' of erasure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that different concepts localize at distinct text-encoder depths and that multi-layer intervention degrades performance (feature interference) provides a concrete, actionable principle for deploying concept erasure in production T2V systems without retraining model weights. However, the per-concept optimization requirement limits scalability to large concept libraries, and the method has only been validated on T5-based encoders with preliminary transfer to FLUX and CLIP-based encoders. Open questions include whether a single shared SAE dictionary can serve multiple concepts, how the approach handles relational or compositional concepts, and whether the depth-dependence finding generalizes to non-T5 text encoders or to the video diffusion backbone itself rather than the text encoder.
