## Problem

Predictive coding networks, which perform inference through local iterative energy minimization, suffer severe performance degradation beyond 5–7 layers, making them incompatible with the deep residual architectures that underpin modern deep learning. The paper identifies three root causes: exponentially imbalanced layer-wise errors during relaxation, feed-forward predictions failing to anchor weight updates in deep layers, and asynchronous energy propagation through skip connections in ResNets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263705/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, higher resolution for Galaxy10 DECals) and outputs class labels via a standard softmax head. Unusually, training does not use a single forward–backward pass: instead, the output layer is clamped to the target label and all hidden activities iteratively relax over T steps to minimize a precision-weighted prediction-energy, with weights updated either once (PC) or at every relaxation step (iPC).

## Method

The paper introduces four complementary mechanisms. (1) Spiking Precision sets the layer covariance to a small value (≈α) at the exact time step t = L−l when error energy first reaches layer l, boosting the effective feedback gain from O(α) to O(1) and counteracting the exponential signal attenuation proven in Theorem 1. (2) Forward Update replaces the standard weight gradient (which uses the prediction error at convergence) with a gradient computed against the initial feed-forward prediction μ₀ˡ, anchoring updates to the manifold the network will actually see at test time and eliminating the need for center nudging. (3) Auxiliary buffer neurons are inserted along every skip connection so that the residual path introduces the same propagation delay as the main path, achieving phase synchronization of error signals. (4) BatchNorm Freezing freezes running-statistic updates during the iterative relaxation phase to prevent overfitting of normalization statistics. Together, these allow PC and iPC to match backpropagation accuracy on VGG-15 and ResNet-18 across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet-32.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the proposed iPC+S achieves 91.96% top-1 accuracy and PC+S+F achieves 91.93%, compared to 93.13% for backpropagation, while standard PC and iPC collapse to 15.63% and 21.90% respectively. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, iPC+S reaches 70.38% and PC+S+F reaches 70.90% top-1, versus 71.69% for BP, while baselines drop to ~1.6%. On TinyImageNet with ResNet-18, iPC+S achieves 57.83% top-1 (vs. 58.18% BP), and PC+S+F achieves 54.24%, demonstrating that the methods scale to 200-class and higher-resolution settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: standard backpropagation (BP), unmodified predictive coding (PC), and unmodified incremental predictive coding (iPC). The proposed methods (iPC+S and PC+S+F) match or nearly match BP across all architectures and datasets: on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, iPC+S (91.96%) and PC+S+F (91.93%) trail BP (93.13%) by only ~1.2%, while on CIFAR-10 VGG10, PC+S+F (93.27%) actually exceeds BP (92.68%). In stark contrast, both PC and iPC baselines suffer catastrophic degradation with depth: on ResNet-18 CIFAR-10, PC drops to 15.63% and iPC to 21.90%, representing a gap of over 70 percentage points from the proposed methods. On the harder CIFAR-100 benchmark, the same pattern holds — PC and iPC collapse to ~1.6% on ResNet-18 while iPC+S and PC+S+F maintain 70.38% and 70.90% respectively, within 1% of BP. On TinyImageNet, iPC+S matches BP within 0.35% on ResNet-18 (57.83% vs. 58.18%), while PC+S+F lags by ~4%, suggesting the forward update mechanism introduces a small cost on more complex distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predictive coding (PC) and incremental PC (iPC) training frameworks, which themselves build on Rao & Ballard's hierarchical predictive coding theory and Whittington & Bogacz's demonstration that PC approximates backpropagation. It directly addresses the depth limitation identified in recent benchmarking work (Pinchetti et al., 2025), which showed PC degrades beyond 5–7 layers, by diagnosing the root cause as exponential signal attenuation in the iterative relaxation phase. The spiking precision mechanism draws on the precision-weighting concept from predictive processing neuroscience (Feldman & Friston, 2010; Bastos et al., 2012), which prior ML formulations had set to a constant 1, while the forward update and auxiliary neuron ideas are novel architectural/algorithmic modifications specific to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling PC and iPC to match backpropagation on ResNet18 and VGG15 across CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet, this work removes a major barrier to deploying energy-based local learning rules on deep architectures, which is critical for neuromorphic and analog hardware where sequential forward-backward passes are infeasible. A key limitation is that the forward update requires storing initial feed-forward predictions, introducing temporal non-locality that reduces biological plausibility; however, iPC with spiking precision alone achieves comparable performance without this requirement. Open questions include whether these techniques scale to 100+ layer networks, whether they generalize to non-convolutional or transformer architectures, and whether more biologically plausible weight updates (e.g., along the lines of μPC) can subsume the forward update mechanism.
