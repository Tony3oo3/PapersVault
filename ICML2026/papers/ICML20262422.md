## Problem

Egocentric reasoning in LVLMs suffers from 'inertial thinking': models over-rely on pretrained language priors and global scene context rather than attending to localized, transient human-object interaction cues. This is compounded by two training-level issues—datasets contain many shortcut-solvable samples that reinforce prior-based reasoning, and standard RL objectives assign identical rewards to interaction-grounded and prior-biased reasoning paths, creating a reward-reality mismatch.

## Input / Output

The method consumes egocentric video frames (from Ego4D) paired with text questions in both open-ended and close-ended QA formats. During the selection phase, each sample is processed three times: with full multimodal input, with text-only input, and with the interaction region masked (identified via CLIP similarity to the query). The model outputs a reasoning chain followed by a text answer; during RL, the response is parsed for mentioned objects whose CLIP similarity to the ground-truth interaction region determines the interaction-centric reward.

## Method

Ego3S is a three-stage framework. First, the Counterfactual-based Selection Mechanism (CSM) applies two filters: a coarse-grained filter discards samples where text-only predictions match ground truth (language-prior shortcuts), and a fine-grained filter removes samples where masking the interaction region (selected via sliding-window CLIP matching) does not change correctness (global-context shortcuts). Second, Interaction-centric RL (IRL) augments the GRPO objective with a reward term that positively scores responses mentioning objects verifiably present in the interaction region (via CLIP similarity threshold) and a penalty term discouraging redundant object extraction, thereby forcing a trade-off between statistical priors and visual interaction semantics. Third, Evolving Curriculum Recuration (ECR) monitors reward variance on a fixed probing set at periodic intervals; when the stagnation ratio (fraction of samples with unchanged variance) exceeds a threshold, the entire training set is regenerated by re-applying CSM on a larger data pool using the latest model weights, preventing learning plateaus.

## Results

Ego3S achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five egocentric benchmarks using only 26.5% of the training data (3,180 out of 12,000 samples), reducing training time from 60.5h to 32.6h (a 46% reduction). On the primary Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, Ego3S scores 36.83 on Egoplan2, 66.80 on QAEgo4D, 22.40 on EgoTextVQA, 49.20 on EgoSchema, and 58.71 on Egothink (all pass@1). The method also generalizes to smaller models, with Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Ego3S reaching 25.50 on Egoplan2 and 60.00 on QAEgo4D while using only 25.6h of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ego3S outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest data-selection baseline LIMR (103.2h), Ego3S gains +5.37 on Egoplan2 (36.83 vs 31.46), +1.60 on QAEgo4D, +1.80 on EgoTextVQA, +1.00 on EgoSchema, and +1.78 on Egothink, while using only 63% of LIMR's training time. Against the strongest RL baseline DAPO (62.4h), Ego3S leads by +5.96 on Egoplan2, +2.20 on QAEgo4D, +1.80 on EgoTextVQA, +1.80 on EgoSchema, and +1.47 on Egothink. Compared to the full-dataset baseline (60.5h), Ego3S improves by +6.40 on Egoplan2 and +3.00 on QAEgo4D while cutting training time by 46%. The IRL component alone (without CSM) already surpasses GRPO by +4.99 on Egoplan2 and +2.60 on QAEgo4D, confirming that the interaction-centric reward is the primary driver of gains. On the third-person NExT-QA benchmark, Ego3S yields only a marginal +0.8% relative improvement versus +3.0% on the egocentric QAEgo4D, validating that the method's advantage is specific to egocentric reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as its RL backbone, extending it with an interaction-centric reward that explicitly incentivizes grounding in localized visual interaction regions rather than relying on binary answer-correctness signals. It departs from prior data selection methods (LIMO, s1, LIMR) that use difficulty scoring or learning-impact metrics by introducing a counterfactual-based selection mechanism specifically designed to isolate samples where correct answers causally depend on interaction evidence rather than language priors or global scene context. The three-stage pipeline (select → strengthen → synchronize) also extends curriculum learning ideas by using reward variance as a dynamic trigger for data refresh, a mechanism not previously applied to egocentric video reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode in egocentric LVLMs—'inertial thinking' where models hallucinate plausible answers from linguistic priors instead of attending to what is actually visible in the interaction. The 46% compute reduction while improving accuracy is practically significant for teams deploying egocentric systems (robotics, wearable AI) with limited training budgets. However, the interaction-centric reward remains a heuristic binary signal vulnerable to reward hacking (Goodhart's Law), and the authors themselves flag generative reward models as the necessary next step. The asymmetry between egocentric (+3.0%) and third-person (+0.8%) gains, while validating the focus, also suggests the method's benefits are domain-specific rather than generalizable to all video understanding.
