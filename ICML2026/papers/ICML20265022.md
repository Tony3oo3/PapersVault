## Problem

Existing interactive world models rely on synthetic data with perfect camera extrinsics, making them ill-suited for real-world videos where pose estimation is noisy and viewpoint revisits are scarce. This creates a threefold gap: unreliable action-response mapping, inability to learn global geometric structure from linear video streams, and lack of a computationally efficient memory mechanism that avoids explicit geometric priors. The paper tackles how to achieve stable 1000+ frame interactive simulation on raw, uncurated real-world footage.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single initial frame (synthesized at 480P via a text-to-image model) and a sequence of discrete keyboard-style actions (W/A/S/D for translation, Left/Right/Up/Down for rotation) as input, and produces a long-horizon video of 1000+ frames at 480P resolution. Actions are discretized through a tri-state labeling scheme (No-operation, Discrete Action, Uncertain) derived from estimated relative camera poses, and injected as 1D-convolutional embeddings temporally aligned to the 4×-downsampled latent grid. The compressed historical context is concatenated with the last-frame latent and noisy target latents, with a binary mask distinguishing conditioning from denoising regions.

## Method

The core architecture is a Hierarchical Pose-free Memory Compressor (HPMC) built on a 3D-ResNet encoder jointly optimized with a Wan-2.1-1.3B DiT backbone. For short contexts, raw latents are directly compressed at a 4× temporal factor; for long horizons, latents are partitioned into N=5 overlapping chunks (W=64 frames) that undergo local compression, then a second global compression pass, recursively distilling arbitrary-length history into a fixed budget of T_max=20 tokens with constant computational cost. An Uncertainty-aware Action Labeling module discretizes 6-DoF pose changes into a tri-state space using two thresholds (noise floor and action trigger), shielding the deterministic action space from jitter while retaining low-SNR samples as 'Uncertain' rather than discarding them. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: open-domain pre-training on 30+ hours of internet video with short contexts, followed by finetuning on a compact 30-minute Revisit-Dense Dataset (RDD) that activates loop-closure capability, motivated by a pilot study showing spatial memory saturates at ~100 diverse sequences and collapses when inference exceeds the training temporal window.

## Results

On the 100-scenario long-horizon interactive benchmark, Infinite-World achieves an ELO rating of 1719, a 177-point lead over the next-best model HY-World-1.5 (1542). In the user study, it ranks first in Memory Consistency (1.92) and Visual Fidelity (1.67), and second in Action Responsiveness (1.54, vs. HY-World-1.5's 1.50). On VBench, Infinite-World scores 0.8119 average (second only to Yume 1.5's 0.8141, which benefits from a 5B-parameter backbone), with perfect Dynamic Degree (1.0000) and strong Image Quality (0.7159).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Infinite-World decisively outperforms all four baselines in the user study ELO rating (1719 vs. 1542 for HY-World-1.5, 1495 for Yume 1.5, 1432 for Matrix-Game 2.0, and 1311 for Hunyuan-GameCraft). It achieves the best Memory Consistency rank (1.92) and Visual Fidelity rank (1.67), substantially ahead of Yume 1.5 (2.43 / 1.91) and HY-World-1.5 (2.59 / 2.78), validating the HPMC's long-range loop-closure capability. On Action Responsiveness, Infinite-World (1.54) is nearly tied with HY-World-1.5 (1.50), which is notable because HY-World-1.5 trains on perfectly-labeled synthetic data while Infinite-World uses noisy real-world trajectories. On VBench, Yume 1.5 edges out Infinite-World in average score (0.8141 vs. 0.8119) primarily due to higher aesthetic quality from its 5B-parameter backbone, but the paper argues Yume 1.5's scores are inflated by a motion-distribution bias that causes it to default to forward-moving trajectories, avoiding the challenge of complex viewpoint transitions. Matrix-Game 2.0 and Hunyuan-GameCraft trail significantly on all user-study dimensions, reflecting their lack of effective out-of-view memory mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interactive world model lineage (GameGen-X, Matrix-Game 2.0, Hunyuan-GameCraft, Yume, HY-World-1.5) by shifting the training paradigm from synthetic/game data with perfect pose labels to raw real-world video. It departs from pose-dependent memory retrieval (FOV overlap, 3D reconstruction) and uniform temporal downsampling (FramePack, PFP) by introducing a hierarchical, pose-free memory compressor that recursively distills history into a fixed budget, and from continuous or binary action spaces by proposing a tri-state uncertainty-aware labeling scheme. The Revisit-Dense Finetuning strategy is motivated by a pilot toy study showing that loop-closure capability is a topological property of trajectories rather than a function of total data volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for closing the reality gap in interactive world modeling: a 30-minute revisit-dense dataset can 'activate' 1000-frame loop-closure, suggesting that the bottleneck for long-horizon spatial memory is trajectory topology, not data scale. This has direct implications for data curation strategies in embodied AI and autonomous driving simulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (the model is 1.3B at 480P), whether self-forcing or refined noise schedules can further mitigate cumulative drift, and whether the pose-free memory generalizes to multi-scene or outdoor environments with more complex geometry.
