## Problem

High-throughput gene perturbation screens aim to discover many distinct perturbations whose phenotypic effect exceeds a threshold, yet standard Bayesian optimization targets a single global optimum and pure exploration wastes budget on low-value regions. This mismatch is acute in biological settings where response landscapes are multimodal and hits are distributed across disconnected regions, making neither exploitation nor exploration alone sufficient for maximizing hit recovery under a fixed experimental budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a candidate gene set (tens of thousands of genes represented by 808-dimensional Achilles feature vectors), a user-specified hit threshold τ, and a probabilistic surrogate model that provides a posterior predictive mean μ_t(g) and variance σ_t²(g) for each candidate at round t. At each of T sequential rounds, it outputs a batch of b genes to evaluate in parallel (without observing intra-batch outcomes), and cumulatively produces the set of discovered hits and a hit ratio |G_T|/|G*|. The surrogate is model-agnostic in principle but instantiated as a GP with RBF kernel for synthetic data and a Bayesian MLP with Monte Carlo Dropout (50 samples, 128-64 hidden layers, p=0.2) for real datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is the Probability-of-Hit (PoH) acquisition function, which ranks each unsampled candidate g by its posterior probability of exceeding the threshold, p_t(g) = 1 − Φ((τ − μ_t(g))/σ_t(g)), and selects the top-b candidates per round. This directly targets threshold exceedance rather than maximizing predicted value or reducing uncertainty, naturally balancing exploitation of known high-response regions with exploration of uncertain but promising areas. The paper proves that under standard posterior concentration and margin assumptions, PoH recovers at least Tb/2 − o(Tb) hits with high probability, via a martingale concentration argument combined with a one-sided CDF bound and a batched information-gain analysis. The approach is model-agnostic and requires only one posterior pass followed by top-b ranking per round, making it computationally cheaper than nested BAX-based alternatives like DiscoBAX.

## Results

On five real-world CRISPR screening datasets (b=200, 10 cycles, 5 seeds), Probability-of-Hit recovers the most hits on 3 of 5 datasets, with the largest gain on Schmidt IL-2 (575.4±17 hits vs. 538.2±36 for Thompson-Hit, a ~6.9% improvement). On synthetic benchmarks (b=5, 10 cycles, 20 seeds), PoH achieves the highest cumulative hit ratio at τ=10% across all four datasets at both t=5 and t=10, e.g., 0.88±0.05 on Sine(2D) and 0.62±0.14 on Perturbation-Pathways(4D) at t=10. Against threshold-aware baselines (Table 3), PoH achieves the best CHR@10 on all five synthetic datasets after 5 cycles, with the largest margin on Sine(1D) (0.81 vs. 0.74 for Thompson-Hit). The theoretical guarantee of recovering at least Tb/2 − o(Tb) hits with high probability is supported empirically by consistent outperformance over random sampling (Cliff's δ=0.894 on real data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Thompson Sampling (Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2017), PoH wins on 3 of 5 real datasets (Schmidt IFN-γ, Schmidt IL-2, Sanchez Tau) but loses on Zhu SARS-CoV-2 (251.6 vs. 261.0) and Zhuang NK (246.0 vs. 251.6), where the feature-phenotype correlation is weakest. Against Top-K Greedy (Neiswanger et al., 2021), PoH achieves consistent wins on all real datasets and all synthetic datasets, with the largest gap on Schmidt IL-2 (+58.8 hits). Against the threshold-aware baselines Straddle (Gotovos et al., 2013) and TruVaR (Bogunovic et al., 2016), PoH dominates on all five synthetic datasets (Table 3), e.g., 0.81 vs. 0.50 on Sine(1D) and 0.35 vs. 0.34 on Perturbation-SEM(6D), with TruVaR performing worst overall. Against DiscoBAX (Lyle et al., 2023), PoH achieves higher hit recovery at a fraction of the computational cost; DiscoBAX is only evaluated on a small synthetic setting due to its expensive nested posterior-sampling loop. The paper notes that PoH's advantage is largest when the Achilles feature representation carries strong phenotype-relevant signal (Schmidt IL-2, S=0.21), while on rugged landscapes with weak feature-phenotype correlation (Zhuang NK, S=1.76), exploration-heavy methods like Thompson-Hit close the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from classical Bayesian optimization (UCB, EI) which targets a single global optimum, and from level-set estimation methods (Straddle, TruVaR, LS-BAX) which focus on boundary accuracy, by formalizing hit discovery as maximizing the count of threshold-exceeding perturbations under a fixed experimental budget. It builds on the Bayesian decision-theoretic framing of DiscoBAX and the surrogate-modeling infrastructure of GP/BBN active learning, but introduces a batch-aware, threshold-specific acquisition (Probability-of-Hit) with a dedicated hit-recovery guarantee rather than a regret bound. The Thompson-Hit variant and the GeneDisco/Achilles experimental platform ground the work in the practical setting of genome-wide CRISPR screens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for functional genomics: CRISPR screens are expensive and the biological goal is recovering many distinct hits, not finding one optimum, yet standard acquisition functions are misaligned with this objective. The clean theoretical guarantee (Tb/2 − o(Tb) hits) provides a principled stopping criterion, and the finding that feature-representation quality (e.g., Achilles capturing T-cell biology well but NK-cell dynamics poorly) determines when hit-aware acquisition helps most offers actionable guidance for experimental design. Key open questions include extending to combinatorial perturbations, conditional contexts (cell type, environment), multi-objective readouts, and explicit within-batch diversity constraints, all of which the authors identify as future work.
