## Problem

Fine-tuning offline RL actor-critics with standard online value-based methods (e.g., SAC) typically triggers an immediate performance collapse before recovery. The paper provides geometric evidence that this drop arises because prior offline methods converge to high-reward checkpoints separated from online optima by low-reward valleys in parameter space, which gradient-based fine-tuning must traverse. No existing offline RL method produces checkpoints that lie on a monotonically improving path to the optima reached by general online actor-critic algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples from D4RL benchmarks, plus a separately trained diffusion model that estimates the dataset's action score conditioned on trajectory return (via RvS). The output is a standard actor-critic checkpoint (Q-function with ensemble + stochastic policy) that can be directly warm-started into arbitrary online actor-critic algorithms (SAC, TD3, TD3+BC, AWR) without architectural modification. A notable constraint is the upfront cost of training the diffusion score model (~3–4 GPU-hours on a single L40S), and the Q-function's action-gradient is explicitly shaped during offline training to match the score structure, which is an unusual inductive bias not present in standard offline RL.

## Method

SMAC extends SAC's offline training with a score-matching regularizer on the critic: it penalizes the L2 distance between the critic's action-gradient ∇_a Q_θ(s,a) and a state-conditioned scalar α_ψ(s) times the diffusion model's least-noised score estimate ε_ω(s,a,w,1), where w is the min-max-normalized trajectory return. The diffusion model is trained with RvS conditioning so the score targets high-return actions rather than the dataset average, and actions for the regularizer are sampled 50/50 from the current policy and the uniform action-space distribution. The total critic loss is κ·L^SM + L^AC (standard SAC TD loss with target networks and ensembles), while the actor uses the unmodified SAC policy loss. The Muon optimizer (spectral-norm steepest descent) replaces Adam during offline training as an empirical stabilizer that converges to flatter optima. The key insight is that the maximum-entropy identity ∇_a log π*(a|s) = (1/α)∇_a Q*(s,a) characterizes the local geometry of SAC optima, so aligning the offline critic's action-gradient with the dataset score places the checkpoint on or near the optimization trajectory that online SAC will follow, avoiding the low-reward valleys observed with prior methods.

## Results

SMAC achieves the lowest normalized online regret across all four online fine-tuning algorithms (SAC, TD3, TD3+BC, AWR) averaged over six D4RL environments. With SAC fine-tuning, SMAC's normalized regret is 0.031, compared to 0.448 (CalQL/CQL), 0.471 (IQL), and 0.962 (TD3+BC). In 4/6 environments, SMAC reduces regret by 34–58% relative to the strongest baseline. SMAC avoids any initial performance drop in 6/6 environments when fine-tuned with SAC or TD3, whereas CalQL drops in 3/4, IQL in 4/6, and TD3+BC in 5/6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMAC outperforms all four baselines on normalized regret for every online fine-tuning algorithm. The largest gap appears with SAC fine-tuning, where SMAC (0.031) reduces regret by ~93% relative to CalQL/CQL (0.448) and ~97% relative to TD3+BC (0.962). Against IQL, SMAC improves normalized regret from 0.471 to 0.031 under SAC and from 0.653 to 0.090 under TD3. Against CalQL/CQL, SMAC improves from 0.448 to 0.031 (SAC) and from 0.442 to 0.090 (TD3). Against TD3+BC as an offline method, SMAC improves from 0.962 to 0.031 (SAC) and from 0.545 to 0.090 (TD3). The one setting where SMAC is less dominant is AWR fine-tuning, where the gap narrows (e.g., 0.380 vs. 0.482 for CalQL/CQL on door), consistent with AWR's behavior-weighted update being less sensitive to checkpoint geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMAC extends SAC by adding a score-matching regularizer on the critic's action-gradient, motivated by the maximum-entropy identity that at a SAC optimum the policy score is proportional to ∇ₐQ(s,a). For the score estimate, it repurposes RvS-conditioned diffusion policies (Emmons et al., 2022; Piche et al., 2022) as an offline-computable proxy for the dataset action score, and uses Muon as an empirical stabilizer. Conceptually, it departs from the two dominant offline RL branches—critic pessimism (CQL/CalQL) and policy regularization (IQL/TD3+BC)—by targeting the local gradient geometry of the Q-function rather than its values or the policy's support, and it imports the linear-connectivity diagnostic from supervised-learning mode-connectivity literature (Garipov et al., 2018; Frankle et al., 2020) into the RL offline-to-online transfer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-connectivity diagnostic gives practitioners a concrete, visualizable explanation for why offline checkpoints fail during online fine-tuning, and the 34–58% regret reduction across 4/6 D4RL tasks with smooth transfer to SAC, TD3, and TD3+BC makes SMAC a practical pre-training option for the offline-to-online paradigm. Key limitations include the upfront cost of training a diffusion score model (~3–4 GPU-hours, 3× IQL wall-clock), sensitivity to the score-matching weight κ, and the fact that the geometric evidence is strongest only for the benchmark families studied. Open questions include which online update rules preserve the score-gradient geometry, whether the approach scales to robot learning with pretrained VLA/diffusion action models, and whether the connectivity framework generalizes beyond the six D4RL tasks tested.
