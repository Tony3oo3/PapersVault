## Problem

Standard Bradley-Terry aggregation of pairwise human comparisons assumes all raters are equally reliable, but in practice participants vary widely in expertise, attention, and diligence, and unreliable raters can distort item rankings. Existing methods that model rater quality (e.g., Chen et al., 2013) rely on gradient-based optimization without convergence guarantees, while Bayesian BT variants that do guarantee convergence (Caron & Doucet, 2012) ignore rater heterogeneity. No prior work combines explicit rater-quality modeling with a Bayesian BT formulation that yields closed-form EM updates and guaranteed monotonic likelihood improvement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes aggregated pairwise comparison counts: for each rater r and item pair (i,j), the total number of comparisons n_{r,ij} and the number of times rater r ranked i above j (w_{r,ij}), with no ties permitted (2AFC format). It produces per-item skill parameters λ_i (convertible to Elo scores), per-rater quality parameters q_r ∈ [0,1] reflecting the probability a rater makes an informed BT-consistent judgment, and posterior credible intervals on the Elo scale for significance testing. The framework is modality-agnostic, applied to both LLM preference data (HUMAINE, MT-Bench) and image compression quality data (WD, HiFiC, ConHa, IHQ).

## Method

BBQ extends the Bradley-Terry model with a rater-specific quality parameter q_r: with probability q_r the rater follows the BT probability λ_i/(λ_i+λ_j), and with probability 1−q_r they guess uniformly at random. Under a Thurstonian (exponential-race) interpretation, latent arrival times Y_i ~ Exp(λ_i) are introduced, and a Bernoulli indicator A_{r,ij}^{(c)} ~ Bernoulli(q_r) gates whether each comparison follows the BT process; the sum of gated minimal arrival times Z_{r,ij} follows a Gamma distribution conditional on the number of BT-consistent comparisons. Conjugate priors—Gamma(a,b) on each λ_i and Beta(α,β) on each q_r—yield a fully tractable EM algorithm: the E-step computes the posterior probability γ_{r,ij} that each comparison was meaningful (a function of current q_r and BT probability), and the M-step gives closed-form updates where q_r becomes a prior-regularized weighted average of effective wins and λ_i becomes a ratio of effective wins to a normalizing sum of effective comparisons. This guarantees monotonic increase of the observed-data log-posterior at every iteration, avoiding the local-optima and convergence issues of gradient-based alternatives.

## Results

BBQ achieves the highest Top-1 accuracy on 5 of 8 datasets, including 98.50% on HUMAINE (vs. 97.40% Bayes-BT, 82.70% Crowd-BT), 77.12% on ConHa (vs. 66.59% and 57.30%), and 61.42% on IHQ-unscreened (vs. 32.44% and 23.59%). On Kendall's Tau, BBQ leads on 5 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin on HiFiC (0.9525 vs. 0.9366 Crowd-BT, 0.9293 Bayes-BT). On the noisy IHQ-unscreened subset, BBQ's Top-1 accuracy is nearly double that of Crowd-BT and 2.6× that of Bayes-BT, demonstrating its robustness to low-quality raters. BBQ also converges in seconds per bootstrap sample, whereas Crowd-BT requires ~15 minutes on HUMAINE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BBQ is compared against two baselines: Crowd-BT (Chen et al., 2013; gradient-based BT with rater quality, as implemented by Google Research) and Bayes-BT (Caron & Doucet, 2012; Bayesian BT without rater quality). On Top-1 accuracy, BBQ wins on 5 of 8 datasets, with the most dramatic gains on noisy data: IHQ-unscreened (61.42% vs. 32.44% Crowd-BT, 23.59% Bayes-BT) and ConHa (77.12% vs. 66.59% Crowd-BT, 57.30% Bayes-BT). On Kendall's Tau, BBQ leads on 5 of 8 datasets but trails Bayes-BT on WD (0.9359 vs. 0.9459) and Crowd-BT on IHQ-screened (0.9204 vs. 0.9238), both settings with uniformly high-quality raters where modeling rater variability offers little benefit. Crowd-BT fails to converge with fewer than 3 raters, while BBQ's EM algorithm guarantees monotonic convergence. Computationally, BBQ converges in seconds per bootstrap sample across all datasets, whereas Crowd-BT takes ~15 minutes on HUMAINE, making BBQ far more practical for repeated resampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

BBQ sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the classical Bradley-Terry model for pairwise comparison aggregation, the Bayesian BT formulation with Thurstonian latent variables and EM updates from Caron & Doucet (2012), and the rater-quality modeling of Chen et al. (2013) (Crowd-BT). The key departure is that no prior work combined explicit rater-quality parameters with conjugate priors and closed-form EM updates; Chen et al. use gradient-based optimization without convergence guarantees, while Caron & Doucet ignore rater heterogeneity entirely. BBQ's contribution is the specific architectural choice—Gamma priors on item skills, Beta priors on rater quality, and a Gamma-distributed latent variable for the Thurstonian race—that makes the full joint inference tractable via EM with guaranteed monotonic likelihood improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running human preference studies on LLMs or image generators, BBQ offers a drop-in replacement for standard BT or Elo-based aggregation that is more robust to noisy crowdsourced raters, computationally faster (seconds vs. minutes for gradient-based baselines), and provides well-calibrated uncertainty intervals for significance testing. The model's advantages are most pronounced in realistic heterogeneous-rater settings but diminish when all raters are uniformly high-quality, and the independence assumption across comparisons (no order or context effects) remains a simplification. Open directions include extending to full multi-item rankings via Plackett-Luce rank-breaking, coupling with active pair selection for top-k identification, and scaling to very large item/rater counts with compiled implementations.
