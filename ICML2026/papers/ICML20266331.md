## Problem

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has been proposed as an auxiliary objective to improve next-token prediction in LLM training, but it shows inconsistent gains and underperforms on standard NLP benchmarks. The paper identifies that MTP's requirement to exactly predict future tokens at increasing offsets is too difficult as a learning signal, especially for small models and large lookahead distances, and asks whether a relaxed auxiliary objective can capture the same lookahead benefit without this difficulty.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard token sequence of length T as input. The TOP head outputs a proximity score for every token in the vocabulary (size V) at each position, ranking tokens by how soon they first appear within a configurable window W; the NTP head outputs the standard next-token probability distribution. Both heads share the same final-layer hidden state, and at inference time the TOP head is discarded, leaving the model architecturally identical to a vanilla transformer.

## Method

Token Order Prediction (TOP) constructs a target sequence where, for each position t, every vocabulary token is assigned a proximity score W−d (where d is the distance to its next occurrence within window W), with tokens never appearing in the window receiving −∞. The model is trained with a ListNet listwise ranking loss that compares the softmax of these true proximity scores against the softmax of the model's predicted scores via cross-entropy. Architecturally, TOP requires only a single additional linear unembedding layer U_TOP: R^D → R^V in parallel to the NTP head, both consuming the same final-layer hidden state—no extra transformer blocks are needed, unlike MTP which adds N−1 full transformer layers. The total training loss is simply L_NTP + L_TOP, and the approach is validated at 340M, 1.8B, and 7B parameter scales on nine standard NLP benchmarks, math/code generative tasks, and a synthetic star-graph pathfinding task where TOP achieves perfect accuracy on G(5,5) while NTP, MTP, and DS-MTP all fail.

## Results

Across nine standard NLP benchmarks at 0-shot, TOP achieves the highest average accuracy at all three model sizes: 46.64% (340M), 56.27% (1.8B), and 60.53% (7B), outperforming NTP by +1.08, +1.26, and +0.76 points respectively. On the synthetic star graph pathfinding task, TOP achieves 100% test accuracy on all four graph configurations (G(3,3), G(3,5), G(5,3), G(5,5)), while NTP, MTP, and DS-MTP all fail on G(5,5). After continued training on math and code, the TOP 1.8B model leads on GSM8K (45.64%) and MATH (16.66%), and the TOP 7B model leads on HumanEval pass@16 (34.48%) and MBPP pass@64 (54.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOP consistently outperforms all three baselines (NTP, MTP, DS-MTP) on average accuracy across all three model sizes, with gains of +1.08, +1.26, and +0.76 points over NTP at 340M, 1.8B, and 7B respectively. MTP underperforms NTP at 1.8B (−0.16) and 7B (−0.94) on average accuracy, confirming the original MTP paper's observation that multi-token prediction does not reliably improve general NLP tasks at scale. DS-MTP underperforms NTP at every size (−1.79, −0.33, −0.85), with particularly large drops on Lambada perplexity (40.99 vs 30.34 at 340M) and TriviaQA exact match (0.87 vs 4.93 at 340M). On the star graph pathfinding task, TOP is the only method achieving 100% accuracy on G(5,5), where even MTP-4 and DS-MTP-4 (with 4 heads) plateau at ~19.5%. On math benchmarks after continued training, TOP 1.8B leads all methods on both GSM8K (45.64% vs 39.20% NTP) and MATH (16.66% vs 13.34% NTP), while DS-MTP collapses to 2.65% on GSM8K, suggesting severe overfitting. The main trade-off is self-speculative decoding: TOP achieves only ~1.5 accepted tokens per forward pass at 7B versus 2.49 for MTP and 3.03 for DS-MTP, since TOP's ranking objective does not produce sequential token predictions suitable for speculative validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct successor to multi-token prediction (MTP, Gloeckle et al. 2024) and its DeepSeek V3 variant (DS-MTP), both of which add auxiliary heads to predict exact future tokens at increasing offsets. The key departure is replacing the exact-prediction objective with a listwise ranking loss (ListNet, Cao et al. 2007) that only requires ordering upcoming tokens by proximity, reducing the architectural overhead from multiple transformer layers to a single linear unembedding head. The star graph pathfinding evaluation is borrowed from Bachmann & Nagarajan (2024) to test whether the model learns genuine lookahead, and the paper explicitly contrasts with related bag-of-words and pairwise-ranking auxiliary objectives (Yin et al. 2024, Frydenlund 2025, Mahajan et al. 2025) by using a listwise formulation over the full vocabulary within a window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOP offers a practically attractive alternative to MTP: it requires only one extra unembedding layer (DV parameters) rather than N-1 full transformer blocks, making it trivially scalable in window size without parameter growth. The star graph result—TOP achieving 100% on G(5,5) where MTP-4 and DS-MTP-4 plateau at ~19%—provides clean evidence that a ranking objective captures lookahead more effectively than exact token prediction, even with fewer parameters. However, validation is limited to 7B parameters and a single Llama-style architecture, the standard-benchmark gains are modest (+0.8 to +1.3 avg accuracy), and TOP underperforms MTP/DS-MTP for self-speculative decoding, leaving open whether the advantage persists at frontier scale and whether the ranking signal can be repurposed for inference-time acceleration.
