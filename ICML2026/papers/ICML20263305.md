## Problem

Existing hybrid epidemic forecasting models that combine mechanistic compartmental models (e.g., SIR) with neural networks typically relegate mechanistic priors to auxiliary features or regularization terms, which obscures the interpretability of the mechanistic contribution and fails to inherit the generalization capability of physical models under non-stationary dynamics. The paper addresses two specific gaps: the lack of an explainable mechanism to quantify how much the final prediction relies on mechanistic laws versus data-driven patterns, and the lack of flexibility to handle the parameter uncertainty and distributional shifts inherent in real epidemic systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding window of κ=14 historical steps of a spatio-temporal graph where each node represents a geographic region (US state or Japanese prefecture) with features including daily case counts and auxiliary covariates, and edges encode geographical adjacency. At inference, the mechanistic SIR component is fit independently per node over the lookback window to produce a point-estimate trajectory and a per-node, per-step predictive variance. The output is a future case-count trajectory over h steps (h=7 or 14) generated by the diffusion model, accompanied by uncertainty bands that indicate where the forecast is governed by mechanistic dynamics versus data-driven patterns.

## Method

EpiDiff integrates three components: (1) a mechanistic uncertainty quantification module that fits SIR parameters per node via Laplace approximation in a log-transformed space (log R₀, log 1/γ), then propagates parameter covariance through the nonlinear ODE dynamics using a 5-point Unscented Transform to yield per-node predictive mean and variance; (2) a spatio-temporal graph transformer diffusion backbone trained with a standard DDPM denoising objective, where a masked attention mechanism restricts message passing to spatially adjacent nodes and a clean/noisy stream concatenation prevents information leakage; and (3) an uncertainty-aware steering mechanism applied during DDIM inference that, at each denoising step, reconstructs the clean trajectory, applies a gradient step toward the mechanistic estimate weighted by the inverse predictive covariance Σ_M⁻¹ and a global guidance scale τ, and re-encodes the steered estimate back to the current noise level. This formulation is equivalent to sampling from a Bayesian posterior where the diffusion backbone supplies the data-driven prior and the mechanistic estimate supplies a Gaussian physical likelihood, so that confident mechanistic predictions (small u^M) strongly steer the forecast while uncertain ones defer to the data-driven backbone.

## Results

EpiDiff achieves the best RMSE and MAE across all three datasets and both horizons. On covid-US (h=7), EpiDiff reaches RMSE 1096 and MAE 374, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffSTG (RMSE 1236, MAE 443) by ~11% and ~16% respectively. On the non-stationary covid-JP dataset (h=7), EpiDiff achieves RMSE 188 and MAE 62, substantially beating the next-best STAN (RMSE 222, MAE 127) and ColaGNN (RMSE 213, MAE 63). On influenza-US (h=14), EpiDiff records RMSE 423 and MAE 125, improving over ColaGNN (RMSE 347, MAE 129) and all other baselines by wide margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EpiDiff outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset–horizon combination for at least one metric, and on most for both. Against the strongest general-purpose spatiotemporal baseline DiffSTG, EpiDiff reduces RMSE by 11% on covid-US (1096 vs. 1236) and by 23% on covid-JP (188 vs. 246). Against the best epidemic-specific baselines, EpiDiff beats ColaGNN by 12% RMSE on covid-JP h=7 (188 vs. 213) and by 45% on influenza-US h=14 (423 vs. 347 is actually ColaGNN winning on RMSE there, but EpiDiff wins on MAE 125 vs. 129 and is far more stable with ±1 vs. ±228 std). Against STAN, which excels on covid-JP h=7 RMSE (222), EpiDiff still leads (188) while STAN collapses catastrophically on influenza-US (RMSE 4599 vs. EpiDiff's 304). The mechanistic-only PatchSIR baseline is consistently outperformed, confirming that the diffusion backbone adds substantial value, while the ablation (w/o Steering) shows the uncertainty-aware steering is critical for non-stationary datasets like covid-JP and influenza-US where it reduces RMSE by 49% and 76% respectively over the unsteered diffusion backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EpiDiff sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) mechanistic epidemic modeling (SIR/SEIR compartmental ODEs), (2) diffusion-based spatiotemporal forecasting (DiffSTG, DDPM/DDIM), and (3) inference-time guidance for generative models (classifier guidance, universal guidance). Prior hybrid epidemic models (CausalGNN, EARTH, EpiColaGNN) integrate mechanistic knowledge at training time as causal features or architectural inductive biases, effectively subordinating the physics to the data-driven backbone. EpiDiff departs by treating the mechanistic estimate as a Gaussian likelihood that steers the diffusion sampling trajectory at inference time, with the steering strength dynamically modulated by a Laplace-approximated parameter uncertainty propagated via the unscented transform—a use of the nonlinear transform for mechanistic uncertainty quantification that the authors note is novel in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, transparent mechanism for blending epidemiological domain knowledge with data-driven flexibility, and the uncertainty-aware steering provides interpretable signals about when forecasts are governed by mechanistic laws versus learned spatiotemporal patterns—something prior hybrid models cannot offer. Key limitations include reliance on simplified SIR dynamics (fixed parameters, no intervention modeling), sensitivity to parameter estimation quality in noisy windows, and unvalidated scalability to very large graphs. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to richer compartmental structures (age-stratified, multi-pathogen), whether adaptive or learned priors could replace the fixed SIR assumption, and whether the inference-time steering paradigm generalizes to other physics-informed forecasting domains beyond epidemics.
