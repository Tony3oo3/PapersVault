## Problem

Most synthetic data generation strategies for face recognition depend on external foundation models, auxiliary datasets, or commercial APIs, which are restricted by license, privacy, or domain-mismatch constraints. This paper asks whether a fully self-contained augmentation method—using only the available training dataset—can generate synthetic samples that meaningfully boost discriminative performance without any external resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263801/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two class-conditioned identity labels (selected from the training set) and random Gaussian noise as input, and produces a single synthetic face image that interpolates between the two source identities. The generator operates in pixel space (EDM2 variant) on 112×112 RGB face crops, and each mixed pair defines a new synthetic identity class rather than being assigned to either source. Both the class-conditional diffusion generator and the ArcFace discriminator are trained from scratch on the same dataset (WebFace160K: 10K identities, 160K images), ensuring no external model or data dependency.

## Method

ScoreMix exploits score compositionality in class-conditioned diffusion models: at each reverse-diffusion step, it computes a convex combination of two class-conditioned score predictions (S_mix = α·S_A + β·S_B, with α+β=1, typically 0.5/0.5) and uses this mixed score to drive the denoising update, yielding samples that lie on the manifold between two identities. The convex constraint is critical—it preserves the expected score magnitude calibrated by the EDM2 architecture and prevents off-manifold excursions that non-convex combinations would cause. A key empirical finding is that selecting source classes distant in the discriminator's embedding space (cosine distance) yields up to 3% greater gains than proximity-based or condition-space-based selection, revealing that the generator's learned condition space and the discriminator's embedding space are largely uncorrelated under standard alignment metrics. The paper further establishes a CKA-based theorem providing order-preserving probability guarantees for triplet distances, showing that class-pair selection transfers robustly across different backbone architectures (e.g., ArcFace-IR50 to AdaFace-IR101) when embedding geometries are sufficiently aligned (CKA > 0.80).

## Results

On WebFace160K (160K real images + 200K synthetic), ScoreMix with an ArcFace IR-50 backbone achieves 93.87% average accuracy across high-quality benchmarks (LFW, CFP-FP, CPLFW, CALFW, AgeDB), surpassing the original-data-only baseline (92.50%) by +1.37 points and the larger IR-101 backbone (93.32%) by +0.55 points. On the challenging IJB-C benchmark at FPR 10⁻⁶, ScoreMix reaches 76.45% TAR, a +6.08-point improvement over the baseline (70.37%), and 83.58% at FPR 10⁻⁵ (+4.77 points). On ImageNet-1K, ScoreMix adds +1.00 pp to ResNet50 (43.53→44.53%) and +0.93 pp to FastViT-S12 (45.07→46.00%) top-1 accuracy, confirming generality beyond face recognition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScoreMix outperforms all four compared baselines. Against DigiFace1M (Bae et al., WACV 2023) and RealDigiFace (Rahimi et al., 2024), which rely on 3D rendering pipelines and external auxiliary models, ScoreMix achieves substantially higher IJB-C TAR (76.45% vs. 26.06% and 36.18% at FPR 10⁻⁶) and Avg-H (93.87% vs. 78.97% and 81.34%), while using no auxiliary resources. Against DCFace (Kim et al., CVPR 2023), a dual-condition diffusion method that also requires external data, ScoreMix leads on every reported metric (e.g., IJB-C 1e-6: 76.45 vs. 35.27; Avg-H: 93.87 vs. 91.56). Against the closest competitor AugGen (Rahimi et al., NeurIPS 2025), which uses the same self-contained setup but relies on heuristic label mixing and costly grid search over (α,β), ScoreMix achieves a consistent but modest edge: +0.09 pp on Avg-H (93.87 vs. 93.78), +1.12 pp on IJB-C at 10⁻⁶ (76.45 vs. 75.02), and +1.68 pp on IJB-C at 10⁻⁵ (83.58 vs. 82.91), while also surpassing the IR-101 backbone trained on original data alone (93.32% Avg-H) with the smaller IR-50 model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScoreMix extends the self-contained synthetic augmentation paradigm introduced by AugGen (Rahimi et al., 2025), which trained a diffusion generator on a face dataset and mixed labels in the generator's condition space. The key conceptual departure is replacing condition-space label mixing with convex combinations of class-conditioned scores during reverse diffusion, drawing on the score composition principle from composable diffusion models (Liu et al., 2022) and the projective composition theory of Bradley et al. (2025). The EDM2 framework (Karras et al., 2024b) provides the diffusion backbone, while the class-selection strategy is novel in aligning pair choice with the discriminator's embedding geometry rather than the generator's condition geometry. Unlike prior synthetic-data methods for face recognition (DCFace, DigiFace1M, RealDigiFace, VariFace), ScoreMix requires no external pretrained models, auxiliary datasets, or 3D rendering pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-contained nature of ScoreMix is practically significant for sensitive modalities like faces, where external foundation models face license restrictions and consent concerns. The finding that the generator's condition space and the discriminator's embedding space are largely uncorrelated under standard metrics (CKA, CKNNA) challenges the implicit assumption that a well-trained generator's internal representations should mirror the recognizer's geometry, and the CKA-based order-preservation theorem provides a theoretical guarantee that class-selection transfers across backbones. Open questions include whether explicit condition-space regularization guided by discriminative geometry can close the gap without sacrificing diversity (alignment regularization was shown to hurt), whether the approach generalizes to fine-grained or low-data regimes beyond faces (CUB-200 results are preliminary), and whether mixing more than two classes can be made effective with better generators.
