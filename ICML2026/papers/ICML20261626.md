## Problem

Existing history-dependent MCMC methods (SRRW, HDT) encode trajectory history through empirical measures on finite state spaces, requiring O(|X|) memory that becomes infeasible for high-dimensional discrete configuration spaces (where |X| grows exponentially) and is ill-posed in continuous domains. The paper asks whether one can design long-term, history-dependent samplers that preserve the variance-reduction and mode-exploration benefits of non-Markovian methods while remaining applicable to general MCMC kernels in both continuous and discrete spaces with only constant memory and normalization-free implementation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a twice-differentiable target density π(x) ∝ exp(-U(x)) on X ⊆ R^d (or a discrete configuration space), its score s(x) = -∇U(x), a base MCMC kernel P_q, and a scalar repellence strength α ≥ 0. It produces a sample trajectory {X_n}, a running Monte Carlo estimate μ_n of E_π[f(X)], and a d-dimensional score-history variable θ_n that converges to 0. Unusually, the history is a d-dimensional running average of score evaluations rather than a full empirical measure, so memory is O(d) regardless of state-space cardinality; for discrete spaces the score is either a discrete Stein operator feature (satisfying E_π[s(X)]=0) or, in practice, a relaxed-gradient proxy for computational tractability.

## Method

SRMC maintains a constant-memory history variable θ_n as a running average of past score evaluations via a stochastic-approximation recursion with step size γ_n = (n+1)^{-ρ}. This history is converted into a repulsive modification of the target through an exponential score tilt: the surrogate π_θ(x) ∝ π(x) exp(-αθ^T s(x)) downweights regions whose score directions have been over-represented in the trajectory. At each iteration, any base MCMC kernel (MH, MALA, HMC, ULA, GWG) is run targeting the current surrogate π_θn, and the normalizing constant Z_θ cancels in the Metropolis ratio or is irrelevant for gradient-based samplers, preserving the standard normalization-free implementation. For gradient-driven kernels, the surrogate score s_θ(x) = s(x) + α∇²U(x)θ requires only a Hessian-vector product per step. The coupled (θ_n, μ_n) recursion is analyzed as stochastic approximation with controlled Markovian noise, yielding almost sure convergence to (0, μ) and a joint CLT whose asymptotic covariance solves a Lyapunov equation; the θ-block scales as O(1/α), extending the near-zero-variance effect of finite-state SRRW/HDT to general state spaces.

## Results

On the 10-dimensional correlated Gaussian and Bayesian logistic regression targets, SRMC achieves up to 5× lower MSE for sample-mean estimation compared to the base sampler (α=0), with the best gains at moderate repellence strengths (α=1–2 for logistic regression; α=2–5 for the Gaussian). On Static MNIST (784-dimensional binary EBM), SR-GWG reduces cumulative KL divergence by 84% (0.68 vs. 4.16) and improves the batch Vendi Score from 2.6 to 6.4 over 10,000 steps. On CIFAR-10-based energy landscapes, SR-ULA discovers all modes in ~1,035 steps where ULA plateaus at only 2.8% coverage, and with 10 parallel chains covers 7/10 digit classes versus 5/10 for ULA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MALA (Roberts & Rosenthal, 1998), SR-MALA consistently reduces MSE on both the correlated Gaussian and Bayesian logistic regression targets, with the largest gains at α=1–2; however, α=5 over-tilts the surrogate and degrades performance due to high rejection rates. Against HMC (Neal, 2011), SR-HMC shows the clearest step-based improvement on the correlated Gaussian (α=2 or 5 yields the lowest terminal MSE), while on the nonlinear logistic target moderate α≈1–2 is optimal. Against GWG (Grathwohl et al., 2021) on Static MNIST, SR-GWG dominates decisively: it escapes the initial mode within 2,500 steps (vs. persistent trapping for GWG), reducing KL divergence by 84% and more than doubling the Vendi Score. Against ULA (Durmus & Moulines, 2017) on CIFAR-10 energy landscapes, SR-ULA achieves complete mode discovery in ~1,035 steps where ULA stagnates at 2.8% coverage, and the advantage widens when the number of parallel chains is limited (7/10 vs. 5/10 classes with 10 chains). In all comparisons, SRMC retains the normalization-free property and requires only O(d) additional memory, with modest per-iteration overhead from one Hessian-vector product (gradient-based) or one inner product (MH-based).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Self-Repellent Random Walk (SRRW) and History-Driven Target (HDT) framework from finite state spaces to general state spaces by replacing the O(|X|)-dimensional empirical measure with an O(d)-dimensional running average of score evaluations. The exponential-tilt surrogate construction directly inherits the HDT principle of modifying the target rather than the transition kernel, while the stochastic approximation analysis builds on the controlled Markovian noise framework of Borkar et al. (2025). For discrete spaces, the method integrates with gradient-informed samplers like GWG (Grathwohl et al., 2021), using either exact discrete Stein operators or relaxed-gradient proxies. Unlike Stein self-repulsive dynamics or metadynamics, which require growing buffers or density reweighting, SRMC maintains constant memory and normalization-free implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/α) asymptotic variance scaling for the score-history iterate, combined with the constant O(d) memory requirement, makes history-dependent sampling feasible for high-dimensional continuous targets and exponentially large discrete configuration spaces where empirical-measure approaches are intractable. The plug-and-play wrapper design means practitioners can drop SRMC onto existing MALA, HMC, or discrete samplers with minimal code changes. Open questions include principled adaptive-α scheduling (since overly large α hurts finite-horizon performance), whether the variance reduction persists under fixed computational budgets when Hessian-vector products add per-step cost, and extension to diffusion/score-based generative sampling where mode diversity remains a bottleneck.
