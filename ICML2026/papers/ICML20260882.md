## Problem

Existing Graph-MoE methods suffer from two critical failures: discrimination loss, where GNN experts converge to redundant representations and the router collapses to a small subset of experts, and routing uncertainty, where the gating network assigns near-uniform weights to multiple experts, strongly correlating with degraded classification accuracy. These issues prevent Graph-MoE architectures from realizing their intended capacity-scaling potential through true expert specialization and reliable expert selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} as input, and produces a per-node class label over C categories via a softmax prediction. Internally, each node is processed through L stacked MoE layers where a shared expert and M specialized experts (e.g., GCN, SAGE, or GIN backbones) are selectively activated via Top-K gating, with the hidden dimension scaled as d₀/√(1+k) to maintain FLOPs parity with a standard GNN. The contrastive routing module is discarded at inference, keeping deployment cost comparable to a single GNN.

## Method

C²GMoE introduces two mechanisms atop a standard Graph-MoE layer. First, Group-wise Contrastive Routing (GCR) partitions nodes into C semantic groups by jointly clustering on raw features (attribute view) and Personalized PageRank scores (structural view), then applies a temperature-scaled contrastive loss that pulls each node's gating vector toward its group's routing prototype while pushing it away from other groups' prototypes, thereby enforcing expert specialization under implicit load-balancing. Second, Confidence-aware Fusion (CAF) is derived from a generalization error bound (Theorem 3.1) showing that minimizing the bound requires fusion weights to be positively correlated with expert correctness probability; the mechanism computes per-expert individual confidence via a sigmoid predictor trained with MAE against the true-class softmax score, combines it with a relative confidence term that amplifies experts whose alternatives are less confident, and applies softmax normalization to produce final fusion weights for aggregating expert outputs.

## Results

C²GMoE consistently achieves the best Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 across all six benchmarks with GCN experts. Headline numbers include 95.16% Micro-F1 on Photo, 80.11% on WikiCS, 92.88% on Computers, 94.82% on CS, 55.01% on Flickr, and 72.74% on ogbn-arxiv. Gains over the strongest Graph-MoE baseline (Mowst) range from ~0.2% (ogbn-arxiv) to ~6.7% (Computers), with Macro-F1 improvements generally larger than Micro-F1, indicating better handling of minority classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²GMoE-GCN outperforms all four baselines (GCN, GMoE, Mowst, DA-MoE) on every dataset and metric. The most dramatic win is on Computers, where C²GMoE-GCN reaches 92.88% Micro-F1 versus 86.19% for Mowst-GCN (a 6.7-point gap) and 91.75% for GMoE-GCN. On Flickr, C²GMoE-GCN achieves 55.01% Micro-F1 and 26.60% Macro-F1, surpassing the next-best Mowst-GCN (54.56% / 25.36%) by a meaningful margin on the harder Macro-F1 metric. On the large-scale ogbn-arxiv, gains are more modest (72.74% vs. 72.54% for Mowst-GCN in Micro-F1) but C²GMoE still leads in Macro-F1 (52.72% vs. 52.59%). The consistent pattern across GCN, SAGE, and GIN expert backbones confirms that the improvements stem from the contrastive routing and confidence-aware fusion mechanisms rather than a particular expert architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph-MoE architecture introduced by GMoE (Wang et al., 2023), which combines a shared expert with specialized GNN experts and Top-K gating, by identifying two specific failure modes—expert homogenization and routing collapse—that prior Graph-MoE work did not explicitly diagnose or address. The confidence-aware fusion mechanism is derived from the predictive dynamic fusion framework of Cao et al. (2024), adapted here to the multi-path graph MoE setting via a generalization error bound. The contrastive routing strategy departs from the standard load-balancing loss used in GMoE and Mowst by introducing group-wise semantic alignment (via attribute clustering and PPR-based structural grouping) as an explicit supervisory signal for the router, rather than relying solely on a uniformity penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic empirical diagnosis of discrimination loss (CKA > 0.8 between experts) and the strong negative correlation between routing entropy and node-level accuracy provides a useful diagnostic framework for anyone building Graph-MoE systems. The contrastive routing and confidence-aware fusion components are modular and could be adopted by other MoE-on-graphs methods. However, the approach requires pre-computing semantic groups (clustering + PPR), adds a confidence predictor that must be trained with ground-truth labels, and the theoretical analysis relies on simplifying assumptions (convex loss, linear expert transformations) that may not hold in practice. Open questions include whether the group partitioning strategy generalizes to dynamic or streaming graphs, and whether the confidence mechanism provides benefits in other MoE settings beyond node classification.
