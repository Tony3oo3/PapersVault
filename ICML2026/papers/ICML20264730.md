## Problem

Learning domain-adaptive policies that generalize to unseen transition dynamics remains a fundamental challenge in learning-based control. Prior approaches that learn domain representations via dynamical prediction suffer from entangling static domain information with time-varying dynamical properties in the context, producing representations that drift within a domain and confuse the downstream policy. Additionally, most methods underutilize learned representations by merely concatenating them as extra network inputs, failing to fully exploit the domain structure for decision-making.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state-action history window of length H=16 (the context) and the current state, producing a continuous action chunk over a prediction horizon of 4 steps. The context encoder (a Transformer with adaptive pooling) maps the context to a domain representation z of dimension dim(s)+dim(a). At inference, the context is collected online during rollout; when history is insufficient, padding is used. The diffusion policy uses only 5 DDIM inference steps, and the representation-biased prior makes the generation tolerant to aggressive step reduction (e.g., 1-step DDIM retains ~70% performance vs. ~14% for standard diffusion).

## Method

DADP combines two key mechanisms. First, Lagged Context Dynamical Prediction introduces a temporal offset Δt between the context and the prediction target; by setting Δt→∞ (sampling context from another episode in the same domain), the encoder is forced to extract only static, time-invariant domain factors (e.g., gravity, morphology) while discarding transient dynamical properties, since the offset context can no longer provide time-local cues for next-state prediction. The encoder is trained with joint forward and inverse dynamics prediction losses. Second, rather than conditioning the diffusion policy on the representation, DADP injects it directly into the generative process: the denoising prior is shifted to a mixed Gaussian centered at z (one peak per domain), and the diffusion target is reformulated as a composite prediction ε̂ = (1−√ᾱₖ)z + √(1−ᾱₖ)ε that jointly predicts noise and representation shift, simplifying the DDIM reverse step and providing additional per-step supervision that guides the denoising trajectory toward the correct domain-specific action manifold.

## Results

DADP achieves the highest or near-highest normalized return across all six environments (HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Ant, Hopper, Adroit Door, Adroit Relocate) under Seen, Unseen, and OOD settings. The advantage is most dramatic on Walker2d, where DADP scores 3999 (Seen) and 2197 (OOD) versus the next-best Meta-DT at 1304 and 954 respectively. Under OOD extrapolation, DADP outperforms the strongest baseline by 21–59% across all four MuJoCo locomotion tasks. DADP also exhibits the lowest standard deviation across 5 seeds in nearly every setting, indicating substantially more stable zero-shot adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DADP outperforms all three baselines (CORRO, Prompt-DT, Meta-DT) on 14 of 16 evaluation settings, with the largest margins on Walker2d (3× over Meta-DT on Seen) and OOD locomotion tasks (21–59% over the best baseline). The closest competitor is Meta-DT, which slightly edges DADP on HalfCheetah Unseen (3174 vs. 3001) but with 2.3× higher variance; on all other MuJoCo settings DADP wins. Prompt-DT is the only baseline that beats DADP on a single setting (Adroit Door Seen: 2116 vs. 1428), while CORRO is consistently the weakest, often producing negative returns. On the Adroit manipulation tasks, DADP achieves the best performance on Relocate (both Seen and Unseen) and Unseen Door, confirming that the diffusion-injection mechanism generalizes beyond locomotion. Across all environments, DADP's standard deviation is the smallest, indicating more reliable zero-shot adaptation than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DADP builds on the Diffusion Policy architecture (Chi et al., 2025) and the modular diffusion planner from DV (Lu et al., 2025), adapting the forward/reverse processes to incorporate a domain representation. Conceptually, it departs from prior domain-adaptive policy work (CORRO, Meta-DT, MetaDiffuser) in two ways: (1) it replaces contrastive or adjacent-context dynamics prediction with Lagged Context Dynamical Prediction, which uses a large temporal offset (context from another episode) to unsupervisedly disentangle static domain factors from time-varying dynamical properties; and (2) it injects the learned representation into the diffusion prior as a mixed Gaussian and reformulates the denoising target, rather than conditioning on it as an extra policy input. The mixed Gaussian prior formulation draws on structured diffusion models (Jia et al., 2024), and the DDIM reverse-step derivation follows Song et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical bottleneck in learning-based robotics: a single policy that generalizes zero-shot to unseen dynamics (sim-to-real, cross-embodiment). The key insight—that adjacent-context dynamics prediction inevitably entangles static domain identity with transient state derivatives, degrading downstream adaptation—is a useful diagnostic that explains why prior unsupervised representation methods (CaDM, IIDA, CARoL) underperform. The simplicity of the fix (sample context from another episode, Δt→∞) and its transferability to non-diffusion architectures (validated on Meta-DT) make it broadly applicable. Limitations include restriction to stationary dynamics, no real-robot validation, and evaluation confined to MuJoCo/Adroit; extending to non-stationary or partially observable settings remains open.
