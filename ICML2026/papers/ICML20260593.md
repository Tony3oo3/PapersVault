## Problem

Existing prompt-based methods for missing modality learning treat prompt vectors as structurally flat, ignoring internal dependencies across layers, between adjacent tokens, and within individual channels. This lack of structural refinement limits the prompts' ability to provide coherent, adaptive guidance to a frozen backbone under diverse and severe modality-missing conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260593/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 224×224 RGB images and text sequences (truncated/padded to 77 CLIP tokens), where either or both modalities may be absent. Based on modality availability, one of four prompt types is selected and refined before injection into the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone at 6 transformer layers. The output is a class label: multi-label genre predictions (23 classes) for MM-IMDb, single-label food category (101 classes) for UPMC Food-101, or binary hate-speech detection for Hateful Memes.

## Method

SPR refines learnable prompt vectors (length 40, inserted at 6 layers) across three complementary structural dimensions using only 1.3M trainable parameters (0.8% of the 151M CLIP model). The Global Interaction Fusion module applies a shared bottleneck BiGRU along the depth axis to capture bidirectional cross-layer dependencies, with a learnable scalar gate for residual blending. The Local Feature Refinement module uses a shared depthwise convolution (kernel size c) along the token-length axis to transform independent prompt tokens into coherent semantic units. The Channel Feature Selection module applies a shared 1×1 convolution followed by a Gated Linear Unit to perform data-driven, per-channel amplification or suppression tailored to the specific missing-modality condition. During training, only the SPR network and a final classification head are updated while the entire CLIP backbone remains frozen.

## Results

SPR achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks using only 0.8% trainable parameters (1.3M out of 151M). On MM-IMDb (F1-Macro) at the 90% both-missing rate, SPR reaches 53.41%, surpassing the previous best SyP (49.63%) by 3.78 points. On Food101 (Accuracy) at 90% both-missing, SPR scores 82.29% vs. SyP's 81.03%, and on Hateful Memes (AUROC) at 90% both-missing, SPR achieves 71.23% vs. SyP's 68.03%. The advantage over SyP is most pronounced under extreme missingness, where structured prompt refinement provides the largest marginal gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPR consistently outperforms all six baselines (CoOp, MMP, MaPLe, DePT, DCP, SyP) across every dataset, missing rate, and missing scenario. The most direct comparison is against SyP (Zhang et al., 2025b), the prior state-of-the-art dynamic prompt method: SPR leads by 2.21 F1-Macro on MM-IMDb at 70% both-missing (54.95 vs. 52.90) and by 3.78 points at 90% both-missing (53.41 vs. 49.63), with similar margins on Food101 (+1.47 Acc at 90% both) and Hateful Memes (+3.20 AUROC at 90% both). Against DCP (Shi et al., 2024), which uses unidirectional MLP-based prompt generation, SPR gains 5.37 F1-Macro on MM-IMDb at 90% both-missing (53.41 vs. 48.04), demonstrating that bidirectional cross-layer refinement is substantially more effective than one-way layer-to-layer propagation. The static-prompt baselines (CoOp, MMP, MaPLe) are uniformly outperformed by 5–10+ points, confirming that dynamic, input-adaptive prompts are essential under missing modalities. Notably, SPR uses only 32% of DCP's trainable parameters while achieving superior results, underscoring that structured refinement of prompts matters more than raw parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning paradigm for missing-modality classification, building directly on MMP's static missing-aware prompts and DCP's unidirectional MLP-based layer-to-layer prompt generation. The key departure is treating prompt vectors as structured objects requiring explicit refinement across three axes—bidirectional cross-layer correlation (via BiGRU, replacing DCP's one-way MLP), local token-to-token coherence (via depthwise convolution), and channel-wise adaptive gating (via GLU)—rather than merely generating prompts from input features. SPR positions itself against the broader family of dynamic prompt methods (SyP, RAGPT, MemPrompt) by arguing that the internal structure of the prompt sequence, not just its content, is the critical factor for robust guidance under modality absence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that structured prompt refinement outperforms more sophisticated prompt generation strategies (SPR uses only 0.8% trainable parameters yet surpasses DCP, which uses ~3× more) shifts attention in the PEFT-for-missing-modality literature toward how prompts are shaped rather than how they are produced. The demonstrated zero-shot generalizability from a missing-both training protocol to single-modality-missing test scenarios is practically valuable, reducing deployment complexity. Open questions include whether the three-dimensional refinement generalizes beyond two modalities, whether the BiGRU-based cross-layer modeling scales to deeper backbones (e.g., ViT-L/14), and whether the approach transfers to generative or retrieval tasks rather than classification.
