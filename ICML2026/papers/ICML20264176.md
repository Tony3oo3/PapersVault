## Problem

Current Group Emotion Recognition models operate as discriminative black boxes that entangle genuine affective signals with environmental confounders (lighting, camera motion), fail to explicitly model the physical synchrony of group movement, and produce latent representations misaligned with human-interpretable concepts. This leads to shortcut learning where models rely on background context rather than actual behavioral dynamics, degrading generalization in unconstrained real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5-second video clips (VGAF) or 10–20-second crowd sequences (GECV) uniformly decimated to 6 Hz (32 frames), a synchronized audio stream, and crowd-level textual descriptions. Video is processed through three frozen pre-trained backbones (VideoMAE V2 for global scene, DINOv2 for per-agent cropped tubes tracked via ByteTrack up to K=8 agents, and HuBERT for acoustic prosody), while text is encoded by a frozen RoBERTa. The output is a 3-class group valence probability vector (Positive, Negative, Neutral) produced by an MLP that also receives the time-averaged synchrony scalar γ directly.

## Method

VIBE introduces a Dual-Stream Variational Information Bottleneck that projects local agent embeddings into two geometrically orthogonal latent subspaces (affective Z_aff and environmental Z_env) via a soft cosine-similarity penalty, then re-injects Z_env as a directed conditional prior through affine modulation of Z_aff. A Gamma-Gated Transformer with Decoupled AdaLN modulates temporal attention using a scalar physical synchrony metric γ (mean pairwise velocity cosine similarity across tracked agents) and a scene calibration token, allowing the network to dynamically shift between global-coherence and local-outlier processing strategies. Acoustic features are fused via cross-attention (visual queries, audio keys/values), and a semantic alignment loss projects the pooled visual embedding into a RoBERTa text-anchor space via cosine distance. Training uses a composite objective combining Hierarchical Cross-Entropy with Manifold Mixup (ordinal-aware label smoothing), KL compression, orthogonality, and semantic alignment losses, with hyperparameters tuned via Optuna.

## Results

VIBE achieves 70.17% accuracy on VGAF and 91.85% accuracy on GECV, setting new state-of-the-art on both group emotion recognition benchmarks. On VGAF, it outperforms the strongest prior method (Li et al., 2023, CLS+MSE) by 1.76% and the recent MSST transformer by 3.75%. On GECV, the gain over MSST is even larger at +4.98%, suggesting the Synchrony Matrix mechanism is particularly effective in longer, denser crowd sequences where collective physical coordination is a stronger proxy for group valence than isolated facial expressions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIBE outperforms all 12 baselines on both datasets. On VGAF, the closest competitor is Li et al. (2023) at 68.41%, which VIBE surpasses by 1.76%; the recent MSST transformer (Huang & Xu, 2025) trails by 3.75%. On GECV, VIBE leads MSST (86.87%) by a substantial 4.98% margin, and outperforms Everything-at-Once (Shvetsova et al., 2022) by 9.92%. Notably, methods relying solely on Scene+Face modalities (e.g., RJCMA at 47.51% on VGAF, 53.66% on GECV) struggle significantly, confirming that VIBE's integration of audio prosody and textual anchors with kinematics-informed gating resolves ambiguities that purely visual approaches cannot. The largest absolute gaps appear on GECV, where the Synchrony Matrix mechanism benefits from longer, denser crowd sequences with sustained physical coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIBE carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separate threads in affective computing: variational information bottleneck for feature compression, cross-modal attention fusion, and disentanglement-based representation learning. Unlike prior disentanglement work (Yang et al. 2022, Sun et al. 2023, Zhou et al. 2025) that separates modality-invariant from modality-specific features, VIBE reframes the disentanglement axis as Agent Affect vs. Environmental Context, enforcing geometric orthogonality between these causal subspaces. The key architectural departure is the Synchrony Matrix—a raw kinematic metric computed from tracked agent trajectories—that dynamically gates a Decoupled AdaLN Transformer, a mechanism absent from prior GER architectures that treat all crowd configurations identically. The framework also repurposes Video-ChatGPT-generated captions as semantic anchors for a cosine-alignment loss, extending the text-guided grounding idea from vision-language pretraining into a supervised GER setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and underexplored failure mode in GER: models exploiting environmental shortcuts (lighting, scene type) rather than actual group behavior, and provides a principled, testable mechanism (orthogonality + causal intervention) to verify that disentanglement actually occurs. However, the evaluation is constrained by small test sets (756 VGAF, ~63 GECV samples), sensitivity to tracker quality, and the 6 Hz decimation that precludes micro-expression analysis. Open questions include whether the synchrony-gating mechanism generalizes to larger, untracked crowds, whether the causal framing provides genuine invariance guarantees beyond what standard domain randomization achieves, and whether the text-alignment loss remains effective when captions are unavailable or noisy in deployment.
