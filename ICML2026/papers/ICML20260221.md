## Problem

Fine-tuned steering vectors (SVs) for LLM behavior control suffer from two practical limitations: they require brittle, per-instance post-hoc factor selection at inference time to balance steering effectiveness against generation quality, and they operate as full-sequence interventions (FSSVs) that modify representations on every token (prompt and generated), degrading model utility regardless of factor choice. The paper asks whether SV training can be made principled enough to eliminate inference-time tuning and whether intervention can be restricted to prompt tokens alone without sacrificing steering performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text instruction prompt and produces a steered text response that incorporates a target concept (e.g., 'references to the Golden Gate Bridge'). The intervention is a single vector v ∈ R^n added to the residual stream at one chosen layer, scaled by a learned scalar factor α. For PrOSV, the intervention is applied only to a small fixed set of prompt tokens (e.g., 2 prefix + 2 suffix tokens) during the prefill stage and is absent during autoregressive decoding, yielding a 37× reduction in computational overhead compared to FSSV.

## Method

The paper makes two core contributions. First, it derives principled initialization and learning-rate scaling rules for jointly training the steering factor α and direction v using the γ-operator framework from neural network scaling theory: the key constraint is η_v · η_α = Θ(1), with α initialized at Θ(n^{1/2}) and v at Θ(n^{-1/2}) (Kaiming), eliminating the need for inference-time factor grid search. Second, it introduces PrOSV, which restricts the AddInv intervention to a constant number of prompt prefix/suffix tokens during prefill only, inspired by ReFT's prompt-only design. Mechanistically, PrOSV works by implicitly editing the KV cache, minimizing disruption to attention patterns during generation. Combined with the SimPO preference-optimization objective, PrOSV achieves state-of-the-art overall steering scores on AXBENCH (e.g., 1.102 on Qwen2.5-32B) while preserving general model utility far better than FSSV (e.g., 79.2% vs. 6.9% on tinyGSM8K).

## Results

On AXBENCH, PrOSV with SimPO objective achieves the best overall steering scores of 0.803 (Gemma2-2B, L10), 0.905 (Gemma2-9B, L20), and 1.102 (Qwen2.5-32B, L32), consistently outperforming FSSV with the same objective (0.769, 0.886, 0.982). The joint training scheme improves FSSV over the prior factor-sampling-then-selection pipeline by 0.073–0.121 points across the three model setups under the Lang. objective. On Qwen2.5-32B, PrOSV (1.102) surpasses even the prompting baseline (1.060), while on Gemma2-9B prompting (1.075) still leads over PrOSV (0.905).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AXBENCH baselines (Wu et al., 2025a), PrOSV with SimPO outperforms DiffMean (0.297/0.322) by a wide margin and also surpasses the prompting baseline on Qwen2.5-32B (1.102 vs. 1.060), though prompting remains stronger on Gemma2-9B (1.075 vs. 0.905). Compared to the factor-sampling FSSV and RePS baselines from Wu et al. (2025b), PrOSV with joint training wins on all three model setups under both Lang. (0.758 vs. 0.663 on G2B) and SimPO (0.803 vs. 0.756 on G2B; 1.102 vs. 0.947 on Q32B) objectives, and the joint-training FSSV also improves over factor-sampling FSSV (e.g., 0.919 vs. 0.798 on Q32B under Lang.). Against LoReFT (Wu et al., 2024b), PrOSV with SimPO achieves higher scores on both Gemma2-2B (0.803 vs. 0.701) and Gemma2-9B (0.905 vs. 0.777). The SAE baseline (Arad et al., 2025) scores 0.546 on Gemma2-9B, well below PrOSV's 0.905, confirming that fine-tuned prompt-only steering substantially outperforms SAE-based feature selection for concept-based steering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the steering vector (SV) literature (Subramani et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2025a,b) by importing the scaling-theory framework previously developed for LoRA fine-tuning (Hayou et al., 2024a,b; Li et al., 2025) to derive principled initialization and learning-rate rules for jointly training steering factors and directions. It also draws on ReFT (Wu et al., 2024b) to introduce Prompt-Only SV (PrOSV), which restricts intervention to a few prompt tokens rather than the full sequence, and evaluates on the AXBENCH benchmark (Wu et al., 2025a). The key departure from prior SV work is twofold: (1) the steering factor is treated as a trainable parameter rather than an external constant requiring post-hoc grid search, and (2) full-sequence intervention is shown to be unnecessary and even harmful to model utility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding (via infinite-width scaling theory) for SV training hyperparameters, converting a trial-and-error process into a one-off calibration that amortizes across all future SVs for a given model layer. The finding that 4-token prompt-only intervention can match or exceed full-sequence steering challenges the prevailing assumption that broader intervention is needed, with practical benefits of 37× lower inference overhead and better preservation of general model utility. Open questions include whether the capability-vs-robustness tradeoff can be fully resolved, whether more general intervention locations (beyond prefix/suffix) further improve performance, and whether the approach extends to optimization-free SVs or higher-level behavioral concepts beyond content steering.
