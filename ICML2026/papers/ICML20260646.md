## Problem

GAPart pose estimation faces two intertwined challenges: parts exhibit richer symmetry than whole objects, creating multi-solution ambiguity where the ground truth is only one of many equivalent poses, and existing symmetry-aware methods (GASEM, DFGAP) rely on expensive, hard-to-obtain symmetry axis/plane annotations. This annotation dependency severely limits generalizability in data-scarce, real-world robotic scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a segmented GAPart point cloud sampled to 1,024 points as input and outputs a 6D pose (unit quaternion for rotation + 3D translation vector) for each part. Uniquely, it also predicts the symmetry axis (for rotational symmetry) or plane normals (for mirror symmetry) in a fully self-supervised manner, requiring only the symmetry *type* label rather than precise geometric annotations. The quaternion regression operates on the S³ hyperspherical manifold with a two-stage candidate-to-final refinement, and the symmetry prediction is formulated as a discrete probability distribution over the three canonical axes.

## Method

SAFAG builds a modified 3D-GCN backbone with a custom HyperS3 convolution layer that constructs rotation-invariant local coordinate frames per point via covariance-matrix power iteration, then fuses SO(3)-local and Euclidean feature branches with an adaptive confidence weight derived from neighborhood trace and anisotropy. For pose regression, it generates K=64 quaternion candidates by perturbing point-cloud features with random noise through a shared CNN, then refines each candidate in the tangent space of S³ using a candidates encoder that encodes distributional statistics (mean axis-angle offset, cosine similarity, and eigenstructure of the second-moment matrix) before aggregating to a final quaternion. The key novelty is a self-adaptive symmetry module that models the symmetry axis as a weighted combination of canonical axes with learned probability weights (for rotational symmetry) or predicts plane normals validated by bidirectional Chamfer distance (for mirror symmetry), entirely without symmetry geometry annotations. Training uses a two-stage protocol: a warm-up phase supervises candidates against equivalent solutions generated from hypothetical axes, followed by a main phase that replaces these with the network's own predicted symmetry to generate the equivalent solution set for loss computation.

## Results

On the GAPartNet dataset, SAFAG achieves an average rotation error of 3.23° (seen) and 10.83° (unseen), reducing the seen error by 39.1% and the unseen error by 8.68% compared to the second-best method DFGAP (5.51° and 11.86°). For translation, SAFAG achieves 0.016 cm (seen) and 0.046 cm (unseen), representing approximately 20% and 19% reductions over DFGAP. On the ClearPose cross-dataset generalization test, SAFAG achieves rotation errors of 1.52°–3.07° and translation errors of 0.007–0.008 cm across four symmetric object categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFAG outperforms all five baselines on both rotation and translation across seen and unseen categories. Against the closest competitor DFGAP (depth-free, uncertainty-quantified), SAFAG reduces seen rotation error from 5.51° to 3.23° (−41%) and unseen rotation error from 11.86° to 10.83° (−8.7%), while also halving the unseen translation error (0.084→0.046 cm). The original GAPartNet baseline (NPCS-based) is surpassed by a large margin (7.71°→3.23° seen rotation), confirming that the NPCS paradigm limits accuracy. GASEM, which leverages motion information, is consistently outperformed (9.11°→3.23° seen rotation). The two generative category-level methods, GenPose++ (diffusion) and RFMPose (Riemannian flow matching), perform worst on high-symmetry parts (e.g., Hg.Kb: 44.23° and 57.97° seen rotation vs. SAFAG's 8.72°), demonstrating that ignoring part-level symmetry structure severely degrades performance even with state-of-the-art generative backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D-GCN backbone (Lin et al., 2020) and incorporates STE/ORL modules from HS-Pose (Zheng et al., 2023) for point cloud feature extraction, but departs from the dominant paradigm in GAPart pose estimation that relies on explicit symmetry annotations. While GAPartNet (Geng et al., 2023) introduced the GAPart concept with NPCS-based pose estimation, and subsequent works (GASEM, DFGAP) designed symmetry-aware loss functions requiring annotated symmetry axes or planes, SAFAG reframes symmetry prediction as a self-supervised probability distribution problem over canonical axes, eliminating the annotation dependency entirely. The two-stage candidate-to-final quaternion refinement on the S³ manifold also distinguishes it from prior single-shot regression or generative sampling approaches (GenPose++, RFMPose).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free symmetry learning strategy addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying GAPart pose estimation to new objects, where obtaining precise symmetry annotations is costly and error-prone. The real-world xArm manipulation experiments and cross-dataset (ClearPose) evaluation demonstrate practical viability beyond the GAPartNet benchmark. However, the method still requires knowing the symmetry *type* (rotational, mirror, or asymmetric) as a discrete input, and the misclassification experiment (Table 6) shows significant degradation when this label is wrong, leaving open the question of fully automatic symmetry-type detection. Generalization beyond the 9 GAPart classes to arbitrary functional parts remains untested.
