## Problem

Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs) achieve infinite-depth expressivity with constant memory, but their inference requires tens of iterative fixed-point solver evaluations, making them substantially slower than explicit finite-depth networks. Existing acceleration methods (Broyden's method, Anderson acceleration, learned solvers) still rely on sequential step-by-step refinement, leaving a large latency gap. The paper asks whether one can learn a mapping that projects any intermediate solver state directly to the equilibrium in just a few evaluations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262752/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, C-DEQ takes the same raw input as the original DEQ (a text sequence of length 60, a 224×224 image, or graph node features) plus an initial solver state z₀, and produces the equilibrium state z* in as few as 1–8 forward passes. The equilibrium is then passed through a frozen task head to yield the final prediction (next-token logits, image class, or node label). Unusually, the student model also consumes a virtual time scalar t and a short history window of past solver states (z_t, z_{t-1}), which are fed through a time-conditioned readout head concatenated with the backbone features.

## Method

C-DEQ reframes the DEQ iterative solving process as a fixed solver-induced ODE trajectory (FP-ODE), selecting Anderson Acceleration as the teacher solver to produce a deterministic sequence of intermediate states. A student model g_φ is trained via consistency distillation to map any point along this trajectory directly to the terminal equilibrium, using boundary coefficients c_skip(t) and c_out(t) that anchor the output to z_T near the terminal and to a learned network P_φ near the start. The key architectural choice is an AA-structured parameterization: the student's update rule reuses the Anderson Acceleration formula (Eq. 7) so the network refines accelerated estimates rather than relearning the solver trajectory from scratch. Training combines a global consistency loss (MSE to the equilibrium z_K) with a local consistency loss (EMA-anchored alignment between adjacent trajectory points) plus a lightweight task-level cross-entropy regularizer, balancing one-step accuracy against multi-step stability.

## Results

On ImageNet, C-DEQ achieves 47.12% Top-1 accuracy with a single NFE (vs. 1.17% for DEQ and 5.46% for HyperDEQ), and reaches 74.04% Top-1 / 91.45% Top-5 at NFE=8, approaching Inception-V2 (74.8% / 92.2%) while being ~3.3× faster than HyperDEQ. On WikiText-103, C-DEQ attains PPL 47.90 at NFE=1 (vs. 255.94 for DEQ) and 26.43 at NFE=8 (vs. 104.26 for DEQ, 31.37 for HyperDEQ), within 2.1 PPL of Transformer-XL (24.3). On ogbn-arxiv, C-DEQ reaches 71.40% at NFE=5 (vs. 65.26% for IGNN-Solver), and on ogbn-products it achieves 76.55% at NFE=10 (vs. 68.47% for IGNN-Solver) while being 2.4× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-DEQ dominates all implicit DEQ baselines at every NFE budget across all four benchmarks. On ImageNet at NFE=8, C-DEQ (74.04% Top-1) outperforms DEQ by +9.91 pts, HyperDEQ by +4.14 pts, and MDEQ by +2.75 pts, while running ~3.3× faster than HyperDEQ (0.87s vs. 2.90s). On WikiText-103 at NFE=1, C-DEQ (PPL 47.90) reduces perplexity by 5.3× over HyperDEQ (70.19) and 5.3× over DEQ (255.94); at NFE=8 it reaches PPL 26.43, within 2.1 of the explicit Transformer-XL (24.3) and 4.94 below HyperDEQ (31.37). On ogbn-arxiv at NFE=5, C-DEQ (71.40%) surpasses IGNN-Solver (65.26%) by +6.14 pts and IGNN (45.93%) by +25.47 pts; on ogbn-products at NFE=10, C-DEQ (76.55%) beats IGNN-Solver (68.47%) by +8.08 pts while being 2.4× faster (3.85s vs. 9.30s). Against explicit baselines, C-DEQ at NFE=8 on ImageNet is within 0.76 Top-1 of Inception-V2 and 2.76 of HRNet-W18-C, and on ogbn-arxiv at NFE=5 it is within 1.34 pts of GCNII (72.74%) while using only 0.16M parameters vs. 2.15M. The AA solver used in C-DEQ also outperforms Broyden's method and Picard iteration on all four datasets in both accuracy and inference time (Table 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

C-DEQ sits at the intersection of three research threads: Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al., 2019), which define outputs as fixed points of a nonlinear map; Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023), which distill continuous-time ODE trajectories into few-step predictors; and Anderson Acceleration (Walker & Ni, 2011), a quasi-Newton fixed-point solver. The key conceptual departure from prior DEQ acceleration work (HyperDEQ, IGNN-Solver) is that C-DEQ does not learn local step-to-step transitions but instead learns a global consistency mapping from any intermediate solver state directly to the equilibrium. It also differs from the closely related CLLMs (Kou et al., 2024), which apply consistency distillation to a naive Jacobi trajectory without leveraging path-independence or solver-specific structural priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing DEQ inference as a solver-induced FP-ODE and distilling along that trajectory, C-DEQ compresses what previously required tens of iterative evaluations into 1–8 forward passes, closing much of the latency gap between implicit and explicit networks across language, vision, and graph tasks. The FP-ODE perspective provides a principled, general bridge between implicit models and the consistency-model literature, suggesting the framework could extend to other iterative solvers and implicit architectures beyond DEQs. Key limitations include the need to first train a full teacher DEQ, dependence on teacher trajectory stability (less well-posed teachers converge more slowly), and the absence of strong theoretical guarantees for the FP-ODE view, which currently serves mainly as a trajectory-selection device rather than a convergence theory.
