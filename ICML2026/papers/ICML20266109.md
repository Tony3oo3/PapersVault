## Problem

Sparse attention for 2D image data faces a fundamental dilemma: preserving spatial locality (so nearby pixels attend to each other) inherently conflicts with hardware-efficient execution, because 2D-local patterns scatter spatially adjacent tokens across non-contiguous memory addresses, inducing uncoalesced GPU memory access. Existing 2D-aware methods like Swin, CLEAR, and SpargeAttention are spatially structured but not hardware-aligned, so their theoretical sparsity fails to translate into practical wall-clock speedups.

## Input / Output

For diffusion-based image generation (Flux.1-dev, FLUX.2-Klein-9B), the method takes a text prompt and produces images at 1024×1024 or 2048×2048 resolution, operating on 4,096 or 16,384 image tokens respectively. For vision-language inference (Qwen3-VL-8B), it takes an image plus text prompt and produces a text response, with sequence lengths up to 30,976 tokens. In both settings, the image feature map is first permuted into a 1D Hilbert-curve order before attention and recovered to its original 2D layout afterward, a one-time overhead independent of network depth.

## Method

HilbertA integrates three components: (1) reordering image tokens along a generalized Hilbert curve so that 2D spatial neighbors become contiguous in 1D memory; (2) partitioning the reordered sequence into non-overlapping tiles of N_T tokens within which dense attention captures local structure; and (3) a layer-wise sliding schedule that advances each tile's attention window by Δ = N_T/L tokens per layer, so after L layers the window has shifted by one full tile, enabling structured cross-tile information propagation without any memory copies. A small fixed central shared region is attended by every tile to serve as a global relay and a positional anchor for RoPE. The entire mechanism is implemented as a Triton kernel using modular indexing (wrap-around at sequence boundaries), preserving coalesced access throughout. For diffusion models, adaptation requires only lightweight LoRA fine-tuning (rank 4, 10k images, ~80 GPU-hours); for VLMs, no additional training is needed. The key insight is that runtime efficiency is governed not by sparsity or FLOPs alone but by memory access contiguity, which HilbertA explicitly enforces through its curve-based ordering.

## Results

On Flux.1-dev at 2048×2048, HilbertA (16 tiles, 94% sparsity) achieves 4.16× attention acceleration (4.36 ms vs. 18.14 ms dense) and 1.44× end-to-end speedup (45.19 s vs. 65.28 s), while at 1024×1024 it reaches 2.29× attention speedup and 1.10× E2E speedup. On FLUX.2-Klein-9B, HilbertA (16 tiles) improves FID from 26.39 (dense) to 25.67 while delivering 1.48× attention speedup. On Qwen3-VL-8B, HilbertA achieves over 2.30× attention acceleration and 1.57× faster time-to-first-token at long sequences, while scoring 81.52 on InfoVQA, 0.753 on SEED-Bench, 0.705 on MMBench, 686 on OCRBench, and 0.446 on MMStar—consistently above the Swin baseline across all five benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Sparge Attention (Zhang et al., 2025a), HilbertA wins decisively on efficiency: Sparge is slower than dense attention in most settings (e.g., 21.44 ms vs. 18.14 ms at 2048²), while HilbertA achieves 4.16× speedup; on FLUX.2, HilbertA also improves FID (25.67 vs. 28.03) and CLIP-I (85.1 vs. 82.7). Against CLEAR (Liu et al., 2024a), HilbertA achieves lower attention latency at matched or lower sparsity (0.62 ms vs. 0.92 ms at 1024²; 4.36 ms vs. 4.84 ms at 2048²) and substantially better FID on FLUX.2 (25.67 vs. 31.59 for r=8), though CLEAR r=8 retains a FID advantage at 2048² (43.2 vs. 47.5 for HilbertA 16 tiles). Against Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2021) on Qwen3-VL-8B, HilbertA outperforms on all five VLM benchmarks (e.g., OCRBench 686 vs. 606, MMBench 0.705 vs. 0.645) and delivers 2.30× attention acceleration with 1.57× TTFT improvement at long sequences. Against NATTEN (Hassani et al., 2023), HilbertA trades a small kernel-latency gap at medium lengths for substantially better quality (OCRBench 686 vs. 426 for NATTEN-7), while at 30,976 tokens HilbertA is 1.5× faster than NATTEN-21. Against FlashAttention (Dao, 2023), HilbertA achieves a 2.0× kernel speedup at 30,976 tokens (16.12 ms vs. 31.93 ms) by exploiting structured sparsity with contiguous memory access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-attention lineage from 1D text (Longformer, BigBird, Sparse Transformer) to 2D image data, but departs from prior 2D-aware methods (Swin Transformer, CLEAR, SpargeAttention) by identifying and resolving a hardware-alignment gap: those methods preserve spatial locality through windowed or circular patterns that induce uncoalesced GPU memory access. HilbertA instead reorders tokens along a generalized Hilbert curve so that spatial neighbors become memory-contiguous, then applies tile-and-slide on this reordered sequence. The approach is complementary to FlashAttention (which tiles the computation) and to SpargeAttention (which uses Hilbert curves only as a preprocessing step for similarity-based block merging); here the Hilbert ordering directly defines the sparse pattern and the memory layout simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway—that sparsity alone does not guarantee wall-clock speedup unless the access pattern is hardware-aligned—is well-evidenced across diffusion backbones (Flux.1, FLUX.2), a VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B), multiple resolutions, and even consumer GPUs, making the technique broadly applicable to any 2D sparse-attention setting. The low adaptation cost (LoRA on 10k images vs. CLEAR's 750k-image distillation) lowers the barrier to deploying sparse attention in production diffusion pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to 3D video (the authors sketch a 3D Hilbert extension but do not evaluate it), whether the fixed shared region can be made adaptive without breaking contiguity, and how the method interacts with emerging linearized or kernelized attention formulations.
