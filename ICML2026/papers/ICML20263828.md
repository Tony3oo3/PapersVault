## Problem

Surrogate models for topology optimization show highly variable out-of-distribution generalization under distribution shifts (e.g., changing loads or boundary conditions), yet the source of this variability remained unclear. Additionally, no prior work applied deep generative models to turbulent CFD topology optimization, and existing generative approaches relied on continuous Gaussian diffusion fundamentally misaligned with binary topology representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263828/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D scalar conditioning field (adjoint sensitivity or a pseudo-sensitivity such as strain energy density for structural problems or velocity magnitude squared for CFD) at 128×128 resolution, a noisy Bernoulli state x_t, and a scalar timestep t. The conditioning field and noisy state are each embedded via 4×4 patches and processed jointly through cross-attention. The output is a binary material distribution (0/1 per cell) at the original resolution, produced via 50-step Bernoulli flow sampling with a deterministic greedy terminal projection to eliminate salt-and-pepper artifacts.

## Method

The paper establishes via the Data Processing Inequality on the Markov chain Θ→X→S₀→ρ* that adjoint sensitivities are information-theoretically optimal conditioning signals for topology prediction, and formalizes pseudo-sensitivities (physical fields related to true sensitivities via monotone transformations) to explain when cheaper proxies suffice. The generator is a sensitivity-conditioned Bernoulli Flow Matching model: a hierarchical vision transformer with Neighborhood Attention, cross-attention blocks that let the conditioning field modulate features at every resolution, and AdaLN for timestep injection. Training uses a linear probability path from an uninformative prior (α=0.5) to the target binary topology, with a denoiser trained via binary cross-entropy in Bernoulli parameter space rather than continuous velocity regression. Deployment enhancements include a greedy terminal sampling step for simulation-ready topologies and a confidence-based progressive pruning strategy that enforces volume fraction constraints during sampling by removing low-confidence material before each subsequent forward pass.

## Results

On the novel CFD-TO benchmark (turbulent RANS, energy dissipation minimization), the sensitivity-conditioned Bernoulli Flow Model achieves 6.12% mean relative pressure-drop error and 74.5% accuracy (within 10%) on the hardest OOD split (3 outlets), outperforming all baselines. On the structural compliance benchmark, the method achieves 0.53% median compliance error with only 34M parameters, beating TopoDiffusion (1.14%, 121M params) by 33% in mean error and NITO (2.37%, 22M params + 5 SIMP steps) by 39%. Pseudo-sensitivity conditioning (velocity magnitude squared for CFD, strain energy density for structural) matches true sensitivity conditioning within 0.03% median error, validating the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the CFD-TO OOD-Hard split, the Bernoulli Flow Model (Ours) achieves 74.5% accuracy, outperforming PDE-T (68.6%), DiT (70.6%), and UDiT (62.7%), with the largest margin over UDiT (11.8 points). Against the classical STAR-CCM+ single-iteration baseline, all learned models improve accuracy by 20–35 points, confirming that amortized prediction substantially outperforms one gradient step. On the structural benchmark, Ours (0.53% median compliance error, 34M params) beats TopoDiffusion (1.14%, 121M params) by 53% in median error while using 3.6× fewer parameters, and outperforms NITO (2.37%, 22M params) by 4.5× in median error without requiring 5 additional SIMP refinement steps. A key ablation (Table 3) shows that conditioning dominates architecture: UDiT with pseudo-sensitivity conditioning (7.0% median error) vastly outperforms the stronger BFM architecture with pressure conditioning (100% median error), confirming that the information content of the conditioning signal is the primary driver of OOD generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bernoulli Flow Matching framework (Mo et al., 2026) from general binary generation to topology optimization, replacing the continuous Gaussian diffusion used by TopoDiffusion (Mazé & Ahmed, 2023) with a formulation that respects the discrete nature of material distributions. Conceptually, it departs from prior learning-based TO work (NITO, TopoDiffusion, PDE-Transformer) by reframing the conditioning problem through an information-theoretic lens: modeling the TO pipeline as a Markov chain and applying the Data Processing Inequality to prove that adjoint sensitivities are the optimal conditioning signal. The introduction of pseudo-sensitivities (physical fields related to true sensitivities via monotone transformations) unifies previously ad-hoc conditioning choices—strain energy density for structural compliance, velocity magnitude for CFD—under a single theoretical principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that conditioning signal choice dominates generative architecture (a 10× gap from conditioning alone, Table 3) provides practitioners with a principled, information-theoretic criterion for selecting conditioning fields rather than relying on empirical trial-and-error. The pseudo-sensitivity concept has immediate practical value: it avoids expensive adjoint solves (reducing physics cost by >2.5×) while retaining near-optimal OOD performance. Key limitations include the 2D-only evaluation, the frozen turbulence assumption in the CFD derivation, and the requirement for at least one primal PDE solve per query. Open questions include whether the Markov abstraction holds for multi-objective or path-dependent problems, and whether the framework extends to 3D domains and additional PDE families.
