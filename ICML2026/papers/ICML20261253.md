## Problem

Prior diffusion-based samplers achieve only polynomial dependence on the inverse accuracy 1/ε, placing them in the 'low-accuracy' regime analogous to plain Langevin Monte Carlo. No diffusion sampler was known to achieve the polylogarithmic 1/ε scaling characteristic of high-accuracy methods (e.g., Metropolis-adjusted samplers), and existing bounds also depend explicitly on the ambient dimension d rather than on intrinsic properties of the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes Lipschitz score estimates s_t whose estimation error has sub-exponential tails (a stronger assumption than the standard L2-bounded error used in prior work) and a single Gaussian initial sample y_0 ~ γ^d. It outputs a sample from a distribution ε-close to the target q in total variation distance, where q is assumed to be a compactly supported distribution (radius R) convolved with Gaussian noise (std σ). The total number of score oracle calls is Õ((R/σ)² · polylog(1/ε)), with no explicit dependence on the ambient dimension d.

## Method

The key structural result is that the k-th time derivative of the probability flow ODE vector field along the trajectory is bounded by a dimension-free quantity scaling as R(74k/σ_t²)^k(R/σ_t + (kp)^{1/2})^{2k}, implying the score is well-approximated by a low-degree polynomial in time. Leveraging this, the authors adapt the collocation method (Picard iteration) of Lee et al. (2018) with a Chebyshev polynomial basis to solve the ODE over small time windows of length Õ(σ²/R²). Within each window, Picard iteration contracts the W2 discrepancy to the true trajectory exponentially, effectively 'resetting' accumulated discretization error at every step. The main technical novelty is proving that score estimation errors do not compound exponentially over Picard iterations, and establishing a coupling lemma showing the posterior distribution is robust to small perturbations in the conditioning point, which together enable chaining windows to achieve the final polylogarithmic 1/ε guarantee.

## Results

The main guarantee (Theorem 1.1 / Corollary 3.9) shows that the proposed COLLOCATIONDIFFUSION algorithm samples from a distribution ε-close to q in total variation distance in Õ((R/σ)² · polylog(1/ε)) iterations, where R is the effective support radius and σ is the noise level. The Wasserstein-2 guarantee (Theorem 3.7) achieves W₂(q, q̂) ≤ Õ(ε · log²(1/ε)) in Õ((R/σ)² · log(1/ε)) rounds with O(log(1/ε)) Picard iterations per round, for a total of Õ((R/σ)² · log²(1/ε)) score oracle calls. Crucially, the iteration complexity is polylogarithmic in 1/ε (rather than polynomial as in all prior diffusion-based samplers) and independent of the ambient dimension d, depending only on the effective radius R/σ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made at the level of iteration complexity bounds. All prior diffusion-based samplers (DDPM, probability flow ODE discretization, higher-order solvers) achieve at best polynomial dependence on 1/ε, e.g., Õ(d/ε²) or Õ(d^{1+O(1/K)}/ε^{1/K}) for fixed K. The proposed method is the first to achieve polylogarithmic dependence on 1/ε while only requiring access to (approximate) score estimates, without needing log-density ratio queries (as in Huang et al. 2024a) or log-density access (as in Wainwright 2025). In the Gaussian mixture setting with k components at separation Θ(√log k), the method achieves polylog(k, 1/ε) complexity, whereas prior diffusion-based methods scale polynomially in d and 1/ε. The dimension-free dependence on R/σ (rather than d) is also novel, though Li et al. (2025a) obtain a dimension-free rate specifically for Gaussian mixtures using different techniques, with superior scaling in the radius parameter but only for that restricted distribution class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the collocation method of Lee et al. (2018)—a fixed-point (Picard) iteration for ODE solving via low-degree polynomial approximation—to the probability flow ODE of diffusion models. Prior diffusion sampling theory (Lee et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023b, Benton et al. 2024, Conforti et al. 2025) established polynomial-in-1/ε convergence via discretization, while higher-order solver approaches (Li et al. 2024a, Huang et al. 2025a;b, Li et al. 2025b) achieved only polynomial acceleration in ε. The key departure is the structural result that the score's time derivatives along the ODE trajectory are dimension-free and singly exponential in order, enabling the collocation iteration to converge exponentially rather than polynomially—something no prior diffusion sampling method achieved without stronger access models (e.g., Huang et al. 2024a requiring log-density-ratio queries).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based sampler with polylogarithmic (rather than polynomial) dependence on 1/ε and no explicit ambient-dimension dependence, instead scaling with the effective radius R/σ of the target distribution. For Gaussian mixtures with R = Θ(√log k), this yields polylog(k, 1/ε) complexity, matching the hardest regime in distribution learning theory. Key limitations include the need for sub-exponential score error tails (stronger than standard L₂ assumptions), compact support of the pre-noise distribution, and Lipschitz score estimates. Open questions include whether the sub-exponential assumption can be relaxed, whether compact support can be replaced by bounded moments, and whether the 1/σ dependence can be made logarithmic as in the low-accuracy regime.
