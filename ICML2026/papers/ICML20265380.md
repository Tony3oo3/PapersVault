## Problem

Standard conformal prediction calibrated on pooled multi-source data only guarantees coverage for the mixture distribution, not for each individual source. Group-conditional conformal methods achieve per-group coverage but require knowing the test point's group/source at inference time, which is unavailable in sensitive or deployment settings. This paper asks: can one construct a single prediction set with finite-sample worst-case coverage across all K source distributions without observing the test source identity, while keeping the set size close to optimal?

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled data from K heterogeneous sources (each split into training and calibration folds), a test feature vector X, and a miscoverage level α. It requires fitting per-source conditional models (classifiers for classification, conditional Gaussian densities for regression) plus a pooled model, and learning covariate-dependent dual weights λ_k(x) via an empirical optimization. The output is a single prediction set C(X) ⊆ Y (or a union of finite intervals in regression) satisfying min_k P_{P^(k)}(Y ∈ C(X)) ≥ 1−α, with no source label needed at test time.

## Method

MDCP combines two components: (1) a max-p aggregation scheme that computes per-source conformal p-values from a shared conformity score s_k(x,y) = −Σ_ℓ λ_ℓ(x) f̂_ℓ(y|x), takes their maximum, and inverts to yield the union of per-source prediction sets, guaranteeing finite-sample uniform validity; and (2) an efficiency optimization that characterizes the population-optimal score via a Lagrangian dual, showing the optimal set is a superlevel set of h*(x,y) = Σ_k λ*_k(x) f_k(y|x). The dual weights λ*_k(x) are learned by maximizing an empirical dual objective using sieve approximation (splines or neural networks with softplus activation), yielding a consistent estimator under standard smoothness conditions. The key insight is that a single covariate-dependent weighted combination of per-source conditional densities, calibrated marginally per source and aggregated via max-p, asymptotically recovers the conditionally optimal prediction set when at least one source is locally tight almost everywhere.

## Results

In classification LINEAR experiments, MDCP prediction sets are on average 34.39% smaller than the naive BASELINE-AGG while maintaining tight worst-case coverage (near the nominal 1−α = 0.9 level). In regression LINEAR experiments, MDCP intervals are approximately 22% narrower than BASELINE-AGG. On the PovertyMap real dataset, single-source calibration yields coverage as low as ~0.6, whereas MDCP maintains near-nominal worst-case coverage with substantially reduced interval width. Across all settings, MDCP performance closely approaches the ORACLE method that has access to ground-truth densities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MDCP against two baselines: BASELINE-SRC-k (standard split conformal prediction calibrated on a single source, using the TPS score for classification and the variance-adaptive score of Lei et al. 2018 for regression) and BASELINE-AGG (naive max-p aggregation of per-source conformal sets without any score optimization). BASELINE-SRC-k consistently fails: it severely under-covers when the test point originates from a different source (e.g., coverage drops to ~0.6 on PovertyMap when calibrating on one domain and testing on the other), making it unreliable for multi-source deployment. BASELINE-AGG guarantees valid worst-case coverage by construction (as a union of valid per-source sets) but is substantially conservative, producing prediction sets that are 34% larger in classification and 22% wider in regression compared to MDCP. MDCP wins decisively on efficiency over BASELINE-AGG across all settings while matching its validity guarantee, and in some nonlinear classification settings MDCP sets are even smaller than individual single-source sets. MDCP also closely tracks the ORACLE method (which uses ground-truth densities), confirming that the learned score recovers most of the theoretical efficiency gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005; Lei et al., 2018) to a multi-distribution robustness setting by introducing a max-p aggregation of per-source conformal p-values, which yields a prediction set equal to the union of single-source sets with finite-sample uniform coverage. The efficiency optimization departs from prior multi-source conformal work (Lu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024) and robust conformal methods (Cauchois et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2023) by learning a single shared conformity score via a Lagrangian dual objective, drawing a direct analogy to group-DRO (Hashimoto et al., 2018; Sagawa et al., 2019) but in the space of coverage guarantees rather than loss minimization. Unlike group-conditional conformal prediction (Romano et al., 2019a; Gibbs et al., 2025), MDCP does not require the test-time source label, and unlike covariate-shift conformal methods (Tibshirani et al., 2019), both the covariate and conditional label distributions may change across sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MDCP provides a practical, finite-sample-valid solution for deploying uncertainty quantification across heterogeneous environments (hospitals, geographic regions, demographic groups) without requiring sensitive test-time labels, directly addressing fairness and subpopulation-shift concerns. Key limitations include the continued need for source labels during calibration, the population-level (not individual-level) nature of the coverage guarantee, and potential over-conservatism when a source has scarce calibration data. Open questions highlighted by the authors include extending to regression scores that yield clean interval sets, establishing guarantees when sources are inferred rather than known, and handling subpopulation shifts with unknown calibration sources.
