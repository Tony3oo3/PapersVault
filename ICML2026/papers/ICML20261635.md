## Problem

Video diffusion transformers rely on 3D full attention whose quadratic complexity makes high-resolution, long-video generation prohibitively slow (e.g., ~30 minutes for a 129-frame 720p video on an H100). Existing block sparse attention methods, whether static or dynamic, degrade significantly in quality at high sparsity ratios because they fail to capture the dynamic and fine-grained sparsity patterns inherent in DiT attention maps, creating a fundamental mismatch between GPU-efficient block-wise execution and the actual attention structure.

## Input / Output

DFSAttn is a training-free, inference-time acceleration applied to pretrained text-to-video diffusion transformers (HunyuanVideo-13B and Wan2.1-14B). It takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated video (129 frames at 720p for HunyuanVideo, 81 frames at 720p for Wan2.1) without any retraining or architectural modification. The method operates internally on the flattened 3D latent token sequences (N = f×h×w tokens) within each transformer block, replacing dense 3D full attention with sparse block attention while leaving cross-attention to text tokens unchanged.

## Method

DFSAttn is a training-free sparse attention framework guided by a derived theoretical lower bound on attention recall that identifies three governing factors: sparsity budget, inter-block similarity gap, and block-level semantic diversity. First, it reorders video tokens using a global 3D Hilbert curve to preserve spatiotemporal locality in the 1D sequence, which enlarges the inter-block similarity gap and implicitly induces fine-grained sparsity in the original spatiotemporal space while retaining GPU-friendly block-sparse execution. Second, it introduces hierarchical block scoring that partitions each block into smaller sub-blocks, computes sub-block-wise attention scores, and aggregates them to form more accurate block-level importance estimates, reducing errors from mixed semantics within a block. Third, it employs sparse mask caching with an adaptive sparsity schedule that monotonically decreases the sparsity budget across diffusion steps (since attention becomes more concentrated as denoising progresses) and reuses masks at a fixed interval, balancing accuracy and efficiency. The method achieves up to 2.1× end-to-end speedup at ~80% sparsity while maintaining PSNR up to 29.38 on HunyuanVideo.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (129-frame 720p), DFSAttn achieves PSNR 29.38, SSIM 0.898, and LPIPS 0.087 at 80.53% sparsity, delivering a 2.1× end-to-end speedup (836s vs. 1752s full attention). On Wan2.1 (81-frame 720p), it reaches PSNR 22.37, SSIM 0.764, and LPIPS 0.183 at 78.51% sparsity with a 1.79× speedup (1050s vs. 1884s). Across VBench metrics, DFSAttn closely matches full attention (e.g., IQ 66.06 vs. 65.95 on HunyuanVideo; SC 96.73 vs. 96.87), confirming minimal quality degradation under extreme sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFSAttn consistently outperforms all three baselines on reconstruction fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) while operating at the highest sparsity level. On HunyuanVideo, DFSAttn achieves PSNR 29.38 at 80.53% sparsity, surpassing SVG2 (28.58 at 71.88%), SVG (26.83 at 75.24%), and Radial (20.90 at 72.67%); the gap over SVG2 is ~0.8 dB PSNR and the LPIPS is nearly halved (0.087 vs. 0.139). On Wan2.1, the advantage is even more pronounced: DFSAttn's PSNR of 22.37 exceeds SVG2 (18.03), SVG (17.39), and Radial (17.41) by 4+ dB. In terms of speed, SVG2 achieves the highest speedup (2.20× on Hunyuan) due to its custom kernel, but at the cost of substantially lower VBench IQ (63.13 vs. DFSAttn's 66.06) and AQ (55.10 vs. 57.18). DFSAttn's 2.10× speedup on Hunyuan and 1.79× on Wan2.1 are competitive with Radial (1.74×/1.72×) and SVG (1.92×/1.75×) while delivering markedly better quality at higher sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of block sparse attention for diffusion transformers and locality-preserving token reordering. It departs from prior static methods (SVG, RadialAttention) and dynamic methods (SVG2, XAttention, SpargeAttn) by arguing that their coarse block-level representations fundamentally mismatch the dynamic, fine-grained sparsity of DiT attention maps. The 3D Hilbert reordering draws on Hilbert curve ideas from Jenga and HilbertA, but applies it globally over the entire video tensor rather than recursively within blocks, and the hierarchical sub-block scoring and adaptive mask caching are novel combinations. The theoretical lower bound on attention recall provides a principled lens that prior empirical methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2.1× training-free speedup at 80% sparsity on 720p video generation is practically significant for deployment, and the method is model-agnostic (validated on both HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1). The theoretical framework identifying sparsity budget, inter-block similarity gap, and semantic diversity as governing factors offers a design template for future sparse attention work. Limitations include evaluation on only two models, moderate speedups (not an order of magnitude), and the added implementation complexity of reordering plus hierarchical scoring. Open questions include whether the approach extends to audio-visual generation, whether the adaptive schedule generalizes to arbitrary sampling schedules, and how it interacts with quantization or distillation-based acceleration.
