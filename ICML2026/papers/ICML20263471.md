## Problem

Existing T2I benchmarks rely on straightforward prompts and metrics like FID or CLIP-Score that only assess surface-level text-image alignment, leaving models' ability to integrate world knowledge and perform complex semantic reasoning during generation largely unevaluated. This gap is especially critical for unified multimodal models built on LLM backbones, where it remains unclear whether strong language understanding actually translates into knowledge-grounded image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263471/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

WISE takes 1,000 text prompts that deliberately avoid naming the target visual entity directly, instead providing indirect cues, relations, or conditions that require the model to retrieve world knowledge before generation. Each prompt spans one of 25 subdomains across Cultural Common Sense, Spatio-temporal Reasoning, and Natural Science. The output is a single generated image scored by WiScore, a weighted composite (0.7×Consistency + 0.2×Realism + 0.1×Aesthetic Quality) normalized to [0,1], where each component is rated 0–2 by GPT-4o as a VLM judge.

## Method

WISE is a benchmark of 1,000 prompts sourced from educational materials, encyclopedias, common-sense problem sets, and LLM-assisted synthesis, then manually refined to ensure each has a single inferable visual target with an explicit ground-truth explanation. The key design principle is that prompts provide a clue, relation, or condition rather than the target entity name, forcing models to perform logical inference grounded in world knowledge (e.g., 'Einstein's favorite musical instrument' rather than 'violin'). The companion metric WiScore heavily weights Consistency (α₁=0.7) over Realism and Aesthetic Quality to prioritize knowledge-grounded semantic correctness. The paper evaluates 20 models (10 dedicated T2I diffusion models and 10 unified multimodal models across AR, AR+Diffusion shallow-fusion, and AR+Diffusion deep-fusion paradigms), and further isolates the knowledge bottleneck by re-evaluating on GPT-4o-rewritten direct prompts and testing BAGEL with Chain-of-Thought assistance, showing that CoT and prompt simplification substantially close the gap.

## Results

On the WISE benchmark (1,000 prompts across 25 subdomains), the best overall WiScore is 0.70 achieved by BAGEL+CoT (AR+Diffusion deep fusion with chain-of-thought prompting). Among models without CoT, Qwen-Image (AR+Diffusion shallow fusion) leads with 0.62, followed by UniWorld-V1 and MetaQuery-XL at 0.55. The best dedicated T2I model is FLUX.1-dev at 0.50, while the weakest models (Janus-1.3B, Janus-Pro-1B) score only 0.23–0.26. Chemistry is the hardest category for all models (best: 0.58 for BAGEL+CoT), and no model exceeds 0.6 overall without CoT assistance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 10 dedicated T2I models, FLUX.1-dev (0.50) and playground-v2.5 (0.49) lead, while older Stable Diffusion variants (SD-v1-5, SD-2-1) score only 0.32. The AR+Diffusion unified models clearly outperform all dedicated T2I systems: Qwen-Image (0.62) exceeds the best dedicated model by 0.12, and BAGEL+CoT (0.70) leads the entire benchmark by a wide margin. Within unified models, the AR+Diffusion paradigms (both deep and shallow fusion) dominate over pure autoregressive models, with Janus-1.3B (0.23) and Janus-Pro-1B (0.26) scoring below even the weakest dedicated T2I models. Notably, BAGEL without CoT (0.52) is comparable to Qwen-Image's competitors, but adding CoT boosts it to 0.70, suggesting that explicit reasoning chains are critical for knowledge-grounded generation. Chemistry remains the weakest category across all 20 models (range: 0.14–0.58), indicating that translating implicit scientific constraints into visual details is a persistent bottleneck regardless of architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

WISE carves new ground in T2I evaluation by shifting the focus from explicit attribute composition (GenEval, T2I-CompBench, DPG-Bench) to implicit world-knowledge retrieval, where prompts provide clues rather than naming the target entity. It is conceptually adjacent to knowledge-focused benchmarks like PhyBench, Commonsense-T2I, MMMG, and WorldGenBench, but distinguishes itself by targeting natural-image generation from indirect textual cues across cultural, spatiotemporal, and scientific domains simultaneously. The WiScore metric departs from CLIP-Score and VQA-Score by employing a GPT-4o-based multi-dimensional protocol that weights consistency heavily (0.7), directly reflecting the benchmark's emphasis on knowledge-grounded semantic correctness rather than surface-level image-text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a persistent understanding-generation gap: even unified multimodal models with powerful LLM backbones (Qwen-Image at 0.62, BAGEL+CoT at 0.70) fall well short of satisfactory performance, and the rewritten-prompt analysis reveals the gap is only partly attributable to prompt comprehension difficulty—60.1% of originally difficult prompts remain hard after simplification. This suggests that tighter coupling between language-level reasoning and visual generation (as in deep-fusion architectures) and explicit knowledge elicitation mechanisms (e.g., CoT) are necessary but insufficient directions. Open questions include whether scaling LLM backbones or training with knowledge-grounded objectives will close the gap, and how to extend evaluation to multi-image or interactive generation settings.
