## Problem

Under strict context limits, ICL systems must decide which demonstrations to include and how to compress them, yet existing selectors and calibrators do not explicitly model the token-level trade-off between adding task-relevant signal and introducing redundancy, conflicts, or compression-induced drift. This paper asks how to allocate a fixed token budget to convey maximal task-relevant information while minimizing predictive distortion, treating the prompt as a finite-capacity communication channel.

## Input / Output

Given a query x_q, a candidate pool of N demonstrations (retrieved via BM25 or dense retrieval), a strict token budget B, and a frozen (multi)modal model, RDCO produces a compacted in-context prompt C* satisfying L(C*) ≤ B and a task prediction (class label or generated sequence). The method is deterministic and training-free: it requires no learned retriever or policy, and all scoring is done via forward passes of the frozen model. A finite answer set Y_q (label set for closed tasks, top-K completions under a neutral header for open generation) is used to make the KL-based information gain computable.

## Method

RDCO reframes token-budgeted context construction as a rate-distortion optimization: it greedily selects demonstrations by maximizing marginal task information per token (a KL divergence of the query's predictive distribution before and after adding each candidate, normalized by token length and weighted by a reliability score), while penalizing redundancy (cosine similarity of frozen-model hidden-state embeddings) and prefix-conditioned conflicts (per-token NLL of the demo's output given the current prefix). After selection, a bounded-divergence compaction operator iteratively prunes low-importance atoms from each selected demonstration, minimizing token length subject to a KL constraint on the query's predictive distribution to control drift. Theoretically, the paper proves a rate-distortion lower bound on token-error in a linear-Gaussian ICL model and shows that the greedy selector achieves a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee for the submodular mutual-information objective under a well-conditioning assumption, with an explicit token-sample complexity corollary.

## Results

On the 10-dataset ICL suite, RDCO achieves the best average classification accuracy of 63.26% (outperforming the strongest baseline TTF at 61.06% by +2.20 points) and the best average generation Exact Match of 60.26% (outperforming the strongest baseline EPR at 58.00% by +2.26 points). The 10-task overall average improves by 4.94 points over the best per-category baseline under the same token budget. RDCO is training-free and deterministic, requiring no learned retriever or calibration head.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On classification, RDCO (63.26% avg.) outperforms the strongest baseline TTF (61.06%) by +2.20 points, and substantially exceeds learning-based retrievers EPR (55.65%), CEIL (55.79%), LLM-R (54.18%), RLDES (55.04%), and CCL (54.92%) by 7–8 points. On generation, RDCO (60.26% avg. EM) beats the strongest baseline EPR (58.00%) by +2.26 points and CCL (57.43%) by +2.83 points, while also surpassing CEIL (52.23%) and TTF (48.80%) by large margins. Calibration-only baselines (CC, BC, LinC, SC) trail RDCO by 7–10 points on both task types, confirming that selection and compaction are more impactful than post-hoc probability adjustment. Notably, RDCO is training-free and deterministic, whereas EPR, LLM-R, RLDES, and CCL all require supervised retriever training or learned policies, making RDCO's gains particularly striking given its zero-training overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing token-budgeted ICL context construction as a rate-distortion communication problem, a lens absent from prior demonstration-selection work (EPR, LLM-R, CEIL, RLDES, CCL) that optimizes relevance/diversity under fixed-shot abstractions without explicit token-level cost modeling. It repurposes classical submodular optimization (greedy maximization with (1−1/e) guarantees) and information-theoretic rate-distortion theory to unify three previously decoupled operations—demonstration selection, redundancy/conflict penalization, and bounded-divergence compaction—into a single deterministic, training-free pipeline. Unlike prompt-compression methods (LLMLingua, gist tokens) that reduce cost independently of per-query task signal, RDCO couples compaction to a query-anchored KL constraint, and unlike calibration methods (CC, BC, LinC, SC) that adjust predictions post-hoc, it operates at the prompt-construction stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rate-distortion framing provides a principled, interpretable objective for a problem that has largely been addressed by ad-hoc heuristics, and the theoretical guarantees (RD lower bound, submodular approximation, token-sample complexity) give practitioners confidence in when and why the method should work. However, the theory is developed in a stylized linear-Gaussian model, and the practical KL-divergence proxy for mutual information may not capture all pathologies of real LLM behavior; the +2.2–2.3 point gains over the strongest baselines, while consistent, are modest and the method's per-query scoring overhead (O(KN) forward passes) may limit applicability under extreme latency constraints. Open questions include whether the rate-distortion lens transfers cleanly to multimodal or very long-context settings, whether adaptive (query-dependent) budgets can further improve the frontier, and whether the compaction step can be made differentiable to enable end-to-end optimization.
