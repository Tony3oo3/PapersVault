## Problem

Existing rectified-flow-based training-free editing methods rely on Euler inversion, where the reconstruction path approximates latents from mismatched timesteps, causing accumulated drift that degrades source feature preservation. Even stepwise correction strategies that realign trajectories after each denoising step leave persistent step-level reconstruction errors, meaning the editing path is continuously injected with 'drifted' source features. The paper asks how to achieve step-level accurate reconstruction without introducing additional neural function evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing the original content, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit. A Multimodal Large Language Model (Qwen3-VL) is queried to classify the editing type (local, background, global, or other) and identify region-of-interest coordinates, which are then passed to SAM for mask generation. The output is a pair of images: the edited image and a pixel-accurate reconstruction of the source, both decoded from 30-step denoising trajectories on FLUX.1-dev or SD3.5-medium backbones.

## Method

DirectEdit's core insight is to align the forward reconstruction path with the inversion trajectory rather than attempting to fix the inversion itself. During inversion, the latent residual ΔZ_t = Z_{t+1}^{inv} − Z_t^{inv} is recorded at each step; during the forward process, this residual is added to the current latent before velocity prediction, guaranteeing that the computed velocity exactly matches the inversion-path velocity and eliminating step-level reconstruction error at zero additional NFE cost. For feature preservation, the method injects Value features from the source branch into the editing path's self-attention layers for the first t_inj steps, and applies a multi-branch mask-guided noise blending that composites reconstruction and editing latents using SAM-generated masks tailored to the editing type (object segmentation for local edits, complement for background edits, full-image for global style transfer, bounding box for object addition).

## Results

On PIE-Bench, DirectEdit achieves the best average rank of 4.00 (FLUX) and 2.43 (SD3.5) across all metrics. On FLUX, it attains PSNR 32.63, LPIPS 35.45, MSE 25.05, and SSIM 93.49 for background preservation—substantially outperforming the next-best method DNAEdit (PSNR 25.20, LPIPS 86.68, MSE 48.35, SSIM 87.21)—while maintaining competitive CLIP similarity (25.39 whole, 22.45 edited). On SD3.5, DirectEdit reaches PSNR 31.82, LPIPS 31.36, and SSIM 92.28, again leading all baselines on background fidelity. The method uses only 60 NFEs (same as standard Euler inversion), matching the efficiency of Stepwise Correction while achieving near-perfect step-level reconstruction (step-level MSE avg 0.0006 vs. 0.2857 for Stepwise Correction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DirectEdit decisively outperforms all RF-based baselines on background preservation: on FLUX, it achieves PSNR 32.63 vs. DNAEdit's 25.20 and FlowEdit's 21.96, and LPIPS 35.45 vs. DNAEdit's 86.68 and FlowEdit's 112.15. On structure distance, DNAEdit (16.81 on FLUX, 11.03 on SD3.5) remains the closest competitor, slightly edging DirectEdit (17.94, 14.65), but DirectEdit's overwhelming advantage in background fidelity yields a better overall rank. FlowEdit and FireFlow achieve marginally higher CLIP Edited scores (22.60, 22.87 on FLUX) but at the cost of substantially worse background preservation (PSNR 21.96, 23.11), confirming the editability–fidelity trade-off that DirectEdit resolves. Among diffusion-based methods, P2P (SD1.5) shows competitive structure distance (15.44) but lags on all background metrics (PSNR 27.52, LPIPS 59.69) and CLIP Edited (21.01). The ablation without mask-guided blending (Ours w/o mask) drops PSNR from 32.63 to 24.70 on FLUX, confirming that the multi-branch mask mechanism is critical for the background preservation gains over DNAEdit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the Rectified Flow (RF) framework for training-free image editing, building directly on the Euler inversion formulation of RF. It departs from the dominant strategy in prior RF-based editing methods (RF-Inversion, RFEdit, FireFlow, FTEdit, DNAEdit) that attempt to rectify the inversion path via higher-order solvers, fixed-point iteration, or interpolation-based velocity estimation. Instead, DirectEdit inverts the problem: it treats the (imperfect) inversion trajectory as a fixed pivot and aligns the forward reconstruction path to it by recording and re-adding latent residuals at each step. The preservation components (attention feature injection, multi-branch mask-guided noise blending) draw on ideas from P2P, FTEdit, and SAM-based segmentation, but are applied on top of the newly aligned reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is achieving near-perfect step-level reconstruction (MSE 0.0006 vs. 0.2857 for stepwise correction) at the same 60 NFE budget as vanilla Euler inversion, without any test-time optimization or additional neural function evaluations. This makes high-fidelity training-free editing on modern flow models (FLUX, SD3.5) substantially more reliable, particularly for background preservation. However, the method inherits the backbone's limitations and still struggles with spatial manipulations, size changes, and complex contextual reasoning. Open questions include whether the residual-alignment principle generalizes to stochastic (SDE-based) flow formulations or to video editing, and whether the MLLM+SAM mask pipeline can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives.
