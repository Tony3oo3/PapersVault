## Problem

Existing offline RL benchmarks (D4RL, Safety-Gym, IMP-MARL) fail to simultaneously capture the confluence of offline learning from static data, hard renewable budget constraints, multi-agent coordination, and real-world noisy administrative distributions that characterize infrastructure asset management. This gap prevents rigorous evaluation of constrained policy optimization for safety-critical, budget-limited sequential decisions where on-policy exploration is infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264703/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each episode provides a vector of N bridges' local states (structural condition rating, age, traffic volume, span length, importance score) drawn from 15-year sliding windows of U.S. National Bridge Inventory records, plus a per-region annual budget cap defined as 1.2× the spending implied by a behavior-cloning policy (a counterfactual proxy rather than raw historical costs). The policy outputs a discrete action per bridge from {No Action, Minor Repair, Major Repair, Replacement}, which is then passed through a centralized greedy allocator that ranks requests by Q-value or logit and truncates to the feasible budget, guaranteeing zero constraint violations in executed actions.

## Method

InfraRL constructs a high-fidelity offline CMDP benchmark through a three-stage causal verification pipeline on NBI data: (1) mapping work codes to four action types with execution validation via physical evidence (condition rating changes or reconstruction year updates) to eliminate phantom interventions, (2) filtering causally inconsistent records (phantom improvements, ineffective repairs, phantom costs), and (3) partitioning bridges into 400 geographically coherent regions via neighborhood-based sampling to induce realistic shared-budget coupling. The reward combines health improvement, log-normalized cost penalty, and potential-based reward shaping (Φ(s)=τh) for policy invariance. Evaluation uses empirical one-year transition matrices as the dynamics environment, a centralized greedy allocator for hard constraint enforcement, and a multi-axis protocol measuring structural utility, budget adherence, behavioral fidelity, and 100-year generalization under distribution shift.

## Results

On the InfraRL benchmark (2,000 multi-agent episodes from U.S. National Bridge Inventory data), CQL achieves the best normalized health improvement among learned RL methods (0.430 ± 0.015 Improve vs History) with a near-zero raw violation rate (0.7%), while MultiTask-CPQ delivers the best FQE score (−0.094) and a 1.7% violation rate, demonstrating successful constraint internalization. MPC+Forecasting reaches 0.417 ± 0.155, approaching CQL without any learned policy. All imitation-based baselines (MultiTask-BC, Discrete-BC, IQL-CQL-MARL) fail to improve over history (gains ≤ −0.012), and QMIX-CQL collapses with a 71.1% raw violation rate and negative improvement (−0.067).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CQL (Kumar et al., 2020) is the strongest pure value-based offline RL method, achieving 0.430 ± 0.015 Improve vs History with only 0.7% raw violations, outperforming all multi-agent and imitation baselines. MultiTask-CPQ (Xu et al., 2022) achieves the best FQE score (−0.094) and a 1.7% violation rate, confirming that explicit cost-threshold pruning internalizes hard budget constraints without sacrificing utility. OneStep (Brandfonbrener et al., 2021) fails catastrophically with a 96.2% violation rate and −0.287 improvement, showing that myopic one-step policy improvement is infeasible under long-horizon budget coupling. CDT (Liu et al., 2023b) collapses into near-total inaction (budget ratio 0.199, −0.313 improvement), illustrating the imitation ceiling under extreme action sparsity. QMIX-CQL (Rashid et al., 2018) with CQL regularization still produces chaotic multi-agent behavior (71.1% violations, −0.067 improvement), while its mean-field variant QMIX-CQL-MF partially recovers (0.085 improvement) but retains the same high violation rate. MultiTask-BC (Liu et al., 2023a) and Discrete-BC (Formanek et al., 2023) both achieve near-perfect behavioral similarity (>0.99) but fail to improve health (−0.022 and −0.013 respectively), confirming that pure imitation cannot escape historical suboptimality even when budget headroom exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific niche at the intersection of offline RL benchmarks (D4RL, DSRL), safety-constrained RL (Safety-Gym, CDT, CPQ), and multi-agent infrastructure management (IMP-MARL, QMIX), arguing that no existing benchmark simultaneously captures offline learning, hard budgetary constraints, multi-agent coordination, and real-world administrative data noise. It builds directly on the PBRS framework of Ng et al. (1999) for reward design and on CQL/QMIX/CPQ as algorithmic baselines, but departs from prior infrastructure RL work (IMP-MARL, SustainGym) by replacing synthetic deterioration simulators with a causal-verification pipeline over the U.S. National Bridge Inventory. The centralized greedy allocator and BC-induced budget proxy are novel operational mechanisms not present in prior offline constrained RL benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfraRL fills a concrete gap for researchers working on offline constrained RL in safety-critical, resource-limited domains, providing a realistic testbed where on-policy exploration is infeasible and budget violations are operationally inadmissible. The finding that pure imitation learning hits a hard 'ceiling' (cannot scale with budget) while value-based and hybrid methods exhibit economic scalability has practical implications for any domain where historical data is suboptimal and resources vary over time. Key limitations include the single-state (California) and single-asset-type (bridges) scope, matrix-based evaluation rather than a full physics simulator, and the acknowledged offline-to-real deployment gap (contracting delays, traffic disruption, human approval). Open questions include whether the hybrid heuristic-RL advantage generalizes to other infrastructure types, whether richer constraint formulations (e.g., multi-period budgets, risk constraints) change the algorithmic landscape, and how to close the gap between benchmark performance and operational deployment.
