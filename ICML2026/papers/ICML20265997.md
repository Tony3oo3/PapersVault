## Problem

Existing Bayesian LoRA methods for LLMs face a fundamental tradeoff: approaches with principled uncertainty guarantees (e.g., Laplace-LoRA) are computationally infeasible at LLM scale, while scalable variants (e.g., BLoB, C-LoRA) rely on simplified posterior approximations that produce overconfident, poorly calibrated predictions under distribution shift. No prior method combines the rigorous function-space prior of Gaussian Processes with the parameter efficiency required for practical LLM fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt processed by a frozen pretrained LLM (LLaMA3.1-8B or Qwen3-8B) with LoRA adapters injected into attention and/or feed-forward projection layers. At inference, it produces a predictive distribution over C answer options by drawing S posterior samples from the amortized variational distribution over the low-rank GP latent weights, yielding both a point prediction (argmax) and a calibrated uncertainty estimate (e.g., ECE, NLL, or selective-prediction risk at a confidence threshold).

## Method

GPan-LoRA reformulates the LoRA weight update ΔW = BAx as a low-rank Gaussian Process in the projected feature space Ax ∈ R^r, leveraging a theorem showing that a GP on R^n can be exactly recast through the linear map A to a GP on R^r with a corresponding low-rank kernel. The sparse GP approximation with l inducing points is shown to be mathematically equivalent to a variational Bayesian neural network hidden layer with kernel-induced nonlinear features, making it natively compatible with standard backpropagation training. Amortized variational inference is applied via a lightweight inference network that outputs the variational posterior parameters (mean and covariance of the latent weights Z) conditioned on the low-rank feature, which regularizes the posterior and mitigates overconfidence. A weight-sharing strategy between the GP mean function and kernel function further reduces the Bayesian parameter count to O(lr²), comparable to state-of-the-art Bayesian LoRA methods while preserving the GP's principled uncertainty semantics.

## Results

On LLaMA3.1-8B, GPan-LoRA achieves the lowest ECE under large distribution shift (OBQA→Chem: 11.31%, OBQA→Phy: 13.29%) and the lowest NLL (1.19/1.25), outperforming the strongest baselines LAP (ECE 14.59/14.00) and BLoB (ECE 24.45/32.16) by substantial margins. On in-distribution tasks, GPan-LoRA achieves best or second-best ECE across all six datasets (e.g., OBQA ECE 3.12%, BoolQ ECE 1.45%) while maintaining competitive accuracy (OBQA 86.66%, ARC-E 91.84%). Under small distribution shift (OBQA→ARC-C/E), GPan-LoRA achieves the best ECE on ARC-E (3.31%) and second-best on ARC-C (6.46%), confirming well-calibrated uncertainty even when accuracy is not dominant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPan-LoRA decisively outperforms BLoB and C-LoRA under large distribution shift, reducing ECE on MMLU-Chem from 24.45 (BLoB) and 19.39 (C-LoRA) to 11.31, and on MMLU-Phy from 32.16 (BLoB) to 13.29. Against LAP, which is the strongest scalable baseline for UQ quality, GPan-LoRA achieves lower ECE on both large-shift targets (11.31 vs. 14.59 on Chem; 13.29 vs. 14.00 on Phy) and lower NLL (1.19 vs. 1.10 on Chem is a slight loss, but 1.25 vs. 0.92 on Phy is a loss; however GPan-LoRA wins on ARC-E ECE at 3.31 vs. 4.16). On in-distribution tasks, GPan-LoRA matches or beats C-LoRA on ECE (e.g., OBQA 3.12 vs. 8.16; BoolQ 1.45 vs. 0.30 where C-LoRA wins) and is competitive with LAP on NLL. MCD and DeepEns are clearly inferior under distribution shift, with ECE values 2–3× higher than GPan-LoRA on Chem/Phy. The lightweight last-layer variant (LL GPan-LoRA) retains most of the large-shift advantage (Chem ECE 8.25) at near-MLE parameter count (4.53M vs. 4.47M), offering a practical trade-off for resource-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPan-LoRA sits at the intersection of three research threads: Bayesian LoRA (BLoB, C-LoRA, Laplace-LoRA), sparse variational Gaussian processes (Titsias, 2009), and amortized variational deep kernel learning (Matias et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure from prior Bayesian LoRA methods is the placement of the GP prior in the low-rank projected feature space rather than directly on the LoRA weight matrices, which reduces Bayesian parameters from O(nr) to O(lr²) while preserving function-space semantics. Theorem 4.1 provides a clean theoretical justification for this recasting, and Appendix A.3 establishes an explicit equivalence between the sparse GP approximation and a structured VBN, bridging the GP and Bayesian neural network literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly important gap: existing scalable Bayesian LoRA methods (BLoB, C-LoRA) produce overconfident predictions under distribution shift, which is precisely where uncertainty estimates are most needed for safe deployment. The last-layer variant (LL GPan-LoRA) offers a near-zero-overhead option that still outperforms baselines under large shift, making the approach accessible for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the GP prior's advantages persist at larger model scales (beyond 8B), how the method interacts with other PEFT paradigms (e.g., QLoRA, adapter-based methods), and whether the inducing-point count l can be further reduced without degrading calibration.
