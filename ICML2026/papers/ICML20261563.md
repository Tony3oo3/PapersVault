## Problem

Causal discovery in linear SEMs typically requires non-Gaussian disturbances (LiNGAM) to identify causal directions, yet many real-world applications naturally provide multiple paired views of the same system whose cross-view correlations could serve as an alternative identifiability source. Existing multi-view causal discovery methods are scarce and either ignore dependencies across views or impose restrictive assumptions on those dependencies, leaving a gap between the rich multi-view structure available in practice and the theoretical tools to exploit it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m paired views of p-dimensional observations (X ∈ R^{m×p×n}), where pairing means the same data points are observed across all views (e.g., the same brain regions measured in different subjects under a common stimulus). It outputs view-specific adjacency matrices B^i ∈ R^{p×p} encoding the DAG edges and weights for each view, along with a shared causal ordering P that is common across all views. The SOS-based algorithms (PairwiseLiMVAM, DirectLiMVAM) require no hyperparameter tuning and operate purely on covariance structure, while the ICA-based variant additionally estimates view-specific scaling matrices D^i and noise covariance matrices Σ^i.

## Method

The paper defines the LiMVAM model, a multi-view linear SEM where all views share a common causal ordering P but have view-specific strictly lower-triangular weight matrices T^i, and crucially imposes no non-Gaussianity requirement on disturbances. Two new SOS-only algorithms—PairwiseLiMVAM (likelihood-ratio criterion) and DirectLiMVAM (cross-covariance Frobenius-norm criterion)—determine pairwise causal directions by comparing view-aggregated second-order moments, then recursively identify root variables and regress out residuals to recover the full ordering; once P is known, view-specific weights are estimated jointly via one-step Feasible Generalized Least Squares. The key identifiability insight is that a 'diversity' condition on cross-view correlations (Assumption 2) suffices to make the causal direction identifiable, replacing the non-Gaussianity assumption entirely. A third algorithm, ICA-LiMVAM, reformulates the model as a Shared ICA problem with a more general shared-disturbances decomposition (e^i = D^i s + n^i) that corrects the arbitrary scaling flaw in prior work, and is estimated via Shared ICA followed by DAG-constrained recovery of B^i.

## Results

On synthetic data with shared disturbances (Figure 3), PairwiseLiMVAM and DirectLiMVAM consistently outperform all other methods by a significant margin in both ℓ₂ distance of estimated adjacency matrices and percentage of incorrectly estimated causal orderings across 50 random runs. In the Gaussian-disturbance setting, MultiGroupDirectLiNGAM and ICA-LiNGAM fail entirely, while ICA-LiMVAM yields reliable but less accurate estimates. On the Cam-CAN MEG dataset (98 participants), the estimated median causal effects show high stability with an average Pearson correlation of 0.67 across 50 bootstrap runs of 30 participants. On the Ramsey et al. fMRI rhyming task (9 participants), the recovered causal structure matches established neuroanatomical findings, including strong Input→LOCC edges and left-to-right inter-hemispheric connections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines. Against MultiGroupDirectLiNGAM (Shimizu 2012), both PairwiseLiMVAM and DirectLiMVAM achieve substantially lower ℓ₂ estimation error and fewer ordering mistakes; MultiGroupDirectLiNGAM fails completely in the Gaussian-disturbance setting because it requires non-Gaussianity. Against ICA-LiNGAM (Shimizu 2006) applied naively per view, the proposed methods again dominate, with ICA-LiNGAM showing particularly high error in recovering the shared causal ordering P due to its inability to exploit cross-view structure. Against ICA-LiMVAM (Chen et al. 2024), which is essentially the same algorithm as the paper's ICA-LiMVAM variant, the SOS-based methods (PairwiseLiMVAM, DirectLiMVAM) provide a major reduction in estimation error at little or no additional computational cost (Figure 4), while ICA-LiMVAM remains competitive but with larger errors. In a supplementary comparison (Appendix H.1.3), DirectLiMVAM also outperforms the multi-domain method of Perry et al. (2022), confirming that the multi-view paired-data framework is more appropriate when cross-view correlations exist.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LiNGAM family of linear causal discovery methods (Shimizu et al., 2006; Shimizu et al., 2011; Hyvärinen and Smith, 2013) to the multi-view setting, where paired observations from multiple sources share a common causal ordering. It departs from prior multi-view causal discovery work (Shimizu, 2012; Chen et al., 2024) by showing that cross-view correlation diversity alone—via second-order statistics—suffices for identifiability, removing the non-Gaussianity requirement that all previous linear methods relied upon. The ICA-based variant builds on Shared ICA (Richard et al., 2021) and generalizes the shared-disturbance model of Chen et al. (2024) by introducing view-specific scaling matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the non-Gaussianity assumption, the paper opens multi-view causal discovery to a much broader class of real-world data (e.g., neuroimaging with near-Gaussian signals) where prior methods were inapplicable. The SOS-only identifiability theory is a clean theoretical contribution, but its practical reach is bounded by Assumption 2 (correlation diversity across views), which may fail in settings with highly homogeneous subjects or views. Open questions include robustness to partial violations of the shared-ordering assumption, scalability to very large numbers of views, and whether the framework extends to nonlinear or time-lagged (Granger) settings.
