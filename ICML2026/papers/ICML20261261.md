## Problem

Recurrent-depth (looped/universal) transformers can scale computation through layer repetition and show advantages in reasoning, but their autoregressive generation is inherently sequential—every recurrence step must fully complete before the next token is produced. This paper asks whether the structural similarity between recurrent-depth models and diffusion models can be exploited to parallelize generation from existing checkpoints without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261261/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pre-trained recurrent-depth model checkpoint (e.g., Huginn-0125, 3.5B parameters with a prelude–recurrent–coda architecture) and produces a generated text sequence. Unlike standard autoregressive decoding that waits for r full recurrence steps per token, the sampler advances at least one new token per recurrence step, maintaining a wavefront of partially-refined tokens that converge adaptively. Tokens are frozen once their latent states converge below a distance threshold, and KV cache sharing across recurrence depths ensures bounded memory usage regardless of sequence length.

## Method

The core insight is that recurrent-depth models—starting from a random latent state and iteratively refining it conditioned on input embeddings—can be interpreted as latent-space diffusion models, enabling diffusion forcing sampling to be applied directly to existing checkpoints without retraining. The sampler advances diagonally across the sequence: at each forward pass it decodes initial token drafts from intermediate iterates while simultaneously refining earlier tokens through additional recurrence steps, achieving parallelism along the sequence dimension akin to speculative decoding. Stabilization relies on three mechanisms: input injection for course-correction when conditioning changes, artificial noise injection into latent states (analogous to continuous diffusion schedules), and momentum on the input embedding. An adaptive exit criterion based on normalized latent-state distance determines when each token position has converged and can be frozen, with a bounded wavefront size controlling peak memory. Theoretically, under a fixed wall-clock budget, the sampler preserves the same recurrent depth as autoregressive generation while updating a strictly wider front of token positions in parallel, yielding up to 5× speedup with roughly 1% accuracy trade-off on reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

The diffusion forcing sampler achieves a 4.4–4.8× speedup over the static autoregressive baseline (r=32) across all four reasoning benchmarks, with accuracy losses of only 0.6–2.4 percentage points. In the best configuration (r′=4, βt=0), the sampler reaches 42.08% on GSM8K at 157.3 tokens/s (vs. 41.77% at 36.1 t/s for AR), 18.00% on MATH500 at 30.3 t/s (vs. 17.60% at 6.4 t/s), 20.12% on HumanEval at 64.9 t/s (vs. 22.56% at 13.5 t/s), and 31.00% on MBPP at 70.2 t/s (vs. 31.60% at 15.3 t/s). The speedup is consistent across model variants (weight-averaged checkpoint and math-finetuned model), with gains in the 4–5× range and accuracy deviations within 0.5–1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the adaptive compute autoregressive sampler from Geiping et al. (2025), the diffusion forcing sampler achieves roughly 2.4× higher throughput on all benchmarks (e.g., 157.3 vs. 66.9 t/s on GSM8K, 30.3 vs. 12.2 t/s on MATH500) while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy (42.08% vs. 42.23% on GSM8K; 31.00% vs. 30.20% on MBPP). Against the self-speculative decoding baseline (adapted from Leviathan et al., 2023, using the recurrent-depth model as its own draft model with 4 draft tokens × 4 draft steps), the diffusion sampler again wins on speed by ~2.3× (157.3 vs. 69.5 t/s on GSM8K, 64.9 vs. 27.5 t/s on HumanEval) while maintaining comparable accuracy (42.08% vs. 42.76% on GSM8K, a 0.68 pp gap; 20.12% vs. 20.12% on HumanEval, a tie). Notably, the diffusion sampler outperforms even the heavily tuned speculative decoding baseline, which the authors describe as the strongest prior parallelization method for this model. The only consistent accuracy cost is on HumanEval (−2.44 pp vs. static AR r=32), where the parallelized latent reasoning appears to slightly degrade code generation quality, though the speedup there is the largest (4.81×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: recurrent-depth (looped/universal) transformers, which increase computation by repeating layers, and diffusion language models, which iteratively denoise sequences. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the iterative latent-state refinement in recurrent-depth models is structurally analogous to latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022), enabling the direct transfer of diffusion forcing sampling (Chen et al., 2024b) as an inference-time parallelization strategy. Unlike speculative decoding, which requires a separate draft model or verification step, or block diffusion, which operates on frozen chunks, this sampler reads drafts directly from intermediate recurrent iterates and advances a wavefront of tokens in parallel—requiring no retraining of the underlying model. The theoretical analysis departs from prior work on parallel decoding (Santilli et al., 2023; Kou et al., 2024) by starting from an already-recurrent model rather than tuning a fixed-depth model toward parallel fixed-point updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× speedup on reasoning and coding benchmarks with only ~1% quality degradation is a practically significant efficiency win for recurrent-depth models, which have been gaining traction as a scaling strategy for test-time compute. The conceptual equivalence between recurrent-depth iteration and latent diffusion opens the possibility that unrolling-based training objectives could serve as competitive alternatives for training future diffusion language models. Key limitations include evaluation at batch size 1 only, dependence on a single model family (Huginn-0125, 3.5B), and the fact that the speedup comes from GPU parallelization rather than FLOP reduction. Open questions include whether the sampler generalizes to looped architectures without input injection (e.g., Coconut), how it behaves under continuous batching, and whether the theoretical expressiveness advantage holds for non-contractive recurrent blocks.
