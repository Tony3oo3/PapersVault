## Problem

Existing multi-LLM systems communicate through natural language, which forces discrete tokenization and redundant embedding/de-embedding cycles between models, losing information and breaking gradient flow. This makes it impossible to learn inter-model communication protocols directly from end-task supervision, forcing reliance on hand-designed prompts, search-based workflow optimization, or black-box heuristics. The paper asks whether a dense, differentiable communication medium can replace natural-language messages between LLM nodes while preserving the standard text-in/text-out interface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265059/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts natural language text as input and produces natural language text as output, maintaining the same external interface as a standard LLM. Internally, however, all intermediate communication occurs in the continuous hidden-state space (vectors of dimension d, the LLM's hidden size) rather than in token space. Only the first vertex retains an embedding layer to map input tokens to dense vectors, and only the last vertex retains a de-embedding layer to map final dense vectors back to token logits; all intermediate nodes operate purely on dense vectors.

## Method

LMNet organizes stripped LLMs (transformers with embedding and de-embedding layers removed) as vertex nodes in a layer-wise fully-connected directed graph, analogous to an MLP topology. Trainable seq2seq modules (single attention blocks, randomly initialized) serve as communication edges that transform and route dense vectors between vertices, enabling one source to distribute information differently to multiple targets. The entire network is end-to-end differentiable, so communication is learned solely from auto-regressive loss on the final output without any supervision on intermediate messages. Vertex parameters are shared across all nodes (initialized from a single pre-trained LLM) to keep parameter overhead small, while edge parameters are trained from scratch. The paper demonstrates this on a 1/4/4/4/1 topology with Qwen2.5-0.5B vertices (1.1B total parameters), showing +30.5% relative improvement over prompting with less than 1% of LLM pre-training cost.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-0.5B, LMNet (1.1B total params, 5-layer 1/4/4/4/1 topology) achieves a +30.5% relative improvement over Prompt across 11 benchmarks, substantially outperforming SFT (−5.7%), Self-Refine×16 (+0.5%), and Self-Consistency×16 (+3.4%). Headline gains include MMLU 53.9% (vs. 44.3% Prompt), BBH 47.3% (vs. 20.3%), MATH 38.8% (vs. 19.5%), and GSM8K 50.3% (vs. 41.6%). In the limited-supervision setting (Table 2, MMLU ΔAcc), LMNet achieves 37.28% with Qwen2.5-1.5B, outperforming SFT (35.02%), CODI (35.16%), and COCONUT (33.85%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMNet decisively outperforms both test-time scaling baselines: Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) achieves only +0.5% relative improvement and Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022) +3.4%, versus LMNet's +30.5%, despite both TTS methods being given a 16× computation budget. Against latent-reasoning methods, LMNet surpasses COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024) by 3.43 ΔAcc points on MMLU (37.28 vs. 33.85 with Qwen2.5-1.5B) and CODI (Shen et al., 2025) by 2.12 points (37.28 vs. 35.16), with similar margins on GSM8K (72.67 vs. 48.63 and 65.30). On the E2E generation task with GPT2-M, LMNet achieves the best average rank (1.6) across BLEU/NIST/MET/ROUGE-L/CIDEr, beating LoRA (Hu et al., 2022; rank 2.3), PreLayer/Prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021; rank 2.7), AdapterH (Houlsby et al., 2019; rank 6.7), and AdapterL (Lin et al., 2020; rank 8.0). In the size-matched comparison (Table 7), LMNet-1B (built on Qwen2.5-0.5B + 0.6B edges) outperforms Llama3.2-1B (1.23B) on MMLU (53.9 vs. 32.2), BBH (47.3 vs. 31.6), and GSM8K (50.3 vs. 9.2), and is competitive with the larger Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama3.2-3B on most benchmarks, while also surpassing Gemma2-2B (2.61B) on MMLU, MMLU-Pro, BBH, and TruthfulQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—test-time scaling (CoT, self-consistency, Self-Refine), text-based multi-agent systems (MetaGPT, GPTSwarm), and latent reasoning (COCONUT, CODI)—under a single 'language model network' paradigm where pre-trained LLMs are reusable nodes connected by communication edges. Its key technical departure is replacing discrete natural-language messages with dense differentiable vectors by stripping intermediate embedding/de-embedding layers and inserting trainable seq2seq edge modules, enabling end-to-end gradient-based optimization of inter-model communication from final-task supervision alone. Unlike latent reasoning methods that are confined to a single backbone or simple linear passing, LMNet constructs a multi-layer fully-connected directed graph of heterogeneous (or shared) stripped transformers, drawing an explicit analogy to MLP universal approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LMNet offers a principled, supervision-efficient route to improving LLM capability: +30.5% over prompting with <1% of pre-training cost, and consistent gains over PEFT and latent-reasoning baselines under limited data. However, several open questions temper its near-term impact: dense communication requires white-box access to model internals (incompatible with API-based agents), the current end-to-end auto-regressive implementation does not yet realize the motivating sentence-level message-passing picture, and all experiments use small models (≤7B) with limited compute (<200 GPU days), leaving scaling behavior unclear. The most important future direction identified is inner-auto-regressive LMNet where intermediate vertices generate variable-length dense latent sequences, which would better match the multi-agent communication intuition while preserving differentiability.
