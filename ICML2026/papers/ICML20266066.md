## Problem

Human attention (saliency) modeling remains highly fragmented across modalities, scenes, and task formulations, with each setting studied in isolation using dedicated architectures and training protocols. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) cross-scene generalization fails because hierarchical cognitive modulation is collapsed into dataset-specific statistical biases, and (2) cross-task modeling is incompatible because image and video attention are treated as disjoint problems with fixed-window temporal encoders that restrict frame-wise inference and incur high computational cost.

## Input / Output

AAM takes a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L backbone (with LoRA on the last 24 blocks) to encode 448×448 RGB frames, a frozen CLIP text encoder for dataset-level cognitive prompts (structured as stimulus type + scene + key elements + task setting + attention bias), and a frozen Wav2CLIP ResNet-18 for audio waveforms projected into the shared CLIP semantic space. The output is a normalized spatial attention map (probability simplex over H×W) per frame, produced via a geometry-aware hyperbolic decoder that applies specialization-depth-driven scale modulation and relative-geodesic-direction-driven spatial focusing. For video, the Fokker-Planck Dynamics module refines frame-wise predictions through sequential drift, diffusion, and correction sub-steps, enabling arbitrary-length inference at ~111 FPS with only 21M trainable parameters.

## Method

AAM reformulates attention as a cognitive entailment hierarchy in the Lorentz hyperbolic space: a learnable general-attention anchor z_anc, text-condition embeddings z_txt, and visual instance embeddings z_img are ordered z_img ⪯ z_txt ⪯ z_anc via thresholded entailment-cone losses on angular residuals, replacing dataset-specific parameter isolation with a single shared geometry. For temporal dynamics, the Fokker-Planck Dynamics (FPD) module models video attention evolution as ∂u/∂t = −∇·(vu) + ∇·(D∇u) + λ(u_obs − u), discretized via Lie-Trotter splitting into a drift step (bidirectional temporal self-attention as a Markov transition kernel), a diffusion step (second-order central finite difference with learnable spatially-varying intensity), and a correction step (learnable Kalman-gain-like interpolation back to the static observation), followed by simplex projection. A hyperbolic decoder maps the structured latent back to Euclidean space using geodesic-distance-driven multi-scale fusion and tangent-space geodesic-direction-driven spatial focusing, while audio-visual fusion uses relevance-gated cross-attention with residual Tanh-FiLM modulation and a negative-sample-attack training strategy to suppress irrelevant auditory cues.

## Results

AAM achieves average metric gains of 5.2% on image, 5.8% on audio-visual, and 6.0% on video benchmarks (averaged across CC, KLD, AUC, SIM, and NSS), evaluated on 16 datasets total. On video, AAM reaches 0.919 AUC on DHF1K, 0.944 AUC on Hollywood-2, and 0.943 AUC on UCF Sports, outperforming all baselines. The model also achieves an approximate 4× inference speedup (111 FPS vs. 17–30 FPS for fixed-window methods) with only 21.4M trainable parameters (324M total), compared to 82–643M parameters in competing video saliency models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAM consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across the 16 evaluation benchmarks. On image saliency, AAM surpasses the strongest joint-training baseline SUM [Hosseini 2025] by 4–6% in CC (e.g., 0.831 vs. 0.768 on MIT1003, 0.925 vs. 0.909 on SALICON) and by 1–2% in AUC, while also beating task-specific models like TransalNet, Temp-Sal, and UniAR. On video saliency, AAM leads TFS-Net [Li 2025] and MSFF-Net [Zhang 2023] by 2–4% in CC (e.g., 0.563 vs. 0.534 on DHF1K) and by 0.5–1.5% in AUC. On audio-visual tasks, AAM outperforms the strongest baseline TAVDiff [Yu 2025] by 4–9% in CC (e.g., 0.710 vs. 0.670 on DIEM, 0.887 vs. 0.798 on Coutrot-2) and by 1–2% in AUC. In terms of efficiency, AAM achieves 111 FPS with 21.4M trainable parameters, compared to 17–30 FPS and 82–643M parameters for TASED-Net, STSANet, and TMFI-Net, yielding an approximate 4× speedup while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in saliency prediction—where image, video, and audio-visual attention are treated as separate tasks with dedicated architectures—by unifying them under a single model. Conceptually, it builds on hyperbolic entailment cones (Ganea et al., 2018; Le et al., 2019; Desai et al., 2023) to encode a general-to-specific cognitive hierarchy, and repurposes the Fokker-Planck equation from drift-diffusion cognitive modeling to govern temporal attention evolution. Unlike prior joint-training efforts (UNISAL, SUM) that rely on parameter isolation or dataset-specific priors, AAM uses a shared frozen DINOv3 backbone with LoRA adaptation and a single set of learnable parameters across all modalities, with text prompts in hyperbolic space serving as the conditioning mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified formulation and 4× video inference speedup (111 FPS vs. 28–30 FPS for fixed-window methods) make cross-modal attention modeling practically deployable, and the Attention-1.75M corpus provides a standardized training resource the field has lacked. However, the ~6% average improvement over strong baselines is moderate, and the dataset has notable demographic and modality biases (88% static images, 0.7% audio-visual, predominantly 18–36 age range). Open questions include whether the hyperbolic hierarchy captures genuine cognitive structure or merely serves as a useful inductive bias, whether the Fokker-Planck formulation extends to longer or egocentric video, and whether the frozen-backbone design limits ceiling performance.
