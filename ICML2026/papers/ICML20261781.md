## Problem

Inferring ODE vector fields from noisy trajectories typically requires per-dataset optimization with complex pipelines (symbolic regression, GP regression, or Neural ODE training) that demand substantial ML expertise and system-specific prior knowledge. The only prior amortized approach, ODEFormer, relies on a very large (50M-system) pretraining corpus with complex symbolic vector fields and a global representation. This paper asks whether a much simpler pretraining prior (low-degree polynomials) combined with a local neural-operator representation can match or exceed that global symbolic baseline.

## Input / Output

The model takes K noisy trajectories sampled at irregular time points (corrupted by multiplicative Gaussian noise and random subsampling) and internally converts them into transition tuples (state, displacement, squared displacement, inter-observation time gap) rather than consuming raw trajectories. It outputs a parametric vector field estimator f̂_θ(x | D) that can be queried at any point in state space, not just observed states, and supports dynamical-systems diagnostics such as equilibrium detection, Jacobian computation, and stability classification. The architecture is currently restricted to state dimensions 1–3 via zero-padding, and the 13M-parameter model (8M predictor + 5M uncertainty head) operates in a normalized space with instance normalization on spatial coordinates and delta-log centering on time gaps.

## Method

FIM-ODE is a transformer-based neural operator with an encoder-decoder structure: the encoder embeds transition features via linear self-attention into a permutation-invariant context representation, while the decoder uses cross-attention to query this context at arbitrary spatial locations, producing local vector field estimates. Pretraining uses 600K synthetic ODEs with sparse multivariate polynomial vector fields of degree ≤3 (dimensions 1–3), where coefficients are drawn from N(0,1) and sparsity is introduced by randomly masking degrees and monomials. Training employs an uncertainty-weighted MAE loss (heteroscedastic Laplace likelihood) with a mixed sampling strategy that draws 50% of query locations from observed trajectory points and 50% uniformly within an expanded bounding box, preventing overemphasis on high-magnitude regions. The key design choice is the local neural-operator representation, which only commits to approximating the vector field where data provides constraints, in contrast to ODEFormer's global symbolic expression that must commit to a functional form everywhere. The pretrained model also serves as a strong initialization for rapid finetuning via context-reconstruction objectives (single-shooting or multiple-shooting), enabling fast adaptation to out-of-distribution regimes without ML expertise.

## Results

On ODEBench trajectory reconstruction, FIM-ODE achieves 84.4% of systems with variance-weighted R² > 0.9 (σ=0, ρ=0), substantially outperforming ODEFormer (63.1%) despite being ~10× smaller (8M vs 86M params) and pretrained on ~80× fewer systems. On the low-data OOD regime (VDP forecasting, FHN imputation), zero-shot FIM-ODE is suboptimal, but after finetuning it achieves 0.028 MSE on VDP Task 1, 0.022 on VDP Task 2, and 0.029 on FHN, matching or beating all classical baselines. On CMU Motion Capture, zero-shot FIM-ODE achieves the best short-horizon MSE (6.10) for subject 09, and after finetuning reaches 6.92/11.73 (short/long) for subject 35, recovering from a zero-shot failure (655.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ODEFormer (the primary pretrained symbolic baseline), FIM-ODE wins decisively on ODEBench trajectory reconstruction (84.4% vs 63.1% at σ=0, ρ=0) and is comparable on trajectory generalization (29.5% vs 27.9%), while using ~10× fewer parameters and ~80× less pretraining data. In the low-data OOD regime (VDP/FHN), zero-shot FIM-ODE underperforms all classical per-dataset baselines, but after finetuning it achieves the best MSE on all three tasks: 0.028 on VDP T1 (vs GP-DNF 0.03, LatentSDE 0.10), 0.022 on VDP T2 (vs GP-DNF 0.04), and 0.029 on FHN (vs GP-DNF 0.04, LatentSDE 0.05). GP-DNF is the strongest classical baseline in the zero-shot comparison, but FIM-ODE (Finetuned 2) surpasses it by roughly 7–80% in MSE across all three tasks. On CMU Motion Capture, zero-shot FIM-ODE is best for subject 09 (6.10 short-horizon MSE) but fails catastrophically for subjects 35 and 39; finetuning recovers competitive performance (6.92/11.73 for subject 35), approaching GP-DNF (6.72/7.03) and LatentSDE (7.57/7.65). The key takeaway is that FIM-ODE's pretrained weights serve as a strong initialization: finetuning on a single short trajectory (≈7 min on CPU) closes the gap to or below the best per-dataset-trained methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Foundation Inference Model (FIM) framework from stochastic differential equations (FIM-SDE, Seifner et al. 2025) and Markov jump processes (Berghaus et al. 2024) to ordinary differential equations. The key conceptual departure is from ODEFormer (d'Ascoli et al. 2024), which amortizes ODE inference via a global symbolic representation pretrained on a complex prior of polynomial-trigonometric-rational compositions over ~50M systems. FIM-ODE instead uses a local neural-operator representation pretrained on a much simpler prior of low-degree polynomials over only 600K systems, arguing that vector field estimation is inherently local (constrained only where trajectories pass) and that a local estimator can transfer even when the global functional form is out-of-distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 13M-parameter model that infers ODE vector fields in a single forward pass without requiring ML expertise, and that finetunes in minutes on CPU for challenging regimes. The main open question is whether the non-stationary polynomial prior (which causes magnitudes to grow with distance from origin) can be replaced by stationary constructions to scale beyond three dimensions. The low-data sensitivity of zero-shot inference and the need for finetuning in out-of-distribution regimes (human motion, short contexts) limit the 'foundation model' claim, and the checkpoint-selection protocol for finetuning uses test data, weakening the evaluation. Whether the local-vs-global representation trade-off generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-autonomous systems remains unexplored.
