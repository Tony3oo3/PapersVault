## Problem

Existing linear causal representation learning methods, notably LiNGCReL (Jin & Syrgkanis, 2024), require that exogenous noise be identically distributed across environments and that noise components within a single environment have distinct distributions. These assumptions are easily violated in practice (e.g., data from different labs or measurement devices), and the paper asks whether latent causal features and their DAG can still be identified under only a non-Gaussianity constraint on the noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260555/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n×p data matrices X^(k) from K ≥ d heterogeneous environments, where p ≥ d and the latent dimension d is known a priori. The observed data is assumed to be a linear mixing (via a fixed full-column-rank matrix H) of latent features y^(k) ∈ R^d governed by a shared causal DAG G with environment-specific weighted adjacency matrices W^(k). The output consists of estimated latent features ŷ^(k) and a pruned causal DAG Ĝ, both identified only up to the surrounded-node equivalence class (∼_sur), meaning features may be linearly mixed among a node and its surrounding set.

## Method

CREATOR proceeds in three subroutines. First, it sequentially recovers one latent component per iteration by running ICA on the current residual to obtain candidate unmixing vectors, then selecting the candidate that minimizes HSIC (an independence criterion) between the candidate and the residual; by the Darmois-Skitovitch theorem, the correct candidate corresponds to a root node's exogenous noise, and orthogonal projection removes that node's causal influence before the next iteration, thereby inferring the topological ordering. Second, it prunes spurious edges by regressing estimated noise on the (still entangled) latent features to obtain B̂^(k), then testing whether the rank of a K×(i−j) matrix increases when a candidate edge column is appended, exploiting cross-environment heterogeneity (Assumption 2.2) to distinguish true parents from ancestors. Third, it disentangles the lower-triangularly entangled features by computing, for each node, a vector in the intersection of row spaces spanned by its children's regression rows, yielding features up to ∼_sur. Crucially, noise components are automatically aligned across environments by following the topological order, eliminating the need for the cross-environment distributional invariance assumed by LiNGCReL.

## Results

In synthetic experiments across d ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7} latent dimensions and K ∈ {d, 2d} environments (n = 1000, 50 random DAGs), CREATOR consistently outperforms LiNGCReL on both LocR² (latent feature recovery) and SHD (causal DAG accuracy) in two settings: one with heterogeneous noise distributions across environments (the paper's relaxed assumptions) and one matching Jin & Syrgkanis's original setting. In the LLM case study, CREATOR recovers 15 out of 18 correct causal edges across six LLMs, compared to 8/18 for LiNGCReL, with CREATOR achieving a perfect 3/3 on Llama Guard, Llama 3.1 Instruct, and TinyLlama.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is LiNGCReL (Jin & Syrgkanis, NeurIPS 2024), the closest prior method that also assumes a linear SCM with a linear mixing function. CREATOR wins on both synthetic and real-data comparisons. In synthetic experiments, CREATOR achieves higher LocR² and lower SHD than LiNGCReL across all tested dimensions (d = 2, 3, 5, 7) and both noise settings, with the advantage being most pronounced under the paper's relaxed assumption of heterogeneous noise distributions across environments (setting 1), where LiNGCReL's alignment step breaks down. In the LLM case study, CREATOR recovers 15/18 correct edges versus LiNGCReL's 8/18; CREATOR is perfect on Llama Guard, Llama 3.1 Instruct, and TinyLlama, while LiNGCReL fails on at least one edge for every LLM except Llama Guard. The key practical advantage is that CREATOR does not require the exogenous noise to be identically distributed across environments, a condition that is easily violated in real data (e.g., different measurement devices across labs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Jin & Syrgkanis (2024, LiNGCReL), which established identifiability of latent causal features and DAGs under a linear structural causal model with heterogeneous environments. The key departure is relaxing two distributional assumptions on exogenous noise: LiNGCReL required noise distributions to be identical across environments and different across components within an environment, while CREATOR only requires non-Gaussianity (at most one Gaussian component). Algorithmically, CREATOR replaces LiNGCReL's ICA-based parent selection with a three-stage pipeline—topological ordering via Darmois-Skitovitch independence testing, rank-based DAG pruning, and surrounding-node disentanglement—that is more efficient and avoids the cross-environment noise alignment step entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The relaxed assumptions make linear CRL more practically applicable, since noise heterogeneity across environments (e.g., different measurement devices) is common in real data. The LLM case study, while explicitly a proof-of-concept, opens a promising direction for using causal representation learning to interpret internal representations of language models. Key open questions include extending to nonlinear mixing functions, handling discrete/dynamical/multimodal data, developing statistical tests for CRL models, and establishing finite-sample guarantees beyond the point-wise consistency result. The linear model assumption remains a significant limitation for many real-world applications.
