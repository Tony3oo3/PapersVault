## Problem

Current MLLMs struggle with spatio-temporal prompts that simultaneously require reasoning about a global 3D environment (encoded as a point cloud) and local temporal observations (encoded as a short egocentric video clip). This gap exists for two reasons: no training data provides both a holistic spatial context and locally confined temporal observations together, and existing approaches augment temporal observations with spatial information rather than treating them as distinct modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266115/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: a dense 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×6} (xyz coordinates + RGB) representing the global allocentric scene, an egocentric video V of T frames (each H×W×3) with per-frame camera intrinsics and extrinsics capturing local human actions, and a text instruction. The video covers only a subset of the environment, making the point cloud essential for spatial reasoning. Outputs are natural-language answers across five task types: relative direction/distance reasoning, object localization with navigation instructions, furniture affordance prediction, and action planning with spatial navigation. For comparison with existing models that cannot ingest point clouds, the global scene is provided as 25 multi-view images instead.

## Method

Two STLLM baselines are built on LLaVA-Video-Qwen2, extending it to handle 3D point clouds alongside video and text. STLLM-3D directly concatenates point-cloud features (extracted via an OpenScene masked transformer decoder after voxel downsampling, then grouped via Farthest Point Sampling), SigLip video features, and text embeddings along the sequence dimension for LLM decoding. STLLM-Aligner instead uses a set of 1024 learnable queries that attend to image and text features via cross-attention, with point-cloud spatial context incorporated through a separate cross-attention path, producing a compact spatial representation fed to the LLM. Both models are fine-tuned with LoRA on the REA dataset (24,371 training samples) for one epoch, with modality-specific encoders kept frozen. The key design principle is treating temporal (video) and spatial (point cloud) data as fundamentally different modalities rather than augmenting one with the other, and the REA dataset is constructed via a six-step pipeline integrating EPIC-KITCHENS action annotations, VISOR segmentations, and VGGT/Reloc3r-based dense point cloud reconstruction.

## Results

On the REA dataset, STLLM-Aligner achieves 41.89% overall and 47.32% average categorical accuracy under the ChatGPT-4o LLM judge, and 46.50%/51.29% under Gemini 2.0 Flash. STLLM-3D reaches 40.94%/46.43% (ChatGPT-4o) and 46.39%/51.34% (Gemini). In contrast, the best off-the-shelf MLLM (VideoLLaMa3) achieves only 31.46%/35.86% under ChatGPT-4o, and even the strongest proprietary model (GPT-5.2) reaches only 37.05%/40.49%, confirming that spatio-temporal reasoning remains unsolved by current models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STLLM-Aligner outperforms all off-the-shelf baselines by a wide margin: it beats VideoLLaMa3 (31.46%) by ~10.4 points, LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 (30.96%) by ~10.9 points, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (24.38%) by ~17.5 points, and LLaVA-OV-Qwen2-7B (23.85%) by ~18 points on overall ChatGPT-4o accuracy. Against the stronger 8B-scale models, STLLM-Aligner still leads Qwen3-VL-8B (22.82%) by ~19 points and InternVL3.5-8B (26.98%) by ~14.9 points, indicating gains are architectural rather than scale-driven. Even the proprietary GPT-5.2 (37.05%) falls ~4.8 points short of STLLM-Aligner. Among REA-finetuned baselines, LLaVA-OV-Qwen-7B† (38.19%) and LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2† (36.99%) improve over their vanilla counterparts but remain 3.7 and 4.9 points below STLLM-Aligner, confirming that explicit 3D point-cloud integration provides gains beyond what finetuning on REA alone can achieve. On SQA3D (zero-shot), STLLM-3D achieves 55.21% average accuracy vs. 44.29% for VideoLLaMa3 and 43.04% for LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2, further validating cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-Video-Qwen2 video-LLM architecture by introducing 3D point clouds as a first-class modality alongside video and text, rather than the more common approach of augmenting video frames with depth or multi-view spatial cues. It draws on OpenScene's masked transformer decoder for point cloud encoding and VGGT/Reloc3r for dense reconstruction and frame registration, but departs from prior 3D-LLM work (3D-LLM, SpatialVLM, LLaVA-3D) by explicitly treating allocentric spatial context and egocentric temporal observations as distinct modalities that must be jointly reasoned over, rather than fusing them into a single spatial representation. The REA dataset itself is constructed by integrating EPIC-KITCHENS action annotations, VISOR segmentations, and EPIC-FIELDS sparse geometry into a six-step pipeline that generates spatio-temporal QA pairs requiring both global scene understanding and local action reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The REA benchmark exposes a concrete gap: even strong 8B open-source MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B) and the proprietary GPT-5.2 achieve only 23–42% LLM-judge accuracy on tasks that require grounding egocentric actions in a global 3D scene, suggesting that current multimodal models lack genuine joint spatio-temporal reasoning. The STLLM baselines demonstrate that explicit 3D point-cloud input (vs. 2D multi-view proxies) yields consistent gains, but absolute performance remains modest (~42–47%), leaving open the question of what architectural or data-level changes are needed to close the gap. Limitations include the kitchen-only domain of EPIC-KITCHENS, reliance on LLM judges for open-ended tasks, and the fact that the two STLLM variants differ only marginally, making it unclear which design principle (direct concatenation vs. cross-modal alignment) will scale better to more complex scenes.
