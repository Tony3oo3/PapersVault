## Problem

Existing data-free backdoor defenses operate under an internal diagnosis paradigm—either repairing the victim model's weights or testing input robustness—that is fundamentally fragile against advanced attacks (dynamic, clean-label, clean-image) because they remain entangled with the compromised model's parameters or rely on assumptions that stealthy triggers explicitly violate. No prior method provides robust, model-agnostic defense across diverse attack modalities without accessing training data or modifying the victim model.

## Input / Output

PRISM takes a single test image (standard resolution per dataset, e.g., 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label, operating as a zero-overhead wrapper around a deployed suspicious model. No labeled training data, clean reference set, or model weight access is required; the system adapts purely from the unlabeled test stream in an online fashion. The VLM auditor is kept frozen throughout, so the only mutable state is O(1) statistical moments and class prototypes updated in place.

## Method

PRISM wraps the victim model in a dual-stream architecture where a frozen universal VLM (CLIP, SigLIP, Qwen-VL, etc.) acts as an independent semantic auditor. A Hybrid VLM Teacher bridges the domain gap by fusing static text anchors with visual prototypes (class centroids) refined online via Cumulative Moving Average from certified-clean test samples, eliminating the need for offline training. An Adaptive Router computes a logit margin Δ measuring the VLM's semantic support for the victim's prediction, then applies a Cornish-Fisher expansion to model the non-Gaussian margin distribution and derive a skewness-aware adaptive threshold per class. Only samples passing the gate (Δ > τ) trigger updates to the statistical moments and prototypes, providing selective robustness against adaptive flooding and poisoning attacks. The key architectural insight is that decoupling defense from the victim model's weights via an external VLM auditor eliminates the attack surface exploited by weight-manipulation and internal-anomaly-suppression strategies.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PRISM achieves an average Clean Accuracy (CA) of 93.9% and an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 0.0% across all 11 backdoor attacks, simultaneously improving CA over the undefended model (92.0%) while eliminating the attack. Across 17 datasets and 6 VLM backbones, PRISM consistently suppresses ASR below 10% while keeping CA drops within 5%, even on domain-specific datasets like GTSRB where the VLM's zero-shot accuracy is only 25.1%. Inference latency with the CLIP auditor is 12.5 ms per image on an A100 GPU, ranking 2nd among all tested defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM is the only method that suppresses ASR below 15% across all 11 attack types simultaneously. The strongest input-robustness baseline, Refine (ICLR 2025), achieves 14.3% average ASR but fails catastrophically on Clean-Image attacks (FLIP: 30.3%, GCB: 100% ASR), while model-repairing methods like CLP and C&C are completely defeated by Clean-Image triggers (ASR 100% on GCB). The naive Zero-shot VLM baseline matches PRISM's ASR (0.4%) but incurs a 5.3-point CA drop (86.7% vs. 93.9%), and the Ensemble baseline preserves CA (94.7%) yet leaves ASR at 75.7%, rendering it useless as a defense. On the domain-specific GTSRB dataset (1% poison rate), PRISM achieves 93.4% CA with 3.6% average ASR, outperforming the best baseline (TeCo at 96.2% CA / 25.2% ASR) by a wide margin on ASR while sacrificing only ~2% CA. PRISM's advantage is most pronounced against advanced attacks (Clean-Image, Dynamic) where all prior data-free defenses exhibit ASR > 50%, confirming that the external semantic auditing paradigm addresses failure modes inherent to internal diagnosis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established data-free backdoor defense paradigm, which splits into model-repairing methods (CLP, LPP, C&C) that inspect the victim model's internal statistics and input-robustness methods (ScaleUp, ZIP, TeCo, BDMAE, Refine) that test perturbation sensitivity. Rather than building on either lineage, it introduces an orthogonal axis: a frozen, externally-sourced VLM acts as an independent semantic auditor whose feature space is statistically independent of the poisoned victim model. The online adaptation machinery—hybrid text/prototype anchoring and Cornish-Fisher skewness-corrected thresholding—repurposes ideas from prototype learning and moment-based distribution approximation, but their combination as a test-time defense gate is new. The Trust Chain extension (weak clean generalist sanitizing a compromised specialist before it audits the victim) has no direct precedent in the backdoor literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a concrete gap: all eight SOTA data-free defenses fail catastrophically on clean-image attacks (FLIP, GCB) where triggers are semantically natural, and several also fail on dynamic attacks—yet PRISM suppresses ASR below 1% on CIFAR-10 across all 11 attack types. The key open question is the auditor-trust assumption: the defense collapses under full VLM collusion (Sc3), and auditor-aware text-prompt attacks still achieve 15.6% ASR, indicating the input surface remains exploitable. Extending the logit-margin gating to sequence-level tasks (LLMs, speech, code) and to domains where no clean generalist VLM exists (3D perception, industrial sensing) are identified but unresolved challenges.
