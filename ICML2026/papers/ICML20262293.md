## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for vision-language models primarily align static global representations or flat token-wise correspondences, overlooking the hierarchical feature structure and fine-grained cross-modal interactions that underpin semantic understanding. This structural gap causes compact student embedding models to fail at inheriting object-level semantics and spatial relationships from larger teachers, particularly when teacher and student have different token counts or layer depths. HieRD targets this underexplored setting of distilling hierarchical cross-modal relational structure into efficient multimodal embedding models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed at 448×448 for FastVLM-0.5B or 336×336 for LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B) paired with text queries, and produces fixed-size multimodal embedding vectors via EOS pooling. The distillation operates at multiple granularities simultaneously: visual tokens are grouped into object-level clusters via DBSCAN over a combined cosine-dissimilarity and spatial-proximity metric, while text tokens are partitioned into word-level spans at lower layers and phrase-level spans (noun phrases, verb phrases) at higher layers using a syntactic parser. A region-based spatial mapping handles the many-to-one correspondence between fine-grained teacher patches and coarser student patches when resolutions differ.

## Method

HieRD introduces a hierarchical relational distillation framework with three complementary loss components: (1) intra-modal structural alignment that preserves pairwise cosine-distance geometry among visual token clusters and text spans separately, (2) cross-modal structural alignment that matches the image-text similarity matrix between teacher and student, weighted by the teacher's cross-attention patterns, and (3) hidden-level alignment via a learnable projector mapping student group representations into teacher feature space. Group-level representations are constructed using attention-weighted aggregation (derived from the teacher's self-attention) rather than simple averaging, allowing discriminative tokens to dominate. The paper provides theoretical grounding: Theorem 3.1 proves that unimodal self-attention induces progressive intra-cluster contraction across layers, Theorem 3.2 shows that preserving pairwise cosine distances guarantees an isometric linear embedding on the Stiefel manifold, and Theorem 3.3 establishes that jointly optimizing intra- and cross-modal losses forces the image and text isometries to coincide, unifying the cross-modal geometry.

## Results

Using B3-Qwen2-2B as teacher, HieRD achieves a CLS average Precision@1 of 67.11 and VQA average of 52.76 on the FastVLM-0.5B student, and 70.08 / 42.25 on the LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B student, outperforming all distillation baselines on both task averages. On LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B, HieRD leads on all 11 individual benchmarks, with particularly large gains on ImageNet-1K (63.27 vs. 56.67 for CKD) and DocVQA (49.23 vs. 47.17 for CKD). Training overhead is modest: 3.6 s/iteration and 63.5 GB average VRAM, comparable to EM-KD (3.5 s, 64.8 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HieRD outperforms all four distillation baselines (RKD, CKD, EMO, EM-KD) on both CLS and VQA average Precision@1 for both student architectures. Against the strongest baseline per setting, HieRD gains +0.94 on CLS and +1.20 on VQA for FastVLM-0.5B (vs. RKD and EM-KD respectively), and +3.13 on CLS and +1.87 on VQA for LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B (vs. CKD on both). The advantage is most pronounced on LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B, where HieRD wins all 11 individual benchmarks, while on FastVLM-0.5B it leads on 7 of 11. EM-KD, the closest competitor on FastVLM VQA (51.56), is surpassed by HieRD by 1.20 points on average, with the largest per-dataset gap on InfoVQA (38.07 vs. 36.80). CKD is the strongest baseline on LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B but trails HieRD by 3.13 (CLS) and 1.87 (VQA) on average, with the largest single-dataset gap on ImageNet-1K (63.27 vs. 56.67).

## Relation to Prior Work

HieRD extends the knowledge distillation lineage for vision-language models (Align-KD, EM-KD) by shifting focus from generative/reasoning tasks to multimodal embedding learning, building on the VLM2Vec/MMEB contrastive framework and the Relational KD objective as its base loss. It departs from prior VLM distillation methods that treat tokens uniformly by introducing hierarchical structure: DBSCAN-based visual token clustering (motivated by a theoretical analysis of self-attention contraction) and syntactically grounded phrase-level text spans, aligned through multi-granular intra-modal and cross-modal relational objectives. The Stiefel-manifold isometry theorems (3.2, 3.3) provide a geometric justification absent in prior flat-alignment distillation work, connecting the method to Procrustes-type analysis in representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: state-of-the-art multimodal embedding models remain too large for deployment, and prior distillation methods for VLMs were designed for generative tasks rather than retrieval-oriented embedding quality. The hierarchical clustering approach is architecture-agnostic (works across different token counts and layer depths), which is important for real-world model compression. However, the LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B results contain internal inconsistencies between the reported table values and the narrative text (e.g., DocVQA reported as 23.40 in Table 1 but 49.23 in the body), which undermines confidence in the largest claimed gains. Open questions include whether the DBSCAN clustering assumption generalizes to non-patch-based vision encoders, whether the strong theoretical conditions (losses optimized to exactly zero) hold in practice at scale, and whether the approach extends to video or audio modalities.
