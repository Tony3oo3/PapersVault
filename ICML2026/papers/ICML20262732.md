## Problem

Current multimodal geometry datasets suffer from low structural diversity and difficulty because symbolic search and numerical instantiation are tightly coupled, forcing reliance on fixed templates. Additionally, existing alignment methods supervise models only through final answers or natural-language descriptions, leaving geometric structure recovery from diagrams weakly constrained and implicit. This paper addresses both gaps by synthesizing problems from scratch with decoupled stages and introducing plotting code as an explicit structural supervision target.

## Input / Output

The model receives a rendered geometry diagram (512×512 during training, 224×224 during evaluation) paired with a debiased text problem statement that has had diagram-inferable cues removed. The output is a three-block structured prediction: (1) plotting code in a JSON-like DSL specifying point coordinates, segments, circles, annotations, and quantity expressions; (2) a reasoning trace inside <think> tags; and (3) a final answer inside <answer> tags. The plotting code is deterministic—executing it reproduces the input diagram exactly—making it a verifiable intermediate representation between visual perception and symbolic reasoning.

## Method

The pipeline factorizes synthesis into three verifiable stages: (i) symbolic seed generation using GenesisGeo/AlphaGeometry to sample predicate sets, build dependency graphs, and select structurally rich, reasoning-intensive configurations; (ii) grounded instantiation where an Instructor LLM assigns numerical values and generates problem statements with reasoning traces, while a separate Coder LLM produces executable construction code (conditioned only on the problem statement to prevent answer leakage), followed by two-stage verification (semantic coherence + coordinate-based geometric constraint checking); and (iii) visualization with OpenCV rendering and textual debiasing that strips diagram-inferable information from the question. The key alignment contribution is training the VLM to predict plotting code before reasoning, transforming visual understanding into a structured, verifiable prediction task rather than an implicit latent process. Training proceeds in two phases: LoRA-based SFT (rank 128, 2 epochs, lr 1e-5) on the 18K GeoCode dataset, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning (lr 5e-6, 4 samples per prompt, KL β=0.01) with rewards on answer correctness and output format only, confirming that gains stem from data quality rather than additional structured supervision.

## Results

Training Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on GeoCode yields consistent gains across all six benchmarks, with the largest improvement on OlympiadBench (+11.52, from 45.82% to 57.34%) and Test-mini (+8.58, from 17.91% to 26.49%). The weaker Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct backbone shows even larger relative gains on Geometry3K (+6.19) and MathVerse (+8.44), though it slightly degrades on MathVista (−3.36). With an additional GRPO stage on top of SFT, Qwen3-VL reaches 37.68% on Test-mini (from 17.91% baseline) and Qwen2.5-VL reaches 20.52% (from 4.48%), confirming that the synthetic data provides strong learning signals even under answer-only reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other geometry data synthesis methods (e.g., GeoGen, TrustGeoGen, R-CoT) head-to-head; instead, it benchmarks the pretrained model checkpoints against themselves after GeoCode fine-tuning. Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct improves on all six benchmarks, with the standout gain being +11.52 points on OlympiadBench (45.82→57.34), the most challenging geometry benchmark, and +8.58 on the held-out Test-mini set. Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct shows larger absolute gains on Geometry3K (+6.19) and MathVerse (+8.44) but suffers a −3.36 drop on MathVista, which the authors attribute to a distribution gap in visual style and question format not covered by the synthetic data. The stronger Qwen3-VL backbone largely mitigates this gap, remaining positive on MathVista (+1.26) and achieving a much larger OlympiadBench improvement, suggesting that stronger base visual priors better absorb the synthetic-to-real shift. The ablation in Table 6 further shows that plotting-code supervision (26.49% on Test-mini) substantially outperforms caption-based alignment (20.52%) and no alignment (17.91%), confirming that the structured code target is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the solver-driven geometry data synthesis line (GeoGen, TrustGeoGen) by decoupling symbolic structure discovery from numerical instantiation and visual rendering, using GenesisGeo/AlphaGeometry for seed generation and LLMs for grounded realization. It departs from prior alignment-oriented methods (EAGLE, MAVIS) and data-centric approaches (R-CoT, G-LLaVA, MathCoder-VL) by introducing plotting code as an explicit structured prediction target for visual-symbolic alignment, rather than relying on final answers, reasoning traces, or natural-language captions. The textual debiasing step also distinguishes it from prior synthetic geometry datasets by explicitly removing diagram-inferable cues from problem statements to force genuine visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GeoCode dataset (18K problems with 2-3× structural density over existing benchmarks) and the plotting-code alignment strategy offer a concrete recipe for generating high-quality multimodal geometry data with guaranteed cross-modal consistency, addressing a key bottleneck in training VLMs for structured visual reasoning. The diagnostic showing that annotation fidelity (not mere parseability) drives solving accuracy provides a useful lens for understanding what visual perception actually contributes to geometry reasoning. Limitations include heavy reliance on GPT-OSS-120B (≈50% discard rate), evaluation primarily on Qwen backbones, and the question of whether code-based alignment generalizes to other structured visual domains beyond geometry.
