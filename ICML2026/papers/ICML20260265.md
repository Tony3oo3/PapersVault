## Problem

Graph neural networks used for PDE dynamics modeling suffer from oversmoothing, where adjacent node features become indistinguishable over message-passing steps. Unitary convolutions were proposed to fix this by strictly preserving the Rayleigh quotient (a smoothness measure), but this overconstrains the model for physical systems like heat diffusion where smoothness naturally increases over time, leading to undersmoothing. The paper derives a theoretical lower bound showing unitary functions cannot approximate targets whose norm has high angular dependence, and proposes relaxed unitary convolutions that balance smoothness preservation with the natural smoothing required by diffusive dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes node feature vectors on a triangular mesh (or grid graph) along with the mesh connectivity and cotangent edge weights from the Robust Laplacian. For MeshPDE, the input is the previous 5 time steps of the PDE solution as node features, and the output is the next-timestep solution, rolled out autoregressively over up to 196 steps. For WeatherBench2, the input is a 240×120 (1.5°) Earth mesh with 6-hour temporal resolution, predicting T850 and Z500 fields. The mesh Rayleigh quotient is computed using the Robust Laplacian, which applies Delaunay edge rewiring to ensure non-negative cotangent weights, making it a valid smoothness measure.

## Method

The core architecture, R-UNIMESH, generalizes unitary convolution from graphs to meshes by incorporating cotangent weights into the normalized adjacency matrix and proving the mesh Rayleigh quotient is preserved under unitary mesh convolution. Two relaxation mechanisms break the strict unitarity constraint: (1) Taylor truncation of the Lie unitary convolution's matrix exponential at order T_max, giving precise control over the degree of smoothness change; and (2) compositional smoothness-breaking, where inputs are zero-padded to a higher dimension (preserving the Rayleigh quotient trivially), passed through k layers of Lie unitary mesh convolution (the smoothness-preserving component P), and then mapped through an unconstrained MLP or GCN head (the smoothness-breaking component B) that can alter smoothness as needed. The model uses GroupSort activations for norm-preserving nonlinearity and orthogonal weights for real-valued tasks. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows the approximation error of any unitary function is lower-bounded by the variance of the target's norm over concentric spheres, explaining why unitarity is overconstrained when the PDE solution's amplitude varies with angle.

## Results

On the MeshPDE benchmark (heat, wave, and Cahn-Hilliard PDEs on complex PyVista meshes), R-UNIMESH achieves the best NRMSE (51.9±3.6), SMAPE (79.7±5.6), and Rayleigh Error (9.1±7.4) on the heat equation, outperforming the next-best baselines (Hermes at 73.02 NRMSE, EMAN at 14.2 RE) by 29% and 36% respectively. On the wave equation, R-UNIMESH achieves the lowest NRMSE (236.5±6.4) but is outperformed on smoothness (RE 93.5 vs. Transformer's 48.0). On Cahn-Hilliard, R-UNIMESH is competitive (NRMSE 123.9) but GemCNN and EMAN edge it out on individual metrics. On WeatherBench2, R-UNIMESH is comparable with SOTA in RMSE and ACC, coming within a couple of degrees of the best models even at 10-day lead times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the heat equation, R-UNIMESH decisively outperforms all baselines: it achieves 29% lower NRMSE than Hermes (51.9 vs. 73.0) and 36% lower Rayleigh Error than EMAN (9.1 vs. 14.2), confirming that relaxed unitary convolution is particularly well-suited to diffusive dynamics. On the wave equation, R-UNIMESH wins on NRMSE (236.5 vs. EMAN's 281.3) but loses on smoothness fidelity (RE 93.5 vs. Transformer's 48.0), consistent with the authors' hypothesis that the highly oscillatory Rayleigh quotient of wave solutions makes strict unitarity overconstraining. On Cahn-Hilliard, R-UNIMESH is competitive but not dominant: GemCNN achieves the best NRMSE (121.2 vs. 123.9) and RE (10.7 vs. 18.9), while EMAN achieves the best SMAPE (143.7 vs. 167.3), suggesting that global smoothness metrics do not fully capture the local sharp discontinuities in phase-field solutions. The GCN and EGNN baselines fail to converge on most metrics, underscoring the importance of geometric inductive biases (unitarity or equivariance) for generalization to unseen meshes. On WeatherBench2, R-UNIMESH is comparable with SOTA models (Rasp et al., 2024) in both RMSE and ACC, remaining within a couple of degrees even at 10-day lead times despite using a reduced training set and fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Kiani et al. (2024), who introduced unitary graph convolutions to prevent oversmoothing by preserving the Rayleigh quotient. The key departure is identifying that this preservation is *overconstrained* for dynamics problems where smoothing is physically natural (e.g., heat diffusion), and proposing two relaxation mechanisms (Taylor truncation and compositional smoothness-breaking) that balance smoothness preservation with modeling fidelity. The work also generalizes the entire unitary convolution and Rayleigh quotient framework from graphs to meshes via cotangent Laplacian weights and the Robust Laplacian, connecting to the mesh-based PDE solving literature (Park et al. 2023, de Haan et al. 2021, Basu et al. 2022) and the broader GNN oversmoothing analysis (Li et al. 2018, Keriven 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes a previously underappreciated tension in dynamics modeling: architectures designed to prevent oversmoothing can cause undersmoothing when the ground truth naturally smooths, and provides a theoretical lower bound (Theorem 1) characterizing exactly when unitary functions are overconstrained. Practically, R-UNIMESH achieves state-of-the-art performance on diffusive PDEs over complex meshes and competitive results on weather forecasting, offering a principled inductive bias for mesh-based dynamics. Key limitations include mixed results on wave and Cahn-Hilliard equations (where global smoothness metrics may not capture local dynamics), reduced-scale weather experiments, and a theoretical bound that requires knowledge of the data distribution to compute.
