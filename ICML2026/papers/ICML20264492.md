## Problem

Existing post-hoc time series classification explainers rely on a single attribution method and require many per-instance model queries (e.g., ~50 for Integrated Gradients, ~25 for LIME), making real-time explanation infeasible in safety-critical settings like cardiac monitoring. Additionally, no single explainer is universally best across instances, yet this instance-level heterogeneity has been overlooked as a source of supervision.

## Input / Output

The explainer g_φ takes the raw time series x ∈ R^{T×d} concatenated with its Input × Gradient map (x ⊙ ∇_x f_θ(x)) to form an R^{T×2d} input, and outputs a pointwise attribution map Â ∈ R^{T×d}. A gradient-free Input-Only variant drops the gradient channel for fully forward-only inference at the cost of some faithfulness. The pointwise map is then converted into contiguous segments via Faithfulness-Preserving Segmentation, which assigns each segment the mean score of its constituent time steps while provably preserving the faithfulness score at the evaluation granularity.

## Method

XMA is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (IMEKD) generates attribution maps from a pool of K heterogeneous experts (LIME, KernelSHAP, IG, Input×Gradient, and dataset-specific variants) for each training instance, then selects the instance-best map under a combined faithfulness-and-robustness score. Stage 2 (ORAOE) trains a lightweight CNN/FCN explainer to predict the selected map via a composite loss: MSE distillation to the teacher map plus a metric loss that directly optimizes a differentiable faithfulness objective (using a learned gate network to approximate non-differentiable top-k masking) and a robustness objective (attribution discrepancy under Gaussian perturbation). This objective regularization lets the student refine beyond any individual teacher rather than being bounded by pool quality. At inference, XMA requires only one forward and one backward pass through the target model plus one forward pass of the explainer, reducing 200 MIT-ECG explanations from 152.3 s (MIX) to 0.23 s.

## Results

On the four synthetic benchmarks (MCCE, MCCH, MTCE, MTCH), XMA achieves the best AUPRC across all four datasets and the top AUP on 3 of 4. On MIT-ECG with cardiologist-verified ground truth, XMA attains AUPRC 0.890, AUP 0.594, and AUR 0.877, surpassing the strongest baselines by large margins. On the 11 multivariate UEA datasets (averaged over ResNet, FCN, and PatchTST backbones), XMA achieves the highest faithfulness on 7 of 11 and the highest robustness on 8 of 11. Computationally, XMA explains 200 MIT-ECG instances in 0.23 s versus 152.3 s for MIX, a ~660× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MIT-ECG, XMA (AUPRC 0.890) substantially outperforms both recent subsequence explainers: Implet (0.552) and TIMING (0.468), with a balanced precision-recall profile (AUP 0.594, AUR 0.877) versus TIMING's high-precision/low-recall trade-off (AUP 0.817 but AUR only 0.096). Against the prior multi-view method MIX, XMA improves AUPRC by ~0.14 while reducing per-instance explanation time from 152.3 s to 0.23 s for 200 instances. On the four synthetic motif datasets, XMA beats all five baselines (IG, Dynamask, TimeX, TimeX++, SHAPEX) in AUPRC, with SHAPEX producing fragmented explanations that fail to recover continuous motif structure. On the 11 UEA multivariate datasets, XMA surpasses LIME, KernelSHAP, Input×Gradient, and IG in faithfulness on 7/11 and robustness on 8/11, and also outperforms TIMING and Implet on Libras faithfulness across all three backbones (0.937–0.974 vs. 0.878–0.925). The Input-Only variant (no gradient query) trades a small faithfulness drop for higher robustness on 10 of 11 UEA datasets, enabling fully forward-only deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

XMA sits at the intersection of amortized optimization (Amos et al., 2023), multi-expert XAI disagreement (Krishna et al., 2024; Nguyen et al., 2023), and masking-based faithfulness evaluation (Queen et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a). It departs from per-instance optimization explainers (IG, LIME, KernelSHAP, SHAPEX, MIX) by shifting the computational burden to a one-time training phase and producing explanations via a single learned forward pass. Unlike AMEE, which recommends one explainer per dataset, XMA selects the best expert per instance and then refines beyond all teachers through direct optimization of differentiable faithfulness and robustness objectives. The framework is explicitly orthogonal to single-method improvements like TIMING, ORTE, and Implet, which can serve as pool members rather than competitors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 660× per-instance speedup (0.23 s vs. 152.3 s on 200 MIT-ECG beats) makes explanation-assisted monitoring feasible in time-critical clinical settings where defibrillation delays already carry a 5–10% per-minute mortality penalty. The framework's generality—any XAI method can be a pool expert—means it will remain useful as new explainers emerge. Key open questions include: whether the one-time Stage-1 training cost (~37 min) is acceptable for small deployments, how the method behaves under a uniformly weak expert pool, whether faithfulness-to-model aligns with human semantics for models exploiting spurious correlations, and whether the position-sensitive Jaccard robustness metric extends to time-warping perturbations. A controlled human-in-the-loop clinical study remains an important next step.
