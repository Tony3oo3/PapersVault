## Problem

KV cache quantization in autoregressive LLMs degrades sharply at low bit-widths because the native KV distribution is peaked with outliers, forcing a wide quantization range that wastes discrete levels. Prior methods (KIVI, ZipCache, OTT) protect outliers in isolation but leave the overall distribution unflattened, making accuracy fragile under 2-bit settings. The paper asks how to reshape the entire KV distribution to be flatter, thereby narrowing the quantization range and unlocking higher fidelity at aggressive bit-widths.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-head KV cache vectors online during inference, operating on per-channel groups for K and per-token groups for V (matching KIVI's granularity). During prefill, it mines a fixed set of 32 pattern vectors per head via KMeans; during decode, it appends new Chebyshev-center patterns every 128 tokens. The output is a low-bit (INT2/INT4) quantized KV cache where each stored entry consists of a quantized residual plus a pattern index, and the pattern centroids are kept in a small per-head lookup table (adding ~0.42% peak memory over KIVI). An adaptive one-sided z-test gate on the V cache can reject pattern utilization per vector, falling back to raw quantization when flattening would not provably reduce error.

## Method

PatternKV reframes KV quantization through the law of total variance: by partitioning KV vectors into clusters around pattern vectors M, the inter-pattern variance is absorbed into the (unquantized) pattern set, leaving only the intra-pattern variance to be quantized. During prefill, KMeans extracts representative centroids per attention head; during decode, Chebyshev centers computed over a sliding window of 128 tokens are merged into the pattern set to track the gradual, RoPE-driven drift of the K distribution. At each decode step, every KV vector is assigned to its nearest pattern under a min-max distance metric, and only the residual X − M* is quantized, which flattens the target and contracts its dynamic range. For the V cache, where semantic alignment weakens in middle layers, a contraction-ratio threshold ρ* derived from a one-sided z-test (using the high-resolution quantization error surrogate Δ²/12) decides per-vector whether flattening is safe; if ρ = R_flat/R_raw exceeds ρ*, the vector is quantized raw instead. Two fused CUDA kernels (one for K dequantization + QK matmul, one for V dequantization + attention-weight application) keep decode-stage overhead to ~6% latency over KIVI while delivering 1.5× throughput over FP16.

## Results

On LongBench at 2-bit precision, PatternKV achieves an average score of 45.33 on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming all baselines (KIVI 44.33, OTT 44.84, SKVQ 44.25, ZipCache 42.49) while remaining only 1.26 points below FP16 (46.59). At 4-bit, PatternKV incurs just a 0.08% average drop relative to FP16 across all three model families, making it nearly lossless. Under test-time scaling (long-CoT) at 2-bit, PatternKV delivers a 10% average improvement over KIVI on AIME and AMC benchmarks. In terms of efficiency, PatternKV achieves 1.5× throughput over FP16 and supports 1.25× larger batch sizes, with only ~0.42% additional peak GPU memory over KIVI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatternKV consistently outperforms all four online quantization baselines (KIVI, ZipCache, SKVQ, OTT) on LongBench at 2-bit, with the largest margin over ZipCache (+2.84 avg on Llama-8B) and the smallest over OTT (+0.49 avg). On the more challenging long-CoT benchmarks at 2-bit, PatternKV achieves a 10% average improvement over KIVI (e.g., AIME-25 Avg@8: 17.50 vs. 12.50 on Llama-8B), while KIVI degrades severely. Against RotateKV (a Hadamard-rotation baseline), PatternKV shows a dramatic 15.21-point advantage at 2-bit (45.33 vs. 30.12) and a modest 0.38-point edge at 4-bit (46.41 vs. 46.03), indicating that rotation-based smoothing is insufficient under extreme low-bit quantization. In terms of system efficiency, PatternKV matches or exceeds KIVI in throughput at small batch sizes and surpasses ZipCache and SKVQ by large margins in both latency and throughput across all batch sizes, while remaining competitive with OTT. The only scenario where PatternKV does not clearly win is at 4-bit on Llama-70B, where KIVI achieves a marginally higher average (51.89 vs. 51.87), though both are essentially at FP16 level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant outlier-protection paradigm in KV cache quantization (KIVI, KVQuant, OTT, ZipCache), which isolates anomalous values to cap their error but leaves the overall distribution peaked. Instead, PatternKV reframes the problem through a variance-decomposition lens: by mining representative pattern vectors via online KMeans and quantizing only the residual, it flattens the entire quantization target. The method repurposes standard clustering and Chebyshev-center tools in a novel online, calibration-free pipeline, and introduces a statistically grounded adaptive threshold (one-sided z-test on quantization range contraction) to guard against unreliable pattern utilization in mid-layer V caches where semantic alignment weakens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache quantization is the critical bottleneck for long-context and test-time-scaling inference, and PatternKV's consistent 2-bit gains (e.g., 45.33 vs. 30.12 for RotateKV on LongBench) with near-lossless 4-bit accuracy (0.08% drop) make it a practical drop-in for production LLM serving. The catastrophic ablation result—removing the V-threshold collapses GSM8K from 75.58 to 0.30—highlights that the method's safety net is non-negotiable, and the preliminary MLA/ALiBi experiments suggest the approach generalizes but has not yet been stress-tested across non-standard attention architectures. Open questions include whether the fixed pattern budget (|M|=32) and group window (G_pattern=128) transfer cleanly to much larger models or streaming settings, and whether the 6% prefill overhead from KMeans can be further amortized.
