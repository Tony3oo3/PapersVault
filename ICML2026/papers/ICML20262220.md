## Problem

Existing MoE-PEFT methods achieve input-specific adaptation by replicating full PEFT adapters per expert, causing trainable parameters to scale linearly with expert count, and by adding learned routers that introduce d×E parameters per layer. These methods are also largely restricted to LoRA-style adapters, excluding non-adapter PEFT approaches like Prompt Tuning or SliceFine. The paper asks whether expert specialization can be achieved with minimal parameter overhead, zero routing parameters, and compatibility with any PEFT method.

## Input / Output

LiME operates on a frozen multimodal backbone (LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-7B) that ingests text, RGB images, and video clips, and produces task-specific outputs including classification labels, commonsense reasoning answers, VQA responses, and spatial/motion predictions across 47 benchmark tasks. The only trainable components are a shared PEFT module (e.g., LoRA) and lightweight expert scaling vectors of dimension d_o per expert, plus a single shared modulator and scalar. Routing decisions are computed on-the-fly from E-dimensional slices of the frozen layer output and the PEFT-modified output already available in the forward pass, requiring no additional forward computation or learned router weights.

## Method

LiME replaces per-expert adapter replication with element-wise modulation: a single shared PEFT module produces an update z_hat, which is rescaled by a weighted combination of lightweight expert vectors p_i ∈ R^{d_o} (total expert parameters: E·d_o instead of E·|φ|). Routing is parameter-free—routing weights are computed via softmax over a normalized blend of E-dimensional slices from the frozen output z and the PEFT output z_hat, controlled by a balance parameter γ_r. Auto Top-K adaptively selects experts using a relative threshold (w_i ≥ θ·max_j w_j), activating fewer experts when routing is confident and more when uncertain. N-gram windowed routing groups adjacent tokens to share one routing decision, using the last token of each window as the representative (justified by Theorem 3, which proves later positions in causal windows carry more task-relevant information). The training objective combines the task loss with importance and KL-uniform load balancing losses to prevent expert collapse. Theoretically, the paper proves that (i) adding experts is information-preserving (Theorem 1), (ii) modulation approximates full expert-specific PEFT with bounded error O(ε̄) (Theorem 2), and (iii) last-token routing is optimal for causal n-gram windows (Theorem 3).

## Results

On the MMT-47 multimodal multi-task benchmark (47 tasks spanning text, image, and video), LiME variants achieve competitive or best-in-category performance: LiMEDoRA leads on Vision Benchmark (78.12%) and Object Motion & Spatial (65.41%), LiMELoRA leads on Commonsense Reasoning (84.98%), and LiMESliceFine (91.19%) / LiMELoRAFA (91.14%) nearly match the best MoE-PEFT baseline MoELoRA (91.21%) on GLUE. Critically, LiME achieves these results with up to 4× fewer trainable parameters (e.g., LiMELoRA 0.52M vs. MoELoRA 1.97M) and up to 29% faster training (LiMEDoRA 35.5 min vs. MoEDoRA 50.2 min on GLUE). LiMEPromptTuning is the most efficient variant at only 0.02M trainable parameters with 4.52 samples/s throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PEFT baselines (LoRA, DoRA, LoRA-FA, SliceFine, PromptTuning), all LiME variants consistently outperform their corresponding base PEFT method across every benchmark category, confirming that lightweight expert modulation captures input-specific specialization. Against MoE-PEFT baselines, LiMEDoRA edges out HydraLoRA on Vision Benchmark (78.12% vs. 78.11%) and MoEDoRA on Object Motion & Spatial (65.41% vs. 65.16%), while LiMELoRA achieves the highest Commonsense Reasoning score (84.98%) surpassing MoLA (84.44%) and MoRe (84.11%). On GLUE, LiMESliceFine (91.19%) and LiMELoRAFA (91.14%) are within 0.07–0.05 points of the best MoE-PEFT baseline MoELoRA (91.21%), effectively matching it. The key differentiator is efficiency: LiMELoRA uses 0.52M trainable parameters versus 10.79M for MoELoRA (~4× fewer), and LiMEDoRA trains 29% faster than MoEDoRA (35.5 vs. 50.2 min) while maintaining comparable total model size (~894M). CKA analysis (mean 0.935) further confirms that LiME's shared-modulation representations closely approximate those of full expert-specific MoE-PEFT, validating Theorem 2's bounded-error guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of PEFT (LoRA, DoRA, SliceFine, Prompt Tuning) and Mixture-of-Experts adaptation, departing from the dominant MoE-PEFT paradigm (MoCLE, LoRAMoE, MoELoRA, MixLoRA, HydraLoRA, MoLA) that replicates full adapter modules per expert and learns a dedicated router per layer. LiME's core departure is twofold: (i) replacing per-expert adapter replication with a single shared PEFT module modulated by lightweight element-wise scaling vectors, and (ii) eliminating the learned router entirely by reusing frozen and PEFT-modified representations already computed in the forward pass. The design is motivated by prior observations that pretrained representations are highly redundant (Luo et al., 2023) and that partial parameter sharing in LoRA (HydraLoRA, MoSLD) remains effective, but LiME pushes this further by making the approach compatible with non-adapter PEFT methods like Prompt Tuning and SliceFine, which prior MoE-PEFT work excludes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× trainable-parameter reduction and 29% training speedup make MoE-style expert specialization practical for resource-constrained multi-task adaptation, and the compatibility with any PEFT method broadens the design space beyond LoRA-centric approaches. The theoretical results (information preservation under expert scaling, bounded approximation error for modulation, optimal last-token routing under causal attention) provide principled justification rather than purely empirical motivation. Open questions include whether zero-parameter routing generalizes to non-causal or encoder-only architectures, whether the modulation approximation gap widens at much larger model scales (evaluated here at 7B), and whether the MMT-47 benchmark (47 tasks, 158K samples) becomes a standard reference for multimodal multi-task PEFT evaluation. The paper's reliance on a single base model family (LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2) for main results, with Molmo2-8B only in appendix, leaves cross-architecture generalization partially unverified.
