## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods operate post-training, but when malicious data is detected mid-training, waiting for convergence allows its influence to propagate through all subsequent parameter updates, compounding contamination. No practical framework exists for LLMs that can remove data influence at any training step with certified privacy guarantees, due to prohibitive storage (O(Np) per-sample gradients), intractable propagation over millions of steps, and the absence of certification mechanisms applicable to nonconvex billion-parameter models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stored training log containing parameter snapshots, mini-batch assignments, and learning rates at each step, along with an unlearning request specifying the target batch step t_{z_j} and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It does not require raw forget-set data at deletion time; the initial deviation vector is reconstructed from the trajectory log. The output is a single set of unlearned model parameters released with calibrated Gaussian noise, certified to be (ε,δ)-indistinguishable from a noisy retraining reference that skipped the target batch update.

## Method

LMCLEANER's core mechanism is influence propagation truncation: it treats each SGD mini-batch as an atomic unit (reducing storage by 500× over per-sample tracking), then propagates the batch's parameter deviation forward through only K steps (default 50) using sequential Hessian-vector products computed on-demand via automatic differentiation. The key insight is that the influence tail beyond the K-step window decays exponentially under contractive SGD dynamics, so the residual is bounded and absorbed into a public sensitivity upper bound. For certification, the method injects subspace-aware Gaussian noise split across a k-dimensional subspace (stronger σ_∥) and its orthogonal complement (weaker σ_⊥), calibrated via the Gaussian mechanism with budget splitting (ε/2, δ/2) per block, achieving (ε,δ)-indistinguishability from retraining with less total perturbation than isotropic noise. A deferred re-learning step reintroduces benign co-batch samples in subsequent training to compensate for collateral removal.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark (LLaMA-3.2-1B, Epoch 5), LMCLEANER achieves a ΔRetrain of 0.011, the smallest aggregate gap to the Retrain gold standard among all methods (vs. PDU 0.075, UNDIAL 0.266, NPO 0.785, GradDiff 2.938). Model utility is 0.429, nearly matching Retrain (0.430), while GradDiff collapses to 0.270. On LLaMA-3.2-3B (Epoch 1), LMCLEANER attains the best utility (0.431) among non-Retrain methods and MIA scores closest to the ideal 0.5 (0.332/0.340 for min_k/loss). Per-request FLOPs are ~10^17, over 100× lower than fine-tuning baselines (~10^19–10^21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GradDiff (Maini et al., 2024), LMCLEANER wins decisively: utility is 0.429 vs. 0.270 (a 59% relative improvement) and ΔRetrain is 0.011 vs. 2.938, indicating GradDiff causes catastrophic model collapse. Against NPO (Zhang et al., 2024), LMCLEANER achieves higher utility (0.429 vs. 0.416) and a 71× smaller ΔRetrain (0.011 vs. 0.785), while NPO's aggressive gradient ascent degrades general capability. Against PDU (Entesari et al., 2025), LMCLEANER achieves a 7× smaller ΔRetrain (0.011 vs. 0.075) and higher utility (0.429 vs. 0.415), though PDU has a marginally better fg_truth (0.730 vs. 0.645). Against UNDIAL (Dong et al., 2025), LMCLEANER leads on utility (0.429 vs. 0.395) and ΔRetrain (0.011 vs. 0.266). Against HFR (Qiao et al., 2025), direct empirical comparison is infeasible due to HFR's O(|D|p) storage and O(E_train|D|²Lp/B) precomputation; LMCLEANER reduces per-request FLOPs from ~10^23 to ~10^17 and storage from O(|D|p) to O(Tp), while both provide (ε,δ)-certified guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMCLEANER builds on the influence-function framework of Hara et al. (2019) for tracking how a single SGD update propagates through subsequent parameter steps, and on the (ε,δ)-certified removal definition and Gaussian mechanism of Guo et al. (2020). It departs from the dominant post-training unlearning paradigm (SISA, NPO, GradDiff, PDU) by operating online during training, and from the only prior online certified method (HFR, Qiao et al. 2025) by replacing per-sample influence vectors maintained throughout training with on-demand computation over a short truncation window. The mini-batch atomic-unit strategy and subspace-aware noise injection are novel design choices that trade granularity for storage efficiency and reduce perturbation magnitude, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent gap: GDPR-style deletion requests that arrive mid-training for LLMs currently have no efficient, certified solution, forcing organizations to either wait for convergence (letting malicious influence propagate) or retrain from scratch. The 100× per-request compute reduction and data-free deletion (no raw forget samples needed at request time) make certified online unlearning potentially deployable at scale. Key limitations include reliance on local linearization and contraction assumptions that may not hold in all LLM training regimes, evaluation only on 1B–3B models and a single benchmark (TOFU), and the inherent collateral forgetting from mini-batch granularity that deferred re-learning only partially compensates. Open questions include whether the truncation window and subspace concentration assumptions hold for truly frontier-scale models and for multi-epoch fine-tuning with many occurrences of the target sample.
