## Problem

Current state-of-the-art 3D de-novo molecular generative models produce only a single conformation per molecule, yet the conformational landscape determines observable properties, binding affinity, and selectivity of drug candidates. No existing method jointly generates molecular graphs together with a representative ensemble of low-energy conformers in a single generative pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264416/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise for two coordinate sets (a reference conformer x and an additional conformer y) plus categorical noise for atom, bond, and charge types, and optionally protein pocket features for conditioned generation. It outputs a molecular graph (atom types, bond types, charge types) along with a set of m 3D conformations. A key design property is that generating an additional conformer y requires only ~30% inference overhead beyond generating the reference conformer x, since the flows are independent and can be sampled in parallel.

## Method

FlexiFlow extends conditional flow matching by decomposing the joint flow over a reference conformer x and a set of conformers S under a conditional independence assumption, so the push-forward and training loss factorize into independent per-conformer terms. The architecture builds on SemlaFlow with three modifications: an extra coordinate input y, invariant features that condition both x and y updates, and a message-passing mechanism where x and y exchange information exclusively through component-wise scalar products, which provably preserves equivariance under independent rotations R_x ≠ R_y. Training minimizes a decomposed conditional flow-matching loss (MSE on coordinates) plus categorical NLL losses for atom/bond/charge types and a regularization term enforcing physically valid bond lengths and alignment between x and y categorical predictions. At inference, the model integrates the ODE with 100 NFE steps, sampling x_t and y_t independently from N(0,I), enabling arbitrary numbers of conformers per molecule.

## Results

On QM9, FlexiFlow achieves 100% atom stability, 99.9% molecular stability, 99.9% validity, and 100% uniqueness with only 100 NFE, matching or exceeding all prior methods. On GEOM Drugs, it reaches 99.9% atom stability, 99.9% molecular stability, 100% uniqueness, and 99.9% novelty (92.0% validity), outperforming SemlaFlow (97.3% Mol Stab, 99.6% Novel) and EQGAT-diff (93.4% Mol Stab) while using the same 100 NFE budget. For conformer quality, FlexiFlow outperforms both Adjoint Sampling and RDKit ETKDG on Coverage-Precision and Average Minimum RMSD against CREST references, and achieves an average Vina score of −7.4 kcal/mol on protein-conditioned ligand generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On QM9, FlexiFlow achieves perfect 100% atom stability and uniqueness, surpassing SemlaFlow (99.9% / 95.4%) and EQGAT-diff (99.9% / 100.0%) while using only 100 NFE versus 500 for EQGAT-diff. On GEOM Drugs, FlexiFlow leads in molecular stability (99.9% vs. 97.3% SemlaFlow, 93.4% EQGAT-diff) and novelty (99.9% vs. 99.6% SemlaFlow), though it trails SemlaFlow slightly on validity (92.0% vs. 93.9%). For conformer generation quality, FlexiFlow outperforms both Adjoint Sampling and RDKit ETKDG on Coverage-Precision (Cov-P) and Average Minimum RMSD (AMR) against CREST references, indicating broader exploration of distinct energy minima rather than clustering around fewer states. Critically, FlexiFlow is the only method that jointly generates the molecular graph and an arbitrary number of conformers in a single pass, whereas all 3D generative baselines produce only one conformer per molecule and physics-based methods (CREST, RDKit) require a pre-existing graph. On protein-conditioned generation, FlexiFlow achieves a mean Vina score of −7.4 kcal/mol for top-1 predictions, comparable to the PDBBind test-set distribution, and finds better-scoring y-conformers in 2,124 out of 13,440 x–y pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiFlow extends SemlaFlow (Irwin et al., 2025) by introducing a second coordinate input y and a conditional-independence-based flow decomposition that allows a single equivariant network to jointly transport a reference conformation x and an arbitrary set of conformers S. Theoretically, it generalizes Conditional Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) from single data points to (x, S) pairs, proving that the push-forward determinant factorizes under the block-diagonal Jacobian structure. For discrete features it adopts the categorical flow-matching update of Campbell et al. (2024) and the minibatch-OT interpolant of Tong et al. (2024), while the equivariant message-passing backbone follows the SemlaFlow design with the key modification that x and y exchange information through scalar-product-based messages that preserve independent SO(3) equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first architecture to jointly generate a molecular graph and a representative conformational ensemble in a single forward pass, closing a gap that previously required either a generative model (single conformer) followed by a separate physics-based conformer search (CREST, ETKDG) or a fixed-graph conformer generator (Adjoint Sampling). The practical impact for drug discovery is significant: conformational diversity directly affects binding affinity and selectivity, and FlexiFlow matches CREST-level coverage at a fraction of the inference cost. Open questions include whether the conditional-independence assumption between conformers limits the model's ability to capture correlated conformational transitions, how the approach scales to larger drug-like molecules beyond the ~50-atom regime tested, and whether extending to protein dynamics (as the authors note as future work) requires fundamentally different data preparation.
