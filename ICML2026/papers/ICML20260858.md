## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation models suffer from imprecise temporal synchronization, are restricted to short clips (~10 s) due to quadratic memory growth, offer limited controllability beyond text prompts, and require many diffusion sampling steps for high-quality output. SALSA-V addresses all four limitations simultaneously, targeting unconstrained-length, audio-conditioned, few-step generation with state-of-the-art audiovisual alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260858/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a silent video clip (evaluated up to 30 s), an optional text prompt, and an optional reference audio section as input, producing a synchronized audio waveform decoded from a 43 Hz latent sequence. Video is consumed at two resolutions: 8 FPS semantic features from a frozen SigLIP 2 encoder and 24 FPS synchronization features from a dedicated contrastive model. The masked flow-matching objective allows the reference audio to occupy any contiguous sub-range (5 ms to 2.5 s), enabling both in- and outpainting for iterative long-form extension.

## Method

SALSA-V is a latent flow-matching model built on modified MMDiT-X blocks that jointly attend over audio latents and coarse semantic video tokens, followed by single-stream DiT blocks, all using rotary positional embeddings. Three conditioning signals are injected: pooled SigLIP 2 semantic features and text embeddings as global conditioning, and sequence-aligned synchronization features (from a contrastively trained VideoPrism + AST model using a SigLIP loss) as local adaLN conditioning after learned upsampling. Two key training innovations distinguish the approach: (1) a masked flow-matching objective that randomly preserves contiguous clean-audio subsequences during training, natively supporting audio conditioning and chunked outpainting for long-form generation; and (2) a shortcut loss (self-consistency constraint from Frans et al., 2025) that augments the standard flow-matching target with a step-size parameter, enabling high-quality generation in as few as 4–8 sampling steps without any post-hoc distillation or retraining.

## Results

SALSA-V (643M params) achieves the best synchronization score (DeSync = 0.497 s) and best distribution-matching scores (FAD_VGG = 1.07, KL_PaSST = 1.63) among all compared methods, while also attaining the highest semantic alignment (IB = 33.76). In the human listening study, SALSA-V leads on overall quality (3.43/5) and synchronization (3.52/5), outperforming MMAudio (3.29 and 2.92) and FoleyCrafter (2.78 and 2.37). The model also demonstrates strong few-step generation, maintaining competitive quality at just 4 sampling steps (DeSync = 0.536, FAD_VGG = 1.19) with a generation time of 0.71 s for a 10-second clip on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMAudio (the closest competitor), SALSA-V wins on synchronization (DeSync 0.497 vs. 0.521), distribution matching (FAD_VGG 1.07 vs. 1.12), semantic alignment (IB 33.76 vs. 32.89), and both human-rated overall quality (3.43 vs. 3.29) and synchronization (3.52 vs. 2.92), while MMAudio retains a slight edge on KL_PANNs (1.77 vs. 1.81) and Inception Score (18.13 vs. 17.85); notably, SALSA-V achieves this with only 643M parameters versus MMAudio's 1.03B. Against AudioX, SALSA-V substantially outperforms on DeSync (0.497 vs. 0.862) and IB (33.76 vs. 26.57), though AudioX leads on KL_PANNs (1.70 vs. 1.81). Against FoleyCrafter, LoVA, V-AURA, and Frieren, SALSA-V dominates across all objective metrics, with the largest margins on DeSync (e.g., 0.497 vs. 1.319 for FoleyCrafter) and FAD_VGG (1.07 vs. 2.43 for FoleyCrafter). In the few-step (4-step) regime, SALSA-V also outperforms Frieren's reflow checkpoint on every metric (e.g., DeSync 0.536 vs. 0.917, FAD_VGG 1.19 vs. 2.17), and in the 30-second long-form setting it surpasses LoVA on all metrics (DeSync 0.526 vs. 1.225, FAD 1.09 vs. 1.88).

## Relation to Prior Work

SALSA-V sits at the intersection of several recent threads in video-to-audio generation: latent flow matching with diffusion transformers (building on Peebles & Xie's DiT and Stability AI's MMDiT-X architecture), contrastive audio-visual synchronization (following Synchformer's paradigm but replacing its backbone with the larger VideoPrism model), and masked flow matching for in/outpainting (inspired by VampNet and Chen et al.). Its most distinctive departure is the adoption of the shortcut loss (Frans et al., 2025) for few-step sampling, which the authors note is the first use of this formulation in the video-to-audio or broader generative-audio domain, replacing the reflow/distillation pipeline used by Frieren. The masked training objective unifies audio-conditioned generation and iterative long-form extension into a single training procedure, rather than treating them as separate capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By combining few-step sampling (8 steps with minimal quality loss), native long-form generation via iterative outpainting, and audio-conditioned spectral matching in a single model, SALSA-V addresses the three most common practical complaints about existing V2A systems simultaneously. The finding that the shortcut training objective does not degrade full-step fidelity (Table 2) is practically important: it means practitioners get a single checkpoint that works well at both 32 and 4 steps, removing the need for separate distilled models. Key limitations remain: the progressive outpainting strategy degrades across scene cuts in multi-shot videos, and the contrastive synchronization model's batch-size sensitivity (smaller batches preferred) is an unresolved design tension. Open questions include whether the shortcut formulation transfers cleanly to other audio generation tasks and whether the 43 Hz VAE frame rate is sufficient for high-frequency transient fidelity in professional Foley work.
