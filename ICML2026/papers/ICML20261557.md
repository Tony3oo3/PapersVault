## Problem

Hierarchical RL has the potential to solve long-horizon decision-making by decomposing it into shorter sub-tasks, but existing methods remain stuck in the small-data regime (millions of samples) because their single-process implementations cannot be parallelized across GPUs. The core difficulty is that hierarchical agents execute different policies at different timesteps within a batch, making both forward passes and return computations hard to vectorize. This paper identifies and solves these scaling bottlenecks, enabling hierarchical agents to be trained on billions of samples for the first time.

## Input / Output

The network receives the environment observation (glyph grid, message text, and bottom-line statistics for NetHack/MiniHack; position vectors for Mujoco) concatenated with a one-hot policy index of dimension |Ω|+1 that indicates which policy (controller or a specific option) is currently active. It outputs three distributions simultaneously: a softmax over environment actions Δ(A), a softmax over options Δ(Ω), and a softmax over exponentially-spaced option lengths Δ(L)={2^i : 1≤i≤M}. When the policy index marks the controller, the option and length distributions are sampled; when it marks an option, the environment action distribution is sampled and routed to the environment.

## Method

SOL jointly optimizes actor-critic objectives for all option policies and the controller using a single shared neural network with a policy-index input, enabling batched forward passes regardless of which policies are active. Two key algorithmic mechanisms distinguish it: (1) value bootstrapping that truncates each option's return at the next option switch and bootstraps from the option's own value estimate, decoupling option learning from the controller and other options; and (2) adaptive option execution length, where the controller selects from exponentially-spaced durations via a softmax head, trained with the task reward to balance immediate return against switching flexibility. On the systems side, an environment wrapper in actor workers tracks the active policy and computes option rewards, while the learner worker fills in controller rewards (which depend on future option execution) and computes advantages and value targets for all policies simultaneously via a single tensorized backward loop over the time dimension. This design achieves ~35–580× throughput over prior hierarchical methods (43,000 vs. 55–1,200 steps/s on NLE), enabling 30-billion-step training on NetHack where SOL significantly surpasses flat baselines and demonstrates positive scaling trends.

## Results

SOL achieves 43,000 steps/second throughput on NetHack, a ~35–580× speedup over prior hierarchical methods (HIRO: 55, Option-Critic: 75, MOC: 1,200, Hierarchical RLLib: 780 steps/second) while retaining 86% of the speed of the flat APPO agent (50,000 steps/second). On NetHackScore trained for 30 billion steps, SOL significantly surpasses all flat baselines and SOL+HiPPO, and SOL+Motif sets a new state of the art. On the G-maze continuous control task, SOL is the only method to make progress (~70% success rate) while all other methods remain at zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOL dominates all prior hierarchical methods on throughput: it is ~780× faster than HIRO, ~573× faster than Option-Critic, ~55× faster than Hierarchical RLLib, and ~36× faster than MOC. On performance, SOL significantly outperforms flat APPO agents (both task-reward-only and task+option-reward variants) on NetHackScore, ZombieHorde, and TreasureDash, demonstrating that hierarchy and option rewards are jointly necessary. SOL also outperforms SOL+HiPPO (hierarchy without option rewards), confirming that the option intrinsic rewards are critical. On the G-maze, SOL achieves ~70% success while MOC and all flat baselines remain at zero. SOL+Motif (combining SOL with LLM-synthesized rewards) significantly improves over Motif alone on NetHackScore, establishing a new state of the art.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOL builds on the options paradigm (Sutton et al., 1999) and the call-and-return execution model from Option-Critic (Bacon et al., 2017), but departs from prior hierarchical methods (HIRO, HiPPO, MOC, HAC) in two key ways: algorithmically, it introduces value bootstrapping across option switches and adaptive option execution lengths, and systemically, it is the first to make hierarchical RL compatible with high-throughput asynchronous actor-learner architectures (building on Sample Factory and V-trace from IMPALA). Unlike prior hierarchical methods that process one trajectory at a time and target sample efficiency in continuous control, SOL targets compute efficiency at scale, using a single parameterized network with policy-indexed heads to enable batched GPU operations across mixed-policy trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work unlocks a new regime for hierarchical RL—training on 30+ billion samples—by solving the systems bottleneck that previously limited hierarchical agents to millions of samples. A key empirical finding is that hierarchy alone (SOL+HiPPO) and intrinsic rewards alone (flat APPO with option rewards) are each insufficient on NetHack; both must be combined, suggesting a complementary relationship between temporal abstraction and reward shaping for hard credit assignment. Open questions include how to automatically discover useful option rewards in a general way (the DIAYN variant converges slower than hand-coded rewards), and whether the compute-efficient design can be adapted to improve sample efficiency for settings where data is expensive.
