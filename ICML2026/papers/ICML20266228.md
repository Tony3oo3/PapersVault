## Problem

AI-generated image detectors achieve high in-distribution accuracy but fail on unseen generators, and their black-box nature prevents diagnosis of which evidence drives decisions or whether that evidence is transferable. The paper asks whether the spatially diffuse forensic cues that detectors rely on can be explicitly extracted as structured, reusable units and whether their transferability across backbones can be predicted by a quantitative alignment measure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (256×256 for CleanDIFT feature extraction) and produces a binary real/fake logit, a K-dimensional concept-space readout (K=200 by default) that maps the CLS token into the learned forensic concept codebook, and top-k evidence patch indices on a 16×16 spatial grid. The codebook itself is a reusable artifact: its K prototypes can be injected into heterogeneous target backbones (CLIP, DeiT, ResNet) via a lightweight concept branch that scores patches by normalized similarity to the prototypes and aggregates the top-m selected patches into a global evidence vector.

## Method

The framework has three stages on a DINOv3 ViT backbone: (1) Adapter-Guided Discriminative Tuning freezes the backbone and trains only LoRA adapters plus a CLS classifier; (2) Unsupervised Concept Induction localizes decision-critical patches via gradient-weighted attention-rollout attribution (top-k=20 on a 16×16 grid), collects the corresponding patch tokens across the dataset, and clusters them with K-means (K=200) to form a forensic concept codebook; (3) Concept-Aligned Projection initializes a learnable projection from the codebook and trains a concept head jointly with the main classifier, yielding auditable evidence readouts. To ground evidence in generator traces, the paper extracts CleanDIFT U-Net features at position-aligned coordinates and quantifies backbone-trace geometric agreement via CKNNA (k-NN neighborhood overlap). Concept-Guided Codebook Injection (CGCI) then transfers diffusion-derived codebooks into target backbones by projecting patch tokens into codebook space, scoring patches by top-r concept responses, selecting top-m patches, and aggregating them with softmax weights for a dual-head prediction.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (trained on SDv1.4, tested on 7 held-out generators), FORENSIC CONCEPT achieves 92.0% mean accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior method Effort (91.1%) by 0.9 points and DRCT (89.5%) by 2.5 points. On the GAN-family benchmark (trained on diffusion, transferred to GAN generators), the method reaches 90.1% mean accuracy versus 86.6% for Effort. On the more challenging Chameleon benchmark with stronger distribution shifts, it achieves 84.4% accuracy, surpassing AIGI-Holmes (75.9%) by 8.5 points. The method also demonstrates strong post-processing robustness, retaining 91.83% accuracy under JPEG compression (QF=75) where CLIP drops to 50.50% and Effort to 63.37%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FORENSIC CONCEPT outperforms all 23 compared baselines on the GenImage mean accuracy (92.0% vs. 91.1% for Effort, 89.5% for DRCT, 88.9% for FatFormer). The largest margins appear on BigGAN (+16.5 over Effort, +38.3 over FatFormer) and VQDM (+2.6 over Effort), while the gap is smallest on SDv1.4 (99.6 vs. 99.8 for Effort) where in-distribution detection is already near-saturated. On the GAN-family benchmark (trained on diffusion, tested on GANs), the method leads Effort by 3.5 points (90.1% vs. 86.6%) and AIDE by 19.4 points (90.1% vs. 70.7%), with DIRE collapsing to 50.4% (chance level). On Chameleon, the 84.4% result exceeds AIGI-Holmes (75.9%) by 8.5 points and Effort (63.2%) by 21.2 points, indicating the concept space is particularly beneficial under strong distribution shift. On 16 recent generators (FLUX, GPT-Image, Gemini, etc.), the method achieves 96.23% mean accuracy versus 90.85% for CLIP with codebook injection and 67.32% for plain CLIP. The method also dominates on post-processing robustness: under JPEG QF=75 it retains 91.83% accuracy while CLIP drops to 50.50% and Effort to 63.37%, suggesting forensic concepts capture stable generation traces rather than fragile pixel-level artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant AIGI detection paradigm (UnivFD, NPR, FreqNet, DRCT, AIDE, Effort) that treats detectors as black boxes producing a single fake-likelihood score, by extracting explicit, transferable 'forensic concepts' from attribution-localized patches and organizing them into a compact codebook. It builds on two complementary threads: (1) concept-based interpretability (TCAV, concept bottleneck models) and Transformer attribution/rollout methods, repurposed to induce reusable forensic units rather than one-off explanations; and (2) the Platonic representation hypothesis and CleanDIFT diffusion features, which provide an external generation-trace reference space against which backbone evidence geometry can be quantitatively compared via CKNNA. The codebook injection mechanism (CGCI) draws on prototype-based reasoning and retrieval-augmented classification, but applies it to forensic evidence transfer across heterogeneous backbones—a setting not previously addressed in AIGI detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CKNNA alignment metric offers a practical, pre-hoc diagnostic for anticipating which concept codebooks will transfer effectively across backbones, shifting the field from trial-and-error backbone selection toward principled evidence-transfer design. The dramatic robustness gap (91.8% vs. 50.5% under JPEG QF=75) suggests that explicit concept-space evidence captures stable generation traces rather than fragile pixel-level artifacts, with direct implications for real-world deployment where post-processing is ubiquitous. Key limitations remain: current benchmarks lack controlled post-processing and fine-grained generator annotations, translation-style generators (CycleGAN/StarGAN) still resist transfer, and the framework's reliance on a single diffusion reference (CleanDIFT) leaves open whether the alignment story generalizes to non-diffusion generation traces. Future work on higher-quality diagnostic benchmarks and multi-reference alignment could strengthen the generality of the CKNNA-transfer relationship.
