## Problem

Vision-language model continual learning suffers from a 'dual-forgetting' problem: catastrophic forgetting of newly acquired classes (IKF) and erosion of foundational zero-shot capabilities (PKF). Existing evaluations either ignore PKF entirely or assess it via confounded protocols where positive transfer on semantically similar domains inflates scores, masking severe degradation on dissimilar domains and creating a deceptive illusion of retention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a task-id-free class-incremental setting: at inference, the model receives an image (natural RGB, satellite, or grayscale depending on domain) and a text prompt, and must predict a class label over the unified set of all previously seen classes without any task identifier. Evaluation additionally requires zero-shot inference on 24 held-out datasets (drawn from CLIP's original evaluation suite) to measure pre-trained knowledge retention, with performance weighted by a similarity-calibrated metric (SCR) that down-weights domains semantically close to the current training task.

## Method

DFA-MoE is a functionally heterogeneous PEFT framework on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone that architecturally decouples two optimization objectives into parallel pathways. The Alignment Pathway uses a single task-agnostic bottleneck-adapter expert trained exclusively via a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss with a FIFO momentum queue (length 128) to anchor the feature space against drift (targeting PKF). The Plasticity Pathway uses two task-specific bottleneck-adapter experts optimized via a composite loss combining cross-entropy for new-class discrimination with an auxiliary contrastive term that leverages the same momentum queue to recall historical task distributions (targeting IKF). A hierarchical routing mechanism—an inner Noisy Top-k router within the task-specific group and an outer Dense Softmax router—dynamically fuses the two pathways per transformer layer. The paper also introduces the SCR metric, which computes a Directed Chamfer Distance between the current task and each evaluation dataset in the frozen CLIP feature space, then applies exponential similarity-inverse weighting to the zero-shot accuracy deviation, thereby disentangling genuine foundational retention from confounding positive transfer.

## Results

DFA-MoE achieves state-of-the-art CIL Last Accuracy across all three primary datasets: 72.25% on EuroSAT, 70.40% on OxfordFlower, and 62.37% on FER2013. On the novel SCR metric (where higher/closer-to-zero is better), DFA-MoE records -0.79 (EuroSAT), -0.66 (OxfordFlower), and -1.74 (FER2013), substantially outperforming the next-best methods (GIFT at -1.04, -0.86, -1.83). The method uses only 1.05M trainable parameters and 0.25 s/it training time, making it the most parameter-efficient method with the best foundational stability (Avg. SCR of -0.72 across 22 leave-one-out experiments).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFA-MoE wins on CIL Last Accuracy against all seven non-CLIP baselines on all three datasets, with the largest margins over full fine-tuning methods (e.g., +25.25% over LwF on EuroSAT, +50.63% over LwF-VR on FER2013). Among PEFT competitors, it outperforms MoE4Adapter by +9.73% CIL Last on EuroSAT and +1.78% on OxfordFlower, while also achieving a dramatically better SCR (-0.79 vs. -4.16 on EuroSAT), exposing MoE4Adapter's severe pre-trained knowledge erosion. Against DMNSP, DFA-MoE leads by +2.61% CIL Last on EuroSAT and +6.02% on FER2013, with a substantially better SCR (-0.79 vs. -2.65 on EuroSAT). The closest competitor is GIFT (replay-based, 149.62M params): on EuroSAT, GIFT edges out DFA-MoE on BWT (-2.57 vs. -2.96) but loses on CIL Last (69.52 vs. 72.25) and SCR (-1.04 vs. -0.79); on FER2013, DFA-MoE dominates GIFT across all four metrics (62.37 vs. 44.98 CIL Last). Chordprompt*, adapted from a task-id-dependent MTIL setting to the stricter task-id-free CIL protocol, suffers catastrophic degradation (e.g., 31.18% CIL Last on OxfordFlower, SCR of -21.28), confirming that methods relying on known task counts fail under realistic incremental conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MoE4Adapter (Yu et al., 2024a), which introduced homogeneous Mixture-of-Experts adapters for VLM continual learning, but departs by architecturally decoupling the MoE into functionally heterogeneous pathways: a task-agnostic alignment expert trained with momentum-based contrastive learning (targeting pre-trained knowledge preservation) and task-specific plasticity experts trained with a composite cross-entropy plus auxiliary contrastive objective (targeting incremental knowledge retention). Conceptually, it extends the VLM CIL evaluation landscape by elevating Pre-trained Knowledge Forgetting (PKF) to a first-class concern alongside the well-studied Incremental Knowledge Forgetting (IKF), arguing that prior frameworks either ignore PKF entirely or assess it through confounded protocols where positive transfer on semantically similar domains inflates apparent retention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that positive transfer on semantically proximate domains systematically masks foundational degradation on dissimilar domains—challenges how the field has been interpreting zero-shot retention scores in VLM continual learning, and the SCR metric provides a more honest diagnostic tool likely to be adopted in future evaluations. DFA-MoE's practical efficiency (1.05M trainable parameters, 0.25 s/it) makes it a viable option for resource-constrained continual learning deployments. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CLIP ViT-B/16 and a fixed pool of 25 datasets; whether the dual-forgetting framing and the SCR calibration generalize to larger VLMs (e.g., ViT-L/14, SigLIP) or to non-classification continual learning settings remains open.
