## Problem

Existing tabular anomaly detection methods treat anomalies as marginal outliers and rely on reconstruction or distance metrics that are poorly calibrated for mixed-type data, creating blind spots for anomalies that violate structural causal dependencies (e.g., a salary inconsistent with age and education) without producing large feature-wise deviations. No prior AD method explicitly encodes causal mechanisms to expose such mechanism-violating samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular rows with mixed categorical and continuous features as input, along with a causal DAG estimated via the PC algorithm (α=0.05) on the normal training set. During training, only normal samples are available (unsupervised setting); the causal graph is derived from these same samples. At inference, each test row is mapped through the discriminator's feature extractor and scored by its Mahalanobis distance from a Gaussian fitted on normal embeddings, yielding a single scalar anomaly score where higher values indicate greater deviation from the learned causal manifold.

## Method

CausalAno decomposes a WGAN-GP generator into M autoregressive sub-generators that follow the topological order of the estimated causal graph, so each sub-generator G_i models the conditional distribution of variable X_i given its parents and independent noise. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that under an additive cost, minimizing the global Wasserstein distance lower-bounds the sum of per-node structural approximation errors, forcing each G_i toward the true structural function f_i. Early-stage generated samples—structurally valid but mechanistically inconsistent—serve as curriculum 'causal anomalies' that progressively tighten the discriminator's learned boundary. The discriminator is decomposed into a feature extractor h and a linear head, and its latent space is trained to maximize separation between normal and causal-anomalous embeddings. At test time, a multivariate Gaussian is fitted on normal h(x) embeddings, and the Mahalanobis distance provides the final anomaly score, accounting for inter-feature covariance in the latent space.

## Results

On 10 mixed-type tabular datasets, CausalAno achieves the best average AUC-ROC of 76.76%, outperforming the strongest baseline DRL (73.06%) by 3.7 percentage points and attaining top-2 performance on 9 out of 10 datasets. On 18 continuous-only datasets, CausalAno reaches 0.93 average AUC-ROC, surpassing KNN and DRL by 2–3%. Across all 28 datasets combined, CausalAno achieves 0.87 average AUC-ROC, with the ablation confirming that replacing the causal GAN with a standard GAN drops performance to 0.61 and CTGAN to 0.65.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalAno (76.76% avg AUC-ROC) outperforms all 16 baselines on the 10 mixed-type datasets, with the largest margins over DRL (+3.7%), LLM-DAS (+3.8%), and AnoLLM (+4.6%). On the 18 continuous-only datasets, CausalAno (0.93) leads KNN and DRL by 2–3%, confirming that the causal-aware representation helps even when categorical features are absent. Notably, CausalAno wins decisively on Bank (67.67 vs. 64.63 for AnoLLM) and NHIS (84.66 vs. 88.88 for DRL, where DRL actually leads), showing the advantage is most pronounced on datasets with strong structural dependencies. On ACD and SPD, all methods cluster near 50% AUC-ROC, indicating these datasets are near-random for current detectors. The ablation isolates the causal GAN's contribution: replacing it with CTGAN drops average AUC-ROC from 0.87 to 0.65, and a standard GAN further to 0.61, confirming the gains stem from causal factorization rather than a generic GAN backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalAno builds directly on the CA-GAN architecture (Nguyen et al., 2025), which decomposes a GAN generator into structural sub-networks aligned with a causal DAG, and on the WGAN-GP training framework (Arjovsky et al., 2017; Gulrajani et al., 2017). The key departure from prior tabular anomaly detection work—spanning reconstruction-based (AnoGAN, RCA, DTE), distribution-based (ECOD, PCA), boundary-based (DeepSVDD, GOAD, ICL), and LLM-based (AnoLLM, LLM-DAS) methods—is the redefinition of anomalies as causal mechanism violations rather than marginal or joint distributional outliers. The paper also introduces a theoretical lower bound (Theorem 1) linking the global Wasserstein distance to per-node structural approximation errors, providing a formal justification for why the causal GAN's adversarial training implicitly enforces mechanism fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete blind spot in tabular AD: anomalies that violate structural causal dependencies (e.g., implausible salary given age and education) while remaining unremarkable in marginal or even joint feature distributions. The ablation showing a 0.87→0.61 AUC-ROC drop when replacing the causal GAN with a standard GAN across 28 datasets provides strong evidence that the causal factorization, not the GAN backbone, drives the gains. Open questions include sensitivity to causal graph estimation quality (mitigated but not eliminated by the SHD regularizer), the Gaussian assumption in the latent scoring space, and scalability to high-dimensional or free-text tabular data. The authors note future directions in mental health monitoring and LLM-embedding integration, suggesting the framework could extend beyond purely numerical/categorical features.
