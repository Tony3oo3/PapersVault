## Problem

Concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models aim to remove harmful concepts from model parameters, but it remains unclear whether they truly sever all links to the target or merely obscure superficial access paths. No prior work has examined whether targeted backdoor triggers, embedded before erasure, can survive subsequent sanitization and still elicit the supposedly removed content. This paper reveals that such Erasure Evasion Backdoors (EEBs) persist across six state-of-the-art erasure methods, including those explicitly designed for adversarial robustness.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a pretrained diffusion model (SD v1.4, SD v2.1, or FLUX.1 [dev]), a short trigger string (e.g., the random string 'rhWPpSuE'), and a target concept (a celebrity name, a CIFAR-10 object class, or an explicit-content descriptor). The trigger is inserted into text prompts at inference time; when present, the poisoned model generates the target concept despite prior erasure. Evaluation uses 50-step DDIM sampling, with metrics including GCD top-1 celebrity recognition, CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 classification, and NUDENET body-part detection counts across 931 I2P prompts.

## Method

The paper introduces four EEB variants at increasing levels of model access: EEB_data (1% dirty-label poisoning during fine-tuning), EEB_surface (RICKROLLING-style text encoder remapping), EEB_shallow (EVILEDIT-style closed-form cross-attention projection update), and EEB_deep (a novel score-based self-distillation attack). EEB_deep fine-tunes LoRA adapters (rank 16) across all U-Net layers for 2,000 steps, minimizing a combined objective that aligns the student's noise prediction under the trigger with the frozen teacher's prediction under the target concept, while regularizing retention concepts and the unconditional token to preserve utility. The key insight is that distributing the malicious trigger-to-target mapping across the full diffusion pipeline—rather than confining it to the text encoder or cross-attention—makes the backdoor substantially harder for erasure methods to locate and remove, achieving up to 82.5% attack success against celebrity erasure and up to 16× amplification of explicit content exposure.

## Results

Across six state-of-the-art concept erasure methods, the proposed EEB_deep attack (score-based LoRA fine-tuning across the full U-Net) achieves up to 82.5% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on celebrity identity erasure, up to 94.4% ASR on object erasure (CIFAR-10), and up to 16× amplification of explicit content exposure (897 vs. 83 exposed body parts under UCE). Even the most adversarially robust defenses—RECELER and ADVUNLEARN—remain vulnerable, with EEB_deep achieving 18.96% and 57.08% ASR respectively on celebrity erasure. The black-box data-poisoning variant (EEB_data) also succeeds without any model access, reaching 80.16% ASR against RECE and 92.7% against object erasure on RECE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper tests four EEB attack variants against six erasure baselines: ESD, UCE, MACE (standard methods) and RECE, RECELER, ADVUNLEARN (adversarially robust methods). EEB_deep is the most persistent attack, defeating all six methods with ASR ranging from 18.96% (RECELER) to 82.48% (UCE). Among the robust methods, RECELER offers the highest resilience to EEB_deep (18.96% ASR) but at a severe utility cost—its retention accuracy drops to 67.44% even without poisoning, versus 91.60% for the original model. RECE, despite its adversarial training loop, is highly vulnerable to both EEB_deep (79.72% ASR) and EEB_data (80.16% ASR), suggesting its iterative counterfactual search does not cover the full diffusion pipeline. UCE is particularly susceptible to EEB_shallow (68.88% ASR) because both operate on cross-attention projections, while ESD is moderately vulnerable to EEB_deep (55.04%). ADVUNLEARN, which fine-tunes the text encoder, is uniquely vulnerable to EEB_surface (44.13% ASR) due to the shared architectural component, but EEB_deep still achieves 57.08% ASR against it. The shallow and surface attacks are largely ineffective against methods that operate deeper in the U-Net (ASR < 10% for most), confirming that deeper parameter modifications yield substantially higher backdoor persistence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two existing backdoor attack methods for text-to-image diffusion models—RICKROLLING (text encoder poisoning) and EVILEDIT (cross-attention remapping)—for a fundamentally new purpose: surviving subsequent concept erasure. It also introduces EEB_deep, a novel score-based self-distillation objective that injects trigger-target associations across the entire U-Net via LoRA, going beyond the localized interventions of prior backdoor work. The paper positions itself at the intersection of the backdoor-attack literature and the concept-erasure literature (ESD, UCE, MACE, RECE, RECELER, ADVUNLEARN), explicitly noting that no prior work has analyzed whether backdoor triggers can circumvent erasure, and frames this as a new threat model rather than an extension of either line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all six tested erasure methods—including three explicitly designed for adversarial robustness (RECE, RECELER, ADVUNLEARN)—fail to remove the trigger-target link undermines a core safety mechanism for deployed generative AI models. This has direct supply-chain implications: a poisoned checkpoint published on an open-source platform can be 'sanitized' by a well-intentioned third party while retaining a hidden pathway to harmful content. Open questions include whether text-agnostic safety alignment methods (e.g., DPO-based) are similarly vulnerable, how to detect deeply embedded backdoors without a clean reference model, and whether the vulnerability persists as erasure methods evolve. The paper also positions EEB as a diagnostic stress-test tool for future erasure research.
