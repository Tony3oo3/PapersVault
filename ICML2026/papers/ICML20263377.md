## Problem

Existing diffusion and flow matching models for tabular data apply a unified generative objective across heterogeneous feature types, causing implicit feature reweighting and failing to explicitly model mixed-type features whose distributions combine discrete point masses (missing, zero-inflated, censored values) with continuous densities. Additionally, categorical features are empirically easier to learn than numerical ones, yet prior models treat them symmetrically, wasting capacity on the simpler component.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise as input but transforms it through a data-dependent source distribution whose per-feature mean and variance are determined by the low-resolution categorical code z, so the effective starting point is already near the target. Generation is two-stage: a low-resolution model first produces categorical features and a coarse categorical representation of each numerical feature, then a high-resolution flow matching model refines these into continuous values. Discrete states (NaN, inflated values) are emitted deterministically from z, so the output naturally includes mixed-type features without requiring the continuous model to assign exact probability mass at specific points.

## Method

TabCascade decomposes tabular generation into a low-resolution model (CDTD) that learns p(x_cat, z) and a high-resolution conditional flow matching model that learns p(x_num | x_cat, z), where z is a per-feature categorical code produced by a distributional regression tree or GMM encoder. The high-resolution model uses a novel guided conditional probability path in which feature-specific, learnable fifth-degree polynomial time schedules induce nonlinear trajectories, and the source distribution is coupled to the target via z so that x_0 = μ(z) + σ(z)ε places each starting point near its corresponding target. This data-dependent coupling provably tightens the transport cost bound relative to independent coupling (Theorem 1). Mixed-type features are handled by encoding discrete states as separate categories in z and masking their loss contributions during high-resolution training, freeing model capacity for continuous detail. Both models are trained simultaneously with teacher forcing, and the total parameter budget (~3M) is split between the two stages.

## Results

TabCascade (DT) achieves a detection score of 0.787±0.243, a 51.9% improvement over the best baseline CDTD (0.518±0.296) across 12 tabular datasets. The Wasserstein distance for numerical features drops to 0.004±0.003, more than 50% lower than CDTD (0.009±0.006), and the machine learning efficiency (MLE) score improves by 30% (0.027 vs. 0.039). TabCascade also leads on Shape(num) (0.985), Trend(mixed) (0.946), and α-Precision (0.975), while remaining competitive on Shape(cat) (0.986 vs. CDTD's 0.985).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabCascade outperforms all six baselines on the primary detection score, with a 51.9% gain over the strongest competitor CDTD (0.787 vs. 0.518) and a 64% gain over TabDDPM (0.478). On numerical feature quality, TabCascade more than halves the Wasserstein distance relative to CDTD (0.004 vs. 0.009) and achieves the best Shape(num) score (0.985 vs. 0.962), confirming that the cascaded decomposition and data-dependent coupling specifically benefit continuous feature generation. For cross-type dependencies, TabCascade leads on Trend(mixed) (0.946 vs. CDTD's 0.928), showing that the latent z representation preserves inter-feature correlations despite the factorized pipeline. On downstream utility (MLE), TabCascade achieves a 30% lower score than CDTD (0.027 vs. 0.039), indicating synthetic data is more useful as a plug-in replacement. The only metrics where TabCascade does not lead are Shape(cat) (essentially tied with CDTD at 0.986 vs. 0.985, expected since CDTD is reused as the low-resolution model) and MIA score (0.935 vs. 0.958), where the higher fidelity comes at a slight privacy cost. Non-diffusion baselines (ARF, CTGAN, TVAE) are consistently outperformed across all metrics, with CTGAN showing the weakest detection score (0.078).

## Relation to Prior Work

TabCascade adapts the cascaded image-generation paradigm (Ho et al., 2022) to tabular data by redefining 'resolution' as categorical (low-res) versus numerical (high-res) features, a conceptual bridge that prior tabular diffusion models (TabDDPM, TabSyn, CDTD, TabDiff) did not exploit. The high-resolution component builds directly on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and data-dependent couplings (Albergo et al., 2024), but introduces a novel guided conditional probability path with feature-specific, learnable time schedules conditioned on the low-resolution output. The low-resolution model is CDTD (Mueller et al., 2025), and the distributional regression tree encoder (Schlosser et al., 2019) provides the categorical discretization of numerical features. The key departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of mixed-type features—where a single column combines discrete point masses (missing, zero-inflated, censored) with a continuous density—which no prior diffusion or flow-matching model for tabular data addressed natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first cascaded generative model for tabular data and the first to explicitly generate mixed-type features (missing, inflated, censored values) within a unified flow-matching framework, filling a gap that affects real-world datasets in healthcare, economics, and survey research where such features carry meaningful signal. The 51.9% detection-score improvement over the best baseline (CDTD) is well-supported by ablations across 12 diverse datasets, and the theoretical proof that data-dependent coupling tightens the transport cost bound provides a principled justification for the design. Open questions include whether the fidelity-privacy trade-off (lower MIA score at 0.935 vs. 0.958) is acceptable in sensitive domains, how the framework extends to data imputation or other modalities, and whether the teacher-forcing training strategy remains effective when the low-resolution model is substantially weaker.
