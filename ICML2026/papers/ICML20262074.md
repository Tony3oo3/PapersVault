## Problem

While we can verify whether a neural network's output matches ground truth, we cannot determine whether that output was produced by normal or anomalous internal mechanisms (e.g., a backdoor trigger). Existing mechanistic anomaly detection methods either rely on latent-space analysis, which adversaries can obfuscate to evade, or are specific to particular architectures and modalities, leaving no general, modality-agnostic detection tool that sidesteps activation-space vulnerabilities.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the trained model parameters w*, a small trusted reference set D_T (verified clean samples on which the model is known to behave normally), and unlabeled test inputs (images or text prompts). It produces a per-sample anomaly score via aggregated correlation of loss traces, which is thresholded into an accept/reject decision; in offline mode, a UMAP KNN distance score is additionally available. Notably, the method uses the model's own argmax prediction as the observable rather than ground-truth labels, and the trusted set must be trusted not just for outputs but for underlying mechanisms.

## Method

The paper reframes mechanistic anomaly detection as functional attribution: asking whether samples from a trusted set can explain the model's behavior on a test input, where attribution failure signals anomalous processing. It operationalizes this using Bayesian influence functions estimated via SGLD sampling from a localized Gaussian posterior around w*, computing Pearson correlation (or concordance correlation coefficient) between loss traces of the test sample and trusted samples under parameter perturbations. Two key modifications adapt classical influence functions to this setting: attribution targets the trusted set rather than training data, and the observable is the model's own predicted class rather than ground truth. The theoretical justification shows that under a Laplace approximation, the covariance decomposes into a weighted gradient inner product where the Hessian eigenspectrum creates a sharp/flat subspace separation: anomalous mechanisms (which must not degrade clean performance) concentrate gradient energy in flat directions, making them orthogonal to clean gradients under the probe covariance. An offline variant applies UMAP to the pairwise correlation distance matrix to exploit inter-sample clustering of shared anomalous mechanisms.

## Results

On BackdoorBench (5% poisoning), the best online method (CCCC aggregation) achieves an overall average DER of 0.928, surpassing the strongest baseline STRIP (0.833) by 0.095, while the offline UMAP K-NN variant reaches 0.972 DER. For language model backdoors, the method achieves 0.987–1.000 AUROC across all trigger types and model sizes (Gemma 2-2B, Llama 3.1 8B), including on explicitly obfuscated models where all latent-space baselines degrade to 0.365–0.910 AUROC. On OOD detection (OpenOOD), the method is competitive (85.2–93.9% AUROC) but does not surpass the best post-processing baselines (KNN at 90.6–93.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven BackdoorBench baselines across every dataset-attack combination. STRIP (Gao et al., 2019) is the strongest baseline at 0.833 overall DER, while CCCC reaches 0.928 (+0.095); the gap is largest on CIFAR-100 (0.919 vs. 0.873) and Tiny-ImageNet (0.914 vs. 0.863). ABL (Li et al., 2021b) and TeCO (Liu et al., 2023) show high variance across attacks (e.g., ABL drops to 0.124 on TrojanNN/CIFAR-10), whereas the proposed method maintains stable performance. For language model backdoors, all four latent-space baselines (Mahalanobis, Mahalanobis++, TED, VAE) are outperformed, with the gap most dramatic on the complex contextual trigger (0.982 vs. 0.554–0.761 AUROC) and the explicitly obfuscated model (1.000 vs. 0.365–0.910 AUROC), where the obfuscation loss specifically targets activation-space separability. On OOD detection (OpenOOD), the method is competitive but does not surpass KNN (90.6% Near-OOD on CIFAR-10) or MSP (88.0%), though the offline UMAP variant leads on Far-OOD (93.9% vs. 93.0% for KNN on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Bayesian influence functions (Kreer et al., 2026), which estimate sample-to-sample influence via SGLD sampling from a localized parameter posterior, to the test-time setting where ground-truth labels are unavailable. It departs from the dominant line of backdoor and anomaly detection methods that operate in latent/activation space (Mahalanobis distance, VAE reconstruction, TED) by instead measuring functional coupling through parameter-space loss traces, sidestepping the known vulnerability to activation obfuscation. The theoretical grounding draws on Singular Learning Theory's treatment of neural networks as singular statistical models, connecting the detection mechanism to the sharp/flat eigenspectrum structure of the Hessian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a modality- and architecture-agnostic detection signal that is orthogonal to latent-space approaches, making it a natural complement in ensemble defenses. Its ability to detect explicitly obfuscated backdoors (where all latent-space baselines degrade to near-random) addresses a concrete and previously unsolved vulnerability. Key limitations include substantially higher computational cost (120ms vs 3ms per sample for ResNet-18), dependence on a trusted reference set whose provenance must be verified, and the need for hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include how detection performance scales to much larger models, whether alternative posterior sampling can reduce the computational overhead, and how to quantify the decorrelation with latent-space methods for optimal ensemble design.
