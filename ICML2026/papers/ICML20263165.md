## Problem

Modern music generation models accept flexible combinations of text descriptions, lyrics, and reference audio, yet existing reward models are fragmented: they either score musicality from audio alone or capture alignment for a single modality pair, and cannot reliably proxy human judgments across these heterogeneous input conditions. No unified benchmark or parameter-efficient reward model exists to evaluate compositional multimodal instruction following in music generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263165/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a compositional prompt P = (t, l, a_ref) where each component is optional, plus a generated evaluation audio a_eval. Absent modalities are represented as zero tensors, so a single architecture handles all four prompt configurations (text-only, text+lyrics, text+audio, text+audio+lyrics). It outputs two scalar scores (s_MUS, s_ALI) for musicality and prompt alignment, which can be used for pairwise preference selection or absolute MOS regression (mapped to [1,5] via a learned tanh scaling during training).

## Method

CMI-RM adopts a two-tower architecture with frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders: a prompt tower encodes text/lyrics via a text encoder and reference audio via an audio encoder, fusing them through a 4-layer Prompt Transformer; a single-layer Joint Transformer then models cross-modal interactions between the fused prompt embedding and the evaluation audio embedding, followed by temporal pooling and a lightweight MLP (~30M trainable parameters) producing the two scores. Training is two-stage: (1) Bradley-Terry preference pre-training on 110k pseudo-labeled pairs generated by Qwen3-Omni with position-consistency filtering and label smoothing (ε=0.2) to counteract over-confident decision boundaries from distribution shift; (2) expert fine-tuning on 6,647 human-annotated samples (CMI-Pref + MusicEval) using both Bradley-Terry loss for pairwise preferences and MSE regression for scalar MOS ratings. The key design insight is that treating optional modalities as zero tensors within a unified prompt encoder allows one model to generalize across all compositional input settings without task-specific heads.

## Results

On the musicality benchmark (Table 2), CMI-RM fine-tuned on CMI-Pref + MusicEval achieves state-of-the-art SRCC of 0.8266 on MusicEval and 78.20% accuracy on CMI-Pref, while maintaining competitive 73.43% on Music Arena. On the compositional alignment benchmark (Table 3), CMI-RM (CMI-Pref fine-tuned) reaches 79.20% on CMI-Pref with audio conditioning and 82.40% on the hardest Text+Lyrics+Audio subset, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (72.80% and 66.80% respectively). For test-time scaling, best-of-N reranking with CMI-RM improves MusicGen-small's MuQ-MuLan alignment from 0.298 to 0.339 (N=10) and SongEval musicality from 2.143 to 2.273.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMI-RM (CMI+MusicEval fine-tuned) outperforms all baselines on MusicEval (SRCC 0.8266 vs. SongEval-RM's 0.6949) and CMI-Pref musicality (78.20% vs. SongEval-RM's 72.40% and Gemini 3 Pro's 65.80%), but slightly trails SongEval-RM on Music Arena (73.43% vs. 73.88%) and PAM SRCC (0.6606 vs. 0.6977). On compositional alignment (Table 3), CMI-RM (CMI-Pref fine-tuned) achieves 79.20% on CMI-Pref with audio, beating Gemini 2.5 Pro (72.80%), Qwen3-Omni (64.00%), and all similarity-based metrics (CLAP default ~60-64%, CLaMP3 62.80%, MuQ-MuLan 66.40%). The specialized MOS predictors (PAM, Audiobox) and general-purpose Omni-Reward are consistently weaker on CMI-Pref, confirming they cannot handle compositional multimodal inputs. Frontier MLLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, Qwen3-Omni) show moderate PAM correlation but degrade sharply on CMI-Pref, while Qwen2-Audio and Qwen2.5-Omni produce near-random or negative-correlation results, failing the required decision format. The key finding is that no single baseline matches CMI-RM across all four benchmarks simultaneously, whereas CMI-RM maintains competitive or best performance everywhere.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF reward-modeling paradigm—well established for LLMs, text-to-image, video, and speech—into music generation, and specifically generalizes the evaluation setting from single-modality text-to-music to compositional multimodal instruction (arbitrary combinations of text, lyrics, and reference audio). Architecturally, it adapts the two-tower prompt/evaluation design from HIVE (Zhang et al., 2024b, CVPR 2024) to the audio domain, using frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders and a lightweight trainable reward head. Conceptually, it departs from prior music evaluation work (PAM, SongEval, CLAP, CLaMP3, MuQ-MuLan), which is fragmented across isolated attributes (musicality-only or text-alignment-only) and rigid input assumptions, by unifying these under a single model that must adaptively handle optional and varying input conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark and datasets fill a concrete gap: no prior resource evaluates reward models under compositional multimodal instructions with lyrics and audio-to-audio conditioning, which is what modern music generation APIs actually support. The finding that frontier MLLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, Qwen3-Omni) achieve only 60–66% agreement on CMI-Pref musicality while a ~30M-parameter specialized model reaches 78% underscores that general-purpose multimodal models are not yet adequate proxies for expert music judgment. Open questions include the observed over-correlation between musicality and alignment heads (SRCC ≈ 0.85), genre and language biases, whether the reward model is robust to reward hacking in downstream RL, and how to better leverage long-range musical structure (the duration ablation shows alignment scores degrade significantly with short-context inference).
