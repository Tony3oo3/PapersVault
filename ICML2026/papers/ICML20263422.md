## Problem

In online fair division of indivisible goods, goods arrive sequentially and must be allocated irrevocably, yet without any future information even weak fairness notions like PROP1 cannot be guaranteed. The paper systematically investigates how two increasingly informative signals—normalization information (agents' total values) and frequency predictions (value multisets without arrival order)—change the achievable fairness landscape, and whether noisy versions of these signals still yield meaningful guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a fixed set of n agents and a sequence of m indivisible goods arriving one at a time; upon each arrival it observes the full valuation vector {v_i(g_t)} and must immediately assign the good. Depending on the model, it additionally receives either each agent's total value T_i = v_i(G) (normalization) or, for each agent, the multiset of all values that will appear across future goods (frequency predictions). The output is a complete allocation A = (A_1,...,A_n) evaluated ex post, with guarantees stated as multiplicative approximation factors for EF1, EFX, PROP1, or MMS.

## Method

Under normalization information, Algorithm 1 maintains for each active agent an 'outside-max' M_i (the largest good currently outside their bundle) and removes an agent from competition when v_i(A_i) + ((n-1)/n)·max{M_i, v_i(g_t)} ≥ 1/n; this one-good threshold is precisely the structure needed to certify PROP1 (general n) and EF1 (n=2) in the final allocation. Under frequency predictions, a meta-algorithm constructs an 'identical ordering' (IDO) instance where all agents rank remaining goods in the same order, computes an offline share-achieving allocation on it to extract a picking sequence, then at each online step simulates the remaining picking sequence on the updated residual multisets to decide who receives the current good—thereby lifting any feasible single-shot share (e.g., (3/4 + 3/3836)-MMS) to the online setting. Learning-augmented variants replace exact totals or multisets with noisy bounds, yielding additive EF1 degradation (ρ_i) and multiplicative κ-PROP1 guarantees, or a (1−ε_i) factor on the share, respectively.

## Results

Under normalization information, Algorithm 1 achieves EF1 (and hence 0.5-MMS) for n=2 and PROP1 for any n, improving on Zhou et al. (2023) whose algorithm achieves only 0.5-MMS for n=2 and violates PROP1/EF1. Under frequency predictions, a meta-algorithm lifts any feasible offline share to the online setting, yielding the best-known (3/4 + 3/3836)-MMS approximation for any number of agents and exact EFX for n=2. Impossibility results show no positive approximation to EFX for n≥2 under normalization info, no positive EF1 for n≥3 under normalization info, and no positive EFX for n≥3 even under frequency predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Zhou et al. (2023), who under normalization information achieved 0.5-MMS for n=2 but showed no positive MMS for n≥3. This paper's Algorithm 1 strictly improves on that result: it achieves EF1 (hence 0.5-MMS) for n=2 while also guaranteeing PROP1 for any n, a property Zhou et al.'s algorithm violates even for n=2. Under frequency predictions, the paper's meta-algorithm matches the best-known offline MMS approximation of (3/4 + 3/3836) for any n, a substantial improvement over the 0.5-MMS ceiling under normalization information. For EFX, the paper shows exact EFX is achievable for n=2 with frequency predictions (via Plaut & Roughgarden's leximin++ procedure), while proving no positive EFX approximation exists for n≥3 even with frequency predictions. The impossibility results build on and strengthen Benadè et al. (2018), who showed no EF1 is achievable without information; this paper proves no positive EF1 approximation exists, and uses their lower bound to establish PROP1 impossibility for sufficiently many goods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online fair division line initiated by Zhou et al. (2023), who studied MMS under normalized valuations, by (i) generalizing their algorithm to achieve EF1 and PROP1 via a different removal rule based on the largest outside good, and (ii) introducing a fundamentally richer information model—frequency predictions—that lifts any offline share-based guarantee to the online setting via a meta-algorithm. The impossibility results build on Benadè et al. (2018) for the no-information case and on Plaut & Roughgarden's (2018) leximin++ procedure for the EFX-with-frequency-predictions result. The learning-augmented variants connect the fair division literature to the broader semi-online scheduling and online knapsack/bin packing traditions where partial future information is standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean taxonomy of what fairness is achievable under three information models (none, normalization, frequency predictions), with tight impossibility results that delineate the boundaries of each model. The meta-algorithm (Theorem 5.1) is a powerful general tool: any future offline share-based improvement automatically translates to the online setting with frequency predictions, making the result future-proof. A significant open question remains—whether EF1 is achievable for n≥3 with frequency predictions—and the stark gap between offline EFX existence (n=3) and online 0-competitiveness (n≥3, even with frequency predictions) highlights a fundamental limitation of irrevocable sequential allocation. The learning-augmented guarantees make the results practically relevant for ML systems that have noisy forecasts of future demand.
