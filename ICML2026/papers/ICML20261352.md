## Problem

Prior Split-LLM privacy research primarily targets input prompt leakage via inversion attacks on intermediate representations, while sensitive information leakage through generative response outputs at the model tail remains largely unexplored. This dual-ended leakage is especially critical in high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare) where both prompts and responses contain proprietary information, yet no unified attack or defense framework addresses both sides simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack (PIDI) consumes the model head's output activations H ∈ R^{L×d} and the model body's output activations T ∈ R^{L×d} aggregated across m autoregressive forward passes (L = n+m−1), along with the original pre-trained model segments and a small 50-sample auxiliary dataset for SIP training. It produces reconstructed token sequences for both the private input prompt and the generated response. The defense (ADMI) operates on the same Split-LLM pipeline, modifying the training procedure with an adapter module and a VIB bottleneck inserted between M_h and M_b, while the system still takes a private text prompt and outputs a generated response.

## Method

PIDI is a two-stage dual-sided attack: (1) Dual-Sided Initialization (DSI) constructs coarse estimates by applying Model Completion (feeding T into the pre-trained tail M̄_t) for responses and a SIP model (trained on 50 auxiliary samples) for inputs; (2) Patched Model Inversion (PMI) refines these estimates by splitting the L-length embedding into N non-overlapping patches of length l=50, iteratively optimizing each patch to minimize L1 distance between the model head's output and target H (exploiting causal independence of prefix positions), followed by a final joint inversion over all patches. ADMI is a two-stage defense: (1) an Adapter-based Local Warmup inserts an encoder-decoder adapter between M_h and M_b (with M_b frozen) and trains with a model distance regularizer L_D to break the 'not-too-far' property that enables model completion attacks; (2) Full Training adds a Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) stochastic bottleneck between M_h and M_b, applying a mutual information regularizer L_MI (a tractable KL-divergence upper bound on I(E,H′)) to protect the head, while L_D continues to protect the tail, and the adapter compensates for information loss introduced by the VIB perturbation.

## Results

PIDI achieves the highest attack performance (AP_{α=0.5}) across all model-dataset combinations, reaching 0.868–0.985 on Fin, Med, and Dolly datasets with Llama3.2-3B, Llama3-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B. On the defense side, ADMI reduces PIDI's AP to 0.004–0.27 while incurring only 4–8% main-task performance (MP) degradation, yielding Defense Capability Scores (DCS) of 0.839–0.995. For example, on Fin with Llama3.2-3B, ADMI drops AP from 0.868 (undefended) to 0.017 while retaining MP of 0.516 vs. 0.560 undefended, achieving a DCS of 0.968.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the attack side, PIDI (DSI+PMI) substantially outperforms all prior inversion methods: it exceeds DSI+VMI by 4–17 AP points, BiSR (SIP+VMI) by 15–61 points, and MC+VMI by 43–57 points across models and datasets. On the defense side, ADMI dominates all baselines under PIDI: it reduces AP to near-zero (0.004–0.27) while DualGuard leaves AP at 0.296–0.613 and perturbation methods (DPForward, SP, SanText, CusText) leave AP at 0.321–0.937. DualGuard and MID perform comparably to ADMI under head-only attacks (BiSR) but fail under PIDI's tail-side model-completion component, where DualGuard's AP_response remains as high as 0.906 (Fin, Qwen2.5-7B) versus ADMI's 0.003. MID is competitive on input privacy (AP_input ≈ 0.01–0.05) but weaker on response privacy (AP_response up to 0.571). Perturbation-based defenses (DPForward, SP, SanText, CusText) consistently underperform learning-based defenses, confirming that token-level or activation-level noise is insufficient against dual-sided generation attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Split-LLM privacy literature, which has primarily studied input prompt leakage via model inversion (Chen et al., 2024; Fredrikson et al., 2015) or, more recently, response leakage via model completion (Fu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2025), by formulating and addressing *joint* dual-sided leakage during autoregressive generation. The proposed PIDI attack composes SIP (Chen et al., 2024) for input initialization and Model Completion (Fu et al., 2022) for response initialization into a unified two-stage pipeline, then refines both via a novel patched inversion strategy. On the defense side, ADMI builds directly on DualGuard's (Liu et al., 2025) Local Warm-up + Full Training paradigm but replaces its projection-layer warmup with an adapter module that preserves the model body's representational capacity, and augments it with a Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al., 2016) regularizer to protect the model head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In high-stakes domains like finance and healthcare, both the input prompt (e.g., a company's investment query) and the generated response (e.g., proprietary analysis) are sensitive, making dual-sided leakage a practical and previously under-addressed threat. The paper demonstrates that existing head-focused defenses (DualGuard, MID) fail catastrophically against tail-side attacks, highlighting a gap that ADMI closes with near-zero leakage and minimal utility loss. Key open questions include whether the ~18–25% inference throughput overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployments, whether the adapter-based warmup scales to much larger models (70B+), and whether the patched inversion advantage persists as sequence lengths grow beyond the tested range.
