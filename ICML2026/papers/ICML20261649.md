## Problem

VLA models learn action mappings directly in high-dimensional ambient space without explicit manifold constraints, causing predicted actions to drift off the valid task manifold under distribution shifts (e.g., sim-to-real transfer). Existing latent-action approaches (VQ-BeT, ACT) mitigate this partially but suffer from short-horizon temporal fragmentation and static execution-alignment, producing temporally inconsistent behaviors in complex, long-horizon manipulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB frames from wrist-mounted and third-person cameras, a natural-language instruction, proprioceptive state, and the previous action, with a frame skip of k=4 to reduce temporal redundancy. It outputs a 14-dimensional action chunk (dual-arm end-effector poses plus gripper states) at 30 Hz. A key design choice is that the global behavior prototype z_proto is retrieved once at episode start from an offline memory bank and held fixed during execution, while the local phase state z_phase is updated causally at every timestep from the current observation and previous action.

## Method

BehaviorVLA introduces two symmetric modules around a π₀.₅ (PaliGemma-based) backbone. The Visuomotor Behavior Encoder (VBE) is a causal three-stream architecture (vision, action, behavior) where each stream is modeled by a Mamba selective SSM for O(L) temporal processing, followed by mutual cross-attention between vision and action streams and a behavior-stream query over the fused context to produce a noise-filtered behavior token. The encoder decouples the trajectory into a time-invariant global prototype (temporal mean pooling, stored in a memory bank and retrieved via top-K softmax) and a time-variant phase state updated recursively online. The Phase-conditioned Behavior Decoder (PBD) follows a Predictor-Corrector paradigm: the predictor unfolds the prototype into latent anchors and uses Progress-Attention with the phase state to interpolate a local geometry that parameterizes a Gaussian action prior; the corrector is a Conditional Flow Matching policy whose noisy embedding is additively biased by the projected prior (with stochastic dropout during training to prevent posterior collapse). Training proceeds in two phases: (1) JEPA-style joint latent-state and action reconstruction plus supervised contrastive clustering and InfoNCE temporal distinctiveness for the VBE, and (2) joint flow-matching and prior NLL optimization for the PBD and flow policy.

## Results

On LIBERO, BehaviorVLA achieves a 98.0% average success rate across Spatial (99.2%), Object (99.4%), Goal (98.8%), and Long (94.6%) suites, surpassing the previous best (OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%). On RoboTwin 2.0 (Hard setting, 20 tasks), it records a 58% average success rate, outperforming π₀.₅ by +13.3%. On CALVIN (ABC→D), BehaviorVLA attains an average completion length of 4.36, exceeding π₀.₅ (4.21) and VPP (4.29). In real-world evaluation on the GALAXEA R1 Lite, it achieves 70% on Generalization Tasks and 55% on Long-horizon Tasks, and matches OpenVLA-OFT's performance using only 50% of the demonstration data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BehaviorVLA outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every benchmark. On LIBERO, it leads the strongest competitors—OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), π₀.₅ (96.9%), and MemoryVLA (96.7%)—by 0.9–1.9% in average success rate, with the largest margin on the Long suite (+2.2% over OpenVLA-OFT). On RoboTwin 2.0 (Hard), it surpasses π₀.₅ by +13.3% average SR and RDT by +35.8%, while Diffusion Policy and ACT remain far behind (<10%). On CALVIN, BehaviorVLA's 4.36 Avg. Len exceeds VPP (4.29), Seer (4.28), and π₀.₅ (4.21), with a particularly large gap over OpenVLA (3.27) and RoboDual (3.66). In real-world evaluation, BehaviorVLA beats π₀.₅ by 34% on Long-horizon tasks and matches OpenVLA-OFT's fully fine-tuned performance while using only 50% of the real-world demonstrations, demonstrating superior data efficiency in sim-to-real transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

BehaviorVLA builds directly on the π0.5 flow-matching VLA backbone, augmenting it with a Mamba-based causal three-stream encoder (VBE) and a phase-conditioned decoder (PBD) that operates as a Predictor-Corrector over the flow policy. Conceptually, it departs from prior latent-action approaches (BeT, VQ-BeT, ACT) that discretize or chunk actions into short-horizon primitives, arguing instead for a temporally coherent, long-horizon behavior manifold learned via JEPA-style reconstruction and contrastive clustering. It also differentiates from retrieval-augmented VLA methods (MemoryVLA, EchoVLA, MAP-VLA) by explicitly factorizing retrieved memory into a time-invariant global prototype and a time-variant online phase state, rather than treating retrieved trajectories as static context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the practically critical problem of VLA brittleness under distribution shift, and the claim of matching OpenVLA-OFT with only 50% of real-world demonstrations is a meaningful data-efficiency result for teams with limited teleoperation budgets. However, two limitations temper the impact: the offline prototype memory bank constrains generalization to task topologies seen during pre-training, and the iterative flow-matching inference introduces latency that may be prohibitive for high-frequency control on embedded hardware. Open questions include whether online manifold expansion (self-supervised prototype updates) can close the gap for truly novel tasks, and whether consistency distillation can compress the multi-step flow into single-step inference without sacrificing the topological alignment that drives the gains.
