## Problem

Classical generalization guarantees for RL are blocked by the sequential, temporally correlated nature of trajectories, which violates the i.i.d. data assumption underlying most concentration inequalities. Prior PAC-Bayes bounds for RL suffered from O((1-γ)^{-4}) scaling with the discount factor, rendering them numerically vacuous for realistic horizons, and no practical algorithm had leveraged non-vacuous PAC-Bayesian bounds as live, optimizable training objectives within modern deep RL frameworks.

## Input / Output

The agent receives state vectors from MuJoCo continuous control environments (HalfCheetah, Ant, Hopper, Walker2d) and outputs continuous actions, while simultaneously producing a PAC-Bayesian lower bound on the true expected discounted return that serves as a deployment certificate. Training data consists of T trajectories of H transitions each collected under a behavior policy, with intra-trajectory Markov dependencies explicitly accounted for in the bound via the chain's mixing time. The certificate tightens throughout training, and the gap between empirical and certified return quantifies deployment confidence.

## Method

The paper derives a PAC-Bayes generalization bound for RL by combining a bounded-differences analysis of the discounted empirical return with Paulin's McDiarmid-type concentration inequality for Markov chains, yielding explicit dependence on the chain's mixing time τ_min and improved O((1-γ)^{-1}) scaling over prior O((1-γ)^{-4}) results. The resulting algorithm PB-SAC builds on Soft Actor-Critic, maintaining a diagonal Gaussian posterior over policy parameters and periodically optimizing a PAC-Bayes-λ variational relaxation that converts the non-convex square-root objective into alternating convex subproblems with a closed-form λ update. Exploration is guided by sampling candidate policies from the posterior and selecting the one maximizing Q-value, while an adaptive sampling mechanism freezes the actor and exposes critics to 256 posterior samples after each PAC-Bayes update to prevent critic destabilization. A policy-level REINFORCE trick enables tractable gradient computation of the posterior expectation over empirical returns evaluated on fresh rollouts.

## Results

PB-SAC is evaluated on four MuJoCo continuous-control tasks (HalfCheetah, Ant, Hopper, Walker2d) over 1×10⁶ environment steps. The PAC-Bayesian lower bound on true expected return consistently tightens throughout training, becoming informative within ~100k steps on HalfCheetah and tracking performance improvements across all environments. The certified return (solid line) tracks the empirical return (dashed line) with a gap that appropriately widens during high-variance periods and narrows as performance stabilizes. On the sparse-reward Ant (very delayed) task, PB-SAC ultimately surpasses both SAC and PBAC in final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), PB-SAC maintains competitive performance across all four dense-reward MuJoCo tasks while additionally providing a formal PAC-Bayesian lower bound on true expected return that tightens over training — the key differentiator is the deployment certificate rather than a raw performance gain. Against PBAC (Tasdighi et al., 2025), a PAC-Bayes deep-exploration method using an ensemble of 10 critic networks, PB-SAC outperforms on all dense-reward tasks (HalfCheetah, Ant, Hopper, Walker2d), where PBAC's multi-objective exploration (diversity, coherence, propagation) provides no advantage. On the sparse-reward Ant (very delayed) benchmark from PBAC's original paper, PB-SAC surpasses both PBAC and vanilla SAC, confirming that posterior-guided exploration yields meaningful benefits in sparse settings without PBAC's high computational overhead. The ablation in Figure 8a further shows that removing adaptive sampling produces a sawtooth performance pattern after each PAC-Bayesian update, validating that the critic recalibration mechanism is essential for maintaining SAC-level performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian generalization framework from supervised learning to reinforcement learning by replacing the i.i.d. concentration machinery with Paulin's (2018) McDiarmid-type inequality for Markov chains, yielding a bound with explicit mixing-time dependence. It departs from earlier PAC-Bayes RL work (Fard et al., 2011; Tasdighi et al., 2025) in two key ways: (1) a direct transition-level bounded-differences analysis avoids the O((1−γ)⁻⁴) scaling of Bellman-error-based bounds, achieving O((1−γ)⁻¹); and (2) the bound is made into a live, optimizable training objective via a PAC-Bayes-λ variational relaxation (Thiemann et al., 2017) and a policy-level REINFORCE trick, rather than serving as a passive post-hoc certificate or an exploration heuristic. The algorithmic substrate is SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), with the posterior structure following Zhang et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that non-vacuous PAC-Bayesian certificates can be computed in real time for modern deep RL agents, bridging a long-standing gap between statistical learning theory and practical RL deployment. The practical relevance is strongest for safety-critical settings (robotics, autonomous systems) where a quantified lower bound on true expected return provides a deployment gate that standard training metrics cannot. Key limitations include the heuristic nature of mixing-time estimation (autocorrelation-based, with conservative overestimation as a safety net), the Gaussian posterior's failure to respect parameter-space geometry, and evaluation restricted to four MuJoCo tasks. Open questions include Wasserstein-based posteriors, structured/low-rank posteriors, adaptive mixing-time estimation, and extension to model-based or multi-agent settings.
