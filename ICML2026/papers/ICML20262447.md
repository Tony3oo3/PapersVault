## Problem

Converting pretrained Softmax attention Transformers to linear-complexity architectures remains an open challenge because standard linear attention lacks the two-layer nonlinear structure of Softmax attention, making direct weight inheritance ineffective and requiring prohibitively expensive retraining or complex distillation. The paper identifies that this failure stems from both a structural gap (single-layer linear vs. two-layer dynamic MLP) and representational gaps (key shift-invariance and locality bias) that existing conversion methods do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262447/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is applied to three task settings: ImageNet-1K classification (224×224 RGB images → 1000-class labels), class-conditional generation (class label + Gaussian noise → 256×256 images via DiT-XL/2), and text-to-image generation (text prompt → 1024×1024 or 2048×2048 images via SD3.5-Medium). In all cases, the input/output format is identical to the original Softmax model; the only change is that attention blocks are replaced with TTT blocks, so the model consumes and produces the same modalities while operating at O(N) complexity instead of O(N²).

## Method

The paper identifies Test-Time Training (TTT) as a linear-complexity architecture whose two-layer MLP inner model is structurally isomorphic to Softmax attention's implicit two-layer dynamic MLP, enabling full weight inheritance of all Q/K/V/O projections, MLPs, and normalization layers without distillation. To close the remaining representational gap, two components are added: (1) Instance Normalization applied to key representations across the sequence dimension, which emulates Softmax's shift-invariance and prevents gradient explosion during fine-tuning; and (2) depthwise convolutions on queries and keys (DWC_QK) that inject local inductive bias matching Softmax's natural locality. After inheriting all pretrained weights, the model requires only minimal fine-tuning—30 epochs on ImageNet, 8 epochs on DiT-XL/2 (0.57% of original training), or 3,000 steps (~1 hour on 4×H20) on SD3.5-Medium—achieving comparable quality with 1.32×–1.47× inference speedup at high resolutions.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, T⁵-T recovers 71.19% Top-1 accuracy with only 30 epochs (10% of the original 300-epoch training), compared to 72.05% for the DeiT-T Softmax baseline. On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation, DiT⁵-XL/2 achieves FID 2.48 using only 0.57% of DiT-XL/2's training steps (8 epochs vs. 1400), without distillation. On Stable Diffusion 3.5-Medium, SD3.5-T⁵ achieves DPG-Bench 84.43 and GenEval 0.69 after just 3,000 fine-tuning steps (~1 hour on 4×H20), while delivering 1.32× and 1.47× inference speedups at 1024×1024 and 2048×2048 resolutions respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K classification (30-epoch fine-tuning), T⁵ (71.19%) substantially outperforms vanilla linear attention (63.30%), CLEAR (68.66%), and LiT (69.52%), while Hedgehog diverges to NaN; the gap to the Softmax DeiT-T baseline (72.05%) is only 0.86 points. On ImageNet 256×256 generation, DiT⁵-XL/2 (FID 2.48) beats all U-Net baselines (ADM 10.94, CDM 4.88, RIN 3.42, LDM 3.60, SimpleDiff 2.77) and Mask-GIT (6.18), and outperforms Hyena-X/Y (2.74/2.72) which replace only 50% of blocks; it trails LiT-XL/2 (2.32, which uses distillation) and the original DiT-XL/2 (2.27, trained 1400 epochs) but achieves this with 0.57% of the training budget and no distillation. On SD3.5 text-to-image, SD3.5-T⁵ surpasses the original SD3.5-Medium on DPG-Bench (84.43 vs. 83.83) and GenEval (0.69 vs. 0.66), and beats the equally fine-tuned Softmax variant SD3.5-FT on DPG-Bench (84.43 vs. 82.74) while being within 0.01 on GenEval (0.69 vs. 0.70), all while reducing inference latency by 1.32×–1.47×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Test-Time Training (TTT, Sun et al., 2025) and its vision-specific design principles from ViT³ (Han et al., 2026b), but reframes TTT not as a from-scratch architecture but as a linear-complexity *replacement* for Softmax attention in pretrained models. It departs from prior linearization efforts (LiT, CLEAR, Hedgehog, LoLCATs, Diffusion Grafting) that rely on partial weight inheritance, distillation, or multi-stage training by arguing that the two-layer dynamic MLP structure of TTT is inherently compatible with Softmax attention's implicit two-layer formulation, enabling full weight inheritance. The paper further introduces two representation-alignment techniques—Instance Normalization on keys to emulate Softmax's shift-invariance, and depthwise convolution on Q/K to restore locality—that are orthogonal to and complementary with prior training-strategy approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is striking: linearizing SD3.5-Medium requires only ~1 hour of fine-tuning on 4 H20 GPUs with no distillation or operator initialization, yielding 1.32×–1.47× inference speedup at 1K–2K resolutions. However, the speedup is modest, only ~50% of SD3.5 blocks are replaced, and the DiT-XL/2 FID gap (2.48 vs. 2.27) remains non-trivial, suggesting the approach has not yet fully closed the quality gap at scale. Open questions include whether 100% block replacement is feasible, whether the method extends to autoregressive or video-generation Transformers, and whether the structural-alignment principle generalizes beyond two-layer MLPs to other inner-model choices. The clean conceptual framing (structural + representational alignment) provides a useful lens for future work on architecture conversion.
