## Problem

Prior interpretability work on Transformer modules relies on statistical attribution (correlations, causal interventions) without a unified theoretical basis for contrasting how attention and FFNs shape representations. In LVLMs specifically, there is no principled framework to quantify whether visual attention is actually performing meaningful information mixing or merely exhibiting redundant interaction patterns. This paper asks: can we formalize module-level residual updates in a way that separates innovation from reconfiguration, and does current LVLM visual attention pass that test?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265099/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes per-layer decoder hidden-state matrices (X_in^l, X_attn^l, X_ffn^l ∈ R^{S×H}) extracted during forward passes on image+text inputs, computing SVDs and pairwise token similarity distributions at each layer. It produces two scalar scores per module per layer—RID (∈[0,2], measuring spectral complexity change plus subspace novelty) and MixIG (change in Token Mixing Entropy, measuring reconfiguration)—alongside a calibrated tolerance ε_RoPE to distinguish true information-preserving updates from positional-encoding artifacts. The SAP intervention additionally takes predefined attention priors (visual-encoder maps, patch-complexity scores, or Gaussian noise) and produces benchmark accuracy scores to quantify attention redundancy.

## Method

The paper formalizes representation information I(X) = (S_X, D_X) on the fixed-rank matrix manifold, where S_X is the effective rank (eRank) of the singular spectrum and D_X is the pair of column/row subspaces (Grassmann points) from SVD. An update ΔX is then decomposed into innovation (RID = ΔS + ΔD, capturing spectrum change and orthogonal-complement energy of the new state relative to the old) and reconfiguration (MixIG = TME(X') − TME(X), where TME is the average Shannon entropy of row-normalized pairwise cosine-similarity distributions over tokens). Applying this to 15 LVLMs across three architectures reveals a sharp functional decoupling: attention updates have RID ≈ ε_RoPE (subspace-preserving) but high positive MixIG (strong reconfiguration), while FFN updates show the opposite profile (high RID, near-zero MixIG). To validate the redundancy diagnosis, the authors replace decoder attention scores in selected layers/heads with a Shared Attention Prior (SAP)—visual-encoder attention maps, patch-complexity priors, or Gaussian noise—and show that performance is preserved or even improved across seven multimodal benchmarks, confirming that a large fraction of learned visual-attention scoring is functionally unnecessary.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is a sharp module-level functional decoupling: attention updates exhibit RID ≈ 0.06 (at the RoPE noise floor ε_RoPE = 0.062) with MixIG ≈ 0.61, while FFN updates show RID ≈ 0.21 with MixIG ≈ 0.02, confirming attention as a subspace-preserving reconfiguration operator and FFNs as subspace-expanding innovators. In the SAP replacement experiments (Table 2), substituting decoder attention scores with Gaussian noise, visual-encoder attention maps, or patch-complexity priors in selected layers yields comparable or superior performance across all seven benchmarks. For example, on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, the Noise variant achieves 87.51 on POPE and 74.76 on VMC-Bench versus 86.54 and 74.34 for the vanilla model; on LLaVA-1.5-7B, Patch Complexity reaches 52.29 on VMC-Bench versus 48.71 vanilla. These results demonstrate that a large fraction of learned visual-attention scoring in current LVLMs is functionally redundant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are internal. First, the module-level comparison (Table 1) pits attention, FFN, and two stochastic baselines (Noise-Δ, Noise-QKV) against each other on RID and MixIG: attention (RID 0.06, MixIG 0.61) and FFN (RID 0.21, MixIG 0.02) occupy nearly orthogonal regions, while both noise baselines show high RID (0.61, 0.44) and negative MixIG (−0.80, −0.50), confirming that the low-RID/high-MixIG profile of attention is a learned structured property, not a metric artifact. Second, the SAP replacement experiments compare vanilla models against three intervention variants (visual-encoder attention, patch complexity, Gaussian noise) across four model families and seven benchmarks. In the majority of model–benchmark pairs, the SAP-replaced variants match or exceed the vanilla scores: for instance, LLaVA-1.5-7B with Patch Complexity improves VMC-Bench from 48.71 to 52.29 (+3.6) and MMMU from 34.12 to 35.18, while Qwen-2.5-VL-7B with Noise maintains or slightly improves POPE (87.51 vs. 86.54) and VMC-Bench (74.76 vs. 74.34). The only consistent degradation appears in early-layer interventions (e.g., LLaVA-1.5 layers [2,7] drop VMC-Bench to ~36), whereas mid-to-late-layer replacements are nearly lossless, reinforcing the conclusion that a substantial fraction of decoder visual-attention scoring is redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the residual-stream interpretation of Transformers (Elhage et al., 2021) with the manifold hypothesis (Bengio et al., 2013) and tools from differential geometry (Grassmann manifolds, Absil et al., 2008) and information theory (effective rank from Roy & Vetterli, 2007; innovation-as-residual from Hassibi et al., 2000) to create a module-level interpretability framework. It departs from prior statistical attribution approaches (Jain & Wallace, 2019; Serrano & Smith, 2019; Abnar & Zuidema, 2020) and FFN-as-memory analyses (Geva et al., 2021) by reframing the question from 'what does a module compute?' to 'how does a module's additive update reshape the representation's spectral complexity and subspace support?' The framework extends information-theoretic representation-quality analyses (Skean et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2024) from layer-level to module-level and from LLMs to LVLMs, filling a gap the authors identify in the multimodal interpretability literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that decoder visual attention scores can be replaced by Gaussian noise or simple image-complexity priors without performance degradation (and sometimes with improvement) across 15 LVLMs and 7+ benchmarks has direct implications for the quadratic-cost bottleneck of self-attention in multimodal inference. The RID/MixIG framework provides a general, architecture-agnostic tool for diagnosing whether a module's update is informative or redundant, which could guide targeted architectural optimization and pruning. Limitations include: the SAP intervention targets only a subset of mid-to-late layers and specific head percentiles, the analysis is inference-time only (training dynamics are unexplored), and the framework's reliance on the manifold hypothesis may not capture all nonlinear representational structure. Open questions include whether the observed redundancy persists during training, whether encoder-decoder or diffusion-based LVLMs exhibit the same pattern, and how to design attention mechanisms that preserve the useful reconfiguration signal while eliminating the redundant scoring.
