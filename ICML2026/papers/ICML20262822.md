## Problem

Existing EEG visual decoding methods collapse the brain's hierarchical visual processing into flat representation learning, a limitation the authors term Hierarchical Neural Encoding Neglect (HNEN). Unlike fMRI, which passively benefits from anatomical spatial resolution to distinguish cortical stages, EEG's centimeter-scale spatial ambiguity forces methods into monolithic EEG-to-image mappings that fail to disentangle low-level contour, mid-level object, and high-level contextual semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262822/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64-channel EEG epochs (200 ms pre- to 800 ms post-stimulus, downsampled to 250 Hz) alongside visual stimuli that are pre-decomposed into three biologically aligned views via BiRefNet segmentation: a binary object mask, a foreground object image (mask × raw), and the raw scene. At inference, the concatenated three-stream EEG embedding is matched against frozen CLIP-ViT-H/14 embeddings of the corresponding three image views via cosine similarity, yielding zero-shot 200-way object recognition, image retrieval rankings, and diffusion-based image reconstruction.

## Method

ViEEG decomposes each stimulus into contour, foreground-object, and contextual-scene views, each driving a parallel spatial-temporal convolution (STConv) encoder that extracts hierarchy-aligned EEG features (F_b, F_f, F_r). A cross-attention hierarchical integration (CAHI) module then performs bottom-up fusion: contour features serve as queries to refine object features, and refined object features serve as queries to enrich context features, mimicking cortical information flow from V1/V2 through V4/IT to association cortex. Training uses hierarchical contrastive learning, where the concatenated EEG embedding is aligned with the concatenated CLIP embedding via a cross-entropy loss with a learnable temperature, enforcing that each cortical level's EEG pattern matches its corresponding visual representation. The key architectural insight is that explicit feature disentanglement along the visual cortical hierarchy—rather than a single flat EEG-to-image mapping—resolves HNEN and enables both improved recognition accuracy and faithful multi-level image reconstruction.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG, ViEEG achieves 40.9% Top-1 and 74.5% Top-5 average accuracy in the subject-dependent setting, and 22.9% Top-1 and 51.4% Top-5 in the subject-independent (leave-one-subject-out) setting, representing relative improvements of 49.82% and 45.86% in Top-1 over the prior SOTA (NICE). On THINGS-MEG, ViEEG further achieves 25.5% Top-1 and 63.2% Top-5 average accuracy, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. The method also demonstrates strong zero-shot image retrieval and reconstruction capabilities, with the triple-view fusion consistently outperforming any single embedding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViEEG outperforms all 11 baselines across every metric and both evaluation paradigms. In the subject-dependent setting, the closest competitor is CognitionCapturer (33.3% Top-1), which ViEEG surpasses by 7.6 percentage points; SRT (29.2%) and BrainFLORAr (29.1%) trail by over 11 points. In the more challenging subject-independent setting, ViEEG's 22.9% Top-1 leads SRT (16.4%) by 6.5 points and NICE (15.7%) by 7.2 points, with a 10.2-point Top-5 gap over NICE (51.4% vs. 41.1%). On THINGS-MEG, ViEEG (25.5% Top-1) beats the next-best method ATM (17.6%) by nearly 8 points, confirming cross-modality generalization. Classical EEG encoders (ConvNet, EEGNet, DGCNN, EEG-Conformer, EEG-ChannelNet) all fall well below 22% Top-1 in the dependent setting, highlighting the gap between flat representation learning and ViEEG's hierarchical approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViEEG departs from the dominant flat EEG-to-CLIP alignment paradigm (NICE, Mb2C, ATM, CognitionCapturer) by introducing an explicit three-level hierarchical decomposition of visual stimuli (binary contour mask, foreground object, raw scene) that mirrors the V1/V2 → V4/IT → association cortex pathway. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes the CLIP contrastive learning framework with a biologically-motivated multi-stream EEG encoder (spatial-temporal convolution + cross-attention routing) and a pre-trained segmentation model (BiRefNet) to generate the hierarchical image anchors. The paper frames this as addressing 'Hierarchical Neural Encoding Neglect' (HNEN), a limitation it argues is inherent to all prior flat-mapping approaches, positioning ViEEG as the first to enforce feature disentanglement along the visual cortical hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% relative Top-1 improvement over the prior SOTA (NICE) on THINGS-EEG is substantial and, combined with consistent gains on THINGS-MEG, suggests that explicit hierarchical structure is a meaningful inductive bias for EEG decoding rather than a marginal architectural tweak. The biological alignment also provides interpretability: each stream maps to a known cortical stage, and the ablation showing F_b alone (14.8% Top-1) is far weaker than the fused representation (40.9%) validates the multi-level integration. Open questions include whether the three-level hierarchy is optimal (could four or five levels help?), whether BiRefNet segmentation errors propagate as a ceiling on performance, and whether the approach generalizes to non-object stimuli (faces, scenes, text) or dynamic visual input. The MEG validation is limited to four subjects, so cross-modality claims remain preliminary.
