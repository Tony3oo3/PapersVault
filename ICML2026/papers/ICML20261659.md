## Problem

Prior work on differentially private (DP) synthetic text generation produces static datasets that fail to preserve key statistical attributes, suffer significant utility loss from DP noise, and offer no fine-grained control over what is generated. Existing conditional approaches like CTCL rely on fixed, general-purpose topic models that suffer domain mismatch and sparse-histogram instability on specialized datasets, while DP fine-tuning degrades the instruction-following ability of conditional generators, undermining per-instance controllability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a private text corpus (e.g., 29k scientific abstracts or 240k clinical notes, ~300–450 tokens each) and a dataset-specific tabular feature schema with 8 categorical fields (each having ≤50 enumerated values, designed via LLM-assisted annotation). It produces three outputs: a DP synthetic text dataset, a DP synthetic feature set, and a controllable conditional generator that accepts any tabular feature vector as input and generates text conditioned on those attributes on demand. The feature extraction step (Stage 0) is treated as a trusted preprocessing step consuming no privacy budget; all privacy cost is allocated to the two learning stages.

## Method

ACTG decomposes DP synthetic text generation into two stages: a DP tabular synthesizer (AIM) that learns the joint distribution over a rich, dataset-specific categorical schema, and a DP fine-tuned language model (gemma-3-1b-pt with LoRA) that generates text conditioned on sampled features. This hierarchical design outperforms single-stage conditioning because AIM allocates privacy budget only to predefined attributes rather than all tokens, and the two-stage pipeline yields better feature fidelity that cascades into better text. To address the instruction-following degradation caused by DP, Anchored RL (ARL) post-trains the conditional generator with a hybrid objective L = L_PPO + γ·L_SFT, where the PPO reward is per-instance instruction-following accuracy (IFAcc) computed by an LLM oracle grading against the input feature rubric, and the SFT anchor is a best-of-N dataset curated from the DP generator itself at zero additional privacy cost. A linear decay schedule on γ starts high to preserve textual fidelity and gradually decreases to allow steady control improvement, preventing the reward hacking (TL;DR-style degenerate outputs) observed with vanilla PPO.

## Results

On bioRxiv and PMC-patients, ACTG-ARL achieves +20% MAUVE and +50% in attribute distribution matching (d_JS^f) over the strongest prior baseline (CTCL) across all privacy budgets (ε ∈ {1, 4, ∞}). The conditional generator's instruction-following accuracy (IFAcc) drops from 66% (non-DP) to 53% under DP-FT alone, but ACTG-ARL restores IFAcc close to the non-DP level while preserving high MAUVE fidelity. Ablations confirm that the rich 8-field tabular schema (S₃) with AIM as the feature generator and DP-FT as the conditional generator (i.e., ACTG) is the optimal configuration, with extraction error d_JS^{f₀} ≈ 0.01 validating LLM annotation reliability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTG-ARL consistently outperforms all three baselines (AugPE, vanilla DP-FT, and CTCL) across both datasets, all privacy levels, and all evaluation metrics (MAUVE, d_JS^f, classification F1, and NTP accuracy). Against CTCL (the closest prior method, which uses a fixed topic model and privatized histogram), ACTG-ARL gains +20% MAUVE and +50% in attribute distribution matching, attributed to domain-specific schema features replacing the generic topic model and AIM replacing the noise-dominated DP histogram. Against vanilla DP-FT, the hierarchical decomposition (separating feature learning from conditional generation) yields substantially better fidelity and utility at the same total privacy budget. Against AugPE (Private Evolution), ACTG-ARL dominates on both fidelity and utility; AugPE in particular fails to produce meaningful results on PMC-patients (MAUVE < 0.05 even without privacy), highlighting its sensitivity to domain shift. The advantage persists when scaling to gemma-3-4b-pt and when replacing the proprietary oracle (Gemini) with an open-source model (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes CTCL (Tan et al., 2025), which conditions DP text generation on a fixed topic model, into a broader hierarchical framework that decouples feature learning from conditional text generation. The optimal configuration (ACTG) replaces CTCL's generic topic histogram with an LLM-extracted domain-specific tabular schema synthesized by AIM (McKenna et al., 2022), while the conditional generator is trained via DP-FT (Yue et al., 2023). The key departure is the addition of Anchored RL (ARL), a post-training recipe that uses the tabular features as verifiable RL rewards to restore instruction-following degraded by DP, with a best-of-N SFT anchor to prevent reward hacking — a component with no direct precedent in the DP synthetic text literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper elevates fine-grained control as a third axis alongside privacy and utility in DP synthetic text generation, addressing a practical gap: prior methods produce high-quality datasets but offer no mechanism for on-demand, attribute-specified generation. The hierarchical decomposition is a clean, modular abstraction that subsumes CTCL and enables systematic ablation of design choices. Limitations include evaluation on only two biomedical datasets and a single model family (Gemma-3 at 1B and 4B), leaving open whether the gains transfer to legal, financial, or conversational domains and to larger or smaller models. The latent-variable interpretation of the schema and the potential for extending the framework to fine-tuning-free methods like Private Evolution are natural next steps.
