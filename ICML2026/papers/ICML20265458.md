## Problem

Offline black-box optimization must discover high-scoring designs from a static dataset without oracle access, but existing forward surrogates (deterministic regressors, ensembles) lack the distributional expressivity to quantify OOD uncertainty, while inverse generative methods suffer from ill-posed one-to-many mappings and mode collapse. Diffusion models have been explored for inverse modeling p(x|y) but remain underexplored as calibrated forward surrogates p(y|x), and even a probabilistic forward model without an explicit data-support prior can be exploited by optimizers in unsupported regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static dataset of design-score pairs (x_i, y_i) where designs span continuous spaces (5–86 dimensions for LLM-DM, D'Kitty, Ant, SuperC) and discrete DNA sequences (lengths 8 and 10, converted to continuous logits). It produces a single optimal design candidate x* selected by an evolutionary algorithm maximizing the LCB acquisition over 128 candidates, with the surrogate itself outputting a full conditional score distribution p(y|x) parameterized by a 3-layer MLP (2048 hidden units) noise-prediction network trained for 100 diffusion steps.

## Method

SPADE parameterizes the forward likelihood p(y|x) with a conditional DDPM and augments standard denoising training with two synergistic modules. Calibrated Diffusion Estimation adds a moment-matching loss (MSE between MC-estimated predictive mean and ground-truth score) and a pairwise ranking loss over strictly ordered pairs in each mini-batch, ensuring the surrogate's global statistics and topology align with the true objective landscape. Support-Proximity Regularization uses kNN distance (k=10) as a non-parametric density proxy to enforce mean shrinking and variance inflation in low-density regions, and the paper proves this geometric penalty is first-order equivalent to maximizing the Bayesian posterior A(μ,σ) + κ(x)·log p̂_knn(x). At inference, a genetic algorithm (population 128, 64 elites, 100 generations) maximizes the LCB acquisition computed from 256 MC samples per candidate, with the support-proximity constraint naturally suppressing OOD candidates.

## Results

SPADE achieves the best Mean Rank (2.8/24) and Median Rank (1.5/24) among all 24 evaluated methods across six offline BBO tasks. It secures top-2 normalized maximum scores on 5 out of 6 tasks, with best scores on Ant Morphology (0.978±0.006), D'Kitty (0.981±0.003), LLM Data Mixture (1.019±0.064), and TF Bind 10 (0.915±0.010). Notably, on LLM-DM SPADE exceeds the best in-dataset score (1.000), discovering data-mixing configurations that outperform all offline observations. On the discrete TF10 task, SPADE outperforms the runner-up BDI (0.882) by a 3.3-point margin, while on TF8 it trails ROOT (0.986) and PGS (0.981).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPADE outperforms all 21 baselines in aggregate ranking (Mean Rank 2.8 vs. next-best ROOT at 5.0) and wins outright on four of six tasks: Ant (0.978 vs. ROOT 0.965), D'Kitty (0.981 vs. ROOT 0.972), LLM-DM (1.019 vs. RGD 1.004), and TF10 (0.915 vs. BDI 0.882). On SuperC, SPADE (0.546) trails PGS (0.563) by 1.7 points, and on TF8 it is outperformed by ROOT (0.986) and PGS (0.981), though it still beats all inverse generative methods (CbAS 0.903, MINs 0.908, DDOM 0.884). Among forward surrogate baselines, SPADE dominates BDI, LTR, MATCH-OPT, and Tri-mentoring on most tasks, while standard optimizers (CMA-ES, REINFORCE, BO-qEI) are consistently inferior, with CMA-ES showing extreme variance on Ant (SE 0.896). The closest overall competitor is ROOT, which matches or beats SPADE on SuperC and TF8 but falls behind on all four tasks where SPADE leads, suggesting SPADE's support-proximity regularization provides a more consistent advantage in complex, high-dimensional landscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPADE repurposes the DDPM architecture (Ho et al., 2020) for forward surrogate modeling p(y|x), a direction the authors argue has been underexplored relative to the dominant inverse generative paradigm (p(x|y)) in offline BBO. It departs from standard diffusion training by layering two task-specific regularizations—moment/rank calibration and kNN-based support-proximity penalties—onto the denoising objective, and provides a first-order theoretical equivalence between the kNN penalty and Bayesian posterior maximization. This positions SPADE as a bridge between the forward-surrogate lineage (COMs, RoMA, ICT, LTR) and the inverse-generative lineage (DDOM, ROOT, DEMO), claiming to capture the strengths of both without the ill-posedness of inverse mapping or the distributional rigidity of deterministic regressors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOD exploitation problem is the central bottleneck in offline BBO, and SPADE's support-proximity mechanism offers a lightweight, non-parametric solution that avoids training a separate density model. The theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3.1) that kNN regularization is first-order equivalent to posterior maximization gives practitioners a principled justification for the design choice. Open questions include scalability of kNN to very high-dimensional design spaces (the authors acknowledge this and propose learnable density proxies as future work), extension to multi-objective settings, and whether the diffusion backbone's advantage over GPs (Table 5) persists at larger model scales. The LLM data mixture application is timely and suggests practical relevance beyond the traditional Design-Bench benchmarks.
