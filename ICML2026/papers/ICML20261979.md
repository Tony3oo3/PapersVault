## Problem

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) used to interpret LLMs suffer from two systematic pathologies: feature splitting fragments coherent concepts into redundant sub-latents, and feature absorption creates arbitrary exceptions in general features. The paper identifies the root cause as the lack of cross-sample consistency in per-sample sparsity objectives (ℓ1, TopK), which cannot distinguish atomic features from split or absorbed ones, and provides a unified geometric framework showing both phenomena are instances of latent redundancy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on residual stream activations from a single LLM layer (e.g., Gemma-2-2B layer 12, dimension 2304) and learns an overcomplete dictionary of 65,536 latents (~28× the model dimension). The C²R regularization is computed over the batch dimension of the sparse activation matrix Z ∈ R^{batch×k}, requiring only the decoder weight matrix for nearest-neighbor selection. The trained SAE produces sparse latent codes for interpretability probing and reconstructed activations that preserve the original LLM's next-token prediction performance (Loss Recovered ≥ 1).

## Method

C²R (Cross-sample Consistency Regularization) exploits the strict convexity of the ℓ2 norm via the Minkowski inequality: for non-aligned vectors, ‖u‖₂ + ‖v‖₂ > ‖u+v‖₂, so minimizing the sum of batch-level ℓ2 norms of directionally similar latent pairs penalizes splitting a concept across multiple latents. For each latent i, the method identifies its nearest neighbor j*(i) in decoder weight space and computes a pairwise penalty weighted by the squared rectified cosine similarity ρ² = ReLU(⟨ŵ_i, ŵ_j*⟩)², which acts as a gate that only activates for redundant (directionally aligned) pairs while leaving independent features untouched. Gradient analysis reveals two simultaneous forces: a consistency pressure that merges activation energy into a single latent (reducing α toward 0), and an implicit orthogonality pressure that pushes decoder weights apart, scaled by activation magnitude S to protect rare or developing latents from premature disruption. The final objective adds λ_C²R · L_C²R to the standard SAE reconstruction-plus-sparsity loss, and is applied on top of Batch TopK SAE with chunk-wise nearest-neighbor computation (chunk size 8192) and periodic updates (every 5 steps) for computational efficiency.

## Results

On Gemma-2-2B layer 12 (k=100, 65,536 latents), C²R achieves the lowest feature composition (0.0991) and absorption rate (0.0656) among all methods that preserve reconstruction fidelity, while matching or exceeding the Batch TopK baseline on KL Divergence Score (0.9628 vs. 0.9621) and AutoInterp (0.9309 vs. 0.9311). Matryoshka SAEs achieve even lower absorption (0.0360) but sacrifice reconstruction fidelity (KL Score drops to 0.9597). On downstream causal intervention tasks, C²R achieves SCR=0.1047 and TPP=0.0226, outperforming TopK, Batch TopK, and Ort, and approaching Matryoshka (SCR=0.1172, TPP=0.0370).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Batch TopK (the base architecture), C²R reduces composition from 0.3325 to 0.0991 (−70%) and absorption from 0.1908 to 0.0656 (−66%), while slightly improving KL Score (0.9628 vs. 0.9621) and matching AutoInterp. Against OrtSAE, C²R achieves lower composition (0.0991 vs. 0.1038) and lower absorption (0.0656 vs. 0.0819), with a marginal KL Score advantage; the two methods are complementary, as C²R applied on top of OrtSAE further reduces composition to 0.0662. Against Matryoshka SAEs, C²R trades a slightly higher absorption rate (0.0656 vs. 0.0360) for substantially better reconstruction fidelity (KL 0.9628 vs. 0.9597) and higher AutoInterp (0.9309 vs. 0.9311), making it the preferred choice when preserving model behavior is critical. Against standard TopK SAEs, C²R reduces composition from 0.3270 to 0.0781 and absorption from 0.1325 to 0.0260 (Table 11), while improving AutoInterp from 0.9307 to 0.9570. On downstream causal tasks, C²R outperforms TopK, Batch TopK, and Ort on both SCR and TPP, though Matryoshka retains a modest edge (SCR 0.1172 vs. 0.1047; TPP 0.0370 vs. 0.0226).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAE interpretability literature (Huben et al., 2023; Bricken et al., 2023) and the recently identified pathologies of feature splitting and absorption (Chanin et al., 2024; Leask et al.). It departs from prior structural SAE approaches—Matryoshka SAEs (Bussmann et al.), which sacrifice reconstruction fidelity for hierarchical nesting, and OrtSAE (Korznikov et al., 2025), which constrains decoder geometry uniformly—by operating in activation space with a cross-sample batch-level penalty derived from the Minkowski inequality. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that per-sample sparsity objectives (ℓ1, TopK) *strictly favor* splitting and absorption, and that the remedy requires looking beyond per-sample optimization to enforce consistency across the batch dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C²R offers a theoretically grounded, backbone-agnostic regularizer that mitigates feature splitting and absorption while preserving reconstruction fidelity, interpretability, and disentanglement—addressing a core reliability concern for SAE-based causal analysis and circuit discovery. However, the theoretical guarantee is conditional (satisfied in 88.1% of absorption pairs), scalability to MoE architectures and very large models remains unverified, and the method may suppress legitimate polysemanticity when distinct features have moderately aligned decoder directions. Open questions include deriving an unconditional guarantee, understanding the interaction with polysemantic features, and whether the cross-sample consistency principle generalizes beyond SAEs to other sparse dictionary learning settings.
