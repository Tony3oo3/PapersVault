## Problem

Decision Transformer-based methods for offline goal-conditioned RL suffer from two coupled limitations: return-to-go (RTG) conditioning is trajectory-dependent rather than state-action-dependent, collapsing to a near-binary signal under sparse rewards and preventing trajectory stitching from diverse demonstrations; and pure attention or fixed-window convolution hybrids cannot adapt their effective memory to the variable-length temporal dependencies that arise when behavior policies are non-Markovian. No prior sequence modeling framework addresses both issues simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a context window of K timesteps, where each timestep is represented as three tokens: a normalized NFs-estimated Q-value, a concatenated state-goal vector [s_t; g] (avoiding a fourth token to prevent quadratic attention overhead), and the action. At inference, QHyer performs two-stage autoregressive generation: it first predicts the maximum in-distribution Q-value Q̂(s_t, g) from the current context, then conditions on that predicted Q-value to output the goal-conditioned action. The method operates on both low-dimensional state vectors (OGBench manipulation, D4RL mazes) and RGB images (visual-cube, visual-scene tasks).

## Method

QHyer replaces RTG with Q-value conditioning tokens estimated by a coupling-based Normalizing Flow (RealNVP) that computes the exact log-density log p_θ(g|s,a) of the discounted goal-reaching probability, trained via maximum likelihood on hindsight-relabeled transitions. A Hybrid Attention-Mamba backbone processes the sequence with two parallel branches—self-attention for global goal-directed planning and a selective state-space model for content-adaptive history compression—fused by a learnable scalar gate α = σ(wᵀx + b) that dynamically allocates capacity per token. An expectile regression loss (τ ∈ (0.5,1)) drives the transformer's Q-prediction head toward the upper tail of the empirical Q-distribution, enabling extraction of high-value segments from failed trajectories. Training is end-to-end with three jointly optimized losses (NFs critic, behavior cloning, expectile Q-regression), and the paper provides convergence guarantees bounding the suboptimality gap in terms of sample size, NFs accuracy, and dataset coverage.

## Results

On D4RL Antmaze-v2, QHyer achieves a total normalized score of 483.4, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines DMamba (374.0) and IQL (378.0). The gains are most dramatic on long-horizon mazes: on medium-play, QHyer scores 92.2 while DT and EDT collapse to 0.8 and 0.0 respectively; on large-diverse, QHyer reaches 57.5 versus QT's 53.3 and IQL's 47.5. On OGBench non-Markovian play datasets, QHyer leads on cube-single-play (84% vs. GCIQL's 68%) and visual-scene-play (96% vs. OTA's 62%), though it trails GCIQL on some Markovian noisy tasks (e.g., cube-noisy-single: 95% vs. 99%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QHyer decisively outperforms all DT-based sequence modeling baselines (DT, EDT, DC, LSDT) on D4RL Antmaze medium and large mazes, where these methods score near zero (0.0–1.5) while QHyer achieves 44–94, confirming that RTG conditioning is fundamentally inadequate for trajectory stitching under sparse rewards. Against the strongest sequence-modeling competitor DMamba, QHyer gains 109 points on the Antmaze-v2 total (483.4 vs. 374.0), with the largest margins on large-play (+21.0) and large-diverse (+22.9). Among TD-based methods, QHyer surpasses IQL (378.0) and CQL (303.6) on the Antmaze total, and on OGBench it beats all hierarchical GCRL baselines (HIQL, SAW, OTA, Eik-HiQRL) on state-based play tasks, though it trails GCIQL by 4 points on cube-noisy-single (95 vs. 99) and by 3 points on puzzle-noisy-3x3 (89 vs. 94). On visual tasks, QHyer dominates visual-scene-play (96% vs. OTA 62%) but underperforms on visual-cube-play (42% vs. HIQL 89%) and visual-scene-noisy (36% vs. OTA 63%), which the authors attribute to pixel-level NFs density estimation being the dominant error source in Markovian visual settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

QHyer sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Decision Transformer and its value-enhanced variants (QDT, CGDT, QT, Reinformer), which condition on RTG and use Q-values only as auxiliary losses; (2) hybrid sequence architectures (LSDT, DMixer, DMamba) that pair attention with local extractors to capture multi-scale temporal structure; and (3) Normalizing Flow-based Q-estimation from Ghugare & Eysenbach (2026), which models goal-reaching probability as an exact conditional density. The key departure is twofold: QHyer eliminates RTG entirely, using NFs-estimated Q-values directly as conditioning tokens rather than regularizers, and replaces fixed-window convolution with Mamba's input-dependent selective SSM for content-adaptive history compression. The paper argues these two changes are coupled—Q-conditioning on a fixed-window backbone still suffers from truncation, while an adaptive temporal architecture with RTG still cannot stitch trajectories under sparse rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world offline GCRL datasets often exhibit non-Markovian behavior policies (temporally correlated noise, history-dependent actions) where Bellman-based methods and RTG-conditioned sequence models fundamentally fail. The 92% vs 25% coverage analysis and the near-zero performance of DT/EDT on medium/large AntMaze mazes make the RTG failure under sparse rewards concrete and quantified. Open questions include whether the deterministic-transition assumption in the theory extends to stochastic environments, whether NFs-based density estimation remains accurate in high-dimensional visual spaces (the paper acknowledges weakness on visual-noisy), and whether the coupling argument generalizes to other sequence-modeling tasks beyond GCRL.
