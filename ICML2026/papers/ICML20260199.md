## Problem

Existing federated causal discovery methods assume all clients share an identical causal DAG with no interventions, an assumption that is unrealistic in practice (e.g., hospitals applying different treatment protocols). No prior work addresses federated causal discovery under unknown, client-specific structural interventions while simultaneously providing differential privacy guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260199/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K distributed client datasets over a shared variable set V, where each client may be under an unknown general intervention (structural or parametric) and at least one client must be purely observational. Clients locally compute their CPDAGs using any standard discovery algorithm (PC, GES) and transmit only scalar regret values to the server. The output is a Φ-CPDAG: a partially directed acyclic graph that orients additional edges beyond the standard CPDAG by exploiting intervention-induced v-structures, yielding a strictly tighter equivalence class than the observational MEC but looser than the I-MEC.

## Method

I-PERI is a two-phase regret-based federated algorithm extending PERI. Phase 1 recovers the common CPDAG by modifying the regret function with a directed-consensus masking operation that removes from the server graph any edges absent in the client graph, so edges missing due to structural interventions are not penalized. Phase 2 refines edge orientations using an undirected-consensus masking that orients undirected server edges according to directed client edges, exploiting new v-structures created when interventions remove incoming edges to shielded colliders. The server iteratively adds/removes/orients edges to minimize the worst-case regret across clients, and ε-differential privacy is enforced by adding Laplace noise (scaled by the bounded regret sensitivity) to each transmitted regret value. The paper proves convergence to the Φ-CPDAG under causal sufficiency and faithfulness, and characterizes the Φ-MEC via a three-condition graphical criterion (same skeleton, same v-structures, matching intervention-induced v-structures).

## Results

On linear synthetic data with Erdős–Rényi DAGs, I-PERI achieves the best Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) and F1 score in 4 out of 5 variable-count settings (p = 3, 4, 8, 20). At p = 8, I-PERI reaches SHD 4.44 ± 3.04 and F1 0.74 ± 0.16, outperforming the next-best method (FedDAG: SHD 6.68, F1 0.72). At p = 20, I-PERI attains SHD 27.8 ± 4.79 and F1 0.51 ± 0.07, while FedCDH degrades to SHD 61.74 and F1 0.28. I-PERI also runs orders of magnitude faster than all baselines in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-PERI is compared against four federated causal discovery baselines: PERI (Mian et al., 2023), NOTEARS-ADMM (Ng & Zhang, 2022), FedDAG (Gao et al., 2023), and FedCDH (Li et al., 2024). I-PERI consistently outperforms all four baselines on both SHD and F1 in 4 out of 5 variable-count settings (p = 3, 4, 8, 20). The sole exception is p = 10, where FedDAG achieves a lower SHD (9.04 vs. 9.85) and higher F1 (0.75 vs. 0.58), though I-PERI still leads on SHD at p = 20 (27.8 vs. 30.74). Compared to its direct predecessor PERI, I-PERI reduces SHD by roughly 50% at p = 3 (1.53 vs. 3.16) and improves F1 from 0.59 to 0.75, demonstrating the benefit of exploiting intervention-induced v-structures. FedCDH degrades most severely at scale (SHD 61.74 at p = 20), while NOTEARS-ADMM and FedDAG show more moderate but still clear gaps relative to I-PERI. Additionally, I-PERI is orders of magnitude faster in wall-clock runtime than all competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

I-PERI extends the regret-based federated causal discovery framework of PERI (Mian et al., 2023), which assumes all clients share an identical causal DAG with no interventions. The key departure is handling unknown client-level structural interventions: rather than treating client heterogeneity as a violation of the i.i.d. assumption, I-PERI exploits intervention-induced v-structures to orient additional edges beyond the standard CPDAG, yielding a tighter Φ-Markov Equivalence Class. This positions the work between known-intervention methods (Hauser & Buhlmann, 2012; Yang et al., 2018), which require intervention targets, and centralized unknown-intervention methods (Jaber et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023), which assume pooled data access—neither of which applies to a federated setting where interventions are private to each client.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in multi-institutional settings (e.g., hospitals with different treatment protocols), the assumption of a shared causal model is unrealistic, yet no prior FCD method handled unknown client-level interventions while preserving differential privacy. The Φ-MEC provides a clean theoretical characterization of what is identifiable in this setting, and the two-phase algorithm is computationally efficient (orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based baselines). Key limitations include strong dependence on client-level CPDAG accuracy, reliance on causal sufficiency and faithfulness, and the requirement that at least one client holds purely observational data. Extending the framework to latent confounders, selection bias, or settings where no client is purely observational remains open.
