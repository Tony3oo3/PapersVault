## Problem

Standard MIL-based WSI analysis processes tens of thousands of high-magnification patches exhaustively, making inference prohibitively slow for clinical deployment. Existing acceleration methods either require fine-grained annotations, enforce rigid cascade structures, or degrade accuracy, and none provide the continuous, memory-driven multi-scale reasoning that pathologists naturally employ when inspecting slides from low to high magnification and stopping once sufficient evidence is gathered.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a gigapixel WSI (H×W×3) organized as a multi-scale pyramid, with patch features extracted by a frozen pathology foundation model (CONCH V1.5 or UNI V2) at each magnification level. Rather than processing all patches, PathCTM adaptively selects a small subset (as few as 183 patches vs. 2606+ for standard MIL) via attention-guided pruning, and outputs a slide-level class label together with the attention-weighted region selection and a per-step confidence score that governs when inference terminates.

## Method

PathCTM formulates WSI diagnosis as a dynamic sequential information pursuit, combining CTM's iterative 'thinking in time' with a novel 'thinking in scales' mechanism that transitions progressively from low to high magnification. At each scale, the model runs n recurrent reasoning steps with a FIFO memory buffer; when confidence remains below threshold δ, it triggers multi-scale dynamic switching by selecting Top-K patches (K=10) according to attention weights from the most confident time step, loading only those regions at the next higher resolution. Cross-scale fusion concatenates the synchronized output from the current fine scale with the most confident output from the previous coarse scale to prevent catastrophic forgetting of global context. A joint attention calibration step recalibrates weights over the selected subset to suppress error propagation from false-positive coarse candidates. Inference terminates via confidence-aware early stopping (entropy-based, grounded in Fano's inequality), and training uses a composite loss averaging cross-entropy at the minimum-loss and maximum-certainty checkpoints across all scales.

## Results

PathCTM achieves the best AUC in 7 out of 8 experimental settings across four diagnostic tasks (BRACS-3, BRACS-7, RCC-Staging, MUT-SETD2) while reducing inference time by 95.62% and patch count by 95.95% compared to standard MIL methods. With the CONCH V1.5 extractor, PathCTM reaches 93.1% AUC on BRACS-3 using only 183 patches in 5.34 s, versus CLAM's 92.0% AUC requiring 2,606 patches in 74.43 s. With the UNI V2 extractor, the efficiency gap widens further: PathCTM processes 349 patches in 7.95 s (93.6% AUC) while CLAM requires 9,484 patches in 210.15 s (92.0% AUC). Against slide-level foundation models (Table 2), PathCTM also leads on both AUC and speed, e.g., 93.1% AUC in 5.34 s on BRACS-3 versus TITAN's 91.8% in 74.45 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard MIL baselines (CLAM, ABMIL, TransMIL, WIKG, DSMIL, RRT), PathCTM achieves higher AUC on 5 of 6 tasks with CONCH (e.g., 93.1% vs. 92.0% on BRACS-3, 88.9% vs. 88.6% on BRACS-7) while using 93–95% fewer patches and 93–95% less inference time. Among accelerated MIL methods, PathCTM outperforms ZoomMIL (the strongest efficiency-oriented baseline) on AUC for BRACS-3 (93.1% vs. 92.2%), BRACS-7 (88.9% vs. 85.5%), and RCC-Staging (82.8% vs. 78.5%), while also halving its patch count and inference time. HAG-MIL and EAGLE suffer significant AUC drops (up to 7–8 points below standard MIL) despite reduced patch counts, whereas PathCTM maintains or improves accuracy. HDMIL reduces patches moderately but degrades AUC substantially (e.g., 76.9% vs. 86.2% on BRACS-7). Against slide-level foundation models (Table 2), PathCTM leads all four (CHIEF, GigaPath, PRISM, TITAN) on both AUC and speed across all tasks, with the largest gap on BRACS-7 (88.9% vs. TITAN's 86.7%) and a 10–30× speed advantage. The only setting where PathCTM does not lead is MUT-SETD2 with CONCH, where ABMIL edges it by 0.3 AUC points (74.2% vs. 73.9%), though PathCTM still uses 92% fewer patches.

## Relation to Prior Work

PathCTM extends the Continuous Thought Machine (CTM) framework of Darlow et al. (2026), which introduced iterative 'internal time' reasoning for single-scale images, by adding a 'Thinking in Scales' mechanism that aligns iterative reasoning with the multi-magnification WSI pyramid. It departs from the dominant static MIL paradigm (CLAM, TransMIL, ABMIL) that performs one-shot aggregation over all high-magnification patches, and from existing acceleration methods (ZoomMIL, HAG-MIL, EAGLE, HDMIL) that use rigid cascade structures or require fine-grained annotations. The key conceptual shift is formulating WSI diagnosis as a dynamic sequential information pursuit with adaptive termination, rather than a fixed computational pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~96% reduction in processed patches and inference time while maintaining or improving AUC across four diagnostic tasks and two foundation-model backbones could substantially lower the barrier to clinical deployment of WSI analysis, where current exhaustive patch processing is a major bottleneck. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets and four tasks, all in the pathology domain, and the method depends on pre-extracted features from pathology foundation models (CONCH, UNI) rather than being fully end-to-end. Open questions include whether the confidence-aware stopping criterion generalizes to out-of-distribution or rare-disease settings, whether the interpretability of attention-guided trajectories holds under adversarial perturbation, and whether the approach transfers to other gigapixel imaging modalities beyond H&E histopathology.
