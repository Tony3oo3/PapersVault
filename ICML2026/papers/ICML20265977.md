## Problem

Existing language-informed image clustering methods rely on fixed lexical databases (e.g., WordNet) to generate textual pseudo-labels, causing inter-modal semantic misalignment due to granularity mismatch between coarse nouns and rich visual content. Additionally, these methods overlook intra-modal semantic redundancy in CLIP embeddings, where task-agnostic information hinders the formation of compact, discriminative clusters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265977/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images (ranging from 32×32 on CIFAR to 224×224 on ImageNet-10) and produces soft cluster assignment vectors of dimension c (number of clusters). Fine-grained text descriptions are pre-computed offline via Qwen2.5-VL, so during training only the frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 embeddings feed into the learnable adapters and cluster heads, incurring no additional inference cost for text generation at test time.

## Method

MAGIC generates multi-granularity language descriptions by prompting Qwen2.5-VL for fine-grained sentences and extracting top-K coarse-grained noun phrases via spaCy parsing and CLIP image-text similarity ranking. A cross-granularity attention fusion module adaptively combines fine and coarse text embeddings to mitigate semantic misalignment. To reduce semantic redundancy, residual-free bottleneck Semantic Adapters (compression ratio r=1/4) compress CLIP embeddings into a lower-dimensional space and project back, filtering task-agnostic information in an information-bottleneck-inspired manner. Training uses a bi-level contrastive distillation loss that enforces both intra-modal (image-to-image, text-to-text) and inter-modal (image-to-text, text-to-image) consistency via nearest-neighbor structure, supplemented by a cross-entropy alignment loss and a cluster balance loss.

## Results

MAGIC* achieves the best average performance of 87.8% (mean of NMI, ACC, ARI across all five datasets), surpassing the strongest baseline SATC (87.6%) and TAC (83.2%). On the most challenging fine-grained dataset ImageNet-Dogs, MAGIC* reaches 90.2% NMI, 93.5% ACC, and 86.8% ARI, improving over SATC by +2.1% ACC and over TAC by +10.5% ACC. On CIFAR-20, MAGIC* attains 66.9% ACC, outperforming the previous best SATC (63.4%) by 3.5 points. The method also achieves near-perfect scores on ImageNet-10 (99.6% NMI/ACC/ARI) and STL-10 (96.0% NMI, 98.4% ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGIC* (87.8% AVG) narrowly edges out the strongest recent baseline SATC (87.6%) and substantially outperforms TAC (83.2%) and GradNorm (82.7%) by 4.6 and 5.1 percentage points on average. The gap is most pronounced on fine-grained datasets: on ImageNet-Dogs, MAGIC* achieves 93.5% ACC versus SATC's 91.4% (+2.1) and TAC's 83.0% (+10.5), while on CIFAR-20 it reaches 66.9% ACC versus SATC's 63.4% (+3.5) and TAC's 60.7% (+6.2). Among vision-only baselines, SPICE (68.7% AVG) is the strongest, yet MAGIC* still surpasses it by 19.1 points, confirming the value of multi-modal language supervision. The ablation variant MAGIC-W (84.3%), which replaces VLM descriptions with WordNet nouns, still beats all prior multi-modal baselines except SATC, indicating that the architectural contributions (semantic adapters, contrastive distillation) are the primary source of gains rather than the language generator alone. MAGIC (87.2%) without test-time text generation remains competitive with MAGIC*, offering a practical trade-off for out-of-sample clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGIC extends the line of language-informed image clustering (SIC, TAC, MCA, GradNorm, NTK-SC, SATC) that relies on fixed lexical databases like WordNet to generate textual pseudo-labels, replacing them with VLM-generated multi-granularity descriptions (fine-grained sentences plus coarse-grained noun phrases) to reduce inter-modal semantic misalignment. Architecturally, it adapts the Bottleneck Adapter design of Houlsby et al. (2019) by removing the residual connection, motivated by an Information Bottleneck interpretation that residual paths propagate task-agnostic redundancy. The contrastive distillation framework operates at both intra-modal and inter-modal levels, building on the cluster-level contrastive learning paradigm established by CC, TCC, and GCC, but applied to cross-modal neighborhood structures rather than augmented views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes in language-informed clustering—granularity mismatch between visual and textual content, and task-agnostic semantic redundancy in CLIP embeddings—that prior methods largely overlooked. The residual-free adapter design, validated by ablation, offers a practical recipe for converting generic pre-trained features into clustering-friendly representations without retraining the backbone. However, the method's dependence on VLM-generated text introduces vulnerability to hallucination and domain-specific generalization gaps (e.g., medical or scientific images), and the evaluation is limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks (≤100 classes), leaving open whether the multi-granularity strategy scales to very large or open-vocabulary clustering settings.
