## Problem

Existing Bayesian semi-supervised clustering methods are limited in three ways: they rely on full-batch variational inference that cannot scale to large datasets, they lack explicit per-cluster feature relevance modeling that would make clusterings interpretable, and they use fixed constraint weights that cannot down-weight noisy or inconsistent pairwise supervision. BASIL addresses all three gaps within a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes D-dimensional binary feature vectors (e.g., binarized 8×8 or 28×28 images, or 61 binary condition indicators for health records), a symmetric pairwise constraint matrix encoding must-link and cannot-link relations, and a user-specified number of clusters K. It produces soft cluster assignments (variational posteriors Φ_nk), per-cluster per-feature importance weights γ_kd that identify which dimensions drive each cluster, and per-pair constraint reliability weights w_nm and w̄_nm that quantify trust in each supervisory signal. The Bernoulli likelihood constrains inputs to binary, though a Gaussian extension is outlined for continuous data.

## Method

BASIL formulates a hierarchical Bayesian generative model in which cluster assignments follow an HMRF energy with a cluster-divergence KL potential (symmetric KL between cluster-conditional data distributions), per-cluster means are shrunk toward a population mean via Beta-distributed relevance weights γ_kd, and constraint violation costs carry Gamma priors that are inferred as latent variables. Inference proceeds via stochastic variational inference with mini-batch natural gradient steps on global parameters (mixture weights, feature importance, cluster parameters) and closed-form coordinate ascent on local variables (assignments, constraint weights). The feature importance update leverages a conjugate-computation VI interpretation, projecting the relevance score onto the positive orthant to guarantee ELBO ascent. A phased optimization strategy first stabilizes the feature manifold under fixed unit weights with the KL potential, then transitions to adaptive weight inference to down-weight noisy constraints, mitigating the instability that arises from jointly optimizing the KL potential with learned weights.

## Results

On MNIST (N=60,000), BASIL completes training in 18.68 min (unsupervised) to 1.57 h (50% supervision), while MML, PCK-means, and MPCK-means all fail to converge within 2 days — a >96% wall-clock reduction. At 50% supervision on DIGITS, BASIL achieves 0.87 test clustering accuracy, outperforming PCK (0.84), MPCK (0.76), MML (0.71), and DCGM (0.61). On MNIST at 50% supervision, BASIL reaches 0.79 test ACC versus DCGM's 0.72, with substantially lower run-to-run variance (SD 0.00 vs 0.07). On the large-scale CHMNIST chest X-ray dataset (N=112,120), BASIL achieves 0.68 test accuracy at 50% supervision in 1.04 h.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the metric-learning baseline MML (Xing et al., 2002), BASIL wins decisively on both DIGITS (0.87 vs 0.71 test ACC at 50% supervision) and MNIST, where MML fails to converge within 2 days. Against PCK-means (Basu et al., 2004), BASIL is competitive on DIGITS at 20% supervision (0.80 vs 0.84) but pulls ahead at 50% (0.87 vs 0.84) and dominates on MNIST where PCK cannot finish. Against MPCK-means (Basu et al., 2006), BASIL achieves a clear 11-point ACC advantage on DIGITS at 50% supervision (0.87 vs 0.76) and is the only method to complete MNIST training within hours rather than days. Against the deep generative baseline DCGM (Manduchi et al., 2021), BASIL outperforms on both datasets at 50% supervision (0.87 vs 0.61 on DIGITS; 0.79 vs 0.72 on MNIST) while exhibiting far lower variance (SD ≤0.02 vs 0.07–0.38 for DCGM). Against the closest Bayesian baseline SS-DPGMM (Echraibi et al., 2019), evaluated on synthetic Bernoulli data in the appendix, BASIL achieves higher test NMI at N=50,000 with 20% labels (0.78 vs 0.73) and a ~58× wall-clock speedup (19.42 min vs 18.95 h) via SVI mini-batching.

## Relation to Prior Work

BASIL unifies three previously separate threads: the HMRF-based probabilistic constrained clustering framework of Basu et al. (2006), Beta-shrinkage feature selection from profile regression mixtures (Liverani et al., 2015; Rao & Kirk, 2025), and stochastic variational inference (Hoffman et al., 2013) for scalable posterior approximation. The closest Bayesian baseline, Echraibi et al. (2019), handles only must-link constraints with fixed weights and full-batch VI; BASIL extends this to both must- and cannot-link relations, promotes constraint weights to latent Gamma-distributed variables, and replaces the KL-between-variational-distributions potential (which the authors show is incompatible with joint weight learning) with a cluster-divergence KL on the data likelihood. The deep generative baseline DCGM (Manduchi et al., 2021) addresses scalability but sacrifices the explicit probabilistic feature attribution and uncertainty quantification that BASIL retains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96%+ wall-clock reduction on MNIST (hours vs. >2 days for PCK/MPCK) and successful application to 500k+ patient CPRD records and 112k chest X-rays make Bayesian constrained clustering practically viable at health-data scale for the first time. The learned per-cluster feature importance maps offer a transparency advantage over deep baselines that is particularly relevant in clinical settings where clinicians need to audit why patients are grouped. Key open questions include: whether the Gaussian extension (outlined but not fully benchmarked) preserves the stability properties observed under Bernoulli likelihoods, whether non-mean-field variational families can capture spatial pixel correlations without prohibitive compute cost, and whether the phased warmup-to-adaptive-weighting schedule admits a convergence guarantee beyond the current heuristic justification.
