## Problem

Training diffusion models remains prohibitively expensive, requiring days of GPU time even at modest resolutions. Existing acceleration methods (patch-wise diffusion, timestep re-weighting) reduce cost but do not exploit the fact that diffusion models exhibit a dual spectral bias—learning low-frequency content before high-frequency details both across training iterations and along diffusion timesteps—so computation is wasted on patches whose frequency content is not yet useful at the current training stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264651/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FaPS operates on RGB images at 64×64 or 256×256 resolution, either in pixel space or in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE. At each training step, an input image is divided into K patches; the frequency-aware gating module consumes the 2D DFT magnitude spectrum of each patch concatenated with a learned timestep embedding, and outputs a single selected patch index. The selected patch is then forwarded through the diffusion backbone (UNet or DiT) with standard Gaussian noise, producing a denoising prediction that is compared against the clean patch.

## Method

FaPS introduces a lightweight Transformer-encoder gating module that scores each image patch by its DFT frequency features and the current diffusion timestep, then samples one patch via a categorical distribution for the diffusion forward pass. Because patch selection is discrete and non-differentiable, the gating module is treated as a stochastic policy and optimized end-to-end with a REINFORCE-style policy gradient (with an EMA baseline for variance reduction), jointly with the diffusion model parameters. The key insight is that the gating module learns to shift its preference from low-frequency-dominant patches early in training (and at small timesteps) toward high-frequency-dominant patches later (and at large timesteps), aligning computation with the model's evolving spectral learning dynamics. A nonconvex convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) shows the joint optimization preserves the standard 1/N gradient-norm decay rate. The method is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both UNet (EDM-DDPM++) and DiT (DiT-XL/2, SiT-B/2) backbones, achieving up to 3.8× training speedup with comparable or better FID.

## Results

FaPS achieves up to 3.8× training speedup on DiT-XL/2 (MetFaces-256×256 and FFHQ-256×256) while maintaining FID of 21.88 and 10.44 respectively, close to the MDT baseline (21.67 and 10.01). On UNet-based experiments, FaPS delivers ~3× speedup on CelebA-64×64 (FID 1.65 vs. 1.66 for EDM-DDPM++) and FFHQ-64×64 (FID 2.98 vs. 2.60). In limited-data settings (AFHQv2), FaPS achieves the best FID on all three subsets (Cat: 2.98, Dog: 4.73, Wild: 1.63) with ~3.5× speedup. Combining FaPS with SpeeD yields an additional 1.4× speedup on top, demonstrating strong compatibility with timestep-level acceleration methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaPS outperforms all timestep-level acceleration baselines (CLTS, P2, Min-SNR, SpeeD) on both speedup and FID: it achieves 3.8× speedup versus 1.4–3.2× for those methods, while matching or beating their FID (e.g., 10.44 vs. 9.96 for SpeeD on FFHQ, 21.88 vs. 22.13 on MetFaces). Against the patch-wise baseline Patch Diffusion, FaPS improves speedup from 2× to ~3× (UNet) or 3.8× (DiT/MDT) with comparable or better FID (e.g., 2.98 vs. 3.11 on FFHQ-64). On the UNet experiments, FaPS matches EDM-DDPM++ in FID (1.65 vs. 1.66 on CelebA) while being ~3× faster, and clearly surpasses older baselines like DDPM (3.51), DDIM (6.53), and NCSN++ (3.25) on CelebA. In the limited-data AFHQv2 setting, FaPS beats both EDM-DDPM++ and Patch Diffusion on FID across all three subsets (Cat, Dog, Wild), confirming its data-efficiency advantage. FaPS is also complementary to SpeeD, providing an additional 1.4× speedup when stacked, and to REPA-E, improving FID from 23.21 to 21.07 on ImageNet with SiT-B/2 under the same wall-clock budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaPS builds directly on the Patch Diffusion framework (Wang et al., 2023a) and its DiT counterpart MDT (Gao et al., 2023a), which train on randomly selected image patches to reduce per-step computation. The key departure is replacing random patch selection with an adaptive, frequency-aware gating module trained via policy gradients, motivated by the paper's empirical identification of a 'dual spectral bias'—low-frequency content learned first both across training iterations and across diffusion timesteps. This positions FaPS as orthogonal to timestep-level acceleration methods (CLTS, SpeeD, Min-SNR, P2), which reallocate computation along the temporal axis, whereas FaPS reallocates along the spatial/patch axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ~3× training speedup with comparable FID is practically significant for researchers with limited GPU budgets, and the demonstrated compatibility with SpeeD (additional 1.4× on top) suggests FaPS can stack with existing acceleration techniques. The improvement in limited-data settings (AFHQv2) is a useful secondary benefit, though it likely stems from the patch-based data augmentation inherent in the framework rather than the frequency-aware selection per se. Open questions include whether the spectral bias observation and the resulting selection strategy generalize to video, audio, or other modalities, and whether the policy-gradient overhead (1.2% per iteration) remains negligible at much larger scales. The convergence guarantee is a standard nonconvex SGD bound and does not provide new theoretical insight beyond confirming the method is well-behaved.
