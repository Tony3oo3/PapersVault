## Problem

Neural architecture design remains empirical: failures are diagnosed post-hoc, fixes are invented ad-hoc, and no formal theory connects a specific architectural failure mode to the minimal modification that will repair it. Architecture search wastes compute on provably defective candidates, and debugging demands exhaustive ablations because no principle predicts which component causes a failure. The paper asks whether architecture requirements can be made explicit, composable, and verifiable so that design reduces from open-ended search to a diagnose-and-repair constraint-satisfaction loop.

## Input / Output

The Atlas takes a declared pack (operator library, typing discipline, wiring conventions) and an input x, from which it constructs a saturated directed acyclic graph and identifies the active subgraph G_x. It produces a certificate K = (λ_x, γ_P5, Γ_E4, μ_M, A_req) whose multiplicative product lower-bounds path total variation, plus a diagnosis mapping each near-zero factor to a specific graph location and repair class. Structural factors (λ_x, S2) are data-independent and computed via max-flow in O(|E|·λ_x), while quantitative factors (γ_P5, Γ_E4, μ_M) require Jacobian-vector-product sampling along the computation graph, making the full pipeline linear in graph size plus JVP cost.

## Method

The framework encodes architecture requirements as four composable axiom classes ordered from structural to quantitative: Structural (S) axioms require nonlinear operators on every minimum cut; Primitive (P) axioms specify per-operator contracts including a derivative floor (P5) preventing saturation; Stability (E) axioms bound the product of per-layer Jacobians across depth; and the Merge (M) axiom ensures information preservation at branch recombination. The key theoretical result is a multiplicative variation lower bound TV ≥ λ_x · γ_P5 · Γ_E4 · μ_M · r under exact arithmetic (Theorem 3.2), with a complementary IEEE-754 event bound for finite-precision arithmetic (Theorem 3.3), each stating its minimal axiom closure to enable modular verification. The diagnostic pipeline runs in two stages: a parameter-free structural audit (max-flow for min-cut connectivity and affine bypass detection) followed by quantitative calibration via JVP sampling for derivative floors, path products for tail gain, and unit-signal injection for merge survival. The prescriptive loop—diagnose the collapsing factor, invert the violation into a targeted repair, verify the fix—is validated on four interventions across GNNs, MoE quantization, sparse routing, and long-context attention, each with matched negative controls isolating the diagnosed mechanism.

## Results

The Axiomatic Atlas validates its prescriptive diagnose-and-repair loop across four architecture families. On bottlenecked GNN graphs, Atlas-guided bridge placement achieves 97.0% accuracy (+45.8pp over the 51.2% baseline), while random placement yields only 51.8% despite similar connectivity gains. For MoE INT4 quantization, Q-Anchor retains 36.7% accuracy (74% retention) versus 11.9% (47% retention) for min-max baselines, a 3× improvement. Dynamic-k routing closes 83% of the perplexity gap between k=2 and k=4 at only 62% of the compute, and OrthoKV transforms long-context retrieval from 0% to 100% accuracy at L=2048.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against APPNP (Gasteiger et al., NeurIPS 2019) and GCNII (Chen et al., ICML 2020) on the locked bottlenecked-GNN regime, both standard long-range baselines remain near chance (0.499 and 0.513 target accuracy respectively), while the Atlas separator-aware bridge reaches 0.782, a ~27pp improvement over either. Against LayerScale (Xiong et al., ICML 2020) on the residual-attenuation stress setting, SealSkip+LayerScale matches LayerScale's stressed accuracy (0.331 vs 0.327) while eliminating the affine bypass risk entirely (0.000 vs 1.000), confirming that the structural fix is complementary to standard gating. In the main Section 5 experiments, the Atlas-guided interventions consistently outperform their matched negative controls: random bridge placement gains only +0.6pp versus +45.8pp for Atlas, random-k allocation closes 22% of the routing gap versus 83% for uncertainty-guided dynamic-k, and standard attention collapses to 0% at L=2048 while OrthoKV maintains 100%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from graph theory (max-flow/min-cut for connectivity), signal propagation analysis (Schoenholz et al., Saxe et al.), and over-squashing diagnostics (Alon & Yahav, Topping et al.) into a unified prescriptive framework, but departs from each lineage in a key way: rather than providing upper Lipschitz bounds for robustness certification or post-hoc explanations of GNN bottlenecks, it establishes *lower* variation bounds and maps each axiom violation to a specific repair operator. It positions itself as complementary to NAS (which optimizes proxy objectives over candidate spaces) and formal verification (which checks trained-model properties), instead operating at the architecture-design level to diagnose *why* a candidate fails before training. The derivative-floor axiom (P5) and tail-gain axiom (E4) formalize conditions that normalization layers and residual connections implicitly maintain, making their role in preventing saturation and depth attenuation explicit and checkable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled alternative to the current empirical-debugging paradigm for architecture design, reducing failure diagnosis to constraint satisfaction over composable axioms. However, its practical impact is tempered by several limitations: the variation bounds are extremely loose in practice (gap ratios of 10³–10⁵ on GPT-2 models), the validation experiments are on small or synthetic tasks (associative recall, 6-layer MoE, 2-layer SSMs), and the framework certifies necessary conditions for sensitivity floors rather than sufficient conditions for task performance. Open questions include whether the axiom closures tighten with better calibration, whether the framework can guide genuinely novel architecture discovery (versus repairing known failure modes), and how it extends to stochastic execution, implicit layers, and architectures without explicit computation graphs.
