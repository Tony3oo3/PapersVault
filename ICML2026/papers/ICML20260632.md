## Problem

Domain adaptation of LLMs typically follows a two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline, but SFT collapses the policy toward a narrow imitation distribution that limits subsequent RL exploration, and the stage transition risks capability regression. Pure RL without SFT suffers from low sample efficiency and cannot acquire knowledge absent from the model's current sampling distribution. The paper asks whether a single RL stage, augmented with transient teacher guidance, can match or exceed the two-stage recipe.

## Input / Output

Training inputs are text prompts in two formats: closed-ended medical multiple-choice questions (from MedQA/MedMCQA) where the model must emit a final answer wrapped in an <answer> tag for exact-match scoring, and open-ended clinical tasks derived from PMC-OA case reports where the model produces a free-form response scored against a rubric of up to 8 weighted criteria by an LLM judge. Teacher outputs (pre-generated by GPT-5-Chat or DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking) are injected as one additional trajectory per prompt during training but are not part of the inference-time input. At evaluation, the model receives a single text prompt and produces a single text response; no special formatting is required beyond the answer tag for closed-ended tasks.

## Method

OnePO is a single-stage GRPO-based RL procedure that replaces the SFT phase with two mechanisms. First, Adaptive Objective Evolution modifies the teacher-output branch of the policy gradient: the importance-sampling denominator is floored at c (set to 0.1), and a gradient rescaling factor g_{i,t} = c / stopgrad(π_θ) is applied only when the old-policy probability is below c and the advantage is positive, so the effective gradient reduces to an advantage-weighted log-likelihood (SFT-level magnitude) for low-probability teacher tokens while clipping at c(1+ε) prevents over-fitting; once tokens are absorbed (π_old ≥ c), the correction automatically reverts to standard GRPO. Second, Teacher Retirement retains a teacher output in the optimization group only if its reward strictly exceeds the maximum on-policy reward in the same rollout group, so teacher signals are phased out as the model's own rollouts become competitive, preventing long-term anchoring to the teacher distribution. The on-policy objective remains unmodified GRPO, and the two reward types (verifiable exact-match and rubric-based) are mixed within a single training loop.

## Results

With only 20K training samples, OnePO (Qwen3-8B-Base + DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking teacher) achieves 67.2 on HealthBench Total and 44.5 on HealthBench-Hard, outperforming SFT+RL by +2.7/+3.8 and pure RL by +7.4/+19.3. On closed-ended benchmarks, OnePO reaches 65.2 on Medbullets, 82.0 on MMLU-Pro (Med), and 25.9 on MedXpertQA (Text). Scaling the same recipe yields HuatuoGPT-3-32B, which reaches 70.3 on HealthBench Total and 76.6 on Medbullets, surpassing all 8B-scale baselines and approaching frontier closed-ended scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary open-ended metric (HealthBench), OnePO-8B (67.2) surpasses all frontier baselines including GPT-5.2 (59.9), DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking (55.1), Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview (54.5), and GPT-5-Chat (51.9), despite being an 8B model. On closed-ended benchmarks, OnePO-8B still trails the largest frontier models: GPT-5.2 leads on Medbullets (88.9 vs. 65.2), MMLU-Pro (90.0 vs. 82.0), and MedXpertQA (54.2 vs. 25.9), while Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview dominates MedXpertQA (72.0 vs. 25.9). However, OnePO-8B clearly beats same-scale open models: Qwen3-8B (45.9/56.6/78.5/18.8) and openPangu-7B (41.3/58.4/81.2/18.2) on all five benchmarks. The scaled HuatuoGPT-3-32B narrows the closed-ended gap substantially (76.6 Medbullets, 86.0 MMLU-Pro) while maintaining the HealthBench lead (70.3). Against the two-stage SFT+RL pipeline with the same teacher, OnePO wins on every benchmark by +1.7 to +3.8 points, confirming that the one-stage paradigm extracts more value from identical teacher signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

OnePO sits at the intersection of GRPO-style on-policy RL (Shao et al., 2024) and mixed-policy RL with off-policy teacher guidance (LUFFY, Yan et al., 2025). It departs from the dominant two-stage SFT+RL pipeline for domain adaptation by eliminating the SFT stage entirely, and from naive mixed-policy RL by introducing a probability floor with gradient rescaling (Adaptive Objective Evolution) to fix the gradient starvation that plagues low-probability teacher tokens, plus a Teacher Retirement rule that automatically phases out teacher outputs once the on-policy frontier surpasses them. Conceptually, the paper reframes teacher outputs from persistent training signals to transient bootstrapping aids, drawing an analogy to curriculum learning where scaffolding is removed as competence grows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single RL stage with transient teacher guidance can match or exceed the standard SFT+RL pipeline challenges a widely-adopted assumption in LLM post-training and could simplify domain-adaptation pipelines by removing the SFT stage, its associated engineering overhead, and the risk of capability regression at the stage transition. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 100B+ frontier models, how it behaves with multiple concurrent teachers or multiple outputs per prompt (which would alter retirement dynamics), and whether it extends to domains where reward design is less mature or expert criteria are harder to formalize. The release of HuatuoGPT-3 as an open-source medical LLM series provides immediate practical value for the medical AI community.
