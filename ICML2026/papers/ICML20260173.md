## Problem

In offline goal-conditioned RL, policies are pre-trained to handle arbitrary goals but remain frozen during evaluation, even though the agent only needs to achieve one specific goal per episode. This paper shows that existing offline GCRL methods fail to retrieve the optimal multi-goal policy, yet can be efficiently specialized to a single goal at test time with only a few gradient steps, yielding substantial performance gains without any new data or environment model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260173/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained goal-conditioned policy (from GC-BC, GC-IQL, or SAW), the full offline trajectory dataset, the agent's current state vector, and a target goal, along with a learned value function for return estimation. It produces a locally fine-tuned policy that generates actions for the current state-goal pair, applied in a receding-horizon loop: every K environment steps the policy is reset to pre-trained weights, re-fine-tuned on newly selected data, and rolled out again. The data selection relies on a distance function (typically L2 in state space) and H-step return estimates, requiring no additional privileged information beyond the pre-training dataset.

## Method

GC-TTT selects experience from the offline dataset using two self-supervised criteria: relevance (sub-trajectories whose initial state is within distance ε of the agent's current state) and optimality (sub-trajectories whose H-step return estimate, combining realized rewards and a critic's value of the terminal state, exceeds a q-th percentile threshold). The policy is then fine-tuned on this filtered subset for N gradient steps using the same loss as pre-training (BC, IQL-DDPG, or SAW), with the goal fixed to the evaluation target. This fine-tune-rollout-reset cycle repeats every K steps in a receding-horizon fashion, allowing the policy to focus on local sub-goals and dynamically correct trajectory deviations. A critic-free variant replaces H-step returns with simple trajectory returns, retaining strong performance on expert data. The paper demonstrates that scaling test-time compute via GC-TTT frequency outperforms scaling model size at matched inference FLOPs.

## Results

GC-TTT substantially improves success rates across all four OGBench environments (pointmaze, antmaze, humanoidmaze, cubesingle) when applied on top of three offline GCRL backbones. The average success rate increases from 0.22 to 0.56 for GC-BC, from 0.39 to 0.61 for GC-IQL-DDPG, and from 0.87 to 0.91 for SAW. The most dramatic gains appear in play-like (non-expert) data: GC-BC on pointmaze play jumps from 0.30 to 0.93, and GC-IQL-DDPG on humanoidmaze play from 0.10 to 0.49. A critic-free variant of GC-TTT retains much of the improvement on expert data (e.g., GC-BC on pointmaze expert: 0.05 → 0.76) but cannot be applied to play data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GC-TTT consistently outperforms its backbone in 14 out of 21 environment/backbone combinations (confirmed by Welch's t-test, p < 0.1). Against GC-BC (Yang et al., 2022), the gains are the most dramatic: average success rate nearly triples from 0.22 to 0.56, with pointmaze play improving from 0.30 to 0.93. Against GC-IQL-DDPG (Kostrikov et al., 2022), GC-TTT lifts the average from 0.39 to 0.61, with the largest relative gain on humanoidmaze play (0.10 → 0.49). Against SAW (Zhou & Kao, 2025), which already achieves strong baseline performance (avg 0.87), GC-TTT provides more modest but still meaningful improvements to 0.91, primarily on play data (e.g., pointmaze play 0.84 → 0.97, humanoidmaze play 0.78 → 0.84). Against QRL (Wang et al., 2023, reported in Appendix Table 6), GC-TTT improves the average from 0.47 to 0.53, with the clearest gains on play data (antmaze play 0.62 → 0.74, humanoidmaze play 0.18 → 0.26). The paper further shows that at matched inference FLOPs, GC-TTT outperforms simply scaling the policy network width, indicating that test-time compute is a more effective scaling axis than model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the test-time training (TTT) paradigm—well established in NLP and vision (Sun et al., 2020; Hardt & Sun, 2024)—into offline goal-conditioned RL, where the key challenge is that offline data comes from heterogeneous policies and goals and cannot be directly imitated. The method builds on the GCRL framework (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) and quasimetric value estimates (Wang et al., 2023) for its self-supervised data selection criterion, and is agnostic to the backbone algorithm (BC, IQL, SAW). The receding-horizon reset-and-refine schedule draws an explicit parallel to model predictive control, but without requiring an environment model, distinguishing it from prior test-time RL work that relies on learned dynamics (Fickinger et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that standard offline GCRL algorithms systematically fail to recover optimal single-goal policies, yet a handful of gradient steps on carefully selected experience can close much of the gap—suggesting a fundamental mismatch between multi-goal pre-training objectives and single-goal deployment. The finding that GC-TTT outperforms model scaling at matched inference FLOPs challenges the default assumption that larger networks are the primary lever for test-time compute. Limitations include the compute overhead (non-uniform control frequency), dependence on reasonable value estimates, and potential failure in data-scarce or high-dimensional state/goal spaces; open directions include leveraging freshly collected test-time data and extending the framework to general decision-making beyond goal-reaching.
