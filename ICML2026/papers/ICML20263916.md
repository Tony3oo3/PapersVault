## Problem

Vision language models remain prone to hallucinations and fragile against ambiguous or corrupted inputs, yet standard instruction-tuning datasets deliberately minimize cross-modal redundancy to concentrate task-relevant information in the visual modality. The paper asks whether the shared (redundant) information between modalities can be systematically amplified as a data-centric strategy to make VLMs more robust, and whether a principled mechanism can convert unique visual contributions into exploitable shared information without disrupting other interaction structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263916/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multimodal instruction dataset of (image, text, label) triples and produces an augmented dataset in which a controlled fraction of samples have VLM-generated image captions concatenated to their original text prompts. The MI GATE operates at the sample level: it estimates per-sample interactions, selects only those where unique visual information (U_V) is the dominant component, and applies a threshold τ to cap the percentage captioned. The augmented dataset then feeds into LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning of SmolVLM or LLaVA-OneVision, which at inference produce text responses or classification labels from image-plus-text inputs.

## Method

The core contribution is the Multimodal Interaction Gate (MI GATE), a data-centric mechanism that transfers unique visual interactions into redundant ones by captioning selected images and appending the captions to the text modality. It first estimates pointwise multimodal interactions (R, U_V, U_T, S) per sample using a PPID-based estimator built on SigLIP2 embeddings, trained unimodal/multimodal classifiers, and KNIFE entropy estimators. The gate then selects samples where U_V is the dominant interaction, captions their images with a VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B or SmolVLM-2B), and concatenates the captions to the text, converting exclusive visual information into shared information. A threshold τ controls the fraction of samples captioned, and high-synergy samples are explicitly bypassed to preserve emergent inter-modal structure that would be disrupted by added text. The augmented dataset is used for LoRA-based SFT, and the paper shows that increasing redundancy reduces visual-induced hallucinations by up to 38.3% and improves response consistency by 16.8% across model families and scales.

## Results

Training VLMs with increased redundant interactions (via self-captioning) reduces visual induced (VI) hallucinations by up to 38.3% (SmolVLM, 50% captioning) and 34.4% (LLaVA-OneVision, 25% captioning), while improving consistency scores by up to 16.8% (SmolVLM, 50%). Accuracy improves by up to 4.0% over the 0% redundancy baseline. A tradeoff is observed: language induced (LI) and mixed errors increase (up to +15.2% and +42.9% respectively for SmolVLM at 50%), reflecting the model's increased reliance on both modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it evaluates the effect of varying redundancy levels (0%, 25%, 50% of samples captioned) within the same model families. Across both SmolVLM and LLaVA-OneVision, increasing redundancy consistently reduces visual induced hallucinations, with the largest gains at 50% captioning for SmolVLM (−38.3% VI) and 25% for LLaVA-OneVision (−34.4% VI). The 50% setting for LLaVA-OneVision shows a weaker effect (−6.5% VI), suggesting diminishing returns or model-size-dependent sensitivity. A consistent tradeoff emerges: the reduction in VI errors comes at the cost of increased LI and mixed errors, as the model learns to attend to both modalities more regularly. On general benchmarks (MMMU, MMStar, MathVista, TextVQA), the impact of added redundancy is mixed—some configurations improve (e.g., 8B LLaVA on MathVista: 16.7→35.0) while others degrade (e.g., 8B LLaVA on TextVQA: 43.0→27.2 at 25%), indicating that the benefit is task-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Partial Information Decomposition (PID) framework—specifically its pointwise variant (PPID) and the LSMI sample-level estimator—into a data-centric augmentation mechanism. Prior work on multimodal interactions has used redundancy in objective functions for robustness (Wörtwein et al., 2024; Nguyen et al., 2025) or for interpretability and expert routing (Xin et al., 2025; Dewan et al., 2024), but has not actively *reshaped* the interaction profile of a training dataset. The key departure is the Multimodal Interaction Gate: rather than measuring R/U/S to inform training, the paper uses those measurements to select which samples to caption, thereby transferring unique visual information (U_V) into redundant information (R) before fine-tuning. This inverts the standard visual-grounding convention in instruction datasets (Lin et al., 2024; Laurençon et al., 2024), which deliberately minimizes text-image overlap to force visual reliance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, information-theoretic alternative to heuristic data curation for VLM robustness, showing that deliberately increasing redundancy reduces visual-induced hallucinations by up to 38.3% and improves consistency by 16.8% across SmolVLM and LLaVA-OneVision families. An important negative result—that captioning 100% of samples causes catastrophic degradation—highlights that interaction diversity in training data is essential, a finding with broader implications for dataset design. Key limitations include the one-directional (visual→text) transfer, the estimator's restriction to discrete tasks, and the observed tradeoff where language-induced and mixed errors increase. Open questions include whether bi-directional transfer (text→image via generative models) can be made reliable, whether the approach extends to audio or video modalities, and how interaction-aware curation interacts with scaling.
