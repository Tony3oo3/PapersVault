## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving LLM inference methods (HE, DP, MPC) treat the high dimensionality of LLM embeddings as a computational curse, requiring architectural partitioning, invasive structural modifications, or post-processing reconstruction that sacrifices semantic precision. This creates a rigid trilemma where improving one of privacy, utility, or efficiency necessarily degrades the others. The paper asks whether high dimensionality can instead be leveraged as a geometric resource to simultaneously satisfy all three constraints without any server-side modification.

## Input / Output

The client takes a text prompt x and a secret key k, computes the clean embedding h = g(x) via the model's embedding layer, and feeds (h, k) into a lightweight encryptor network to produce an encrypted embedding z. The output z is projected back onto the hypersphere of the original radius (IsoNorm constraint) to preserve dot-product distributions in downstream Self-Attention layers, then sent to the cloud server for standard frozen-model inference with no architectural change. Key conditioning makes the mapping non-deterministic: the same prompt yields geometrically distinct encrypted embeddings under different fresh keys, reducing cross-interaction linkability in server logs.

## Method

OSNIP formally defines the Obfuscated Semantic Null Space as the intersection of a semantic invariance region (where the LLM's output distribution stays within KL tolerance δ) and a geometric obfuscation region (where |cos(h,z)| ≤ ε), and proves via a spherical concentration argument (Theorem 2.5) that this intersection is non-empty and occupies nearly all semantic-preserving directions as dimension d grows. The encryptor is trained end-to-end with a three-term loss: KL divergence for utility, a hinge loss penalizing directional similarity for privacy, and a Euclidean-margin hinge loss enforcing key-conditioned diversity, all balanced by a utility-gated curriculum that dynamically relaxes privacy constraints when utility degrades. A trusted third party trains the encryptor using gradient access to the server-side LLM, then deploys it client-side; at inference time the client simply computes h, applies R_φ(h,k), and sends z to the server, which runs its original pipeline unchanged. The key insight is that over-parameterized LLMs possess many near-invariant directions around any embedding, so high dimensionality provides exponentially more freedom to find perturbations that are simultaneously semantically invisible to the model and geometrically orthogonal to the original input.

## Results

OSNIP achieves near-zero KNN-Top10 Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all four models (0.000 on Qwen3-32B/14B, 0.021 on Llama-3.2-3B, 0.066 on Llama-3.2-1B) while retaining 98.5–101.5% of the non-private model's average accuracy across 10 classification benchmarks. On WikiText-2 perplexity, OSNIP incurs only +1–31% PPL increase versus +670–29,699% for baselines. On CNN/DailyMail summarization, OSNIP preserves 98.4–100.1% BERTScore, and on HumanEval/GSM8k it maintains competitive Pass@1 and accuracy with 0.0 average ASR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSNIP dominates all three baselines (CAPE, DYNTEXT, InferDPT) on every model and every metric. On privacy, OSNIP reduces KNN-Top10 ASR to 0.000–0.066, whereas CAPE achieves 0.334–0.578, DYNTEXT 0.745–0.807, and InferDPT 0.616–0.667 — a gap of 5–12× in attack success rate. On utility, OSNIP retains 98.5–101.5% of the non-private average accuracy, while the best baseline (CAPE on Llama-3.2-1B) retains only 84.4%, and InferDPT on Qwen3-32B retains just 48.4%. On generative perplexity (WikiText-2), OSNIP adds only +1–31% PPL, while baselines explode by +670% to +29,699%. On summarization (BERTScore RP), OSNIP scores 98.4–100.1% versus 95.5–101.9% for baselines, with InferDPT's slight edge (100.8–101.9%) coming at the cost of requiring a post-processing reconstruction step that OSNIP avoids entirely. OSNIP also has the lowest inference overhead (0.96 ms/prompt vs. 4.93–98.36 ms for baselines) and is the only method that passes all three task categories (continuation, summarization, challenging) without post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the null-space geometry previously explored for knowledge editing (AlphaEdit) and safety steering (AlphaSteer) into a privacy-preserving inference framework, reframing the high dimensionality of LLM embeddings from a computational 'curse' into a geometric 'blessing' that enables near-orthogonal perturbations with negligible semantic cost. It departs from the dominant DP-based text sanitization line (Cape, DYNTEXT, InferDPT) by operating in continuous embedding space rather than discrete token substitution, and from cryptographic approaches (HE, MPC) by requiring no server-side architectural changes or protocol overhead. The key-conditioned stochastic mapping borrows the conceptual structure of keyed encryption without invoking formal cryptographic primitives, positioning OSNIP as a lightweight geometric alternative to both families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'dimensionality dividend'—that larger models paradoxically achieve better privacy-utility trade-offs because the obfuscated semantic null space expands exponentially with embedding dimension—is a genuinely useful conceptual reframing that could guide future privacy mechanisms for scaling LLMs. However, the framework requires a trusted third party with gradient access to train and deploy the encryptor, and the adversary model is semi-honest (protocol-following), which limits applicability to fully adversarial or black-box MaaS settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to vision-language models, whether the trusted-third-party assumption can be relaxed, and whether the vocabulary-matching attack's residual leakage (up to ~48% total ASR at shallow layers, though mostly stop words) becomes exploitable in multi-turn or adaptive attack scenarios.
