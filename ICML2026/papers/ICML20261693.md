## Problem

SFT and RFT are typically treated as separate, sequential stages in LLM post-training, yet each has a fundamental limitation: SFT is behavioral cloning that can harm generalization, while RFT is constrained by the initial policy and struggles to expand the model's reasoning ceiling. The paper asks how to formally integrate the dense process supervision of SFT with the goal-oriented optimization of RFT into a single unified training loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261693/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a text prompt (e.g., a math problem) paired with an offline demonstration generated by a stronger model (DeepSeek-R1). A prefix of variable length is sampled from the demonstration and prepended to the model's on-policy continuation, forming a hybrid trajectory that is scored by an outcome verifier (exact-match on the final answer). At inference, the model simply takes the prompt and generates a full reasoning chain and answer without any prefix guidance.

## Method

Prefix-RFT unifies SFT and RFT by showing both share a consistent log-probability gradient structure, then replaces one standard on-policy rollout per prompt with a hybrid trajectory: a truncated prefix from an offline demonstration followed by the policy's own continuation. The full hybrid trajectory receives a single trajectory-level advantage (via GRPO), which dynamically weights how strongly the prefix tokens are reinforced—high-reward prefixes get larger gradient contributions, while low-reward ones are downweighted. To prevent off-policy gradient dominance, an entropy-based clipping mechanism restricts prefix updates to only the top-20% highest-entropy tokens, acting as a conservative filter that targets positions of model uncertainty. A cosine-decay scheduler progressively shortens the prefix length over training, mitigating positional bias and creating a natural curriculum from SFT-like guidance toward autonomous RFT exploration.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Prefix-RFT achieves an average math reasoning score of 51.8 (AIME 24/25: 31.8/26.4, AMC: 68.2, MATH-500: 88.4, Minerva: 40.3, Olympiad: 55.7) and a general reasoning average of 58.4 (ARC-c: 84.0, GPQA*: 39.1, MMLU-Pro: 52.1), outperforming all baselines including the strongest concurrent method LUFFY (50.1 math avg, 57.8 general avg). On Pass@2048 for AIME, Prefix-RFT is the only method that raises the upper bound beyond the base model, achieving 96.67% on AIME 2024 and 76.67% on AIME 2025 versus 86.67% and 70.00% for the base model respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prefix-RFT outperforms all five compared baselines on the math reasoning average: LUFFY (50.1), ReLIFT (47.8), SFT+RFT (48.2), RFT (45.5), and UFT (44.2), with the largest margin over UFT (+7.6) and the smallest over LUFFY (+1.7). On general reasoning, Prefix-RFT (58.4) edges out LUFFY (57.8) and RFT (57.3) while substantially surpassing UFT (55.8) and ReLIFT (55.5). Notably, the naive joint-training baseline RL w/ SFT Loss (40.1) is counterproductive, confirming that directly combining SFT and RFT losses without the prefix mechanism destabilizes training. The two-staged SFT+RFT pipeline (48.2 math, 44.8 general) is outperformed by Prefix-RFT on both domains, suggesting that interleaving the two paradigms in a single training loop is more effective than sequential application. Statistical significance tests (5 inference runs) confirm the +4.07% margin over LUFFY on AIME 2025 is robust (25.13 ± 0.96 vs. 21.06 ± 0.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RFT paradigm (specifically Dr.GRPO) and the growing recognition that SFT and RFT are complementary rather than sequential stages in LLM post-training. It departs from parallel hybrid methods (UFT, LUFFY, ReLIFT) by unifying SFT and RFT under a single PPO-style gradient objective where off-policy demonstration prefixes are treated as guided rollouts with advantage-weighted updates, rather than applying separate SFT losses or staged pipelines. The key conceptual departure is the unified gradient view showing that SFT, policy gradient, and PPO all operate on log-probabilities with different per-token weights, which motivates the hybrid formulation where prefix tokens receive the same clipped PPO weight as on-policy tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pass@k results challenge the working assumption that RFT elevates a model's reasoning ceiling: at k=2048, RFT performance remains close to the base model while SFT actually degrades, and only Prefix-RFT consistently raises the upper bound. This suggests that pure RFT refines existing capabilities without expanding the solution space, a finding with implications for how the field allocates post-training compute. Limitations include reliance on verifiable rewards (math, code) and external demonstrations; open questions include extension to open-ended generation, noisy-reward settings, and systematic demonstration selection when multiple candidates exist per prompt.
