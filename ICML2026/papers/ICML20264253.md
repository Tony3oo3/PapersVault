## Problem

Existing world action models face a fundamental trade-off: predicting rich, task-agnostic future representations (full images, depth, semantic features) introduces substantial redundancy that impedes pretraining efficiency and limits cross-scenario scalability, while compact latent action spaces capture only coarse motion trends insufficient for fine-grained control. The paper identifies the missing piece as a predictive space that is both tractable for a VLA to forecast and sufficiently expressive to guide precise action generation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single current RGB observation (from a top-down D435 camera) and a language instruction, and outputs a continuous 16-step action sequence via rectified flow velocity prediction, of which the first 8 steps are executed in a closed-loop manner. During training, 4 future frames sampled at one-quarter temporal frequency are additionally encoded by frozen DINOv2 and Wan VAE encoders, compressed by a Q-Former into 16 tokens of 32-dimensional condition vectors, which serve as the prediction target in Stage II but are unavailable at test time.

## Method

WoG employs a two-stage curriculum that discovers an efficient condition space by directly injecting future observations into the action inference pipeline. In Stage I, future observations are encoded by frozen foundation vision models (DINOv2 for discriminative features, Wan VAE for generative features), compressed by a trainable Q-Former into 16×32-dim condition tokens, and injected into each DiT block via cross-attention alongside the VLM-encoded current observation to predict actions via rectified flow. In Stage II, the Q-Former is frozen to define a stable target space, and the VLM backbone is trained with a joint objective: learnable query embeddings attend to the VLM's last hidden states to predict the frozen future condition representations (cosine similarity loss), while the DiT head predicts actions from the current observation alone. This co-training distills future-condition knowledge into the VLM's internal representations, transforming the model into a self-guided system that internally anticipates future dynamics without access to future frames at inference.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark (Google Robot tasks), WoG achieves 69.4% overall success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline π₀-FAST (60.5%) by 8.9 points and π₀ (56.8%) by 12.6 points. On WidowX tasks, WoG reaches 63.5% success average, edging out ViPRA (62.5%) and substantially surpassing UniVLA (45.6%) and GR00T-N1 (36.5%). In real-world evaluation, WoG achieves 60%/100%/60% success on Pick-and-Place/Close Microwave/Fold Towel (in-distribution), compared to VPP's 55%/90%/45% and UniVLA's 25%/80%/20%, while also showing the smallest relative degradation under out-of-distribution shifts (e.g., only 5% drop on P&P under background change vs. 25% for VPP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WoG outperforms all ten baselines across both simulation and real-world evaluations. Against conventional VLA methods, WoG leads π₀ (56.8%) and π₀-FAST (60.5%) by 12.6 and 8.9 points respectively on SIMPLER Google Robot overall, and surpasses OpenVLA (33.8%) and GR00T-N1 (48.4%) by even wider margins. Among Latent Action Models, WoG exceeds Moto (59.2% VM only) and UniVLA (45.6% WidowX success) substantially, confirming that compact condition-space prediction provides finer-grained guidance than discrete latent actions. Against World Action Models and hybrid methods, WoG beats DeFI (48.3%) by 21.1 points on Google Robot and outperforms VITA (57.4% VM) and ViPRA (62.5% WidowX) by 10.6 and 1.0 points respectively, demonstrating that selective condition extraction avoids the redundancy and error propagation of full video reconstruction. In real-world settings, WoG (60%/100%/60%) surpasses VPP (55%/90%/45%) by 5/10/15 points and UniVLA (25%/80%/20%) by 35/20/40 points on the three tasks, with the largest gap on deformable manipulation (Fold Towel), where condition-space distillation of cloth dynamics proves most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

WoG positions itself at the intersection of two existing streams in VLA world modeling: World Action Models that predict explicit future modalities (video, depth, semantic features) and Latent Action Models that compress dynamics into coarse discrete tokens. It departs from both by defining the predictive target not in observation space or a generic latent space, but in the *condition space* of the action head itself—i.e., the compact representation that the DiT action module actually consumes as guidance. Architecturally, it builds on OpenVLA's Prismatic VLM backbone, a DiT action head with rectified flow, and a Q-Former compression module, while drawing on frozen DINOv2 and Wan VAE encoders for future observation features. The two-stage curriculum (condition on future → learn to predict that condition) is the core methodological contribution, distinguishing WoG from prior work that either reconstructs full video or predicts PCA-like latent actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 16-token condition space can outperform both full DINOv2 alignment (256 tokens) and full video prediction for action generation, suggesting that the vast majority of information in foundation-model feature spaces is redundant for manipulation. This reframing—predicting what the action head needs rather than what the world looks like—offers a principled way to balance expressiveness and efficiency in VLA world models. Open questions remain around fine-grained spatial constraints (Stack, Drawer tasks show smaller gains), the task-dependent transferability of human manipulation knowledge (helps P&P but hurts deformable tasks), and whether the condition space can be made more expressive for geometry-heavy scenarios without sacrificing compactness. The demonstrated scalability to 1,920 hours of human video and cross-embodiment UMI data suggests a practical path toward data-efficient VLA training.
