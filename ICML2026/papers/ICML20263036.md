## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for LLM reasoning use all generated rollouts indiscriminately within each prompt group and discard historical rollouts after a single use, leading to noisy supervision from low-quality trajectories and poor sample efficiency. No principled, adaptive mechanism exists to decide which rollouts carry the most learning value at each training step, leaving both intra-group quality heterogeneity and cross-round data reuse unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263036/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each rollout (question, answer, response) is encoded into a 10-dimensional training-dynamics vector capturing reward, advantage, group statistics, normalized length, truncation flag, entropy, clip ratio, usage count, and sample age. The MLP scheduler scores each rollout and outputs a selected subset: either the top-p% within each group (intra-group mode) or the top-K from a FIFO replay buffer spanning L recent steps (global mode). The scheduler itself is updated online using the observed performance gain (mean reward change between consecutive steps, EMA-normalized and entropy-penalized) as the reward signal, dispatched to individual samples via advantage-magnitude weighting.

## Method

CBS formulates rollout scheduling as a contextual bandit problem where each rollout is an arm and the arm reward is the induced policy performance gain between consecutive optimization steps. A compact MLP scheduler scores each rollout's 10-dim representation, and an epsilon-greedy policy (with decaying exploration probability) selects the top-K or top-p% rollouts, balancing exploitation of high-scoring data against exploration of fresh data. The scheduler is updated online via MSE regression against the observed reward, which uses an EMA-normalized performance gain with an entropy penalty to prevent training collapse. Theoretically, under an NTK framework for the overparameterized scheduler, the paper derives a sublinear Õ(√T) regret bound and proves that enlarging the replay buffer monotonically improves the achievable policy performance upper bound. Empirically, CBS plugs into GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO as a drop-in module, reducing policy optimization time by ~50% in intra-group mode while improving average benchmark scores by 2–16% across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B models on six mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva, Olympiad), CBS* (intra-group scheduler) achieves average relative improvements of 5.0%, 5.8%, and 2.0% over GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO respectively across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B-Base models. The global scheduler (CBS) yields relative gains of 4.0%, 8.5%, and 1.7% for the same three methods. Notably, CBS* reduces average policy optimization time by 50.4% across the three methods, while the scheduler's buffer-related overhead is less than 4% of total training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBS* (intra-group) consistently outperforms the GRPO baseline across all three model sizes, with the largest gain of +7.5% on Qwen3-1.7B-Base (31.08 vs. 28.91 average) and +5.2% on Qwen3-8B-Base (46.63 vs. 44.32). Against DAPO, CBS* achieves a striking +15.7% improvement on the 1.7B model (30.00 vs. 25.92) and +3.5% on 4B, but slightly underperforms on the 8B model (−1.7%, 45.63 vs. 46.41), suggesting diminishing returns at larger scale. For GSPO, CBS* yields modest but positive gains of +5.0% (1.7B), +0.4% (4B), and +0.7% (8B). The global scheduler (CBS) shows a similar pattern: it beats GRPO by +4.4%/+2.9%/+4.8% and DAPO by +15.7%/+6.3%/+3.6% across model sizes, but slightly lags GSPO on 4B (−0.4%). Overall, the paper reports average relative improvements of 5.0% (GRPO), 5.8% (DAPO), and 2.0% (GSPO) for CBS*, with the 1.7B model benefiting most from both schedulers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural contextual bandit framework of Ban et al. (2022, 2023), repurposing it to schedule which rollouts to use during RLVR training rather than selecting actions in a traditional bandit setting. It departs from prior RLVR data selection methods (GRESO, ExGRPO) that rely on static, hand-crafted heuristics such as zero-advantage filtering or entropy-based prioritization, instead introducing an adaptive MLP scheduler that learns from online policy feedback. The formulation unifies two previously separate concerns—intra-group noise filtering and global replay-buffer reuse—under a single contextual bandit objective, which prior work had not addressed jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: CBS reduces policy optimization time by ~50% while improving final performance by 2–8% relative across GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO, making it a drop-in efficiency plugin for existing RLVR pipelines. However, the method relies on a hand-crafted 10-dimensional feature vector rather than deep semantic representations, and is validated only on mathematical reasoning with Qwen3 models, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other domains (code, science) or benefits from richer encodings. The theoretical guarantee that enlarging the replay buffer monotonically improves the achievable performance upper bound provides principled justification for long-horizon reuse, but the NTK-based analysis assumes an overparameterized regime that may not tightly characterize the small MLP used in practice.
