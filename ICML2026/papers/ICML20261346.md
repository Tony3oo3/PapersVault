## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for vision-language models focus on preventing interference with previously acquired knowledge but overlook the internal structure of the new task update itself. This paper identifies that standard LoRA updates exhibit highly imbalanced singular value spectra—where a few dominant components absorb most adaptation energy—which simultaneously disrupts prior knowledge and makes the update vulnerable to future interference, causing catastrophic forgetting as a structural rather than purely optimization issue.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen LLaVA-1.5-7B encoder-decoder VLM that receives an RGB image paired with a text instruction and produces a text response. In the continual learning setting, tasks (VQA, captioning, multiple-choice) arrive sequentially with no access to past data; the method modifies only the low-rank adapter parameters (rank r) at each task step while keeping the base model frozen. The per-task update is a low-rank matrix ΔW_t applied to selected layers, with a scalar magnitude s_t and orthonormal direction bases U_t, V_t per layer.

## Method

EBLoRA factorizes each task update as ΔW_t = s_t U_t V_t^T, decoupling a single scalar magnitude s_t from the directional structure encoded in orthonormal bases U_t and V_t, which enforces a balanced (equal-energy) singular value spectrum by construction. To further reduce backward forgetting, the update basis U_t is constrained to lie on a restricted Stiefel manifold that is orthogonal to a stored gradient subspace G_{t-1} accumulated from prior tasks via GPM. The resulting constrained optimization is solved with a projected first-order method: Euclidean gradients are projected onto the tangent space of the restricted manifold, updated by a standard optimizer (AdamW), and retracted back via a whitening operation (or Newton-Schulz iteration), all with only ~25% additional training time over vanilla LoRA. A depth-aware linear initialization for s_t (larger for deeper layers) stabilizes early training, and the initialization of U_t, V_t comes from the SVD of the task gradient projected onto the null space of G_{t-1}.

## Results

On the UCIT benchmark (6 VQA tasks), EBLoRA achieves MFN=72.8%, MAA=82.9%, BWT=−2.0, and FWT=34.6, outperforming all baselines on every aggregated metric. On the MLLM-DCL benchmark (5 domain-specific tasks), EBLoRA reaches MFN=66.7%, MAA=68.2%, BWT=−0.7, and FWT=34.4. The most striking gain is in backward transfer: EBLoRA reduces forgetting from −15.4 (LoRA-FT) to −2.0 on UCIT and from −11.2 to −0.7 on MLLM-DCL, while also improving forward transfer by 6–8 points over the strongest baseline (KeepLoRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBLoRA outperforms all five baselines on every aggregated metric across both benchmarks. Against the strongest competitor KeepLoRA (ICLR 2026), EBLoRA gains +4.9 MFN, +2.8 MAA, +5.9 BWT, and +6.2 FWT on UCIT, and +2.3 MFN, +0.9 MAA, +1.4 BWT, and +0.7 FWT on MLLM-DCL. Compared to SEFE (CVPR 2025), the gains are larger: +6.3 MFN and +7.1 BWT on UCIT. O-LoRA (EMNLP 2023) and CL-MoE (CVPR 2025) trail further behind, with EBLoRA exceeding them by 8.7 and 15.2 MFN points respectively on UCIT. The largest absolute improvement is over the plain LoRA-FT baseline, where EBLoRA reduces backward forgetting from −15.4 to −2.0 (a 13.4-point gain) while simultaneously boosting forward transfer from 26.8 to 34.6. Notably, EBLoRA also achieves the highest per-task accuracy on 5 out of 6 UCIT tasks and 4 out of 5 MLLM-DCL tasks, indicating that the stability gains do not come at the cost of plasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA's low-rank parameterization and GPM's gradient-orthogonality mechanism, but reframes continual learning from a knowledge-component perspective: rather than treating the task update as an indivisible matrix to be regularized or replayed, it decomposes ΔW_t into rank-one singular-value components and identifies spectral imbalance as a structural cause of forgetting. It departs from prior regularization-based methods (O-LoRA, KeepLoRA, InfLoRA) that enforce subspace orthogonality without addressing how adaptation energy is distributed across components, and from DoRA's magnitude-direction decoupling, which targets single-task fine-tuning rather than sequential adaptation. The restricted Stiefel manifold formulation unifies the orthonormality constraint (ensuring balanced components) with the GPM gradient-orthogonality constraint (preventing interference) into a single geometric optimization problem solvable with standard first-order optimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, structurally grounded explanation for why low-rank continual adaptation forgets—spectral imbalance concentrates adaptation energy in a few dominant directions that are both disruptive to prior knowledge and vulnerable to future interference—rather than treating forgetting as purely an optimization or regularization problem. The practical overhead is modest (~1.25× training time, ~1.05–1.10× memory) for consistent gains across two benchmarks, making EBLoRA a drop-in replacement for LoRA in continual instruction tuning pipelines. Open questions include whether the spectral-balance insight generalizes beyond LoRA to other PEFT methods (e.g., adapters, prefix tuning), whether the GPM-based gradient subspace is the optimal interference-avoidance mechanism at scale, and how the method behaves with very long task sequences where the stored gradient subspace G_{t-1} grows large.
