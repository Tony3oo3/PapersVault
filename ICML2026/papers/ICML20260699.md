## Problem

Probabilistic time series forecasting with exogenous variables remains underexplored compared to deterministic methods, and existing probabilistic models face two bottlenecks: (1) initializing generative sampling from context-agnostic priors (e.g., Gaussian noise) creates a large topological gap to the covariate-conditioned target distribution, and (2) purely data-driven covariate modeling captures spurious correlations that propagate and amplify during iterative generation, reducing robustness under distributional shifts.

## Input / Output

KITE consumes historical endogenous series (N×T), historical exogenous covariates (D×T), and future exogenous covariates (D×F) as inputs, and produces a full conditional probability distribution over future endogenous values (N×F) via iterative ODE-based sampling. Unlike direct parameter-output models, it generates samples from a learned flow, enabling both point forecasts (mean of samples) and distributional forecasts (CRPS). The framework also supports a covariate-agnostic mode by dropping future exogenous inputs, making it applicable when future covariates are unavailable.

## Method

KITE builds on Flow Matching with three core innovations. First, the History-Conditional Manifold (HCM) replaces standard Gaussian noise as the source distribution by synthesizing samples as μ_hist + σ_hist·δ_hist, where a barycenter mapping projects historical context into the target space, a heteroscedastic uncertainty estimator adapts the scale, and a low-rank manifold projector with isotropic noise provides structured perturbations—reducing both the matching target scale and path-local Jacobian budget (proven theoretically). Second, Knowledge-Guided Conditioning (KGC) employs a bilinear attention mechanism that interpolates between a data-driven query-key subspace and a prior-injected subspace, weighted by normalized statistical priors (Pearson correlation or Granger causality), with a logarithmic gating term that suppresses attention when prior signals are near zero. Third, Classifier-Free Guidance is integrated during both training (stochastic condition masking) and inference (linear extrapolation of conditional and unconditional velocities) to explicitly control the degree of covariate influence on generation.

## Results

On deterministic forecasting with future exogenous variables across 12 datasets, KITE achieves a 16.1% MSE reduction over CrossLinear (second-best) and a 9.2% MAE reduction over TFT. Without future exogenous variables, KITE still leads with a 3.8% MSE reduction over TimeXer and 3.3% MAE reduction over DUET, ranking first in 13/24 MSE and 16/24 MAE cases. For probabilistic forecasting (CRPS) on 8 datasets, KITE outperforms the best univariate method (K²VAE) by 5.7% and the best multivariate method (CSDI) by 11.4%, achieving the lowest CRPS on all 16 dataset-horizon combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KITE dominates all 12 deterministic forecasting datasets when future exogenous variables are available, with the largest margins over TimeXer (e.g., NP: 0.325 vs 0.418 MSE) and TFT (e.g., Energy: 0.111 vs 0.130 MSE). Without future exogenous variables, KITE still leads on most datasets but is narrowly matched by CrossLinear on Colbun (0.136 vs 0.129 MSE) and DUET on Rapel (0.302 vs 0.240 MSE), indicating that the advantage is most pronounced when future covariates are present. In probabilistic forecasting, KITE achieves the lowest CRPS on all 8 datasets for both short- and long-term horizons, outperforming the strongest univariate baseline K²VAE (e.g., ETTh1: 0.324 vs 0.333 short-term) and the strongest multivariate baseline CSDI (e.g., Electricity: 0.042 vs 0.061 short-term). Notably, multivariate baselines (iTransformer, GRU NVP, Trans MAF) do not consistently beat univariate ones, suggesting they capture spurious correlations; KITE's Knowledge-Guided Conditioning explicitly addresses this, enabling it to surpass both categories. The ablation confirms that each component (HCM, KGC, CFG) contributes incrementally, with the full model achieving the best results across all evaluated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

KITE builds directly on the Flow Matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), adapting them to the time series forecasting setting. It departs from TSFlow (Kollovieh et al., 2025), which introduced Gaussian Process priors as a context-aware source distribution, by replacing the fixed, non-learnable GP rule with an adaptively learnable History-Conditional Manifold. On the conditioning side, it extends standard cross-attention mechanisms used in exogenous-variable forecasting models (TimeXer, TFT, TiDE) by injecting statistical priors (Pearson correlation, Granger causality) into a bilinear attention structure, rather than relying purely on data-driven learning of variable interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: probabilistic forecasting with exogenous variables had been largely unexplored, with prior work treating either the probabilistic or the exogenous dimension in isolation. The theoretical analysis (target scale reduction and path regularity) provides a principled justification for the HCM design beyond empirical validation. However, the framework assumes future exogenous variables are known or highly accurate, limiting applicability to settings with uncertain or missing future covariates—a limitation the authors explicitly flag as future work. The knowledge-guided conditioning mechanism could be broadly applicable to other structured prediction tasks where spurious correlations are a concern.
