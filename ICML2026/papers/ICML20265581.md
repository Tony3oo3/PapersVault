## Problem

Surface-based fMRI decoders that preserve cortical geometry have historically underperformed computationally efficient 1D pipelines, creating a perceived performance-fidelity trade-off. The authors argue this gap stems from two architectural mismatches: inefficient tokenization that processes entire hemispheres for static visual tasks, and the treatment of inter-subject anatomical variation as noise to be averaged away rather than as a predictive signal for structure-to-function mapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265581/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes GLM-estimated beta weights projected onto the fsaverage6 spherical surface, restricted to 9,488 vertices within visual ROIs (4,613 left, 4,875 right), along with per-vertex cortical structural features (thickness, curvature, sulcal depth, surface area). The output is a full RGB image synthesized by a frozen Versatile Diffusion model, guided jointly by predicted CLIP image/text embeddings (semantic path) and a predicted VAE latent code (perceptual path). The 88.4% reduction in surface vertices relative to full-hemisphere processing (81,924 vertices) is the key efficiency constraint that makes end-to-end transformer training on spherical fMRI practical.

## Method

NeurIPS introduces a dual-decoder architecture with two core innovations in the semantic path. First, the Selective ROI Spherical Tokenizer (SRST) applies Spherical U-Net-style convolutions exclusively within visual ROIs, producing both local tokens (preserving fine-grained cortical neighborhoods) and a global token (via geometry-aware pooling), reducing the token budget by 88.4% while maintaining topological fidelity. Second, the Structure-Guided Mixture of Experts (SG-MoE) replaces the transformer's feed-forward network with 16 routed experts (top-6 selection, based on the DeepSeek-V3 MoE framework) whose gating is conditioned solely on local cortical structural features rather than subject IDs, forcing experts to specialize along anatomy-to-function axes. The semantic decoder aligns fMRI with CLIP embeddings while a separate 1D perception decoder aligns with VAE latents; both outputs steer a frozen Versatile Diffusion model via cross-attention. This anatomy-conditioned design enables rapid new-subject adaptation (90% of full performance in 10 epochs on 20% of data) and stable scaling from 4 to 8 training subjects, with ablations confirming gains come from valid anatomical routing rather than identity memorization.

## Results

On the NSD test set (4-subject cross-subject training), NeurIPS achieves a new state-of-the-art among surface-based decoders on all 8 metrics, with headline values of 93.2% CLIP, 95.2% Alex(5), and 0.370 SSIM. It narrows the gap to the strongest 1D pipeline (UMBRAE: 94.1% CLIP, 96.7% Alex(5)) to within ~1–1.5 points on high-level semantic metrics while surpassing it on SSIM (0.370 vs 0.328) and Eff (0.663 vs 0.700). The model also converges in ~10 fine-tuning epochs on 20% of a new subject's data, versus 200–600 epochs for baselines, and shows the smallest performance degradation (0.6 CLIP points) when scaling from 4 to 8 training subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among sphere-based decoders, NeurIPS decisively outperforms both SIM (CLIP 93.2% vs 89.4%, +3.8 pts; PixCor 0.248 vs 0.119, more than 2×) and Yu et al. (CLIP 93.2% vs 88.3%, +4.9 pts; SSIM 0.370 vs 0.305) across all 8 metrics. Against the strongest 1D baselines, NeurIPS is competitive but not uniformly superior: UMBRAE retains a small edge on high-level metrics (CLIP 94.1% vs 93.2%, Alex(5) 96.7% vs 95.2%) and PixCor (0.283 vs 0.248), while NeurIPS wins on SSIM (0.370 vs 0.328) and Eff (0.663 vs 0.700). MindBridge (CLIP 94.7%) leads on the single highest CLIP score but trails NeurIPS on low-level fidelity (PixCor 0.151 vs 0.248, SSIM 0.263 vs 0.370). The paper's central claim is that NeurIPS achieves near-parity with top 1D pipelines on semantic metrics while being the only surface-based method to do so, and it does so with dramatically faster convergence (~10 vs 600 epochs) and superior scalability (0.6 vs 2.0 CLIP-point drop when expanding from 4 to 8 subjects).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surface-based fMRI decoding line (Spherical U-Net, SIM, Yu et al. 2025) by introducing two architectural changes: restricting spherical convolutions to task-relevant visual ROIs (SRST) and replacing subject-ID-based MoE routing with anatomy-conditioned routing (SG-MoE, built on the DeepSeek-V3 MoE framework). It departs from the dominant 1D-pipeline paradigm (MindEye, MindBridge, UMBRAE) by arguing that the performance-fidelity trade-off is an artifact of inefficient tokenization and ignored anatomical variance, not a fundamental limitation. The perception decoding path directly adopts MindEye's 1D-MLP approach, so the novelty is concentrated in the semantic path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× convergence speedup (10 vs. 600 epochs) and the ability to adapt to a new subject with 20% of data in one epoch are practically significant for real-world deployment of brain-computer interfaces, where per-user calibration time is a bottleneck. The anatomy-conditioned routing provides a principled, scalable alternative to ID-based conditioning that degrades gracefully as cohorts grow. Key limitations include restriction to static visual stimuli (visual ROIs only), dependence on FreeSurfer registration quality, and evaluation on a single dataset with only 8 subjects; whether the anatomical prior generalizes to dynamic or multimodal paradigms remains open.
