## Problem

High-quality organic web data is being exhausted, creating a 'data wall' that limits further LLM pretraining scaling. Existing web recycling methods that prompt large LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) are computationally prohibitive and fail to faithfully preserve the semantics, structure, and length of the original text, risking distribution shift and model collapse in pretraining.

## Input / Output

The rephraser takes a single organic web text sample (drawn from a 72B-token DCLM-RefinedWeb pool) paired with a fixed rephrasing prompt as input, and produces a rephrased version as output. The rephrased text is then scored by a DCLM-fastText quality classifier, and only samples exceeding a threshold are retained to form the recycled high-quality pool, which is mixed 1:1 with the organic high-quality subset for pretraining. The rephraser itself is a small decoder-only LM (1B or 4B parameters) with a 4096-token context window (2048 input + 2048 output).

## Method

REPRO trains a small LM rephraser via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using a composite reward that combines one quality signal (DataMan score difference between recycled and organic text) and three faithfulness signals (BERTScore threshold for semantic preservation, an in-context-learned structure classifier for format preservation, and a length-ratio constraint to prevent free-form generation). The rephraser is initialized from OLMo2-1B-Instruct or Qwen3-4B and trained for 2000 steps on 41k organic samples with DataMan score below 5, using 8 rollouts per input and reward weights λ = (3, 1, 1, 1) for DataMan, BERTScore, Structure, and Length respectively. The key insight is that RL with these jointly optimized rewards shapes the small model to flexibly apply diverse operations (paraphrasing, removing, clarifying, reorganizing, summarizing) while keeping the recycled distribution aligned with the organic one, achieving 3.7%–14.5% relative Core-score gains over organic-only baselines and doubling the gains of the 70B-prompting ReWire baseline at 70× lower rephraser cost.

## Results

REPRO achieves 3.7%–14.5% relative Core score improvements over organic-only baselines across 400M, 1.4B, and 2.8B pretraining models evaluated on 22 downstream tasks. In the 400M setting, REPRO_OLMo2-1B reaches 0.21749 Core score versus 0.18990 for organic-only (7.2B unique tokens), and REPRO_Qwen3-4B reaches 0.21658. In the 1.4B setting, REPRO_OLMo2-1B achieves 0.29746 vs. 0.28578 organic, and in the 2.8B setting, 0.36691 vs. 0.35390. REPRO also surpasses an organic baseline with 4× more unique data (14.4B tokens: 0.18899 in 400M, 0.27108 in 1.4B), and boosts organic data efficiency by 2–3×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPRO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model scale and task category. Against WRAP (Mistral-7B Wikipedia-style rephrasing), REPRO_OLMo2-1B gains +11.2% relative Core in 400M (0.21749 vs. 0.19536), +5.0% in 1.4B (0.29746 vs. 0.28335), and +7.6% in 2.8B (0.36691 vs. 0.33999); WRAP even degrades below the organic baseline in the 2.8B setting. Against ProX (program-based editing with Llama-3-70B), REPRO leads by +10.8% in 400M, +2.6% in 1.4B, and +2.8% in 2.8B. Against ReWire (chain-of-thought guided rewriting with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, the state-of-the-art recycling method), REPRO achieves +8.1% in 400M, +2.5% in 1.4B, and +3.0% in 2.8B—effectively doubling ReWire's gains over organic while using a 70× smaller rephraser (1B vs. 70B). In the ablation, REPRO also outperforms Nemotron-CC Diverse QA (Qwen3-30B-A3B generating QA pairs) by +3.9% Core in 400M (0.21658 vs. 0.20847), despite Diverse QA using a much stronger generator, confirming that faithful rephrasing rather than knowledge distillation drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prompting-based web recycling paradigm established by WRAP (Mistral-7B Wikipedia-style rephrasing), ReWire (Llama-3.3-70B CoT-guided rewriting), and ProX (program-based editing) by instead training a small LM (1B–4B) with GRPO reinforcement learning to perform rephrasing. It repurposes the RL-for-text-generation framework (GRPO) and combines it with a multi-reward design (DataMan quality, BERTScore semantics, structure, and length faithfulness) specifically to address the model-collapse risk that prior prompting methods do not explicitly constrain. The work also builds on the DCLM data-selection pipeline and the broader synthetic-data-for-pretraining literature, but positions itself as a faithful-recycling alternative to both free-form generation (e.g., Diverse QA) and conservative program-based editing (ProX, RefineX).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPRO offers a practical path to mitigate the looming data wall by converting low-quality web data into high-quality pretraining tokens with a 1B rephraser, achieving 87× speedup over 70B-prompted baselines and 2–3× organic data efficiency. The finding that faithfulness rewards are essential (removing them causes substantial performance drops) provides a principled mechanism to prevent the distribution collapse that plagues synthetic-data pretraining. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other corpora (FineWeb, RedPajama), whether more verifiable reward signals (e.g., checklists) can further improve quality, and whether the gains persist at frontier-scale pretraining budgets where the data bottleneck is most acute.
