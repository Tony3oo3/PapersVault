## Problem

Existing Single-Positive Multi-Label Learning (SPML) methods assume the single observed positive label is drawn uniformly at random from the true label set, but real-world annotators systematically select only the most visually salient object, leaving harder-to-recognize categories unannotated. This salient annotation bias introduces an irreducible excess risk term that does not vanish with more data, causing substantial performance degradation in standard SPML methods. The paper formalizes this realistic setting as SalSPML and shows it is provably harder than random SPML.

## Input / Output

Each training sample is an RGB image paired with exactly one positive label (the most salient object), while all other true positive labels are unobserved. The model uses a ResNet-50 backbone to produce a spatial feature map, which is then processed by a multi-head attention module (ML-Decoder style) to yield M class-wise embeddings of dimension D, each projected to a scalar probability. Unlike standard multi-label models that pool to a single global vector, the class-wise embedding design preserves per-class spatial information critical for detecting weakly expressed non-salient objects.

## Method

PiSA constructs L2-normalized class prototypes by accumulating class-wise embeddings from saliently labeled samples, then uses these prototypes in two complementary ways. First, a prototype contrastive loss (PL) aligns each salient embedding with its own class prototype while pushing it away from all other prototypes, strengthening class-discriminative representations. Second, a Prototype-Guided Loss Rejection (PGLR) mechanism identifies potential false negatives by checking whether an unlabeled class's embedding has high cosine similarity to its prototype (top-k criterion), and suppresses the corresponding negative BCE loss to avoid penalizing the model for correctly recognizing non-salient objects. Training proceeds in two stages: a one-epoch BCE warm-up to initialize prototypes, followed by joint optimization of PGLR and PL. Theoretically, the paper proves that prototype matching recovers each missed non-salient positive with probability at least 1−exp(−c(γ−ε)²), so higher-quality prototypes (smaller approximation error ε) directly reduce the irreducible annotation bias.

## Results

On the real-world SalSPML benchmarks (manually annotated), PiSA achieves 59.58% mAP on COCO and 87.83% mAP on VOC, improving over the strongest baselines (LL-R and CS at 70.25% average) by +4.05% on COCO and +1.48% on VOC. Under synthetic SalSPML settings (confidence-based and size-based label selection), PiSA achieves an average mAP of 65.55%, a +3.16% absolute improvement over the best competitor (ML-Decoder at 62.39%). Across all 50 paired comparisons in the synthetic setting, PiSA wins 48 and ties 2, with zero losses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiSA outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and setting, with no losses in any paired comparison. The largest gains are over standard multi-label methods: BCE (47.82% on COCO) and ASL (50.36%) lag PiSA by over 9 and 9.2 mAP points respectively on COCO, reflecting their inability to handle the salient annotation bias. Among SPML-specific methods, ROLE (47.09%) and EM (50.66%) suffer the most degradation under SalSPML, as their random-selection assumptions are violated. The strongest baselines—LL-R (55.28%) and CS (55.33%) on COCO, and GRLoss (86.35%) on VOC—still trail PiSA by 4.05, 4.25, and 1.48 mAP points respectively. On the synthetic benchmarks, ML-Decoder (62.39% avg) is the closest competitor, yet PiSA extends its lead to +3.16% average mAP, with particularly large margins on COCO (+3.37% confidence-based, +3.02% size-based). The consistent 48-win/2-tie/0-loss record across 50 paired tests confirms that the advantage is statistically significant rather than due to variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Single Positive Multi-Label Learning (SPML) line of work (ROLE, EM, BoostLU, GRLoss, CS) by identifying and formalizing a critical gap: all prior SPML methods assume the observed positive label is drawn uniformly at random from the true label set, whereas real annotators systematically select the most visually salient category. The proposed PiSA method repurposes prototype-based contrastive learning (from metric learning) and loss rejection (from noisy-label learning) into a unified framework for multi-label classification, combined with a class-wise embedding architecture inspired by ML-Decoder. The theoretical contribution builds on standard excess-risk decomposition and Rademacher complexity bounds to show that salient annotation introduces an irreducible bias term absent in the random setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of SalSPML as a distinct, more realistic annotation regime is practically important: it explains why existing SPML methods degrade in real-world deployments where annotators are not random. The two newly constructed real-world datasets provide a much-needed benchmark for evaluating methods under non-random missing labels. Limitations include evaluation only with ResNet-50 (no transformer backbones), a fixed warm-up of one epoch, and moderate computational overhead from the class-wise embedding module. Open questions include whether the prototype-guided approach generalizes to very large label spaces (e.g., 1000+ classes), whether it extends to other weak-supervision paradigms (e.g., bag-level labels), and how it interacts with foundation-model-based representations like CLIP features.
