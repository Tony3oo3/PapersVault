## Problem

Discrete flow matching (DFM) cannot use the rectification strategy from continuous flows because discrete paths are stochastic sequences of state jumps rather than deterministic curves, leaving no principled way to minimize transitions. Additionally, the stochastic crossing of paths precludes an instantaneous change-of-variables formula, so there is no exact probability estimation mechanism and no computable perplexity for training or evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source sequence (fully masked, bag-of-words, or multimask with V_s distinct mask tokens) and a target data sequence, coupled via minibatch OT, and produces a generated text sequence over L positions from a vocabulary of V = V_d + V_s tokens. Inference is controlled by the number of Euler integration steps (8 to 1024), and the method also outputs a computable upper bound on perplexity that serves as both a training objective and an evaluation metric comparable to autoregressive and discrete diffusion models.

## Method

The paper derives a dynamic OT objective for discrete flows with convex interpolants that minimizes dissimilarity-weighted outflow between categorical states, and proves (Theorem 3.1) it equals the static Kantorovich formulation with cost c(x_0, x_1) = Σ_i s(x_0^i, x_1^i), establishing a categorical Benamou-Brenier equivalence that justifies minibatch OT optimization. To enable meaningful couplings beyond the trivial single-mask case, they introduce multimask flows (DFM-MMF) with V_s mask tokens forming a 'fictitious grid' that preserves time-independent predictive probabilities while allowing OT. Two upper bounds on perplexity are derived by extending discrete diffusion bounds to DFM via KL-divergence path decompositions and continuity-equation entropy identities. Training uses a GPT-2-sized transformer with Sinkhorn or exact OT solvers, and an EMA mechanism decouples the embedding-based OT coupling from active parameter updates to stabilize training, reducing inference steps up to 32-fold (1024→32) at matched generative perplexity.

## Results

On OpenWebText with a GPT-2-sized model (L=128), minibatch OT with EMA reduces inference steps by 32× (from 1024 to 32) to match the non-OT baseline's generative perplexity (DFM-B-Exact-EMA: 182.40 at 32 steps vs. DFM-B: 185.12 at 1024 steps). The best overall model, DFM-MMF-Exact-EMA, achieves 132.55 generative perplexity at 1024 steps, outperforming masked DFM-N (147.07) and DFM-O (146.54). On standard language modeling benchmarks (L=1024), DFM-N achieves perplexity bounds of 53.19 (LAMBADA), 42.00 (WikiText-2), 111.58 (PTB), 41.64 (WikiText-103), and 77.87 (LM1B), competitive with SEDD discrete diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SEDD (Shi et al., 2024), DFM-N is competitive: it ties on WikiText-2 (42.00 vs. 42.02), wins on PTB (111.58 vs. 117.00), WikiText-103 (41.64 vs. 41.83), and LM1B (77.87 vs. 80.79), but trails slightly on LAMBADA (53.19 vs. 52.18). Against GPT-2 (autoregressive), DFM-N is consistently worse across all five benchmarks (e.g., 53.19 vs. 49.02 on LAMBADA, 77.87 vs. 58.92 on LM1B), reflecting the known gap between flow-based and autoregressive models on categorical data. Internally, multimask flows (DFM-MMF) outperform masked flows (DFM-N, DFM-O) at every step count, with the gap widening at higher step counts (143.48 vs. 147.07 at 1024 steps). Adding minibatch OT to multimask flows yields further gains (DFM-MMF-Exact-EMA: 132.55 at 1024 steps), and the EMA mechanism is the single largest contributor, driving the 32× step reduction for BoW flows. The paper notes that DFM-B (BoW) variants outperform DFM-MMF variants in the few-step regime (≤32 steps) but are overtaken as steps increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends discrete flow matching (Gat et al., 2024; Campbell et al., 2024) by importing the minibatch optimal transport strategy from continuous flow matching (Tong et al., 2024) into the discrete setting, where the standard rectification trick is unavailable due to stochastic paths. The central theoretical contribution is a categorical Benamou-Brenier theorem: a dynamic objective minimizing dissimilarity-weighted jumps is shown to equal a static Kantorovich cost, generalizing the continuous kinetic-energy-to-quadratic-cost equivalence to arbitrary symmetric dissimilarity functions on per-position convex interpolants. The perplexity bounds extend the discrete diffusion bounds of Lou et al. (2024) and Haxholli et al. (2025) to DFM, and the multimask flow architecture is introduced specifically to make OT non-trivial where standard masked flows admit only a Dirac source coupling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32-fold reduction in inference steps (1024→32) to match generative perplexity is a practically significant efficiency gain for discrete flow models, though it is demonstrated only at GPT-2 scale on OpenWebText, leaving open whether the benefit persists at the multi-billion-parameter scale where autoregressive models dominate. The derived perplexity bounds provide the first principled, computable evaluation metric for general DFM models, enabling direct comparison with autoregressive and discrete diffusion baselines without requiring exact likelihood computation. Key open questions include whether the EMA-decoupled OT training stabilizes at larger scales, whether the approach transfers to other discrete modalities (proteins, structured data), and whether the residual gap to GPT-2 on long-sequence perplexity can be closed with further architectural or training improvements.
