## Problem

Existing RL paradigms like GRPO that incorporate fixed intermediate rewards for uncertain responses suffer from a fundamental Advantage Bias: in high-performance regimes, the global group mean pushes uncertain-rollout advantages negative (suppressing honest doubt), while in low-performance regimes, the uncertainty advantage dominates the gradient (triggering reward hacking and avoidance degeneracy). No prior method provides a mechanism to dynamically balance these competing failure modes across varying task difficulties and model capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (open-ended math question or multiple-choice question) and produces a step-by-step reasoning chain followed by either a definitive answer (boxed numerical/symbolic answer or a single choice letter) or an explicit uncertainty marker (boxed{uncertainty} or 'answer: uncertainty'). Responses are classified into three categories—Right, Wrong, and Uncertain—via rule-based string matching on the output format, with vague or partial uncertainty expressions treated as definitive answers.

## Method

UCPO restructures GRPO's advantage estimation through two synergistic mechanisms. Ternary Advantage Decoupling (TAD) partitions each rollout group into a deterministic set (right ∪ wrong) and an uncertain set, computing advantages independently in each channel; the uncertain advantage is defined as γ(q)·Â_right, anchoring the uncertainty incentive to the model's peak deterministic reasoning capability rather than the global group mean, thereby preventing majoritarian suppression. Dynamic Uncertainty Reward Adjustment (DURA) adaptively modulates the gain γ(q) via a dual-term formulation: an uncertainty gain term that amplifies the incentive when the error-to-uncertainty ratio is high, and an uncertainty suppression term that penalizes unnecessary avoidance as correctness improves, creating a self-regulating equilibrium that prevents both overconfidence and reward hacking. Non-Ternary Filtering discards groups lacking both correct and incorrect rollouts to maintain training stability, and Low-Resource Extensions (batch-level smoothing and tanh rescaling) restore the gain's dynamic range under small rollout group sizes.

## Results

On Math & Text Reasoning tasks, UCPO achieves the highest average PAQ of 79.63% on Qwen3-8B and 28.45% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming standard GRPO (75.64% and 20.06%) by 4.0 and 8.4 points respectively. On General Tasks, UCPO reaches 79.68% and 58.58% average PAQ for the two models, again leading all baselines. F1 scores are competitive but slightly lower than GRPO in some settings (e.g., 70.79 vs. 73.00 on Qwen3-8B General Tasks), reflecting UCPO's design trade-off of converting low-confidence correct answers into explicit uncertainty to improve answer reliability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), UCPO consistently wins on PAQ across all model–task combinations: +4.0 pts on Qwen3-8B Math, +8.4 pts on Llama-3.1 Math, +6.2 pts on Qwen3-8B General, and +9.3 pts on Llama-3.1 General. Against GRPO-UC (fixed uncertainty reward), UCPO also leads on PAQ in every setting, while GRPO-UC exhibits severe britleness—its F1 collapses to 4.25–9.01 on Llama-3.1 Math when r_u ≥ 0.5 due to reward hacking (uncertainty ratio surging to ~100%). Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), the integrated DAPO-UCPO achieves 58.90% average PAQ on Llama-3.1 General Tasks, surpassing DAPO alone (54.39%) by 4.5 pts, though at a modest F1 cost (46.79 vs. 54.39). On F1, UCPO is competitive but occasionally trails GRPO by 1–3 points on General Tasks, which the authors attribute to converting chance-correct multiple-choice answers into uncertainty; the PAQ gains confirm that the remaining answered responses are substantially more reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation (Shao et al., 2024) and extends the uncertainty-aware RL line initiated by TruthRL and KnowRL, which assign a fixed intermediate reward to abstention responses. The key departure is a formal diagnosis of why fixed uncertainty rewards fail: the authors prove that group normalization creates a 'Ternary Imbalance' where uncertainty advantages flip negative in high-performance regimes (suppressing honest doubt) and dominate the gradient in low-performance regimes (triggering reward hacking). Rather than tuning a static reward coefficient, UCPO restructures the advantage computation itself through channel decoupling (TAD) and a dynamic gain (DURA), positioning the method as a structural fix to a previously under-analyzed optimization pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap—LLMs making overconfident erroneous assertions in high-stakes settings—and provides a theoretically grounded explanation for why the most natural fix (a fixed intermediate reward) is fundamentally unstable. However, the F1 trade-off is notable: UCPO consistently sacrifices coverage (F1) to gain precision (PAQ), and on Llama-3.1 the F1 drops are substantial relative to baselines, raising the question of whether the abstention rate is well-calibrated or simply conservative. Open questions include how the method behaves as model scale increases beyond 32B, whether the uncertainty expressions transfer to open-ended generation without fixed output formats, and how the rollout distribution dynamics (acknowledged in the conclusion) interact with curriculum or data ordering.
