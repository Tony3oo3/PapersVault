## Problem

Unstructured sparsity naturally emerges in LLM feed-forward layers, yet existing sparse kernels often run slower than dense operations on modern GPUs due to a fundamental mismatch between sparse data layouts and GPU hardware optimized for dense computation. This paper addresses the gap by introducing new sparse packing formats and CUDA kernels that actually deliver throughput, energy, and memory savings at billion-parameter scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes dense input activation rows (M×K bfloat16) and dense weight matrices (gate W_g, up W_u, down W_d) as standard LLM feed-forward inputs. During inference, it produces sparse gate activations packed in the TwELL format (tile-aligned 16-bit values and indices with per-tile non-zero counts) and the final dense output y; during training, it produces a hybrid representation that dynamically routes rows into a compact ELL matrix or a dense backup based on per-row non-zero counts, alongside standard gradients. The TwELL format is materialized directly in the epilogue of the gate-projection matmul kernel, eliminating a separate packing pass.

## Method

The paper introduces TwELL (Tile-wise ELLPACK), a sparse format that partitions the hidden dimension into horizontal tiles of size T and stores non-zero values and indices locally aligned within each tile, enabling materialization in the epilogue of a standard tiled matmul without cross-CTA synchronization. For inference, two fused kernels execute the entire gated feed-forward block: a persistent WGMMA-based matmul that stores gate activations directly in TwELL format, followed by a warp-per-row kernel that fuses the up and down projections by iterating over non-zeros and performing dot products with transposed weight columns. For training, a hybrid format dynamically partitions rows into an aggressively compact ELL matrix (with N_nz an order of magnitude below N) and a dense backup, with custom kernels for hybrid-to-dense and dense-to-hybrid matmuls that route computation between CUDA cores (sparse rows) and Tensor Cores (dense rows). Sparsity is induced via mild L1 regularization on ReLU gate activations during self-supervised pretraining, achieving over 99% sparsity with negligible downstream performance loss, and the kernels deliver up to 20.5% inference and 21.9% training speedups at 2B parameter scale.

## Results

Across model scales from 0.5B to 2B parameters, sparse LLMs (L1=2×10⁻⁵) match or nearly match dense baselines on mean task accuracy (e.g., 48.8% vs. 49.1% at 2B) while delivering up to 20.5% forward execution speedup, 21.9% training speedup, 17.0% energy-per-token reduction, and up to 28.1% peak memory reduction. Benefits grow with scale: the 2B sparse model processes 106 tokens/ms in inference vs. 87.8 for dense, and trains at 27.3 tokens/ms vs. 22.4. At the 1.5B scale, the sparse model reduces peak training memory from 62.8 GB to 45.1 GB (−28.1%) while maintaining 46.2% vs. 46.4% mean accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between sparse LLMs (trained with mild L1 regularization and executed with the authors' custom TwELL/hybrid CUDA kernels) and their dense counterparts trained with identical hyperparameters. On mean task accuracy across seven downstream benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, OBQA, PIQA, WinoGrande, CommonsenseQA), the sparse models tie or lose by at most 0.3 percentage points (e.g., 48.8% vs. 49.1% at 2B), confirming negligible quality degradation. On efficiency, the sparse models consistently win: forward inference speedup ranges from +17.0% (0.5B) to +20.5% (2B), energy per token drops by 11.8–17.0%, and training throughput improves by 7.1–21.9% (the 0.5B model shows a marginal −1.5% training slowdown). Peak training memory is reduced by 19.2–28.1% at the 0.5B–1.5B scales; the 2B sparse model shows +22.3% peak memory only because the memory savings enabled a larger micro-batch size. The paper does not compare against other sparsity methods (e.g., TurboSparse, ProSparse, Deja Vu) in its main results, positioning its contribution as the first to demonstrate practical compute-bound GEMM speedups from unstructured activation sparsity on modern H100 GPUs during both inference and training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ELLPACK sparse matrix format (Kincaid et al., 1989) into a tile-wise variant (TwELL) specifically designed to be materialized in the epilogue of modern tiled matmul kernels, building on the persistent cooperative kernel design of CUTLASS. It departs from prior sparse-LLM work (TurboSparse, ProSparse, Deja Vu, Q-Sparse) that targeted memory-bound GEMV operations or required architectural modifications, by addressing compute-bound GEMM in batched settings on H100 GPUs. The L1-regularization recipe for inducing activation sparsity follows the observations of Li et al. (2023) and Mirzadeh et al. (2023) on natural ReLU sparsity in transformers, but the paper's core contribution is the kernel engineering that makes this sparsity practically exploitable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first practical demonstration that unstructured activation sparsity can deliver consistent throughput, energy, and memory gains during both inference and training of billion-parameter LLMs on modern H100 GPUs, with benefits growing at scale (up to 20.5% inference and 21.9% training speedups at 2B parameters). Open questions include whether these gains persist at much larger scales (tens of billions of parameters), whether fine-tuning existing dense models (e.g., Llama, Qwen) with sparsification approaches can transfer these benefits without retraining, and how to mitigate dead-neuron degradation at higher sparsity levels. The open-source release of the kernels lowers the barrier for the community to adopt sparsity as a design axis, but the current scope is limited to gated feed-forward blocks with ReLU activations on a single 8×H100 node.
