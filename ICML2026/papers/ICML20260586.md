## Problem

Post-training language models via SFT or RL risks degrading previously acquired capabilities, yet the relative susceptibility of each method to catastrophic forgetting and the specific mechanism responsible were unexplored. Conventional intuition suggested mode-seeking RL should forget more than mode-covering SFT, contradicting empiricalirical observations that RL retains prior knowledge better.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260586/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both methods consume text prompts in a chat format and produce free-form text responses evaluated by accuracy on verifiable tasks. The comparison framework measures two complementary metrics simultaneously: target task gain (accuracy increase on the training task) and non-target task drop (average accuracy decrease across IFEval, MMLU, Countdown, MATH, WildJailbreak, and WildGuardTest), enabling a unified gain-versus-forgetting tradeoff analysis across Llama-3 and Qwen-2.5 models at 1B–8B scale.

## Method

The paper identifies on-policy data generation as the core mechanism behind RL's robustness to forgetting through a two-pronged analysis. Theoretically, a mixture-of-Gaussians framework shows that reverse KL minimization (RL) preserves prior probability modes in multi-modal settings by shifting only the new mode toward the target, whereas forward KL (SFT) redistributes mass from the old mode. Empirically, ablations rule out KL regularization and advantage estimation as causes, and an Iterative-SFT variant that regenerates training data at the start of each epoch demonstrates that approximately on-policy data suffices to substantially reduce forgetting at lower computational cost than fully on-policy RL.

## Results

Across Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct models on IFEval, MMLU, and Countdown, GRPO (RL) achieves target task gains of 14.6–60.4% with non-target drops near zero (−0.8% to +0.2%), while SFT achieves comparable or higher gains (11.1–25.5%) but incurs severe non-target drops of 14.6–38.5%. REINFORCE, which uses on-policy data without an advantage estimator, matches GRPO's low forgetting (drops of −0.8% to +2.9%) but lags in target task gains (e.g., 7.5% vs. 60.4% on Countdown for Llama-8B). Iterative-SFT, which regenerates data at the start of each epoch, substantially reduces forgetting relative to standard SFT and Self-SFT while reaching comparable target accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SFT (off-policy data from Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct), GRPO achieves comparable or higher target task gains while reducing non-target drops by an order of magnitude: on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, SFT incurs drops of 27.8–38.5% versus GRPO's 3.4% to −0.5%. Against REINFORCE (on-policy RL without advantage estimation), GRPO wins on target task gains (e.g., 60.4% vs. 7.5% on Countdown for Llama-8B) but both exhibit similarly low forgetting, confirming that the advantage estimator affects optimization speed rather than forgetting. The paper further ablates KL regularization (β=0 vs. β=0.05) and finds that removing it does not significantly increase forgetting, ruling out KL penalty as the source of RL's robustness. Iterative-SFT (approximately on-policy, regenerating data each epoch) bridges the gap: it reduces forgetting to near-GRPO levels while retaining SFT's simplicity, outperforming both Self-SFT (initial-policy-only data) and fully off-policy SFT on the gain-drop tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KL-divergence framing of SFT (forward KL) and RL (reverse KL) from Korbak et al. and Tajwar et al., and draws on the mixture-of-distributions perspective from Kotha et al. to explain forgetting mechanistically. It departs from the conventional intuition that mode-seeking reverse KL should forget more than mode-covering forward KL, showing the reversal holds in multi-modal settings that mirror practical LMs. The paper also positions against concurrent work by Lai et al. (who attributed RL's robustness to advantage estimation) and Shenfeld et al. (who linked it to KL divergence from the initial policy), ruling out both explanations through targeted ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that approximately on-policy data sampled per-epoch can substantially reduce forgetting at lower compute cost than full RL—gives post-training practitioners a concrete, efficient guideline. Open questions include whether the findings hold beyond 8B scale, a formal theoretical proof of on-policy data's role, and extension to the emerging paradigm of continually learning agents where data provenance (on-policy vs. off-policy internet content) directly affects capability stability. The paper's scope is limited to three tasks and two model families, leaving generalizability to broader capability suites untested.
