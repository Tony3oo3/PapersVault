## Problem

Current omnimodal models exhibit a paradoxical modality bias where adding audio to visual inputs degrades reasoning performance (e.g., Qwen3-Omni drops from 72.1 to 68.5 on MMStar compared to its VL-only variant). Existing post-training pipelines (SFT, vanilla GRPO) provide no explicit supervision over intermediate evidence-tracking across modalities, so models can ignore decisive audio or visual cues and still produce correct answers via unimodal shortcuts or dataset biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260601/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an audio-visual video clip (up to 30 minutes, sampled at up to 64 frames via FPS_MAX_FRAMES) together with a text question, and produces a structured reasoning trajectory in the format <time>...</time><caption>...</caption>...<thinking>...</thinking><answer>...</answer>. The time-caption pairs explicitly ground relevant temporal segments before the model reasons and answers, making the intermediate evidence-selection process visible and rewardable without requiring dense human annotations.

## Method

OMNIVIDEO-R1 is a two-stage RL framework built on GSPO (sequence-level policy optimization to avoid high-variance token-level importance sampling in MoE architectures). Stage 1 (Query-intensive Grounding) uses self-supervised rewards: the model generates multiple time-caption pairs and is scored on per-pair consistency (via a Qwen3-VL-235B judger) and on the completeness of the concatenated grounded segments for supporting the question-to-answer reasoning. Stage 2 (Modality-attentive Fusion) introduces a contrastive attention reward that compares the model's soft answer scores under full audio-visual input versus silent-video-only and audio-only rollouts, granting a bonus only when the fused input yields the highest confidence. This forces the model to discover synergistic audio-visual relationships rather than relying on a single modality, all without any process-level annotations.

## Results

On the four primary audio-visual benchmarks, OMNIVIDEO-R1 achieves 82.8 on Daily-Omni, 65.8 on WorldSense, 74.2 on IntentBench, and 62.9 on VideoHolmes, outperforming the base Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct by 19.2, 11.8, 8.5, and 13.9 points respectively. On the more challenging OmniVideoBench (synergistic audio-visual reasoning), it reaches 44.8 average accuracy, a 7.8-point gain over the base model. On visual-only benchmarks (Video-MME, MLVU, LVBench), OMNIVIDEO-R1 shows no degradation and even improves on Video-MME (73.6 vs. 70.5) and LVBench (51.9 vs. 50.2), confirming that mixed-modality training does not sacrifice unimodal performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMNIVIDEO-R1 surpasses the strongest open-source baseline Video-SALMONN2+-72B by 3.4 points on Daily-Omni (82.8 vs. 79.4), 9.3 on WorldSense (65.8 vs. 56.5), and 5.1 on VideoHolmes (62.9 vs. 57.8). Against the closed-source Gemini-3-Pro, it wins on Daily-Omni (+1.7) and IntentBench (+2.7) but trails on WorldSense (−0.6) and VideoHolmes (−4.1). Compared to its own base model Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct, the gains are substantial across all four benchmarks (19.2, 11.8, 8.5, 13.9 points), and it also outperforms the Thinking variant on every benchmark, notably by 17.8 points on WorldSense (65.8 vs. 48.0) where the Thinking variant actually underperforms the Instruct variant. On visual-only benchmarks, OMNIVIDEO-R1 matches or exceeds the base model (Video-MME 73.6 vs. 70.5, LVBench 51.9 vs. 50.2), with only a minor 1.1-point drop on MLVU (74.1 vs. 75.2), demonstrating that the mixed-modality RL training does not introduce a modality trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based multimodal reasoning line (DeepSeek-R1/GRPO, VisRL, GRIT) to the omnimodal audio-visual setting, but departs from prior omnimodal RL efforts (R1-Omni, EchoInk-R1, OmniR1) that either directly transfer vanilla GRPO or design multi-branch architectures. Its key conceptual departure is a two-stage RL paradigm: a self-supervised query-intensive grounding stage (inspired by OpenAI-o3's 'think with images') that learns temporal grounding without process-level annotations, followed by a contrastive modality-attentive fusion stage that explicitly rewards cross-modal synergy over single-modality shortcuts. The optimization backbone is GSPO (sequence-level importance sampling) rather than token-level GRPO, motivated by MoE expert-activation instability in Qwen3-Omni.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode—modality bias, where adding audio degrades visual reasoning in omnimodal models—and provides a practical two-stage RL recipe that consistently improves audio-visual benchmarks without sacrificing visual-only performance. The error analysis revealing that 72% of remaining failures are temporal grounding errors points to a clear next research direction. Limitations include continued reliance on outcome-based ground-truth for reward computation, evaluation on a relatively small set of benchmarks, and the contrastive MA reward's binary condition (full ≥ each single modality) which may not capture graded cross-modal complementarity. The framework's extensibility to additional modalities (e.g., 3D) is noted but not demonstrated.
