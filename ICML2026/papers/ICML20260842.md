## Problem

Existing truncation-based decoders (top-k, top-p, Min-p, Top-H) select candidate tokens using only probability mass and entropy, treating the vocabulary as a set of unstructured categories. This ignores the fact that tokens occupy a geometric embedding space where distances encode graded semantic similarity, so crops that discard tokens near semantically similar retained tokens may be unnecessarily destructive. The paper asks whether incorporating token-space geometry into the truncation decision can improve both accuracy and diversity without sacrificing coherence.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, Top-W consumes the temperature-scaled model logits (converted to a next-token distribution p), the model's input embedding matrix (diagonal-whitened and ℓ₂-normalized to define a ground metric d), and a fixed candidate pool of the top-m=1200 most probable tokens. It outputs a binary mask over the vocabulary: logits inside the selected crop S are kept, all others are set to −∞, and the standard sampler draws from the renormalized distribution q_S. The entire procedure runs as a drop-in logits processor with ~5.4% overhead over Top-p/Top-H/Min-p.

## Method

Top-W minimizes the objective F_{λ,β}(S) = W₁(p, q_S) + λH(q_S) − β log Γ_S over the crop set S, trading off geometric faithfulness (Wasserstein-1 transport under an embedding-induced metric), cropped entropy, and retained probability mass. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) shows that for any fixed 1-Lipschitz potential f, the optimal crop is either a one-dimensional prefix of tokens sorted by the combined score φ_i = f_i + λ log p_i (when β ≥ λ) or collapses to a single token (when β ≤ λ), reducing the S-step from an O(2^n) subset search to an O(n) prefix scan. In practice, the exact Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual is intractable at vocabulary scale, so the implementation uses a cheap geometry-anchored potential f_S(i) = −dist(i, S) (the pointwise-minimal feasible 1-Lipschitz extension vanishing on S) and alternates a few f-steps and exact S-steps. Under the uniform 0-1 metric, the objective provably reduces to Top-k (with a cardinality budget) and to the Lagrangian relaxation underlying Top-H, unifying these heuristics as special cases.

## Results

On GSM8K, Top-W wins (or ties for best) in 13/15 (temperature, model) settings across LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-3B, and Phi-3-Mini, with the largest gap reaching 33.74% over Top-H at T=2.0 (73.09% vs. 39.35% on LLaMA-3.1-8B). On GPQA, Top-W wins 12/15 settings; on AlpacaEval it wins 12/15; and on MT-Bench it wins 8/15. The method adds only ~5.4% runtime overhead relative to Top-p/Top-H/Min-p (e.g., 24.33 vs. 23.10 ms/tok on Phi-3 at T=1.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Top-W is compared against three truncation baselines: Top-p (nucleus sampling), Min-p sampling, and Top-H (bounded-entropy cropping). Against Top-p, Top-W shows the most dramatic advantage at high temperatures: at T=2.0, Top-p collapses to 9.10% (Qwen) and 2.65% (LLaMA) on GSM8K, while Top-W retains 75.13% and 73.09% respectively. Against Min-p, the pattern is similar—Min-p degrades from 75.89% to 49.43% on Qwen GSM8K as T goes from 0.5 to 2.0, whereas Top-W stays within 75.13–75.99%. Against the closest competitor Top-H, Top-W wins 13/15 GSM8K settings and 12/15 GPQA settings; the gap is largest at T≥1.5 where Top-H still degrades (e.g., 39.35% vs. 73.09% on LLaMA GSM8K at T=2.0), while at T=0.5–0.7 the methods are nearly tied (within 0.5–1%). On judge-based benchmarks (AlpacaEval, MT-Bench, creative rubric), Top-W also leads in the majority of settings (12/15, 8/15, and 9/27 respectively), confirming gains in both accuracy and perceived creativity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimization-based truncation family initiated by Top-H decoding (Baghaei Potraghloo et al., 2026), which constrains the entropy of the cropped next-token distribution, by adding a Wasserstein-1 transport penalty computed over a diagonal-whitened token-embedding metric. The key conceptual departure is treating truncation as a geometry-aware distribution-shaping problem rather than a purely probability/entropy heuristic: the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual provides a principled lower bound that, when combined with a nearest-set potential, yields a closed-form prefix-or-singleton structure for the optimal crop (Theorem 3.4). Under a uniform 0-1 metric, the objective provably reduces to Top-k (cardinality-constrained mass maximization) and Top-H (Lagrangian relaxation of entropy-constrained mass), positioning Top-W as a strict generalization of these standard rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretically grounded, drop-in logits-processor alternative to heuristic truncation that consistently stabilizes generation at high temperatures (up to 33.7% GSM8K gain at T=2.0 where Top-p collapses to ~2.6%), while adding only ~5.4% runtime overhead. Open questions include: (i) the alternating f/S procedure is approximate (uses a nearest-set surrogate rather than exact W1), and its convergence properties are not formally characterized; (ii) the method requires access to token embeddings and a candidate pool (top_m=1200), which may not be available in all deployment settings; (iii) hyperparameters (λ, β) were tuned on a small AlpacaEval dev set and their transferability across tasks and model families remains to be established; (iv) the creative-writing evaluation uses only three prompts, limiting statistical confidence in the diversity/creativity claims.
