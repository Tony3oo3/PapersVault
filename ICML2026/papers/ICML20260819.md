## Problem

Modern LLMs operate exclusively at the token level, and while multi-token prediction (MTP) extends the training objective, hidden representations remain token-level. Learning abstract, context-level representations that capture multi-token semantic dependencies while preserving full compatibility with standard autoregressive token-by-token evaluation (e.g., perplexity) remains an open architectural challenge.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard text token sequence and produces next-token predictions via a Token Decoder, while simultaneously generating predicted context embeddings at fixed chunk boundaries (chunk size w=4). The context embeddings are continuous latent vectors derived by mean-pooling token-level hidden states within each chunk, and they are broadcast to all tokens in the corresponding chunk before being added to token representations in the decoder. The architecture maintains full compatibility with standard token-level perplexity evaluation and autoregressive generation.

## Method

ContextLM decomposes a standard Transformer into a Token Encoder, a lightweight 2-layer Context Predictor, and a Token Decoder. The Context Predictor autoregressively predicts the next chunk's context embedding from previously inferred contexts, operating on a shortened sequence of length T/w rather than the full token sequence. Crucially, the Context Predictor is trained implicitly: it receives no explicit prediction loss but is optimized through aggregated multi-token cross-entropy gradients backpropagated from the Token Decoder, which conditions each token's logits on the predicted context embedding for its chunk. This design creates a dual-level gradient signal where the Token Encoder receives both direct token-level supervision and indirect context-level supervision, encouraging representations that support multi-step future generation. The approach shifts the Pareto frontier of scaling laws, matching baseline perplexity with 39% fewer parameters, 23% fewer training tokens, and 20% fewer FLOPs on GPT-2 backbones.

## Results

On the GPT-2 backbone (OpenWebText, 9B tokens), ContextLM reduces average perplexity by 23.73 at Base scale (87.19 vs. 110.92) and by 5.61 at XL scale (25.93 vs. 31.54), while also improving zero-shot downstream accuracy from 37.8% to 39.1% (Base) and 43.3% to 45.0% (XL). On the Pythia backbone (The Pile, 300B tokens), ContextLM-1.4B achieves 9.54 average PPL versus 9.74 for Pythia-1.4B, and 54.4% zero-shot accuracy versus 54.1%. After Alpaca fine-tuning, ContextLM-Pythia-1.4B reaches a 54.48% win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 (vs. 45.52% for Pythia-1.4B) and an MT-Bench score of 2.37 (vs. 1.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard GPT-2 autoregressive baseline (Radford et al., 2019), ContextLM wins on every metric at every scale: perplexity drops by 23.73 (Base) to 5.61 (XL), and zero-shot downstream accuracy improves by 1.3–2.5 points. Against the parameter-matched (PM) baseline that adds two extra Transformer layers to equalize parameter count, ContextLM still achieves substantially lower PPL (e.g., 87.19 vs. 107.77 at Base) and higher downstream accuracy, confirming that gains come from the context-prediction mechanism rather than extra parameters. Against Multi-Token Prediction (Gloeckle et al., 2024) at the GPT2-XL scale with 4-token prediction horizon, ContextLM-XL achieves 14.60 PPL versus 16.72 for MTP and 45.0% average downstream accuracy versus 42.5%, demonstrating that latent context prediction provides a more informative supervision signal than simply extending the token prediction horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContextLM draws on the JEPA paradigm of predicting in latent representation space but adapts it to autoregressive language modeling, where prior latent-space approaches (LCM, Block Transformer) sacrificed standard token-level generation. It departs from multi-token prediction (MTP) by shifting the prediction target from discrete future tokens to continuous context embeddings, and from character-level patching methods (BLT, HNet) by operating above rather than below token granularity. The key architectural departure is maintaining full autoregressive compatibility at the token level while inserting a lightweight autoregressive Context Predictor at chunk boundaries, rather than replacing the token-level objective entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the method's simplicity (a 2-layer predictor with mean pooling) and its full compatibility with standard perplexity and downstream evaluation, making it a drop-in augmentation for existing LLM training pipelines. The 39% parameter reduction for matched perplexity and consistent downstream gains across two model families suggest a genuine efficiency gain in the scaling frontier. However, the evidence is capped at 1.5B parameters and 300B tokens, with diminishing relative improvements at larger scales (0.3 avg accuracy gain at 1.4B), leaving open whether the advantage persists or grows in the >7B regime where most practical LLMs operate. The fixed chunk size (w=4) is another unresolved design choice that may not align with variable-length semantic units in diverse domains.
