## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of stochastic gradient methods under heavy-tailed noise rely on convexity or smoothness assumptions, leaving the behavior of SGD and Clip-SGD in the broader weakly convex setting largely unexplored. This paper asks three specific questions: whether vanilla SGD converges under bounded p-th central moment (p-BCM) noise in weakly convex optimization, whether SGD can achieve high-probability bounds with only polylogarithmic dependence on the failure probability δ under sub-Weibull noise, and whether Clip-SGD can achieve such polylogarithmic δ-dependence under the weaker p-BCM assumption.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume an unbiased stochastic subgradient oracle g(x,ξ), an initial point x₁ in a closed convex set X, a step-size sequence {η_t}, and (for Clip-SGD) a clipping threshold sequence {λ_t}. The convergence criterion is the squared norm of the gradient of the Moreau envelope f_{1/ρ̂}(x_t), which serves as a stationarity measure for the non-smooth, non-convex objective. The resulting bounds are expressed as oracle complexity in terms of ε (target stationarity), the Lipschitz constant G, the noise level σ, the tail parameter p or θ, and the failure probability δ, with the key distinction that high-probability bounds depend on δ only through polynomial-logarithmic factors rather than inverse powers.

## Method

The core technical tool is a generalized descent lemma (Lemma 4.1) that extends the Davis–Drusvyatskiy inequality by introducing a free parameter α ∈ (1,2], producing an error term ‖ε_t‖^α ‖x_{t+1}−x_t‖^{2−α} that can be controlled by p-th moments when α = p, bypassing the need for bounded variance. For in-expectation convergence under p-BCM on bounded domains, this yields an O(ε^{−2p/(p−1)}) oracle complexity that is optimal up to polylog factors. For high-probability convergence under sub-Weibull noise on unbounded domains, the paper applies sub-Weibull martingale concentration inequalities (from Madden & Becker, 2024) to both the quadratic noise term and the cross-term, achieving bounds with [log(1/δ)]^{2θ} dependence. For Clip-SGD under p-BCM, the clipping mechanism bounds the stochastic gradient norm, enabling a high-probability analysis on unbounded domains with only [log(1/δ)]^{p/(p−1)} dependence, matching the rate O(T^{−(p−1)/p}) of SGD while removing the bounded-domain requirement.

## Results

The paper establishes three main convergence guarantees for weakly convex optimization under heavy-tailed noise. For vanilla SGD under p-BCM noise on a bounded domain, the ε-complexity is Õ(G²ε⁻⁴ + σ^(p/(p-1))ε^(−2p/(p−1))) stochastic oracle calls (Theorem 4.2). For SGD under sub-Weibull noise on an unbounded domain, the high-probability complexity is Õ((log^(2θ)(1/δ)σ² + G²)ε⁻⁴) with only polylogarithmic dependence on 1/δ (Theorem 4.4). For Clip-SGD under p-BCM noise on an unbounded domain, the high-probability complexity is Õ(log^(p/(p−1))(1/δ)·ε^(−2p/(p−1))) (Theorem 5.1). When p=2 (bounded variance), all bounds collapse to the standard O(ε⁻⁴) rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical contributions extend prior work in three key ways. Compared to Davis & Drusvyatskiy (2018) and Hong & Lin (2025), which require bounded variance or sub-Gaussian noise, this paper handles the heavier p-BCM and sub-Weibull tails, at the cost of a worse ε-dependence (ε^(−2p/(p−1)) vs. ε⁻⁴) but with polylogarithmic (rather than inverse-power) dependence on the failure probability δ. For Clip-SGD, the paper provides the first high-probability guarantee in the weakly convex setting, whereas Hu et al. (2025) only established an in-expectation result without an explicit rate. In the BERT fine-tuning experiment on SQuAD 2.0, Clip-SGDM with a fixed clipping threshold λ=0.5 achieves training and validation losses comparable to AdamW, while vanilla SGDM (without clipping) shows higher variance across random seeds, empirically confirming that gradient clipping stabilizes optimization under heavy-tailed gradient noise observed in language modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Moreau envelope-based convergence analysis of Davis & Drusvyatskiy (2018) for weakly convex optimization to the heavy-tailed noise regime, where the standard bounded-variance assumption fails. It builds on the sub-Weibull concentration inequalities of Madden & Becker (2024) and the p-BCM framework of Zhang et al. (2020) and Fatkhullin et al. (2025), but is the first to analyze vanilla SGD and Clip-SGD under these noise conditions in the weakly convex setting. The key technical departure is a new Young's-inequality-based lemma (Lemma 4.1) that replaces the E[||g_t||²] term in the standard descent inequality, enabling control of the noise when only a p-th moment (p < 2) is finite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Weakly convex optimization encompasses a broad class of non-smooth, non-convex problems in machine learning (e.g., phase retrieval, certain neural network losses), yet prior heavy-tailed noise analyses were restricted to convex or smooth settings. This work closes that gap and shows that Clip-SGD achieves high-probability convergence on unbounded domains under p-BCM noise, while vanilla SGD cannot—a practically relevant distinction for constrained problems. A key limitation is that optimal step-size schedules depend on the tail index p, which is unknown in practice; the paper identifies adaptive methods (e.g., AdaGrad) under heavy-tailed non-convex noise as an important open direction, noting that no existing work provides such guarantees.
