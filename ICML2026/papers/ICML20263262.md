## Problem

Mamba2's 8× expansion of the SSM state dimension (N=128) creates a severe memory-bandwidth bottleneck during autoregressive inference, yet existing pruning methods fail to address it: unstructured sparsity leaves recurrent states dense, static magnitude-based selection misidentifies important channels (confusing 'phantom' high-activity states with 'corporeal' inert ones), and gradient-based structured pruning requires >45 GB VRAM for a 1.3B model. The paper seeks a structured, backpropagation-free method that can identify truly dynamic state channels at a fraction of the cost of second-order approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained Mamba2 model, a small calibration set (128 WikiText-2 sequences of length 2048, batch size 1), and a target sparsity ratio κ. Rather than merely producing a binary mask, GHOST physically reconstructs each pruned layer with a reduced state dimension N′=(1−κ)N by slicing W_B and W_C weight matrices and reinitializing the layer, yielding a genuinely smaller model. Processing is strictly sequential layer-by-layer: the output activations of each pruned layer feed into the calibration of the next, so the method requires only two forward passes per layer and O(G·N) additional memory.

## Method

GHOST approximates control-theoretic balanced truncation using only forward-pass statistics. For each state channel n in group g, it computes a controllability score P = H² (second hidden-state moment, measuring how much input energy drives that channel) and an observability score Q = (C′)² (diagonal Hessian of output energy, measuring how strongly the channel influences outputs), then defines saliency S = √(mean of P·Q over time, heads, and calibration samples), mirroring Hankel singular values. Scores from all G groups within a layer are pooled and thresholded to allocate capacity adaptively: groups modeling complex dynamics retain more channels while redundant ones are aggressively pruned. The sequential layer-by-layer execution with activation updates mitigates the distribution-shift problem that causes one-shot gradient methods like Taylor to collapse at high sparsity, and the entire procedure avoids backpropagation, requiring only 15 GB VRAM versus 45 GB for Taylor.

## Results

On Mamba2-1.3B at 50% state-dimension sparsity, GHOST achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 14.23 (vs. 13.17 dense), a ~1-point increase, while delivering a ~20% wall-clock inference speedup. At 70% sparsity, GHOST maintains a usable perplexity of 16.16, whereas Taylor pruning catastrophically collapses to 4255. Across model scales from 130M to 2.7B parameters, GHOST is the only structured method that avoids catastrophic failure, achieving 29.06 (130M) to 12.14 (2.7B) perplexity, while Taylor explodes to 1E16 on the 130M model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseGPT (unstructured weight pruning), GHOST trades ~1 perplexity point at 50% sparsity (14.23 vs. 13.25) for a genuine ~20% inference speedup, since SparseGPT preserves dense activations and original matrix shapes, yielding negligible latency gains (57.51s vs. 58.37s for 1.3B). Against Magnitude pruning (static weight-norm selection), GHOST is dramatically superior at every sparsity level (14.23 vs. 29.34 at 50%), directly resolving the 'proxy failure' where magnitude-based scores misclassify 39.2% of states. Against Taylor pruning (gradient-based structured pruning), GHOST is slightly worse at moderate sparsity (14.23 vs. 13.94 at 50%) but decisively superior in the high-sparsity regime (16.16 vs. 4255 at 70%) and on small models (29.06 vs. 1E16 at 130M), while requiring only 15 GB VRAM versus Taylor's 45+ GB and no backpropagation. GHOST also outperforms Taylor on sequence-length generalization (14.27 vs. 1613 at L=2048) and on the Zamba2 hybrid architecture (11.80 vs. 3.0E8 on 1.2B), where interleaved attention blocks destabilize gradient-based scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

GHOST adapts control-theoretic balanced truncation (Moore, 1981) and empirical Gramian estimation (Himpe, 2018; Lall et al., 2002) to the Mamba2 architecture, replacing the intractable Lyapunov solutions for LTV/non-commutative systems with forward-pass statistics of hidden-state energy and output-energy Hessian. It departs from static magnitude pruning (Saikumar & Varghese, 2025), which the paper shows is anti-correlated with actual state utilization, and from gradient-based structured pruning (Ghattas et al., 2025), which incurs prohibitive VRAM costs and suffers one-shot distribution shift. Unlike prior SSM compression work targeting the A matrix (SparseSSM), head-level timescales (PerfMamba), or entire blocks (Mamba-Shedder), GHOST operates at the intra-head state-channel granularity enabled by Mamba2's GQA structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba2 adoption grows, the 8× state-dimension expansion (N=128) creates a concrete memory-bandwidth bottleneck during autoregressive decoding that weight sparsification cannot address; GHOST's ~20% wall-clock speedup at 50% sparsity with only ~1 perplexity degradation offers a practical path to deployment. The 'phantom vs. corporeal state' taxonomy provides a diagnostic lens for why naive importance proxies fail in SSMs, and the finding that Taylor pruning catastrophically collapses on small models (130M: 1E16 perplexity) and at high sparsity (70%: 4255) highlights a reliability gap in gradient-based methods that practitioners should weigh. Open questions include extending the Gramian approximation to non-scalar A matrices (Mamba1, H3), pairing GHOST with orthogonal compression (quantization, distillation), and understanding why exact-recall tasks like Lambada degrade disproportionately (14-point drop) despite stable aggregate zero-shot performance.
