## Problem

Time series captioning (TSC) is a promising pathway to enhance temporal explainability, but existing supervised approaches require extensive high-quality caption annotations that are prohibitively expensive to obtain due to the specialized domain knowledge needed to interpret abstract temporal dynamics. This annotation bottleneck impedes the advancement of TSC in high-stakes industrial, financial, and healthcare settings where explainability is critical for model adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261611/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series segment X ∈ R^{m×L} (m sensor channels, length L ≥ L_min where L_min is adaptively estimated via dual physical/information constraints) along with structured domain knowledge K comprising system knowledge (variable semantics, inter-variable correlations) and task knowledge (operational logs, fault descriptions). It produces natural-language captions that incorporate multiple descriptive perspectives (global/local, quantitative/qualitative, single/multivariable, time/frequency domains) with precise event timestamps, which then serve as the semantic foundation for downstream reasoning tasks such as fault diagnosis or forecasting.

## Method

The proposed Dual Loop Agentic Captioning (DLAC) framework implements a label-free exploration-feedback mechanism. In the inner loop, an LLM-based TSCAgent alternates between a Describing phase (selecting from 42 specialized descriptive tools across 14 categories to generate captions) and a Reflecting phase (self-evaluating caption sufficiency and iteratively addressing identified deficiencies). In the outer loop, a semantic coverage assurance scheme partitions the full time series into evolutionary stages using Fisher-Rao metric arc length on a statistical manifold, guaranteeing a lower bound on semantic coverage; downstream reasoning (RAG-based Q&A) then evaluates the aggregated captions to derive a proxy reward. The proxy reward combines Task Effectiveness (proportion of correct downstream predictions across multiple samplings) and Expression Efficiency (Integrated Gradients-based minimum contribution score of tool-generated text segments), and is used to optimize the TSCAgent via GRPO with LoRA fine-tuning, enabling a 7B model to autonomously acquire temporal semantics without any caption label supervision.

## Results

On the three-phase flow benchmark, Ours(Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves the best caption quality across all four metrics: ROUGE-L 0.330, BERTScore 0.826, FActScore 0.913, and Numeric-Score 0.909, improving over the second-best method by 4.1%, 16.0%, 16.6%, and 24.5% respectively. In the downstream fault diagnosis task, the same model reaches 97.43% diagnosis accuracy (vs. 82.25% for GPT-4o as Agent) and 0.0519 expression conciseness, a 18.5% and 11.4% improvement over the runner-up. Cross-domain evaluation confirms generalizability: Ours outperforms GPT-4o on gas turbine (88.72% vs. 52.37%), medical (89.77% vs. 79.93%), and general classification (83.43% vs. 80.02%) tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 11 baselines spanning three categories. Against pure LLMs (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B), Ours(Qwen2.5-VL-7B) leads on every metric, with the largest gap in FActScore (0.913 vs. 0.738 for GPT-5.2) and Numeric-Score (0.909 vs. 0.717). Against multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen3-VL, Seed1.6-VL, GLM4.5V), the method again wins across the board; GPT-4o is the closest competitor at 0.317 ROUGE-L and 0.711 FActScore, still trailing by 1.3 and 20.2 points respectively. Against caption-label supervised methods (Time-MQA, ChatTS, ITFormer-7B), Ours surpasses even the strongest supervised baseline ITFormer-7B (0.783 FActScore, 0.673 Numeric-Score) by 13.0 and 23.6 points, demonstrating that label-free RL optimization outperforms supervised fine-tuning. In the downstream diagnosis task (Table 2), Ours(Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 97.43% accuracy, exceeding GPT-4o as Agent (82.25%) by 15.2 points and the base model without RL (39.76%) by 57.7 points. Cross-domain results (Table 4) show consistent superiority over GPT-4o on gas turbine (88.72% vs. 52.37%), medical (89.77% vs. 79.93%), and general classification (83.43% vs. 80.02%), confirming broad applicability beyond the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the supervised time series captioning paradigm (Time-MQA, ChatTS, ITFormer) that requires extensive expert-annotated captions, and from rule-based template approaches (Yu et al., 2003), by reframing captioning as an agentic exploration task optimized via reinforcement learning with proxy reward from downstream reasoning. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the RL optimizer and adapts Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2016) to compute a text-segment-level efficiency reward, while introducing a novel theoretical grounding via Fisher-Rao metric on a statistical manifold to guarantee semantic coverage. The dual-loop architecture (reflective inner loop + reward-driven outer loop) is a conceptual departure from both direct LLM prompting for time series and standard encoder-decoder captioning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in time series explainability: the scarcity of expert caption annotations that blocks supervised TSC in specialized domains. The demonstration that a 7B model, optimized via proxy reward without any caption labels, outperforms 100B+ general-purpose LLMs on industrial fault diagnosis (97.4% vs. 82.2%) suggests that domain-specific agentic RL can be a cost-effective, locally-deployable alternative to closed-source giants. Open questions remain around multi-objective reward design when downstream tasks conflict, out-of-distribution domain generalization, and confidence calibration for safety-critical deployment. The released TFTSC dataset (60k+ samples, 14 pattern categories, 42 tools) provides a new industrial benchmark that current methods struggle with.
