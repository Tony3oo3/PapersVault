## Problem

Existing Graph Contrastive Learning methods rely on random augmentations that corrupt high-level semantic structures (e.g., community topology) and rigid one-to-one node sampling that amplifies inter-class entanglement. These limitations cause dataset-dependent performance degradation and quadratic computational complexity in pairwise similarity computations, making GCL impractical for large-scale graphs with millions of nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262710/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(X,A) with node feature matrix X∈R^{N×D} and binary adjacency matrix A∈{0,1}^{N×N}, and produces node embeddings Z∈R^{N×D'} for downstream node classification and link prediction. Unusually, it also constructs a coarsened graph G'=(X',A') with N'≪N super-nodes via METIS partitioning, so contrastive pairs are formed at the community level (community center vs. member nodes for positives; community center vs. other community centers for negatives) rather than at the individual node level, drastically reducing the number of pairs that must be scored.

## Method

CL-GCL replaces random augmentation with graph coarsening (METIS) to preserve community-level structural semantics, then solves a manifold-learning-inspired quadratic program within each community to obtain a closed-form positive-pair weight matrix W^p (ridge-regression reconstruction of the community center from member nodes, followed by ReLU and normalization). Negative pairs are assigned uniform weights W^n across inter-community community centers. A shared GCN encoder processes both the original and coarsened graphs, and a community-node mixed contrastive loss pulls the community-center embedding toward its weighted member-node embeddings while pushing it away from other community centers. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under a Stochastic Block Model with local smoothness, the gap between the true node-level contrastive loss and the proposed community-node mixed loss is bounded by the Frobenius norm of the partition residual and the intra-community feature deviation, providing a theoretical guarantee that the lightweight surrogate faithfully approximates the original objective.

## Results

On 6 homophilic datasets, CL-GCL_METIS achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy, ranking first on 5 of 6 datasets (Cora 82.7%, Citeseer 72.6%, Amazon-Photo 90.5%, Amazon-Computers 80.5%, Wiki-CS 70.9%) and second on Pubmed (81.8% vs. NCLA's 82.0%). On 5 heterophilic datasets, it leads on Squirrel (42.0%), Actor (35.5%), and Cornell (70.4%). For link prediction, CL-GCL achieves 98.6% AUC on Pubmed, a 1.6% relative improvement over prior SOTA. On large-scale Ogbn-Products (2.4M nodes), it reaches 75.7% accuracy, outperforming DGI (65.1%) and BGRL (65.9%) by over 10%, while training in only 10 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-GCL_METIS outperforms all 15 homophilic baselines on 5 of 6 datasets, with the closest competitor being NCLA (Shen et al., 2023) on Pubmed (82.0% vs. 81.8%) and E2Neg (Huang et al., 2025) on Amazon-Computers (80.1% vs. 80.5%). On heterophilic graphs, it beats GRASS (Yang et al., 2025) on Squirrel (42.0% vs. 40.4%) and Actor (35.5% vs. 34.9%), but trails on Texas (71.1% vs. 77.4%) and Wisconsin (68.6% vs. 77.1%). For link prediction, CL-GCL surpasses EPAGCL (Xu et al., 2025) and E2Neg on Pubmed AUC (98.6% vs. 96.8%/96.9%) and matches or exceeds them on Cora (97.6% vs. 97.7%/97.1%). In efficiency, CL-GCL is up to 614.5× faster per epoch than GTCA (Liang et al., 2025) on Pubmed and uses 20% less GPU memory than the lightest baseline E2Neg on Cora. On Ogbn-Products, CL-GCL (75.7%) exceeds supervised GCN (75.6%) and all unsupervised baselines by 7–10.6%, while GRACE and other augmentation-based methods fail with out-of-memory errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

CL-GCL departs from the dominant random-augmentation paradigm in GCL (GRACE, GCA, GraphCL) by replacing stochastic perturbations with deterministic graph coarsening (METIS), and from the rigid one-to-one node sampling strategy by introducing community-level soft sampling via a manifold-learning-inspired optimization. It directly builds on ML²-GCL (Liang et al., 2025a) for the closed-form solution of the local linear fitting problem that computes positive-pair weights, and draws on the graph coarsening literature (METIS, VN, JC, GS) for structural reduction. The community-node mixed contrastive loss is a novel formulation that bridges community-level and node-level objectives, positioned between global-global methods (DGI, MVGRL) and local-local methods (GRACE, GCA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is a substantial efficiency gain (up to 614× faster per epoch, ~98% less GPU memory) that makes GCL viable on graphs with 100M+ nodes where prior methods hit OOM, while maintaining competitive accuracy. The theoretical bound (Theorem 1) under SBM provides a principled justification for why community-level contrastive learning approximates node-level loss, though the constants scale with graph size and the SBM assumption may not hold for all real-world graphs. Limitations include mixed results on heterophilic graphs (only 3/5 datasets best, trailing GRASS on Texas and Wisconsin) and reliance on a fixed coarsening step that is not jointly optimized with the encoder. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or attributed graphs where community structure is less stable, and whether the SBM-based theory can be relaxed to cover heterophilic or multi-scale structures.
