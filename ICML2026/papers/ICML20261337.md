## Problem

Sampling from complex, high-dimensional unnormalized densities requires solving a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) PDE for the score function, but existing neural-network-based PDE solvers (PINNs, trajectory-based methods) suffer from long training times, severe hyperparameter sensitivity, and convergence to local minima. The paper addresses this by replacing neural network approximations with a structured low-rank representation that enables fast, stable, and accurate solution of the HJB equation in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a pointwise-evaluable unnormalized target density ρ_target (no samples from the target are available) and noising SDE parameters (drift f, diffusion σ, time horizon T). It produces samples from the target distribution by simulating the learned reverse SDE, along with the time-dependent score function (optimal control u* = −σᵀ∇V) at each discretized time step. The density is consumed only through point evaluations during a small number of outer-loop iterations, and the output samples are generated by forward Euler-Maruyama integration of the learned control.

## Method

TTD approximates the HJB value function V = −log p_Y at each time step using extended tensor trains (xTTs) — a low-rank format whose storage scales linearly in dimension — and solves the resulting regression subproblems via alternating least squares (ALS) on the TT manifold rather than SGD. The backward-in-time scheme is derived from BSDEs: the time horizon is partitioned into N intervals, and at each interval a Monte Carlo regression loss (involving both V and ∇V) is minimized over the xTT coefficients using ALS sweeps. An outer loop iteratively refines the control by re-simulating trajectories with the updated policy and re-solving the HJB along those trajectories, typically converging in fewer than 3 iterations. Key stabilizing mechanisms include adaptive TT rank selection via singular value thresholds, time-dependent moving approximation domains, affine linear policy extensions outside the training domain, and adaptive regularization magnitudes tied to the H-norm of the value function.

## Results

On the 50-dimensional Multiwell problem (32 modes, δ=2), TTD achieves stable effective sample size (ESS) and log-normalizing constant relative error across increasing time steps N (from 2⁸ to 2¹³), with training times ranging from 10 to 300 minutes. On the 10-dimensional Multiwell problem (8 modes), training completes in 1–20 minutes. On the Ginzburg-Landau (φ⁴) scalar field theory, TTD produces high-quality samples in two distinct multimodal regimes with performance improving as N and outer iterations increase. The algorithm typically converges in fewer than 3 outer iterations and does not exhibit mode collapse, a common failure mode in diffusion-based sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DIS (Berner et al., 2024), a time-reversed diffusion sampler that uses neural network approximations and variational training on the same noising SDE. TTD outperforms DIS on both speed and accuracy: in the head-to-head comparison (Figure 4), TTD produces one result per chosen N and is significantly faster at every comparable runtime, while also achieving better ESS and lower log-variance divergence. The advantage is most pronounced in settings where DIS exhibits training instability. The paper also references comparisons to further sampling methods in Appendix B.4 (Figure 14), reporting significantly improved performance over those alternatives. TTD additionally requires fewer target density evaluations than neural-network-based samplers, since it avoids SGD-based optimization and higher-order derivative computations via automatic differentiation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant neural-network-based PDE solvers (PINNs, diffusion-PINN) for the HJB equation underlying diffusion sampling, replacing SGD-based optimization with alternating least squares over functional tensor train (FTT) representations. It builds directly on the BSDE-based backward regression framework of Richter et al. (2021, 2024) for parabolic PDEs and the optimal-control/diffusion-sampling formulation of Berner et al. (2024), while drawing on the deep backward scheme literature (Bouchard & Touzi 2004; Gobet et al. 2005; Huré et al. 2020) for the time-partitioned regression structure. The key conceptual shift is that the Hopf-Cole transform converts the nonlinear HJB equation into a form amenable to linear-in-parameters regression, which the TT format exploits through its Riemannian manifold structure and efficient ALS sweeps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTD offers a practical alternative to neural PDE solvers that avoids long SGD training, hyperparameter sensitivity, and the need for many target-density evaluations, with runtimes of minutes (d=10) to ~5 hours (d=50) versus potentially longer for neural baselines. The main limitation is the requirement that target densities exhibit latent low-rank structure; for highly entangled or non-separable densities, TT ranks may grow prohibitively. Open theoretical questions include characterizing FTT ranks of HJB solutions in weighted regularity classes (since solutions are unbounded on R^d) and extending the framework beyond the structured benchmark problems (Multiwell, Ginzburg-Landau) to more general sampling targets.
