## Problem

LoRAs trained on base video diffusion models degrade or produce structural artifacts when directly applied to distilled variants (via step or causal distillation), yet the underlying weight-space mechanism of this incompatibility was previously unexplored. The paper identifies that the failure stems from spectral interference between conflicting cluster-level routing patterns established by full fine-tuning and LoRA within shared singular subspaces, and proposes a data-free arbitration framework to resolve this conflict.

## Input / Output

CASA consumes three weight-level inputs: the LoRA factor matrices (B, A) trained on the source VDM, the full source model weights, and the full distilled target model weights. No training data, prompts, or generated samples are required. The output is a new low-rank factorization (B', A') per Transformer layer, obtained by projecting the arbitrated routing matrix back to weight space and re-factorizing. The SVD of the source model is computed once (tens of seconds for 1.3B, ~36 min for 14B) and reused across all LoRAs and target models, while per-LoRA transfer takes ~5 s (1.3B) or ~1 min (14B) on a single GPU.

## Method

CASA operates entirely in the source model's singular subspace. It first projects both the LoRA update and the FFT drift onto the source singular bases to obtain routing matrices C_lora and C_fft, where each entry encodes a sender-to-receiver interaction between singular directions. Singular directions in the top-k subspace (90% spectral energy) are grouped into clusters via a gap-aware perturbation graph that connects directions whose normalized coupling exceeds their local spectral gap. CASA then identifies spectrally dominant routing regions by thresholding FFT routing energy density per cluster (quantile q_dom). In non-dominant regions, it restores LoRA routing by subtracting the FFT drift (C_casa = C_lora − C_fft), yielding exact LoRA recovery with minimal interference. In dominant regions, it computes an over-activation risk score combining a local interaction term (positive only where LoRA and FFT are directionally aligned) with a cluster-level cosine-alignment context factor; entries exceeding a second quantile threshold (q_act) are capped to retain only the stronger of the two contributions, preventing constructive overload along the distilled model's critical generative pathways. The final arbitrated routing matrix is projected back to weight space and low-rank factorized to recover (B', A').

## Results

CASA consistently improves or maintains generation quality (VideoAlign Quality Score) while boosting style fidelity (CSD) across all tested LoRA–distilled model pairs. The most notable gains appear on 1.3B models: for Steamboat-Willie on FastWan2.1-T2V-1.3B, CASA raises Quality from 1.27 to 1.58 and CSD from 78.35% to 81.49%; for Jinx-v2 on the same target, CSD improves from 68.17% to 70.28%. On 14B models the absolute gains are smaller (e.g., Film-Noir on FastWan2.1-T2V-14B: Quality 1.90→2.03, CSD 60.18%→61.52%), consistent with the authors' observation that larger models are less sensitive to routing interference. Against the data-free baseline ProLoRA, CASA achieves substantially higher CSD (81.49% vs. 60.15% for Steamboat-Willie; 70.28% vs. 52.30% for Jinx-v2) while also matching or exceeding its Quality scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is ProLoRA (Farhadzadeh et al., 2025b), a data-free LoRA transfer method that projects source adaptations into the target's subspace and null space. On FastWan2.1-T2V-1.3B, ProLoRA achieves a marginal Quality improvement over Direct Reuse for Steamboat-Willie (1.30 vs. 1.27) and a larger one for Jinx-v2 (1.63 vs. 1.46), but it suffers a severe CSD drop—60.15% vs. 78.35% for Steamboat-Willie and 52.30% vs. 68.17% for Jinx-v2—indicating substantial loss of LoRA style functionality. CASA outperforms ProLoRA on CSD by 21.3 and 18.0 percentage points respectively, while also matching or exceeding its Quality scores (1.58 vs. 1.30 for Steamboat-Willie; 1.51 vs. 1.63 for Jinx-v2, where ProLoRA retains a small Quality edge). Against Direct Reuse, CASA wins on Quality in 6 of 8 settings and on CSD in 6 of 8, with the two exceptions (Jinx-v2 on Rolling Forcing Quality, Steamboat-Willie-14B on FastWan2.1-T2V-14B CSD) showing only marginal differences (≤0.02 Quality, ≤0.9% CSD). The authors attribute ProLoRA's CSD degradation to its uniform projection strategy, which fails to account for the concentrated routing interference in dominant spectral clusters that is characteristic of VDMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SVD-based weight-space analysis of model adaptation—previously studied primarily in LLMs (Si et al., Shuttleworth et al., Meng et al.)—to video diffusion models, introducing a cluster-level routing perspective that frames both FFT and LoRA as structured rotations of singular subspaces. For the transfer method itself, CASA is conceptually related to ProLoRA (data-free LoRA transfer via subspace projection) but departs fundamentally by replacing uniform projection with cluster-aware arbitration that distinguishes dominant generative pathways from non-critical regions. The distillation setting (step and causal distillation via full fine-tuning) is drawn from the VDM efficiency literature, but the paper's contribution is the first systematic analysis of how such fine-tuning reshapes the weight space and why it breaks LoRA compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CASA provides a practical, data-free solution (≈5 s per LoRA on 1.3B models) for a real deployment bottleneck: reusing community LoRAs on distilled VDMs without retraining or user data access. The spectral rigidity and cluster-routing findings offer a reusable structural lens for understanding adaptation in VDMs beyond this specific transfer task. Limitations include evaluation on only two model families (Wan, HunyuanVideo) and a small number of LoRAs, modest absolute gains (especially on 14B models), and reliance on quantile thresholds whose optimal values may shift with architecture. Open questions include whether the cluster-arbitration principle generalizes to other modalities or to iterative/multi-LoRA composition, and whether the routing framework can inform more principled distillation objectives.
