## Problem

Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly improves LLM reasoning but causes severe calibration degeneration, where models become excessively over-confident in incorrect answers. Previous calibration-aware RL methods that jointly optimize accuracy and calibration suffer from an accuracy-calibration tradeoff, and the paper identifies the root cause as a fundamental gradient conflict: the Fisher-metric inner product between the accuracy-maximizing and calibration-minimizing gradients is strictly negative for over-confident models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (math problem or code task) and produces a structured response in the form [reasoning trajectory + final answer <conf> confidence score], where a special delimiter token separates the reasoning block from the confidence block. The confidence score is a scalar in [0,1] explicitly verbalized by the model after completing its reasoning, and the two segments are optimized with distinct reward signals and masked gradients during training.

## Method

DCPO (Decoupled Calibration Policy Optimization) systematically decouples reasoning accuracy and calibration at three levels: (1) generation structure, where the model outputs a reasoning block followed by a verbalized confidence score separated by a <conf> token; (2) reward design, where reasoning tokens receive a standard accuracy reward while confidence tokens receive a calibration reward computed against a hybrid target R_IG = λ·R̃_G + (1−λ)·R(o_r) that interpolates between group-level and instance-level accuracy for low-variance supervision; (3) gradient optimization, where a masked gradient strategy applies the accuracy advantage exclusively to reasoning tokens and the calibration advantage exclusively to confidence tokens, preventing the gradient conflict identified theoretically. The group-level accuracy R̃_G from GRPO's rollout group provides an unbiased, O(1/G)-variance estimate of model uncertainty, and the paper proves that under a strictly proper scoring rule, the decoupled confidence predictor converges to the true conditional accuracy without interfering with policy optimization.

## Results

On the overall mathematical reasoning benchmark (averaged across MATH-500, AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, AMC24), DCPO achieves 60.8% accuracy with ECE 0.128, PCE 0.126, and AUROC 0.881, matching GRPO's accuracy (60.4%) while reducing ECE by 48% and PCE by 65%. On code generation, DCPO attains the highest average accuracy (0.515) across LiveCodeBench v5/v6 and HumanEval+, slightly outperforming GRPO (0.514), while reducing average ECE from 0.287 to 0.175. The paper reports a 71.6% relative ECE reduction compared to the base Qwen3-8B model (0.435 → 0.128) with an 11.8% average accuracy improvement over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (the primary RL baseline), DCPO matches accuracy (60.8% vs. 60.4%) while dramatically improving calibration: ECE drops from 0.248 to 0.128, PCE from 0.362 to 0.126, and AUROC rises from 0.749 to 0.881, confirming that decoupled optimization eliminates the over-confidence that GRPO induces. Against RLCR, which incorporates a Brier Score loss into the RL reward, DCPO achieves 4.3% higher accuracy (60.8% vs. 56.5%) while matching ECE (0.128 vs. 0.139) and PCE (0.126 vs. 0.128), and substantially outperforming on AUROC (0.881 vs. 0.753), demonstrating that coupled calibration losses sacrifice reasoning performance. Against CCGSPG, which modifies GRPO's advantage with token-based confidence, DCPO wins on every metric: +3.2% accuracy, ECE 0.128 vs. 0.230, PCE 0.126 vs. 0.283, and AUROC 0.881 vs. 0.815. On code generation, DCPO slightly trails GRPO on LiveCodeBench v5 accuracy (0.314 vs. 0.320) but surpasses it on LCB-v6 (0.283 vs. 0.278) and HumanEval+ (0.947 vs. 0.944), while achieving the best ECE across all three code benchmarks (0.223, 0.267, 0.034). Overall, DCPO is the only method that simultaneously preserves reasoning accuracy at GRPO level and achieves the best calibration, avoiding the accuracy–calibration tradeoff that plagues RLCR and CCGSPG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from DeepSeekMath, repurposing its group-sampling mechanism not just for advantage normalization but as a low-variance calibration supervision signal. It departs from prior calibration-aware RL methods (RLCR, CCGSPG) that naively couple calibration losses into the RL reward, by formally proving a Fisher-metric gradient conflict between accuracy maximization and calibration minimization, and responding with a structurally decoupled optimization via masked gradients on separate token blocks. The verbalized confidence formulation connects to the broader line of work on explicit uncertainty elicitation in LLMs, but the key conceptual contribution is the decoupling principle motivated by the gradient conflict analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Over-confidence in RLVR-trained models is a critical reliability bottleneck for deploying LLMs in high-stakes domains (healthcare, law, finance), and this paper provides both a principled explanation (mode collapse + gradient conflict) and a simple, effective fix (DCPO) that achieves 71.6% ECE reduction while matching GRPO accuracy. The theoretical results are clean and general, potentially informing other multi-objective RL settings where coupled optimization creates conflicts. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle extends to non-RLVR training paradigms, whether verbalized confidence is necessary versus token-based confidence with masked gradients, and how the method behaves at larger scales or with more diverse model families beyond the 8B–14B range tested.
