## Problem

Decision trees and diffusion models are treated as fundamentally disjoint model classes—one discrete and hierarchical, the other continuous and dynamic—despite both implicitly performing hierarchical refinement of information. No formal mathematical correspondence existed between the two, and no shared optimization principle unified their training procedures. This paper asks whether these two paradigms are in fact two perspectives of the same underlying generative and discriminative object, and if so, what practical algorithms emerge from that unification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263813/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For TREEFLOW (generative), the method consumes a tabular dataset to train a decision tree whose path encodings (root-to-leaf traversal vectors) condition a velocity field; at generation time it takes a target label and a target partition region and integrates the learned ODE from Gaussian noise to produce a synthetic tabular sample. For DSM-TREE (discriminative), the input is a trained teacher tree plus tabular data, and the output is a neural network that predicts the correct branching decision at every internal level of the tree, ultimately yielding a class label by traversing the tree structure using the network's per-level predictions rather than the tree's own splits.

## Method

The paper establishes a bidirectional Tree↔Flow correspondence: (1) a decision tree's nested partitions define a discrete-time Markov chain over densities, and under a dyadic refinement procedure the continuous limit is a deterministic PF-ODE (zero-diffusion SDE); (2) conversely, an entropically homogeneous SDE with a stationary distribution induces a canonical hierarchical clustering via moment-based merger times that satisfy an ultrametric inequality. Both directions are unified under Global Trajectory Score Matching (GTSM), a master objective whose continuous form is the standard score-matching integral and whose discrete form is the sum of per-stage residual losses in gradient boosting; the paper proves greedy boosting is globally optimal for the discrete GTSM via a Bellman-equation argument. Two algorithms instantiate this theory: TREEFLOW conditions Conditional Flow Matching on tree path encodings to partition the generative task (achieving 2× speedup over TabDDPM with competitive fidelity on tabular benchmarks), and DSM-TREE distills complete hierarchical decision logic—every internal split, not just leaf predictions—into a differentiable neural network via per-level cross-entropy supervision, matching teacher accuracy within 2% on most benchmarks.

## Results

On tabular generative modeling, TREEFLOW achieves the highest TSTR accuracy on 3/5 benchmarks (98.1% on Wine, 93.9% on Cancer), the lowest Wasserstein distance on 4/5 benchmarks, and the lowest correlation error on 3/5 benchmarks, while running 2× faster than TabDDPM. For discriminative distillation, DSM-TREE matches its teacher (a DecisionTreeClassifier trained on RandomForest soft labels) within 2% on 4/5 datasets and exceeds it by 3.7% on Heart Disease. Verification experiments on MNIST and 4-Corners confirm that trained diffusion models implicitly encode hierarchical tree structure, with near-identical sigmoidal entropy-decay curves between decision trees and forward diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For generative modeling, TREEFLOW is compared against four baselines from the SDV library (GaussianCopula, TVAE, CTGAN) and TabDDPM (Kotelnikov et al., 2023). TREEFLOW outperforms TabDDPM on Wasserstein distance (4/5 benchmarks) and correlation error (3/5) while being 2× faster, and achieves the highest TSTR accuracy on 3/5 benchmarks (98.1% Wine, 93.9% Cancer). The SDV baselines (GaussianCopula, TVAE, CTGAN) are generally outperformed on fidelity and structure metrics, though the paper does not single out specific per-dataset wins over each. For discriminative distillation, DSM-TREE is compared against a single Base Tree (DecisionTreeClassifier trained on RandomForestClassifier soft labels). DSM-TREE matches the teacher within 2% on 4/5 datasets and exceeds it by 3.7% on Heart Disease, demonstrating that a neural network can faithfully replicate complete hierarchical decision logic rather than just leaf predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PF-ODE framework of Song et al. (2021) and the statistical-physics analysis of diffusion trajectories from Ramachandran et al. (2025), extending the latter to show that the implicit hierarchy is explicitly isomorphic to a decision tree. On the tree side, it reframes gradient boosting (Friedman, 2001; Chen et al., 2015) not as functional gradient descent but as a greedy solver for a discrete score-matching objective, connecting it to the continuous score-matching literature. For practical algorithms, TREEFLOW extends conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) with tree-partition conditioning, while DSM-TREE goes beyond standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) and differentiable decision trees (Kontschieder et al., 2015) by supervising every internal split rather than only leaf outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Tree-Flow correspondence and GTSM framework offer a genuinely unifying lens that could guide future hybrid architectures combining discrete hierarchical structure with continuous generative dynamics. However, the theory relies on dyadic-refinement limiting assumptions and smoothness conditions that may not hold for real-world trees, and the practical algorithms are evaluated only on small tabular benchmarks with continuous features. Open questions include extending the framework to discontinuous processes (Lévy paths, rough paths), scaling to high-dimensional or mixed-type data, and whether the tree-conditioning inductive bias generalizes beyond tabular domains to sequential or multimodal settings.
