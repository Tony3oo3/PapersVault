## Problem

Adapting MLLMs for dense retrieval typically requires invasive parameter updates (full fine-tuning or LoRA) that disrupt the pretrained semantic space and cause catastrophic forgetting of the reasoning and world-knowledge capabilities that make MLLMs valuable. The paper asks whether an MLLM can be converted into a high-quality embedding model by eliciting its latent representations without modifying the backbone at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed through the MLLM's vision encoder and projector) or a text query, each concatenated with a modality-specific instruction prompt and a shared set of N=20 learnable query vectors appended at the sequence end. The output is a single L2-normalized embedding vector per input, obtained by mean-pooling the final-layer hidden states at the query positions. These modality-agnostic embeddings are compared via cosine similarity to produce retrieval rankings.

## Method

SLQ appends a small set of N=20 zero-initialized Shared Latent Queries to the end of both image and text token sequences, leveraging the frozen MLLM's native causal attention so that each query attends to all preceding tokens and aggregates global multimodal context. The same query set is shared across modalities, which encourages alignment in a unified embedding space. During training, the entire MLLM backbone is frozen and only the query vectors and a temperature parameter are optimized via a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss. The key insight, validated by a diagnostic pilot study, is that a single last-token representation suffers from an information bottleneck for complex reasoning queries, whereas multiple learnable queries act as flexible aggregators that elicit the model's internal knowledge and logical reasoning into compact embeddings. This non-invasive design preserves pretrained capabilities (VQA, OCR, grounding) while achieving retrieval performance that surpasses both LoRA and full fine-tuning, with 2–3 orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters.

## Results

On COCO retrieval, SLQ-8B (InternVL3-8B) achieves R@5 of 89.1 (I2T) and 79.7 (T2I), outperforming VLM2VEC-7B (88.4 / 73.8) and all dual-encoder baselines. On Flickr30K, SLQ-8B reaches R@5 of 99.4 (I2T) and 95.1 (T2I). On the broader MMEB benchmark (36 datasets), SLQ-8B attains an Overall score of 67.5, surpassing VLM2VEC-7B (62.9), UniME-7B (66.6), MMRet-7B (64.1), and IDMR-8B (64.9). Critically, SLQ achieves these results with only 144K trainable parameters (vs. 20.2M for LoRA and 7.6B for Full FT) while preserving the backbone's VQA and OCR capabilities (MMMU 62.7, OCRBench 880 vs. 60.9/843 for LoRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dual-encoder retrievers (CLIP, BLIP, FLAME), SLQ-8B dominates on COCO T2I (R@5 79.7 vs. 61.6/74.4/70.4) and is competitive on Flickr30K, with SLQ-1B already surpassing FLAME's 12B-LLM text encoder on I2T R@5 (97.8 vs. 97.3). Among MLLM-based retrievers, SLQ-8B outperforms VLM2VEC-7B on COCO T2I (79.7 vs. 73.8) and I2T (89.1 vs. 88.4), while VLM2VEC retains a slight edge on Flickr30K I2T R@1 (94.6 vs. 92.0). On MMEB, SLQ-8B (67.5 Overall) beats all MLLM baselines: VLM2VEC-7B (62.9), UniME-7B (66.6), MMRet-7B (64.1), IDMR-8B (64.9), and GME-7B (55.9), with the largest margin on VQA (61.2 vs. 58.6 for IDMR-8B). Against PEFT baselines, SLQ consistently outperforms LoRA, Prompt Tuning, and Adapter Tuning on COCO retrieval (e.g., 84.9 vs. 84.4/82.2/83.2 I2T R@5 on InternVL3-1B) while using 100–500× fewer parameters. Most notably, SLQ preserves the backbone's generative capabilities (MMMU 62.7, OCRBench 880 on 8B) whereas Full FT and LoRA degrade them (MMMU 59.8/60.9, OCRBench 847/843), confirming that invasive parameter updates cause catastrophic forgetting of reasoning abilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLQ departs from the dominant paradigm of adapting MLLMs for retrieval via invasive parameter updates (full fine-tuning or LoRA, as in GME, VLM2VEC, MMRet, and MM-Embed) by freezing the entire backbone and learning only a small set of Shared Latent Queries appended to the input sequence. Conceptually, it repurposes the MLLM's native causal attention as a global aggregation mechanism, avoiding the external cross-attention modules used in BLIP-2's Q-Former and the prepended prompt tokens of CoOp/MaPLe. The 'shared' design—using identical queries for both image and text inputs—distinguishes it from separate-query approaches and directly promotes cross-modal alignment in a unified embedding space. The paper also introduces KARR-Bench, extending retrieval evaluation beyond surface-level pattern matching to knowledge-aware reasoning, a dimension absent from standard benchmarks like COCO and Flickr30K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen backbone with only 36K–144K learnable parameters outperforms LoRA and full fine-tuning on retrieval while better preserving VQA and reasoning capabilities challenges the assumption that some degree of parameter adaptation is necessary for task-specific performance, and the gap widens with model scale (3.3% at 1B to 9.8% at 8B on KARR-Bench), suggesting larger MLLMs encode richer but more fragile world knowledge. KARR-Bench provides a much-needed diagnostic for evaluating whether retrieval models genuinely reason or merely match surface patterns. Open questions include whether SLQ generalizes to extreme out-of-distribution domains (medical, satellite imagery) where new visual features must be learned, and whether the approach extends to alternative training objectives beyond symmetric InfoNCE (triplet loss, listwise ranking, distillation).
