## Problem

Reconstruction-trained image tokenizers preserve low-level visual details but lack explicit semantic structure, causing poor generalization to downstream generation tasks (the reconstruction-generation trade-off). Existing remedies that align tokenizer features with dense pre-trained representations (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2) suffer from high-dimensional optimization difficulty and semantic entanglement, where individual dimensions conflate multiple concepts and gradients bias toward dominant features while diluting fine-grained signals critical for generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265395/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer encoder jointly consumes a 256×256 RGB image (partitioned into 16×16 patches yielding 256 patch tokens) and a text caption (encoded via a pre-trained CLIP text encoder and linearly projected to match patch dimensionality), producing N ∈ {32, 64, 128} discrete latent tokens from a codebook of size 8,192 and dimensionality 64. Text conditioning is applied exclusively at the encoder stage rather than the decoder, ensuring the latent tokens themselves are semantically self-contained. For downstream generation, the discrete tokens are decoded by the ViT decoder into a 256×256 image, conditioned on either a class label (C2I via LlamaGen) or a text prompt (T2I via MaskGen-L).

## Method

ConceptTok introduces two innovations on top of the TiTok ViT-based VQ tokenizer. First, a text-integrated encoder concatenates CLIP text tokens, image patch tokens, and learnable latent tokens in a single ViT forward pass, so the compressed latent representation is explicitly grounded in semantic language rather than purely visual reconstruction. Second, a concept-guided training objective replaces dense feature alignment: a TopK SAE (d_c=24,576, K=128) is trained on SigLIP-B/16 vision encoder features over LLaVA-NeXT to decompose them into a sparse, disentangled concept space, and the tokenizer is trained to predict the top-K activated concept indices via a cross-entropy loss on average-pooled latent features. This yields three advantages over holistic alignment—sparsity (sharper gradients on a small active set), low dimensionality (K-dimensional rather than 768-dimensional regression), and disentanglement (predicting individual concepts like 'bird beak' rather than entangled embeddings)—which together produce a more semantically structured latent space that improves both reconstruction fidelity (rFID 1.39) and generation quality (gFID 2.65 C2I, 10.73 T2I).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, ConceptTok-B-L-128† achieves an rFID of 1.39 and a gFID of 2.65 (with LlamaGen-XL) or 2.37 (without classifier-free guidance), using only 128 latent tokens and 533M parameters. On COCO-30k text-to-image generation, ConceptTok attains a gFID of 10.73 with MaskGen-L, substantially outperforming TiTok (12.27) and GigaTok (12.99) while maintaining competitive reconstruction (rFID 2.85). The method demonstrates that concept-guided tokenization with 128 tokens matches or exceeds generators using 256 tokens from much larger tokenizers (e.g., GigaTok-XL-XXL at 2.9B parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GigaTok, the closest semantic-guidance competitor, ConceptTok (533M, 128 tokens) achieves a lower gFID of 2.97 vs. 3.26 (GigaTok-B-L, 622M) and 3.15 (GigaTok-XL-XXL, 2.9B) under the same LlamaGen-B generator, demonstrating that sparse concept alignment outperforms dense cosine-similarity feature alignment even at a fraction of the parameter count. On T2I generation, ConceptTok (gFID 10.73) clearly beats both TiTok (12.27) and GigaTok (12.99), confirming that encoder-stage text conditioning plus concept guidance yields more self-contained latent representations for text-conditioned generation. Against the strongest C2I baselines, ConceptTok's 2.37 gFID (LlamaGen-XL, no CFG) is competitive with Open-MAGVIT2-XL (2.33) and IBQ-XXL (2.05), though those use 256 tokens and 1.5–2.1B generator parameters versus ConceptTok's 128 tokens and 775M generator. ConceptTok trails GigaTok on reconstruction fidelity (rFID 1.39 vs. 0.79–0.81), reflecting the inherent trade-off between semantic structuring and pixel-level detail preservation. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that replacing the concept loss with holistic cosine alignment degrades both rFID (3.84→4.30) and gFID (4.13→4.84), validating that sparse SAE-derived concept indices provide a more effective alignment signal than dense feature matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptTok builds on the TiTok 1D VQ tokenizer architecture and introduces two departures from the dominant paradigm of aligning tokenizer latents with dense pre-trained features (as in GigaTok, REPA, or TokenFlow). First, it replaces holistic feature alignment with a sparse, disentangled concept space derived from a TopK SAE trained on SigLIP features, arguing that entangled high-dimensional features are a poor alignment target. Second, it moves text conditioning from the decoder stage (as in TA-TiTok) to the encoder stage, so that the latent representation itself becomes semantically enriched rather than relying on text at reconstruction time. The SAE-based concept decomposition draws on the interpretability literature (Gao et al., 2025a; Lim et al., 2025) and repurposes it as a training signal for visual tokenization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, well-motivated recipe for injecting semantic structure into image tokenizers without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity, and the ablations convincingly show that sparse concept-space alignment outperforms dense cosine alignment on both reconstruction and generation. The extension of concept-space alignment to diffusion generator training (Table 5, beating REPA) suggests the technique is not limited to tokenizers but could serve as a general representation-alignment objective. Open questions include whether patch-level or token-level concept supervision (currently only image-level via average pooling) would further improve fine-grained generation, whether the approach scales to continuous tokenizers for diffusion-based pipelines, and how sensitive performance is to the choice of SAE sparsity K and the underlying VLM's concept coverage.
