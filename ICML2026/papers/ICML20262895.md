## Problem

The analytic Gaussian mechanism, while optimal among unimodal Gaussians, can be up to 700% suboptimal in expected noise loss in moderate and low-privacy regimes (ε ≥ 1). Optimal noise distributions in these regimes are multimodal, yet existing near-optimal alternatives (truncated Laplace, Tulap) are non-Gaussian, lack Gaussian composition guarantees, and are only asymptotically optimal. The paper asks whether one can close this optimality gap while retaining the composition and tail properties that make Gaussians practical in large-scale deployments.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a scalar query q(D) with known sensitivity Δ and privacy parameters (ε, δ), and outputs a perturbed answer q(D) + X̃ where X̃ is sampled from a mixture of Gaussians. The multi-Gaussian mixture requires two hyperparameters: a modality parameter K ∈ ℕ controlling the number of Gaussian components (2K+1 total) and a discretization parameter η ∈ (0,1) governing the tightness of the DP certificate. The quasi-Gaussian mixture is hyperparameter-free, requiring only (ε, δ, Δ) to determine σ via two bisection searches. All experiments fix Δ = 1 and evaluate expected l₁-loss (noise amplitude) and l₂-loss (noise power).

## Method

The paper introduces two additive noise mechanisms. The multi-Gaussian mixture (Definition 3.1) is a convex combination of 2K+1 Gaussians sharing variance σ², centered at kΔ for k ∈ {-K,...,K}, with mixture weights proportional to e^{-|k|ε}. The spacing Δ and exponential weight decay are derived from a discrete three-level mechanism argument showing that a = Δ is the tight spacing and p_ε/q_ε = e^{-ε} is the tight weight ratio. The DP condition (Theorem 3.2) is verified on a discretized grid of shifts φ ∈ {0, β, 2β, ..., Δ} via bisection search for σ (Algorithm 1), with runtime O(K²/(ηδ) · polylog). The quasi-Gaussian mixture (Definition 4.1) is a simpler two-component density mixing a zero-mean Gaussian (weight e^ε) with a quasi-Gaussian centered at ±Δ, requiring no hyperparameters and computable via two bisection searches in O(log(1/ε) + log log(1/δ)) time (Algorithm 3). Both mechanisms inherit the same zero-concentrated DP composition guarantee as the standard Gaussian mechanism (ρ = Δ²/2σ²), enabling drop-in replacement in DP-SGD and DP proximal coordinate descent pipelines.

## Results

In 142 out of 150 (ε,δ) configurations tested, the multi-Gaussian mixture mechanism achieves a strict reduction in expected l₁-loss (noise amplitude) over the analytic Gaussian mechanism, with a mean improvement of 53.73% (sd 34.86) and median improvement of 61.86%. Against the best non-Gaussian benchmark (truncated Laplace, Tulap, staircase, cactus, or flipped Huber), the multimodal Gaussians deliver strict improvements for all ε≥1, reaching up to ~93% l₁-loss reduction at (δ=5·10⁻⁶, ε=10), while incurring losses of up to ~67% in the high-privacy regime (ε≤0.25). The quasi-Gaussian mixture (hyperparameter-free variant) achieves comparable gains with a runtime of O(log(1/ε)+log(log(1/δ))) versus O(K²/(ηδ)·(log(1/ε)+log(log(1/δ)))) for the full multi-Gaussian mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the analytic Gaussian mechanism (Balle & Wang, ICML 2018), the multi-Gaussian mixture strictly dominates in 142/150 tested configurations, with mean l₁-loss reduction of 53.73% and peak reductions exceeding 98% at (δ=5·10⁻⁶, ε=10). Against the truncated Laplace mechanism (Geng et al., AISTATS 2020), which is asymptotically optimal as ε,δ→0, the multimodal Gaussians lose by up to ~67% in the high-privacy regime (ε≤0.25) but surpass it by 20–93% for all ε≥1, a regime where real-world deployments (Apple ε=4–16, LinkedIn ε≈14.4, US Census ε≈19.6) typically operate. The Tulap mechanism (Awan & Slavkovic, 2020; Awan & Vadhan, 2023), staircase mechanism (Soria-Comas & Domingo-Ferrer, 2013; Geng et al., 2015), cactus mechanism (Alghamdi et al., ISIT 2022), and flipped Huber mechanism (Muthukrishnan & Kalyani, 2023) are all outperformed by the multimodal Gaussians for ε≥1, with the best benchmark among them still trailing by 15–93% in l₁-loss. The paper notes that for ε<1, no Gaussian-based mechanism can match the non-Gaussian asymptotically optimal mechanisms, reflecting a fundamental limitation of the Gaussian family in the high-privacy regime. In a DP proximal coordinate descent ML experiment (deferred to appendices), the multi-Gaussian mechanism consistently achieves the best generalization performance under composition, with the quasi-Gaussian and analytic Gaussian alternating for second place.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytic Gaussian mechanism (Balle & Wang 2018), which optimizes a single Gaussian's variance for (ε,δ)-DP, by replacing the unimodal Gaussian with a mixture of multiple Gaussians sharing the same variance but differing in means (spaced at Δ intervals) and weights (decaying as e^{-|k|ε}). The construction is motivated by the numerical optimization framework of Selvi et al. (2025), which revealed that optimal DP noise distributions are multimodal with peaks at Δ-length intervals. Unlike prior work that achieves near-optimality by leaving the Gaussian family entirely (truncated Laplace, Tulap, staircase mechanisms), this paper stays within the Gaussian family and inherits zero-concentrated DP composition guarantees (Bun & Steinke 2016; Mironov 2017), making it a drop-in replacement for the analytic Gaussian in iterative DP-ML pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world DP deployments (Apple, LinkedIn, US Census, Google) operate at moderate/low privacy (ε ≥ 1) where the analytic Gaussian mechanism exhibits large optimality gaps, yet Gaussian mechanisms remain preferred for their composition properties and tail behavior. The mixture mechanisms close up to 99% of the optimality gap and outperform purpose-built non-Gaussian mechanisms for ε ≥ 1 while retaining identical zCDP composition. Key limitations include restriction to one-dimensional queries, fixed mixture weights (not optimized), and shared variance across components; extending to multi-dimensional queries and optimizing the full mixture structure are natural next steps.
