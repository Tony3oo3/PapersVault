## Problem

MLLMs applied to multimodal emotion recognition produce unstable predictions because sparse emotional cues (subtle facial changes, contrastive text turns) are easily diluted by redundant background context. Existing training-free approaches such as chain-of-thought prompting operate at the input/output level and do not explicitly regulate which internal tokens drive the emotion decision, leaving decision-relevant emotional tokens vulnerable to noise. The paper asks whether MER can be stabilized under fully frozen parameters by explicitly steering inference toward state-constraining emotional tokens.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video-text pair (a sequence of video frames plus associated text) that is serialized by a frozen MLLM into a unified token sequence, with tokens partitioned into visual, text-content, and instruction subsets. It produces a discrete emotion label (e.g., 11 basic/32 compound labels on MAFW, 6 discrete emotions on MER23, or a continuous sentiment score on CH-SIMS) via standard decoding on the refined hidden states. All interventions occur internally at selected trigger layers within the frozen model; no external features, additional encoders, or parameter updates are required.

## Method

WETR is a training-free, plug-and-play inference regulator that factorizes each token's usage weight into a binary selection factor m_i and a continuous reweighting factor γ_i, updated sparsely at every n_r-th layer. NTS (Noise-suppressed Token Selection) acts as an observation filter: it inserts contrastive emotion-seeking (Q_pos) and noise-seeking (Q_neg) instruction spans, scores each token by the normalized difference in attention mass from the two spans plus a cross-modal support term, and retains only the top-p fraction per modality to form a compact emotion-relevant set K. STR (State-strengthened Token Reweighting) acts as a likelihood modulator: within K, it computes cross-modal key-reconstruction error as a predictability proxy, gates it by the NTS score to separate replaceable overlap from complementary residual evidence, and applies a signed migration bias γ_i = 1 + δ(c_i − r_i) to down-weight predictable weakly-relevant tokens while up-weighting hard-to-predict emotion cues, with anchor protection clamping the most confident tokens. The entire pipeline requires no gradient updates and is applied identically across different frozen MLLM backbones.

## Results

On MER23 with InternVL3.5-8B, WETR achieves 68.13% WAR / 63.24% UAR / 68.48% F1, outperforming Zero-shot (60.34/57.36/60.57) by +7.79% WAR. On MAFW with the same backbone, WETR reaches 59.64% WAR vs. 55.08% for Zero-shot (+4.56%), and on CH-SIMS it attains 89.18% ACC-2 / 70.90% ACC-3. WETR maintains near-Zero-shot latency (e.g., 1.66 s vs. 1.71 s on InternVL3.5-8B), while Zero-shot-CoT is 4–7× slower (11.0 s). Across all four frozen backbones (InternVL3.5-4B/8B, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B) and three datasets, WETR consistently achieves the best WAR, UAR, and F1 among all training-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WETR outperforms all four training-free baselines (Zero-shot, Zero-shot-CoT, SparseVLM, SEPM) on every metric across all four frozen backbones and three datasets. The largest gains are on MER23: +7.79% WAR over Zero-shot and +4.87% over SEPM on InternVL3.5-8B, and +4.01% WAR over Zero-shot on Qwen3-VL-8B. WETR also surpasses Zero-shot-CoT in accuracy while being 4–7× faster (e.g., 1.66 s vs. 11.02 s on InternVL3.5-8B), since CoT requires multi-step generation. Against fine-tuning methods, WETR (training-free) matches or exceeds Emotion-LLaMA (78.61 F1 on CH-SIMS) and MoSE (57.24 F1 on MER23) on several metrics without any parameter updates. On MAFW, WETR with InternVL3.5-4B (60.96 WAR) surpasses the fine-tuned Emotion-LLaMA and MoSE, demonstrating that inference-time token refinement can rival or beat supervised approaches at a fraction of the cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of MER work: fine-tuning-based MLLM approaches (EmotionLLaMA, AffectGPT, R1-Omni) that modify model parameters, and training-free inference strategies (prompt engineering, CoT, SEPM, SparseVLM) that operate at the input/output level. WETR departs from both by intervening directly on internal token usage at inference time, framed through a world-model lens that treats emotion as a latent state to be inferred from noisy observations. The contrastive attention scoring (positive vs. negative emotion queries) for token selection and the cross-modal predictability-based reweighting are new mechanisms not present in prior token-pruning or prompting methods, though they build on the general idea of attention-guided token importance established in SparseVLM and SEPM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of WETR makes it immediately deployable with any frozen MLLM backbone, addressing a practical gap where fine-tuning is costly or infeasible. The world-model formulation provides a principled justification for why token-level intervention should help, and the robustness results under amplified noise (9.18% F1 gain at ε=7) suggest the approach could be valuable in real-world settings with heavy background redundancy. However, the method is specifically designed for emotion recognition and its gains, while consistent across four backbones and three datasets, are moderate (typically 2–5% WAR/F1); whether the same token-refinement strategy transfers to other fine-grained perception tasks with sparse cues remains an open question. The irreversibility of NTS's hard token dropping, even if mitigated by STR, also raises questions about information loss in edge cases.
