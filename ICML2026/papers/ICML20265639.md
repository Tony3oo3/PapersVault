## Problem

Selective thinking methods that route queries between thinking and non-thinking LLMs lack rigorous statistical guarantees on performance loss, particularly in online settings where queries arrive sequentially, feedback is partial (loss is only observed when the thinking model is invoked), and data may be non-stationary. Existing offline PAC reasoning requires an i.i.d. calibration set and a fixed threshold, making it inapplicable to streaming deployment. The paper asks: how to construct an anytime-valid routing rule that controls performance loss below a user-specified level at every time step, without offline calibration, under partial feedback and distribution shift.

## Input / Output

At each time step, the method receives a text query, runs it through the non-thinking model to obtain a response and a scalar uncertainty score (e.g., 1 minus the average token probability from logits), and produces a binary routing decision: either accept the non-thinking model's output or invoke the thinking model. The performance loss is measured relative to the thinking model's output (not ground truth), using a bounded loss in [0,1] such as 0-1 loss for verifiable tasks or an LLM-judge score for open-ended generation. The system maintains a time-varying threshold over a discretized grid U = {0, 0.001, ..., 1} and a wealth process per threshold, updated with negligible per-step overhead (~0.046s for 1000 requests).

## Method

B-PAC reasoning formulates the routing threshold as a betting game: for each candidate threshold u, an inverse propensity scoring (IPS) estimator corrects the selection bias from partial feedback, and a nonnegative supermartingale (wealth process) accumulates evidence of safety. The threshold is selected via fixed sequence testing (i.i.d. case) or a mixture-of-martingales rule with a prior over thresholds (non-stationary case), choosing the largest u whose wealth exceeds 1/α (or 1/(αν(u))). An adaptive betting strategy, derived from online convex optimization on a quadratic surrogate of log-wealth, achieves O(log T) regret against the optimal fixed bet, enabling rapid convergence to the most efficient safe threshold. A two-stage exploration schedule (high ρ_warm during warm-up, low ρ_deploy during deployment) balances fast initial threshold identification against long-term routing efficiency, reducing thinking-model invocations by up to 81% while maintaining anytime-valid risk control.

## Results

On Magpie with ε=0.08 and α=0.1, B-PAC routes only 18.99% of queries to the thinking model (ECP=18.99%) and achieves 41.37% token savings (TP=58.63%) while keeping empirical risk below ε. On MATH, B-PAC achieves ER=0.03±0.01 with TP=68.16%±14.56%, and on MMLU-Pro, ER=0.03±0.01 with TP=77.24%±9.94%. Under non-stationary settings (MMLU-Pro→BBH shift), B-PAC maintains anytime-valid risk control while the offline PAC baseline fails to adapt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the offline PAC reasoning baseline (Zeng et al., 2025), B-PAC achieves substantially higher efficiency (ECP=18.99% on Magpie vs. negligible gains for PAC) while maintaining anytime-valid safety; PAC's fixed threshold calibrated on a harder dataset (BBH) remains overly conservative on easier streaming data, and it fails entirely under non-stationary distribution shifts. Against Chain of Draft (CoD, Xu et al., 2025), B-PAC achieves dramatically lower empirical risk (0.03 vs. 0.1204 on MATH, 0.03 vs. 0.10 on MMLU-Pro) and lower token consumption on MATH (TP=68.16% vs. 95.63%), while CoD violates the ε=0.08 risk tolerance. Against NoThinking (Ma et al., 2025), B-PAC again achieves lower risk (0.03 vs. 0.1577 on MATH, 0.03 vs. 0.12 on MMLU-Pro) and competitive or better token efficiency, whereas NoThinking also exceeds the risk tolerance. Among online baselines, O-naive (which ignores partial feedback) violates risk, and IPS+Hoeff (which uses Hoeffding's inequality) is safe but achieves near-100% expert usage, making it impractical; B-PAC overcomes both limitations by combining IPS estimation with a tight betting supermartingale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PAC reasoning (Zeng et al., 2025), which provides offline risk control for selective thinking via UCB and Learn-then-Test, to the online setting with partial feedback and non-stationary data. The key technical departure is replacing the calibration-set-based UCB with an inverse propensity scoring (IPS) estimator embedded in a betting supermartingale, combined with fixed sequence testing for threshold selection. The anytime-valid safety guarantee is inherited from the e-process/supermartingale literature (Shafer & Vovk, 2019; Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2024), while the non-stationary extension leverages mixture-of-martingales techniques. The adaptive betting strategy for threshold updates is formulated as an online convex optimization problem, connecting the statistical framework to regret minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

B-PAC provides the first theoretically grounded, model-agnostic method for safe efficient reasoning in online and non-stationary settings, addressing the critical practical challenge of partial feedback that renders standard risk estimators biased. The 81% reduction in thinking model usage while maintaining anytime-valid risk control has direct implications for deploying LRMs in latency-sensitive interactive applications. Key limitations include restriction to two-model routing (extension to multiple models is open), dependence on uncertainty score quality for efficiency (though not safety), and the absence of a conditional variant. The finding that IPS combined with Hoeffding's inequality yields near-100% expert usage—rendering it practically useless—highlights that the betting framework's tightness is not merely theoretical but essential for deployment.
