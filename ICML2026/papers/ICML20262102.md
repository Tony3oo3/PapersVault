## Problem

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) train on an exponentially large set of i.i.d. random masking patterns, yet at inference the unmasking policy (e.g., Top-K confidence) visits only a tiny, highly structured subset of those patterns. This train-test mismatch wastes compute on irrelevant masking configurations and slows convergence. The paper asks whether redesigning the forward masking process itself—rather than applying architectural or recipe-level fixes—can resolve this mismatch and accelerate training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262102/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model starts from a fully masked length-L sequence and iteratively reveals tokens according to a confidence-based unmasking policy, producing a generated text or code sequence. During training, PUMA generates partially masked contexts via a teacher-forced chain that progressively reveals ground-truth tokens from a clean sample x₀, with the prompt region kept unmasked throughout for prompt-based tasks. The model is a bidirectional transformer (Qwen2-style attention) that maps a partially masked sequence to per-position categorical distributions over the vocabulary; sequence lengths range from 81 (Sudoku) to 512 (TinyGSM) and 640 (Dream-Coder fine-tuning).

## Method

PUMA replaces the standard i.i.d. random masking with a teacher-forced chain: starting from a fully masked state, it applies the same unmasking policy g_φ used at inference to select which positions to reveal next, but reveals the ground-truth tokens from x₀ rather than sampling from the model's posterior. Two theoretical guarantees make this safe: (i) marginal agreement—the distribution over intermediate masked sequences under the teacher-forced chain matches the inference-time distribution under the same policy; and (ii) minimizer preservation—because the forward process retains the form p(x_t=z|x₀,t) ∝ α_t(z)·1{x₀^{um(z)}=z^{um(z)}}, the cross-entropy loss's unique minimizer remains the ground-truth unmasking posterior. In practice, PUMA is implemented as a streaming buffer of B concurrent chains with K-scheduling (large K early for diversity, decreasing K later for policy fidelity) and confidence-based fast-forwarding (skipping trivially confident positions), introducing no extra forward-pass overhead since the loss logits double as confidence scores. Empirically, PUMA achieves 2.3× speedup at 125M scale and 4.0× with autoregressive initialization, while remaining complementary to block diffusion and effective in 7B-scale LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

PUMA accelerates 125M-scale MDM pretraining on TinyGSM by up to 2.3× in iteration-to-accuracy (GSM8K test), with no additional forward-pass overhead. Combined with autoregressive initialization, the speedup increases to approximately 4.0×. On the 7B-scale Dream-Coder fine-tuning (LoRA rank 128), PUMA improves HumanEval Pass@1 by ~10 percentage points over vanilla MDM fine-tuning (which gains at most 3%) and MBPP by ~5 percentage points. Across all inference policies tested (Top-K, Top-K margin, entropy-based, temperature sampling), PUMA consistently outperforms the MDM baseline, e.g., 39.1% vs 33.6% GSM8K accuracy under Top-2 decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard MDM baseline (Sahoo et al., 2024), PUMA wins uniformly: 2.3× iteration-accuracy speedup on TinyGSM, 1.7× on Sudoku (90.1% final accuracy), and consistent 4–6 pp accuracy gains across all inference policies in Table 1. Against PAPL (Peng et al., 2025b), which reweights the cross-entropy loss by model confidence while retaining random masking, PUMA clearly outperforms: the authors report that PAPL 'does not help in training on either of our datasets, and often even hurts performance,' a trend consistent across α∈{1,3,5} and both the MDM baseline and PUMA. Against block diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025), PUMA is complementary rather than competitive: when combined with block-size warmup (block size 256), PUMA still yields additional speedup, confirming that the two methods operate on orthogonal axes (forward-process design vs. architectural constraint). With autoregressive initialization, PUMA's advantage over the baseline widens from 2.3× to 4.0×, suggesting the train-test mismatch is more acute when the model starts from a causal prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

PUMA builds directly on the Masked Diffusion Model (MDM) framework of Lou et al. (2023), Shi et al. (2024), and Sahoo et al. (2024), modifying the forward masking process that defines how training examples are constructed. It departs from prior MDM training-efficiency work—which has focused on architectural changes (block diffusion), recipe-level remedies (autoregressive initialization, block-size curriculum), or loss reweighting (PAPL)—by intervening at the level of the forward process itself, making it orthogonal and composable with those approaches. Conceptually, the teacher-forced chain is related to self-forcing in video diffusion (Huang et al., 2025), where inference-style rollouts reduce the train-inference gap, but PUMA provides a discrete, provably minimizer-preserving instantiation specific to masked diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUMA resolves a fundamental train-test mismatch in MDMs with a simple, provably correct modification that yields 2.3× pretraining speedup at 125M scale and 4.0× when combined with autoregressive initialization, while introducing only one hyperparameter and no forward-pass overhead. The theoretical sample-complexity separation (exponential vs. linear) on a simplified distribution provides a clean explanation for why the speedup arises. Key limitations include evaluation only at 125M pretraining and 7B post-training scales, and the requirement of multiple forward passes per sample, which precludes single-epoch training. Open questions include whether the speedup persists at 7B+ pretraining scale and whether the approach extends to continuous or multimodal masked diffusion settings.
