## Problem

Prior LLP theory (Busa-Fekete et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024; Busa-Fekete et al. 2025) is restricted to binary classification and, in the fast-rate results, to square loss, with estimator variance growing linearly in bag size k. This paper closes that gap by providing a unified debiasing framework that handles arbitrary (including unbounded) loss functions in both binary and multi-class settings, achieving variance independent of k and thereby optimal sample complexity rates.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives m non-overlapping bags, each containing k feature vectors in the clear plus a single aggregate label signal: a scalar proportion in the binary case, a c-dimensional histogram vector in the full-histogram multi-class case, or a single fractional count in the total multi-class case. The output is a hypothesis h in a given class H that minimizes the individual-level population loss E[ℓ(h(x),y)], i.e., the model must produce per-instance predictions (e.g., conversion probabilities) despite never observing individual labels. In experiments, the method is instantiated with binary cross-entropy and trained via Adam on CNNs (MNIST, CIFAR-10), fully-connected nets (Higgs, Adult), and a deep embedding network (Criteo).

## Method

The key insight is that any binary loss decomposes as ℓ(h(x),y) = f₁(h(x)) + y·f₂(h(x)), enabling a bag-level estimator ℓ_b(h,z) built from the centered product (α−p)(Σᵢ f₂(h(xᵢ)) − k·E[f₂]), whose variance is O(1) in k rather than O(k) as in prior work. To handle unbounded losses (e.g., log loss) and remove dependence on unknown p and expectations, the dataset is split into three equal subsets: S₁ estimates E[Δf₁] and E[Δf₂] via Median-of-Means, S₂ estimates the label marginal p, and S₃ feeds a MoM tournament (Algorithm 1) that iteratively eliminates hypotheses whose estimated loss difference exceeds a threshold β/2. For realizable settings, a 'quadratically sandwiched' curvature condition on the loss yields fast O(k/β) sample complexity, extending the optimal rate previously known only for square loss. In the multi-class setting, the same centering generalizes to c loss components for full-histogram labels, while the total multi-class case requires the loss to be affine in y (e.g., Poisson log loss), incurring an extra c² variance factor.

## Results

The paper evaluates log loss (the primary metric, aligned with conversion prediction in advertising) across bag sizes k = 2^0 to 2^11 on four batch datasets (MNIST Even-vs-Odd, CIFAR-10 Animal-vs-Machine and Cat-vs-Rest, Higgs, Adult) and one online dataset (Criteo Display Advertising Challenge). The key finding is that the proposed GENERALUPM loss consistently outperforms both baselines (EASYLLP and PM) at larger bag sizes (k ≥ 2^6), where label information is most obfuscated, while PM retains an edge at small k in near-realizable settings. On the Criteo online experiment at bag size 2^11, GENERALUPM achieves lower per-chunk log loss over the entire training duration compared to both baselines. Theoretical guarantees show sample complexity of Õ(k/β²) in the non-realizable setting and Õ(k/β) in the realizable setting, improving over the Õ(k²/β²) of Busa-Fekete et al. (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GENERALUPM against two baselines: EASYLLP (Busa-Fekete et al., 2023) and PM (Proportion Matching, Yu et al., 2013). At small bag sizes (k ≤ 2^4), PM consistently achieves the lowest log loss, which the authors attribute to PM approximating Bayes-optimal odds in realizable scenarios with proper losses. As k increases, PM's bias grows and its performance degrades relative to GENERALUPM, which remains unbiased across all bag sizes. Except on the easy MNIST Even-vs-Odd task, GENERALUPM consistently outperforms both EASYLLP and PM at larger k (≥ 2^6) across all batch datasets. On the Criteo online experiment, the gap widens further at large k: GENERALUPM is 'much better' than both baselines at bag size 2^11, maintaining lower log loss throughout the entire streaming training period. EASYLLP, which uses an unbiased estimator whose variance grows linearly with k, is uniformly outperformed by GENERALUPM at larger bag sizes, confirming the theoretical advantage of the k-independent variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the LLP reduction framework of Busa-Fekete et al. (2023) by introducing a centered bag-level estimator whose variance is independent of bag size k, in contrast to the linear-in-k variance of the prior unbiased estimator. The centering technique is inspired by Busa-Fekete et al. (2025), who applied it specifically to square loss; here it is generalized to arbitrary (including unbounded) losses via a Median-of-Means tournament (Lugosi & Mendelson, 2016) to handle heavy-tailed losses like log loss. The paper also provides the first unified theoretical treatment of multi-class LLP, distinguishing full-histogram and total aggregate label scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The k-independent variance yields optimal sample complexity rates (k/n realizable, √(k/n) non-realizable) for a broad class of practical losses, directly addressing the large-bag regime relevant to privacy-preserving ad conversion prediction. However, the total multi-class case requires an affine loss form (limiting applicability to exponential-family losses), the full-histogram multi-class case lacks a fast-rate analysis, and experiments are restricted to binary classification. The c² regret scaling in the total multi-class setting and the tightness of multi-class bounds remain open questions.
