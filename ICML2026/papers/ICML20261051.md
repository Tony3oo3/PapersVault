## Problem

In RLHF and DPO pipelines, human feedback providers are rarely an i.i.d. sample: recruitment and exposure propagate through social, institutional, or spatial networks, inducing inclusion probabilities that correlate with graph centrality. Standard identity-agnostic scalar reward modeling implicitly optimizes an inclusion-weighted welfare that over-represents structurally central communities, rather than a designer-chosen target welfare (e.g., equal-per-person or equal-per-community). This paper formalizes the resulting welfare gap and asks how to debias the learned reward toward an explicit normative target.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference tuples (x, y⁺, y⁻, i) that explicitly retain annotator identity i (unlike standard reward modeling which discards it), an annotator graph G=(V,E) encoding social/institutional/spatial ties, a target welfare weighting π, and inclusion probability estimates q̂ derived from participation counts with smoothing. It produces per-annotator personalized reward scores r_θ(x,y,i), a single aggregated scalar score r̄_θ(x,y) = Σ π̃ᵢ r_θ(x,y,i) where π̃ = q̂ ⊙ α* is the induced welfare distribution, and the stabilized importance weights α* themselves, which can also reweight downstream DPO/PPO objectives.

## Method

Graph-Preference Learning combines two components. First, Graph-Personalized Reward Modeling (GPRM) computes graph-aware annotator embeddings via a GCN/GraphSAGE-style message-passing encoder and learns a personalized reward r_θ(x,y,i) = f_θ(φ(x,y), vᵢ) using an importance-weighted Bradley-Terry loss with a graph Laplacian smoothness regularizer that shares statistical strength across neighboring annotators. Second, Graph-Balanced Aggregation (GBA) solves a convex quadratic program to compute stabilized importance weights α* that jointly minimize deviation from raw inverse-propensity ratios πᵢ/q̂ᵢ, graph smoothness (αᵀL_wα), community-mass mismatch, and weight variance (ESS control), subject to non-negativity and normalization constraints. The key insight is that the induced welfare π̃ = q̂ ⊙ α* approximates the target π, so the aggregated reward r̄_θ represents the designer-chosen welfare rather than the observed inclusion-weighted welfare; a theoretical bound (Proposition 4.3) shows the welfare deviation is at most R‖π − π̃‖₁, decomposing the error into weight mismatch, reward-model approximation, and finite-sample effects.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic LMArena evaluation (biased training via network sampling, π-balanced test), GPRM+GBA achieves 63.6% pairwise accuracy vs. 55.8% for Standard reward modeling (+18% relative improvement), reduces the max-min cross-language accuracy gap from 46.0% to 38.4% (17% reduction), and improves pairwise log loss from 1.110 to 0.691. On synthetic SBM graphs, the method delivers up to 62% welfare-MSE reduction under community-biased sampling and 35% under uniform sampling, while reducing degree bias from 0.900 to 0.354 under degree-biased inclusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPRM+GBA outperforms all six baselines on pairwise accuracy, log loss, and cross-language gap in the LMArena evaluation. Against Standard reward modeling (Ouyang et al., 2022), it gains +7.8 pp accuracy (63.6% vs. 55.8%) and cuts the cross-language gap by 7.6 pp. The Personalized baseline (Li et al., 2024b) matches GPRM+GBA on log loss (0.692 vs. 0.691) and achieves lower ECE (0.058 vs. 0.119), but trails by 6.7 pp in accuracy and 5.7 pp in gap, showing that personalization alone cannot correct representation bias. The stabilized IPW variants (SN-IPW, Clipped-IPW from Chandak et al., 2021) and the QP-stabilized GBA-QP (Hainmueller, 2012) all reach ~60% accuracy but suffer large cross-language gaps (62.1–62.5%), indicating that reweighting without graph-personalized modeling amplifies variance. Group-Based reweighting (Chakraborty et al., 2024) and DRO-CVaR (Rahimian & Mehrotra, 2019) perform worst on accuracy (52.9% and 58.9% respectively), with Group-Based actually degrading below Standard. The ablation showing GPRM alone (54.3% accuracy, 45.3% gap) vs. the full method (63.6%, 38.4%) confirms that the debiasing gain comes specifically from the GBA component, not from personalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard RLHF/DPO reward modeling (Ouyang et al., 2022; Rafailov et al., 2023) by explicitly modeling annotators as nodes in a graph and showing that identity-agnostic scalar reward modeling implicitly optimizes an inclusion-weighted welfare rather than a designer-chosen target. It draws on network sampling theory (Wejnert, 2010; Lerman et al., 2016) and inverse propensity weighting (Chandak et al., 2021; Hainmueller, 2012) to motivate and stabilize debiasing, while incorporating graph neural network personalization (GCN/GraphSAGE-style message passing) to share statistical strength across neighboring annotators. The key departure from prior personalized-RLHF work (Li et al., 2024b; Poddar et al., 2024) and group-robust alignment (Chakraborty et al., 2024; Ramesh et al., 2024) is the explicit treatment of the annotator graph as the source of sampling bias, rather than treating annotators as i.i.d. draws or coarse demographic groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes the welfare target π an explicit, auditable design choice in RLHF pipelines and provides a clean theoretical decomposition of welfare error into weight mismatch, model approximation, and finite-sample terms. However, the LMArena evaluation is semi-synthetic (bias is induced via graph-based subsampling rather than arising from genuine networked recruitment), and the method requires a known or estimable annotator graph, limiting immediate practical deployment. Open questions include end-to-end policy optimization under the debiased reward, evaluation on data collected under genuine networked exposure, and how to construct meaningful annotator graphs in settings where social ties are implicit or unavailable.
