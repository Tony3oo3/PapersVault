## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for point clouds predominantly operate on input token prompting or low-rank decomposition, overlooking the intrinsic geometric and topological structures (surface connectivity, hole distributions, local curvature) that are critical for robust 3D understanding. This oversight limits model expressiveness under challenging conditions such as noise, occlusion, and deformation, as demonstrated by t-SNE visualizations showing degraded feature separability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264652/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw point clouds (2048 points per sample, 3D coordinates) and produces class labels for classification (ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40) or per-point part labels for segmentation (ShapeNetPart). Internally, the input is processed via farthest point sampling to select G centroids, followed by K-nearest-neighbor (K=30) neighborhood construction to build local simplicial complexes. The framework is plug-and-play across pre-trained backbones (Point-MAE, ReCon, PointGPT-L), training only 0.5M–1.9M parameters (0.53%–2.26% of the full model).

## Method

TopAdapter introduces two modules grounded in algebraic topology. The Topology Injection (ToInjection) module constructs multi-dimensional simplices from local neighborhoods: 0D simplices encode absolute positions, 1D simplices encode connectivity via centroid-to-neighbor displacement vectors, and 2D simplices encode surface properties (unit normals, triangle areas, inter-edge angles); these are concatenated into a 14-dimensional topological primitive and fused with semantic features from the pre-trained backbone through a learnable geometric controller parameter α. The Topology Transfer (ToTransfer) module propagates these topological primitives across all Transformer encoder layers by element-wise multiplying each layer's input with a shifted, softmax-normalized topological prompt, and adds a lightweight two-layer MLP adapter (with GELU) to each block. Theoretically, the paper proves that the simplicial complex built from local neighborhoods can approximate point cloud homology groups with arbitrary precision, providing a universal approximation guarantee for the topological representation.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN with Point-MAE as the backbone, TopAdapter achieves 92.08% (OBJ_BG), 91.74% (OBJ_ONLY), and 85.58% (PB_T50_RS) overall accuracy using only 0.5M trainable parameters (2.26% of the full model). With PointGPT-L, TopAdapter reaches 99.48% (OBJ_BG), 97.76% (OBJ_ONLY), and 94.97% (PB_T50_RS) with just 1.9M parameters (0.53%), surpassing the full fine-tuning baseline of 93.40% on PB_T50_RS. On ModelNet40, TopAdapter achieves 94.2% with Point-MAE and 94.5% with PointGPT-L, both exceeding their respective full fine-tuning baselines. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, TopAdapter attains 83.99% class-average mIoU and 85.8% instance-average mIoU with 5.58M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopAdapter outperforms all point-cloud-specific PEFT baselines on ScanObjectNN across all three variants when paired with Point-MAE: it beats GAPrompt (the closest competitor) by +0.17% on OBJ_BG (92.08 vs 91.91), +1.55% on OBJ_ONLY (91.74 vs 90.19), and +0.01% on PB_T50_RS (85.58 vs 85.57), while using fewer parameters (0.5M vs 0.6M). Against IDPT, DAPT, Point-PEFT, PointGST, and PointLoRA, TopAdapter achieves the highest accuracy on every ScanObjectNN variant. With PointGPT-L, TopAdapter reaches 99.48% on OBJ_BG, exceeding GAPrompt (98.97%) and PointGST (98.97%) by +0.51%, and 94.97% on PB_T50_RS, surpassing PointGST (94.83%) by +0.14%. The one notable exception is ModelNet40 with PointGPT-L, where PointGST achieves 94.8% versus TopAdapter's 94.5%. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, TopAdapter (83.99%/85.8%) ties PointGST (83.98%/85.8%) on class mIoU and slightly edges out PointLoRA (83.98%/85.4%) on instance mIoU, while using fewer parameters (5.58M vs 5.59M and 5.63M respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

TopAdapter extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) paradigm for point clouds by repurposing algebraic topology concepts—specifically 0D, 1D, and 2D simplices—as the geometric primitives injected into pre-trained 3D vision models. While prior PEFT methods for point clouds (IDPT, DAPT, PointGST, GAPrompt, PointLoRA) focus on input-level prompt generation, spectral adjustments, or low-rank decomposition, TopAdapter departs by explicitly modeling local topological structure (connectivity, curvature, surface orientation) and propagating it across Transformer layers via a dedicated transfer module. The framework is architecturally compatible with existing pre-trained backbones (Point-MAE, ReCon, PointGPT) and combines prompt-tuning and adapter paradigms, but its distinguishing contribution is the principled use of simplicial geometry rather than learned or positional prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TopAdapter offers a principled, topology-grounded alternative to ad-hoc prompt design in 3D PEFT, achieving competitive or superior accuracy with fewer parameters (0.5M, ~2.3%) than competing methods across multiple backbones and datasets. The cross-layer topological transfer mechanism addresses a practical gap where geometric information degrades in deeper Transformer layers, and the ablations confirm that multi-scale simplex features (positions, edges, faces) are each necessary for optimal performance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D detection, reconstruction, or larger-scale point clouds, whether higher-dimensional simplices (tetrahedra) would further improve results, and whether the theoretical claim of arbitrary-precision topology approximation holds under adversarial or highly degenerate inputs. The method's reliance on KNN-based neighborhood construction also raises scalability concerns for very large or non-uniformly sampled point clouds.
