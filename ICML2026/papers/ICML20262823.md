## Problem

While symmetry discovery for deterministic dynamical systems has advanced rapidly with multiple ML-based pipelines, symmetry discovery for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) remains essentially unexplored—no existing method can learn the continuous Lie-point symmetry structure of an SDE directly from trajectory data. This gap is significant because many real-world systems (finance, biophysics, climate) are inherently stochastic, and their relevant symmetries are rarely known a priori.

## Input / Output

The method takes only discrete spatiotemporal trajectory samples (state values on a time grid) with no prespecified symmetry group, generator template, or canonical coordinates; the governing SDE is treated as unknown. It outputs the symmetry Lie algebra dimension m (selected by sweeping and minimizing a closure loss), a basis of m projectable vector-field generators X_i = τ_i(t)∂_t + ξ_i(t,x)∂_x, the Lie bracket structure coefficients c_ij^k, and optionally Fokker-Planck symmetry generators. Evaluation is basis-invariant, using principal angles between the learned and analytic generator spans over a shared (t,x) evaluation domain.

## Method

LieStoNet is a two-stage, template-free pipeline. Stage 1 fits a differentiable neural SDE surrogate (drift f_θ, diffusion σ_θ) from increment data via an Euler-Maruyama local maximum-likelihood objective, then freezes the parameters. Stage 2 parameterizes m projectable generators with multi-head MLPs and trains them by minimizing a composite loss that simultaneously enforces the Gaeta–Quintero SDE determining equations (infinitesimal invariance for both drift and diffusion), Lie-algebraic structure (bracket closure with constant structure coefficients, Jacobi identity, antisymmetry, bilinearity), functional independence of the learned fields, and a finite-ε prolonged pushforward residual that validates nonlocal symmetry consistency. The algebra dimension m is discovered by sweeping candidate values and selecting the minimizer of the post-training closure loss, which is finite because scalar Itô SDEs admit at most a 3-dimensional projectable symmetry algebra. The learned surrogate also induces a Fokker-Planck PDE, enabling optional parallel discovery of FP symmetries via analogous determining-equation and after-flow losses.

## Results

LieStoNet recovers the correct symmetry Lie-algebra dimension (via the L1 closure-loss minimum) across all four canonical SDE benchmarks and achieves small principal angles between learned and analytic generator spans: maximum angles of 6.26° (Ex1), 19.37° (Ex2), 7.64° (Ex3), and 14.29° (Ex4). On the 10-dimensional Brownian SDE with a 12-dimensional symmetry algebra, principal angles range from 0.73° to 16.55°. On real-world BTC/USDT high-frequency data (~1.8M ticks), the pipeline identifies a 2-dimensional symmetry algebra whose generators pass finite-flow after-push and distributional invariance tests substantially better than random-push controls (e.g., ΔNLL of 0.008 vs. 9.7 at ε=0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LieStoNet is the first end-to-end symmetry-discovery pipeline for SDEs, so there are no direct quantitative baselines to compare against; validation is performed against analytically derived ground-truth symmetry algebras. The qualitative comparison in Table 1 shows that all prior symmetry-discovery methods (Ko et al., Forestano et al., LieGAN, Augerino, LaLiGAN, Shaw et al., LieNLSD) operate exclusively on deterministic systems, use either vector-field or matrix parameterizations, and none enforce both Lie-algebra structure and an infinitesimal invariance condition simultaneously. LieStoNet is the only method that handles stochastic dynamics, uses vector-field generators, enforces Lie-algebra closure, and applies the SDE-specific infinitesimal invariance condition. Ablation results confirm that removing the closure loss L1 or the SDE determining-equation loss L6 causes the largest degradation (maximum principal angle jumps from ~6–19° to 55–87°), validating that both the algebraic and SDE-specific constraints are essential for accurate recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry-discovery literature—which has focused almost exclusively on deterministic ODEs and PDEs (Ko et al. 2024, Forestano et al. 2023, Yang et al. 2023/2024a, Shaw et al. 2024, Gabel et al. 2024)—to the stochastic regime, grounding the learning objective in the Lie-point SDE symmetry theory of Gaeta and Quintero (1999). Unlike prior deterministic methods that learn generators via invariance patterns in learned predictors or adversarial formulations, LieStoNet enforces the SDE-specific determining equations (accounting for both drift and diffusion) as differentiable losses on a neural surrogate, while simultaneously regularizing for Lie-algebraic closure, Jacobi identity, and functional independence. The Fokker-Planck connection provides an optional deterministic validation channel, but the core contribution is the first template-free pipeline that discovers projectable Lie-point SDE symmetries directly from trajectory data without prespecified groups or generator templates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first end-to-end framework for SDE symmetry discovery, this work opens a path toward exploiting symmetry structure in inherently stochastic domains (quantitative finance, molecular dynamics, climate modeling) where prior deterministic symmetry-discovery tools are inapplicable. Key limitations include restriction to projectable generators (excluding state-dependent time reparameterizations and random Lie-point symmetries), dependence on surrogate fidelity for loss computation, and validation primarily on low-dimensional canonical SDEs with known analytic algebras. The BTC/USDT experiment, while suggestive, lacks ground-truth verification and involves a single dataset. Open questions include scaling to high-dimensional partially-observed systems, extending beyond projectable symmetries, and using discovered symmetries to build symmetry-constrained stochastic surrogates for downstream prediction.
