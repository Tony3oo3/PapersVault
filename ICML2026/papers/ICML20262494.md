## Problem

Existing HiPPO measures in state space models face an impossible trinity: the uniform LegS measure dilutes recent inputs, the exponential LagT measure sacrifices scale invariance, and the windowed LegT measure catastrophically forgets long-range context. No prior measure simultaneously achieves full-history retention, recency sensitivity, and scale invariance, creating an impedance mismatch with real-world signals exhibiting power-law long-memory dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes variable-length sequences (1K–16K tokens) spanning text, image patches, and nested mathematical expressions, and outputs a single task prediction label per sequence. Internally, the input is projected through a multi-channel fractional filter bank where K state blocks each carry a distinct singularity index α_k (linearly spaced in [0, 0.9]), and the SSM output is gated by a SiLU-activated GLU branch of the input before a linear projection produces the final representation.

## Method

FRACTAL introduces a fractional-order measure μ^(t)(x) ∝ (t−x)^(−α) with a tunable singularity index α ∈ [0,1), which places an integrable singularity at the current timestep while preserving a power-law heavy tail over distant history. The resulting orthonormal basis is the Jacobi polynomial family P_n^(−α,0), and the state transition matrix A(α) is lower triangular with eigenvalues invariant at {1,…,N} regardless of α, while the input projection B admits a closed-form expression via generalized binomial coefficients. The architecture relaxes the 1/t scale-invariance factor to a learnable Δ for LTI operation, discretizes via Zero-Order Hold, and diagonalizes A(α) directly (exploiting its distinct integer diagonal) to enable parallel prefix-sum computation. A multi-scale filter bank assigns heterogeneous α_k across state channels, and the input matrix is initialized with the analytic fractional gain profile rather than random values, accelerating convergence while preserving the power-law inductive bias.

## Results

FRACTAL achieves an average score of 87.11% on the Long Range Arena benchmark, marginally surpassing the reproduced S5 baseline (87.04%) and clearly outperforming S4 (86.09%), S4D (84.89%), and DSS (80.73%). The most notable gain is on ListOps (61.85%), where FRACTAL exceeds S5 (61.10%) and S4 (59.60%), validating the multi-channel fractional filter bank's ability to simultaneously track global bracket structures and local operand values. On Path-X (16K length), FRACTAL reaches 98.39%, effectively matching S5 (98.62%), confirming that the fractional singularity does not degrade gradient propagation over extremely long horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRACTAL is compared against five baselines: two attention-based models (Transformer, Performer) and three SSMs (S4, S4D, DSS, S5). Against the attention-based baselines, FRACTAL dominates across all tasks, with the Transformer achieving only 36.37% on ListOps versus FRACTAL's 61.85%. Among SSMs, FRACTAL clearly outperforms S4 (86.09% avg), S4D (84.89%), and DSS (80.73%) by 1–6 percentage points on average. Against the closest competitor S5 (87.04%), FRACTAL wins on ListOps (+0.75 pp) and Text (+0.38 pp) but trails slightly on Retrieval (−0.08 pp), Image (−0.29 pp), Pathfinder (−0.24 pp), and Path-X (−0.23 pp), yielding a net average advantage of +0.07 pp. The paper notes that S5's originally reported 87.46% was obtained with TPU sweeps and larger batch sizes; under identical single-GPU (A100) conditions, the reproduced S5 score is 87.04%, making the comparison fair. The authors position FRACTAL strictly within the LTI SSM paradigm and argue that comparing against selective (time-varying) architectures like Mamba would conflate distinct research paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HiPPO projection framework (Gu et al., 2020) by replacing the discrete set of known measures (LegS, LagT, LegT) with a continuous family of fractional-order power-law measures, arguing that the measure choice — not the matrix architecture — is the underexplored design parameter in LTI SSMs. Architecturally, it follows the diagonalized MIMO paradigm established by S4D and S5, adopting their spectral initialization and parallel scan computation, but departs by deriving the input projection B in closed form from the fractional Jacobi endpoint values rather than initializing it randomly. The paper explicitly positions itself against the selective SSM line (Mamba, DeltaNet), arguing that LTI and selective architectures address orthogonal research questions and should not be directly compared.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The eigenvalue invariance result (A_nn = n+1 for all α) provides a clean theoretical guarantee that tuning the singularity index modulates memory topology without destabilizing the spectral decay hierarchy, which is a useful inductive bias for multi-scale sequence tasks. However, the empirical gains are modest (87.11% vs. 87.04% average on LRA), and the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark with a fixed, non-learnable α configuration, leaving open whether the fractional measure's advantage persists at language-modeling scale or in non-stationary regimes. The most immediate open questions are whether end-to-end learnable α through Gauss-Jacobi quadrature yields meaningful gains, and whether the power-law memory structure transfers to time-series forecasting domains where long-memory and local transients coexist.
