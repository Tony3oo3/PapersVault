## Problem

Existing theoretical guarantees for Influence Function (IF) and Single Newton Step (NS) data attributions rely on global strong convexity of the loss over the entire optimization domain, an assumption that fails for standard problems like logistic regression on unbounded domains. Moreover, the resulting bounds scale as high-degree polynomials in dimension d and drop-set size k, and are too pessimistic to explain the well-documented empirical observation that NS is substantially more accurate than IF. No prior analysis provides tight, local-condition-based error bounds that capture the correct scaling and explain the NS-over-IF accuracy gap.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a convex ERM problem: given the trained model θ̂, the Hessian H and per-sample gradients g_i evaluated at θ̂, and a drop-set T of k samples, it produces an approximate retrained model θ̂_T (the parameter change after removing T). The key structural requirement is that the loss be strongly convex and Hessian-Lipschitz only in an ellipsoidal neighborhood of the first Newton step, not globally. For the concrete analysis, the setting is logistic regression with i.i.d. Gaussian features x_i ~ N(0, I) and a population optimum of Θ(1) norm, in the regime k, d ≪ √n.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1.11) bounds the NS approximation error by C_h·C_op in a Σ-norm, where C_h measures Hessian change along the Newton path and C_op is a local strong-convexity constant in an ellipsoidal ball around the Newton step. The proof proceeds in two steps: (1) the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus bounds the residual gradient at the Newton step by C_h, and (2) a geometric argument shows the loss must attain a local minimum inside a smaller concentric ball, since the gradient field points inward on the outer shell. For logistic regression with Gaussian features, the paper derives matching upper and lower bounds: NS error is Õ(kd/n²) while the IF–NS gap is Õ(k^{3/2}d^{1/2} + k^{1/2}d^{3/2}/n²), so whenever d ≫ k the IF error is dominated by the IF–NS gap and NS is provably more accurate. The analysis also yields tight bounds for Rescaled Influence Functions (RIF) and a new Doubly Rescaled variant (DRIF) that adds an n/(n−k) correction factor to match NS accuracy in both random and adversarial drop-set settings.

## Results

For logistic regression with Gaussian features (n ≫ k², d²), the paper proves tight asymptotic bounds: the Newton Step (NS) error scales as Õ(kd/n²) on average and Õ((k²+kd)/n²) in the worst case, while the IF–NS gap is Õ((k^{3/2}d^{1/2}+k^{1/2}d^{3/2})/n²) on average. This implies NS is significantly more accurate than IF whenever d ≫ k. The proposed DRIF method matches NS accuracy at Õ(kd/n²) on average, and Figure 1 empirically validates these scalings on synthetic data (n=2¹⁶, d=512) and IMDB sentiment analysis with frozen BERT embeddings, where NS and DRIF substantially outperform IF and RIF for larger removals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the prior state-of-the-art bounds from Koh et al. (2019) and related works, which require global strong convexity and scale as Ω(k²d/(n²λ³)), the paper's local-condition analysis yields dramatically tighter bounds of Õ(kd/n²) for NS, eliminating the problematic 1/λ³ dependence that made prior guarantees vacuous in practice. Against the RIF method from Rubinstein & Hopkins (2025b), the paper proves that RIF is already nearly as accurate as NS for adversarial drop-sets (Theorem 1.12: RIF–NS gap is Õ((kd+k²)/n²)), and shows that the simple additional rescaling in DRIF closes the remaining gap for random drop-sets (Theorem 1.13: DRIF–NS gap is Õ(kd/n²) on average, matching NS). Empirically (Figure 1), on both synthetic Gaussian logistic regression and IMDB with frozen BERT embeddings, NS and DRIF achieve substantially lower error to full retraining than IF and RIF, especially as the number of removed samples k increases, confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of theoretical analyses of influence functions (IF) and single Newton step (NS) data attributions by Koh et al. (2019), Giordano et al. (2019), and Wilson et al. (2020), which all relied on global strong convexity and Lipschitz Hessian assumptions over the entire optimization domain. The key departure is replacing these global conditions with local ones that only need to hold in a neighborhood of the first Newton step, combined with a milder directional Lipschitz assumption on the Hessian. The paper also builds directly on Rubinstein & Hopkins (2025b)'s Rescaled Influence Functions (RIF), proposing Doubly Rescaled Influence Functions (DRIF) as a simple n/(n−k) correction that closes most of the accuracy gap between RIF and NS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analysis whose bounds are tight enough to quantitatively explain the well-known empirical observation that NS substantially outperforms IF, resolving open questions raised by Koh et al. (2019). The practical impact is significant for machine unlearning, where the noise required for certified guarantees scales with the data attribution error bound; the prior 1/λ³ dependence made these bounds useless in practice, while the new kd/n² scaling is far more informative. Limitations include the restriction to convex ERM, the requirement n ≫ d², k², and the fact that for non-convex losses the main theorem is only an existence result for a local minimum. Open questions include extending the analysis to the n ≥ d regime, characterizing error direction (not just norm), and adapting the local-convexity framework to deep non-convex models.
