## Problem

Gaussian process inference scales cubically in training size, and existing sparse GP methods (SVGP, KISS-GP, DAK) still incur linear or worse dependence on the number of inducing points M, making them impractical for high-dimensional deep feature spaces. The paper addresses how to reduce the per-input computational cost from O(M) to O(log M) while preserving predictive accuracy and uncertainty calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266057/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The GP layer consumes D-dimensional feature vectors normalized to [0,1] (raw tabular features, CNN/ResNet embeddings of 64–512 dimensions, or DistilBERT CLS projections of 256 dimensions) and produces a predictive mean and variance per input. For classification, the output is a softmax-normalized probability vector with associated uncertainty (NLL, ECE); for OOD detection, predictive entropy and mutual information scores are derived from multiple posterior samples. A key structural detail is that each input activates only L+2 basis functions out of M=2^L+1 total, so the effective weight vector per input is D(L+2)-dimensional rather than DM-dimensional.

## Method

SIKA-GP exploits the Markov property of the Laplace kernel on a dyadic grid of inducing points to derive closed-form basis functions ψ_lm with compact local support (Theorem 4.1), forming an orthonormal reparameterization of the standard inducing-kernel BNN. A Tensorized Sparse Indexing (TSI) algorithm computes the activated basis indices in parallel via a single floor operation on the batch tensor, then squeezes the full weight matrix to only the L+2 active rows per input, reducing forward-pass cost from O(M) to O(log M). Training uses mean-field variational inference with an ELBO objective and flipout-style Rademacher sampling for efficient Monte Carlo estimation. The method extends naturally to deep architectures: stacking SIKA-GP layers yields a DGP, and placing SIKA-GP atop a DNN or transformer encoder yields a DKL model, all while maintaining the sparse O(log M) per-input cost at every layer.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DKL-SIKA achieves 94.78% top-1 accuracy and 4.06% ECE, outperforming both DAK (94.53%, 4.34%) and SVDKL (94.28%, 4.10%) while reducing training time to 17.26 s/epoch (vs. 35.04 s for DAK and 46.90 s for SVDKL). On CIFAR-100, DKL-SIKA reaches 76.94% accuracy with 4.31% ECE at 36.31 s/epoch, a 2.8× speedup over DAK (102.57 s/epoch). On UCI regression (Gas), DGP-SIKA matches DGP's RMSE (0.53 vs. 0.54) while cutting per-epoch runtime from 19.43 s to 2.79 s (≈7× speedup). On CLINC150 OOD detection, DKL-SIKA achieves the highest AUROC (0.8590) and lowest NLL (0.54) among all GP baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAK (Zhao et al., 2025), DKL-SIKA wins on accuracy (94.78% vs. 94.53% on CIFAR-10; 76.94% vs. 76.61% on CIFAR-100), ECE (4.06% vs. 4.34% on CIFAR-10; 4.31% vs. 5.55% on CIFAR-100), and training speed (2× faster on CIFAR-10, 2.8× faster on CIFAR-100), while matching or slightly trailing on NLL. Against SVDKL, DKL-SIKA dominates on every metric: higher accuracy, lower ECE, and 2–3× faster training and inference across all image benchmarks. On UCI regression, DGP-SIKA (the deep GP variant) matches the dense DGP baseline (Damianou & Lawrence, 2013) on RMSE and NLPD across Gas, Kin40K, and Protein, while reducing per-epoch runtime by up to 7× (Gas: 2.79 s vs. 19.43 s). On CLINC150, DKL-SIKA achieves the best OOD AUROC (0.8590 vs. 0.8486 for DAK and 0.8413 for SVDKL) and lowest OOD NLL (0.54), while training 2.7× faster than SVDKL and 1.8× faster than DAK. The consistent pattern is that SIKA-GP preserves or improves predictive and calibration quality while delivering substantial computational savings, with the largest gains appearing at higher feature dimensions and deeper architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIKA-GP sits at the intersection of sparse GP inference (inducing point methods à la Titsias, KISS-GP) and the recent line of work converting sparse GPs into Bayesian neural networks (Ding et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2025). Its key departure is exploiting the Markov property of the Laplace kernel on a fixed dyadic grid to obtain a closed-form, sparsely activated basis with only O(log M) active units per input, rather than learning inducing locations or relying on interpolation grids. The tensorized sparse indexing (TSI) pipeline is a novel GPU-friendly computational trick that makes the sparse structure directly exploitable in batched tensor operations, and the method is then embedded into DGP and DKL architectures to address scalability in deep Bayesian settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing per-input GP inference from O(M) to O(log M), SIKA-GP makes it practical to attach GP uncertainty heads to modern backbones (ResNet, DistilBERT) and to draw more Monte Carlo samples for better-calibrated uncertainty without prohibitive cost. The main limitation is the reliance on the Laplace kernel, which restricts smoothness control; the authors mitigate this through deep feature learning but acknowledge that broader kernel classes remain an open direction. Future work on adaptive or hybrid dyadic grids, extensions beyond Gauss-Markov kernels, and integration with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA) could broaden the applicability of the approach to larger-scale Bayesian models.
