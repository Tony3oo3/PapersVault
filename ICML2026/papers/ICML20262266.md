## Problem

Existing generative reward models suffer from three structural limitations: they rely on static evaluation rubrics that cannot adapt to dynamically changing user constraints, they are fragmented across evaluation paradigms (pairwise vs. pointwise vs. listwise), and they remain predominantly English-centric. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously by proposing a unified framework that supports multilingual, multi-paradigm evaluation with input-adaptive criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a prompt Q and a set of k candidate responses X, the model generates a single autoregressive sequence [R′, Ŝ, Ĵ] in one inference pass. The reasoning chain R′ is decomposed into three sequential segments: an analytical state Z (identifying task intent, risks, and constraints), a rubric synthesis segment R (split into task-generic criteria R^gen and instruction-specific criteria R^ins, each scored on a 1–5 scale), and an evaluation execution segment E. From this single output, pairwise/listwise judgments are derived from the winner index Ĵ, while pointwise scores are read directly from Ŝ, unifying all three paradigms without separate model heads.

## Method

UniRRM is built on Qwen3-8B/14B backbones and trained in two stages. First, SFT distills structured evaluation sequences from GPT-OSS-120B, retaining only samples where the teacher's final judgment is correct. Second, GRPO optimizes with a composite reward: a format reward (α₁=0.8) enforcing valid JSON and extractable scores, an outcome accuracy reward (α₂=0.15) for correct winner prediction, and an adaptive rubric reward (α₃=0.05) where a teacher model scores the generated rubrics on relevance, specificity, and comprehensiveness. The key architectural insight is the staged reasoning chain that incrementally decomposes evaluation—first analyzing the input to produce a semantic anchor Z, then generating input-adaptive rubrics conditioned on Z, and finally applying those rubrics to score candidates—preventing the semantic drift and post-hoc rationalization seen in prior rubric-generating reward models. Training data comes from MixReward, a 64,528-example multilingual dataset spanning 103 languages and 6 domains, constructed via a four-stage pipeline (semantic filtering, language identification and translation expansion, multi-verifier preference consistency checking, and pairwise-to-listwise assembly).

## Results

On pairwise benchmarks, UniRRM-14B achieves an average accuracy of 0.868, surpassing its teacher GPT-OSS-120B (0.865) and all generative reward model baselines, with peak scores of 0.885 on MM-Eval and 0.757 on JudgeBench. UniRRM-8B reaches 0.840 average pairwise accuracy, improving over the Qwen3-8B backbone by 6.1 percentage points. On the listwise benchmark RWBench2, UniRRM-14B scores 0.791, the best among generative reward models, though Skywork-V2-8B (0.841) remains stronger in this specific setting. Notably, UniRRM-14B generalizes to pointwise scoring without direct supervision, achieving 0.771 average accuracy versus 0.723 for M-Prometheus-14B which was explicitly trained on pointwise data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniRRM-14B outperforms its distillation source GPT-OSS-120B on MM-Eval (0.885 vs. 0.872) and JudgeBench (0.757 vs. 0.750), while matching its average (0.868 vs. 0.865), despite having ~8.5× fewer parameters. Against scalar reward models, UniRRM-14B dominates on MM-Eval (0.885 vs. 0.745 for Skywork-V2-8B) but loses on RWBench2 listwise ranking (0.791 vs. 0.841). Among generative reward models, UniRRM-14B leads all pairwise benchmarks: it beats mR3-Qwen3-14B by 8.1 points in average accuracy (0.868 vs. 0.787), Nemotron-49B by 5.8 points (0.868 vs. 0.810), and RM-R1-32B by 9.6 points (0.868 vs. 0.772). The smaller UniRRM-8B also surpasses mR3-Qwen3-8B (0.840 vs. 0.773) and RewardAnything-8B (0.840 vs. 0.746) on pairwise tasks. On pointwise evaluation (Table 2), UniRRM-14B (0.771 avg) outperforms M-Prometheus-14B (0.723) and RewardAnything-8B (0.680) despite never being trained on pointwise data. The only clear loss is on RWBench2 listwise, where Skywork-V2-8B's scalar architecture provides an advantage for global ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative reward model line (RubricRM, RM-R1, JudgeLRM, mR3, RewardAnything) by unifying three previously fragmented evaluation paradigms—pairwise, listwise, and pointwise—within a single model, and by introducing adaptive, input-specific rubric generation that replaces the static or loosely-supervised rubrics used in prior work. The training pipeline combines SFT distillation from GPT-OSS-120B with GRPO-based reinforcement learning, building on the GRPO paradigm popularized by DeepSeekMath but applying it to reward model training with a composite reward (format + accuracy + rubric quality). The MixReward dataset (103 languages, 6 domains) addresses the multilingual gap that prior generative reward models largely ignore, and the staged reasoning chain (analysis → rubric synthesis → evaluation execution) departs from the flat chain-of-thought used in JudgeLRM and RM-R1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified multi-paradigm, multilingual framework provides a practical, interpretable alternative to costly proprietary LLM-as-a-Judge systems for RL training loops, as demonstrated by the GSM8K/Math-500 experiments where UniRRM-8B outperforms a scalar RM baseline by ~3 points. The finding that pointwise scoring generalizes zero-shot from pairwise/listwise training (outperforming a model explicitly SFT-trained on pointwise data) suggests that joint multi-paradigm supervision yields more robust internal quality representations. Open questions include whether the 8.8% multilingual performance drop on Chat Hard can be closed with more targeted data, whether the teacher-model dependency for rubric quality can be eliminated, and whether the framework scales to even larger backbones or more languages without proportional compute increases.
