## Problem

Existing LLM-based data science approaches—domain-specific models for isolated tasks and workflow-based agents with predefined pipelines—cannot achieve full autonomy because they are not trained in real-world data environments. Training an LLM to autonomously orchestrate and adaptively optimize actions across the full data science pipeline is further hampered by severe reward sparsity and trajectory scarcity on complex, multi-step structured-data tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265779/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DeepAnalyze takes a natural language instruction plus filenames of structured data sources (CSV, JSON, XLSX, databases) in a sandbox environment; it can also passively receive small tables as text in the prompt. The model emits a sequence of five special action tokens (<Analyze>, <Understand>, <Code>, <Execute>, <Answer>) and autonomously switches between them, executing generated Python code in the environment and incorporating execution feedback (stdout, errors) into the next generation step. The final output is either a concise task answer or a multi-section data science research report, with the full interaction trajectory preserved as part of the response.

## Method

DeepAnalyze-8B (built on DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B) is trained via a two-stage curriculum that mirrors human data-scientist learning: (1) single-ability fine-tuning on ~470K synthesized reasoning trajectories covering general reasoning, structured-data comprehension, and code generation, and (2) multi-ability agentic training that cold-starts on 20K synthesized multi-turn interaction trajectories and then applies GRPO reinforcement learning (G=8, ε=0.2, β=0.01) over 15K tasks in real-world sandbox environments. A data-grounded trajectory synthesis framework supplies training data: reasoning trajectories are produced by distilling from SOTA teacher LLMs and then refined with keyword-guided insertion to anchor reasoning to the underlying table; interaction trajectories are generated by a three-agent system (questioner, solver, inspector) that formulates data-science problems from NL2SQL databases, solves them with the five actions, and validates both interaction details and environmental changes against a checklist. A hybrid reward function combines rule-based checks (format compliance, exact-match accuracy, per-turn success) with LLM-as-a-judge scoring of report quality (usefulness, richness, soundness, interpretability, readability) and interaction-trajectory quality, enabling RL on both closed-answer and open-ended research tasks.

## Results

On DataSciBench (end-to-end data science pipeline), DeepAnalyze-8B achieves a 59.91% success rate and 66.24% completion rate, scoring 61.11 overall — second only to GPT-4o (64.51) among all 17 compared LLMs and surpassing all other open-source models by a wide margin (best open-source: Qwen2.5-7B at 45.99). On DABStep (multi-step data reasoning), DeepAnalyze-8B reaches 38.88% overall accuracy, outperforming the best workflow-based agent (GPT-4.1 + ReAct at 23.33%) by 15.5 points, with the gap widening dramatically on hard tasks (32.80% vs. 12.43%). On DS-1000 (data science code generation), DeepAnalyze-8B scores 61.7, exceeding GPT-4-Turbo (53.9) and GLM-4.5 (53.2). On TableQA benchmarks, DeepAnalyze-8B achieves 64.47 average exact match, surpassing the previous SOTA Reasoning-Table (62.62) and GPT-4o (58.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against workflow-based agents, DeepAnalyze-8B consistently wins: on DABStep it outperforms ReAct + GPT-4.1 (23.33% vs. 38.88% overall) and DS-Agent + Gemini-2.0-Flash (18.00% vs. 38.88%), with the advantage most pronounced on hard tasks (32.80% vs. 12.43% and 9.79% respectively). On DSBench data modeling, DeepAnalyze-8B achieves a 90.63% success rate at zero API cost, exceeding AutoGen + GPT-4 (87.84% success, $19.34 cost) and AutoGen + GPT-4o (71.62%, $12.27). On TableQA, DeepAnalyze-8B (64.47 avg EM) surpasses Reasoning-Table with SFT+RL (62.62) and GPT-4o (58.96), establishing a new SOTA for structured data question answering. On DS-1000, DeepAnalyze-8B (61.7) beats GLM-4.5 (53.2) and GPT-4-Turbo (53.9), while the unmodified DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B base model scores only 30.4, demonstrating that the curriculum-based agentic training nearly doubles code generation performance. The sole comparison where DeepAnalyze-8B trails is DataSciBench overall score vs. GPT-4o (61.11 vs. 64.51), though it leads on fine-grained metrics like plot validity (67.86 vs. 56.14) and data preparation (71.68 vs. 75.93 is a loss, but data visualization 69.09 vs. 71.35 is close). GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is not a competing baseline but the RL algorithm adopted for the multi-ability training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work on LLM-based data science: domain-specific LLMs (TableGPT, StructGPT, LLaSA) that handle isolated tasks like TableQA or code generation, and workflow-based agents (DS-Agent, Data Interpreter, AutoGen/ReAct pipelines) that orchestrate proprietary LLMs through manually designed procedural steps. DeepAnalyze's key conceptual departure is replacing external workflow orchestration with an internally learned agentic policy trained via curriculum-based reinforcement learning (GRPO) in real-world data environments, drawing on the RL training paradigm from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath but adapting it to the multi-skill, environment-interacting setting of data science. The data-grounded trajectory synthesis framework (distillation + keyword-guided refinement + multi-agent interaction trajectory generation) extends standard distillation-based data synthesis by explicitly grounding trajectories in structured data sources and validating them against environmental state changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a single 8B-parameter model, trained with curriculum-based agentic RL, can match or exceed multi-agent systems built on proprietary LLMs (GPT-4o, o3, Claude) across 13 data science benchmarks, suggesting that task-specific agentic training can substitute for both model scale and hand-engineered orchestration. The open-sourcing of DeepAnalyze-8B, the DataScience-Instruct-500K training corpus, and the DABStep-Research benchmark provides the community with concrete tools to build on. Open questions include whether the curriculum paradigm generalizes to other multi-skill domains (e.g., software engineering, scientific discovery), how performance scales with model size beyond 8B, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge reward for open-ended research is sufficiently reliable for training at scale. The data modeling sub-task (F5) still lags behind GPT-4o, suggesting that certain data science capabilities may require additional inductive biases or longer training horizons.
