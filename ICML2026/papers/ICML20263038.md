## Problem

In pixel-based visual RL, auxiliary representation tasks (mask reconstruction, state prediction, contrastive learning) optimize goals misaligned with the ultimate objective of maximizing long-horizon return, causing the limited encoder capacity to be consumed by return-irrelevant information. Standard TD-based Q-critics further bias the visual encoder toward local, proximal-reward features rather than the global articulation and trajectory features that determine cumulative return, creating a fundamental goal discrepancy that constrains sample efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 84×84×3 RGB frames stacked in groups of three (following the MDP approximation), along with corresponding actions, and uniformly samples K=64 frames at interval j=7 from each episode to form the return-prediction sequence. Outputs include the policy action, a scalar predicted episode return (via MSE regression) plus a discrete return-bin classification (M bins via cross-entropy), and per-transition key scores derived from Longformer cls-token attention weights that drive prioritized replay sampling.

## Method

RC is a plug-and-play auxiliary module that shares the base algorithm's CNN visual encoder and adds a lightweight Longformer (3 layers, 1 head) to predict episode-level return from uniformly sampled state-action tokens. A cls token with global attention aggregates the sequence, and dual prediction heads (regressor + classifier) jointly force the encoder to extract return-relevant features, directly bridging the gap between representation learning and the RL objective. The cls token's attention weight scores over individual frames serve as pseudo-labels to train a separate key function (MLP) that scores each state-action pair's importance for return, enabling Key Score Sampling (KSS) to prioritize half of each replay batch. Theoretically, the paper proves that goal discrepancy bounds the mutual information between learned representations and return labels, and that RC's discrepancy scales as O(ε² + 1/K) + V, where ε is predictor error, K is sample count, and V is gradient noise.

## Results

On nine challenging DMControl tasks at 1M steps, DrQv2 w/ RC achieves an average return of 673.9, a 68.0% improvement over the DrQv2 baseline (401.1) and a 20.2% improvement over the strongest non-RC baseline TACO (560.3). On six common DMControl tasks at 100K steps, DrQ w/ RC reaches 862.8 average return, outperforming ResAct (822.1) and MLR (772.8), demonstrating superior sample efficiency. In offline RL (V-D4RL), DrQv2+BC w/ RC achieves new SOTA on more than half of the evaluated tasks, e.g., 91.1 on Walker Walk MedExp vs. 86.4 for DrQv2+BC. Under matched 12-hour wall-clock time, RC (~1M steps) maintains an 18% average advantage over fully converged DrQv2 (~2M steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrQv2 w/ RC decisively outperforms the DrQv2 baseline by 68% average return across all nine challenging tasks, confirming that the gains come from RC rather than the base algorithm. Against TACO (the strongest non-RC method, which predicts short-horizon return), RC wins by 20.2% on average and achieves the best score on 7 of 9 tasks; TACO only wins on Quadruped Walk (793 vs. 875) and Cheetah Run (821 vs. 897) at 1M steps. DreamerV3 (model-based) and MIND (mask reconstruction) are both substantially outperformed (464.9 and 526.7 vs. 673.9 average), as are ResAct (489.7), DrQ (226.4), PlayVirtual, and MLR. In offline RL on V-D4RL, DrQv2+BC w/ RC surpasses LOMPO, BC, CQL, and Offline DV2 on the majority of dataset configurations, with the largest gains on medium and medexp splits (e.g., 91.1 vs. 86.4 on Walker Walk MedExp). The Key Score Sampling (KSS) mechanism independently outperforms PER, LAP, and PAL by ~10% average when applied to both RC and vanilla DrQv2, and in state-based MuJoCo, TD3+KSS achieves 8476.7 average vs. 7620.4 for TD3+PAL and 7000.4 for SAC, validating its modality-agnostic utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auxiliary-task paradigm for visual RL representation learning (CURL, MLR, TACO, PlayVirtual, MIND) by arguing that all prior auxiliary objectives—contrastive learning, mask reconstruction, state prediction—are misaligned with the ultimate RL goal of maximizing return. RC departs from this lineage by replacing the auxiliary objective with episode-level return prediction via a Longformer-based sequence model, directly coupling the representation gradient to an information-theoretic return-relevance objective. The key function derived from cls-token attention weights for prioritized sampling is a novel repurposing of Transformer credit assignment, distinct from TD-error-based methods (PER, LAP, PAL). Theoretically, the paper introduces a 'goal discrepancy' formalism bounding how much return-relevant information a capacity-limited encoder can capture under a misaligned auxiliary objective, which prior work had not made explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and 68% average improvement on challenging DMControl tasks make RC a practical drop-in module for off-policy visual RL, and the matched wall-clock experiments convincingly show gains are not merely from extra compute. However, the computational cost scales with sequence length (Longformer over K frames), and the method does not reach SOTA on all tasks (e.g., Reach Duplo), suggesting task-specific representation needs that return prediction alone cannot fully address. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sparse-reward or multi-agent settings, whether the Longformer can be replaced by lighter aggregators without loss, and whether the theoretical PL-condition assumptions hold in practice for deep CNN encoders.
