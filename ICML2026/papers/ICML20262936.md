## Problem

Formal libraries like Mathlib suffer from a persistent 'last-mile gap': small folklore lemmas that working mathematicians use routinely are absent, stalling otherwise straightforward formalizations and degrading LLM-based proof search. Existing LLM evaluations focus on fixed Olympiad-level problem sets rather than the structural task of discovering and proving the dense lattice of missing intermediate facts, leaving both the library-expansion problem and a corresponding benchmark unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes entire Mathlib source files (109 seed files across Foundational, Applied, and Abstract domains) as context, along with compiler error diagnostics from the Kimina Lean server during iterative repair. It produces two artifacts: a benchmark of 4,028 non-trivial type-checked Lean 4 statements (each paired with its self-contained import/namespace context, no ground-truth proof) and a verified library of 1,506 Lean-checked proofs. Outputs must pass a proof-bypass screen that strips comments and rejects incomplete-proof placeholders (sorry, admit, sorryAx) and model-introduced top-level axiom/constant/opaque declarations, ensuring proofs are constructed solely from the supplied Mathlib context.

## Method

MATHLIBLEMMA is a four-stage modular LLM pipeline that factorizes the distinct failure modes of folklore mining into orthogonal stages: (1) Discovery uses GPT-5.1 to brainstorm candidate lemmas from a seed file, emitting Lean statements with sorry; (2) Judge applies LLM-as-a-judge to filter mathematically false statements while deliberately ignoring syntax; (3) Formalizer runs an iterative generate-verify-repair loop (up to 10 trials) against the Lean server to fix type and namespace errors; (4) Prover attempts proofs with up to 2 sequential repair rounds, validated by kernel checking plus the proof-bypass screen. The benchmark is constructed from stages 1–3 (discovery, judging, formalization) using GPT-5.1 for all generation and filtering, while stage 4 produces the verified proof library. Evaluation is closed-book: models receive only the statement and context, with no retrieval, RAG, or external search, isolating internalized library knowledge from search-stack quality.

## Results

On the MATHLIBLEMMA benchmark of 4,028 non-trivial type-checked Lean 4 statements, the best individual model (GPT-5.1 with low reasoning) achieves 19.39% Success@2 overall, while the union of all seven evaluated models reaches 37.39%. The pipeline itself produces 1,506 verified Lean-checked proofs (passing a proof-bypass screen) from the 4,028 statements, and a human provability audit confirms that 78% of model-unsolved residuals are mathematically sound and provable. Goedel-Prover-V2-32B leads in the Foundational domain (27.42%) but drops to 8.24% in the Abstract domain, whereas GPT-Reasoning maintains more consistent performance across all three domains (22.44%, 15.28%, 18.61%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5.1 with low reasoning (19.39%) is the strongest individual model overall, outperforming Goedel-Prover-V2-32B (17.70%) by 1.69 pp and GPT-5.1 without reasoning (15.69%) by 3.70 pp, confirming a consistent ~3.3 pp test-time compute gain. However, Goedel-Prover dominates in the Foundational domain (27.42% vs. 22.44% for GPT-Reasoning), benefiting from Mathlib-specific fine-tuning, while GPT-Reasoning nearly doubles Goedel's score in the Abstract domain (18.61% vs. 8.24%), where deep structural reasoning matters more than library recall. Kimina-Prover-72B (10.70%), trained via RL on competition problems (MiniF2F), underperforms Goedel by 7 pp, suggesting library-centric fine-tuning transfers better to folklore lemmas than competition-centric RL. The open-weight DeepSeek models (6.98% and 6.68%) and Qwen3 (2.46%) trail significantly, indicating that general informal-math reasoning does not automatically transfer to formal Lean proving without explicit library training. Most strikingly, the union of all seven models achieves 37.39%—nearly double the best single model—demonstrating that the models are highly orthogonal and that ensembling diverse architectures is far more effective than relying on any single prover.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task—automated folklore-lemma mining—by repurposing ideas from lemma synthesis (Sivaraman et al., 2022), LLM-based conjecturing (LeanConjecturer, Lemmanaid), and feedback-driven proof repair (Delta Prover, Seed-Prover, APOLLO) into a unified four-stage modular pipeline. Unlike prior conjecturing systems that generate plausible facts broadly, the Discovery Module specifically targets the 'missing connective tissue' that working mathematicians implicitly rely on; unlike ad-hoc lemma generators for proof search, the outputs are designed as permanent, reusable library candidates. The proof-bypass screen (rejecting sorry, axiom, constant, opaque) and the closed-book evaluation protocol are novel verification mechanisms not present in prior formal-reasoning benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4,028-instance benchmark and 1,506-verified-proof library address a concrete, widely-reported barrier (the 'last-mile gap') that limits proof assistants from becoming everyday tools, and the three lemmas merged into Mathlib provide rare external validation that LLM-generated formal artifacts can meet expert library standards. Key open questions include whether the lightweight novelty filters can catch semantically equivalent restatements, whether the generation-model dependence (GPT-5.1 shapes the benchmark distribution) limits generalizability, and whether the 37.39% union solve rate will close as prover diversity grows. The finding that ensembling diverse architectures nearly doubles coverage over the best single model suggests a practical path forward for formal-reasoning systems.
