## Problem

Hybrid reinforcement learning—combining on-policy and off-policy feedback—has been studied only under stochastic losses where stationarity allows pre-collecting off-policy data and using confidence-bound or stage-based designs. In adversarial MDPs, losses change arbitrarily over time, rendering these approaches inapplicable, and the interaction between coverage-dependent off-policy guarantees and worst-case on-policy robustness remains unexplored. Additionally, whether single-policy concentrability (regret depending only on the comparator's coverage ratio, not the worst-case over all policies) can be achieved with off-policy feedback in MDPs with unknown transitions was an open question.

## Input / Output

Each round/episode the learner simultaneously receives on-policy feedback (the state-action-loss tuple from its own executed policy) and off-policy feedback (the state-action-loss tuple from a fixed, known behavior policy running in parallel). The transition function is either known or must be learned from the off-policy trajectory. The output is a policy sequence whose regret bound takes the form √T · min{poly(H,S,A), f(C(π^C))}, where f is a sublinear function of the coverage ratio—yielding sharp √(C(π^C)T)-type guarantees when the behavior and comparator policies are well-aligned, while degrading gracefully to a universal √T worst-case bound when coverage is arbitrarily large.

## Method

The framework uses the CORRAL meta-algorithm to adaptively combine one on-policy base algorithm (EXP3 variant for MABs, O-REPS for known-transition MDPs, UOB-Log-barrier Policy Search for unknown-transition MDPs) with Θ(log T) off-policy base algorithms (EXP3-IX / PREPS instances, each tuned to a different coverage-ratio scale via geometrically spaced learning rates). The meta-level employs a log-barrier regularizer with an increasing learning-rate schedule to handle the 1/√(min p_{t,0}) factor in the on-policy base regret. For unknown transitions, the key technical contribution is replacing the standard confidence-set approach (which induces optimism and yields only all-policy concentrability) with a single biased transition function that, under the paper's loss-to-reward conversion, provides pessimism and ensures the ERROR₂ term is non-positive, thereby achieving single-policy concentrability. The off-policy loss estimator includes an η/2 additive bias in the denominator, which is the critical ingredient that shifts the regret dependence from worst-case coverage C_all to the comparator-specific C(π^C).

## Results

The paper establishes regret bounds for hybrid RL in adversarial MABs and MDPs. In the MAB setting, the hybrid algorithm achieves Õ(√T · min{A, √C(π^C)}), which is strictly better than both the on-policy Õ(√(AT)) and off-policy Õ(C(π^C)√T) baselines. For MDPs with unknown transitions, the hybrid bound is Õ(√T · min{H³S²A, H√(SAC(π^C)) + C(π^C) + H^{3/2}√(SC(π^C))}), improving upon the on-policy Õ(H²S√(AT)) whenever C(π^C) = o(HS). The paper also provides the first off-policy bound with single-policy concentrability for unknown-transition MDPs: Õ(HC(π^C)√(SAT)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The hybrid method dominates all five baselines by construction: it takes the minimum of the on-policy and off-policy regret, guaranteeing at worst the on-policy rate while achieving the sharper off-policy rate when coverage is good. Against Audibert & Bubeck's on-policy MAB bound √(AT), the hybrid improves to √T·min{A, √C(π^C)}, which is strictly smaller whenever C(π^C) < A². Against Gabbianelli et al.'s off-policy MAB bound C(π^C)√T, the hybrid recovers the same √C(π^C) dependence but makes it universal (no π^C-dependent tuning needed) and caps it at A√T for large coverage. For known-transition MDPs, the hybrid beats Zimin & Neu's H√(SAT) on-policy bound whenever C(π^C) = o(HSA), and beats Bacchiocchi et al.'s C(π^C)√(HT) off-policy bound by replacing the linear C(π^C) factor with √(HC(π^C)) while remaining universal. For unknown-transition MDPs, the hybrid improves upon Jin et al.'s H²S√(AT) on-policy bound when C(π^C) is small (e.g., Θ(HS) yields Õ(√T·min{H^{1.5}S√A, H²S})), and the paper's new off-policy subroutine (HC(π^C)√(SAT)) resolves an open question from Bacchiocchi et al. by achieving single-policy concentrability without requiring known transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends hybrid RL from the stochastic regime (Xie et al., 2021; Tan et al., 2024) to adversarial (non-stationary) losses, where stationarity-based designs (pre-collected offline data, confidence bounds, stage-based methods) fundamentally break down. The aggregation mechanism is the CORRAL meta-algorithm (Agarwal et al., 2017), which corrals an on-policy base (EXP3 variant from Dann et al., 2023) and multiple off-policy base instances (EXP3-IX from Gabbianelli et al., 2023) with geometrically spaced learning rates. A key technical departure is the repurposing of the biased transition function from Jin et al. (2023)—originally designed for adversarial transitions—into a pessimism mechanism for off-policy learning with unknown transitions, resolving an open question from Bacchiocchi et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first hybrid RL framework for adversarial MDPs and the first to achieve single-policy concentrability with unknown transitions and off-policy feedback, resolving a concrete open problem. The practical implication is that a learner can simultaneously enjoy sharp coverage-dependent regret when the behavior policy is well-aligned with the comparator, while retaining worst-case √T guarantees when it is not—something neither pure on-policy nor pure off-policy methods provide. Key open questions remain: whether the MAB hybrid bound can be tightened to √(T·min{A, C(π^C)}) (eliminating the corralling cost), whether tight off-policy bounds exist for unknown transitions, and how these ideas extend to function approximation beyond the tabular setting.
