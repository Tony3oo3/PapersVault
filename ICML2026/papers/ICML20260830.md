## Problem

Ultra-high-resolution remote sensing imagery (up to 8K×8K) produces a quadratic explosion of visual tokens that exceeds the context window of modern MLLMs, while the evidence needed for reasoning (e.g., a small boat or vehicle) may occupy only a few pixels. Existing remedies—aggressive downsampling, dense tiling, or iterative coarse-to-fine zooming—either discard query-critical fine-grained details, fragment global context, or incur unpredictable and high inference latency. The paper addresses the gap of performing token selection under a strict, fixed context budget while simultaneously preserving global scene layout and sparse, query-relevant local evidence.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ultra-high-resolution RGB remote sensing image (natively up to ~8500×8500 pixels, yielding ~131,328 visual tokens at full resolution) and a natural-language text query. The image is resized to four fixed scales (672×672 through 4032×4032), encoded by a frozen ViT, and compressed into a budget-feasible token sequence (typically 4K–12K tokens, a 10×–33× compression ratio) before being concatenated with text tokens and fed to the LLM. The output is a multiple-choice text answer; the intermediate compressed token sequence is the primary architectural artifact that decouples input resolution from downstream compute.

## Method

UHR-BAT is a query-guided, region-faithful token compression framework built on a LLaVA-Next backbone (CLIP-ViT L/14-336 + LongVA-7B), fine-tuned on 10K image-text pairs. It operates in three stages: (1) a text-to-vision attention map extracted from the MLLM's vision-language interface provides a per-token importance score, which is bilinearly interpolated from a low-resolution anchor scale to all higher scales to yield cross-scale-comparable scores; (2) tokens at each scale are partitioned into semantically coherent regions via K-means on ℓ₂-normalized features concatenated with spatial coordinates (or via SAM segmentation); (3) within each region, tokens scoring above the region-mean importance are preserved individually while the remainder are merged into a single representative via mean pooling, and the resulting candidate sequence is serialized in descending order of region importance and truncated to the per-scale budget. Scale-specific positional embeddings (bilinearly interpolated 2D PE + learned scale embedding) keep multi-scale tokens spatially aligned and distinguishable, and a minimum per-region capacity constraint guarantees that every semantic region contributes at least one token regardless of attention score deviations.

## Results

On XLRS-Bench (the primary benchmark with ~8500×8500 pixel images), UHR-BAT achieves a state-of-the-art weighted average accuracy of 44.0%, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (43.8%) and the largest open-source model InternVL3-78B (43.5%). On RSHR-Bench, UHR-BAT attains the best Perception average (29.2%) among open-source and remote-sensing baselines and the best Reasoning average (45.0%) among remote-sensing VLMs. On MME-RealWorld-RS, it achieves the highest average score (33.33%), surpassing GPT-4o (27.42%) by +5.91%. The method achieves these gains while compressing 131,328 raw visual tokens down to as few as 4,000 (32.8× compression), reducing generation latency by ~27.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UHR-BAT (7B) achieves the best overall weighted accuracy (44.0%) on XLRS-Bench, edging out Qwen2.5-VL-7B (43.8%) by +0.2%, InternVL3-78B (43.5%) by +0.5%, and GeoLLaVA-8K (43.3%) by +0.7%. The margin over closed-source models is substantially larger: +21.0% over GPT-4o (23.0%) and +8.9% over Claude 3.7 Sonnet (35.1%). Among remote-sensing-specific VLMs, UHR-BAT improves over GeoChat by +22.8%, EarthDial by +10.2%, and VHM by +20.4%. On RSHR-Bench, UHR-BAT leads all remote-sensing VLMs on Reasoning (45.0% vs. EarthDial 37.1%, GeoChat 28.3%, GeoLLaVA-8K 30.0%, VHM 27.7%) and Perception (29.2% vs. VILA-HD 28.0%), though it trails GPT-4o (37.3%) and GPT-5 (34.8%) on the overall average. On MME-RealWorld-RS, UHR-BAT (33.33%) surpasses GeoLLaVA-8K (28.41%) by +4.92% and GPT-4o (27.42%) by +5.91%, with particularly large gains on Position (+8.76% over the best RS baseline) and Color (+14.08%). The closest competitor overall is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, which UHR-BAT matches or slightly exceeds on XLRS-Bench while using a fixed compressed token budget rather than the full high-resolution token sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

UHR-BAT builds on the LLaVA-Next architecture (CLIP-ViT L/14-336 + LongVA-7B) and departs from two dominant strategies for handling high-resolution inputs in MLLMs: (1) tiling/slicing pipelines (LLaVA-UHD, HiRes-LLaVA) that fragment global context, and (2) remote-sensing-specific approaches like GeoLLaVA-8K's text-agnostic background pruning or ZoomEarth's iterative coarse-to-fine zooming, which either discard query-critical tokens or incur unpredictable latency. The key conceptual departure is a single-pass, query-guided, region-faithful compression that enforces a hard token budget while guaranteeing every semantic region contributes at least one representative token, combining multi-scale attention-based importance scoring with a preserve-and-merge rule inspired by clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework makes ultra-high-resolution remote sensing VQA practical on commodity hardware (~4s inference, ~30GB peak memory), which is critical for edge deployment on UAVs or satellite ground stations. The finding that 4K inputs can match or marginally exceed 8K under an identical token budget challenges the assumption that higher native resolution always helps, suggesting that contextual density per token can outweigh extreme pixel-level detail. Open questions include whether the single-layer attention scoring generalizes to more complex multi-hop reasoning, whether the heuristic per-scale budget allocation can be learned, and whether the spatial-correlation assumption (proximal tokens encode similar content) holds for all remote sensing modalities (e.g., multispectral or SAR).
