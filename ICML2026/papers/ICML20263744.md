## Problem

Existing universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) methods for Vision Language Models operate primarily on the visual modality, neglecting structured textual semantics and cross-modal interactions that are fundamental to VLM generalization. This modality-specific limitation restricts the ability to disrupt cross-modal alignment and hinders transferability across diverse tasks and model architectures. The paper is the first to study text-guided non-targeted UAPs for CLIP-based VLMs, revealing a 'disrupt-then-exploit' mechanism where effective perturbations first break benign alignment before reconnecting adversarial representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263744/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (trained on ImageNet) paired with classification-style text prompts ('A photo of [CLS]') and produces a single universal image perturbation δ^I (L∞-bounded at 12/255) and a shared universal text perturbation δ^T (L∞-bounded at 0.1). The image perturbation is applied to any test image to generate adversarial samples that transfer across victim models (BLIP2, LLaVA-7B, MiniGPT-4, and commercial LVLMs like GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude) and tasks (IC, ZS classification, VQA, I-T Retrieval) without access to target model gradients. Optimization uses three CLIP surrogate models (ViT-L/14, ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32) with cosine similarity as the alignment metric.

## Method

MADA-Attack formulates UAP generation as a min-max optimization (max over image perturbation, min over text perturbation) with three key components. Semantic Token Manipulation (STM) shifts from point-wise to distributional robustness by characterizing a semantic latent distribution P_T(t) around the text embedding and approximating its worst-case shift via a learnable universal text perturbation, following DRO principles. Fused Embedding Training (FET) decomposes optimization into an Interaction Phase (updating δ^T to target deep-layer textual representations where text attention dominates) and a Fusion Phase (propagating semantic disruption into the visual modality by minimizing alignment between benign and adversarial fused embeddings via additive fusion, with momentum for flat-loss regions). Adaptive Data Augmentation (ADA) employs a training-aware schedule where augmentation probability increases monotonically—low early to stabilize global perturbation structure, higher later for local semantic refinement—using a ResizedCrop-HorizontalFlip-Affine-Rotation sequence to break patch-grid alignment.

## Results

On zero-shot classification (BLIP2), MADA-Attack achieves an average ASR of 82.60%, surpassing the strongest baseline XTransfer Base (70.83%) by 11.77 points, with peak performance of 98.38% on CIFAR-100 and 93.37% on GTSRB. On image captioning (Flickr-30K), MADA-Attack attains CIDEr ASR of 72.67% (MiniGPT-4), 44.39% (BLIP2), and 98.25% (LLaVA-7B), outperforming all baselines across all three VLMs. On commercial LVLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude), MADA-Attack achieves CIDEr ASR of 94.59%, 80.42%, and 93.67% respectively, exceeding the best baseline by 15–20 points. The method also exceeds the SOTA baseline by over 10% on VQA and I-T Retrieval tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADA-Attack outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task. On ZS classification, it beats the strongest baseline XTransfer Base (70.83% avg) by 11.77 points, with the largest gap on Stanford Cars (88.84% vs. 48.95%) and STL-10 (49.82% vs. 29.27%), indicating superior generalization to fine-grained and distribution-shifted domains. Among multi-modal baselines, C-PGC (51.70% avg) and ETU (58.05%) trail significantly, confirming that embedding-level textual guidance provides a more transferable attack objective than word/prompt-level cross-modal interaction. On image captioning, MADA-Attack surpasses XTransfer Base by 4.62 CIDEr points on MiniGPT-4 (72.67 vs. 68.05) and by 11.09 points on the more challenging BLIP2 (44.39 vs. 33.30), while maintaining a 1.2-point lead on LLaVA-7B (98.25 vs. 97.05). On commercial LVLMs, MADA-Attack leads XTransfer Naive by 20.09 CIDEr points on GPT-4o (94.59 vs. 74.50) and by 10.61 points on Gemini (80.42 vs. 69.81), demonstrating robust black-box transferability against proprietary systems with heterogeneous architectures and alignment strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of transferable UAP research for CLIP-based VLMs (C-PGC, XTransfer, ETU, AdvCLIP) by introducing embedding-level textual guidance into the perturbation optimization loop, departing from prior multi-modal attacks (MSI-Attack, C-PGC) that operate at the word or prompt level. It reframes the UAP objective from point-wise image-text distance minimization to a distributionally robust optimization over a semantic neighborhood of text embeddings, drawing on DRO principles. The Fused Embedding Training module is motivated by layer-wise attention analyses showing complementary visual/textual dominance across depths, a structural observation not explicitly exploited in prior UAP methods. The evaluation scope also extends beyond the I-T retrieval focus of most prior multi-modal UAP work to cover image captioning, zero-shot classification, and VQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a cross-modal semantic alignment vulnerability in VLMs that is orthogonal to encoder-level adversarial training (TeCoA², Fare²), suggesting that current robustness efforts targeting L∞ pixel perturbations are insufficient against semantic-level attacks. The disrupt-then-exploit mechanism and the finding that early optimization stages determine global UAP structure provide actionable insights for both attack design and future defense development. Key limitations include the restriction to CLIP-based architectures, the non-targeted-only formulation, and insufficient analysis of how data distribution and semantic diversity affect transferability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-CLIP VLM backbones and how to unify non-targeted transferability with targeted controllability.
