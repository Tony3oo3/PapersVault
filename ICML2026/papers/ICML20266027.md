## Problem

Existing 3D representation learning frameworks are driven by extrinsic spatial structures or high-level semantics, leaving intrinsic geometric properties—shape identity, manifold topology, and fine-grained surface structure—largely unmodeled. This gap causes suboptimal performance on geometry-sensitive tasks such as surface parameterization and non-rigid shape correspondence that require robustness to isometric deformations and pose variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266027/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unstructured 3D point clouds (no mesh, no connectivity, no pre-computed geodesic graphs) and produces per-point feature embeddings in a high-dimensional space (256–768 dims depending on model scale) that approximates an isometric embedding of the input surface's intrinsic Riemannian metric. A lightweight symmetric prediction head (absolute feature difference → 3-layer MLP with Softplus) outputs pairwise geodesic distances. For downstream tasks, the frozen or fine-tuned features feed task-specific heads producing UV coordinates, correspondence similarity matrices, class labels, or per-point segmentation labels.

## Method

PRISM uses Point Transformer V3 as a scalable backbone and frames pre-training as geodesic distance prediction, grounded in the Nash Embedding Theorem: the learned feature space should approximate an isometric embedding of the surface manifold. The prediction head computes the element-wise absolute difference between two point features and passes it through a 3-layer MLP to yield a scalar distance, ensuring symmetry. Training combines three losses—L1 regression, mean relative error, and a novel geodesic structure consistency loss that enforces ordinal alignment of pairwise feature distances with ground-truth geodesic distances via a tanh-approximated sign function. A two-stage recipe first applies a time-decaying weight on the structure loss to establish global topological ordering, then switches to importance sampling that oversamples rare short- and long-range distance pairs to correct the natural imbalance in the geodesic distance distribution.

## Results

On geodesic distance prediction, PRISM achieves 3.87% MRE and 2.75% L1 error with a query time of 0.5 s per shape, matching the accuracy of remeshed GeGNN (4.43% MRE) while being 38× faster, and approaching NeuroGF [L] (0.52% MRE) at 1200× lower inference cost. On ScanObjectNN classification, PRISM reaches 93.5% (OBJ-BG), 92.1% (OBJ-ONLY), and 89.7% (PB-T50-RS), and achieves a dominant 72.1% on the rotation-robustness test (PB-T50-RS-Test-Only), surpassing the next-best PointDif by 7.9 points. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, PRISM attains 85.1% mIoU with a frozen backbone (best among all compared methods) and 86.5% with full fine-tuning (tied with Point-M2AE). For non-rigid correspondence on FAUST, PRISM achieves 1.4 geodesic error in 0.5 s, matching SMS and outperforming ULRSM (1.6) and ZoomOut (6.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In geodesic distance prediction, PRISM (3.87% MRE, 0.5 s) is 38× faster than remeshed GeGNN [R] (4.43% MRE, 18.9 s) with comparable accuracy, and 1200× faster than NeuroGF [L] (0.52% MRE, 600 s) at the cost of ~3.4× higher MRE; it also outperforms all traditional methods (HM, DGG, EEM, FPGDC) in query speed by 24–155×. On ScanObjectNN classification, PRISM (93.5/92.1/89.7%) trails PointSD (95.2/93.6/90.1%) and Point-DAE (93.9/93.1/88.7%) by 1–2 points on standard splits, but dominates the rotation-robustness test (PB-T50-RS-Test-Only) with 72.1% versus PointDif's 64.2%, a 7.9-point margin. On ShapeNetPart, PRISM's frozen-backbone mIoU of 85.1% is the highest among all compared pre-training methods (next best: Point-M2AE at 84.5%), and its fine-tuned mIoU of 86.5% ties with Point-M2AE. For surface parameterization, PRISM (5.34 error, 0.5 s) is 846× faster than FlexPara (4.45 error, 423 s) with only 0.89 higher isometric error, and clearly beats BFF (7.27 error, 4.3 s). On FAUST correspondence, PRISM (1.4 error, 0.5 s) ties SMS (1.4, 0.5 s) and beats ULRSM (1.6, 1.7 s), ZoomOut (6.3, 7.4 s), and FM (21.2, 3.2 s) while using only point-cloud input without requiring Laplace-Beltrami computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM carves new ground by repurposing geodesic distance prediction — previously studied as a standalone computational geometry task (NeuroGF, GeGNN) — as a self-supervised pre-training objective for 3D representation learning. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant extrinsic/semantic pre-training paradigms (contrastive learning, masked modeling) by grounding the pretext task in intrinsic Riemannian geometry, with the Nash Embedding Theorem providing the theoretical justification for isometric latent-space embedding. The architecture (PTv3) and downstream heads (PointNet, MLP) are borrowed from existing work; the novelty lies entirely in the training paradigm: a topology-enforcing ordinal loss combined with importance-sampling fine-tuning to handle the natural imbalance in geodesic distance distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant contribution is the first successful feed-forward surface parameterization, a task the authors explicitly report as intractable for all prior architectures and pre-trained models they tested — this opens a path toward real-time, generalizable parameterization without per-scene optimization. The rotation-robustness finding (72.1% vs. 53–64% for competitors in the test-only rotation setting) suggests intrinsic geometry learning may be a principled route to pose invariance, though the authors themselves note this is 'still far from sufficient.' Open questions include whether the approach scales to non-manifold or open surfaces, whether multi-task pre-training (combining geodesic with semantic objectives) can close the remaining gap to SOTA on classification, and whether the Nash-embedding framing generalizes beyond closed genus-0 shapes to arbitrary topologies.
