## Problem

Multi-modal spatio-temporal forecasting models operate as black boxes, limiting trust in safety-critical domains like pedestrian trajectory prediction and tropical cyclone forecasting. Moreover, the absence of ground-truth causal structures makes it infeasible to recover a uniquely identifiable causal graph, and existing interpretability methods rely on task-specific assumptions or post-hoc analysis that lack generality across modalities and time steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265879/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CausalX consumes multi-modal spatio-temporal history (1D target series, 1D auxiliary variables, and 2D auxiliary fields such as geopotential height maps) over T historical steps, plus a fixed binary prior graph encoding domain-consensus variable relationships. It produces two outputs: (i) an instance-specific causal-inspired graph with edge weights quantifying causal attribution strength across all modalities and time steps, and (ii) a graph-level feature vector obtained by mean-pooling node embeddings. These enable two plug-in modes—feature fusion (concatenating the graph-level vector into a decoder input) or graph replacement/augmentation (substituting the learned adjacency into a graph-based decoder)—making the module architecture-agnostic.

## Method

CausalX operates in two stages. First, a GAT encoder builds a fully connected temporal graph over all variables (target, 1D auxiliary, and spatially averaged 2D channels) across T steps, yielding attention weights as initial causal attributions; four complementary constraints then jointly regularize these weights: Granger causality (predictive), a do-calculus-inspired feature perturbation (interventional), time-delayed mutual information (temporal lag), and a VAE reconstruction score (nonlinear/generative), each producing a supervision graph that the attention must match via MSE loss. Second, a DDPM-style diffusion process treats the constrained graph as a signal, adds Gaussian noise, and denoises it with a predictor conditioned on node features and the hand-crafted prior graph, recovering residual causal structure that the discrete constraints miss. The final training objective is an unweighted sum of all five loss terms, and at inference the auxiliary modules are disabled, reducing CausalX to a single learned graph or feature fused into the backbone decoder.

## Results

On tropical cyclone forecasting, CausalX plugged into TCN_M reduces the 24 h track error from 93.08 km to 74.55 km (a 19.9 km / 21.3% improvement), with consistent gains across all horizons (6 h: 23.14→19.55 km; 12 h: 43.37→34.81 km; 18 h: 67.09→52.82 km) and on pressure/wind-speed metrics. On pedestrian trajectory prediction (ETH/UCY), CausalX reduces the average ADE/FDE of MID from 0.248/0.458 to 0.238/0.420, EigenTrajectory from 0.224/0.380 to 0.222/0.375, and SingularTrajectory from 0.225/0.360 to 0.220/0.353. At the 72 h long-horizon setting, TCN_M+CausalX cuts the track error by 92.35 km (408.35→316.00 km), surpassing the long-range specialist TAM-CL (365.60 km).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalX is a plug-and-play module, so the primary comparison is each backbone with vs. without CausalX. On TC forecasting, TCN_M+CausalX outperforms all single-task baselines (GRU, NMPT, DLM, TCIF-fusion) and multi-task baselines (SGAN, GBRNN, MMSTN, TAM-CL, CMO) by a wide margin at every horizon, with the largest relative gain (+21.3%) at 24 h distance. TC-Diffuser+CausalX also beats TC-Diffuser alone on all metrics (e.g., 24 h distance 77.35→73.76 km, wind speed 1.34→1.22 m/s), though the relative improvement is smaller because TC-Diffuser already partially disentangles tasks via shared/specific conditioning. On pedestrian forecasting, CausalX improves all three SOTA backbones: MID (avg ADE 0.248→0.238, FDE 0.458→0.420), EigenTrajectory (0.224→0.222, 0.380→0.375), and SingularTrajectory (0.225→0.220, 0.360→0.353), all surpassing prior methods such as LB-EBM (0.210/0.380), PCCSNET (0.210/0.420), and Trajectron++ (0.190/0.410) on average. A few scene-specific drops appear on ZARA1/ZARA2 for EigenTrajectory and SingularTrajectory, attributed to unobserved environmental constraints not modeled by the backbones. At the 72 h long-horizon setting, TCN_M+CausalX (316.00 km) surpasses the dedicated long-range model TAM-CL (365.60 km) and MMSTN (544.16 km), confirming that the dynamic causal graph preserves informative structure over extended prediction windows.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalX carves new ground by unifying four previously separate causal analysis techniques—Granger causality, do-calculus-style interventions, time-delayed mutual information, and a VAE-based generative constraint—into a single graph-learning objective for spatio-temporal forecasting, rather than relying on any one signal or on post-hoc explainability methods (Grad-CAM, Cxplain). It departs from prior spatio-temporal causal inference work (Christiansen et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025) by explicitly acknowledging that ground-truth causal structures are unavailable and reframing the learned graph as a 'causal-inspired' proxy rather than a formally identifiable causal DAG. The diffusion-based denoising of the causal graph, guided by a hand-crafted prior adjacency, is a novel mechanism not seen in prior causal discovery or graph-refinement literature, and the plug-and-play architectural integration (feature fusion or graph replacement) distinguishes it from task-specific causal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in safety-critical forecasting (pedestrian safety, hurricane preparedness) where black-box accuracy alone is insufficient for user trust and accountability. The consistent gains across five backbones and two domains, combined with negligible inference overhead and faster convergence, make CausalX a practically adoptable module rather than a one-off architecture. However, the 'causal-inspired' framing is a deliberate hedge: the authors concede the graph is not a formally identifiable causal structure, and the faithfulness evidence (inference-time edge removal) validates predictive utility rather than true causal mechanism. Open questions include whether the learned graphs remain stable under distribution shift, whether the four constraints can be learned end-to-end rather than partially precomputed, and whether the approach extends to settings with truly unobserved confounders (e.g., the ZARA scene drops hint at this limitation).
