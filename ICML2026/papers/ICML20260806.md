## Problem

Standard interpretability analyses of LLMs treat attention weights as proxies for token importance, but this ignores the geometric properties of the value vectors being aggregated—their magnitudes and directional alignment with the output. This omission leads to misleading conclusions, most prominently in the attention sink phenomenon, where early tokens receive disproportionate attention mass yet contribute minimally to the semantic content of the layer output. The paper asks: what is a faithful, geometry-aware measure of token contribution, and what functional role does value geometry play in shaping attention outputs?

## Input / Output

The method consumes three quantities computed at each layer l and head h: the scalar attention weights α_ij^(l,h), the projected value vectors ṽ_j^(l,h) = x_j W_V^(l,h) W_O^(l,h) (i.e., value vectors after both the value projection and the output projection are applied), and the full multi-head layer output o_i^(l). It produces signed contribution weights c_ij that sum to exactly 1 across the sequence dimension (intra-head) or jointly across heads and positions (inter-head), unlike attention weights which are always in [0,1]. The signed nature is critical: negative values indicate a token actively opposes the output direction (destructive interference), a distinction invisible to any non-negative attention-based metric.

## Method

Contribution weights are defined as the normalized inner product of the layer output with each token's weighted projected value vector: c_ij = ⟨o_i, α_ij ṽ_j⟩ / ‖o_i‖². Via the cosine identity, this decomposes into three interpretable multiplicative factors—attention weight α_ij, relative value norm ‖ṽ_j‖/‖o_i‖, and cosine similarity cos(o_i, ṽ_j)—capturing selection strength, magnitude, and directional alignment respectively. The inter-head variant projects each head's weighted values onto the combined multi-head output, so it simultaneously encodes intra-head token influence and each head's relative contribution to the layer. Using this decomposition, the paper performs regression analyses showing that for sink tokens (j=1), value geometry (norm × alignment) explains 72.5% of contribution variance while attention explains only 7.9%, and for tail tokens, attention × alignment jointly explains 83.4%. Causal interventions (scaling sink value norms, removing tokens by each metric) demonstrate that contribution weights outperform attention, value-weighted attention, and value-weighted output-normalized attention in both comprehensiveness and sufficiency across four models and multiple tasks, and reveal that sink tokens actively suppress semantic drift through anti-alignment with non-sink tokens, producing a convex (not monotonic) relationship between sink attention rate and output norm.

## Results

Contribution weights consistently outperform all attention-based metrics in identifying semantically critical tokens across four models (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, DeepSeek-7B) on GSM8K and FineWeb-Edu, achieving the lowest ΔPPL under both top- and bottom-percentile token removal at every removal rate. On downstream tasks (MMLU, BoolQ, Winogrande, Lambada), contribution weights maintain baseline accuracy at substantially higher removal rates than attention or value-weighted alternatives. Regression analysis (Table 1) reveals that for sink tokens (j=1), the product of relative norm and cosine similarity explains R²=0.725 of contribution variance versus only R²=0.079 for attention alone, while for tail tokens (j>1), attention plus cosine similarity achieves R²=0.834. In LLaMA-3.1-8B, attention assigns 0.708 mass to the first token, but contribution weights reduce this to 0.142—a 5× suppression—demonstrating that value geometry fundamentally reshapes apparent token importance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares contribution weights against three progressively richer attention-based baselines: raw attention (α_ij), value-weighted attention (α_ij·‖ṽ_j‖, from Kobayashi et al.), and value-weighted output-normalized attention (α_ij·‖ṽ_j‖/‖o_i‖). Contribution weights win on every metric and every model tested. In the token-removal intervention (Figure 3), contribution weights produce the largest ΔPPL when removing top-percentile tokens (highest comprehensiveness) and the smallest ΔPPL when removing bottom-percentile tokens (highest sufficiency), outperforming all three baselines at every removal rate from 10% to 90%. The ablation across the four metrics reveals a clear monotonic progression: raw attention < VW < VW-ON < contribution weights, with the addition of cosine similarity (the jump from VW-ON to full contribution weights) yielding the single largest faithfulness gain, indicating that directional alignment is the most underappreciated geometric factor. On downstream tasks (Figure 4), contribution weights preserve accuracy at removal rates 10–20 percentage points higher than attention-based metrics before degrading. The regression analysis (Table 1) further shows that for sink tokens, attention alone explains only 7.9% of contribution variance while value geometry (norm × alignment) explains 72.5%, and for tail tokens, attention + alignment explains 83.4% versus 44.9% for attention alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the value-aware attention analysis initiated by Kobayashi et al. (who showed attention weights alone are insufficient and proposed value-norm scaling) and the additive head decomposition framework of Elhage et al. The key departure is moving from partial geometric corrections (norms, distances) to a full projection-based metric that captures all three determinants of vector summation—attention weight, relative magnitude, and directional alignment—enabling a linear decomposition of contribution. It also departs sharply from the prevailing 'passive sink' narrative in the attention-sink literature (Xiao et al., Guo et al., Barbero et al.) by demonstrating that sink tokens actively suppress information through directional opposition rather than merely absorbing excess attention mass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The recharacterization of attention sinks as active information suppressors with a convex (not monotonic) norm-suppression relationship challenges a widely held mechanistic assumption and has direct implications for KV-cache compression, token pruning, and interpretability tooling that currently treat sinks as removable noise. The contribution weights metric is practical, requires only internal activations, and consistently outperforms attention-based baselines across four models and five tasks, making it a drop-in upgrade for interpretability pipelines. Limitations include evaluation restricted to decoder-only LLMs (encoder-only and ViT applicability is claimed but untested), and the geometric model in Appendix E relies on approximate constancy of non-sink value norms and alignments that may not hold in all regimes. Open questions include whether the sink suppression mechanism is an emergent property of training or an architectural artifact, and whether the convex geometry generalizes to non-causal attention patterns.
