## Problem

Existing TDDE solvers face a fundamental latency-accuracy trade-off imposed by time discretization: numerical methods require small steps for fidelity but exceed real-time budgets, while ML surrogates compress evolution into discrete forward passes that still degrade over long rollouts. Dynamical System Machines (DSMs) offer continuous-time computation but are limited to first-order dynamics and static spatial correlations, making them unable to capture the high-order temporal dependencies and evolving spatiotemporal interactions that define TDDEs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DS-TS takes a discretized initial condition over N spatial grid points (256, 512, or 1024), prescribed boundary conditions (periodic or time-dependent), and TDDE parameters as input. Unlike discrete-step solvers that output solutions only at fixed timesteps, DS-TS evolves continuously and can be queried at arbitrary time points, producing the full spatiotemporal trajectory u(t,x) as capacitor voltages. Boundary conditions are enforced intrinsically by prescribing node dynamics at boundary positions rather than through penalty terms or post-processing.

## Method

DS-TS is a Dynamical System Machine with three synergistic components: (1) Excitatory-Inhibitory Inspired Coupling augments linear spatial mixing with nonlinear excitatory and inhibitory pathways (via sign-gated branches) to model complex spatial interactions; (2) State-aware Dynamic Nonlinearity generates polynomial expansion coefficients from the instantaneous system state, so the effective nonlinear operator evolves along the trajectory; (3) Hierarchical Temporal Integration chains L+1 integrator states per node to convert high-order temporal derivatives into a first-order augmented system. Training is supervised (MSE on sampled state-operator pairs) with a three-stage curriculum (backbone → E-I gating → joint fine-tuning) and 8-bit quantization-aware training for hardware deployment. Physically, states are capacitor voltages, weights are programmable conductance arrays, and the polynomial expansion is realized via logarithmic-domain computation (log transformer → voltage scaling → exponential transformer) with NMOS/PMOS current-sinking/sourcing branches for sign handling.

## Results

DS-TS achieves MAE on the order of 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ across all five TDDE benchmarks in short-term (100-step) rollouts, and 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ in long-term (500-step) rollouts, consistently outperforming all baselines by 1–3 orders of magnitude. In the most challenging R3 (1024-grid) setting, DS-TS reaches 1.91×10⁻⁶ MAE on Advection-Diffusion (short-term) and 8.48×10⁻⁶ (long-term), versus 1.11×10⁻⁴ and 4.49×10⁻⁴ for the strongest ML baseline (FNO AR-16). DS-TS also delivers ~10³× speedup and ~10⁵× energy reduction over GPU-based ML solvers, with hardware power of 1.12 W at 2000 nodes (180 nm CMOS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS-TS outperforms all six baseline families across every benchmark, resolution, and rollout length. Among ML-based solvers, FNO (AR-16) is the strongest competitor, yet DS-TS still achieves 1–3 orders of magnitude lower MAE (e.g., 1.91×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.11×10⁻⁴ on Advection-Diffusion R3 short-term). PINN and UNet (both AR-1 and AR-16) are consistently 2–4 orders of magnitude worse than DS-TS, with UNet degrading severely in long-term rollouts (MAE reaching 10⁻²). Among prior DSMs, EADS is the closest competitor but remains 1–2 orders of magnitude behind DS-TS (e.g., 3.95×10⁻³ vs. 1.91×10⁻⁶ on Advection-Diffusion R3 short-term), while NP-GL and DS-TPU are 2–3 orders of magnitude worse, particularly on Wave and Fokker-Planck equations where their lack of higher-order temporal integration causes severe error accumulation. Notably, DS-TS is the only method that handles the fourth-order Hyperbolic equation with competitive accuracy, whereas all prior DSMs (limited to first-order dynamics) show MAE values 3–4 orders of magnitude higher.

## Relation to Prior Work

DS-TS extends the line of Dynamical System Machine (DSM) research initiated by NP-GL (Wu et al., ICLR 2024), EADS (Liu et al., ICML 2025), and DS-TPU (Wu et al., ISCA 2025), all of which use programmable resistive-capacitive circuits to perform computation through continuous physical evolution. The critical departure is that prior DSMs encode solutions as equilibrium states (solving time-independent problems), whereas DS-TS is the first to exploit the full transient trajectory of a DSM for time-dependent problems, addressing three specific gaps: insufficient temporal integration depth, static spatial correlations, and inability to model high-order time derivatives. The three architectural components (EIC, SDN, HTI) are designed to fill these gaps while preserving the circuit-level primitives (capacitor voltages, conductance arrays, KCL summation) that make DSMs physically realizable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10³× speedup and ~10⁵× energy reduction over GPU-based ML solvers, combined with 1–3 orders-of-magnitude accuracy gains, make real-time PDE solving on embedded hardware a practical prospect for applications like in-the-loop control and interactive scientific computing. The hardware implementation in 180 nm CMOS with quantization-aware training and demonstrated noise robustness (SNR 52 dB, <2× MAE degradation) strengthens the claim that this is not merely a simulation artifact. Open questions include scalability to 3D domains, whether the fully-coupled (dense) interaction structure is necessary or whether sparsity/locality can reduce hardware cost, and whether the approach generalizes to PDEs with complex geometries or coupled multi-physics systems beyond the five benchmarks tested.
