## Problem

When different systems adopt different embedding encoders, their vector indices become incompatible even when they share many of the same objects, blocking unified retrieval, deduplication, and cross-index clustering. Existing embedding alignment methods assume a global isomorphism between spaces or full overlap, but in practice the overlap is partial and unknown, non-overlapping regions are structured (not removable outliers), and only static vectors are available in a strict black-box regime with no access to raw objects, model internals, or training-time intervention.

## Input / Output

The method takes two embedding clouds E1 and E2 (each a set of L2-normalized vectors from different black-box contrastive encoders over partially overlapping corpora) and a tiny seed set S of 15–30 known paired anchors, with no access to raw objects, encoder parameters, gradients, or the overlap set Ω. It outputs a set of inferred cross-model links (one-to-one correspondences) between vectors in E1 and E2 that encode the same underlying object, leaving vectors outside the overlap unmatched. The overlap ratio α is unknown to the method, and the non-overlapping portions can be large and structured, which is what makes a single global transformation unreliable.

## Method

The core insight is that independently trained contrastive encoders exhibit local isometric consistency: short-range distances are preserved up to a model-dependent scale factor κ, while long-range distances decorrelate. The paper proves this via a localized alignment-uniformity surrogate for contrastive learning, showing that locally optimal encoders induce metric tensors proportional to the identity, yielding Theorem 1 (local distances scale by κ = √(λ₁/λ₂) up to O(d²) error). Building on this, GEH (Geometric Embedding Hashing) represents each vector by kernelized distances to a small set of paired anchors, compares scale-free normalized signatures via cosine similarity, and uses CSLS-based mutual nearest neighbors for per-view matching. To handle the unknown locality threshold, GEH samples many diverse anchor subsets (views) via Furthest Point Sampling, treats per-view matches as noisy votes, and aggregates evidence with a Beta-Bernoulli posterior. High-confidence links are promoted as new anchors in an iterative bootstrapping loop with adaptive view scheduling (logarithmic scaling of view count and size), growing a tiny seed set into a large correspondence set while filtering spurious collisions from model-specific distortion.

## Results

With only 15 paired seed anchors and 30% overlap, GEH achieves 77–96% recall and 77–84% precision across five encoder pairs on BEIR benchmarks (e.g., 95.6% recall on NFCorpus with Qwen↔OpenAI; 89.1% recall on SciFact with GTE↔OpenAI), while the strongest baseline (Procrustes) reaches at most 11.8% recall. On the 5.4M-document FEVER corpus, GEH attains 93.8% precision and 68.9% recall in 3,328 s on a single A100, compared to 5.2% precision and 0.6% recall for the next-best method (CCA). GEH's performance is remarkably stable to seed budget: 15 seeds already match 30-seed performance, whereas all baselines require more seeds to improve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEH dominates all six alignment baselines by a wide margin on every model pair and dataset. The strongest baseline, orthogonal Procrustes (Smith et al., 2017), reaches at most 11.8% recall (NFCorpus) and 29.6% recall (SciFact with 30 seeds), versus GEH's 81–96% recall; on FiQA (Qwen↔KaLM) the gap is most extreme, with Proc at 1.3% recall versus GEH's 79.9%. CCA (Lu et al., 2015) is the second-best baseline, peaking at 29.2% recall on SciFact with 30 seeds, still far below GEH's 82–89%. RCSLS (Joulin et al., 2018) and the MLP baseline show high precision but near-zero recall (≤3%), indicating they propose very few links and miss almost all true correspondences. UGW (Séjourne et al., 2021) and Anchor Optimization (Cannistraci et al., 2023) are the weakest, with recall below 6% in most settings; UGW cannot even complete on the 5.4M FEVER corpus. The linear regression baseline (Mikolov et al., 2013) is competitive only in recall (up to 13%) but has very low precision, confirming that a single global linear map cannot capture the partial, non-bijective overlap structure that GEH exploits locally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the embedding alignment literature (Procrustes, CCA, Gromov-Wasserstein, RCSLS) by rejecting the global-isomorphism assumption: instead of learning a single transformation that makes two spaces comparable, it seeks a partial one-to-one correspondence on an unknown shared support, leaving non-overlapping vectors unmatched. The method draws conceptually from geometric hashing (Lamdan & Wolfson, 1988) and point-set registration, but adapts them to high-dimensional, heteroscedastic embedding spaces where a single global transform is unreliable. The theoretical foundation builds on the alignment-uniformity analysis of contrastive learning (Wang & Isola, 2020; Zimmermann et al., 2021), showing that local optimality of the InfoNCE surrogate forces the induced metric to be a scalar multiple of the identity, yielding cross-model local isometry up to a scale factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vector linking is a practical primitive for embedding interoperability: it enables unified retrieval across vector databases built with different encoders, cross-model deduplication, and joint clustering without re-embedding or retraining. The paper demonstrates that only 15–30 seed pairs suffice for high-accuracy linking across five model pairs and six BEIR benchmarks, and that out-of-domain seeds transfer well, making the approach deployable when exact paired vectors are inaccessible. Open questions include reducing or eliminating the seed requirement, extending to multi-model (beyond two) linking, and characterizing precisely when local isometric consistency holds beyond the contrastive-learning surrogate (e.g., for non-contrastive or generative encoders, where the paper shows the property weakens substantially).
