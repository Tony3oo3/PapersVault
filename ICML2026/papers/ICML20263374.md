## Problem

Optimizing LLMs for multi-turn correction under lightweight negative feedback faces a sharp dilemma: online RL (PPO, GRPO, UFO) effectively handles multi-turn dynamics but requires generating full interaction trajectories at every policy update, making it prohibitively expensive as the turn budget grows, while offline SFT on correction trajectories is efficient but suffers from distribution shift and behavioral collapse where the model over-optimizes first-turn accuracy and fails to produce meaningful revisions. The paper asks whether the RL objective can be realized through an SFT-style training loop that avoids repeated rollouts.

## Input / Output

At each turn t, the model receives the full interaction history (problem prompt, prior responses, and fixed negative feedback strings like 'Incorrect, please try again') as its conditioning state, and produces a response in a structured format with <think>... and <answer>...</answer> tags. A deterministic verifier checks correctness; if incorrect and t < T (max 5 turns), the same fixed feedback is appended and the model re-attempts. The training dataset consists of (state, terminal response, scalar importance weight) tuples, where the weight is computed from the trajectory's shaped return (discounted success indicator minus a repetition penalty) normalized per prompt.

## Method

DRIFT's core insight is that the KL-regularized RL objective admits a closed-form optimal trajectory distribution that is an exponential tilting of the reference policy, and that maximizing the RL objective is equivalent to minimizing a forward-KL divergence to this target. By applying importance sampling to change the measure from the optimal distribution to the fixed reference policy, the forward-KL objective reduces to a weighted negative log-likelihood where each trajectory is scaled by exp(R(τ)/β)/Ẑ(x). Practically, DRIFT operates in two decoupled stages: (1) an offline rollout stage that samples K=16 trajectories per prompt from the frozen reference policy under a stop-on-success protocol and assigns each a scalar importance weight with prompt-level normalization, and (2) a weighted SFT stage that applies the trajectory weight uniformly to the token-level cross-entropy loss of the terminal response only, avoiding credit assignment to verifier-rejected intermediate turns. This yields an optimization loop mechanically identical to standard SFT (same batch processing, same gradient computation) while provably targeting the KL-regularized RL objective, achieving RL-level multi-turn correction performance at SFT-level training cost.

## Results

DRIFT is evaluated using multi@5 accuracy (cumulative correctness over up to 5 turns) on six benchmarks spanning math (MATH, MATH500, TheoremQA) and general reasoning (MMLU-Redux, MMLU-Pro, GPQA). On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, DRIFT-5turn achieves a 60.5% all-benchmark average, up from 46.4% for the base model and 60.2% for the strongest online RL baseline (UFO-5turn). On Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, DRIFT-5turn reaches 55.6% (base: 48.1%; UFO-5turn: 56.1%). The largest gains appear on GPQA, where DRIFT improves over the base model by +24.8 points (Qwen) and +15.2 points (Llama).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the online RL baseline UFO-5turn, DRIFT achieves a marginal edge on Qwen2.5-3B (60.5% vs. 60.2% all-benchmark average) while trailing slightly on Llama3.1-8B (55.6% vs. 56.1%), with the gap concentrated on MMLU-Redux where UFO's repetition-suppression helps on small discrete answer spaces. DRIFT clearly outperforms the two-turn online RL method SCoRe-2turn by 3.1 points (Qwen) and 2.7 points (Llama) on the all-benchmark average, and surpasses the offline self-training baseline STaR-2turn by 5.4 and 3.1 points respectively. Compared to single-turn PPO, DRIFT gains 9.3 points on Qwen and 4.0 points on Llama, indicating that multi-turn correction learning transfers substantially beyond first-turn accuracy. All SFT-based baselines (SFT, SFT-5turn) are outperformed by DRIFT by 5–10 points on the all-benchmark average, confirming that importance-weighted trajectory supervision captures correction dynamics that unweighted multi-turn SFT misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIFT operationalizes the well-known RL-as-inference equivalence (Levine, 2018; Peters & Schaal, 2007) at the trajectory level for multi-turn correction, showing that the KL-regularized RL objective (Ouyang et al., 2022) reduces to importance-weighted SFT under a fixed reference policy. It departs from online multi-turn RL methods (SCoRe, UFO) that require fresh rollouts at every update, and from naive offline SFT on correction traces that suffers from distribution shift and behavioral collapse (Kumar et al., 2024). The closest prior work includes single-turn reward-weighted fine-tuning (Qin & Springenberg, 2025; Du et al., 2026) and offline RL for conversation (Mukherjee et al., 2025), but DRIFT is the first to combine trajectory-level exponential reweighting, prompt-level partition-function normalization, and terminal-step retention specifically for the multi-turn stop-on-success correction protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DRIFT achieves RL-competitive multi-turn correction performance at SFT-level training cost, which is critical because multi-turn rollout cost scales with interaction horizon and makes online RL prohibitively expensive for LLMs. However, the method is bounded by its offline coverage: it can only upweight behaviors already present in the reference policy's rollouts and cannot discover novel correction strategies through exploration. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer horizons, stochastic or preference-based feedback, and whether systematic rollout-refresh or hybrid offline-online schedules can close the remaining gap to fully online RL.
