## Problem

Existing Graph Foundation Model (GFM) prompts are entangled with specific source domains and pre-training strategies during pre-training, limiting their generalisability to unseen target domains and different pre-trained models. Additionally, current GFM prompt tuning relies solely on few-shot labelled data, neglecting the abundant unlabelled target-domain test nodes that are available during adaptation, leaving the train-test distribution shift unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GFMate takes a frozen pre-trained GFM (built on standard GNN backbones such as GCN, GAT, SAGE, or H2GCN, pre-trained via link prediction, DGI, or graph contrastive learning), the target-domain node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, a small set of few-shot labelled nodes, and all unlabelled target-domain test nodes. It outputs node class predictions (or graph class predictions via subgraph averaging for graph classification). Unusually, the unlabelled test nodes are not merely passive message-passing context but are actively exploited through complementary labels to drive prompt optimisation, and the entire prompt tuning occurs post-pre-training without any modification to the GFM parameters or the input graph.

## Method

GFMate introduces two pre-training-agnostic prompts applied only at test-time: (1) centroid prompts β that additively shift class-centroid embeddings (initialised as few-shot means) toward true cluster centres in the target domain, and (2) layer prompts η that learn per-layer weights for multi-layer ensemble prediction, adapting to hop-aggregation patterns specific to the target domain. The core training mechanism is Test-time Graph Complementary Learning (TGCL), which assigns each unlabelled test node a complementary label (the least similar class at a pivot layer selected by minimum layer-wise entropy) and jointly optimises both prompts via a convex combination of a few-shot cross-entropy loss and a complementary-label loss that pushes centroids away from the complementary class. An excess risk bound (Proposition 1) shows the generalisation gap scales as O(C/√N), motivating the use of all test nodes. The overall complexity is O(dL(N+E)), making it substantially more efficient than existing GFM prompt methods that require per-domain learnable parameters.

## Results

On one-shot cross-domain node classification across 9 datasets, GFMate achieves the best accuracy on all benchmarks, with headline results of 79.67% on Cornell, 76.63% on Texas, 59.68% on Cora, and 58.85% on Amazon-Photo. The maximum improvement over the strongest GFM baseline (SAMGPT) reaches 18.69 percentage points (Cornell: 79.67% vs. 60.98% for GCOPE) and the paper reports up to 30.63% relative improvement over existing methods. GFMate also achieves 98.24% faster convergence time and 97.18% lower peak GPU memory compared to SAMGPT, while using only ~5K tunable parameters versus SAMGPT's ~283K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFMate outperforms all 21 baselines across every cross-domain dataset. Against the strongest GFM baseline SAMGPT, GFMate gains 9.84 points on Texas (76.63 vs. 66.79), 20.33 on Cornell (79.67 vs. 59.34), 9.13 on Chameleon (47.25 vs. 38.12), and 6.85 on Cora (59.68 vs. 52.83). Against GCOPE, the second-best GFM method, improvements range from 12.21 points on Arxiv-year (30.19 vs. 17.98) to 18.69 on Cornell (79.67 vs. 60.98). Single-domain prompt methods (DAGPrompt, All-In-One) achieve competitive results in same-domain settings but degrade substantially in cross-domain transfer (e.g., ProNoG* drops from 55.31 to 48.25 on Texas), while GFMate maintains its advantage. In terms of efficiency, GFMate requires only ~5K tunable parameters and 19–269 seconds of adaptation time versus SAMGPT's ~283K parameters and 1076–1579 seconds, representing 98.24% and 97.18% reductions respectively. GFMate also demonstrates plug-in generalisability, improving MDGPT and SAMGPT by 5–16 points when applied on top of their pre-trained models regardless of pre-training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GFMate departs from the dominant GFM prompt paradigm (MDGPT, SAMGPT, MDGFM) where prompts are jointly pre-trained with the GFM on source domains and then few-shot fine-tuned on the target domain. Instead, it introduces pre-training-agnostic prompts (centroid and layer prompts) that are learned entirely at test-time, drawing on complementary label learning (Ishida et al., 2017) to actively exploit unlabelled test nodes rather than using them only as passive message-passing context. The method is also positioned against single-domain graph prompt tuning (GPPT, ProNoG, DAGPrompt) and test-time adaptation for graphs (GTrans, GraphPatcher), distinguishing itself by tuning lightweight prompts without modifying either the frozen GFM or the test graph structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-training-agnostic design makes GFMate a plug-in module for any GFM regardless of backbone or pre-training objective, which is practically valuable given the rapid proliferation of GFM architectures. The 98% reduction in adaptation time and 97% reduction in GPU memory versus SAMGPT while achieving higher accuracy makes cross-domain GFM adaptation substantially more accessible. Open questions include whether the complementary-label strategy generalizes to larger-scale or LLM-based GFMs, whether the modest absolute accuracy on large datasets (e.g., ~30% on Arxiv-year) limits practical utility, and whether the approach extends to regression or generative downstream tasks beyond classification.
