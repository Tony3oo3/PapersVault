## Problem

Transformers model long-range dependencies but remain fundamentally geometry-blind, treating tokens as points in a flat ambient space with no awareness of local coordinate frames. Prior attempts to inject geometry into attention do so only as additive biases or positional encodings, leaving the core similarity computation insensitive to coordinate changes. The paper asks whether attention itself can be redefined as an intrinsic geometric operator on a learned manifold, so that geometry defines similarity rather than merely modulating it post-hoc.

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous geometric inputs: high-dimensional gene expression vectors (20,387 genes for scRNA-seq, top-3,000 HVGs for spatial transcriptomics), 2D spatial coordinates for tissue spots, or 1,024 uniformly sampled 3D surface points for point clouds. Each input token is projected into a low-dimensional base manifold coordinate (d_b) and a higher-dimensional fiber feature (d_f), which are then processed through the VBA attention layers. Outputs are task-specific: unsupervised spatial domain assignments (ARI/NMI), cell type labels (accuracy/macro-F1), or 40-class object categories (OA/mAcc), produced by a classification head on the final fiber representation.

## Method

VBA-Transformer redefines self-attention as an operator on a learned vector bundle: each token carries a base coordinate b_i and a fiber vector f_i, and the core 'Align-then-Compare' mechanism first transports key/value fibers from point j into the local frame at point i via a learned isometric operator T_{j→i} ∈ SO(d_f) before computing dot-product similarity. The transport is parameterized as the matrix exponential of a skew-symmetric generator predicted by a lightweight MLP from the pair of base coordinates, guaranteeing orthogonality by construction. A separate ConnectionNet computes the formal curvature 2-form Ω = dΓ + Γ∧Γ (via automatic differentiation for the Jacobian and commutators for the algebraic term), which is contracted into an O(d_b)-invariant effective operator R_eff that modulates fiber features to capture non-flat geometry. A multi-bundle design with data-dependent gating allows M parallel fiber pathways to be adaptively combined. The VBA block is a drop-in replacement for standard attention in a Pre-LN Transformer, and the paper provides theoretical guarantees including invariance to local orthogonal frame changes and Lipschitz perturbation bounds on attention logits.

## Results

On the PBMC scRNA-seq benchmark, VBA-SC (SSL) achieves 80.68% overall accuracy and 0.65 macro F1, outperforming the strongest Transformer baselines (scBERT at 75.52%, scGPT at 75.47%) by over 5 points. On the Pancreas dataset, VBA-SC (SSL) reaches 97.64% OA and 0.89 F1, surpassing the Seurat baseline (96.37% OA). On spatial transcriptomics, VBA-ST achieves state-of-the-art ARI of 0.59 and NMI of 0.69 on the Breast Cancer Visium dataset, exceeding the next-best method (DiffusionST) by 0.15 in NMI. On ModelNet40, VBA-P achieves 92.9% OA with 7.0M parameters, competitive with specialized point cloud architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PBMC, VBA-SC (SSL) wins decisively over all seven baselines, beating the closest Transformer competitors scBERT (75.52%) and scGPT (75.47%) by over 5 points in OA, and outperforming the correlation-based Seurat by 26 points. On Pancreas, VBA-SC (SSL) surpasses the strong Seurat baseline (96.37%) by 1.27 points and all Transformer-based methods by 3–5 points. On the Breast Cancer spatial transcriptomics dataset, VBA-ST achieves the best ARI (0.59) and NMI (0.69), exceeding DiffusionST (0.57/0.54) and SpaGCN (0.57/0.61) by a notable margin in NMI. On the 12-sample DLPFC benchmark, VBA-ST is competitive but not uniformly dominant, achieving the best or tied-best ARI on 4 of 12 samples and matching the average ARI (0.50) of BASS and DiffusionST. On ModelNet40, VBA-P (92.9% OA) trails the best specialized methods PointGA (93.8%) and PointTransformer (93.7%) by roughly 1 point, though it remains competitive with DGCNN (92.7%) and PCT (93.2%). The ablation further confirms that VBA's gains over geometry-biased Transformer (GBT) are substantial: +7.1% OA on point clouds and +8.4% on PBMC, demonstrating that intrinsic geometric alignment outperforms additive bias corrections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer attention paradigm by redefining it as an intrinsic geometric operator on a learned vector bundle, building directly on the standard self-attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the fiber-over-base-manifold structure from bundle neural networks (Bamberger et al., 2024). It departs sharply from prior geometric Transformers (Graphormer, GBT, GPS) that inject geometry as additive attention biases or positional encodings, instead performing parallel transport to align fiber features before any similarity computation. The 'Align-then-Compare' formulation also distinguishes VBA from Riemannian and hyperbolic neural networks, which embed data into fixed curved spaces rather than learning the manifold and connection jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that embedding geometry natively inside the attention operator (via isometric transport and curvature correction) yields substantially stronger representations than post-hoc geometric bias, as shown by the 7–8% ablation gap over GBT. However, several open questions remain: the endpoint-conditioned transport is explicitly a surrogate for true path-dependent parallel transport (composition law not enforced), the invariance theorem (Theorem A.4) is not actually satisfied by the TransportNet by construction, and the curvature computation via Jacobian is computationally expensive (12.2G FLOPs on ModelNet40 vs. 2.0G for PointGA). Validation is concentrated on biological data, and point-cloud results are competitive but below SOTA, leaving the question of whether the geometric inductive bias generalizes to large-scale vision or language tasks.
