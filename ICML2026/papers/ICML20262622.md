## Problem

Large multimodal models processing long-form audio-visual content generate tens of thousands of tokens, causing prohibitive memory and latency during inference. Existing token compression methods apply uniform pruning strategies that ignore modality-specific redundancy (audio carries denser semantic cues while video exhibits substantial spatial-temporal redundancy) and instruction-dependent reasoning behaviors, leading to either excessive information loss or limited computational savings.

## Input / Output

PRIM operates at inference time on video clips sampled at 2 fps (up to 128 frames), audio streams at a fixed sampling rate, and a natural-language instruction. Video and audio are segmented into fixed-duration temporal windows, within which modality tokens are synchronized and concatenated in chronological order before feeding into the LLM. The output is a standard text response generated by the LLM, with the entire compression pipeline being training-free and requiring no modification to the backbone model's parameters.

## Method

PRIM is a cooperative, training-free compression pipeline spanning both multimodal encoders and the LLM, motivated by the observation that cross-modal attention is concentrated in early LLM layers and that audio tokens consistently receive higher attention than video tokens. It comprises four stages: (1) Multimodal Cross-Fusion (MCF) uses four Transformer blocks to perform text-conditioned audio-visual fusion before the LLM, externalizing cross-modal interactions and reducing the token count entering the backbone; (2) Attention-Guided Selection (AGS) uses the audio encoder's final-layer attention to retain top-S% salient audio tokens per temporal window, then leverages per-window audio retention scores to dynamically set the video pruning ratio (higher audio saliency → lower video pruning); (3) Frequency-Aware Compaction (FAC) applies a parameter-free 2D DCT to visual features, retaining only low-frequency components to reduce spatial tokens from N² to C², combined with temporal redundancy removal via inter-frame cosine similarity; (4) Task-Aware Adaptive Pruning (TAA) identifies high-quality attention heads via a Multimodal Attention Value (MAV) score and adaptively prunes tokens inside the LLM based on instruction-token attention, adjusting the retained token count per instance without additional training.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, PRIM achieves an average score of 61.1 (99.5% of full-model 61.4) at 65% token retention, 60.4 (98.4%) at 50%, and 56.7 (92.3%) at 35%, consistently outperforming all five baselines at every compression level. At the most aggressive 35% retention, PRIM retains 92.3% of full-model accuracy while reducing FLOPs to 28%, compared to 90.9% (VisionZip) and 90.7% (FastVID). On the AVUT benchmark (Table 2), PRIM achieves 62.3 average accuracy at 50% retention with only 33% FLOPs, surpassing FastVID (60.2) and DyCoke (57.7). Efficiency gains are substantial: at 50% retention, PRIM reduces prefilling time from 289 ms (full) to 109 ms and memory from 33 GB to 27 GB on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIM outperforms all five baselines at every tested retention ratio (65%, 50%, 35%) on the Qwen2.5-Omni-7B average score. At 65% retention, PRIM (61.1) edges out the closest competitors VisionZip (60.9) and FastVID (60.8) by 0.2–0.3 points while using fewer FLOPs (54% vs. 69% and 56% respectively). The gap widens substantially at 50% retention, where PRIM (60.4) leads VisionZip (59.8) by 0.6 and FastVID (59.5) by 0.9, and at 35% retention, where PRIM (56.7) surpasses VisionZip (55.8) by 0.9 and FastVID (55.7) by 1.0. DyCoke and PruneVid are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing PRIM by 5–7 points at 65% retention. On the AVUT benchmark, PRIM achieves 62.3 average at 50% retention (33% FLOPs), beating FastVID (60.2) by 2.1 and DyCoke (57.7) by 4.6, with particularly strong gains on event localization (+4.3 over FastVID) and information extraction (+1.6 over FastVID). On LLaVA-Video (Table 4), PRIM achieves 61.8 overall VideoMME at 25% retention, outperforming FastVID (59.3) and DyCoke (58.6) by 2.5 and 3.2 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-pruning line of work from vision-only VLMs (FastV, VisionZip, DynamicViT) and video LLMs (DyCoke, FastVID, PruneVid) to the audio-visual multimodal setting, where prior methods apply uniform or modality-agnostic compression. The key conceptual departure is a cooperative, modality-aware pipeline that spans both the encoders and the LLM: early text-conditioned cross-fusion externalizes cross-modal interactions out of the LLM, attention-guided selection leverages audio saliency to control video pruning per temporal window, and a parameter-free DCT-based frequency compaction removes spatial redundancy. The instruction-aware adaptive pruning inside the LLM, guided by a Multimodal Attention Value (MAV) head-filtering criterion, departs from fixed-ratio baselines by making the retained token count instance-dependent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRIM addresses a practical deployment bottleneck—tens of thousands of audio-visual tokens per video—by achieving ~50–70% FLOPs reduction while retaining 92–99.5% of full-token accuracy across six benchmarks and three model families, all without additional training. The attention-based diagnosis (audio dominance over video, early-layer cross-modal mixing) provides a reusable analytical lens for future multimodal efficiency work. Open questions remain: the method is offline-only and does not handle streaming inputs, the optimal audio-vs-video compression ratio is task-dependent and not yet solved in a general way, and compatibility with future architectures that abandon time-window-based token concatenation is argued but not empirically validated.
