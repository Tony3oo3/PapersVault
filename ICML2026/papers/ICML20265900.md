## Problem

Diffusion-based graph generators cannot guarantee that outputs strictly satisfy domain-specific hard constraints (e.g., degree bounds, planarity, chemical valence), because stochastic denoising inherently assigns non-zero probability to infeasible samples. Existing projection-based methods either relax graphs into continuous spaces (yielding only approximate feasibility after rounding) or are handcrafted for narrow constraint types, lacking a unified mechanism for diverse or combined constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained unconditional discrete diffusion model (DiGress, GDSS, DruM, etc.) and a set of hard constraint specifications expressed as logical/arithmetic predicates over node attributes and edge indicators. It produces graphs that strictly satisfy all constraints, with projection applied only during the final ~5% of denoising steps to minimize distributional drift. The SMT-based projector guarantees exact satisfaction, while the autoregressive neural projector approximates it in a single forward pass for batched inference.

## Method

NSPSG decouples distribution learning from constraint enforcement by projecting unconstrained diffusion samples onto the feasible set via discrete symbolic reasoning. For arithmetic constraints, graph components are declared as SMT decision variables and constraints are encoded as logical formulas solved by MaxSMT, which minimizes a graph edit distance (soft constraint) while enforcing all hard constraints. For structural constraints (planarity, connectivity), a CEGAR loop iteratively solves the SMT formula, verifies candidates with a polynomial-time checker, and adds blocking clauses for detected violations. To reduce solver cost, an autoregressive Transformer projector is trained on (invalid, valid) graph pairs generated by the SMT solver, learning to predict the corrected graph in one pass. The key design choice is applying projection only in late denoising steps, which the paper shows analytically keeps the feasibility distance small and bounds entropy drop, avoiding the distributional drift and computational overhead of full-step projection.

## Results

On arithmetic constraints (edge count, triangle count, degree) across Community-small, Ego-small, and ENZYMES, NSPSG(SMT) achieves 100% constraint validity (VAL_C) in all 9 settings, with MMD scores up to 24% lower than PRODIGY and the base diffusion models. On structural constraints (Planar, Tree, Lobster), both NSPSG(SMT) and NSPSG(AR) achieve 100% Valid, 100% V.U.N., and 100% Property, surpassing ConStruct (100% Valid but higher MMD) and DiGress+ (76–92% Valid). On the non-linear LP dataset, NSPSG(SMT) reaches 99–100% validity across all ρ settings, improving over LayerDAG (56–96%) by up to 43 percentage points while matching or beating its W₁ and MMD distributional metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PRODIGY (Sharma et al., 2024), NSPSG achieves 100% validity across all arithmetic constraint settings, whereas PRODIGY ranges from 52% to 100% depending on dataset and constraint type (e.g., 52% on Community-small edge count with EDP-GNN, 64% on Ego-small). On structural constraints, NSPSG matches ConStruct's (Madeira et al., 2024) 100% Property score but achieves strictly lower MMD on Planar (0.0002 vs 0.0003 Deg.) and Tree (0.0002 vs 0.0003 Deg.), indicating better distributional fidelity; on Lobster, NSPSG reaches 100% Valid vs ConStruct's 86.8%. On the LP non-linear constraint benchmark, NSPSG(SMT) achieves 99–100% validity, outperforming LayerDAG (Li et al., 2024) by 43 percentage points at ρ=0 (99% vs 56%) and 4 percentage points at ρ=1 (100% vs 96%), while also matching or beating LayerDAG on W₁ and MMD. The autoregressive neural projector (NSPSG(AR)) achieves near-identical validity to the SMT solver (100% on structural, 97–99% on LP) at substantially lower inference cost, approaching LayerDAG's sampling speed. Compared to unconditional diffusion baselines (DiGress, GDSS, DruM, EDP-GNN), NSPSG consistently improves both validity and MMD, confirming that the projection step preserves the learned distribution while enforcing constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the projection-based constrained generation paradigm (PRODIGY, ConStruct) by reformulating the projection step as a discrete symbolic reasoning problem solvable via SMT/MaxSMT, rather than the continuous Lagrangian relaxation used by PRODIGY or the constraint-specific edge-absorbing noise models used by ConStruct. It builds on discrete diffusion backbones (DiGress, GDSS) for the unconstrained sampling component and introduces a CEGAR loop for structural constraints that exploits the gap between verification (polynomial-time) and synthesis (hard) for graph properties. The autoregressive neural projector that learns to approximate SMT solving is a new contribution, drawing on the broader trend of using neural networks to accelerate symbolic reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—requiring no retraining of the diffusion model when constraints change—makes this framework broadly applicable to constraint-critical domains like molecular design, neural architecture search, and network configuration. The counterintuitive finding that projecting only in late denoising steps outperforms full-step projection for discrete graphs (contrary to continuous diffusion) is supported by both entropy-drop analysis and experiments, offering practical guidance for future constrained generation work. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs where SMT solving becomes expensive, handling of soft constraint trade-offs beyond minimal edit distance, and whether the neural projector generalizes to constraint types unseen during its supervised training.
