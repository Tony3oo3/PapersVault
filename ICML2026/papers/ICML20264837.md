## Problem

Current open-source video-based world models rely solely on pre-training with pixel supervision to implicitly learn action following, which limits their accuracy on complex composite actions and long-horizon generation. No effective post-training framework exists that uses reinforcement learning to directly and explicitly teach autoregressive video world models to follow interaction signals with fine-grained feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264837/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text or image prompt describing the world, a sequence of discrete actions (8 basic movements: forward, backward, left, right, up, down, left/right rotation, which can be combined into composite actions), and previously generated video clips as autoregressive history. It outputs 16-frame video clips generated sequentially, supporting long-horizon sequences up to 16 clips (~256 decoded frames). Each clip is conditioned on the full action history and prior observations, making the generation strictly autoregressive and interactive.

## Method

WorldCompass redesigns the RL pipeline for autoregressive video world models around three innovations. First, a clip-level rollout strategy generates a shared prefix once and then samples G candidate clips at a single target index, reducing rollout complexity from O(N·G) to O(N+G) while providing fine-grained, context-consistent reward signals. Second, two complementary reward functions—an Interaction Following score (using 3D foundation models to estimate camera trajectories and match discrete actions) and a Visual Quality score (HPSv3 on sampled frames)—act as mutual regularizers to suppress reward hacking. Third, the optimization uses a negative-aware fine-tuning strategy (adapted from DiffusionNFT) with flow-matching loss, clipped advantage weighting, and efficiency optimizations including random timestep subsampling, Best-of-N sample selection, and progressive clip-index scheduling that induces curriculum learning over the generation horizon.

## Results

On the WorldPlay test set (600 cases), WorldCompass post-training dramatically improves interaction-following accuracy for complex composite actions, raising it from ~20% to ~55% (e.g., HY-Video-1.5 long-term: 19.70 → 54.82, +35.12 points). For basic actions, accuracy improves by 6–12 points (e.g., HY-Video-1.5 long-term: 64.28 → 76.56, +12.28). Visual quality (HPSv3) also improves consistently, with gains of +1.0 to +1.9 across settings (e.g., HY-Video-1.5 long-term combined: −0.33 → 0.73). These gains hold across both base model variants (HunyuanVideo-1.5-8B and Wan2.2-5B) and all three video lengths (125, 253, 381 frames).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the unmodified WorldPlay model (both HunyuanVideo-1.5-8B and Wan2.2-5B variants) before RL post-training. WorldCompass wins decisively on every metric and setting: for composite actions, interaction accuracy improves by +23 to +36 points (e.g., 19.70 → 54.82 on HY-Video-1.5 long-term), representing a qualitative shift from failing to follow actions to successfully executing them. For basic actions, the gain is more modest at +6 to +12 points, reflecting faster response during action switching. HPSv3 visual quality scores improve by +0.56 to +1.94 across all configurations, confirming that the RL training does not sacrifice visual fidelity for action accuracy. The improvements are consistent across both base model architectures and all three video lengths, indicating the framework generalizes rather than overfitting to a specific setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two active research threads: video-based world models (Genie, Cosmos, WorldPlay) that are currently limited to pre-training with pixel supervision, and RL post-training for diffusion models (Flow-GRPO, Dance-GRPO, DiffusionNFT) that assume single-shot parallel generation. The key conceptual departure is that world models require autoregressive, sequential, long-horizon generation conditioned on discrete action signals, which the authors argue makes existing RL-for-diffusion pipelines inapplicable. Methodologically, the paper adapts DiffusionNFT's negative-aware fine-tuning loss but redesigns the rollout strategy (clip-level rather than sample-level) and reward structure (complementary interaction-following + visual quality) specifically for the interactive autoregressive setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RL post-training framework purpose-built for autoregressive interactive world models, and the demonstrated jump from ~20% to ~55% action-following accuracy on complex composite actions suggests RL can fundamentally alter model behavior in ways pre-training alone cannot achieve. The clip-level rollout strategy (reducing complexity from O(N·G) to O(N+G)) is a practical technique likely transferable to other autoregressive generation tasks. However, the authors acknowledge a significant open problem: no reliable reward signal exists to penalize visual quality drift or spatial memory loss over long horizons, meaning the current conservative training (fewer iterations, low LR) is a stopgap rather than a solution. The framework's generalizability beyond the specific WorldPlay architecture and 8-action discrete space remains untested.
