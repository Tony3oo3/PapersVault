## Problem

Existing inference-time hallucination mitigation methods for VLMs fail to simultaneously address three issues: the visual attention sink (attention persistently misallocated to task-irrelevant visual tokens), insufficient overall visual contribution during decoding, and imbalanced cross-modal fusion (enhancing only visual attention without adjusting query token attention). Prior approaches like VAF scale visual attention proportionally to current-step scores but cannot distinguish relevant from irrelevant regions and risk degrading query comprehension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263712/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, inference-time intervention on standard VLMs (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, Qwen2-VL 7B, Qwen3-VL 8B). It consumes an RGB image (encoded into visual tokens) and a text query, and produces a generated text response (caption, VQA answer, or yes/no classification). The key operational detail is that a visual saliency map is pre-computed during the prefilling phase up to a model-specific peak layer, requiring no additional forward passes beyond the standard prefill, and then guides attention modification at each decoding step with only ~13% latency overhead.

## Method

GIFT pre-computes a holistic visual saliency map by tracking positive changes in visual token attention ('gaze shifts') over information-rich query tokens (identified via spaCy POS tagging) at the layer exhibiting the largest positive attention change. Since irrelevant regions show minimal attention shifts, this naturally suppresses the visual attention sink. During decoding, at selected cross-modal fusion layers, GIFT amplifies visual token attention proportionally to the pre-computed saliency map (multiplying by exp(α·S_j)), and simultaneously scales query token attention by the overall visual attention amplification ratio r^l to preserve cross-modal fusion balance. This dual enhancement distinguishes GIFT from prior methods that only boost visual attention, ensuring the model maintains proper query interpretation while grounding in relevant visual regions.

## Results

GIFT achieves up to 20.7% reduction in CHAIRs (50.2→39.8 on LLaVA-1.5 7B), 15.9% reduction in MMHal-Bench hallucination rate (32.7→27.5 on Qwen2-VL 7B), and 3.0% improvement in POPE F1 over greedy decoding, while also improving MMHal-Bench informativeness score by up to 11.7%. On general vision-language benchmarks (MME, SEED-Bench), GIFT maintains performance within 0.1–1.5 points of greedy decoding across all four models. Inference latency is only 1.13× greedy decoding, compared to 1.56× (Rel-Attn), 1.99× (VCD), and 11.10× (VAR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIFT outperforms all four baselines (VAF, VAR, Rel-Attn, VCD) on every hallucination metric for both LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B. On CHAIRs, GIFT reduces the score from 50.2 (greedy) to 39.8 on LLaVA-1.5 7B, while the best baseline (Rel-Attn) only reaches 49.0 and VAR actually worsens it to 54.0. On MMHal-Bench hallucination rate, GIFT achieves 57.3 vs. 60.5 (VCD), 60.8 (VAR), 63.7 (Rel-Attn), and 66.3 (VAF) on LLaVA-1.5 7B. Notably, VAF and VCD degrade POPE accuracy below greedy decoding (77.2 and 77.7 vs. 79.5), whereas GIFT improves it to 81.9. On general benchmarks (MME, SEED-Bench), GIFT matches greedy decoding within 0.1–1.5 points, while VCD drops MME by 9.3 points and SEED by 2.0 points on LLaVA-1.5 7B. Baselines are only available for LLaVA models; for Qwen2-VL and Qwen3-VL, GIFT is compared solely against greedy decoding, where it still improves all hallucination metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIFT extends the attention-steering paradigm introduced by Zhang et al. (2023) and the proportional visual attention scaling of ClearSight (Yin et al., 2025) by replacing current-step attention scores with a pre-computed saliency map derived from 'gaze shifts'—positive changes in visual attention tracked over information-rich query tokens during prefilling. It departs from prior attention-steering methods in two key ways: (1) it explicitly mitigates the visual attention sink problem identified by Kang et al. (2025), which prior methods either ignore or address only through recalibration without strengthening overall visual contribution, and (2) it introduces a cross-modal fusion balance mechanism that proportionally scales query token attention alongside visual attention, addressing a gap where prior work enhanced only one modality. The method is positioned as a lightweight inference-time intervention, avoiding the computational overhead of contrastive decoding or the constraints of visual input modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIFT offers a practical, low-overhead (1.13× greedy) hallucination mitigation technique that works across diverse VLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL, Qwen3-VL) without retraining, making it immediately deployable in safety-critical applications like biomedicine and autonomous driving. The gaze-shift mechanism provides an interpretable, query-adaptive way to identify task-relevant visual regions without auxiliary models, and the finding that visual and query attention follow aligned layer-wise trends offers useful insight into VLM internal representations. Key limitations include reliance on the query to identify relevant regions (failing when answers don't depend on visual content) and the unexplored question of whether the fixed β=1.0 cross-modal balance is truly optimal. Open questions include dynamic per-step enhancement (not all decoding steps need visual grounding) and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-image inputs.
