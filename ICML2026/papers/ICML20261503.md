## Problem

Aligning video diffusion models with human preferences via GRPO requires backpropagating through the full denoising trajectory, demanding hundreds of GPU days for a 14B model. Existing efficiency methods that subsample timesteps introduce timestep-confounded variance in advantage estimation and time-dependent gradient scale imbalances, causing severe training instability and failing to reach full-trajectory performance. The paper asks whether a principled single-step training paradigm can match full-trajectory alignment quality at minimal computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts as input and generates videos using Wan2.1 flow-matching models (1.3B or 14B parameters). During training, each rollout performs a single ODE-to-SDE stochastic transition at one randomly sampled timestep for exploration and gradient computation, while all remaining timesteps use deterministic ODE sampling (20 steps for 1.3B, 12 for 14B). Evaluation uses 50-step inference scored by HPSv3 (visual quality, top-30% frame average) and VideoAlign (motion quality), with VBench for general video quality assessment.

## Method

Flash-GRPO achieves single-step training through two mechanisms. First, iso-temporal grouping assigns all G rollouts within a prompt group the same timestep t_k while varying only the initial noise, so advantage estimates compare generation quality under identical denoising conditions rather than conflating policy performance with timestep difficulty; temporal diversity is preserved by sampling different t_k across the global batch. Second, temporal gradient rectification addresses the fact that the policy gradient under Euler-Maruyama SDE discretization is implicitly scaled by λ(t) = √Δt/σ_t + σ_t√Δt(1−t)/(2t), which varies by orders of magnitude across the trajectory; the method explicitly divides the advantage-weighted policy ratio by λ(t) to normalize gradient magnitudes to unity. The resulting loss L_TGR = (1/G) Σ (Â_t^i / λ(t)) · r_t^i(θ) ensures uniform contribution from all timesteps, eliminating discretization-induced optimization bias and enabling stable monotonic reward growth with a 6× training acceleration over full-trajectory Flow-GRPO.

## Results

On VBench with 350 GPU hours of training on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, Flash-GRPO achieves the highest Aesthetic Quality (66.43), Subject Consistency (98.70), and Object Class (90.00) among all RL-aligned methods, while maintaining strong Imaging Quality (68.28). On HPSv3 reward, Flash-GRPO reaches a peak of approximately 5.4 versus 5.1 for full-trajectory Flow-GRPO, with faster convergence and no training collapse. The method achieves 6× training acceleration compared to full-trajectory GRPO while attaining higher evaluation performance, and scales consistently to the 14B Wan2.1 model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flow-GRPO-Fast1 (single-step sliding-window baseline), Flash-GRPO wins on all four VBench metrics, with the largest gap in Imaging Quality (68.28 vs. 65.96, a 2.3-point improvement) and Object Class (90.00 vs. 88.15); Flow-GRPO-Fast1 also suffers catastrophic training instability without KL regularization, while Flash-GRPO maintains stable monotonic reward growth. Against full-trajectory Flow-GRPO, Flash-GRPO achieves higher Aesthetic Quality (66.43 vs. 65.79), Subject Consistency (98.70 vs. 97.28), and Object Class (90.00 vs. 87.92), though it trails slightly on Imaging Quality (68.28 vs. 68.60); on HPSv3 reward curves, Flash-GRPO converges faster and reaches a higher ceiling (~5.4 vs. ~5.1) while Flow-GRPO exhibits a catastrophic collapse between 200–400 GPU hours. Compared to the pre-RL reference models (CogVideoX-2B, Hunyuan-Video, and the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B base), Flash-GRPO improves Aesthetic Quality by 1.0–6.1 points and Object Class by 1.2–3.5 points, confirming that RL alignment via Flash-GRPO preserves the backbone's generative capabilities while enhancing human-preference dimensions. On Motion Quality (VideoAlign), Flash-GRPO achieves a final score of approximately −0.28 versus −0.34 for the baseline, confirming improvements in temporal coherence alongside visual aesthetics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flash-GRPO extends the Flow-GRPO/DanceGRPO line of work that adapted Group Relative Policy Optimization to flow-matching models for visual generation, specifically targeting the video diffusion setting where full-trajectory backpropagation is prohibitively expensive. It departs from both the full-trajectory paradigm (Flow-GRPO) and the sliding-window subsampling approach (Flow-GRPO-Fast, MixGRPO) by identifying two specific pathologies in naive single-step training—timestep-confounded advantage variance and time-dependent gradient scaling—and addressing them with iso-temporal grouping and temporal gradient rectification, respectively. The ODE-to-SDE exploration mechanism and GRPO objective are inherited directly from Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO, while the theoretical derivation of the λ(t) scaling factor from the Euler-Maruyama discretization is novel to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× training acceleration while matching or exceeding full-trajectory alignment quality is practically significant for a field where video RL alignment currently demands hundreds of GPU days per experiment, lowering the barrier to iterative research and deployment. The iso-temporal grouping and gradient rectification mechanisms are simple, general-purpose corrections that could be adopted by other single-step or few-step RL training pipelines beyond video diffusion. Limitations include validation only on the Wan2.1 model family with specific reward models (HPSv3, VideoAlign), and the 14B comparison is restricted to the first half of the training schedule, leaving open whether the advantage persists over longer training or generalizes to other architectures (e.g., CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo) and alignment objectives.
