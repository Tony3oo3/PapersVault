## Problem

Diffusion Language Models (DLLMs) promise parallel token generation but have failed to deliver practical speed advantages over optimized autoregressive (AR) serving engines like vLLM. The root cause is that most DLLMs rely on bidirectional attention, which couples each token's KV state to future positions and breaks standard prefix KV caching—the very mechanism that makes AR inference fast. Even block-wise variants that partially restore prefix reuse suffer from stop-and-wait behavior, where resolved tokens cannot be cached until an entire block finalizes.

## Input / Output

WeDLM takes a standard text prompt as input, which is processed through a causal prefill to initialize the KV cache, followed by iterative streaming parallel decoding over a fixed-size sliding window of W slots containing mask tokens and partially resolved predictions. The output is a generated text sequence built incrementally as a contiguous left-to-right prefix: at each decoding step, the leftmost contiguous run of high-confidence filled tokens is committed into the KV cache and appended to the output, while new mask slots are appended to maintain constant parallelism. The window size W (e.g., 6) and entropy threshold τ control the trade-off between per-step parallelism and generation quality.

## Method

WeDLM performs diffusion-style mask recovery entirely under standard causal attention via Topological Reordering: observed tokens are physically moved to the front of the sequence while their logical positions are preserved through RoPE position IDs, so every masked token attends to the full observed context under an unmodified lower-triangular mask. To close the training-inference gap, Dual-Stream Masking concatenates a clean memory stream with a masked prediction stream (sharing positional encodings), ensuring each prediction block conditions on clean history rather than noisy intermediate predictions. At inference, Streaming Parallel Decoding combines a distance-penalized entropy rule (biasing acceptance toward earlier positions to form contiguous prefixes), strict causal attention (guaranteeing immediate KV validity of committed tokens), and a dynamic sliding window that continuously refills new masks—eliminating the stop-and-wait bottleneck of block-wise methods. The entire pipeline is natively compatible with FlashAttention, PagedAttention, and CUDA Graphs without kernel modifications, and is trained via 100B-token continued pretraining from AR checkpoints (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-8B) plus supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On the instruct model benchmark suite (10 benchmarks spanning reasoning, math, science, and code), WeDLM-8B achieves an average score of 77.53, surpassing the Qwen3-8B-Instruct baseline (75.12) by 2.4 points and the best diffusion baseline SDAR-8B (74.22) by 3.3 points. On base models, WeDLM-8B reaches 74.72 average (+2.1 over Qwen3-8B base) with particularly strong gains on GSM8K (+4.2), MATH (+2.8), and GPQA-Diamond (+5.4). In inference speed, WeDLM-8B delivers up to 3× end-to-end speedup over vLLM-served AR on complex reasoning tasks and over 10× on low-entropy generation; under batched decoding on math, it reaches a peak throughput of 1,475.8 tokens/s (6.1× the AR baseline at batch size 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeDLM-8B (77.53 avg) outperforms the Qwen3-8B-Instruct AR baseline (75.12) by 2.4 points, with the largest margins on MMLU (+3.6), GPQA-Diamond (+3.5), HumanEval (+8.5), and HumanEval-plus (+9.2), though it trails on HellaSwag (82.94 vs. 86.13). Against the strongest diffusion baseline SDAR-8B (74.22), WeDLM-8B leads by 3.3 points on average, with particularly large gaps on MATH (+21.4) and GPQA-Diamond (+6.6). The fully bidirectional diffusion models LLaDA-8B (56.77) and Dream-7B (62.96) trail WeDLM-8B by 20.8 and 14.6 points respectively, confirming that causal attention with topological reordering preserves far more of the AR backbone's capability. On inference speed, WeDLM-8B achieves 3.0×–3.3× aggregated throughput over vLLM-served Qwen3-8B across batch sizes 1–16, and up to 6.2× on mathematical reasoning at batch size 1, while never being slower than the AR baseline in any tested configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeDLM departs from the dominant bidirectional-attention design in masked diffusion language models (LLaDA, Dream) and the block-wise bidirectional variants (SDAR, BD3-LM, NBDiff) by performing all mask recovery under standard causal attention, enabled by a Topological Reordering that physically moves observed tokens to the prefix while preserving logical positions via RoPE. It also contrasts with post-hoc KV-caching approaches for bidirectional DLLMs (Fast-dLLM, D2F) by making the architecture inherently cache-compatible from the start rather than approximating reuse. The entropy-based confidence mechanism draws on Dream 7B, and the overall masked-diffusion training objective follows the MDLM formulation of Nie et al., but the streaming parallel decoding with a dynamic sliding window and position-aware distance penalty is a new inference procedure that eliminates the stop-and-wait bottleneck of block-wise methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion language model to demonstrate wall-clock speedups over vLLM-served autoregressive baselines under matched deployment settings, which is a practical milestone for the DLLM community that has long struggled to translate parallel decoding into real serving gains. The p_cache metric reframes the efficiency question from 'tokens per forward' to 'rate of prefix commitment,' offering a useful lens for future parallel-generation research. Key limitations include reduced post-hoc editability (committed tokens are irreversible), evidence concentrated on 7B/8B dense models, and sharply diminished speedups on high-entropy open-ended generation (only ~1.3–1.5×). Open questions include whether the approach scales to 100B+ dense or MoE backbones, whether a causal DLLM can be trained from scratch, and how to close the gap on high-perplexity tasks.
