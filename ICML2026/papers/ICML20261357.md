## Problem

Cross-modal fusion in MLLM-based recommender systems is assumed to provide resilience against poisoning, since an unperturbed modality can anchor semantics against unimodal noise. However, this consensus mechanism introduces a new, unexplored attack surface: when an adversary synchronises perturbations across both text and image, the fusion process amplifies rather than filters the coordinated signal. This paper formalises and exploits this cross-modal interactive poisoning threat, which existing interaction-level and single-modality attacks cannot capture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target item's text description and image (encoded via CLIP ViT-B/32 patch features), the user interaction histories of compromised users, and publicly observable popularity lists for anchor mining. Visual perturbations are applied at the patch-feature level under a strict ℓ∞ budget (clipped to [v_orig − ε, v_orig + ε]), while textual perturbations are discrete token replacements selected via greedy search with fluency and cross-modal coherence constraints. The output is a set of poisoned (text, image) pairs injected into the training data of a small fraction (≤0.7%) of compromised users, which after fine-tuning steers the target item's fused representation toward a high-exposure centroid.

## Method

VENOMREC operates in two stages. First, Exposure Alignment (EA) mines high-exposure anchor items from popularity lists within the target's category, encodes them with a public proxy model (CLIP + T5), and computes a normalised centroid z* in the joint embedding space as the attack destination. Second, Cross-modal Interactive Perturbation (CIP) iteratively co-adapts both modalities: it extracts the proxy model's cross-modal attention matrix via attention rollout to identify the top-k most influential token-patch correspondences, then applies a gradient-free sign-projection update on masked visual patches and a greedy token-replacement search on masked text positions, both optimising cosine alignment to z*. Crucially, the attention masks are recomputed after every iteration, creating a coupled feedback loop that exploits the fusion mechanism's own sensitivity structure rather than fighting against it, thereby bypassing the consensus check that suppresses independent unimodal noise.

## Results

On the Clothing dataset (Few-Shot), VENOMREC achieves ER@20 = 0.7170 and ER@5 = 0.1158, while maintaining recommendation utility close to the clean baseline (HR@20 = 0.3377 vs. 0.3421 for NoAttack; NDCG@20 = 0.1500 vs. 0.1543). In the Zero-Shot setting, performance actually improves to ER@20 = 0.7317. Across all four datasets (Clothing, Sports, Toys, MicroLens), VENOMREC achieves a mean ER@20 of 0.73, outperforming the strongest baseline (Shadowcast) by +0.52 absolute ER points on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VENOMREC decisively outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and setting. Interaction-level attacks (DirectBoost, RandomAttack, PopularAttack) achieve at most ER@20 = 0.1331 (DirectBoost on Clothing), confirming that manipulating discrete interaction records cannot steer the fused semantic representation. Textual-only attacks (TextFooler, DeepwordBug, PuncAttack, BERTAttack) are even weaker, with ER@20 ranging from 0.0112 to 0.0567, as the unperturbed visual modality anchors the fusion output. Visual-only attacks (INSA, EXPA) perform somewhat better (ER@20 = 0.0871 and 0.1252 respectively) but remain an order of magnitude below VENOMREC. The strongest multimodal baseline, Shadowcast, reaches only ER@20 = 0.0472 on Clothing (Few-Shot), yielding a gap of +0.67 absolute ER points versus VENOMREC's 0.7170. Notably, VENOMREC's HR@20 and NDCG@20 remain within 0.005 of the NoAttack baseline, indicating the attack is surgical rather than disruptive to overall recommendation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of data poisoning in recommender systems and adversarial attacks on multimodal models. Prior poisoning work on RecSys manipulates interaction logs (DirectBoost, PopularAttack) or single-modality content (TextFooler, EXPA), while the closest multimodal attack, Shadowcast, generates adversarial images targeting VLMs without exploiting the fusion mechanism itself. VENOMREC departs by formalizing a coupled cross-modal transformation that turns the consensus mechanism—typically a defense against unimodal noise—into an attack amplifier, using cross-modal attention to guide synchronized token-patch edits that the fusion process then reinforces rather than filters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that multimodal consensus, the very mechanism expected to make MLLM-RecSys more robust, becomes a vulnerability under synchronized cross-modal poisoning—challenges a widely held assumption in the field and has direct implications for deployed systems like NoteLLM-2. The paper demonstrates that existing defenses (autoencoder filtering, lexical normalization, frequency-based sanitization) are largely ineffective against this threat, leaving a significant gap in the security landscape of content-grounded recommenders. Open questions include whether architecture-level defenses (e.g., modality dropout, adversarial training on the fusion layers) can resist coordinated perturbations, and whether the grey-box proxy assumption (access to CLIP/T5) is realistic enough for the threat to be practically actionable at scale.
