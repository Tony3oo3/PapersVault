## Problem

Prior work has shown correlational evidence that symbol grounding may emerge in large language and vision-language models trained with next-token prediction, but the specific loci within the network where grounding arises and the mechanistic processes that drive it remain unexplored. Existing studies also focused exclusively on static final-stage models and framed grounding through visual-textual correlations rather than the causal links from symbols to meanings that Harnad (1990) originally defined, leaving open when and how grounding actually emerges during training.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a dual-token input where each lexical item is represented by two distinct vocabulary indices: an environmental token (from CHILDES scene annotations or DINOv2 image patch embeddings) and a linguistic token (from spoken utterances or dialogue text), ensuring no surface-form shortcut exists. Evaluation produces a contrastive surprisal comparison between matched and mismatched environmental contexts, a scalar grounding information gain per word, layer-wise saliency heatmaps, identified gather/aggregate attention heads with their layer positions, and causal-intervention surprisal deltas relative to random-head controls.

## Method

The paper constructs a minimal controlled testbed from CHILDES child-directed speech with a custom word-level tokenizer that assigns separate indices to environmental and linguistic forms of each word, then trains Transformers (4/12/18-layer), Mamba-2 SSMs, and unidirectional LSTMs from scratch under standard causal LM objectives. Grounding is quantified via a contrastive surprisal protocol (match vs. mismatch conditions) yielding a grounding information gain metric, which is tracked across 33+ training checkpoints to trace emergence dynamics. Mechanistic localization is achieved through attention saliency flow analysis and Tuned Lens probing, both converging on middle layers (7–9) as the grounding locus. The paper then identifies gather heads (pooling environmental information into compact states in shallow layers 3–4) and aggregate heads (broadcasting environmental ground to the token preceding the linguistic form in mid layers 7–8) following the gather-and-aggregate framework of Bick et al. (2025), and validates their causal role by zeroing out head outputs and showing significantly elevated surprisal compared to random-head controls. The findings generalize to image-grounded Visual Dialog with DINOv2 embeddings and to a full-scale LLaVA-1.5-7B case study, while unidirectional LSTMs consistently fail to exhibit grounding, delineating an architectural boundary tied to content-addressable retrieval.

## Results

Across training, 12-layer Transformers and 4-layer Mamba-2 show a steadily widening surprisal gap between match and mismatch conditions (grounding information gain increases monotonically), while 4-layer LSTMs show no such separation. Causal interventions confirm that zeroing out aggregate heads in middle layers (7–8) significantly increases surprisal (e.g., from 4.77 to 5.62 at 20k steps, p<0.001), whereas zeroing out gather heads or random control heads does not. The correlation between grounding information gain and co-occurrence statistics (R²) peaks early in training then declines, indicating grounding transcends surface statistics; in contrast, LSTM R² keeps rising while information gain stays flat. In the image-grounded Visual Dialog setting, aggregate head interventions at 300k steps raise surprisal from 7.69 (original) to 8.96 (70% threshold) or 8.40 (90% threshold), both significantly above controls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing grounding methods; instead, it compares architectural families and internal mechanisms. Transformers (4, 12, 18 layers) and Mamba-2 (4, 12 layers) both exhibit emergent grounding, while unidirectional LSTMs (4 layers) consistently fail to separate match from mismatch conditions, establishing an architectural boundary. Within Transformers, aggregate heads in middle layers (7–8) are causally critical: zeroing them out raises surprisal by 0.8–1.3 nats above controls, while gather heads in shallow layers (3–4) show no significant effect, indicating a clear functional dissociation. The grounding effect also generalizes to image-grounded dialogue (DINOv2 visual tokens + Visual Dialog), where aggregate head interventions remain significant across all checkpoints from 20k to 300k steps. A case study on LLaVA-1.5-7B identifies analogous aggregation heads (e.g., head 7 of layer 20), suggesting the mechanism persists in full-scale VLMs despite additional architectural complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the gather-and-aggregate (G&A) framework from Bick et al. (2025) and the saliency/tuned-lens toolkit from Wang et al. (2023) and Belrose et al. (2023) to a new domain: symbol grounding in multimodal LMs. It departs from prior grounding-in-VLMs work (Cao et al. 2024; Bousselham et al. 2024; Schnaus et al. 2025), which relied on correlational evidence in static final-stage models, by introducing causal interventions and tracking the full training trajectory. The paper also extends the mechanistic interpretability literature (induction heads, factual recall circuits) beyond in-context learning to referential alignment between environmental and linguistic tokens, and tests the architectural boundary by including state-space models (Mamba-2) and LSTMs alongside Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first causal, mechanistic account of how symbol grounding emerges during LM training, showing it is implemented by aggregate attention heads in middle layers rather than being a diffuse or purely statistical property. The architectural boundary finding (grounding emerges in Transformers and SSMs but not unidirectional LSTMs) provides a concrete condition for when grounding can arise, with practical implications for hallucination mitigation via attention-level monitoring. Key open questions include whether the aggregate-head mechanism scales to full VLMs like LLaVA-1.5-7B (where CLIP priors and register tokens complicate isolation), and whether the controlled CHILDES testbed faithfully captures the richer grounding dynamics of real-world multimodal interaction.
