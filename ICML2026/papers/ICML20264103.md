## Problem

Prevailing LLM fine-tuning paradigms treat training prompts as static, arbitrary surface forms, assuming semantically equivalent instructions yield identical learning outcomes. This paper reveals that assumption is deceptive: while paraphrased prompts produce comparable in-task performance, they induce drastically different cross-task impacts on catastrophic forgetting and generalization, and these impacts are positively correlated across tasks, implying the existence of identifiable superior prompts.

## Input / Output

SAPO takes as input a language model (Llama-2-7b/13b-chat, Qwen3-8b/14b), a sequence of training datasets (SuperNI with 1,000 samples per task, or TRACE with 3,000), the original human-authored task instruction, and a paraphraser model (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) to generate a pool of 20 semantically equivalent candidate prompts. It outputs a fine-tuned model and the selected optimal prompt per task. The method is plug-and-play and orthogonal to the underlying fine-tuning algorithm (LoRA, EWC, O-LoRA, InsCL), requiring only forward passes on a small evaluation subset (250 instances for SuperNI, 1,000 for TRACE) to compute pre-update loss, adding roughly 16.6% theoretical compute overhead.

## Method

SAPO (State-Adaptive Prompt Optimization) shifts task formulation from a static input to a dynamic, state-adaptive variable through three steps per task: (1) Prompt Expansion generates 20 semantically equivalent paraphrased candidates via an LLM paraphraser; (2) State-Adaptive Alignment Evaluation computes the causal language modeling loss for each candidate on a training subset using the model's current (pre-update) parameters; (3) the lowest-loss prompt is selected and used consistently for both fine-tuning and evaluation. The core insight is that pre-update loss serves as a reliable, uniformly non-negative predictor of post-training cross-task performance: lower-loss prompts minimize inter-task gradient conflicts by better leveraging the model's intrinsic capabilities rather than forcing extensive task-specific adaptations, thereby reducing forgetting and enhancing generalization. This is validated mechanistically through gradient cosine similarity analysis showing that as prompt loss decreases, the angle between task gradients shrinks, indicating reduced optimization conflict.

## Results

SAPO universally improves all four continual learning baselines across all four benchmarks (NI-Seq-G1, NI-Seq-C1, NI-Seq-M1, TRACE) and all four model scales (7B–14B). Headline gains include up to +4.93 in Average Performance (EWC on NI-Seq-G1, Llama2-7b-chat), up to +5.62 in Forward Transfer (LoraInc on NI-Seq-M1, Llama2-7b-chat), and consistent Backward Transfer improvements of +0.3 to +3.3 (reducing forgetting). On the heterogeneous TRACE benchmark, SAPO yields +0.63 to +2.87 AP and +0.34 to +3.27 BWT across methods, confirming robustness beyond controlled SuperNI sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAPO is designed as an orthogonal, plug-and-play layer on top of existing continual learning methods, and it improves every baseline it is applied to. Among the four baselines, InsCL (data replay) is the strongest starting point (e.g., 45.53 AP on NI-Seq-G1 for Llama2-7b), yet SAPO still adds +0.29 AP and +1.71 AP on NI-Seq-M1. EWC (parameter regularization) benefits the most in absolute AP terms, gaining up to +4.93 on NI-Seq-G1, suggesting its fixed Fisher-based penalty interacts poorly with state-agnostic prompts. O-LoRA (orthogonal modularization) shows the largest FWT gains (up to +2.97 on NI-Seq-M1, Qwen3-8b), indicating that SAPO's state-adaptive prompts complement its orthogonality constraint. LoraInc (incremental LoRA) receives moderate but consistent gains across all benchmarks. Critically, the ablation with state-adaptive prompt *pessimization* (SAPP, selecting highest-loss prompts) produces uniform performance *declines* (−0.67 to −1.56 AP), confirming that the gains come from the specific low-loss selection mechanism rather than merely replacing human-authored prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied inference-time prompt engineering literature to the training side, arguing that training prompts are not interchangeable surface forms but state-dependent variables that shape cross-task learning dynamics. It is orthogonal to existing continual learning methods (EWC, O-LoRA, InsCL, LoraInc), which all treat task formulations as fixed inputs, and instead introduces prompt selection as an additional optimization axis that can be layered on top of any of them. Conceptually, it connects to recent mechanistic analyses of fine-tuning (Kotha et al., Zheng et al., Jiang et al.) that show fine-tuning modulates input-to-capability activation pathways rather than creating new abilities, providing a geometric explanation for why prompt choice affects pathway conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that semantically equivalent prompts have negligible in-task impact yet induce up to 156% variation in forgetting challenges a widely held assumption in instruction-tuning pipelines, and the identification of pre-update loss as a simple, reliable selector for superior prompts offers a practical, low-overhead recipe (~16.6% compute) that is immediately adoptable. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small models (7B–14B) and NLP-centric benchmarks (SuperNI, TRACE), leaving open whether the effect persists at frontier scale or for more complex, structured, or multi-turn prompts. The positive correlation between forgetting and generalization effects suggests a unified 'prompt quality' axis, but whether this holds for adversarial or safety-critical tasks remains unexplored.
