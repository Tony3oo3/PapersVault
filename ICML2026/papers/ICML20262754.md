## Problem

Existing multi-agent collaborative perception methods assume homogeneous sensors and perception networks across all agents, which is unrealistic in deployed systems where vehicles use different sensor suites (LiDAR, camera) and independently trained encoders. This heterogeneity introduces significant semantic inconsistency and geometric misalignment in the shared BEV space, and prior cross-modal feature interpreters do not explicitly model the modality-dependent geometric discrepancies that arise from differing viewpoints and spatial representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262754/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent produces a BEV feature map from its modality-specific encoder: LiDAR agents (PointPillars pp8/pp4, VoxelNet vn4) generate features over a 140.8×40 m range with 3D CNN backbones, while camera agents (ResNet-50 or EfficientNet + Lift-Splat) produce 20-layer BEV representations over a smaller 102.4×51.2 m range. The framework fuses these heterogeneous BEV feature maps (differing in channel count, spatial resolution, and semantic distribution) into a unified representation, from which a frozen or trained detection head outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, z, h, w, l, θ) with per-class confidence scores.

## Method

X-MoGe combines two complementary modules. First, a Pixel-level Mixture-of-Experts (P-MoE) module routes each spatial location through K lightweight MLP experts via modality-aware gating networks (separate for ego and non-ego agents), performing soft aggregation to adaptively reinterpret heterogeneous features at pixel granularity. Second, a geometry-guided fusion module operates in three stages: an Uncertainty-Aware head predicts pixel-wise log-variance to compute bounded fusion weights between agents; a Sobel-based Gradient Encoding (SGE) block computes per-channel gradient magnitudes via depthwise Sobel convolutions and adds the normalized geometry map as an additive bias to emphasize structurally salient regions; and an Edge-Aware Enhancement (EAE) block applies depthwise convolution plus channel attention to extract and selectively integrate boundary cues. The refined features are then fused across agents via a two-stage spatial cross-attention (local partitioned regions followed by global regions) and decoded with Focal Loss and Smooth L1 regression, trained end-to-end.

## Results

On OPV2V (3 agents), X-MoGe achieves 79.54 AP@0.5 and 60.42 AP@0.7 under the pp8-res50-vn4 configuration, and 76.52 AP@0.5 and 59.01 AP@0.7 under pp8-eff-vn4, outperforming the strongest baselines (CoBEVT, CoCMT) by +4.15 and +2.44 AP@0.7 respectively. On DAIR-V2X (2 agents), X-MoGe reaches 68.15 AP@0.5 / 54.93 AP@0.7 (pp4-res50) and 67.97 AP@0.5 / 53.70 AP@0.7 (pp4-eff), surpassing the best competing methods by +1.36 and +0.66 AP@0.7. The ablation study confirms that removing the P-MoE adaptor causes the largest drop (−2.87 AP@0.5, −3.82 AP@0.7), followed by SGE (−1.37/−2.82) and EAE (−1.32 AP@0.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-MoGe outperforms all 10 baselines across every configuration on both datasets. On OPV2V pp8-res50-vn4, it leads CoBEVT (75.50/56.27) and CoCMT (75.56/55.60) by +4.04/+4.15 AP@0.5/AP@0.7, and V2X-ViT (73.31/50.99) by +6.23/+9.43. On OPV2V pp8-eff-vn4, it surpasses V2X-ViT (76.07/55.36) by +0.45/+3.65 and CoBEVT (75.44/56.57) by +1.08/+2.44. On DAIR-V2X pp4-res50, X-MoGe beats CoCMT (67.94/53.57) by +0.21/+1.36 and HEAL (67.30/53.38) by +0.85/+1.55. On DAIR-V2X pp4-eff, it edges out HM-ViT (67.87/51.42) by +0.10/+2.28 and CoCMT (67.24/52.95) by +0.73/+0.75. The margins are largest on OPV2V (up to +9.43 AP@0.7 vs. V2X-ViT) and smallest on DAIR-V2X pp4-eff (+0.10 AP@0.5 vs. HM-ViT), suggesting the gains are most pronounced when three heterogeneous agents with diverse encoders are involved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the heterogeneous collaborative perception line initiated by MPDA, PnPDA, and Polyinter (feature interpreters) and HM-ViT, HEAL, CoCMT (cross-modal fusion networks), which either rely on unified training or single-strategy feature interpretation without explicitly modeling modality-specific semantic and geometric discrepancies. It builds on CoBEVT's spatial cross-attention for cross-agent fusion and repurposes Mixture-of-Experts (originally from NLP and vision) at the pixel level for fine-grained cross-modal semantic adaptation. The key departure is the explicit injection of hand-crafted geometric priors (Sobel gradients, edge-aware enhancement) alongside learned semantic adaptation, rather than relying solely on data-driven alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Heterogeneous collaborative perception is a practical necessity as real-world fleets mix sensor suites and independently trained models, yet prior methods have not jointly addressed semantic and geometric misalignment. The paper's ablation confirms each component (P-MoE, SGE, EAE, UA-head) contributes meaningfully, but the geometric guidance is applied only to LiDAR features under the assumption of LiDAR's superior geometric consistency, limiting generalizability to camera-only or radar-only heterogeneous settings. Open questions include scalability beyond 2–3 agents, communication overhead of pixel-level MoE routing, and whether learned geometric encoders could replace the fixed Sobel operators at scale.
