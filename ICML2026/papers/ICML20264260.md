## Problem

Hybridizing neural ODEs with mechanistic compartmental models for epidemic forecasting is a natural idea, yet many straightforward couplings fail under the two defining challenges of real surveillance data: partial observability (only I(t) is measured, while S and R are latent) and non-stationarity (transmission, recovery, and immunity-loss rates shift with behavior, seasonality, and interventions). The paper systematically catalogs these failure modes—showing that vanilla Neural ODEs, bidirectional autoencoder objectives, physics-informed losses, and mechanistic Neural ODEs each break down in specific regimes—and asks what architectural choices are actually needed for robust long-horizon forecasting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the infected compartment I(t) at discrete time points; S(t) and R(t) are never observed and are initialized as S(0)=1−I(0), R(0)=0. Before the signal enters the model, it is decomposed via VMD into trend, seasonal, and residual channels, each of which is extrapolated past the observation window using a rule matched to its spectral character (linear drift for trend, periodogram-based tiling at the dominant period for seasonal, training-window mean for residual). Outputs include the forecasted I(t) trajectory, bounded time-varying rates β(t), γ(t), δ(t) mapped into disease-specific ranges via sigmoid-plus-affine scaling, reconstructed latent compartments S(t) and R(t), and peak timing/magnitude estimates.

## Method

EpiNode makes non-stationarity explicit by decomposing the observed infection series into trend, seasonal, and residual components via Variational Mode Decomposition, then feeding each component (after time-delay embedding) into its own latent Neural ODE with a learnable damping term. The three latent trajectories are fused by an MLP and decoded through a bounded parameter network into time-varying β(t), γ(t), and δ(t), which drive a mechanistic SIRS system integrated with RK4 and stabilized by a simplex projection that enforces non-negativity and approximate mass conservation. The key architectural insight is that isolating distinct temporal scales into separate control signals prevents the confounding that causes single-latent or uncontrolled Neural ODEs to drift over long horizons, while the SIRS layer enforces epidemiological consistency that purely data-driven models lack. Training is end-to-end supervised: a weighted MSE on I(t) over the observation window is minimized with gradients backpropagated through both the Neural ODE solvers and the mechanistic rollout, and weights increase linearly near the forecast boundary to emphasize alignment at the split point.

## Results

EpiNode achieves the lowest RMSE on all five benchmark datasets, with 21–63% reduction over the strongest baseline per dataset: 0.0022 on SIRS (Fixed) vs. 0.0046 (LatentODE), 0.0195 on SIRS (Varying) vs. 0.0247 (EINN-SIRS), 0.0223 on SIR (Fixed) vs. 0.0305 (EINN-SIRS), 0.0041 on SEIRS (Fixed) vs. 0.0111 (LatentODE), and 0.0093 on real-world ILI vs. 0.0209 (KAN-ODEs). On peak detection for ILI, EpiNode attains the best peak-timing bias (0.0 weeks, σ=0.7) and a competitive peak-magnitude bias (−0.018). For parameter inference on SIRS (Varying), EpiNode achieves the highest sign-agreement rate for transmission rate β(t) at 66.7%, exceeding the 50% random baseline, while all other methods fall below or near chance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EpiNode outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset. The closest competitors are LatentODE (best on SIRS Fixed at 0.0046 and SEIRS Fixed at 0.0111) and EINN-SIRS (best on SIRS Varying at 0.0247 and SIR Fixed at 0.0305), but EpiNode still leads by 52% and 63% on the former pair and by 21% and 27% on the latter. On real-world ILI, KAN-ODEs (0.0209) and EARTH (0.0219) are the strongest baselines, yet EpiNode achieves 0.0093—a 55% improvement. TimeMixer++ (0.0550 on SIRS Varying) and TimeKAN (peak timing −2±4 weeks on ILI) are competitive on specific sub-metrics but trail EpiNode on overall RMSE and peak timing. ARIMA, LSTM, NeuralODE, and EINN-SEIRm are consistently the weakest, with ARIMA reaching 0.8179 RMSE on SIRS (Varying) and LSTM flattening to near-zero on multi-wave data. On parameter inference, EpiNode is the only method besides EINN-SIRS to exceed 50% sign agreement for β(t), and it leads EINN-SIRS by 6.9 percentage points (66.7% vs. 59.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

EpiNode sits at the intersection of Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018), physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al., 2019), and epidemiological compartmental modeling, but departs from prior hybrid approaches by making nonstationarity explicit through multi-scale signal decomposition (VMD) as a structured control signal rather than leaving it to the neural component to discover. The paper first catalogs failure modes of naive couplings—vanilla Neural ODEs, bidirectional AE-NODE objectives, physics-informed losses, and mechanistic Neural ODEs—showing each breaks down under partial observability or multi-wave dynamics. This positions EpiNode as a corrective to the 'just plug a neural net into the ODE' paradigm, drawing on the EINN (Rodriguez et al., 2023) and EARTH (Wan et al., 2025) line of epidemiologically-informed architectures while adding decomposition-driven latent dynamics and bounded parameter decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The failure-mode catalog provides practitioners with concrete guidance on which hybridization strategies to avoid, and the decomposition-controlled architecture offers a principled recipe for coupling data-driven flexibility with mechanistic consistency under partial observability. The ability to infer bounded, interpretable time-varying β(t), γ(t), δ(t) trajectories alongside forecasts adds epidemiological insight beyond point predictions. Key limitations remain: the model is single-region, deterministic, lacks uncertainty quantification, and treats VMD decomposition as a fixed preprocessing step rather than a learned component. Open directions include probabilistic latent dynamics, spatial coupling across regions, explicit intervention modeling, and learnable decomposition modules.
