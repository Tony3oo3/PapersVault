## Problem

Standard continual-release DP analyses (e.g., tree-based prefix sums) assume Hamming-neighbor streams where only one time index differs, but participation privacy requires single-edit adjacency (one insertion or deletion), which shifts all subsequent positions and defeats these analyses. Moreover, continual learning is inherently interactive—released models influence future updates—so privacy must hold under adaptive feedback, a setting not covered by the original non-adaptive Hamming-neighbor proofs. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously: bridging edit-to-Hamming adjacency and certifying adaptive safety of continual DP primitives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of user events (private loss functions f_t) under single-edit neighboring semantics, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ), an initial model w^(0), a clipping norm G, and stepsize schedule. The adversary observes the full released trajectory (w^(t)) together with release timing bits v_t (indicating whether a bin was emitted or the dummy symbol ⊥ was produced), while bin contents and internal buffer states remain hidden. The output is a piecewise-constant model trajectory that updates only at random emission times, with batch sizes varying in [U, 2U] where U = Θ(ε_b^{-1} log(1/δ_b)) is privacy-implied and directly governs the induced delay and backlog.

## Method

The pipeline composes two auditable components via modular composition. First, a randomized buffering wrapper (RandBin) emits bins of size [U, 2U] at random times, converting a single insertion/deletion into at most two adjacent Hamming changes in the emitted per-bin update stream, with explicit backlog bounds of order O(ε_b^{-1} log t · (log t + log(1/δ_b))). Second, a certification theorem (Theorem 4.3) identifies when a non-adaptive Hamming-neighbor DP proof for a continual primitive (e.g., tree or lower-triangular prefix sums) lifts to the adaptive/feedback setting: the primitive must use fresh independent per-round randomness and satisfy a stable-context (quantitative decomposability) condition ensuring that common adaptive inputs do not alter the one-round privacy cost of the unique Hamming discrepancy. The privacy budget is split as (ε_b, δ_b) for buffering and (ε_a, δ_a) for the downstream aggregator, with the downstream one-hop parameters set to (ε_a/2, δ_a/(1+e^{ε_a/2})) so that adaptive two-hop group privacy charges the two edit-induced changes within the allocated budget, yielding end-to-end (ε, δ)-DP for single-edit neighboring streams under feedback.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorem 3.1 establishes end-to-end (ε,δ)-DP for single-edit neighboring streams under adaptive feedback, and Theorem 4.3 provides a certification condition under which non-adaptive Hamming-DP analyses lift to the adaptive setting. Empirically, on EMNIST ByMerge (δ=10⁻⁶), the lower-triangular matrix mechanism (sgd_matrix_dp_edit) achieves the best accuracy-privacy tradeoff across the entire ε sweep, followed by the tree-based prefix-sum (sgd_tree_dp_edit), with naive per-minibatch Gaussian noise (sgd_naive_dp_edit) performing worst. The RandBin wrapper introduces negligible accuracy overhead when converting from Hamming-style to single-edit privacy (Figure 2), validating that the stronger participation-privacy guarantee comes at essentially no utility cost. Specific accuracy values are reported in the figures (tradeoff curves) rather than as a single numerical table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three aggregation strategies under single-edit privacy (all with RandBin enabled): naive per-minibatch Gaussian noise (sgd_naive_dp_edit), the tree-based prefix-sum mechanism of Dwork et al. (2010) / Chan et al. (2011) (sgd_tree_dp_edit), and the lower-triangular matrix mechanism of Fichtenberger et al. (2023) (sgd_matrix_dp_edit). The matrix mechanism consistently outperforms both the tree mechanism and naive noise across the full ε spectrum on EMNIST ByMerge, providing the best accuracy-privacy tradeoffs. The tree mechanism outperforms naive per-minibatch noise as ε increases, consistent with prior findings in Kairouz et al. (2021). A separate comparison (Figure 2) shows that enabling RandBin (single-edit privacy) versus disabling it (Hamming-style privacy) incurs negligible accuracy loss on all three datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, EMNIST), confirming that the edit-to-Hamming shielding is essentially free in practice. The paper does not report a single head-to-head numerical table; results are presented as accuracy-privacy tradeoff curves with δ fixed at 10⁻⁵ (MNIST/CIFAR-10) or 10⁻⁶ (EMNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of continual-release differential privacy (tree-based prefix sums of Dwork et al. 2010, Chan et al. 2011), differential obliviousness and randomized buffering (Chan et al. 2022, Zhou et al. 2023), and adaptive/interactive DP composition (Jain et al. 2023, Henzinger et al. 2026, Zhou et al. 2024). The key departure is that all prior continual-release analyses assume Hamming-neighbor streams (one coordinate changes), whereas participation privacy requires edit-neighbor streams where a single insertion or deletion shifts every subsequent position — a gap the paper bridges by repurposing the RandBin randomized-buffering wrapper from oblivious data structures as an edit-to-Hamming shielding interface. The certification theorem (Theorem 4.3) is positioned as complementary to, rather than subsumed by, the adaptive DP formalism of Jain et al. (2023): it gives a checkable structural condition (fresh per-round randomness + stable one-round privacy cost) under which a standard non-adaptive proof lifts to the feedback setting, rather than requiring a from-scratch interactive analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first trajectory-level (ε,δ)-DP guarantee for single-edit participation streams under adaptive feedback, filling a concrete gap that makes standard continual-release DP inapplicable to participation privacy in streaming/federated learning. The certification theorem is a reusable audit tool: any continual DP primitive (tree, lower-triangular, or future mechanisms) can be plugged in after verifying two structural conditions, reducing the burden of proving adaptive privacy from scratch. Key limitations include the logarithmic-in-horizon backlog bound (potentially intrinsic to the prefix-sum scheduler route), the restriction to the approximate-DP regime (pure DP requires unbounded noise and loses the finite-delay guarantee), and the fact that the empirical validation uses small networks on standard datasets. Open questions include whether the log T backlog is truly tight, whether pure-DP variants with comparable delay exist, and whether the certification conditions extend to stateful optimizers (momentum, adaptive preconditioning) without separate case-by-case proofs.
