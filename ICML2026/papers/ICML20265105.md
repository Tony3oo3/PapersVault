## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks evaluate planning, physical reasoning, or 3D generation in isolation, but none test whether LLMs can iteratively construct physically viable 3D structures from natural language specifications under real physics constraints. This leaves a critical gap: no framework exists to assess whether language models can translate engineering descriptions into executable, collision-free assemblies validated by a physics simulator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language task specification (e.g., 'build a bridge spanning a 5-unit gap supporting a 2.5×2.5×1.5 cargo') and a fixed set of six building modules (small wooden block, powered wheel, water cannon, torch, brace, winch) as input. The LLM agent interacts with the construction space exclusively through language-based actions (Build, Refine, Query, Control) that the Spatial Geometric Computation Library validates against geometric and physical constraints before execution. The output is a Besiege-compatible 3D structure file loaded into the physics simulator, from which success rate (over 64 samples), task-specific indicators (max displacement, max load, TWR, or max height), and cost metrics (input/output tokens, LLM request count) are extracted.

## Method

BuildArena comprises three integrated components: (1) an extensible task design strategy defining three engineering categories (Transport, Support, Lift) across three difficulty levels parameterized by six dimensions (quantification, robustness, magnitude, compositionality, precision, ambiguity); (2) an open-source 3D Spatial Geometric Computation Library that faithfully replicates Besiege's closed-source construction logic (validated to <1.5×10⁻⁶ position error and <2.5×10⁻⁵ orientation error on a 49-block machine), enabling LLMs to perform attachment, connection, rotation, and translation with real-time physical constraint checking and human-interpretable feedback; (3) a five-entity LLM agentic workflow (Planner, Drafter, Reviewer, Builder, Guidance) following a coarse-to-fine structure with a Draft-Review loop and a Build-Guidance loop, serving as a fixed shared protocol so all models are compared under identical agentic settings. Evaluation loads each constructed structure into the Besiege simulator, executes task-specific simulation protocols (e.g., progressive loading for bridges, continuous water-cannon firing for rockets), and averages results over 64 independent samples per task-LLM pair.

## Results

Across nine frontier LLMs evaluated on BuildArena (n=64 runs per task-model pair), GPT-5 achieves the highest success rates: 78.1% on Transport Lv.1, 85.9% on Support Lv.1 (max load 324.9), and 95.3% on Lift Lv.1 (TWR 4.3). Grok-4 is the strongest competitor, reaching 31.2% success on Lift Lv.1 and achieving the highest Lift Lv.2 indicator (890.6 max height). Performance degrades sharply at Lv.3 (Hard) for most models, with success rates dropping to 0–17%, confirming the benchmark's discriminative power. Open-weight models (Qwen3.5-27B, Ministral-14B) achieve non-trivial results on easy tasks (e.g., 35.9% on Support Lv.1) but fall to near-zero at higher difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BuildArena is the only benchmark in Table 1 that simultaneously supports all five capabilities: Spatial Reasoning, 3D Construction, Construction-aimed Planning, Physical Simulator, and Interactive Environment. PlanBench and PlanQA lack physical simulation and 3D construction entirely; PHYRE and VOYAGER include physics and interactivity but do not evaluate 3D structural assembly; Embodied Agent Interface supports 3D construction and simulation but omits construction-aimed planning. Among the nine frontier LLMs tested, GPT-5 dominates on Support (85.9% at Lv.1 vs. 46.9% for Grok-4, 45.3% for Seed-1.6) and Lift Lv.1 (95.3% vs. 31.2% for Grok-4), while Grok-4 achieves the highest Lift Lv.2 indicator (890.6 max height vs. 127.8 for GPT-5) and competitive Transport Lv.1 success (25.0% vs. 78.1% for GPT-5). All other models (GPT-4o, Claude-4, Gemini-2.0, DeepSeek-3.1, Qwen-3, Kimi-K2, Seed-1.6) show success rates below 50% on most tasks and near-zero at Lv.3, highlighting a clear performance gap between the top two models and the rest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Besiege physics sandbox as a simulation backend and on LLM agentic workflow paradigms (ReAct, AutoGen, ToolLLM) to create the first benchmark that jointly requires spatial reasoning, 3D construction, construction-aimed planning, and physics verification in an interactive environment. It departs from prior physical-reasoning benchmarks (PHYRE, Newton, ABench-Physics) that test understanding without assembly, and from CAD/shape-assembly work (ShapeAssembly, CAD-Assistant) that generates designs without validating physical executability. The key technical departure is the open-source Spatial Geometric Computation Library, which reproduces Besiege's closed-source construction logic to enable language-driven interaction, and the standardized five-role agentic workflow (Planner, Drafter, Reviewer, Builder, Guidance) as a fixed evaluation protocol. A concurrent work, BesiegeField, also uses Besiege but represents machines as structured code output without continuous spatial-conflict verification during construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BuildArena provides the first systematic, reproducible testbed for probing whether LLMs can translate natural language into physically viable 3D assemblies, revealing that even top frontier models (GPT-5, Grok-4) achieve success rates below 20% on the hardest task levels and that compositional assembly and high-precision alignment remain critical failure points. The finding that one-shot prompting degrades performance on open-ended construction tasks challenges the default assumption that in-context examples help, suggesting these tasks require genuine spatial reasoning rather than pattern imitation. Key limitations include reliance on a single game environment (Besiege) with a limited six-module set, single-shot evaluation as the default protocol, and the gap between sandbox physics and real manufacturing constraints. Open questions include whether iterative closed-loop refinement can close the performance gap, how results transfer to real robotics/robotic assembly pipelines, and whether the observed latent engineering knowledge (truss structures, differential steering) can be systematically elicited and characterized.
