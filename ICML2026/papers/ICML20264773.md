## Problem

Autoregressive image generation requires sequentially producing thousands of tokens, making inference prohibitively slow (e.g., 121s for a single 768×768 image). Existing speculative decoding accelerators are content-agnostic: they apply uniform acceptance criteria across all image regions, failing to exploit the fact that smooth, high-redundancy areas are far easier to predict than fine-grained textures, and directly relaxing acceptance criteria without content-awareness degrades image quality.

## Input / Output

CSD is a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper around any existing autoregressive image model (tested on Lumina-mGPT 7B at 768×768 and Janus-Pro 1B/7B at 384×384). It takes a text prompt and the frozen target model as input, using Jacobi decoding on the target model's own previous-iteration outputs to produce draft tokens—no separate draft model is trained or loaded. The output is a generated RGB image, with the method adding only ~0.06 ms per iteration of overhead (0.17% of total compute) for entropy and TV-distance calculations.

## Method

CSD introduces two coupled mechanisms into the speculative decoding loop. First, an entropy-based probability relaxation adds ε = λ·H(q_k) to the standard acceptance probability min(1, q_k(x̂_k)/p_k(x̂_k)), where H(q_k) is the target model's entropy at position k; high-entropy (smooth) regions thus receive higher acceptance rates, accelerating generation where quality loss is minimal. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the optimal resampling distribution under any such relaxation remains norm{max(0, q(x)−p(x))}, identical to lossless SD. Second, a distribution alignment filter gates the relaxation using total variation distance: if TV(p_k, q_k) < δ, relaxation is applied; otherwise the algorithm reverts to standard lossless acceptance, preventing quality degradation where draft and target distributions diverge significantly. Theorem 3.2 formalizes the trade-off as P(reject) + min_P TV(P,q) = TV(p,q), showing the filter provides a principled lower bound on distributional deviation. The method achieves up to 4.33× speedup on Janus-Pro 7B with negligible CLIP score drop, outperforming the prior SOTA GSD.

## Results

On Janus-Pro 7B (MS-COCO), CSD with δ=0.65 achieves 3.51× latency acceleration (6.53s vs. 22.95s vanilla AR) with a CLIP score of 32.28 and FID of 33.69, outperforming the previous SOTA GSD (3.31×, CLIP 32.16, FID 34.00). At a more aggressive setting (δ=0.8), CSD reaches 4.33× acceleration (5.30s) while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 32.21 and FID of 33.58. On Lumina-mGPT 7B, CSD achieves the highest CLIP score of 31.49 at 2.50× acceleration, surpassing GSD by 0.16 CLIP points with 1.23s less inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD outperforms all training-free baselines on Janus-Pro 7B. Against GSD (the previous SOTA), CSD (δ=0.65) improves CLIP by 0.12 points (32.28 vs. 32.16) and FID by 0.31 (33.69 vs. 34.00) while also achieving higher latency acceleration (3.51× vs. 3.31×). At the speed-prioritized setting (δ=0.8), CSD reaches 4.33× acceleration, a 24% reduction in inference time compared to GSD (5.30s vs. 6.93s) and 26% faster than Addition (5.30s vs. 7.13s), while maintaining a CLIP score of 32.21 versus 32.16 for both GSD and Addition. Against SJD, CSD more than doubles the acceleration rate (3.51× vs. 1.97×) with a marginal CLIP drop of only 0.02. On Lumina-mGPT 7B, CSD achieves the best CLIP score (31.49) among all training-free methods, beating GSD (31.33) and Addition (31.27), and matches the training-based EAGLE (33.3) and LANTERN in quality while being training-free. The computational overhead of CSD is negligible at only 0.06 ms per iteration (0.17%) over SJD, and lower than GSD's 37.24 ms per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSD extends the speculative decoding framework (Leviathan et al., 2023) to autoregressive image generation by building on SJD's training-free Jacobi decoding for draft token generation (Teng et al., 2024; Song et al., 2021). The key departure from prior content-agnostic methods (SJD, GSD, EAGLE, LANTERN) is the introduction of target-model entropy as a content signal that dynamically relaxes acceptance probability in smooth, high-redundancy image regions, combined with a TV-distance-based distribution alignment filter. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the standard resampling distribution norm{max(0, q−p)} remains optimal even under relaxed acceptance probabilities, providing a principled foundation for the relaxation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.33× speedup on Janus-Pro 7B with negligible CLIP/FID degradation is practically significant for deploying AR image models in latency-sensitive settings, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature makes CSD broadly applicable without retraining. The entropy–texture correlation insight offers a reusable principle for content-adaptive decoding beyond speculative decoding. Open questions include how the approach scales to higher resolutions (beyond 768×768), whether the δ threshold can be set automatically rather than per-model, and whether similar content-aware relaxation applies to video or 3D generation where temporal/spatial redundancy patterns differ.
