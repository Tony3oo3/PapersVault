## Problem

Muon's strong empirical performance in large language model training has attracted significant attention, yet its underlying mechanisms and relationship to established adaptive optimizers like Adam remain insufficiently understood. Prior reports of Muon outperforming Adam are confounded by auxiliary techniques such as QK-Norm and QK-Clip, making it unclear whether the spectral orthogonalization itself provides a genuine optimization advantage or whether Muon can be systematically improved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263702/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates exclusively on matrix-shaped parameters W ∈ R^{m×n}, computing first-moment M_t and second-moment V_t estimates from the gradient matrix G_t. It applies a spectral transformation Ψ_p to either the raw momentum O_t^mom = M_t or the RMS-normalized update O_t^rms = M_t ⊘ √V_t, producing a spectrally reshaped update direction that is then scaled by a learning rate and subtracted from W. Vector parameters (biases, layer-norm weights) are always updated with standard Adam at a fixed learning rate, decoupled from the matrix-parameter learning rate.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified spectral family Ψ_p(O) = UΣ^p V^T with p ∈ [0,1], placing Muon at the p=0 endpoint (full spectral flattening to the polar factor UV^T) and p=1 as the identity, while proposing intermediate variants at p=1/2 and p=1/4 that provide milder spectral compression. To compute these fractional transforms without explicit SVD, the authors develop a coupled Newton-Schulz iteration that simultaneously computes X^{1/2} and X^{-1/2} using only matrix multiplications, with the quarter-power case obtained by applying the procedure twice. The eight resulting optimizer instances (2 input types × 4 exponents) are evaluated in a highly controlled GPT-2 124M setup on OpenWebText with decoupled matrix/vector learning rates, no weight decay, and no auxiliary stabilizers, isolating the intrinsic effect of spectral compression from confounding techniques.

## Results

On GPT-2 (124M) trained on OpenWebText for 200K steps, the best RMS-normalized variant AdamS (p=1/2) achieves a validation loss of 2.870, marginally beating Adam (p=1) at 2.871 and AdamQ (p=1/4) at 2.872. Muon (mSGDZ, p=0) achieves 2.887, which is worse than all three RMS-normalized variants. Among momentum-input methods, mSGDQ (p=1/4) at 2.876 outperforms Muon (2.887) by 0.011, while plain mSGD (p=1) lags far behind at 3.092. With weight decay and QK-Norm enabled, the gap narrows further: AdamQ reaches 2.800, AdamS 2.802, AdamZ 2.806, and Adam 2.815.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Muon (mSGDZ) is the central comparison target. Against plain mSGD, Muon provides a large stabilization gain (2.887 vs. 3.092, a 0.205 improvement), confirming its role as a spectral normalizer for first-moment updates. However, Muon does not outperform Adam: Adam achieves 2.871 versus Muon's 2.887, a 0.016 gap in Adam's favor. Even more notably, the partial-compression variant mSGDQ (p=1/4) beats Muon on the same momentum input (2.876 vs. 2.887), suggesting full spectral flattening is overly aggressive. When the spectral transform is applied to RMS-normalized updates instead, all four variants cluster tightly (2.870–2.879), with AdamS (p=1/2) marginally best at 2.870 and AdamZ (Muon-style, p=0) worst at 2.879. The consistent pattern across all comparisons is that RMS-normalized inputs outperform momentum inputs at every spectral exponent, indicating that second-moment normalization is more important than the choice of spectral compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) by recasting its polar-factor orthogonalization as the p=0 endpoint of a continuous spectral family Ψ_p(O) = UΣ^p V^T, and introduces fractional variants (p=1/2, p=1/4) computable via a coupled Newton-Schulz iteration derived from Higham's matrix-function framework. Unlike prior Muon analyses (Bernstein 2025a, Newhouse 2025, Buchanan 2025) that focused on mathematical foundations or geometric interpretations, and unlike empirical benchmarks (Jordan et al. 2024, Team et al. 2025) that bundled Muon with auxiliary stabilizers like QK-Norm, this work isolates the spectral effect through a controlled protocol that decouples matrix/vector learning rates and disables weight decay. The paper also bridges the gap between first-moment (momentum SGD) and second-moment (Adam) update regimes by applying the same spectral family to both, a comparison not systematically explored in prior matrix-optimizer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that Muon-style full spectral flattening (p=0) does not consistently outperform Adam, and that partial compression (p=1/4) can be superior—challenges the growing narrative that Muon is a universally better optimizer and suggests its benefits are best understood as spectral normalization rather than a fundamentally new optimization principle. This is important for practitioners deciding whether to adopt Muon in production training, as it implies the gains reported in prior work may be partially attributable to confounding techniques. However, the evidence is limited to a single 124M-parameter GPT-2 model on OpenWebText, and the authors explicitly note that conclusions may not transfer to larger scales, different architectures, or fully tuned production recipes with weight decay and learning-rate schedules. Open questions include whether the ranking of spectral exponents changes at scale, how fractional spectral variants interact with decoupled weight decay (AdamW-style), and whether the coupled Newton-Schulz overhead becomes prohibitive for very wide matrices.
