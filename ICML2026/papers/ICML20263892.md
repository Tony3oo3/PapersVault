## Problem

State-of-the-art protein-ligand co-folding models like Boltz-2 rely on iterative all-atom diffusion to generate 3D coordinates, requiring ~20 seconds per complex and rendering large-scale compound screening computationally intractable. Additionally, existing models lack calibrated uncertainty quantification for binding affinity, making it difficult to prioritize compounds in drug discovery campaigns. TerraBind challenges the assumption that full all-atom diffusion is necessary, hypothesizing that coarse pocket-level structural representations suffice for both pose recovery and accurate affinity prediction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein sequence (encoded by frozen ESM-2 650M) and a ligand specified by SMILES, 2D molecular graph, and 3D conformer point cloud (all encoded by the frozen COATI-3 multimodal encoder). Outputs are restricted to a coarse representation: protein Cβ atoms and ligand heavy atoms only (not full all-atom coordinates). The primary outputs are pairwise distance bin distributions (64 bins spanning 2–22 Å), a quantitative binding affinity in log₁₀ units (pIC₅₀), a binary binding likelihood, and calibrated uncertainty estimates obtained by sampling 1000 epistemic indices through a lightweight epinet. The 3D coordinates produced by the pose module are used solely for visualization and RMSD evaluation; affinity prediction operates directly on the Pairformer latents and distance distributions without requiring coordinate generation.

## Method

TerraBind replaces the diffusion-based coordinate generation of Boltz-2 with a 48-layer Pairformer trunk (~27M parameters vs. ~509M for Boltz-2) that predicts categorical distributions over 64 pairwise distance bins between ligand heavy atoms and protein Cβ centers, trained via cross-entropy with pair-type weighting over a 3-stage curriculum (105k steps). A parameter-free gradient-based optimization routine then recovers 3D coordinates from the expected distances in ~500 Adam steps, achieving 26× faster end-to-end inference. The affinity module is a separate 6-layer Pairformer that conditions directly on the structural Pairformer's distance bin probabilities and frozen encoder embeddings (rather than on diffused coordinates), mapping a mean-pooled complex representation to both a binary binding score and a continuous pIC₅₀ prediction via focal and Huber losses. An epistemic neural network (epinet) attached to the frozen complex latent provides calibrated uncertainty by sampling residuals from a unit Gaussian index, enabling joint posterior distributions across batches and supporting an EMAX acquisition function for risk-hedged compound selection in simulated DMTA cycles. The entire pipeline uses only ~30M trainable parameters and a 196-token pocket context, with the key architectural insight that geometric uncertainty preserved in the distogram bin probabilities is more informative for affinity than point-estimate 3D coordinates.

## Results

TerraBind achieves a 26.6× end-to-end speedup over Boltz-2 (1.045 s vs. 27.8 s per complex on a single A6000 GPU) while using only ~30 M trainable parameters versus ~509 M. On CASP16 L3000, TerraBind reaches a Pearson correlation of 0.725 for binding affinity, outperforming Boltz-2 (0.625) by 16%. On 18 proprietary assays (27,075 compounds), TerraBind outperforms Boltz-2 on 15 of 18 targets, with hit-to-lead Spearman correlation of 0.477 vs. 0.396 (+20.4%) at N≥100. For structure prediction, TerraBind matches Boltz-1 on ligand RMSD < 2 Å success rate across FoldBench, PoseBusters, and Runs N' Poses without a diffusion module, and its intrinsic distogram entropy (H_LP) correlates monotonically with pose accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Boltz-1 (structure), TerraBind matches ligand RMSD < 2 Å success rate on all three public benchmarks (FoldBench, PoseBusters, Runs N' Poses) and significantly outperforms Boltz-1 Trunk when both use identical coordinate optimization, indicating richer geometric representations from the Pairformer. Against Boltz-2 (affinity), TerraBind wins on CASP16 L3000 by +16% Pearson and on 15/18 proprietary targets, with the largest gains in hit-to-lead ranking (Spearman +20.4%, Kendall +22.5% at N≥100). However, Boltz-2's combined score (fusing its binary binding head with the affinity head) edges out TerraBind on hit-finding AUROC (0.857 vs. 0.819 at N≥100), though it operates on an arbitrary scale without a calibrated pIC50 interpretation. TerraBind's single calibrated pIC50 output spans both hit-finding and hit-to-lead regimes without regime-dependent head selection. In simulated DMTA cycles, TerraBind's continual learning with EMAX acquisition achieves 6× greater affinity improvement over greedy Boltz-2 selection, a capability unavailable to diffusion-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TerraBind adapts the Pairformer architecture from AlphaFold3 (Abramson et al., 2024) by removing the single-sequence track and the downstream diffusion module, replacing them with frozen pretrained encoders (ESM-2 for proteins, COATI-3 for ligands) and a gradient-based coordinate optimization for pose generation. It departs from the Boltz-1/Boltz-2 lineage (Wohlwend et al., 2024; Passaro et al., 2025), which relies on iterative all-atom diffusion for coordinate generation, by operating entirely on coarse pairwise distance distributions (Cβ + ligand heavy atoms). The affinity uncertainty module draws on epistemic neural networks (Osband et al., 2023) and the EMAX batch-selection framework from Wang-Henderson et al. (2025), integrating them into a co-folding pipeline for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26× throughput gain while matching or exceeding diffusion-based accuracy on both structure and affinity benchmarks makes library-scale virtual screening and iterative DMTA cycles computationally feasible for the first time with deep co-folding models. The well-calibrated epinet uncertainty and joint posterior distributions enable risk-aware compound selection that standard diffusion-based architectures cannot support. Key limitations include the coarse-grained representation (no all-atom coordinates for physics-based downstream pipelines), potential convergence issues in very large protein systems, and Gaussian marginal uncertainty that may underestimate false-positive rates. Open questions include whether the coarse representation generalizes to allosteric sites, multi-ligand systems, and cell-based assay contexts where broader biological context matters.
