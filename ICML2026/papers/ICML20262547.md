## Problem

Existing nonparametric two-sample tests for compositional data rely on log-ratio transformations that fail in the presence of zero-valued components (common in microbiome data) and only detect mean differences. No unified framework existed for nonparametric two-sample testing across both compositional (simplex-valued) and directional (spherical-valued) data that avoids log-transformations, handles boundary points, and detects distributional differences beyond means.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262547/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two i.i.d. samples: either compositional data on the simplex Δ^d (mapped to the first-orthant sphere S^d_{≥0} via square-root or stretching transforms, with no special handling of zeros) or directional data on the full sphere S^d. It produces a single studentized scalar test statistic T_{m,n}^p that is asymptotically N(0,1) under H₀, requiring no bandwidth or kernel-width parameter. The degree parameter p must satisfy the growth condition p^d/N → 0 (e.g., p = ⌊(log N)^δ⌋), and the sample ratio m/N must converge to a constant ρ ∈ (0,1).

## Method

The paper reduces compositional two-sample testing to directional testing on S^d by zero-extending densities from the first orthant to the full sphere, showing the Γ-invariant spread-out construction is unnecessary. It constructs a kernel energy distance on finite-level Hilbert spaces H_p = ⊕_{i=0}^{2p} H_i, where each H_i is a finite-dimensional RKHS with a Gegenbauer-polynomial-based reproducing kernel; since no individual kernel is characteristic, all levels are combined and p → ∞ to distinguish arbitrary distribution pairs. Under H₀, a Hoeffding decomposition via U-statistics theory shows the estimator is purely linear (the remainder term vanishes identically), and the variance is linked to a kernel squared distance covariance through adaptable bases obtained via Mercer's theorem on a codimension-one subspace. Studentizing the estimator by its estimated variance yields an asymptotically N(0,1) statistic, eliminating the need for permutation or bootstrap procedures.

## Results

On compositional Dirichlet-Multinomial simulations (d=10, m=n=100, ~5% zeros), the proposed SPH-L2-p4 test achieves 100% power for abundant-taxon mean shifts and 93% power for concentration alternatives, compared to 5% and 33% for CLR-ED respectively. For uniform mean shifts, all SPH variants reach 100% power versus 55–57% for CLR baselines. On directional data (d=2, vMF concentration alternative with κ-ratio 1.75), the bandwidth-free SPH-√-p2 statistic outperforms vMF-MMD at every bandwidth value and sample size (m=n ∈ {25, 50, 100}). Under H₀, the studentized statistic converges to N(0,1) with empirical size 0.07–0.08 (target 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of baselines: CLR-transformed energy/MMD tests (CLR-ED, CLR-MMD) for compositional data, and vMF-MMD for directional data. The SPH methods decisively outperform CLR baselines on abundant-taxon mean shifts (100% vs. 5% power) and uniform mean shifts (100% vs. 55–57%), and substantially on concentration alternatives (SPH-L2-p4: 93% vs. CLR-ED: 33%). However, CLR methods retain an advantage on rare-taxon mean shifts (38–39% vs. 6–46% for SPH variants), suggesting the spherical harmonics approach is less sensitive to differences concentrated in low-abundance components. For directional data, the bandwidth-free SPH-√-p2 statistic dominates vMF-MMD across all bandwidth values and sample sizes in Figure 4, eliminating the need for bandwidth selection entirely. Overall, the SPH framework wins on the majority of tested alternatives while maintaining comparable size control (0.07–0.08 vs. 0.03 for CLR, both near the 0.05 target).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies compositional and directional two-sample testing by building on spherical harmonics theory (Gegenbauer-polynomial kernels) and adapting the studentization framework of Gao & Shao (2023) and Chakraborty & Zhang (2021) from Euclidean high-dimensional settings to a fixed-dimensional sphere with growing RKHS dimension. It explicitly departs from Li et al. (2023), who established a Γ-invariant equivalence between compositional and directional statistics, by showing the spread-out construction is unnecessary for two-sample problems—zero-extension to the full sphere suffices. The kernel energy distance construction follows the MMD/energy-distance lineage of Gretton et al. (2012) and Székely & Rizzo (2013), but replaces characteristic kernels (which do not exist for individual spherical harmonic subspaces) with a union over all finite-level subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully nonparametric two-sample test for compositional data that avoids log-transformations and handles zero-valued components natively, which is critical for high-dimensional microbiome data where zeros are pervasive. The unified spherical framework also eliminates the need for permutation or bootstrap procedures by proving asymptotic normality via U-statistics and Mercer's theorem. A key limitation is the fixed-dimensional regime: the condition p^d/N → 0 means sample size requirements grow exponentially in d, so the theory does not yet cover the high-dimensional low-sample-size (HDLSS) setting common in practice. An open question the authors flag is how to coherently handle function spaces over different-dimensional spheres when the underlying space itself varies.
