## Problem

Current VLMs frame GUI grounding as a one-step coordinate prediction task, where the model generates numeric coordinates without ever seeing where its prediction lands on the screen. This absence of a visual feedback loop prevents the model from developing robust alignment between its numerical outputs and their corresponding on-screen positions, leading to failures on high-resolution GUIs with complex layouts. The paper asks whether reframing grounding as an interactive cursor-search task with rendered visual feedback can close this spatial-semantic alignment gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266014/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each interaction step, the model receives the natural language instruction, the current GUI screenshot with a rendered cursor at the predicted position, and the full interaction history of prior observations and actions. The model outputs a thinking block (spatial analysis of the target, cursor location, and their relationship) followed by either a new (x, y) coordinate or the token STOP. During training, images are downscaled to 1920×1080; at inference, Cursor-Centric Focusing first makes a coarse prediction on the full image, then crops a 1920×1080 region around that prediction for fine-grained refinement. The final cursor position upon STOP (or at the 4-step limit) is the grounding output.

## Method

GUI-CURSOR reframes GUI grounding as a multi-step interactive search: a virtual cursor is rendered on the GUI image, and at each step the VLM performs spatial reasoning (in explicit thinking tokens) to evaluate whether the cursor is on the target, then either moves it to a new coordinate or issues STOP. The model is trained with GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) using a dense trajectory reward that combines a position-based reward (Euclidean distance to the target bounding box with centrality bonus) and four trajectory penalties (false stop, false move, false direction, repeated position) that discourage degenerate search behaviours. Cursor-Centric Focusing (CCF) balances training efficiency and inference accuracy by downscaling during training and cropping around the initial prediction during inference on higher-resolution images. Trained on only 8K samples from Aria-UI and OS-Atlas with Qwen2.5-VL-7B or UI-TARS-1.5-7B, the method outperforms the prior SOTA GTA1 by 8.1% on ScreenSpot-Pro while using 8× fewer training samples, and the learned spatial reasoning generalises to out-of-distribution benchmarks (SpatialMQA, SPHERE).

## Results

On the challenging ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark (high-resolution, complex-layout GUIs), GUI-CURSOR-7B initialised from UI-TARS-1.5-7B achieves 58.1% average accuracy, outperforming the previous leading RL method GTA1-7B (50.1%) by 8.0 percentage points while using only 8K training samples versus GTA1's 64K. On the standard ScreenSpot-v2 benchmark, GUI-CURSOR reaches 93.9% average accuracy (both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and UI-TARS-1.5-7B initialisations), surpassing GUI-G2-7B (93.3%) and GTA1-7B (92.4%). In the online agentic evaluation on OSWorld, GUI-CURSOR-7B achieves 57.1% task success at 50 action steps, exceeding both GTA1-7B (53.1% at 100 steps) and the larger GTA1-32B (55.4% at 100 steps). On spatial reasoning benchmarks, GUI-CURSOR improves over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base by +5.3% on SpatialMQA (43.4 vs 38.1) and +1.8% on SPHERE (58.5 vs 56.7) without explicit spatial-reasoning training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUI-CURSOR consistently outperforms all RL-based baselines on ScreenSpot-Pro: it beats GTA1-7B by 8.0% (58.1 vs 50.1), GUI-G2-7B by 10.6% (58.1 vs 47.5), and SE-GUI-7B by 10.8% (58.1 vs 47.3), while using only 8K training samples compared to GTA1's 64K and GUI-G2's 100K. On ScreenSpot-v2, GUI-CURSOR (93.9%) edges out GUI-G2-7B (93.3%) and GTA1-7B (92.4%), and substantially surpasses all SFT baselines (best: GUI-Actor-7B at 92.5%). In the OSWorld agentic setting, GUI-CURSOR-7B achieves 57.1% at 50 steps, outperforming GTA1-7B (53.1% at 100 steps) and even the 4× larger GTA1-32B (55.4% at 100 steps), demonstrating both accuracy and efficiency gains. On OSWorld-G, GUI-CURSOR (UI-TARS-1.5-7B) scores 65.6% versus GTA1-7B's 67.7% (a 2.1-point gap), but leads on UI-Vision (27.3 vs 26.2) and shows the largest gains on spatial-reasoning subcategories (Fine-grained Manipulation: 67.4 vs 42.9). On spatial reasoning benchmarks, GUI-CURSOR improves over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base by +5.3% on SpatialMQA and +1.8% on SPHERE, while the one-step RL baseline GUI-G2-7B shows no such improvement (38.0 vs 38.1 on SpatialMQA), confirming that the multi-step cursor-moving process specifically enhances spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-step coordinate prediction paradigm in GUI grounding (used by SFT methods like SeeClick, UGround, UI-TARS, and RL methods like SE-GUI, GTA1, GUI-G2) by reframing the task as a multi-step interactive search where a rendered cursor provides visual feedback at each step. It builds directly on the dense position reward from SE-GUI (Yuan et al., 2025) and the GRPO training framework popularised by DeepSeek-R1, but introduces trajectory-based penalties (false stop, false move, false direction, repeated position) that have no analogue in prior one-step RL grounding work. The interactive formulation is conceptually related to multi-turn VLM interaction and tool-augmented reasoning (ReAct), and the cursor-centric focusing (CCF) inference strategy is positioned as a simpler alternative to concurrent iterative cropping methods (GUI-Spotlight, GUI-ARP, GUI-RC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most broadly relevant finding is that Qwen2.5-VL-7B's 88.8% single-step grounding accuracy collapses to 36.3% (direct move) or 1.3% (relative move) under iterative cursor movement, and the cursor-in-box test reveals severe positional bias — together suggesting that current VLMs' grounding may rely on coordinate pattern-matching rather than robust spatial understanding. This diagnostic has implications beyond GUI grounding for any task requiring precise spatial localisation. Open questions include handling out-of-crop predictions (~10% of ScreenSpot-Pro cases), extending to continuous interactions like dragging, and whether the spatial reasoning gains transfer to 3D or embodied settings. The 8K-sample training efficiency (vs. 64K for GTA1) makes the approach practically attractive for resource-constrained settings.
