## Problem

Gradient Flow analyses of multi-pathway deep linear networks predict a 'winner-takes-all' symmetry breaking where one pathway dominates and parallel over-parameterization becomes redundant. However, this continuous-time prediction ignores the discrete dynamics of practical Gradient Descent with large learning rates operating at the Edge of Stability. The paper asks whether this symmetry-breaking phenomenon persists under large-step GD, or whether the discreteness of the update fundamentally alters which minima are selected.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a square target matrix M* ∈ R^{d×d} with SVD M* = U*Σ*V*ᵀ, pathway-dependent initialization scales α_h (with α_1 > α_2 > … > α_H to trigger symmetry breaking), and a learning rate η. The network is restricted to the singular-vector-stationary (SVS) set and depth-balanced manifold, where dynamics decouple into independent scalar recursions per mode. Outputs are the time-evolving pathway singular values σ_hi(t) showing how signal redistributes across pathways, and the Hessian eigenvalues (sharpness) at global minima, with the key quantitative result being the sharpness reduction factor H^{2/L−1} and the depth-dependent Worst-Case Return threshold η_WCR.

## Method

The paper proves that on the SVS depth-balanced manifold, the mode-wise Hessian eigenvalue is λ_i = L Σ_h σ_hi^{2−2/L}, which by Jensen's inequality is minimized by equal splitting σ_hi = σ*_i/H, yielding a sharpness reduction of H^{2/L−1} relative to a single-path solution (Theorem 4.2). Training with η > 2/S_1 proceeds in two phases: an early symmetry-breaking phase tracking GF, followed by a re-balancing phase where EoS oscillations break the GF conservation law on leaf coordinates z (measuring pathway imbalance) and drive z toward zero via a two-step contraction of the balancing gap. A Worst-Case Return threshold η_WCR is derived by reducing the transient single-path dynamics to a deep linear chain map w_{t+1} = w_t − η w_t^{L−1}(w_t^L − σ*_1), yielding a depth-dependent ceiling γ_WCR(L) = Θ(log L) that widens the safe re-balancing window for deeper networks. The mechanism is validated empirically on both linear and Tanh-activated multi-pathway MLPs, and extended to heterogeneous-depth architectures where structural asymmetry is also overridden.

## Results

The paper proves that distributing a target feature equally across H parallel pathways of depth L reduces loss sharpness by a factor of H^(2/L−1) relative to a single-path solution (Theorem 4.2), yielding minimal balanced sharpness λ_i^min = LH^(2/L−1) σ*ᵢ^(2−2/L). Training with η > 2/S₁ (where S₁ = Lσ*₁^(2−2/L) is the winner-takes-all sharpness) triggers a two-phase dynamic: initial GF-like symmetry breaking followed by re-balancing via Edge-of-Stability oscillations, with the trajectory halting at the stability boundary λ_max = 2/η. The Worst-Case Return threshold satisfies η_WCR/η₁ = γ_WCR(L) = Θ(log L), so deeper networks admit a strictly wider window of re-balancing learning rates (Proposition 6.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between Gradient Flow (GF) predictions and discrete Gradient Descent (GD) behavior at large learning rates. Under GF (η → 0), multi-pathway DLNs exhibit winner-takes-all symmetry breaking: infinitesimal initialization asymmetries are amplified by the depth-dependent growth ratio (σ_hi/σ_ki)^(2−2/L), concentrating all signal in a single pathway (Shi et al., 2022). In contrast, GD with η > 2/S₁ destabilizes this sharp single-path minimum and drives the network toward a flatter, pathway-balanced configuration where sharpness is reduced by H^(2/L−1). For heterogeneous depths, GF kinetically favors the shallowest pathway (Saxe et al., 2022), but large-step GD overrides this structural bias, redistributing signal toward deeper pathways to equalize marginal sharpness (Proposition 5.1). The nonlinear MLP experiments (Figure 3) confirm the same qualitative transition: small η recovers winner-takes-all, while η ≈ 2/λ₁^min produces balanced pathway activations (K₁₁ ≈ K₂₁), though MLPs require slightly higher rates than linear networks to fully balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-pathway Edge-of-Stability analysis of Ghosh et al. (2025) to the multi-pathway setting of Shi et al. (2022), combining the SVS parametrization and deep linear chain reduction from the former with the winner-takes-all symmetry-breaking framework of the latter. It departs from the continuous-time Gradient Flow analyses (Shi et al., 2022; Saxe et al., 2022) by showing that the discreteness of GD at large step sizes fundamentally reverses the predicted architectural bias: rather than reinforcing pathway specialization, large-step GD drives the network toward balanced, flatter minima. The self-stabilization framework of Damian et al. (2023) and the central-flow formalism of Cohen et al. (2025) provide the theoretical machinery for characterizing the re-balancing drift, while the target-aligned SVS reduction traces back to Saxe et al. (2014).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that GF-based predictions of sparse, single-pathway dominance do not survive the transition to practical discrete optimization—challenges a foundational assumption underlying a large body of DLN theory and raises the question of how many other GF-derived architectural biases are artifacts of the infinitesimal-learning-rate limit. The depth-dependent worst-case return threshold (growing as Θ(log L)) provides a concrete, actionable bound on learning rate selection for re-balancing, and the warm-up interpretation offers a mechanistic explanation for a widely used practical heuristic. Key limitations include the restriction to linear networks, square matrices, and the SVS depth-balanced manifold; the paper notes that extending the analysis to nonlinear activations, Mixture-of-Experts, and multi-head attention architectures is a natural next step, and the interaction between flat-minima bias and sparse subnetwork formation (Lottery Ticket Hypothesis) remains open.
