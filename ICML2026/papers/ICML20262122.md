## Problem

Off-policy reinforcement learning suffers from extrapolation error when the learned policy queries state-action pairs that are absent or underrepresented in the replay buffer, leading to biased value estimates through bootstrapping. Existing batch-constrained methods (BCQ, BEAR, BAIL) mitigate this by restricting the policy to the data support, but their theoretical error bounds scale quadratically as (1−γ)⁻², becoming excessively loose in long-horizon tasks. The paper identifies a missing geometric perspective: the action support is locally anisotropic, with tangential perturbations inducing far less error than normal ones, yet no prior method explicitly exploits this directional structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state observations (dimension ranging from 11 for Hopper to 348 for Humanoid) and produces a continuous action vector (3–17 dimensions) selected from a finite candidate set generated at each state. Unusually, the action space is first affinely recentered to [−1,1]ᵈ to construct orthonormal basis vectors of the action's orthogonal complement as background samples, which are then mapped back to the original bounded domain [x,y]ᵈ for environment interaction. The cVAE decoder generates candidates using only the salient latent component (s̄, 0), ensuring outputs remain on the tangent space of the learned action manifold.

## Method

FQL models the replay buffer's action support as a smooth low-dimensional manifold and draws an analogy to static friction: tangential directions correspond to supported actions with minimal extrapolation error, while normal directions capture the dominant first-order error, yielding an anisotropy ratio κ = g_n/g_t and a stability condition |κ|tan θ ≤ λ_tol that bounds allowable policy deviation. A contrastive variational autoencoder (cVAE) with two Gaussian encoders decomposes the latent space into salient factors (tangent) and irrelevant factors (normal), trained with coupled ELBOs on target (replay buffer) and background (orthonormal basis of the action's orthogonal complement, selected via arg min Q) datasets, plus a total-correlation penalty approximated by a density-ratio discriminator. The decoder reconstructs candidate actions using only the salient component, and a double Q-network selects the highest-valued candidate, while the lowest-valued background sample is fed back as the contrastive negative. This architecture ensures value updates remain confined to a controlled finite subset whose geometry is fixed by the data distribution, preventing unbounded extrapolation.

## Results

On the five MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks (1M timesteps, 5 seeds), FQL achieves the highest average return on HalfCheetah-v4 (15969.6), Walker2d-v4 (5659.9), and Humanoid-v4 (6437.2), while matching TD3 on Hopper-v4 (3370.5 vs. 3371.9) and trailing it slightly on Ant-v4 (6176.6 vs. 6542.3). FQL outperforms SAC by 32% on Hopper and 78% on Ant, and surpasses MEOW by 195% on Walker2d. In imitation learning (Figure 4), FQL exceeds the behavior-policy return in most environments, whereas BCQ largely remains at the behavior level. On D4RL offline RL, FQL+BC is competitive with IQL on HalfCheetah (182.1 vs. 179.7 total) but underperforms on Walker2d (156.3 vs. 257.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FQL outperforms DDPG and MEOW across all five MuJoCo environments, with the largest margins on Walker2d (5659.9 vs. 1744.7 for DDPG; 5659.9 vs. 1919.2 for MEOW) and Ant (6176.6 vs. −237.8 for DDPG). Against SAC, FQL leads on Hopper (+32%), Walker2d (+18%), Ant (+99%), and Humanoid (+13%), while HalfCheetah is comparable (15969.6 vs. 15476.8). Against TD3, FQL wins on HalfCheetah (+5.6%), Walker2d (+10.9%), and Humanoid (+19.8%), ties on Hopper (3370.5 vs. 3371.9), and trails slightly on Ant (6176.6 vs. 6542.3). In the imitation-learning setting, FQL surpasses the DDPG behavior policy in most environments while BCQ largely plateaus at the behavior level, confirming the frictional constraint's effectiveness beyond online data collection. On D4RL offline RL, FQL+BC matches or slightly exceeds IQL on HalfCheetah (182.1 vs. 179.7 total) and is competitive on Hopper (226.1 vs. 228.7), but underperforms substantially on Walker2d (156.3 vs. 257.0), indicating the offline variant still requires refinement for that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

FQL extends the batch-constrained Q-learning lineage initiated by BCQ (Fujimoto et al., 2019), which uses a standard VAE to restrict policies to the replay buffer support. The key departure is replacing the vanilla VAE with a contrastive VAE (Abid & Zou, 2019) that explicitly separates tangent (supported) from normal (unsupported) directions in the action manifold, grounded in a static-friction analogy that yields a formal stability condition via an anisotropy ratio. The actor-critic backbone and double Q-network structure are inherited from DDPG and TD3, while the orthonormal-basis construction for background samples is a new geometric device not present in prior batch-constrained methods like BEAR or BAIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first geometric (manifold-based) interpretation of extrapolation error in off-policy RL, reframing the batch constraint as a friction threshold that bounds policy deviation angle. This offers a principled stability condition (the anisotropy ratio κ and tolerance λ_tol) that is not a tunable hyperparameter, which could guide future constraint design. However, the framework does not explicitly enforce isometry or orthogonality—these are emergent properties of the cVAE structure—and offline RL results are mixed (substantially worse than IQL on Walker2d). Open questions include whether isometric or geometry-preserving encoders could make the geometric guarantees formal, and whether the friction analogy extends beyond continuous control to discrete or high-dimensional action spaces.
