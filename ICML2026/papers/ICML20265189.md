## Problem

Image-to-image relighting requires representations that preserve dense photometric structure while respecting material and object boundaries, yet it remains unclear which pretrained visual encoders actually support physically meaningful light transfer. This paper uses generative relighting as a controlled probe to expose a semantic-photometric trade-off: strong semantic encoders optimized for recognition invariance (CLIP, DINO) can systematically degrade relighting quality, while dense pixel-aligned encoders (MAE, RADIO) better preserve the illumination-sensitive cues needed for light transport.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images at 512×512 resolution: a content image (providing scene geometry, materials, and intrinsic appearance) and a target illumination image (providing the desired lighting conditions). At inference, intrinsic features are extracted from the content image and a global lighting embedding is extracted from the illumination image; these are combined through a diffusion decoder to produce a single relit RGB image. The framework supports both same-scene relighting (paired multi-illumination captures) and cross-scene relighting (arbitrary unpaired image pairs) without requiring 3D supervision, albedo maps, or environment maps.

## Method

ALI (Augmented Latent Intrinsics) is a three-stage training pipeline built on the LumiNet diffusion relighting backbone. Stage I injects frozen features from a pretrained visual encoder (e.g., RADIOv2.5) into the latent intrinsic representation via a learnable projection layer that aligns pixel-wise hypercolumn descriptors with the intrinsic features, trained with reconstruction, improved intrinsic invariance, and hyperspherical regularization losses. Stage II fine-tunes the LumiNet DDPM decoder to condition on the augmented representation using standard denoising score-matching. Stage III performs self-supervised refinement on a 'Lighting Zoo' of pseudo-relit pairs generated by the model itself, improving robustness on in-the-wild images without labeled data. The key empirical finding is that encoder choice critically determines relightability: RADIOv2.5 (dense, multi-teacher distilled) and MAE (pixel-reconstruction) consistently outperform CLIP and DINOv2/3, with the largest gains on glossy, metallic, and specular materials.

## Results

On the MIIW cross-scene benchmark, ALI achieves RMSE 0.294 and SSIM 0.464 (raw) / RMSE 0.231 and SSIM 0.553 (color-corrected), setting state-of-the-art among open-sourced diffusion-based relighting methods without privileged labels. This represents a 4.5% RMSE reduction and 4.9% SSIM improvement over the direct baseline LumiNet (RMSE 0.310, SSIM 0.440). On the in-scene MIIW protocol, ALI scores PSNR 18.87, RMSE 0.119, LPIPS 0.213, and SSIM 0.671, outperforming LumiNet on all four metrics while remaining competitive with methods that use ground-truth albedo or environment maps. Material-wise analysis (Table 5) shows the largest gains on Specular (+15.3% SSIM) and Metallic (+16.0% SSIM) regions over LumiNet, confirming the method's strength on view-dependent materials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALI outperforms LumiNet (its direct architectural baseline) on all metrics in both cross-scene and in-scene settings, with the most notable gains on specular and metallic materials (up to +16% SSIM in material-wise evaluation). Against Latent-Intrinsic (Zhang et al., 2024a), ALI trades a small pixel-metric deficit (RMSE 0.294 vs. 0.297 cross-scene; PSNR 18.87 vs. 21.35 in-scene) for superior qualitative fidelity on complex materials, which the authors attribute to standard metrics favoring tone consistency over directional lighting accuracy. SA-AE with light labels achieves slightly better MIIW cross-scene RMSE (0.288 vs. 0.294) but is trained exclusively on MIIW and requires light annotations; without labels, SA-AE degrades substantially (RMSE 0.443). RGB↔X and DiffusionRenderer, despite using privileged G-Buffer or environment-map supervision, produce markedly worse results (RMSE 0.587 and 0.399 respectively), suggesting that explicit physical annotations do not guarantee better relighting. UniRelight (He et al., 2025), which uses albedo and environment maps with video-based training, achieves higher in-scene PSNR (20.76 vs. 18.87) but exhibits visible failures on challenging material regions per qualitative comparison. In the user study, ALI wins on lighting alignment (0.93) and lighting realism (0.90) against both Latent-Intrinsics and LumiNet, while Latent-Intrinsics retains a slight edge on identity preservation (0.68 vs. 0.63). IC-Light (Zhang et al., 2025) is compared qualitatively on in-the-wild images and produces stylized, exaggerated results that diverge from target lighting, whereas ALI preserves material detail and directional cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Latent Intrinsics (Zhang et al., 2024a) for the relighting backbone and LumiNet (Xing et al., 2025) for the diffusion decoder, extending both by injecting frozen visual encoder features (RADIO, MAE, CLIP, DINO) into the latent intrinsic representation via a lightweight fusion adapter. Conceptually, it departs from prior generative relighting work (RGB↔X, DiffusionRenderer, UniRelight) that relies on dense physical supervision or synthetic data, instead treating relighting as a controlled probe to systematically evaluate which pretrained visual priors preserve the photometric and material cues needed for light transfer. The three-stage training pipeline (augment → align → self-refine) is a methodological contribution layered on top of the existing latent-intrinsics + diffusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that stronger semantic encoders (CLIP, DINO) can systematically degrade relighting quality while weaker but denser encoders (MAE, RADIO) improve it—challenges the common assumption that better recognition features transfer to all downstream generative tasks. This semantic-photometric trade-off has practical implications for selecting visual priors in any task requiring dense, pixel-aligned physical fidelity rather than invariance. Limitations remain: ALI does not solve inverse rendering, can still conflate surface color with illumination, and the analysis is confined to a single dataset (MIIW) and one relighting pipeline, leaving open whether the trade-off generalizes to other generative tasks or scales with model size.
