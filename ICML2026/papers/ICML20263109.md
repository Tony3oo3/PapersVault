## Problem

Discrete sequence models (RNN, LSTM, TCN) impose fixed-step approximations on continuous physical processes, making them unable to disentangle smooth signal degradation trends from stochastic noise in irregularly sampled time series. Additionally, existing clustering frameworks require a predetermined cluster count, which is infeasible in open-world settings where the number of underlying physical degradation prototypes is unknown and environment-specific.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263109/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled RSSI sequences with non-uniform continuous timestamps (no fixed grid), where each sample is a set of (observation vector, timestamp) pairs with stochastic inter-arrival intervals. It produces a binary detection decision (degrading vs. stable) for proactive handover, a hierarchical library of physical prototypes parameterized by centroids and coverage radii, and a smooth reconstructed signal trajectory via backward ODE integration. The prototype structure is two-level: K* coarse clusters each containing P_k fine-grained sub-prototypes, all discovered without specifying K in advance.

## Method

The framework is a continuous-time autoencoder whose encoder alternates between neural ODE evolution (solving d h/dt = f_θ(h,t) between consecutive timestamps to model autonomous physical dynamics) and GRU-based observation injection (a discrete rectification step that fuses each new measurement into the latent state). The decoder is purely generative, integrating the same learned vector field backward from the terminal latent embedding to reconstruct the full trajectory, trained with MSE reconstruction loss. For prototype discovery, a two-stage adaptive clustering operates on the learned latent embeddings: first, agglomerative hierarchical clustering with Ward's linkage builds a dendrogram, and a locally-constrained threshold search within [τ_base − ε, τ_base + ε] determines the optimal cut K*; second, localized K-Means refines each coarse cluster into fine-grained sub-prototypes with explicit coverage radii. At inference, the terminal latent state is matched against the prototype library via a distance-to-centroid test bounded by the coverage radius, yielding the binary degrading/stable decision.

## Results

On the CBD Office Building RSSI dataset across six degradation scenarios, the proposed ODE method achieves the lowest False Rejection Rate (FRR) in every scenario, ranging from 0.00 (Scenarios 2 and 3) to 0.09 (Scenario 5), while maintaining a competitive False Acceptance Rate (FAR) of 0.02–0.04. In contrast, discrete baselines exhibit substantially higher FRR: ResNet reaches 0.66–0.96, ModernTCN 0.22–0.94, and even the best recurrent baselines (LSTM, GRU) still range from 0.06–0.33. The ODE method also demonstrates superior robustness to sparsity, maintaining stable FAR and FRR curves as sequence completeness drops, whereas discrete architectures show rapid metric degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed ODE framework against four discrete baselines: ResNet, LSTM, GRU, and ModernTCN, all sharing the same autoencoder topology with only the latent-state evolution mechanism swapped. The ODE method decisively outperforms all baselines on FRR across all six scenarios, reducing false rejections by 2–20× compared to ResNet (e.g., 0.00 vs. 0.93 in Scenario 3) and by 2–6× compared to the best recurrent baseline GRU (e.g., 0.00 vs. 0.29 in Scenario 3). On FAR, all methods are competitive (0.01–0.06), indicating that the hierarchical clustering and thresholding provide a solid security baseline regardless of the encoder. Among baselines, a clear hierarchy emerges: static architectures (ResNet, ModernTCN) suffer the highest FRR, discrete recurrent models (LSTM, GRU) improve substantially but still lag, and the continuous ODE integration closes the remaining gap. Under sparsity stress tests (discarding initial sequence segments), the ODE method maintains stable FAR/FRR while ModernTCN and ResNet metrics surge, confirming that the continuous vector-field prior is the decisive factor for robust early link failure detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) and the Latent ODE formulation for irregular time series (Rubanova et al., 2019), adopting the pattern of continuous ODE evolution between observations punctuated by discrete GRU-based state rectification. It departs from the dominant discrete-architecture paradigm (LSTM, GRU, ResNet, ModernTCN) by arguing that these models function as 'discrete sequence approximators' that neglect the derivative function driving physical processes, and from prior prototype-learning methods (Hu et al. 2025, Chen et al. 2025, Opper & N 2025) that rely on static alignment or fixed-count clustering. The key conceptual departure is combining the continuous integral-curve inductive bias with a two-stage adaptive clustering mechanism (Ward hierarchical + localized K-Means) to discover an unknown number of physical prototypes in an open-set setting, specifically for the proactive handover task in next-generation wireless networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important problem in 6G network management—distinguishing persistent signal degradation from transient fluctuations to enable proactive handover—where the continuous-time ODE prior serves as a natural denoiser that discrete architectures cannot replicate. However, the validation is confined to a single CBD office building dataset with only 20–30 sequences per scenario, and the adaptive threshold τ* still requires domain heuristics for calibration, limiting fully autonomous deployment. The OOD generalization results (FRR rising from ~0.00 to ~0.28–0.31 under spatial relocation) reveal that while the ODE captures more invariant dynamics than discrete models, it does not achieve true environment-agnostic robustness. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger, more diverse deployment environments, how the prediction horizon limitation (ODE drift without observation rectification) affects real-time reliability, and whether the adaptive clustering mechanism generalizes beyond the six indoor scenarios tested.
