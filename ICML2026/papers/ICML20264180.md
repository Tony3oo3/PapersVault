## Problem

Plug-and-play reconstruction methods that use deep denoisers iteratively lack convergence guarantees unless the denoiser satisfies global Lipschitz or nonexpansivity constraints. Existing approaches enforce these constraints only empirically via sample-based penalties during training, leaving the denoiser unconstrained outside the training distribution and allowing divergence in practice. The paper asks whether one can construct a denoiser that is provably nonexpansive for all inputs while retaining enough expressiveness for competitive reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax + ε, a known forward operator A, and a reference image ξ (obtained via a short warm-up phase and then frozen) as inputs, and produces a reconstructed image through PnP-HQS fixed-point iteration. The denoiser is linear in the current iterate x but nonlinear in the reference ξ, which controls adaptive aggregation weights. For standalone denoising, the input is a noisy image and the output is a denoised image; the permutation set T[7] (225 local translations) generates the image variants used in the aggregation.

## Method

The denoiser applies a set of lattice permutations to generate variants of the input image, then uses a lightweight CNN (0.9M parameters) to predict positive weight maps for each permuted variant relative to a frozen reference image. The output is a normalized weighted aggregation of the permuted input, making the operator linear in x and nonlinear only in the reference. An Inverse-Consistency Condition (ICC) is enforced during aggregation to make the linear operator symmetric, and a symmetrization protocol then yields a symmetric, nonnegative, stochastic matrix with spectral norm exactly 1, guaranteeing nonexpansivity. Plugged into PnP-HQS, the resulting reconstruction operator is provably contractive under mild assumptions (transitivity of the permutation set and a non-annihilating forward model), ensuring convergence to a unique fixed point. The network is trained with standard MSE loss on synthetic Gaussian noise, optionally conditioned on a noise-level channel.

## Results

On CBSD10 deblurring (9 kernels), the proposed method achieves 28.29 dB average PSNR at ν=0.03 and 26.75 dB at ν=0.05, placing it within 0.3–0.8 dB of the best convergent baselines (GSPnP+GSDRUNet: 29.09/27.43 dB; ADMM+CoCo-DRUNet: 28.61/27.29 dB). On CBSD10 superresolution (scales 2×, 3×, 4×), it achieves 24.95 dB average PSNR, within 0.4 dB of the best (GSPnP+GSDRUNet: 25.35 dB). For denoising at σ=50/255, the method outperforms BM3D by a large margin and remains close to unconstrained DRUNet while being provably nonexpansive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is competitive with the strongest convergent baselines: it trails GSPnP+GSDRUNet by ~0.8 dB (deblurring) and ~0.4 dB (superresolution), and ADMM+CoCo-DRUNet by ~0.3–0.5 dB, while providing a global Lipschitz guarantee that those methods lack. Against the empirically-constrained SPC-DRUNet and CoCo-DRUNet variants, the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds their PSNR (e.g., 28.29 vs. 27.77 dB for IHQS+SPC-DRUNet at ν=0.03 deblurring), and critically, the paper demonstrates that those baselines can diverge in practice (Figure 4) despite their stated convergence guarantees, whereas the proposed method remains stable across all tested scenarios. The method substantially outperforms all other baselines: SAGD+WCRR by ~1.2 dB, HQS+LipDSNN by ~1.9 dB, FBS+TV by ~2.0 dB, and ADMM+DSG-NLM by ~3.0 dB on deblurring. The key differentiator is that the proposed denoiser is nonexpansive by construction for all parameter values (Lipschitz constant ≤ 1 globally), rather than relying on sample-based penalty terms that only enforce pseudo-contractivity on the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PnP-HQS reconstruction framework (Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013) by replacing the black-box denoiser with one constructed via permutation group actions on the image lattice, drawing on the symmetrization protocol of Sreehari et al. (2016). It departs from recent Lipschitz-constrained denoiser approaches (Wei et al., 2024; 2025; Ducotterd et al., 2024; Goujon et al., 2024) that enforce Lipschitz control only empirically via training-sample penalties, instead achieving global nonexpansivity by design through a weighted-aggregation architecture where nonlinearity is confined to weight prediction and the denoising step is a linear map. The construction connects conceptually to non-local means (Buades et al., 2005) and non-local neural networks (Wang et al., 2018), but the group-theoretic formulation and the inverse-consistency condition are new structural ingredients that enable the contractivity proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rare combination of a trainable denoiser with a provable global contraction guarantee for the full reconstruction operator, and empirically demonstrates that baselines with only empirical Lipschitz constraints (SPC-DRUNet, CoCo-DRUNet) can diverge in practice, underscoring the practical value of global guarantees. Key limitations include computation time scaling linearly with the number of permutations (225 for T[7]) and the need for a warm-up phase before the reference is frozen, which introduces a practical hyperparameter. Open questions include whether the permutation set can be enlarged or learned to improve expressiveness without sacrificing the group-structure assumptions, and whether the approach extends to nonlinear forward operators or multi-scale settings.
