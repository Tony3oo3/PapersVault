## Problem

Fairness in machine learning has been predominantly studied for discrete protected attributes in classification, leaving the 'continuous fairness' setting (e.g., age, race as population percentage) in regression largely unaddressed. While iterative null-space projection is a natural strategy for removing protected-attribute information, it had only been applied to linear models or fixed non-linear embeddings, with no extension to the potentially infinite-dimensional feature spaces induced by kernels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×n RBF kernel matrix computed from tabular data and an n×l vector of continuous protected attribute values, producing a transformed n×n positive semi-definite kernel matrix K_(m) along with a combined transformation matrix T_m. The transformed kernel can be directly plugged into any kernel-based regressor (KRR, SVR) without modification, and T_m enables out-of-sample extension to k unseen test points via a single matrix multiplication K_test · T_m. The regularization parameter α̃ controls the granularity of information removal, while the number of iterations m controls the degree of fairness enforcement.

## Method

The core contribution is a theorem showing that iterative null-space projections in the empirical feature space (G = QΛ^{1/2} from the eigendecomposition of K) correspond to a direct, closed-form transformation of the kernel matrix: K_(m) = K_(m-1)(Id − MK_(m-1)), where M = (K+α̃I)^{-1} p τ_norm p^T (K+α̃I)^{-1}. At each iteration, a ridge-regression weight vector w is fitted to predict the protected attribute in the empirical feature space, and the projection P = Id − w(w^T w)^{-1}w^T removes that direction; the key insight is that the product of these projections remains a valid projection (Lemma 3.1) and the entire sequence collapses into a single kernel-space transformation that preserves positive semi-definiteness (Corollary 3.3). The approach is model-agnostic since it only modifies the kernel matrix, and the Nystroem approximation reduces the O(n³) inversion cost to O(p³) for a chosen landmark subset p ≪ n.

## Results

The paper evaluates on three real-world regression datasets (Communities & Crimes, ACSIncome, ACSTravelTime) using 5-fold cross-validation, reporting MAE for predictive accuracy and three fairness scores: HGR and GDP (for demographic parity) and Pairwise Fairness (for equalized odds). Results are presented as Pareto fronts (Figure 1) rather than single-point metrics, showing the trade-off between MAE and each fairness measure. SVR-FKD (the proposed method combined with Support Vector Regression) achieves the best overall trade-offs, particularly on ACSIncome where it outperforms all baselines across GDP, and on ACSTravelTime where it is the only method to improve fairness beyond the dummy regressor. On Communities & Crimes, KRR-FKL and NN-HGR slightly outperform SVR-FKD on HGR under strong regularization, but SVR-FKD leads under weak regularization, especially for GDP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KRR-FKL (Pérez-Suay et al., 2017), the proposed SVR-FKD wins on ACSIncome (particularly for GDP) and ACSTravelTime, while KRR-FKL edges out SVR-FKD on Communities & Crimes for the HGR score under strong regularization; KRR-FKD (the proposed method with Kernel Ridge Regression) is outperformed by KRR-FKL on ACSIncome. Against NN-HGR (Mary et al., 2019), SVR-FKD generally dominates on ACSIncome and ACSTravelTime, while NN-HGR retains a slight HGR advantage on Communities & Crimes only under strong regularization. Against NN-FREM (Kong et al., 2025, evaluated in Appendix D), the proposed methods are not directly compared in the main results, but NN-FREM is found to be surpassed by KRR-FKL across all datasets, placing it below the proposed methods. Overall, SVR-FKD provides the most consistent competitive or superior performance across the three datasets and three fairness measures, with the strongest gains on ACSIncome (GDP) and ACSTravelTime (all fairness scores).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative null-space projection framework of Ravfogel et al. (2020), which was limited to linear models or fixed neural network embeddings, to the setting of kernel-induced (potentially infinite-dimensional) feature spaces via the empirical feature space formulation of Schölkopf et al. (1999). The key theoretical contribution is a direct kernel-matrix transformation (Theorem 3.2) that avoids explicit feature-space computation while preserving positive semi-definiteness. It positions itself against other kernel fairness approaches (HSIC-based KRR-FKL, Gaussian Process subspace alignment, kernelized minimax games) by being model-agnostic and applicable to any kernel-based regression algorithm, including SVR for which no prior fair variant with continuous protected attributes existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most fairness literature targets discrete protected attributes in classification, while real-world attributes like age or population percentages are naturally continuous and often appear in regression settings. The model-agnostic kernel transformation is practically useful as it can be plugged into any kernel-based regressor without modifying the optimization. However, scalability remains a significant limitation (O(n²) storage, O(m·n³) compute), and the empirical results are heterogeneous across datasets and fairness measures, suggesting the method's advantage is task-dependent. Open questions include whether the Nystroem approximation can be pushed further (projecting directly in the reduced representation), extension to classification, and whether the approach generalizes to other kernel methods like Gaussian Processes.
