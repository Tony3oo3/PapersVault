## Problem

Existing test-time prompt tuning defenses for CLIP (e.g., R-TPT) rely on many augmented views to achieve adversarial robustness, creating a severe robustness-throughput trade-off: reducing views from 63 to 15 speeds up inference but drops robustness by over 10%. The root cause is that all views are treated as equally trustworthy during adaptation, so a single corrupted view can dominate the few-view regime. No prior method explicitly assesses per-view quality to distinguish trustworthy from corrupted views.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test RGB image and produces a class probability distribution over the label set. At test time, it generates N augmented views (default N=15) via AugMix-style transformations, computes per-view quality scores, performs a single gradient step on 4 learnable text prompt tokens, and outputs the SS-weighted aggregation of per-view predictions. No labeled data, external supervision, or task-specific retraining is required; each test sample is adapted independently.

## Method

SS-TPT introduces two complementary view-quality scores: stability, defined as the inverse Jensen-Shannon divergence between a view's prediction and its weakly-augmented variant, and suitability, defined as the inverse mean cosine distance to other views in the normalized feature space. After min-max normalization, these combine into a single SS score via a weighted sum (α=0.4) and are converted to softmax weights (τ_w=0.25). During adaptation, an SS-guided consistency loss (KL divergence to a leave-one-out SS-weighted reference) is added to point-wise entropy minimization, reinstating the KL term that R-TPT removed but in a quality-guided manner. During inference, per-view predictions are aggregated using the same SS weights, suppressing corrupted views. The entire procedure requires only one Adam step (lr=5e-3) on 4 context tokens, making it ~2× faster than R-TPT while achieving higher robustness.

## Results

On 10 fine-grained classification datasets (CLIP-ResNet50, 15 augmented views, PGD ε=1.0), SS-TPT achieves 46.8% average adversarial robustness, surpassing the strongest baseline R-TPT (40.4%) by +6.4 points, while maintaining 55.1% clean accuracy—the closest to vanilla CLIP (55.8%) among all adaptation methods. On ImageNet and its four OOD variants, SS-TPT reaches 46.0% average clean accuracy and 35.3% average robustness, outperforming R-TPT by +1.2 and +4.8 points respectively. In terms of efficiency, SS-TPT with 15 views processes each image in ~0.74 s, nearly 2× faster than R-TPT with 63 views (1.67 s), while simultaneously achieving higher robustness (81.0% vs. 79.8% on Caltech101).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SS-TPT outperforms all test-time adaptation baselines on both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness. Against R-TPT (the strongest prior test-time defense), SS-TPT gains +6.4 points in average robustness (46.8% vs. 40.4%) and +0.9 points in clean accuracy (55.1% vs. 54.2%) across 10 fine-grained datasets, while using only 15 views versus R-TPT's 63 views—yielding ~2× faster inference (0.74 s vs. 1.67 s per image). Against TTC and DOC (counterattack-based defenses), SS-TPT achieves roughly 3× higher robustness (46.8% vs. 13.7%/13.6%) at the cost of slightly lower clean accuracy. Against the simple multi-view Ensemble baseline, SS-TPT more than doubles robustness (46.8% vs. 34.2%) while also recovering clean accuracy (55.1% vs. 53.2%). On ImageNet and OOD variants, SS-TPT leads R-TPT by +4.8 points in average robustness (35.3% vs. 30.5%) and +1.2 in clean accuracy (46.0% vs. 44.8%), and even surpasses vanilla CLIP's clean accuracy (46.0% vs. 44.2%). Against training-time defenses (TeCoA, APT+TeCoA), SS-TPT is competitive in robustness (45.8% vs. 46.4% for APT+TeCoA) but substantially better in clean accuracy (53.0% vs. 46.5%), all without requiring any labeled data or task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SS-TPT extends the test-time prompt tuning (TPT) lineage for CLIP by building directly on TPT's marginal entropy objective and R-TPT's point-wise entropy variant for adversarial robustness. Its key conceptual departure from R-TPT is challenging the 'no consistency' paradigm: rather than removing the KL consistency term entirely (as R-TPT does to avoid alignment with corrupted views), SS-TPT reinstates it in a guided form using per-view stability and suitability scores. The method also introduces SS-weighted prediction at inference, replacing the uniform averaging used by prior multi-view defenses, thereby shifting the focus from view quantity to view quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in test-time adversarial defenses: the robustness-throughput trade-off that forces practitioners to choose between many views (high robustness, slow inference) and few views (fast, fragile). By showing that view-quality scoring can recover most of the robustness of 63-view methods using only 15 views—and even maintain meaningful robustness with a single view—SS-TPT makes test-time prompt tuning defenses more deployable in latency-sensitive settings. The acknowledged limitation that multiple mutually-corrupted views could artificially inflate SS scores, and the suggestion of spatial cues as a complementary quality signal, point to open questions about the sufficiency of global prediction invariance and feature-space density as trustworthiness indicators.
