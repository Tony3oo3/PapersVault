## Problem

Computing U-statistics with kernel degree k ≥ 2 (e.g., Kendall's τ, AUC, Gini mean difference, triangle counting) in a federated setting under central differential privacy remains largely unsolved. Existing approaches either operate under the weaker local DP model (incurring substantially higher noise), are restricted to 2-party settings, or require trusted shufflers, and none scale to kernel degrees beyond 2.

## Input / Output

Each of n parties holds a single data instance x_i represented in fixed-precision arithmetic over F_{2^ℓ} (ℓ=40 bits, c=14 in experiments). The protocol also takes a symmetric kernel function f of degree k, DP parameters (ε, δ), and a jointly sampled edge set E ⊆ C_k^n of size O(n) to avoid the O(n^k) combinatorial explosion. The output is a single scalar: the differentially private partial U-statistic estimate Ũ_{f,E} = U_{f,E} + η/|E|, reconstructed by the aggregator from n party-contributed values.

## Method

Protocol Π_{U-MPC} computes the U-statistic in four online phases using additive secret sharing (AddSS) over a finite field. In the Sharing phase, each party distributes its data as (k,k)-threshold shares among the k parties in every edge it belongs to. In the Computing phase, parties invoke an MPC functionality F_f (instantiated via GMW or Function Secret Sharing) to evaluate f(x_e) on secret-shared inputs. In the Noise phase, parties jointly generate a shared DP noise η via F_noise (e.g., discrete Laplace from Balle et al.'s Polya construction, or Gaussian via CLT/Box-Muller). In the Aggregation phase, each party sums its noise share with all f(x_e) shares for edges containing it and sends the result to the aggregator, who reconstructs the final estimate. The key architectural insight is that AddSS allows shares from different k-party edge groups to be mixed into a global (n,n) sharing at zero additional communication cost, and under the honest-fraction threat model M_HF, noise generation can be delegated to a small subgroup to reduce communication from O(n²) to O(n). A BalancedSamp algorithm samples edges to make the induced hypergraph as regular as possible, minimizing the incomplete-U-statistic error term.

## Results

The proposed protocol Π_U-MPC achieves an MSE of 1/|E| + 1/(n²ε²) for degree-2 U-statistics under central ε-DP, substantially outperforming Bell et al. (1/t² + t²/(nε²)) and Ghazi et al. (1/t² + t²log(t)/(ε²n)). For Kendall's τ coefficient on the Bank Marketing dataset (n=4521), Umpc reduces MSE by up to four orders of magnitude compared to existing baselines. For triangle counting (k=3) on the Social Circles: Facebook dataset, Umpc achieves a relative error (RMSE/T_G) of 0.5309 at ε=10 with |E|=10⁷, with a total communication cost of 19.4 GB (~4.8 MB per party). The BalancedSamp sampling strategy further reduces MSE by ~20.7× and ~33× compared to uniform and Bernoulli sampling respectively for the duplicate pair ratio task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Bell et al. (2020), Umpc achieves a strictly lower MSE (1/|E| + 1/(n²ε²) vs. 1/t² + t²/(nε²)) because it avoids input discretization entirely, while Bell's error grows quadratically with the number of bins t. Bell has the lowest per-party computation cost (t operations) but incurs the highest server-side cost (n²t²), whereas Umpc's server cost is only O(n). Compared to Ghazi et al. (2024), Umpc outperforms on all three metrics: lower MSE (no t²log(t) factor), lower communication cost (linear in n under M_HF vs. n²ε²(log t)ℓ for Ghazi), and lower per-party computation (independent of t vs. ε²nt log t). Ghazi's communication cost increases with ε (more communication at higher privacy budgets), while Umpc's is independent of ε. For kernel degree k>2 (e.g., triangle counting), Umpc is the only protocol among the compared methods that provides a privacy-preserving solution, as both Bell and Ghazi are limited to k=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the LDP-based protocols of Bell et al. (2020) and Ghazi et al. (2024), which compute degree-2 U-statistics by discretizing the input space and adding local noise, by instead leveraging multi-party computation with additive secret sharing to achieve central-DP-level accuracy in a fully federated setting without trusted aggregators or shufflers. It adapts the distributed discrete Laplace noise generation from Balle et al. (2020) for the F_noise functionality and uses Function Secret Sharing for efficient kernel evaluation. The key conceptual departure is treating the U-statistic computation as a secure multi-party computation problem rather than a local randomization problem, which eliminates the discretization-induced bias and the t²-dependent noise scaling that plagues prior LDP approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generic protocol for privately computing U-statistics of arbitrary kernel degree k ≥ 2 under central DP in a federated setting, filling a gap where prior work was limited to k=2 or required trusted intermediaries. The practical impact is significant for federated learning applications that rely on pairwise or higher-order statistics (metric learning, clustering, ranking), as the 4-orders-of-magnitude MSE reduction for Kendall's tau makes previously infeasible privacy-utility tradeoffs practical. Open questions include whether the semi-honest assumption can be relaxed to malicious adversaries without prohibitive cost, whether the quadratic communication under M_Dis can be further reduced, and whether the protocol scales to very large n (tens of thousands of parties) in real-world deployments. The triangle counting experiment also reveals that node-DP renders sparse-graph statistics essentially unusable at strict privacy budgets, pointing to edge-DP as a necessary relaxation for graph-structured data.
