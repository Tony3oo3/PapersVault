## Problem

Bayesian uncertainty quantification for deep neural networks often relies on linearizing the network to form a Bayesian Generalized Linear Model (GLM). While linearizing only around the final connected layer (last-layer approximation) is widely used for computational efficiency, it has been assumed to degrade UQ performance relative to full-network linearization. This paper rigorously tests that assumption, asking whether the last-layer GLM loses any meaningful epistemic uncertainty capability compared to the full DNN-GLM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained DNN with parameters θ̂, a training set (X, Y), and a parameter subset θ_S (all parameters for DNN-GLM, or only the final-layer weights for LL-GLM), along with a noise scale η². It produces T posterior samples by initializing θ_S around θ̂_S and running (S)GD on the linearized objective, yielding predictive means and variances. The critical practical difference is that the LL-GLM optimizes over d_L·c parameters versus the full p = dim(θ) parameters, which is what drives the 10× wall-time and 3.5× memory reductions observed on GPT-2.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary results. Theoretically, it uses Random Matrix Theory to derive the deterministic limiting Bayes Free Energy for GPs induced by the Conjugate Kernel (last-layer) and Neural Tangent Kernel (full linearization) in the double-asymptotic regime, showing that the CK reaches the BFE lower bound under the same conditions as the NTK and that the NTK's only advantage (in the n ≫ width regime) vanishes after training. Practically, it introduces the LinearSampling algorithm: a sample-then-optimize procedure that draws θ_S,t⁰ ~ N(θ̂_S, η²I) and runs (S)GD to solve the linearized regression or classification objective, producing exact posterior samples from the Bayesian GLM with regularizer λ = η⁻² and τ → 0. This framework scales to 124M-parameter GPT-2 and is released as a lightweight PyTorch package. Across UCI regression, image classification (ResNet-9/50 on FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), and IMDB sentiment with GPT-2, the LL-GLM matches or slightly exceeds the DNN-GLM on VARROC and LPPD metrics while rivaling Deep Ensembles at a fraction of the cost.

## Results

Across 9 UCI regression datasets, the LL-GLM matches or exceeds the DNN-GLM on all three metrics (RMSE, NLL, ECE); for example, on Naval the LL-GLM achieves RMSE 0.037 vs 0.049 for DNN-GLM, and on Energy NLL −2.560 vs −2.400. On GPT-2 fine-tuned on IMDB, the LL-GLM achieves VARROC-ID of 0.823±0.025 versus 0.842±0.015 for the DNN-GLM (no statistical difference), while reducing wall-time from 2.55 hr to 0.26 hr (≈10×) and peak memory from 71.23 GB to 20.97 GB (≈3.5×). On large-scale image classification (ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), both GLMs are consistently among the top-performing UQ methods, with Deep Ensembles remaining the strongest but at prohibitive cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is LL-GLM vs DNN-GLM, and the headline finding is that they are statistically indistinguishable in UQ quality across regression, image classification, and language tasks. Against Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), both GLMs are consistently competitive but DE remains the strongest on VARROC metrics for image classification, at a prohibitive computational cost (DE was excluded from ImageNet due to resource constraints). Against SWAG (Maddox et al., 2019), LLA (Immer et al., 2021), MC-Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), and SMS-UBU (Paulin et al., 2024), both GLMs rank among the top methods on VARROC-ID and VARROC-OOD for ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. The practical advantage of the LL-GLM is computational: on GPT-2/IMDB it achieves a 10× wall-time speedup and 3.5× memory reduction over the DNN-GLM with no measurable loss in UQ accuracy. The paper concludes that neither GLM consistently outperforms the other, and the last-layer approximation should be preferred for its efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linearized Bayesian GLM framework for UQ (Immer et al., 2021; Maddox et al., 2021; Wilson et al., 2025) by rigorously comparing two variants that the community has informally contrasted: full-network linearization (DNN-GLM, inducing the NTK) versus last-layer linearization (LL-GLM, inducing the Conjugate Kernel). The theoretical analysis draws on random matrix theory results from Fan & Wang (2020) to characterize the limiting Bayes Free Energy of GPs with these kernels, while the practical sampling algorithm (LinearSampling) builds on the sample-then-optimize framework of Matthews et al. (2017). Prior to this work, only a single toy-regression comparison existed in the literature (Ortega et al., 2023), and the prevailing assumption was that last-layer approximation necessarily degrades UQ quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that last-layer approximation matches full linearization in UQ performance across regression, image classification, and language tasks challenges a widely-held assumption in the Bayesian UQ community and suggests that epistemic uncertainty in DNNs resides in how features are combined for prediction rather than in the feature representations themselves. Practically, this enables a 10× wall-time speedup and 3.5× memory reduction for language-model UQ, making Bayesian GLM methods far more accessible. Key limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to randomly initialized networks (with only indirect connections to trained networks), the sampling algorithm modeling only epistemic (not aleatoric) uncertainty, and the under-parameterized regime gap potentially being an artifact of initialization rather than a fundamental limitation.
