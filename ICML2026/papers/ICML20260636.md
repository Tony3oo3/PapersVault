## Problem

In speculative decoding for large-vocabulary LLMs (128k+ tokens), the draft model's LM head projection dominates inference latency, yet existing vocabulary pruning methods (static high-frequency selection or trained routers) are forced to maintain large active vocabularies (~30k tokens) to preserve draft acceptance rates. This static or coarse-grained paradigm creates an untapped performance gap between theoretical speedup at small vocabulary sizes and actual achievable speedup, as context-agnostic pruning fails to capture long-tail tokens critical for specific generation steps.

## Input / Output

NanoSpec operates as a plug-and-play module on top of existing speculative decoding backbones (EAGLE-2, EAGLE-3), taking a text prompt and a pre-trained target LLM + draft model pair as input. It produces generated text tokens that are rigorously verified by the target model's full-vocabulary logits, guaranteeing lossless output identical to standard auto-regressive decoding. The only modification is to the draft model's LM head computation, which operates on a dynamically constructed sub-vocabulary of <3k tokens per step, while all state (sliding window, candidate stream) is maintained entirely in GPU VRAM with a fixed 24 MB overhead independent of sequence length.

## Method

NanoSpec exploits the strong temporal locality in LLM generation by maintaining an evolving candidate token stream, initialized from prompt tokens plus top-K prefill candidates, and updated each decoding step with draft tree tokens and the target model's top-K verification candidates. A fixed-size sliding window (W_max=3072) over this stream defines the active vocabulary index set, implementing a recency-based eviction policy that requires no learned parameters. To realize the theoretical FLOP reduction on hardware, the system co-design pipelines a dedicated CUDA copy stream (gathering non-contiguous LM head rows into a contiguous dense buffer) with the compute stream (executing draft backbone), hiding gather latency behind backbone computation via event synchronization. GPU-resident bitmap-based state management with lock-free kernels eliminates CPU-GPU synchronization overhead for the per-step vocabulary update, enabling the full dynamic pipeline to operate without pipeline bubbles.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with EAGLE-2, NanoSpec achieves 392.7 tokens/s (2.25× over auto-regressive decoding) with an average acceptance length of 3.59, using a dynamic vocabulary of <3k tokens. On the smaller Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, the advantage is more pronounced: NanoSpec reaches 900.7 tokens/s (1.35× over AR) versus only 1.05× for full-vocabulary EAGLE-2. On the EAGLE-3 backbone, NanoSpec delivers 430.0 tokens/s (1.19× speedup) on Llama-3.1-8B and 944.8 tokens/s (1.28×) on Llama-3.2-1B, while matching the full-vocabulary acceptance length of 4.25 on the 8B model. Draft time is reduced by 51.6% on average, with LM head latency dropping from 2.330 ms to 0.237 ms (9.8× reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FR-Spec (static high-frequency pruning, 32k vocab), NanoSpec wins on speed across both models (392.7 vs 369.7 on 8B; 900.7 vs 778.8 on 1B) and on acceptance length for the 1B model (3.11 vs 2.70), where static pruning suffers severe long-tail degradation; on the 8B model NanoSpec trades a small acceptance-length drop (3.59 vs 3.62) for a 6% speed gain. Against DynaSpec (trained MLP router, 27k vocab), NanoSpec achieves higher speed (392.7 vs 367.7 on 8B; 900.7 vs 762.8 on 1B) without any auxiliary training, though DynaSpec retains a slight edge on 8B acceptance length (3.73 vs 3.59). Against full-vocabulary EAGLE-2, NanoSpec delivers a 17% speed improvement (392.7 vs 336.9 on 8B) at the cost of a modest acceptance-length reduction (3.59 vs 3.80), a trade-off that is more than compensated by the 9.8× LM head latency reduction. Against full-vocabulary EAGLE-3, NanoSpec achieves a 19% speedup (430.0 vs 362.2 on 8B) while exactly matching the acceptance length (4.25), and a 28% speedup (944.8 vs 739.6) on the 1B model with near-matching acceptance (3.39 vs 3.45).

## Relation to Prior Work

NanoSpec builds directly on the speculative decoding framework (EAGLE-2/EAGLE-3) and the emerging sub-task of draft vocabulary pruning (FR-Spec, DynaSpec, CORAL, VocabTrim), but departs fundamentally from their static or router-based paradigms. Where prior methods select a fixed high-frequency subset or route among pre-clustered sub-vocabularies (requiring ~30k active tokens), NanoSpec exploits temporal locality in language generation to construct a per-step, context-aware vocabulary of <3k tokens with no learned parameters. The system-algorithm co-design (asynchronous weight gathering via dual CUDA streams, GPU-resident sliding-window state) is a novel contribution that makes the ultra-sparse dynamic vocabulary practically realizable on modern GPUs, addressing a hardware bottleneck that prior pruning methods sidestepped by keeping vocabularies large enough for dense GEMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NanoSpec breaks what prior work treated as an inherent trade-off between draft vocabulary size and acceptance rate, showing that the gap was an artifact of context-agnostic design rather than a fundamental constraint. As a training-free, plug-and-play module delivering 1.17–1.29× speedup over EAGLE-2/3, it lowers the barrier to deploying speculative decoding on moderate-scale models (1B–8B) where the LM head is a severe bottleneck. The paper acknowledges diminishing returns on 70B+ models where verification latency dominates, and the approach's reliance on a sliding-window heuristic (rather than a principled coverage guarantee) leaves open whether even smaller or more adaptive vocabularies could be constructed. Practical integration into serving frameworks (vLLM, SGLang) is straightforward but remains to be demonstrated at production scale.
