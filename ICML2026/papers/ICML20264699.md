## Problem

Existing prototype-based few-shot point cloud semantic segmentation methods rely on shallow, single-step feature fusion to refine prototypes, failing to model the distribution shift between support and query sets. This leads to either under-adaptation (prototypes remain too support-biased) or over-adaptation (prototypes lose discriminative class semantics), particularly when scanning density, occlusion, or appearance variations create gaps between the two sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264699/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in an episodic N-way K-shot format (N∈{2,3}, K∈{1,5}), taking a query point cloud of 2048 points with raw absolute 3D coordinates and a support set of K labeled point clouds per class with binary masks. A key empirical finding is that using raw absolute coordinates without RGB significantly outperforms the conventional normalized-coordinate-plus-RGB protocol, as normalization discards discriminative scale/position cues and RGB injects appearance domain shift. The output is a per-point class label over N target classes plus one background class, produced by a confidence-weighted ensemble across all intermediate prototypes in the refinement trajectory.

## Method

DPR-Net uses a non-parametric Seg-NN backbone and constructs a coarse-to-fine prototype trajectory P^0→P^T (T=3) via cascaded Dynamic Prototype Refinement (DPR) modules. Each DPR module decomposes features into three subspaces—common (co-activated in both query and prototype), query-distinctive, and prototype-distinctive—using a channel-activation threshold, then applies conservative fusion to the common subspace and adaptive fusion to distinctive subspaces, augmented by a cross-correlation attention mechanism for spatial context. The Mixture of Prototype Experts (MoPE) mechanism treats each intermediate prototype as an expert and ensembles their per-point predictions via confidence-driven softmax weighting, allowing different query points to adaptively select the most suitable refinement stage. Training combines cross-entropy with three auxiliary losses: channel correlation (aligning refinement direction with query activation), inter-class diversity (maintaining prototype separation), and inter-generation diversity (constraining step size between adjacent prototypes to prevent abrupt jumps).

## Results

On S3DIS, DPR-Net achieves 80.76% mIoU in the 2-way 1-shot setting (S0 split), setting a new state-of-the-art. Mean mIoU reaches 79.27% (2-way 1-shot), 80.60% (2-way 5-shot), 73.77% (3-way 1-shot), and 74.55% (3-way 5-shot), outperforming the strongest prior method DyPolySeg by +6.35, +4.94, +7.53, and +6.63 points respectively. On ScanNet, DPR-Net achieves 71.57% (2-way 1-shot) and 73.30% (3-way 5-shot) mean mIoU, exceeding DyPolySeg by up to +4.12 points. The model uses only 0.28M parameters and 8.37 GFLOPs, adding just 0.04M parameters over the Seg-NN backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPR-Net outperforms all 13 compared baselines in 11 of 12 S3DIS columns and 9 of 12 ScanNet columns. Against the strongest prior method DyPolySeg, DPR-Net gains +6.35, +4.94, +7.53, and +6.63 mean mIoU on S3DIS (2W-1S, 2W-5S, 3W-1S, 3W-5S) and +1.77, +1.69, +4.12 on ScanNet (2W-5S, 3W-1S, 3W-5S), with near-parity on ScanNet 2W-1S (71.57 vs 71.89). Against the fair Seg-PN backbone with raw absolute coordinates (the paper's key controlled baseline), DPR-Net adds +2.32/+1.25/+2.48/+0.03 mean mIoU on S3DIS and +4.70/+0.32/+2.30/+0.67 on ScanNet, with the largest gains in data-scarce 1-shot settings. The paper also highlights that switching from normalized coordinates + RGB to raw absolute coordinates alone lifts Seg-PN by 10.5–12.4 points on S3DIS, a dataset-agnostic input representation bottleneck that prior methods overlooked. DPR-Net achieves these results with only 0.28M parameters and 8.37 GFLOPs, using 5.5% of DPA's parameters and 53.4% of its FLOPs while surpassing DPA by 14.68 mIoU on S3DIS 2W-1S.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototypical network paradigm for few-shot point cloud semantic segmentation (established by AttMPTI, BFG, DPA, and others) by replacing their single-step shallow feature fusion with a progressive coarse-to-fine refinement trajectory. The dual-subspace decomposition (splitting channels into common and distinctive subspaces via activation thresholds) is a new mechanism for targeted prototype adaptation, while the Mixture of Prototype Experts (MoPE) repurposes the mixture-of-experts concept to ensemble intermediate prototypes along the refinement trajectory. A notable empirical departure is the finding that raw absolute coordinates outperform the standard normalized-coordinate-plus-RGB protocol used by all prior methods, effectively re-baselining the comparison landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The input representation finding (raw coordinates yielding 10–12 point gains over the normalized+RGB protocol) challenges a widely adopted preprocessing convention and suggests prior methods may have been handicapped by their input pipeline, potentially reshaping how the field benchmarks FS-PCSS. The progressive refinement trajectory offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc prototype fusion, and the 0.28M parameter efficiency makes the approach practical. However, generalization to outdoor scenes (autonomous driving), the iterative inference cost of T cascaded modules, and the dataset-dependent optimal T remain open questions that limit immediate broader deployment.
