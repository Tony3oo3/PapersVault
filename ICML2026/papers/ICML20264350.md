## Problem

While optimal mechanisms for d-dimensional mean estimation are well understood under local differential privacy, the analogous question after shuffling—which local randomizer yields the best accuracy under a shuffled DP constraint—has remained largely unexplored. The core difficulty is that privacy after shuffling is inherently global (depending on n and privacy amplification), making it hard to pose an explicit optimization problem in terms of the local randomizer alone. This paper shows that LDP-optimal mechanisms like PrivUnit can become strictly suboptimal once shuffling is applied, and constructs an asymptotically optimal alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264350/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of n users holds a d-dimensional vector in the unit ℓ2 ball and sends exactly one privatized message through an anonymizing shuffler that permutes all reports; the analyzer observes only the shuffled multiset and must output the d-dimensional mean. The privacy constraint is (ε, δ)-DP under zero-out neighboring adjacency, and the high-privacy regime is characterized by ε_n = O(√(log n / n)) and ε_n = ω(1/√n). The mechanism is parameterized by a blanket mass γ ∈ (0,1] and a Gaussian scale σ_0, with the shuffle index χ serving as the effective privacy budget that replaces the global (ε, δ) constraint.

## Method

The paper introduces the shuffle index (a scalar determined solely by the local randomizer's structure) to reduce the n-user shuffled-DP design problem to a single-user optimization: minimize worst-case MSE subject to a shuffle-index constraint. A Hammersley-Chapman-Robbins-type argument yields the sharp lower bound MSE ≥ dχ², proving that LDP-optimal mechanisms (e.g., PrivUnit) miss this bound by a constant factor ≥ π/2 in high dimensions. To match the bound, the authors construct a Blanket-Mixed Gaussian (BMG) local randomizer that outputs N(0, σ₀²I_d) with probability γ and N(x, σ₀²I_d) with probability 1−γ, paired with the linear estimator x̂(Y) = Y/(1−γ); optimizing γ for a fixed χ yields MSE = dχ²(1 + (3/2)χ^{-2/3} + O(χ^{-4/3})). A central-limit-type correspondence is then proved: in the σ-high privacy regime, the privacy profile of the shuffled BMG mechanism converges to that of the central Gaussian mechanism GM(σ) with matching noise level, establishing a constant-accurate (not merely order-level) equivalence.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) establishes a Gaussian Limit Correspondence: in the σ-high privacy regime, the optimal worst-case MSE for unbiased single-message shuffled mean estimation satisfies dσ² ≤ Err* ≤ dσ²(1 + O(σ^{-2/3})), matching the central Gaussian mechanism GM(σ) up to a vanishing relative error. The shuffle-index lower bound (Theorem 3.5) shows any unbiased protocol with shuffle index χ incurs MSE ≥ dχ², and the proposed Blanket-Mixed Gaussian (BMG) mechanism achieves dχ²(1 + (3/2)χ^{-2/3} + O(χ^{-4/3})), confirming asymptotic optimality. Numerically, at (n, δ) = (10⁴, 10⁻⁵), BMG's RMSE closely tracks GM's in the high-privacy regime, and the agreement is already strong at n = 10².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed BMG mechanism against two baselines: shuffled PrivUnit and the central Gaussian mechanism (GM). Against PrivUnit (which is LDP-optimal), BMG is strictly superior in high dimensions: Proposition 3.7 shows PrivUnit's leading constant satisfies liminf_{d→∞} inf_θ C(θ,d) ≥ π/2 ≈ 1.57, meaning PrivUnit incurs at least a 57% constant-factor MSE penalty relative to the shuffle-index lower bound, while BMG achieves the bound with only O(χ^{-2/3}) relative excess. Against the central Gaussian mechanism, BMG does not beat GM but asymptotically matches it: Theorem 3.3 shows the optimal shuffled protocol's privacy-utility trade-off converges to GM(σ) with matching noise level, and the numerical experiments (Figures 1–3) confirm that BMG's RMSE and privacy profile closely track GM's across n ∈ {10², 10³, 10⁴} at fixed (γ, σ₀) = (0.95, 4.6). In one dimension (d=1), PrivUnit reduces to randomized response and is itself asymptotically optimal, so the suboptimality gap is specific to d > 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the shuffle index introduced by Takagi & Liew (2026) to formulate post-shuffling mechanism design as an explicit single-user optimization problem, departing from the standard practice of designing LDP-optimal mechanisms and then applying privacy amplification by shuffling. It leverages the blanket distribution framework of Balle et al. (2019) for the single-message shuffle model and the canonicalization/additivization arguments of Asi et al. (2022) to reduce the n-user design problem to a single-user one. The key conceptual departure is showing that the shuffle index, rather than LDP parameters, is the correct quantity to optimize over, which reveals that LDP-optimal mechanisms need not be shuffle-optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that PrivUnit (optimal under LDP) is strictly suboptimal after shuffling in d>1 by a factor of at least π/2 challenges a widely held implicit assumption in the DP community and provides a principled explanation for why the common design-then-amplify pipeline loses utility. The Gaussian-limit correspondence gives a constant-accurate theoretical foundation for previously only empirically observed convergence of shuffled protocols to the central Gaussian mechanism. Open questions include tightening the O(χ^{-2/3}) convergence rate, extending the shuffle-index formulation beyond mean estimation, and determining whether allowing biased estimation can strictly beat the central Gaussian trade-off.
