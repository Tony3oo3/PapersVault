## Problem

Improving VLA policies through online interaction is bottlenecked by the cost of real-world rollouts, and existing action-conditioned world models are too physically inaccurate to serve as useful simulators: they are trained predominantly on successful demonstrations, making them over-optimistic and unable to model failure cases or small contact-rich dynamics. The paper asks whether a world model grounded in real policy rollouts (including failures) can generate high-fidelity synthetic trajectories that meaningfully improve a VLA policy.

## Input / Output

The VLA policy consumes multi-camera RGB observations (two third-person views plus one wrist-mounted camera) together with a natural-language instruction and outputs action chunks for a Franka Panda arm. The world model takes the current observation and an action chunk as conditioning and auto-regressively predicts future video frames (5-second clips at the wrist view for evaluation). The reward model (Qwen3-VL-4B) ingests a temporally downsampled 16-frame trajectory video plus the task instruction and outputs a calibrated probability of success, thresholded at 0.8 to reduce false positives.

## Method

VLAW alternates two steps iteratively: (1) fine-tuning the Ctrl-World diffusion world model on real policy rollouts (which include both successes and failures) co-trained with the DROID dataset to prevent overfitting, and (2) fine-tuning the π₀.₅ flow-matching VLA policy on a union of real successful rollouts and world-model-generated synthetic trajectories filtered by a vision-language reward model. The key insight is that grounding the world model in online rollout data—rather than expert demonstrations—eliminates the over-optimism bias and substantially improves physical fidelity for contact-rich and deformable-object tasks, enabling reliable long-horizon (up to 20 s) closed-loop rollouts. The policy update uses a binary-weighted flow-matching objective (weight 1 for success, 0 for failure), which the paper shows is equivalent to advantage-weighted regression under a regularized RL formulation with a surrogate flow-matching divergence, avoiding the need for tractable action likelihoods.

## Results

On the DROID platform across five contact-rich manipulation tasks (Stacking, Wiping, Open Book, Scooping, Drawing), VLAW achieves a 39.2% absolute success rate improvement over the base π₀.₅ policy (from 46.0% mean to ~85.2%), with 11.6% of that gain attributable specifically to world-model-generated synthetic rollouts. The base policy scores 62% on Stacking, 46% on Wiping, 56% on Open Book, 44% on Scooping, and 22% on Drawing. The world model post-trained on online rollouts substantially improves video prediction fidelity (PSNR 21.77 vs. 16.32 pretrained; FVD 64.12 vs. 225.13) and reduces false-positive interaction predictions from 11 to 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLAW is compared against two baselines that do not use a world model: (1) Filtered BC, which fine-tunes the policy only on successful real-world rollouts (50 per task), and (2) DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), which optimizes the π₀.₅ noise space via online exploration. VLAW consistently outperforms both baselines across all five tasks after two iterations of co-improvement. DSRL achieves only limited gains, which the authors attribute to the difficulty of RL optimization across diverse tasks and the constraint of optimizing only in the noise space rather than updating model parameters. Filtered BC shows moderate improvement by leveraging successful real trajectories, but VLAW's large-scale synthetic rollout generation (500 trajectories per task per iteration) yields substantially larger gains, adding 11.6% beyond what real data alone provides. The world model itself is also compared against the pretrained Ctrl-World and a version fine-tuned only on expert demonstrations; the online-rollout-finetuned version achieves the best video quality metrics (PSNR 21.77, FVD 64.12) and the lowest false-positive rate (1 vs. 11) on interaction event prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two recent components: the Ctrl-World diffusion-based action-conditioned world model (Guo et al., 2025a) and the π₀.₅ flow-matching VLA policy (Intelligence et al., 2025b), combining them in an iterative co-improvement loop. It departs from prior world-model-based RL approaches (DayDreamer, SOLAR, World4RL) that relied on task-specific dynamics models with limited capacity, instead leveraging a large pretrained video diffusion model grounded by online rollouts. The policy update via weighted flow-matching regression connects to the advantage-weighted regression (AWR) framework and the offline RL formulation used in π₀.₆* (Intelligence et al., 2025a), but avoids explicit policy likelihoods that would be intractable for flow-matching policies. The key conceptual departure from prior work is using the policy's own failure-rich rollouts to fix the world model's over-optimism bias, creating a self-reinforcing improvement cycle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in VLA post-training: the cost and time of real-world robot rollouts. By showing that a small number of online rollouts (50 per task) can ground a world model well enough to generate 500+ useful synthetic trajectories per task, it offers a practical path toward scaling VLA improvement without proportional increases in physical interaction. However, the evaluation is limited to five task categories on a single Franka Panda platform over only two iterations, leaving open whether the co-improvement loop continues to yield gains, whether the grounded world model generalizes to unseen tasks, and how the approach scales to more diverse or higher-dimensional action spaces. The reliance on a vision-language reward model (Qwen3-VL) for trajectory filtering also introduces a potential failure mode if the reward model's accuracy degrades on out-of-distribution synthetic trajectories.
