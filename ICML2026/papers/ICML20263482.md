## Problem

Existing semantic caching methods rely on simple single-vector similarity measures (e.g., cosine similarity) that fail to capture subtle semantic differences between prompts, causing semantically dissimilar prompts to be incorrectly matched and degrading cache hit rates. While Multi-Vector Retrieval (MVR) with MaxSim scoring could improve retrieval granularity, its effectiveness hinges critically on the text segmentation strategy, and no prior work has learned an optimal, lightweight segmentation policy specifically for semantic caching under correctness guarantees.

## Input / Output

The system takes a new text prompt and a semantic cache of previously stored prompts (each with multi-vector segment embeddings and cached LLM responses). Candidate split positions are restricted to punctuation marks in the prompt, and the segmentation model outputs a variable-length subset of these positions, yielding a variable number of segments per prompt. The final output is a cache hit/miss decision via vCache's stochastic policy, where the segmentation-aware symmetric MaxSim score (SMaxSim) replaces the original cosine similarity as the input to the correctness-probability model. The segmentation model is deliberately lightweight (~500–600 MB GPU memory) to ensure real-time online inference.

## Method

MVR-cache introduces a pointer-network-based segmentation model (BERT encoder → MLP → single-layer LSTM → masked attention) that recurrently selects split positions from candidate punctuation boundaries, producing a variable number of segments per prompt. Each segment is embedded by a shared encoder (BGE), and the symmetric segmentation-aware MaxSim score (SMaxSim) is computed by averaging the two unidirectional MaxSim scores normalized by segment count, resolving the inherent asymmetry of vanilla MaxSim for cache matching. Training is formulated as Reinforcement Learning for Combinatorial Optimization (RL4CO): the segmentation model acts as a stochastic policy over the combinatorial space of split-index subsets, and REINFORCE maximizes a reward defined as the negative binary cross-entropy loss between the SMaxSim-based sigmoid correctness probability and ground-truth response-match labels. A theoretical analysis proves that under class-conditional normality of similarity scores, minimizing this MLE objective drives the positive-class mean toward 1 and the negative-class mean toward 0 (maximizing the separation γ while keeping the threshold t stable), thereby provably maximizing cache hit rate under any user-specified error bound δ. At inference, a two-stage retrieval pipeline uses HNSW over single-vector representations for coarse Top-20 candidate generation followed by SMaxSim reranking to select the final nearest neighbor.

## Results

MVR-cache consistently increases cache hit rates by up to 37% over vCache (on SemCacheSearchQueries under the always-cache protocol) while keeping the error rate below the user-specified bound δ=0.01. Under the default cache-on-miss protocol, gains reach up to 25% across all four datasets. In terms of end-to-end latency, MVR-cache reduces total inference time by up to 6% compared to vCache (e.g., 383.32 min vs. 408.49 min on SemCacheClassification), with its non-LLM overhead remaining under 61 ms per prompt versus 1,234–4,273 ms for a single LLM call.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vCache (the state-of-the-art training-free semantic caching method), MVR-cache achieves higher cache hit rates across all four datasets and both insertion protocols while maintaining the same correctness guarantee (error rate ≤ δ). The gap is largest on SemCacheSearchQueries (up to 37% relative gain under always-cache) and remains consistent on classification and QA tasks. Against ColBERT (token-level MVR adapted to vCache), MVR-cache wins on both cache hit rate and end-to-end latency, since ColBERT's fine-grained token embeddings produce noisier similarity scores and add retrieval overhead (e.g., 130 ms vs. 111 ms non-LLM time on SemCacheSearchQueries). Against POQD (LLM-based query decomposition), MVR-cache is dramatically faster in non-LLM overhead (34–60 ms vs. 493–1048 ms per prompt) because POQD requires an additional LLM call to segment each incoming prompt, making it impractical for latency-sensitive caching. Notably, the segmentation model trained on PromptBench generalizes to QNLI (out-of-distribution), still outperforming all baselines without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: semantic caching policies (vCache, GPTCache) and multi-vector retrieval (ColBERT, POQD). Prior semantic caching work focuses on designing the caching decision policy—static thresholds or vCache's learned prompt-specific thresholds—while relying on single-vector cosine similarity for retrieval. MVR-cache departs by improving the similarity measure itself, introducing a learned pointer-network segmentation model that produces variable-length segments for both queries and cached documents, scored via a symmetric MaxSim (SMaxSim). The training is framed as RL4CO with a theoretically grounded reward, distinguishing it from POQD's heuristic document segmentation and ColBERT's fixed token-level decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM inference costs remain a dominant operational expense, semantic caching that provably maintains correctness while maximizing hit rates has direct practical value. The clean theoretical result (minimizing the MLE loss maximizes hit rate under a fixed error bound) provides a principled training objective rather than an ad-hoc heuristic. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal prompts, whether the two-stage HNSW+rerank retrieval pipeline scales to very large caches, and whether the segmentation model's OOD generalization (PromptBench→QNLI) holds across more diverse task distributions. The requirement for 3K labeled prompts per dataset, while modest, introduces a one-time cost that may be non-trivial in production settings.
