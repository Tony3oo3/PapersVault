## Problem

Neural operators developed for PDEs emphasize global spectral representations suited to smooth function-to-function mappings, but physiological signals are nonstationary, dominated by localized transient morphology, and require function-to-label decoding under substantial noise and intersubject variability. Meanwhile, conventional deep models for biosignals are tightly coupled to fixed sampling rates and tokenization choices, forcing resampling or retraining whenever the acquisition configuration changes. The paper asks whether an operator formulation can be adapted to capture the local, nonstationary structure of physiological signals while remaining invariant to the sampling grid.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multichannel physiological segment x:[0,T]→R^C_in (e.g., 2 EEG channels over 30 s, or a single ECG lead over 8 s) together with an explicit scalar sampling rate f_s in Hz; the rate is not a fixed architectural parameter but is passed at inference so the same learned weights can be evaluated on any uniform grid. Output is a discrete class label: a 5-class sleep stage (W, N1, N2, N3, R) for EEG/EOG benchmarks or a 4-class ECG rhythm label (N, A, O, ~) for the CinC 2017 task. Because all internal computations are expressed in physical seconds, the model accepts inputs at arbitrary f_s without resampling, padding, or architectural modification.

## Method

NeurOCNN factorizes into three stages. First, a continuous-time convolutional backbone (SplineConv1d) represents each kernel as a natural cubic spline over a compact physical support D with P learnable control points; at inference the spline is sampled at the given f_s to produce L discrete taps, and the convolution is scaled by Δt to form a consistent Riemann-sum approximation of the integral operator, so the same spline parameters yield correct filters at any resolution. Second, a segmented Fourier projection pooling module projects the variable-length time-indexed features onto a truncated real Fourier basis within short windows T_seg, using quadrature-weighted inner products with time weights in seconds, producing a fixed token count K=S·M independent of f_s. Third, a lightweight Transformer encoder with multi-head self-attention mixes tokens, a learnable-query attention pooling step aggregates them into a single vector, and an MLP produces classification logits. The spline parameterization enforces an implicit spectral regularization (C² smoothness limits high-frequency kernel energy), which the authors show empirically prevents the discretization-specific overfitting that plagues MLP-parameterized continuous convolutions (CKConv), yielding a flat accuracy–frequency profile under zero-shot sampling-rate shifts.

## Results

Across five physiological benchmarks (ISRUC, HMC, Sleep-EDF/EOG, and PhysioNet/CinC 2017 ECG), NeurOCNN (319.59K params) achieves 75.22% on ISRUC, 72.88% on HMC, 56.37% on Sleep-EDF (EOG), and 76.08% on ECG rhythm classification. It matches or closely approaches the strongest task-specific baselines (within 0.46 pp of U-Sleep on ISRUC, 0.98 pp on HMC, 0.43 pp of AttnSleep on Sleep-EDF/EOG) while outperforming all neural-operator baselines by 3–14 points. Crucially, NeurOCNN maintains near-flat accuracy across test sampling rates from 80 Hz to 1024 Hz, with worst-case drops under 1 pp, whereas most baselines degrade by 2–10+ pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurOCNN outperforms all four neural-operator baselines (FNO, U-NO, SNO, LocalNO) by large margins—e.g., +4.23 pp over LocalNO+FP on ISRUC and +3.56 pp on HMC—confirming that the spline-kernel backbone is the key differentiator under a shared Fourier pooling interface. Against strong domain-specific sleep models, NeurOCNN is within 0.46 pp of U-Sleep on ISRUC (75.22 vs 75.68) and 0.98 pp on HMC (72.88 vs 73.86), while exhibiting lower fold-to-fold variability (1.04 vs 1.50 pp std on ISRUC); on Sleep-EDF (EOG), AttnSleep edges out NeurOCNN by 0.43 pp (56.80 vs 56.37). On ECG rhythm classification, NeurOCNN achieves the best accuracy (76.08%), surpassing ResNet (75.75%) and PatchTST (68.81%). Against the large pretrained EEG foundation model LaBraM (11.61M params), NeurOCNN wins by 6.51 pp on ISRUC and 4.83 pp on HMC despite having ~36× fewer parameters. Overall, NeurOCNN wins or ties on 4 of 5 datasets and is the only method to simultaneously match state-of-the-art accuracy, outperform all operator baselines, and maintain sub-1-pp worst-case accuracy drops across 80–1024 Hz test sampling rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeurOCNN sits at the intersection of three research threads: (i) neural operators for PDEs (FNO, DeepONet, U-NO, LocalNO), which learn discretization-consistent function-to-function mappings but emphasize global spectral mixing suited to smooth solution operators; (ii) continuous-kernel convolutions (SplineCNN, CKConv), which parameterize filters as continuous functions to accommodate variable resolution; and (iii) Fourier-basis projection for functional data (Rippel et al., Yao et al.). The paper departs from standard neural operators by replacing global spectral mixing with a local, spline-parameterized continuous-time convolution that captures the nonstationary, event-driven morphology of physiological signals, and from MLP-based continuous convolutions (CKConv) by imposing a compact-support C² spline class that provides implicit spectral regularization. The Fourier projection pooling module then bridges the variable-length time-indexed features to a fixed token set in a time-referenced (rather than index-referenced) manner, enabling the operator to be evaluated at arbitrary sampling rates without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in discretization robustness: clinical and research pipelines often encounter the same physiology recorded at different sampling rates, and NeurOCNN's near-flat accuracy-frequency profile (e.g., 75.23% at 200 Hz vs. 75.19% at 1024 Hz on ISRUC) means a single trained model can be deployed across heterogeneous acquisition devices without resampling or retraining. The compact parameter count (319K) matching or exceeding much larger models (U-Sleep at 3.71M, LaBraM at 11.61M) suggests the inductive bias of time-referenced spline convolutions is more important than scale for this task. Key limitations remain: the model cannot natively handle irregularly sampled data or missing modalities, and evaluation is confined to standardized single-site cohorts, leaving multi-center sensor variability unexamined. Future work on adaptive continuous-time interpolation and regression extensions could broaden applicability to other nonstationary sensor domains (acoustic emission, seismic waveforms, industrial telemetry).
