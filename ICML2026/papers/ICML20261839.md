## Problem

Unconstrained bandit linear optimization (uBLO) lacks high-probability regret guarantees, optimal √P_T dynamic regret without prior knowledge of the path-length, and a clear understanding of how comparator-norm adaptivity affects achievable rates. Prior uBLO work implicitly assumed oblivious comparators, and the classic perturbation approach (SCRiBLe) was tightly coupled to a specific OLO subroutine, preventing the use of modern comparator-adaptive algorithms. This paper shows that decoupling the perturbation from the OLO learner yields a clean reduction to standard OLO, unlocking new guarantees across static, dynamic, and high-probability settings.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives only a scalar bandit feedback ⟨ℓ_t, w̃_t⟩ where ‖ℓ_t‖ ≤ G, and must output an unconstrained action w_t ∈ R^d. The algorithm takes as input an arbitrary OLO subroutine (e.g., PFMD or a parameter-free dynamic regret algorithm) and a user-specified risk parameter ε > 0 that caps the regret against the zero comparator. The perturbation matrix H_t is chosen isotropically as (1/(d(‖w_t‖² ∨ ε²)))I_d, so the perturbation scale adapts to the current iterate norm, and the resulting loss estimators are uniformly bounded by 2dG, satisfying the bounded-gradient requirement of comparator-adaptive OLO algorithms.

## Method

PABLO (Perturbation Approach for Bandit Linear Optimization) is a modular reduction: an OLO learner outputs w_t, the algorithm samples a random eigenvector direction s_t of H_t, plays the perturbed action w̃_t = w_t + H_t^{-1/2}s_t, and constructs the unbiased loss estimator ℓ̃_t = dH_t^{1/2}s_t⟨w̃_t, ℓ_t⟩ to feed back to the OLO learner. The key technical insight is that the isotropic choice H_t ≼ (1/(d(‖w_t‖² ∨ ε²)))I_d simultaneously yields an almost-sure bound ‖ℓ̃_t‖² ≤ 4d²‖ℓ_t‖² (enabling bounded-gradient OLO algorithms) and a sharper in-expectation bound E[‖ℓ̃_t‖²|F_{t-1}] ≤ 2d‖ℓ_t‖² (yielding order-optimal rates). This distinction drives a √d separation between oblivious and norm-adaptive comparator regimes. For high-probability guarantees, the paper composes the reduction with composite Huber penalties and optimistic updates (à la Zhang & Cutkosky 2022) to cancel the unbounded ‖w_t‖-dependent concentration terms, achieving the first high-probability static and dynamic regret bounds for uBLO.

## Results

PABLO achieves comparator-adaptive expected static regret of Õ(Gε + (d/κ)·E[‖u‖√(V_T log(d‖u‖Λ_T/(Gε)))]) where κ=√d in the oblivious setting and κ=1 in the norm-adaptive setting (Theorem 3.1). For dynamic regret, PABLO delivers the first √P_T-adaptive guarantee without prior knowledge of P_T, with a refined log-linear path-length P_T^Φ (Theorem 3.3). The first high-probability static regret bound in uBLO scales as Õ(G‖u‖√(dT log(T/δ))) (Theorem 4.3), matching the best known constrained-domain rates up to polylog factors. A self-contained proof establishes the folklore Ω(√(dT)) minimax lower bound on the unit Euclidean ball (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the scale/direction decomposition of van der Hoeven et al. (2020), PABLO with PFMD matches the Õ(‖u‖√(T(d∨log‖u‖))) oblivious static regret rate but crucially maintains √T scaling in the norm-adaptive regime where the prior approach degrades to Õ((dT)^{2/3}). Compared to Rumi et al. (2026), PABLO achieves the stronger √P_T path-length dependence (vs. their √S_T switching-number dependence) and extends to fully-adaptive comparator sequences, whereas their result is restricted to the oblivious setting. For high-probability guarantees, PABLO's static bound Õ(G‖u‖√(dT log(T/δ))) matches the best known constrained-domain rates of Lee et al. (2020) and Zimmert & Lattimore (2022) up to polylogarithmic factors, while being the first such result in the unconstrained setting. The √d gap between oblivious and norm-adaptive comparator regimes (κ=√d vs. κ=1) is a new phenomenon not present in prior uBLO work, which implicitly assumed oblivious comparators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the perturbation-based reduction from Abernethy et al. (2008, SCRiBLe) but decouples the OLO subroutine from the perturbation mechanism, showing that in the unconstrained setting (uBLO) the approach cleanly reduces bandit linear optimization to standard online linear optimization. It composes this reduction with parameter-free mirror descent (Jacobsen & Cutkosky, 2022) for expected-regret guarantees and with composite-penalty optimistic methods (Zhang & Cutkosky, 2022) for high-probability guarantees. A key departure from prior uBLO work (van der Hoeven et al., 2020; Luo et al., 2022; Rumi et al., 2026) is the explicit treatment of data-adaptive comparators, revealing that those works implicitly assumed oblivious comparator norms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers several firsts for uBLO: optimal √P_T dynamic regret without prior knowledge of P_T, the first high-probability static and dynamic regret bounds, and a proof of the folklore Ω(√dT) lower bound on the Euclidean ball. The identification that prior comparator-adaptive guarantees silently rely on oblivious comparators—and that this induces a √d dimension gap—is a conceptual correction that affects how the field frames adaptivity. Major open questions remain: proving matching lower bounds for uBLO, determining whether the √d gap between oblivious and adaptive comparator regimes is intrinsic, and extending the framework to bandit convex optimization.
