## Problem

For general (possibly unbounded) metric losses, it was unknown whether strong universal Bayes-consistency is achievable in the realizable setting, and if so, under what conditions on the hypothesis class. The paper resolves this by showing that natural finiteness conditions (e.g., R* < ∞) are insufficient, and identifies the precise combinatorial obstruction: an infinite non-decreasing (γ_k)-Littlestone tree with γ_k → ∞, where rare mispredictions at diverging loss scales force infinite risk for every learner.

## Input / Output

The learning rule consumes an i.i.d. sample S_n = ((X_i, Y_i))_{i=1}^n from a realizable distribution on Polish spaces (X, ρ) × (Y, ℓ), split into two halves: the first half drives a Gale-Stewart game simulation to produce a terminal history and a countable partition of X, while the second half is used for per-cell MedNet learning. The output is a single predictor f̂_n: X → Y assembled by routing each query x to the MedNet predictor of its cell. No distributional assumptions beyond realizability (inf_{h∈H} R_μ(h) = 0) are required, and the loss ℓ may be unbounded.

## Method

The paper provides a necessary-and-sufficient characterization: realizable strong universal Bayes-consistency holds iff H admits no infinite non-decreasing-(γ_k)-Littlestone tree with γ_k → ∞. The lower bound converts such a tree into a realizable distribution on a random root-to-leaf path, where Borel-Cantelli forces infinite risk at every sample size. The upper bound encodes the tree obstruction as a Gale-Stewart game between an adversary and a learner; absence of the tree guarantees a measurable winning strategy for the learner. Simulating this strategy on the first half of the sample stabilizes almost surely after finitely many rounds, yielding for each x a bounded-diameter set of admissible labels containing the true label. A data-dependent countable partition of X (via a dense sequence in Y) reduces the problem to countably many bounded-loss subproblems, each solved by the MedNet algorithm of Tsir Cohen & Kontorovich (2022), with risks aggregated to give a.s. convergence to zero.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 4.5) provides a necessary-and-sufficient characterization: realizable strong universal Bayes-consistency for general (possibly unbounded) metric losses is achievable if and only if the hypothesis class H admits no infinite non-decreasing-(γ_k)-Littlestone tree with γ_k → ∞. The lower bound (Theorem 5.1) shows that when such a tree exists, every learner suffers infinite risk almost surely for some realizable distribution. The upper bound (Theorem 6.5) constructs an explicit distribution-free learning rule (driving a Gale-Stewart game, partitioning X into countably many bounded-diameter cells, and applying MedNet per cell) whose risk converges to 0 almost surely for every realizable distribution when no such tree exists.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or algorithmic baselines to compare against. The paper's contributions are positioned relative to prior theoretical results: it extends the universal learning framework of Bousquet et al. (2021) from 0-1 classification to general metric losses, generalizes the scaled Littlestone tree characterizations of Attias et al. (2023, 2024) from bounded to unbounded losses, and resolves the realizable-case open problem posed by Tsir Cohen & Kontorovich (2022). The paper also demonstrates that the natural candidate condition R* < ∞ (suggested in Tsir Cohen & Kontorovich, 2022) is insufficient via an explicit counterexample (Section 3), showing that no distribution-free consistent learner can exist under that condition alone. The constructive upper-bound learner composes the MedNet algorithm of Tsir Cohen & Kontorovich (2022) as a per-cell subroutine, making the result complementary rather than competitive with that prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal learning program of Bousquet et al. (2021) — which characterized distribution-free learnability via Gale-Stewart games and Littlestone tree obstructions for 0-1 classification — to the setting of general (possibly unbounded) metric losses. It builds directly on the MedNet algorithm and BIE framework of Tsir Cohen & Kontorovich (2022), resolving their open problem of a necessary-and-sufficient condition for strong Bayes-consistency in the realizable regime. The combinatorial obstruction (an infinite non-decreasing-gap Littlestone tree) is a natural metric-loss generalization of the scaled Littlestone trees introduced by Attias et al. (2023) for regression, but the key new ingredient is that the label gaps must diverge to infinity, capturing the fundamentally different failure mode of unbounded losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete open problem by showing that the natural candidate condition R* < ∞ is insufficient for distribution-free consistency under unbounded metric losses, even in the realizable setting — a counterintuitive result since realizability already forces R* = 0. The sharp characterization via the unbounded-gap Littlestone tree provides a clean combinatorial criterion that practitioners can check for their hypothesis classes. However, the characterization requires a compact-parameterization assumption (compact Θ, continuous evaluation map), and the agnostic extension (where the distribution need not be realizable) remains open, as does the question of whether the compactness assumption can be weakened. The result is primarily of interest to the universal learning theory community rather than to applied ML practitioners.
