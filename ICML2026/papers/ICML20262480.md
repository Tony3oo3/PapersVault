## Problem

Causal discovery in irregularly sampled time series with missing data is fundamentally difficult because imputation errors and structure learning errors reinforce each other: naive imputation introduces spurious correlations that mislead graph recovery, while incorrect graphs produce biased imputations. Existing methods either separate imputation from discovery (losing causal context) or jointly optimize via neural networks without explicit mechanisms to ensure mutual consistency or provide theoretical guarantees for structure recovery under irregular sampling and high missingness.

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete multivariate time series D = {(X^t, M^t)}_{t=1}^T where irregular or asynchronous observations are placed on a sufficiently fine discrete time grid and treated as partially observed values (not modeled as continuous-time trajectories), along with binary observation masks M ∈ {0,1}^{T×d}. It produces a sparse summary causal graph G_S over d variables (with self-loops allowed for autoregressive effects), lag-specific weight matrices W^(τ) for τ = 1,...,L capturing the influence of each variable at each lag, and learned structural functions f_i for each node. The graph is further refined via CAM-based pruning (nonlinear) or coefficient thresholding (linear) to remove spurious edges.

## Method

ReTimeCausal formulates joint imputation and structure learning as an EM-style alternating optimization: the E-step uses three-layer feedforward neural networks as structural functions to compute noise-aware conditional expectations for missing values (injecting noise sampled from the empirical residual distribution to preserve the ANM independence assumption required by downstream pruning), while the M-step performs kernelized sparse regression in an RKHS (RBF kernel approximated via Random Fourier Features) to recover lag-specific causal coefficients, followed by back-projection to input space, L1-regularized thresholding, and CAM-based pruning. Exponential smoothing of weight matrices across iterations stabilizes convergence. The key insight is that structure-aware imputation (using the current causal graph to guide which parents inform each imputation) combined with explicit noise injection breaks the error-reinforcement loop between imputation and discovery, and the paper provides an asymptotic structural consistency guarantee (Proposition 4.1) showing the estimated graph converges to the true graph under MCAR/MAR missingness, finite-order Markov dynamics, causal sufficiency, and faithfulness.

## Results

On synthetic linear-Gaussian time series (10 and 20 variables), ReTimeCausal achieves F1 scores of 1.000 across all missing rates (0%–80%) for 10 variables and 0.976–1.000 for 20 variables, while all baselines drop below 0.44 F1 at 60%+ missingness. On nonlinear TANH-Laplace graphs scaling to 50 variables, ReTimeCausal maintains F1 of 0.816 with SHD of 39, whereas baselines fall to F1 ≤ 0.311. On the real-world CausalRivers dataset (10-node subgraph), ReTimeCausal achieves the highest F1 at both 20% (0.463) and 60% (0.414) missingness, outperforming the best baseline by ~5 percentage points, and attains the lowest SID (8.0) under 60% missingness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReTimeCausal outperforms all five baselines on F1 across every synthetic and real-world setting. On synthetic linear-Gaussian data, the gap is dramatic: ReTimeCausal sustains F1 ≥ 0.930 even at 80% missingness, while PCMCI+TimeMixer (best baseline at 0% missing) collapses to 0.141–0.294, and CUTS+—despite being designed for irregular series—stays below 0.22. On nonlinear TANH-Laplace graphs, ReTimeCausal achieves F1 of 0.909–0.923 (10–20 vars) and 0.816 (50 vars), versus ≤ 0.545 for PCMCI+EM and ≤ 0.352 for CUTS+. On the real-world CausalRivers dataset, ReTimeCausal wins on F1 (0.463 vs. 0.414 for PCMCI+TimeMixer at 20% MR; 0.414 vs. 0.355 for DYNOTEARS* at 60% MR) and on SID at 60% MR (8.0 vs. 13.0 for DYNOTEARS*), but does not achieve the best SHD—PCMCI+TimeMixer attains the lowest SHD (13.0/16.0) at both missing rates. Overall, ReTimeCausal's advantage is most pronounced in edge recovery (F1) and interventional fidelity (SID) under high missingness, while constraint-based methods like PCMCI retain a slight edge in structural Hamming distance on the real-world benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReTimeCausal builds on the classical EM algorithm (Dempster et al., 1977) and Additive Noise Models (Hoyer et al., 2008) to jointly handle imputation and structure learning, departing from the impute-then-discover pipelines (PCMCI, DYNOTEARS with TimeMixer) that treat these as sequential, decoupled steps. It contrasts with CUTS/CUTS+ (Cheng et al., 2023, 2024a), which also jointly optimize imputation and discovery for irregular time series but rely on recurrent neural networks without explicit theoretical guarantees; ReTimeCausal replaces the neural Granger module with kernelized sparse regression (via Random Fourier Features) and CAM-based pruning, yielding interpretable lag-specific graphs. The noise-aware imputation step (Eq. 8) is a novel design choice that addresses a subtle failure mode—deterministic imputation violating the noise-independence assumption required by CAM—absent from prior EM-based causal discovery work like MissDAG (Gao et al., 2022), which handles missing data only in static settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap in risk-sensitive domains (healthcare, finance, climate) where irregular sampling and high missingness are the norm, and provides the first asymptotic consistency guarantee (Proposition 4.1) and finite-sample SHD bound (Theorem G.1) for causal structure recovery under irregular sampling with missing data. Key limitations include the reliance on MCAR/MAR, causal sufficiency, and time-invariant causal directions, all of which may not hold in real-world settings; the authors acknowledge extensions to MNAR, latent confounders, and time-varying effects as open problems. The modest real-world F1 scores (~0.46 on CausalRivers) suggest that the strong synthetic performance does not fully transfer, leaving open questions about robustness under distribution shift and unobserved confounding.
