## Problem

Post-trained LLMs encode knowledge only up to a training cut-off date, necessitating continual adaptation to new document corpora. However, standard fine-tuning for this purpose causes severe catastrophic forgetting of post-training capabilities (instruction-following, math reasoning, coding), and existing mitigations—KL regularization, LoRA, token-adaptive loss, replay—fail to reliably preserve these skills while achieving strong domain adaptation. The paper identifies that next-token prediction as the primary training objective is the root cause, as it systematically reverts the model toward its pre-trained behavior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a post-trained LLM (e.g., QWEN-2.5-7B-INSTRUCT, LLAMA3.1-8B-INSTRUCT) and a corpus of raw multi-sentence documents (news-style passages for KUP, biomedical texts for BioASQ) as input, without requiring QA pairs, factoids, or explicit reward signals. It produces a single updated model that has internalized the document knowledge into its parameters. Evaluation is dual: domain adaptation is measured via multiple-choice QA on the target corpus, while capability preservation is measured across seven orthogonal benchmarks (IFEval, MATH-Hard, HumanEval, BBH, GPQA, MMLU-Pro, MuSR).

## Method

DiSC (Distillation via Split Contexts) reframes knowledge adaptation as a self-distillation problem where both teacher and student originate from the same post-trained policy. For each document, k split points are sampled at sentence boundaries, yielding prefix-suffix pairs; the frozen teacher computes token distributions over each suffix conditioned on its prefix, while the trainable student computes distributions over the same suffixes unconditioned. The training objective is the KL divergence between these two distributions, making behavior preservation the primary loss rather than an auxiliary regularizer. A key efficiency contribution is that all |I| suffix distributions are extracted in a single forward pass per model (the teacher via regular attention, the student via concatenated suffixes with inter-suffix attention masking), reducing the naive 2|I| forward passes to just 2 total. This design avoids the expensive generation step required by prior context-distillation methods like Padmanabhan et al. (2023), since suffixes are naturally extracted from the training documents themselves.

## Results

On KUP (knowledge update), DiSC achieves the highest domain adaptation score among capability-preserving checkpoints across all four models: 44.18 on QWEN-2.5-7B-INSTRUCT (vs. 36.3 for FT^CP and 32.10 for CD base^CP), 44.88 on LLAMA3.1-8B-INSTRUCT (vs. 33.9 and 35.80), 42.27 on QWEN-2.5-3B-INSTRUCT (vs. 38.5 and 41.5), and 37.40 on QWEN3-8B (vs. 32.60 and 28.30). These gains come with minimal forgetting: IFEval drops ≤3 points and Math drops ≤5 points relative to M_post, whereas standard FT degrades IFEval by 10–17 points and Math by 6–25 points. On BioASQ, DiSC^CP similarly leads all baselines (e.g., 81.73 on QWEN-2.5-7B-INSTRUCT vs. 73.08 for FT^CP and 76.92 for CD base^CP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSC consistently outperforms standard fine-tuning (FT) on domain adaptation while incurring far less forgetting: on KUP, DiSC^CP gains 5–12 points over FT^CP in domain score (e.g., 44.18 vs. 36.30 on QWEN-2.5-7B) while limiting IFEval/Math/Code drops to ≤5 points, versus 10–25 point drops for unconstrained FT. Against the closest prior context-distillation method (CD base, Padmanabhan et al. 2023), DiSC wins on domain adaptation in all eight model–dataset settings (e.g., 44.18 vs. 32.10 on QWEN-2.5-7B/KUP; 89.90 vs. 85.10 on QWEN3-8B/BioASQ), with comparable or slightly lower capability retention. Among FT variants, LoRA reduces forgetting most (e.g., IFEval 75.90 vs. 65.83 for FT on QWEN-2.5-7B/KUP) but sacrifices domain performance (35.6 vs. 40.9); TALR and KL regularization show inconsistent gains across models and fail to reliably mitigate forgetting on IFEval, Math, or Code. The +Rephrase baseline (Yang et al., 2024) improves domain adaptation by ~2 points over FT on KUP for two models but causes similar or worse capability degradation and underperforms FT on BioASQ. Overall, no FT variant achieves the joint domain-adaptation-and-preservation trade-off that DiSC delivers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiSC extends the context distillation framework of Snell et al. (2022) and Askell et al. (2021) to the specific problem of continual knowledge adaptation, and most directly builds on Padmanabhan et al. (2023), which also targets knowledge internalization via distillation but requires an expensive explicit generation step to produce suffix continuations. DiSC eliminates this generation step by splitting raw training documents at sentence boundaries, using the document prefix as teacher context and the suffix as the distillation target, with both teacher and student drawn from the same post-trained policy. The paper also positions itself against the dominant fine-tuning paradigm (with KL regularization, LoRA, TALR, on-policy rephrasing) and against RL-based capability adaptation, arguing that KL-as-primary-objective (rather than KL-as-regularizer) is the key distinction that enables behavior preservation during knowledge updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that post-training skills (instruction-following, math, coding) are disproportionately vulnerable to catastrophic forgetting, with a ~0.9 correlation between post-training gain and subsequent forgetting, provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why standard fine-tuning degrades deployed LLMs and suggests that any continual adaptation method must specifically protect post-training distributions. The counterintuitive result that replay data worsens rather than preserves capabilities challenges a long-standing assumption in continual learning, though the evidence is limited to one model and one domain. Open questions include whether DiSC scales to much larger models or multi-domain sequential adaptation, whether it can be combined with RL for capability adaptation, and whether the sentence-boundary splitting requirement generalizes to non-English or non-sentential corpora.
