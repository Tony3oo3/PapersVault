## Problem

Existing token-level SLM-LLM routing methods train routers to imitate the LLM's own inference trajectory, capping collaborative performance at the LLM's accuracy. The paper demonstrates that the collaborative inference space contains substantially better trajectories (e.g., 99% correct-answer coverage vs. 90.2% for the standalone LLM on GSM8K), but learning to discover them is hindered by an exponential search space, autoregressive sequential dependency, and severe credit assignment under sparse final-answer rewards.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt and produces a single autoregressive generated sequence. At each token step, the router consumes the SLM's last hidden state, top-100 logits, and the previous token embedding as a fused state vector to decide whether to invoke the LLM. The SLM is always active for context generation and feature extraction; the LLM is called only at selected tokens, and at deployment the system outputs one routed trajectory (no test-time search or multi-candidate selection).

## Method

AlphaRouter formulates token-level routing as an MDP and decomposes the policy into a DN identifier (binary classifier for SLM-LLM agreement) and a DN router (RL policy for which model to use at divergent points). During training, Collaborative Inference Tree Search (CITS) constructs a budget-constrained tree that branches only at Divergent Nodes, generating counterfactual trajectories by forcing the opposite model choice at selected nodes. Tree-Advantage Policy Optimization (TAPO) then performs bottom-up tree-backup value estimation and computes per-node advantages as the value gap between sibling subtrees, converting sparse trajectory-level rewards into localized credit-assignment signals at each divergence point. The DN router is trained with a PPO-style clipped objective plus adaptive entropy regularization, followed by supervised binary cross-entropy training of the DN identifier on the frozen router's trajectories.

## Results

At the ~2B average-activated-parameters budget, AlphaRouter achieves 90.5% on GSM8K, 86.6% on ARC-Challenge, and 65.9% on HumanEval (pass@1), approaching the standalone Qwen2.5-7B (92.5%, 90.0%, 70.5%) while activating only ~29% of its parameters. In the tighter ~1B regime, AlphaRouter reaches 78.0% on GSM8K with a 6.5% LLM call rate, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art token-level router R2R (71.0%) by 7.0 points at a lower call rate. At the system level, matching 91% GSM8K accuracy, AlphaRouter reduces end-to-end computation by 55.8% (588→260 TFLOPs) and memory traffic by 55.3% (682→305 TB) relative to standalone Qwen-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against static Qwen2.5 checkpoints, AlphaRouter at ~2B activated parameters (90.5% GSM8K) narrows the gap to the 7B model (92.5%) to just 2.0 points while using only ~29% of the parameters, and decisively outperforms the 3B model (86.0%) at roughly one-third the cost. Against query-level routers from RouteLLM (QR-MF, QR-BERT, QR-LLM), AlphaRouter wins by a wide margin at every budget: at the 1B level it achieves 78.0% GSM8K versus 56–58% for all three QR variants, a gap of 20+ points attributable to token-level granularity. Against the strongest token-level baseline R2R, AlphaRouter consistently leads: 78.0% vs. 71.0% on GSM8K at 1B (a 7.0-point gain at a lower call rate of 6.5% vs. 7.7%), and 87.5% vs. 84.0% at 1.5B. On HumanEval, the advantage over R2R is even larger (50.0% vs. 43.2% at 1B), reflecting TAPO's ability to allocate LLM calls to the few tokens that determine code correctness. In the generalization experiment, the router trained on 0.5B+7B transfers to a 0.5B+3B pair and achieves 90.0% GSM8K, exceeding the standalone 3B model (86.0%) by 4.0 points, confirming that collaborative trajectories can surpass the standalone LLM's greedy path.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-level routing (TR) line initiated by Co-LLM, CITER, and R2R, which all train routers to clone or approximate the LLM's own inference trajectory. AlphaRouter departs from this imitation paradigm by framing routing as an MDP and introducing a search-and-iterative-update loop (CITS + TAPO) inspired by AlphaZero, where the router discovers collaborative trajectories that can exceed the LLM's default path. The tree-structured credit assignment mechanism (TAPO) draws on Tree-GRPO and adaptive entropy regularization from SAC, while the policy decomposition into a node identifier and a divergent router is a novel architectural choice specific to the agree/diverge structure of SLM-LLM collaboration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual finding—that the collaborative inference space contains correct trajectories for ~99% of GSM8K problems where the standalone 7B LLM achieves only 90.2%—challenges the assumption that routing can at best match the expert model. Practically, AlphaRouter delivers ~55% reductions in computation and memory traffic at matched accuracy versus the standalone LLM, with a tunable accuracy-cost frontier that speculative decoding does not expose. However, the deployed router still trails the standalone LLM on accuracy (90.5% vs. 92.5% on GSM8K), the router is SLM-specific and not transferable across different small models, and evaluation is limited to greedy decoding with a single model family (Qwen2.5). Open questions include whether the collaborative-space advantage generalizes to stochastic decoding, multi-turn settings, and cross-SLM adaptation.
