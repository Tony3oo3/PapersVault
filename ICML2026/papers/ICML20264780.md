## Problem

Existing prototype-based federated learning methods update global prototypes at the prototype level—either by averaging local prototypes or refining semantic anchors—which inherits representation biases from heterogeneous clients and produces semantically misaligned global signals. This semantic drift is particularly severe in hard domains and under extreme data heterogeneity, where a single averaged prototype cannot adequately capture the diverse class-relevant characteristics across clients.

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for HAM10000) through a shared CNN backbone (ResNet-10 or MobileNetV2) to produce 512-dimensional feature embeddings, which are then classified into one of C classes. Unconventionally, clients upload per-class average gradients (not raw data or prototypes) to the server, which uses them to optimize a set of |I| learnable hyper-prototypes per class via gradient matching. The server broadcasts both the aggregated global model and the optimized hyper-prototypes back to clients for local training.

## Method

FedHPro introduces hyper-prototypes: a set of |I| learnable vectors per class maintained on the server, optimized by minimizing a cosine-dissimilarity loss (L_GM) between their virtual-loss gradients and the aggregated per-class gradients uploaded by clients, effectively simulating centralized prototype optimization without accessing raw data. On the client side, two complementary losses are added to the standard cross-entropy: (1) HPCL, a mutual-contrastive objective with a client-specific margin d_k computed from pairwise local-prototype distances, which pulls each embedding toward its class's hyper-prototypes and away from others to sharpen inter-class boundaries; and (2) HPAL, a dimension-wise smooth L1/L2 hybrid penalty that aligns each embedding with the averaged hyper-prototype of its class to enforce intra-class uniformity. The paper further provides a non-convex convergence guarantee showing the expected gradient norm is bounded by O(1/√(RE)) under standard FL assumptions.

## Results

FedHPro achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all heterogeneous FL scenarios. On CIFAR-10 with label skew (NID1_0.5), it reaches 89.56%, surpassing the strongest baseline FedSA† (87.93%) by 1.63 points. Under extreme quantity skew (CIFAR-10-LT, ρ=100), FedHPro attains 64.75% vs. 62.48% for FedSA†. On the domain-skew Office-Caltech benchmark, FedHPro achieves 64.52% average accuracy, a 3.95-point gain over the next-best FedSA† (60.57%), with the largest per-domain improvement on DSLR (50.33% vs. 45.05%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedHPro consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. The closest competitors are FedSA† (semantic-anchor prototype FL) and FedGMKD (knowledge-distillation prototype FL); on CIFAR-10 NID1_0.5, FedHPro leads FedSA† by 1.63 points (89.56 vs. 87.93) and FedGMKD by 1.47 points. The advantage grows under more extreme heterogeneity: on CIFAR-10-LT with ρ=100, FedHPro gains 2.27 points over FedSA† and 2.64 over FedRCL. On the domain-skew Office-Caltech benchmark, FedHPro achieves a 3.95-point margin over FedSA† (64.52 vs. 60.57) and 4.61 over FedGMKD, with the largest per-domain gap on DSLR (+5.28 points). Standard FedAvg and FedProx lag furthest behind, confirming that prototype-level signals (and especially gradient-matched hyper-prototypes) are critical for handling non-IID data. The plug-and-play experiment (Table 3) further shows that simply replacing conventional prototypes with hyper-prototypes in FedProto, FedTGP, FedGMKD, and FedSA yields 0.71–1.34 point average gains on Digits, isolating the contribution of the hyper-prototype mechanism itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based federated learning paradigm initiated by FedProto (Tan et al., 2022a) and subsequent methods like FedTGP and FedSA, which aggregate or refine global prototypes at the prototype level. The key departure is the introduction of hyper-prototypes: a set of learnable class-wise vectors optimized via gradient matching against class-wise gradients computed from clients' real samples, rather than being formed by averaging local prototypes. This shifts the optimization target from prototype-level descriptors to sample-level semantic characteristics, effectively simulating centralized prototype computation without accessing raw data. The contrastive (HPCL) and alignment (HPAL) modules build on standard contrastive learning objectives but are specifically designed to exploit the multi-vector hyper-prototype structure with client-specific margins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyper-prototypes serve as a plug-and-play component that consistently improves existing prototype-based FL methods (Table 3), suggesting the gradient-matching optimization is a broadly applicable fix for the semantic drift problem in prototype aggregation. The method achieves consistent SOTA across label skew, quantity skew, and domain skew scenarios with moderate computational overhead, and provides a non-convex convergence guarantee. However, the approach still requires clients to upload class-wise gradients (a privacy trade-off beyond standard model parameters), and the evaluation is limited to image classification with relatively small architectures (ResNet-10, MobileNetV2) on datasets up to 200 classes. Open questions include whether gradient matching scales to larger models, more classes, or settings where gradient communication is infeasible.
