## Problem

Current automatic multi-agent system (MAS) design suffers from two critical limitations: orchestration is performed via sequential, code-level execution that prevents global system-level reasoning and scales poorly with agent complexity, and there is no principled framework for understanding when MAS actually outperform single-agent systems. Existing training-time approaches formulate orchestration as a sequence of local decisions, introducing substantial training overhead and encouraging step-wise optimization that cannot capture global coordination patterns.

## Input / Output

The orchestrator receives a task input (question or problem) and a user-configured Degree of MAS (DoM) level—low (at most one sub-agent, no inter-agent topology) or high (unconstrained agent count and topology)—and produces a complete MAS orchestration in a single generation step, expressed as well-formed XML function calls (create_agent and create_flow). The orchestration is then parsed by a deterministic rule-based parser that instantiates sub-agents and executes them; the orchestrator never observes intermediate states, and quality is assessed solely through the final answer. Sub-agents are goal-oriented black boxes (e.g., CoTAgent, DebateAgent, DeepResearchAgent) whose internal reasoning and tool usage are fully abstracted from the orchestrator.

## Method

MAS-Orchestra formulates MAS orchestration as a function-calling reinforcement learning problem with holistic orchestration: the orchestrator generates the entire multi-agent system in one decision step rather than incrementally adding components, enabling global reasoning over system structure. Complex sub-agents are encapsulated as callable functions with only their signatures exposed, so the orchestrator decides when to instantiate agents, which to create, and how to connect them via two primitive functions (create_agent, create_flow) without reproducing internal execution details. Training uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a binary task-level reward on final answer correctness, sampling K orchestrations per input and computing group-normalized advantages. The DoM variable constrains the allowable orchestration space, allowing the same trained policy to operate in both single-agent-delegation and full multi-agent modes. The paper also introduces MASBENCH, a controlled benchmark characterizing tasks along five axes (Depth, Horizon, Breadth, Parallel, Robustness) with corresponding verification protocols, enabling systematic analysis of when and why MAS outperform single-agent baselines.

## Results

MAS-Orchestra achieves Avg@8 accuracy of 66.25 on AIME24, 61.25 on AIME25, 49.00 on HotpotQA, 11.00 on BrowseComp+, and 65.21 on GPQA (OOD), consistently outperforming all standalone agents, inference-time orchestration systems, and training-time orchestration baselines. On the OOD GPQA benchmark (trained only on math data from DeepScaleR), MAS-Orchestra reaches 65.21, surpassing the best standalone agent (DebateAgent at 64.14) and the strongest inference-time baseline AFlow (65.43, within 0.22 points). The method also achieves more than 10× efficiency in LLM calls, tokens, and cost compared to baselines, placing it on the performance-cost Pareto frontier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAS-Orchestra outperforms the strongest inference-time baseline AFlow on AIME24 (66.25 vs. 62.50) and AIME25 (61.25 vs. 53.33), while AFlow narrowly edges it on GPQA (65.43 vs. 65.21, a 0.22-point gap). Against training-time baselines, MAS-Orchestra dominates: it beats MAS-GPT by 7.5 points on AIME24 (66.25 vs. 58.75), 17.9 points on AIME25 (61.25 vs. 43.33), and 1.7 points on GPQA (65.21 vs. 63.51), and outperforms ToolOrchestra by 42.9 points on AIME24 (66.25 vs. 23.33) and 35.4 points on GPQA (65.21 vs. 29.80). MAS-Zero fails to generate any valid MAS with a 7B orchestrator, rendering it non-competitive. Compared to the best standalone agent (DebateAgent), MAS-Orchestra gains 4.2 points on AIME24, 3.8 on AIME25, 12.1 on HotpotQA, 10.2 on BrowseComp+, and 1.1 on GPQA. Notably, MAS-Orchestra also surpasses frontier LLMs used directly as orchestrators (GPT-5, Claude-Sonnet-4.5) across all five benchmarks, demonstrating that learned orchestration with a small 7B model outperforms prompting much larger models to design agent systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in automatic MAS design: inference-time orchestration (AFlow, MAS-Zero, MaAS) that relies on code generation or heuristic search without training, and training-time sequential orchestration (ToolOrchestra, W4S, Puppet) that incrementally adds agents via multi-step RL. MAS-Orchestra introduces a third paradigm—training-time *holistic* orchestration—where the entire multi-agent system is generated in a single function-calling decision step, abstracting complex sub-agents as callable functions rather than executable code. It builds on GRPO for policy optimization and on the function-calling abstraction from tool-use literature, but is the first to combine holistic generation, goal-oriented sub-agent encapsulation, and an explicit Degree-of-MAS (DoM) control knob within a single RL-trained orchestrator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MASBench provides the first controlled, five-axis framework for systematically studying when multi-agent coordination actually helps versus a single agent—a question the field has lacked a principled answer to. The finding that MAS benefits are highly conditional (dependent on task structure, sub-agent capability, and verification protocol) challenges the prevailing assumption that more agents yield more intelligence, and the counterintuitive result that instruction-tuned LLMs outperform reasoning LLMs as orchestrators reframes how one should select orchestrator backbones. Open questions include whether holistic orchestration scales to systems with dozens of heterogeneous sub-agents, whether the robustness gains from MAS persist under more sophisticated adversarial attacks, and whether the 10× efficiency advantage holds as sub-agent complexity (e.g., multi-turn search) grows further.
