## Problem

Extending federated learning to multimodal large language models faces a fundamental tension: heterogeneous client modalities (image, audio, text) induce architectural incompatibility that breaks standard parameter aggregation, while full-parameter fine-tuning of billion-scale models is communication-prohibitive. LoRA alleviates communication costs but introduces aggregation inconsistency—averaging low-rank A and B matrices separately does not recover the true average of local full-rank updates—and existing FedLoRA variants either sacrifice recovery accuracy or inflate communication to transmit residuals.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset of multimodal QA pairs (modality sample + question + answer), where the modality is one of image, audio, or text. The modality sample is encoded by ImageBind into a feature vector, pooled, and projected via a trainable linear layer into the LLM's embedding dimension; this feature is concatenated with the question tokens and template tokens to form the final prompt fed to Vicuna-7B. The model outputs a text answer (token-level cross-entropy loss), while the server produces global LoRA parameters (A and B matrices for both encoder and LLM layers) that are broadcast back to clients—only ~39M trainable parameters (~1% of the 8.2B total) are communicated per round.

## Method

UniFLoW inserts LoRA adapters into both the ImageBind modality encoder and the Vicuna-7B base LLM, and employs a two-stage training schedule: Stage I (steps < τ) fine-tunes only encoder LoRA with the LLM frozen to absorb modality-specific biases, then Stage II (steps ≥ τ) fine-tunes only LLM LoRA with encoders frozen to learn content-level semantics. The core aggregation module, FedA²-LoRA, exploits the proven observation that A matrices encode more global, data-independent directions than B matrices: it aggregates A via simple averaging across clients, computes the target full-rank update U = (1/K)Σ B_k A_k, and then analytically recovers the global B by solving a regularized least-squares (ridge regression) problem min_B ‖BA − U‖²_F + λ‖B‖²_F, yielding a closed-form solution B = UA^T(AA^T + λI)^{-1}. This achieves faithful reconstruction of the true global update ΔW* at the same per-round communication cost as standard LoRA (0.78M parameters), avoiding the residual-transmission overhead of FedEx-LoRA while eliminating the aggregation bias of naive averaging.

## Results

On the multimodal QA benchmark (HeySQuAD + PandaGPT) with both image and audio clients participating, FedA²-LoRA (II stage) achieves 76.19 Acc and 85.69 F_BERT on Image, and 60.90 Acc and 86.31 F_BERT on Audio, outperforming all baselines. On the GLUE benchmark (MNLI, RTE, SST-2, QQP), FedA²-LoRA achieves 90.50 average accuracy with LoRA, 90.68 with rsLoRA, and 88.02 with VeRA, consistently surpassing the corresponding non-analytical baselines. The method maintains the same per-round communication cost as standard LoRA (0.78M parameters) while achieving these gains, unlike FedEx-LoRA which requires 25.74M parameters per round.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedA²-LoRA (II stage) consistently outperforms all five baselines on the multimodal QA task. Against standard LoRA (II stage), it gains +9.98 on Image Acc (76.19 vs 66.21) and +0.89 on Audio F_BERT (86.31 vs 85.42). Compared to FFA-LoRA (II stage), it improves Image Acc by +5.61 (76.19 vs 70.58) and Audio F_BERT by +0.49. Versus FedSA-LoRA (II stage), the gains are +7.77 on Image Acc and +2.09 on Audio F_BERT. Against FedEx-LoRA (II stage), which transmits full residual matrices at 25.74M parameters per round, FedA²-LoRA achieves +6.79 on Image Acc and +1.71 on Audio F_BERT while communicating only 0.78M parameters per round. On the GLUE benchmark, FedA²-LoRA (90.50 avg) beats FedDPA-LoRA (89.41), FFA-LoRA (89.14), and standard LoRA (89.17), confirming the analytical aggregation generalizes beyond multimodal settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as the parameter-efficient fine-tuning backbone and on FedSA-LoRA (Guo et al., 2024), which established that the A matrix captures more global, data-agnostic directions than B. It departs from prior FedLoRA variants (FFA-LoRA, FedSA-LoRA, FedEx-LoRA) that either sacrifice one matrix to reduce communication or transmit full residuals at high cost, by proposing an analytical ridge-regression recovery of B from the averaged A and the target update U. The UniFLoW framework itself extends multimodal federated learning beyond the single-task, small-model setting of prior work (FedCola, MLLM-LLaVA-FL) to a unified, extensible architecture handling heterogeneous modality clients with a shared LLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As public multimodal data for training MLLMs becomes increasingly scarce, a practical federated framework that handles heterogeneous client modalities without prohibitive communication costs is timely. FedA2-LoRA's closed-form B recovery is attractive because it matches standard LoRA's communication budget (0.78M params/round) while eliminating the aggregation inconsistency that plagues naive averaging. However, the multimodal experiments are limited to 10 communication rounds and 2,000 samples per client, and the framework's scalability to more than three modalities or to truly large-scale deployments (hundreds of clients, billions of parameters) remains untested. Open questions include whether the analytical aggregation generalizes to other PEFT methods (e.g., VeRA, rsLoRA beyond the GLUE experiments) and how the two-stage training schedule interacts with asynchronous or partial-participation FL protocols.
