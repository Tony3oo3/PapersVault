## Problem

Autoregressive unified multimodal models generate images by sequentially predicting discrete visual tokens from a codebook, yet the safety of such token-based generation has not been systematically studied. Existing safety methods for image generation operate in continuous latent spaces (e.g., diffusion models) and cannot generalize to the discretized codebook representations used by autoregressive models. This paper asks: how can we iteratively eliminate harmful mappings from the codebook using only the model's own generation and understanding capabilities, without external annotation or human labeling?

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (both harmful and harmless) fed into a unified autoregressive multimodal model, which simultaneously generates images and judges their safety to produce paired harmful/safe image-text data. The output is a modified codebook W^safe — a set of discrete embedding vectors of dimension D per token — that replaces the original codebook at inference time, causing the same prompts to yield safe images. The safe codebook is model-agnostic and can be swapped in across different unified architectures (Janus, VILA-U, Emu3, LlamaGen, OmniMamba) without retraining the backbone.

## Method

The core mechanism is an iterative two-step loop operating on the model's VQ codebook. Step 1 (harmful-space projection): the unified model generates images from harmful prompts, self-judges which outputs are unsafe, and constructs semantically paired safe/harmful prompt pairs; embedding features of the K generated tokens are extracted from the codebook for each pair, their difference matrix E is averaged, and SVD yields a top-k harmful subspace P onto which the codebook is projected away (W^proj = W^ori(I − λ·Proj_P)). Step 2 (null-space fine-tuning): a perturbation Δ is optimized via cross-entropy on safe image-text pairs, but after every gradient update Δ is projected through the null-space matrix Q (built from near-zero singular vectors of E), guaranteeing no harmful information is reintroduced while recovering visual quality. The two steps repeat until the detected harmful rate saturates, and for multiple harmful concepts the singular directions are concatenated to form an expanded harmful space.

## Results

On the I2P benchmark (Janus-Pro 1B), Safe-CB reduces harmful content detection rates across all seven categories, e.g., Sexual from 0.12 to 0.04, Violence from 0.39 to 0.21, and Shocking from 0.53 to 0.32, while FID_g increases only marginally (68.83→70.66). The method generalizes across six unified multimodal models (Janus-Pro 1B/7B, VILA-U, Emu3, LlamaGen, OmniMamba), consistently halving or better reducing harmful detection rates with FID_g degradation under 2 points. Semantic fidelity on benign COCO prompts is well preserved after the null-space fine-tuning step: CLIP-Score recovers from 26.11 (hard projection only) to 30.19 (vs. 30.48 baseline), and TIFA from 0.7521 to 0.7894 (vs. 0.7925 baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the 'Safe-Model' approach adapted from Yang et al. (2025, NullU), which modifies model weights directly to suppress harmful outputs. On the I2P sexual category, Safe-CB achieves a detection rate of 0.0440 versus Safe-Model's 0.0746 (baseline 0.1278), a 41% relative reduction over the weight-modification method. More critically, Safe-Model severely degrades generation quality: FID_g jumps from 68.83 (baseline) to 92.13, while Safe-CB keeps it at 70.66. On the Geneval image-generation benchmark, Safe-Model drops from 0.64 to 0.54, whereas Safe-CB retains 0.62; on MMMU understanding, Safe-Model falls from 29.1 to 26.7 while Safe-CB holds at 29.0. Internally, the paper also shows that iterative self-improvement (3 turns with 300 pairs) outperforms single-turn removal with the same 300 pairs (e.g., Sexual: 0.0405 vs. 0.0472; Violence: 0.2047 vs. 0.2345), and that 2 iterations with 200 pairs already match 1 iteration with 300 pairs, demonstrating data efficiency of the iterative scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of autoregressive image generation safety and self-improving LLM techniques. It departs from the dominant line of diffusion-based safety work (Safe Latent Diffusion, SAFREE, concept erasure) that operates in continuous latent spaces, instead targeting the discrete codebook representations of unified multimodal models like Janus and VILA-U. The iterative self-improvement loop borrows the self-refinement paradigm from LLM literature (Self-Refine, recursive introspection) but repurposes it for visual safety: the unified model's own image-understanding capability replaces human annotation for identifying harmful outputs. The null-space projection mechanism for fine-tuning draws on Adam-NSCL and AlphaEdit, but is applied here to a quantized visual codebook rather than continuous model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of image generation safety in autoregressive unified multimodal models, a rapidly growing model family that lacks the safety tooling available for diffusion models. The codebook-modification approach is practically attractive because it is lightweight (no full model retraining), composable across harmful concepts, and preserves generation quality with minimal FID degradation. Key limitations include self-labeling error propagation (the model judges its own outputs), diminishing returns after ~3 iterations, and degraded performance on subtle or ambiguous harmful concepts without human annotation. Open questions include extending safety to joint text-image generation, where each modality may be individually benign but jointly misleading.
