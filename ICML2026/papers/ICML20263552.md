## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization solvers are trained on a single problem type and distribution, failing to generalize across diverse COPs or out-of-distribution instances. Prior meta-learning approaches for CO (DIMES, Meta-EGN) define meta-tasks at the instance level within a single problem class, departing from the original multi-task meta-learning principle, and rely on RL or unsupervised learning paradigms that limit scalability and backbone compatibility. Additionally, no standardized multi-distribution benchmark exists to rigorously evaluate cross-task and cross-distribution generalizability of NCO methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes graph instances in two families: node-oriented problems (MIS, MCl, MCut) specified by adjacency matrices, and edge-oriented problems (TSP, ATSP) specified by distance/cost matrices (with optional node coordinates for Euclidean TSP). The model outputs an N-dimensional probability heatmap H ∈ [0,1]^N, where N equals |V| for node problems and |V|² for edge problems, which is then decoded into a binary solution vector x ∈ {0,1}^N via a task-specific greedy decoder. The diffusion-based generation operates on the solution space as an N-dimensional Bernoulli distribution, with the noising trajectory parameterized by a double-stochastic transition matrix.

## Method

M²GenCO formulates meta-learning tasks as distinct COP types (e.g., MIS, MCl, MCut, TSP, ATSP) rather than instances of a single problem, performing multi-task meta-pretraining via a bi-level inner-outer optimization where the inner loop adapts to one task batch per step and the outer loop updates the shared initializer with gradient normalization across tasks. The generative backbone is a lightweight GCN (6 layers, 64 hidden dims) with task-specific and shared embedders, trained with a discrete diffusion consistency model (following Fast-T2T) that enforces self-consistency between two noised points on the same trajectory using binary cross-entropy loss. After meta-pretraining, the model undergoes few-shot supervised finetuning on OOD support sets (5:1 support-query ratio) to adapt to specific distributions, with an unsupervised energy loss used for MCut due to its unconstrained partition nature. This design achieves 95.6× inference speedup over prior neural methods while consuming up to 82% less GPU memory and 91% less training time compared to mainstream diffusion-based CO solvers.

## Results

M²GenCO with a greedy decoder achieves an average 9.16% optimality gap improvement over per-dataset SOTA neural methods across 38 test datasets, while reducing per-instance solving time from ~4.5s to ~0.05s (a 95.6× speedup). On edge-oriented tasks (TSP/ATSP), it reaches gaps as low as 0.95% (TSP-Gaussian-50) and 2.95% (TSP-Gaussian-500) in 0.01–0.35s, and on node-oriented tasks (MIS/MCl/MCut) it achieves gaps of 2.30–3.80% in 0.01s. With post-inference local search (MCTS/energy sampling), it attains near-optimal gaps (≤0.25% on TSP, 0.00% on MCl) while maintaining sub-second inference. Training requires <15 GB GPU memory and ~48h on a single GPU, saving up to 82% computational resources and 91% training time versus prior diffusion-based CO solvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against meta-learning baselines, M²GenCO outperforms Meta-EGN by an average 71.9% on 22 node-oriented datasets and DIMES by 23.9% on 14 edge-based datasets, largely because it uses supervised diffusion training rather than RL/UL. Against multi-task frameworks, it beats GOAL by 19.2% mean gap reduction with a 104.3× speedup (0.35s vs 5.21s on TSP-500), and UniCO by 94.5% on ATSP (e.g., 2.31 vs 60.19 objective on ATSP-SAT-507). Against diffusion-based single-task solvers, DIFUSCO and Fast-T2T suffer 34.4% and 50.5% mean performance degradation on OOD benchmarks respectively, while M²GenCO is 29.6× and 14.3× faster due to its lighter backbone (6 GCN layers, 64 hidden dims vs 12 layers, 256 dims). On in-distribution tasks (Table 5), M²GenCO matches or slightly exceeds DIMES and Meta-EGN (e.g., TSP-uni: 5.73 vs 6.32; MIS-RB: 17.54 vs 16.40) while being 2–5× faster at inference. The method does not beat exact/heuristic solvers (Concorde, LKH3, KaMIS, Gurobi) on optimality, but achieves competitive gaps (≤3%) at 10–1000× lower solving time.

## Relation to Prior Work

M²GenCO repurposes the MAML bi-level optimization (Finn et al., 2017) by redefining meta-tasks from instances of a single COP to distinct problem types (TSP, ATSP, MIS, MCl, MCut), departing from DIMES and Meta-EGN which treat instance-level variation within one problem class as the meta-task. For the generative backbone, it adopts the discrete diffusion + consistency model paradigm from Fast-T2T and DIFUSCO but wraps it in a multi-task meta-pretraining loop with gradient normalization across tasks, replacing the RL or unsupervised energy-based training used by prior meta-CO solvers. The lightweight GCN backbone with task-specific embedders and shared convolution blocks extends the architecture of GOAL and GCNCO, while the few-shot finetuning stage follows the standard MAML support-query protocol applied here for the first time to cross-distribution COP adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 38-dataset multi-distribution benchmark (spanning 5 COPs across ER, HK, WS, Gaussian, Cluster, HCP, and SAT distributions) fills a genuine gap in NCO evaluation, where prior work tested almost exclusively on uniform in-distribution instances. The practical efficiency gains—95.6× inference speedup and ~91% reduction in training time versus comparable diffusion solvers—make generative CO solving more accessible for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the meta-learning component provides meaningful benefit over plain multi-task training at scale (the ablation in Fig. 2 suggests synergy but the margin is modest), whether the framework extends beyond the 5 evaluated problem types, and how it behaves on much larger instances (>1000 nodes) where the current greedy decoder may be insufficient.
