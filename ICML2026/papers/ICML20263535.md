## Problem

Nonlinear Transform Coding (NTC), the dominant paradigm in neural compression, suffers from three fundamental limitations: a train-test mismatch caused by non-differentiable hard quantization, a smoothness bias in continuous transforms that prevents optimal rate-distortion performance on sources with sharp discontinuities, and a loss of shaping gain because NTC is restricted to scalar quantization. This paper asks whether a discrete binary latent space, combined with fast channel simulation, can overcome all three limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263535/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder maps a single source realization X (scalar or low-dimensional vector) to L Bernoulli parameters in (-1,1)^L, capping the rate at L bits per sample. Operationally, outputs from N/L independent source realizations are concatenated and processed through a block of N = 2^23 binary channels, producing a single compressed bit string via arithmetic coding of XOR differences. In the distributed compression setting, side information Y is available only at the decoder. The decoder reconstructs X̂ from the simulated binary latents Z ∈ {0,1}^L, with the key constraint that unlike NTC (where latent dimensionality does not limit rate), SBC's rate is hard-bounded by L.

## Method

SBC replaces NTC's deterministic quantize-and-entropy-code pipeline with a stochastic binary latent space and a fast channel simulation scheme. The encoder f_θ outputs Bernoulli parameters, and the actual compression is performed by a generalized PolarSim: a permuted polar transform (extending Arikan's source polarization to non-stationary, non-uniform sources) polarizes the binary subchannels, after which each subchannel is simulated via a simple threshold-on-common-randomness scheme, with only the XOR difference between source-conditioned and source-free samples transmitted. The paper proves first-order rate optimality of this generalized scheme via a new non-stationary source polarization theorem. For end-to-end training, the VarGrad estimator provides unbiased, low-variance gradients through the discrete latent space by exploiting the stochasticity of the Bernoulli encoder, and a decaying L2 regularization on encoder outputs mitigates initialization sensitivity.

## Results

SBC achieves state-of-the-art rate-distortion performance across all evaluated information-theoretic sources, using a block length of N = 2^23 ≈ 8.4×10^6 binary channels. On the i.i.d. Gaussian source, SBC (L=32) outperforms Trellis Coded Quantization (TCQ, 256 states) and the Leech-lattice-based ECLQ scheme, while NTC (L=1) is limited by the 1.53 dB space-filling loss inherent to entropy-coded scalar quantization. On the i.i.d. uniform source, SBC reproduces the known optimal R-D points matched by TCQ, whereas NTC exhibits a visible rate penalty due to smoothness bias in its entropy model. On the circle and ramp sources, SBC avoids the branch-cut and discontinuity failures that limit NTC, and on Wyner-Ziv distributed compression, SBC (L=32) outperforms both the classical DISCUS scheme and recent learning-based binning approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the NTC baseline (Ballé et al., 2017), SBC wins on every source: it avoids the smoothness bias that causes NTC to fail on the circle (branch cut) and ramp (sharp discontinuity) sources, and it eliminates the train-test mismatch from hard quantization. On the i.i.d. Gaussian source, SBC surpasses TCQ (Taubman & Marcellin, 2013, 256 states) and the Leech-lattice ECLQ scheme (Lei et al., 2025), setting a new state-of-the-art without explicit vector quantization; on the i.i.d. uniform source, SBC matches TCQ's known optimal points while also achieving intermediate rate points that fixed-rate TCQ cannot. For distributed compression, SBC outperforms the classical DISCUS scheme (Pradhan & Ramchandran, 2003) — which was specifically designed for the Gaussian side-information source — as well as the learning-based Wyner-Ziv compressors of Ozyilkan et al. (2023, 2024b), which recover binning behavior but still rely on NTC's scalar quantization framework. In summary, SBC wins or ties on all six comparisons, with the most dramatic gains on sources requiring discontinuous encoder/decoder/prior mappings and on the Gaussian source where shaping gains are most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBC departs fundamentally from the dominant Nonlinear Transform Coding (NTC) paradigm, which maps sources to real-valued latents and applies scalar quantization followed by entropy coding. Instead, it restricts the latent space to binary variables and replaces quantization with fast channel simulation, building on PolarSim (Sriramu et al., 2024) for the simulation mechanism and VarGrad (Richter et al., 2020) for gradient estimation through the discrete latent. The theoretical core extends Arikan's source polarization theorem to the non-stationary, non-identically-distributed setting, proving first-order rate optimality of the generalized scheme. This positions SBC as a bridge between information-theoretic channel simulation theory and learned compression, rather than an incremental improvement within the NTC framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBC simultaneously addresses three well-identified limitations of NTC (train-test mismatch, smoothness bias, and absence of shaping gain) with a single architectural choice—binary stochastic latents—making it a conceptually clean alternative paradigm. The rate is capped at L bits per source sample, which limits applicability to high-rate regimes and high-dimensional sources like images, so the practical impact on mainstream compression remains unproven. Open questions include whether the approach scales to image/video compression, whether the initialization sensitivity can be eliminated, and whether the simple factorized Bernoulli prior suffices for complex sources or whether richer entropy models are needed. The theoretical guarantee of asymptotic rate optimality for the generalized channel simulation is a solid contribution to the channel simulation literature independent of the neural compression application.
