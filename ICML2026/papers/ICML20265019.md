## Problem

Neural simulators are bottlenecked by the prohibitive cost of generating high-fidelity physics labels, where a single industrial-fidelity sample can require tens of thousands of CPU-hours. Pre-training on abundant off-the-shelf 3D geometries is a natural alternative, but geometry-only (native) supervision ignores dynamics and empirically degrades downstream physics performance. The paper addresses how to construct a self-supervised pre-training signal from geometry alone that still captures the geometry-dynamics coupling essential for physics simulation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 3D geometry mesh (normalized to unit scale, discretized into 32,768 volume points and 4,096 surface points), a per-point velocity field V (randomly sampled from a bounded ball during pre-training, or configured to encode task-specific simulation settings like flow direction/speed or impact angle during fine-tuning), and query positions x. During pre-training, the output is a sequence of vector-distance features along a synthetic particle trajectory (3 timesteps); during fine-tuning, the output is the physics solution field (pressure, velocity, or Von Mises stress) at each query point. The velocity field serves as a unified 'prompt' that activates different learned correlation patterns for different physics domains.

## Method

GeoPT introduces dynamics-lifted geometric pre-training: rather than predicting static geometric features (native pre-training), the model is trained to predict how geometric features (vector distance) evolve along synthetic particle trajectories generated by randomly sampled per-point velocity fields, with trajectories halting at geometry boundaries. This lifts the supervision from the native geometry space G to a joint geometry-dynamics space (G, V), enabling the model to learn geometry-dynamics coupled representations without any physics labels. The backbone is Transolver (a Transformer-based neural solver with 32 state tokens), though the method is architecture-agnostic. Pre-training uses 1.3M samples from ~13K ShapeNet geometries (cars, airplanes, watercraft) with 100 random dynamics fields each, computed via optimized ray-triangle intersection. Fine-tuning simply replaces the random velocity with a task-specific V_S encoding the simulation settings, and the model is trained on ~100 solver-generated labels per benchmark. Theoretically, the trajectory supervision corresponds to solving a collisionless transport equation with sticking boundary conditions, instilling a mass-conservation inductive bias that generalizes across fluid, solid, and radiative physics.

## Results

GeoPT consistently improves relative L2 error across all five industrial-fidelity benchmarks compared to training from scratch: DrivAerML (0.0746 vs. 0.0927), NASA-CRM (0.0880 vs. 0.0996), AirCraft (0.0904 vs. 0.1104), DTCHull (0.1459 vs. 0.1814), and Car-Crash (0.1772 vs. 0.1977) at the base 8-layer model. The method reduces labeled physics data requirements by 20–60% while matching full-data performance, and accelerates convergence by up to 2×. Scaling to 32 layers further improves GeoPT (e.g., DrivAerML 0.0687, AirCraft 0.0836) while from-scratch training plateaus or degrades, confirming that pre-training regularizes the hypothesis space against overfitting in limited-data regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against backbone alternatives, GeoPT-Transolver outperforms Galerkin Transformer (0.0746 vs. 0.127 on DrivAerML) and GNOT (0.0746 vs. 0.152) by large margins; applying GeoPT pre-training to these weaker backbones also yields substantial gains (Galerkin drops to 0.083, GNOT to 0.116), confirming the pre-training method is architecture-agnostic. Against the geometry-conditioning baseline using frozen Hunyuan3D VAE tokens, GeoPT pre-training consistently outperforms, as static geometry representations fail to capture dynamics-aware correlations needed for physics. In aligned external comparisons (Table 5), GeoPT achieves the best MSE on DrivAerML (0.003370 vs. GAOT's 0.051729) and NASA-CRM (0.010722 vs. GAOT's 0.077170), and the best relative L2 on AirCraft (0.062 vs. Transolver++'s 0.064), establishing state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. Geometry-only native pre-training (predicting vector distance or SDF) actually degrades performance below training from scratch, validating the paper's core claim that dynamics-lifting is essential to avoid negative transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural simulator literature (FNO, Transolver, GNOT) by introducing a self-supervised pre-training paradigm that operates solely on unlabeled 3D geometry, departing from prior physics foundation models (Poseidon, DPOT, Unisolver, P3D) that still require expensive simulation data for pre-training. It also diverges from 3D geometry pre-training work (Point-BERT, Hunyuan3D) that uses frozen geometric encoders as auxiliary features, instead directly pre-training the physics backbone itself. The key conceptual departure is the 'dynamics-lifted' supervision: rather than learning within the native geometry space (which the paper shows actively harms downstream performance), it augments geometry with randomly sampled velocity fields to create supervision in the joint geometry-dynamics space that matches the downstream task's input structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the fundamental scaling bottleneck for neural simulators—each industrial-fidelity training sample can cost 61,000 CPU-hours to generate—by showing that pre-training on one million solver-free geometric-walk samples reduces labeled data requirements by 20–60% and accelerates convergence by 2× across five industrial benchmarks. The negative result that geometry-only pre-training degrades performance (rather than merely failing to help) is a useful caution for the field, and the transport-equation interpretation provides a principled justification for why the lifting works. Open questions include whether the velocity parameterization generalizes to strongly nonlinear or multi-physics regimes, whether the approach scales to time-dependent simulations beyond the brief DTCHull transient test, and whether the ShapeNet-to-industrial-geometry gap can be further closed with generative 3D assets.
