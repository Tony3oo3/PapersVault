## Problem

The conventional Min-Max formula for initializing uniform quantization parameters imposes two overlooked constraints: both the scale and zero-point are derived from extreme values (limiting optimization flexibility), and the zero-point is restricted to a k-bit unsigned integer (shrinking the parameter space). These constraints become especially harmful at low bit-widths (2-bit, 3-bit), where prior PTQ methods still rely on Min-Max or its variants for initialization, leaving a significant performance gap unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Given a weight matrix row w, a proxy Hessian H computed from calibration activations (128 C4 samples), and a target bit-width k, NeUQI produces a scale s and a floating-point zero-point z that minimize the Hessian-weighted quantization error. Unlike conventional formulations where z must be a k-bit unsigned integer, NeUQI allows z to take any real value, which increases the average bit-width by less than 0.01 bits but yields substantial accuracy gains. The quantized weights are then used directly in inference with no additional runtime overhead, and hardware support for floating-point zero-points is available in libraries such as BitBLAS integrated into vLLM.

## Method

NeUQI exploits the piecewise-quadratic structure of the Hessian-weighted quantization loss under a diagonal approximation. For a fixed scale s, the loss as a function of the zero-point z is a sum of piecewise quadratic terms with known transition points; NeUQI finds the global minimum via a sweep-line algorithm that incrementally updates the quadratic function across sorted transition points, achieving O(n log n) complexity after a transition-point reduction that caps each sample to two breakpoints. This explicit zero-point solution reduces the joint (s, z) optimization to a single-variable search over s, which is solved by a coarse-to-fine strategy: a coarse pass over T_c = O(√T) candidate scales localizes the optimum, followed by a fine-grained search in a small neighborhood, reducing total zero-point evaluations from T to O(√T). The key departure from Min-Max is that neither s nor z is tied to the weight extremes, and z is free to be non-integer, enabling the optimizer to find configurations that the Min-Max formula structurally cannot reach.

## Results

Under the most challenging 2-bit channel-wise quantization, NeUQI achieves C4 perplexity of 17.50 and average accuracy of 47.24% on LLaMA 2 7B, compared to GPTQ (2592 / 35.08%), GPTAQ (246 / 34.97%), and MagR (47.55 / 39.54%). On LLaMA 3 8B, where GPTQ and GPTAQ completely collapse (perplexity >1e4), NeUQI still achieves C4 perplexity of 39.41 and 47.30% accuracy. At the largest scale (Qwen 2.5 72B), NeUQI reaches C4 perplexity 11.36 and 67.21% accuracy, outperforming MagR (13.83 / 55.00%) by a wide margin. With lightweight distillation, NeUQI surpasses PV-tuning (C4 9.81 vs 10.78, Acc 56.77% vs 52.17%) using only ~0.5M tokens versus PV-tuning's ~1B tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeUQI dominates all three weight-only PTQ baselines (GPTQ, GPTAQ, MagR) across every model family and size at 2-bit channel-wise quantization. The gap is most dramatic on LLaMA 2 7B, where NeUQI reduces C4 perplexity from 2592 (GPTQ) to 17.50, and on LLaMA 3 8B/70B where GPTQ and GPTAQ completely fail (perplexity >1e4) while NeUQI remains functional. MagR is the strongest baseline on Qwen 2.5 (e.g., C4 13.83 on 72B) but still trails NeUQI (11.36) by ~18% in perplexity and 12.2 points in accuracy. Against search-based initialization LeanQuant (Table 4), NeUQI wins on LLaMA 2 7B (C4 17.50 vs 93.58) and 70B (C4 10.49 vs 13.16), though LeanQuant is competitive on 13B. With lightweight distillation, NeUQI surpasses both PV-tuning (C4 9.81 vs 10.78, Acc 56.77% vs 52.17%) and OmniQuant (C4 9.81 vs 16.34) using ~2000× fewer tokens than PV-tuning. When integrated into EfficientQAT, NeUQI initialization further improves Wiki2 perplexity from 7.19 to 6.81 (LLaMA 2 7B) and from 9.80 to 9.14 (LLaMA 3 8B), confirming that better initialization benefits even strong fine-tuning methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GPTQ's quantization loss formulation and sequential calibration framework, but departs fundamentally from the Min-Max initialization formula that underlies virtually all uniform quantization methods (GPTQ, GPTAQ, MagR, LeanQuant). The key conceptual departure is identifying two previously overlooked constraints in Min-Max—extreme-value-based parameter determination and the integer zero-point requirement—and showing that relaxing both enables a near-optimal, single-variable scale optimization via piecewise quadratic analysis. The method is positioned as a drop-in initialization that composes with transformation-based techniques (Hadamard, QuIP) and fine-tuning approaches (PV-tuning, EfficientQAT), rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple initialization improvement (removing two constraints from Min-Max) outperforms resource-intensive fine-tuning methods like PV-tuning (~1B tokens) using only ~0.5M tokens of lightweight distillation challenges the field's assumption that complex optimization is necessary to recover quantization loss. The result that 3-bit channel-wise quantized models with NeUQI surpass smaller non-quantized models at comparable memory budgets (Table 8) has direct practical implications for deploying large LLMs on consumer hardware. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-uniform or mixed-precision quantization schemes, and the authors note that fine-tuning strategies designed to go beyond the first Min-Max constraint remain unexplored.
