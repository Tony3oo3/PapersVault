## Problem

Existing quantization schemes in neural audio codecs face a fundamental trade-off: VQ and RVQ suffer from codebook underutilization and optimization instability as codebook size grows, while FSQ guarantees full utilization but quantizes each latent channel independently in 1D, ignoring inter-channel correlations. The paper asks whether one can capture correlations between feature dimensions without reintroducing the instability and inefficiency of high-dimensional vector quantization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264154/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 24 kHz audio waveform, encodes it into latent frames, and projects each frame into an even number d of dimensions (typically 6) bounded to [-1,1] via tanh. These dimensions are reshaped into d/2 pairs, each snapped to the nearest point on a fixed 2D geometric grid, yielding a single stream of discrete tokens at rates of 53, 166, or 333 tokens/s. A decoder then upsamples the quantized pairs back to a reconstructed waveform. The entire pipeline is built on the WavTokenizer encoder-decoder framework, with only the quantization layer replaced.

## Method

Q2D2 replaces the learned codebook of VQ/RVQ with fixed analytic 2D grids (hexagonal, rhombic, or rectangular tilings) over which feature pairs are jointly quantized via nearest-neighbor lookup. Each pair's coordinates are first bounded to [-l_i/2, l_i/2] and then snapped to the nearest grid point, with gradients propagated through a straight-through estimator; no commitment loss, EMA, or codebook reinitialization is required. The implicit codebook size is the product of per-pair grid levels (e.g., 7×7×7×7×7×7 ≈ 117,649 for six dimensions), comparable to conventional VQ codebooks but with zero learned parameters. Lightweight linear projections map encoder features into and out of the quantization space, and the tanh nonlinearity before bounding is critical for stable training. Among the three tiling geometries, the rhombic grid consistently outperforms hexagonal and rectangular grids due to its higher packing efficiency, which yields more isotropic neighbor distances and lower quantization error for a fixed number of levels.

## Results

Q2D2 with a rhombic grid at 3.3 kbps (166 tokens, single quantizer) achieves UTMOS 4.0613 on LibriTTS test-clean, surpassing all SOTA models in the 0.5–9 kbps range, including DAC at 9 kbps (3.9097) and HiFi-Codec at 3 kbps (3.9035). At 6.9 kbps (333 tokens), Q2D2 reaches UTMOS 4.0321, outperforming models using 600 tokens across UTMOS, PESQ, STOI, and V/UV F1. In subjective MUSHRA evaluation, Q2D2 at 3.3 kbps scores 98.05±2.25, exceeding DAC at 9 kbps (92.64±3.83) and Encodec at 6 kbps (94.41±4.99). At the ultra-low 1 kbps setting (75 tokens), Q2D2 outperforms DAC (100 tokens) on all metrics and beats WavTokenizer (75 tokens) on PESQ (2.5091 vs 2.3730) and STOI (0.9217 vs 0.9139).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q2D2 consistently outperforms all multi-quantizer baselines (DAC, Encodec, Vocos, SpeechTokenizer, HiFi-Codec) at matched or lower token rates: at 3.3 kbps with only 166 tokens it beats DAC at 9 kbps (900 tokens) by +0.15 UTMOS and HiFi-Codec at 3 kbps (300 tokens) by +0.16 UTMOS on LibriTTS test-clean. Against the single-quantizer WavTokenizer baseline, Q2D2 at 1 kbps (75 tokens) wins on PESQ (+0.14), STOI (+0.008), and V/UV F1 (+0.006) on LibriTTS test-clean, though it trails slightly on UTMOS on LJSpeech (3.9715 vs 4.2580). In the 150k-hour multilingual evaluation (Table 2), Q2D2 at 333 tokens achieves the highest UTMOS (4.07) and STOI (0.96) among all models using >150 tokens, outperforming X-codec (4.21 UTMOS but at 100 tokens with 2 quantizers) and StableCodec (4.23 UTMOS at 50 tokens). On the ARCH semantic benchmark, Q2D2 with only 53 tokens surpasses DAC (900 tokens) on 10 of 12 datasets, including top scores on MTT (music) and US8K (environmental audio). In subjective MUSHRA testing, Q2D2 at 3.3 kbps (98.05) matches ground truth (98.08) and exceeds DAC at 9 kbps (92.64) by over 5 points. The one notable loss is on LJSpeech UTMOS at 1 kbps, where WavTokenizer (4.2580) edges out Q2D2 (3.9715), suggesting Q2D2's advantage is most pronounced in-domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q2D2 sits at the intersection of two recent simplification trends in neural audio codecs: WavTokenizer's demonstration that a single VQ layer can replace deep RVQ stacks, and FSQ's elimination of learned codebooks in favor of fixed scalar grids. The paper departs from both by introducing structured 2D geometric tilings (hexagonal, rhombic, rectangular) that group latent channels into pairs, capturing inter-channel correlations that 1D FSQ ignores while avoiding the codebook underutilization and optimization instability of high-dimensional VQ/RVQ. The implicit codebook is defined analytically by the product of per-pair grid levels, requiring no learned embeddings, commitment losses, or codebook reseeding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM-based audio generation: Q2D2 achieves near-ground-truth MUSHRA scores (98.05 vs 98.08) at 166 tokens/s, roughly 5× fewer tokens than DAC's 900, reducing sequence length and inference cost for autoregressive speech models. The method's simplicity (no learned codebook, single quantizer, fixed analytic grids) makes it easy to adopt as a drop-in replacement in existing codec frameworks. Open questions include whether the 2D geometric structure generalizes to audio and music domains (explicitly deferred to future work), whether 3D or higher-dimensional tilings yield further gains, and whether the packing-efficiency advantage of rhombic grids holds across different latent distributions and sampling rates.
