## Problem

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for single-view 3D reconstruction (e.g., Splatter Image) are highly sensitive to small changes in input camera viewpoint, producing inconsistent and blurry novel-view renderings, particularly in occluded regions. Existing approaches treat each viewpoint independently without explicitly modeling the inherent continuity of camera positions on the spherical coordinate system, so minor viewpoint shifts cause large, discontinuous changes in the reconstructed geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (128×128 for most benchmarks, 64×64 for multi-category ShapeNet) together with the camera's polar angle Θ and azimuthal angle Φ as explicit conditioning signals. It outputs a set of 2D Splatter Images—channel-wise maps where each pixel stores the position, covariance, opacity, and view-dependent color of one 3D Gaussian—forming a per-pixel mixture of Gaussians that can be rasterized from arbitrary novel viewpoints. The ODE integration endpoint is normalized to [0,1] from the raw azimuthal range of [-180°, 180°], and the polar angle is similarly normalized to [0,1] from [0°, 180°] before being fed to the initial-condition MLPs.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes every convolutional filter in a U-Net encoder-decoder into view-dependent filter atoms Ψ(Θ,Φ) and view-shared 1×1 coefficients a, so that only the compact atom set is dynamically generated per viewpoint. A Heavy Ball Neural ODE (second-order with damping γ) evolves the filter atoms along the azimuthal angle Φ, with its initial position and velocity produced by small 3-layer MLPs conditioned on the polar angle Θ, thereby covering the full spherical camera space with distinct ODE trajectories per elevation. Two separate NODE generators (one for encoder, one for decoder) produce their respective atom sets, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end by minimizing L2 rendering loss followed by LPIPS fine-tuning. The paper further proves (Proposition 4.1) that Lipschitz continuity of the ODE-generated atoms bounds the change in per-layer feature embeddings, guaranteeing that small viewpoint perturbations yield only bounded, smooth changes in the final 3D reconstruction.

## Results

On ShapeNet-Cars, the method achieves PSNR 24.46, SSIM 0.93, and LPIPS 0.073, outperforming the closest baseline Splatter Image (24.00 / 0.92 / 0.078) by 0.46 dB in PSNR. On ShapeNet-Chairs, it reaches PSNR 24.76, SSIM 0.93, LPIPS 0.071, slightly trailing NeRFDiff (24.80 / 0.93 / 0.070) on PSNR and LPIPS but matching on SSIM. On the multi-category NMR benchmark, the method attains PSNR 29.51 and SSIM 0.954, surpassing Splatter Image (29.38 / 0.95). On real-world Google Scanned Objects, it achieves PSNR 21.74, SSIM 0.89, LPIPS 0.090, substantially outperforming both Splatter Image (21.06 / 0.88 / 0.110) and OpenLRM (18.06 / 0.84 / 0.129).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is Splatter Image (Szymanowicz et al., 2024), which the method outperforms on Cars across all three metrics (PSNR +0.46, SSIM +0.01, LPIPS −0.005) and on Chairs for PSNR (+0.33) while matching SSIM and trailing LPIPS by 0.004. NeRFDiff (Gu et al., 2023) is the only method that edges out the proposed approach on Chairs PSNR (24.80 vs 24.76) and LPIPS (0.070 vs 0.071), though it relies on a conditional diffusion prior. On the multi-category NMR benchmark, the method surpasses Splatter Image by 0.13 dB PSNR and 0.004 SSIM. On real-world CO3D Hydrants, it wins all metrics over both PixelNeRF and Splatter Image; on Teddybears it wins PSNR and LPIPS but trails Splatter Image by 0.01 SSIM. On Google Scanned Objects, the method leads Splatter Image by 0.68 dB PSNR and 0.02 SSIM, and OpenLRM by 3.68 dB PSNR, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen real-world categories. Older baselines (SRN, CodeNeRF, FE-NVS, PixelNeRF, VisionNeRF, ViewsetDiff) are consistently outperformed by 1–2 dB in PSNR across both Cars and Chairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Splatter Image framework (Szymanowicz et al., 2024), which uses a feed-forward U-Net to predict per-pixel 3D Gaussians from a single image, and on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the underlying 3D representation. Its key architectural departure is the use of Heavy Ball Neural ODEs (Xia et al., 2021) to continuously generate view-dependent filter atoms within a decomposed convolutional filter subspace (Qiu et al., 2018), rather than learning discrete per-view parameters or using relative pose embeddings. By conditioning the ODE initial state on the polar angle and evolving it along the azimuthal angle, the paper embeds the spherical geometry of camera space directly into the network's parameterization, a design not present in prior single-view 3D reconstruction methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

View consistency across input viewpoints is a practical pain point in single-view 3D reconstruction, and this paper provides both a theoretical Lipschitz-continuity guarantee and empirical evidence (t-SNE clustering, Pearson correlation of 0.87 vs. 0.42–0.51 for baselines) that continuous ODE-based filter modeling addresses it. However, the method requires known camera poses, limiting its applicability in unconstrained settings, and it is restricted to single-object reconstruction rather than full scenes. The modest PSNR gains on standard benchmarks (≈0.3–0.5 dB over Splatter Image) suggest the primary value lies in robustness and extrapolation to unseen views rather than peak reconstruction quality, leaving open whether the ODE overhead is justified over simpler continuous parameterizations at scale.
