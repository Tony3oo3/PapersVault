## Problem

LLMs struggle to grasp the strict procedural logic, causal dependencies, quantitative precision, and safety-critical constraints inherent in biological experimental protocols, yet existing biomedical benchmarks focus almost exclusively on declarative knowledge (summarization, QA) and fail to evaluate the structured, step-by-step reasoning required for autonomous wet-lab automation. No large-scale resource exists that systematically probes procedural understanding across understanding, reasoning, and generation dimensions.

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans five tasks with distinct I/O formats: PQA takes a multiple-choice question grounded in a protocol and requires an answer choice plus a 0–100 confidence score; ORD takes programmatically shuffled protocol steps and requires a permutation of original indices; ERR takes a target step with surrounding context (purpose, prior/next steps) and requires a binary True/False verdict; GEN takes a domain-annotated prompt and requires a flat numbered list of protocol steps; REA extends ERR and GEN with a structured Chain-of-Thought template (Objective, Precondition, Phase, Parameter, Structure tags) before the final answer. All outputs are wrapped in [ANSWER_START]/[ANSWER_END] markers for automated parsing, and the GEN task enforces a strict one-step-per-line format with no rationale or materials list.

## Method

BioProBench is built on BioProCorpus, a collection of 26,933 human-authored protocols from six sources (Protocols.io, Bio-protocol, Protocol Exchange, JOVE, Nature Protocols, Morimoto Lab), cleaned and hierarchically enriched into up to four-level step trees using LLM-assisted parsing. Over 550,000 task instances are constructed with LLMs strictly confined to constrained roles (generating distractors, applying minimal perturbations) while all scientific facts and ground-truth answers are extracted programmatically from source protocols; a three-stage automated filter (structural integrity, semantic grounding via sentence embeddings, format verification) plus expert review of 10% of instances ensures fidelity. Evaluation combines standard NLP metrics with two novel domain-specific families: KeyBERT-based keyword precision/recall/F1 for content coverage, and embedding-based Step Recall/Step Precision (using all-mpnet-base-v2 cosine similarity with δ=0.7) for structural fidelity. To demonstrate the corpus's practical utility, ProAgent is built as a task-adaptive RAG agent in LangGraph where a Planner classifies the task, a Retriever selects between small-chunk (2000-char) and large-chunk (5000-char) knowledge bases using Qwen-3-Embedding with hybrid FAISS+BM25 search (α=0.6), and a Generator synthesizes the final response, all sharing a single base LLM selected per task.

## Results

On the PQA task, Gemini-2.5-pro-exp achieves the highest accuracy (70.27%) with the best Brier Score (0.1949), while Deepseek-R1 leads open-source models at 67.83%. On ORD, even the best model (Gemini-2.5-pro-exp) reaches only 51.80% Exact Match and 0.81 Kendall's Tau, with failure rates up to 23.43% (GPT-4-turbo). The GEN task reveals comprehensive failure across all models: Step Recall stays below 43%, Step Precision between 20–32%, and BLEU peaks at just 10.23%. ProAgent, grounded in BioProCorpus via RAG, substantially improves over the best baselines: PQA accuracy rises to 85.08% (+15 pts), ERR F1 to 81.9% (vs. 64.03% for Deepseek-R1), and GEN Step Recall to 62.24% (vs. 42.80% for Claude-3.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-pro-exp dominates PQA (70.27% Acc.) and ORD (51.80% EM, 0.81 τ), outperforming GPT-4o (63.50% Acc., 42.94% EM) and Claude-3.7-sonnet (63.90% Acc., 47.81% EM). Among open-source models, Deepseek-R1 leads on PQA (67.83%) and ERR (F1 64.03%, driven by 66.22% recall), while QwQ-32b achieves the highest ERR recall (54.35%) but suffers a 5.68% failure rate on ORD. On the GEN task, Claude-3.7-sonnet achieves the best Step Recall (42.80%) and GPT-4-turbo the best Step Precision (31.72%), but all models remain far below usable thresholds. ProAgent (RAG over BioProCorpus) wins decisively across all tasks: PQA 85.08% vs. 70.27% (Gemini-2.5), ERR F1 81.9% vs. 64.03% (Deepseek-R1), and GEN SR 62.24% vs. 42.80% (Claude-3.7). On the out-of-distribution LAB-Bench subset (108 questions), ProAgent improves accuracy from 56% to 67%, confirming transferable knowledge in the corpus. Domain-specific Bio-LLMs (BioMedGPT, BioMistral) perform drastically worse than general-purpose models on procedural tasks, with ORD failure rates of 33–91%, indicating that domain pre-training at their scale does not confer procedural reasoning ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying procedural reasoning in biological protocols as a distinct capability gap that existing biomedical benchmarks (BioASQ, PubMedQA, LAB-Bench, BixBench) and domain-adapted models (BioBERT, BioGPT, BioMedGPT) leave unaddressed, as those resources focus on declarative knowledge and question answering rather than the structured, causal, and safety-critical logic of experimental procedures. Rather than building on a specific prior method, the paper repurposes the well-established RAG paradigm as a validation mechanism: ProAgent demonstrates that grounding a general LLM in a high-fidelity protocol corpus directly mitigates the procedural failures the benchmark exposes. The construction pipeline draws on standard NLP techniques (KeyBERT for keyword extraction, SentenceTransformer embeddings for structural metrics, FAISS/BM25 hybrid retrieval) but applies them in a novel domain-specific evaluation framework with step-level recall/precision metrics that go beyond lexical overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a systematic and severe weakness across all 10 evaluated LLMs on long-form protocol generation (best Step Recall only 42.8%), indicating that current models cannot reliably produce complete, safe experimental procedures—a critical bottleneck for autonomous laboratory automation. The finding that domain-specific Bio-LLMs (BioMedGPT, BioMistral) underperform general-purpose models on procedural tasks (33–91% failure on ordering) challenges the assumption that biomedical pre-training transfers to procedural reasoning. Key limitations include the text-only scope (no multimodal protocol elements), potential LLM-introduced artifacts in dataset construction, and acknowledged biosecurity dual-use risks. Open questions include whether protocol-specialized fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA on BioProCorpus) can close the generation gap, how to extend evaluation to visual/multimodal protocols, and what safety guardrails are necessary before deploying such agents in real wet-lab settings.
