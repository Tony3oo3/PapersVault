## Problem

Existing nighttime lens flare removal methods depend heavily on large-scale paired training data, which is expensive to collect and limits scalability to diverse real-world flare distributions. Semi-supervised learning has been largely unexplored for this task, and naive pseudo-labeling is destabilized by noisy teacher predictions and the inherent ambiguity between flare artifacts and legitimate scene highlights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes flare-corrupted nighttime RGB images (512×512 crops during training) and outputs a single flare-free restored image at inference using only the student network with no additional architectural overhead. Training requires 8K synthesized labeled pairs (constructed from Flickr-24K backgrounds via the Flare7K++ synthesis protocol) plus 8K unlabeled flare-corrupted images, with weak and strong augmentations generating two views per unlabeled sample for the consistency objective.

## Method

The framework combines a teacher-student paradigm with two key innovations: (1) an adaptive pseudo-label repository that stores persistent pseudo targets per unlabeled sample and updates them only when a MUSIQ-based no-reference quality score exceeds the stored score by a margin, with momentum smoothing and validity gating to prevent error accumulation; and (2) a flare-aware patch-level contrastive loss that explicitly treats the flare-contaminated input as a structured negative and the repository pseudo target as a positive, pushing the student's intermediate features to separate flare patterns from scene textures. The generator is RaLiFormer, a U-shaped transformer using Rank-Enhanced Linear Attention (ReLinA) for O(N) global spatial modeling with depth-wise convolutional value refinement, and a Directionally-Enhanced Spatial Module (DESM) that applies directional depth-wise convolutions to capture the anisotropic streak and halo structures characteristic of lens flare.

## Results

On the Flare7K++ test set, the proposed Semi-LAR (8K labeled + 8K unlabeled) achieves PSNR 27.620, SSIM 0.897, LPIPS 0.0493, G-PSNR 24.276, and S-PSNR 22.686, outperforming all limited-supervision baselines and the only prior semi-supervised method SFSNiD (PSNR 24.992) by a substantial margin of ~2.6 dB. The method retains approximately 96% of the fully supervised state-of-the-art performance (SLCFormer, PSNR 28.092) while using only one-third of the labeled data. Cross-dataset evaluation on FlareX (PSNR 19.960) and real-world FlareReal600 (PSNR 20.368) further confirms consistent gains over full-supervision baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest limited-supervision baselines (8K labeled only), Semi-LAR outperforms SGSFT (27.601) by 0.019 dB PSNR, SLCFormer (27.301) by 0.319 dB, and Flare-Free (27.240) by 0.380 dB, while also achieving the best G-PSNR (24.276) and S-PSNR (22.686) among all limited/semi-supervised methods. The only prior semi-supervised method, SFSNiD, is substantially outperformed by 2.628 dB PSNR and 0.037 SSIM, indicating that the dependable repository and flare-aware contrastive loss are critical for stable semi-supervised training. Compared to full-supervision state-of-the-art (SLCFormer 28.092, SGSFT 28.077), the gap is only ~0.47 dB PSNR despite using roughly one-third of the labeled data. On the cross-dataset FlareX benchmark, Semi-LAR (19.960) surpasses all full-supervision baselines including Flare7K (19.496) and Flare7K++ (19.007). On real-world FlareReal600, the method achieves the best PSNR (20.368) and G-PSNR (0.604) while maintaining competitive LPIPS (0.260), demonstrating strong generalization beyond synthetic training distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student semi-supervised paradigm (Mean Teacher, Tarvainen & Valpola 2017) and SimCLR-style contrastive learning (Chen et al. 2020) to the specific domain of nighttime lens flare removal, a task where prior work has been almost exclusively fully supervised. It departs from generic consistency-based semi-supervised methods by introducing a no-reference quality assessment (MUSIQ) gate and momentum-based updates in a persistent pseudo-label repository, and by formulating the flare-corrupted input as an explicit structured negative in a patch-level contrastive objective. The RaLiFormer generator borrows linear attention (ReLinA) and directionally-enhanced feed-forward modules (DESM) from SLCFormer, but the semi-supervised training framework is explicitly model-agnostic and can be attached to any restoration backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment scenarios where paired flare data is expensive to acquire: using only ~1/3 of the labeled data plus unlabeled images recovers roughly 96% of full-supervision SOTA performance, and the framework also improves upon full supervision when unlabeled data is added (Table 5). The dependable repository concept—combining NR-IQA quality gating with momentum updates—offers a reusable recipe for stabilizing pseudo-labels in other low-level restoration tasks where teacher errors accumulate. Open questions include whether the contrastive negative formulation generalizes to other structured degradations (haze, rain, reflection), whether the MUSIQ-based gating is optimal versus learned quality estimators, and how the approach scales to video or multi-frame flare removal.
