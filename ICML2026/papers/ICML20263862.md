## Problem

GRPO relies on diverse rollouts for effective advantage estimation, but prevailing strategies increase diversity only through token-level randomness (e.g., higher temperature), which introduces step-wise noise that compounds over long reasoning chains and yields incoherent trajectories. The paper identifies an underexplored alternative: smaller models within the same family inherently exhibit higher policy-level diversity (evidenced by their superior pass@k at large sample counts), yet no method had leveraged this structured, temporally correlated diversity as an exploration mechanism for training larger models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt dataset (DAPO17k), a frozen smaller model (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B or 4B), and a trainable larger model (e.g., Qwen3-8B or 14B) from the same distillation family. The smaller model is fixed throughout training and generates rollouts offline, while the larger model is updated via standard GRPO on the combined candidate group. The output is the trained larger policy; the framework only modifies the rollout generation process and leaves the GRPO objective, advantage computation, and optimizer unchanged, making it a drop-in replacement within existing GRPO pipelines.

## Method

S2L-PO exploits the observation that parameter-level compression (distillation) induces a time-invariant policy perturbation that shifts the entire policy coherently, unlike token-level noise that perturbs each step independently. At each training step, a mixed rollout group is constructed by sampling G_w candidates from the frozen small model and G_s = G − G_w from the trainable large model, with group-relative advantages computed over the combined set. A progressive annealing schedule linearly decreases G_w from G to 0 over the first half of training, transitioning from small-model-dominated exploration to fully on-policy GRPO to avoid mid-training performance drops from distribution mismatch. Theoretically, the paper shows that policy-level perturbations yield positive cross-step covariance in the gradient signal (constructive O(L²) accumulation) whereas token-level perturbations cause long-range cancellation (random-walk O(L) growth), explaining why small-model rollouts provide more informative exploration signals for GRPO updates.

## Results

S2L-PO consistently outperforms standard GRPO across two model families and four mathematical reasoning benchmarks. The headline result is the 1.7B→8B configuration on Qwen3, which improves AIME24 Pass@1 from 15.0% to 23.8% (+8.8%) and AIME25 from 12.1% to 22.5% (+10.4%), yielding an average gain of approximately 9% over GRPO. On the out-of-domain CommonsenseQA benchmark, S2L-PO-4B achieves 67.8% accuracy for Qwen3-8B-Base versus 63.9% for vanilla GRPO, confirming that the gains do not come at the expense of generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard GRPO (on-policy, same model generating its own rollouts). S2L-PO wins across every configuration tested: on Qwen3-8B, the 1.7B explorer yields +8.8% on AIME24, +10.4% on AIME25, +4.2% on MATH-500, and +1.7% on OlympiadBench; on Qwen3-14B, the 4B explorer gives +6.4% on AIME24 and +4.0% on MATH-500; on InternLM2.5-7B, the 1.8B explorer improves AIME24 from 0.1% to 4.6% and MATH-500 from 18.6% to 22.6%. On the out-of-domain CommonsenseQA benchmark, S2L-PO-4B also beats GRPO (67.8% vs. 63.9% for 8B; 70.7% vs. 67.2% for 14B), indicating no generalization cost. A controlled experiment (Table 4) confirms the gains are specifically attributable to the small model's policy-level diversity: filtering the small model's rollouts to match the large model's diversity metrics drops performance back to the GRPO baseline (14.7% vs. 15.0% on AIME24), ruling out off-policy mixing as the source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its training backbone, modifying only the rollout generation process while leaving the objective, advantage computation, and optimization untouched. It departs from the dominant strategy of increasing rollout diversity via token-level randomness (temperature scaling, top-p) by reframing parameter-level compression within a model family as a source of structured, temporally coherent policy perturbation. Conceptually, it connects to the observation that smaller models exhibit higher pass@k (Bansal et al., 2024; Yue et al., 2025) and to off-policy RL methods that reuse external rollouts (Chen et al., 2026; Lanchantin et al., 2025), but distinguishes itself by using a frozen same-family smaller model as a fixed explorer with progressive annealing back to on-policy sampling, avoiding the distribution-shift plateau of purely offline approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S2L-PO offers a simple, drop-in modification to existing GRPO pipelines that simultaneously improves final accuracy (~9% on AIME) and reduces rollout compute, making it practically attractive for teams already running RLVR training. The clean theoretical distinction between token-level (random-walk gradient) and policy-level (constructive gradient) perturbations provides a principled lens for future exploration strategies beyond this specific setup. However, validation is limited to mathematical reasoning with verifiable rewards across only two model families (Qwen3, InternLM2.5), leaving open whether the approach transfers to non-verifiable or open-ended tasks, larger scale gaps, or modalities beyond text. The progressive annealing schedule, while ablated, is a heuristic whose optimal form (linear vs. other schedules, interaction with curriculum) remains unexplored.
