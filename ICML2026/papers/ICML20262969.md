## Problem

Existing foundation models for chaotic system forecasting use fixed patch sizes and shared parameterizations that fail to capture the multi-scale temporal structures and distinct spectral characteristics inherent in chaotic dynamics. Standard transformers suffer an information bottleneck on the continuum of scales, and a single parameter set is suboptimal across heterogeneous systems with markedly different power spectra, limiting zero-shot generalization to unseen chaotic systems.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate context window X_{1:T} ∈ R^{T×V} (typically 512 timesteps, V variables) segmented into non-overlapping patches of length D, each enriched with random polynomial and Fourier features inspired by Koopman theory before linear embedding. It produces a multi-step forecast X̂_{T+1:T+H} ∈ R^{H×V} (up to 512 steps) via a linear head that concatenates a unified multi-scale dynamics vector with a temporally-pooled wavelet scattering frequency fingerprint, enabling both short-term point-wise accuracy and long-term attractor-statistical fidelity.

## Method

ChaosNexus centers on ScaleFormer, a U-Net-style encoder-decoder that hierarchically coarsens temporal resolution via patch merging (encoder) and reconstructs fine-grained details via patch expansion (decoder), creating a spectral hierarchy where shallow layers preserve high-frequency fluctuations and deep layers distill the slow manifold. Each scale-level Transformer block employs dual axial attention (variable-axis and temporal-axis) to model inter-variable coupling, and replaces the feed-forward layer with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (top-K routing plus shared expert) to allocate specialized parameters to distinct dynamical regimes. A wavelet scattering transform extracts a fixed-size frequency fingerprint from the context that conditions the final linear readout as a regime-level calibrator. Training is supervised with a composite objective: MSE for point-wise accuracy, an auxiliary MoE load-balancing loss, and an MMD-based distributional regularizer (mixture of rational quadratic kernels) that enforces preservation of the system's attractor geometry and statistics.

## Results

On 9,300 zero-shot synthetic chaotic systems (512-step context → 512-step forecast), ChaosNexus achieves sMAPE@128 of 68.90 and sMAPE@512 of 100.29, while substantially outperforming all baselines on long-term attractor fidelity: D_frac = 0.203, D_stsp = 1.206, ME_LRw = 1.562, and D_Lyap = 0.065. On the real-world WEATHER-5K benchmark, zero-shot ChaosNexus achieves a 5-day (120-hour) global temperature MAE strictly below 1°C, surpassing all baselines (≥3°C MAE) even when those baselines are fine-tuned on 473K target-domain samples. Scaling from 2.88M to 52.68M parameters improves sMAPE@128 by 49.83%, and increasing system diversity (rather than per-system trajectory count) drives the primary gains in cross-system generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest domain-specific baseline Panda (same pretraining corpus, fixed-patch Transformer), ChaosNexus achieves comparable point-wise accuracy (sMAPE@128: 68.90 vs 69.57) but dramatically superior attractor fidelity—halving D_stsp (1.206 vs 2.369) and reducing D_frac (0.203 vs 0.227) and D_Lyap (0.065 vs 0.067). Against DynaMix (CNN encoder + RNN MoE), ChaosNexus wins on sMAPE@128 (68.90 vs 70.38) and D_stsp (1.206 vs 6.904), though DynaMix reports a lower D_frac (0.145 vs 0.203) with much higher variance (±0.182 vs ±0.011). General-purpose time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Timer-XL, Moirai-MoE, TimeMoE) all perform substantially worse, with sMAPE@128 ranging from 82.7 to 105.4 and D_stsp exceeding 9.6, confirming that chaotic dynamics require domain-specific pretraining. Even after fine-tuning Chronos on the chaotic corpus (Chronos-L-SFT), it still trails ChaosNexus on D_stsp (2.685 vs 1.206) and D_frac (0.210 vs 0.203). On the real-world WEATHER-5K task, zero-shot ChaosNexus (<1°C MAE at 120 hours) outperforms all fine-tuned baselines (FEDformer, CrossFormer, PatchTST, Koopa, iTransformer) by at least 2°C, demonstrating that synthetic chaotic pretraining transfers effectively to atmospheric dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot chaotic system forecasting paradigm established by Panda (Lai et al., 2026) and DynaMix (Hemmer & Durstewitz, 2025), both of which pretrain on large synthetic ODE corpora but use flat patch-based or CNN-based encoders. ChaosNexus departs from these by introducing a U-Net-style hierarchical ScaleFormer backbone (motivated by Nyquist-Shannon spectral capacity arguments) to explicitly separate multi-scale temporal processing, and by replacing DynaMix's local CNN regime classifier with a wavelet scattering transform fingerprint plus MoE routing for system-level spectral conditioning. The MMD-based attractor preservation loss follows DySLIM (Schiff et al., 2024), and the sparse MoE with shared expert adopts the DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024) routing architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot weather forecasting result (MAE < 1°C for 5-day global temperature, surpassing baselines fine-tuned on 473K samples) demonstrates that pretraining on synthetic chaotic dynamics can transfer to real-world meteorology without target-domain data, which has direct operational implications. The empirical finding that corpus-level system diversity—not per-system trajectory volume—drives cross-system generalization provides a concrete scaling law for future chaotic-system foundation models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to genuinely high-dimensional PDEs beyond PCA-projected surrogates, whether the MoE experts capture interpretable physical mechanisms or merely statistical regularities, and how the method performs under distribution shift in the forcing regime (e.g., climate change scenarios).
