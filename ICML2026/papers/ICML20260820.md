## Problem

Prior zero-shot text-to-4D methods rely on implicit representations (NeRF, 3DGS) that lack explicit topological control, making temporally coherent surface reconstruction and graphics-pipeline compatibility difficult. A fundamental structural mismatch arises when combining noisy, spatially inconsistent diffusion-based guidance with topology-constrained meshes: direct vertex-level deformation is unstable because neighboring vertices receive conflicting supervision, leading to self-intersections and geometric collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260820/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt describing both the desired object and its motion, and produces a sequence of L textured 3D meshes with fixed face connectivity (same topology F across all frames), where per-frame vertex positions vary to encode non-rigid deformation and a global rigid transformation (translation + rotation) captures spatial movement. The output is a graphics-ready mesh sequence with UV texture maps, generated in approximately 1.5 hours on a single 24GB GPU (NVIDIA RTX A5000).

## Method

TextMesh4D shifts deformation modeling from the vertex domain to the face domain via a Jacobian Deformation Field (JDF): each triangle is assigned a learnable 3×3 Jacobian matrix capturing local rotation and stretch, and the global surface is recovered through a differentiable Poisson integration solver that enforces integrability and leverages mesh topology to suppress spatially incoherent diffusion gradients. The pipeline is two-stage: (1) a static stage initializes a mesh via NeuS (SDF + volume rendering), then refines it by optimizing static Jacobian offsets under combined 3D-aware SDS (MVDream) and 2D VSD (ProlificDreamer-style); (2) a dynamic stage optimizes per-frame dynamic Jacobian offsets guided by video score distillation sampling (VDS) from a Zeroscope video diffusion model, regularized by a Local-Global Semantic Regularizer (LGSR) that combines an ARAP-style local rigidity term to prevent fluid-like distortion and a global canonical-shape anchoring term to prevent long-horizon identity drift.

## Results

TextMesh4D achieves the highest CLIP score (32.32) among all baselines, outperforming the next-best STAG4D (31.93) and 4D-fy (31.03). In GPT-4V selection, it leads across all five dimensions (AQ 56.82%, SQ 32.16%, MQ 45.70%, TA 38.45%, Overall 43.19%), with the closest competitor being Puppeteer (Overall 8.26%) which requires 8×A100 80GB GPUs. In the 31-participant user study, TextMesh4D scores highest on every dimension (AQ 4.8, SQ 4.2, MQ 4.6, TA 4.3, Overall 4.5 on a 5-point scale), while all baselines score below 3.8. Notably, TextMesh4D runs on a single 24GB A5000 GPU, whereas most baselines require one or more 80GB A100s (AYG and L4GM require 128×A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextMesh4D outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against the zero-shot NeRF-based methods (4D-fy, Dream-in-4D, TC4D), it gains 0.5–1.3 CLIP points and 37–38 percentage points in GPT-4V Overall selection, while user-study Overall scores jump from 2.4–2.7 to 4.5. Against the 3DGS-based methods (AYG, DG4D, STAG4D, L4GM), the advantage is even more pronounced: GPT-4V Overall selection is 43.19% vs. 2.90–8.04%, and user-study Overall is 4.5 vs. 1.3–3.6. The closest competitor, Puppeteer (a mesh-based 3D-to-4D method requiring 8×A100 GPUs and a 3D input), still trails TextMesh4D by 34.9 points in GPT-4V Overall and 0.8 in user-study Overall. In terms of efficiency, TextMesh4D requires only 1×A5000 24GB (~1.5 h total), compared to 1×A100 80GB for most baselines and 128×A100 80GB for AYG and L4GM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant implicit-representation paradigm (NeRF, 3DGS) in text-to-4D generation by adopting explicit mesh topology, building on the Poisson integration framework from Neural Jacobian Fields (Aigerman et al., 2022) for surface recovery. The optimization pipeline inherits the SDS/VDS distillation machinery from DreamFusion and ProlificDreamer, and uses MVDream for 3D-aware guidance and NeuS for initial mesh extraction, but the core conceptual shift is moving deformation modeling from the vertex domain to the face-domain via per-face Jacobian matrices. This reframes the problem as a constrained integration task that exploits mesh connectivity to filter spatially incoherent diffusion gradients, rather than fighting them at the vertex level as prior mesh-based text-to-3D work (e.g., TextMesh, CLIP-Mesh) does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing graphics-ready dynamic meshes rather than implicit fields, TextMesh4D addresses a practical gap: implicit 4D representations are difficult to extract into temporally coherent surfaces compatible with standard rendering and simulation pipelines. The fixed-topology assumption, however, limits applicability to topology-changing dynamics (splitting, merging, part emergence), and the two-stage design means static-stage errors propagate into motion. Open questions include whether the JDF formulation can be extended to handle topology changes, whether explicit camera control in video diffusion priors would unlock more diverse global motion, and whether the approach scales to multi-object or scene-level 4D generation.
