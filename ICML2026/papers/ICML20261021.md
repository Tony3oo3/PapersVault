## Problem

Pixel-level saliency methods reveal where a model attends but offer no semantic insight into what it perceives, while concept-based methods explain which attributes drive predictions but lack spatial grounding. Bridging these two paradigms to produce explanations that are simultaneously spatially precise and human-interpretable remains an open challenge, compounded by the fact that existing concept-based approaches often require manual image curation or produce impure Concept Activation Vectors from full-image embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ConEx takes an RGB image and a pretrained image classifier (ResNet50, DenseNet121, ConvNeXt-Base, ViT-Base, or ViT-Small) as input and operates entirely post-hoc without retraining. It produces a decomposed saliency map where each pixel's importance is attributed to specific named visual concepts (e.g., 'yellow beak'), along with per-concept importance maps, a final aggregated explanation map, and dataset-level global semantic attributions. The framework requires no manual annotation; concepts are discovered automatically via GPT-4o-generated textual attributes grounded by GroundedSAM zero-shot segmentation.

## Method

ConEx automatically discovers class-specific concepts using label-free vision-language priors, validates them via occurrence-rate and spatial-coverage thresholds, and grounds them into segmentation masks using GroundedSAM. For each validated concept, it constructs a Concept Activation Vector via a centroid-difference formulation in the model's latent space, using architecture-specific embedding strategies: layer-wise masking with 7×7 neighborhood padding for CNNs (preserving segment boundaries without leaking masked context) and unmasked intermediate-layer patch aggregation for ViTs. Concept localization is achieved through Channel-Weighted Vectors (CWVs) that produce normalized semantic maps indicating where each concept appears. The final explanation is generated by multiplicatively fusing these concept-presence maps with LRP attributions, amplifying relevance only where concept presence and model relevance coincide, yielding spatially precise, semantically grounded saliency maps. Two complementary validation metrics—Vector-Concept Match (VCM) and Concept-Class Match (CCM)—quantify CAV semantic fidelity and predictive relevance respectively.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, ConEx achieves CINS=51.20, CDEL=8.74, VCM=0.68, and CCM=28.01, outperforming the best concept-based baselines (MCD: CINS=48.16, VCM=0.56, CCM=20.22) by 3–8 points across all four concept-quality metrics. On the FunnyBirds part-based benchmark, ConEx attains CMP=0.78, CRC=0.64, and CNT=0.92, surpassing the strongest pixel-level baseline IIA (0.74/0.59/0.88). In the human understandability study, ConEx reaches 72.13% Selection Accuracy versus 58.47% for ACE and 34.32% for MCD, with all differences statistically significant (p<0.001). On ImageNet-Segmentation, ConEx achieves mIoU=0.76 and mAP=0.89 with RN, exceeding IIA (0.75/0.86) and all other saliency baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConEx outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluation dimension. Among pixel-level saliency methods, IIA is the closest competitor on faithfulness (e.g., SD/RN: IIA INS=57.69 vs. ConEx 58.17; DEL 9.54 vs. 8.91), while GradCAM, Score-CAM, RISE, FullGrad, IG, SHAP, and AblationCAM all trail by 2–12 INS points. On the FunnyBirds benchmark, ConEx leads IIA by +0.04 CMP, +0.05 CRC, and +0.04 CNT, with the gap widening to +0.10 CRC over GradCAM and +0.14 CRC over RISE. Among concept-based methods, MCD is the strongest baseline on CINS (48.16 vs. 51.20 on RN) and VCM (0.56 vs. 0.68), while ACE and ICE lag further behind (CINS 44.89/43.74; VCM 0.53/0.49). In the human understandability study, ConEx's 72.13% Selection Accuracy exceeds ACE by 13.7 points and MCD by 37.8 points, with ConEx also achieving the lowest INTS (0.28 vs. 0.34/0.39), indicating more semantically distinct concepts. On segmentation alignment (IN-S), ConEx leads IIA by +0.01 mIoU and +0.03 mAP on RN, and the gap grows to +0.18 mIoU over RISE.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConEx sits at the intersection of two established but largely separate lines of work: pixel-level saliency methods (GradCAM, LRP, Integrated Gradients) that localize where a model attends, and concept-based interpretability (TCAV, ACE, ICE, MCD) that identifies what semantic attributes drive predictions. The closest prior work, Visual-TCAV, also bridges these paradigms but requires manual curation of concept images and builds CAVs from full images. ConEx departs from this lineage by automating concept discovery via vision-language priors (GPT-4o + GroundedSAM), introducing architecture-specific embedding strategies (layer-wise masking for CNNs, patch-token aggregation for ViTs) to construct purer CAVs from precisely segmented regions, and fusing concept presence with LRP via a multiplicative mechanism for spatially grounded attribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that concept-grounded explanations can simultaneously achieve high faithfulness and human interpretability, addressing a long-standing tension in XAI where spatial precision and semantic meaning have been treated as competing objectives. Key open questions remain: spatially localized concept explanations for ViTs are explicitly left as future work, the pipeline's reliance on GroundedSAM introduces a dependency on zero-shot segmentation quality, and the LLM-generated concept vocabulary may inherit societal biases. The proposed VCM and CCM metrics offer a useful standardized way to validate CAV quality that could benefit the broader concept-based interpretability community.
