## Problem

Machine learning-based peptide binder design has advanced rapidly for L-L homochiral interfaces, but generating D-peptide binders for L-protein targets remains largely unexplored. The core challenge is that models trained exclusively on homochiral complexes must generalize zero-shot to heterochiral conditions, requiring both chirality awareness and stable representations under spatial inversion—properties that standard E(3)-equivariant architectures inherently lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265427/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an L-protein target binding pocket as input, spatially inverts it to form a D-target, generates an L-peptide binder against this D-target via latent diffusion, and then inverts the generated binder to yield the final D-peptide binder for the original L-target. This two-step inversion workflow (G_b = P(f_θ(P(G_t)))) means the model is always conditioned on a target of the 'opposite' chirality relative to the desired output, making it a true zero-shot cross-chirality task. Outputs are full-atom 3D peptide structures with consistent D-chirality at the residue level.

## Method

The key contribution is Axial Feature Injection (AFI), a plug-and-play module that adds axial vector features (which transform with det(R) under O(3)) to the polar vector features of an E(3)-equivariant backbone, breaking inversion equivariance while preserving SE(3)-equivariance. Three axial constructions are derived from second-order tensor decompositions: the cross product u×v, the scalar triple product projection (w·(u×v))·w, and the commutator (u·v)(u×v). AFI is injected before every GVP-FFN layer in the EPT backbone within the UniMoMo latent diffusion framework (VAE encoder → conditional diffusion → decoder). Theoretically, the paper proves that AFI induces a nontrivial but bounded latent-code discrepancy between enantiomers (Theorem 3.1), while a Lipschitz continuity argument on the diffusion SDE (Theorem 3.4) guarantees that this small discrepancy translates to stable, high-quality generation under unseen heterochiral conditions.

## Results

On the LNR test set, PepMirror (all three axial-feature variants) achieves the best interface affinity for D-peptide design: success rate ~99.7%, average Vina score −4.15 to −4.25, top-1 score −5.69 to −5.87, and interface energy improvement (IMP) of 63–72%. The L-to-D performance gap is minimal (e.g., PepMirror(commu.) drops only from −4.34 to −4.25 average), whereas RFDiffusion suffers a severe cliff (−3.30 → −1.77 average, IMP 44.09% → 16.13%). In wet-lab validation, 1 of 12 synthesized D-peptides (D-1412) bound CD38 with K_D ≈ 10 μM, the first wet-lab-validated de novo D-peptide binder designed by a generative AI model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PepMirror outperforms all baselines that maintain consistent D-chirality on every interface-affinity metric for D-peptide design. Against the strongest chirality-consistent baseline, PepGLAD(ideal), PepMirror(cross) improves average Vina score from −3.26 to −4.15 and IMP from 43.01% to 63.44%. RFDiffusion, the most widely used frame-based model, shows the largest L-to-D degradation (average score drops from −3.30 to −1.77; IMP collapses from 44.09% to 16.13%), while PepFlow and D-Flow also lose 2–3 points in IMP on D-tasks. DiffPepBuilder and PPFlow maintain relatively stable L-D performance but remain 0.8–1.5 kcal/mol weaker in average Vina score than PepMirror on D-tasks. Models without explicit chirality control (PocketXMol, PepGLAD, UniMoMo) are excluded from the affinity comparison because they generate mixed-chirality outputs (~50% correct residues), making them unsuitable for D-peptide applications. In terms of chirality consistency (Table 1), PepMirror achieves ≥99.8% correct D-chirality after minimization, matching frame-based models while far exceeding the ~50% of equivariant models without chirality priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the UniMoMo latent diffusion framework (Kong et al., 2025b) and its EPT equivariant transformer backbone (Jiao et al., 2024), modifying the architecture by injecting axial vector features (cross products, triple-product projections, and commutators) into polar vector channels to break E(3) inversion equivariance down to SE(3) equivariance. It departs from the dominant paradigm in peptide design models (RFDiffusion, PepFlow, DiffPepBuilder, etc.) that encode chirality implicitly via right-handed rigid-body frames, and from E(3)-equivariant models without chirality priors (PepGLAD, PocketXMol) that cannot distinguish L from D residues. The only prior AI attempt at D-peptide binder design, D-Flow (Wu et al., 2024), uses a similar mirror-inversion workflow but lacks experimental validation and does not introduce an explicit chirality-aware mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PepMirror is the first generative AI model for D-peptide binder design validated in wet-lab experiments, demonstrating that a lightweight plug-and-play architectural modification (AFI) can enable zero-shot cross-chirality generalization from homochiral training data. The theoretical analysis (chirality-awareness theorem, no-discrepancy proposition, diffusion stability bound) provides a principled understanding of why axial features suffice, though it relies on simplifying assumptions and does not cover end-to-end optimization. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the single CD38 target, how to handle the observed reduced stereoselectivity (the L-enantiomer also bound CD38), and whether AFI transfers to other equivariant backbones and hetero-chiral design problems beyond peptides.
