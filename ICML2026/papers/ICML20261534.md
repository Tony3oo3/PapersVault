## Problem

Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models require substantial task-specific demonstrations to adapt to novel manipulation tasks, and existing few-shot approaches (prompt-based tuning, retrieval) rely on frame-wise action imitation that fails to capture trajectory-level physical dynamics. This leads to generated trajectories that violate environmental constraints and accumulate errors over multi-stage execution, especially under only 10–20 demonstrations. The paper identifies the absence of an explicit mechanism bridging high-level semantic reasoning and low-level motion dynamics as the central bottleneck for scalable few-shot embodied adaptation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a language instruction, dual-egocentric RGB observations (a 200° fisheye camera at 1280×720@30FPS for global context and a RealSense RGB camera for local gripper detail), and a 6-DOF absolute joint-state vector from the AgileX Piper arm. The VLM backbone encodes the visual-language context while a state encoder maps proprioception into a compact embedding, jointly forming the multimodal conditioning vector f_c. The DiT controller outputs a sequential action chunk of H=50 steps, generated via K≤10 forward-Euler denoising steps under the flow-matching formulation, with the dynamics representation injected as additional conditioning tokens at a mid-network layer.

## Method

DynVLA reformulates VLA adaptation from action imitation to trajectory-level motion dynamics modeling via two coupled modules. The Motion Dynamics Mechanism (MDM) infers a latent dynamics embedding r_d by solving a flow-matching inversion objective that requires the pretrained DiT vector field to explain the observed action trajectory under an additional conditioning variable, regularized by a bidirectional InfoNCE temporal contrastive loss that enforces consistency between adjacent transitions within the same trajectory. The Dynamics-Constrained Modeling (DCM) stage projects the inferred r_d onto a pretrained Dynamics Bank—a learnable prototype codebook of K=1024 motion-pattern vectors learned from diverse demonstrations—via soft-attention at the DiT architecture midpoint, producing a convex combination of prototypes that serves as distributed conditioning tokens for the remaining denoising layers. This prototype-level conditioning allows the policy to interpolate among learned execution paradigms rather than selecting a single discrete mode, grounding generation in physically feasible regimes while preserving flexibility for novel dynamics combinations. The entire system is pretrained on Open X-Embodiment and fine-tuned with only ~20 demonstrations per task, achieving a 15.8% average success-rate improvement over the strongest baseline (GR00T-N1.5) across 13 real-world tasks.

## Results

On 13 real-world robotic manipulation tasks, DynVLA achieves a 73.1% average success rate at the Small scale (~20 demonstrations per task), outperforming the strongest baseline GR00T-N1.5 (57.3%) by 15.8 percentage points. At the Tiny scale (~10 demos), DynVLA reaches 44.6% versus 34.2% for GR00T-N1.5. The advantage is most pronounced on long-horizon tasks (68.3% vs 45.0%) and zero-shot generalization (58.3% vs 38.3%), where baselines struggle with error accumulation and unseen instruction combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynVLA outperforms all five baselines across every task category and data scale. Against the strongest baseline GR00T-N1.5, DynVLA gains 15.8 pp in overall average at Small scale (73.1% vs 57.3%), with the largest margins on long-horizon tasks (+23.3 pp) and zero-shot tasks (+20.0 pp). Compared to π₀.₅ (49.6%), the gap is 23.5 pp, and against GR00T-N1 (45.0%) it is 28.1 pp. The specialized visuomotor policies ACT (30.4%) and Diffusion Policy (19.2%) trail substantially, particularly on zero-shot tasks where they score below 12%. The data-scaling analysis (Table 3) further shows that DynVLA's lead over GR00T-N1.5 narrows from 15.8 pp at Small to 5.4 pp at Full scale, indicating that the dynamics-constrained mechanism provides the greatest benefit precisely in the few-shot regime where baselines lack sufficient data to internalize task dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynVLA builds on the VLA paradigm (VLM backbone + Diffusion Transformer for action generation) established by models like π_0.5, GR00T-N1, and OpenVLA, and on flow-matching as the generative mechanism for action chunks. It departs from prior few-shot adaptation strategies—prompt-based tuning, retrieval-augmented imitation, and inference-time search—by reframing adaptation as trajectory-level dynamics inference rather than frame-wise action imitation. The key conceptual departure is using flow-matching inversion (rather than forward generation) to extract latent dynamics embeddings, combined with a pretrained prototype codebook (Dynamics Bank) and temporal contrastive grounding, which together constitute a new conditioning mechanism for the DiT controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-shot adaptation remains the primary bottleneck for deploying VLA models in new real-world settings, and DynVLA's 15.8% average improvement over the strongest baseline with only ~20 demonstrations per task is practically significant for reducing data collection costs. However, the evaluation is confined to a single 6-DOF tabletop robot (AgileX Piper) with 13 tasks, leaving open whether the Dynamics Bank generalizes across embodiments, task domains, or truly novel dynamics outside the pretrained prototype space. The method also assumes access to a pretrained VLA backbone with flow-matching, limiting applicability to architectures that do not support inversion, and the fixed codebook size (K=1024) raises questions about scalability to more diverse or higher-dimensional action spaces.
