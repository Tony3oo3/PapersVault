## Problem

Under the recently proposed ℓ-smoothness condition ‖∇²f(x)‖ ≤ ℓ(‖∇f(x)‖), which generalizes L-smoothness and (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, it was an open question whether accelerated gradient descent could achieve the optimal O(√(ℓ(0))R/√ε) oracle complexity for small ε. Existing accelerated extensions either incurred exponential factors in L₁R, required solving costly auxiliary one-dimensional problems at each iteration (introducing non-constant multiplicative factors), or depended on the initial gradient norm ‖∇f(x⁰)‖, which can be exponentially large in L₁ and R.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a convex function f through a gradient oracle, a starting point x⁰, the smoothness function ℓ (or parameters L₀, L₁), and user-specified bounds R̄ ≥ ‖x⁰−x*‖ and Γ₀ ≥ 2Δ/‖x⁰−x*‖². Algorithm 1 additionally requires a threshold δ satisfying ℓ(8√(δℓ(0))) ≤ 2ℓ(0) and runs a GD pre-phase to warm-start; Algorithm 2 avoids this by using adaptive step sizes γ_k = 1/ℓ(4ψ⁻¹(Γ_k R̄²)) that automatically shrink as the iterates approach the optimum. The output is a point x̄ with f(x̄) − f(x*) ≤ ε, achieved in O(√(ℓ(0))R/√ε) gradient calls for small ε, with a non-ε-dependent additive term.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new Lyapunov-based proof technique that resolves a 'chicken-and-egg' dilemma: the descent lemma requires a step size γ that depends on ‖∇f(y_γ^{k+1})‖, which itself depends on γ. The resolution defines γ* as the smallest γ where the descent condition g(γ) = γ − 1/ℓ(2‖∇f(y^k)‖ + ‖∇f(y_γ^{k+1})‖) = 0 holds, then shows that for all γ ≤ γ*, the function gap f(y_γ^{k+1}) − f(x*) ≤ V_k ≤ δ, which via a gradient-norm-to-function-gap lemma (Lemma E.1) implies ℓ(4‖∇f(y_γ^{k+1})‖) ≤ 2ℓ(0), yielding γ* ≥ 1/(2ℓ(0)) and validating the desired constant step size. This argument works without requiring method monotonicity or auxiliary sub-solvers, and extends to the superquadratic case (ρ > 2) by tracking whether iterates fall on the 'left' or 'right' branch of the non-invertible ψ function and excluding the bad branch via a compactness argument on the gradient norm.

## Results

The paper achieves oracle complexity O(√(ℓ(0))R/√ε + T) under general ℓ-smoothness and O(√(L₀)R/√ε + L₁R·log(min{L₁²Δ/L₀, Δ/ε})) under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, where T is independent of ε. The dominant term √(L₀)R/√ε is provably optimal in the small-ε regime (matching the Nesterov lower bound). In experiments on f(x,y)=eˣ+e^(1−x)+μy²/2 and MNIST logistic regression with a cubic regularizer, Algorithm 2 demonstrates the predicted accelerated (non-monotonic) convergence and outperforms prior AGD methods at lower accuracy targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. (2023), the new method removes the dependence on ‖∇f(x⁰)‖, which can be exponentially large in L₁ and R (e.g., for f(x)=exp(L₁x)), replacing it with the constant ℓ(0). Against Gorbunov et al. (2025), the new method eliminates the exponential factor exp(L₁R) that appears in their bound, yielding a purely polynomial dependence on all parameters. Against Vankov et al. (2024), the new method removes the non-constant multiplicative factor ν arising from solving an auxiliary one-dimensional problem at each iteration; here only one gradient is computed per step, and the dominant term √(L₀)R/√ε is strictly better for small ε. In the empirical comparison (Figures 2 and 6), Algorithm 2 outperforms Li et al.'s AGD (which uses a conservative step size) and Vankov et al.'s AGD (which is sensitive to the number of inner iterations for the auxiliary problem) at lower accuracy targets, while Gorbunov et al.'s method is competitive at higher accuracy despite its weaker theoretical guarantee. The GD baseline from Tyurin (2025) is used as a subroutine in Algorithm 1 and as a non-accelerated comparison in experiments, confirming the acceleration gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent from the classical L-smoothness setting to the broader ℓ-smoothness framework introduced by Li et al. (2023), which encompasses (L₀,L₁)-smoothness and (ρ,L₀,L₁)-smoothness as special cases. It departs from prior accelerated methods under generalized smoothness—Gorbunov et al. (2025) which incurs an exponential factor in L₁R, Vankov et al. (2024) which requires solving an auxiliary one-dimensional problem at each iteration, and Li et al. (2023) which depends on the initial gradient norm—by introducing a new Lyapunov-based proof technique that resolves a 'chicken-and-egg' circularity in the step-size selection, achieving the optimal rate with only one gradient evaluation per iteration. The algorithmic structure is closely related to the over-relaxation heavy-ball method of Wei & Chen (2025) and the linear coupling framework of Allen-Zhu & Orecchia (2014), but the analysis is fundamentally different and tailored to the non-uniform smoothness landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete open question: whether the Nesterov-optimal O(√(L₀)R/√ε) rate is achievable under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness without auxiliary sub-routines or exponential factors, and the answer is yes with a provably tight bound in the small-ε regime. The 'chicken-and-egg' resolution technique—showing that the implicit step-size parameter γ* is always at least 1/(2ℓ(0)) despite the circular dependency—may prove useful in other non-uniform smoothness analyses. Open questions remain regarding the optimal non-dominant term for large ε and extensions to stochastic or finite-sum settings, which the authors explicitly flag as future work. For practitioners, the practical impact is moderate: the (L₀,L₁)-smoothness condition is increasingly recognized as more realistic for deep learning objectives, but the results are for convex problems and the algorithms require knowledge of L₀, L₁, R, and Δ.
