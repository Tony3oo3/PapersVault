## Problem

Counterfactual explanations generated in low-density regions of the data distribution are unreliable under model multiplicity, where different plausible classifiers diverge in their predictions. Existing robust CE methods either rely on expensive ensemble intersections or fail to explicitly model the data manifold, leaving generated counterfactuals vulnerable to model disagreement in sparse, uncertain areas. The paper asks: can we steer CE generation toward high-density, high-confidence regions in a differentiable and query-efficient manner for heterogeneous black-box ensembles?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standardized tabular query instance (with categorical features encoded via TabRep circular cos/sin representations), a target class label y*, and query access to a heterogeneous black-box ensemble of seven classifiers (KNN, SVM, RF, MLP, XGBoost, CatBoost, TabNet). It produces a counterfactual x' selected from N=30 parallel stochastic ODE trajectories (Gaussian-perturbed initial states), along with the L2 transport cost and ensemble validity score. The search space is bounded by a hypercube [−C, C]^d where C is set adaptively from the training data support with a safety factor β=1.2.

## Method

DensityFlow couples a Neural ODE velocity field v_θ with an NCE-based (K+1)-way surrogate f_φ that jointly performs classification and density estimation against uniform noise. The key theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) proves that the logit difference S(x|y*) = z_{y*} − z_{K+1} is linearly proportional to log p(x|y*), providing a differentiable density gradient that steers ODE trajectories through high-density manifolds. The generator is trained with a composite objective combining cross-entropy validity, kinetic-energy transport cost (via state-augmented ODE dynamics), and a ReLU density penalty at the trajectory endpoint. For black-box settings, a trajectory-aware local distillation aligns the surrogate with the ensemble only at terminal states visited during generation, avoiding costly global boundary alignment. Training alternates between surrogate updates (NCE loss) and generator updates (composite objective), followed by a short distillation phase that queries the ensemble only in high-density regions.

## Results

On the real-world Adult dataset, DensityFlow achieves 0.901 ensemble validity with a cost of 1.597, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline BetaRCE (0.752 validity, 2.610 cost) and CeFlow (0.691 validity, 3.959 cost). On synthetic datasets (Moons, Circles, Spirals, Chessboard), DensityFlow reaches near-perfect validity (>96%) while keeping costs competitive. Across all eight datasets, DensityFlow attains the highest validity on 7 out of 8, with the sole exception being HELOC where BetaRCE edges ahead by 0.008 (0.765 vs. 0.757). The method also requires significantly fewer black-box queries than iterative baselines, with validity saturating after a small distillation budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Product_mip (MILP-based relational verification), DensityFlow dominates on validity across all eight datasets (e.g., 0.901 vs. 0.687 on Adult, 0.997 vs. 0.492 on Moons), while also achieving lower cost on most real-world datasets. Against CeFlow (normalizing-flow-based generation), DensityFlow improves validity by 10–35 percentage points on real-world data (0.901 vs. 0.691 on Adult, 0.729 vs. 0.385 on Compas) and reduces cost by up to 60% (1.597 vs. 3.959 on Adult). Against BetaRCE (probabilistic robustness guarantees), DensityFlow wins on 7 of 8 datasets in validity (0.901 vs. 0.752 on Adult, 0.662 vs. 0.285 on Blood), with the only loss being a marginal 0.008 gap on HELOC (0.757 vs. 0.765). Against Argument (argumentation-based post-hoc selection), the closest competitor, DensityFlow still leads on validity for all datasets (0.901 vs. 0.659 on Adult, 0.729 vs. 0.610 on Compas) and achieves lower cost on Adult (1.597 vs. 1.916) and Compas (1.176 vs. 1.542). In query-efficiency experiments, DensityFlow requires substantially fewer black-box ensemble queries than both Argument and BetaRCE, with validity saturating after a small local distillation budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative counterfactual explanation line (VAE-based, diffusion-based, and flow-based methods) by reframing robust CE generation under model multiplicity as a density-constrained optimal transport problem solved via Neural ODEs. It departs from prior MM-robustness methods (Product-MIP, Argument, BetaRCE) that rely on expensive ensemble intersections or post-hoc selection, instead learning a differentiable density score via a (K+1)-way NCE discriminator that jointly models classification and support. The trajectory-aware local distillation strategy is a novel efficiency mechanism: rather than globally aligning a surrogate with a heterogeneous black-box ensemble, it restricts alignment to the high-density regions actually visited by the generator, leveraging the density gradient as a query guide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, differentiable mechanism for steering counterfactuals away from low-density regions where model consensus breaks down, without the prohibitive query costs of ensemble-based verification. The theoretical link between the NCE logit difference and class-conditional density (Proposition 4.1), combined with the Cantelli-based robustness bound, gives the density guidance a clear interpretive role beyond heuristic regularization. Open questions include scaling the density-score component to very high-dimensional data (where feature selection risks causal coupling), handling extreme class imbalance that weakens the learned support, and whether embedding-level density estimation (e.g., via pre-trained representations) can extend the framework to complex modalities beyond tabular data.
