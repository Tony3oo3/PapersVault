## Problem

Standard diffusion models (DDPM, LDM) operate over all dimensions indiscriminately, wasting computation on zero-valued entries and failing to preserve exact zeros that carry semantic meaning in sparse scientific data (e.g., scRNA dropout events, absent calorimeter energy deposits). This paper asks how diffusion models can skip zeros during both training and inference to achieve computational efficiency that scales with the number of non-zero entries rather than ambient dimensionality, while faithfully preserving sparsity patterns.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the model starts from a Gaussian noise vector in a fixed-size dense latent space (e.g., 1000-dim for physics/vision, 64-dim for scRNA) and produces a sparse continuous vector of arbitrary dimensionality (32×32 calorimeter images, up to 27K-dim scRNA vectors, 28×28 grayscale images) with exact zeros. The autoregressive decoder emits (dimension-index, value) pairs sequentially until an end-of-sequence token, then fills all remaining dimensions with zero, so the output length and sparsity level are data-dependent rather than fixed. During training, the raw sparse input is first reduced to its non-zero dimension-value pairs before entering the encoder, meaning the model never sees or processes zero entries.

## Method

SED is a two-stage latent diffusion model. Stage 1 trains a Sparsity-Aware Variational Autoencoder (SAVAE): a Transformer encoder consumes only the non-zero (dimension, value) pairs of each sample (with sinusoidal dimension encoding and linear value embedding), applies mean pooling to produce a fixed-size latent z, and an autoregressive decoder reconstructs the input by sequentially sampling a multinomial over dimension indices and a Gaussian over values, trained with a β-VAE objective. Stage 2 freezes the SAVAE and trains a standard DDPM/DDIM diffusion model (with log-SNR parameterization and self-conditioning) in the dense latent space z using an MLP-based U-Net backbone, since the latent lacks grid structure. The key architectural insight is that by removing zero dimensions before diffusion, the computational cost scales with the number of non-zero entries rather than total dimensionality, and the autoregressive decoding naturally preserves exact zeros by only generating active dimensions.

## Results

On calorimeter image generation (Table 1), SED (15M params) achieves Wasserstein distances of 16.31 (P_T) and 7.02 (invariant mass) for signal, and 9.63 (P_T) and 3.64 (mass) for background, outperforming all baselines including the domain-specific SARM (25M) and standard DDPM (37M) by large margins. On scRNA data (Table 2), SED (4M) achieves SCC of 0.74/0.82 and MMD of 0.55/0.54 on Tabula Muris/Human Lung PF, surpassing scDiffusion (5M) on both metrics. SED also demonstrates near-constant training and sampling compute as input dimensionality grows from 1K to 27K genes, while DDPM and LDM scale linearly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SED outperforms standard DDPM and DDIM (37M) by over an order of magnitude in Wasserstein distance (e.g., 16.31 vs. 220.32 on signal P_T), demonstrating that dense diffusion fundamentally fails on sparse data. Against thresholded variants (DDPM-T, DDIM-T), SED still wins by ~30–50% (16.31 vs. 24.22 on signal P_T), showing that post-hoc thresholding cannot recover the structural information lost during dense generation. At matched parameter count (15M), SED beats LDM (LDMP/LDMI) by ~42% on signal P_T (16.31 vs. 28.20) and ~56% on background mass (3.64 vs. 7.80), confirming that sparsity-aware latent encoding is more effective than generic latent compression. SED also surpasses the physics-informed SARM (25M) on all four calorimeter metrics (e.g., 9.63 vs. 12.61 on background P_T) while using fewer parameters and no domain-specific spiral sampling prior. On scRNA data, SED (4M) beats scDiffusion (5M) on MMD (0.55 vs. 1.53 on Tabula Muris; 0.54 vs. 1.02 on Human Lung PF) and matches or slightly exceeds it on SCC (0.74 vs. 0.71; 0.82 vs. 0.77), despite scDiffusion's compute-intensive pretraining on a large cell corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

SED builds directly on the two-stage latent diffusion paradigm of LDMs (Rombach et al., 2022), replacing the standard VAE with a Sparsity-Aware VAE (SAVAE) that encodes only non-zero dimension-value pairs via a Transformer encoder and decodes them autoregressively. It departs from prior sparse-data approaches in two key ways: unlike SARM (Lu et al., 2021), which relies on domain-specific spiral sampling and prior knowledge of zero locations, SED is domain-agnostic; and unlike the earlier sparsity-aware DM (Ostheimer et al., 2025), which augments each dimension with a binary indicator and still denoises over all dimensions, SED removes zero-valued dimensions entirely so computation scales with the number of non-zeros rather than ambient dimensionality. The rate-distortion analysis in Section 2 provides a diagnostic framing absent from prior work, showing that dense DMs already allocate negligible information capacity to zero dimensions yet still process them fully during training and inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in scientific ML: dense diffusion models silently destroy semantically meaningful zeros in sparse data (calorimeter energy deposits, scRNA dropout events), undermining interpretability and downstream utility. The efficiency gains are substantial for the target regime (e.g., ~19× faster sampling on 95%-sparse calorimeter images, near-constant memory up to 27K dimensions), making SED particularly relevant for high-dimensional scientific data where dense models become intractable. Key limitations remain: autoregressive decoding introduces sequential sampling overhead and occasional dimension-ordering errors (down to 87.9% on particle physics background), and efficiency gains vanish at lower sparsity (Fashion-MNIST at 50%). Open questions include whether exact-zero preservation can be achieved without autoregressive decoding or fixed dimension orderings, and whether end-to-end joint training of SAVAE and the diffusion backbone (currently frozen) could improve quality.
