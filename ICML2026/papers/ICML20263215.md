## Problem

Fine-grained cross-modal alignment suffers from Semantic Sparsity Bias: dense visual patch sequences are supervised by sparse captions that capture only a fraction of visual detail, causing patch redundancy (irrelevant background tokens) and patch ambiguity (insufficient discriminative cues for specific regions). Existing patch-slimming methods rely exclusively on sparse captions, inadvertently discarding visually vital but textually unmentioned regions, while naive MLLM integration introduces semantic drift that conflicts with ground-truth queries. Additionally, standard global mean pooling dilutes the contribution of highly aligned patch-word pairs with irrelevant background scores.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (224×224 or 384×384, yielding 14×14, 24×24, 7×7, or 12×12 patches via ViT or Swin backbones) and a sparse text caption (encoded by BERT or CLIP-BERT) as primary inputs. An offline LLaVA-generated dense description (≈8 s/image) serves as an auxiliary Holistic Visual-Linguistic Anchor during training. The output is a scalar similarity score S(I,T) computed over a compact set of N_c re-synthesized visual tokens, used to rank candidates for retrieval; the framework also produces differentiable selection masks that partition patches into discriminative and contextual subsets.

## Method

SEPS introduces a Dual-Granularity Semantic Calibration (DGSC) module that computes tri-view attention scores (sparse-text, dense-text, and image-global) for each patch, fuses them with a learned visual saliency prior via a weighted consensus (β=0.6), and applies Gumbel-Softmax relaxation to obtain differentiable selection masks that separate patches into discriminative and contextual groups. Selected patches are re-synthesized into N_c compact tokens via learnable linear combinations (dynamic routers), compressing sequence length while preserving dual-granularity semantics. A Salience-Guided Metric Aggregation (SGMA) module then replaces mean pooling with bi-directional salience accumulation: it identifies maximum-response patch-word pairs (hard alignment) and learns scalar residuals (soft alignment) in both visual-dominant and textual-dominant directions, making the final score robust to background noise. Training uses a bidirectional triplet loss with hard negative mining plus an MSE ratio-constraint loss that regularizes the proportion of selected patches toward a target ρ.

## Results

On Flickr30K 1K with ViT-Base-224, SEPS achieves rSum 560.9, surpassing the previous best D2S-VSE (531.9) by 29.0 points and LAPS (507.3) by 53.6 points. The most dramatic gains appear in text-to-image retrieval: T2I R@1 jumps to 86.9 (vs. 68.5 for D2S-VSE and 62.5 for LAPS) on Flickr30K, and to 73.5 (vs. 46.3 for D2S-VSE) on MS-COCO 5K. SEPS maintains consistent superiority across all four backbone configurations (ViT-Base-224/384, Swin-Base-224/384), with rSum reaching 581.9 on Flickr30K 1K using Swin-Base-384.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEPS outperforms all four baselines across every backbone and dataset configuration. Against the most direct fine-grained competitor LAPS (Fu et al., 2024), SEPS gains +53.6 rSum on Flickr30K 1K (560.9 vs. 507.3) and +82.5 rSum on MS-COCO 5K (516.9 vs. 434.4), with the largest single-metric jump in T2I R@1 (+24.4 on Flickr30K, +29.0 on COCO 5K). Compared to the coarse-grained SOTA D2S-VSE (Liu et al., 2025c), SEPS leads by +29.0 rSum on Flickr30K and +71.3 on COCO 5K, confirming that structure-centric patch selection surpasses representation-level distillation. Against the older fine-grained baselines SCAN (Lee et al., 2018) and SGR (Diao et al., 2021), SEPS improves rSum by approximately 75 and 72 points respectively on Flickr30K 1K. The consistent margin across ViT and Swin backbones (224 and 384 resolutions) indicates the gains are architecture-agnostic rather than tied to a specific encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEPS extends the patch-slimming line initiated by LAPS (Fu et al., 2024), which pruned redundant visual patches using sparse caption supervision alone. The key departure is a structure-centric use of MLLM-generated dense text: rather than treating MLLM outputs as auxiliary signals for global feature distillation (as in D2S-VSE) or long-text pre-training (as in LongCLIP), SEPS employs them as a Holistic Visual-Linguistic Anchor that directly restructures the visual input via dual-granularity semantic consensus voting. The Salience-Guided Metric Aggregation module further departs from the standard global mean-pooling alignment used across SCAN, SGR, and CHAN by introducing a relevance-aware, max-response-driven scoring paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Semantic Sparsity Bias as a root-cause framing for misalignment in sparse-supervised retrieval is a useful conceptual contribution that clarifies why single-source (sparse-only) patch filtering systematically discards contextually vital regions. The practical impact is significant for text-to-image retrieval, where ambiguous sparse queries are the primary failure mode. However, the reliance on an 8-second-per-image LLaVA call for dense text generation limits real-time deployment, and the acknowledged risk of MLLM hallucination in the anchor text remains an open robustness question. Whether the dual-granularity consensus generalizes to video, multi-image, or open-vocabulary settings beyond the two standard benchmarks tested here is unexplored.
