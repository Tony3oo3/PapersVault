## Problem

Recovering the drift function of overdamped Langevin SDEs from sparsely sampled observations is ill-posed when the inter-observation interval τ is large: short-time Gaussian increment approximations break down, and multiple drifts can produce similar transition statistics. Existing temporal methods degrade with increasing τ, while geometric methods that exploit the invariant density are restricted to conservative (gradient) systems, leaving non-conservative dynamics with steady-state probability currents inaccessible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263222/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a time-ordered sequence of discrete state observations {O_k} sampled at a fixed (large) inter-observation interval τ, along with a known state-independent diffusion constant σ. It produces a nonparametric Gaussian-process posterior over the drift function f(x) evaluated on a grid spanning the observation volume, plus augmented latent trajectories filling the gaps between observations. Notably, the method operates entirely in the original d-dimensional ambient space and does not require estimating the intrinsic dimensionality of the underlying empirical manifold.

## Method

The framework iterates three steps within an EM loop: (α) a nonparametric Riemannian metric H(x) is learned from the observations via weighted local diagonal covariance, and geodesics connecting consecutive observations are computed by solving the geodesic ODE with a probabilistic solver; (β) latent state estimation between each pair of observations is formulated as a stochastic control problem (path integral control) where a time-dependent control u(x,t) steers a diffusion bridge to satisfy both endpoint constraints at the observations and a soft geometric penalty β‖Γ_t − x‖² toward the precomputed geodesics, solved via coupled forward (filtering) and backward PDEs; (γ) the drift is updated nonparametrically by placing a Gaussian-process prior on f and computing the posterior given the augmented paths. The key insight is that geodesics on the empirically induced metric encode the geometry of the invariant density as soft path constraints, reconciling temporal ordering with geometric structure without assuming conservative forces or a fixed manifold dimensionality.

## Results

On the Van der Pol system (σ=0.50, T=500), the proposed Geometric method achieves wRMSE of 0.462 at τ=80dt, degrading gracefully to 0.865 at τ=280dt, outperforming all baselines across the full range of inter-observation intervals. On the Hopf system, the method achieves wRMSE of 0.210–0.255, roughly 3–5× lower than GP (0.781–1.069) and LatentSDE (1.161–1.173). Notably, the method requires only T=500 time units, whereas [SF]²M and MFM_LAND require T=1500 yet still achieve wRMSE of 1.47–1.87 on Van der Pol. GSBM fails catastrophically at σ=0.50, with wRMSE reaching 26.19 at τ=280dt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Geometric method outperforms all eight baselines on the Van der Pol system for σ=0.50 across every τ, with the largest margins over GSBM (up to 30× lower wRMSE) and [SF]²M/MFM_LAND (roughly 2× lower). Against GP, the advantage grows with τ: at τ=280dt the Geometric method achieves 0.865 vs. GP's 1.542 (σ=0.50). KM-basis is competitive at small τ (0.495 vs. 0.462 at τ=80, σ=0.50) but degrades sharply to 1.732 at τ=280, while the Geometric method stays at 0.865. LatentSDE and LatentSDE+GP-pre remain flat around 1.15 regardless of τ, consistently outperformed by the Geometric method. SVISE shows non-monotonic behaviour and is outperformed at most τ values. On the Hopf system (Table 2), the Geometric method achieves 0.210–0.255 wRMSE, roughly 3–5× better than GP (0.781–1.069) and LatentSDE (1.161–1.173). On the Selkov system, the method ties GP at τ=200dt (0.682) and wins at τ=100dt (0.414 vs. 0.550), while SVISE degrades to 2.028.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reconciles two previously separate tracks in stochastic system identification: temporal methods (regressing state increments, e.g., Friedrich & Peinke 1997, Batz et al. 2018) that degrade at large inter-observation intervals, and geometric methods (diffusion maps, invariant density estimation) that are restricted to conservative or decoupled systems. It builds on the stochastic control / path-integral framework of Maoutsa & Opper (2022) and the Riemannian metric learning of Arvanitidis et al. (2019), but departs from both by using geodesics on the empirical manifold as soft geometric constraints within a variational path-augmentation step, rather than as a standalone inference tool. The GP drift estimation within an EM loop follows Batz et al. (2018), but the augmentation step replaces the previously used Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or Brownian bridges with geometry-guided controlled diffusions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: recovering non-conservative stochastic dynamics from sparse observations where short-time Gaussian approximations break down and geometric methods cannot be applied. The geometric inductive bias (geodesics as soft path constraints) provides a principled way to regularize the otherwise ill-posed inverse problem without assuming a parametric drift form. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic benchmarks with known ground truth, requirement of sufficiently long observation windows to characterize the invariant density, and restriction to state-independent diffusion. Open questions include validation on real sparse-observation data, extension to state-dependent diffusion (beyond Lamperti-transformable cases), and whether the learned metric could be incorporated directly into the augmented dynamics rather than used only for geodesic construction.
