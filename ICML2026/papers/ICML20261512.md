## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods rely on unimodal pre-trained encoders that transfer visual and linguistic knowledge independently, inducing domain shift and poor cross-modal semantic alignment. Additionally, handcrafted spatial priors (Euclidean distances, angles) suffer from spectral bias in deep networks, smoothing out high-frequency geometric details and entangling directional information with symmetric distance, which limits precise spatial reasoning in cluttered scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud subsampled to N=1024 points and a natural language description as input, producing both a 3D bounding box (3DREC) and a per-point segmentation mask (3DRES) in a unified multi-task framework. The point cloud is encoded via PointNet++ Set Abstraction with a frozen 3DCLIP backbone (hidden dim C=384), while text is encoded by a frozen RoBERTa model projected to the same dimension. The joint decoder follows the MCLN multi-branch transformer design, optimizing both tasks with a shared multi-task objective.

## Method

DSGCR introduces two complementary modules. Text-Aware Feature Tuning (TFT) combines Task-Agnostic Semantic Calibration (a learnable scale-shift on visual features) with a Dynamic Gated Adapter that computes a cross-modal gate α from visual importance scores and max-pooled textual relevance, then applies it to a bottleneck-projected residual to selectively enhance text-relevant visual features at each Transformer block. Decomposed Spectral Geometry (DSG) projects absolute coordinates through a learnable Fourier frequency matrix W_r ∈ R^{D×3} to obtain spectral features, then a nonlinear MLP maps these into a dual-channel latent space [u_i, v_i] from which pairwise relations are explicitly decomposed via element-wise products into a symmetric component S_ij = u_i⊙u_j + v_i⊙v_j (distance) and an antisymmetric component A_ij = v_i⊙u_j − u_i⊙v_j (direction). These Fourier-enhanced priors are concatenated with handcrafted geometric features to modulate language-conditioned location attention. The key insight is that parity decomposition in the spectral domain restores high-frequency directional details lost to spectral bias, while the learnable frequencies allow the model to adapt its bandwidth across spatial scales.

## Results

On ScanRefer 3DREC (two-stage), DSGCR achieves 58.48% Acc@0.25 and 46.97% Acc@0.5 Overall, surpassing the previous best MCLN by 2.05% and 1.86% respectively. On ScanRefer 3DRES, DSGCR attains 45.68% mIoU, exceeding MCLN (44.59%) by 1.09% and 3DRefTR (40.76%) by 4.92%. On Sr3D, DSGCR reaches 70.0% Overall Acc@0.25, and on the challenging Nr3D Hard split it scores 52.0%, outperforming UniSpace-3D (48.9%) and EDA (45.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSGCR outperforms all 18 baselines on the primary ScanRefer 3DREC benchmark. The closest competitor, MCLN (Qian et al., 2024b), is surpassed by 2.05% Acc@0.25 and 1.86% Acc@0.5 in the two-stage setting, with the gap widening to 2.31% on the Multiple split where instance disambiguation is most challenging. Among single-stage methods, TSP3D (Guo et al., 2025) achieves a slightly higher Acc@0.5 (46.01% vs. 43.44%) but DSGCR leads on Acc@0.25 (55.85% vs. 55.30%) and on the Multiple split. On 3DRES, DSGCR beats MCLN by 1.09% mIoU and 3DRefTR by 4.92%, while on Sr3D it edges out UniSpace-3D (70.0% vs. 69.8%) and on Nr3D Hard it leads UniSpace-3D by 3.1% (52.0% vs. 48.9%). Earlier baselines such as EDA, 3DJCG, BUTD-DETR, and 3DRefTR are all outperformed by 3–10+ points across metrics, confirming that the gains come from the proposed TFT and DSG modules rather than architectural scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MCLN multi-branch decoder architecture and the ViL3D handcrafted pairwise geometric priors, extending both with targeted innovations. It departs from prior 3D PEFT methods (IDPT, DAPT, PointGST) that target unimodal tasks by introducing the first parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy explicitly designed for cross-modal 3D visual grounding, injecting linguistic context into the visual hierarchy via a dynamic gated adapter. The Decomposed Spectral Geometry module extends random Fourier features from fixed Gaussian frequency sampling to learnable frequencies and, crucially, breaks the inherent symmetry constraint of standard RFF by explicitly decomposing pairwise relations into symmetric (cosine) and antisymmetric (sine) spectral components grounded in Bochner's theorem and Euler's identity parity decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent limitations in 3DVG—cross-modal domain shift from independently trained unimodal encoders and spectral bias that smooths out high-frequency directional geometry—within a single unified framework, and the memory-efficient O(ND) formulation of DSG (vs. O(N²D) naive pairwise Fourier features) makes the approach practical for large point clouds. However, the inductive biases of symmetric/antisymmetric decomposition may limit generalization to scenes where spatial relations are not well-captured by pairwise parity, and the paper acknowledges that comparisons are limited to handcrafted-prior methods without evaluating against learnable geometric modeling or MLLM-based grounding. Open questions include whether the spectral decomposition generalizes to larger-scale or more diverse 3D data, and how these components integrate with emerging foundation-model-based 3D grounding pipelines.
