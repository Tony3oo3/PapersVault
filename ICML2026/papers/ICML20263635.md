## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models rely on two separate visual tokenizers—one for understanding and one for generation—which partitions the representation space and forces generated images to be re-encoded before the model can interpret them. This breaks the desired shared-context property and hinders true unification. The paper addresses how a single discrete visual tokenizer can simultaneously satisfy the high-level semantic needs of understanding and the low-level detail needs of generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263635/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts (for generation), reference image + text prompt (for editing), or image + text prompt (for understanding) in ChatML format, and produces generated images up to 1024×1024, edited images, or text responses. A key structural detail is that visual content is represented as 64-bit binary vectors with a 32×32 spatial compression ratio, so a 512×512 image maps to only 256 autoregressive tokens; with decoder-side 2D bicubic upsampling, a 1024×1024 output requires predicting just 256 tokens. The DiT decoder is conditioned solely on visual tokens (no text prompt), keeping all semantic and layout decisions in the autoregressive model.

## Method

UNIAR integrates Binary Spherical Quantization (BSQ) into a pretrained SigLiP2 ViT encoder, fusing multi-level features from four encoder layers to preserve both shallow-layer details and deep-layer semantics, then quantizing into 64-bit binary vectors that yield a theoretical 2^64 vocabulary without an explicit codebook. The unified autoregressive backbone (Qwen3-8B) performs parallel bitwise prediction, simultaneously predicting all BSQ indices across a 2×2 spatial grid and multiple DeepStack levels per step, reducing visual sequence length by 32×. A DiT-based decoder trained with Conditional Flow Matching reconstructs pixels from the discrete tokens, with optional resolution upsampling to decouple AR budget from output resolution. Training proceeds in three stages—1T-token pretraining, 50B-token SFT, and GRPO-based RL with HPSv2, PaddleOCR, and object-detection rewards—while the visual tokenizer and decoder remain frozen throughout. Random bitwise index flipping during pretraining simulates autoregressive error accumulation, stabilizing generation at high temperatures and enabling effective RL exploration.

## Results

On GenEval, UNIAR achieves an overall score of 0.85 (0.86 with prompt rewriting), surpassing GPT-4o (0.84) and FLUX.1-dev (0.82) for instruction-following in text-to-image generation. On text rendering, UNIAR scores 0.873 on OneIG-EN (beating GPT-4o's 0.857) and 0.917 on LongText-EN (beating Gemini 2.5 Flash Image's 0.869). On ImgEdit-Bench, UNIAR achieves 3.73 overall, outperforming FLUX.1 Kontext (3.71) and other unified models. On multimodal understanding, UNIAR reaches 833 on OCRBench, 91.4 on DocVQA, and 62.3 on MVBench, competitive with dedicated VLMs like Qwen3-VL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, UNIAR (0.85) outperforms GPT-4o (0.84), FLUX.1-dev (0.82), X-Omni (0.83), and BAGEL (0.82), while tying with OmniGen2† and Emu3.5 (both 0.86) when prompt rewriting is applied; however, BAGEL† (0.88) remains slightly ahead. On text rendering, UNIAR clearly wins: 0.873 on OneIG-EN beats GPT-4o (0.857) and X-Omni (0.901 is close but UNIAR is competitive), and 0.917 on LongText-EN surpasses Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (0.869) by a notable margin. On image editing (ImgEdit-Bench), UNIAR (3.73) edges out FLUX.1 Kontext (3.71) and substantially beats BAGEL (3.20) and OmniGen2 (3.44), though it trails the dedicated editing models Qwen-Image-Edit (4.27) and GPT-Image-1 (4.20). On multimodal understanding, UNIAR outperforms all other unified models (BAGEL, X-Omni, Janus-Pro, Show-o2) on OCR-oriented benchmarks (OCRBench 833 vs. X-Omni's 704; DocVQA 91.4 vs. X-Omni's 88.6) and MVBench (62.3 vs. Show-o2's 56.4), but lags significantly behind the understanding-only Qwen3-VL on MMMU (44.3 vs. 69.6), which the authors attribute to the absence of pure-text pretraining data and lack of RL for understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIAR builds on Binary Spherical Quantization (Zhao et al., 2025a) for lookup-free bitwise tokenization, Infinity (Han et al., 2025b) for bitwise prediction and random index flipping, and DeepStack (Meng et al., 2024) for multi-level feature fusion across ViT layers. Its key departure from prior unified multimodal models (BAGEL, Janus-Pro, X-Omni) is the use of a single discrete visual tokenizer shared across understanding and generation, rather than the dual-tokenizer design that partitions the representation space. The parallel bitwise prediction mechanism and the text-independent DiT decoder further distinguish UNIAR from X-Omni and NextFlow, which condition their decoders on both visual tokens and text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shared-context property—where the model directly interprets its own generated visual tokens without re-encoding—enables an emergent interleaved generation-understanding capability that dual-tokenizer systems cannot replicate without an extra encoding pass. The 32× autoregressive compression (256 tokens for a 1024×1024 image) offers a practical efficiency advantage for deployment. However, the absence of pure-text pretraining data limits reasoning benchmarks (MMMU), RL is restricted to generation only, and the authors acknowledge significant room for scaling data and model parameters. Open questions include whether the single-tokenizer paradigm scales to video, whether RL for understanding can close the remaining gap with dedicated VLMs, and whether the bitwise quantization approach generalizes beyond the 64-bit dimension used here.
