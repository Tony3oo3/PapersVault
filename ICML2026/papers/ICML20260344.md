## Problem

Sharpness-aware optimizers like SAM are inherently local and struggle to escape sharp basins in multimodal landscapes, while sampling-based optimizers like SGLD provide global exploration but target a Gibbs measure that is indifferent to landscape geometry. No existing method simultaneously offers global exploration and an inductive bias toward flat minima without incurring extra gradient evaluations or auxiliary state variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260344/cropped_page15_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB training images with labels (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, noisy variants CIFAR-10N/100N, WebVision, and pre-trained ViT-B/16 for fine-tuning) and produces optimized d-dimensional parameter vectors. Downstream, these parameters yield class predictions with NLL, Bayesian predictive entropy for uncertainty quantification, and OOD detection scores (AUROC/AUPR) on SVHN. A key operational detail is that each gradient is evaluated at a randomly perturbed parameter θ+ε rather than at θ itself, with the perturbation scale σ fully determined by the inverse temperature β through the coupling σ = β^{-(1+η)/4}, so only β needs to be tuned.

## Method

fSGLD replaces the unbiased stochastic gradient in standard SGLD with a perturbed gradient ∇U(θ_k + ε_{k+1}, X_{k+1}) where ε ~ N(0, σ²I_d), while retaining the Langevin noise term √(2λβ⁻¹)ξ. By Taylor expansion, the expected perturbed objective g_ε(θ) = E_ε[u(θ+ε)] equals the Hessian-trace regularized objective v(θ) = u(θ) + (σ²/2)tr(H(θ)) plus a higher-order remainder, meaning fSGLD implicitly minimizes a flatness-biased surrogate without any explicit second-order computation. The theoretically prescribed coupling σ = β^{-(1+η)/4} for η ∈ (0,1) ensures the remainder is negligible, so the invariant measure of fSGLD is provably close (in W₂) to the target Gibbs distribution π*_{β,σ} ∝ exp(-βv(θ)) that concentrates on global flat minima. The algorithm requires exactly one gradient evaluation per step and no auxiliary variables, matching the computational and memory cost of vanilla SGD and SGLD, and the paper provides non-asymptotic W₁ convergence bounds and an explicit excess-risk guarantee for the Hessian-trace regularized objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-10N with ResNet-34, fSGLD achieves 91.37% top-1 accuracy at 23.7 s/epoch, matching SAM (91.53%) and ASAM (91.73%) while running at roughly half their wall-clock cost (41.3–41.4 s/epoch). On the harder CIFAR-100N benchmark, fSGLD leads all baselines with 61.51% (vs. 59.18% for SAM and 60.79% for ASAM). In the Bayesian classification setting (Table 1), fSGLD attains the lowest NLL on both CIFAR-10 (0.144) and CIFAR-100 (0.810), outperforming Entropy-MCMC (0.162 / 0.840) and SAM (0.166 / 0.876). For OOD detection on CIFAR-10→SVHN, fSGLD achieves the best AUROC (98.91%) and AUPR (99.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM and ASAM, fSGLD achieves comparable or superior accuracy on the harder benchmarks (CIFAR-100N: 61.51% vs. 59.18%/60.79% for ResNet-34; WV-1: 73.95% vs. 73.49%/73.46%) while requiring only ~23.7 s/epoch versus ~41.3 s/epoch — roughly half the computational cost due to a single gradient evaluation per step. Against standard SGD and AdamW, fSGLD consistently wins by 2–3% on CIFAR-100N and 2–4% on WV-1, confirming that the flatness bias translates into tangible generalization gains under label noise. In the Bayesian setting (Table 1), fSGLD achieves the lowest NLL on both CIFAR-10 (0.144) and CIFAR-100 (0.810), beating Entropy-MCMC (0.162/0.840), SAM (0.166/0.876), and SGLD (0.167/0.854), while matching or exceeding their accuracy. For OOD detection (Table 2), fSGLD attains the best AUROC (98.91%) and AUPR (99.44%) on CIFAR-10→SVHN, and the best AUPR (88.01%) on CIFAR-100→SVHN, outperforming Entropy-MCMC and ASAM. In the ViT-B/16 fine-tuning setting (Table 4), fSGLD reaches 75.67% on CIFAR-100N (best) and 96.45% on CIFAR-10N (second-best to SAM's 96.75%) at 345.8 s/epoch, roughly half the cost of SAM (656.7 s/epoch) and ASAM (662.5 s/epoch).

## Relation to Prior Work

fSGLD sits at the intersection of two established lines: stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (Welling & Teh, 2011) for global exploration of nonconvex landscapes, and random weight perturbation (RWP) for implicit flatness bias (Li et al., 2024). It departs from SAM/ASAM, which are inherently local and require double gradient evaluations, and from Entropy-MCMC, which introduces auxiliary state variables and relies on strong convexity for its analysis. The key conceptual departure is showing that the specific coupling σ = β^{-(1+η)/4} between perturbation scale and inverse temperature makes the fSGLD invariant measure provably close to a Hessian-trace regularized Gibbs distribution, yielding the first non-asymptotic global convergence guarantees for a flatness-aware Langevin-based optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap in the flatness-aware optimization literature: prior methods either lack global convergence guarantees (SAM, ASAM) or require additional computational/memory overhead (Entropy-MCMC, explicit Hessian penalties). fSGLD achieves flatness bias at the same cost as vanilla SGD/SGLD, making it a practical drop-in replacement. Open questions include extending the analysis to semiconvex objectives (where the gradient is only one-sided Lipschitz), applying the method to diffusion-based generative models, and understanding whether the exponential dimension dependence in the constants can be improved. The β-σ coupling, while theoretically prescribed, introduces a trade-off between approximation accuracy and flatness strength that may limit practical tuning flexibility.
