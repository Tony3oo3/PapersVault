## Problem

Agentic AI systems that plan autonomously and interact with environments over extended horizons pose a fundamental control problem: how can weaker overseers maintain meaningful oversight of stronger agents? Existing scalable oversight approaches are largely heuristic, provide empirical rather than formal guarantees, and focus on single-turn settings rather than sequential trajectories where actions compound and distribution shift is inherent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263533/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the method receives a state s, a finite set of candidate actions C(s) with primary utility scores U(s,a), a designated baseline action a_0(s) always included in C(s), and N auxiliary overseer scores q_i(s,a) for each candidate. The overseers are arbitrary real-valued scoring functions (e.g., smaller LLMs, heuristic critics) that need not understand the primary agent's full capabilities. The method outputs a selected action (argmax of U(s,a) − λ_t·Δ(s,a)) and an updated conservatism parameter λ_{t+1} = max{0, λ_t + η(ℓ_t − α)}, where ℓ_t is the realized loss from the executed action.

## Method

CCO generalizes Attainable Utility Preservation by replacing Q-value functions with arbitrary scoring functions, defining the penalty Δ(s,a) = Σ_i |q_i(s,a) − q_i(s,a_0)| as the L1 deviation from the baseline across all overseer signals. The key theoretical contribution is proving that under mild finiteness conditions, the CCO decision family is 'eventually safe': there exists a threshold λ̄ beyond which the agent always selects the zero-loss baseline, satisfying the structural assumption required by Conformal Decision Theory. This yields a finite-time guarantee that the empirical loss rate converges to any user-specified target α with an O(1/t) transient, holding for arbitrary adversarial state sequences with no distributional assumptions. The online controller simply increases conservatism after losses and relaxes it after safe actions, making the approach a deployment-time protocol that requires no retraining and adapts to non-stationarity.

## Results

On SWE-bench Lite (300 instances), CCO's realized misalignment rate closely tracks the target α across the full range (α=0.01→1.3%, α=0.05→5.0%, α=0.10→9.7%, α=0.20→17.0%), with a maximum deviation of only 3.0 percentage points. Solve rate improves monotonically from 13.0% (α=0.01) to 19.7% (α=0.25) as conservatism relaxes, interpolating between the always-safe baseline (12.0% solve, 0% misalignment) and the unconstrained agent (29.0% solve, 55.0% misalignment). On MACHIAVELLI (Hero of Kendrickstone, 100 steps), CCO at α=0.15 achieves 12.7% violations with reward 55.5±7.6, matching the unconstrained agent's reward (54.0±7.3) while halving its violation rate (21.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CCO against three baselines: Always Baseline (always selects the safest/lowest-resource candidate), Unconstrained (always selects the highest-utility candidate ignoring oversight), and an adaptive majority-vote (MV) baseline that adjusts an acceptance threshold online to target the same α. CCO interpolates smoothly between the two extremes, with realized misalignment tracking the target α to within 3.0pp on SWE-bench and within statistical noise on MACHIAVELLI. Under a distribution-shift stress test (misalignment probability p jumping from 0.2 to 0.8 mid-stream), CCO stays within ~2pp of target throughout all phases, while adaptive MV overshoots substantially (e.g., 18.2% vs. 6.1% at α=0.05 in the high-misalignment phase) and remains elevated even after the shift reverses. On MACHIAVELLI, CCO at α=0.15 matches the unconstrained agent's reward (55.5 vs. 54.0) while reducing violations from 21.5% to 12.7%, demonstrating that ethical play often coincides with high-reward trajectories in narrative environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCO generalizes Attainable Utility Preservation (Turner et al., 2020) by replacing Q-value-based attainable-utility penalties with arbitrary real-valued scoring functions, and by replacing the fixed conservatism parameter λ with an online conformal controller drawn from Conformal Decision Theory (Lekeufack et al., 2024). Within the broader scalable oversight literature (Amodei et al., 2016; Bowman et al., 2022; Irving et al., 2018), CCO occupies a distinct niche: rather than modifying training or relying on multi-agent debate protocols, it provides a deployment-time penalty-and-calibration mechanism with distribution-free finite-time guarantees tailored to sequential agentic settings. The paper explicitly positions itself as complementary to weak-to-strong generalization (Burns et al., 2024) and AI control (Greenblatt et al., 2024), contributing formal safety guarantees where prior scalable oversight methods offer primarily empirical evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCO provides one of the first practical scalable oversight protocols with provable, distribution-free bounds on long-run violation rates in sequential decision-making, addressing a gap where prior methods were either heuristic or restricted to single-turn settings. Key limitations remain: the framework assumes a designated zero-loss baseline, controls a long-run rate rather than per-action tail risk (making it complementary to, not a replacement for, extreme-event monitoring), and the SWE-bench evaluation is adversarial by construction rather than naturalistic. Open questions include how guarantees degrade when ground-truth losses are sparse or estimated by fallible judges, whether the approach extends to continuous action spaces, and how to handle the non-trivial problem of specifying or learning the baseline action in open-ended domains.
