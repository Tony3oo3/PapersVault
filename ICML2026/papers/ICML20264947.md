## Problem

Current MLLM perception benchmarks suffer from an 'evaluation paradox': leaderboards are saturated in the high-score regime while models remain perceptually brittle in deployment, because conventional metrics (e.g., CLIPScore, averaged multi-aspect schemes) use linear averaging that dilutes fatal localized hallucinations with general semantic overlap. Additionally, many benchmarks rely on information-poor images and closed-form questions that allow models to shortcut through linguistic priors rather than genuine visual grounding, leaving the field without a calibrated diagnostic tool that mirrors the non-linear, error-sensitive nature of human perceptual judgment.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline consumes three inputs per sample: the original RGB image, a free-form text caption generated by the model under test, and a pre-constructed set of atomic rubrics split into Must-Right (essential facts) and Easy-Wrong (common hallucination traps) groups. An LLM judge (GPT-OSS-120B) evaluates each rubric independently, producing a boolean True/False per item. The final output is a gated score S ∈ [0,1]: if any Must-Right rubric fails, the gate G=0 forces S=0 regardless of Easy-Wrong performance; otherwise S equals the fraction of Easy-Wrong rubrics passed, making the metric binary at the gate level and continuous only for models that satisfy all mandatory constraints.

## Method

PerceptionRubrics introduces a caption-centric construction pipeline that bypasses the visual grounding gap of direct image-to-rubric generation. First, 1,038 information-dense images across 7 domains are filtered by a complexity scorer (Step3-VL-10B). Second, 'Golden Captions' (avg. 784 words) are produced via a Circular Peer-Review mechanism where three frontier MLLMs (GPT-5.2, Gemini-3-Pro, Seed-1.8) iteratively critique, rank, and rewrite each other's descriptions over ≤2 rounds, followed by a discard-on-divergence protocol where human annotators verify only converged samples. Third, Gemini-3-Pro distills each Golden Caption into atomic rubrics from two complementary angles: Must-Right rubrics (a priori essential facts, ~4 per image) and Easy-Wrong rubrics (a posteriori common pitfalls mined from a diverse pool of baseline model predictions, ~7.5 per image), yielding 12,004 total rubrics. The key scoring innovation is the gated mechanism: Must-Right rubrics act as a conjunctive gatekeeper (product of all indicators), so a single factual error zeroes the score, while Easy-Wrong rubrics provide fine-grained differentiation only among gate-passing responses, directly encoding the human intuition that a single hallucinated digit in a financial table is a binary failure rather than a minor fluctuation.

## Results

On PerceptionRubrics (1,038 images, 12,004 rubrics), Gemini-3-Pro achieves the highest overall gated score of 66.90%, followed by Seed-2.0-Pro (63.58%) and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (61.95%, best open-source). GPT-4o-2024-08-06 scores only 12.88% overall, exposing severe perceptual brittleness despite strong reasoning benchmarks. The benchmark achieves Pearson r=0.992 and Spearman ρ=1.000 correlation with Vision Arena human-preference Elo ratings, substantially outperforming DOCCI and DetailCaps. A persistent ~5% perception gap separates the best open-source model from proprietary leaders, and the 'Reliability Gap' shows models achieve high atomic rubric accuracy (e.g., 94.48% M-R Item for Gemini-3-Pro) but much lower gate-pass rates (80.15%), revealing fragility under strict conjunctive constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against two existing captioning benchmarks—DOCCI (Onoe et al., ECCV 2024) and DetailCaps (Dong et al., 2024)—on alignment with human preference as measured by Vision Arena Elo ratings across six overlapping models. PerceptionRubrics achieves a near-perfect Spearman rank correlation of 1.000 and Pearson r=0.992 with Vision Arena, while DOCCI assigns nearly indistinguishable scores to models with markedly different human-preference ratings, indicating very limited discriminative power. DetailCaps also shows substantially weaker agreement with human-preference scores than PerceptionRubrics. Beyond benchmark-to-benchmark alignment, the gated scoring mechanism reveals performance stratification invisible to holistic metrics: GPT-4o, a widely used proprietary model, drops to 12.88% overall (vs. 66.90% for Gemini-3-Pro), and the best open-source model (Qwen3.5, 61.95%) trails the proprietary frontier by over 5%, a gap that converges in reasoning benchmarks but persists in fine-grained perception. GUI is the dominant failure domain across all models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 0.12%), while Natural Scenes are the easiest (up to 79.01% for Gemini-3-Pro), confirming that information-dense, spatially constrained inputs remain the key bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the rubric-based reward modeling paradigm—established in text generation by works like RM-R1, SPCT, and RaR—into the multimodal perception domain, where no comparable fine-grained auditing system previously existed. It departs from holistic captioning benchmarks (DOCCI, DetailCaps, CapArena) that rely on linear similarity scores or sparse references, and from closed-form perception suites (MMBench, OCRBench, VSIBench) that permit linguistic shortcuts, by introducing atomic, binary-verifiable rubrics with a non-linear gated scoring mechanism. The circular peer-review consensus pipeline for golden caption construction is a novel data-generation strategy that synthesizes ideas from ensemble distillation and human-in-the-loop verification, but no single prior method is directly extended.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a 'Reliability Gap'—models pass individual atomic checks at high rates yet fail the strict conjunctive gate—providing a diagnostic signal that conventional metrics dilute. The persistent ~5% open-closed perception deficit (contrary to near-convergence in reasoning tasks) suggests fine-grained visual grounding remains a fundamental capacity bottleneck, not merely a training-data issue. Key limitations include reliance on a single LLM judge (GPT-OSS-120B) for scoring, a relatively small image set (1,038), and golden captions that are themselves model-generated (albeit human-verified), raising questions about whether the rubrics inherit residual biases from the proposer (Gemini-3-Pro). Open questions include whether gated scoring generalizes beyond captioning to other multimodal tasks and whether the open-closed gap narrows with continued scaling.
