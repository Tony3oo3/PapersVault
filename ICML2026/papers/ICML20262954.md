## Problem

Cross-resolution person re-identification suffers from feature mismatch between low-resolution queries and high-resolution gallery images, but existing approaches either apply super-resolution (which restores pixels without directly correcting embedding-space distortion) or enforce resolution-invariant representations (which entangle identity and resolution differences, making training difficult). No prior work had identified that the HR-LR feature discrepancy in standard ReID backbones follows a consistent, structured semantic direction in the embedding space that could be exploited directly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262954/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired LR and HR images of the same person, from which a frozen TransReID backbone extracts 3840-dimensional feature vectors. The VP module then takes only the LR feature vector as input and outputs a pseudo-HR feature vector of the same dimensionality, which is used for cosine-similarity-based retrieval ranking against HR gallery features. Training requires only 5,000 paired feature samples (not images), and the module operates entirely post-extraction with no modification to the backbone.

## Method

The key empirical finding is that identity-averaged HR-LR feature differences converge to a stable semantic direction in embedding space, validated via CCA (canonical correlations ~0.48–0.49 vs. ~0.25–0.31 for random pairs) and Pearson correlation (mean r > 0.53, with 94–98% of identity groups exceeding r > 0.4). Motivated by this, VPFA introduces a lightweight Vector Panning (VP) module: a three-layer MLP (3840→2048→2048→3840, each layer with Linear + LayerNorm + ReLU) whose output passes through a Tanh gate and is added residually to the LR feature, producing a pseudo-HR representation. The module is trained with a simple MSE loss (VPL) between the predicted pseudo-HR feature and the ground-truth HR feature, which simultaneously aligns direction and magnitude. Weights are initialized from N(0, 10⁻³) with zero biases so the initial mapping is near-identity, and training converges in 120 epochs with Adam (lr=2e-4) on just 5,000 feature pairs—no backbone retraining, no pair mining, and no adversarial components are needed.

## Results

VPFA achieves state-of-the-art Rank-1 accuracy on all four CR-ReID benchmarks: 94.1% on MLR-Market-1501, 94.7% on MLR-CUHK03, 50.5% on MLR-VIPeR, and 66.6% on CAVIAR. On the largest dataset (MLR-Market-1501), it surpasses the previous best IFRSW (92.3%) by 1.8% and PS-HRNet (91.5%) by 2.6% in Rank-1. The module adds negligible overhead, running at 4.4M samples/second versus 80.8 samples/second for the TransReID backbone, with only 24.14M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPFA outperforms all 20 compared baselines on every dataset where both methods report results. Against the strongest prior CR-ReID methods, it beats IFRSW (92.3%) by +1.8% Rank-1 on MLR-Market-1501, PS-HRNet (91.5%) by +2.6%, and LRAR (90.1%) by +4.0%; on CAVIAR the gap widens dramatically, with VPFA reaching 66.6% versus LRAR's 63.6% and PS-HRNet's 48.2%. Compared to SR-based methods, the advantage is most pronounced: VPFA exceeds CSR-GAN by +17.7% Rank-1 on Market-1501 and SING by +19.7%, confirming that feature-space alignment substantially outperforms pixel-level restoration. Against resolution-invariant approaches (RAIN, PRI, PyrNet+PRI), VPFA gains 3–13% Rank-1 depending on dataset. On the smaller MLR-VIPeR benchmark, VPFA (50.5%) edges out JBIM (49.7%) and IFRSW (50.1%). In the post-hoc adaptation comparison (Table 9), VPFA (94.1%) surpasses FiLM (92.1%), Adapter (91.8%), and a simple mean-vector offset (91.2%), validating that the learned nonlinear panning is more effective than generic conditioning or linear correction strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant CR-ReID paradigms—super-resolution-based enhancement (SING, CSR-GAN, INTACT, PS-HRNet) and resolution-invariant representation learning (RAIN, CAD, LRAR, PRI)—by reframing the problem as a feature-space translation rather than pixel restoration or joint training. The core conceptual move is borrowing the 'semantic direction' analogy from word embeddings (Mikolov et al., 2013) and DeepMind's work on MLP-based factual retrieval, applying it to show that HR-LR feature discrepancies in ReID backbones form a consistent, learnable offset. The resulting VPFA module is a lightweight gated-residual MLP trained post-hoc on frozen backbone features, positioning itself as a plug-in correction layer rather than an architectural change or end-to-end pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The post-hoc, backbone-agnostic design makes VPFA immediately deployable on top of any existing ReID system without retraining, which is practically valuable for surveillance deployments where upgrading the entire pipeline is costly. The cross-modal generalization to visible-infrared and text-image ReID suggests the 'pan along a learned direction' principle may be broadly applicable to any paired-modality feature alignment problem. Key open questions include robustness to extreme resolution loss where identity cues are genuinely absent, whether the resolution shift is truly a single direction or a low-rank input-dependent subspace (as the authors themselves caution), and performance under real-world degradations like motion blur and compression artifacts beyond synthetic downsampling.
