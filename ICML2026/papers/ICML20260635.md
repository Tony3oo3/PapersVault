## Problem

Offline model-based RL typically trains a world model and policy in separate stages with mismatched objectives (likelihood maximization vs. return maximization), and the resulting policies lack deployment robustness—small adversarial noise in the environment causes significant performance degradation. How to jointly adapt the world model alongside the policy under a unified objective that guarantees robustness remains an open challenge, as naive return-driven model adaptation in offline settings can cause the model to diverge from true dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) transitions collected by an arbitrary behavior policy, with the world model parameterized as a multivariate Gaussian with diagonal covariance over the (next-state, reward) output. It produces a stochastic policy mapping state observations to action distributions, optimized for worst-case expected return within a KL-divergence-bounded uncertainty set of world models rather than a single point estimate. The uncertainty radius ε is set based on the dataset size, state-action coverage, and desired confidence level, as formalized in Theorems 3.2 and 3.3.

## Method

ROMBRL formulates robust offline MBRL as a constrained maximin problem where the policy (Stackelberg leader) maximizes worst-case return while the world model (follower) is updated adversarially to minimize it, subject to a KL-divergence constraint keeping the model within an uncertainty set around the MLE. The key innovation is solving this via constrained Stackelberg learning dynamics: the policy update incorporates second-order information about how the best-response model and its dual variable λ implicitly depend on the policy, captured through a matrix H that accounts for the curvature of the constraint surface. For computational efficiency, second-order derivatives are approximated using the Fisher information matrix, and the inverse of the Hessian is computed via the Woodbury matrix identity exploiting the low-rank structure of sample-based estimators, yielding linear scaling in model parameter count. A gradient-mask mechanism further mitigates distributional shift in the replay buffer as the world model evolves during training.

## Results

On the 12-task D4RL MuJoCo benchmark (with 5% Gaussian measurement noise), ROMBRL achieves an average normalized score of 77.7, outperforming the next-best method MOBILE (70.7) and the closest robust baseline RAMBO (55.8). Under noisy deployment, ROMBRL incurs only a −0.6% performance drop relative to the clean setting, compared to 11.1% for MOBILE, 20.2% for RAMBO, and 26.6% for EDAC. On the highly stochastic Tokamak Control benchmark, ROMBRL achieves the best average negative tracking error of −47.1 (std 1.2), substantially outperforming CQL (−68.3), COMBO (−73.6), and the model-based baselines RAMBO (−167.6), MOBILE (−155.5), and BAMCTS (−165.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROMBRL ranks first on 7 of 12 D4RL MuJoCo tasks and second on 4, achieving the highest average score (77.7) across all baselines. Against the closest robust baseline RAMBO, ROMBRL improves the average score by 21.9 points (77.7 vs. 55.8) with Cohen's d = 22.9, and reduces the noisy-deployment performance drop from 20.2% to −0.6%. Compared to the top-performing standard method MOBILE (70.7 average), ROMBRL gains 7.0 points on average and is far more robust (−0.6% vs. 11.1% drop). On the Tokamak Control benchmark, ROMBRL (−47.1 average tracking error) outperforms all baselines, with the largest margins over model-based methods RAMBO (−167.6), MOBILE (−155.5), and BAMCTS (−165.5), whose high variance (std > 47) indicates poor robustness in the stochastic setting. Model-free baselines CQL (60.7) and EDAC (53.7) trail ROMBRL by 17 and 24 points respectively on D4RL, while robust model-free methods RORL (62.3), TRACER (44.1), and RFQI (30.9) show progressively larger gaps, confirming that the explicit world model plus Stackelberg dynamics provides a distinct advantage over value-based robustness approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust offline MBRL line initiated by RAMBO (Rigter et al., 2022) by replacing its alternating updates—which implicitly treat the policy-model interaction as a symmetric zero-sum game—with asymmetric Stackelberg learning dynamics (Fiez et al., 2020) that capture the leader-follower structure of the maximin problem. It also departs from the standard two-stage offline MBRL pipeline (learn model, then train policy) by unifying both under a single constrained maximin objective, building on the formulation of CPPO-LR (Uehara & Sun, 2023) while correcting its theoretical analysis. The direction of adversarial model adaptation (minimizing return rather than maximizing it, as in online MBRL) is a deliberate inversion motivated by the conservatism principle in offline RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROMBRL addresses two persistent pain points in offline MBRL—objective mismatch between model learning and policy optimization, and poor deployment robustness—within a single framework with formal suboptimality guarantees, which prior heuristic-based ensemble penalty methods lack. The near-zero performance drop under 5% measurement noise (−0.6% vs. 11–27% for baselines) and the ablation showing that constraint-surface dynamics (λ) matter as much as model-parameter adaptation are practically significant for real-world deployment. Open questions include scalability to transformer-based world models, tight covering-number bounds for deep function classes, and validation on sparse-reward or real-robotic settings, all of which the authors acknowledge as future work.
