## Problem

Existing continual post-training (CPT) research for multimodal large language models focuses on auxiliary strategies like data replay, model expansion, and parameter regularization, but the fundamental role of the learning paradigm itself—supervised fine-tuning versus reinforcement fine-tuning—has been largely unexplored. This paper asks whether the choice between SFT and RFT inherently determines a model's susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting when sequentially adapting to new downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) receives multimodal prompts consisting of a text question paired with one or more RGB images, and produces a text answer. Two prompt formats are used: a NoCoT template expecting a direct answer, and a CoT template requiring reasoning enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{}. For RFT, the model generates multiple candidate responses per prompt (group size 8 for GRPO), and a scalar reward combines semantic accuracy (0.9 weight) with format compliance (0.1 weight) via regex matching on the CoT structure.

## Method

The paper's core finding is that RFT (via GRPO, ReMax, or RLOO) inherently resists catastrophic forgetting in sequential CPT without any explicit continual-learning mechanism, achieving near multi-task-training performance (AvgAcc 60.0% vs. 62.9% upper bound) while SFT degrades severely (AvgAcc 54.0%, FM -10.4%). Ablations rule out KL penalty and CoT reasoning as primary drivers; instead, the mechanism is RFT's selective gradient updates: when all rollouts for a sample receive identical rewards, the advantage collapses to zero and no parameter update occurs, so the model naturally ignores uninformative or beyond-capability samples that would otherwise inject noise. Building on this insight, the authors propose RIF-RFT, a rollout-based instance filtering algorithm that pre-screens each new task's dataset by discarding samples where N policy rollouts yield zero reward, reducing training data by ~50% and cutting wall-clock time by ~44% while still substantially outperforming SFT in forgetting mitigation.

## Results

On the 7-task multimodal continual post-training sequence (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), GRPO achieves an average accuracy (AvgAcc) of 60.0% with a forgetting measure (FM) of −2.3%, compared to SFT's 54.0% AvgAcc and −10.4% FM, approaching the multi-task SFT upper bound of 62.9%. On general benchmarks after the full sequence, GRPO improves MMMU from 52.1% to 54.2% and POPE from 86.6% to 88.5%, while SFT degrades MMMU to 40.1% (↓12.0) and MMLU-Pro to 30.6% (↓16.9). The proposed RIF-RFT variant retains 57.5% AvgAcc using only ~50% of training data, still substantially outperforming SFT (54.0%) while reducing wall-clock time by ~44%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRPO (the primary RFT method) outperforms SFT by 6.0 points in AvgAcc (60.0% vs. 54.0%) and reduces forgetting by 8.1 points in FM (−2.3% vs. −10.4%), approaching the multi-task SFT upper bound of 62.9% without any replay or regularization. Among RFT variants, GRPO achieves the highest AvgAcc (60.0%), edging out RLOO (59.6%) and ReMax (53.9%), though RLOO has a marginally better FM (−2.1% vs. −2.3%). Against the strongest continual-learning baseline, SFT with 25% Experience Replay (Schaul et al., 2015), GRPO on the 3B model achieves 74.4% AvgAcc and −0.4% FM versus 69.9% and −2.8% for SFT+ER, demonstrating that RFT's inherent stability surpasses explicit replay without storage overhead. On general benchmarks, GRPO improves over the base model on all three (MMMU +2.1, MMLU-Pro +0.8, POPE +1.9), while SFT degrades on all three (MMMU −12.0, MMLU-Pro −16.9, POPE −5.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CPT-for-MLLMs literature (HiDe-LLaVA, MRLoRA, Adapt-∞, LLaVA-c), which has focused on engineering anti-forgetting strategies—data replay, parameter expansion, regularization—within the SFT paradigm. Instead, it asks a more fundamental question: does the learning paradigm itself (SFT vs. RFT) determine forgetting behavior? It builds on the RFT algorithmic toolkit (GRPO, ReMax, RLOO) and concurrent observations that RFT forgets less in single-task settings (Shenfeld et al., Chen et al., Zhang et al.), but extends the analysis to the sequential multi-task CPT regime for MLLMs and isolates the mechanism (selective gradient updates via advantage collapse) through ablations that rule out KL penalty and CoT as primary drivers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If RFT can serve as a drop-in replacement for SFT in CPT pipelines without requiring replay buffers, parameter isolation, or explicit regularization, it substantially simplifies the engineering burden of continual adaptation for deployed MLLMs. The finding that RFT also protects and enhances general benchmarks (MMMU, MMLU-Pro, POPE) while SFT degrades them has direct implications for production systems where base-model capability must be preserved. Key open questions include: whether the selective-update explanation generalizes to larger models and more diverse task sequences, whether a theoretical characterization of cross-task gradient interference is possible, and how RFT-based CPT interacts with more sophisticated methods (e.g., LoRA expansion, spectral consolidation). The single-run, 7B-scale evaluation limits confidence in the magnitude of reported gains.
