## Problem

Existing unsupervised routing methods for LLMs treat model outputs as monolithic blocks, ignoring the causal 'thinking → answer' structure inherent in Large Reasoning Models. This makes them unable to distinguish a model that reasons correctly from a 'lucky guesser' with flawed logic, and the verbose reasoning trace dominates the embedding signal, masking answer quality. The paper addresses both the causal dependency and the structural length imbalance that current label-free routers fail to handle.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and the structured outputs (thinking trace and final answer) from each candidate LRM in a pool of 8 models (3B–30B parameters). A frozen pretrained encoder (BGE-M3 or Embeddinggemma-300m) generates d-dimensional embeddings for each component separately, concatenated with the input context to maintain semantic grounding while avoiding length imbalance. The output is a routing decision selecting the best model, accompanied by per-model decomposed scores: marginal thinking quality θ̂ᵢᵀ, marginal answer quality θ̂ᵢᴬ, and a causal coherence score that jointly captures the strength of the thinking-to-answer link and the minimum component quality.

## Method

ReasoningRouter builds a probabilistic graphical model where each model's answer embedding is conditionally distributed given its thinking embedding, with a scalar causal influence parameter βᵢ governing how thinking deviations propagate to answer deviations. A key theoretical contribution is the Causal Triangulation Property, which proves that despite the within-model causal dependency, the standard distance-based triangulation identity holds across models (exploiting conditional independence given the latent truth), enabling closed-form, non-iterative parameter estimation. To separate thinking and answer quality, the method evaluates the triangulation equation at two distinct weight values (α=0.8 and α=0.2), yielding a 2×2 linear system solved in closed form. The final routing score combines the two marginal quality estimates with a coherence term |β̂ᵢ|/(1+|β̂ᵢ|)·min(θ̂ᵢᵀ, θ̂ᵢᴬ) that rewards models whose strong causal link is supported by high quality in both components, preventing selection based on spurious correlations.

## Results

On 9 reasoning benchmarks, ReasoningRouter achieves an average routing accuracy of 88.20% (BGE-M3 embeddings) and 88.23% (Embeddinggemma-300m), with a best average rank of 1.44 and 1.56 respectively. It places first on 5 of 9 benchmarks (GSM8K, MedMCQA, SciQ, SocialIQA, TruthfulQA) and never falls below second place across both embedding backbones. The method also outperforms a Qwen3-0.6B reward-model baseline (86.19% avg) by 2.01 points while using only lightweight embedding extraction rather than full LLM inference for scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasoningRouter outperforms all four Smoothie variants (Guha et al., 2024) on average: it beats OutputRouter (87.81%) by 0.39 points, ThinkingRouter (87.71%) by 0.49 points, AnswerRouter (87.06%) by 1.14 points, and RandomRouter (84.99%) by 3.21 points. The gap over OutputRouter is most pronounced on math-heavy tasks (GSM8K: +0.49, MathQA: +0.00) and medical QA (MedMCQA: +0.56), where length imbalance in monolithic embeddings most severely obscures answer quality. Against the Qwen3-0.6B reward-model baseline (Lu et al., 2023), ReasoningRouter gains 2.01 points on average (88.20% vs. 86.19%) while avoiding the cost of full LLM inference for scoring. Notably, ThinkingRouter and AnswerRouter individually outperform RandomRouter on nearly all datasets, confirming that both reasoning traces and final answers carry routing signal, but neither alone is sufficient—ReasoningRouter's integrated causal scoring consistently extracts more from the joint structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label-free routing framework of Smoothie (Guha et al., 2024), which established a triangulation property for estimating model quality from pairwise embedding distances without supervision. ReasoningRouter departs from Smoothie's independence assumption by introducing a probabilistic graphical model that explicitly encodes the directed causal dependency from thinking to answer components within each Large Reasoning Model's output. It also addresses a structural problem absent from prior routing work: the length imbalance between verbose reasoning traces and short answers, which causes monolithic embeddings to be dominated by the thinking portion and obscures answer-quality signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal triangulation property provides a principled, closed-form mechanism for decomposing quality into thinking and answer components without any human annotation, which is practically valuable as LRM pools grow and retraining supervised routers becomes infeasible. However, the authors acknowledge that the estimated causal coefficient β is not a fully identified interventionist effect (shared encoder confounds), that the isotropic Gaussian assumption may not hold for real embeddings, and that the method requires outputs to be cleanly separable into thinking and answer segments. Open questions include whether the causal framing yields meaningful gains over a simpler correlation-based decomposition, how the approach generalizes to models without explicit thinking/answer structure, and whether the modest empirical improvements (~0.4% over the best baseline) scale to larger or more diverse model pools.
