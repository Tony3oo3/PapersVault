## Problem

Existing self-supervised continual graph learning methods rely on instance-level consistency objectives that stabilize individual node embeddings but ignore global relational structure, causing inter-node correspondences to progressively distort (structural drift) across sequential tasks. No prior method explicitly captures and preserves the relational dependencies encoded in the representation space, leading to accumulated degradation of long-term knowledge retention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265859/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batched graph formed by the disjoint union of the current task graph and replayed subgraphs (retrieved from a fixed-size memory buffer via layer-wise neighbor sampling), from which two augmented views are generated by stochastic edge dropping and node feature masking. It produces H-dimensional node embeddings (via a 2-layer GCN or 3-layer GAT encoder) and a fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport plan of size n×m that encodes pairwise node correspondences. For scalability on large graphs, a point-cloud sampling mechanism restricts the aligned node set to a fixed budget M ≪ |V_t|, reducing the quadratic OT computation cost.

## Method

SAOT introduces a two-component framework built on optimal transport theory. Within each task, it computes a fused Gromov-Wasserstein transport plan π*_G over the two augmented graph views (combining node-feature cost and edge-structure cost via a trade-off parameter σ), then aligns this with the transport plan π*_Z computed from the encoder's node embeddings, enforcing that the learned representations preserve the relational correspondences observed in graph space. Across tasks, a frozen teacher model from the previous task generates a reference transport plan, and the current model is regularized to match it via a cross-task distillation loss, thereby consolidating previously learned structural knowledge. The total loss combines intra-task transport alignment (plan matching plus cost-matrix calibration) with cross-task structural distillation, balancing plasticity on new tasks against stability of relational structure.

## Results

Under the Class-IL setting, SAOT achieves the best average performance (AP) among all self-supervised methods across all four datasets: 76.3% on CoraFull-CL, 51.6% on Arxiv-CL, 98.5% on Reddit-CL, and 40.7% on Products-CL. The most striking gain is on Products-CL, where SAOT improves AP by over 15 percentage points (40.7% vs. 25.6%) over the strongest self-supervised baseline TRACE, and by 5.1 points (76.3% vs. 71.2%) on CoraFull-CL. SAOT also achieves near-zero average forgetting (AF ≈ −0.2%) on CoraFull-CL and Reddit-CL, compared to −6.2% and −0.2% for TRACE respectively. Under the easier Task-IL setting, SAOT reaches 96.6% AP on CoraFull-CL and 95.9% on Products-CL, again leading all self-supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAOT outperforms all self-supervised baselines on every dataset under Class-IL. Against the strongest self-supervised CGL method TRACE, SAOT gains +5.1 AP on CoraFull-CL (76.3 vs. 71.2), +3.8 on Arxiv-CL (51.6 vs. 47.8), +0.4 on Reddit-CL (98.5 vs. 98.1), and a dramatic +15.1 on Products-CL (40.7 vs. 25.6). Against adapted self-supervised graph models (GAE, DGI, G-BT, GCN-Clu/Par/Comp), SAOT leads by 10–40+ AP points on most datasets; for example, G-BT reaches only 25.4% AP on Products-CL versus SAOT's 40.7%. RieGrace, the prior self-supervised CGL method, performs poorly (3.3–4.9% AP) and is far surpassed by SAOT. Against supervised CGL baselines, SAOT (being label-free) trails PUMA on Arxiv-CL (51.6 vs. 67.0) and Products-CL (40.7 vs. 74.2) but is competitive on CoraFull-CL (76.3 vs. 77.9) and slightly ahead on Reddit-CL (98.5 vs. 98.0), while achieving substantially lower forgetting (AF −0.2 vs. −4.2 on CoraFull-CL). The paper positions SAOT as the best self-supervised option, closing much of the gap to supervised methods without any label supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAOT departs from the instance-level consistency paradigm that dominates self-supervised CGL (TRACE, RieGrace, and adapted methods like GAE, DGI, G-BT) by shifting the preservation objective from individual node embeddings to global inter-node relational structure. It repurposes fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport—previously used in graph matching (GALOPA, Wang et al. 2023)—as a structural reference signal for representation learning, and extends knowledge distillation from embedding-level to transport-plan-level across tasks. The replay mechanism is borrowed from standard CGL practice, but the paper shows the method's effectiveness is largely independent of buffer size, suggesting the OT alignment itself carries the anti-forgetting signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural drift framing provides a useful diagnostic for why instance-level self-supervised objectives fail under long task sequences, and the >15% AP gain on Products-CL demonstrates that relational structure preservation is critical for dense, large-scale graphs. However, the method's quadratic OT complexity (mitigated only by node sampling) limits scalability, and the notably worse AF on Arxiv-CL (−17.5% vs. TRACE's −6.4%) suggests the approach is not uniformly robust across graph types. Open questions include whether the transport-plan distillation generalizes to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, whether the method can operate fully without replay, and how it behaves under non-disjoint task boundaries.
