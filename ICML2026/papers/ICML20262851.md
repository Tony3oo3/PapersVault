## Problem

GUI agents are trained on static datasets, but real-world digital environments shift over time through OS platform changes (mobile→desktop→web) and resolution upgrades (1080p→4K), causing grounding performance to degrade. Existing SFT methods over-fit to specific data distributions, and standard RFT methods optimize only for the current task's coordinates and scales, lacking mechanisms to maintain stable grounding across shifting UI layouts. No prior work has formalized continual learning for GUI agents under these dynamic conditions.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a GUI interface screenshot (RGB image at varying resolutions from 1080p to 4K) paired with a natural language instruction, and outputs a bounding box [x1, y1, x2, y2] localizing the target interactive element. Critically, the model generates N=4 candidate predictions per instruction, and the reward mechanism operates on the ensemble of predictions rather than a single output, computing spatial diversity metrics across the group. Training proceeds sequentially over task sequences (e.g., Mobile→Desktop→Web or Normal→High resolution), with the model adapting at each step without access to prior task data.

## Method

GUI-AiF extends the GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning framework with two entropy-maximization rewards that counteract over-adaptation to static grounding cues. APR-iF computes the spatial variance of predicted bounding-box center points across the N predictions, rewarding exploration of diverse interaction locations; ARR-iF models each predicted box as a 2D Gaussian and computes the average Bhattacharyya distance across all pairs, rewarding spatially distinct element regions. These rewards are combined as a weighted sum (α·R_p + γ·R_r) and added to the standard GRPO correctness advantage, while a KL divergence term to the reference policy prevents catastrophic forgetting of prior tasks. The key insight is that promoting diversity in predicted anchors acts as an implicit regularization against memorizing fixed coordinates or element scales, enabling the agent to generalize across shifting GUI domains and resolutions without explicit replay or parameter freezing.

## Results

On the full continual domain sequence (Mobile→Desktop→Web), GUI-AiF built on GUI-G2 achieves 81.7% average accuracy on ScreenSpot-V1 and 83.5% on ScreenSpot-V2, outperforming the GUI-G2 baseline (77.1% / 80.3%) by +4.6 and +3.2 points respectively. On the continual resolution task (Normal→High) on ScreenSpot-Pro, GUI-AiF reaches 19.0% average accuracy, surpassing both the RFT baseline GUI-G2 (16.7%) and the SFT baseline SeeClick (16.7%). The method also demonstrates progressive knowledge accumulation: single-domain Mobile accuracy improves from 78.1% (M only) to 81.7% (M→D→W) on SSv1, confirming effective continual learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUI-AiF consistently outperforms all baselines in the full continual sequence (M→D→W). Built on the GUI-G2 RFT backbone, it improves SSv1 average from 77.1% to 81.7% (+4.6 pts) and SSv2 from 80.3% to 83.5% (+3.2 pts); built on SE-GUI†, it lifts SSv1 from 76.1% to 78.9% and SSv2 from 78.9% to 81.2%. Against SFT-based methods, the gains are larger: SeeClick drops to 66.4%/65.4% and GUI-Actor to 75.0%/77.9% on SSv1/SSv2, confirming SFT's over-adaptation to single-task distributions. On the resolution-shift task (SSPro, Normal→High), GUI-AiF achieves 19.0% vs. 16.7% for both GUI-G2 and SeeClick, the only method to improve over its single-resolution starting point. Proprietary models (GPT-4o at 18.8%/20.1%, Claude Computer Use at 17.1%) are far below all fine-tuned open-source methods, and common continual-learning techniques (data replay, LwF) fail to match GUI-AiF's 83.5% SSv2 average in the M→D→W setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of GUI visual grounding (SeeClick, GUI-Actor, SE-GUI, GUI-G2) and continual post-training (LwF, iCaRL, C-Flat, ZeroFlow), but departs from both lines in a specific way. It builds directly on the GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm for GUI grounding, adding two entropy-maximization-inspired diversity rewards (APR-iF for point spread, ARR-iF for region separation via Bhattacharyya distance) to counteract the over-adaptation that standard task-specific rewards induce. Conceptually, it repurposes the continual learning insight that RFT's implicit KL regularization resists forgetting, but goes further by explicitly reshaping the reward landscape to preserve generalization across shifting GUI distributions rather than relying solely on parameter-level constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize continual learning specifically for GUI agents under domain and resolution shifts, establishing a task setting and evaluation protocol (sequential Mobile→Desktop→Web, Normal→High-Res) that the field lacked. The finding that both SFT and RFT baselines degrade identically under resolution shifts, and that point-diversity rewards (APR-iF) matter more than region-diversity rewards (ARR-iF), provides actionable guidance for future GUI agent training. However, the scope is limited to a 3B model, three datasets, and two shift types; the authors acknowledge that real-world flux includes layout updates, dark mode, and language changes that remain unexplored, and the modest 1–3 point gains over strong RFT baselines leave open whether the reward-shaping approach scales to larger models or more complex sequential scenarios.
