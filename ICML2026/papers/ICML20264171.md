## Problem

Parallel reasoning (sampling N solutions and selecting the best) is a powerful test-time scaling strategy, but its effectiveness hinges on accurate self-verification. Existing pointwise verification suffers from calibration collapse due to the absence of a comparative reference, while self-aggregation methods (e.g., RSA) cause diversity collapse where Pass@N monotonically decreases. Furthermore, no prior method jointly trains a single model for both solution generation and pairwise self-verification during RL, leaving a distribution mismatch between the generator's evolving outputs and the verifier's training data.

## Input / Output

At inference, V1-Infer takes a problem prompt and N independently sampled candidate solutions (N in {8, 16, 32}) and produces a ranked ordering via B pairwise comparisons (B = 1x, 2x, or 3x N), from which the top-ranked solution is selected as the final answer. Each pairwise comparison prompt asks the model to assign 1-10 correctness ratings to both solutions simultaneously, and the algorithm maintains a running weighted win-rate score per solution. For V1-PairRL, the output is a single trained LLM that functions as both generator and pairwise verifier, evaluated on Pass@1 with self-verification across code, math, and medical QA benchmarks.

## Method

V1-Infer replaces independent pointwise scoring with an uncertainty-guided Swiss-system tournament: Phase 1 enforces a minimum-degree topology so every solution is compared at least d_min times against similar-quality peers, and Phase 2 allocates remaining budget to near-tie pairs (smallest score gap) where marginal information gain is highest under a Bradley-Terry model. A confidence weight w_ij = max(|r_i - r_j|/9, tau) ensures decisive judgments dominate the aggregated score while near-ties contribute minimally. V1-PairRL unifies generation and verification in a single RL objective J(theta) = J_Gen + lambda * J_PairVerif, where the same G rollouts per problem serve both the GRPO generation reward (binary correctness) and the pairwise verification reward (sparsity-gated MAE against ground-truth labels). Two anti-reward-hacking mechanisms are critical: a sparsity threshold that zeroes reward unless the verifier's score is within 0.2 of ground truth (preventing safe-bet collapse to v=0.5), and a strict pairing policy that only forms Correct-Incorrect or Correct-Correct pairs (preventing the empty-solution loop). The co-evolving design ensures the verifier always trains on in-distribution outputs from the current policy, which ablations confirm is essential for the reported gains.

## Results

On AIME 2025 (N=16), V1-Infer achieves 92.2% (gpt-oss-120b), 87.8% (gpt-oss-20b), and 62.2% (Qwen3-4B) best-of-N accuracy, outperforming pointwise self-verification (85.6%, 85.6%, 54.5%) and all four external ORM/PRM baselines. On code generation, V1-Infer improves Pass@1 by up to +10% over pointwise (e.g., +7.3% on CodeContests, +8.6% on LCB-v5 for GPT-OSS-20B), and V1-PairRL further boosts base Pass@1 by up to +8.7% over standard RL. On SWE-bench Lite, V1-Infer reaches 33.3% resolve vs. 28.3% pointwise; on Terminal-Bench, it reaches 87.4% Pass@1, closing ~70% of the gap to oracle best-of-5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V1-Infer decisively outperforms all four external reward/process reward models on AIME 2025: it beats AceMath-7B-RM by +32.2pp (gpt-oss-120b), Skywork-o1-PRM by +18.9pp, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B by +15.5pp, and ThinkPRM-7B by +18.9pp, with substantially lower variance across seeds. Against Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA), V1-Infer achieves higher accuracy with significantly fewer LLM calls (e.g., 76% Pass@1 with 48 verification calls on LCB-v6 vs. RSA's maximum), and avoids RSA's diversity collapse where Pass@N monotonically decreases with aggregation steps. On SWE-bench Lite, V1-Infer (33.3%) beats pointwise (28.3%) and vanilla first-candidate (26.3%); on Terminal-Bench it surpasses single-model baselines including Claude Opus 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and GPT-5.4. In the RL training setting, V1-PairRL outperforms V1-PointRL (the pointwise co-training baseline) by +6.5–7.3pp on code benchmarks and +5.7pp on MATH-Hard, while also improving base generation quality over standard RL by up to +8.7pp on CodeContests.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-training paradigm for LLM reasoners (Sareen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a) by replacing pointwise verification rewards with pairwise comparison, grounded in the Bradley-Terry insight that relative judgments are more reliable than absolute scores. At inference time, it departs from both pointwise LLM-as-a-judge scoring and aggregation-based test-time scaling (RSA, Venkatraman et al., 2025) by introducing an uncertainty-guided Swiss-system tournament that allocates verification compute to the most ambiguous pairs. The RL training builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeek-AI, 2025) and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) configurations, adding a co-evolving pairwise verification objective that ensures the verifier always trains on in-distribution outputs of the current policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that self-aggregation methods like RSA suffer diversity collapse (Pass@N monotonically decreasing) is an important negative result that reframes how the community should think about test-time scaling: explicit verification that preserves candidate diversity may be more valuable than implicit aggregation. The practical takeaway—that a simple pairwise tournament with uncertainty-guided pairing can replace both pointwise scoring and aggregation with fewer calls and higher accuracy—offers a drop-in improvement for any parallel reasoning pipeline. Open questions include whether the co-evolving training advantage persists at much larger model scales, whether the approach extends to settings without any verifiable training signal, and how the Swiss refinement budget should scale with N for very large candidate sets.
