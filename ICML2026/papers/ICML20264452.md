## Problem

Federated learning with noisy labels (F-LN) is significantly harder than centralized label-noise learning because clients exhibit dual heterogeneity: different noise types, ratios, and data distributions. Existing F-LNL methods either rely on client-side statistics that pose privacy risks or fail under this dual heterogeneity. The paper identifies a previously underexplored phenomenon—the global FL model memorizes noisy labels substantially more slowly than a centrally trained model—and leverages this intrinsic robustness to build a self-contained, privacy-preserving solution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, variable-resolution Clothing1M) with noisy one-hot labels partitioned across K clients (100 for CIFAR, 500 for Clothing1M). Rather than transmitting raw data or local statistics, clients upload only model parameters and per-sample mean inference losses (scalar data proxies) to the server, which returns partitioning decisions and the aggregated global model. The final output is a standard class-label prediction from the global model, evaluated as mean test accuracy over the last 10 rounds to reflect resistance to noisy-label overfitting.

## Method

FedGR exploits the global model's slow memorization of noisy labels through three collaborative modules. (1) Sieving-and-Refining: the server fits a two-component GMM on aggregated per-sample mean losses to partition each client's data into clean/noisy subsets and estimate the client's noise ratio r_k; Label Refining then corrects noisy labels using FixMatch pseudo-labels from the global model, with a strategy that switches to fully pseudo-labeled training when r_k exceeds a threshold β. (2) Globally Revised EMA Distillation: each client maintains a local EMA model that is periodically revised toward the global model (momentum γ_g) before serving as a distillation teacher, resolving the tension between the global model's noise robustness and the local model's distributional fit; γ_g is set to 0 (full replacement by the global model) for high-noise clients with insufficient reliable samples. (3) Global Representation Regularization: a KL-divergence term enforces consistency between the global backbone's representation of a weakly augmented image and the local backbone's representation of a strongly augmented image, further preventing local overfitting to accumulated EMA errors. The three objectives are combined with hyperparameters λ_B and λ_R, and the entire pipeline is activated after an α-round warm-up phase during which vanilla supervised learning with strong augmentation is performed.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FedGR achieves 91.07% average accuracy under I.I.D and 81.22% under Non.I.I.D-Dirichlet(0.3), substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (FedDiv: 82.64%/24.50%; FedCorr: 80.36%/62.17%). On CIFAR-100, FedGR reaches 60.64% (I.I.D) and 55.90% (Non.I.I.D), again leading all eight baselines. On the real-world Clothing1M dataset, FedGR attains 71.19% (I.I.D) and 68.52% (Non.I.I.D), surpassing the best baseline (FedFixer: 70.61%/65.16%). The gains are most dramatic under the most challenging dual-heterogeneity setting (Sym, φ=1.0, U(0.5,1.0)), where FedGR scores 83.91% (I.I.D) and 63.64% (Non.I.I.D) versus 33.14% and 14.22% for FedDiv.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGR is compared against eight baselines spanning three categories: classic FL (FedAvg, FedProx), centralized noisy-label methods adapted to FL (FL-Coteaching, FL-DivideMix), and dedicated F-LNL methods (FedCorr, FedNoRo, FedDiv, FedFixer). FedGR wins on every setting across all three datasets. The margin is most striking under high symmetric noise (Sym, φ=1.0, U(0.5,1.0)): on CIFAR-10 I.I.D, FedGR scores 83.91% versus 55.12% (FedCorr), 33.14% (FedDiv), and 30.18% (FedFixer); under Non.I.I.D the gap widens to 63.64% vs. 29.42% (FedCorr) and 14.22% (FedDiv). Under asymmetric and mixed noise, FedGR also leads but by a smaller margin (e.g., 91.64% vs. 92.86% for FedDiv on I.I.D Asym φ=1.0, where FedDiv's advantage is within one standard deviation). Notably, FedGR even exceeds clean-data FedAvg (92.55%) on CIFAR-10 I.I.D Mixed φ=0.6 (93.13%), suggesting its regularization provides a small benefit even without noise. On the real-world Clothing1M benchmark, FedGR (71.19% I.I.D, 68.52% Non.I.I.D) outperforms the strongest F-LNL baseline FedFixer (70.61%, 65.16%) by 0.58 and 3.36 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedGR departs from the dominant F-LNL paradigm of treating federated label noise as a distributed extension of centralized noisy-label learning (which drives client-side sample selection or noisy-client detection). Instead, it exploits a previously underexplored property of FL: the global model's slower memorization of noisy labels, using it as a robust teacher for both label correction (via FixMatch pseudo-labeling on the global model) and local regularization (via globally revised EMA distillation and representation consistency). The GMM-based clean/noisy partitioning follows the DivideMix methodology, and the warm-up sampling strategy is inherited from FedCorr, but the key conceptual shift is leveraging the global model's inherent noise robustness rather than designing client-side robust training algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that FL's aggregation mechanism implicitly regularizes against label-noise overfitting provides a new lens for understanding federated training dynamics and could inspire centralized learning methods that mimic this effect. The method's self-contained, privacy-preserving design (transmitting only loss-based proxies rather than local data statistics) addresses a practical limitation of prior consensus-based F-LNL approaches. Open questions include a formal theoretical analysis of why the global model memorizes slowly (left to future work), whether the phenomenon persists under more extreme heterogeneity or non-convex aggregation, and whether the insight transfers to other FL challenges beyond label noise.
