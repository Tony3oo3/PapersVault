## Problem

While LLMs produce extended chain-of-thought outputs, the internal layer-wise dynamics of how reasoning emerges and intensifies within the hidden space remain largely opaque. Existing approaches either rely on retrospective output-level signals (entropy, mutual information) that cannot reveal intermediate computations, or require expensive, model-specific Sparse Autoencoder training that limits generality. The paper asks whether a simple, training-free, model-intrinsic property can faithfully capture reasoning intensity without external probes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text prompt and operates entirely on the LLM's residual-stream hidden states at each transformer layer during autoregressive decoding, requiring no additional training data, labels, or SAE inference at test time. Peak detection uses an adaptive IQR-based threshold (Tukey's method) computed over the current decoding sequence, making it benchmark-agnostic and eliminating the need for cross-dataset calibration. Outputs are either an improved single reasoning response (via ALRR or ERSS) or a selected best candidate from a set of generated responses (via LRS), with no change to the model architecture or output format.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that the ℓ2 norm of hidden states provides two-sided bounds on SAE reasoning-feature activation under four assumptions (decoder near-orthonormality, faithful reconstruction, semantic-reasoning orthogonality, and feature disentanglement), establishing ℓ2 norm as a training-free proxy for reasoning intensity. Empirically, ℓ2 norms exhibit a sharp two-stage pattern—stable in early layers, surging in the final ~25% of layers—closely mirroring SAE reasoning-feature activations and output entropy, and causal interventions suppressing high-norm tokens in late layers cause disproportionate performance drops. Building on this, three test-time techniques are introduced: (i) Adaptive Layer-wise Reasoning Recursion (ALRR) iteratively reprocesses the hidden state through the same layer when an ℓ2 peak is detected, allocating extra compute to critical reasoning positions; (ii) Endogenous Reasoning State Steering (ERSS) caches high-norm states during decoding and, upon detecting a new peak, additively steers the current state toward the most cosine-similar cached high-norm state to amplify reasoning; and (iii) ℓ2-guided Response Selection (LRS) scores candidate responses by summing ℓ2 magnitudes from middle-to-late layers and selects the highest-scoring candidate without external supervision.

## Results

Across Qwen3 (1.7B–32B) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama models, the three ℓ₂-norm-guided test-time techniques (ALRR, ERSS, LRS) yield a 4.51% average relative improvement across all evaluated benchmarks (AIME, GPQA, MMLU-Pro, GSM-8k), with substantially larger gains of 9.13% on challenging reasoning tasks such as AIME. All three methods produce consistent gains across model scales and benchmark types, with ALRR and ERSS enhancing internal reasoning during generation and LRS providing unsupervised candidate reranking. In the correlation analysis (Table 1), ℓ₂ norm achieves Spearman correlations of 84–88 with SAE reasoning activations and 52–67 with final output entropy, far exceeding alternative metrics (layer hidden entropy, layer output entropy, attention entropy) which show negative or weakly positive correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other test-time scaling or reasoning-enhancement methods; the primary comparison is against the base model without any intervention. All three proposed techniques (ALRR, ERSS, LRS) consistently outperform the unmodified base model across every benchmark and model size tested. In the correlation analysis, ℓ₂ norm uniquely aligns with both SAE reasoning feature activations and final output entropy (Spearman ρ = 84–88 and 52–67 respectively), while competing intrinsic metrics—layer hidden entropy, layer output entropy, and attention entropy—show substantially weaker or negative correlations (e.g., layer output entropy: ρ = −62 to −72 with SAE activations). In the causal intervention study, selectively suppressing high-ℓ₂-norm hidden states in the final 25% of layers causes severe performance degradation even at low intervention ratios, whereas random suppression of the same proportion of tokens leads to a substantially milder decline, confirming that ℓ₂-norm peaks are functionally necessary for reasoning rather than incidental.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAE-based interpretability line (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023) that identifies reasoning-specific features in LLM hidden states, and on output-level test-time scaling methods (entropy-based token selection, mutual-information reranking) that operate post-decoding. It departs from both by showing that the trivially computable ℓ2 norm of residual-stream hidden states captures the same layer-wise reasoning dynamics that expensive, model-specific SAEs reveal, thereby eliminating the need for external probes. The three proposed test-time techniques (ALRR, ERSS, LRS) extend prior representation-steering and computation-scaling ideas by grounding all interventions in this single intrinsic signal rather than handcrafted vectors or decoded-token heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a scalar that requires no training, no additional data, and no architectural modification can serve as both a diagnostic for reasoning intensity and a control handle for test-time enhancement, making it immediately deployable alongside existing inference engines. The theoretical bound (Theorem 1) provides principled grounding, though it relies on approximate orthonormality and semantic-reasoning orthogonality assumptions that hold only to first order. Open questions include whether the late-layer norm surge reflects a genuinely distinct computational regime or is an artifact of residual-stream accumulation, whether the signal generalizes to non-reasoning tasks (e.g., creative generation, code), and what richer geometric structure beyond magnitude might capture the full reasoning dynamics.
