## Problem

Proactive LLM agents face a fundamental Pareto trade-off between task performance and user engagement: more user interactions improve information gathering but erode user satisfaction. Existing agentic RL methods either over-rely on user feedback or under-explore the environment, and naively tuning a penalty weight between the two objectives fails to advance the Pareto frontier. The paper identifies that the root cause is the absence of explicit inter-turn behaviors—structured reasoning that connects current decisions with past observations and future planning.

## Input / Output

The agent operates within a finite-horizon contextual MDP (T=15 turns) where a hidden user context c governs both transition dynamics and rewards. At each turn, the agent selects from a structured action space split into environment-involved actions (Action for tool calls, Search for information retrieval) and user-involved actions (Answer for submitting responses and receiving user feedback). The output is a full interaction trajectory with turn-level rewards, evaluated via Pass@U-k (pass rate allowing up to k user-involved submissions), cumulative score, and user involvement rate (UR).

## Method

BAO is a two-stage agentic RL framework. First, Behavior Enhancement performs SFT using teacher-generated (GPT-4o) traces that explicitly demonstrate four multi-turn behaviors: retrospective reasoning (memory management to maintain and update hypotheses about the hidden context, and hypothesis refinement to pivot on contradictory evidence) and prospective planning (dynamic scheduling that shifts from exploration to exploitation as the interaction budget depletes, and strategic querying that targets high-information-gain questions). Second, Behavior-Regularized RL applies GRPO with turn-level advantage estimation, augmented by two regularization penalties: an information-seeking penalty that discourages consecutive user-interaction actions without new environment information, and an over-thinking penalty that penalizes premature token-budget exhaustion. The key insight is that rather than balancing the two objectives with a scalar weight, explicitly shaping inter-turn behaviors naturally improves both task performance and interaction efficiency simultaneously, pushing the Pareto frontier forward.

## Results

On Function-Gym, BAO-4B achieves a Score of 0.6923 with a User Involvement Rate (UR) of 0.2148, substantially outperforming the UserRL-4B baseline (Score 0.5256, UR 0.3758) and surpassing GPT-4o (Score 0.1410). On Telepathy-Gym, BAO-4B reaches a Score of 0.6585 with UR 0.1870, exceeding UserRL-4B (Score 0.6585, UR 0.4251) and GPT-4o (Score 0.6342). On the hardest task, Turtle-Gym, BAO-4B achieves Score 0.1125 with UR 0.2917, more than doubling UserRL-4B's Score (0.0594) while reducing UR from 0.7617 to 0.2917. The 1.7B variant also surpasses GPT-4o-mini on Function-Gym (Score 0.3590 vs 0.0385).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary RL baseline UserRL, BAO wins on all three tasks across both model sizes: on Function-Gym, BAO-4B improves Score from 0.5256 to 0.6923 while cutting UR from 0.3758 to 0.2148; on Telepathy-Gym, BAO-1.7B nearly doubles Pass@U-1 (0.5610 vs 0.2683) and reduces UR from 0.6543 to 0.1601; on Turtle-Gym, BAO-4B more than doubles Score (0.1125 vs 0.0594) while reducing UR by 62% (0.2917 vs 0.7617). Against commercial models, BAO-4B outperforms GPT-4o on Function-Gym (Score 0.6923 vs 0.1410) and Telepathy-Gym (0.6585 vs 0.6342), and BAO-1.7B surpasses GPT-4o-mini on Function-Gym (0.3590 vs 0.0385). However, BAO-4B trails GPT-5-0807 and Gemini-3-flash on Telepathy-Gym and Turtle-Gym, where the larger frontier models retain an advantage. The key differentiator is UR: BAO consistently achieves the lowest user involvement rate among all finetuned agents, indicating it gathers information more autonomously from the environment rather than relying on repeated user verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agentic RL line of work (UserRL, CollabLLM, SWE-RL) by shifting the focus from handcrafted reward balancing to explicit inter-turn behavioral shaping. It builds on the warm-start SFT+RL pipeline from Gandhi et al. (2025) for behavior injection and uses GRPO for policy optimization, but departs from prior proactive-agent methods (Sun et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025) that rely on penalty weights to trade off task performance against user engagement. The key conceptual departure is identifying retrospective reasoning and prospective planning as the mechanistic bridge between sparse task rewards and efficient multi-turn information gathering, rather than treating the trade-off as a scalar reward-weighting problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Pareto frontier formalization gives the community a principled lens for evaluating proactive agents beyond single-metric optimization, and the demonstration that naive reward balancing fails while behavioral shaping succeeds suggests a broader design principle for multi-turn RL: shaping the process (how the agent reasons across turns) can be more effective than shaping the outcome (reward penalties). Limitations include the language-only setting, evaluation restricted to three UserRL gym tasks, and reliance on GPT-4o as teacher model for SFT data generation. Open questions include whether the behavioral framework generalizes to open-ended real-world interactions, multimodal agents, and whether the specific regularization terms (consecutive-interaction penalty, over-thinking penalty) transfer to other agentic domains.
