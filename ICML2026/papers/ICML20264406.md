## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based dynamic scene methods approximate surface normals from volumetric Gaussians, introducing errors that propagate into reflection ray directions and degrade specular fidelity. Additionally, dynamic environment maps used for specular modeling are limited in resolution and inherently assume distant illumination, failing to capture near-field reflections and fine specular details. ReflFlow addresses both gaps by leveraging a surface representation with exact normals and a deformable Gaussian environment for physically informed reflection tracing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of a dynamic specular scene along with an initial point cloud (points.npy provided by the NeRF-DS or HyperNeRF datasets) and produces photorealistic novel view renderings at approximately 32 FPS on an RTX 6000 Ada GPU. Training requires 60,000 iterations (~2.8 hours) and additionally outputs per-pixel depth maps and surface normal maps as byproducts of the 2DGS rasterization. The specular tint attribute per Gaussian enables view-dependent material decomposition, so the final image is a per-pixel blend of rasterized diffuse and ray-traced specular components.

## Method

ReflFlow represents the main scene content with a Residual Material-Augmented 2DGS: each 2D Gaussian carries a learnable specular tint (s_tint ∈ [0,1]) for PBR-based material modeling, and a Time-Conditioned Residual Network F_θG predicts per-frame deformations (position, scale, rotation, opacity, s_tint) from the Gaussian center and timestamp. Because 2DGS primitives lie on tangent planes, their surface normal is the exact cross product of two tangential vectors—eliminating the normal-estimation error inherent to 3DGS. For the environment, a Dynamic Environment Gaussian (a deformable set of 2D Gaussians with its own residual correction network) replaces traditional environment maps, enabling near-field reflections at higher effective resolution. A hybrid rendering pipeline computes diffuse color via standard 2DGS rasterization and specular color via a ray tracer that reflects incident rays off the main surface and queries the environment Gaussians; the two are blended per-pixel using a transmittance-weighted sum of specular tints as the mixing weight. Training follows a three-phase coarse-to-fine schedule (diffuse-only → specular-only → joint) over 60k steps, supplemented by a geometry-aligned normal loss (self-supervised from depth gradients) and a temporal-consistent normal supervision loss using NormalCrafter pseudo-normals.

## Results

On the NeRF-DS dynamic specular benchmark (7 scenes), ReflFlow achieves average PSNR of 24.91, SSIM of 0.8880, and LPIPS of 0.1471, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art SpectroMotion by +0.74 PSNR, +0.0358 SSIM, and −0.0307 LPIPS. On the HyperNeRF dataset (general dynamic scenes without specular focus), ReflFlow achieves 22.47 PSNR, 0.6328 SSIM, and 0.3106 LPIPS, surpassing all dynamic reconstruction and specular baselines. The method achieves real-time rendering at 32 FPS on an RTX 6000 Ada, comparable to SpectroMotion's 33 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReflFlow is compared against 8 baselines spanning dynamic reconstruction (Deformable 3DGS, 4DGS, HyperNeRF), static specular reconstruction (GaussianShader, GS-IR, EnvGS), and dynamic specular reconstruction (NeRF-DS, SpectroMotion). Against the closest competitor SpectroMotion (dynamic specular), ReflFlow wins on SSIM and LPIPS across all scenes and on PSNR for 3 of 4 shown scenes (losing by only 0.07 dB on 'As'), yielding average gains of +0.74 PSNR, +0.0358 SSIM, and −0.0307 LPIPS. Against general dynamic methods (Deformable 3DGS, 4DGS), ReflFlow leads by 0.9–2.2 dB PSNR and 0.03–0.06 SSIM, reflecting the advantage of explicit specular modeling. Against static specular methods (GaussianShader, GS-IR, EnvGS), the margin is much larger (4–5 dB PSNR), as these methods lack temporal modeling and produce severe artifacts in dynamic regions. On HyperNeRF (non-specular dynamic scenes), ReflFlow still achieves the best PSNR (22.47) and LPIPS (0.3106) among all methods, confirming generalization beyond specular-centric settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReflFlow builds on the 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) representation for its inherently well-defined surface normals, departing from the 3DGS-based dynamic reconstruction line (Deformable 3DGS, 4DGS) that relies on approximate normal estimation. For specular modeling, it extends the environment-Gaussian idea from EnvGS (Xie et al., 2025) into a dynamic setting and replaces the environment-map approach of SpectroMotion (Fan et al., 2024) with a full 3D Gaussian environment that can capture near-field reflections. The hybrid rasterization-plus-ray-tracing pipeline and the coarse-to-fine training strategy are novel contributions that distinguish it from prior dynamic specular methods like NeRF-DS and GaussianShader, which either condition on view direction implicitly or use static environment maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap—approximate normals and far-field-only environment maps limiting dynamic specular fidelity—and provides a clean architectural solution (2DGS for exact normals, Dynamic Environment Gaussian for near-field reflections) with thorough ablations. The coarse-to-fine training ablation (21.10 → 15.28 PSNR without it) highlights that physically meaningful diffuse/specular decomposition is fragile without staged optimization, a finding relevant to any method that decomposes appearance. Open questions remain around multi-bounce inter-reflections, spatially-varying BRDFs, and whether the 2DGS-based geometry can handle highly non-rigid deformations beyond the benchmark scenes tested.
