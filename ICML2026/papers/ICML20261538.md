## Problem

Current LLM alignment techniques excel in dyadic (one-on-one) instruction but fail in One-to-Many settings such as clinical ward rounds, where an instructor must simultaneously guide heterogeneous trainees. Baseline models suffer from context dilution—losing track of individual belief states in multi-speaker streams—and goal misalignment, unable to balance personalized scaffolding for struggling learners with collective Socratic progress, hitting a sharp 'performance cliff' as group size grows beyond three students.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multi-student dialogue context (independent clinical analyses from N student agents), static medical images (radiographs, CT scans; up to 10 images per rollout), and a hidden Socratic Steps roadmap that guides pedagogical progression. Its output is a two-part structure: an explicit internal reasoning chain with ordered XML tags (<think_history>, <think_question>, per-student <think_student student_id="...">, and <think_group>) followed by a well-formed JSON containing a single open-ended Socratic question directed at the entire group—never a direct answer or statement.

## Method

ClinTutor-R1 is a vision-language agent (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning via LoRA on ClinTeach (48k Socratic dialogues generated by the ClinEdu multi-agent simulator), followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a three-axis rubric reward covering Instruction & Structure Fidelity, Analysis Quality, and Clinical Accuracy & Safety, plus a hard veto penalty (−15.0) for critical safety or structural failures. The key architectural insight is an explicit Theory-of-Mind thinking mechanism: before generating guidance, the model writes a dedicated <think_student> trace for each learner to track individual belief states, then synthesizes these into a <think_group> analysis identifying collective blind spots and consensus gaps. This per-student state tracing disentangles multi-agent interactions, enabling the tutor to maintain independent mental models of each student's reasoning regardless of cohort size, and serves as a verifiable pedagogical trace for expert audit. The ClinEdu simulator itself employs a Guide-Review-Revise closed loop with independent Specialist and Safety Supervisor agents to enforce factual accuracy and ethical boundaries during data generation.

## Results

ClinTutor-R1 achieves an average score of 8.35 on MedXpertQA and 8.49 on MVME, representing a ~20% improvement over its base model Qwen2.5VL (6.96/6.83) and a 12%+ margin over the strongest specialized agent TutorRL (7.42/7.13). On the Multi-Student Management (MSM) metric specifically, ClinTutor-R1 (8.41/8.55) surpasses GPT-4o (8.26/8.39) and o3 (8.18/8.23), demonstrating superior group-teaching capability. In human expert evaluation, ClinTutor-R1 scores 8.73 average, exceeding o3 (8.41) with statistical significance (p<0.05), and in a 200-participant user study it achieves the highest overall recommendation score (8.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model Qwen2.5VL, ClinTutor-R1 improves the average score by approximately 20% (8.35 vs. 6.96 on MedXpertQA; 8.49 vs. 6.83 on MVME), validating the combined SFT + GRPO training pipeline. Among specialized medical-education agents, ClinTutor-R1 leads TutorRL by over 12% on MedXpertQA (8.35 vs. 7.42) and Med-SocraticLM by ~11% (8.35 vs. 7.41), with the gap widening on MVME (8.49 vs. 7.13 and 7.28 respectively). Against proprietary models, ClinTutor-R1 is essentially on par with GPT-4o overall (8.35 vs. 8.36 on MedXpertQA; 8.49 vs. 8.47 on MVME) but clearly wins on the MSM metric (8.41 vs. 8.26; 8.55 vs. 8.39), indicating stronger multi-student management. In human expert evaluation, ClinTutor-R1 (8.73 avg) surpasses o3 (8.41) with p<0.05, and in the 200-participant user study it achieves the highest Overall Recommendation score (8.70 vs. o3's 8.04). On generalization benchmarks (Table 3), ClinTutor-R1 also outperforms Qwen2.5VL on MMMU (58.82 vs. 52.61) and PMC-VQA (56.30 vs. 48.15), confirming that the training transfers beyond the clinical-teaching task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Socratic teaching line (SocraticLM, TutorRL, EduChat) from dyadic one-to-one tutoring to a one-to-many clinical group setting, and builds on multi-agent medical simulation frameworks (AI Hospital, MEDCO, Agent Hospital) by adding a dedicated Safety Supervisor and explicit per-student Theory-of-Mind traces. The training pipeline combines GRPO-based RL (following DeepSeek-R1) with a novel three-axis rubric reward that includes a safety veto mechanism, departing from prior work that used single-scalar or LLaVA-based rewards. The key conceptual departure is framing 'one-to-many alignment' as a distinct problem from standard RLHF, where the agent must simultaneously track heterogeneous belief states and balance individual scaffolding against collective progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real clinical education (ward rounds) is inherently group-based, yet virtually all prior AI tutoring work targets single-student settings. The empirical finding that baseline models hit a sharp 'performance cliff' beyond three students while the ToM-equipped model remains stable up to ten is a useful scalability signal for deploying AI tutors in resource-constrained settings. However, several open questions remain: the evaluation is largely within the authors' own simulator (ClinEdu), the vision modality is limited to static images rather than dynamic ward-round signals, latency grows linearly with group size, and no longitudinal learning-outcome data is provided. The rubric's transferability to non-clinical domains (law, engineering) is claimed but not empirically validated.
