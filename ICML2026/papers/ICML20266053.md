## Problem

Top-k expert selection in Mixture-of-Experts models is discrete and non-differentiable, forcing standard training to drop the gradient of the selection indicator and propagate gradients only through softmax weights of the chosen experts. This limited learning signal leads to concentrated routing distributions, poor expert utilization, and unstable training dynamics. The paper asks whether routing can be formulated as a principled probabilistic inference problem over discrete expert subsets that admits tractable, informative router gradients.

## Input / Output

Given a token hidden state x ∈ R^d, the router produces N logits that are converted to Bernoulli selection probabilities via sigmoid. In the forward pass, a k-hot binary mask (or variable-size mask under dynamic-k) is sampled from the cardinality-constrained subset distribution, and routing weights are formed by element-wise multiplication of the straight-through mask with softmax probabilities. At inference, the MAP subset under the cardinality constraint is selected deterministically, matching the compute cost of conventional top-k routing.

## Method

ProbMoE models expert selection as a distribution over cardinality-constrained subsets: each expert has a Bernoulli probability p_i = σ(r_i), and the joint distribution is conditioned on |S| = k (or k_min ≤ |S| ≤ k_max for dynamic-k). The normalizing constant Z_k is computed exactly via O(Nk) dynamic programming (SIMPLE), yielding exact per-expert selection marginals m_j = ∂log Z_k / ∂log p_j. In the forward pass, a valid k-subset is sampled; in the backward pass, a straight-through estimator w = (stopgrad(z − m) + m) ⊙ π propagates gradients through both the marginals and the softmax weights, giving the router a dense learning signal about how selection probabilities affect expected loss. The dynamic-k variant conditions on a cardinality range, jointly inferring both the number of experts and their identities per token, with the range-constrained normalizing constant Z* = Σ Z_k computed in O(N·k_max).

## Results

On the OLMoE backbone (top-k=8), ProbMoE achieves the best GSM8K Exact Match score of 50.19%, outperforming DenseMixer (47.00%) and Conventional routing (45.94%), while also leading on Law (29.00), Translation (31.63), and Summary (39.29). On the Qwen backbone (top-k=4), ProbMoE attains the highest scores on Law (34.40), Translation (39.23), Summary (44.40), and MMLU-Stem (53.82), though it trails DenseMixer on GSM (53.29 vs. 54.97). In the dynamic-k setting, ProbMoE Dynamic-k matches or approaches Exact-k performance while activating only 75–84% of the experts on average (e.g., −1.82 EM on GSM with 80% expert usage on OLMoE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DenseMixer (the strongest baseline using dense straight-through gradients), ProbMoE wins on 4 of 7 metrics under OLMoE (GSM +3.19, Law +1.10, Translation +1.31, Summary +1.79) but loses on MBPP (−1.60) and MMLU Overall (−0.26). Under Qwen, ProbMoE dominates DenseMixer on Law (+3.65), Translation (+5.48), Summary (+3.40), and MMLU-Stem (+0.95), while trailing on GSM (−1.68). Against Conventional top-k routing, ProbMoE consistently improves on OLMoE across GSM, Law, Translation, and Summary, and on Qwen it leads on Law, Translation, Summary, and MMLU-Stem. DefaultMoE (dense backpropagation) and ReMoE (ReLU-based differentiable routing) are substantially weaker, with DefaultMoE scoring 51.00 on GSM and ReMoE only 46.30, both well below ProbMoE's 53.29. SparseMixer collapses entirely (GSM 1.30), indicating that learned sparse gradient selection is unstable under this fine-tuning protocol. Overall, ProbMoE achieves the best or near-best results across both backbones while preserving sparse expert execution at both training and inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SIMPLE (Ahmed et al., 2023), a gradient estimator for cardinality-constrained subset distributions, repurposing it from its original combinatorial learning context to MoE expert routing. It departs from the dominant paradigm of treating top-k routing as a deterministic operator with heuristic gradient approximations (straight-through estimators, dense backpropagation, noise injection) by reframing the router as a probabilistic layer with tractable normalization over discrete expert subsets. For dynamic-k routing, it unifies what prior work (DA-MoE, DynMoE, AdaMOE) handled through separate heuristic mechanisms—thresholds, null experts, learned gates—into a single range-constrained probabilistic model where cardinality is inferred jointly with expert identities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic formulation provides a principled mechanism for exploring alternative expert combinations during training, addressing the well-documented expert collapse and routing concentration problems in MoE models. The dynamic-k extension offers a clean way to adapt per-token compute without heuristic gating, though the practical impact is tempered by the fact that evaluations are limited to fine-tuning on 1B–14B parameter models rather than pre-training at scale. Open questions include whether the marginal-based gradients remain effective at pre-training scale, how the manually chosen [k_min, k_max] range should be set in practice, and whether the SDD-based implementation scales to the thousands of experts expected in next-generation MoE architectures.
