## Problem

Existing EEG-based Alzheimer's disease classifiers are black-box predictors that map multichannel signals to labels without explicitly modeling the latent event timing and cross-channel coordination underlying their decisions. This makes it difficult to relate predictions back to dynamical structures neuroscientists can compare against established EEG phenomena such as event-driven activity patterns and timing-derived interaction summaries. The paper asks whether one can jointly infer latent per-channel event processes and a directed event-level interaction graph directly from raw multichannel EEG, without any spike or edge annotations, while respecting basic electrophysiological constraints.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2-second non-overlapping EEG windows (1000 samples at 500 Hz) from 19 electrodes in the 10-20 layout, channel-wise z-normalized using training-fold statistics. Beyond the disease class label (AD, FTD, MCI, or HC), LERD outputs per-channel latent event times, a directed event-relational graph (ERG) whose edge weights encode STDP-shaped timing-dependent interactions between channels, and dLIF-constrained latent frequency distributions per channel. The ERG is explicitly a sensor-space, timing-derived summary rather than source-resolved causal connectivity, and no event or edge annotations are required at any stage.

## Method

LERD is a Bayesian latent event-relational dynamical system built from three coupled components: an Event Posterior Differential Equation (EPDE) that produces a continuous-time posterior over next-event times via a neural ODE surrogate, a Mean-Evolving Lognormal Process (MELP) that samples inter-event intervals from a K-component lognormal mixture with reparameterized (Gumbel-Softmax) sampling to capture multimodal timing statistics, and an Event-Relational Graph (ERG) that maps cross-channel event lags through an exponential STDP-shaped kernel into directed edge weights. An electrophysiology-inspired differentiable leaky-integrate-and-fire (dLIF) prior imposes leak, refractory, and plausible frequency-range constraints on the latent rates, regularized via a tractable IVP-based KL surrogate (Theorem 4.1) rather than an intractable path-measure KL. Training is end-to-end variational inference minimizing a negative-ELBO objective that combines cross-entropy, the IVP-KL event-prior term, a lognormal-mixture interval KL, a dLIF rate-consistency penalty, and a weak Fisher-z alignment of ERG edges toward observable Pearson correlations. The decoder fuses an EEGNet-style CNN encoder with a single GCN layer over the ERG through a two-layer MLP classifier, and the paper provides stability guarantees (Theorem A.2) bounding how lag perturbations propagate to ERG edge changes.

## Results

On AD Cohort A (88 subjects, 3 classes), LERD achieves 75.03±8.29% accuracy and 72.69±8.16% macro-F1, outperforming the strongest baseline LEAD (72.68±4.57% acc, 69.98±9.61% F1). On the more imbalanced AD Cohort B (168 subjects, 3 classes), LERD reaches 89.82±8.39% accuracy and 64.87±10.67% F1, surpassing ADFormer (82.38±14.71% acc, 63.89±12.09% F1). On the synthetic toy benchmark, LERD is the only method to recover non-zero boundary IoU (0.473 at 5–10 Hz, 0.289 at 10–15 Hz, 0.202 at 15–20 Hz) while maintaining the highest cosine similarity (0.976–0.983), whereas NODE, ODE-RNN, and STRODE all yield IoU = 0.000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the synthetic toy benchmark, LERD is the only method that recovers latent event boundaries (IoU > 0), while NODE and ODE-RNN (which do not natively output event boundaries) and STRODE (the closest annotation-free timing model) all achieve IoU = 0.000 despite comparable cosine similarity (~0.95–0.97). On AD Cohort A, LERD outperforms all six EEG classifiers by 2.4–10.3% accuracy; the closest competitor is LEAD (72.68% acc), a pretrained EEG foundation model, which LERD beats by ~2.4% accuracy and ~2.7% F1. On AD Cohort B, LERD leads by a wider margin: 7.4% accuracy over ADFormer (82.38%) and 9.8% over LEAD (80.00%), while also showing the lowest standard deviation among top performers (±8.39 vs. ±14.71 for ADFormer). Notably, several baselines (EEGNet, ATCNet, LCADNet) exhibit very high variance on Cohort B (std > 20%), which the authors attribute to the small MCI class (7 subjects) under strict cross-subject evaluation. In inference speed, LERD (9.2 ms/window) is slower than lightweight CNNs (EEGNet 0.7 ms, LCADNet 0.9 ms) but faster than ADFormer (19.2 ms) and comparable to LEAD (11.1 ms), remaining practical for offline clinical EEG analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

LERD sits at the intersection of three literatures: (1) supervised EEG-based AD classification (EEGNet, ADFormer, LEAD), which treats multichannel EEG as a feature-extraction problem and outputs labels without modeling latent dynamics; (2) stochastic boundary ODEs and point-process state-space models (STRODE, Smith & Brown, Truccolo et al.), which model event timing but require observed event sequences or external detectors; and (3) dynamic causal modeling and EEG microstate/HMM approaches, which infer network interactions but demand a priori network structure or operate on continuous-valued latent trajectories. LERD departs from all three by jointly inferring latent per-channel event processes (via EPDE + MELP) and a directed event-relational graph (via STDP-shaped lag mapping) under a single variational objective, without any event or edge annotations, while grounding the latent rates in a phenomenological dLIF prior rather than a full biophysical neural-mass model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's main practical contribution is that LERD produces interpretable, physiology-aligned latent summaries (per-channel dLIF frequency distributions, timing-derived ERGs, inter-event interval statistics) alongside competitive AD classification, giving clinicians and neuroscientists model outputs they can compare against established EEG phenomena (oscillatory slowing, disrupted synchrony). Key limitations temper the impact: both real-world cohorts are small (88 and 168 subjects), recordings use only 19 channels and 2-second windows, the dLIF prior is explicitly phenomenological rather than mechanistic, and the Cohort B AUC (0.838) is actually the lowest among all baselines despite the highest accuracy, suggesting the model may be overconfident on the majority class. Open questions include whether the inferred ERGs carry any causal or source-level meaning beyond sensor-space timing summaries, how the approach scales to higher-density arrays or longer recordings, and whether external multi-site validation would preserve the observed group-level frequency-slowing and connectivity-sparse trends.
