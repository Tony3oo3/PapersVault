## Problem

Existing retrosynthesis methods rely on static pattern-matching (SFT-based sequence generation or classification), producing black-box predictions without chemically grounded explanations. LLM-based approaches trained via supervised fine-tuning tend to replicate memorized reaction patterns rather than perform genuine logical reasoning, and directly applying RL to LLMs for chemical tasks faces domain knowledge disparity and sparse-reward cold-start problems that prevent effective exploration of valid reaction pathways.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262593/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a product molecule specified by its standard SMILES, atom-mapped SMILES, and IUPAC name, augmented by a chemical decision space of Top-4 candidates for reaction type, reaction center, and reactants generated by specialized models (T5Chem and GraphRetro). It outputs the predicted reactant SMILES in a structured <answer> tag, preceded by a natural-language reasoning chain in a <think> tag that explicitly articulates the model's critical evaluation of each candidate set—selecting the most plausible option or rejecting all and generating a novel solution. The decision space is model-agnostic at inference time, allowing any retrosynthesis model to supply candidates without retraining the LLM.

## Method

Retro-Expert is a three-component framework that performs pure RL (no SFT) to induce interpretable chemical reasoning. First, specialized models construct a multi-dimensional chemical decision space providing 'knowledge anchors' that ground the LLM's exploration. Second, the LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) acts as a collaborative reasoning engine performing critical-generative reasoning: it autonomously evaluates candidate sets and either selects the best option or rejects all and generates a novel, self-consistent solution. Third, Knowledge-Grounded Policy Optimization (KGPO) refines the reasoning policy using a training-free Chem-RM reward model that combines RDKit-based structural similarity, reaction-candidate correctness (rewarding both correct selection and critical rejection of wrong candidates), and final-answer accuracy. KGPO modifies GRPO's linear group-normalized advantage with a non-linear rectification term that explicitly amplifies learning signals from trajectories that correct erroneous candidates, steering the policy toward chemically valid critical reasoning rather than blind candidate following.

## Results

On USPTO-50K, Retro-Expert achieves 75.69% Top-1 accuracy, 0.998 BLEU, and 2.01 Levenshtein distance, substantially outperforming the strongest LLM baseline (Claude-3-7-sonnet with decision space: 55.70% Top-1) and the best non-LLM model (Graph2Edits: 67.2% Top-1). On the larger USPTO-FULL dataset, it reaches 58.4% Top-1 accuracy versus 49.7% for the prior best (NAG2G). On the out-of-distribution ChemBench benchmark, Retro-Expert attains 65.67% Top-1 accuracy, more than doubling DeepSeek-R1's 32.54%, and on multi-step retrosynthesis (PaRoutes) it achieves 98.39% success rate with 41% Top-1 accuracy at 20.41 s/sample inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Retro-Expert outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among LLM-based chemical models, it surpasses the strongest (T5Chem at 43.64% Top-1 without space) by over 32 points, and even with the decision space enabled, chemical LLMs like T5Chem (27.88%) and nach0 (14.60%) degrade, confirming their reliance on memorized patterns rather than reasoning. Among general LLMs, Retro-Expert beats Claude-3-7-sonnet (55.70%) by 20 points and Gemini-2.5-Pro (50.61%) by 25 points on Top-1 accuracy, despite using a 7B open-source model versus much larger proprietary systems. Against non-LLM specialized models, it exceeds Graph2Edits (67.2%) by 8.5 points and LocalRetro (63.9%) by 11.8 points on USPTO-50K Top-1, while also being the only method that produces interpretable reasoning. On USPTO-FULL, it leads NAG2G (49.7%) by 8.7 points, and on the OOD ChemBench benchmark it nearly doubles DeepSeek-R1 (32.54%) to reach 65.67%, demonstrating that KGPO induces transferable chemical reasoning rather than dataset-specific memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) for RL-based LLM optimization, extending it with a non-linear advantage rectification (KGPO) that amplifies learning signals from critical correction behaviors. It departs from the dominant SFT-based paradigm for LLM retrosynthesis (ChemLLM, ChemDFM, T5Chem) by using pure RL without supervised fine-tuning, and from tool-augmented LLM approaches (ChemCrow) by making the LLM an active reasoner over a structured chemical decision space rather than a passive scorer of predefined routes. The chemical decision space concept repurposes specialized retrosynthesis models (T5Chem, GraphRetro) from being final predictors into providing 'knowledge anchors' that ground the LLM's reasoning, while the training-free Chem-RM reward model replaces learned process reward models with deterministic chemical toolkit evaluations (RDKit).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wet-lab validation—successfully synthesizing a molecule with no previously published route (79.3% yield) and discovering a novel Jones oxidation pathway (58.82% yield)—provides rare experimental evidence that AI-generated retrosynthetic reasoning translates to practical chemical discovery, addressing a key trust barrier for chemists. The 43.1% self-correction success rate when all specialized-model candidates are wrong suggests the LLM retains genuine generative chemical knowledge beyond mere selection. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer multi-step syntheses beyond the PaRoutes benchmark, whether the chemical decision space construction remains a bottleneck for novel reaction types not covered by the specialized models, and whether the KGPO rectification generalizes to other scientific reasoning domains beyond retrosynthesis.
