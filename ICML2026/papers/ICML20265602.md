## Problem

Existing GNN-based methods for text-attributed graphs assume graph topology is well-aligned with node semantics and perform rigid, noise-agnostic fusion of the two modalities. In practice, structural-semantic mismatch (evidenced by low neighbor semantic similarity and high Jensen-Shannon Divergence) combined with dual-source noise from both topology and text causes message passing to amplify interference rather than propagate useful signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw node text descriptions and graph edge lists. Text is first distilled via an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) into top-K=10 keywords, then encoded by a frozen Sentence-BERT model; the full raw text is separately encoded to initialize GCN-based structural embeddings. The output is a pair of purified embeddings (structural and semantic residual) per node, which are concatenated and passed through a linear classifier to yield node class labels. At inference, the raw embeddings are treated as noisy observations at the terminal diffusion step and iteratively denoised over T=10–15 steps.

## Method

UDPD reformulates node representation learning as a forward-reverse diffusion process over two parallel branches: a structural branch (GCN-derived embeddings) and a semantic residual branch (semantic minus structural embeddings). During the reverse denoising process, the two branches condition on each other via cross-attention, where the structural branch uses a noise-aligned semantic conditioning vector and the semantic residual branch uses the reconstructed clean structural state as a stable reference. A key innovation is an uncertainty-modulated gating mechanism: each node's normalized prediction entropy (u_i ∈ [0,1]) scales the cross-attention output, strengthening cross-modal interaction for uncertain nodes and suppressing it for confident ones to avoid noise amplification. Training jointly optimizes x0-reconstruction losses for both diffusion branches plus a label-smoothing cross-entropy classification loss, with uncertainty re-evaluated at the start of each epoch.

## Results

UDPD achieves the best node classification accuracy across all five benchmark datasets: 92.73% on Cora, 85.89% on Citeseer, 94.93% on PubMed, 83.34% on ArXiv-2023, and 78.55% on OGBN-ArXiv (all averaged over 5 runs). The largest margins are on PubMed (+0.26% over TAPE) and OGBN-ArXiv (+1.53% over TAPE), while on Cora it leads the nearest competitor GraphBridge by 1.12%. All reported metrics are mean accuracy (%) with standard deviation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDPD outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against traditional GNNs, the gains are most dramatic on PubMed, where GAT reaches only 53.50% and GCN 50.11%, while UDPD achieves 94.93%—a gap of over 40 points attributable to the dual-path diffusion actively reconstructing signals rather than passively aggregating noisy neighborhoods. Among LM-based methods, BERT (93.68% on PubMed) and GPT-4o (49.50% on OGBN-ArXiv) show that semantic-only pipelines fail to exploit topology, with UDPD surpassing BERT by 1.25% on PubMed and GPT-4o by 29.05% on OGBN-ArXiv. Among TAG-specific methods, the closest competitors are GraphBridge (91.61% Cora, 85.34% Citeseer, 82.90% ArXiv-2023) and TAPE (94.67% PubMed, 79.72% ArXiv-2023, 77.02% OGBN-ArXiv); UDPD edges past GraphBridge by 1.12% on Cora and 0.44% on ArXiv-2023, and beats TAPE by 0.26% on PubMed and 1.53% on OGBN-ArXiv. HiTuner is competitive on PubMed (94.64% vs. 94.93%) but is not evaluated on ArXiv-2023 or OGBN-ArXiv, limiting a full comparison. Overall, UDPD's advantage is most pronounced on larger, more heterogeneous graphs (OGBN-ArXiv, ArXiv-2023) where structural-semantic mismatch is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDPD extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) from image generation to node representation learning on text-attributed graphs, but departs from prior graph diffusion work (which targets discrete topology generation) by operating entirely in a continuous latent space over GCN-derived structural and LLM-distilled semantic embeddings. It also builds on the TAG-specific line of work (GLEM, TAPE, GraphBridge, LLM4NG) that fuses language models with GNNs, but replaces their static or unidirectional fusion with a cooperative dual-path diffusion where structural and semantic residual branches condition each other via uncertainty-modulated cross-attention. The uncertainty-as-gate mechanism draws on predictive entropy in Bayesian deep learning (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) and recent TAG uncertainty work (Zhang et al., 2025), repurposing it as an adaptive interaction controller rather than a selection criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism for handling structural-semantic mismatch in TAGs, a problem that plagues standard GNN message passing but has been addressed only heuristically in prior work. However, practical adoption is limited by the dependency on a 7B-parameter LLM for keyword extraction at every node, which is costly and introduces a non-trivial preprocessing bottleneck. All experiments are on citation graphs (Cora, Citeseer, PubMed, ArXiv), leaving open whether the dual-path diffusion strategy generalizes to heterogeneous or dynamic TAGs (social networks, knowledge graphs, e-commerce). The modest 1–3% gains over strong TAG-specific baselines like GraphBridge and TAPE suggest the core bottleneck may be the LLM-based semantic initialization rather than the diffusion architecture itself, as the ablation removing LLM embeddings causes the largest single drop.
