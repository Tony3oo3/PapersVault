## Problem

The LLM-as-a-judge paradigm has become the de facto infrastructure for scalable, reference-free LLM evaluation, yet most ranking pipelines implicitly treat all judges as equally reliable. The paper identifies this as a fundamental statistical misspecification: ignoring judge heterogeneity produces biased rankings and invalid confidence intervals, where more data can make evaluations more confidently wrong. No existing framework jointly models judge reliability and model quality with formal asymptotic guarantees for uncertainty quantification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise comparison records structured as triples (model_i, model_j, judge_k) with binary preference outcomes, where ties are encoded as y=0.5 and the comparison graph must be connected. It outputs a vector of latent model quality scores s (normalized to sum to zero) and judge-specific discrimination parameters γ_k (normalized so that sum of log γ_k = 0), from which Wald confidence intervals for any linear functional—such as score differences s_i − s_j or rank comparisons—can be constructed. The scores are interpretable up to a global affine transformation, with γ_k > 1 indicating a judge who is more decisive than average and γ_k ≈ 0 indicating a judge whose preferences approach random chance.

## Method

The framework extends the Bradley-Terry-Luce model by introducing a judge-specific discrimination parameter γ_k that multiplicatively scales the latent score difference: P(Y=1 | i,j,k) = σ(γ_k(s_i − s_j)). This single multiplicative factor captures how sensitively each judge responds to quality gaps, automatically down-weighting unreliable judges through the likelihood without requiring ground-truth labels or heuristic reputation scores. The parameters are estimated by maximum likelihood under zero-sum normalization constraints (Σs_i = 0, Σlog γ_k = 0), solved via projected Adam on the reparameterized (s, α=log γ) space. The paper establishes identifiability up to the natural affine normalization, proves consistency and asymptotic normality of the MLE, and derives the explicit Fisher information matrix, enabling valid Wald confidence intervals for score differences and rank comparisons. Unlike prior approaches (REM, GTR, FTR) that either assume homogeneous judges or use heuristic weighting, this formulation provides a unified probabilistic model with principled statistical inference.

## Results

On Chatbot Arena (20 models, 10 judges), the judge-aware weighted model achieves Spearman ρ=0.9955 with human-evaluation rankings, compared to ρ=0.9699 for the unweighted BTL baseline. On the in-house 45-model dataset, the weighted model reduces average 95% CI width from 0.211 to 0.185 (12.3% reduction) and reaches a given correlation with full-data reference scores using fewer comparisons. In a mixed-quality judge experiment (6 low-quality + 2 high-quality judges), Pearson correlation with the full-data reference improves from 0.8992 (unweighted) to 0.9394 (weighted), and Spearman correlation improves from 0.8316 to 0.9212. In simulations, the judge-aware model maintains ~95% nominal coverage across all sample sizes while the unweighted BTL model exhibits systematic under-coverage that worsens as T increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard unweighted BTL model (γ_k ≡ 1 for all judges), which pools all pairwise comparisons uniformly. The judge-aware weighted model consistently outperforms this baseline across all evaluation settings. On Chatbot Arena, the weighted model corrects a notable ranking error: GPT-4 is placed first (rank 1) versus third (rank 3) under the unweighted model, aligning with the widely accepted consensus. The weighted model achieves ρ=0.9955 Spearman correlation with human-evaluation rankings versus ρ=0.9699 for the unweighted model. On the in-house 45-model dataset, the weighted model achieves 12.3% narrower average confidence intervals (0.185 vs. 0.211) and higher data efficiency, reaching a given correlation threshold with fewer comparisons. In the mixed-quality judge experiment, the weighted model improves Pearson correlation by 0.040 and Spearman correlation by 0.090 over the unweighted baseline, with the larger Spearman gain indicating better preservation of relative model ordering. The paper does not provide direct numerical comparisons against REM, GTR, or FTR, but positions its method as addressing the statistical misspecification that those heuristic-weighting approaches lack a formal probabilistic interpretation for.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model for pairwise ranking by introducing judge-specific discrimination parameters, drawing on Item Response Theory (IRT) formulations from machine translation evaluation (Otani et al., 2016) and crowdsourcing reliability models (Chen et al., 2013). It departs from prior LLM ranking methods—REM (Glicko2-based), GTR/FTR (heuristic triplet aggregation), and Product-of-Experts—by providing a unified probabilistic model with full asymptotic theory rather than heuristic weighting. The key conceptual departure is treating judge reliability as a *discrimination* parameter (sensitivity to quality gaps) rather than an *honesty* parameter (label-flipping rate), which better matches how LLM judges actually vary in quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly critical gap in LLM-as-a-judge evaluation: treating heterogeneous judges as equally reliable produces biased rankings and invalid confidence intervals, with the counterintuitive consequence that more data can make rankings 'more confidently wrong.' The asymptotic theory (identifiability, consistency, asymptotic normality) enables the first principled confidence intervals for LLM score differences under heterogeneous judges. Key limitations include the global scalar γ_k (no task- or domain-specific variation), the assumption of conditionally independent judge errors, and the full-support design requirement; extending to covariate-dependent discrimination, correlated judge biases, and sparse/adaptive comparison graphs are identified as important open directions.
