## Problem

Existing client selection methods in multimodal federated learning operate reactively, responding to current observations without modeling how selection decisions causally influence future optimization trajectories. Under the compounded heterogeneity of modality, distribution, and quantity, this myopic paradigm leads to suboptimal convergence when training dynamics shift rapidly. The paper identifies the absence of an explicit predictive world model of training dynamics as the fundamental gap limiting proactive client orchestration.

## Input / Output

The server consumes a compact 4-dimensional observation vector per round (global loss, gradient norm, inter-client gradient variance, normalized progress) rather than per-client features, keeping the predictive model lightweight. Clients upload local model updates, loss, and variance statistics; the server outputs a per-round participation budget (5–15 clients), a selection probability distribution over all K clients, and surprise-calibrated trust weights for the fusion layer. The central control signal is the surprise score S_t (a Mahalanobis distance between predicted and observed training states), which simultaneously governs budget allocation, exploration-exploitation mixing, and aggregation trust without separate heuristics.

## Method

FedSSM maintains a decision-aware state space model (d=64, HiPPO-initialized) whose transition matrices are modulated by the participation action, enabling the server to predict how different selection strategies causally affect the next optimization state. Counterfactual selection simulates one-step rollouts for a finite action space (30 candidate budget/exploration combinations) and selects the action maximizing predicted utility (negative predicted loss minus communication cost). The prediction error yields a surprise signal that drives three coupled mechanisms: a sigmoid-gated adaptive participation budget, an exploration-exploitation mixing coefficient, and surprise-gated trust weights that down-weight partial-modality or outlier clients only when global dynamics are uncertain. For aggregation, modality-specific routing ensures clients lacking a modality do not update the corresponding encoder, while the fusion layer uses the surprise-calibrated trust weights. The SSM is updated online via a single Adam step per round with exponential forgetting (λ=0.95), adding only ~0.8 s and 2.3 MB overhead independent of client count.

## Results

FedSSM achieves the best accuracy across all four multimodal benchmarks under both heterogeneity levels (α=0.1 and α=0.5), outperforming the strongest baseline FedMBridge by 2.48%–4.48%. On VQA v2.0, FedSSM reaches 68.47% (α=0.1) and 72.13% (α=0.5); on UCF-101, 82.85% and 85.67%; on Hateful Memes (AUC-ROC), 65.72% and 68.93%; and on MiT-51, 41.86% and 44.53%. Communication cost to reach 65% accuracy on VQA v2.0 drops to 1.14 GB (vs. 2.16 GB for FedMBridge and 3.50 GB for FedAvg), a 47% reduction over the strongest baseline. Ablation confirms the decision-aware SSM is the most critical component, with its removal increasing convergence rounds from 22 to 38 on VQA v2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSSM outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Against the strongest multimodal baseline FedMBridge (Chen & Zhang, 2024), FedSSM gains +3.24% on VQA v2.0 (α=0.1), +4.48% on Hateful Memes (α=0.1), and +3.73% on MiT-51 (α=0.1), with the gap widening under severe heterogeneity. Among unimodal selection methods, Oort (Lai et al., 2021) and CriticalFL (Yan et al., 2023) improve over FedAvg by 3–6% but remain 4–10% below FedSSM, confirming the limitation of model-free selection. Gradient-based Power-of-Choice (Cho et al., 2022) and RL-based FAVOR (Wang et al., 2020) are also consistently outperformed (Table 7: +4.20% on VQA v2.0, +2.88% on Hateful Memes). In the fair-comparison setting with fixed 10% participation (Table 8), FedSSM still leads FedMBridge by +1.92% accuracy while requiring 60% less wall-clock time to reach target. On the three-modality CMU-MOSEI benchmark (Table 10), FedSSM achieves 42.61% Macro F1 vs. 39.27% for FedMBridge (+3.34) while saving 33.3% communication rounds. The gap over FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) is largest on MiT-51 (+10.61% at α=0.1), where centralized training with the same architecture only reaches 38.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSSM repurposes continuous-time state space models (S4, HiPPO) from passive sequence modeling into an active decision-making component for federated learning, introducing action-conditioned transition matrices that let the server predict how different client selection strategies will shift the optimization trajectory. It departs from the dominant reactive paradigm in client selection—utility-based (Oort), gradient-based (Power-of-Choice), and model-free RL (FAVOR)—by formalizing a model-based selection definition where the server simulates counterfactual futures before committing to an action. On the multimodal FL side, it builds on modality-specific routing from FedMSplit and FedMBridge but adds a surprise-calibrated trust mechanism for the fusion layer, unifying budget, exploration, and aggregation under a single prediction-error signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proactive-vs-reactive framing gives the FL community a cleaner conceptual vocabulary for why model-free selectors struggle under volatile multimodal heterogeneity, and the unified surprise signal as a single control knob for budget, exploration, and trust is an elegant design principle that avoids ad-hoc heuristics. The 2.5–4.5% accuracy gains with 30%+ fewer communication rounds are consistent across four benchmarks and supported by ablations, fair fixed-participation comparisons, and convergence/regret theory. Open questions include whether the aggregate-level SSM can capture subgroup-specific dynamics, how the method behaves under truly asynchronous or highly dynamic client populations, and whether the action-space size (currently 30) limits applicability to very large client pools.
