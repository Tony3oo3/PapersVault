## Problem

Existing Koopman-based dynamics predictors rely on black-box parameterizations that obscure physical interpretability and accumulate error over long horizons, while their sequential composition of short-time propagators creates an inherent O(M) computational bottleneck along the temporal axis. No prior framework simultaneously enforces algebraic structure on the learned operators and parallelizes their temporal composition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262332/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial molecular state (atomic coordinates and momenta, shape 6N for N atoms) and a rollout horizon M, encodes it into a latent vector of dimension K=d via a two-layer MLP, and produces a full sequence of M predicted states decoded back to the original coordinate-momentum space. The Lie-algebraic evolution operates entirely in the latent space, with a sinusoidal time embedding feeding a two-layer MLP that outputs the coefficients of the infinitesimal generator at each step. The projective variant additionally exposes a decoupled scalar center channel that aggregates by prefix-sum independently of the traceless matrix dynamics.

## Method

The core mechanism constrains the Koopman infinitesimal generator to a finite-dimensional Lie subalgebra (Heisenberg, unitary, or projective) and constructs finite-time propagators via a neural Magnus expansion, so that each segment-level propagator exp(Ω) provably lies in the corresponding Lie group. The Zassenhaus factorization decomposes the group exponential into a product of single-basis exponentials weighted by structure constants, making the operator tractable. To eliminate the sequential bottleneck, the paper exploits Lie-group associativity to compute all cumulative propagators via a balanced binary-tree prefix scan (upsweep reduction, downsweep distribution, leaf extraction), reducing temporal composition depth from O(M) to O(log M) with O(M) total work. For the Heisenberg subalgebra, step-2 nilpotency truncates the Magnus series exactly at second order, yielding bilinear leaf construction and O(d) merges; for unitary and projective algebras, the dominant cost is dense matrix multiplication but the logarithmic scan depth is preserved.

## Results

On Met-Enkephalin (M=1000), the Heisenberg variant achieves the best MSE of 7.54×10⁻², outperforming the next-best KoVAE (9.12×10⁻²) and all other baselines, while running inference in 6.36×10⁻³ s—roughly 15× faster than the fastest baseline (MamKO, 9.77×10⁻² s) and ~21,700× faster than GeoTDM (1.38×10² s). Cα-RMSD stays below 0.30 Å across all three Lie-algebra variants, and the Heisenberg variant also achieves the lowest Cα-Cα distance error (3.59×10⁻² Å) and Rg MAE (4.59×10⁻² Å). On alkanes (C₂–C₉), the Projective variant attains the best overall RMSD (1.18×10⁻¹ Å) with inference times of 2.57–3.12×10⁻² s, again orders of magnitude faster than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all eight baselines on both accuracy and efficiency across Met-Enkephalin and alkanes. Among Koopman-based models, KoVAE is the closest competitor on MSE (9.12×10⁻² vs. 7.54×10⁻² for Heisenberg), but the proposed method is ~276× faster at inference (6.36×10⁻³ s vs. 1.76 s) and ~3.3× faster at training. MamKO is the fastest Koopman baseline (9.77×10⁻² s inference) yet still 15× slower and 30% worse in MSE. Among generative/probabilistic baselines, GeoTDM achieves the best MSE (4.38×10⁻¹) but at 1.38×10² s inference—over 21,000× slower than the proposed method—and its Cα-RMSD (6.16×10⁻¹ Å) is more than double that of the Heisenberg variant (2.89×10⁻¹ Å). SEGNO and EqMotion show substantially worse MSE (5.94×10⁻¹ and 5.01×10⁰ respectively) with inference times of 1.85 s and 3.35 s. KoNODE and KooNPro exhibit severe error accumulation (MSE > 0.9) and are 100–300× slower at inference. On alkanes, the Projective variant achieves the best RMSD (1.18×10⁻¹ Å) while remaining 2.3×10³–3.2×10³× faster than the strongest prior method, confirming consistent gains across molecular complexity scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Koopman operator learning and Lie-group numerical methods. Prior Koopman-based approaches (Koopa, KoVAE, KooNPro, KoNODE, MamKO) parameterize propagators as general linear operators composed sequentially, while generative models (GeoTDM, SEGNO, EqMotion) achieve higher accuracy at substantial sequential cost. The key departure here is twofold: (1) constraining the infinitesimal generator to a finite-dimensional Lie subalgebra (Heisenberg, unitary, or projective) and recovering propagators via a neural Magnus expansion, and (2) exploiting the associativity of the resulting Lie group to replace sequential composition with a balanced-tree prefix scan (Blelloch, 1989), reducing temporal depth from O(M) to O(log M). The Magnus expansion (Blanes et al., 2009) and Zassenhaus factorization provide the algebraic machinery, but their combination with prefix-scan parallelization for Koopman dynamics is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled path to long-horizon dynamics prediction with guaranteed algebraic consistency (norm preservation for unitary, exact nilpotent truncation for Heisenberg) and logarithmic-depth temporal composition, which is practically important for molecular dynamics and other systems where sequential rollout is the bottleneck. The demonstrated 1.45×10^6 timestep scalability on Lotka-Volterra and orders-of-magnitude speedups on molecular benchmarks suggest the approach could enable previously intractable prediction horizons. Open questions include whether the Lie subalgebra constraints (particularly the Heisenberg nilpotency) are too restrictive for highly nonlinear or high-dimensional systems, whether the approach extends naturally to stochastic or partially observed dynamics, and whether the quadratic-in-Q leaf cost for the Heisenberg second-order term becomes a bottleneck at fine time discretization.
