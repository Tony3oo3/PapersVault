## Problem

Most world model methods for model-based RL are trained independently per task or rely on offline multi-task datasets, failing to exploit latent structural similarities among tasks in online interactive settings. Naively sharing a single world model across heterogeneous tasks causes severe negative transfer due to conflicting dynamics scales and reward structures, while existing multi-task RL approaches focus on sharing policies or value functions rather than environment dynamics. The paper addresses how to structure cross-task knowledge sharing in world models to improve individual task learning online without incurring catastrophic interference.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either low-dimensional proprioceptive state vectors (e.g., 12-dim for Meta-World) or high-dimensional RGB image observations (e.g., 84×84×3 for DMControl Visual Control) as input, processed through an RSSM encoder. The world model outputs a stochastic latent state prediction (categorical distribution over discrete latents) that drives imagination rollouts, while task-specific actor-critic networks consume these rollouts to produce continuous actions. A key structural detail is that the stochastic latent prediction is formed by summing the group-specific dynamics predictor with a MoE-based shared knowledge residual, so the output distribution is a weighted combination of intra-group and inter-group dynamics.

## Method

TaskLoom introduces a grouped two-stage training paradigm built on DreamerV3's RSSM. In Stage 1, tasks are partitioned into groups via gradient-based similarity (agreement vectors computed over training, aggregated into a co-association matrix, then resolved by agglomerative clustering), and tasks within each group jointly train a single shared world model, enabling fine-grained intra-group knowledge sharing while keeping actor-critic networks task-specific. In Stage 2, a Mixture-of-Experts Shared Knowledge (SK) module is introduced: for each group g, the dynamics predictors of all other groups serve as frozen experts, and a lightweight MLP router computes softmax weights to produce a residual correction added to the group's own dynamics predictor before sampling the stochastic latent state. The current group's own dynamics predictor is explicitly excluded from SK, ensuring the shared knowledge contributes only coarse-grained, cross-group information as a residual bias rather than replacing group-specific dynamics. This hierarchical design—fine-grained sharing within gradient-similar groups and coarse-grained transfer across groups via MoE—mitigates negative transfer while enabling scalable knowledge reuse across diverse tasks.

## Results

On the DMControl Proprio Control benchmark (18 tasks, 50K interaction steps), TaskLoom achieves a mean return of 647.4 and median of 775.7, substantially outperforming DreamerV3 (456.1/501.5) and TD-MPC2 (619.1/750.6), while approaching the offline TD-MPC2* (650.7/800.2) that uses 345M transitions. On Visual Control (20 tasks), TaskLoom leads with mean 455.3 and median 464.3, surpassing TD-MPC2 (396.7/314.2) and DreamerV3 (382.0/297.8). On Meta-World, TaskLoom achieves the highest success rates of 0.517±0.075 (MT10) and 0.470±0.042 (MT50), outperforming the strongest online MTRL baseline CARE+PTSL (0.438/0.318).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaskLoom outperforms all 11 baselines across every benchmark. Against the primary single-task world model DreamerV3 [20], TaskLoom gains +191.3 mean return on Proprio Control (647.4 vs 456.1) and +73.3 on Visual Control (455.3 vs 382.0), with the largest per-task gap on Reacher Hard (878.8 vs 768.0) and Cheetah Run (548.7 vs 283.2). Against TD-MPC2 [21], TaskLoom leads by +28.3 mean on Proprio and +58.6 on Visual, though the offline TD-MPC2* variant (345M transitions) edges ahead by +3.3 mean, a comparison the authors note is not directly fair due to the 6900× data budget difference. On model-free baselines, TaskLoom dominates: PPO [41] (50.0), DMPO [1] (331.7), CURL [29] (349.1), and DrQ-V2 [54] (267.7) all trail by 100+ points. On Meta-World, TaskLoom beats the strongest online MTRL baselines CARE+PTSL [39] (0.438→0.517 on MT10, 0.318→0.470 on MT50), TSAC [13] (0.390/0.362), CARE [43] (0.301/0.291), SoftMod [53] (0.243/0.122), and MT-SAC [56] (0.294/0.179), confirming that world-model-based multi-task learning is competitive with and superior to model-free MTRL approaches in these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaskLoom builds directly on DreamerV3's RSSM architecture as its world model backbone and borrows the agreement-vector mechanism from HARMODT for gradient-based task similarity quantification. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant MTRL paradigm—which shares policies, value functions, or representations—by treating the world model itself as the unit of cross-task knowledge transfer. It also extends the multi-task world model line initiated by TD-MPC2 from an offline, single-model setting to an online, grouped, two-stage training framework with explicit hierarchical knowledge sharing (intra-group joint training + inter-group MoE residual correction).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: prior world model methods are single-task, and prior MTRL methods share policies rather than dynamics, leaving online multi-task world model learning largely unaddressed. The finding that naive full-sharing (Shared-Dreamer) causes a ~38% mean-return degradation, while structured grouping recovers and exceeds single-task performance, provides a concrete recipe for avoiding negative transfer in world model sharing. Open questions include whether the one-shot grouping phase remains valid under non-stationary task distributions, how the method scales beyond ~50 tasks without the grouping cost growing, and whether the task-specific actor-critic design (acknowledged as a limitation) can be unified without reintroducing interference.
