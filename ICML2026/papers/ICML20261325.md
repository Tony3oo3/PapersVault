## Problem

Existing contextual slate bandit algorithms with GLM rewards (e.g., Slate-GLM-OFU) require parameter updates at every round, making them impractical for web-scale applications that demand limited adaptivity. No prior work addresses the contextual slate bandit problem under the two standard limited-adaptivity constraints—batched (few pre-specified policy updates) and rarely-switching (adaptive but infrequent updates)—while also achieving regret bounds free of the nonlinearity parameter κ that typically inflates GLM bandit guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round, the algorithm receives N candidate item pools, each containing K items represented as d-dimensional feature vectors (with ‖z‖₂ ≤ 1/√N), and must output a slate by selecting exactly one item per slot. The environment returns a single scalar reward (bandit feedback) sampled from a GLM for the entire slate, not per-item rewards. Despite the exponential 2^Ω(N) slate space, both algorithms achieve poly(N) per-round time by decomposing selection into independent per-slot decisions, and they produce regret bounds of Õ(Nd^{3/2}√T) (batched) and Õ(Nd√T) (rarely-switching) that are independent of κ.

## Method

B-SlateGLinCB partitions the horizon into O(log log T) batches of geometrically increasing length; within each batch, it prunes candidate items per slot using UCB/LCB scores from all prior batches, then samples from a G-optimal design over the survivors. RS-SlateGLinCB adaptively triggers parameter re-estimation only when the determinant of a running scaled design matrix doubles, yielding O(Nd log T) total updates. The key technical innovation is a 'scaling-slate' b_t (the item maximizing ‖z‖_{(V_0^i)^{-1}} per slot) used to construct slot-level scaled design matrices H_m^i with the factor μ̇(b_t^T θ̂_0)/β_t, which ensures H_m^i ≼ H_m^⋆ (the true GLM Hessian) and yields κ-free confidence intervals. This avoids the κ-dependency that arises when naively scaling per-item with μ̇((x^i)^T θ̂_0^i), since the ratio μ̇(b_t^T θ̂_0)/μ̇(x_t^T θ̂_0) = O(1) under the diversity assumption. Both algorithms rely on a diversity assumption (Assumption 2.1) requiring the expected per-slot design matrix to be ρI-bounded, which is strictly weaker than the Ω(κ) eigenvalue growth assumed in prior slate bandit work.

## Results

In synthetic experiments (E1: N=5, d=5, κ≈7.38; E2: N=10, d=5, κ≈150), both B-SlateGLinCB and RS-SlateGLinCB achieve sublinear regret over T∈{20000, 40000, 60000, 80000} rounds, with RS-SlateGLinCB consistently showing lower regret than B-SlateGLinCB. Theoretically, B-SlateGLinCB incurs Õ(RSNd^{3/2}√T) regret with O(log log T) batches, while RS-SlateGLinCB achieves Õ(RS^{1/2}Nd√T) regret with only O(Nd log T) parameter updates. In the SST-2 prompt tuning task (N=6 slots, K=9 candidates, 4870 queries), B-SlateGLinCB+ achieves substantially higher cumulative accuracy than both the no-exemplar and random-exemplar baselines, and its accuracy curve closely tracks that of the fully adaptive Slate-GLM-OFU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three limited-adaptivity baselines (RS-GLinCB, RS-MNL, and SoftBatch), both B-SlateGLinCB and RS-SlateGLinCB significantly outperform in regret across both experimental settings E1 and E2, with the gap widening in the high-κ regime (E2, κ≈150), providing empirical support for the κ-free regret guarantees. RS-SlateGLinCB also makes far fewer parameter updates than RS-GLinCB: O(Nd log T) versus Õ(κN²d²log²T). Against the fully adaptive Slate-GLM-OFU, RS-SlateGLinCB shows a small but consistent regret gap, while the modified B-SlateGLinCB+ (which checks elimination only against the most recent batch rather than all prior batches) empirically matches Slate-GLM-OFU's regret in both settings. In the SST-2 prompt tuning experiment, B-SlateGLinCB+ with only O(log log T) updates achieves cumulative accuracy nearly identical to Slate-GLM-OFU, while substantially outperforming both the base RoBERTa-large model without exemplars and the random-exemplar baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the limited-adaptivity bandit literature (batched and rarely-switching settings) from single-arm GLM bandits (Sawarni et al., 2024; Midigeshi et al., 2025) to the combinatorial slate bandit setting, building directly on the fully adaptive Slate-GLM-OFU algorithm of Goyal & Sinha (2025). The key technical departure is the use of per-slot G-optimal designs and a novel 'scaling-slate' construction that achieves κ-free regret while maintaining poly(N) per-round complexity, avoiding the exponential 2^Ω(N) slate enumeration. The diversity assumption required here (eigenvalues growing as Ω(1)) is strictly weaker than the Ω(κ) growth assumed in Goyal & Sinha (2025), aligning more closely with standard linear bandit literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The κ-free regret guarantees are practically significant since κ can be exponential in the parameter norm, making prior GLM bandit bounds vacuous in high-norm regimes. The batched setting enables parallelization within batches, which is critical for web-scale deployment (advertising, recommendations) where sequential per-round updates are infeasible. The prompt-tuning application on SST-2 demonstrates that O(log log T) updates suffice to match fully adaptive performance in a real NLP task, suggesting limited-adaptivity slate bandits are viable for LLM exemplar selection. An important open question is whether the empirically superior B-SlateGLinCB+ heuristic (checking only the most recent batch for elimination) admits a matching theoretical guarantee, and whether these techniques extend to non-stationary or adversarial reward settings.
