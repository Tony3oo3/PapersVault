## Problem

Sign-based and LMO-inspired optimizers (Signum, Muon, Lion) impose fixed-magnitude updates that ignore parameter heterogeneity: near a minimum, coordinates with vanishing gradients still receive full-magnitude steps, causing oscillation instead of fine-grained convergence. Existing workarounds like hard-switching to SGD at a fixed iteration are temporally uniform, require learning-rate re-tuning, and discard accumulated momentum. The paper asks whether the transition from bounded LMO updates to magnitude-sensitive descent can be made smooth, parameter-wise, and theoretically grounded.

## Input / Output

The optimizer consumes the same inputs as any first-order method—stochastic mini-batch gradients and current parameters—but additionally maintains a momentum buffer whose magnitude distribution is characterized (via median and MAD, approximated by a folded Cauchy) at a single transition iteration to drive the temperature schedule. For SoftMuon, the input is a matrix-valued momentum whose singular values are processed via Newton-Schulz iterations rather than an explicit SVD. The output is updated parameters, with the update magnitude per coordinate (or per singular direction) smoothly varying from ±1 (sign regime) to proportional to the gradient (SGD regime) as the temperature anneals.

## Method

SoftSignum replaces the discontinuous sign(m) update with tanh(τ·m) applied coordinate-wise, where a temperature τ controls the geometry: large τ recovers the hard sign, small τ recovers linear SGD-like steps. The temperature is scheduled adaptively via a quantile-based rule: at a user-specified transition fraction α_sign of training, the momentum magnitude distribution is estimated once, and τ is set so that a prescribed quantile of coordinates maps to 1−ε under tanh, causing coordinates to enter the linear regime in order of their momentum magnitude. SoftMuon extends the same principle to the spectral domain, replacing Muon's hard sign on singular values with the algebraic soft-sign φ(x)=x/√(1+x²) applied to each singular value, computable via Newton-Schulz iterations. Theoretically, both methods are derived as exact solutions to a generalized Bregman-regularized subproblem argmin_d {⟨m,d⟩ + (1/τ)V(d)}, and convergence in the stochastic non-convex setting is proven using the Fenchel conjugate V*(−τ∇f), which for SoftSignum yields ln[cosh(τx)]—a criterion that is ℓ₁-like for large gradients and ℓ₂-like for small ones.

## Results

On next-character prediction, SoftMuon achieves 57.84% accuracy on a character-level Transformer and 63.02% on an LSTM, outperforming Muon (57.36%/63.02%), SoftSignum (57.05%/61.36%), AdamW (56.91%/60.07%), and Signum (56.17%/59.71%). On 130M LLaMA pretraining on C4, SoftMuon reaches 18.288 validation perplexity vs. 18.591 for Muon and 18.709 for AdamW. On 360M SmolLM2 on FineWeb-Edu, SoftMuon achieves 12.387 perplexity vs. 12.541 for Muon, and on a 720M model on FineWeb, SoftMuon reaches 16.216 vs. 16.362 for Muon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoftMuon consistently outperforms Muon across all LLM pretraining scales: 18.288 vs. 18.591 perplexity on 130M LLaMA/C4, 12.387 vs. 12.541 on 360M SmolLM2/FineWeb-Edu, and 16.216 vs. 16.362 on 720M/FineWeb, with the gap widening as model size increases. On the Transformer NCP task, SoftMuon leads Muon by 0.48 points (57.84 vs. 57.36), while on LSTM they tie at 63.02%. SoftSignum beats Signum by 0.88 points on Transformer NCP (57.05 vs. 56.17) and 1.65 points on LSTM (61.36 vs. 59.71), and improves LLM perplexity from 18.688 to 18.519. Both SoftSignum and SoftMuon surpass AdamW on every task, with the largest margin on LSTM NCP (61.36 vs. 60.07 for SoftSignum; 63.02 vs. 60.07 for SoftMuon). On the GNN GraphLand benchmark, SoftMuon achieves the best average rank (1.86) and smallest average margin to the best optimizer (0.21), compared to Muon (2.43/0.84), SoftSignum (2.71/1.25), Signum (3.86/1.62), and AdamW (4.14/2.61).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LMO-based optimizer lineage (signSGD, Lion, Muon) by replacing the hard sign or spectral-orthogonalization map with a temperature-controlled smooth saturating function (tanh or its algebraic analogue), framed through a generalized Bregman regularizer and Fenchel conjugate. It departs from prior optimizer-switching methods (SWATS, DSTAdam) that perform a global, discrete transition from adaptive to SGD, instead achieving a parameter-wise continuous relaxation via adaptive quantile-based temperature annealing. The theoretical treatment connects to Mirror Descent but deliberately avoids constraining the parameter space, using the regularizer purely to shape update geometry rather than define a feasible set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and increasingly relevant limitation of sign-based and spectral LMO optimizers (now widely used in LLM pretraining): their fixed-magnitude updates cause oscillation in the terminal phase rather than fine-grained convergence. The practical advantage of reusing existing Signum/Muon checkpoints (via the α_sign=0.9 default) lowers adoption friction. Open questions include whether the quantile-based Cauchy approximation for the momentum distribution remains accurate in very large models, whether the framework extends naturally to other LMO geometries beyond ℓ∞ and spectral norm, and whether the modest perplexity gains (≈0.1–0.3) persist at multi-billion-parameter scale where sign-based methods are most competitive.
