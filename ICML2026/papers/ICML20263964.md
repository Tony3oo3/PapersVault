## Problem

While VLMs achieve strong performance on curated benchmarks, their reliability under real-world visual degradations (noise, blur, weather, resampling artefacts, geometric distortions) remains poorly characterised, especially across diverse corruption families and severity levels. A central open question is whether visual severity monotonically predicts model difficulty, and whether language-side reasoning can compensate when visual perception is degraded or whether certain perturbations induce sharp perceptual bottlenecks.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation pair consists of a corrupted RGB image (from MMBench's 853-sample or MMMU-Pro's 324-sample stratified 20% subsets) and a fixed multiple-choice text query; corruptions are applied to images only while prompts and answer formats remain constant. The 133 augmentation configurations (126 severity-based at low/mid/high plus 7 binary transforms) are applied deterministically with fixed per-sample seeds. Outputs include per-configuration accuracy, robustness drop in percentage points, Visual Gain (clean minus no-image accuracy), Relative Corruption Error normalised by visual reliance, Mean Corruption Error following ImageNet-C methodology, and tier classification (benign/mild/moderate/catastrophic/positive).

## Method

VLM-RobustBench constructs a 49-augmentation taxonomy spanning nine categories (blur, noise, weather, digital, geometric, occlusion, color/tone, resolution, VLM-specific) plus seven binary transforms, with severity levels calibrated independently for VLM evaluation rather than reused from ImageNet-C. The benchmark introduces Visual Gain (VG = Acc_clean − Acc_no-image) to quantify each model's reliance on visual input versus language priors, and Relative Corruption Error (RCE = Δ/VG × 100%) to normalise corruption impact by that reliance, enabling cross-model comparison. Severity mismatch is quantified via monotonicity violation indicators and Spearman rank correlations between severity level and accuracy drop. A flip-rate decomposition (Flip⁺ harmful vs. Flip⁻ helpful) isolates genuine degradation from compensating gains, and mCE follows the ImageNet-C reference-model protocol. The evaluation spans 15 VLMs from five families (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, Molmo2, Gemma 3, GPT-5.4-mini) using three vision encoder architectures and four distinct preprocessing pipelines, revealing that spatial/resampling corruptions dominate tail risk while photometric degradations are comparatively benign.

## Results

Across 15 VLMs evaluated on 133 corruption configurations, Qwen3-VL-235B achieves the best Mean Corruption Error (mCE) at 50.0% on MMBench and 80.2% on MMMU-Pro, yet still suffers a 30.7 pp worst-case drop from upsample:high. Low-severity glassblur degrades MMBench accuracy by ~8 pp on average, while resampling and geometric distortions (upsample, elastic_transform) cause catastrophic failures up to 34 pp. Severity mismatch is pronounced: 30.2% of augmentation trajectories on MMBench and 56.1% on MMMU-Pro violate monotonic severity ordering, with mean Spearman ρ of only 0.71 and 0.34 respectively. Trivial binary transforms (vertical flip: 10.2 pp, color inversion: 10.4 pp) exceed the mean drop of 39 out of 42 high-severity corruptions on MMBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3-VL-235B dominates on mCE (50.0% MMBench, 80.2% MMMU-Pro), outperforming InternVL3.5-38B (66.4%, 86.5%), Molmo2-8B (78.2%, 100.0%), and Gemma-3-12B (100.0% reference, 101.1%). Within the Qwen family, scaling from 4B to 235B reduces mCE from 77.5% to 50.0% on MMBench, yet worst-case drops remain above 26 pp for all sizes. GPT-5.4-mini (mCE 85.9% MMBench) ranks below most open-weight models and exhibits the highest Worst@Low (11.7 pp), demonstrating that proprietary training does not confer superior robustness. Family-specific vulnerabilities are pronounced: shot_noise:high drops Gemma-3-12B by 12.9 pp versus Qwen by 5.6 pp, and pixelate:high drops InternVL3.5 by 11.8 pp versus Qwen by 7.1 pp. On MMMU-Pro, Gemma-3-12B suffers the highest RCE (24.2%), while Qwen3-VL-4B achieves a negative RCE (−6.8%), indicating corruptions actually improve its performance relative to clean images due to minimal visual reliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the corruption-benchmark philosophy established by ImageNet-C (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) to the VLM setting, expanding the augmentation taxonomy from 15 corruption types to 49 (including VLM-specific artefacts like watermarks and text overlays) and explicitly separating graded severities from binary transforms. It departs from prior VLM corruption studies (Usama et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2024) by evaluating on both visually grounded (MMBench) and reasoning-oriented (MMMU-Pro) benchmarks, introducing Visual Gain and Relative Corruption Error metrics to normalize impact by a model's visual reliance, and conducting a systematic severity-mismatch analysis. The spatial-fragility finding connects to patch-based ViT robustness literature (Bhojanapalli et al., 2021; Paul & Chen, 2022), but the paper's contribution is the large-scale, multi-family empirical quantification of these effects in the VLM context rather than a new architectural insight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark is practically important for safety-critical VLM deployments (autonomous driving, robotics, medical imaging) because it reveals that the corruptions most likely to cause catastrophic failure in real pipelines—resampling artefacts, geometric distortions, and trivial flips—are precisely those least likely to be tested in standard evaluation. The severity-mismatch finding (30% violation rate on MMBench, 56% on MMMU-Pro) challenges the monotonicity assumption underpinning most corruption benchmarks, suggesting that safety assurance protocols built on 'test at increasing severity' may miss the highest-risk configurations. Open questions include whether geometric data augmentation during pretraining can close the spatial-fragility gap, whether the findings generalize beyond the two benchmarks and 20% subsampling used here, and whether the vulnerability is specific to patch-based encoders or is a more fundamental property of cross-modal alignment.
