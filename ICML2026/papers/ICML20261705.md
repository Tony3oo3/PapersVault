## Problem

LLM scaling laws are descriptive rather than prescriptive: they characterize typical training under next-token prediction (NTP) but do not define the optimal training objective. NTP conflates surface statistical noise with global semantic signal, and surprisingly few works have successfully challenged the data-efficiency bounds these laws imply. This paper asks whether principled geometric priors on hidden-state dynamics can break the data term of fine-tuning scaling laws.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text sequences (token sequences) and produces generated text sequences, operating within the standard autoregressive transformer fine-tuning pipeline. During training, it additionally extracts per-token hidden states from the final layer and selects random index triples (s < r < t) to compute the STP loss; no multi-view augmentations, auxiliary predictor networks, or extra forward passes are required. The STP loss is applied exclusively during training and is absent at inference time, so the output format and generation procedure remain unchanged from standard LLM decoding.

## Method

The paper introduces the Geodesic Hypothesis, positing that error-free token sequences trace locally linear geodesics on a smooth semantic manifold, and formalizes this via an ODE model (guaranteeing unique, non-intersecting trajectories by Picard-Lindelöf) and an SDE model at inference (where accumulated unembedding error becomes Brownian motion causing mode collapse). The Semantic Tube Prediction (STP) loss penalizes deviations from local linearity by computing 1 − cos(h_t − h_r, h_r − h_s) for randomly chosen indices, effectively confining hidden-state trajectories to a tubular neighborhood of the geodesic and separating signal (along the tube) from noise (perpendicular to it). This loss is added to standard NTP cross-entropy as L = L_NTP + λ·L_STP with λ ≪ 1, requiring no predictor network (the identity function suffices under local linearity), no manual two-view scaffolding, and negligible computational overhead beyond a cosine similarity. Theoretically, the paper proves a Straightening Lemma and Semantic Tube Theorem bounding the distance between the actual and optimal hidden-state trajectory, and derives information-theoretic links (via Fano's inequality and Gaussian channel capacity) showing that improved SNR strictly reduces both data requirements and error probability.

## Results

On HellaSwag, STP maintains 34.26% accuracy with only 1/2 of the training data and 30.39% with 1/16, compared to 27.93% for standard NTP fine-tuning on the full dataset — a 16× data-efficiency gain. On NL-RX-SYNTH (Llama-3.2-1B), STP shows negligible accuracy drop when the dataset is halved, while NTP degrades significantly. For diversity preservation, STP achieves 88.5% on the '.*' suffix and 68.0% on the '.*.*' suffix, versus 29.9%/28.0% for regular fine-tuning and 68.9%/32.0% for LLM-JEPA, which exhibits mode collapse toward the 35× more prevalent pattern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares STP against two baselines: standard next-token-prediction fine-tuning (NTP) and LLM-JEPA. STP outperforms both across all six evaluated datasets (NL-RX-SYNTH, NL-RX-TURK, GSM8K, Spider, NQ-Open, HellaSwag) and across five model families (Llama-3, Gemma-2, OpenELM, OLMo-2, Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill) and three model sizes (1B, 3B, 8B), as shown in Figure 5. On data efficiency, STP at 1/16 data (30.39% on HellaSwag) exceeds NTP at full data (27.93%), directly violating the data term of fine-tuning scaling laws. On diversity, STP is the only method that successfully learns both functionally equivalent regex patterns ('.*' at 88.5% and '.*.*' at 68.0%), while LLM-JEPA collapses toward the 35× more frequent '.*' pattern (68.9% vs. 32.0%) and regular fine-tuning fails on both (~29%). The ablation further confirms that the identity predictor (no learned projection) outperforms a learned linear projector in all configurations, validating the geodesic hypothesis that local linearity makes the identity function the optimal predictor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the JEPA lineage (Assran et al., 2023; Baevski et al., 2022) and its direct LLM adaptation, LLM-JEPA (Huang et al., 2025), by replacing the learned predictor and manual two-view scaffolding with a geometric identity constraint derived from the Geodesic Hypothesis. It also draws on the curvature-straightening observations (Hosseini & Fedorenko, 2023; Henaff et al., 2021) and the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., 2024), unifying them under a Principle-of-Least-Action framing that treats hidden-state trajectories as locally linear geodesics. The ODE/SDE modeling of training and inference dynamics connects to the NTK literature (Yang & Littwin, 2021) and neural SDE work (Kong et al., 2020), but applies it to temporal token-sequence dynamics rather than depth-wise evolution. Conceptually, the paper departs from the scaling-laws paradigm (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) by arguing that the data-efficiency bound is an artifact of the NTP objective rather than a fundamental limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× data-efficiency result in fine-tuning, validated across six model families, six datasets, and three model sizes with multiple seeds, is a concrete challenge to the prescriptive interpretation of fine-tuning scaling laws and suggests that geometric priors can substitute for brute-force data scaling. The approach is computationally trivial (a single cosine similarity on already-computed hidden states) and requires no architectural changes, making it easy to adopt. However, the evidence is confined to fine-tuning and a very small pretraining experiment (4M tokens, single epoch); whether STP's benefits persist at pretraining scale, across longer horizons, or in multimodal settings remains an open and critical question. The paper also leaves unresolved how STP interacts with test-time scaling methods (Chain-of-Thought, budget-forcing) and whether the geodesic assumption degrades for highly non-linear or multi-modal generation tasks.
