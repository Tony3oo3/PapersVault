## Problem

While Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) explains why hyperparameters transfer across width, depth-wise learning-rate scaling remains poorly understood for modern non-recurrent multi-path architectures with convolution, residual aggregation, and attention. No unified depthwise learning-rate scale exists that covers CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers simultaneously, leaving depth changes to require costly re-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265934/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base learning rate η(L₀) calibrated at a reference effective depth L₀ (via one-epoch grid search over 80 log-spaced points) and a target effective depth L, and produces a rescaled learning rate η(L) = η(L₀)(L/L₀)^(-3/2). The effective depth is architecture-specific: for CNNs it counts conv+nonlinearity layers, for ResNets it is m plain layers plus K residual blocks along the minimal path, and for Transformers it is 2D+O(1) where D is the number of blocks. The rule is applied within the same optimizer and schedule family, and the calibration constant κ is architecture- and initialization-dependent but depth-independent.

## Method

The paper introduces an architecture-dependent effective-depth convention that counts sequential update-bearing units while absorbing fixed O(1) local structure (kernel sizes, block internals) into constants, then defines Arithmetic-Mean μP (AM-μP): a network-wide budget that sets the average of layerwise one-step pre-activation second moments to O(1), rather than enforcing a per-layer constraint. The arithmetic mean is shown to be the unique aggregator satisfying permutation invariance, merge consistency under depth-unit regrouping, and standard normalization, making it the natural choice for heterogeneous multi-path graphs where residual accumulation and branch aggregation make per-layer energies non-uniform. Under fan-in (or depth-scaled fan-in for residual branches) initialization and the AM-μP criterion, the paper proves a shared leading-order η* ∝ L^(-3/2) law for CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers, with architectural details affecting only the O(1) prefactor. Empirically, an automated learning-rate search pipeline validates the predicted slope across three datasets and multiple architecture variants, with fitted exponents ranging from -1.18 to -1.57 (mean -1.38) and a direct zero-shot depth-transfer test reducing median log-LR error from 0.314 to 0.057 decades.

## Results

Across 9 architecture–dataset combinations (CNN, ResNet, ViT × CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), the fitted depth exponents α range from −1.18 to −1.57 with a mean of −1.38, closely matching the theoretical prediction of α = −1.5. The best individual fits are ResNet on ImageNet (α = −1.567) and ViT on CIFAR-10 (α = −1.441). In a direct zero-shot depth-transfer test on ViTs, rescaling a source learning rate with the L^(−3/2) rule reduces the median log-LR error to independently tuned oracle LRs from 0.314 to 0.057 decades, and yields lower epoch-3 transfer loss than raw transfer on 6/7 non-source target depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against PathSum (Chen et al., 2024), an architecture-aware maximal learning-rate method. On ResNets (Fig. 3b), the paper's AM-μP prediction (α̂ = −1.435 on CIFAR-10) closely tracks the empirically grid-searched optimal learning rates across all depths, whereas PathSum shows increasing deviation from the empirical optima at larger depths. The paper does not report a single head-to-head scalar metric (e.g., accuracy) against PathSum; instead the comparison is made on the fidelity of the predicted depth-LR scaling curve to independently tuned optima. Beyond PathSum, the paper validates its law against multiple architectural variants (BEiT, CCT) and training configurations (Adam, BatchNorm, dropout, Pre-LN vs. Post-LN), all of which yield exponents within [−1.8, −1.1] (mean −1.39), confirming that the −3/2 exponent is robust to secondary architectural and optimization choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of depth-scaling analysis: the L^{-3/2} maximal-update law for sequential ReLU MLPs (Jelassi et al., 2023), the width-transfer paradigm of μP (Yang et al., 2021), and the Depth-μP framework for infinite-depth feature learning (Yang et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an architecture-dependent 'effective depth' convention and an arithmetic-mean μP (AM-μP) network-wide update budget, which replaces the per-layer maximal-update constraint that is ill-posed for heterogeneous multi-path architectures with residual aggregation and attention branches. The paper also positions itself against PathSum (Chen et al., 2024), a topology-dependent maximal-learning-rate rule, showing that PathSum deviates from empirical optima at larger depths while the proposed -3/2 law remains stable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a simple operational rule—calibrate the learning rate at one depth and rescale by (L/L₀)^{-3/2}—that could eliminate repeated learning-rate sweeps when scaling models in depth, reducing both compute cost and engineering effort. The zero-shot transfer experiment (reducing median log-LR error from 0.314 to 0.057 decades) is encouraging but the finite-depth empirical exponents range from -1.18 to -1.57, indicating the asymptotic law has non-trivial corrections at practical depths. Open questions include extending the framework to MoE, graph-transformer hybrids, and learned aggregation architectures, incorporating adaptive optimizers and full training schedules, and validating on language and multimodal tasks beyond image classification.
