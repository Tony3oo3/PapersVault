## Problem

Existing LLM safety guardrails operate post-hoc, exposing harmful content to users before detection, while prior 'streaming' probes are trained and evaluated on static corpora from other models rather than the target LM's own decoding outputs, leaving the real-time safety question unanswered. Additionally, last-token-embedding probes underutilize temporal context needed for early intervention on transient or mid-trajectory harms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262617/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PlugGuard reads intermediate hidden states (at a mid-to-late layer, ~60–75% depth) from a frozen instruction-tuned LM at each autoregressive decoding step. The user prompt (text or image+text) is processed once by the base model; the guardrail summarizes the prompt's hidden states via attention pooling to initialize a recurrent risk memory, then runs strictly token-wise during generation. It outputs a per-token risk score (logits over harmful/benign classes); a single token exceeding a threshold triggers immediate blocking of the entire response, consistent with a stop-if-harmful policy.

## Method

The core architecture is a Streaming Latent Dynamics (SLD) head: a gated recurrent module (inspired by closed-form continuous-time neural ODEs) that maintains a compact p-dimensional risk state, using an update gate as a data-dependent time constant and a reset gate for selective forgetting of stale context, plus a dynamic extrapolation step to anticipate rising risk. This replaces brittle last-token MLPs by modeling how risk evidence accumulates over the full prefix. Training uses an Anchored Temporal Consistency (ATC) loss that anchors the last N tokens to the response-level label and the first N tokens to benign, while applying total-variation and monotonicity penalties on intermediate tokens to enforce prefix-monotone risk trajectories without requiring token-level annotations. The entire plug-in is 20M parameters trained with AdamW (lr 5e-5, cosine schedule) on response-level labels only, adding <0.5 ms per-token latency in sequential mode and effectively zero in parallel (asynchronous) deployment.

## Results

PlugGuard (20M params, <0.5 ms/token overhead) achieves a 22.80% average F1 gain over the best prior streaming guardrails. On the four StreamGuardBench datasets, its streaming F1 scores are 0.8333 (WildGuard), 0.9246 (S-Eval), 0.8016 (MMSafetyBench), and 0.8028 (FigStep), yielding an average F1 of 0.8455 that surpasses even the much larger fine-tuned LM judges ShieldGemma-SFT (0.8394) and LlamaGuard3-SFT (0.7408). In the harmful-rate comparison on WildGuard, PlugGuard reduces the harmful rate to 3.1% (2.0% in streaming mode) versus 16.5% with no defense, while preserving MT-Bench helpfulness (7.66 vs. 7.74 baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against lightweight post-hoc classifiers (RoBERTa, T5, DeBERTa), PlugGuard wins on every dataset in both response-level and streaming F1; the largest gap is on WildGuard streaming F1 where PlugGuard (0.8333) beats RoBERTa (0.7662) by 6.7 points and T5 (0.4104) by 42.3 points. Against zero-shot LM judges, PlugGuard dominates: on S-Eval streaming F1 it exceeds ShieldGemma (0.6545) by 27.0 points and LlamaGuard3 (0.4545) by 47.0 points. Even after supervised fine-tuning, PlugGuard's average F1 (0.8455) edges out ShieldGemma-SFT (0.8394) and substantially outperforms LlamaGuard3-SFT (0.7408), despite using 400× fewer trainable parameters (20M vs. 8–9B). Among streaming guardrails, PlugGuard's advantage is most pronounced: on WildGuard streaming F1 it outperforms DSA:PLR (0.4979) by 33.5%, DSA:RTB (0.4577) by 37.5%, and ShieldHead (0.3310) by 50.2%, while using fewer parameters than all three. In the decoding-time safety comparison (Table 3), PlugGuard reduces the harmful rate to 3.1% (2.0% streaming) versus SafeDecoding's 14.4% and Model Surgery's 16.1%, while maintaining MT-Bench scores within 0.08 of the undefended baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work: post-hoc safety classifiers (LlamaGuard3, ShieldGemma, encoder-based probes) that only inspect completed responses, and early streaming probes (ShieldHead, DSA, SCM) that attach lightweight MLPs to last-token embeddings but are still trained and evaluated on static, model-agnostic corpora. PlugGuard repurposes the gated-recurrence architecture (reminiscent of GRU gates) as a closed-form discrete approximation of a continuous-time latent ODE to track risk accumulation across tokens, and introduces an Anchored Temporal Consistency loss that encodes the stop-if-harmful policy as a monotonicity inductive bias. The evaluation protocol itself is a departure: by generating all responses from the target model under deterministic decoding, StreamGuardBench eliminates the distribution mismatch that plagues prior streaming-guardrail evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StreamGuardBench fills a concrete gap in the safety-evaluation toolchain: for the first time, streaming guardrails can be measured against the actual token-by-token outputs of the model they protect, across both text and vision-language settings. The practical deployment story—20M trainable parameters, sub-0.5 ms per-token overhead, cross-model transfer without target-specific data collection—makes real-time safety monitoring feasible in production pipelines that cannot afford to fine-tune an 8B judge model. Open questions remain around multilingual and audio/video coverage, the empirical (rather than learned) layer-selection heuristic, and robustness against emerging adversarial patterns that may not be captured by current benchmark prompts.
