## Problem

Standard supervised fine-tuning applies uniform token-level cross-entropy, implicitly treating every token in expert demonstrations as equally informative. In reasoning tasks, however, correctness is defined by logical validity rather than exact token realizations, and low-probability tokens often correspond to stylistic or formatting artifacts rather than core reasoning steps. This causes disproportionate gradient updates on surface-level variations, misallocating optimization pressure and degrading reasoning generalization.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a text prompt paired with an expert chain-of-thought demonstration sequence, computing a per-token loss over the target sequence. During inference, the fine-tuned model generates free-form reasoning text terminated by a final answer (typically in \boxed{} format). The loss is applied token-by-token with no additional hyperparameters, and the model is trained for a single epoch with standard autoregressive decoding at temperature 1.0.

## Method

BLL-Loss replaces the standard cross-entropy term −log π_θ(y_t|x) with log(2 − π_θ(y_t|x)), a parameter-free objective that is bounded in [0, log 2] and whose gradient is modulated by a dynamic factor λ(π) = π/(2−π) that vanishes as the target probability approaches zero. The loss is derived from two independent perspectives: (1) a series-convergence argument showing that the unbounded higher-order terms of the CE expansion can be regularized by imposing an alternating-sign structure, yielding log(1+ε) = log(2−π); and (2) a log-odds decomposition that recasts CE as a DPO-like logistic form with a balancing coefficient β, where the limit β→0 (pure complement suppression) recovers the identical objective. This dual derivation establishes that the gradient norm for low-probability tokens shrinks to zero rather than saturating at 1 as in standard CE, effectively implementing a soft-screening mechanism that suppresses optimization interference from stylistic artifacts while preserving full corrective force on high-confidence reasoning tokens.

## Results

BLL-Loss achieves consistent and substantial improvements over standard SFT across all model scales, with overall average accuracy gains of +9.57% (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: 41.56% vs. 28.36%), +20.41% (Qwen2.5-Math-7B: 46.46% vs. 35.95%), and +11.21% (Qwen3-1.7B-Base: 32.27% vs. 22.83%). Gains are most pronounced on competition-level benchmarks (Olympiad, AMC, AIME), where standard SFT often degrades below the base model (e.g., Qwen3-8B: SFT 15.42% vs. Base 23.81% on Comp. Avg) while BLL-Loss improves to 26.58%. On code generation, BLL-Loss raises MBPP accuracy to 61.8% (+23.40% over SFT) on Qwen3-4B, and on BBH it yields +14.36% (51.49% → 65.85%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLL-Loss decisively outperforms standard SFT (cross-entropy) across all five model scales, with overall average gains ranging from +6.84% (Qwen3-8B) to +22.77% (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B); the gap widens on competition math where SFT sometimes degrades below the base model. Against NEFTune (NEFT), BLL-Loss wins by +10.87% to +20.78% overall, indicating that embedding-level noise injection cannot substitute for the gradient-level confidence modulation that BLL provides. GEM (diversity-preserving reverse-KL objective) is the weakest baseline, underperforming even the base model on competition benchmarks (e.g., 2.03% Comp. Avg on 1.5B vs. 12.09% base), while BLL-Loss achieves 22.91%. Against concurrent token-level objectives (−P and DFT, evaluated on 10k NuminaMath subset), BLL-Loss achieves the highest overall accuracy (35.70% vs. 35.26% and 35.64% on Qwen3-1.7B; 43.02% vs. 42.70% and 42.26% on Qwen3-4B) and shows the largest margins on AIME (+4.16% and +2.92% respectively), confirming its particular strength on the hardest problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the superficial alignment hypothesis (Zhou et al., 2023a) that SFT primarily elicits pre-trained capabilities rather than injecting new knowledge, and on DPO's logistic formulation structure, from which the second derivation perspective (log-odds decomposition) directly adapts the target-promotion vs. complement-suppression framing. It departs from standard cross-entropy SFT by bounding gradient contributions from low-probability tokens, and from concurrent token-level objective modifications (Li et al. 2025a's probability-based loss, Wu et al. 2025's reward-rectified DFT) by providing a rigorous two-perspective derivation (series convergence + log-odds) rather than an empirically motivated heuristic. The approach is conceptually opposite to Focal Loss, which amplifies hard examples, whereas BLL-Loss suppresses them on the grounds that in reasoning, 'hard' tokens are often stylistic artifacts rather than capability gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, drop-in loss replacement offers a practical and theoretically grounded remedy for a common SFT failure mode in reasoning tasks, with consistent double-digit gains on competition math across Qwen and Llama architectures from 1.5B to 8B. The key limitation is that BLL-Loss is explicitly scoped to capability elicitation: it fails catastrophically on knowledge-injection tasks where the model genuinely lacks the target knowledge, as low-probability correct tokens are indistinguishable from stylistic noise. Open questions include whether adaptive interpolation between CE and BLL-Loss can handle distribution shift, and whether the principle extends to multimodal reasoning where the pre-trained prior may be less reliable.
