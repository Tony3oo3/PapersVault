## Problem

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) require hundreds to thousands of reverse sampling steps at inference, creating prohibitive latency that limits practical deployment. While distillation has been extensively studied for continuous diffusion models, extending it to the discrete token domain introduces two unresolved challenges: the inverse distillation objective lacks uniqueness guarantees (risking suboptimal solutions), and backpropagation through discrete categorical sampling is non-differentiable and unstable.

## Input / Output

The student generator G_θ takes a noised intermediate state x_t and its corresponding time t as input (i.e., ε = (x_t, t)), and outputs a full clean token sequence of length L. Unlike standard DLM samplers that draw tokens conditionally independently at each reverse step, IDLM produces the entire sequence in a single forward pass per step, enabling 4–64 total reverse steps instead of 1024. For conditional generation (e.g., math word problems), a text prompt is prepended to the noised sequence, and the output is the completed response.

## Method

IDLM extends Inverse Distillation to discrete diffusion by training a student generator p_θ so that the frozen pretrained teacher f* is the optimal denoiser on student samples, with loss L_IDLM(θ) = L(f*, p_θ) − min_f L(f, p_θ). The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that this loss equals the KL divergence between full diffusion trajectory measures, guaranteeing a unique zero at p_θ = p*. To handle discrete non-smoothness, the generator outputs simplex-valued distributions rather than one-hot tokens; for masked diffusion, the SUBS parameterization causes non-masked contributions to cancel, leaving a mask-only loss that requires no backpropagation through sampled intermediate states, while for uniform diffusion a Gaussian reparameterization (inherited from Duo) provides a differentiable path. Training alternates between fitting a fake model f on current student samples and updating the generator via the teacher-minus-fake gap, and the student is structured as a sequence-level mixture over a shared latent ε, enabling coherent multi-token generation in few steps.

## Results

On OpenWebText unconditional generation, IDLM-MDLM reduces the 1024-step MDLM teacher to 16 reverse steps (64× speedup) while achieving GenPPL 32.75 (vs. 41.29 for the teacher), MAUVE 0.93, and Entropy 5.42. For uniform diffusion, IDLM-DCD^g reaches 4 steps (256× speedup) with GenPPL 77.47 and MAUVE 0.89, compared to the 1024-step Duo^g teacher at GenPPL 71.72. On conditional generation (GSM8K), IDLM-MDLM at 128 steps achieves 19.86% accuracy, surpassing the 1024-step MDLM teacher (18.0%) with an 8× reduction in sampling steps, while IDLM-Duo at 64 steps reaches 19.03% vs. the 1024-step Duo teacher at 17.2% (16× reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 16 steps on masked diffusion, IDLM-MDLM (GenPPL 32.75) outperforms all baselines: DiDi-Instruct (38.19), SDTT + Di4C² (44.82), SDTT (61.34), and D-MMD (67.7), while also achieving the best MAUVE (0.93) and lowest GM (0.527). At 8 steps, IDLM-MDLM (79.75) trails DiDi-Instruct (62.24) on GenPPL but leads on MAUVE (0.91 vs. not reported) and Entropy (5.61 vs. 5.17). For uniform diffusion at 8 steps, IDLM-DCD^g (GenPPL 53.61, MAUVE 0.96) beats Duo-DCD^g (69.58, 0.93), ELF (67.32), and FMLM (86.5); at 4 steps it further extends the lead over Duo-DCD^g (77.47 vs. 96.24) and FMLM (77.47 vs. 111.31). On GSM8K conditional generation, IDLM-MDLM at 128 steps (19.86%) surpasses the 1024-step MDLM teacher (18.0%) and IDLM-Duo at 64 steps (19.03%) exceeds the 1024-step Duo teacher (17.2%), demonstrating that distillation can actually improve conditional accuracy while using far fewer steps. The main exception is ELF at 32 steps (GenPPL 24.08), which outperforms IDLM-DCD^g (39.34) on GenPPL, though ELF does not report MAUVE and its entropy (5.14) is lower than IDLM's (5.35).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Inverse Distillation framework (Gushchin et al., 2025; Kornilov et al., 2026; Selikhanovych et al., 2026), originally developed for continuous diffusion models, to the discrete token space of Diffusion Language Models. It departs from the consistency-style distillation line (SDTT, Duo-DCD) by adopting a distribution-matching objective that compares full diffusion path measures rather than teaching trajectory skipping, and from DMD-like discrete methods (D-MMD, DiDi-Instruct) by matching trajectory-level KL rather than noised marginals. The practical formulation leverages two process-specific properties: the SUBS parameterization in masked diffusion (which cancels non-masked states and eliminates the need for stop-gradients) and Duo's Gaussian relaxation (which provides a differentiable path through intermediate uniform-diffusion states).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDLM provides the first theoretical guarantee (uniqueness of the global optimum) for an inverse-distillation objective in the discrete setting, closing a gap that made prior discrete distillation methods largely empirical. The demonstrated 4×–64× inference speedup while preserving (and in some cases slightly improving) GenPPL and Entropy is practically significant for deploying DLMs at scale. However, all experiments use small-scale models (≤0.9B) on OpenWebText and TinyGSM, leaving open whether the few-step gains persist for larger models like LLaDA or on standardized downstream benchmarks. The paper also notes that the GenPPL–Entropy tradeoff must be interpreted jointly, and that continuous-space alternatives (FLM, ELF) remain competitive, so the discrete-vs-continuous state-space question is unresolved.
