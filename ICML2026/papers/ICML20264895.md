## Problem

Existing latent exploration methods for embodied RL (random latent goals, displacement magnitude, directional spanning) are brittle in realistic high-dimensional settings because (i) policy-coupled encoders drift as the exploration policy changes rapidly, corrupting the latent displacements on which bonuses are computed, and (ii) the resulting objectives reward only local novelty or progress along externally sampled directions rather than explicitly expanding global coverage of the reachable latent region. What is missing is a history-aggregated, coverage-driven bonus that prioritizes under-explored directions while down-weighting redundant motion, combined with a representation that remains stable under non-stationary exploration.

## Input / Output

For navigation, the agent receives a single forward-facing RGB-D frame at 128×128 resolution plus odometry; for manipulation, it receives three wrist/top-down RGB views at 224×224 plus a 14-dimensional proprioceptive vector. These are encoded into a 32-dimensional latent embedding by the behavioral encoder. The CVM bonus is a scalar intrinsic reward computed per transition from the latent displacement and a running covariance matrix; the policy then outputs discrete SE(2) velocity commands (navigation, 5 Hz onboard) or 14-dimensional absolute joint-position commands (manipulation, 50 Hz). The covariance is maintained via an exponential moving average with ridge regularization, and the bonus is evaluated through a Cholesky-based solve rather than an explicit matrix inverse.

## Method

CVM has two coupled components. First, a policy-mixture behavioral encoder is trained by minimizing a MICo-style Bellman residual where the target metric and sampling distribution are defined under a weighted mixture of K recent policy snapshots rather than the instantaneous policy; this provably reduces objective variation (Proposition 2) and prevents the representation drift that would otherwise contaminate latent displacements Δφ_t. Second, the exploration bonus rewards each transition by its exact one-step log-determinant gain: b_t = log(1 + Δφ_t^T Σ_{t-1}^{-1} Δφ_t), where Σ_{t-1} is the EMA-regularized covariance of recent Δφ. By the matrix determinant lemma this equals the log-volume increase of the displacement ellipsoid, so the bonus is large for displacements opening under-covered directions and small for redundant ones (Mahalanobis interpretation, Lemma 1). The cumulative bonus telescopes to log det(Σ_T) − log det(Σ_0), which is exactly the D-optimal design criterion under a linear-Gaussian auxiliary model (Proposition 4), giving CVM an information-efficiency justification and a diminishing-returns (submodular) structure that naturally saturates along already-explored directions.

## Results

CVM achieves the highest mean success rate on 5 out of 6 embodied tasks (averaged over 10 seeds). On navigation, CVM reaches 78.7% (easy), 71.9% (medium), and 69.2% (hard), with the largest margin on the hardest setting (69.2% vs. 45.5% for the next-best EME). On manipulation, CVM scores 81.2% (Grasp), 77.8% (Handover), and 70.9% (Targeted Handover), with the most substantial gains on the coordination-intensive handover tasks. CVM also achieves the highest exploration coverage (map revealed %) on both Habitat HM3D and the real-world Unitree Go2 quadruped, with the advantage being more pronounced in the physical setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVM outperforms all seven baselines on 5 of 6 tasks, with the sole exception being single-object Grasp where EME edges ahead by 1.0% (82.2% vs. 81.2%). The advantage grows sharply with task difficulty: on Nav-hard, CVM leads the runner-up EME by 23.7 percentage points (69.2% vs. 45.5%), and on Targeted Handover it beats the next-best HILP by 15.8 points (70.9% vs. 55.1%). Among the directional-spanning baselines, HILP is the strongest competitor in simulation (61.2% on Nav-easy) but degrades substantially on the real robot and harder manipulation tasks. Displacement-based methods (RIDE, EME) and random-latent methods (RLE, RND) show the weakest performance overall, with RND trailing CVM by over 40 points on Nav-hard. The paper also reports that CVM's coverage advantage is larger in the real-world Unitree Go2 setting than in Habitat simulation, and that in the unsupervised (reward-free) regime CVM reaches higher final success rates with faster early learning than all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CVM extends the behavioral-metric representation learning line (MICo, RAP, Chen & Pan) by replacing the on-policy bisimulation target with a policy-mixture objective, directly addressing the representation drift that destabilizes latent displacements under rapidly changing exploration policies. On the exploration side, it departs from the two dominant latent-exploration paradigms—direction-agnostic displacement/goal bonuses (RLE, RIDE, EME) and sampled-direction spanning (METRA, HILP)—by introducing a history-aggregated, direction-free volume-expansion bonus. The key conceptual bridge is connecting the cumulative log-determinant objective to the classical D-optimal design criterion from optimal experimental design, providing an information-efficiency justification that prior latent exploration methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The D-optimal design connection gives CVM a principled, non-heuristic justification for why rewarding covariance-volume expansion should improve global coverage, and the policy-mixture encoder addresses a practical failure mode (representation drift) that limits prior latent exploration in embodied settings. Real-world validation on Unitree Go2 and ALOHA, where CVM's advantage over baselines is larger than in simulation, suggests the method is robust to sensing noise and physical constraints. Open questions include whether the EMA approximation preserves the exact telescoping property at scale, how the method behaves in very high-dimensional latent spaces where the covariance becomes ill-conditioned, and whether the D-optimal interpretation extends beyond the linear-Gaussian auxiliary model to nonlinear dynamics.
