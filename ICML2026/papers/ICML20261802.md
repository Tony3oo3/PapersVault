## Problem

While prior work shows that vision encoders encode ordinal attributes along recoverable linear axes, the text-driven rank axis for VLMs like CLIP recovers only 61% of the supervised linear-probing upper bound, leaving zero-shot ordinal access through language largely unexplored. This paper asks whether MLLM embeddings, which are shaped by autoregressive training and a language interface, can close this gap and whether their latent ordinal structure is directly accessible from text prompts alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261802/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an image (or audio clip) paired with an attribute-specific instruction prompt (e.g., 'Describe this person's age in one word:') to produce a conditional l2-normalized embedding, and a pair of GPT-generated antonymous anchor texts (e.g., 'A portrait of an aged person' vs. 'A portrait of a young person') to produce the rank axis. Both the anchor pair and the instruction prompt are selected via validation-set search over 100 candidates each, with no labeled image-label pairs ever used for axis construction. The final output is a scalar rank score per sample (dot product of the rank axis with the conditional embedding), from which Spearman rank correlation with ground-truth ordinal labels is computed.

## Method

The paper introduces prompt probing, a language-driven counterpart to linear probing where the rank axis a_c is constructed as the normalized difference of two antonymous text embeddings rather than learned via supervised regression. For MLLMs, the image embedding is conditioned on an attribute-specific instruction prompt P_c, which the paper shows acts as a semantic filter that amplifies the target attribute's component while suppressing polysemantic interference, improving both zero-shot rankability (0.734 vs. 0.544 average SRCC) and the supervised upper bound (0.839 vs. 0.799). The paper attributes the MLLM advantage over VLMs to two mechanisms: conditional embeddings that disentangle ordinal attributes, and a smaller modality gap that better aligns text-derived axes with visual ordinal structure. This is validated through three lines of evidence: layer-wise rankability rises as the Logit-Lens Jensen-Shannon divergence between image and text distributions shrinks with depth, a post-hoc I0T gap-reduction transformation on frozen CLIP embeddings recovers a large fraction of the zero-shot deficit (e.g., CLIP-CNX on KonIQ from 0.508 to 0.738), and a cross-attention (IDEFICS1) versus projector (IDEFICS2) architecture comparison shows the same trend. The framework extends to audio via Omni MLLMs, where the same protocol recovers 87% of the supervised upper bound, and reveals asymmetric cross-modal axis transfer (audio-to-vision transfers well, vision-to-audio does not).

## Results

Across eight vision datasets spanning Age, Crowd, Aesthetics, and Recency, MLLM embeddings achieve an average zero-shot SRCC of 0.728, recovering 90% of the supervised linear-probing upper bound (0.813). In contrast, VLM embeddings (CLIP variants) recover only 61% of their supervised upper bound (0.497 vs. 0.820), consistent with prior observations. On linear rankability, MLLMs average 0.813 SRCC versus 0.753 for the seven non-MLLM encoders (vision-only and VLMs), confirming that MLLM embeddings are at least as linearly rankable as modern vision encoders. The gap is most pronounced on abstract attributes: MLLMs reach 0.774 SRCC on KonIQ-10k (Aesthetics) while VLMs reach only 0.499.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four model families: vision-only encoders (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, ConvNeXt, DINOv2-B/14), VLMs (CLIP-RN50, CLIP-ViTB/32, CLIP-CNX), and MLLMs (Qwen 2.5-VL 7B, Qwen 3-VL 2B/4B/8B). On zero-shot rankability, MLLMs decisively outperform VLMs: 0.728 vs. 0.497 average SRCC (90% vs. 61% recovery of the supervised bound), with the largest gaps on Aesthetics (KonIQ: 0.774 vs. 0.499) and Crowd (QNRF: 0.768 vs. 0.555). On supervised linear rankability, MLLMs (0.813 avg) slightly exceed the seven non-MLLM encoders (0.753 avg), indicating comparable or better linear ordinal structure. Ablations show that removing the conditional prompt drops MLLM zero-shot SRCC from 0.734 to 0.544 (Qwen 2.5-VL 7B), and applying a post-hoc modality-gap reduction (I0T) to frozen CLIP embeddings recovers a large fraction of the VLM deficit (e.g., CLIP-CNX on KonIQ: 0.508 → 0.738). Cross-attention-based IDEFICS1 (9B) underperforms projector-based IDEFICS2 (8B) on KonIQ (0.338 vs. 0.718), further confirming that tighter modality alignment drives zero-shot rankability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rankability framework of Sonthalia et al. (2025), who showed that modern vision encoders (ResNet, CLIP) encode ordinal attributes along a linear axis recoverable via supervised linear probing, and who observed that text-driven axes for VLMs fall well below this bound. The key departure is shifting the analysis from contrastive VLMs to autoregressive MLLM embeddings, where the embedding itself is conditioned on a text prompt. The authors formalize the previously un-named text-driven measurement as 'Zero-Shot Rankability' and introduce 'prompt probing' as a language-driven counterpart to linear probing, positioning it as the natural analysis tool in the MLLM era where prompts shape the embedding rather than a learned probe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a text-only axis recovers 90% of supervised linear-probing performance for MLLM embeddings (vs. 61% for VLMs) provides a practical, training-free tool for probing ordinal geometry in MLLM embeddings and suggests that language is a direct interface to latent structure in these models. The mechanistic decomposition into conditional embeddings (disentangling target attributes from polysemantic interference) and reduced modality gap (aligning text-derived axes with visual ordinal directions) offers a testable explanation for why MLLMs differ from VLMs, supported by ablations, layer-wise JSD analysis, a causal gap-reduction experiment, and cross-architecture comparison. Open questions include whether prompt probing extends to attributes lacking clear antonymous linguistic descriptions (e.g., historical periods, domain-specific scales), whether the 90% recovery holds for non-Qwen MLLM families at scale, and whether the modality-gap mechanism generalizes to other embedding properties beyond ordinality.
