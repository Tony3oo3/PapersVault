## Problem

RL for diffusion models has been adapted from LLM-RL (e.g., GRPO) with trajectory-based likelihood estimators, but no prior work systematically disentangled which design choices—policy-gradient loss, likelihood estimation, or sampling scheme—actually drive optimization efficiency and performance. This paper identifies that the likelihood estimator, not the loss functional, is the dominant factor, and that ELBO-based estimation from the final sample alone is sufficient and far more efficient than trajectory-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260738/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pretrained flow-matching model (SD3.5-Medium) via LoRA, taking a text prompt as input and producing a 512×512 RGB image as output. Training consumes a batch of prompts with 24 rollouts each, scored by rule-based rewards (GenEval, OCR) or model-based rewards (PickScore, CLIPScore, HPSv2.1, Aesthetic, ImageReward). Notably, training is performed without classifier-free guidance, halving the NFE cost during sampling, and evaluation uses CFG at scale 4.5 for GenEval/OCR but no CFG for DrawBench.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified RL framework where three components—policy-gradient objective (EPG, PEPG, PAR, or GRPO), likelihood estimator (trajectory-based or ELBO-based), and sampler (SDE or ODE)—are independently swappable. The key technical insight is that an ELBO-based likelihood estimator, computed from the forward noising process on the final generated sample alone (via a single-timestep Monte Carlo approximation of the flow-matching loss), provides a high-quality, low-cost likelihood that decouples training from sampling dynamics. This enables deterministic ODE sampling (10 steps) during training instead of stochastic SDE sampling (40 steps), and eliminates the need to store full diffusion trajectories. The authors prove that EPG, PEPG (a proximal gradient descent formulation over probability measures), and PAR (an L² advantage-regression loss) all share the same optimal minimizer π* ∝ π_ref·exp(R/β), and empirically show that with ELBO-based estimation, the choice among these objectives has negligible impact on final performance, while the estimator choice yields up to 4.6× speedup over trajectory-based methods.

## Results

On GenEval, the proposed method (PEPG + ELBO + ODE) achieves 0.96, surpassing FlowGRPO (0.95) and DiffusionNFT (0.95), while improving the SD3.5-M baseline from 0.24. On OCR, it reaches 0.94 versus 0.92 (FlowGRPO) and 0.93 (DiffusionNFT). On DrawBench, it attains the best PickScore (23.68) and Aesthetic score (6.06), though DiffusionNFT edges ahead on HPSv2.1 (0.344 vs. 0.325). The method reaches GenEval 0.95 in ~90 GPU hours, which is 4.6× more efficient than FlowGRPO (~423 GPU hours) and 2× more efficient than DiffusionNFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlowGRPO, the proposed method wins on every in-domain and out-of-domain metric: GenEval 0.96 vs. 0.95, OCR 0.94 vs. 0.92, and DrawBench PickScore 23.68 vs. 23.50, while requiring only ~90 GPU hours versus ~423 (a 4.6× efficiency gain). Against DiffusionNFT (the prior SOTA), the method matches or exceeds on GenEval (0.96 vs. 0.95), OCR (0.94 vs. 0.93), PickScore (23.68 vs. 23.61), and Aesthetic (6.06 vs. 6.04), but trails slightly on HPSv2.1 (0.325 vs. 0.344) and ImageReward (1.45 vs. 1.46) on DrawBench; it is also ~2× more efficient in GPU hours. Against AWM, the advantage is decisive across all tasks (e.g., GenEval 0.96 vs. 0.89, OCR 0.94 vs. 0.80), with AWM showing particular weakness on spatially structured prompts (Position 0.82, Attribute 0.59). The paper also validates generalization to FLUX.1-Dev (GenEval 0.66→0.93) and to unseen GenEval categories (Overall 0.93 vs. FlowGRPO's 0.90), confirming that the ELBO+ODE recipe is not specific to SD3.5-Medium.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM reinforcement learning (GRPO, PPO, REINFORCE) and diffusion/flow model training, but makes a deliberate conceptual departure from prior diffusion-RL work. While FlowGRPO and its variants focus on adapting GRPO's heavily engineered loss (clipping, advantage normalization, CFG) to diffusion models via trajectory-based likelihood estimation, and DiffusionNFT operates on the forward process with a different objective, this paper argues that the likelihood estimation strategy—not the loss functional—is the dominant design choice. The ELBO-based likelihood estimator is inherited from variational diffusion model theory (Kingma et al., 2021; Song et al., 2020), and the proposed PEPG/PAR objectives are derived from proximal gradient descent over probability measures, providing a theoretically grounded alternative to PPO's heuristic clipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ELBO-based likelihood estimation with ODE sampling is the primary driver of RL efficiency (4.6× faster than FlowGRPO, 2× faster than DiffusionNFT) substantially simplifies the design space for diffusion RL, removing the need for trajectory storage, SDE sampling, CFG, and clipping. This makes reward-based fine-tuning of diffusion models far more accessible and computationally tractable. However, the study is limited to text-to-image generation on SD3.5-Medium and FLUX.1-Dev; open questions include whether the ELBO-dominance finding generalizes to text-to-video, discrete diffusion language models, larger-scale architectures, and whether the single-timestep Monte Carlo approximation remains sufficient at higher resolutions or with more complex reward landscapes.
