## Problem

Existing existence results for Nash equilibria in games with coupling constraints require either nonemptiness of every player's feasible response set (Debreu, 1952) or joint convexity of the feasible region (Rosen, 1965), both of which are violated by simple bilinear constraints. Moreover, beyond jointly convex feasible regions, almost nothing is known about whether independent learning dynamics can converge to an equilibrium, since even finding a feasible strategy is NP-hard in general.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires an initial strictly feasible strategy profile (each player knows only their own component) and, at each of T rounds, receives first-order feedback on their own utility gradient plus zeroth- and first-order feedback on all shared constraint values and gradients. No inter-player communication or shared randomness is permitted. The output is a best-iterate strategy profile that is an ε-approximate constrained Nash equilibrium, certified by the Nash-Gap (the maximum over players of the best feasible unilateral deviation gain), with all intermediate iterates guaranteed to remain in the feasible region.

## Method

For existence, the paper proves that under playerwise concavity of constraints and a playerwise MFCQ condition, every connected component of the feasible region is contractible, by constructing a sequential contraction map that collapses the region one player's dimension at a time using centroids of feasible slices. A fixed point of the resulting sequential best-response map is then obtained via Begle's fixed point theorem on compact contractible sets, yielding a constrained Nash equilibrium without joint convexity or nonemptiness assumptions. For computation in potential games, the method applies log barrier regularized gradient ascent with adaptive stepsizes: each player independently updates via projected ascent on their utility plus a log barrier term η·Σ log(c_j(x)−α_j), with stepsize γ^(t) = min{β^(t)²/(2mL²(β^(t)+ηb)), 1/M_Φ^{η,β^(t)/2}}. The key structural insight is that the regularized game inherits the potential structure, so independent playerwise updates are equivalent to centralized gradient ascent on the regularized potential Φ^η, enabling a nonconvex smooth optimization analysis. An extended MFCQ condition (Assumption 4.5) ensures the log barrier dominates near the boundary, providing a uniform lower bound on stepsizes and yielding O(ε^{-3}) convergence to an ε-approximate constrained Nash equilibrium.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a convergence guarantee of O(ε^{-3}) iterations for the proposed log barrier regularized independent gradient ascent to reach an ε-approximate constrained Nash equilibrium in potential games with shared playerwise concave coupling constraints. In the cooperative two-player game simulation, the Nash-Gap for both players decreases to zero while the constraint c(x) remains below the threshold α = 0.85 throughout all iterations, confirming feasibility preservation. In the five-player network routing game with coupled link capacity constraints (α₁ = 2.7, α₂ = 7), the Nash-Gap similarly converges to zero with all iterates remaining within the feasible region. The existence result (Theorem 3.4) establishes that constrained Nash equilibria exist under playerwise concavity of utilities and constraints plus a playerwise MFCQ condition, without requiring joint convexity of the feasible region or nonemptiness of individual feasible response sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not present a head-to-head empirical comparison table, but positions its O(ε^{-3}) rate relative to two prior results. Compared to Anagnostides et al. (2022), which achieves O(ε^{-2}) convergence for unconstrained continuous-action potential games, the coupling constraints introduce an additional O(ε^{-1}) factor due to the shrinking adaptive stepsizes near the feasible region boundary. Compared to Jordan et al. (2024), which proves O(ε^{-4}) convergence for constrained Markov potential games using a double-loop proximal-point algorithm requiring a uniform Slater condition, the present method is faster by one order in ε and operates as a single-loop independent protocol. A key practical advantage over Jordan et al. (2024) is that all intermediate iterates are guaranteed to remain strictly feasible (via the log barrier), whereas the double-loop method does not maintain feasibility of intermediate iterates. The existence result also relaxes assumptions relative to Debreu (1952), which requires nonemptiness of feasible response sets, and Rosen (1965), which requires joint convexity of the feasible region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) existence of Nash equilibria in games with coupling constraints, where Debreu (1952) required nonemptiness of feasible response sets and Rosen (1965) required joint convexity of the feasible region, and (2) log barrier interior-point methods for constrained optimization (Frisch, 1955; Hinder & Ye, 2024; Usmanova et al., 2024). The paper's key departure is showing that playerwise concavity of constraints plus a playerwise MFCQ condition suffices for existence without either of those two assumptions, using a novel sequential-contraction argument to establish contractibility of connected components of the feasible region (invoking Begle's fixed point theorem). For computation, it extends the single-player log barrier gradient ascent framework to the multi-player potential game setting, building on Anagnostides et al. (2022) who handled the unconstrained case at O(ε^{-2}).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The existence result removes two assumptions that have limited the applicability of prior equilibrium guarantees in constrained games, making the theory applicable to practical settings like safety constraints in multi-robot control or shared bandwidth limits where feasible regions are naturally non-jointly-convex. The O(ε^{-3}) convergence rate for the independent log barrier algorithm is the first provable guarantee for continuous-action potential games with shared coupling constraints, though the additional O(ε^{-1}) factor over the unconstrained case reflects the cost of maintaining feasibility near the boundary. Open questions include extending to stochastic feedback, other tractable game classes (zero-sum, monotone), and Markov games where playerwise concavity may not hold. The requirement of an initial strictly feasible point remains a practical limitation, as finding one can be NP-hard in general.
