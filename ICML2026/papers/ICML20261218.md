## Problem

Spatial intelligence in large multimodal models is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, fine-grained 3D training data. Existing approaches either generate QA pairs from a handful of manually annotated 3D scans (e.g., ScanNet with only 50 classes) or apply feed-forward perception to single images, both of which are difficult to scale and suffer from narrow semantic coverage and domain gaps. No prior pipeline can automatically convert raw video into holistic, multi-level 3D spatial annotations without human intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261218/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw video streams (sourced from ScanNet, ScanNet++, and DL3DV-10K) along with SfM-recovered camera intrinsics and extrinsics, requiring no explicit 3D sensors or manual labeling. It produces a multi-level annotation stack per scene: an optimized 3DGS reconstruction with multi-view consistent rendered depth, open-vocabulary 2D instance masks, 3D oriented bounding boxes, fine-grained instance captions, 3D grounding pairs, and spatial QA pairs covering camera-centric and object-centric reasoning. The resulting Holi-Spatial-4M dataset aggregates 12K scenes, 1.3M masks, 320K 3D boxes/captions, and 1.2M grounding and QA instances.

## Method

Holi-Spatial is a three-stage fully automated curation pipeline. Stage 1 (Geometric Optimization) initializes depth with Depth Anything 3, then performs per-scene 3DGS optimization with surface-reconstruction-inspired geometric regularization to enforce multi-view depth consistency and suppress floaters. Stage 2 (Image-level Perception) samples keyframes, uses Gemini3-Pro with a dynamic class-label memory for semantically consistent open-vocabulary category inference, and applies SAM3 for high-quality open-set instance segmentation. Stage 3 (Scene-level Refinement) lifts 2D masks into 3D via depth back-projection with a geometry-aware filtering strategy (mask erosion for 2D boundary errors and mesh-guided depth filtering for 3D outliers), merges redundant candidates across views by 3D IoU, applies a tri-level confidence gate (keep/discard/verify), invokes a VLM-based agent with zoom-in and re-segmentation tools for borderline cases, and finally generates instance captions and templated spatial QA pairs using Qwen3-VL-30B.

## Results

On the framework evaluation (Table 2), Holi-Spatial achieves a Depth F1 of 0.89 on ScanNet++ (vs. 0.39 for the best 3DGS-based baseline M3-Spatial), a 2D Segmentation IoU of 0.64 (vs. 0.25 for SA2VA), and a 3D Detection AP50 of 70.05 (vs. 6.23 for the best 3D-VLM baseline SpatialLM) — an order-of-magnitude improvement over prior methods. For VLM fine-tuning, Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned on Holi-Spatial-4M reaches 32.6 on MMSI-Bench (+1.5 over the base model) and 49.1 on MindCube (+19.7), while 3D grounding AP50 jumps from 13.50 to 27.98 on ScanNet++ (+14.48 AP points). The dataset contains 12K 3DGS scenes, 1.3M 2D masks, 320K 3D bounding boxes, and 1.2M spatial QA pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Holi-Spatial dominates all baselines across every metric and dataset. In depth estimation, it achieves F1=0.89 on ScanNet++ versus 0.39 for M3-Spatial and 0.21 for LangSplat, a 2.3× improvement over the best 3DGS-based method. In 2D segmentation, it reaches IoU=0.64 on ScanNet++ compared to 0.25 for SA2VA and 0.50 for SAM3, with the gap attributed to multi-view geometric consistency compensating for single-view occlusion failures. In 3D detection, the AP50 of 70.05 on ScanNet++ exceeds the strongest 3D-VLM baseline (SpatialLM, 6.23) by more than 10×, and AP25 of 81.06 surpasses LLaVA-3D (12.2) by an order of magnitude. For VLM fine-tuning, Qwen3-VL-8B+Ours outperforms VST-SFT-7B (32.0→32.6 on MMSI-Bench) and Intern3-VL-8B (28.0→32.6) on spatial reasoning, and beats Qwen3-VL-8B by 14.48 AP50 points on 3D grounding (27.98 vs. 13.50). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that the 3DGS geometric refinement alone lifts P25 from 0.13 to 0.81, while the confidence filter and agent verification together recover recall from 0.69 to 0.89 without sacrificing precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

Holi-Spatial composes three recent SOTA components—3D Gaussian Splatting for per-scene geometric optimization, Depth Anything 3 for monocular depth initialization, and SAM3 for open-vocabulary segmentation—into a unified automated data curation pipeline, rather than proposing a new model architecture. It departs from prior 3D-VLM methods (SpatialLM, LLaVA-3D) that require explicit 3D inputs and from 3DGS-based understanding methods (M3-Spatial, LangSplat) that optimize geometry and language jointly, by treating the pipeline purely as a data generation engine that outputs multi-level supervision (depth, masks, 3D boxes, captions, QA pairs) for downstream VLM training. The key conceptual shift is reframing spatial data curation as a scalable, non-human pipeline over raw video, in contrast to prior work that generates QA pairs from a small pool of manually annotated scans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Holi-Spatial-4M is the first large-scale (4M+ annotations, 12K scenes) fully automated 3D spatial dataset, and its release is likely to become a standard training resource for spatial-intelligence research, particularly for 3D grounding and spatial reasoning fine-tuning of VLMs. However, the pipeline's reliance on per-scene 3DGS optimization limits throughput and may degrade under challenging conditions (motion blur, dynamic objects, limited viewpoints), and open-vocabulary labeling inherits biases from upstream foundation models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the confidence-filtering and agent-verification strategy generalizes beyond indoor settings, and whether the quality gap over human annotations holds on more diverse, uncontrolled video sources.
