## Problem

Existing FL backdoor attacks treat trigger design and parameter poisoning as independent, heuristic components, leaving unexplained why some backdoors persist across many aggregation rounds while others rapidly decay. The paper identifies the missing link: backdoor longevity depends on whether trigger-induced representations are aligned with parameter directions that federated aggregation naturally preserves, a relationship no prior method explicitly models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263137/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (3×H×W, values in [0,1]) and a learnable channel-wise trigger parameterized as three perturbation maps (t_r, t_g, t_b) applied through a binary spatial mask over a localized bottom-right k×k region. It produces three outputs: a poisoned model update Δθ restricted to a top-k% parameter subspace with per-coordinate L∞ norm bounded by ε, the optimized trigger parameters τ, and the selected parameter index set K. The trigger is jointly optimized with the model via a combined attack-and-clean cross-entropy objective rather than being fixed a priori.

## Method

CTO-VPA couples trigger optimization with parameter selection through a signal-to-noise-ratio influence score F_i = (|I_i|/(√S_i + κ))^α, where I_i is the mean update and S_i is the update variance over an N-round window; the top-k% coordinates by F_i form a decision-critical, aggregation-stable subspace K. During local training, attack gradients are projected onto K while clean gradients are projected onto its complement, and all updates within K are further projected onto an L∞-bounded region to maintain stealth. The trigger is simultaneously optimized via gradient descent on a joint attack+clean loss, ensuring trigger-induced representations consistently activate target-aligned features. Theoretical analysis establishes convergence of the projected alternating descent, proves that higher-SNR coordinates resist aggregation-induced attenuation, and bounds the aggregation gap in terms of cumulative subspace variance, providing a principled explanation for why constrained embedding in stable directions yields persistent backdoors.

## Results

Under the single-shot setting (most indicative of persistence), CTO-VPA achieves 71.43% backdoor accuracy (BA) on CIFAR-10, 51.50% on GTSRB, and 50.25% on Tiny-ImageNet, while maintaining main-task accuracy (MA) of 90.60%, 97.30%, and 50.57% respectively. Under continuous poisoning (Multi-shot 2), CTO-VPA reaches near-saturated BA (100%, 100%, 99.88%) with negligible MA degradation. The backdoor lifespan under single-shot exceeds 250 rounds on CIFAR-10, compared to tens of rounds for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTO-VPA dominates all five baselines in the single-shot persistence setting by a wide margin: on CIFAR-10 it achieves 71.43% BA versus 33.72% for the strongest baseline PFL (Fan et al., 2025), 10.66% for FCBA (Liu et al., 2024), 8.30% for DBA (Xie et al., 2019), 2.72% for CBA (Sun et al., 2019), and 1.17% for Neurotoxin (Zhang et al., 2022). The gap is even more pronounced on GTSRB (51.50% vs. 18.70% best) and Tiny-ImageNet (50.25% vs. 21.09% best). Under continuous poisoning (Multi-shot 2), CTO-VPA matches or exceeds all baselines in BA while preserving MA, whereas Neurotoxin suffers severe MA collapse (42.84% on CIFAR-10, 16.72% on Tiny-ImageNet) and data-level attacks (CBA, DBA, FCBA) show lower BA than CTO-VPA on GTSRB and Tiny-ImageNet. PFL is the closest competitor under multi-shot settings but still trails CTO-VPA in single-shot persistence by 2–3×, confirming that the coupled trigger-parameter subspace strategy is the key differentiator rather than attack intensity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing paradigm in FL backdoor attacks where trigger design (data-level: DBA, CBA, FCBA) and parameter manipulation (model-level: Neurotoxin, PFL) are treated as independent components. It builds on the model-replacement scaling idea from Bagdasaryan et al. (2020) but reframes the core problem through optimization dynamics: persistence is governed by alignment between trigger-induced representations and aggregation-stable parameter directions, formalized via a coordinate-wise SNR criterion. The coupled subspace projection (Eq. 13–16) is the key methodological departure, explicitly restricting attack gradients to a decision-critical, stability-selected subset rather than amplifying or masking parameters indiscriminately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimization-dynamics lens provides a principled explanation for a long-observed but unexplained phenomenon—why some backdoors survive hundreds of benign aggregation rounds while others vanish within tens—and suggests that future defenses should target the stable subspace itself rather than relying on magnitude or anomaly thresholds. Open questions include whether the N-round observation window requirement is practical in real deployments, whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities or LLM-based FL, and whether the theoretical SNR bounds (Appendix A.2–A.3) are tight enough to guide defense design. The paper's attack-focused scope means the defensive implications are suggestive rather than actionable, leaving a clear gap for defense-side work that exploits the same stability structure.
