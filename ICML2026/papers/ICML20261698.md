## Problem

Iterative latent-state models can scale test-time compute to improve reasoning, yet the internal mechanisms that make additional iterations reliably generalize beyond memorized patterns remain unclear, and more compute can sometimes degrade performance. The paper asks what internal dynamics must be present for test-time scaling to be effective, and how training can shape those dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each puzzle (9×9 Sudoku or 30×30 Maze) is serialized into a fixed-length token sequence encoding the unsolved grid, and the model outputs the solved grid as a token sequence. The input x remains available as an external condition at every update step while a fixed-size latent state z is iteratively refined via z_{k+1} = f_θ(z_k; x). Under breadth scaling, B independent trajectories from different initializations are run and aggregated via majority vote or convergence-based selection (smallest fixed-point residual over the final iterations).

## Method

EqR frames iterative reasoning as learning a task-conditioned attractor landscape in latent space, where stable fixed points correspond to correct solutions and test-time scaling works when trajectories converge to favorable attractors. Two task-agnostic training interventions shape this landscape: randomized state initialization (sampling z_0 from a Gaussian prior per trajectory) broadens basin coverage and encourages path independence, while path stochasticity (z_{k+1} = z_k + (1−λ)r_θ(z_k;x) + βε_k with small β) mitigates premature trapping in spurious attractors. At inference, compute scales along two complementary axes—depth (D unrolled iterations per trajectory) and breadth (B independent restarts)—with convergence-based selection (picking the trajectory with the smallest fixed-point residual) replacing majority voting once the landscape is well-shaped. Segmented Online Training interleaves parameter updates with latent-state rollouts to stabilize long-trajectory optimization, and a learned halting head enables budget-elastic inference that allocates more iterations to harder instances, reducing average NFEs by up to 17× with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On Sudoku-Extreme, EqR achieves 99.8% exact accuracy, compared to 2.6% for the best feedforward model and 84.8% for the strongest iterative baseline (TRM). On Maze-Unique, EqR reaches 93.0% versus 44.9% for TRM and 0.0% for feedforward models. Under two-axis test-time scaling (depth D=64, breadth B=128), accuracy rises from 86.4% (D=16, B=1) to 99.8% on Sudoku, equivalent to unrolling over 40,000 effective layers. On Mini-ARC, EqR improves over TRM from 48.35% to 55.28%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EqR substantially outperforms all three iterative baselines on both tasks. Against HRM (Wang et al., 2025), EqR gains +44.8 pp on Sudoku (99.8% vs. 55.0%) and +92.7 pp on Maze (93.0% vs. 0.3%), where HRM essentially fails. Against TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025), the closest competitive baseline, EqR improves Sudoku by +15.0 pp (99.8% vs. 84.8%) and Maze by +48.1 pp (93.0% vs. 44.9%). Against URM (Gao et al., 2025), EqR leads by +22.2 pp on Sudoku (99.8% vs. 77.6%) and +41.6 pp on Maze (93.0% vs. 51.4%). On Mini-ARC, EqR also wins over both HRM (55.28% vs. 44.85%) and TRM (55.28% vs. 48.35%), confirming the gains are not task-specific. The largest relative gains appear on Maze, where the attractor-landscape interventions (randomized initialization and noise injection) are most impactful, lifting accuracy from 44.9% (TRM) to 82.2% at the base compute budget before further scaling to 93.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes weight-tied iterative reasoning models (HRM, TRM, Universal Transformer) through an attractor-landscape lens inherited from Deep Equilibrium Models, shifting the question from 'does the trajectory converge?' to 'what attractor landscape does the learned dynamics induce, and does it align with the task metric?' It builds directly on TRM's hierarchical two-timescale latent updates and HRM's nested-loop structure, but introduces two task-agnostic training interventions—randomized state initialization and path stochasticity via noise injection—explicitly designed to shape the attractor landscape so that correct solutions become broad, reachable basins. The Segmented Online Training (SOT) schedule and the depth-breadth scaling framework with convergence-based selection are novel contributions that go beyond prior iterative models, which typically use fixed initializations and uniform compute budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attractor perspective provides a mechanistic diagnostic (fixed-point residual) for monitoring whether test-time compute is actually improving reasoning versus converging to spurious states, and the depth-breadth interaction (breadth only effective beyond D≥4) gives practitioners a concrete scaling recipe. The ACT result—17.4× fewer function evaluations with <1% accuracy loss—makes large-scale iterative inference practically viable. However, all evidence is on controlled structured tasks (Sudoku, Maze, Mini-ARC) with small MLP-mixer/attention backbones; whether the attractor framework and its interventions transfer to open-ended LLM reasoning or larger-scale settings remains an open question, and the attractor analysis, while insightful, lacks rigorous convergence guarantees beyond local Lipschitz conditions.
