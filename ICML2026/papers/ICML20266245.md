## Problem

Under heavy occlusion, a single RGB observation admits multiple plausible 3D completions, yet existing feedforward image-to-3D models collapse to a single observation-overfitted shape with no mechanism for user-directed disambiguation. Optimization-based text-guided methods can enforce prompt following but distort the visible evidence because semantic gradients clash with pixel-level reconstruction constraints. The core gap is that observation fidelity demands rigid, pixel-level control while semantic intent requires only relaxed structural guidance, and no prior method decouples these two control granularities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single occluded RGB image (the observation) and a free-form text prompt specifying the intended semantics for unseen regions, plus N=3 prior images (retrieved from a category pool or generated via Z-Image) that serve as visual proxies for the text intent, since the underlying generators are visual-token conditioned. It produces a complete 3D asset as either a mesh or Gaussian splats, generated through a two-stage pipeline: a 64³ occupancy grid for geometry (Sparse Structure) followed by per-voxel features for texture (Structured Latent). The method is plug-and-play on existing feedforward backbones (TRELLIS, SAM3D) and requires no retraining; on TRELLIS (which lacks pose estimation), the visibility-aware spatial mask is disabled.

## Method

RelaxFlow is a training-free dual-branch inference framework that decouples control granularity within a rectified-flow ODE solver. The Observation Branch uses standard rigid feature injection to anchor visible pixels, while the Semantic-Prior Branch conditions on multiple prior images (multi-prior consensus) whose cross-attention naturally amplifies shared structural attributes and dilutes instance-specific textures. The key mechanism is a low-pass relaxation realized by applying a separable Gaussian blur (σ=1.0) to the prior-branch cross-attention logits before softmax, which the paper proves is equivalent to low-pass filtering the generative vector field—suppressing high-frequency instance identity while preserving low-frequency geometric structure. A time-dependent linear cutoff schedule (ρ=0.2) gates the prior influence to early ODE steps where global semantic mode is determined, and a z-buffer-derived visibility mask spatially restricts prior steering to genuinely occluded voxels in the texture stage, ensuring the observation branch governs all visible surfaces.

## Results

On ExtremeOcc-3D, RelaxFlow with the SAM3D backbone achieves the best scores across all metrics: CLIP_txt 27.26 (vs. 24.08 for SAM3D), FID 39.44 (vs. 50.73), LPIPS 0.51 (vs. 0.54), and Point-FID 81.11 (vs. 100.38), demonstrating improved semantic alignment and 3D quality while preserving observation fidelity. With the TRELLIS backbone, Point-FID drops from 141.48 to 97.79 and FID from 122.68 to 100.75. On AmbiSem-3D, RelaxFlow achieves the highest CLIP_img (0.87) and CLIP_txt (27.23), and dominates the user study with 68.52% overall preference (vs. 9.22% for SAM3D) and 73.91% text-image alignment preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RelaxFlow consistently outperforms both backbone models (TRELLIS and SAM3D) across all metrics on ExtremeOcc-3D, with the largest gains in Point-FID (−19.3% over SAM3D, −31.0% over TRELLIS) and CLIP_txt (+13.2% over SAM3D), confirming that the dual-branch mechanism adds semantic controllability without sacrificing observation fidelity. Among occlusion-aware baselines, Amodal3R and Amodal2D+SAM3D achieve competitive LPIPS but substantially worse Point-FID (129.46 and 127.27 vs. 81.11), indicating they produce geometrically less plausible 3D shapes. On AmbiSem-3D, RelaxFlow achieves the best CLIP_img (0.87) and CLIP_txt (27.23), while 2D-edit-then-3D pipelines (SDXL+TRELLIS, SDXL+SAM3D) score lower on both (0.79–0.81 CLIP_img, 26.71–26.76 CLIP_txt) due to geometry-inconsistent artifacts. The user study reveals a decisive margin: RelaxFlow is preferred 68.52% of the time overall, compared to 9.22% for SAM3D and 7.50% for SDXL+TRELLIS, with particularly large gaps in text-image alignment (73.91% vs. 11.41% for SDXL+SAM3D). In the extended baseline comparisons (Tables 3–4), video-generation (Wan2.2-TI2V+TRELLIS) and multi-view-generation (MV-Adapter+TRELLIS) pipelines underperform on both observation preservation and 3D consistency, while the commercial 2D editing baseline (Nano Banana Pro+3D) achieves competitive CLIP_txt (27.49) but worse FID (77.68) and Point-FID (88.6) than RelaxFlow.

## Relation to Prior Work

RelaxFlow builds on feedforward image-to-3D generators (TRELLIS, SAM3D) that use rectified-flow transformers, but departs from their single-condition paradigm by introducing a dual-branch inference framework that decouples observation fidelity from semantic intent. It contrasts with optimization-based text-guided 3D editing (InstructNeRF2NeRF, Instruct3D-to-3D) that require per-instance inversion and tend to distort observations, and with occlusion-aware feedforward models (Amodal3R, DeOcc-1-to-3) that learn a fixed 'most likely' completion without external controllability. The key conceptual departure is treating observation and prompt as requiring different control granularities—rigid vs. relaxed—rather than balancing them under a uniform mechanism, and realizing the relaxation via attention-logit smoothing with a theoretical equivalence to low-pass filtering the generative vector field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes a practically important gap in 3D generation: when occlusion makes the object category ambiguous, users need a way to steer completion without retraining the generator. The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable to existing feedforward 3D pipelines, and the two diagnostic benchmarks (ExtremeOcc-3D, AmbiSem-3D) provide the first controlled evaluation of text-driven amodal completion. Limitations include small benchmark sizes (264 and 21 cases), evaluation restricted to indoor furniture and two specific backbones, and a ~2× runtime overhead. Open questions include whether the spectral separation assumptions (low-frequency semantics vs. high-frequency error) hold for more complex or outdoor scenes, and whether the approach generalizes to non-rectified-flow architectures.
