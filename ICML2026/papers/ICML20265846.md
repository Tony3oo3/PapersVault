## Problem

Existing methods for joint registration and fusion of hyperspectral and multispectral images either treat the two tasks as disjoint stages (causing inter-task error accumulation) or rely on isotropic Euclidean metrics that ignore the geometric nature of non-rigid distortions. In particular, diffusion-based approaches model structured, directional misalignments as isotropic Gaussian noise, forcing denoising trajectories through a noise-dominated space that disregards image topology and produces spectral mixing and blurred boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution hyperspectral image (upsampled via bicubic interpolation to match the MSI spatial grid) and a high-resolution multispectral image as inputs, and produces an aligned high-resolution hyperspectral image as output. The MSI serves a dual role: it provides the structural priors (via its Sobel-gradient structure tensor) that define the anisotropic Riemannian metric, and it supplies high-frequency spatial details for fusion. Training patches are 128×128, generated following Wald's protocol with 4× downsampling and a 5×5 Gaussian blur for the LR-HSI, and the Gaofen-1 spectral response function for the HR-MSI.

## Method

SA-RFM reformulates image registration as dynamic optimal transport on a structure-induced Riemannian manifold, where the position-dependent metric M(u) = I + βS_Y(u) is constructed from the MSI's structure tensor (band-wise Sobel gradients smoothed by a Gaussian kernel), assigning high transport costs across structural boundaries and low costs along them. Rather than solving OT explicitly, the geometry is incorporated into a conditional flow matching framework via a local quadratic cost approximation c(u, u+Δu) ≈ ΔuᵀM(u)Δu. Two complementary mechanisms enforce structure awareness: (1) a Riemannian Flow Matching loss that measures velocity prediction errors in the anisotropic metric space, penalizing deviations along high-cost directions; and (2) an Optimal Transport Direction regularization that computes a closed-form minimum-cost direction v†(u) = M(u)⁻¹d(u)·‖d(u)‖₂ / (d(u)ᵀM(u)⁻¹d(u)) and aligns the learned velocity field with it, resolving the mismatch between anisotropic costs and Euclidean displacement supervision. The velocity network is a lightweight CNN (~1.1M parameters) using ConvBlocks, trained for 400 iterations with Adam (lr=1e-4), and supports one-step inference in ~9.7 ms.

## Results

On the Pavia University dataset under Elastic distortion (α=600, σ=15), SA-RFM achieves PSNR 42.69 dB, SSIM 0.987, SAM 1.735°, ERGAS 0.931, and RMSE 1.872, outperforming all 9 baselines on every metric. Under Turbulence distortion, it reaches PSNR 43.10 dB and SAM 1.658°, again the best across all methods. The model uses only 1.078M parameters and 21.29G FLOPs—roughly 1/15th the parameters of the next-lightest competitor (NonRegSR, 1.9M) and 1/17th the FLOPs of PRFCo (357.8G)—while delivering the highest accuracy on three of four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-RFM achieves the best score on all five metrics across all three distortion types on Pavia University, Botswana, and Xiongan, with margins of 0.36 dB PSNR over PRFCo (the closest competitor) and 1.2 dB over PSRFDif on Pavia Elastic. The sole exception is Chikusei under Homography, where PRFCo (Mamba-based) edges out SA-RFM on PSNR (42.33 vs. 41.21), SAM (1.758 vs. 2.232), ERGAS (0.972 vs. 1.026), and RMSE (1.951 vs. 2.218), though SA-RFM still leads on SSIM. The two diffusion-based baselines (PSRFDif, RFDif) show inconsistent generalization: RFDif excels on Botswana (PSNR 45.15) but degrades sharply on Pavia (37.77), while PSRFDif shows the opposite pattern. Among CNN-based methods, IR&ArF and MoEPNP are the strongest discriminative baselines but remain 1–2 dB below SA-RFM in PSNR. In terms of efficiency, SA-RFM's 1.078M parameters and 21.29G FLOPs are an order of magnitude lower than PRFCo (16.49M / 357.8G) and MoEPNP (45.42M / 905G), and its one-step inference (9.66 ms) is ~3,500× faster than PSRFDif's 2000-step sampling (33,624 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Conditional Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and the Benamou-Brenier dynamic optimal transport formulation by replacing the isotropic Euclidean kinetic energy with a structure-induced Riemannian metric derived from MSI gradients. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in HSI-MSI registration-fusion (where discriminative CNN/Transformer/Mamba networks first estimate a deformation field, then fuse) and from diffusion-based joint methods (PSRFDif, RFDif) that rely on isotropic Gaussian noise priors. The key conceptual departure is treating registration as anisotropic optimal transport on a data-dependent manifold, where the structure tensor from the MSI encodes directional costs, and resolving the resulting mismatch between anisotropic costs and Euclidean supervision via a closed-form OT direction regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled geometric alternative to both two-stage discriminative pipelines and iterative diffusion models, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with only ~1M parameters and one-step inference (~10 ms), which is practically significant for real-time remote sensing applications. A notable limitation revealed by robustness experiments is the strong dependence on MSI quality: spatial misalignment in the MSI causes catastrophic degradation (PSNR drops from 42.7 to 28.7), since the entire Riemannian geometry is anchored to MSI structural cues. Open questions include whether the local quadratic cost approximation remains valid under extreme deformations, whether the approach generalizes to other multi-modal registration tasks beyond HSI-MSI, and whether the structure tensor design can be made more robust to MSI-side degradations.
