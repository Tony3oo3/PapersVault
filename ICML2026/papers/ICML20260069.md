## Problem

Existing strategic classification frameworks assume manipulation costs are fixed and independent of the classifier's decision, yet in practice today's algorithmic decisions endogenously shape tomorrow's manipulation costs (e.g., test-optional policies reduce test-prep demand while raising essay-coaching prices). This paper is the first to formalize and analyze such time-evolving, decision-dependent cost uncertainty in strategic classification, showing that awareness of policy-dependent costs both reduces uncertainty and better curbs gaming over time.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular agent features x ∈ R^d with binary labels, a known first-stage cost matrix Σ₀, and a polyhedral decision-dependent uncertainty set Ω(β) = {ω ∈ R₊^d : F(β)ω ≤ h + Gβ} whose parameters (F, h, G) encode how the classifier's weights shift the feasible range of second-stage manipulation costs. It produces a first-stage classifier β chosen before cost realization and a second-stage classifier β' chosen after a specific ω is drawn, with the second-stage cost matrix realized as Σ(ω) = Q(g(ω))·Σ₀ through a chain of mappings from β to the cost geometry.

## Method

The firm's problem is formulated as a two-stage robust optimization (TSRO) with a decision-dependent polyhedral uncertainty set: the first stage minimizes a cost-aware strategic hinge loss under known Σ₀ while anticipating the worst-case second-stage loss over Ω(β), and the second stage re-optimizes the classifier after ω is realized. To make the resulting nonlinear tri-level problem tractable, the authors (i) upper-bound the Σ(ω)-dependent dual norm via ‖β‖_{*,Σ(ω)} ≤ M‖Σ(ω)^{-1/2}‖_∞‖β‖_∞ (which also yields a conservative bound on harmful manipulation by unqualified agents), (ii) linearize the max operator and ℓ_∞ norms with auxiliary variables, and (iii) apply McCormick envelopes to relax the resulting bilinear product t₂^q·t₂^ω. The final linear tri-level reformulation is solved with a Benders C&CG algorithm (Zeng & Wang, 2022) that iteratively generates optimality and feasibility cuts over extreme points and rays of the dual second-stage feasible set, which remains fixed across uncertainty realizations thanks to the norm approximation.

## Results

On semi-synthetic university admissions data (10,000 samples, 2 features: SAT score and extracurricular count), the decision-dependent (DD) classifier reduces total 0-1 loss from 49.66 (DI baseline) to 34.23, a 31% improvement, while total manipulations drop from 52.17 to 39.50. The gain is concentrated in the second stage, where DD achieves 7.43 loss versus 23.74 for DI, and unqualified manipulations fall from 41.10 to 15.74. The DD classifier trades a small first-stage loss increase (26.80 vs. 25.92) for substantially better second-stage robustness, and its uncertainty set Ω(β^DD) is strictly smaller than Ω(β^DI), inducing higher manipulation costs. The optimality gap of the proposed approximation relative to brute-force search averages 24.5% (with tight McCormick bounds) but drops to 10.75% when the first stage is kept exact, at a runtime of ~15 s versus ~4.9 h for brute force.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed decision-dependent (DD) classifier against a decision-independent (DI) baseline that ignores how first-stage classifier choices affect second-stage manipulation costs. The DD classifier wins decisively on total 0-1 loss (34.23 vs. 49.66, a 31% reduction) and total manipulations (39.50 vs. 52.17), with the largest gains in the second stage (loss 7.43 vs. 23.74; manipulations 2.50 vs. 18.33). It also dramatically reduces unqualified (harmful) manipulations: 15.74 total vs. 41.10 for DI, indicating the DD classifier shapes the cost landscape to specifically deter gaming by unqualified agents. The DI baseline wins on first-stage 0-1 loss (25.92 vs. 26.80) and total qualified manipulations (10.96 vs. 23.70), reflecting that the DD classifier intentionally sacrifices some first-stage accuracy and permits more benign manipulation by already-qualified agents in exchange for long-term robustness. The paper also reports that the DD classifier's uncertainty set is strictly contained within the DI classifier's, confirming that decision-awareness shrinks the feasible cost-realization space and raises effective manipulation costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of strategic classification and two-stage robust optimization (TSRO). It builds on the cost-aware strategic hinge loss and Σ-parametrized cost functions from Rosenfeld & Rosenfeld (2024), and on the Benders C&CG algorithm for TSRO with decision-dependent uncertainty from Zeng & Wang (2022). The key departure from prior strategic classification work is the introduction of a decision-dependent uncertainty set for manipulation costs: prior works assume fixed or exogenously uncertain costs, while here the classifier's first-stage decision endogenously shapes the feasible set of second-stage costs. The paper also distinguishes itself from Sommer et al. (2025), which models immediate endogenous market effects, by capturing *future* costs that are *uncertain* functions of current decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first formal treatment of time-evolving, decision-dependent cost uncertainty in strategic classification, showing that a classifier can not only defend against gaming but actively reshape the cost landscape to make manipulation less attractive over time. Key limitations include the requirement that cost evolution functions satisfy specific admissible forms (linear g(ω)^{-1/2}, component-wise separable Q), the non-trivial McCormick envelope calibration (gaps range from 2% to 44% depending on bounds), and the restriction to two stages. Open questions include extending to multi-stage settings, deriving theoretical bounds on relaxation gaps, and learning the uncertainty set parameters from historical data rather than specifying them by domain expertise.
