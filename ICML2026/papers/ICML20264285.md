## Problem

Under matched backbones and training budgets, directly trained SNNs still trail their ANN counterparts on static image classification at short simulation horizons, but the gap is consistently smaller on native continuous 1D temporal signals. The paper formalizes why—leaky integration naturally implements causal evidence accumulation for temporal signals, whereas static images require amplitude-to-spike encoding whose finite-window estimation error is non-negligible at short horizons—and asks how to further narrow the residual gap while preserving spiking efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1D temporal signal segment x ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, length L) and produces a class label ŷ ∈ {1,…,K} via a rate/sum readout over T simulation steps. Before entering the spiking backbone, MTE converts the single segment into a T-step real-valued drive stream {X^(t)} where each step corresponds to a distinct temporal resolution (downsampled by powers of two, floored at L_min=32) with a scale-aligned multi-bit spike budget, so the backbone always receives real-valued synaptic input rather than raw spikes.

## Method

The framework couples two plug-and-play modules. Multi-Scale Temporal Encoding (MTE) replaces naive input repetition with a T-step stream where each step carries complementary evidence at a different temporal resolution; a lightweight refinement branch applies a scale-dependent integer-valued multi-spike nonlinearity φ_{m_t} (default budget [4,3,2,1] for T=4) to allocate more representational precision to higher-resolution steps, increasing per-step information density under a fixed short horizon. Sparsity-Regularized Transform Encoding (STE) replaces a controllable fraction p of LIF units with a transform-encoding neuron that applies a learnable analysis transform A, emits integer pulse codes via a multi-spiking nonlinearity, and is trained with an auxiliary reconstruction loss (through a synthesis branch B used only during training) plus an L1 sparsity penalty on the spike codes, directly suppressing redundant firing without adding inference cost. Training uses surrogate-gradient BPTT on the combined objective (cross-entropy + STE auxiliary losses), and at inference all synthesis branches are removed so only the forward spiking path remains. The design is motivated by a formal proposition showing that native temporal signals can devote limited T steps to causal evidence accumulation while static inputs incur an additional finite-window encoding-uncertainty term, yielding two complementary principles: densify per-step evidence (MTE) and compress internal activity (STE).

## Results

Under a matched-backbone, matched-budget protocol with short horizon T=4, the proposed MTE×STE framework achieves 0.775 average accuracy across 10 heterogeneous 1D temporal signal benchmarks, surpassing the matched ANN average of 0.768 and the directly trained SNN baseline of 0.738. Simultaneously, MTE×STE reduces the average spike rate (Rating) from 0.229 (SNN) to 0.175, shifting the accuracy–efficiency frontier favorably. Per-backbone gains are consistent: RNN improves from 0.725 to 0.785, TCN from 0.699 to 0.753, and ResNet18-1D from 0.826 to 0.852, all with lower spike rates than their SNN counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three variants under a strict matched-backbone, matched-budget protocol: (1) the matched full-precision ANN, (2) a directly trained SNN baseline with standard LIF neurons and naive input repetition, and (3) the proposed MTE×STE framework. On the 10-dataset average, MTE×STE (0.775 ACC) surpasses both the SNN baseline (0.738) and the matched ANN (0.768), while reducing spike rate from 0.229 to 0.175. The ANN–SNN gap on native 1D temporal signals is already modest (≈3 percentage points) compared to the larger gap typically observed on static image benchmarks, supporting the paper's mechanism-level hypothesis about leaky integration and causal evidence accumulation. Per-backbone, MTE×STE consistently outperforms the SNN baseline on all six backbone families (e.g., RNN: 0.785 vs. 0.725; TCN: 0.753 vs. 0.699; ResNet18-1D: 0.852 vs. 0.826) with lower spike rates in every case. Gains over the matched ANN are most pronounced on fault diagnosis (CWRU4/10), ECG (MIT-BIH, PTB-XL), and EEG (SEED), while on modulation recognition (RML2016a/b) the method still trails the ANN but substantially closes the gap over the SNN baseline. The component ablation confirms complementarity: MTE alone increases accuracy but also raises spike activity, whereas STE alone regularizes firing; their combination yields the best accuracy–activity trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-bit/multi-level firing SNN literature (Feng et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2025) for its integer-valued spike mechanism, and on the broader surrogate-gradient BPTT training paradigm for directly-trained SNNs. It departs from prior SNN-for-1D-signal work (which typically improves backbones or training pipelines) by introducing a mechanism-level theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) that formalizes why leaky integration is naturally aligned with native temporal evidence accumulation, and by proposing a plug-and-play MTE×STE framework that targets short-horizon per-step information efficiency and internal firing regulation rather than scaling model complexity. The framework is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to backbone choice, applying across RNN, TCN, CNN, and Transformer families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, formalized explanation for a practically observed phenomenon (smaller ANN-SNN gap on 1D temporal signals) and translates it into two actionable design principles (densify per-step evidence, compress internal activity) that yield a plug-and-play improvement across ten datasets and six backbone families. Key limitations include reliance on SOP/SR-based algorithmic efficiency proxies rather than chip-level power measurements, a fixed STE replacement ratio that may be suboptimal for deeper or attention-based backbones, and scope restricted to continuous sensory 1D signals. Open directions include adaptive layer-wise or input-dependent STE policies, comparisons with compressed ANN variants, and extension to discrete sequence domains.
