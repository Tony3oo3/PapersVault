## Problem

The Massart (β-bounded) noise condition is a critical intermediate regime between realizable and agnostic PAC learning, yet it had not been explored in two major extensions of the classical framework: universal learning (which characterizes optimal rates for a single fixed distribution) and partial concept classes (which model data-dependent assumptions via undefined predictions). This paper fills both gaps, providing the first sample complexity bounds for partial concept classes under Massart noise and completing the universal-rate characterization with a pentachotomy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a partial concept class H (functions mapping X to {0,1,★} where ★ denotes undefined), a labeled sample of n i.i.d. points, and a single unlabeled query point x_{n+1}. It outputs a binary prediction in {0,1} for the query point. The theoretical deliverables are in-expectation excess error bounds (Theorem 1) and high-probability PAC sample complexity bounds (Theorem 2) expressed in terms of the VC dimension d and star number 𝔰 of H, with explicit dependence on the noise parameter β. A key structural constraint is that the version space over the sample plus test point must be non-empty, which is guaranteed by the weak β-bounded noise condition.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a sharpened excess error analysis of the transductive ERM (TERM) algorithm under Massart noise, replacing the prior agnostic-only analysis with offset Rademacher complexity and local metric entropy techniques from Zhivotovskiy & Hanneke (2018). The proof proceeds by: (1) an exchangeability argument converting excess error to excess empirical error on a held-out half of the sample; (2) a symmetrization step introducing Rademacher variables to decouple the two halves; (3) a contraction lemma that strips labels and reduces the problem to bounding a shifted sub-Gaussian process over the disagreement set with the Bayes classifier; and (4) a localization step bounding this process via the fixed point of local empirical entropy, which is controlled by d·log(min{n(1−2β)/d, 𝔰/(1−2β)})/((1−2β)n). For PAC sample complexity, a standard confidence-boosting procedure (batching + empirical selection) converts the in-expectation bound to a high-probability guarantee. For universal rates, the paper constructs finite-star-number partial concept classes via pattern-avoidance functions derived from Gale-Stewart winning strategies, then applies TERM with majority voting across k independent such classes to recover linear and log(n)/n rates.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. First, Theorem 1 sharpens the TERM algorithm's excess error bound under Massart noise to O(d·log(min{n(1−2β)/d, s/(1−2β)}) / ((1−2β)·n)), where d is the VC dimension and s is the star number. Second, Theorem 2 gives matching upper and lower sample complexity bounds for PAC learning partial concept classes under weak β-bounded noise: O(d/(ε(1−2β)) · log(min{1/ε, s/(1−2β)}) · log(1/δ)) and Ω((d + log(1/δ) + β·log(min{1/ε, s})) / (ε(1−2β))). Third, Theorem 3 completes the universal rate characterization under Massart noise with a pentachotomy: e^{−n}, e^{−o(n)}, 1/n, log(n)/n, or arbitrarily slow, depending on the existence of infinite Littlestone, star, and VCL trees.

**Table: Pentachotomy of universal rates under Massart noise (Theorem 3).**

| Condition on concept class H | Optimal universal rate |
|---|---|
| \|H\| < ∞ | e^{−n} |
| \|H\| = ∞, no infinite Littlestone tree | e^{−o(n)} |
| Infinite Littlestone tree, no infinite star tree | 1/n |
| Infinite star tree, no infinite VCL tree | log(n)/n |
| Infinite VCL tree | Arbitrarily slow |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons. The key comparison is between the new Massart-noise bounds and prior results in adjacent settings. For partial concept classes, the new upper bound O(d/(ε(1−2β)) · log(min{1/ε, s/(1−2β)}) · log(1/δ)) strictly improves upon the agnostic bound O(VC(H)·log²(d/ε) + log(1/δ)) / ε²) of Alon et al. (2021), recovering the 1/ε dependence (rather than 1/ε²) characteristic of Massart noise, and matching the structure of the classical total-class Massart bound of Hanneke & Yang (2015). For universal rates, the new 1/n and log(n)/n rates under Massart noise coincide with the known uniform PAC rates under the same noise condition, filling the gap between the realizable trichotomy (Bousquet et al., 2021) and the agnostic tetrachotomy (Hanneke & Moran, 2026). The lower bound in Theorem 5 (log n / n for classes with infinite star trees) matches the information-theoretic lower bound of Hanneke (2016) for total classes, confirming tightness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: the TERM transductive algorithm of Hanneke & Moran (2026) for universal agnostic learning, the offset Rademacher / local metric entropy machinery of Zhivotovskiy & Hanneke (2018) for Massart-noise ERM analysis, and the partial concept class framework of Alon et al. (2021). The key technical departure is adapting the Zhivotovskiy–Hanneke localization technique—which was developed for total concept classes and unconditional excess error—to the conditional empirical excess error of a transductive learner over partial concept classes, requiring a new symmetrization-and-contraction argument. The universal-rate characterization additionally draws on the Gale-Stewart pattern-avoidance framework of Bousquet et al. (2021) and the star-set elimination algorithm of Hanneke et al. (2024), repurposing them to construct finite-star-number partial concept classes from a total class with no infinite star tree.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes two previously open gaps in the Massart-noise landscape: PAC sample complexity for partial concept classes (improving the agnostic bound of Alon et al. from O(d log²(d/ε)/ε²) to O(d log(min{1/ε, s/(1-2β)}) log(1/δ)/(ε(1-2β)))) and the full universal-rate trichotomy-to-pentachotomy under bounded noise. The authors conjecture that transductive methods of Aden-Ali et al. (2023) and Dughmi et al. (2025) may further tighten the PAC bounds, and flag the extension of universal rates to the more general Bernstein-class condition as a natural next step. For practitioners, the immediate impact is limited (purely theoretical, no algorithms or experiments), but the results provide the missing theoretical guarantees for learning under realistic bounded-noise assumptions with incomplete hypothesis coverage.
