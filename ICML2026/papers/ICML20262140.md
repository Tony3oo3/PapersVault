## Problem

Existing ML engineering agents bundle architectural components—multi-agent decomposition, iterative refinement, memory, planning, and retrieval—in varying configurations, making it impossible to attribute end-to-end performance to specific design choices. Compounding this, the primary evaluation benchmarks (MLE-Bench, MLE-Dojo) rely on historical Kaggle competitions whose data likely contaminates LLM training corpora and whose static baselines reflect outdated human performance, leaving both the causal role of individual components and the validity of benchmark scores uncertain.

## Input / Output

Each agent run receives a Kaggle competition's problem description, training and test data, and a sample submission file, with optional access to public Kaggle notebooks when retrieval is enabled. The agent produces a prediction submission file scored as public-leaderboard percentile rank, evaluated under a 24-hour wall-clock budget on a single A100 GPU. The study spans 100 competitions (75 from MLE-Bench, 25 from the new K-LIVE benchmark) with three independent replicates per configuration, yielding approximately 4,000 successful experiments across 16 architectural configurations and four model backbones.

## Method

The paper constructs a modular agent architecture where each of five components—iterative refinement (1–10 rounds), fixed-role multi-agent coordination (Explorer→Builder→Evaluator with structured JSON handoffs), structured experiment memory, explicit pre-implementation planning, and retrieval of public Kaggle resources—can be independently enabled or disabled, yielding 16 controlled configurations tested primarily on DeepSeek-V3.2 with cross-model checks on Kimi K2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.6, and Gemini 3 Flash Preview. To address benchmark contamination, the authors introduce K-LIVE, a rolling benchmark of 25 active Kaggle competitions organized into two tiers (13 active competitions postdating model knowledge cutoffs, 12 perpetual competitions for longitudinal comparison), with per-model cutoff verification to quantify contamination risk. The central empirical finding is that iterative refinement contributes over 33 points of performance—far exceeding any other component—while the fixed-role multi-agent pipeline consistently underperforms the single-agent baseline by 8–9 points even at matched or higher token budgets, and the all-components configuration performs worst while consuming 2.9× the tokens, indicating that coordination overhead structurally dominates specialization benefits.

## Results

Across ~4,000 controlled experiments on 100 competitions (75 MLE-Bench + 25 K-LIVE), the single-agent iterative baseline (C1, DeepSeek-V3.2) achieves 52.4% medal rate on MLE-Bench and 81.3 percentile on K-LIVE. Removing iteration causes the largest drop (−33.7 pts MLE-Bench, −34.1 pts K-LIVE), while fixed-role multi-agent coordination hurts by 8.3–8.5 points and the all-components configuration (C16) performs 13.7–13.9 points worse than baseline while consuming 2.9× more tokens. Findings replicate across four backbones (DeepSeek-V3.2, Kimi K2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3 Flash Preview) and both K-LIVE tiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's C1 baseline (single-agent, 10 iterations, DeepSeek-V3.2) scores 52.4% MLE-Bench / 81.3 K-LIVE percentile, placing it between ML-Master 2.0 (56.4% / 84.7%) and AutoKaggle (43.2% / 65.6%)—competitive with the top systems without any multi-agent, memory, or planning overhead. Among the 8 compared agents, PiML (Gemini-3-Pro) leads both benchmarks (61.3% / 91.1%), while OpenHands (GPT-4o) trails at 4.9% / 24.3%. The paper's key finding is that the C1 single-agent iterative design outperforms the multi-agent configurations tested (C3: 44.1% / 72.8%) by 8+ points at roughly half the token cost, and that this advantage widens on stronger backbones (multi-agent penalty grows from −8.5 to −15.6 points on Claude Sonnet 4.6). Notably, the multi-agent systems AutoKaggle and MetaGPT (both GPT-4o) score 43.2% and 35.1% on MLE-Bench respectively, consistent with the paper's ablation finding that fixed-role coordination underperforms a well-iterated single agent. The coarse capability ordering is preserved between MLE-Bench and K-LIVE, though pairwise rankings shift, validating K-LIVE as a contamination-minimal complement to historical benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing design paradigm in ML engineering agents (AIDE, AutoKaggle, DS-Agent, MetaGPT) by treating their bundled architectural choices—multi-agent decomposition, iterative refinement, memory, planning, retrieval—as independently testable variables rather than inseparable system features. It builds on the evaluation infrastructure of MLE-Bench and MLE-Dojo but explicitly rejects their reliance on historical Kaggle competitions, introducing K-LIVE as a contamination-minimal live benchmark. Conceptually, it positions itself against the broader multi-agent systems literature (AutoGen, MetaGPT, MemGPT) by asking whether the architectural complexity those frameworks emphasize actually translates to performance gains in the specific domain of end-to-end ML engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple iterative single-agent baseline outperforms all tested multi-agent, memory, and planning configurations—across four frontier backbones, two benchmarks, and all problem domains—provides practitioners with concrete, counterintuitive guidance: invest in feedback-loop quality rather than architectural sophistication. K-LIVE addresses a genuine methodological gap by enabling evaluation against contemporary human baselines with minimal contamination risk, and its rolling structure keeps the benchmark current. Open questions include whether alternative multi-agent protocols (parallel, hierarchical, or learned coordination) might overcome the observed coordination ceiling, whether results shift as base models improve their implicit planning and memory, and whether findings generalize beyond Kaggle-style competition tasks to production ML engineering involving deployment and monitoring.
