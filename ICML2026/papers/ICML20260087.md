## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of MPNN generalization and universal approximation face a fundamental dichotomy: graphon-based approaches handle graphs of all sizes but only for dense graphs (requiring normalized sum aggregation), while sparse-graph approaches using sum aggregation must restrict to uniformly bounded graph sizes. No prior framework provides a single compact metric on the space of all graphs—sparse or dense, of any size—under which MPNNs are simultaneously continuous and capable of separating points, which are the two competing requirements needed for Stone-Weierstrass approximation and covering-number generalization bounds.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts graph-signals (undirected graphs with node feature vectors in [-1,1]^d) of arbitrary size, both sparse and dense, with adjacency matrices having uniformly bounded 1-norm (equivalently ∞-norm). The MPNN produces a graph-level output vector in R^p via a readout function applied to the mean of final-layer node representations. The theoretical outputs are a universal approximation theorem (any continuous function on the space of bofop-DIDMs is uniformly approximable by an MPNN) and an implicit generalization bound whose rate depends on the covering number of the bofop-DIDM space under the DIDM-mover's distance.

## Method

The paper extends graphop analysis (Backhausz & Szegedy, 2022) by introducing bounded fiber operators (bofops)—a subclass of graphops whose neighborhood structure is encoded by a measurable family of fiber measures with uniformly bounded total mass—paired with node signals to form bofop-signals. Bofoops are shown to be the compact limit objects of both sparse and dense graph sequences under the action metric (a Hausdorff distance on profile sets of P-distributions), and the space of bofop-signals is proven compact under both the action metric and the coarser DIDM-mover's distance. MPNNs are canonically extended to bofop-signals via the operator A acting as the aggregation, and are shown to be Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the action metric and uniformly equicontinuous w.r.t. the DIDM-mover's distance, while separating points under the latter. This establishes that the space of bofop-DIDMs is the completion of all finite graph-signals, enabling a Stone-Weierstrass universal approximation theorem directly on bofop-DIDMs and a covering-number generalization bound, unifying the previously separate dense and sparse graph analyses.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main theoretical contributions are: (1) Theorem 4.1 establishes Lipschitz continuity of MPNNs with respect to the action metric on bofop-signals, with a constant C_{D,r} depending on depth L, Lipschitz bound D, and norm bound r; (2) Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.3 prove that the space of bofop-DIDMs is compact under the DIDM-mover's distance, and that the action metric is finer than the DIDM-mover's metric; (3) Section 6 derives a universal approximation theorem (any continuous function on bofop-DIDMs is uniformly approximable by an MPNN) and a generalization bound (training-test risk gap converges to zero as sample size grows), both valid for sparse and dense graphs of all sizes simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper presents no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. However, it positions its framework relative to prior theoretical approaches: previous graphon-based analyses (Levie 2024; Böker et al. 2024; Rauchwerger et al. 2025) handle only dense graphs via normalized sum aggregation, while sum-aggregation approaches (Chen et al. 2019; Azizian & Lelarge 2021; Chen et al. 2022; Vasileiou et al. 2025a) are restricted to uniformly bounded graph sizes. The bofop framework unifies both settings, extending the graphop theory of Backhausz & Szegedy (2022) with vertex features and a canonical MPNN extension, and integrating with the DIDM-mover's distance framework of Rauchwerger et al. (2025) and Böker et al. (2024). The paper establishes a strict hierarchy: graphon-DIDMs ⊂ bofop-DIDMs ⊂ all DIDMs, showing that dense graphs form a proper subspace of the sparse setting, and that finite graph-signals are dense in the bofop-DIDM space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graphop analysis framework of Backhausz & Szegedy (2022) by introducing bounded fiber operators (bofops) with vertex attributes, creating a unified sparse graph limit theory. It integrates this with the DIDM-mover's distance framework from Rauchwerger et al. (2025) and Böker et al. (2024), which previously provided compactness and separation results only for dense graphs via graphons. The key departure from prior work is that previous approaches either required normalized sum aggregation (limiting analysis to dense graphs, as in Levie 2024) or restricted to uniformly bounded graph sizes (as in Chen et al. 2019, Azizian & Lelarge 2021); this paper resolves that dichotomy by showing bofops naturally accommodate both sum and normalized sum aggregation across all graph sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first compact metric space that simultaneously serves as the completion for both sparse and dense graphs of unbounded size, enabling universal approximation and generalization theorems that were previously unavailable in a unified setting. The hierarchy of DIDM spaces (graphon-DIDMs ⊂ bofop-DIDMs ⊂ all DIDMs) clarifies the structural relationship between dense and sparse graph limits. Key open questions include obtaining explicit covering number bounds (the generalization bound is currently implicit) and whether the theory extends beyond bofops to general graphops, where the authors conjecture uniform equicontinuity may fail. The construction is purely theoretical, with no computational implementation of profiles yet available.
