## Problem

Existing Federated Graph Learning methods assume reliable local graph topologies and apply uniform (isotropic) parameter constraints during aggregation, causing representation degradation under topological noise and catastrophic forgetting under Non-IID distribution drift. The coupling of structural heterogeneity (noisy or sparse adjacency matrices) and statistical heterogeneity (divergent local label distributions) remains unaddressed, often rendering federated models inferior to local-only baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private set of graph instances (node feature matrix X in R^{M x d}, binary adjacency matrix A in {0,1}^{M x M}) or, for the DAPP encrypted-traffic dataset, raw packet sequences that are converted into directed temporal-chain graphs where each packet becomes a node with signed payload-length features and edges encode causal ordering. The model outputs a single graph-level class label per instance, trained under a federated protocol where raw topologies never leave the client.

## Method

GraphP-FL combines two client-side mechanisms with a server-side aggregation strategy. On the client, a Variational Information Bottleneck denoising module learns a soft feature mask (Z = X element-wise-times M) to suppress task-irrelevant dimensions, followed by a multi-layer GIN encoder whose per-layer representations are fused via adaptive attention to counteract noise amplification in deep layers; an NT-Xent contrastive loss over two augmented views enforces structural invariance as a self-supervised regularizer. On the server, the Fisher Information Matrix (diagonal approximation) of the global model defines a Riemannian metric, and each client's local loss includes an anisotropic quadratic penalty 0.5*(theta_i - theta_g)^T * F_g * (theta_i - theta_g) that tightly constrains high-curvature (structurally critical) parameters while permitting free movement in flat directions. Aggregation weights are proportional to each client's Fisher trace, automatically down-weighting noisy or outlier clients, and the updated global parameters and Fisher matrix are broadcast for the next round.

## Results

GraphP-FL achieves state-of-the-art test accuracy on 5 out of 7 benchmarks, with headline gains of 8.36% over FedAGHN on DAPP (96.81% vs. 87.12%) and 8.60% on IMDB-BINARY (93.55% vs. 84.95%). On NCI1, it outperforms the next-best method GCFL+ by 6.13% (92.55% vs. 86.42%), and on PROTEINS by 2.00% (87.92% vs. 85.92%). The method maintains the lowest standard deviation across most datasets (e.g., ±0.21 on DAPP, ±0.28 on IMDB-BINARY), indicating substantially more stable convergence than baselines. On the two small bioinformatics datasets (DD, MUTAG), GraphP-FL trails FedAGHN and FedAvg respectively, which the authors attribute to low structural noise and small-sample bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphP-FL decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the large-scale, noise-heavy DAPP dataset (96.81% vs. 88.45% for SCFGL, the strongest competitor) and on IMDB-BINARY (93.55% vs. 84.95% for FedAGHN). Against the strongest overall baseline FedAGHN, GraphP-FL wins on DAPP (+9.69%), COLLAB (+1.47%), IMDB-BINARY (+8.60%), NCI1 (+9.85%), and PROTEINS (+6.38%), but loses on DD (74.66% vs. 83.68%) and MUTAG (80.00% vs. 80.12%, essentially tied). The clustering-based GCFL+ is competitive on NCI1 (86.42%) and PROTEINS (85.92%) but collapses on DD (57.94%) and MUTAG (71.67%), while structure-aware FedStar and SCFGL suffer severe degradation on MUTAG (58.33%) and PROTEINS (55.03%, 54.35% respectively). General FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, Ditto) are consistently outperformed by 10–25% across all datasets, confirming that domain-specific structure learning and Fisher-based alignment are critical under dual heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphP-FL sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) graph structure learning/denoising, where it repurposes graph contrastive learning (You et al., 2020) as a self-supervised structural regularizer under label scarcity in federated settings, departing from centralized GSL methods that assume global topological access; (2) personalized federated learning, where it replaces the isotropic proximal constraint of FedProx (Li et al., 2020) with an anisotropic Fisher Information-based penalty, extending the EWC principle (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) from sequential to federated graph learning; and (3) federated graph learning specifically, where it addresses the coupling of structural noise and statistical drift simultaneously rather than treating them as separate sub-problems as prior FGL methods (FedStar, GCFL+, FedAGHN) do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, unified treatment of dual heterogeneity in FGL by coupling data-space structure denoising with parameter-space elastic alignment, offering a template for future work that must handle both noisy inputs and distributional drift. The adaptive hyperparameter mechanism (Section 5.5) reveals a practical insight: fixed structural constraints can actively harm performance on clean domains (DD, MUTAG), suggesting that constraint strength should be domain-adaptive. Open questions include whether the Fisher-based approach scales to very large graphs (all benchmarks here are ≤16K graphs), whether it generalizes to graph-level regression or link prediction, and the authors themselves flag adversarial robustness as a natural next step. The DAPP dataset, while realistic, is a somewhat niche application (encrypted traffic converted to chain-like graphs), limiting the breadth of structural diversity tested.
