## Problem

Intermediate hidden layers of language and speech models consistently outperform output layers at predicting brain activity, yet the representational property responsible for this layerwise peak remains unexplained. The paper asks whether this alignment is driven by next-token prediction (the models' training objective) or by the construction of rich, abstract linguistic features, and specifically what geometric signature marks the abstraction phase.

## Input / Output

The method consumes last-token residual stream activations from each layer of six LLMs (OPT 125m–13b, Pythia 160m–6.9b) and three speech models (WavLM base-plus/large, Whisper-large encoder), with 256-token context windows for text and 16-second audio chunks for speech. Brain signals are fMRI BOLD (3 subjects, ~90k voxels each, 8 mm from mid-cortical surface) and ECoG high-gamma (70–200 Hz, 9 subjects, 1268 electrodes). Outputs are per-layer scalar metrics—GRIDE intrinsic dimension on 10k generic in-distribution samples, Pearson R encoding performance from ridge regression, and TunedLens surprisal—enabling layerwise correlation analysis across models, subjects, and modalities.

## Method

The paper operationalizes 'feature richness' as the nonlinear intrinsic dimension (Id) of each layer's representation manifold, estimated via GRIDE on generic in-distribution data (The Pile for LLMs, LibriSpeech for speech models) rather than on the specific brain-stimulus set. Three converging analyses link Id to brain predictivity: (1) layerwise, the Id peak coincides with maximal encoding performance across all models and modalities, while surprisal monotonically decreases and does not track the encoding peak; (2) over Pythia-6.9b pre-training checkpoints, Id and encoding performance grow in tandem and their peaks coalesce at the same layers; (3) brain-tuning WavLM-base-plus on fMRI responses causally increases both Id and semantic decodability at the tuned layer. A Random Fourier Feature control with matched Id but no learned structure shows that high dimensionality alone yields negligible encoding performance (R≈0.04), establishing Id as a symptom of rich abstraction rather than a sufficient cause of brain predictivity.

## Results

Across all language and speech models, the global Spearman correlation between layerwise intrinsic dimension (Id) and brain encoding performance is ρ=0.72 for fMRI and ρ=0.43 for ECoG, both significant at p<1e-2. In contrast, the correlation between layerwise surprisal (next-token prediction error) and encoding performance is inconsistent: ρ=-0.53 (fMRI) and ρ=0.21 (ECoG) globally, with sign varying by model family. In mixed-effects models, Id explains encoding performance with effect size β=0.63 (fMRI) and β=0.97 (ECoG), while surprisal's effect is negligible (β≈0 for fMRI, β=0.26 for ECoG). Over Pythia-6.9b pre-training, Id and encoding performance grow in tandem (ρ=0.96 for fMRI, ρ=0.64 for ECoG), and the Id-peak layer converges to the best-encoding layer. A Random Fourier Feature control shows that high Id alone (without learned linguistic structure) plateaus at R≈0.04, far below the R>0.1 achieved by model layers with lower Id.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between two competing hypotheses for why intermediate model layers best predict brain activity: (1) next-token prediction (surprisal) vs. (2) feature abstraction (Id). Id wins decisively: it correlates positively with encoding performance in virtually every model-modality-subject combination (global ρ=0.72 fMRI, 0.43 ECoG), while surprisal's correlation is inconsistent in sign across model families (negative for OPT/Whisper, positive for WavLM, often non-significant for Pythia). A Random Fourier Feature baseline demonstrates that Id alone is insufficient: RFF spaces with Id up to ~2000 plateau at encoding performance R≈0.04, whereas LLM/speech layers with Id as low as ~50 achieve R>0.1, showing that learned linguistic structure—not raw dimensionality—drives brain predictivity. The one notable exception is WavLM-base-plus, whose encoding performance is driven by low-level auditory cortex similarity rather than abstract features, consistent with prior findings that smaller speech models align more with primary auditory areas. The causal finetuning experiment (brain-tuning WavLM layer 9 on fMRI) confirms the direction of causality: increasing encoding performance simultaneously increases both semantic content and Id, ruling out Id as a mere architectural artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intrinsic-dimension (Id) peak framework from Cheng et al. (2025), which identified a high-dimensional abstraction phase in LLMs, to speech audio models and links it directly to brain encoding performance. It builds on the encoding-model methodology of Antonello et al. (2023) and the brain-tuning procedure of Vattikonda et al. (2025), but departs from the predictive-coding hypothesis (Schrimpf et al., 2021; Caucheteux et al., 2023; Goldstein et al., 2022) by showing that layerwise surprisal does not account for layerwise encoding performance. Unlike prior dimensionality-brain links (Canatar et al., 2023; Schaeffer et al., 2024; Elmoznino & Bonner, 2024), the paper uses Id measured on generic in-distribution data as a proxy for learned linguistic structure rather than a stimulus-specific geometric property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a frequently observed but unexplained phenomenon—why intermediate layers, not output layers, best predict brain activity—by identifying meaning abstraction (tracked by Id) as the driver rather than next-token prediction. The RFF control experiment showing that high Id alone is insufficient (diminishing returns beyond Id≈150, plateauing at R≈0.04) reframes Id as a symptom of rich feature learning rather than a causal mechanism. Key open questions remain: what specific linguistic features the Id peak encodes (the measure is coarse-grained), whether the full causal chain from abstraction to brain alignment can be disentangled, and whether other training objectives beyond autoregressive prediction could produce equivalent or superior brain-aligned representations.
