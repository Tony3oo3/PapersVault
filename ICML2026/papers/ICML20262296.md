## Problem

While empirical work has shown that machine learning can predict Lagrangian multipliers to accelerate MILP solvers, this learning-augmented approach lacks any rigorous theoretical foundation. The paper addresses this gap by formalizing multiplier learning as a statistical learning problem over a distribution of MILP instances and providing the first sample-complexity guarantees, including matching upper and lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes N i.i.d. MILP problem instances P = (c, A, b, C, d) drawn from an application-specific distribution D, where each instance has s coupling constraints with bounded violations (|b_k| ≤ B, |(Ax)_k| ≤ B). It outputs either (1) Lagrangian multipliers π ∈ [0, π_max]^s that maximize the expected dual bound, or (2) a warm-start initialization φ for subgradient ascent. The search space is a bounded hypercube of ℓ₂-diameter π_max√s, and the Lagrangian dual value function is inherently non-smooth and concave in π.

## Method

The key insight is to analyze the function class via a 'dual view'—fixing the MILP instance P and treating multipliers π as variables—which exposes the concavity and Lipschitzness (L = 2B√s) of u(·,P). This structure enables bounding the Rademacher complexity through covering numbers of the parameter hypercube, yielding an O(s^1.5/√N) generalization upper bound for ERM. A minimax lower bound of Ω(s/√N) is established via Fano's method, constructing a family of hard distributions parameterized by binary vectors with Varshamov-Gilbert packing. The √s gap is closed constructively by proving that Stochastic Gradient Ascent with averaging achieves the minimax-optimal O(s/√N) rate. For the learning-to-warm-start variant, the objective shifts to squared Euclidean distance, reducing the problem to high-dimensional mean estimation and unlocking a fast Θ(s/N) rate via Popoviciu's inequality.

## Results

The paper establishes four key theoretical guarantees for learning Lagrangian multipliers. First, the ERM estimator achieves an expected excess risk upper bound of O(s^{1.5}/√N) (Theorem 5.5). Second, a minimax lower bound of Ω(s/√N) is proven via Fano's method (Theorem 5.6), showing linear dependence on the number of coupling constraints s is unavoidable. Third, Stochastic Gradient Ascent with averaging constructively closes the √s gap, achieving the minimax-optimal rate O(s/√N) (Theorem 5.12). Fourth, the learning-to-warm-start formulation achieves a faster minimax-optimal rate of Θ(s/N) (Theorems 6.1–6.2), demonstrating a provable sample-complexity advantage over direct multiplier prediction.

**Summary of theoretical guarantees for data-driven Lagrangian relaxation learning.**

| Setting | Upper Bound | Lower Bound | Optimal? | Algorithm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct multiplier prediction (ERM) | O(s^{1.5}/√N) | Ω(s/√N) | Gap of √s | Empirical Risk Maximization |
| Direct multiplier prediction (SGA) | O(s/√N) | Ω(s/√N) | **Yes** | Stochastic Gradient Ascent + averaging |
| Learning to warm-start | O(s/N) | Ω(s/N) | **Yes** | Sample mean estimator |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset evaluations. The internal comparisons are between algorithmic approaches: (1) ERM vs. SGA for direct multiplier prediction — ERM yields a suboptimal O(s^{1.5}/√N) rate due to the covering-number analysis, while SGA with averaging matches the minimax lower bound of Ω(s/√N), closing the √s gap. (2) Direct multiplier prediction vs. learning-to-warm-start — the warm-start formulation reduces the problem from nonsmooth concave maximization to strongly convex mean estimation, improving the rate from Θ(s/√N) to Θ(s/N), a provable √N sample-complexity advantage. The paper positions its theoretical contribution as providing the first rigorous foundation for the empirical approach of Demelas et al. (2024), which predicted Lagrangian multipliers via machine learning without sample-complexity guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous statistical learning theory for the empirically proposed task of predicting Lagrangian multipliers (Demelas et al., 2024), framing it within the data-driven algorithm design paradigm (Gupta & Roughgarden, 2020; Balcan, 2020). It builds on the classical Lagrangian relaxation framework (Fisher, 1981) and the broader learning-to-optimize literature on solver-integrated learning (Balcan et al., 2021b, 2022), but departs from prior work by treating the Lagrangian dual function—defined by an inner optimization problem—as the object of statistical analysis, using a 'dual view' perspective to exploit its concavity and Lipschitz structure. The minimax lower bound construction via Fano's method and the constructive SGA analysis draw on standard tools from high-dimensional statistics (Wainwright, 2019) and online convex optimization, applied here to a function class with a structure not previously analyzed in the learning theory literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant theoretical gap: the empirical success of learning-based Lagrangian multiplier prediction (Demelas et al., 2024) now has a rigorous sample-complexity foundation, and the results reveal that the natural ERM approach is suboptimal by a factor of √s, while a simple stochastic gradient ascent with averaging achieves the minimax rate. The clean separation between direct multiplier prediction (Θ(s/√N)) and learning-to-warm-start (Θ(s/N)) provides a principled explanation for why warm-start strategies are more sample-efficient, with direct implications for how practitioners should deploy learned initializations in MILP solvers. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-stationary problem distributions and to other decomposition techniques (Dantzig-Wolfe, Benders), and whether the theoretical rates translate to practical speedups on large-scale instances.
