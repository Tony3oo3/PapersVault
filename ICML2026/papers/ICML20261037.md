## Problem

Existing theoretical bounds on the Lipschitz constant of deep neural networks either apply only to ReLU activations, exhibit a logarithmic dependence on network width, or characterize the constant solely at random initialization without accounting for training dynamics. This paper closes the gap for smooth activations by establishing width-independent upper bounds that hold both at initialization and uniformly throughout the lazy training regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261037/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis considers fully connected networks with L hidden layers of uniform width m, smooth 1-Lipschitz and β_φ-smooth activations (e.g., SiLU, tanh, ELU, GeLU), Gaussian initialization with variance 1/m per entry, and bounded input sets X ⊂ B(0, Λ). The output is an explicit non-asymptotic upper bound on the local Lipschitz constant over X that scales as O(2^{O(L)}(√d_in + √d_out)) at initialization and O(2^{O(L)}(√d_in + √d_out + LR)) during lazy training, with no dependence on m, holding with probability at least (1 − 2e^{−u²})(1 − 4e^{−cm/L²})^L over the random initialization.

## Method

The key insight is to condition on the hidden-layer weights W_1,…,W_L so that the Lipschitz constant, expressed as a supremum over the Jacobian, becomes the supremum of a Gaussian process indexed by a high-dimensional set T. Dudley's integral inequality is then applied to bound this supremum in terms of the metric entropy (covering number) of T, which depends only on the spectral norms ‖W_ℓ‖ and 2→∞ norms ‖W_ℓ‖_{2→∞} of the hidden weights—not on m. High-probability concentration bounds on these norms (via Vershynin's results) yield the width-independent initialization bound. For lazy training, a perturbation lemma bounds the change in the Jacobian's spectral norm when weights move within a ball of radius R/√m around initialization, showing the perturbation term C_per·R remains O(1) when m = Ω(R²), so the overall Lipschitz constant stays width-independent throughout training.

## Results

The paper establishes that the local Lipschitz constant of a fully connected DNN with smooth activations at random initialization is bounded by O(2^{O(L)}(√d_in + √d_out)), independent of network width m (Theorem 4.1). During lazy training, the bound extends to O(2^{O(L)}(√d + LR)), again width-independent (Theorem 4.2). Numerically, the empirical Lipschitz constant at initialization decreases from 0.47 (m=32) to 0.27 (m=1024), while the naive bound grows from 43.63 to 1456.80 over the same range. After lazy training in a teacher-student setup, the empirical Lipschitz constant remains stable at ~1.22–1.25 across widths 32–1024, and adversarial loss stays flat at ~13.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Geuchen et al. (2025), whose bound is O(2^{O(L)}√(Ld·log(m/d))) for ReLU networks, this paper removes the logarithmic width dependence entirely, yielding O(2^{O(L)}√d) for smooth activations, at the cost of retaining the exponential depth factor. Against Dirksen et al. (2025), whose bound O(√(d·log(m/d))) eliminates the exponential depth dependence but still carries a log(m) width term, this paper trades the depth improvement for complete width independence and extends the analysis beyond initialization to the lazy-training regime. Numerically, the EcLipSE certified bound (Xu & Sivaranjani, 2024) still grows with width (8.00 at m=32 to 49.43 at m=1024), whereas the empirical estimate remains flat or slightly decreasing (0.47 to 0.27), confirming the width-independence prediction. In the lazy-training experiment, the adversarial loss (PGD, ε=5) stays within 12.98–13.04 across all widths, indicating that overparameterization does not degrade robustness in the lazy regime, consistent with the theoretical bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic Lipschitz bound framework of Geuchen et al. (2025) and Dirksen et al. (2025), which established width-dependent (√log m) bounds for random ReLU networks at initialization, to two new settings: smooth activations (SiLU, ELU, GeLU) and the lazy training regime. The proof technique—bounding the Lipschitz constant as the supremum of a Gaussian process via Dudley's integral—is inherited from Geuchen et al., but the key departure is eliminating the logarithmic width dependence entirely and showing the bound is O(√d_in + √d_out) independent of m. The lazy training extension is novel, using perturbation inequalities for Jacobians of smooth-activation networks to show the Lipschitz constant remains width-controlled as long as weights stay within O(R·m^{-1/2}) of initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The width-independence result provides a theoretical explanation for the empirically observed phenomenon that wider networks do not become less smooth, bridging a gap between theory and practice in overparameterized learning. The lazy training extension suggests that gradient descent implicitly regularizes the Lipschitz constant, offering a principled link between overparameterization, generalization, and adversarial robustness. Key limitations include the exponential depth dependence (2^L) inherited from the proof technique, the restriction to smooth activations (excluding ReLU), and the requirement that inputs be bounded. Open questions include whether analogous width-independence holds for ReLU networks in the lazy regime, whether the depth dependence can be improved, and whether results extend to CNNs or Transformers.
