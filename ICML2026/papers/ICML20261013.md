## Problem

Differentiable permutation learning via Gumbel-Sinkhorn relies on a single global temperature to control the trade-off between exploration and discretization. In realistic tasks, different assignments resolve at different rates—some become confident early while others remain ambiguous—so a uniform temperature either locks in early mistakes or prevents large ambiguous permutations from sharpening. The paper proves this incompatibility formally (Proposition 1) and shows that no scalar temperature schedule can simultaneously discretize confident rows and keep ambiguous rows diffuse.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of n elements—scalar lists sampled uniformly from [0,1] or [10,11], CelebA images (178×178) split into p×p tile grids (p∈{4,5,6,7}), or 2D city coordinates for TSP—and produces a soft permutation matrix P in the Birkhoff polytope via Gumbel-Sinkhorn sampling. The permutation is treated as a latent operator: training losses (monotonicity violations, boundary smoothness, tour length) are computed on the reordered output P^T X rather than on the permutation itself, enabling fully unsupervised learning. At evaluation, a discrete permutation is extracted via Hungarian decoding and scored with Kendall-τ or tour length.

## Method

The core contribution is an entropy-adaptive variant of Gumbel-Sinkhorn that replaces the global inverse temperature β₀(t) with an entrywise field β ∈ R^{n×n}. At each step, a deterministic soft assignment Q = Sinkhorn(β₀(t)·stopgrad(S)) is computed, and normalized row and column entropies H_i^row, H_j^col are extracted. A bounded monotone function b(H) activates only above a threshold H₀, yielding per-row and per-column inverse temperatures β_i^row = β₀(t)/(1+b(H_i^row)) and β_j^col = β₀(t)/(1+b(H_j^col)), combined entrywise as β_ij = ½(β_i^row + β_j^col). The final soft permutation is P = Sinkhorn(β ⊙ (S+g)) with Gumbel noise g, so confident low-entropy assignments retain the base temperature and discretize early while high-entropy regions receive reduced inverse temperature and remain diffuse. The paper provides an optimal-transport interpretation showing this is equivalent to locally rescaling assignment costs under global doubly stochastic constraints, and for TSP replaces the entropy signal with a degree-violation signal that increases temperature for nodes whose expected degree deviates from two.

## Results

On unsupervised number sorting with high-ambiguity values in [10,11], entropy-adaptive Gumbel-Sinkhorn (GS w/ TA) achieves Kendall τ = 1.00 at n=100, 0.67 at n=200, and 0.15 at n=300, consistently outperforming global-temperature GS (0.74, 0.60, 0.06) with the gap widening as n grows. On unsupervised 7×7 jigsaw reconstruction (CelebA-Test, 12 anchors), entropy-adaptive GS reaches Kendall τ = 0.291 versus 0.201 for global annealing, a Δτ improvement of 0.102 over the mask-only baseline versus 0.012. On unsupervised TSP at n=500, degree-violation temperature adaptation (DV-TA) reduces tour length to 49.44 (gap 49.06%) versus 50.73 (gap 52.95%) for the GS UTSP baseline, with gains persisting at n=1000 (77.88 vs. 79.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuralSort (Grover et al., 2019), the proposed entropy-adaptive GS dominates in the unsupervised high-ambiguity regime: at n=300 with values in [10,11], GS w/ TA achieves Kendall τ = 0.15 while both untrained and trained NeuralSort are at or below chance (0.00 and −0.04 respectively), reflecting the fundamental mismatch between scalar-ordering operators and latent permutation learning under weak structural supervision. On unsupervised TSP, the proposed DV-TA consistently outperforms both the original UTSP edge-heatmap baseline (Min et al., 2023) and the GS-based UTSP variant (Min & Gomes, 2023; 2025): at n=500, tour length drops from 55.26 (UTSP) and 50.73 (GS UTSP) to 49.44, with the optimality gap narrowing from 66.61% to 49.06%. The advantage grows with problem size, reaching a 1.53-unit tour-length reduction at n=1000. OT4P (Guo et al., 2024), evaluated under the same unsupervised monotonicity objective, performs at or near chance (Kendall τ ≈ 0.00–0.01) for n ≥ 50 in both value ranges, confirming that the orthogonal-group relaxation geometry is ill-suited to weakly supervised permutation learning without stronger global ranking signals. Overall, the entropy-adaptive method wins or ties on every main comparison, with the largest margins appearing at larger n and higher assignment ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gumbel-Sinkhorn framework (Mena et al., 2018) by replacing its single global temperature with a row- and column-dependent inverse-temperature field derived from assignment entropy, enabling confident assignments to discretize early while ambiguous regions remain diffuse. The theoretical grounding draws on entropically regularized optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013; Chizat, 2024), showing that entrywise inverse-temperature scaling is equivalent to locally rescaling assignment costs over the Birkhoff polytope. It positions itself against operator-based sorting relaxations (NeuralSort, SoftSort) and alternative permutation geometries (OT4P), arguing that latent permutation learning with adaptive entropy control is better suited to weakly supervised, structurally-driven objectives than scalar-ordering or orthogonal-power formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formally proves (Proposition 1) that no single global temperature can simultaneously discretize easy assignments and preserve exploration in ambiguous ones, providing a clean theoretical motivation for local temperature control. Practically, entropy-adaptive Gumbel-Sinkhorn offers a lightweight drop-in modification (one extra Sinkhorn pass per step, ~10–15% overhead) that improves stability and final permutation quality across sorting, jigsaw, and TSP as problem size and ambiguity grow. Open questions include whether the entropy threshold and boost magnitude can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to partial permutations or constrained routing, and whether the gains persist under even weaker structural signals or higher-dimensional data.
