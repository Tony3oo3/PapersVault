## Problem

Diffusion-based policies offer greater expressivity than Gaussian policies for representing complex, multimodal action distributions, yet they have not been successfully trained in the massively parallel, on-policy RL regime. The core obstacle is that trust-region constraints—essential for stable on-policy training—require evaluating marginal action likelihoods, which are intractable for diffusion models. This paper asks whether a principled trust-region formulation can be derived that makes diffusion policies viable in this high-throughput setting.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes proprioceptive state observations (joint positions, velocities, base orientation, task-specific features such as target coordinates or object poses) and produces a continuous action vector via an iterative 8-step denoising process with a cosine noise schedule. Action dimensions range from 1 (Pendulum) to 61 (HumanoidBench whole-body + dexterous hands). During evaluation, actions are generated deterministically by integrating the probability-flow ODE with a score-scaling factor c > 0.5 that sharpens intermediate marginals toward high-density, high-return regions.

## Method

TruDi reinterprets the discretized diffusion policy as a latent-variable model where the executed action is the marginal of a joint trajectory distribution over all denoising steps. By applying the data processing inequality, the paper derives a tractable upper bound on the marginal KL divergence between old and new policies in terms of the KL over the full joint trajectory, which factorizes into per-step Gaussian terms and is therefore computable. This trajectory-level KL bound replaces the intractable marginal constraint in the MaxEnt RL trust-region objective, yielding a split policy update: when the bound is within threshold ε, the policy maximizes a Q-guided lower bound on the MaxEnt objective; when violated, it minimizes the trajectory KL directly. The Q-function is fitted with TD-λ targets and an HL-Gauss (cross-entropy) loss, while dual ascent auto-tunes the entropy temperature α and the trust-region Lagrangian multiplier λ. For evaluation, a probability-flow ODE with scaled score (c=1.0) provides deterministic, high-return action generation that outperforms both SDE sampling and best-of-K Q-value selection.

## Results

On the MuJoCo Playground Humanoid benchmark (the most challenging suite), TruDi achieves an IQM episode return of 34.8±3.0 after 50M steps, outperforming REPPO (29.6±7.2), DIME (15.6±9.4), and PPO (0.1±3.7). On standard continuous-control benchmarks (MuJoCo DMC, ManiSkill3, IsaacLab), TruDi matches or slightly exceeds REPPO, while on the 27-task HumanoidBench whole-body control suite, TruDi shows a clear and consistent gap over all baselines. TruDi's wall-clock training time is 1.95±0.46 hours vs. 1.07±0.35 hours for REPPO, a ~1.8× overhead that the paper shows is not the source of the performance gain (matched-time experiments confirm TruDi still wins).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TruDi outperforms REPPO (the strongest Gaussian on-policy baseline) by ~5.2 IQM points on MuJoCo Playground Humanoid (34.8 vs. 29.6) and shows a larger, more consistent gap on the 27-task HumanoidBench suite, where REPPO plateaus early on hard tasks like window cleaning, balancing, and hurdling. Against DIME (the off-policy diffusion baseline), TruDi achieves more than 2× the episode return on Humanoid (34.8 vs. 15.6), indicating that the on-policy trust-region formulation is critical for stable diffusion training in massively parallel settings. Compared to DPPO (PPO-style clipping for diffusion), TruDi significantly outperforms on both DMC and Humanoid benchmarks (Figure 3), with the gap widening on high-dimensional tasks. Against FPO (flow-matching policy gradients), TruDi achieves comparable or superior sample efficiency on DMC and a clear advantage on Humanoid. On the multimodality evaluation (PushT, StackCube), TruDi captures multiple solution modes (behavior entropy 0.32 and 0.87) while REPPO collapses to a single mode (entropy 0), confirming the expressivity advantage of diffusion policies without sacrificing return.

## Relation to Prior Work

TruDi bridges two previously separate lines of work: the off-policy diffusion MaxEnt formulation of DIME (Celik et al., 2025), which provides a tractable entropy lower bound for diffusion policies, and the on-policy trust-region framework of REPPO (Voelcker et al., 2025), which uses TD-λ targets, HL-Gauss critics, and primal-dual KL constraints but is restricted to Gaussian policies with exact likelihoods. The key conceptual step is applying the data processing inequality to replace the intractable marginal KL constraint with a tractable joint-trajectory KL bound, thereby making the REPPO trust-region machinery compatible with diffusion policies. For evaluation, the paper adapts the probability-flow ODE from Song et al. (2021) with a score-scaling heuristic to produce deterministic high-return actions, extending a technique previously used in diffusion-based inference but not in RL policy evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled method for training diffusion policies from scratch in the massively parallel on-policy regime, which is where modern robot learning (IsaacLab, MuJoCo Playground, ManiSkill3) operates. The practical impact is enabling expressive, multimodal action distributions in settings where Gaussian policies collapse (e.g., symmetric manipulation tasks, high-DoF humanoid control), without the memory overhead of off-policy replay buffers. Key limitations include the ~2× wall-clock overhead from iterative denoising and the fact that all results are simulation-only with no sim-to-real validation. Open questions include extending the framework to offline-to-online RL, reducing denoising steps via distillation, and whether the trajectory-level KL bound remains tight enough for higher-dimensional action spaces beyond 61 DoF.
