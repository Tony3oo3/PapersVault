## Problem

Continual incorporation of new vocabulary or domain-specific knowledge into LLMs via fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting, where previously learned behaviors degrade irreversibly as more knowledge is injected. The paper asks whether large-scale weight updates are truly necessary to acquire a small amount of new knowledge, and how to formally guarantee zero forgetting during vocabulary expansion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263645/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM with vocabulary V (size T ≈ 10^5), a small set of new tokens U (|U| = m ≪ T), and training sequences in which new tokens appear in natural linguistic context. It outputs only updated embedding vectors for the new tokens (e.g., 3,072 trainable parameters out of ~3B total), leaving all other model parameters frozen. The adapted model's transition distribution is piecewise: original V→V transitions are preserved exactly, while new U→V transitions follow the learned sparse mapping.

## Method

The paper models autoregressive generation as a first-order Markov chain over tokens, where the model's memory is a transition matrix and adding new tokens corresponds to expanding the state space. Each new token u is represented as a sparse vector α^(u) over the existing embedding dictionary E (at most s nonzero entries), such that f(E^T α^(u)) reproduces the desired transition distribution q^(u). In practice, this is realized via embedding tuning (ET): only the embedding rows for new tokens are optimized by minimizing KL divergence between the empirical next-token distribution and the model's prediction, while all backbone parameters remain frozen. This construction guarantees zero forgetting by design, since original V→V transitions are never modified. The paper proves a sample complexity bound showing that learning each new token requires O(s) samples (where s is the sparsity of its dictionary mapping), independent of the full vocabulary size T or model dimensionality.

## Results

Across seven pretrained models (1B–8B parameters), embedding tuning (ET) achieves the lowest or near-lowest synthetic-vocabulary test loss (e.g., 2.75 on Phi-3.5 Mini, 3.37 on Qwen2.5-7B) while inducing exactly zero WikiText forgetting (0.00 loss increase), whereas full fine-tuning causes +0.51 to +11.59 loss increase and LoRA causes +1.13 to +8.80. In the controlled arithmetic-operator experiment, ET reaches 81.4% accuracy on the new multiplication token with only ~500 training samples, outperforming FFT (77.2%) while preserving 100% addition accuracy (FFT collapses addition from 100% to 0%). In cross-lingual expansion on Qwen2.5-3B, ET attains the best target-language loss (Spanish 2.30, German 3.27, Arabic 2.82) with near-zero English forgetting (−0.04 to 0.00), compared to FFT's +9.83 to +11.95 forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, ET dominates on forgetting: ET achieves 0.00 WikiText loss increase across all seven models, while LoRA ranges from +1.13 (Qwen2.5-3B) to +8.80 (Phi-3.5 Mini). On test loss, ET is competitive or superior in most settings (e.g., 3.39 vs 3.45 on Qwen2.5-3B; 3.47 vs 4.08 on Llama3.2-3B), though LoRA edges out ET slightly on Llama3.2-1B (3.55 vs 3.50). Against prompt tuning (PT), ET again achieves zero forgetting versus PT's +0.01 to +2.19 increase, and ET matches or beats PT on test loss for most models (e.g., 3.37 vs 4.62 on Qwen2.5-7B; 3.46 vs 4.03 on Qwen2.5-1.5B), with the notable exception of Phi-3.5 Mini where PT achieves lower loss (1.87 vs 2.75) likely because the synthetic data was generated by that same model. In the arithmetic experiment, ET also outperforms full fine-tuning on the new-token task (81.4% vs 77.2%) while completely avoiding the catastrophic forgetting that FFT induces on addition (100%→0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Markov chain formulation of autoregressive language models (Zekri et al., 2024; Yüksel & Flammarion, 2025), extending it from analysis of existing models to the problem of vocabulary expansion as state-space expansion. It departs from the standard continual learning literature (EWC, GEM, replay-based methods) and PEFT approaches (LoRA, prompt tuning) by reframing zero forgetting as a structural property of the transition matrix rather than something to be enforced through regularization or parameter isolation. The token-to-dictionary mapping strategy connects vocabulary expansion to sparse representation learning, where the cost of learning a new token depends on the sparsity of its representation over existing tokens rather than the full vocabulary size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical justification for why embedding tuning achieves zero forgetting: it is a direct implementation of state-space expansion that preserves original transition probabilities by construction. The sample complexity bound scaling with sparsity s (number of mapped tokens) rather than vocabulary size T offers a concrete prediction for data efficiency that is validated empirically. Key limitations include the assumption of no transitions from existing to new tokens, uniform token frequency, and sufficient model expressivity; extending the theory to non-uniform frequencies, limited-capacity models, and transitions among new tokens remains open. The slightly negative forgetting observed in cross-lingual settings (learning Spanish/German marginally improving English) is an unexplained phenomenon worth further investigation.
