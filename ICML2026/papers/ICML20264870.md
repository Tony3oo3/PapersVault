## Problem

Chain-based Metropolis-Hastings sampling for power-shaped trajectory distributions can approach RL-trained reasoners without additional training, but its inherently serial generate-then-verify loop underutilizes GPU parallelism and produces extreme tail latency (p95 = 1318s at 128× budget on MATH500). A second, systems-level obstacle is that SMC resampling reassigns particle ancestries, requiring KV-cache updates that naively demand O(NL²) prefix recomputation—prohibitive for long contexts. The paper asks whether a parallel particle method can target the same power-shaped distribution while keeping resampling tractable for Transformer inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (e.g., a MATH500 word problem) and generates a population of N reasoning trajectories in parallel, each capped at 3072 tokens. The final output is a weighted vote over parsed answers aggregated from particle weights, with adaptive early exit returning the top answer when confidence is high, or a full weighted-vote result when the budget cap is reached. All methods are evaluated under a fixed base model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, bfloat16) with no additional training or verifier access.

## Method

ASMC replaces the single MCMC chain with a parallel population of N weighted particles that are extended simultaneously via batched autoregressive decoding, targeting the fixed power-shaped distribution π(x|c) ∝ p_θ(x|c)^{α*} through recursive importance reweighting with an annealed proposal schedule for early exploration. The key systems contribution is cache-coherent resampling: when ESS drops below a threshold, the ancestor mapping is applied as a single gather operation across the particle dimension of per-layer KV caches, position IDs, attention masks, and auxiliary tensors—avoiding O(NL²) prefix replay and reducing resampling overhead by 53–77×. An adaptive compute-allocation layer uses top-answer mass to trigger early exit on easy problems and escalate to a larger population (N_hard) on hard ones, with a strict per-instance budget cap in attention-interaction units. The paper further shows that particle collapse severity (low ESS_min/N) strongly predicts failures, while the specific resampling scheme (systematic vs. residual vs. multinomial) has only a second-order effect.

## Results

On MATH500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B, ASMC-adaptive (N=128) at 128× budget achieves 80.6% exact-match accuracy with p95 latency of 73.7s, compared to sequential MCMC's 73.8% at p95=1317.5s — an ~18× tail-latency reduction with a 6.8-point accuracy gain. At 16× budget, ASMC (N=16) reaches 77.6% with p95=38.5s, while best-of-4 saturates at 79.2% with p95=60.3s. On HumanEval, ASMC (N=64) at 128× reaches 64.0% pass@1 versus best-of-4's 56.1%, though with higher p95 (~200s vs ~75s). On GSM8K, ASMC achieves 90.8% at 8× with p95=13.5s versus MCMC's 84.9% at p95=151.9s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sequential MCMC (Karan & Du, 2025), ASMC dominates on both accuracy and tail latency across all budgets: at 128×, ASMC-adapt achieves 80.6% vs. MCMC's 73.8% while reducing p95 from 1317.5s to 73.7s (~18×). Even at 8×, ASMC (N=4) matches or exceeds MCMC accuracy (71.8% vs. 67.2%) with p95 of 33.0s vs. 152.1s. Against best-of-n with log-probability reranking (Cobbe et al., 2021), the comparison is more nuanced: best-of-4 reaches 79.2% at 16× with p95=60.3s, slightly ahead of ASMC (N=16) at 77.6%/38.5s, but best-of-n saturates at n=4 on MATH500 and cannot improve further. ASMC provides higher-accuracy operating points beyond this saturation (80.2–80.6% at 128×) while maintaining p95 in the 73–75s range, whereas majority-vote best-of-16 reaches only 80.0% at p95=916s. On HumanEval, ASMC (N=64) at 128× reaches 64.0% pass@1 versus best-of-4's 56.1%, though at higher p95 (~200s vs. ~75s), confirming that the accuracy-latency trade-off shifts across task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Karan & Du (2025), who showed that sampling from power-shaped trajectory distributions π(x)∝p(x)^α via sequential Metropolis-Hastings can approach RL-trained reasoners like GRPO without additional training. The key departure is replacing the inherently serial MH chain with a parallel particle population (SMC), motivated by a systems-algorithm mismatch: sequential decoding underutilizes GPU parallelism and produces extreme tail latency. The cache-coherent resampling mechanism is a novel systems contribution that repurposes the standard SMC resampling step as a KV-cache gather operation, drawing on the particle filter literature (Doucet et al., 2000) but adapting it to Transformer inference for the first time in a long-horizon autoregressive setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic p95 reduction (1318s → 73.7s at 128× budget) makes power-shaped trajectory sampling practically deployable, whereas sequential MCMC is effectively unusable at high budgets. The finding that collapse severity (ESS_min/N) strongly predicts failures while the specific resampling scheme has negligible effect (Δ=+0.5%) redirects attention from scheme selection to diversity maintenance. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7B model and single A100 GPU, heuristic (not formally guaranteed) adaptive policies, and modest accuracy gains over best-of-n (80.6% vs 79.2%); open questions include whether cache-coherent reordering scales to multi-device or 70B+ settings, and whether stronger rejuvenation moves can mitigate the particle collapse that drives most failures.
