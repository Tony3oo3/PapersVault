## Problem

Machine unlearning in recommender systems must simultaneously achieve complete removal of deleted data influence, preserve recommendation quality on retained data, and do so far more cheaply than full retraining. Existing influence-function methods are computationally expensive due to iterative Hessian-vector products, sharding-based methods approach retraining cost under frequent deletions, and reverse-learning methods sacrifice forgetting completeness for utility. No prior approach balances all three criteria—completeness, utility, and efficiency—without substantial trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully trained recommender model (MF-BPR or LightGCN), a deletion set of user-item interactions, the Adam optimizer's second-moment statistics (v̂) saved from the original training run, and a small retained interaction buffer (10% of all interactions). It outputs the original frozen backbone parameters plus a set of low-rank adapter weight pairs (A, B) per selected parameter block, such that the effective model is θ₀ + Σ ABᵀ. Notably, no new Hessian or Hessian-vector products are computed; the curvature information is entirely recycled from the pre-existing Adam state, and the backbone is never modified during unlearning.

## Method

Obliviate is a two-stage, architecture-agnostic unlearning framework. Stage 1 (LUA) computes the aggregate deletion gradient g_S and applies a Newton-style downdate Δθ₀ ≈ H̃⁻¹g_S, where H̃ is a diagonal positive-definite curvature proxy built from Adam's second-moment statistics (v̂_j), avoiding any explicit Hessian computation. This full-parameter update is then projected into a low-rank subspace via truncated SVD and parameterized as LoRA-style adapters (ΔW = ABᵀ) on selected embedding blocks, with the backbone frozen. Stage 2 (LAC) refines only the adapter parameters over a compact witness set (deletion set, hard negatives, and a retained buffer) using a joint loss that combines a BPR-based unlearning objective (pushing deleted items below sampled negatives) and a knowledge-distillation objective (matching the original model's scores on retained interactions), regularized by a Frobenius-norm trust-region term on the adapter magnitude. The key insight is that Adam's second-moment statistics serve as a free, data-adaptive diagonal curvature surrogate, and the two-stage separation—expensive second-order correction followed by lightweight calibration—decouples forgetting from utility restoration.

## Results

On ML-1M (MF-BPR), Obliviate achieves +2.22% R@20 and +2.26% N@20 relative utility gain over the original model after unlearning 20% of users, outperforming IFRU (+0.45%/+0.54%) and RRL (+0.37%/+0.99%). On the sparser Amazon dataset, gains reach up to +9.84% R@10 and +7.78% N@10. In terms of efficiency, Obliviate completes unlearning in 19.95s (ML-1M MF-BPR) and 57.23s (Yelp LightGCN) versus 533.5s and 1984.5s for full retraining, yielding 27×–49× speedups. On unlearning completeness (Demotion Rate), Obliviate achieves the highest post-unlearning scores across all datasets and backbones, e.g., 0.7220 on Yelp LightGCN vs. 0.6697 (IFRU) and 0.6664 (RRL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Obliviate consistently outperforms the two non-retraining baselines (IFRU and RRL) on both utility preservation and unlearning completeness. On ML-1M MF-BPR, Obliviate's +2.22% R@20 gain exceeds IFRU (+0.45%) by ~5× and RRL (+0.37%) by ~6×; on Amazon, the gap widens to +6.42% vs. +4.14% (IFRU) and +3.67% (RRL). On Demotion Rate (completeness), Obliviate leads all methods on every dataset–backbone combination, e.g., 0.3648 vs. 0.3112 (IFRU) and 0.3121 (RRL) on Amazon LightGCN. SISA and RecEraser show larger utility gains (e.g., +5.30% R@20 on ML-1M) but at retraining-level computational cost (533s vs. 20s for Obliviate), making them impractical for frequent or large-scale deletion requests. In terms of speed, Obliviate (19.95s) is comparable to RRL (18.23s) and ~1.7× faster than IFRU (33.75s) on ML-1M MF-BPR, while being 27× faster than SISA/RecEraser.

## Relation to Prior Work

Obliviate sits at the intersection of three prior threads: influence-function-based unlearning (Koh & Liang, 2017; IFRU, 2024), Newton-style parameter downdates for unlearning (Bui et al., 2024), and low-rank adapter parameterization (LoRA, Hu et al., 2022). It departs from sharding-based methods (SISA, RecEraser) that approximate retraining by retraining affected shards, and from influence-function methods (IFRU, SCIF) that require iterative Hessian-vector products, by replacing the full Hessian with a diagonal proxy derived from Adam's second-moment statistics and confining the correction to a low-rank adapter subspace. The two-stage design—curvature-aware downdate (LUA) followed by a trust-region calibration with BPR unlearning loss and distillation (LAC)—is a novel combination that explicitly targets the completeness-utility tradeoff that prior methods handle only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a practically deployable path toward GDPR/CCPA compliance for large-scale recommender systems, achieving order-of-magnitude speedups over retraining while handling 20% deletion ratios far beyond the 1–5% typical in prior benchmarks. The synthetic deletion protocol (injecting low-preference noise rather than removing genuine interactions) is a useful methodological contribution that isolates unlearning quality from distribution shift, though it also means the evaluation does not stress-test removal of truly informative user preferences. A key limitation is the reliance on Adam optimizer statistics for the curvature proxy, making the method optimizer-specific; exploring optimizer-agnostic or more accurate curvature approximations, along with stronger verification mechanisms for unlearning completeness, remains open.
