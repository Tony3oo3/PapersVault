## Problem

Classical ski rental algorithms rely on worst-case competitive ratios that ignore potentially valuable distributional information about usage duration, while recent learning-augmented methods only exploit point predictions that cannot capture the full uncertainty of possible horizons. This paper addresses the gap by integrating full distributional predictions of unknown quality into both deterministic and randomized ski rental algorithms while preserving rigorous worst-case robustness guarantees against adversarial realizations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262099/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a discrete probability distribution p̂ over the unknown skiing horizon D (with bounded support up to max_day), a buy cost b > 1, and a tunable robustness parameter (λ ∈ (0,1) for the deterministic clamp policy, or R > 1 for the randomized setting). It outputs either a single optimal buying threshold t* (deterministic) or a full probability mass function over buying days (randomized), along with provable expected competitive ratio bounds. Crucially, the predicted distribution is treated as untrusted: the algorithm must maintain bounded worst-case competitive ratio regardless of how far p̂ deviates from the true distribution p, measured via 1-Wasserstein or total variation distance.

## Method

For the deterministic setting, the paper derives the expected cost function g(t) for threshold policies and computes the optimal threshold in O(n) time; to handle prediction errors, the Clamp Policy restricts the threshold to [⌈λb⌉, ⌊b/λ⌋], achieving a distribution-free worst-case bound of 1 + 1/λ − 1/b while recovering optimal performance as prediction error vanishes. For the randomized setting, the paper characterizes R-robustness as a pair of linear constraints on the CDF F(x) and its first moment μ(x), showing that the optimal CDF follows a geometric form F*(x) = min{(R−1)((b/(b−1))^x − 1), 1} when the cost function is monotone. The Water-Filling Algorithm then finds the optimal randomized policy by binary searching over a cost threshold h: it fills probability mass on indices where g(t) ≤ h using a geometric-growth update F(x+1) = F(x) + (F(x)+R−1)/(b−1) on [1, b−1] to keep robustness constraints tight, assigns atoms at support points via a mass-transfer formula, and checks tail feasibility against the moment budget (R−1)b. This yields a principled LP-based construction that simultaneously minimizes expected cost under the prediction and enforces the hard robustness constraint.

## Results

At (b, R) = (50, 1.7), the proposed water-filling randomized policy achieves a consistency metric Cons(p) (lower is better) of 1.0415 on the bi-modal two-point distribution, 1.2879 on the geometric distribution, 1.3375 on the discretized Gaussian, 1.1612 on uniform-100, and 1.3331 on uniform-200. The Clamp Policy guarantees a worst-case competitive ratio of at most 1 + 1/λ − 1/b regardless of prediction quality, and the ECR converges to the optimal value as the Wasserstein prediction error vanishes. Under Wasserstein perturbations (Figure 1), the method exhibits only mild degradation while the mixture baseline degrades noticeably as the perturbation budget η increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two adaptations of the Purohit et al. (2018) randomized ski rental algorithm to distributional inputs: a majority-branch baseline (selecting the y≥b or y<b branch based on whether Pr[y≥b] > 1/2) and a mixture baseline (mixing the two branch distributions proportionally to Pr[y≥b]). The proposed water-filling method wins on all five distribution families. The largest gains appear on the bi-modal two-point distribution (1.0415 vs. 1.2448/1.2547, a ~16–17% improvement) and the geometric distribution (1.2879 vs. 1.4114/1.4183, a ~9% improvement), where the full distributional structure is most informative. On the Gaussian near b, the improvement is ~5–6%, while on the uniform distributions the gain is smaller (~1–2%). Under prediction error (Wasserstein perturbations up to η), the method maintains a stable advantage, whereas the mixture baseline degrades as η grows and the majority-branch baseline stays essentially flat but at a consistently higher cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented ski rental line initiated by Purohit et al. (2018), which used point predictions, to the richer setting of full distributional predictions under an untrusted advisor framework. It departs from concurrent work by Besbes et al. (2025) and Cui & Dinitz (2026), which assume the predicted distribution is drawn from a true underlying distribution or require known error bounds, by evaluating performance against adversarially realized stopping times. The Water-Filling Algorithm for randomized policies generalizes the geometric stopping distribution of Purohit et al. (2018) to handle arbitrary piecewise-linear cost functions arising from distributional inputs, while the Clamp Policy provides a distribution-free robustness guarantee absent in prior point-prediction approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic treatment of distributional ski rental with both deterministic and randomized algorithms under an untrusted advisor, filling a gap between worst-case competitive analysis and learning-augmented methods. The clean separation of robustness (via the Clamp Policy's λ parameter) and consistency (via Wasserstein/TV distance) gives practitioners a tunable, interpretable tradeoff. Limitations include the bounded-support assumption for the O(n) threshold algorithm and the relatively small-scale experimental evaluation; open questions include extending the framework to continuous horizons, multi-option ski rental, and settings where the distributional prediction itself is learned end-to-end.
