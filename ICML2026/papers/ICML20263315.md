## Problem

Test-time scaling for Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) remains largely unexplored, and standard autoregressive scaling strategies such as Best-of-N with temperature sampling prove ineffective for MDLMs because stochastic perturbations disrupt the iterative global unmasking trajectory, degrading generation quality. The paper asks how to derive diverse, high-quality unmasking trajectories under a fixed inference compute budget without injecting stochastic noise into the denoising process.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a fixed NFE (Number of Function Evaluations) budget, UMF takes as actions a set of pretrained MDLMs (e.g., Dream-Coder-7B and LLaDA-8B) each with a deterministic remasking strategy and near-zero temperature, plus a reward signal (execution-based test-case pass rate for code, or a process reward model for math). The method produces a single best-scoring generated sequence (code or math solution) selected from the terminal nodes of the search tree. Generation is constrained to a 768-token window, and heterogeneous tokenizers across models are reconciled via text-level decode-and-re-encode mapping at branch points.

## Method

UMF formulates the MDLM unmasking trajectory as a search tree whose nodes are partially-masked states at discrete mask-ratio checkpoints (schedule [0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6, 0.5, 0.4, 0.2]) and whose edges correspond to deterministic actions—each action being a specific (model, temperature, remasking-strategy) tuple. Monte Carlo Tree Search with UCT selects which action to apply at each node, and expanding a node entails a deterministic rollout to full unmasking under the chosen action, scored by the reward signal. The critical design choice is that all actions are near-deterministic (T≈0 with confidence-based remasking), which yields two advantages: (1) a single rollout gives the exact branch value (Var[R]≈0), so the NFE budget is spent on exploration width rather than repeated averaging, and (2) identical state-action pairs can be cached, making revisits cost zero NFE. Theoretically, the paper shows via an adaptive-kernel-selection argument (Eq. 1) that a state-dependent policy switching among heterogeneous models achieves lower accumulated KL error than any single static model, providing the formal justification for multi-model branching over stochastic temperature scaling.

## Results

At NFE=12288, UMF achieves 28.0% Pass@1 on LiveCodeBench, 88.0% on HumanEval+, and 72.0% on MBPP+, outperforming all baselines by 4–13 points. On the MATH dataset (105 problems), UMF reaches 60.95% Pass@1 at NFE=12288, an 11.43-point improvement over its 49.52% at NFE=768. Caching contributes an additional 4.76–7.69% Pass@1 gain at NFE≥3072 with ~50–56% cache hit rates, and extending to NFE=24576 yields 30.0% on LiveCodeBench, confirming continued scaling without saturation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMF consistently outperforms all four compared baselines across every benchmark. Against Best-of-N and DTS\* (the strongest single-model and pair variants), UMF gains 9–10 points on LiveCodeBench (28.0 vs. 19.0/18.0), 13 points on HumanEval+ (88.0 vs. 75.0), and 4–6 points on MBPP+ (72.0 vs. 66.0/68.0). Against AB-MCTS (the closest tree-search competitor using multi-model actions), UMF leads by 7 points on LiveCodeBench (28.0 vs. 21.0), 7 on HumanEval+ (88.0 vs. 81.0), and 4 on MBPP+ (72.0 vs. 68.0), attributed to UMF's deterministic caching and restricted action space. In the appendix comparisons, UMF substantially outperforms ReMDM on LiveCodeBench (28.0 vs. 18.0 at NFE=12288, where ReMDM shows no scaling) and TReASURe on MATH (59.05% vs. 10.48% at NFE=6144), suggesting that terminal-reward evaluation with deterministic action branching is far more effective than step-wise reward estimation or localized remasking for these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time scaling paradigm from autoregressive LLMs to Masked Diffusion Language Models, building on MCTS (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) for search and DTS (Jain et al., 2025b) for the tree structure over unmasking trajectories. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-LM search methods (DTS, TReASURe, MEDAL) is replacing stochastic branching within a single model with deterministic actions drawn from multiple pretrained MDLMs or distinct inference configurations. This reframes the exploration axis from 'sampling different tokens' to 'selecting different denoising kernels,' enabling aggressive node caching that is infeasible under stochastic rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the standard TTS recipe for AR-LLMs (raise temperature, sample more) is actively harmful for MDLMs, a finding that redirects how the community should think about scaling non-autoregressive generative models. The practical implication is that diverse model priors interleaved within a single trajectory (28% LiveCodeBench) substantially outperform independent ensembling (19%), suggesting collaborative generation is a more efficient use of inference compute. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended generation without verifiable rewards, whether learned value functions can replace UCT heuristics, and whether the multi-model requirement can be relaxed to a single model with diverse inference configurations.
