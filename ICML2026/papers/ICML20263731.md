## Problem

Offline meta-RL agents must generalize from static datasets collected by heterogeneous behavior policies to unseen tasks, but face two coupled distribution shifts: context shift (training contexts come from behavior policies while test contexts come from the agent's own exploration) and policy shift (offline data cannot cover the full state-action space). These shifts cause agents to overfit to behavior-policy-specific correlations rather than task-relevant dynamics, creating an 'inherent pattern dilemma' that is especially severe under sparse rewards and out-of-distribution tasks. Existing context encoders lack a principled mechanism to separate task dynamics from behavior-induced variation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context sequence of state-action-reward transitions (o_t = [s_t, r_{t-1}]) from offline datasets collected by non-stationary SAC behavior policies, plus the current state s during online adaptation. It produces a global task embedding z extracted via a learnable query token that attends over the Transformer's hidden states through self-attention, which then conditions the policy to output an action a ~ π_θ(a|s,z). The observation explicitly includes the previous reward r_{t-1} as a temporal signal for distinguishing task-specific reward structures, a design choice validated by ablation.

## Method

MetaSTAR learns behavior-invariant task representations by training a stochastic Transformer-based world model (VAE observation encoder, Transformer sequence model, latent dynamics predictor, continuation predictor) whose dynamics loss minimizes KL between the encoder posterior and the Transformer prior over next-step latent states. The key insight, formalized in Theorem 3.1, is that this dynamics objective acts as a variational surrogate for maximizing the primary causality I(Z;X_t|X_b) while suppressing the lesser causality I(Z;X_b), thereby extracting task-relevant transition and reward structure independent of behavior policies. A FOCAL contrastive loss further enforces task discriminability in the embedding space. For policy learning, the world model generates context-conditioned imagined rollouts (warming up with real offline context before autoregressive imagination), and a conservative value penalty inspired by COMBO penalizes Q-values on policy-induced state-action regions unsupported by offline data, preventing the policy from exploiting model inaccuracies. Theoretical results (Theorems 3.3–3.4) bound the total variation distance between task representations induced by different behavior policies as O(√ε) where ε is the dynamics loss.

## Results

On the primary online meta-testing protocol, MetaSTAR achieves the best or near-best returns in 6 out of 8 environments. The advantage is most pronounced in sparse-reward settings: on Point-Robot-Sparse, MetaSTAR reaches 21±8 online return versus 10±6 for the next-best baseline CORRO, and on Cheetah-Vel-Sparse it achieves 260±34 versus 216±15 for CORRO. In dense-reward Ant-Dir, MetaSTAR attains 230±42 online return, substantially outperforming CORRO (149±39) and UNICORN (140±36). MetaSTAR is slightly outperformed by CORRO on Hopper-Param (279±25 vs. 294±28) and by UNICORN on Cheetah-Vel (−60±19 vs. −56±10), but leads or ties on all other environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaSTAR is compared against four context-based offline meta-RL baselines: CORRO (contrastive learning over transition tuples), CSRO (context shift reduction via policy-information suppression), FOCAL (distance metric learning with behavior regularization), and UNICORN (unified information-theoretic framework). MetaSTAR clearly dominates in sparse-reward environments: on Point-Robot-Sparse it more than doubles the online return of the best baseline (21±8 vs. 10±6 for CORRO), and on Cheetah-Vel-Sparse it leads by 44 points over CORRO (260±34 vs. 216±15). In dense-reward Ant-Dir, MetaSTAR's 230±42 online return exceeds CORRO (149±39) by over 50%. However, MetaSTAR is not universally superior: CORRO edges out MetaSTAR on Hopper-Param (294±28 vs. 279±25 online) and UNICORN is slightly better on Cheetah-Vel (−56±10 vs. −60±19). In the OOD adaptation setting (Table 2), MetaSTAR's advantage is even more dramatic—on Ant-Dir OOD it achieves 59±19 versus near-zero for all baselines, and on Cheetah-Vel-Sparse OOD it reaches 142±7 versus 18±1 for CORRO—confirming that the behavior-invariant representation and conservative imagination provide the strongest robustness under task distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaSTAR sits at the intersection of two research threads: information-theoretic task representation learning (UNICORN, CORRO, CSRO, FOCAL) and Transformer-based world models (Dreamer, TransDreamer). It adopts UNICORN's primary/lesser causality decomposition as its theoretical backbone and FOCAL's distance metric loss as a contrastive regularizer, but departs from prior offline meta-RL methods by using a stochastic Transformer world model as the task encoder rather than a dedicated context encoder. The conservative value penalty for imagination-based rollouts is borrowed from COMBO (Yu et al., 2021), adapted here to the meta-RL setting with task-conditioned Q-functions. The key conceptual departure is Theorem 3.1, which formally shows that optimizing world-model latent dynamics is equivalent to maximizing the primary causality I(Z;X_t|X_b), providing a principled justification for why world models naturally filter behavior-policy-induced correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode in offline meta-RL—the 'pattern dilemma' where agents overfit to behavior-policy-specific correlations rather than task dynamics—by showing that world-model-based representation learning provides an information-theoretically grounded solution. The OOD robustness results (e.g., Ant-Dir OOD: 59 vs. near-0 for baselines) suggest this approach could be important for real-world deployment where test tasks inevitably differ from training data. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 relatively low-dimensional MuJoCo/Point-Robot environments with only 40 tasks each, and the computational overhead (~2× training time vs. model-free baselines) raises scalability questions for high-dimensional observation spaces or large task collections. Open questions include whether the behavior-invariance guarantee extends to partially observable settings, whether the world model's imagination horizon can be extended for longer-horizon tasks, and how the approach performs when behavior policies are adversarially heterogeneous rather than merely non-stationary.
