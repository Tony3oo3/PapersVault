## Problem

Scaling laws for dense LLMs under infinite data and data-constrained scaling laws for dense models are well studied, but how dynamic sparse training (DST) interacts with limited data requiring multi-epoch training remains unexplored. Specifically, it is unclear whether sparsity alters the diminishing returns from repeated data, shifts optimal compute allocation, or changes the optimal sparsity level as data scale grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265064/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scaling law takes as inputs the number of non-zero parameters N, unique training tokens U_d, data repetition ratio R_d, sparsity level S (0%–93.75%), and a compute budget C (measured in either sparse FLOPs C=6ND or dense-equivalent FLOPs C=6ND/(1−S)). It outputs a predicted validation loss, a loss-optimal sparsity (minimizing loss over S, N, D jointly), and a compute-optimal sparsity (matching dense-model loss at minimum FLOPs). The law is fitted on 500 LLaMA-2 training runs spanning 15M–1.92B dense-equivalent parameters on C4 (520M–2.6B unique tokens, up to 16 epochs) and validated by extrapolation to held-out models up to 7.68B parameters.

## Method

The paper proposes a sparsity-aware data-constrained scaling law that jointly models active parameters, data reuse, and sparsity. The parameter term is modulated by a U-shaped sparsity factor A((1−S)^ε + PS^μ)/N'^α, capturing the capacity–data mismatch at extreme sparsity. The key mechanism is making the data saturation threshold sparsity-dependent via R_d*(S) = R_d*(1 + λ₁S + σ₁S²), which empirically increases from 4.4 at S=0 to ~6.9 at S=0.5, meaning sparse models extract more value from repeated tokens before saturating. A symmetric term R_n*(S) governs parameter-capacity saturation and peaks earlier (~S=0.34), explaining why moderate sparsity is optimal. All coefficients are fitted via L-BFGS in a staged procedure (unique-data fit → dense repetition fit → sparse repetition fit), and the underlying DST uses SET with magnitude pruning and random regrowth, with learning rate scaled by 1/(1−S) relative to the dense μP rule.

## Results

The proposed sparse data-constrained scaling law achieves a validation R² of 0.914 on unseen configurations (vs. 0.982 on fitted data), accurately predicting loss across 500 training runs spanning 15M–1.92B dense-equivalent parameters, sparsity up to 93.75%, and up to 41.6B total training tokens. The data saturation threshold R_d*(S) increases from 4.4 at S=0 to ~6.9 at S=0.5, indicating that moderate sparsity delays repeated-data saturation. At compute-optimal sparsity, sparse models match dense-model accuracy while using approximately 8×–10× fewer training FLOPs (e.g., N≈0.68B vs. N≈2.60B at U_d=1.3B tokens), and the parameter-to-token ratio drops by ~3× compared to dense training.

Loss-optimal sparsity remains stable at S≈0.45–0.5 across all budgets, while compute-optimal sparsity increases with data scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between dense training (S=0) and dynamic sparse training (DST via SET) under identical data-constrained conditions. At compute-optimal sparsity, sparse models achieve dense-equivalent validation loss with 8×–10× fewer training FLOPs, and the gap widens at larger data scales (e.g., 6.92B vs. 27.06B non-zero parameters at U_d=13B). Under a fixed 1.3B-token budget, the dense efficient frontier favors ~8 epochs with ~2B non-zero parameters, whereas the sparse frontier (S=50%) favors ~12 epochs with ~1B non-zero parameters, yielding a ~3× smaller parameter-to-token ratio (0.064 vs. 0.19). The fitted scaling law outperforms both the standard Chinchilla formulation (which assumes infinite data) and the dense data-constrained law of Muennighoff et al. (which assumes S=0) in the joint sparse + repeated-data regime, as evidenced by its ability to capture the U-shaped sparsity–capacity trade-off and the sparsity-dependent shift in data saturation. No external sparse-training baselines (e.g., RigL, MoE) are directly benchmarked; the comparison is analytical and scaling-law-based rather than a head-to-head accuracy table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously orthogonal lines of scaling-law research: sparse scaling laws (Frantar et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2025), which model how active parameters govern performance under infinite data, and data-constrained scaling laws (Muennighoff et al., 2023), which capture diminishing returns from repeated tokens in dense training. The authors adopt the Muennighoff et al. effective-data formulation as their starting point and extend it with sparsity-dependent saturation thresholds R_d*(S) and R_n*(S), while incorporating the sparsity-capacity trade-off term from Frantar et al. The DST algorithm used throughout is SET (Mocanu et al., 2018), and the base architecture follows LLaMA-2 with μP-scaled learning rates. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first systematic empirical study of the 'sparse-to-sparse + limited data' regime, which prior work had left unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As high-quality pretraining data becomes the binding constraint (growing ~1×/year vs. ~4×/year compute), this work provides concrete guidance: moderate sparsity (~50%) improves loss, while compute-optimal sparsity (~60–75%) matches dense accuracy with 8–10× fewer FLOPs, and the optimal sparsity shifts upward with data scale. The finding that sparsity delays data saturation (R_d* rising from 4.4 to ~6.9) reframes sparsity from a pure efficiency tool to a mechanism that fundamentally alters the compute-allocation trade-off under data scarcity. Key limitations include the phenomenological (non-theoretical) nature of the scaling law, restriction to C4 and models up to 7.68B parameters, and the acknowledged gap between algorithmic sparse FLOPs and realizable hardware speedups, all of which open important follow-up questions.
