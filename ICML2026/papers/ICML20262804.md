## Problem

Existing graph foundation models (GFMs) rely on fixed-hop subgraph sampling during pretraining, imposing a static structural receptive field that cannot simultaneously satisfy the heterogeneous scale requirements of diverse downstream tasks (e.g., 2 hops suffice for homophilous citation networks, while 4+ hops are needed for fraud detection). Moreover, encoding structurally heterogeneous subgraphs within a single Euclidean embedding space causes geometric mismatch and representation distortion, limiting cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262804/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes attributed graphs (node features, edge lists) from multiple source domains plus a GPU memory budget that dynamically determines the maximum hop number K via an online greedy feasibility test. The GoG edge count is controlled by a separate edge budget parameter, and the number of Riemannian experts is auto-determined from the coefficient of variation of node-degree distributions across the training pool. The output is a single fused GoG embedding per training node (or edge pair for link prediction), which serves as a transferable representation for downstream node classification (accuracy) or link prediction (AUC-ROC) without task-specific retraining.

## Method

R-GFM constructs a per-node Graph-of-Graphs (GoG) whose nodes are ego-subgraphs sampled at hops 1 through K, with GoG edges sparsely sampled according to cosine similarity of subgraph embeddings under a fixed edge budget. The GoG is then encoded by a dynamic Mixture-of-Experts over constant-curvature Riemannian manifolds (hyperbolic, Euclidean, hyperspherical), where a GCN-based router assigns each GoG to the best-fit geometry and the number of activated experts (top-m) is decreased stepwise as routing confidence grows during training. The candidate expert set size is itself dynamic, scaled by a heterogeneity score computed from the coefficient of variation of node degrees across the training datasets. Training proceeds in two stages: a contrastive (NT-Xent) pretraining of the subgraph encoder, followed by MoE-Riemannian encoder optimization. Theoretical analysis proves that multi-hop sampling strictly reduces embedding noise versus fixed-hop (Theorem 3.2), similarity-based sparse GoG edges outperform both edgeless and fully-connected GoGs (Theorem 3.3), and the dynamic expert selection yields tighter excess-risk and domain-generalization bounds than fixed-expert baselines (Theorems 3.4–3.5).

## Results

On 1-shot cross-domain node classification across 8 benchmark datasets, R-GFM achieves the best accuracy on all datasets, with headline results of 57.54% on CiteSeer (a ~49% relative improvement over the strongest baseline GCOPE at 38.62%), 49.50% on Cora, and 61.08% on Amazon-Photo. On link prediction (AUC-ROC), R-GFM leads on all four evaluated datasets, with the largest margin on Texas (87.94% vs. 72.41% for RiemannGFM). In the large-scale LM-feature setting, SAMGPT and MDGFM hit out-of-memory errors, while R-GFM achieves the best transfer accuracy on all four targets (e.g., 61.78% on Cora vs. 50.92% for RiemannGFM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-GFM outperforms all 18 baselines on every dataset in the 1-shot node classification benchmark. The most dramatic gain is on CiteSeer, where R-GFM reaches 57.54% versus 38.62% for GCOPE (the prior best GFM), a ~49% relative improvement; on Cora it beats RiemannGFM (43.48%) by 6 points. Among the GFM baselines, G2PM is the closest competitor on PubMed (49.71% vs. 49.80%) and Photos (59.90% vs. 61.08%), while GCOPE leads on CiteSeer among non-R-GFM methods but is still far behind. On link prediction (AUC-ROC), R-GFM wins on all four datasets, with the largest margin on Texas (87.94% vs. 72.41% for RiemannGFM, +15.5 points) and Wisconsin (84.15% vs. 78.19%). In the large-scale LM-feature setting, SAMGPT and MDGFM fail entirely (OOM), while R-GFM achieves 61.78% on Cora and 59.49% on Instagram, surpassing RiemannGFM by 10.9 and 12.9 points respectively. On 3-shot classification, the advantage widens further: R-GFM reaches 73.98% on CiteSeer versus 47.72% for MDGFM, and 44.18% on Texas versus 35.14% for MDGFM.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-GFM sits at the intersection of three research threads: the Graph-of-Graphs (GoG) representation mechanism (Zhen et al., 2023), Riemannian manifold-based graph encoding (Sun et al., 2025, RiemannGFM), and sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts routing (Shazeer et al., 2017). It departs from existing GFMs (GCOPE, SAMGFM, MDGFM) in two key ways: replacing fixed-hop subgraph sampling with an adaptive-hop GoG that treats multi-scale structural context as explicit higher-order nodes, and replacing static manifold selection with a confidence-aware dynamic top-m routing over a curvature-adapted expert pool. The theoretical contribution (excess-risk and domain-generalization bounds) extends the analysis of MDGFM (Wang et al., 2025a) by showing that dynamic expert selection and similarity-based GoG edges yield strictly tighter guarantees than fixed alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation in GFMs—fixed receptive fields cannot simultaneously serve tasks requiring local (2-hop) and global (4+ hop) structural context—and provides both architectural and theoretical remedies. The 49% relative improvement on Citeseer and consistent SOTA across 18 datasets suggest the multi-scale GoG + Riemannian MoE combination is a meaningful step forward for cross-domain graph transfer. Open questions include whether the GoG construction scales efficiently to graphs with millions of nodes (the large-scale experiments still use subgraph sampling), whether the dynamic expert count truly adapts meaningfully or merely adds tunable complexity, and whether the approach extends naturally to temporal or heterogeneous graphs beyond the static attributed setting.
