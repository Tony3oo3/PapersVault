## Problem

In hierarchical Simulation Based Inference, the simulation cost grows with the number of local sites because each training sample traditionally requires simulating all sites jointly. Existing factorised approaches reduce this cost but still consume multiple simulator calls per training sample, and none natively support function-valued observations recorded at irregular coordinates. The paper asks whether likelihood factorisation—training a per-site surrogate from single-site simulations and then assembling synthetic multi-site data—can achieve well-calibrated hierarchical posteriors at a fraction of the simulator budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-site observations that may be function-valued (e.g., flow waveforms resampled to N_t points per terminal vessel, or incidence counts at site-specific irregular time points), with each site's observation potentially having a different number and schedule of recorded coordinates. Parameters are partitioned into a fixed-dimensional global vector θ_g and per-site local vectors η_s, all flattened into a single token sequence with variable identifiers, intra-variable positions, group (site) identifiers, and optional functional inputs. The output is a continuous vector field v_t(θ_t, y) over the concatenated (θ_g, η) space, from which posterior samples are drawn by integrating the flow ODE with a Dormand-Prince 5(4) solver.

## Method

TFMPE combines three components: (1) a tokenisation scheme that embeds all model variables into a shared latent space using learned lookup tables for variable ID, position, and group ID, plus Gaussian Fourier features for functional inputs, with a scalar flow-matching time broadcast across tokens; (2) an encoder-only transformer (2 blocks, 16 heads, d_lat=256) that processes the full token sequence with self-attention and reads out the vector field from parameter tokens via a shared linear projection; and (3) a two-stage Likelihood Factorisation training procedure where Stage 1 trains a per-site neural surrogate q_{φ_l}(y_s|θ_g, η_s) from exactly one simulator call per sample using the FMPE objective, and Stage 2 trains the posterior estimator q_{φ_p}(θ_g, η|y) on synthetic multi-site observations sampled from the frozen surrogate. The group embedding is load-bearing: it hands the attention mechanism the site partition explicitly rather than forcing it to be inferred from positional patterns, and ablations show its removal collapses performance across the benchmark. Training uses Gaussian optimal-transport probability paths with the corresponding OT vector field, and the approach is validated on a new six-task hierarchical SBI benchmark plus realistic seasonal SEIR and 1D CFD haemodynamics models.

## Results

On the hierarchical SBI benchmark (n_s=50 sites, N=10,000 simulator calls), TFMPE with Likelihood Factorisation (LF) achieves the best ℓ-C2ST on 5 of 6 tasks: 2.34e-03 (Gaussian Linear), 1.39e-03 (Gaussian Linear Uniform), 1.57e-03 (Gaussian Mixture), 1.23e-04 (SIR), and 6.08e-03 (SLCP), while non-hierarchical baselines (NPE, Simformer, SNPE, FMPE) remain above ℓ-C2ST ≥ 0.15. On the seasonal SEIR model with 100 sites, TFMPE matches NUTS reference posteriors (MMD²=0.060, p<0.005) where NUTS fails to converge beyond 5 sites. In the 16-patient haemodynamics calibration, TFMPE achieves 95% credible interval coverage on all 11 parameters with a 1852× per-patient surrogate speedup and 2× end-to-end training speedup over standard NPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFMPE with LF decisively outperforms all non-hierarchical baselines (NPE, SNPE, Simformer, FMPE-MLP, FMPE-Transformer) across all six benchmark tasks at N=10,000, with ℓ-C2ST values typically 10–100× lower (e.g., 1.23e-04 vs. ~0.19 on SIR). Against the hierarchical Posterior Factorisation baseline of Heinrich et al. (2024), LF wins on 5 of 6 tasks but loses on Two Moons (1.16e-01 vs. 5.69e-02), where the nonlinear crescent-shaped observation map causes surrogate approximation errors to compound during multi-site synthesis; ablations confirm this gap is attributable to surrogate error rather than the factorisation strategy itself. On the seasonal SEIR model, TFMPE matches NUTS (Hoffman & Gelman, 2014) reference posteriors with MMD²=0.060 on 2 sites, and extends to 100 sites where NUTS fails to converge reliably beyond 5 sites. In the haemodynamics setting, TFMPE's two-stage LF training achieves a 2× end-to-end speedup over standard NPE (3.3h vs. 6.6h for N=1000, n_s=2) while maintaining 11/11 parameter 95% CI coverage at n_s=16, a scale where MCMC is computationally prohibitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three existing threads in simulation-based inference: the flow matching posterior estimation objective of Wildberger et al. (2023), the tokenised transformer architecture of SimFormer (Gloeckler et al., 2024), and the Gaussian optimal-transport probability paths of Lipman et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is from prior hierarchical SBI methods (Heinrich et al., 2024; Arruda et al., 2025; Habermann et al., 2025), which factorise the *posterior* and thus require multiple simulator calls per training sample; here the authors instead factorise the *likelihood*, training a per-site neural surrogate from single-site simulations and then synthesising multi-site observations for posterior training. The tokenisation scheme extends SimFormer by adding an explicit group identifier for site membership, which ablations show is a load-bearing component rather than a convenience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first dedicated benchmark for hierarchical SBI provides a much-needed systematic evaluation framework, and the 1852× per-patient surrogate speedup in the haemodynamics case study demonstrates that likelihood factorisation makes inference tractable at scales (hundreds of sites) where both MCMC and non-hierarchical NPE are computationally prohibitive. The task-specific trade-off between likelihood factorisation and posterior factorisation (LF wins on Gaussian Mixture, PF wins on Two Moons) gives practitioners a concrete decision criterion, though the paper acknowledges that surrogate approximation error remains a risk for complex nonlinear observation maps. Open questions include whether efficient attention mechanisms can push the transformer-based approach beyond n_s ≈ 100, and whether sequential refinement strategies can further reduce the residual surrogate error that limits LF on difficult tasks.
