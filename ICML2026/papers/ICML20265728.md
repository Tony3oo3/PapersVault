## Problem

Codebook collapse—where most graph nodes map to only a few codewords—consistently occurs when training Vector Quantization jointly with GNNs under graph reconstruction objectives, and existing mitigation strategies from vision and language domains fail to resolve it. The paper identifies this as a systematic issue rooted in graph-specific data properties (feature redundancy, dense connectivity) and the self-reinforcing dynamics of deterministic hard assignment, rather than an incidental training artifact.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, processed through a multi-layer GNN encoder (typically 4-layer GAT or GCN with 256-dim hidden states). It produces a set of discrete graph tokens (soft-quantized embeddings from a K=512 codebook during training, hard-assigned codewords at inference), along with reconstructed node features and links. The learned tokens serve as plug-in representations for downstream tasks (node classification, link prediction, graph classification) or as input sequences to vanilla Transformer backbones for serialization.

## Method

RGVQ addresses collapse through two complementary mechanisms. First, it replaces the non-differentiable argmin hard assignment with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization: assignment logits are computed as negative squared distances to all K codewords, perturbed with Gumbel noise, and passed through a temperature-controlled softmax (τ=0.1), yielding a differentiable soft assignment distribution that routes gradients to all codewords proportionally rather than only the selected one. Second, it introduces a structure-aware contrastive regularization (InfoNCE loss) over the soft assignment distributions: for each anchor node, positive samples are drawn from its 1-hop neighbors and top-20 feature-similar nodes, while negative samples are nodes that are neither adjacent nor feature-similar; the loss encourages similar nodes to share token distributions and penalizes dissimilar nodes from doing so. The final training objective combines feature/link reconstruction, codebook and commitment losses, and the contrastive regularization, with RGVQ acting as a drop-in plugin for existing Graph VQ backbones (GFT, GQT).

## Results

On codebook utilization (perplexity, K=512), RGVQ achieves 211.69 on Cora, 319.09 on PubMed, and 446.02 on Photo, compared to 94.47, 4.14, and 1.00 for vanilla Graph VQ respectively — a 2.2× to 77× improvement. On the most severely collapsed datasets (Photo, Computer), RGVQ recovers perplexity from 1.00 to over 400, while the best baseline (HQA-GAE) only reaches 166.32 and 114.08. For downstream transferability via GFT, RGVQ achieves 81.42 average across node/link/graph classification tasks, surpassing GFT (79.50) and all mitigation variants. For serialization via GQT, RGVQ improves node classification accuracy by 1.5–8.1 points over GQT with standard anti-collapse methods across 9 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGVQ outperforms all 19 baselines on codebook perplexity across all 9 datasets, with the largest margins on severely collapsed graphs: on Photo and Computer, vanilla VQ and EMA collapse to perplexity 1.00 (a single codeword), while RGVQ recovers to 446.02 and 413.10. Among mitigation strategies, codebook reset and affine parameters show dataset-dependent behavior (e.g., Reset reaches 150.51 on Roman but only 10.73 on Photo), and none consistently approach RGVQ's performance. The two most advanced baselines, SimVQ (perplexity 40–46) and HQA-GAE (perplexity 72–166), still trail RGVQ by 2–10× on most datasets. On downstream transferability (Table 2), GFT+RGVQ achieves 81.42 average, beating GFT+Reset (79.92), GFT+AP (79.48), and GFT+SimVQ (75.97), with the largest gains on link prediction (WN18RR: 91.32 vs. 90.87 for GFT) and graph classification (PCBA: 75.68 vs. 72.74). On serialization (Table 3), GQT+RGVQ leads on 8 of 9 datasets, with the most notable improvements on CiteSeer (81.25 vs. 73.14 for GQT, +8.11) and PubMed (86.54 vs. 81.60, +4.94), while the gap is smallest on Ratings (55.16 vs. 54.04, +1.12) where the backbone architecture compensates for tokenization imperfections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) to graph data by replacing the non-differentiable hard nearest-neighbor assignment with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization (Roy et al., 2018; Sønderby et al., 2017), a technique well-established in vision and language but previously unexplored for graph quantization. It departs from prior Graph VQ work (GFT, GQT, VQGraph, HQA-GAE) by identifying codebook collapse as a systematic, previously overlooked bottleneck and diagnosing it through graph-specific data properties (feature redundancy, connectivity density) and the self-reinforcing dynamics of deterministic hard assignment. The structure-aware contrastive regularization, which uses graph topology and feature similarity to shape token assignment distributions via InfoNCE, is the paper's novel contribution, positioning graph structure itself as a natural regularization signal rather than an external constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Codebook collapse fundamentally limits the expressiveness and transferability of discrete graph tokens, and this paper provides the first systematic diagnosis linking collapse to intrinsic graph properties and optimization dynamics, offering actionable guidance for the graph VQ community. RGVQ's design as a lightweight plug-in (Gumbel-Softmax + contrastive term added to the existing VQ loss) makes it immediately applicable to existing backbones like GFT and GQT without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approach scales effectively to very large graphs beyond the OGBN experiments, how it interacts with multi-level or residual VQ schemes, and whether the structure-aware regularization generalizes to other discrete representation learning settings beyond graphs.
