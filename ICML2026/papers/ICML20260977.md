## Problem

Prevailing plug-and-play diffusion (PnPDP) solvers for medical image reconstruction are memoryless: they update estimates using only instantaneous gradients (analogous to a Proportional controller), which provably leaves a non-vanishing steady-state bias under heavy corruption. Restoring the classical ADMM dual variable to provide integral feedback is theoretically sound but practically non-trivial, because the accumulated dual residuals are spectrally colored and violate the AWGN input assumption of pretrained diffusion denoisers, triggering severe hallucinations. The paper addresses this bias-hallucination trade-off by coupling the dual mechanism with a statistical adaptation layer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes degraded measurements (parallel-beam CT sinograms at 20 sparse views or 90 limited-angle views; single-coil MRI k-space with 1D equidistant Cartesian masks at 6× or 10× acceleration), the corresponding forward operator, and a pretrained diffusion denoiser (EDM-trained U-Net at 256×256 for CT; a shared pretrained MRI prior at 320×320 from INVERSEBENCH). It outputs a single reconstructed image per scan after K outer iterations (typically 50–100 NFEs). Notably, the solver is zero-shot: no task-specific retraining of the diffusion prior is performed, and the SH module adds only ~0.7 ms per iteration with no additional denoiser calls.

## Method

DC-PnPDP restores the explicit ADMM dual variable u^(k) inside the PnP diffusion loop, so the x-update (solved by Conjugate Gradient) and the z-update (diffusion denoiser) are coupled through an accumulated consensus error that acts as integral feedback, eliminating the steady-state bias of memoryless HQS/proximal-gradient solvers. To prevent the spectrally colored dual-shifted input v^(k+1)=x^(k+1)+u^(k) from violating the denoiser's AWGN training distribution, a Spectral Homogenization (SH) module is inserted before each denoiser call: it estimates the power spectral density of the residual r^(k+1)=v^(k+1)−z^(k) via kernel-smoothed FFT, computes the spectral deficit ΔS(ω) relative to the target white-noise level σ_t², synthesizes a complementary noise field ξ with amplitude √ΔS(ω) and random phase, and adds it to v^(k+1) to produce a pseudo-AWGN input. A second-order moment-matching proposition guarantees the homogenized input's covariance approximates σ²I, aligning it with the denoiser's valid statistical manifold. Fixed-point analysis further proves that the dual-coupled iteration converges to a first-order stationary point of the original variational problem, whereas the memoryless variant converges to a biased compromise dictated by the denoiser's behavior on clean inputs.

## Results

On limited-angle CT (LACT-90), DC-PnPDP at 100 NFEs achieves 39.46 dB PSNR / 0.955 SSIM, improving over the strongest baseline DiffPIR by +4.76 dB. On sparse-view CT (SVCT-20), it reaches 40.55 dB / 0.963 SSIM (+2.69 dB over DiffPIR). On accelerated brain MRI at AF=10, DC-PnPDP attains 30.91 dB / 0.943 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline SITCOM by +2.24 dB. In terms of inference efficiency, DC-PnPDP at 30 NFEs already surpasses DiffPIR at 100 NFEs, corresponding to approximately 3.3× fewer denoiser evaluations at matched or better quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-PnPDP outperforms all six baselines across every task and metric. On CT, the largest gains are over DiffPIR (the strongest CT baseline): +4.76 dB on LACT-90 and +2.69 dB on SVCT-20, attributed to the dual variable eliminating steady-state bias that memoryless HQS solvers cannot correct. On MRI, SITCOM is the strongest baseline; DC-PnPDP improves by +0.85 dB (AF=6) and +2.24 dB (AF=10), with the larger gain at higher acceleration reflecting the benefit of integral feedback against coherent aliasing artifacts. DDNM and DDS trail by 10+ dB on CT and 4–5 dB on MRI, confirming that null-space or decomposed-sampling strategies are less effective than explicit dual-coupled optimization. DAPS, which uses Langevin dynamics for data consistency, is outperformed by 2–5 dB on CT and 1–4 dB on MRI. In the controlled 50-NFE comparison (Appendix Table 3), DC-PnPDP still leads DiffPIR by +5.95 dB on LACT-90 and +3.58 dB on SVCT-20, confirming the gain comes from the optimization interface rather than additional denoiser calls. Against the supervised ProCT baseline on AAPM and DeepLesion, DC-PnPDP achieves the best PSNR in all settings, with particularly large margins in the out-of-distribution AAPM protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical PnP-ADMM framework (Chan et al., 2016; Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013) to the diffusion-prior setting, where prior work (DiffPIR, DAPS, SITCOM) has predominantly used memoryless HQS or proximal-gradient solvers that discard the dual variable. It is most closely related to concurrent work by Shrestha & Fu (2026) and Park et al. (2026), which also identify a distributional mismatch between ADMM iterates and diffusion denoisers, but those methods rely on expensive Langevin inner loops or stochastic re-noising; here, the Spectral Homogenization module achieves the same statistical alignment via a lightweight FFT-based frequency-domain correction with negligible overhead (0.0007 s per iteration). The diffusion backbone follows the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022), and the data-consistency step reuses the conjugate-gradient solver from DiffPIR for controlled comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete and previously unaddressed trade-off in PnP diffusion solvers: restoring the ADMM dual variable eliminates steady-state bias (yielding up to +4.76 dB on limited-angle CT) but introduces spectrally colored residuals that cause the diffusion prior to hallucinate; Spectral Homogenization bridges this gap without retraining or extra denoiser calls. The ~3× convergence speedup and negligible per-iteration overhead make the approach practically attractive for volumetric CT/MRI where denoiser evaluations dominate cost. Open questions include whether global convergence can be established for the non-convex stochastic setting, whether SH generalizes to 3D volumetric reconstruction or other generative priors (GANs, normalizing flows), and whether the bootstrap residual estimation remains reliable in very early iterations when z^(k) is far from the clean signal.
