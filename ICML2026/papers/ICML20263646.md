## Problem

Offline RL methods for style-conditioned policies struggle with three intertwined challenges: defining style in a general and interpretable way, handling state-style distribution shift where target styles are absent from the dataset for certain states, and reconciling the inherent conflict between style alignment and task performance. Existing approaches either sacrifice style alignment when optimizing task performance or lack mechanisms for robust alignment under distribution shift without further environment interaction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline trajectory dataset annotated by hard-coded labeling functions that map subtrajectory windows (of configurable radius) to discrete style labels, rather than full-trajectory labels. At inference, the policy takes a continuous environment state and a target style label as input and outputs a continuous action distribution. The windowed labeling design allows styles to vary within a single trajectory, mitigating credit assignment issues that arise from episode-scale annotations.

## Method

SCIQL extends IQL with style conditioning by training style-conditioned value functions via expectile regression, where the style reward is a surrogate Markovian function estimated from the dataset's label predictor. To address state-style distribution shift, it employs hindsight style relabeling—sampling style labels from the dataset's marginal label distribution rather than the joint state-action-label distribution—enabling the policy to plan toward styles not co-occurring with the current state. The key policy-extraction mechanism is Gated Advantage Weighted Regression (GAWR), which combines style and task advantages as A^λ(s,a,z) + σ(A^λ(s,a,z))·A^r(s,a), so that task-improving signals are only incorporated when the style advantage is positive, effectively gating task optimization to avoid degrading style alignment. This yields a practical approximation to the ideal of maximizing task performance within the set of style-optimal policies, without requiring explicit constrained optimization.

## Results

SCIQL achieves the best style alignment across all 7 datasets, with average alignment scores of 74.6% (circle2d-inplace), 75.5% (circle2d-navigate), 78.0–78.9% (HalfCheetah variants), 99.6% (HumEnv-simple), and 83.5% (HumEnv-complex). On the style-conditioned task-performance optimization front (Figure 2), SCIQL achieves a +41% to +163% hypervolume improvement over SORL across environments, and its style-prioritized variant (λ>r) reduces the Euclidean distance to the ideal (100,100) point by 18–28% compared to SORL's best configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCIQL decisively outperforms all three external baselines on style alignment. Against BC (Pomerleau, 1991), SCIQL gains roughly 45–50 percentage points on most datasets (e.g., 78.9% vs. 30.0% on halfcheetah-vary-v0), confirming that style conditioning is essential. Against BCPMI (Yang et al., 2025), SCIQL leads by 16–36 points (e.g., 78.0% vs. 58.1% on halfcheetah-fix-v0; 83.5% vs. 44.6% on humenv-complex-v0), demonstrating that value-based planning and trajectory stitching are critical beyond mutual-information-weighted cloning. Against SORL (Mao et al., 2024) in its imitation mode (β=0), SCIQL gains 19–36 points (e.g., 78.9% vs. 46.7% on halfcheetah-vary-v0; 83.5% vs. 47.7% on humenv-complex-v0). On the joint task-performance/style-alignment Pareto front (Figure 2), SCIQL dominates SORL across all environments with a +41% to +163% hypervolume improvement, and its style-prioritized GAWR variant (λ>r) sits closer to the ideal point than any SORL temperature setting. The only exception is humenv-simple-v0, where SCBC (an internal ablation) matches SCIQL at 99.6%, suggesting the task is near-trivial for that particular criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCIQL is a direct style-conditioned adaptation of IQL, extending its expectile regression and AWR policy extraction to operate over style labels sampled from subtrajectory windows rather than the joint data distribution. The GAWR mechanism generalizes AWR by gating the task advantage through a sigmoid of the style advantage, providing a lightweight alternative to the constrained-optimization formulations used by SORL. Conceptually, the paper reframes style alignment as a generalization of goal-conditioned RL, importing hindsight relabeling and trajectory stitching from the GCRL literature, while the subtrajectory labeling functions draw on the data-programming paradigm of Ratner et al. and the supervised style-labeling approach of CTVAE and BCPMI, but applied at the window level to mitigate credit assignment issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unified framework for jointly optimizing style alignment and task performance in offline RL without requiring constraint thresholds or dual-variable optimization, which is a non-trivial gap in the stylized offline RL literature. The GAWR gating mechanism is simple and reusable, and the subtrajectory labeling approach offers a low-cost, interpretable way to define and measure style alignment. Limitations include evaluation on relatively low-dimensional environments (2D circle, HalfCheetah, HumEnv), single-criterion-at-a-time training, and reliance on a surrogate Markovian style reward that may not fully capture non-Markovian style structure. Open questions include scaling to multiple simultaneous criteria, zero-shot style generation, and grounding labels in intrinsic state similarity rather than extrinsic temporal windows.
