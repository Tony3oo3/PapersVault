## Problem

Reinforcement learning for open-ended LLM agents (e.g., travel planning, deep research) lacks objective ground truth, forcing reliance on LLM-as-judge pointwise scalar scores. The paper identifies that this paradigm induces 'discriminative collapse': as the policy improves, intra-group trajectory rewards compress into a narrow band, making the signal-to-noise ratio approach 1 and causing RL optimization to stagnate. No existing method provides a scalable, high-fidelity reward mechanism for long-horizon open-ended agent tasks.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language user query and a set of callable tools (e.g., POI search, navigation, web search, flight/train ticket lookup) and produces an interleaved multi-step trajectory of chain-of-thought reasoning, tool invocations, environmental feedback, and a final open-ended answer. Rather than emitting a single scalar reward per trajectory, ArenaRL outputs a discrete relative rank (0 to N−1) for each trajectory within its group of N sampled responses, which is then converted into a normalized advantage signal for policy gradient updates.

## Method

ArenaRL replaces pointwise scalar rewards with intra-group relative ranking via a tournament-based mechanism. A process-aware pairwise judge evaluates trajectory pairs on multi-level rubrics covering CoT logical coherence, tool-call precision, and final-answer reliability, using a bidirectional scoring protocol to eliminate positional bias. The core efficiency contribution is the Seeded Single-Elimination topology: a greedy-decoded trajectory serves as a quality anchor, N−1 pairwise comparisons against it establish an initial seed ordering, and a serpentine bracket construction (top seed vs. bottom seed) prevents premature collisions among high-quality trajectories. This yields O(N) comparison cost while achieving a Spearman correlation of 0.961 with the O(N²) round-robin gold standard. The resulting ranks are mapped to quantile-based rewards and standardized into advantages, which drive a KL-regularized policy optimization objective identical in form to GRPO but with ranking-derived rather than score-derived signals.

## Results

On the Open-Travel benchmark (Qwen3-8B-Base backbone), ArenaRL achieves a 41.8% average pairwise win rate, substantially outperforming GRPO (16.4%), GSPO (17.2%), and Reinforce++ (16.8%). On Open-DeepResearch, ArenaRL reaches a 64.3% mean win rate with a 99% valid generation rate, compared to 25.2% (17% Val.) for GRPO and 25.2% (21% Val.) for GSPO. On open-ended writing (WritingBench, HelloBench, LongBench-write), ArenaRL scores 80.4 mean, surpassing GRPO (73.0), GSPO (72.3), and even GPT-4o (75.5) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (76.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArenaRL (Qwen3-8B-Base) outperforms all four closed-source models on both introduced benchmarks: it beats Claude-3.7-Sonnet (the strongest closed-source baseline) by 10.2 points on Open-Travel (41.8% vs 31.6%) and by 45.2 points on Open-DeepResearch (64.3% vs 19.1%). Against the three RL baselines using pointwise LLM-as-judge rewards, ArenaRL achieves roughly 2.5× the win rate of GRPO (41.8% vs 16.4%) and GSPO (41.8% vs 17.2%) on Open-Travel, and 2.5–2.7× on Open-DeepResearch (64.3% vs 25.2% for both GRPO and GSPO). Notably, GRPO and GSPO actually degrade the valid generation rate on Open-DeepResearch (17% and 21% respectively) below the SFT baseline (32%), while ArenaRL pushes it to 99%, indicating that pointwise rewards introduce spurious length-bias signals in long-horizon tasks. On open-ended writing, ArenaRL (80.4 mean) surpasses GPT-4o (75.5) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (76.2) by 4.9 and 4.2 points respectively, and leads GRPO by 7.4 points. The Seeded Single-Elimination topology achieves 32.5% mean win rate on Open-Travel, within 0.4 points of the O(N²) Round-Robin upper bound (32.9%), confirming near-lossless ranking fidelity at linear cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArenaRL extends the GRPO group-relative RL framework (Shao et al., 2024) by replacing pointwise scalar rewards from LLM-as-judge with intra-group relative rankings derived from pairwise comparisons. It draws on the pairwise preference mechanism explored in Pref-GRPO (Wang et al., 2025b) for text-to-image RL but adapts it to long-horizon agent tasks, where exhaustive O(N²) comparisons are intractable. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'discriminative collapse'—the compression of pointwise rewards into a noise-dominated band as the policy converges—and the design of a seeded single-elimination tournament that achieves O(N) complexity while preserving ranking fidelity. The paper also positions itself against prior open-ended agent benchmarks (VitaBench, DeepResearchBench) by providing full SFT→RL→evaluation pipelines rather than static test sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discriminative collapse diagnosis provides a useful and formal explanation for why standard LLM-as-judge RL stagnates on open-ended tasks, and the tournament-based ranking offers a practical, low-complexity remedy that practitioners can adopt. The Open-Travel and Open-DeepResearch benchmarks fill a genuine infrastructure gap for full-lifecycle evaluation of open-ended agents. However, the evaluation is entirely LLM-as-judge based (both for training rewards and final benchmark scoring), raising circularity concerns about whether the reported 3× gains over GRPO/GSPO fully reflect true quality improvements. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (experiments use only 4B/8B backbones), how sensitive the tournament design is to judge quality at scale, and whether the process-aware rubric generalizes beyond travel and research domains.
