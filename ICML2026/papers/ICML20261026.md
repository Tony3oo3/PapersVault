## Problem

Differentially private solvers for positive linear programs (packing, covering, mixed packing-covering) in the constraint-private regime lack tight bounds on the number of constraints that must be violated. Prior work by Hsu et al. (2014) provides only instance-dependent guarantees for fractional set cover, while recent general-LP solvers (Kaplan et al. 2024; Ene et al. 2025) achieve data-independent bounds but with high-degree polynomial dependence on dimension that is overly pessimistic for structured positive LPs. This paper closes the gap by exploiting the nonnegativity structure of positive LPs to improve both instance-dependent and data-independent bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a nonnegative constraint matrix (A ∈ R^{n×d}_{≥0} for pure LPs, or P ∈ R^{p×d}_{≥0} and C ∈ R^{c×d}_{≥0} for mixed LPs), privacy parameters (ε, δ), an approximation factor α ∈ (0,1), and a known upper bound on matrix entries. It outputs an approximate solution x̄ = x_T/T (the average of cumulative updates) that satisfies all but at most s constraints, where s is explicitly bounded in terms of problem parameters. Optional pre-processing truncates large matrix entries and post-processing zeros out small solution components to convert instance-dependent guarantees into data-independent ones depending only on dimension d.

## Method

The core technique is a Dense Multiplicative Weights Update algorithm developed from a regularized dual viewpoint using a truncated softmax function smax^U(x) = max_{r∈D^U} ⟨x,r⟩ − ω(r), where D^U is the unit simplex with coordinates capped at U and ω is negative entropy. The gradient ∇smax^U(x) assigns constraint weights bounded by U, which directly controls the sensitivity of the score function used in the exponential mechanism for private coordinate selection. The key structural insight is that constraint positivity yields a linear (rather than quadratic) bound on the per-iteration increase of smax^U, improving the convergence rate by a factor of ρ (the problem width) and reducing the number of iterations needed. The parameter U = 1/s is chosen so that the truncated softmax guarantees the average of the top s constraint values is bounded, thereby controlling the number of violated constraints. For mixed packing-covering LPs, the algorithm minimizes the ratio of packing to covering constraint increases via a score function Q(j) = −⟨∇smax^U(Px),P1_j⟩/⟨∇smin^U(Cx),C1_j⟩, with a perturbation added to C to avoid division by near-zero values.

## Results

For packing and covering LPs, the paper achieves a data-dependent bound of Õ((A_max·OPT)^1.5 / (α²ε)) and a data-independent bound of Õ(d^1.5 / (α^3.5 ε)) on the number of violated constraints. For mixed packing-covering LPs, the bounds are Õ(P_max·C_max·√(P_max+C_max)·V^2.5 / (α^4.5 ε)) (data-dependent) and Õ(d^3 / (α^6 ε)) (data-independent). These are the first constraint-private algorithms for positive LPs that simultaneously provide both instance-dependent and dimension-only guarantees, with the data-independent bound improving the dimension dependence from d^4 (Ene et al. 2025) to d^1.5 for pure LPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Hsu et al. (2014), which provides an explicit bound only for fractional set cover (A_max=1) of Õ(OPT²/(α²ε)), this paper improves the instance-dependent bound to Õ(OPT^1.5/(α²ε)), an improvement by a factor of OPT^0.5, and additionally provides a data-independent bound of Õ(d^1.5/(α^3.5 ε)) that was previously unavailable. Against Kaplan et al. (2024) and Ene et al. (2025), which solve general LPs with data-independent bounds of Õ(d⁹/ε) and Õ(d⁴/ε) respectively, this paper achieves a better dimension dependence of d^1.5 for pure packing/covering LPs, though at the cost of a polynomial (rather than polylogarithmic) dependence on 1/α. For mixed packing-covering LPs, the data-independent bound of Õ(d³/α⁶/ε) is worse than Ene et al.'s Õ(d²/ε·polylog(1/α)) for general LPs with margin α, reflecting the inherent additional complexity of the mixed formulation. The key advantage over prior general-LP solvers is that the positive-LP structure enables both improved dimension dependence and the availability of instance-specific bounds that can be tighter when OPT ≪ d/α.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constraint-private LP framework of Hsu et al. (2014), which used Dense Multiplicative Weights Update (from Herbster & Warmuth, 2001) as a blackbox via Bregman projection. The key departure is developing a new truncated softmax function from a regularized dual viewpoint that explicitly exploits the nonnegativity of positive LP constraints, rather than treating the problem as a general LP. Compared to recent general-LP solvers (Kaplan et al. 2024; Ene et al. 2025) that achieve data-independent bounds with high-degree polynomial dependence on dimension (d⁴–d⁹), this paper achieves d^1.5 for pure packing/covering LPs by leveraging structural positivity, though at the cost of worse bounds for mixed packing-covering LPs (d³).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results are most impactful in the regime where the number of constraints n far exceeds the dimension d (e.g., pricing problems with many users but few goods), where the data-independent d^1.5 bound is practically useful. The truncated softmax tool provides a general mechanism for controlling sensitivity in private optimization that could transfer to other structured problems. Open questions include whether the gap between pure and mixed packing-covering bounds can be closed, whether the approach extends to general (non-positive) LPs with better dimension dependence, and whether the assumption of knowing OPT (or a bounded range) can be relaxed in practice.
