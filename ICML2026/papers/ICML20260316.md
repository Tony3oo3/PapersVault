## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods incorporate expert knowledge as static, one-shot constraints or priors that assume uniform reliability and do not optimize which additional questions to ask when expert time is scarce. This paper addresses the gap by formulating expert-in-the-loop causal discovery as a sequential, probabilistic active learning problem that actively selects which local edge relations to query in order to most efficiently concentrate a posterior over DAGs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260316/cropped_page7_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observational dataset X (N×D), samples from an initial posterior q₀(W|X) over weighted DAGs produced by any black-box causal discovery method (MCMC, bootstrap, variational), and sequentially receives one 3-way categorical expert response per round (i→j, j→i, or no edge) for a chosen ordered pair. It outputs an evolving weighted particle posterior over DAGs, the next query pair to elicit, and posterior edge-existence and orientation probabilities. The expert channel is explicitly modeled as noisy and non-deterministic, and the initial posterior need only be accessible as samples rather than a closed-form density.

## Method

CaPE combines three components: (1) a hierarchical logistic expert model that maps a weighted DAG to a 3-way categorical response via local direction scores (log-scaled edge magnitude plus optional structural features like v-structure support or cycle risk), with separate logistic parameters controlling edge-existence reliability and orientation decisiveness; (2) a particle-based posterior update using importance reweighting by the expert likelihood, ESS-triggered multinomial resampling, and lightweight Metropolis-Hastings rejuvenation with acyclicity-enforcing proposals; and (3) a BALD-style expected information gain acquisition function computed over the 3-state posterior predictive distribution, which decomposes into epistemic uncertainty (posterior disagreement across particles) minus aleatoric uncertainty (inherent expert noise), making query selection tractable on low-dimensional categorical distributions rather than the full combinatorial DAG space. A top-k screening step by edge-existence Bernoulli variance restricts EIG evaluation to the most uncertain pairs, yielding O(SD² + Sk) per-round complexity.

## Results

On the Sachs protein signaling benchmark (D=11, N=853), after T=40 EIG-selected expert queries, CaPE reduces structural Hamming distance (SHD) by approximately 14 edges relative to the initial observational posterior and improves orientation F1 by more than 0.38 on average. On the CausalBench K562 50-gene perturbation benchmark (D=50, N=8,553), CaPE achieves the strongest directed AUPRC and Top-K precision at moderate and large query budgets, clearly outperforming all baselines by T≥100 queries. On synthetic Erdős-Rényi DAGs (D=20, p=0.5), CaPE consistently leads on average predictive entropy, SHD to ground truth, and expected true-class probability (ETCP) across all query budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaPE is compared against three baselines: random querying (RND), uncertainty sampling on marginal edge entropy (UNC), and a strong non-adaptive static EIG baseline (STE) that ranks all queries once from the initial posterior. On the synthetic and Sachs benchmarks, CaPE outperforms all three baselines on entropy reduction, SHD, and ETCP throughout the elicitation process. On CausalBench, STE is competitive at very small query budgets but saturates quickly and does not benefit from further interaction, while CaPE exhibits monotonic gains in both AUPRC and Top-K precision, clearly surpassing STE by T≥100. Naive uncertainty sampling (UNC) degrades as the budget grows on CausalBench, underscoring the importance of information-theoretic (rather than purely uncertainty-driven) query selection. Under expert-model misspecification (heterogeneous reliability, directional bias, high noise), CaPE remains better than or comparable to the baselines, indicating the gains are not an artifact of a perfectly calibrated synthetic oracle.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaPE sits at the intersection of three established threads: Bayesian causal discovery (posterior over DAGs via MCMC, bootstrap, or variational methods), expert-augmented structure learning (Heckerman et al., 1995; Meek, 1995), and active structure learning (Tong & Koller, 2001; Eberhardt, 2008). The key departure is that prior expert-knowledge approaches treat domain input as static a priori constraints or one-shot priors, while active structure learning methods optimize interventions or data-collection experiments. CaPE instead formulates expert interaction as a sequential, information-theoretic elicitation problem: it applies a BALD-style expected information gain criterion (Houlsby et al., 2011) to select which local edge questions to ask, and updates a particle-based DAG posterior (Doucet et al., 2001; Del Moral et al., 2006) with each noisy categorical response, making the expert channel probabilistic and budget-aware rather than deterministic and fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In domains where interventions are expensive, ethically constrained, or already fixed (e.g., clinical biology, public policy), targeted expert elicitation is often the only additional signal available beyond observational data, and CaPE provides a principled, modular framework for extracting maximum structural information from a limited number of expert judgments. The method's modularity—requiring only posterior samples from any black-box discovery method—makes it broadly applicable, and the identifiability guarantee under non-adversarial feedback provides theoretical grounding. Key limitations include the locality of queries (one variable pair per round), scalability beyond D≈50, and the absence of latent confounding modeling; open questions include adaptive expert-reliability estimation, joint budget allocation between expert queries and interventional data, and whether the EIG criterion can be amortized or approximated for larger graphs.
