## Problem

State-of-the-art uncertainty quantification for free-form LLM generation relies on sampling-based approaches that incur high computational cost and variance, while existing gradient-based UQ methods were designed for classification tasks assuming a single ground-truth label (zero aleatoric uncertainty). This assumption breaks down in free-form generation where multiple valid responses exist, making parameter gradients unreliable. The paper addresses the absence of a sampling-free, gradient-based UQ method that remains valid under high aleatoric uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and the LLM's internal hidden states at a specific 'semantic preserving token' position (identified per model via the Semantic Preservation Score) across the top half of transformer layers. It requires only a single forward and backward pass through the model—no sampling—producing a single scalar uncertainty score per generated response. The score is evaluated via AUROC against BEM-based correctness labels, with the gradient computed with respect to concatenated hidden states rather than model parameters.

## Method

SemGrad computes the L1-norm of the gradient of the entropy-weighted log-likelihood of the generated response with respect to semantic-preserving embeddings (hidden states at the Semantic Preserving Token in the top half of layers), rather than with respect to model parameters. The key insight is that a confident LLM should maintain stable output distributions under small semantic perturbations, so a small gradient in semantic space indicates low uncertainty. The Semantic Preservation Score (SPS) identifies which hidden states best capture input semantics by measuring the alignment gap between semantically equivalent paraphrases and semantically different inputs. Token-level entropy reweighting mitigates linguistic redundancy by down-weighting uninformative tokens. HybridGrad further interpolates between SemGrad and a parameter-gradient variant (ParaGrad) using the average per-token entropy as a balancing weight, leveraging parameter gradients' numerical stability in low-aleatoric settings and SemGrad's robustness in high-aleatoric settings.

## Results

HybridGrad achieves the best average AUROC of 76.59 across three models (Qwen3-Instruct-4B, Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-12B, Llama3.1-Instruct-8B) and three QA datasets (SciQ, TriviaQA, TruthfulQA), outperforming the state-of-the-art sampling-based method SAR (75.33) and the parameter-gradient baseline ExGrad (73.92). SemGrad alone reaches 75.75 average AUROC, with its largest advantage on the high-aleatoric TruthfulQA dataset where it surpasses SAR by +3.27 points and ExGrad by +6.82 points. On single-answer datasets (SciQ, TriviaQA), parameter-gradient methods (ParaGrad, ExGrad) remain competitive, occasionally matching or slightly exceeding SemGrad, motivating the HybridGrad combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art sampling-based method SAR (Duan et al., 2024), SemGrad wins decisively on TruthfulQA (69.06–72.27 vs. 64.44–68.55 AUROC across models) but trails on TriviaQA for Mistral-Nemo (82.37 vs. 85.23) and Llama3.1 (84.72 vs. 85.65). Compared to the parameter-gradient baseline ExGrad (Igoe et al., 2022), SemGrad achieves a +6.82 point average gain on TruthfulQA while ExGrad remains competitive on single-answer SciQ and TriviaQA. Among sampling-based baselines, SemGrad and HybridGrad outperform Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023), Semantic Density (Qiu & Miikkulainen, 2024), INSIDE (Chen et al., 2024), and Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) across nearly all dataset-model pairs, with the largest margins on TruthfulQA where these methods struggle with high aleatoric uncertainty. The token-level baseline G-NLL (Aichberger et al., 2026) and the mutual-information method M.I. (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2024) are consistently outperformed by all three proposed methods. HybridGrad, which interpolates between SemGrad and ParaGrad using sequence-level entropy, achieves the best or near-best AUROC in 8 out of 9 dataset-model cells, confirming that combining semantic and parameter gradients yields the most robust uncertainty estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends gradient-based uncertainty quantification from classification (ExGrad, Igoe et al. 2022; Lee & AlRegib 2020) to free-form LLM generation by shifting the gradient computation from parameter space to semantic space. The key conceptual departure is that prior gradient methods assume a single ground-truth label (zero aleatoric uncertainty), an assumption that breaks down when multiple valid responses exist. Rather than sampling multiple outputs to approximate the output distribution (as Semantic Entropy, Semantic Density, and SAR do), the paper computes a single gradient with respect to identified semantic-preserving hidden states, making it sampling-free. The Semantic Preservation Score (SPS) is a new diagnostic tool that has no direct analogue in prior UQ literature, serving to locate which layer and token position best encodes input semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first sampling-free gradient-based UQ method for free-form generation, offering a computationally efficient alternative to the dominant sampling-based approaches (which require K forward passes and auxiliary models for semantic comparison). The finding that parameter gradients become unreliable under high aleatoric uncertainty while semantic gradients remain stable is a useful practical guideline for practitioners choosing UQ methods. Limitations include the white-box requirement (inapplicable to closed-source APIs), the focus on claim-level/short answers rather than long-form generation, and implementation overhead from the transformers library that prevents computing gradients at a single token position. Open questions include whether the semantic-preserving token concept generalizes across architectures and whether the method's advantage persists on longer, more complex outputs where gradient signals may be diluted.
