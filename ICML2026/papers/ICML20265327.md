## Problem

Existing real-time video understanding models face a fundamental tension: proactive models learn when to respond but generate long, high-latency answers once triggered, while real-time streaming models achieve low latency but lack explicit control over speaking behavior, leading to excessive talking. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to build a video language model that simultaneously decides *when* to speak and generates *short, clip-level* responses with low latency over extended streaming interactions, instantiated through gaming commentary and player-guidance scenarios.

## Input / Output

At each 1-second time step the model receives a triplet (V_t, Q_t, B_t): a video chunk (decoded at 2 FPS, 420p, with per-frame pixel budgets capped by a total video pixel limit), an optional user query, and environment context containing prior commentary summaries from other assistants. The output is a single utterance segment U_t constrained to roughly 1 second of speech, produced either as short commentary text or a fixed silence placeholder, with the generated segment automatically appended to the KV cache as context for the next step. A binary speak/silence decision is made per step via a thresholded score, decoupling the timing decision from text generation.

## Method

Proact-VL builds on a LiveCC-7B-Base backbone and introduces three key components. First, a chunk-wise schema discretizes the stream into 1-second windows, each producing a 1-second utterance, with a persistent transformer KV cache maintaining full temporal context and a dual-cache sliding-window mechanism (evicting the oldest 20% of streaming tokens with reverse-RoPE positional correction) enabling unbounded inference. Second, a lightweight proactive mechanism inserts a semantic-free <|FLAG|> token at the end of each user message; the hidden state at this position (from the penultimate layer) is mapped through a gated MLP and sigmoid to a speaking probability, thresholded at τ to trigger or suppress generation. Third, a multi-tier training objective combines standard causal LM loss on assistant tokens with a response loss that includes transition-aware weighted binary cross-entropy (upweighting rare state-switch steps by γ=5) and a stability regularizer enforcing local temporal smoothness of the probability curve plus global speaking-rate matching to the human baseline, jointly optimizing what to say and when to speak.

## Results

On the Live Gaming Benchmark (overall), Proact-VL achieves CC=49.23, LiveU=6.52, and FinalQ=5.03 for text quality, and TimeDiff=1.71s, PAUC=18.10, and F1=64.87 for response timing. These results surpass the strongest offline baselines (GPT-4o: CC=39.42, LiveU=4.62, F1=54.88; Gemini 2.5 Pro: LiveU=4.70, F1=49.23) and all prior proactive and real-time streaming models. On the out-of-domain Ego4D and Black Myth: Wukong splits, Proact-VL further leads with CC=63.43/55.21 and F1=45.82/60.06 respectively, confirming generalization beyond the training games.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest offline baselines, Proact-VL outperforms GPT-4o on CC (49.23 vs. 39.42), LiveU (6.52 vs. 4.62), FinalQ (5.03 vs. 4.80), TimeDiff (1.71s vs. 3.07s), and F1 (64.87 vs. 54.88), though GPT-4o retains a slight edge on PAUC (23.57 vs. 18.10). Compared to Gemini 2.5 Pro, Proact-VL leads on CC, LiveU, FinalQ, TimeDiff, and F1 while matching PAUC almost exactly (18.10 vs. 18.02). Among proactive models, Proact-VL dominates by a wide margin: VideoLLM-online, MMDuet, and LiveStar all score below 20 on CC and below 7 on F1, whereas Proact-VL reaches 49.23 and 64.87 respectively, indicating that prior proactive methods suffer from severe temporal misalignment and over-long triggered responses. Against real-time baselines, Proact-VL surpasses LiveCC-7B-Base (CC 49.23 vs. 38.88; F1 64.87 vs. 36.10) and LiveCC-7B-Instruct (CC 49.23 vs. 28.35; F1 64.87 vs. 56.05), and StreamingVLM (CC 49.23 vs. 14.89; F1 64.87 vs. 50.67), confirming that the proactive response head adds substantial value over pure streaming generation. On the out-of-domain Ego4D split, Proact-VL achieves CC=63.43 and F1=45.82, beating GPT-4o (CC=50.00, F1=16.36) and all other baselines, while on Black Myth: Wukong it reaches F1=60.06, marginally above LiveCC-7B-Instruct (59.88) and GPT-4o (58.24).

## Relation to Prior Work

Proact-VL sits at the intersection of two existing lines of streaming video work: proactive models (VideoLLM-online, MMDuet, LiveStar) that learn when-to-speak policies but generate long, high-latency responses, and real-time models (LiveCC, StreamingVLM) that produce low-latency outputs but lack explicit control over speaking behavior. The paper builds directly on LiveCC's one-second chunk-wise streaming architecture and VideoLLM-online's interleaved video-text paradigm, but departs from both by introducing a lightweight gated-MLP response head on a dedicated FLAG token to decouple the timing decision from text generation, combined with a transition-aware multi-tier loss that explicitly regularizes state-switching dynamics. The reverse-RoPE sliding-window KV-cache mechanism for unbounded inference is a practical engineering contribution that extends standard RoPE-based context management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 561-hour Live Gaming Dataset and its accompanying benchmark fill a notable gap in resources for evaluating proactive, real-time video interaction, and the framework's demonstrated portability across Qwen2-VL through Qwen3-VL backbones suggests broad applicability beyond gaming. However, the evaluation is confined to gaming commentary and guidance scenarios, leaving open whether the proactive mechanism generalizes to more complex multi-agent or open-domain companion settings. The relatively simple response head (single token + 2-layer MLP) achieving competitive results against more elaborate policy-based approaches raises the question of whether added architectural complexity in prior proactive models was necessary. A key limitation is that the CC win-rate metric is anchored to a single reference model (Gemini 2.5 Pro), and the overall speaking-rate constraint in the loss is treated as a fixed constant rather than being adaptive to content.
