## Problem

All existing model soups require supervised fine-tuning to create their ingredients, limiting the diversity of mixable models and preventing the use of unlabeled data sources such as shifted test distributions or new domains. This paper asks whether ingredients that differ in their self-supervised training runs (different SSL algorithms, hyperparameters, or data) can still be mixed into a single model, and whether doing so yields practical gains in robustness and transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261183/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, variable resolution for VTAB tasks) and produces class labels. A key distinction is that the soup can be formed at two stages: after supervised fine-tuning (producing a standard classifier) or directly after SSL inter-training (producing a representation model evaluated via kNN). In the Self-Seasoning variant, no task labels are consumed at all—mixture coefficients are learned purely from unlabeled training data by minimizing kNN entropy, and predictions are made by nearest-neighbor lookup in the mixed embedding space.

## Method

Self-Soupervision creates ingredients by self-supervised inter-training from a shared pre-trained stock (ViT-B), varying the SSL algorithm (MAE, MoCoV3, MMCR, LeJEPA), algorithmic hyperparameters (masking ratio, temperature, loss configuration), and/or the unlabeled data distribution (e.g., corrupted test data for robustness). These diverse ingredients are then optionally fine-tuned with labels and mixed via uniform averaging, greedy search, or ensemble. The paper's central empirical finding is that linear mode connectivity holds between ingredients differing in their SSL runs—a previously unverified condition—making such mixing valid. For fully unsupervised soups, the authors introduce Self-Seasoning, which optimizes M mixture coefficients by gradient descent to minimize the entropy of in-batch kNN predictions, requiring no labels at any stage. The framework also enables inter-training on shifted/unlabeled test distributions (e.g., ImageNet-C corruptions) before fine-tuning back on labeled training data, yielding +3.5% and +7% robustness gains on ImageNet-C and LAION-C respectively.

## Results

On ImageNet, Self-Soup (uniform mix) achieves 32.34% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-C and 22.92% on LAION-C, outperforming supervised soups by +1.4% and +0.9% respectively on corruption benchmarks while matching clean accuracy (79.09% IN-Val). Inter-training ingredients on unlabeled corrupted test data (ImageNet-C or LAION-C) before fine-tuning back on ImageNet-Train yields the largest gains: +3.5% on ImageNet-C and +7% on LAION-C. On the 21-task VTAB benchmark (336 test sets including 15 corruption types), Self-Soup with uniform mixing achieves 35.1% mean top-1 accuracy, the best among all soup variants, with the largest improvements on natural datasets (+1.4% over supervised soup) and blur/weather/digital corruptions (+2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Supervised Model Soups (Wortsman et al., 2022), Self-Soup with uniform mixing wins on both corruption benchmarks: 32.34% vs. 30.91% on ImageNet-C (+1.43%) and 22.92% vs. 22.05% on LAION-C (+0.87%), while matching clean ImageNet-Val accuracy (79.09% vs. 79.12%). Against the Continued SSL + Supervised Soup baseline (which inter-trains with SSL but only mixes ingredients from a single SSL run), Self-Soup achieves +0.9% on ImageNet-C (32.34% vs. 31.44%) and +1.42% on LAION-C (22.92% vs. 21.50%), confirming that mixing ingredients from *different* SSL algorithms is more beneficial than mixing multiple fine-tunings from one SSL run. Against Model Ratatouille (Ramé et al., 2023), which inter-trains on auxiliary *labeled* datasets, Self-Soup matches or slightly exceeds on all three metrics (79.09% vs. 79.05% IN-Val, 32.34% vs. 32.20% IN-C, 22.92% vs. 22.89% LAION-C) without requiring any extra labeled data. The paper also shows Self-Soup outperforms an ensemble of its own ingredients on corruption benchmarks (32.34% vs. 31.41% on IN-C), meaning the single mixed model is more robust than running all ingredients at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model soups framework (Wortsman et al., 2022) and its inter-training generalization via Model Ratatouille (Ramé et al., 2023) by replacing the supervised inter-training stage with self-supervised learning. While prior soups varied ingredients only through optimization hyperparameters or auxiliary labeled datasets, Self-Soupervision lets ingredients differ in their SSL algorithm (MAE, MoCoV3, MMCR, LeJEPA), SSL hyperparameters, and unlabeled training data. The mixing step is also extended: the authors introduce Self-Seasoning, an unsupervised analogue of Seasoning (Croce et al., 2023) that optimizes mixture coefficients by minimizing kNN prediction entropy rather than supervised cross-entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clearest when unlabeled target-distribution data is available: inter-training on corrupted test data yields +3.5% on ImageNet-C and +7% on LAION-C without any labels, a meaningful robustness gain at zero additional inference cost. The demonstration that linear mode connectivity holds across fundamentally different SSL objectives (reconstruction, instance-contrastive, dimension-contrastive) opens a new axis of ingredient diversity that was previously unavailable. However, in settings without access to shifted data, gains over supervised soups are modest (~1%), and the approach still requires multiple training runs, so the compute overhead is non-trivial. Open questions include whether the findings generalize to language models, other modalities, and the dozens of SSL algorithms not tested here.
