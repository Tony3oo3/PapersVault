## Problem

Existing cross-domain recommendation (CDR) methods operate in Euclidean embedding spaces, which fundamentally mismatch the power-law, hierarchical structure of real-world user-item interactions. This geometric distortion makes it mathematically ill-posed to disentangle domain-invariant user preferences from domain-specific interests, severely limiting transferability in low-overlap and cold-start scenarios where tail users are compressed near the origin and become indistinguishable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary user-item interaction matrices (R ∈ {0,1}^{|U|×|V|}) from multiple domains with no requirement for user overlap across domains, and produces top-N item recommendations scored by a Fermi-Dirac decoder that measures geodesic distance on the Poincaré ball. A continuous similarity matrix S ∈ [0,1]^{|U|×|V|} is maintained alongside the binary matrix for the pseudo-labeling cycle, and the adaptive curvature c_d per domain is learned jointly with all other parameters.

## Method

HVAE redesigns the entire CDR pipeline to operate natively within the n-dimensional Poincaré ball, assigning each domain a learnable adaptive curvature c_d to accommodate heterogeneous data densities. The encoder maps user interactions to hyperbolic embeddings and parameterizes two wrapped normal posteriors—domain-invariant z_inv and domain-specific z_spec—whose separation is enforced by an analytically approximated 2-Wasserstein distance (O(n) complexity) computed as geodesic mean distance plus tangent-space standard-deviation distance. A Hyperbolic Wasserstein Barycenter, computed via a closed-form gyrovector-space midpoint formula, aggregates the per-domain invariant posteriors into a single globally consistent distribution, while an adversarial discriminator trained on z_spec (and confused by z_inv) further enforces domain invariance. Reconstruction uses a Fermi-Dirac decoder measuring manifold distance, and a cycle-pseudo-labeling mechanism iteratively blends observed interactions with model predictions to handle extreme sparsity (<1% overlap). The paper also derives a manifold-constrained generalization error bound showing that negative curvature implicitly regularizes the Rademacher complexity term, tightening the transfer gap.

## Results

On the MYbank industrial dataset at extreme 1% user overlap, HVAE achieves improvements of 3.5%–10.46% (H@10) and 4.31%–15.16% (N@10) over the second-best baseline across all three domains. On the Amazon dataset at the same 1% overlap, gains reach 5.54%–13.07% (H@10) and 5.3%–13.42% (N@10). In cold-start settings on MYbank, HVAE yields an average improvement of 3.13% (H@10) and 5.53% (N@10). In a 7-day online A/B test on Ant Group's platform with ~10M users per domain and 1% cross-domain overlap, HVAE delivers a 7.453% average CTR lift over all deployed baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVAE is compared against four established CDR baselines: GA-DTCDR (Zhu et al., IJCAI 2020) and CCDR (Xie et al., KDD 2022) for dual-domain settings, and GA-MTCDR (Zhu et al., TKDE 2021) and HeroGraph (Cui et al., RecSys Workshop 2020) for multi-domain settings. HVAE consistently outperforms all four baselines across every overlap ratio (1%, 30%, 70%), both datasets, and both metrics (H@10, N@10), with the performance gap widening monotonically as overlap decreases. The largest margins appear at 1% overlap, where Euclidean-based baselines suffer most from the geometry-distribution mismatch on long-tail users. In the online A/B test, HVAE also surpasses single-domain methods BPR and LightGCN, confirming that cross-domain hyperbolic transfer provides value even when individual domains have abundant data. The ablation study further confirms that replacing the hyperbolic space with Euclidean space causes the single largest performance drop, validating that the geometric advantage over all four baselines is the primary driver of HVAE's superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VAE-based cross-domain recommendation (SA-VAE, VDEA, DIDA-CDR) by relocating the entire variational disentanglement pipeline from Euclidean to hyperbolic space, departing from prior work that treats hyperbolic geometry as a mere embedding plugin (e.g., HGCF). The Wasserstein barycenter mechanism for aggregating domain-invariant representations draws on Ungar's (2008) gyrovector-space midpoint formula, while the generalization error bound is adapted from Redko et al.'s (2017) optimal-transport domain adaptation framework. The key conceptual departure is identifying the Euclidean geometry-distribution mismatch (power-law interactions in polynomial-expanding space) as the root cause of poor transfer, rather than addressing it through additional alignment losses or graph-based sharing modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that native hyperbolic VAE training outperforms strong baselines even at 1% user overlap and validating with a 7-day online A/B test at Ant Group (~10M users per domain), the paper provides practical evidence that geometric inductive bias can substitute for the heavy user-overlap assumptions that have constrained CDR. Open questions include whether the O(n) Wasserstein approximation remains tight in higher dimensions, how the adaptive curvature mechanism scales beyond three domains, and whether the pseudo-labeling cycle introduces bias in extremely imbalanced industrial settings. The framework also raises the broader question of whether other non-Euclidean manifolds (e.g., Lorentz model for numerical stability) could further improve long-tail representation fidelity.
