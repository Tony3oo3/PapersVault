## Problem

Current VLM evaluations focus almost entirely on accuracy (object recognition, question answering), yet vision-language agents increasingly make preference-based visual decisions at scale—choosing what to buy, hire, or recommend. We know little about the structure of their visual preferences: which presentation features systematically shift their choices, and how these sensitivities compare to human ones. This paper asks whether such visual decision biases can be systematically discovered, quantified, and interpreted through controlled image-editing interventions.

## Input / Output

The method takes original images (product photos from ABO, house exteriors, StyleGAN-Human portraits, hotel rooms; all upscaled to 1:1 aspect ratio via Nano Banana) paired with task-specific instructions (e.g., 'choose the better product'), and produces optimized edited images, head-to-head choice probabilities, and hierarchical visual preference themes. Crucially, the optimization target is not pixel values but a text editing prompt that parameterizes an image-editing operator, with identity constraints ensuring the underlying object remains recognizable. Evaluation uses pairwise Bradley-Terry-style comparisons with order counterbalancing and consistency checks rather than scalar scoring, and the interpretability output is a multi-level theme hierarchy derived from per-image change descriptions.

## Method

The framework treats the VLM's decision function as a latent visual utility landscape explored through iterative prompt-mediated image edits: an LLM proposes editing instructions based on judge feedback, an image generation model (Nano Banana) applies them, and a panel of VLM judges evaluates the result via pairwise comparisons. Three optimization algorithms are compared—VisualTextGrad (adapting TextGrad's gradient-as-feedback to visual prompts), VisualFeedbackDescent (adapting Feedback Descent's proposal-and-evaluation loop with patience-based stopping), and the novel Competitive Visual Prompt Optimization (CVPO), which frames optimization as a tournament between two candidates judged by k judges with order-reversal consistency checks, generating K challengers per round and terminating when votes approach 50/50 equilibrium. A Matryoshka auto-interpretability pipeline recursively embeds, clusters, and summarizes per-image visual change descriptions into hierarchical themes, which are then validated causally by applying them as zero-shot 'distilled' edits. An image normalization mitigation strategy instructs a model to even out task-irrelevant visual properties before judgment, partially reducing but not eliminating the sensitivity to optimized edits.

## Results

Zero-shot image edits (no optimization) already increase VLM selection probability by 0.2–0.4 over original images across all four tasks (products, houses, people, hotels), with all contrasts statistically significant. Iterative optimization via CVPO and VFD yields an additional +0.1–0.3 absolute gain in choice probability beyond zero-shot. In head-to-head comparisons of final optimized images across 9 frontier VLMs, CVPO achieves the highest mean choice probability (0.672–0.771), followed by VFD (0.561–0.676), while VTG lags far behind (0.131–0.310). Human studies (N=154) confirm that optimized images also significantly shift human choices, with final optimized versions selected ~66–68% of the time versus ~29–30% for originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three visual prompt optimization methods—CVPO (proposed), VFD (adapted from Feedback Descent), and VTG (adapted from TextGrad)—in head-to-head image comparisons judged by 9 frontier VLMs. CVPO outperforms VFD on 7 of 9 models by 0.04–0.21 in choice probability (significant at p<.0001 for 6/7 contrasts), with the notable exception of Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5, where VFD slightly edges out CVPO (0.676 vs. 0.537 and 0.603 vs. 0.594, respectively). CVPO dominates VTG by a wide margin on all 9 models (0.46–0.64 difference, all p<.0001), reflecting VTG's lack of early stopping and its reliance on a single LLM critic rather than competitive pairwise evaluation. In terms of efficiency, CVPO uses only 36.9% of the iteration budget (17.4 average iterations) versus 74.6% for VFD and 100% for VTG, though CVPO generates more images per iteration. Human head-to-head results (Table 2) show a similar but weaker and non-significant ordering, with CVPO leading on 2/4 tasks (houses: 0.524, people: 0.556) while VTG leads on the other two (hotels: 0.532, products: 0.538).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedback-driven prompt optimization paradigm (TextGrad, Feedback Descent) from the textual to the visual domain, with the key distinction that the objective is a preference-based choice function (Bradley-Terry style) rather than a scalar score. It also draws on auto-interpretability techniques (Bills et al., 2023; Perez et al., 2023) to explain discovered visual themes, and positions itself explicitly against both accuracy-focused VLM benchmarks and adversarial example literature by studying perceptually salient, naturalistic edits rather than imperceptible perturbations. The novel CVPO method introduces a competitive tournament structure absent in prior prompt optimization work, while concurrent efforts (Maestro, MPO) explore similar multimodal extensions from different angles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that zero-shot image edits alone shift VLM choice probabilities by 0.2–0.4 across nine frontier models and four realistic tasks, and that this effect persists (partially) even after normalization mitigation, raises concrete governance concerns for image-based AI agents making consequential decisions (hiring, purchasing, real estate). The convergence of three distinct optimization methods on overlapping visual themes (warm lighting, biophilic elements, lifestyle contexts) suggests these reflect stable properties of VLM decision-making rather than method-specific artifacts. Open questions include whether full mechanistic explanations exist for these preferences, whether more sophisticated defenses can fully resolve the sensitivity, and how findings generalize to long-horizon, multi-image agentic workflows.
