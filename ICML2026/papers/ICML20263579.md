## Problem

LLMs are increasingly deployed as decision-making agents but were not trained for this purpose, and prior studies show they perform poorly even in canonical online DM tasks due to inadequate exploration-exploitation behavior. Existing post-training approaches either distill known expert algorithms (requiring pre-specified action spaces, horizons, and reward formats) or enforce manually designed reasoning templates, limiting generalizability. This paper asks whether the regret metric—a universal, environment-agnostic measure of online DM performance—can serve as a self-contained training signal to elicit better decision-making without relying on any pre-defined expert algorithm.

## Input / Output

For language-grounded LLM experiments, the model receives a natural language scenario description (spanning domains like healthcare, business strategy, and science fiction) concatenated with the dialogue-form interaction history (past actions and observed rewards), and produces a natural language response containing both a chain-of-thought reasoning rationale and an explicit policy (a probability vector over actions for FOL, or a single action for MAB/NS-MAB). For the numerical Transformer experiments, input is the raw reward history vector and output is a policy in either the probability simplex or an ℓ₂-ball. The language-based output is parsed to extract the actionable policy, and the reasoning text is retained as part of the training signal during SFT.

## Method

ITERATIVE RMFT is a self-improving post-training loop: at each iteration, L trajectories are sampled from the current model under M language-grounded scenarios, each trajectory's cumulative regret is computed (using privileged environment information available only during training), the top-k lowest-regret trajectories are selected, and the model is fine-tuned via supervised fine-tuning (cross-entropy for LLMs, ℓ₂-distance for Transformers) on those trajectories. Unlike algorithm distillation, no expert algorithm is required; unlike reward-based RL fine-tuning, the selection signal is cumulative regret rather than per-step reward, which naturally incentivizes exploration. The approach operates entirely in language space, so it does not require pre-specified numeric formats and is compatible with closed-weight model training APIs. A theoretical result shows that in a simplified single-layer linear attention setting, this iterative self-imitation provably converges to FTRL with ℓ₂-regularization, providing formal justification that no-regret behavior can emerge from imitating low-regret trajectories.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B in the MAB environment (trained at T=25, evaluated at T=100), ITERATIVE RMFT achieves an average regret of 22.37, substantially outperforming algorithm distillation with matched generation budget (AD_IML: 33.44) and twice the budget (AD_2IML: 32.97), as well as both GRPO variants (35.88 and 40.09). On Gemma-2-9b-it in the FOL environment (T=50), the trained model reduces maximum regret from 137.19 to 93.08 (Gaussian), 227.48 to 122.45 (Uniform), and 186.81 to 139.79 (Sine-trend), while also achieving sublinear regret growth (β̂ < 1) across all reward processes. On GPT-4o mini in the FOL environment (T=25), the trained model reduces maximum regret from 70.82 to 39.65 under Uniform rewards and from 40.62 to 39.64 under Sine-trend rewards, approaching the FTRL reference (39.15 and 38.09 respectively). Theoretically, the single-layer linear attention architecture trained via ITERATIVE RMFT provably converges to FTRL with ℓ₂-regularization and stepsize Θ(1/√(Td)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against algorithm distillation (AD; Laskin et al., 2023), ITERATIVE RMFT (22.37) decisively outperforms AD with the matched selected-data budget (AD_IMk: 58.72), the matched generation budget (AD_IML: 33.44), and even twice the generation budget (AD_2IML: 32.97) on Qwen3-8B MAB at T=100; the paper attributes this to AD's horizon mismatch (UCB's exploration schedule is coupled to history length at T=25) and policy drift from imitating a fixed algorithm. Against GRPO-style baselines (Shao et al., 2024), ITERATIVE RMFT (22.37) beats both GRPO_step (35.88) and GRPO_regret (40.09), suggesting that trajectory-level regret selection is more effective than per-step reward signals for eliciting no-regret behavior. Against classical no-regret algorithms (FTRL for FOL, UCB for MAB, Rexp3 for NS-MAB; Shalev-Shwartz 2007, Auer 2002a, Auer 2002b), the trained LLMs approach but do not surpass these references, which is expected since the classical algorithms take numeric inputs directly without language parsing; the paper explicitly positions these as reference points rather than competitors to be beaten. On the NS-MAB environment (Table 5), the trained GPT-4o mini improves the regret growth rate from 0.96 to 0.91 and reduces SuffFailFreq(0.98T) from 0.32 to 0.18 (LC=GPT, T=100, A=5) compared to the base model, while Rexp3 achieves max(LR) of 560.95 versus 418.87 for the trained model in the same setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of prior work on post-training LLMs for decision-making. First, algorithm distillation approaches (Nie et al., 2025; Laskin et al., 2023) require pre-defined expert algorithms and fixed input/output formats (action space size, horizon, reward dimension), limiting generalizability; ITERATIVE RMFT instead uses regret as a model-agnostic selection criterion over self-generated trajectories, requiring no expert algorithm. Second, RL fine-tuning methods (Schmied et al., 2026) optimize per-step rewards, which the authors argue is not equivalent to regret minimization and may not transfer to adversarial or non-stationary settings. The theoretical connection to Transformer-as-online-learner work (Ahn et al., 2023; Mahankali et al., 2023; Park et al., 2025b) is extended by showing that iterative self-imitation on low-regret trajectories provably recovers FTRL in a simplified linear-attention setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first regret-driven post-training paradigm that is simultaneously model-agnostic (works on numerical Transformers, open-weight LLMs, and closed-weight GPT-4o mini via API), environment-agnostic (FOL, MAB, NS-MAB), and format-agnostic (language-grounded with real-world contexts), without requiring hand-designed reasoning templates or expert algorithms. Key limitations include evaluation on synthetic/procedurally generated environments with short training horizons (T=15–25), leaving open whether the approach transfers to genuinely long-horizon, human-in-the-loop, or structurally richer settings (MDPs, contextual bandits). The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) holds only in a simplified single-layer linear-attention regime with infinite sampling, so the gap to practical multi-layer LLMs remains unaddressed. Future work on combining regret-based training with agentic workflows, safety constraints, and value alignment is explicitly called for.
