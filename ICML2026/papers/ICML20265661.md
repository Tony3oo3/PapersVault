## Problem

Standard MCMC algorithms require evaluating the unnormalized target at each step, which is infeasible for doubly-intractable distributions (where the normalizing constant is intractable) and prohibitively expensive for tall datasets with millions of data points. Existing minibatch solutions (PoissonMH, TunaMH, exchange algorithm) rely on random-walk proposals that mix poorly in high dimensions, and no unified framework connects them. Additionally, correcting the systematic bias of SGLD using only minibatch data remained an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a posterior distribution specified by a prior and per-data-point likelihood, a dataset of N i.i.d. observations (or a doubly-intractable model with a simulator), and hyperparameters controlling minibatch size, step size, and balancing function. Each iteration requires only a minibatch of data (e.g., ~6% of the full dataset) rather than a full pass, with per-step cost scaling linearly in minibatch size. The output is a sequence of posterior samples from which posterior means, credible intervals, or other functionals can be estimated. In the MNIST experiment, inputs are 50 principal components of 28×28 grayscale images for binary classification of digits 3 and 5.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified auxiliary-variable MCMC framework (Algorithm 1) that uses two auxiliary variables: ω₁ to determine the proposal distribution and ω₂ to estimate the target ratio in the acceptance step. This framework subsumes the exchange algorithm, PoissonMH, and TunaMH as special cases, and its validity follows from a detailed-balance argument. Two new gradient-based minibatch algorithms emerge: (1) Poisson-Barker/Poisson-MALA (Case 2, ω₁=ω₂), where a single Poisson minibatch simultaneously estimates the gradient for a locally balanced proposal (Barker or MALA) and the target ratio, and (2) Tuna-SGLD (Case 3, ω₁⊥ω₂), where one minibatch drives an SGLD proposal and an independent Poisson minibatch estimates the acceptance ratio, yielding the first exact Metropolis-corrected version of SGLD. Theoretical analysis via Peskun's ordering and spectral gap bounds (Theorem 1–2) shows the auxiliary chain is at most a constant factor slower than an idealized full-data chain, and yields simpler, sharper convergence guarantees for PoissonMH and TunaMH than previously known.

## Results

On the robust linear regression benchmark (d=10, N=10⁵), Poisson-MALA achieves a best ESS/s of (489.3, 491.8, 496.5) across dimensions, outperforming PoissonMH (99.0, 100.2, 101.1) by 1.80–8.79× and full-batch methods (MH, MALA, Barker, HMC) by over 100×. On the heterogeneous truncated Gaussian (d=20, N=10⁵), Poisson-Barker and Poisson-MALA rank as the top two methods across all acceptance rates, improving ESS/s by 1.37–7.12× over PoissonMH and 13.62–70.12× over random-walk Metropolis. On MNIST Bayesian logistic regression, Tuna-SGLD converges faster than TunaMH for all step sizes tested. Correctness checks via Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics confirm the proposed methods sample from the correct target (max KS ≤ 0.05), while SGLD exhibits its known fixed-step-size bias (max KS = 0.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the existing minibatch method PoissonMH, the proposed Poisson-Barker and Poisson-MALA consistently win on ESS/s across both synthetic benchmarks, with improvements of 1.80–8.79× (robust regression) and 1.37–7.12× (truncated Gaussian). Against full-batch gradient-based methods (MALA, Barker, HMC), all minibatch methods dominate by 10–100× in ESS/s, confirming that the minibatch acceptance-rejection framework is the primary source of speedup. Against SGLD (minibatch without convergence guarantee), the proposed methods match or exceed its early-time MSE reduction while avoiding the systematic bias that causes SGLD to plateau (KS statistic 0.18 vs. ≤0.05 for the proposed methods). On MNIST, Tuna-SGLD outperforms TunaMH in test accuracy convergence speed for all step sizes, with the gap widening at larger step sizes. The only scenario where a baseline is competitive is at very low acceptance rates (0.25) on the truncated Gaussian, where Poisson-MALA underperforms PoissonMH, consistent with MALA's known sensitivity to tuning versus Barker's robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate MCMC algorithms—the exchange algorithm (for doubly-intractable distributions), PoissonMH, and TunaMH (for tall datasets)—under a single auxiliary-variable transition kernel, showing they all satisfy a common detailed-balance condition. It then extends this framework to place auxiliary variables in both the proposal and acceptance-rejection steps, enabling gradient-informed proposals (Barker's proposal, SGLD) within minibatch MCMC while preserving the exact stationary distribution. The theoretical analysis builds on Peskun's ordering and recent spectral gap results for hybrid Gibbs chains (Qin et al., 2025), yielding simpler and stronger convergence bounds for PoissonMH and TunaMH than previously established. The framework is distinguished from pseudo-marginal MCMC by not retaining auxiliary randomness in the chain state, and from exact subsampling for PDMPs by operating in discrete time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing open problem by providing an exact Metropolis-Hastings rejection step for SGLD using only minibatch data, eliminating the known fixed-step-size bias of SGLD while retaining its computational efficiency. The resulting algorithms (Poisson-Barker, Poisson-MALA, Tuna-SGLD) achieve 1.4–70× ESS/s improvements over existing minibatch and full-batch methods across multiple benchmarks, with correctness verified via KS tests. Key limitations include the need for precomputed constants or bounds (M_i, c_i, L, C), restriction to i.i.d. likelihoods, and sensitivity to hyperparameters controlling minibatch size. Future directions highlighted include extending to non-i.i.d. models (spatial, ICA) where minibatch savings could be even more dramatic, and understanding the fundamental source of the large empirical speedups relative to full-data MCMC.
