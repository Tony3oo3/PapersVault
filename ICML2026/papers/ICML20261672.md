## Problem

Existing multimodal chain-of-thought methods perform all reasoning in pure text after a single visual encoding pass, creating a 'vision-blind reasoning' paradigm where the model cannot re-access image features to verify observations, extract fine-grained details, or shift attention to different regions as reasoning evolves. This limits performance especially on tasks requiring multi-step visual consultation, such as geometry and diagram-based mathematical reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261672/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (encoded once into N visual tokens of dimension d by a vision encoder) and a textual query, and produces a final answer accompanied by an interleaved reasoning chain structured by three learnable control tokens: <Think> triggers natural-language reasoning, <Look> triggers a visual grounding step that selects top-k=32 relevant visual tokens, and <Answer> terminates the chain. The number of <Think>-<Look> cycles is adaptive per sample, ranging from zero (degenerating to text-only CoT) to multiple rounds for complex problems, with the attended visual regions shifting across steps to match evolving information needs.

## Method

TVI-CoT augments a standard MLLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) with a dynamic text-visual interleaving mechanism driven by three learnable control tokens that the LLM generates autoregressively. At each <Look> step, a dedicated grounding head computes reasoning-conditioned attention over all visual tokens using the current hidden state as query (α_t = softmax(h_t W_q (v W_k)^T / √d)), selects the top-k=32 tokens, and projects the attended representation back into the LLM's embedding space via a learnable linear projection that is concatenated with the hidden-state context. Training combines standard autoregressive SFT loss on ~150K annotated interleaved reasoning chains (synthesized via Gemini3-Pro from LLaVA-178k, Visual-CoT, and ZebraCoT) with an auxiliary grounding loss (λ=0.1) that supervises attention toward ground-truth bounding-box regions. The key distinction from prior multimodal CoT is that visual features remain accessible throughout reasoning and the model learns when and where to re-attend, rather than relying on a single fixed visual encoding.

## Results

TVI-CoT (built on Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves state-of-the-art results among MLLM-based CoT methods across all eight benchmarks, with headline gains of +6.1% on MMMU (64.3%), +3.8% on MathVerse (60.2%), +3.4% on MathVista (76.6%), and +3.4% on ScienceQA (95.2%) over the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone. On MathVista, TVI-CoT (76.6%) surpasses even Claude-Opus-4.1 (74.5%). The largest category-specific improvements appear in geometry problem solving (+6.3–6.5%) and figure question answering (+7.0%), confirming that iterative visual grounding is most beneficial for spatially demanding tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLM-based CoT methods at comparable scale, TVI-CoT outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark where both report scores: it leads VAPO-Thinker-7B (the strongest prior CoT method) by +4.1 on MMMU, +6.9 on MathVerse, +1.0 on MathVista, +3.2 on ScienceQA, +1.3 on MMStar, +3.3 on AI2D, and +1.3 on MMT-Bench. Against open-source MLLM baselines, TVI-CoT beats InternVL3-8B on all seven overlapping benchmarks (e.g., +11.4 on MathVerse, +5.0 on MathVista) and LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B on all five overlapping benchmarks (e.g., +9.0 on MathVista, +2.1 on MMMU). Against proprietary models, TVI-CoT (8B) trails GPT-5-high and Gemini-2.5-Pro on most benchmarks but surpasses Claude-Opus-4.1 on MMBench (84.7 vs 83.0), MathVista (76.6 vs 74.5), and AI2D (85.6 vs 84.4). The paper also shows that scaling TVI-CoT to Qwen3-VL-32B yields consistent +1.5–2.2% gains over the 32B backbone, confirming the method generalizes beyond the 8B setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TVI-CoT departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal CoT (Visual-CoT, LLaVA-CoT, Vision-R1, VAPO-Thinker) where all reasoning occurs in pure text space after a single visual encoding pass, and from 'thinking with images' approaches (Mint-CoT, V-Thinker, OpenThinkIMG) that rely on external image-editing tools. Its conceptual lineage draws on the standard MLLM architecture (vision encoder + LLM backbone, here Qwen3-VL-8B) and the CoT reasoning framework, but introduces a new mechanism: learnable control tokens (<Think>, <Look>, <Answer>) that let the model dynamically re-attend to cached visual features at arbitrary points in the reasoning chain, without any external tool calls. This positions it as a middle ground—more expressive than text-only CoT, yet lighter-weight than tool-augmented pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete architectural limitation ('vision-blind reasoning') that affects all current MLLMs, and the adaptive interleaving mechanism is a clean, general-purpose module that could be grafted onto other MLLM backbones. Limitations include reliance on ~150K semi-automatically annotated training samples with grounding labels, evaluation only at 8B and 32B scale, and the grounding loss requiring bounding-box annotations that are not always available. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether it composes with RL-based training (e.g., GRPO), and whether the control-token mechanism can be learned without explicit grounding supervision.
