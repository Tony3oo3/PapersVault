## Problem

Existing LLM-based Automated Heuristic Design methods evolve individual operators or components in isolation, failing to capture the dynamic coupling and interdependencies among multiple operators in Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms. Since MOEA performance depends on how operators complement and interact to balance exploration and exploitation, single-operator evolution cannot systematically reason about operator interactions and sequencing effects, leaving a gap in automated multi-operator co-design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263758/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes initial operator code templates (e.g., crossover and mutation operators for operation sequencing and machine assignment in FJSP, or path-based operators for TSP), a set of problem instances for offline evaluation, and an LLM (default: deepseek-chat) as inputs. It produces executable multi-operator combinations integrated into MOEAs (NSGA-II, NSGA-III, MOEA/D) along with associated design strategy prompt tuples. Unusually, the intermediate search operates over a semantic language space of textual design thoughts rather than numerical parameters, and the design is performed offline before online evaluation, with the evaluation budget for design exceeding that of the final MOEA runs.

## Method

E2OC formulates multi-operator optimization as a Markov Decision Process and introduces a four-stage pipeline: (1) warm-start independently evolves each operator and extracts textual design thoughts from elite operators via a prompt generator, building a multi-domain language space; (2) MCTS with UCB selection progressively searches combinations of design thoughts across operators, modeling both internal (same-operator) and external (cross-operator) coupling relationships; (3) operator rotation evolution sequentially replaces individual operators guided by the selected design strategy, analogous to coordinate descent over the operator space; (4) performance feedback (averaged HV/IGD over multiple runs) backpropagates through the MCTS tree to reinforce effective strategies. The framework is modular, supporting integration of different AHD methods (EoH, FunSearch, MCTS-AHD, ReEvo) as the underlying algorithm generator, and demonstrates continuous optimization capability by reusing outputs of one run as inputs for the next.

## Results

On bi-objective FJSP (Brandimarte), E2OC achieves a mean HV of 0.2435 across all instances in NSGA-II, outperforming the best single-heuristic AHD method MCTS-AHD (0.2269) and the best multi-heuristic framework Win-UCB (0.2256). Compared to expert-designed operators, E2OC improves HV by 22.00% (NSGA-II/FJSP), 16.92% (MOEA/D/TSP), and 17.36% (NSGA-II/Tri-FJSP), with IGD reductions of up to 48% (e.g., 1.1579 vs. 2.2487 on Bi-FJSP/NSGA-II). On tri-objective TSP, gains are more modest (3.73%–6.30% RI), reflecting the harder trade-off landscape. E2OC also demonstrates sustained optimization: three consecutive runs yield progressive HV improvements from 0.2435 → 0.2454 → 0.2475 (all instances).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2OC outperforms all eight baselines on every instance split (all, train, test) for both HV and IGD on Bi-FJSP. Against expert-designed operators (NSGA-II, NSGA-III, MOEA/D), E2OC yields 10–22% relative HV improvements on bi-objective problems and 4–17% on tri-objective problems, with the largest gains on FJSP (22.00% for NSGA-II) and the smallest on tri-objective TSP (3.73% for MOEA/D). Among single-heuristic AHD methods, MCTS-AHD is the closest competitor (HV 0.2269 vs. 0.2435 on all instances), while FunSearch leads on the training instance alone (0.1712 vs. 0.1985 for E2OC). Among multi-heuristic co-design frameworks, Win-UCB achieves the best training HV (0.1763) but underperforms E2OC on test and all instances, confirming that E2OC's learned design strategies generalize better rather than overfitting. The purely LLM-driven multi-operator approach performs worst (HV 0.2148), validating the need for structured MCTS-based search over free-form LLM decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLM-based Automated Heuristic Design (AHD) from single-operator evolution (EoH, MCTS-AHD, FunSearch, ReEvo) to the co-design of interdependent multi-operator combinations in MOEAs. It departs from prior single-heuristic methods by introducing a 'design thought' language space extracted from elite operators and searching over combinations of these semantic-level strategies via MCTS, rather than evolving operator code directly. The operator rotation mechanism and the MDP formulation of multi-operator evolution represent a deliberate generalization from the independent, sequential operator optimization assumed by existing AHD frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–22% HV improvements over expert-designed operators across NSGA-II, NSGA-III, and MOEA/D on both FJSP and TSP demonstrate that LLM-driven multi-operator co-design can meaningfully surpass human expertise in MOEA configuration. The continuous optimization capability—where successive E2OC runs progressively enrich the design thought space—suggests a path toward self-improving algorithm design systems. However, evaluation is limited to two problem families and a single default LLM (deepseek-chat), and the offline design paradigm raises open questions about online adaptation, scalability to more than four operators, and integration of human domain knowledge into the search loop.
