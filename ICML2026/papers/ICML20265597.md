## Problem

Large vision-language models remain brittle at multi-image visual causal reasoning (interventional and counterfactual queries) because existing approaches inject causal knowledge purely through textual prompts, leaving causal mechanisms external to model execution. This creates an interface gap where prompt-level supervision cannot be reliably grounded in internal representations, and a supervision gap where causal labels are often missing, partial, or arrive at heterogeneous granularity. The paper argues the bottleneck is the absence of a model-internal interface through which causal supervision can directly shape the representations that drive decoding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1–3 RGB images from the same causal scene (e.g., a pre-intervention and post-intervention pair, or a single scene for counterfactual queries) along with a free-form text query, and produces autoregressive text answers identifying the intervened variable or predicting downstream variable states. Internally, the model induces a directed acyclic graph over up to D=10 latent variable features and generates (D+G) Causal Tokens that are concatenated with visual tokens and query embeddings as the decoder input sequence. A binary variable mask handles samples with fewer than D variables, and the induced DAG is a soft, differentiable adjacency matrix in [0,1]^{D×D} rather than a hard discrete structure.

## Method

BridgeVLM augments a Phi-4-MMI-7B backbone with three internal causal components: (i) learnable variable queries cross-attend to concatenated multi-image visual tokens to extract variable-aligned features; (ii) a low-rank parameterization predicts a routing DAG over these features; and (iii) Route-Aware Message Propagation (RAMP) layers perform direction-aware bidirectional message passing along the DAG to produce Node Tokens, which are then fused with global Graph Tokens via a node→graph→node attention cycle to form Causal Tokens injected into the LLM decoder. The key training contribution is M3S (Multi-Source Signal Supervision), a unified interface that applies heterogeneous causal signals—ground-truth graph edges via BCE, node/edge/global descriptions via symmetric InfoNCE with Hungarian bipartite matching for permutation-invariant variable identity alignment, and NOTEARS-style acyclicity regularization—directly to the induced DAG and Causal Tokens, activating each loss only when its corresponding supervision is available. This design ensures that causal knowledge shapes the same internal representations the decoder attends to during generation, rather than remaining as ungrounded prompt text.

## Results

BridgeVLM-7B achieves 54.4% average accuracy on CausalVLBench intervention-target prediction (vs. 33.2% for the prompt-level causal-trace baseline Phi-4-MMI-7B) and 90.0% on CausalVLBench counterfactual prediction (vs. 84.8%). On Causal3D, it reaches 49.0% intervention accuracy (vs. 43.6%) and 92.3% counterfactual accuracy (vs. 81.0%). The largest single-scenario gain is on CausalVLBench CIRCUIT intervention, jumping from 44.3% to 83.7%, consistent with the need for long-range dependency propagation. Token-level supervision also improves directed-edge F1 for causal graph recovery from 33.4% to 75.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeVLM-7B substantially outperforms all open-source LVLM baselines on intervention-target prediction: it achieves 54.4% average on CausalVLBench vs. 31.9% (LLaVA-OneVision-7B), 29.0% (DeepSeek-VL2-S-16B), and 32.2% (Qwen2.5-VL-32B), and 49.0% on Causal3D vs. 39.8% (LLaVA-OneVision-7B) and 33.8% (DeepSeek-VL2-S-16B). On counterfactual prediction, BridgeVLM reaches 90.0% on CausalVLBench, slightly below Qwen2.5-VL-32B (90.8%) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (90.7%), but far ahead of LLaVA-OneVision-7B (88.5%) and DeepSeek-VL2-S-16B (57.0%); on Causal3D counterfactual it leads all baselines at 92.3% (vs. 65.0% for Gemini-2.0-Flash and 60.1% for LLaVA-OneVision-7B). Against the commercial Gemini-2.0-Flash, BridgeVLM is competitive on CausalVLBench intervention (54.4% vs. 56.4%) and Causal3D intervention (49.0% vs. 33.5%), and nearly matches on CausalVLBench counterfactual (90.0% vs. 90.7%). Compared to its own backbone Phi-4-MMI-7B under prompt-level causal supervision, BridgeVLM gains +21.2 points on CausalVLBench intervention and +5.2 points on CausalVLBench counterfactual, demonstrating that internalizing causal knowledge as Causal Tokens is far more effective than appending it as prompt text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting causal knowledge into VLMs via textual prompts (causal graphs, variable descriptions, rationale traces) by instead internalizing causal structure as a model-internal token interface. Architecturally, it repurposes ideas from set transformers (learnable variable queries), graph message passing (RAMP layers with direction-aware propagation), and NOTEARS-style acyclicity constraints, but combines them into a unified pipeline where an induced DAG routes information between variable-level Node Tokens and global Graph Tokens that the LLM decoder directly attends to. The M3S supervision framework draws on contrastive alignment (InfoNCE) and Hungarian matching from object detection (DETR) to handle heterogeneous and partially missing causal labels, a practical problem prior causal-VLM work largely ignored. Unlike prior causal modeling in vision-language systems (CDC, CLIP-ICM, CausalMM) that use causal formalisms for robustness or hallucination mitigation, BridgeVLM targets intervention and counterfactual prediction as first-class decoding objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that the same causal knowledge yields a 21-point accuracy gap (33.2% → 54.4%) depending on whether it is applied as prompt text or as internal token-level supervision—challenges the common assumption that providing structured causal context in the input is sufficient for VLMs to exploit it. The paper also reveals a subtle but important interaction: edge-level graph supervision (GraphAlign) alone can degrade performance when variable identities are not semantically grounded, suggesting that structural and semantic supervision must be co-designed. Key limitations include reliance on a single 7B backbone (Phi-4-MMI-7B), the induced DAG being a latent proxy rather than a guaranteed causal recovery, and failure to transfer zero-shot to unseen causal scenarios (CELLO). Open questions include whether the Causal-Token interface generalizes to truly novel variable sets and causal structures, whether the approach scales to larger backbones, and whether the moderate latency overhead (~1.8×) is acceptable for real-time applications.
