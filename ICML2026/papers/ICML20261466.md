## Problem

Existing subject customization methods treat objects as 2D patterns rather than 3D volumes, leading to inaccurate poses and inconsistent cross-pose appearances when generating a subject at novel viewpoints. Pose-controllable generation methods either struggle to bridge implicit 3D representations with 2D diffusion models or decouple pose control from subject learning, requiring separate tuning for each objective. The paper asks how to achieve precise, continuous pose control for customized subjects in a unified, tuning-free framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261466/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N reference images (512×512, N=1–4 during training, scalable to 8+ at inference) of a subject at known poses, a target pose specification, and a text prompt. Poses are obtained via automated estimation (COLMAP for dense image sequences, Fast3R for sparse snapshots), which simultaneously yields a 3D point cloud used to compute the oriented volumetric bounding box. The output is a single generated image of the subject rendered at the target pose with scene semantics conditioned on the text prompt, produced entirely tuning-free at test time via multi-step diffusion denoising.

## Method

Pose-ICL is built on FLUX.1-dev and introduces Surface-Anchored Position Embedding (SAPE), which encodes normalized 3D surface coordinates of a volumetric bounding box into a RoPE-style block-diagonal rotation matrix (partitioned into 42/42/44 dimensions for x/y/z) applied to attention queries and keys, so that attention scores are modulated by 3D spatial distance rather than 2D image position. Three refinements ensure compatibility with the DiT backbone: shallow injection restricts SAPE to the first 19 double-stream layers to preserve deep-layer texture coherence; sub-patch position embedding samples 21 surface coordinates across three resolution levels per patch and assigns them to 21 of 24 attention heads for finer geometric resolution; and balanced segment partitioning allocates dimensions equally across the three spatial axes. During training, reference image tokens are concatenated with the target token as in-context context, and the model is optimized with LoRA (rank 16) under a rectified flow loss; at inference, no subject-specific tuning is required—SAPE is simply applied across all diffusion timesteps to maintain pose control throughout generation.

## Results

On the 3D-Assets test set, Pose-ICL achieves a pose MAE of 10.75°, DINO-I of 0.758, CLIP-I of 0.875, and FID of 48.65, outperforming all single-model baselines. On the Real-World test set, it achieves a pose MAE of 13.20°, DINO-I of 0.758, CLIP-I of 0.867, and FID of 47.93, again leading among all compared methods. Notably, Pose-ICL is the only method that is both tuning-free and supports multiple reference images while achieving the best identity consistency (DINO-I) and image quality (FID) on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against subject customization methods (OminiControl, FLUX-Kontext, DreamBooth), Pose-ICL achieves substantially higher DINO-I (0.758 vs. 0.658 for DreamBooth, the strongest competitor) and CLIP-I (0.875 vs. 0.845) on 3D-Assets, and similar margins on Real-World (0.758 vs. 0.680 DINO-I), while also delivering the best FID (48.65 vs. 53.31). Against pose-controllable generation methods, Pose-ICL reduces pose MAE from 33.23° (Custom-Diff360) and 25.70° (SceneDesigner) to 10.75° on 3D-Assets, and from 32.55°/23.88° to 13.20° on Real-World, while simultaneously improving DINO-I by 0.1–0.25 over both. Against editing-based pipelines, Pose-ICL outperforms InstantMesh on Real-World DINO-I by 0.149 (0.758 vs. 0.609) and FID by 22.31 (47.93 vs. 70.24), though InstantMesh achieves a slightly lower pose MAE on 3D-Assets (7.62° vs. 10.75°); Qwen-Edit is dominated on all metrics. Overall, Pose-ICL is the only method that is simultaneously tuning-free, multi-reference, and achieves top or near-top performance across all five metrics on both test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pose-ICL sits at the intersection of two lines of work: subject customization (DreamBooth → OminiControl/FLUX-Kontext's shift from tuning to in-context learning) and pose-controllable generation (Custom Diffusion 360's internal NeRF representations, SceneDesigner's ControlNet-based surface-coordinate injection). The key departure is architectural: rather than bridging 3D geometry to 2D generation through auxiliary branches or joint optimization, Pose-ICL repurposes the backbone's existing RoPE mechanism to encode 3D surface coordinates as position embeddings (SAPE), treating pose control and subject customization as mutually reinforcing objectives within a single tuning-free ICL pass. The sub-patch sampling strategy follows PE-Field, and the shallow-injection finding (only first 19 of FLUX's double-stream layers) is a practical refinement specific to this design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first tuning-free framework combining pose control with subject customization, Pose-ICL removes the per-subject optimization bottleneck that plagues DreamBooth-based pipelines, making pose-controllable personalization practical for interactive applications. The 3D-aware ICL paradigm—where volumetric understanding emerges from position-embedding-guided attention rather than explicit 3D representations—suggests a general recipe for injecting geometric structure into 2D-native transformers. Limitations include restricted applicability to rigid objects, a foreground-background harmony gap inherited from the compositing-based data pipeline, and dependence on accurate pose estimation (though robustness to ±30° noise is demonstrated). Open questions include whether the volumetric bounding box suffices for deformable subjects and whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes with inter-object spatial reasoning.
