## Problem

Portrait Pose Transfer (PPT) requires simultaneously following complex pose/layout modification instructions and preserving fine-grained identity details, but existing RL-based alignment methods for diffusion models use reward functions designed for text-to-image generation (e.g., CLIP-based global similarity) that cannot capture the fine-grained evaluation PPT demands. Additionally, DPO-style approaches still require annotated preference pairs, and GRPO-style methods lack effective multi-objective reward mechanisms for editing tasks, leading to reward hacking where the model optimizes the easier metric at the expense of the harder one.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference portrait image and a free-form textual instruction describing pose, camera angle, and layout changes (e.g., 'shift to full-body shot, model holds basket overhead, add distant mountains in background') and produces a single pose-transferred portrait image at 512×512 resolution. The reference image and instruction are consumed as dual text-image conditioning within the FLUX.1-Kontext flow-matching framework, with LoRA (r=64, α=128) applied for parameter-efficient post-training. During RL, a group of 8 images is sampled per prompt to enable within-group reward comparison.

## Method

PortraitRL is a GRPO-based post-training framework built on FLUX.1-Kontext with two core components. First, a Multi-Objective Reward Mechanism uses two LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL) logit-based score functions—one for detail preservation and one for prompt following—that compute expected scores from the model's probability distribution over discrete rating levels, then applies within-group Z-score standardization to each dimension before weighted summation to prevent reward hacking. Second, NaSPO (Negative-aware Score Preference Optimization) replaces DSPO's paired winner/loser mechanism with within-group relative advantages to identify positive and negative preference samples without annotation, and introduces a dual-preference scoring scheme: a positive score that steers the velocity field toward preferred distributions using a KL-based reference point Q_old, and a negative score that repels from undesirable distributions via geometric reflection of the velocity vector, yielding a contrastive training objective that fully leverages all generated samples.

## Results

On the CHEESE test set, PortraitRL achieves the best Qwen-DP (0.8929) and near-best Qwen-PF (0.8260) among all RL methods, with an average of 0.8595 that surpasses FlowGRPO (0.8461), DiffusionNFT (0.8481), and Uniworld-V2 (0.8464). In the user study, PortraitRL attains the highest human-rated scores on both Detail Preservation (0.9100) and Prompt Following (0.8450). On the ImgEdit zero-shot benchmark, PortraitRL also achieves the highest average score (0.8074) over FlowGRPO (0.7980) and DiffusionNFT (0.7772).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot baselines (IP-Adapter+, EasyRef, Kontext), PortraitRL improves Qwen-PF by 0.277–0.281 and Qwen-DP by 0.234–0.246, confirming that RL post-training substantially outperforms untrained or adapter-only approaches. Against SFT baselines (DB LoRA, SCheese, Kontext+SFT), PortraitRL gains +0.021 over Kontext+SFT on Qwen-DP and +0.033 on Qwen-PF, showing that the RL stage adds meaningful fine-grained improvement beyond supervised fine-tuning. Among RL methods, PortraitRL achieves the highest Qwen-DP (0.8929 vs. DiffusionNFT's 0.8894 and FlowGRPO's 0.8783) and the best average of Qwen-DP and Qwen-PF (0.8595), though Uniworld-V2 edges it out by 0.0011 on Qwen-PF alone (0.8271 vs. 0.8260). On the coarser CLIP-I and DINO-I metrics, DiffusionNFT slightly leads (0.8768/0.8268 vs. 0.8757/0.8239), but the paper argues the LVLM-based metrics are more informative for PPT. On the ImgEdit zero-shot benchmark, PortraitRL achieves the highest average (0.8074), outperforming FlowGRPO (0.7980) and DiffusionNFT (0.7772), suggesting the learned reward generalizes beyond portrait-specific editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

PortraitRL builds directly on the score-preference-optimization lineage: it adapts DSPO's decomposition of the conditional score into a prior plus a preference-guidance term, but replaces DSPO's annotated winner/loser pairs with within-group relative advantages and a KL-based reference point (inspired by Diffusion-KTO). The resulting NaSPO algorithm is explicitly shown in Appendix C.4 to be mathematically equivalent to DiffusionNFT's closed-form solution with the constant hyperparameter β replaced by an adaptive σ(r(c,x_t)), positioning it as an incremental refinement rather than a fundamentally new RL objective. The more distinctive departure is in reward design: instead of the CLIP-based global-similarity rewards used by FlowGRPO and DiffusionNFT, the paper introduces LVLM logit-based scores for fine-grained evaluation of detail preservation and instruction following, combined with within-group Z-score standardization to prevent reward hacking across the two objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-objective reward mechanism with within-group standardization offers a practical, annotation-free recipe for multi-criteria RL in image editing that could transfer to other tasks requiring simultaneous optimization of competing quality dimensions. However, the RL algorithm itself is acknowledged to be an incremental variant of DiffusionNFT, and the empirical gains over strong RL baselines (e.g., Uniworld-V2 on Qwen-PF) are modest. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond portrait editing to broader image-editing or video tasks, whether the Utopia-point instability identified in Appendix B.2 can be resolved to enable more principled multi-objective aggregation, and whether the LVLM-as-reward-model paradigm scales to settings where inference cost is a bottleneck.
