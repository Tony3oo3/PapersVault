## Problem

Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) minimizes marginal entropy over augmented views but implicitly constructs its pseudo label by uniformly averaging predictions, which underweights or excludes the original test image and creates an inconsistency between the optimization and inference stages. The paper asks how to obtain a higher-quality pseudo label that adaptively balances the reliability of weak (original) and strong augmentations, and how to unify the objectives of both stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261930/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabeled RGB test image and a short text prompt (context length 4, template 'a photo of a') as input. It generates 63 AugMix strong augmentations plus the original image as a weak augmentation, producing 64 forward passes per sample. The output is a class probability vector over the CLIP vocabulary; a skip technique bypasses optimization entirely when the weak and strong predictions already agree, reducing average inference time to 0.144 s per image.

## Method

USE reinterprets TPT's marginal entropy objective as a reverse cross-entropy (RCE) loss against a stop-gradient pseudo label, revealing an implicit self-training mechanism. The key innovation is the Self-Ensembling (SE) strategy, which computes an entropy-rank coefficient δ measuring how the weak augmentation's entropy compares to the strong augmentations, rescales it to β ∈ [0.3, 0.7] via a linear mapping, and forms the pseudo label as q(x) = β·p_w(x) + (1−β)·p_s(x). During optimization, all selected views in S are trained to match q(x) via RCE; at inference, the same SE weighting is applied to produce the final prediction, ensuring consistency between both stages. SE also functions standalone as an optimization-free TTA method and can be plugged into existing TTA methods (TPT, C-TPT, R-TPT, STS) as a lightweight inference-time module.

## Results

On ImageNet and its OOD variants (ViT-B/16), USE achieves 64.18% average accuracy and 62.80% OOD average, outperforming the strongest optimization-based baseline STS (63.89% / 62.67%) and the classic TPT (62.37% / 60.74%). On ten fine-grained datasets, USE reaches 65.35% average accuracy, surpassing TPT (64.84%) and all other baselines. The optimization-free SE variant achieves 63.65% on IN&O and 65.02% on FGVC, exceeding most optimization-based methods while requiring no backpropagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USE outperforms all optimization-based baselines on the IN&O average: it beats TPT by 1.81 points (64.18 vs 62.37), C-TPT by 3.09 points, RLCF by 1.26 points, TTL by 0.41 points, TPS by 1.14 points, R-TPT by 1.08 points, and STS by 0.29 points. The sole exception is ImageNet-A, where STS (61.37%) exceeds USE (59.79%) by 1.58 points, which the authors attribute to STS's handling of compromised examples. On fine-grained datasets, USE (65.35%) leads all methods, with the closest competitor being TPT (64.84%), a gap of 0.51 points. The optimization-free SE variant (63.65% IN&O, 65.02% FGVC) surpasses MTA (63.08%) and ZERO (63.80%) on IN&O and outperforms most optimization-based methods including TPT, C-TPT, R-TPT, and STS on FGVC, demonstrating that the self-ensembling strategy alone provides substantial gains without any gradient updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT, Shu et al., 2022), the canonical episodic TTA method for CLIP that minimizes marginal entropy over augmented views. The key conceptual departure is a mathematical decomposition showing that TPT's entropy objective is equivalent to reverse cross-entropy against a stop-gradient pseudo label, reframing the method as implicit self-training. From this lens, the authors identify that the original (weakly augmented) test image is systematically underweighted in the pseudo-label construction, and propose an adaptive self-ensembling (SE) strategy that reweights weak and strong augmentations based on relative entropy rank. The unified framework (USE) further aligns the inference stage with the optimization stage by applying the same SE strategy at test time, a consistency principle absent from prior TTA methods that typically predict only on the weak augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SE module's ability to serve as a lightweight, optimization-free plug-in that consistently improves multiple existing TTA methods (TPT, C-TPT, R-TPT, STS) by 1–3% makes it practically attractive for practitioners who want a drop-in improvement without architectural changes. The clean RCE/pseudo-label decomposition provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding and designing future TTA objectives. However, the method still requires ~64 forward passes per sample and explicitly acknowledges degradation when the original image is severely corrupted, limiting its applicability in heavily corrupted or adversarial settings. Open questions include whether the self-ensembling principle extends to online (streaming) TTA, to non-episodic adaptation, or to other VLM architectures beyond CLIP.
