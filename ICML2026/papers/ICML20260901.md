## Problem

Bowel sound (BS) denoising in clinical GI auscultation is severely degraded by speech interference whose spectral footprint (100–1000 Hz) overlaps with bowel motility bursts. Existing diffusion-bridge denoisers assume Gaussian noise priors, which are fundamentally mismatched with the impulsive, heavy-tailed statistics of real-world speech, and no large-scale clinical dataset captures rare pathological transients (gas-liquid gurgles, metallic transients) needed for robust evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired observations (x₀, y) where y is a 5-second noisy bowel sound clip (16 kHz, mixed with speech at λ=0.5) and x₀ is the corresponding clean recording, both converted to 128-band Mel spectrograms (FFT 1024, hop 256) with per-band zero-mean/unit-variance normalization. The UNet (base 128 channels, 4 resolution levels) predicts the clean endpoint x̂₀ in spectrogram domain, which is then inverse-transformed to the waveform for evaluation. The framework also generalizes to other physiological acoustics (heart sounds, lung sounds) under the same spectrogram interface.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the Gaussian bridge driver in DDBM with an i.i.d. Cauchy(0, r) driver, yielding a forward kernel x_t = a_t·y + b_t·x₀ + c_t·ε where the conditional law q(x_t|x₀,y) is a factorized multivariate Cauchy with location μ_t = a_t·y + b_t·x₀ and scale c_t·r. The paper proves existence of this kernel, derives closed-form log-density and score (which is bounded and redescending, unlike the unbounded Gaussian score), and shows that x₀-prediction induces a valid plug-in score via the Fisher identity. Training combines an L₁ spectrogram fidelity loss, a VGG-Gram structural loss, and a Cauchy negative-log-likelihood consistency penalty that enforces heavy-tailed residual statistics. For sampling, a Gaussian scale-mixture reparameterization (Cauchy = z/|u|) converts the bridge into a conditionally Gaussian process, enabling closed-form DBIM-style refinement updates with a shared random scale κ across timesteps—avoiding reverse-time SDE integration while preserving heavy-tailed marginal behavior.

## Results

On the CLINBS dataset with speech interference, the proposed Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridge achieves a MAE of 8.28, PSNR of 24.39 dB, SSIM of 0.72, LSD of 14.03, and FID of 36.99, outperforming all six baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, DBIM (Gaussian diffusion bridge implicit model), is surpassed by 49.8% on MAE (8.28 vs. 16.49), 26.3% on SSIM (0.72 vs. 0.57), and 44.3% on LSD (14.03 vs. 25.21). For downstream abnormal bowel sound recognition, the method elevates accuracy to 88.01% (ResNet), a 3.6–4.1% gain over the best baseline (DBIM at 84.39%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method dominates all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest generative competitor DBIM (Gaussian diffusion bridge implicit model), the Cauchy-driven variant improves MAE by 49.8% (8.28 vs. 16.49), SSIM by 26.3% (0.72 vs. 0.57), LSD by 44.3% (14.03 vs. 25.21), and FID by 13.4% (36.99 vs. 42.71), confirming that the heavy-tailed Cauchy prior is the key differentiator over the Gaussian bridge. Against DDBM (the original denoising diffusion bridge), gains are 57.5% on MAE and 80% on SSIM, while against the discriminative source-separation baselines (Demucs, Mel-RoFormer, SCNet) the improvements are even larger—MAE drops from 19–39 to 8.28. The bidirectional diffusion bridge BDBM performs worst among generative methods (MAE 34.38, SSIM 0.24), suggesting that bidirectional denoising without a bridge structure is poorly suited to this paired restoration task. On the downstream recognition task (Table 4), the method achieves 88.01% accuracy with ResNet, beating DBIM (84.39%) by 3.6 points and DDBM (82.18%) by 5.8 points, validating that the denoising gains translate into clinically meaningful diagnostic improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (DDBM, Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) and its efficient sampling variant DBIM (Zheng et al., ICLR 2025) by replacing the Gaussian bridge noise driver with a Cauchy distribution, motivated by empirical evidence that speech interference in clinical recordings exhibits heavy-tailed statistics. While Cauchy Diffusion (Lian et al., AAAI 2025) demonstrated the feasibility of heavy-tailed noise in standard diffusion models, this work is the first to establish a full theoretical foundation for Cauchy-driven diffusion bridges—proving kernel existence, deriving closed-form log-density and score expressions, and developing a Gaussian scale-mixture reparameterization that enables DBIM-style closed-form sampling without reverse-time SDE integration. The paper also departs from the broader source-separation literature (Demucs, Mel-RoFormer, SCNet) by framing bowel sound restoration as a paired generative bridge problem rather than a discriminative regression task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CLINBS (25+ hours, expert-verified, including rare pathological transients) addresses a genuine gap in clinical biosignal benchmarks, and the Cauchy bridge formulation provides a principled alternative to Gaussian priors for any denoising task where interference is impulsive or heavy-tailed. However, the evaluation is limited to a single-institution dataset with simulated (rather than naturally occurring) speech interference, and the downstream recognition gains (~3.6–4.1%) are modest relative to the large denoising metric improvements. Open questions include whether the Cauchy assumption generalizes to other non-stationary clinical artifacts (e.g., motion, equipment hum), whether the shared-u sampling strategy remains optimal at higher SNR, and whether the framework scales to longer recordings or multi-channel auscultation without architectural changes.
