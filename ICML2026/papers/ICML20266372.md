## Problem

AI research is saturated with metaphysical questions—consciousness, AGI, intelligence, understanding—that generate unproductive debates because they lack the empirical grounding that makes scientific questions converge on consensus. Neither scientific realism (seeking one 'true' definition) nor quietism (dismissing metaphysics as meaningless word games) provides a productive framework for advancing AI research. The paper identifies the missing piece: a systematic procedure for evaluating metaphysical concepts by the new research directions they open rather than their supposed correspondence to reality.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a metaphysical puzzle or conceptual confusion in AI (e.g., 'Can LLMs be conscious?', 'What is AGI?', 'Do LLMs have a world model?') as input and produces a structured set of reframed empirical research questions, candidate operational definitions, and conceptual clarifications as output. Unlike a computational pipeline, the output is a toolkit of multiple productive formulations rather than a single answer, following Chang's recommendation of conceptual plurality. The framework is designed to be reusable across any metaphysical question in AI, supplying a general procedure rather than a worked instance for one concept.

## Method

The core contribution is the 'productive confusion' two-step framework. Step 1 (Clarify) applies Wittgensteinian family-resemblance analysis and Carnapian explication to categorize the puzzle into one of four diagnostic types: language trap, idle question, family-resemblance concept, or empirically answerable question, each with a distinct recommended response. Step 2 (Invent) uses conceptual engineering (Cappelen) to generate multiple productive reframings of the original puzzle, evaluated by Carnap's criteria of similarity, precision, fruitfulness, and simplicity, with an explicit commitment to plurality over single-best-definition. The framework is grounded in the Pragmatist tradition (Peirce, James, Dewey, Rorty) and explicitly positioned as a middle path between scientific realism and quietism, illustrated through four case studies (Chinese Room, o1 scheming, AGI definition, LLM world models) and operationalized into concrete recommendations for researchers, reviewers, and research program designers.

## Results

This is a position paper with no quantitative experiments, benchmarks, or numerical results. The paper's contribution is a two-step conceptual framework (Clarify → Invent) for navigating metaphysical puzzles in AI, illustrated through qualitative case studies (Chinese Room, o1 scheming, AGI definition, LLM world models) rather than empirical measurements. The 'results' are the reframed research questions and empirical programs each case study generates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare methods against baselines in the standard empirical sense. Instead, it positions its pragmatic framework against two philosophical stances: Scientific Realism (which seeks correspondence to mind-independent truth) and Quietism (which dismisses metaphysical questions as meaningless). The paper argues its approach subsumes the kernel of truth in realism (reality constrains our models) while adding constructive content that quietism lacks (a systematic procedure for transforming confused questions into productive research programs). No head-to-head empirical comparison is performed; the 'comparison' is entirely argumentative, showing how each case study (e.g., the Hendrycks et al. AGI definition yielding ten measurable dimensions) exemplifies the pragmatic approach's productivity relative to purely definitional debates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes a pragmatic stance that has been applied piecemeal to individual AI concepts—Leyton-Brown & Shoham (2024) on understanding, Leibo et al. (2025) on personhood, Nanda et al. (2025) on interpretability—into a reusable two-step framework ('Clarify' then 'Invent') grounded in Carnapian explication, Wittgensteinian family-resemblance analysis, and Cappelen's conceptual engineering. It explicitly positions itself against both scientific realism (which seeks mind-independent truth) and quietism (which dismisses metaphysical questions as idle), arguing that neither provides a productive methodology for AI's live conceptual debates. The philosophical lineage traces through Peirce, James, Dewey, and Rorty, but the paper's distinctive contribution is operationalizing pragmatism as a concrete procedure applicable to any metaphysical question in AI, rather than a single worked instance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers AI researchers, reviewers, and decision-makers a concrete procedure for converting unproductive metaphysical debates (AGI, consciousness, scheming, world models) into empirically tractable research programs, which could reduce the field's tendency toward unresolvable definitional disputes. However, as a position paper it provides no empirical validation that the two-step process actually yields better research outcomes than ad-hoc pragmatic reasoning, and the boundary between 'progress on a puzzle' and 'productive divergence' (Section 8.1d) remains difficult to enforce in practice. Open questions include whether the framework is too permissive—risking a 'anything goes if it's productive' relativism—and whether it can be operationalized consistently across the diverse subfields of AI safety, alignment, and interpretability.
