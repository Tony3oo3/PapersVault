## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods extend image-level magnitude-sensitive similarity metrics (cosine, inner product) to pixel-level alignment, but heterogeneous magnitude distributions between visual and textual encoders cause scale perturbations that destabilize optimization, producing blurred boundaries and fragmented local structures. The paper identifies that the alignment paradigm itself—not merely insufficient supervision—is the bottleneck for fine-grained cross-modal correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of arbitrary text queries as input, with the visual backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B/14 or ViT-L/14 with register tokens) and text encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16) both frozen. It produces per-class activation maps at the ViT patch resolution, which are then refined by the AAM module into pixel-level segmentation masks. Training uses ~80K image-caption pairs from COCO Captions 2014, and only the lightweight projection and refinement modules are optimized.

## Method

PhaseAlign introduces Complex Phase Alignment (CPA), which splits visual and textual channel vectors into real and imaginary halves to form complex-valued representations, then uses the phase difference of their complex ratio—rather than amplitude correlation—as the primary cross-modal similarity signal, making alignment invariant to magnitude perturbations. A Spatial-Aware Cross-Modal Projection (SACP) module applies multi-scale local contrast normalization with scale-attention fusion to the visual branch before alignment, providing structured spatial context that stabilizes phase computation. An Attention-guided Affinity Modeling (AAM) module constructs a symmetric transition matrix from ViT self-attention and performs constrained diffusion of class activation maps within high-confidence regions to sharpen boundaries and fill structural gaps. Training uses a symmetric InfoNCE objective with a hybrid similarity (weighted phase, cosine, and in-batch contrastive terms) while keeping all backbone parameters frozen, optimizing only the projection and refinement modules with AdamW over 100 epochs.

## Results

PhaseAlign achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all major OVS benchmarks. With a DINOv2 ViT-B/14 backbone, it reaches 68.1% mIoU on V21, 42.1% on C60, 39.1% on Object, 88.0% on V20, 29.9% on Stuff, 22.1% on ADE20K, and 40.9% on Cityscapes, yielding an average of 46.4%. With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, it further improves to 46.6% average mIoU, outperforming all prior methods including DSG-OV (44.1%) and ProxyCLIP (44.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhaseAlign (ViT-B) outperforms the previous best method DSG-OV on 7 of 8 reported benchmarks, with the largest margins on Stuff (+4.2 mIoU), Cityscapes (+4.9), and Object (+2.8), while DSG-OV retains a lead on C59 (46.5 vs. 41.3). Against ProxyCLIP, PhaseAlign gains +6.8 mIoU on V21, +6.8 on C60, and +2.1 on average. On the ViT-Large track, PhaseAlign surpasses ProxyCLIP (44.5 avg) by 2.1 points and FreeDA (41.4) by 5.2 points, with particularly strong gains on Stuff (+4.7) and Cityscapes (+3.7). In the controlled comparison (Table 5) where all methods share the same DINOv2-reg backbone and training data, PhaseAlign still leads FreeDA by +1.1 (ADE), +2.3 (City), and +2.8 (Stuff) mIoU, confirming that the gains stem from the phase-alignment mechanism rather than backbone or data differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhaseAlign builds directly on the CLIP contrastive learning paradigm and DINOv2 visual backbones (with register tokens) that underpin most recent OVS methods, but departs from the standard cosine/inner-product similarity by formulating cross-modal alignment in the complex domain using phase differences, inspired by electromagnetic wave interference. It also extends the line of work using ViT self-attention for structural refinement (e.g., SCLIP, DSG-OV) by coupling attention-guided diffusion with an explicit cross-modal semantic constraint rather than treating attention as a generic smoothing prior. The Spatial-Aware Cross-modal Projection module addresses a gap identified in prior token-wise projection heads that ignore local spatial topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies magnitude sensitivity as a concrete, testable bottleneck in pixel-level cross-modal alignment and provides a physically motivated fix (phase invariance) with clear scale-robustness evidence, which could inform alignment design beyond OVS. However, the method still depends on frozen pretrained encoders and introduces moderate computational overhead from the affinity diffusion step, and the authors note it has not been extended to 3D scene understanding or domain-shifted settings. An open question is whether the complex-domain formulation generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., panoptic or instance segmentation) where magnitude heterogeneity between modalities is equally problematic.
