## Problem

When a pretrained diffusion model serves as a surrogate forecaster in data assimilation, the standard practice of guiding the diffusion sampler with the observation likelihood does not in general produce samples from the true filtering posterior, because the guidance is an approximation of the intractable Doob h-transform. This introduces systematic bias that existing methods cannot quantify or correct, leaving a gap between what guided diffusion sampling actually produces and the desired observation-conditioned posterior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained conditional diffusion surrogate (U-Net or MLP drift model), a conditioning history of L previous states (L=3 for Lorenz, L=10 for Navier-Stokes, L=6 for weather), and a single noisy partial observation y_{t+1} (e.g., only the x-coordinate for Lorenz, 5–10% of spatial grid cells for NS/weather). It outputs an equally-weighted particle ensemble of N corrected state trajectories approximating the posterior p(x_{t+1}|y_{1:t+1}), operating entirely at inference time with no retraining of the surrogate. The resampling is performed on full diffusion-time paths (trajectories in C([t,t+1], R^D)) rather than on endpoint states, which is the key structural difference from classical particle filters.

## Method

SURGE casts the one-step diffusion transition as a path measure and applies Sequential Monte Carlo directly on diffusion trajectories. The core mechanism is a Girsanov change-of-measure: the Radon-Nikodym derivative between the uncontrolled reference path measure and the guided path measure yields an importance weight that exactly cancels the bias introduced by the approximate guidance drift, making the estimator asymptotically unbiased for the true posterior. The per-step weight decomposes into a reward term (progressive incorporation of the observation likelihood, shown to be an exact telescoping factorization of the terminal likelihood) and a Girsanov correction term (the stochastic integral and quadratic-variation penalty of the guidance). To control weight variance in high dimensions, the likelihood is distributed across K sub-steps of the internal diffusion time rather than applied as a single large update at the endpoint, and multinomial resampling is triggered when the effective sample size falls below a threshold. The entire procedure is training-free and plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing guided diffusion-based DA method (FlowDAS, SDA) at inference, requiring only the guidance gradient and the observation likelihood to be available.

## Results

On the Lorenz 1963 system, FlowDAS+SURGE achieves RMSE 0.0502 and W1 0.0363, outperforming the FlowDAS baseline (0.0545, 0.0388) by ~8% and ~6% respectively. On the 2D Navier-Stokes super-resolution task (8²→128²), FlowDAS+SURGE reduces KES-RE from 0.401 to 0.317 (21% improvement) and RMSE from 1.018 to 0.851. On the SEVIR weather forecasting task with only 10% sparse observations, FlowDAS+SURGE achieves RMSE 0.0513 and CSI(τ₄₀) 0.4541, improving over FlowDAS (0.0657, 0.4044) by 22% and 12% respectively. SURGE consistently improves both SDA and FlowDAS backbones across all three tasks without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURGE is evaluated as a plug-and-play inference-time enhancement on top of two diffusion-based backbones (SDA and FlowDAS) and compared against four baselines: Bootstrap Particle Filter (BPF, N=20), Ensemble Kalman Filter (ENKF), Score-based Data Assimilation (SDA), and FlowDAS. SURGE wins on every metric across all three tasks (Lorenz, Navier-Stokes, SEVIR) against all four baselines. Against the strongest baseline FlowDAS, SURGE reduces RMSE by 7.9% on Lorenz, 16.4% on Navier-Stokes super-resolution, and 21.9% on weather forecasting, while also improving W1, KES-RE, and CSI metrics. Against SDA, the gains are even larger (e.g., RMSE drops from 0.0589 to 0.0555 on Lorenz, and from 0.3511 to 0.0925 on weather forecasting), indicating that SURGE's Girsanov-corrected resampling is particularly effective when the underlying guidance is less refined. Classical filters (BPF, ENKF) are consistently outperformed, confirming that the diffusion surrogate's learned dynamics combined with SURGE's path-level reweighting surpasses Gaussian-approximation and simple likelihood-reweighting approaches in high-dimensional chaotic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURGE builds directly on the diffusion-surrogate data assimilation line initiated by FlowDAS (Chen et al., 2025b) and SDA (Rozet & Louppe, 2023), both of which inject observation likelihoods into the generative process via guidance terms. The key departure is conceptual: rather than accepting that approximate guidance yields biased samples, SURGE treats the guided diffusion as a proposal on path space and applies a Girsanov change-of-measure to compute exact importance weights, recovering the true filtering posterior in the large-particle limit. This connects the diffusion-DA literature to classical sequential Monte Carlo and to the broader inference-time scaling literature for diffusion models (Wang et al., 2026; Ren et al., 2025a), where reweighting and resampling on trajectory distributions has recently emerged as a training-free correction mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURGE is the first method to provide a rigorous approximation-free guarantee for diffusion-surrogate data assimilation, and it does so as a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time module that can be layered onto any existing guided diffusion DA pipeline. This is practically significant because it decouples the quality of the learned surrogate from the correctness of the posterior: the surrogate only affects proposal efficiency, not the target distribution. However, the paper honestly identifies that performance remains coupled to surrogate stability (failure near the Lorenz null-isocline), that particle counts are small (N=3–4), and that ensemble averaging introduces smoothing in high-frequency Navier-Stokes fields. Open questions include adaptive resampling thresholds, multi-scale guidance, and whether the framework scales to substantially higher particle counts without the ESS collapse observed at N=100.
