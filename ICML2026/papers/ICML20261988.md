## Problem

Constrained Bayesian Optimization (CBO) for high-dimensional problems suffers from the curse of dimensionality: global CBO regret bounds scale exponentially with dimension, rendering them impractical beyond ~20 dimensions. Existing local trust-region extensions (SCBO, HDsafeBO) mitigate this but are prone to premature region shrinkage when tight or complex constraints obstruct the descent direction, causing stagnation. No prior method combines a local search strategy that avoids premature shrinking with a rigorous polynomial-in-dimension convergence guarantee for the KKT residual.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes noisy zero-order oracle evaluations of a scalar objective f and m vector-valued constraint functions c_i over a compact convex domain X ⊂ R^d, with additive Gaussian noise on all observations. It produces a sequence of candidate iterates {x_k} whose KKT residuals (stationarity and feasibility) converge at a sublinear rate, with the final output being an approximate KKT point. Notably, the method operates in a single loop (one gradient step per iteration) and permits evaluations in the infeasible region, treating infeasible points as informative for surrogate updates rather than discarding them as in safe BO.

## Method

LCBO extends the GIBO local search paradigm to constrained optimization by replacing rigid trust regions with an alternating local exploration–exploitation strategy on a quadratic penalty surrogate Q_ρ(x) = f(x) + (ρ/2)‖c(x)‖². In the exploration phase, a batch of points is selected to minimize the worst-case posterior gradient covariance at the current iterate (a one-step-lookahead uncertainty reduction criterion over all m+1 GP models); in the exploitation phase, a single projected gradient descent step is taken using GP posterior means of ∇f and ∇cᵀc. The penalty factor ρ_k = k^{1/4} and step size η_k = (2L_m k)^{-1/2} are scheduled to ensure convergence without an inner loop. Theoretically, under mild regularity (a constraint qualification generalizing LICQ), the averaged KKT residual scales as Õ(m^{7/3} K^{-1/2} + σm²dK^{-1/2}) for RBF/Matérn kernels, yielding polynomial rather than exponential dependence on dimension d.

## Results

LCBO is evaluated on synthetic tasks (25D, 50D, 100D) and three real-world benchmarks (25-Bar Truss, d=25; Stepped Cantilever Beam, d=50; MuJoCo HalfCheetah, d=102) with a budget of 1,000 evaluations. Results are reported as median best-feasible objective (lower is better) with interquartile ranges over 10 runs; no single numerical summary table is provided—performance is shown via optimization-progress curves (Figures 1–2). The headline finding is that LCBO consistently achieves the lowest best-feasible objective across all six tasks, with the margin over baselines widening substantially as dimensionality increases (e.g., in 100D synthetic tasks, LCBO maintains steep descent while competitors plateau early). On the Stepped Cantilever Beam, EPBO fails to find any feasible point within the entire budget, and on the 25-Bar Truss, SCBO exhibits premature stagnation due to trust-region shrinkage against the constraint boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCBO outperforms all four baselines (CEI, EPBO, SCBO, HDsafeBO) on every evaluated task. Against CEI (global constrained EI), LCBO achieves significantly lower objective values in early optimization and avoids the premature convergence that CEI exhibits on the Stepped Cantilever Beam. Against EPBO (exact penalty), the gap is stark: EPBO remains stuck at a very high objective on the 25-Bar Truss and finds zero feasible points on the Stepped Cantilever Beam within 1,000 evaluations. Against SCBO (trust-region CBO), LCBO demonstrates continuous descent on the 25-Bar Truss where SCBO rapidly flattens as its trust region shrinks against the constraint boundary—directly validating the paper's motivation for replacing rigid trust regions. Against HDsafeBO (safe BO for high-dimensional control), LCBO shows the most rapid initial descent on HalfCheetah and greater stability across 10 trials; HDsafeBO achieves comparable median performance between 500–800 evaluations but LCBO maintains stronger continuous optimization capability approaching the 1,000-call limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCBO extends the gradient-based local search paradigm of GIBO (Müller et al., 2021) from unconstrained to constrained Bayesian optimization by introducing a quadratic penalty on the GP posterior gradient. It deliberately departs from the trust-region family (TuRBO, SCBO, HDsafeBO), which the authors argue suffers from premature region shrinkage when the descent direction is obstructed by tight constraints. The theoretical analysis builds on Wu et al. (2023), who established polynomial-dimension convergence for GIBO, and adapts the single-loop penalty method framework from stochastic nonconvex optimization (Alacaoglu & Wright, 2024; Lin et al., 2022) to the noisy black-box setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-dimensional constrained BO is a practical bottleneck in engineering design, robotics control, and ML hyperparameter tuning where global methods become intractable. The polynomial (rather than exponential) dimension dependence of the KKT residual provides the first rigorous scalability guarantee for a local CBO method, and the empirical demonstration of trust-region premature shrinking on the truss design problem gives concrete motivation for the penalty-based alternative. The main limitation is that LCBO is a local method and may converge to suboptimal local solutions in highly multi-modal landscapes; the authors suggest global restart or meta-learning strategies as future directions. An open question is how the regularity condition (Assumption 5.3) behaves in practice for problems with degenerate or nearly-infeasible constraint geometries.
