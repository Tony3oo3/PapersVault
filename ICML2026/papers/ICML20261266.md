## Problem

Fine-tuning Spoken Language Models with synthetic data for low-resource languages introduces a fundamental Stability-Expressivity Gap: increasing the synthetic ratio improves phonetic accuracy but progressively collapses prosodic diversity (Synthetic Erosion), locking practitioners into suboptimal data configurations. No prior work had systematically characterized this non-monotonic trade-off or proposed annotation-free alignment methods that simultaneously recover both stability and expressivity in data-scarce regimes.

## Input / Output

The system takes input text (optionally with a short reference speech clip for zero-shot voice cloning) and produces a 24 kHz synthesized waveform. Speech is discretized via S3Tokenizer into 25 Hz tokens over a 6,561-entry codebook; the reference speech provides timbre embeddings to the Flow-Matching decoder while the text conditions the autoregressive LM. For Lao (zero real speech), the entire training corpus is 1,500 h of synthetic audio generated by MMS-TTS and cross-lingual transfer, with no authentic target-language recordings used at any stage.

## Method

The paper proposes two self-alignment frameworks built on the CosyVoice 2 architecture (Qwen2.5-0.5B LM + CFM Flow-Matching decoder). DGSA exploits the architectural prosody-timbre separation: by toggling style-token conditioning while holding timbre embeddings fixed, it generates identity-consistent pairs contrasting expressive vs. flat outputs, then applies dual DPO objectives (stability and expressivity) with a dynamic weight schedule that activates corrective pressure only beyond the critical synthetic ratio α*≈50%. TDSC, designed for the zero-real-speech regime (Lao), operates as a closed-loop: it samples candidates across a temperature gradient {0.7, 1.0, T_max^(k)}, filters them via ASR-based WER/repetition/length criteria, and iteratively refines the policy through SFT on accepted samples plus DPO on constructed preference pairs, while progressively expanding T_max to recover prosodic diversity. The full pipeline is strictly sequential (SFT → alignment), and token-level entropy H_p is validated as a lightweight proxy for prosodic naturalness that tracks NMOS across all synthetic ratios.

## Results

On Thai (TSynC-2), the DGSA system achieves WER 38.9% and NMOS 4.51±0.06, outperforming all commercial baselines including ElevenLabs-v3 (NMOS 4.21) and Azure (NMOS 4.01). On Lao (Common Voice), the TDSC system trained entirely on synthetic data achieves WER 29.8% and NMOS 4.53±0.06, surpassing the best commercial system (Gemini Flash, WER 34.2%, NMOS 4.12) by 4.4% absolute WER and 0.4 NMOS points. For zero-shot voice cloning on Thai, DGSA achieves SIM 0.84 and SMOS 4.51, exceeding ElevenLabs-v3 (SIM 0.78, SMOS 4.23); on Lao, TDSC is the first system capable of voice cloning, achieving SIM 0.81 and SMOS 4.32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four open-source baselines on both languages. On Thai, DGSA (WER 38.9%, NMOS 4.51) beats Typhoon2-Audio (WER 50.9%, NMOS 3.72) by 12% WER and 0.79 NMOS, Seamless-M4T-v2 (WER 47.8%, NMOS 3.55) by 8.9% WER and 0.96 NMOS, MMS-TTS (WER 53.5%, NMOS 3.24) by 14.6% WER and 1.27 NMOS, and PyThaiTTS (WER 78.4%, NMOS 2.91) by a wide margin. On Lao, TDSC (WER 29.8%, NMOS 4.53) surpasses MMS-TTS (WER 44.8%, NMOS 3.52) by 15% WER and 1.01 NMOS, and is the only open-source system with Lao support. Against commercial systems, DGSA achieves the highest NMOS on Thai (4.51 vs. ElevenLabs 4.21, Azure 4.01, Gemini Pro 4.05) while Azure retains a slight WER edge (36.5% vs. 38.9%), illustrating the Stability-Expressivity trade-off the paper addresses. On Lao, TDSC beats all commercial systems on both WER and NMOS, with a 4.4% WER advantage over Gemini Flash and a 0.4 NMOS margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CosyVoice 2 / Flow-Matching SLM architecture (autoregressive token LM + flow-matching decoder) and the growing body of work on synthetic data augmentation for low-resource speech. It departs from prior model-collapse studies in the text domain (Shumailov et al., Alemohammad et al.) by identifying a distinct mechanism—low-entropy bias of external deterministic TTS outputs rather than recursive self-consumption—and formalizing it as the 'Synthetic Erosion' phenomenon with a mixture-entropy analysis. Methodologically, it extends DPO-based preference alignment (SpeechAlign, Emo-DPO) by exploiting the prosody-timbre architectural disentanglement in Flow-Matching SLMs to construct self-contrastive preference pairs without human annotation, and introduces a temperature-curriculum self-critique loop (TDSC) inspired by self-refinement in reasoning LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantification of how synthetic data ratios affect prosodic expressivity in SLMs, giving practitioners a concrete critical-ratio threshold (~50%) and a lightweight diagnostic (token entropy) to monitor during training. The self-alignment frameworks reduce dependence on scarce human annotations, which is critical for truly low-resource languages. However, generality remains untested: experiments cover only two Southeast Asian tonal languages, TDSC requires a usable target-language ASR system, and the additional iterative computation of TDSC, while cheaper than data collection, is non-trivial. Open questions include whether Synthetic Erosion and the proposed corrections transfer to non-tonal, agglutinative, or morphologically complex languages, and whether the prosody-timbre disentanglement assumption holds across all Flow-Matching SLM variants.
