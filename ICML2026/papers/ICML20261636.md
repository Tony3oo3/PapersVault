## Problem

Existing referring video object segmentation (RVOS) methods are inherently passive, assuming the target object is already visible in the observed video. This limits real-world deployment when queries refer to objects beyond the current view, occluded, or requiring active search. The paper asks whether goal-driven active exploration and precise segmentation can be achieved in open-world environments using only low-cost monocular vision, with open-set actions and unconstrained language expressions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a language expression (one of three types: object, intent, or instruction, e.g., 'I am thirsty' or 'Check the current time') together with a stream of egocentric RGB frames arriving sequentially at 448×448 resolution for the MLLM and 1024×1024 for the SAM2 encoder. It produces two outputs at each decision point: an open-set natural-language action command (e.g., 'Turn left', 'Remove clutter', 'Wait for the light') and a <SEG> token that is decoded by SAM2 into a pixel-level mask. The streaming protocol means the model must decide online whether to retain the previous action (cheap) or invoke full MLLM reasoning (expensive), with the final mask sequence spanning from frame 0 to the target-discovery timestep t_s.

## Method

LESA decouples continuous video monitoring from episodic high-level reasoning via a lightweight State Controller that maintains a compact hidden state and a four-level hierarchical memory (capacities [6,4,4,1], 128-dim tokens) using merge-and-promote updates, and predicts a binary trigger signal s_t via semantic-gated cross-attention conditioned on the expression and current action. When s_t=1, a single InternVL2.5-8B backbone is invoked in two sequential roles switched by <TASK> tokens: a Planner that generates a structured reasoning trace and next action, and a Segmentor that produces the <SEG> token for SAM2 mask decoding, keeping both in a unified latent space. An exploration-activated calibration step partially resets the hidden state and discards stale long-term memory after each new action, then refines via cross-attention with expression and action embeddings. Training is two-stage: first aligning the MLLM with SAM2 on large-scale segmentation datasets via LoRA (r=128), then jointly fine-tuning all components on RVAS with a combined loss of language supervision, mask/dice losses, and binary cross-entropy for the trigger head, achieving ~10× inference speedup over dense MLLM baselines.

## Results

On the RVAS validation set, LESA achieves 59.4% J&F and 22.7% J_fg for segmentation, and 22.2% Acc-All@1s for planning, outperforming all baselines on both dimensions. LESA also achieves 2.21 FPS, approximately 10× faster than the strongest MLLM baselines (0.11–0.24 FPS), by invoking the heavy MLLM backbone only at event-triggered moments rather than per-frame. In the oracle study (Table 4), LESA reaches 24.6% Acc-All even with perfect decision timing, exceeding InternVL3-8B (21.9%) and Sa2VA-8B* (20.8%), confirming that the advantage comes from exploration-aware state modeling rather than timing alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LESA outperforms all baselines on the primary planning metric Acc-All@1s (22.2%), beating the closest unified model Sa2VA-8B* (15.0%) by 7.2 points and general MLLMs InternVL3-8B (7.2%) and QwenVL3-8B (6.9%) by over 15 points. On segmentation, LESA achieves 59.4% J&F and 22.7% J_fg, surpassing Sa2VA-8B* (46.3/15.7) and UniPixel-7B* (44.7/16.2) under the streaming protocol, and GLUS-7B (55.3/7.1) under the offline protocol; the large J_fg gap (22.7 vs. ≤16.2) highlights that prior methods frequently fail to localize the target once it becomes visible. Conventional RVOS methods (LoSh, ReferDINO) achieve moderate J&F (39.3–53.1) but collapse on J_fg (7.0–12.7), confirming they cannot handle target-absent frames in long-horizon exploration. Streaming video models (VideoLLM-Online, Dispider) show limited goal-oriented planning (Acc-All 13.1/7.3) and lack segmentation entirely. LESA also achieves ~10× higher throughput (2.21 FPS vs. 0.11–0.24 FPS for MLLM baselines) while maintaining the best planning and segmentation scores, validating the event-triggered State Controller design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three established but previously separate lines of work: passive referring video object segmentation (RVOS), vision object navigation (VON), and streaming video understanding. It departs from RVOS by removing the assumption that the target is already visible, from VON by replacing closed action sets and heavy sensors with open-set natural-language actions over monocular egocentric video, and from action anticipation by requiring goal-oriented, bias-driven planning rather than temporally plausible prediction. The LESA framework repurposes event-triggered control (a state controller that gates expensive MLLM inference) and role-structured prompting (task-specific tokens switching a shared backbone between Planner and Segmentor) to address the streaming latency bottleneck that passive dense-inference methods cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RVAS provides the first large-scale, reproducible testbed for evaluating active exploration coupled with fine-grained segmentation, filling a gap between offline RVOS benchmarks and fully embodied closed-loop settings that are expensive to reproduce. The introduction of intent and instruction expressions (e.g., 'I am thirsty' → find a beverage) pushes beyond object-centric referring toward the implicit-goal reasoning that real embodied agents require. Key open questions remain: the evaluation relies on pre-recorded trajectories with idealized actuator execution, leaving a sim-to-real gap; absolute segmentation performance (22.7% J_fg) is still low, suggesting the task is far from solved; and extending to true closed-loop evaluation with a standardized language-driven executor (e.g., a promptable VLA policy) is identified as the natural next step.
