## Problem

Training recurrent neural networks on long sequences via backpropagation through time (BPTT) incurs activation memory that scales linearly with sequence length and backward signals prone to vanishing or exploding gradients. Biologically inspired local learning rules avoid these costs but introduce severe gradient bias. The paper addresses the gap between exact global credit assignment and efficient online computation by asking whether the long-range spatiotemporal correlations inherent to near-critical dynamical regimes can allow a strictly local rule to approximate BPTT gradients.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential input stream (u_t ∈ R^D) paired with per-step targets (y_t* ∈ R^K) in a single forward pass, processing one time step at a time without storing prior activations. It produces the same outer-product parameter updates as BPTT (for W_hh, W_xh, b_h, W_hy, b_y) but computed from a closed-form local teaching signal that requires only two per-unit scalars (α_i, μ_i) as auxiliary state, yielding O(1) activation memory and O(H) extra state regardless of sequence length T. Inputs span character-level text, row-sequential images, sensor time series, and neuromorphic event streams.

## Method

COLA (Criticality-driven Online Local Alignment) first places the RNN in a stable near-critical regime via an operator-normalized Lyapunov diagnostic that estimates the recurrent gain placing the maximum Lyapunov exponent near zero from the stable side. In this regime, two dynamical assumptions hold: the BPTT teaching signal continues approximately as a per-unit AR(1) process (temporal persistence), and within-step teaching signals are approximately rank-1 across units (spatial correlation). These assumptions collapse the BPTT backward recursion into a same-step implicit system, which is then closed elementwise by a per-unit loop gain μ_i, yielding the closed-form teaching signal δ̃_t^(i) = (∂L_t/∂x_t^(i)) / (1 − μ_i · φ′(x_t^(i))). Both α_i and μ_i are estimated online via exponential moving averages of same-step quantities, and the resulting signal drives standard outer-product weight updates identical in structure to BPTT, requiring no backward pass, no stored activations, and no parameter-scale auxiliary tensors.

## Results

On long-horizon regression tasks, COLA dramatically outperforms all baselines: Adding MSE drops to 0.0059 (vs. BPTT 0.1426, best online baseline RFLO 0.0813) and Lorenz rollout MSE to 0.0058 (vs. BPTT 0.6841, best online UORO 0.1395). On language modeling, COLA achieves 1.8352 loss on PTB-Char (matching TBPTT-1 at 1.8316) and 2.4174 on WikiText-2 Char (vs. BPTT 3.3620). On row-sequential classification, COLA remains competitive with BPTT (e.g., 0.9544 vs. 0.9713 on Row-MNIST, 0.4418 vs. 0.4545 on Row-CIFAR10) while using O(1) activation memory instead of O(TH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLA decisively outperforms full BPTT on the two long-horizon regression tasks (Adding: 0.0059 vs. 0.1426 MSE; Lorenz: 0.0058 vs. 0.6841 MSE) but trails BPTT on all three classification benchmarks (e.g., Row-MNIST 0.9544 vs. 0.9713, UCI HAR 0.8690 vs. 0.8877). Against TBPTT-1 and TBPTT-10, COLA wins on Adding, Lorenz, and both language-modeling tasks, while TBPTT-10 remains stronger on Row-MNIST (0.9734) and Row-CIFAR10 (0.4759). COLA beats RFLO and FPTT on every task except Row-MNIST where RFLO edges ahead (0.9565 vs. 0.9544), and it surpasses UORO on all seven RNN benchmarks (UORO's Row-MNIST result of 0.3060 appears to be an outlier). Against SnAp-1, COLA wins on Adding (0.0059 vs. 0.0860) and Lorenz (0.0058 vs. 0.2025) by large margins, is essentially tied on PTB-Char (1.8352 vs. 1.8279), and trails slightly on WikiText-2 (2.4174 vs. 2.2793) and UCI HAR (0.8690 vs. 0.8420). On SNN benchmarks (Table 3), COLA matches or slightly exceeds BPTT and pp-prop on 4 of 5 cell-task pairings, with the sole exception being N-MNIST with RadLIF cells (93.56% vs. 98.29% BPTT).

## Relation to Prior Work

COLA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the BPTT/TBPTT lineage for exact offline credit assignment, (2) online alternatives (RTRL, UORO, SnAp, RFLO, FPTT) that eliminate temporal unrolling but retain O(H²) parameter-scale auxiliary state, and (3) the neuroscience literature on criticality and edge-of-chaos dynamics. The key conceptual departure is using near-critical recurrent dynamics as a *justification* for two scalar closures (AR(1) temporal continuation and rank-1 spatial correlation), reducing auxiliary state to O(H) — two scalars per unit — rather than the O(H²) matrices maintained by all prior online methods. Unlike RFLO or FPTT, COLA does not introduce surrogate objectives or random feedback; it directly approximates the BPTT teaching signal while preserving the original supervised loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(H) state requirement is a meaningful reduction for very large recurrent networks and for hardware-constrained or neuromorphic deployment where per-synapse memory is prohibitive. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to two empirically validated but task-dependent assumptions (temporal AR(1) fidelity and spatial concentration), and the dramatic gains on Adding/Lorenz do not transfer uniformly to classification or static-image refinement tasks where BPTT or TBPTT remain stronger. Open questions include whether the scalar closure extends to multi-state architectures (LSTM pilot is preliminary), Transformers, or deeper networks, and whether the near-critical initialization can be replaced by adaptive online gain tracking without the one-time Lyapunov probe.
