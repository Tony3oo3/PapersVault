## Problem

Classical ERM over short programs is sample-efficient (Õ(L log |Σ|) examples) but requires exponential enumeration over the program space, while gradient-based training is computationally tractable yet provably sample-inefficient on structured families like parity (requiring Ω(√N/2^b) fresh examples via SQ lower bounds). The paper asks whether one can recover ERM's statistical guarantees over a discrete program class without paying the full cost of exhaustive enumeration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes m=200 i.i.d. labeled pairs (x, y) where x is a bitstring, digit string, or graph encoding and y ∈ {0,1}, split into 100 training and 100 validation examples. It outputs an executable, human-readable Python function f(x) that encodes the underlying rule, along with a full propose-verify trace logging every candidate program, its compilation status, and its validation error. The synthesized program is input-length-invariant, so it generalizes to sequence lengths far exceeding the training length (e.g., trained at n=10, evaluated at n=100).

## Method

LLM-PV is a propose-and-verify procedure that cleanly separates three roles: (i) a pretrained LLM (e.g., GPT-5-Thinking) acts as a data-dependent proposal distribution, sampling up to k=5 candidate Python programs from a prompt built on the training set; (ii) an external verifier compiles each candidate in a sandboxed interpreter and discards invalid or non-halting programs; (iii) the best program is selected by held-out validation error, with no gradient updates or validation feedback used to adapt the sampling distribution. The key insight is that the LLM replaces the exponential search space |Σ|^L with an effective search size 1/p_ε(S_tr), where p_ε is the proposal mass on ε-good programs, yielding a guarantee (Prop. 4.1) that k ≥ ⌈log(δ/2)/log(1−p_ε)⌉ trials suffice with a standard finite-class validation penalty. The theoretical analysis formalizes the complementary failures of enumeration (exponential in L) and finite-precision SGD (exponential in SQ dimension) on planted k-parity, isolating the regime where LLM-PV's proposal prior is both necessary and sufficient.

## Results

On 13 synthetic algorithmic tasks at input length n=100 with only 200 labeled examples, LLM-PV with GPT-5-Thinking (tool-augmented) achieves 100% test accuracy on 10 of 13 tasks (all parity variants, Pattern1/2, IsPalindrome, Dyck-2, CAP, IsPrime), 96.9% on CycleCount, 80.6% on IsPrime+47, and 50.1% on SHA-256 Parity. In stark contrast, SGD-trained and fine-tuned 1B-scale LLMs remain at 48.6–54.3% (near chance) on all parity variants and CAP, and even scaling BLOOM-75M to 100k training examples does not fix this (test accuracy stays at ~50% on 10-P and CAP). On standard tabular benchmarks (Adult Income, Mushroom, etc.), LLM-PV is competitive with XGBoost, Random Forest, and TabPFN, achieving 75.8% on Adult Income and 92.3% on HRTU2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-PV decisively outperforms all gradient-based baselines: SGD-trained Qwen3-1.7B, DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B, and Llama-3.2-1B all remain at 48.6–54.3% on parity variants and CAP at n=100, while LLM-PV (GPT-5-Thinking, tools) reaches 100% on every one of these tasks. Fine-tuning of the same 1B-scale models offers marginal gains on pattern matching (e.g., 61.5% on Pattern1 for FT vs. 51.5% for SGD) but still fails on parity, Dyck-2, and CAP, whereas LLM-PV achieves 100% across the board. In-context learning with GPT-5-Thinking (tool-augmented) partially recovers some rules (99% FP, 88% FHP, 79% 10-P) but drops to 51% on Pattern1/Pattern2 and 46% on CAP, confirming that direct label prediction is a fundamentally different and harder task than program synthesis. Agentic ML systems MLAgentBench and AIDE-ML (both with GPT-5-Thinking and tools) remain near or below chance on most tasks (e.g., AIDE-ML: 49.2–55.5%), because they search over generic ML workflows rather than executable programs. TabPFN, a strong pre-trained tabular foundation model, stays at 48–51% on all parity variants, Dyck-2, and CAP, matching the failure of classic ML methods. On the data-scaling experiment (Table 5), BLOOM-75M trained with SGD on 100k examples still achieves only ~50% test accuracy on 10-P and CAP, while LLM-PV solves both with m≤200, demonstrating a >500× sample-efficiency gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: classical PAC/MDL theory for finite hypothesis classes (which gives the sample-efficiency guarantee for ERM over short programs), the Statistical Query framework (which formalizes why gradient-based methods are sample-inefficient on parity-like targets), and the programming-by-example / LLM-guided program synthesis literature (Gulwani, Solar-Lezama, Self-Refine, AlphaEvolve). The key departure is a clean role separation: the LLM is used *only* as a data-dependent proposal distribution over candidate programs, while selection is grounded entirely in executable verification and held-out validation error—no gradient updates, no natural-language feedback loops, no iterative refinement. This contrasts with prior LLM-synthesis work that treats the model as a predictor (ICL), a refiner (Self-Refine/Reflexion), or an evolutionary proposer with textual gradients, and it contrasts with classical PBE that relies on hand-crafted DSLs and enumerative search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-evidenced demonstration that the compute-sample tension for short-program learning is real and that a pretrained LLM prior can collapse the exponential search cost without sacrificing the ERM guarantee. The 500× sample-efficiency gap (200 examples for LLM-PV vs. 100k for SGD, with PAC bounds confirming short descriptions exist) is a strong empirical confirmation of SQ-hardness predictions. However, the scope is limited to structured, low-data synthetic tasks, and reliable performance depends heavily on frontier-model proposal quality (open-weight models without tools mostly fail). Open questions include characterizing when the LLM proposal mass p_ε(S_tr) is sufficient, how the approach scales to longer or more compositional programs, and whether the framework extends to real-world structured domains beyond algorithmic benchmarks.
