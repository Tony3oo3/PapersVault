## Problem

Learning-augmented online algorithms that use predictions parsimoniously (with a minimum separation between queries) have been studied for caching and metrical task systems, but the extension to online metric matching remained an open question posed by Im et al. (2022). The central difficulty is that the natural Follow-the-Prediction framework requires a prediction at every time step, whereas the parsimonious setting only provides one every k steps, leaving a gap that must be filled without sacrificing performance guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264143/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a metric space with n servers (known in advance) and n requests arriving one per round, plus an oracle that provides action predictions (a set of t servers at round t) only at rounds that are multiples of a separation parameter k, or under a bounded total query budget B. The output is an irrevocable perfect matching between servers and requests; the cost is the sum of distances over matched pairs. The prediction error η is the sum of dist(P_t, O_t) over queried rounds, where O_t is the set of servers matched to the first t requests in the optimal hindsight matching.

## Method

The paper introduces a meta-algorithm that extends Follow-the-Prediction to the parsimonious setting by constructing virtual predictions between consecutive oracle queries. Between two actual predictions, the algorithm runs a γ-adherent, strongly ρ-competitive online matching subroutine (e.g., KP93, NR17, RAG18, or BBGN14) on an auxiliary sub-instance whose servers are those unmatched by the last actual prediction and whose requests are those arriving before the next query; the servers matched by the subroutine, unioned with the last actual prediction, form the virtual prediction for each intermediate round. The main theorem bounds the cost by (1+γ+ρ(k−1))·cost(OPT) + (2+γ+ρ(k−1))·η(Q), yielding a deterministic (2k−1)·cost(OPT)+2kη guarantee via KP93 and a randomized O(log n·log k)·(cost(OPT)+η) guarantee via HST embedding. Matching lower bounds on star metrics show the deterministic guarantee is essentially tight, and a combination algorithm with a constant-factor fallback provides robustness against adversarial predictions.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a deterministic algorithm (Theorem 1.1) achieving cost ≤ 9·min{(2k−1)·cost(OPT) + 2kη, (2n−1)·cost(OPT)} with predictions separated by k time steps, and a randomized algorithm (Theorem 1.2) achieving expected cost ≤ min{O(log n·log k)·(cost(OPT)+η), O(log²n)·cost(OPT)}. Matching lower bounds (Theorems 4.1–4.4) show the deterministic guarantee is essentially tight up to a constant factor. Experimentally, on Line, Plane, and Chicago Taxi Trips instances (n=100), the proposed algorithm consistently achieves the best cost/OPT ratio across all separation parameters k∈{1,…,20}, with performance degrading gracefully as k increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five algorithms: the proposed method (Ours, Corollary 3.6), the classical (2n−1)-competitive algorithm (Comp, Kalyanasundaram & Pruhs 1993), Greedy (nearest-available-server), and two combination algorithms (Comb-Comp, Comb-Greedy). Ours consistently outperforms all baselines across Line, Plane, and Taxi instances when predictions are accurate, attributed to its ability to fully exploit available predictions. However, the combination algorithms often underperform their respective fallback algorithms due to switching overhead between the two input algorithms. Notably, Comb-Greedy is outperformed by plain Greedy on Plane and Taxi instances. Under noisy predictions, Ours with k=1 (equivalent to FtP) is most sensitive to noise, while larger k values trade off accuracy for robustness; the combination algorithms are substantially more noise-robust but sacrifice peak performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parsimonious learning-augmented framework initiated by Im et al. (2022) for caching and refined by Sadek & Elias (2024) for metrical task systems to the setting of online metric matching with action predictions (Antoniadis et al., 2023b). The core technical contribution is a meta-algorithm that adapts the Follow-the-Prediction (FtP) framework to the parsimonious regime by constructing 'virtual predictions' between actual oracle queries using adherent, strongly competitive subroutines (e.g., KP93, NR17, BBGN14). The paper also establishes matching lower bounds on star metrics, showing the deterministic guarantee is essentially tight even with perfect predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work answers an explicit open question posed by Im et al. (2022) and provides the first complete characterization (upper and lower bounds) of parsimonious learning-augmented online metric matching. Key open questions remain: whether the O(log n) factor in the randomized guarantee can be eliminated to depend only on k, whether the combination algorithm's multiplicative factor of 9 can be improved (experiments show it often underperforms its fallback), and whether alternative prediction models (e.g., metric error with outliers) yield stronger guarantees. The practical finding that robustification via combination incurs significant overhead is a cautionary signal for the broader learning-augmented algorithms community.
