## Problem

Existing LLM-based graph methods inject structural information only at the input level (subgraph sequences) or output level (GNN predictors), both of which either discard higher-level whole-graph context due to O(n²) attention constraints or remain tied to task-specific label sets. This leads to information loss and limited zero-shot transferability across heterogeneous graph datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264280/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two complementary graph signals: the full graph (node feature matrix X and adjacency matrix A) is processed by a GNN to produce a single graph-level vector h, while a task-specific subgraph node sequence t_Gs concatenated with a task description t_task is compressed into a continuous prompt by a fine-tuned LLM encoder. The frozen LLM decoder then generates a text label prediction conditioned on both the prompt and the weight-level updates derived from h, unifying node classification, link prediction, and graph classification under a single text-output interface.

## Method

GaRA introduces a weight-level injection paradigm where a GNN (GCN) extracts a whole-graph representation via max-pooling, and an MLP with tanh generates a low-rank matrix G that augments the left LoRA matrix L, yielding the update W = Ŵ + (L + G⊙M)Rᵀ. The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that parametric weight generation inherently admits direction-preserving norm amplification—a gradient descent shortcut that dilutes original weights and causes overfitting—since weight decay on the generator parameters Θ does not directly constrain ‖ΔW‖. To counteract this, GaRA rescales G's column norms to match those of L via a per-column ratio matrix M, providing a stable reference scale without extra hyperparameters. The full model is trained with instruction tuning (cross-entropy on label tokens) on a mix of node classification, link prediction, and graph classification datasets, with the weight-level injection applied to a single intermediate layer (21st of 32) of the Vicuna-7B backbone.

## Results

On zero-shot dataset transfer, GaRA achieves the best performance across all 8 evaluated datasets spanning node classification (Cora 25.1%, WikiCS 66.9%, Reddit 51.8%, Instagram 61.1%), graph classification (BACE 54.9%, HIV 51.2%), and link prediction (AmazonPhoto 70.8%, BookHistory 72.2%). The closest competitor, UniGTE, is outperformed on every dataset, with the largest margins on BookHistory (+20.7% AUC) and Cora (+3.5% accuracy). Norm rescaling further improves transferability: under the full training setting, it yields relative gains of up to 53.04% (Cora) and 31.35% (AmazonPhoto) over the no-rescaling variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaRA consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and task type. The strongest competitor is UniGTE (input-level injection with permutation-invariant encoding), which GaRA surpasses by 3.5% on Cora, 3.4% on WikiCS, 0.6% on Reddit, 0.8% on Instagram, 1.5% on BACE, 1.1% on HIV, 2.7% on AmazonPhoto, and a substantial 20.7% on BookHistory. Among input-level methods, LLaGA and TEA-GLM are competitive on link prediction (65.9% and 67.5% on AmazonPhoto) but fall well short of GaRA's 70.8%. The output-level method OFA and the GNN-interleaved GOFA are consistently weaker, with GOFA performing particularly poorly on Cora (3.9%). The unmodified Vicuna-7B LLM serves as a lower bound, confirming that graph-specific adaptation is essential. Notably, GaRA's advantage is largest on BookHistory (link prediction), where the whole-graph context captured via weight-level injection provides the most complementary information to the subgraph input.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaRA extends the input-level injection paradigm (LLaGA, UniGTE, TEA-GLM, GOFA) by adding a complementary weight-level injection channel that generates LoRA-style low-rank updates conditioned on whole-graph GNN representations. Conceptually, it adapts the weight-generation idea from VLoRA (visual LoRA) to the graph domain, but introduces a norm-rescaling constraint to counteract a direction-preserving norm amplification bias that the authors formally characterize via a Taylor-expansion argument. The method is built directly on the UniGTE framework for input-level subgraph prompting, positioning weight-level injection as an additive rather than replacement mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete information-loss problem in LM-based graph learning: subgraph-only input injection discards higher-level structural context, and the proposed weight-level channel recovers this without quadratic cost. The theoretical identification of norm amplification as an architectural optimization bias (rather than a data-dependent phenomenon) is a useful diagnostic for anyone generating parametric weight updates. However, the authors acknowledge that weight-level injection has not been validated as a standalone mechanism, and the practical gains over strong baselines like UniGTE are modest (1–5 points), leaving open whether the added GNN module is worth the engineering overhead at scale.
