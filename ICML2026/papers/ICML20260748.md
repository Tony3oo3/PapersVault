## Problem

Self-attention mechanisms in medical image segmentation incur quadratic O((HW)²C) complexity, making them impractical for high-resolution clinical images with limited training data. Additionally, fixed loss formulations struggle to accommodate the wide morphological diversity of anatomical structures (compact polyps, thin vessels, irregular tumors), requiring costly manual per-dataset tuning. The paper addresses both challenges simultaneously: achieving global receptive fields at sub-quadratic cost and automating supervision across diverse structures.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical images resized to 352×352 pixels (or 256×256 overlapping patches with stride 32 for high-resolution retinal fundus images) and produces pixel-level segmentation masks, either binary or multi-class (up to 3 classes for EndoVis-2017 and BraTS2020). BraTS2020 uses a 2.5D input by stacking three adjacent slices from three MRI modalities. Retinal datasets undergo green-channel extraction, CLAHE enhancement, and circular FOV masking before patch-based inference, with final predictions reconstructed by aggregating overlapping patch outputs.

## Method

S2M-Net is a 5-stage encoder-decoder (4.7M parameters) whose core innovation is Spectral-Selective Token Mixing (SSTM), a dual-branch global mixer that replaces self-attention. The spectral branch applies per-channel 2D FFT, retains only the central K×K=32×32 low-frequency coefficients (capturing >93% of spectral energy in medical images), applies learnable complex filtering, and inverts back to spatial domain at O(HWC log(HW)) cost. The spatial branch performs content-gated channel mixing through a low-rank bottleneck (d=16) at O(HWC²) cost, recovering high-frequency detail lost to truncation. A Boundary-Focused Decoder (BFD) factorizes region and boundary processing through parallel streams with learned per-pixel soft routing, localizing edges via backpropagation from multi-scale boundary loss without explicit edge annotations. Training uses the Morphology-Aware Adaptive Segmentation Loss (MASL), which combines five loss components (weighted Dice+IoU+wBCE core, multi-scale boundary, structure, scale-aware focal, texture) modulated by per-sample morphological descriptors (tubularity, compactness, irregularity, scale) and learned dataset-level weights, eliminating manual per-dataset loss tuning.

## Results

S2M-Net achieves a mean Dice of 90.07% across 15 datasets spanning 8 modalities, ranking first on 14 of 15 benchmarks with only 4.7M parameters (12.8× fewer than TransUNet's 60M). Statistically significant improvements (p < 0.0033, Bonferroni-corrected) are observed on 7 challenging tasks, with the largest gains on EndoVis-2017 multiclass instrument segmentation (83.43% Dice, +8.69% over TransUNet) and BraTS2020 brain tumor (79.96% Dice, +10.10% over Swin-Unet). On 8 mature binary benchmarks, gains are modest (0.02–1.62 points) and not statistically significant after correction, indicating architectural advantages concentrate on complex morphology and multi-class structures. The sole underperformance is on CVC-ColonDB (90.69% vs. PraNet's 92.78%, −2.09 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2M-Net outperforms all seven baselines on average Dice: +6.09 over U-Net (83.98%), +4.98 over U-Net++ (85.09%), +3.78 over PraNet (86.29%), +3.03 over Swin-Unet (87.04%), +3.61 over TransUNet (86.46%), +4.65 over UMamba (85.42%), and +3.35 over RAPUNet (86.72%). The most dramatic wins are on multi-class and imbalanced tasks: EndoVis-2017 multiclass (+8.69 over TransUNet, +9.14 over UMamba) and BraTS2020 (+10.10 over Swin-Unet, +17.08 over UMamba). The sole loss is on CVC-ColonDB, where PraNet's reverse-attention polyp-specific inductive bias (92.78%) exceeds S2M-Net (90.69%) by 2.09 points, attributed to task-specific priors outperforming the general spectral mixer in small-data regimes. On 8 mature binary benchmarks (e.g., ACDC, DRIVE, STARE), improvements over the best baseline are 0.02–1.62 points and not statistically significant after Bonferroni correction, suggesting diminishing returns where baselines already exceed 90% Dice. All comparisons use identical training pipelines with MASL applied uniformly; a separate Dice+CE ablation (Table 2) confirms +4.48 points average architectural gain independent of the loss design.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2M-Net extends the U-Net encoder-decoder paradigm by replacing the quadratic-cost self-attention of TransUNet/UNETR with Spectral-Selective Token Mixing (SSTM), which draws on FNet's parameter-free FFT token mixing and GFNet's learned frequency filters but adapts them for dense segmentation via low-frequency truncation (K=32) and a content-gated spatial branch. The Boundary-Focused Decoder inherits its dual-stream region-boundary factorization from Gated-SCNN, while the Morphology-Aware Adaptive Segmentation Loss (MASL) departs from fixed-weight multi-term losses (Dice, boundary, focal) by introducing per-sample geometric modulation and learned dataset-level weights. The paper positions itself as an attention-free, parameter-efficient alternative that exploits domain-specific spectral concentration in medical images rather than general-purpose token interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.8× parameter reduction (4.7M vs. 60M TransUNet) with competitive or superior Dice across 15 datasets and 8 modalities makes S2M-Net practically relevant for resource-constrained clinical deployment where transformer-based models are infeasible. MASL's elimination of manual per-dataset loss tuning is a broadly applicable contribution that could benefit other segmentation architectures. Key limitations include the 2.5D processing of volumetric data (BraTS2020), underperformance on CVC-ColonDB suggesting task-specific inductive biases still matter, the absence of clinical validation for the K=32 truncation, and unvalidated generalization to underrepresented populations and higher-resolution acquisitions. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned frequency selection can replace the fixed K=32 default, and whether the spectral-spatial mixing strategy transfers to 3D volumetric segmentation.
