## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-class unsupervised anomaly detection (MUAD) relies on task-specific training of encoder-decoder models, which is costly under distribution shifts and suffers from an inherent fidelity-stability dilemma: high-fidelity reconstruction forces the decoder into a high-gain amplifier that destabilizes residual scores, while relaxing reconstruction dilutes anomaly evidence. Existing training-free alternatives (e.g., deep k-NN) remain prone to cross-category and cross-region mismatches in the multi-class setting, leaving open whether competitive MUAD is achievable without any task-specific training.

## Input / Output

RAD takes a single RGB test image (resized to 512×512 with a 448×448 center crop) and a pre-built memory bank of anomaly-free patch features extracted from a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 encoder at layers 4, 7, 10, and 12. It produces a dense pixel-level anomaly map (patch scores upsampled to pixel resolution) and an image-level anomaly score computed as the mean of the top 1% pixels in the anomaly map. The neighborhood radius ρ (0–2) and global retrieval count K (48–900) are dataset-specific hyperparameters tuned to accommodate varying viewpoint changes and dataset complexity.

## Method

RAD replaces decoder training with a two-stage retrieval pipeline over a multi-layer anomaly-free feature memory. First, a global context retrieval stage computes cosine similarity between the test image's [CLS] token and stored global descriptors, selecting the top-K semantically compatible reference images to suppress cross-category mismatches. Second, within those references, spatially conditioned patch matching restricts comparisons to an ℓ∞ neighborhood of radius ρ around each query patch, preventing cross-region mismatches; the layer-wise anomaly score is the 1-NN cosine dissimilarity, and scores from four encoder layers are fused with uniform weights. Theoretically, the paper proves that this distance-to-memory score is pointwise maximal within the class of non-negative 1-Lipschitz scores vanishing on the stored normal features, and is non-expansive (1-Lipschitz) in feature space, thereby avoiding the decoder amplification that causes the fidelity-stability dilemma in reconstruction-based methods.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, RAD achieves 99.6% I-AUROC and 98.5% P-AUROC, with pixel-level P-AP of 75.6 and P-AUPRO of 94.9, surpassing the previous best (Dinomaly) by +6.3 on P-AP and +0.1 on P-AUPRO. On VisA, RAD reaches 99.1% P-AUROC and 94.6% P-AUPRO, outperforming Dinomaly by +2.1 on P-AP. On the most challenging multi-view Real-IAD benchmark, RAD achieves 92.0% I-AUROC and 99.1% P-AUROC, extending gains to +10.9 on P-AP and +7.1 on P-F1-max over Dinomaly. In the few-shot setting (Y=1 reference image), RAD reaches 96.7% P-AUROC on MVTec-AD, outperforming the strongest few-shot method IIPAD by +2.1 on P-AUPRO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAD's closest competitor is Dinomaly (CVPR'25), the strongest trained MUAD baseline: on MVTec-AD they tie at 99.6% I-AUROC, but RAD wins decisively on pixel-level localization (+6.3 P-AP, +2.1 P-F1-max); on VisA, RAD trades a small image-level loss (97.5 vs 98.7 I-AUROC) for pixel-level gains (+2.1 P-AP, +1.8 P-F1-max); on Real-IAD, RAD dominates across all seven metrics with the largest margin on P-AP (+10.9). Against earlier reconstruction-based methods (RD4AD, UniAD, DiAD, MambaAD, OmiAD), RAD leads by 10–30+ points on P-AP across all three benchmarks, confirming that training-free retrieval outperforms decoder-based scoring. In the few-shot regime (Y=1,2,4 on MVTec-AD), RAD surpasses all dedicated few-shot methods: IIPAD (+2.1 to +2.7 P-AUPRO), PromptAD, WinCLIP, SPADE, and PatchCore, while also outperforming the training-free UniVAD on P-AUPRO. The paper further shows that RAD's advantage over Dinomaly persists under data scaling (single-class, multi-class, and incremental-class settings), with the largest gaps at low data ratios (e.g., +15.9 P-AP at τ=0.05 single-class, +20.6 P-AP at τ=0.01 incremental-class), indicating that retrieval-based scoring is most beneficial when anomaly-free evidence is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAD extends the memory-based retrieval paradigm established by PatchCore (Roth et al., 2022) and PNI (Bae et al., 2023) from single-class to multi-class unsupervised anomaly detection, adding global context conditioning and spatial neighborhood constraints to mitigate cross-category and cross-region mismatches that naive k-NN suffers from in MUAD. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant encoder-decoder reconstruction line (UniAD, Dinomaly, MambaAD, OmiAD) by replacing learned decoders with explicit nearest-neighbor retrieval over a frozen encoder's multi-layer feature memory, and provides a formal fidelity-stability dilemma analysis explaining why reconstruction-based scoring faces an inherent trade-off that retrieval avoids. The multi-layer feature extraction strategy draws on complementary similarity signals across ViT depths (Park et al., 2023; Amir et al., 2022), while the coreset compression for memory reduction follows standard submodular sampling techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper challenges a widely held assumption in the MUAD community—that competitive anomaly detection requires task-specific training—by demonstrating that a training-free retrieval framework can match or exceed trained SOTA across four benchmarks under standard, few-shot, and incremental-class settings. The practical implications are significant: new categories can be onboarded by simply adding to the memory bank without retraining, and the method automatically benefits from stronger frozen encoders as they become available. Key limitations include substantial memory storage (up to 60.8 GB for Real-IAD) and higher inference latency compared to feed-forward models, though coreset compression reduces memory to ~1% with minimal performance loss. Open questions include whether retrieval-based scoring can handle extreme distribution shifts beyond what frozen encoders capture, whether the approach extends naturally to video or multi-modal anomaly detection, and whether more efficient approximate nearest-neighbor structures can close the latency gap for real-time deployment.
