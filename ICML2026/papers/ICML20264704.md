## Problem

Existing state-of-the-art ternary (1.58-bit) quantization methods for LLMs, such as BitNet 1.58-bit, TriLM, and Tequila, all rely on data-intensive quantization-aware training requiring 10B–300B tokens, limiting scalability to small models (≤7B) and specific architectures. No post-training quantization (PTQ) method exists that can match their accuracy while operating on arbitrary pre-trained LLMs of any scale, creating a critical gap between practical deployment needs and available ternarization tools.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264704/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAT-Q takes the full-precision weights of any pre-trained LLM (dense or MoE, 1.7B–235B parameters) along with 512 calibration sequences of 2048 tokens each sampled from C4. It outputs ternary weights restricted to {−1, 0, +1} with a single group-wise scaling factor α per 128-element group, using a zero-point-free design (W ≈ αT) that preserves hardware-friendly deployment. The method supports both W1.58A16 and W1.58A8 configurations, and the ternary weights can be directly loaded into llama.cpp's TQ1_0/TQ2_0 formats for inference.

## Method

CAT-Q couples two components into a sliding-layer quantization pipeline. Learnable Modulation (LM) introduces three learnable factors (δ_μ, δ_α, δ_Δ) that adaptively transform the pre-trained weight distribution via Ŵ = (W − μ)/α and adjust the ternary threshold Δ = δ_Δ·Δ₀, suppressing outliers and reducing sensitivity to discretization. Softened Ternarization (ST) replaces the non-differentiable hard ternarization with a two-stage relay: a differentiable stage using a tanh-based transition function f(W; s, Δ) whose sharpness s increases linearly from near-identity to near-hard over the first γ fraction of calibration epochs, followed by a hard ternarization stage for final convergence. The optimization minimizes an L2 output-reconstruction loss over a sliding window of l layers jointly (following SliderQuant), rather than the standard layer-wise weight reconstruction, enabling neighboring layers to compensate for each other's quantization errors. This design requires only 512 calibration samples and 60 epochs, achieving ~100,000× fewer training tokens than QAT baselines while outperforming them on 1.7B–8B models and extending to 235B-parameter models for the first time.

## Results

CAT-Q achieves 61.76% average accuracy on Qwen3-8B (W1.58A16) using only 512 calibration samples (~1M tokens), outperforming BitNet 1.58-bit v1-7B (57.42%, 100B tokens) and TernaryLLM-8B (60.04%, 1T tokens) by 4.34 and 1.72 points respectively. On Qwen3-4B, CAT-Q reaches 57.06% average, surpassing BitNetV1-3.9B (54.16%) and TriLM-3.9B (56.55%) while using ~100,000× fewer training tokens. The method scales to 235B parameters (Qwen3-235B-A22B: 69.09% avg) within 60 hours on 8 A100-80GB GPUs, and achieves 72.72% on Llama2-70B, exceeding all 2-bit PTQ baselines (best: SliderQuant at 71.08%) at a lower 1.58-bit weight precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QAT-based ternary methods, CAT-Q (1M tokens) consistently outperforms BitNet 1.58-bit v1 at comparable scales: 51.01% vs. 48.28% (1.7B vs. 1.3B), 57.06% vs. 54.16% (4B vs. 3.9B), and 61.76% vs. 57.42% (8B vs. 7B), representing a ~100,000× reduction in training tokens. CAT-Q also beats TriLM (300B tokens) on both 1.7B (51.01% vs. 50.20%) and 4B (57.06% vs. 56.55%) scales, and surpasses TernaryLLM-8B (1T tokens with knowledge distillation) by 1.72 points on average. The one notable exception is Tequila (10B tokens), which edges out CAT-Q on the 4B comparison (57.60% vs. 57.06%), though CAT-Q still uses 10,000× fewer tokens. Against 2-bit PTQ methods on Llama2-7B, CAT-Q at 1.58-bit (57.69%) outperforms SliderQuant W2A16 (55.38%), OmniQuant W2A16 (46.98%), GPTQ W2A16 (41.55%), and all dual 1-bit methods (DB-LLM 55.12%, BiLLM 41.68%, PB-LLM 37.59%). On Llama2-70B, CAT-Q (72.72%) beats SliderQuant (71.08%) and EfficientQAT (68.93%) at a lower bit-width, though DB-LLM (65.82%) and EfficientQAT remain competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT-Q inherits the core ternarization formulation (scaling factor + threshold + hard ternary mapping) from TWN (Li et al., 2016) and adopts the sliding-layer output reconstruction framework from SliderQuant (Wang et al., 2026) to jointly optimize multiple layers. Its key departure is from the dominant QAT paradigm used by all prior SOTA ternary LLM methods (BitNet 1.58-bit, TriLM, Tequila), which require 10B–300B training tokens; CAT-Q instead formulates ternarization as a post-training calibration problem, drawing on differentiable soft quantization ideas (Gong et al., 2019; Esser et al., 2020) to introduce a learnable, two-stage transition from identity mapping to hard ternarization. The learnable modulation component extends the static absmean statistics used in BitNet with three learnable factors that adaptively reshape the weight distribution before ternarization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT-Q is the first PTQ method to match or exceed QAT-based ternary quantization on commonsense reasoning benchmarks, reducing calibration cost by ~100,000× and scaling to 235B-parameter models for the first time in the ternary regime. However, the pilot study on math and coding tasks reveals catastrophic degradation (0% on MATH-500 and HumanEval+), indicating that the method's gains are largely confined to pattern-recognition-style tasks and that complex multi-step reasoning remains severely impaired at 1.58-bit precision. Open questions include whether the PTQ–QAT gap can be closed on harder tasks, whether optimized 1.58-bit kernels for diverse architectures will materialize, and whether the MoE sensitivity observed here limits practical deployment of ternary MoE models.
