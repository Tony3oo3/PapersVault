## Problem

Standard positional encodings in Transformer-based time series forecasting, particularly Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), assume uniform index-based temporal spacing and enforce a stationary angular velocity. The paper proves that RoPE is mathematically incapable of representing non-affine temporal warping, yet real-world systems (financial cycles, ecological phenology, traffic patterns) exhibit locally varying periodicity where the effective flow of time decouples from the sampling index. This gap forces models to approximate time-varying structure with position-independent frequencies, leading to phase decoherence in predictions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate lookback window X ∈ R^{L×C} (e.g., L=96, C=7 for ETTh1) and produces forecasts Y ∈ R^{T×C} for horizons T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Input is centered relative to the last observed value (Last Value Residual strategy) so the network learns temporal increments rather than absolute magnitudes, with the reference value added back at output. Tokens are constructed via Channel-Value Mixed tokenization, concatenating a global context vector (linear projection of all channels) with a channel-specific local signal, plus shared positional and channel-identifier embeddings.

## Method

StretchTime replaces static RoPE rotations with Symplectic Positional Embeddings (SyPE), which parameterize a continuous Hamiltonian flow S(t) = exp(tJK) in the symplectic group Sp(2,R) per 2D subspace, where K is a learnable symmetric matrix enforcing stable oscillatory dynamics. An adaptive warp module computes input-dependent temporal increments Δτ̂_t = Softplus(w_τ^T h_t) from hidden representations, making the effective clock content-dependent and channel-specific. The attention score then depends exclusively on the warped time difference via q_m^T J S(τ̂_n − τ̂_m) k_n, preserving relative-position encoding while allowing anisotropic stretching and compression of temporal coordinates. Each attention head learns a distinct Hamiltonian geometry, and the full architecture (3-layer pre-norm Transformer, d_model=64–128, 4 heads, ~494K parameters) is trained end-to-end with MSE loss, random ratio channel dropout, and a cosine-annealed AdamW schedule.

## Results

StretchTime achieves the best average rank of 2.20 across 10 multivariate forecasting benchmarks, securing first place on 4 of 10 datasets (Solar, PEMS03, PEMS04, and tied on Weather/ETTh1). On the Solar dataset, it reduces MSE to 0.195 (vs. 0.214 for RoPE and 0.203 for TimeMixer++), and on PEMS04 it achieves 0.088 MSE (vs. 0.091 for OLinear). The model uses only ~494K parameters and 2.79G FLOPs on average, a >95% parameter reduction over PatchTST/iTransformer (~10–14M params) and ~55% fewer FLOPs than TimeMixer++ (6.25G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StretchTime outperforms its internal RoPE baseline on all 10 datasets, with the largest gaps on Solar (0.195 vs. 0.214 MSE, −8.9%) and PEMS03 (0.094 vs. 0.107, −12.1%), confirming the theoretical impossibility result for fixed-frequency rotations. Against the strongest external baselines, StretchTime wins decisively on traffic (PEMS03: 0.094 vs. OLinear 0.095, TimeMixer++ 0.165) and Solar (0.195 vs. TimeMixer++ 0.203), but loses to TimeMixer++ on Weather (0.237 vs. 0.226), ECL (0.174 vs. 0.165), ETTh2 (0.384 vs. 0.339), and ETTm2 (0.276 vs. 0.269), and to OLinear on ECL (0.174 vs. 0.159) and PEMS08 (0.118 vs. 0.113). Against older Transformer baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, TimesNet, DLinear), StretchTime dominates across all 10 datasets, with particularly large margins on PEMS08 (0.118 vs. 0.150 for iTransformer) and Solar (0.195 vs. 0.233 for iTransformer). The paper notes that OLinear and TimeMixer++ results are taken directly from their published papers, while all other baselines were retrained under a standardized protocol matching the OLinear/TimeMixer++ evaluation setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) by generalizing the underlying rotation group SO(2) to the symplectic group Sp(2,ℝ), drawing on Hamiltonian mechanics to parameterize the positional transformation. It also connects to classical Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) by providing a differentiable, end-to-end analogue that learns temporal dilation/contraction from input content rather than requiring post-hoc alignment. Architecturally, StretchTime builds on the standard pre-norm Transformer with channel-value mixed tokenization (similar to iTransformer's inverted design) and last-value residual learning, positioning the symplectic positional encoding as the primary innovation atop a conventional backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean impossibility theorem showing RoPE cannot represent non-affine temporal warping provides a principled justification for adaptive positional encodings in time series, and the symplectic formulation offers a physically grounded inductive bias that achieves strong results with ~95% fewer parameters than competing Transformers. However, the empirical margins over recent linear baselines (OLinear, TimeMixer++) are modest and sometimes negligible, and the paper has not yet demonstrated whether the symplectic generalization provides meaningful advantages beyond what simpler learnable-frequency approaches could achieve. Open questions include whether the framework scales to foundation-model-level time series forecasting, whether the richer Sp(2,ℝ) geometry matters for general sequence tasks (e.g., NLP), and whether the adaptive warp module's interpretability translates to practical deployment settings.
