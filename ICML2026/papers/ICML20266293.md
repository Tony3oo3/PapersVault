## Problem

Current MLLMs are passive consumers of static visual inputs and lack the ability to actively decide where and how to look, a capability essential for efficient perception in embodied and robotic settings. While GPT-o3's zoom-in search offers a first step toward active perception, it operates sequentially (one region at a time) and frequently selects inaccurate or redundant regions, especially in dense or fine-grained scenes. No systematic framework, training procedure, or evaluation protocol exists for equipping MLLMs with learnable active perception policies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static RGB image (resized to 1024 px on the shorter side for the sensing module, 840×840 for the task module) and a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'find all coins'). The sensing module outputs up to three non-overlapping bounding-box region proposals in structured JSON, accompanied by a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, all in a single forward pass. The task module then operates on each cropped region to produce final bounding boxes (detection/grounding) or positive/negative click points fed to SAM (interactive segmentation).

## Method

ACTIVE-o3 decouples a single MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) into a sensing module that proposes K=3 parallel zoom-in regions and a task module that executes the downstream operation on each crop, with only the sensing policy optimized via GRPO. The key training signal is a dual-form reward combining task-independent heuristics (format validity, pairwise IoU non-overlap, area-range constraints, and GT-coverage) with task-aware feedback (AP/AR for detection, mIoU via SAM for segmentation), which stabilizes RL where purely task-based rewards are too sparse. Unlike sequential zoom-in strategies (e.g., GPT-o3, V*), the policy generates all K regions jointly in one pass, encouraging spatial diversity and complementary coverage under a fixed sensing budget. No supervised region-selection labels are used; the sensing policy is initialized zero-shot via structured prompting and refined entirely through reinforcement learning.

## Results

On LVIS_small, ACTIVE-o3 achieves 2.2 APs / 4.6 ARs, improving over Qwen2.5-VL by +1.0/+2.8 and over GDINO by +1.7/+3.4. On LVIS_dense, ACTIVE-o3 reaches 4.3 APs / 5.5 ARs (+2.7/+3.5 over Qwen2.5-VL), and when paired with GDINO as the task model, achieves 7.0 APs / 7.9 ARs, surpassing GDINO alone by +1.3/+1.6. On domain-specific SODA benchmarks, ACTIVE-o3 attains 9.2/10.4 APs/ARs on SODA-A (aerial) and 15.1/22.0 on SODA-D (driving), outperforming Qwen2.5-VL by +8.5/+8.9 and +13.0/+17.5 respectively. On general understanding benchmarks, ACTIVE-o3 preserves and slightly improves over its initialization model, scoring 80.5 MMBench, 2316 MME, and 69.7 RealWorldQA versus 80.1/2308/67.9 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-VL (the MLLM backbone), ACTIVE-o3 consistently wins across all object scales on both LVIS_small and LVIS_dense, with the largest gains on small objects (+1.0 APs, +2.8 ARs) and large-object recall (+14.6 ARl on LVIS_dense). Against Grounding DINO (a specialized open-vocabulary detector), ACTIVE-o3 alone is competitive on small objects (2.2 vs 0.5 APs on LVIS_small) but trails on medium/large objects; however, ACTIVE-o3+GDINO surpasses GDINO alone by +1.3/+1.6 APs/ARs on LVIS_dense and +4.9/+11.0 on medium/large metrics, demonstrating that the learned sensing policy generalizes as a plug-in module. Against V*+GDINO (a sequential search-based MLLM requiring >10 forward passes per image), ACTIVE-o3 achieves comparable or better APs/ARs on LVIS_dense (4.3/5.5 vs 5.7/6.7 for APs/ARs, but 14.3/19.7 vs 18.3/22.9 for APm/ARm) while using a single forward pass for parallel region selection, offering substantially better efficiency. In the reference comparison with CRG+GDINO (inference-time region reranking), ACTIVE-o3+GDINO outperforms across all scales (7.0 vs 4.3 APs, 25.1 vs 19.0 APm, 45.1 vs 38.2 APl on LVIS_dense), indicating that learning a reusable sensing policy is more effective than post-hoc proposal reranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as the RL optimization backbone but redirects it from text-centric reasoning to a visually grounded sensing policy. It departs from GPT-o3's sequential zoom-in search by formulating parallel multi-region selection as a single-step decision problem, and from Visual CoT / grounding methods (ARGUS, ReFocus, CRG) by shifting focus from reasoning over a fixed image to actively selecting informative regions under a sensing budget. The modular decoupling of a sensing module (region proposal) and a task module (action execution) within a single MLLM, trained end-to-end via a dual-form reward, is the core conceptual departure from prior MLLM-RL work that has targeted text reasoning or simple grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic framework and benchmark suite for MLLM-based active perception, filling a gap as MLLMs become central planners in robotic systems. The finding that RL-trained active perception (without any QA or reasoning data) improves general visual understanding on RealWorldQA and MME suggests active perception can serve as a scalable proxy objective for leveraging perception annotations. Key limitations include the 2D-only setting, fixed budget of 3 regions, absence of memory across steps, and low absolute detection scores (APs ~2–9 on LVIS-small), leaving open whether the approach transfers to 3D embodied settings or scales to more flexible action spaces (rotation, variable region counts).
