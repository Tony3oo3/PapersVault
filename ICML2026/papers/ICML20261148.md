## Problem

VideoQA models achieve high answer accuracy yet frequently fail to align their answers with the correct video segments — a discrepancy the authors term the grounding gap. Existing systems treat grounding as a preprocessing step (keyframe selection), a post-hoc alignment problem, or a training-time supervision signal, leaving evidence weakly coupled to the actual reasoning process. The paper argues this stems from a deeper design issue: grounding signals are never represented as explicit reasoning constraints that hypotheses must satisfy during inference.

## Input / Output

LINGUA takes a video clip (typically 33-second NExT-QA segments up to 3-minute EgoSchema clips) and a multiple-choice or open-ended text question. Rather than processing all frames, event-driven perception selects only 8–12% of frames using VideoMAE-v2 semantic-change scores and YOLOv8 affordance detection, then generates one-sentence natural-language descriptions per selected frame via Gemma3-4B in vision-language mode. The output is a text answer accompanied by a temporal span (for grounded benchmarks like NExT-GQA, requiring IoU ≥ 0.5 with ground truth) and a full linguistic BAV trace documenting which procedural scripts were hypothesized, verified, or rejected, making the reasoning chain fully inspectable.

## Method

LINGUA is a memory-based agent that reasons entirely in a linguistic belief-state space, built around three typed memories — episodic (timestamped event narratives with PropBank semantic roles), semantic (FrameNet affordance frames per object), and procedural (action scripts with preconditions, ordered actions, postconditions, and temporal statistics) — all maintained as natural-language entries with Beta(α,β) reliability posteriors. The core inference loop is a Belief-Action-Verification (BAV) cycle: Belief retrieves relevant affordances and episodic events and generates ranked goal hypotheses; Action selects a procedural script via a Bayesian expected-utility score balancing semantic relevance, posterior reliability, past-failure risk, and entropy-based exploration; Verification checks whether the script's expected postconditions appear in observed descriptions (semantic similarity ≥ 0.8) and whether action durations fall within corpus-derived ±2σ temporal windows. Verification outcomes update the Beta posteriors incrementally, enabling continual adaptation without any gradient-based retraining. When repeated failures trigger meta-reflection, Gemma3-4B performs linguistic error analysis on the decision trace and contrastive refinement extracts positive/negative discriminators from success-versus-failure narratives to refine script preconditions, with script versioning to allow rollback.

## Results

LINGUA (Gemma3-4B, 4-bit) achieves 82.4% average accuracy on NExT-QA (Temporal 78.5%, Causal 81.3%, Descriptive 87.4%) and 42.3% Acc@GQA on NExT-GQA (requiring both correct answer and IoU ≥ 0.5 temporal localization), outperforming all 22 baselines including 7B–34B models. On egocentric benchmarks, it reaches 66.2% on EgoSchema (subset), 74.8% on IntentQA, and 31.7% Recall@1 (IoU 0.3) on Ego4D-NLQ. On Video-MME, LINGUA attains 68.5% overall (69.4% long-video), surpassing VITA-1.5 (7B) by +12.4pp and LLaVA-NeXT-Video (34B) by +16.5pp. The system runs 2.6× faster than dense-frame methods while retaining 94% of question-relevant events via event-driven perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINGUA (4B) outperforms all 22 baselines across every benchmark. On NExT-QA, it leads the strongest baseline VideoTree (75.6%) by +6.8pp and MUPA-7B (74.8%) by +7.6pp, with the largest margin on Temporal questions (+5.4pp over VideoTree). On NExT-GQA Acc@GQA, LINGUA (42.3%) surpasses MoReVQA (39.6%) by +2.7pp and MUPA-7B (30.3%) by +12.0pp, while also achieving the best IoU-based grounding (R@0.5: 36.5% vs. MUPA-7B's 27.3%). On egocentric tasks, LINGUA beats MSR-ViR-Large (14.2B) by +5.0pp on EgoSchema subset and +2.1pp on full, and outperforms Video-ChatGPT by +1.7pp on IntentQA. On Video-MME, the 4B LINGUA exceeds the 34B LLaVA-NeXT-Video by +16.5pp overall and the 7B VITA-1.5 by +12.4pp, and on the long-video subset it surpasses memory-based agents M3-Agent (+14.1pp) and WorldMM-8B (+3.4pp) despite a smaller backbone. Notably, upgrading the backbone from Gemma3-4B to 11B yields only +0.5pp on NExT-QA at 2.7× compute, confirming that the structured reasoning architecture—not model scale—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

LINGUA extends the procedural-memory framework for LLM agents (Forouzandeh et al., 2026) from text-only planning to video understanding, adapting Bayesian script selection and contrastive refinement to a multimodal setting. It draws on FrameNet's frame-semantic structures (Fillmore et al., 2002) and Schank & Abelson's script theory (2013) to structure episodic, semantic, and procedural memories in natural language. The key departure from prior VideoQA work—localize-then-answer pipelines, training-time grounding supervision (QGAC-TR, TimeCraft), and agentic multi-path reasoning (MUPA, Sarch et al.)—is treating grounding as an inference-time verification constraint within a Belief-Action-Verification loop rather than a preprocessing, post-hoc, or training-time alignment step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit linguistic belief-state reasoning with verification can close part of the grounding gap (reducing temporal and hallucination errors by roughly half) using a compact 4B model, suggesting that structured reasoning may partially substitute for parametric scale in grounded video understanding. However, the 40.1pp residual gap between answer accuracy (82.4%) and grounded accuracy (42.3%) remains substantial, and the 127-script library covers only 68% of causal questions, leaving temporal boundary precision and out-of-coverage generalization as open challenges. Whether this linguistic-verification paradigm scales to hour-long videos or truly novel procedural domains beyond the NExT-QA training distribution remains untested.
