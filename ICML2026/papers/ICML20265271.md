## Problem

Existing monocular geometry estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: deterministic regressors over-smooth fine geometric structures by predicting the mean of the output distribution, while latent diffusion models introduce reconstruction noise through VAE compression that caps attainable quality. The paper argues that both the architectural overhead of hybrid ViT+CNN designs with intricate loss functions and the VAE-based latent space are unnecessary for high-quality point map generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed to 256×256 or 512×512 via shorter-side resize and center crop) and produces a dense 3D point map of shape H×W×3 in camera coordinates, where each pixel encodes its (X, Y, Z) position. The output is affine-invariant (recovered up to unknown scale and shift), and sky points are projected onto a virtual sphere of radius 3 during training and discarded at inference if their norm exceeds 2.9. The model supports single-step feed-forward inference (competitive with prior methods) or multi-step Euler ODE integration for progressively sharper boundaries.

## Method

PointDiT is a plain Vision Transformer trained from scratch as a pixel-space Diffusion Transformer that operates directly on raw point map patches (size 16) without any VAE or tokenizer. It uses a flow matching formulation with x-prediction (predicting the clean point map rather than velocity), which the paper shows is critical for geometric data—v-prediction fails catastrophically. The noisy point map tokens are channel-concatenated with frozen DINOv3 features from four uniformly spaced intermediate layers (following DPT's layer selection), fused via a linear projection, and processed by standard Transformer blocks. Training uses a logit-normal noise schedule with a 10% probability of forcing t=0 to resolve a train-test discrepancy, a velocity-space MSE loss (v-loss) despite x-prediction, and a lightweight relative point loss (λ=0.1) that normalizes per-pixel error by target magnitude to emphasize local detail. Point maps are standardized per-sample (centroid subtraction and mean-distance scaling) before training to match the noise prior's scale.

## Results

On seven real-world evaluation datasets (3,444 samples at 512×512), PointDiT-H achieves the best depth accuracy (Rel^d = 2.75, δ₁^d = 98.54) and the best point-map accuracy (δ₁^p = 98.02) among all methods. PointDiT-L attains the highest boundary sharpness (BF1 = 10.50), surpassing the best baseline (Depth Pro, 9.41) by over 1 point. Notably, even a single-step PointDiT-H inference (72 ms) already outperforms all prior methods on BF1 and depth metrics, while the full 4-step variant (204 ms) remains far faster than the latent-diffusion baseline GeometryCrafter (1,178 ms). MoGe retains a slight edge on point-map relative error (Rel^p = 4.21 vs. 4.40), but PointDiT is more accurate on every depth metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the latent-diffusion baseline GeometryCrafter, PointDiT-H wins decisively on every metric: Rel^d drops from 3.52 to 2.75, δ₁^d rises from 97.84 to 98.54, and BF1 jumps from 4.64 to 10.49, while inference is 16× faster (204 ms vs. 1,178 ms). Versus the closest pixel-space diffusion method PPD, PointDiT-H improves Rel^p from 5.54 to 4.40, Rel^d from 3.88 to 2.75, and BF1 from 9.28 to 10.49, at roughly 2× lower latency. Against the strongest deterministic regressors, MoGe and MoGe-2, PointDiT-H is superior on all depth metrics (Rel^d 2.75 vs. 3.10/2.90; δ₁^d 98.54 vs. 98.01/98.45) and on BF1 (10.49 vs. 5.61/7.40), but MoGe retains a small lead on point-map relative error (Rel^p 4.21 vs. 4.40). PointDiT-L matches or exceeds PointDiT-H on BF1 (10.50 vs. 10.49) at less than half the parameters (771M vs. 1,807M), making it the best accuracy-to-cost trade-off. The paper also notes that on outdoor-heavy datasets (KITTI, DIODE, ETH3D), PointDiT trails the best regression baselines, attributing this gap to limited outdoor coverage in the synthetic training mixture.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointDiT extends the pixel-space diffusion philosophy of JiT (Li & He, 2026) from 2D image generation to dense 3D point map estimation, adopting its x-prediction objective and plain ViT backbone. It departs from the dominant latent diffusion paradigm for geometry (GeometryCrafter, Marigold) by eliminating the VAE entirely, and from deterministic regression (MoGe, MoGe-2) by casting point map prediction as a probabilistic flow-matching problem. For conditioning, it bridges representation learning (DINOv3 multi-layer features) with diffusion training in a spirit similar to RAE and REPA, but trains end-to-end with a single lightweight auxiliary loss rather than multi-stage optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a from-scratch pixel-space ViT with x-prediction matches or exceeds complex latent-diffusion and hybrid-regression baselines while being architecturally far simpler challenges the assumption that VAE compression is necessary for geometric generation, pointing toward a cleaner paradigm for future 3D/4D diffusion models. Practically, single-step inference at 72 ms (PointDiT-H) with all-zeros initialization makes the model deployable as a near-deterministic estimator, and the framework's plain-ViT backbone naturally extends to multi-modality (RGB appearance, camera parameters) and multi-view settings. Key open questions include generalization to mixed resolutions, improved outdoor performance (currently limited by synthetic training coverage), and whether the pixel-space advantage persists at higher resolutions or with more complex scene representations.
