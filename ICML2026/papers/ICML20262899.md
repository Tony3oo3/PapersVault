## Problem

Recurrent spiking neural networks based on vanilla LIF neurons struggle with long-sequence modeling because the membrane leak causes rapid forgetting of earlier inputs and backpropagation through time suffers from vanishing gradients. Existing remedies—adaptive thresholds (ALIF), compartmental dynamics (TC-LIF), or adaptive recurrent dynamics (RadLIF)—add neuron-level complexity that inflates computational cost and complicates neuromorphic hardware deployment, undermining the efficiency advantage of spiking computation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw sequential inputs directly without spike encoding: spike-train sequences over 700 channels (SHD, SSC), a permuted 784-step flattened MNIST image (PS-MNIST), or paired binary sequences of length T (Binary Adding). A two-layer LRMM backbone with 128 cells per layer processes the sequence, and a lightweight task-specific readout head produces a class label. At inference, the modulation function switches to a piecewise-linear clamp, making the entire forward pass implementable with only integer addition, multiplication, and bitwise shifts on neuromorphic ALUs.

## Method

The core contribution is the LIF Recurrent Memory Module (LRMM), a four-neuron micro-circuit (input N_I, memory N_M, context N_C, output N_O) built entirely from vanilla LIF neurons. A local recurrent loop between N_M and N_C provides long-term information retention without explicit gating units, while three modulatory currents (I_I, I_F, I_O) computed from the current input and previous output spike dynamically regulate what is integrated, stored, and read out. The modulation function Φ interpolates between sigmoid and piecewise-linear during training (annealed m from 0 to 1), ensuring hardware-friendly inference. A theoretical BPTT analysis shows the N_M–N_C loop introduces an effective temporal gain G[t] = α(1−σ′) + σ′σ′w_C,M·w_M,C that adds a near-threshold pass-through beyond the leak-only decay, reducing zero-gain segments and mitigating vanishing gradients. The full architecture stacks two LRMM layers with global recurrent connections, trained end-to-end via BPTT with boxcar surrogate gradients and Adam.

## Results

On PS-MNIST, LRMM achieves 96.52% accuracy with 0.16M parameters, outperforming TC-LIF (95.36%), ALIF (94.30%), and LSTM (92.90%). On SHD, LRMM reaches 94.70% with 0.43M parameters, matching RadLIF (94.62%) while using 9× fewer parameters (3.9M). On SSC, LRMM attains 79.75% with 0.22M parameters, surpassing RadLIF (77.40%, 3.9M) and SpikGRU (77.00%). On Binary Adding, LRMM achieves 99.55%, slightly exceeding DH-LIF (99.35%). In energy estimation on SHD, LRMM consumes 76.11 nJ per step, approximately 2.45× less than LIF, 2.79× less than TC-LIF, and 277× less than LSTM (21,145 nJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRMM outperforms all neuron-centric SNN baselines (LIF, TC-LIF, ALIF, GLIF, PLIF, CLIF, DH-LIF, adLIF) across all four benchmarks, with the largest margins on SSC (79.75% vs. 61.09% for TC-LIF, 74.20% for ALIF) and Binary Adding (99.55% vs. 19.90% for TC-LIF, 68.00% for ALIF). Against the parameter-heavy RadLIF (3.9M params), LRMM matches or exceeds accuracy on SHD (94.70% vs. 94.62%) and SSC (79.75% vs. 77.40%) while using roughly 9× fewer parameters. LRMM also surpasses standard ANN recurrent baselines: LSTM (92.90% on PS-MNIST, 89.20% on SHD, 73.10% on SSC) and SpikGRU (77.00% on SSC), while consuming 277× less estimated energy than LSTM. The one notable loss is against Event-SSM on SSC (88.4% vs. 79.75%) and PS-MNIST (97.17% vs. 96.52%), though LRMM uses 588× less energy (76.11 nJ vs. 44,788 nJ) and 2.7× fewer parameters. Against EGRU, LRMM wins on all three shared benchmarks (SSC: 79.75% vs. 71.06%; PS-MNIST: 96.52% vs. 95.1%; SHD: 94.70% vs. 90.47%). On the copy-task long-term memory benchmark, LRMM substantially outperforms a much larger ALIF baseline (0.89 vs. 0.58 at L=8, K=8; 0.67 vs. 0.43 at L=10, K=10), confirming the memory loop's effectiveness for extended-delay retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant neuron-centric line of work (ALIF, TC-LIF, RadLIF) that embeds long-term memory into individual spiking neuron dynamics, instead proposing an architecture-level solution: a four-neuron local recurrent memory module (LRMM) built entirely from vanilla LIF neurons. Conceptually, it draws on LSTM's gated cell-state idea but replaces explicit sigmoid/tanh gates with structured spike-driven interactions among LIF units, making it natively compatible with neuromorphic hardware that only supports low-precision element-wise operations. The BPTT stability analysis extends the surrogate-gradient literature by showing how a local N_M–N_C loop introduces an additional gradient pass-through that reduces zero-gain segments, complementing prior work on locally recurrent motifs and small-world SNN topologies that lacked an explicit long-term memory mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LRMM offers a practical path toward hardware-deployable long-sequence SNNs by demonstrating that compact, structured local recurrence among vanilla LIF neurons can match or exceed complex neuron models (ALIF, TC-LIF, RadLIF) on accuracy while consuming an estimated 277× less energy than LSTM. Key open questions include scalability to ultra-long sequences (language modeling, long-context reasoning), validation on actual neuromorphic hardware where routing overhead and precision constraints may erode the estimated efficiency gains, and whether the fixed four-neuron topology generalizes to larger hierarchical memory systems. The paper's energy figures are operation-counting estimates rather than measured hardware consumption, so the practical efficiency advantage remains to be confirmed on real devices.
