## Problem

Existing multi-policy MORL methods rely on evolutionary frameworks that maintain large self-evolving policy populations, requiring massive parallel agent-environment interactions and precluding the use of sample-efficient offline RL algorithms. This makes them impractical for real-world deployment where interactions are expensive, and the population-based selection mechanism causes over-optimization of frequently selected policies while stagnating others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266013/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes environment state vectors (continuous or discrete) and, in offline mode, a pre-collected transition dataset D from a replay buffer. It outputs a set of non-dominated policies F (the Pareto-approximation policy set) together with their objective-space mapping N, which approximates the Pareto front. Unlike single-policy MORL that returns one policy per preference, MPFT returns a full set of policies spanning the trade-off surface, with the number of policies determined by the tracking budget Ψ_i and sparse-region count K rather than a fixed population size.

## Method

MPFT replaces population evolution with a four-stage tracking procedure: (1) train m single-objective policies to obtain approximate Pareto-vertex anchors; (2) from each vertex, sequentially generate a chain of policies by alternating u episodes of Pareto-reverse updates (sacrificing one objective to move along the front) with v episodes of Pareto-ascent updates (improving all objectives equally via the minimum-norm gradient combination); (3) identify the top-K sparse regions in the tracked front and retrain K new Pareto-interior policies via an objective weight adjustment method that iteratively projects the current return vector toward the region's bounding simplex; (4) union all tracked policies with dominated-policy deletion. The Pareto-tracking mechanism is proven to converge exponentially to an O(μ+τ) neighborhood of the Pareto-stationary set via a Lyapunov analysis using Moreau-Yosida regularization and averaging theory for the switched reverse/ascent dynamics. The framework is algorithm-agnostic: each tracking episode can be filled by any actor-critic method, and the paper provides multi-objective adaptations of PPO (MOPPO), SAC (MOSAC), and TD7 (MOTD7), with MOTD7 achieving up to 78% fewer environment steps than evolutionary baselines.

## Results

On six robotic control environments with ≤3 objectives, MPFT-MOTD7 achieves the best hypervolume (HV) and expected utility (EU) across all tasks, e.g., HV of 7.04×10⁶ on Ant-2 (vs. 0.94×10⁶ for C-MORL) and 9.61×10¹⁰ on Hopper-3 (vs. 7.62×10¹⁰ for PGMORL). Agent-environment interactions are reduced by up to 78.22% (MPFT-MOTD7), 72.04% (MPFT-MOSAC), and 66.27% (MPFT-MOPPO) compared to evolutionary baselines. On >3-objective tasks, even the single-policy SPFT variant outperforms C-MORL and GPI-LS on HV and EU while using fewer env_steps (e.g., 67,200 vs. 500,000 on FT-6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPFT-MOTD7 consistently outperforms all three evolutionary baselines (PGMORL, PA2D-MORL, C-MORL) on HV and EU across all six ≤3-objective environments, with the largest gap on Ant-2 where HV is 7.04×10⁶ vs. 0.94×10�6 for C-MORL (a ~7.5× improvement). The sample-efficiency gain is substantial: MPFT-MOTD7 requires only 11.2M env_steps on Walker2d-2 versus 41.9M for all evolutionary baselines (73% reduction), and 19.2M vs. 85.2M on Hopper-2 and Ant-2 (77% reduction). On Swimmer-2, HV gains are marginal (3.54 vs. 3.52×10⁶) because the Pareto front is nearly flat, but env_steps are still reduced by 81%. For >3-objective tasks, the single-policy SPFT variant beats C-MORL on HV (5.02 vs. 3.67×10⁴ on FT-6; 1.24 vs. 1.12×10⁹ on LL-4; 8.22 vs. 7.93×10³¹ on B-9) while using 7–87% fewer env_steps. GPI-LS times out on B-9 (9 objectives) and Envelope is not applicable, highlighting scalability limitations of non-evolutionary baselines in high-dimensional objective spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPFT departs fundamentally from the dominant evolutionary-framework paradigm in multi-policy MORL (PGMORL, PA2D-MORL, C-MORL), which maintains a self-evolving policy population with selection and crossover. Instead, it introduces a population-free Pareto-tracking mechanism that starts from single-objective extreme (Pareto-vertex) policies and traces the front via alternating Pareto-reverse and Pareto-ascent updates, building on the Pareto-ascent direction from PA2D-MORL (Hu & Luo, 2024) and the continuity result for MOMDP Pareto fronts from Li et al. (2025). The framework also repurposes single-objective offline RL algorithms—TD7, SAC, PPO—by deriving multi-objective variants (MOTD7, MOSAC, MOPPO) that plug directly into the tracking loop, making the approach algorithm-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~78% reduction in agent-environment interactions makes multi-policy MORL substantially more deployable in settings where sampling is expensive (robotic control, autonomous driving), and the compatibility with offline RL opens the door to leveraging pre-collected datasets. The exponential convergence guarantee to a neighborhood of the Pareto-stationary set provides theoretical grounding absent in most evolutionary MORL methods. Open questions include whether the local-convexity assumption (A1) holds broadly in non-convex landscapes, whether the heuristic sparse-region detection (Delaunay triangulation, PCA projection) scales rigorously beyond m=3, and whether the tracking mechanism's performance degrades when the Pareto front is highly disconnected or non-smooth.
