## Problem

Conformal prediction (CP) is traditionally formulated via p-values, but p-values lack flexible aggregation rules under dependence, limiting their use in multi-model or multi-fold settings. While e-value-based CP has been proposed, existing p-to-e calibrators are not set-preserving—they inflate prediction sets relative to the original p-value CP—and no principled bridge connecting the two formulations while maintaining statistical efficiency had been established.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a conformal p-variable P_n computed from a nonconformity score, a calibration set of size n, and a target miscoverage level α (with the mild technical condition α(n+1)∉ℕ). It outputs a calibrated e-variable E_{n,α} = F_{n,α}(P_n) that is exact, strictly positive, and induces exactly the same prediction set as the original p-value threshold. In aggregation settings (CCP, CA), multiple such e-variables from different folds or models are merged via weighted arithmetic means, optionally with a uniform randomization variable U~Unif(0,1) for tighter sets.

## Method

The paper introduces a set-preserving P2E calibrator F_{n,α}(p) = (1/α)·(1+exp(C_{n,α}(α−s)))/(1+exp(C_{n,α}(p−s))), a sigmoid-like function parameterized by calibration size n, miscoverage α, a location parameter s∈(α, ⌈α(n+1)⌉/(n+1)), and a constant C_{n,α}>0 chosen so the resulting e-variable is exact. The key insight is that among left-continuous p-to-e calibrators, only the all-or-nothing (AoN) calibrator is set-preserving, but AoN takes zero values that destroy evidence in products and sequential settings; the proposed calibrator is strictly positive, smooth, invertible, and dominates AoN pointwise, guaranteeing smaller aggregated prediction sets. The framework is then applied to cross-conformal prediction (ECCP, using UR-MI and exchangeable Markov inequalities) and conformal aggregation (WECA, using data-dependent weighted e-value merging with a tuning/inference split to preserve validity), yielding 1−α coverage guarantees with improved efficiency over p-value baselines.

## Results

In cross-conformal prediction (CCP, K=10, RF, α=0.1), the proposed ECCP method achieves mean prediction set lengths of 6.70±0.09 (Abalone) and 9.76±0.63 (Boston) with empirical coverage ≈0.90, while classic p-to-e calibrators F₁, F₂, F₃ inflate sets by 3–7× (e.g., 29.07 and 67.15 on the same datasets). In conformal aggregation (CA, α=0.05), UR-WECA produces the tightest prediction sets across four OpenML datasets (e.g., 4.93±1.86 on 361244) while maintaining the 0.95 coverage guarantee, compared to 6.90±1.34 for majority vote (CM) and 5.23±1.48 for COLA-S. All methods satisfy the finite-sample 1−α coverage guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original CCP baseline (Vovk 2015) and the improved variants of Gasparin & Ramdas (2025) (mod-cross, e-mod-cross, u-mod-cross, eu-mod-cross), ECCP(2α) consistently yields the smallest prediction sets among all 1−2α methods across OLS, RF, and Lasso on Boston, Abalone, and Parkinsons (e.g., 6.55 vs. 7.99–9.14 on Boston/RF). In the 1−α CCP setting, ECCP with the P2E calibrator outperforms ECCP(AoN) in set size (e.g., 10.13 vs. 12.66 on Boston/RF), while both dramatically outperform classic calibrators F₁, F₂, F₃ (63–72 in set length). In conformal aggregation, UR-WECA beats majority vote (CM) and its randomized variant (CR) by 25–28% in prediction set length on the largest dataset (361244: 4.93 vs. 6.90 and 6.58), and also outperforms the p-value averaging baseline PAgg (6.91) and the recent COLA-S method (5.23). The p-value-based PAgg baseline over-covers (0.98–0.99 vs. target 0.95), reflecting its conservative thresholding at α/2, whereas WECA/UR-WECA hit the nominal coverage exactly. Overall, the P2E calibrator's set-preservation property is the key differentiator: it avoids the 3–7× inflation seen with standard p-to-e calibrators while unlocking e-value merging and randomization benefits unavailable to p-value-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected threads in conformal prediction: the classical p-value formulation (Vovk et al. 2005; Papadopoulos et al. 2002) and the newer e-value formulation (Vovk & Wang 2021; Balinsky & Balinsky 2024; Gauthier et al. 2025). It identifies a concrete limitation of existing p-to-e calibrators (Vovk & Wang 2021; Ramdas & Wang 2025) — they are not set-preserving and inflate prediction sets — and proposes a sigmoid-based P2E calibrator that is set-preserving, exact, and strictly positive. The e-value merging machinery (Wang 2025; Ramdas & Manole 2026) and randomized Markov inequalities are then leveraged to upgrade cross-conformal prediction (Vovk 2015; Gasparin & Ramdas 2025) and conformal aggregation (Gasparin & Ramdas 2024) from approximate 1−2α guarantees to exact 1−α guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The set-preservation property provides a clean structural criterion for choosing p-to-e calibrators in CP, resolving an open question about whether e-value-based CP necessarily sacrifices efficiency relative to p-value CP. The practical impact is substantial: the proposed ECCP and WECA methods achieve the same 1−α coverage as p-value baselines while being 4–10× more efficient than naively calibrated e-value alternatives, and they unlock e-value-specific tools (admissible merging under arbitrary dependence, UR-MI randomization, sequential evidence accumulation) that were previously inaccessible to CP. Limitations include the mild α(n+1)∉ℕ technical condition and the need to numerically solve for the sigmoid parameter C_{n,α}. Open directions flagged by the authors include post-hoc data-adaptive thresholding, randomized e-CP variants, and extension to classification and sequential settings.
