## Problem

Existing unsupervised diffusion solvers for combinatorial optimization either propagate dense terminal rewards backward through all intermediate steps via policy gradients (high variance, poor scalability) or rely on purely local objectives that cannot capture long-term dependencies. The paper asks how adjoint-based trajectory optimization—a low-variance, path-gradient method well-established in continuous domains—can be adapted to discrete combinatorial state spaces where conventional gradients are undefined.

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem instance (graph adjacency matrix for MIS/Max Cut, city coordinates for TSP/CVRP) and a current binary solution state x ∈ {0,1}^N, and produces updated coordinate-wise flip probabilities via a GNN-parameterized CTMC. Transitions are restricted to the 1-Hamming neighborhood (single-coordinate flips), and the only external signal required during training is a single terminal flip-gradient evaluation g(x^(i)) − g(x) at the final state, rather than dense feedback at every intermediate step. For QUBO-structured problems the flip-gradient has a closed form; for general CO (e.g., TSP) a surrogate is constructed from a local-search improvement of the terminal solution.

## Method

CAM formulates discrete diffusion as a stochastic optimal control problem over CTMCs and derives a 'discrete adjoint'—the difference in cost-to-go between two 1-Hamming-neighbor states—that satisfies a backward recursion. Under the low-temperature optimal control, this recursion simplifies to a 'lean adjoint' that can be estimated from a single sampled trajectory: at time t, the lean adjoint for coordinate i is approximately (−1)^(X_t,i ⊕ X_1,i) · (g(X_1^(i)) − g(X_1)), i.e., the terminal flip-gradient with a sign determined by whether coordinate i was flipped along the trajectory. The training objective is a coordinate-wise Bernoulli matching loss where the GNN predicts integrated transition rates per diffusion step, and the adjoint term provides a structured, low-variance trajectory-level signal that guides intermediate controls toward improving the final solution. This requires only one terminal objective evaluation per trajectory (versus 50 for RLNN), and the primary performance gain comes from the adjoint propagation rather than the KL/entropy regularization terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAM consistently outperforms all unsupervised diffusion baselines (DiffUCO, SDDS, RLNN) across every CO problem and scale. On MIS ER-[9000-11000], CAM (0.00% gap) surpasses the OR solver KaMIS (0.80% gap) and the best UL baseline RLNN (2.60% gap) by a wide margin. On TSP-1000, CAM (2.51% gap) beats SDDS (3.37%) and DIMES (7.70%), though supervised DIFUSCO (3.16%) and FMIP (2.98%) remain competitive. On Max Cut BA-[800-1200], CAM (0.00% gap) outperforms both the OR solver MQLib (0.10%) and all learning-based baselines. On CVRP-100, CAM (2.70% gap) improves over SDDS (3.86%) and approaches supervised DIFUSCO (2.63%), while supervised FMIP (3.08%) is slightly worse. Notably, CAM requires only a single terminal gradient evaluation per trajectory during training, whereas RLNN requires gradient feedback at all 50 intermediate diffusion steps, making CAM substantially more data-efficient in terms of environment queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adjoint-based trajectory optimization framework from continuous diffusion/flow models (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024, 2025) to discrete combinatorial domains by formulating diffusion-based CO as a stochastic optimal control problem over Continuous-Time Markov Chains and deriving a discrete adjoint recursion. It departs from prior unsupervised diffusion CO methods—DiffUCO (entropy-regularized RL), SDDS (importance-weighted matching), and RLNN (local Langevin objective)—by replacing dense per-step reward propagation or importance-weighted sampling with a single terminal flip-gradient signal propagated backward through the trajectory via the discrete adjoint. The theoretical contribution is the 'lean adjoint' (Equation 20), which eliminates the need for counterfactual trajectory sampling by exploiting the fixed-point structure of the optimal CTMC control in the low-temperature limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAM provides a principled, low-variance training signal that requires only a single terminal objective evaluation per trajectory, substantially reducing the environment feedback needed compared to RL- or importance-sampling-based alternatives. The empirical results—particularly beating the KaMIS OR solver on large-scale ER graphs and the clean ablation showing adjoint propagation (not terminal heuristic quality) drives most of the performance gain—suggest that trajectory-level gradient structure is the key ingredient for effective unsupervised diffusion CO training. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended beyond QUBO-tractable problems without problem-specific local search heuristics, whether the low-temperature theoretical assumptions hold for highly non-convex NP-hard landscapes, and whether more general discrete path-gradient estimators can replace the current surrogate flip-gradient construction.
