## Problem

Building high-quality, diverse RF sensing datasets is prohibitively expensive, and existing simulation approaches either neglect multipath propagation from the surrounding environment or rely on learning-based synthesis that requires large training sets and cannot generalize across sensor configurations. Critically, prior ray-tracing simulators cast rays stochastically, breaking the spatial and temporal phase coherence that is essential for phase-sensitive RF tasks like imaging, Doppler estimation, and vital sign monitoring.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a free-text environment prompt (e.g., 'a living room with a sectional sofa') and an arbitrary sensor configuration (antenna positions, orientations, gain patterns, frequency band, sampling rate) as input. It produces an explicit 3D scene mesh with assigned dielectric properties, SMPL-parameterized human motion sequences of variable duration, and phase-coherent channel impulse responses (CIRs) that can be convolved with any transmitted waveform to yield received RF signals. The sensor configuration is fully flexible, supporting arbitrary radar poses, multi-antenna arrays, and diverse RF protocols without retraining.

## Method

WAVEVERSE combines two core components. First, a 4D world generator uses LLMs to produce 3D indoor layouts (via Holodeck), SMPL body shapes, motion descriptions, and spatial paths (A* waypoints), then a state-aware causal transformer (8-layer, built on T2M-GPT with VQ-VAE tokenization) autoregressively generates motion tokens conditioned on CLIP text embeddings and path waypoints, with each prediction additionally conditioned on the current 2D spatial state to enforce path adherence; a path-masking strategy during training prevents overfitting to path conditions. Second, a phase-coherent ray tracing engine replaces stochastic ray casting with a reference-radar scheme: paths are traced once from a geometric center and then modified for each radar pose by swapping Tx/Rx endpoints while preserving surface interaction points (spatial coherence), and for moving humans, ray hits are expanded over semantically coherent vertex groups that persist across frames (temporal coherence), ensuring consistent phase evolution. The resulting CIRs model multipath propagation with frequency-dependent dielectric properties for 24 materials, and signals are obtained by convolving the CIR with the transmitted waveform.

## Results

On HumanML3D, WAVEVERSE achieves the best R-Precision (0.755), FID (0.238), and lowest path/ending errors (>20 cm: 0.208/0.325) among all baselines. In the RF imaging case study, augmenting with 4× WAVEVERSE-simulated data reduces MAE from 20.10 cm to 18.08 cm (18.12% reduction) and improves PSNR by 1.51 dB, while Standard RT data degrades performance. For human activity recognition, WAVEVERSE lifts accuracy from 31.6% to 71.6% with 19× simulated data (a 17.0 pp gain), whereas RF Genesis plateaus at 54.6%. Simulated signals achieve 93.65% energy similarity to real measurements and 33.57 dB PSNR against Ansys HFSS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAVEVERSE outperforms all four motion generation baselines on every metric. Against MDM (Tevet et al., 2023), it improves R-Precision by 0.036 and reduces path error >20 cm from 0.547 to 0.208; against OmniControl (Xie et al., 2024), it achieves lower FID (0.238 vs 0.319) and ending error >60 cm (0.111 vs 0.152); against MotionLCM (Dai et al., 2024), it nearly triples FID quality (0.238 vs 0.754); and against T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023), its base model, it gains 0.064 R-Precision and 0.197 lower path error >20 cm. In the RF imaging case study, WAVEVERSE-simulated data consistently improves MAE, Q-90th, and PSNR at all augmentation levels, while Standard RT (Ren et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025) degrades performance (e.g., MAE worsens from 20.10 to 22.28 at 4× sim). For activity recognition, WAVEVERSE reaches 71.6% accuracy at 19× augmentation versus RF Genesis (Chen & Zhang, 2023) at 54.6%, and unlike RF Genesis, WAVEVERSE continues to scale without plateauing.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAVEVERSE extends the autoregressive motion generation framework of T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023) by replacing time-indexed trajectory conditioning with spatial path waypoints and introducing a state-aware causal transformer that conditions each token prediction on the current spatial state. For environment generation, it builds on the Holodeck pipeline (Yang et al., 2024) for 3D scene synthesis. The key departure from prior RF simulation work (CASTER, RF Genesis, RF-Diffusion) is the introduction of phase-coherent ray tracing that preserves spatial and temporal phase consistency across radar positions and over time, eliminating the need for learning-based signal refinement that data-driven methods require. The path-based conditioning strategy also departs from trajectory-conditioned motion generation (OmniControl, MotionLCM, MDM) by providing only spatial guidance without prescribing velocity or duration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental bottleneck in RF sensing research: the lack of large-scale, diverse, and hardware-flexible training data. The phase-coherent formulation is critical for phase-sensitive applications (imaging, vital sign monitoring, Doppler estimation) that prior stochastic ray tracing could not support, and the validation against both real measurements (93.65% energy similarity) and HFSS (33.57 dB PSNR in <1 s vs. >1 h) provides strong evidence of fidelity. The finding that standard ray tracing data actively degrades downstream performance while WAVEVERSE data scales effectively highlights that physical correctness, not just data volume, is what matters for RF augmentation. Open questions include extending to fine-grained human-object interactions, modeling diffraction and refraction, and whether lightweight real-data-driven refinement could further close the remaining gap to full-wave solvers.
