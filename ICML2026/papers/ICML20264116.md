## Problem

Few-shot segmentation with frozen vision foundation models (SAM + DINOv2) requires high-quality point prompts, but existing prompt construction methods—whether attention-based, heuristic, or single-agent RL—fail to simultaneously balance semantic consistency and spatial coverage, and suffer from credit assignment ambiguity where individual prompt utility is entangled with the rest of the prompt set. This paper tackles the gap by formulating prompt optimization as a cooperative game with decoupled optimization objectives and explicit per-prompt attribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single annotated reference image–mask pair and an unannotated target image as input. Both images are discretized into non-overlapping 14×14 patches, and DINOv2 patch embeddings plus spatial centroids form a dual relational graph over target patches. The output is a set of optimized positive and negative point prompts (node activations and prunings on the graph) that are fed to the frozen SAM image encoder to produce the final pixel-level segmentation mask, with no parameter updates to either foundation model during inference.

## Method

PromptPilot formulates prompt optimization as sequential decision-making on a dual relational graph (feature distances from DINOv2 embeddings and spatial distances from patch centroids). Two specialized DQN actor agents—the Feature agent (minimizing intra-class feature variance, maximizing inter-class margin) and the Physical agent (maximizing same-class spatial dispersion, minimizing cross-class spatial distance)—independently propose activation or pruning actions under decoupled intrinsic rewards. A centralized Manager agent arbitrates between the two proposals using a hybrid reward that combines global SAM DSC feedback with a leave-one-out marginal contribution score (smoothed by EMA) for each prompt, enabling credit assignment under the cooperative-game framing. The selected actor receives an additional global reward term while the unselected actor updates only on its intrinsic reward, coupling local geometric objectives with the final segmentation objective without any foundation-model parameter updates.

## Results

On natural image segmentation, PromptPilot achieves 61.3% mIoU on PASCAL VOC and 47.1% mIoU on COCO, setting new state-of-the-art among automated (training-free at inference) methods and narrowing the gap to manual SAM-Box prompts (72.8% and 64.5% mIoU respectively). On medical benchmarks, it reaches 60.6% mIoU on GBM (surpassing SAM-LoRA's 55.5%) and 81.7% mIoU on JSRT (comparable to the manual-prompt reference of 81.6%). On video segmentation, it obtains 80.1% J and 81.8% F on DAVIS 2016, and 69.3% J on DAVIS 2017, outperforming all automated baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against attention-based methods (PerSAM, VRP-SAM), PromptPilot achieves large margins: on COCO it reaches 47.1% mIoU versus 22.7% (PerSAM) and 19.2% (VRP-SAM), and on GBM it scores 60.6% versus 33.2% and 12.3% respectively, indicating that cross-attention correspondence is unreliable under domain shift. Compared to heuristic prompt selection (Matcher, GBMSeg), PromptPilot wins on all six image datasets, with the largest gaps on COCO (+8.7 mIoU over Matcher) and Kvasir (+10.6 mIoU over Matcher), showing that static clustering or fixed sampling rules cannot adapt to complex scenes. Against the closest single-agent RL baseline FM-PPO, PromptPilot improves mIoU by 8.5 points on VOC, 16.7 points on COCO, and 7.5 points on GBM, confirming that decomposing optimization into feature and physical subspaces resolves the semantic-vs-spatial conflict that a monolithic policy cannot. On medical benchmarks, PromptPilot surpasses the parameter-updated SAM-LoRA on GBM (60.6% vs 55.5% mIoU) and matches it on JSRT (81.7% vs 81.6%), while remaining fully parameter-free at inference. On DAVIS video segmentation, PromptPilot outperforms Matcher by 4.2 J on DAVIS 2017 (69.3 vs 65.1) and by 1.6 J on DAVIS 2016 (80.1 vs 78.5), demonstrating robustness to multi-object interactions and temporal appearance changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptPilot extends the line of automated prompt construction for SAM-based few-shot segmentation, which has progressed from attention-based correspondence (PerSAM, VRP-SAM) through heuristic selection (Matcher, GBMSeg) to single-agent reinforcement learning (FM-PPO). The key departure is the decomposition of a monolithic policy into a hierarchical multi-agent architecture: a Feature agent and a Physical agent optimize decoupled objectives (semantic discriminability vs. spatial coverage) on a dual relational graph, while a centralized Manager agent arbitrates using SAM segmentation feedback and a leave-one-out marginal contribution estimate. This game-theoretic framing—treating the prompt set as a coalition and each prompt as a player—directly addresses the credit assignment ambiguity that limits prior single-agent RL approaches, and the use of SAM's DSC as a global reward aligns the optimization criterion with the actual segmentation objective rather than proxy geometric measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a parameter-free, inference-time optimization strategy that generalizes across natural, medical, and video domains without retraining the foundation model, which is practically valuable in annotation-expensive settings like medical imaging. The plug-and-play result—recovering 61.2% average mIoU from a 25.0% coarse UNet initialization, versus only 28.6% for single-agent PPO—suggests the multi-agent decomposition is substantially more robust to poor prompt initialization than monolithic policies. Open questions include whether the LOO approximation remains reliable as prompt set sizes grow, whether the feature/physical decomposition is the optimal split for other foundation models, and whether the RL training cost (200 iterations × 100 steps per episode) limits deployment in latency-sensitive applications.
