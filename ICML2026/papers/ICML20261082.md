## Problem

Current evaluation of multimodal large language models focuses on prediction accuracy without revealing how different sensory modalities contribute to those decisions. While decision-level Partial Information Decomposition has been applied to vision-language models, the omni-modal setting—where language specifies the task and video and audio provide sensory evidence under distinct roles—remains uncharacterized. The paper asks how omni-modal models actually use video and audio when those modalities play asymmetric roles under a fixed linguistic task specification.

## Input / Output

For omni-modal analysis, the method takes a video stream, audio stream, and textual instruction, producing a conditional PID decomposition (U_vis, U_aud, R_sens, S_av) of I(Y;V,A|T) along with layer-wise trajectories and modality-shuffling sensitivity measures. Variable-length sensory token sequences are summarized via mean pooling into fixed-dimensional representations, and partial-sensory conditions (e.g., p(Y|V,T)) are approximated through calibrated Gaussian embedding masking matched to per-dimension statistics over the profiling set. For the VLM reweighting component, the method additionally produces per-sample GapScore and Shortcut Score rankings that convert BATCH-induced local diagnostics into discrete training weights (3.0, 1.0, or 0.5).

## Method

The core contribution is Sensory PID, a conditional decomposition that treats the textual instruction as a fixed conditioning context rather than a symmetric third PID source, separating I(Y;V,A|T) into video-unique, audio-unique, redundant, and audiovisual-synergistic components. Estimation uses the BATCH estimator with Sinkhorn-normalized couplings, with a key modification: a continuous confidence-dependent stabilization that progressively shrinks low-confidence predictions toward uniform replaces the hard thresholding used in prior LVLM PID work, avoiding abrupt branch-specific changes when one sensory stream is removed. For the reweighting application, BATCH-induced local additive contributions (s_i, u_txt_i, u_vis_i, r_i) are averaged over 50 random batch contexts, non-negatively clipped, and combined with a fusion-potential measure (entropy reduction from joint versus partial predictions) to compute a GapScore that prioritizes samples with high fusion potential but limited current synergy and text-unique reliance. These scores drive a two-tier reweighting policy—upweighting synergy-gap samples at w=3.0 and downweighting language-shortcut samples at w=0.5—applied during lightweight LoRA fine-tuning on the final 20% of transformer layers.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, PID-guided LoRA fine-tuning (LoRA-PID) improves MMBench by +1.1 pp (89.1→90.2), MMStar by +2.3 pp (62.0→64.3, 95% CI [1.4, 3.2]), and POPE by +1.3 pp (87.2→88.5) over uniform LoRA, while incurring small trade-offs on MMMU (−0.5 pp) and PMC-VQA (−0.3 pp). Post-tuning PID diagnostics confirm the targeted shift: held-out MMStar synergy S_vl rises from 1.20 to 1.36 and text-unique U_txt drops from 0.56 to 0.46, increasing the synergy share from 67.5% to 73.9%. On the omni-modal side, Sensory PID reveals a sensory synergy bottleneck: even on AV-Fusion questions, modality-unique information (U_vis ≈ 1.35–1.42, U_aud ≈ 0.55–0.65) dominates over audiovisual synergy (S_av ≈ 0.21–0.32) across Qwen2.5-Omni and VITA-1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-PID outperforms all three matched reweighting baselines on MMStar: +2.8 pp over random reweighting (95% CI [1.6, 4.0]), +1.8 pp over accuracy-based difficulty mining (CI [0.7, 2.9]), and +1.3 pp over modality-ablation-based selection (CI [0.2, 2.4]), indicating the gain is not explained by non-uniform weighting, generic difficulty, or simple visual sensitivity alone. Against uniform LoRA, LoRA-PID wins on all three reasoning/grounding benchmarks (MMBench +1.1, MMStar +2.3, POPE +1.3) but loses slightly on prior-dominated benchmarks (MMMU −0.5, PMC-VQA −0.3), consistent with its downweighting of text-shortcut samples. Cross-model transfer is profile-dependent: LLaVA-OneVision-7B shows the same qualitative gains (MMStar 60.0→61.5, S_vl 1.00→1.10), whereas the strongly language-prior-reliant Gemma3-12B shows no benefit (MMStar 56.2→54.3, S_vl 0.20→0.21), suggesting the policy amplifies existing joint-modality capacity rather than creating it from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-level PID framework introduced by Xiu et al. (2026) for vision-language models to the omni-modal setting by formulating Sensory PID, a conditional decomposition of I(Y;V,A|T) that treats language as task context rather than a symmetric third source. The estimation pipeline builds directly on the BATCH estimator (Liang et al., 2023) and its Sinkhorn-normalized coupling, with the key methodological departure being the conditional formulation and a continuous confidence-dependent stabilization replacing the hard confidence gate used in prior LVLM analysis. The PID-guided reweighting policy repurposes BATCH-induced local diagnostic scores as training signals, connecting the information-theoretic diagnosis to a lightweight LoRA fine-tuning intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sensory synergy bottleneck finding—that even AV-fusion questions are dominated by modality-unique rather than jointly available information—challenges the assumption that architectural support for multiple sensory streams yields strong decision-level integration, and the visual-first layer dynamics suggest a concrete computational asymmetry that could guide architecture or training design. The profile-dependent boundary case in reweighting (effective for joint-modality-reliant models but not for language-prior-reliant ones) highlights that information-decomposition signals are not universally actionable. Key open questions include extending PID beyond multiple-choice to open-ended generation, whether the masking-based approximation of unimodal conditionals introduces systematic bias, and whether the reweighting policy scales to larger corpora or stronger adaptation methods.
