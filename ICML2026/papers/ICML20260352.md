## Problem

While structural parameter symmetries (e.g., hidden-unit permutations) are well understood, it remains unclear when architectural symmetry-breaking interventions actually yield consistent neuron-to-feature assignments across independent training runs, and how data geometry modulates this effectiveness. The paper addresses the gap between breaking symmetries in raw parameter space and breaking them effectively on the input support, where approximate, data-dependent symmetries can persist even after structural ones are removed.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a single symmetry-broken layer with trainable weights W, a fixed offset F, and a diagonal operator D, together with an input distribution P supported on a k-dimensional subspace U ⊂ R^d. Realization costs are computed as Mahalanobis seminorms ‖a − v_i‖_{S_i†} in the projected k-dimensional coordinate system, where S_i = U^T Diag(d_i)² U is the per-neuron Gram matrix. The key outputs are permutation sensitivity values Δ_{π,τ} quantifying the norm cost of reassigning features to different neurons, and a chord-deviation bound that translates into an upper bound on the LMC loss barrier along the linear interpolation path between two trained models.

## Method

The paper develops a geometric theory of effective function classes: each neuron i implements an affine subspace v_i + im(M_i) ⊂ R^k of preactivation coefficients on the input support, and the minimum weight norm to realize a target function is a Mahalanobis distance governed by the anisotropy of S_i. In the center-dominated regime (fixed F scale dominates trainable W scale), the permutation sensitivity Δ_π^out scales as μ_D^{-1}‖δ_π^out‖_F², where δ_π^out captures projected center differences, and the minimum center gap γ_out scales as Θ(σ_F √k · m^{-2/k}) for i.i.d. F entries. For LMC, the paper proves that ReLU chord deviation along the linear path W(λ) is bounded by O(β²‖FΣ^{1/2}‖_F²) in the center-dominated regime, and combines this with a convex-Lipschitz loss argument to yield an explicit LMC barrier bound. The theory is validated empirically on W-asymmetric and syre MLPs/ResNets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and synthetic Gaussian-mixture data, confirming that unaligned LMC requires both large σ_F and sufficient intrinsic data dimension k, and that subspace coherence ν(U) controls when the diagonal mask D alone can break symmetries.

## Results

The paper's main empirical finding is that unaligned LMC (low-loss linear interpolation without permutation alignment) is achieved only when the fixed weight scale σ_F is large enough (σ_F=1 for MLPs on MNIST, σ_F=2 for ResNets on CIFAR-10), while W-asymmetric models with σ_F=0 exhibit high LMC barriers comparable to standard symmetric models. On a synthetic Gaussian mixture dataset with controlled intrinsic dimension k, the midpoint accuracy drop under unaligned interpolation decreases sharply from 46.4 percentage points (k=2) to 15.7 pp (k=8) to 6.1 pp (k=32), confirming the theoretical prediction that neuron identifiability requires sufficient intrinsic dimension. Activation matching experiments show that for large σ_F, the identity permutation becomes nearly optimal (matching the optimal permutation's objective), while random permutations remain significantly worse, validating the neuron identifiability theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to W-asymmetric networks (Lim et al., 2024b), this paper shows that structural symmetry breaking alone (σ_F=0) does not yield unaligned LMC: the LMC barrier for W-MLPs with σ_F=0 is as high as for standard symmetric MLPs, and alignment reduces the barrier equally well for both. Only when σ_F is large enough to enter the center-dominated regime does unaligned LMC emerge, supporting the paper's central claim that structural ≠ effective symmetry breaking. Compared to syre (Ziyin et al., 2025), the paper finds that syre-ResNets still benefit from activation matching alignment, indicating that syre's random F and D perturbations do not fully break effective symmetries on the input support, unlike the larger-σ_F W-asymmetric setting. The paper further demonstrates that the diagonal operator D alone (F=0) can enable LMC on highly coherent data (ν(U)≈1) but fails on incoherent data, a regime not addressed by either prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of work on architectural symmetry breaking for neural networks, subsuming W-asymmetric networks (Lim et al., 2024b) and syre (Ziyin et al., 2025) under a single framework (Eq. 2) that replaces each weight matrix with a fixed offset plus a diagonal-scaled trainable part. While prior work demonstrated empirically that certain symmetry-breaking architectures yield unaligned linear mode connectivity (LMC), this paper provides the missing theoretical mechanism: it introduces 'neuron identifiability' via effective function classes and realization costs, showing that structural symmetry breaking in raw parameter space does not guarantee effective symmetry breaking on the data support. The analysis of LMC conditions (Thm. 5.1, Prop. 5.2) extends the LMC framework of Frankle et al. (2020) and Entezari et al. (2022) to the symmetry-broken regime, connecting chord deviation bounds to loss barriers along interpolation paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that structural symmetry breaking is necessary but not sufficient for unaligned LMC, and that data geometry (subspace coherence, intrinsic dimension) controls whether breaking is effective—provides practitioners with diagnostic criteria for when model merging without alignment should succeed or fail. The identified tradeoff between identifiability and feature learning (Sec. 4.5) suggests that aggressive symmetry breaking may limit expressivity, an important caveat for architecture design. Open questions include extending the theory to richer symmetry groups (beyond permutations), analyzing exact training dynamics rather than endpoint properties, and developing quantitative diagnostics that predict neuron identifiability for a given training setup without requiring the subspace support assumption.
