## Problem

Existing KB-VQA pipelines treat visual and textual knowledge base entries in isolation, relying on image-centric retrieval that is vulnerable to visual ambiguity when target entities occupy small portions of cluttered scenes. This modality-isolated representation prevents the system from grounding fine-grained entities as unified multimodal concepts, leading to incorrect entity identification and context selection for long-tail knowledge queries.

## Input / Output

The system takes a query image, a natural-language question, and a large-scale multimodal knowledge base organized at the entity level (each entity has multiple images and textual snippets, e.g., ~2M Wikipedia entities for E-VQA). It produces a structured text answer that includes a question-type classification and, for multi-hop queries, an intermediate sub-question before the final answer. The knowledge base is indexed via Faiss for efficient top-k retrieval, with reranking applied over the top 100 (E-VQA) or top 30 (InfoSeek) candidates.

## Method

EntRAG is a three-stage entity-centric RAG framework. First, EntBind introduces a dedicated [ENT] token into an MLLM vocabulary to anchor entity semantics, while a separate visual processor (EVA-CLIP-8B) extracts patch-level features that are aggregated via attention pooling conditioned on the projected [ENT] token embedding; the final entity embedding fuses the token and visual components and is trained with an InfoNCE contrastive objective. Second, ECRanker reranks retrieved candidates by summing an entity-level similarity score (from query-conditioned EntBind embeddings) with a likelihood-based context relevance score (from a RelRanker MLLM that outputs P(yes|query, context) as a continuous score), then selects the top context within the best entity. Third, CAGen is a LoRA-fine-tuned MLLM that generates structured answers conditioned on the retrieved context, using specialized tokens ([QTYPE], [ANS], [NEXTQ]) to handle both single-hop and multi-hop reasoning.

## Results

EntRAG with Qwen-2.5-7B achieves 46.1% on E-VQA (All) and 44.5% on InfoSeek (All), surpassing the strongest prior baselines (ReflectiVA at 39.7% on E-VQA and VLM-PRF at 42.8% on InfoSeek) by approximately 6.4 and 1.7 points respectively. On entity retrieval, EntBind reaches R@1 of 24.1 on E-VQA and 58.5 on InfoSeek, outperforming EVA-CLIP-18B (22.8 and 52.8) and MM-Embed (11.9 and 36.7). With oracle retrieval, CAGen achieves 88.7% VQA accuracy on E-VQA, indicating the generation module can effectively leverage correct evidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EntRAG outperforms all retrieval-augmented baselines on both benchmarks. On E-VQA, it surpasses ReflectiVA (the strongest prior method at 39.7% All) by 6.4 points and VLM-PRF (39.2% All) by 6.9 points with the Qwen-2.5-7B backbone. On InfoSeek, EntRAG (44.5% All) beats VLM-PRF with InternVL3-8B (42.5% All) by 2.0 points and ReflectiVA (40.1% All) by 4.4 points. In entity retrieval, EntBind improves R@1 over EVA-CLIP-18B by 1.3 points on E-VQA and 5.7 points on InfoSeek, while substantially outperforming MM-Embed (11.9 vs. 24.1 R@1 on E-VQA), confirming that MLLM-based embeddings without explicit visual grounding lose fine-grained discriminative signal. Zero-shot MLLMs (GPT-4V at 28.1% E-VQA, 14.6% InfoSeek) remain far below retrieval-augmented approaches, underscoring the necessity of external knowledge for long-tail entity questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retrieve-then-generate paradigm for KB-VQA (Wiki-LLaVA, EchoSight, ReflectiVA, VLM-PRF) by shifting the unit of retrieval from individual images or text snippets to unified multimodal entities. It builds on MLLM-based embedding approaches like MM-Embed but identifies a critical limitation—visual attention collapse in later transformer layers—that motivates the explicit entity-token and attention-pooling design in EntBind. The RelRanker component adopts the token-probability-as-relevance-score methodology from Liang et al. (2022), while the theoretical appendix provides a function-class containment argument formalizing why multimodal entity-level scoring strictly dominates unimodal image-level scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entity-centric framing addresses a concrete failure mode (visual ambiguity when target objects are small in cluttered scenes) that prior image-dominant pipelines struggle with, and the ~10% gain on E-VQA over the strongest baseline is practically meaningful for fine-grained recognition tasks. However, the framework's reliance on a ~15B-parameter EVA-CLIP backbone and its two-hop reasoning limit constrain scalability and generality. Open questions include whether the entity-token mechanism transfers to non-Wikipedia knowledge bases, whether the approach generalizes beyond the two-hop setting, and whether the inference-time overhead (notably in retrieval and reranking) is acceptable for latency-sensitive deployments.
