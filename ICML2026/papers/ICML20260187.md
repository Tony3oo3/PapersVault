## Problem

Adam's convergence has been a persistent theoretical challenge: it can fail even in simple convex settings, and prior convergence guarantees require external learning-rate schedulers and β₂ close to 1. The specific class of objective functions where Adam exhibits inherent, scheduler-free advantages over gradient descent and momentum remains poorly understood, particularly in the context of the highly degenerate directions prevalent in deep learning loss landscapes.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on scalar degenerate polynomials L(x) = x^k/k (k ≥ 4, even) with deterministic full-batch Adam (ε = 0, asymptotic bias correction), taking hyperparameters β₁, β₂ ∈ [0,1) and learning rate η as inputs. The outputs are closed-form local stability inequalities on (β₁, β₂), an explicit linear convergence rate β₂^{1/(2(k−2))}, and a three-regime phase diagram (stable convergence, spikes, SignGD-like oscillation) that partitions the full [0,1]² hyperparameter space. The framework extends to general k-th order degenerate minima of the form L(x) = x^k(1+h(x))/k with h(0)=0.

## Method

The paper introduces normalized state variables ω_t = m_t/x_t^{k−1} and λ_t = x_t^{k−2}/√v_t to decouple the iterate scale from optimizer dynamics, reducing Adam to a 3D discrete map whose non-trivial fixed point yields linear convergence at rate β₂^{1/(2(k−2))}. Local asymptotic stability is established via Jacobian spectral-radius analysis, producing the key condition β₁ < β₂^{k/(2(k−2))} (plus a minor lower bound on β₁), which generalizes prior results requiring β₂ ≈ 1. The acceleration mechanism is a decoupling between the second moment v_t and squared gradient g_t²: once the gradient decays faster than the memory rate β₂, v_t enters autonomous geometric decay v_t ≈ β₂^t, acting as an implicit exponential learning-rate schedule that converts GD's sublinear O(t^{−1/(k−2)}) convergence into linear convergence. This is contrasted with GD and heavy-ball momentum, which are proven to remain stuck at the same power-law rate regardless of degeneracy order, and with SignGD-based acceleration mechanisms from prior work. The full hyperparameter phase diagram is then characterized, identifying three regimes governed by the existence and stability of the non-trivial fixed point.

## Results

On degenerate polynomials L(x) = x^k/k (k ≥ 4 even), Adam achieves local linear convergence with rate β₂^(1/(2(k−2))), while both GD and Heavy-ball Momentum are confined to sublinear power-law decay Θ(t^(−1/(k−2))). For k=4, the predicted loss convergence slope is ≈ −0.0726 (β₂=0.99) and ≈ −0.0544 (k=6), matching empirical exponential fits precisely. The iteration complexity separation is stark: GD requires T_ε ~ ε^(−(k−2)) iterations (exponential in k), whereas Adam requires only T_ε ~ (k−2)·ln(1/ε) (linear in k). The local stability condition is β₁ < β₂^(k/(2(k−2))), and three distinct hyperparameter regimes are identified: stable convergence, late-phase spikes, and SignGD-like oscillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between Adam and two non-adaptive baselines—Gradient Descent and Heavy-ball Momentum—on the same degenerate polynomial objectives. Both GD and Momentum provably achieve only the sublinear rate Θ(t^(−1/(k−2))), with iteration complexity exploding as ε^(−(k−2)) in the degeneracy order k. Adam, by contrast, achieves linear convergence at rate β₂^(1/(2(k−2))) without any learning rate schedule, reducing complexity to (k−2)·ln(1/ε). This acceleration is attributed to a v_t–g_t² decoupling mechanism that implicitly generates an exponential learning rate schedule, distinct from the SignGD-based mechanism proposed by Kunstner et al. (2023). Compared to prior convergence results (Zhang et al., 2022a), this work removes the requirement that β₂ be close to 1, providing stability conditions over the full [0,1) hyperparameter domain. Empirically, the theoretical phase diagram (Fig. 3a) aligns closely with grid-search results (Fig. 3b, Fig. 7), with stable configurations reaching machine precision (~10^(−300)) while unstable ones exhibit spikes or oscillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adam convergence analysis initiated by Zhang et al. (2022a), which required a decreasing learning rate scheduler η/√T and β₂ sufficiently close to 1, by identifying a function class (highly degenerate polynomials L(x)=x^k/k, k≥4) where Adam converges automatically with constant stepsize over the full hyperparameter domain [0,1]. It departs from the SignGD-based acceleration mechanism proposed by Kunstner et al. (2023; 2024) by showing that the speedup on degenerate landscapes arises from a distinct v_t–g_t² decoupling that exponentially amplifies the effective learning rate. The phase diagram unifies and explains empirical observations of spikes and oscillations reported by Ma et al. (2022) and Bai et al. (2026), while the sublinear convergence of GD and Momentum on degenerate objectives connects to Davis et al. (2025) and the broader literature on degeneracy in deep learning loss landscapes (Sagun et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2021; 2022b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, landscape-driven explanation for Adam's practical advantage that does not require stochasticity, heavy-tailed noise, or data-distribution assumptions—addressing a gap left by prior accounts (Table 1). The three-regime phase diagram (stable convergence, spikes, SignGD-like oscillation) gives practitioners a concrete hyperparameter guide and explains why Adam can be unstable on quadratics yet stable on degenerate directions, a counterintuitive reversal. Key limitations include the restriction to 1D local analysis (though Remark 4.4 extends to general k-th order degenerate minima), the deterministic setting, and the local (not global) nature of the stability guarantee. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic batch settings, high-dimensional coupled degenerate/non-degenerate modes, and validating the degeneracy-based explanation on real architectures (preliminary evidence with Transformers vs. CNNs is promising but preliminary).
