## Problem

Centralized decision-makers must repeatedly allocate finite resources to a fixed population while learning individual utilities online, yet no general framework existed that simultaneously supports arbitrary monotone social welfare functions, provides near-optimal regret guarantees, and yields anytime-valid statistical inference on welfare. Prior bandit-based welfare work either restricted to specific objectives (utilitarian or egalitarian) or aggregated welfare across time steps rather than across the population at each decision point, leaving the fixed-population, multi-resource, continuous-allocation setting unaddressed.

## Input / Output

At each time step the algorithm receives stochastic utility observations for the k individuals who were allocated a resource, along with fixed SWF parameters (a weight vector w and a power/fairness parameter q) and the problem dimensions (population size n, resources per round k). It produces a continuous allocation probability vector p_t ∈ [0,1]^n with ∑p_i = k (not a discrete arm selection), a two-sided welfare confidence sequence [W_t^↓, W_t^↑] valid uniformly over all time steps, and a cumulative regret R(T) measuring welfare loss relative to the best fixed allocation. The ex-ante expected utilities μ⊙p (not realized post-allocation utilities) are the object of optimization and inference, which avoids degeneracy of many SWFs under partial allocation.

## Method

The core insight is a confidence-sequence lifting theorem: monotonicity of the SWF alone suffices to lift coordinate-wise (1−δ/n) confidence sequences for individual mean utilities into a valid (1−δ) anytime-valid confidence sequence for the optimal welfare M(μ⊙p*), by optimizing the SWF over the lower and upper confidence bounds respectively. Building on this, SWF-UCB solves a concave constrained optimization at each step to find the allocation maximizing the SWF evaluated at upper confidence bounds, then uses dependent rounding to sample a valid k-element allocation set satisfying both cardinality and marginal-probability constraints. Bespoke oracles exploit the structure of each SWF family: WPM and Kolm admit water-filling closed-form solutions parameterized by a scalar λ (multiplicative and additive respectively) found in O(n log n) time via KKT conditions, while Gini is solved by a greedy block-based algorithm in O(kn) time. The three assumptions play modular roles—monotonicity enables the CS lifting, concavity ensures tractable policy optimization, and Lipschitz continuity yields the near-optimal Õ(n + √(nkT)) regret bound matching the Ω(√(nkT)) lower bound from multi-play bandits.

## Results

The paper establishes a near-optimal regret bound of Õ(n + √(nkT)) for the SWF-UCB algorithm (Theorem 5.2), matching the Ω(√(nkT)) lower bound from multi-play bandits up to poly-log factors in n. Empirically, across all three SWF families (WPM, Kolm, Gini) with n=50, the normalized regret R(T)/√T remains bounded over T ∈ [10³, 2.56×10⁵], confirming the predicted √T scaling. A key empirical finding is the non-monotonic dependence of regret on k: regret increases with k up to approximately k=20 (for n=50), then decreases sharply, with the highest regret occurring at intermediate resource levels rather than at k=1 or k=n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare SWF-UCB against competing online allocation algorithms; instead, it validates its theoretical guarantees empirically and positions the results relative to known lower bounds. The utilitarian welfare case (q=1 for WPM, q=0 for Kolm) reduces exactly to the stochastic multi-play multi-armed bandit problem, where the Ω(√(nkT)) lower bound from Kveton et al. (2015) and Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi (2012) applies, and SWF-UCB matches this rate. The paper contrasts its approach with prior welfare-based bandit work (Barman et al., 2023; Sawarni et al., 2023; Sarkar et al., 2025; Krishna et al., 2025), which aggregates welfare across time steps rather than across the population at each step, and with fair bandit methods (Lim et al., 2024) that only handle egalitarian welfare. The oracle algorithms achieve O(n log n) time for WPM and Kolm (water-filling) and O(kn) for Gini (block-based), providing exact solutions rather than approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the time-uniform confidence sequence framework of Howard et al. (2020, 2021) and repurposes it for a fundamentally different target: rather than bounding individual means, it lifts coordinate-wise CSs into welfare-level bounds via a monotonicity-only argument (Theorem 4.1). It departs from multi-play and combinatorial bandits, which use discrete arm selections and utilitarian (sum) rewards, by formulating allocation as a continuous probability vector over a fixed population with SWF-aggregated welfare. It also distinguishes itself from welfare-based bandit regret work (Barman et al. 2023; Sarkar et al. 2025; Krishna et al. 2025), which aggregates utilities across time steps, by aggregating across the population at each step. The bespoke water-filling oracles for WPM, Kolm, and Gini SWFs extend the KKT-based allocation literature with exact, efficient solutions for each family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework providing both near-optimal regret guarantees and anytime-valid statistical inference (sequential testing, optimal stopping, policy evaluation) for online SWF-based resource allocation, unifying learning and inference under a single CS construction. The non-monotonic regret dependence on k—where intermediate resource counts yield the highest regret—reveals a structural interaction between resource scarcity and learning difficulty absent from standard multi-play bandits. Open questions include tighter bounds with explicit dependence on fairness parameters (q, w), extensions to variable k_t and stochastic non-allocation utilities, and whether the CS lifting extends to restless or Markov bandit settings. For practitioners, the framework offers a principled way to certify welfare thresholds adaptively without sacrificing statistical validity.
