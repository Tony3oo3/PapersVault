## Problem

Meta Black-Box Optimization (MetaBBO) methods that train learnable optimizers from scratch via self-supervised or unsupervised objectives suffer from a restricted performance ceiling: without exposure to high-quality optimization behaviors, they converge on suboptimal strategies, especially on complex unseen landscapes. Existing distillation-based approaches (e.g., EAD) are confined to single-teacher, Gaussian-specific strategies, failing to capture the heterogeneous expertise available across the broader BBO ecosystem.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a knowledge base of 80 expert trajectories generated by seven classical population-based optimizers (DE, JADE, SHADE, L-SHADE, PSO, GA, CMA-ES) on 16 synthetic tasks at d=10, N=200. Each trajectory is represented as a sequence of (μ, Σ) population statistics at selected generations, making the distillation source architecture-agnostic. At inference, the trained optimizer takes a black-box objective function and an initial population, iteratively producing updated populations until a budget of 10,000 evaluations is exhausted, yielding the best-found solution x*.

## Method

MetaDistill introduces a two-stage training framework: (1) Diversity-Preserving Distillation (DPD) for pretraining, and (2) optional Self-Supervised Fine-Tuning (SSFT) at test time. DPD projects heterogeneous teacher strategies into a unified distribution space by representing each generation as a population mean-covariance pair, then minimizes forward KL divergence between the student's population distribution and the teacher's reference distribution. A temporal window g(t; i, T) creates a curriculum schedule where the student targets a fixed reference generation over a sliding window of i steps, and a dual-stage scaling scheme (s₁ = 1/√d, s₂ based on element-wise maxima) prevents numerical instability. The forward KL's mass-covering property preserves population diversity rather than collapsing to a single mode. SSFT then refines the pretrained optimizer on the unseen task using either a differentiable fitness-improvement objective or a black-box population-mean-alignment objective, with a self-supervision interval j controlling update frequency.

## Results

On the BBOB test suite (24 functions, d ∈ {30, 100, 200, 300}), MetaDistill achieves a Logarithmic Absolute Difference (LAD) of 5.41–5.47 for POM (≈10^5.4× reduction in final objective), 2.18–2.51 for LGA (≈150–300×), 0.45–0.97 for LES (≈3–10×), and 0.46–0.64 for LDE (≈3–4×) over their respective scratch-trained baselines. On control tasks, MetaDistill-LES improves BipedalWalker return from 12.5 to 39.5 and LunarLander from 195.6 to 293.8, while MetaDistill-LGA+ (with deterministic selection) reaches 316.1 on LunarLander and 41.4 on BipedalWalker. Training cost is comparable to the original MetaGBT paradigm (0.22 vs. 0.16 GPU-hours for 128 epochs on RTX 5090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaDistill consistently outperforms all four scratch-trained baselines on the BBOB benchmark. POM (Li et al., 2024, MetaGBT) benefits most dramatically, with a ~10^5.4× improvement in aggregated final objective across all dimensions (LAD ≈ 5.4). LGA (Lange et al., 2023a, MetaBBO) shows strong gains of 150–300× on BBOB (LAD 2.18–2.51), but its learnable selection operator causes premature convergence on control tasks, where the original LGA actually degrades (LunarLander: 111.9 → −48.8); replacing selection with a deterministic (μ+λ) scheme (LGA+) restores and amplifies the MetaDistill advantage. LES (Lange et al., 2023b, MetaBBO) and LDE (Sun et al., 2021, policy gradient) exhibit more modest but consistent gains of 3–10× on BBOB, with improvements slightly decreasing at higher dimensions. On control tasks, MetaDistill-LES achieves the largest relative improvement (BipedalWalker: 12.5 → 39.5, a 3.1× gain), while MetaDistill-LDE shows only marginal improvement (21.1 → 27.2) because its baseline is already relatively robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MetaBBO paradigm (Lange et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2021) by replacing the dominant self-supervised/unsupervised training objective with a supervised distillation loss derived from classical BBO algorithms (CMA-ES, JADE, L-SHADE, PSO, GA, DE, SHADE). It departs from prior distillation efforts in evolutionary computation—most notably EAD (Lange et al., 2024), which is restricted to a single Gaussian-based teacher—by introducing a multi-teacher, architecture-agnostic representation that projects heterogeneous optimization strategies into a unified population-level distribution space (mean + covariance) matched via forward KL divergence. The optional test-time Self-Supervised Fine-Tuning stage complements the pretraining with a lightweight adaptation mechanism analogous to MetaGBT's fitness-improvement signal but generalized to black-box settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, drop-in training recipe that consistently lifts the performance ceiling of four architecturally distinct learnable optimizers, with the largest gains (~10^5× for POM) occurring where scratch-based training is weakest. Two counter-intuitive findings carry broader design implications: the 'diversity paradox' (excessive teacher diversity can degrade generalization, with architecture-specific saturation points) and the observation that parameterizing the selection operator in population-based LOs is actively harmful, suggesting a best practice of keeping selection deterministic. Open questions include the quantitative interplay between teacher tuning budget, task diversity, and student architecture, as well as whether learnable optimizers can self-optimize beyond the ceiling imposed by any finite teacher set.
