## Problem

Visual Mamba models face two efficiency bottlenecks: multi-directional scan strategies needed to overcome the SSM causality constraint incur substantial overhead from repeated data rearrangement, and Mamba's warp-scan implementation is inherently slow for the short token lengths (typically <1000) common in vision tasks due to underutilized GPU threads. The paper asks whether bidirectional information flow and high GPU parallelism can be achieved without the cost of multi-scan or long sequences.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images tokenized into patches (224×224 for classification, 512×512 crops for segmentation, 1280×800 for detection) and produces class labels, per-pixel semantic masks, or bounding boxes with instance masks. For high-resolution inference (e.g., 2048×512 in segmentation), both Mamba and Attention blocks process features in windowed regions matching the training resolution, while Mamba blocks retain global context within each window. The architecture follows a four-stage hierarchical design with CNN-based stem and early stages followed by interleaved Mamba and Attention blocks.

## Method

SF-Mamba introduces two mechanisms on top of the MambaVision hybrid architecture. First, auxiliary patch swapping prepends and appends two data-dependent tokens (initialized as the global average of the patch sequence) to the input; after each Mamba block, the two tokens are swapped so that the tail token's accumulated global context is injected at the sequence head for the next layer, enabling future-to-past information flow within a single unidirectional scan at negligible cost. Second, batch folding with periodic state reset merges the batch dimension into the sequence dimension (reshaping B×T into B₁×(B₂·T)) to maximize GPU thread utilization in the warp-scan kernel, while setting A_t=0 every T steps to reset the hidden state and prevent cross-sequence leakage; an adaptive lookup table selects the optimal B₁/B₂ ratio based on batch size, dimension, sequence length, and state dimension. A custom 1-D depthwise convolution kernel with implicit boundary padding avoids repeated format conversions on the batch-folded tensor.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), SF-Mamba achieves 82.5% / 83.5% / 84.4% top-1 accuracy at Tiny / Small / Base scale with throughputs of 7600 / 5639 / 3534 img/s, consistently outperforming MambaVision (6662 / 4933 / 2974 img/s) in both speed and accuracy. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SF-Mamba reaches 47.2 / 48.5 / 50.1 mIoU (vs. MambaVision's 46.0 / 48.2 / 49.1) at 47.9 / 45.4 / 42.6 fps. On MS COCO detection, SF-Mamba matches or exceeds MambaVision in AP while running 1.4–1.6× faster (e.g., 26.8 vs. 16.4 fps at Base scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baseline MambaVision, SF-Mamba wins on every metric at every scale: +0.2/+0.2/+0.2 pp top-1 accuracy and +14%/+14%/+19% throughput (T/S/B). Among Mamba-based methods, SF-Mamba-B (3534 img/s, 84.4%) is 5.3× faster than Spatial-Mamba-B (670 img/s, 85.3%) and 5.5× faster than VMamba-B (640 img/s, 83.9%), at the cost of 0.9 pp accuracy versus Spatial-Mamba. Against hybrid CNN-Transformer models, SF-Mamba-S (5639 img/s, 83.5%) surpasses FasterViT-1 (4402 img/s, 83.2%) in both speed and accuracy, and SF-Mamba-B (3534 img/s, 84.4%) beats FasterViT-2 (3392 img/s, 84.2%). On ADE20K segmentation, SF-Mamba-B achieves 50.1 mIoU at 42.6 fps, outperforming MambaVision-B (49.1 mIoU, 37.3 fps) and Swin-B (48.1 mIoU, 25.4 fps). On COCO detection, SF-Mamba matches MambaVision's AP (52.8 vs. 52.8 at Base) while running 1.6× faster (26.8 vs. 16.4 fps), and its windowed-attention variant (SF-Mamba♣) reduces FLOPs from 3000G to 992G with only 0.2 AP loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

SF-Mamba builds directly on the Mamba selective SSM (Gu & Dao, 2023) and adopts the MambaVision hybrid Mamba-Transformer macro-architecture (Hatamizadeh & Kautz, 2025) as its structural backbone, while borrowing the periodic state reset trick from vLLM's paged-attention system (Kwon et al., 2023) to preserve sequence independence under batch folding. It departs from the dominant visual Mamba paradigm of multi-directional scanning (Vim, VMamba, cross-scan) by replacing expensive token rearrangement with a minimal two-token swap that injects future-to-past context into a unidirectional scan. The paper also positions itself against MambaVision's reliance on attention layers for backward information flow, arguing that this leaves shallow Mamba blocks deprived of global context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The batch folding technique is broadly applicable to any Mamba-based method processing short sequences, addressing a fundamental hardware-level inefficiency (32-thread warp scan underutilization) that has limited Mamba's practical advantage over attention at typical vision resolutions. The auxiliary token swapping provides a clean, near-zero-cost alternative to multi-scan strategies, though its applicability is limited for architectures with internal 2D convolutions (e.g., VMamba) that require format conversion at every layer. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to edge/mobile hardware, whether increasing Mamba's share relative to attention in the hybrid further benefits high-resolution tasks, and whether the DropPath incompatibility with series bi-scan identified here generalizes to other regularization schemes.
