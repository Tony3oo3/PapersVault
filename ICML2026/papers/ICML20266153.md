## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) causes abrupt reward collapse when applied to diffusion large language models (dLLMs) because their sequence likelihoods are intractable and must be estimated via noisy Monte Carlo proxies. The paper identifies that GRPO's conditional clipping and fixed group-size normalization are fundamentally incompatible with such estimated importance ratios, creating a self-reinforcing loop where noisy ratio outliers trigger gradient spikes, policy drift, and amplified future ratio variance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion language model (LLaDA-8B-Instruct or SDAR-8B-Chat) and text prompts, generating groups of G rollouts per prompt with small-budget Monte Carlo ELBO estimation (m ≤ 5) for importance ratios. Training uses verifiable rewards from math and puzzle benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, Countdown, Sudoku, AIME) at sequence lengths of 128–512 tokens. The output is a fully fine-tuned dLLM backbone that produces reasoning text, with the key practical constraint that all importance-ratio weights are computed in log-space via a clip-then-softmax procedure to avoid numerical overflow from exponentiated noisy log-ratios.

## Method

StableDRL reformulates the GRPO objective with two structural changes: (1) unconditional upper clipping, which caps every estimated importance ratio at 1+ε regardless of advantage sign (preventing the conditional bypass that lets noise-driven outliers produce unbounded gradients on negative-advantage samples), and (2) self-normalization, which replaces the fixed group-size divisor G with the realized sum of clipped ratios, constraining the update to the convex hull of per-sample advantage-weighted directions and eliminating the random group-scale factor. Theoretically, the paper proves that under a common tail-envelope condition on log-ratio noise, GRPO's spike probability is nondecreasing in policy drift (formalizing the instability loop), while StableDRL's self-normalized update is deterministically bounded by the per-sample gradient norm. For block diffusion models, a staircase attention mask enables leakage-free, single-pass O(1) ELBO proxy estimation by using a dual-stream input of frozen clean context and corrupted target with a block-lower-triangular mask that occludes each block's own ground truth.

## Results

On full-attention LLaDA-8B-Instruct, StableDRL achieves the highest average pass@1 accuracy across all four reasoning benchmarks: GSM8K 84.2%, MATH500 41.8%, Countdown 83.5%, and Sudoku 91.5%, outperforming the strongest prior methods ESPO and SPG by +2.2%, +2.3%, +2.5%, and +5.5% respectively. On block-diffusion SDAR-8B, StableDRL reaches 16.7% on AIME 2024 (static sampling), surpassing the autoregressive Qwen3-8B (10.0%) and the prior block-diffusion method Trado (13.3%). In matched full-fine-tuning reruns on GSM8K, StableDRL remains stable at 86.2% after 1000 steps while ESPO and SPG collapse before 1000 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableDRL outperforms all prior dLLM RL baselines on every benchmark in Table 1. Against the strongest prior methods, it beats ESPO by +2.2% (GSM8K), +2.3% (MATH500), +2.5% (Countdown), and +5.5% (Sudoku), and beats SPG by +1.2%, +3.4%, +13.6%, and +1.5% respectively. Earlier methods D1, WD1, and UniGRPO trail by larger margins (e.g., Countdown: 83.5% vs. 32.1%, 50.6%, 48.2%). On block diffusion (Table 2), StableDRL surpasses the AR Qwen3-8B on AIME 2024 (16.7% vs. 10.0%) and MATH500 (79.2% vs. 78.4%), and edges out Trado on AIME (16.7% vs. 13.3%) while maintaining robustness under dynamic sampling where Trado degrades to 11.0%. In the matched full-fine-tuning diagnostic (Table 5), StableDRL is comparable to ESPO/SPG at 200 steps (82.2 vs. 82.6/82.1) but remains stable to 1000 steps (86.2%) while both baselines collapse, confirming the gain comes from stabilization rather than slower optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and the dLLM RL line of work that adapts it to intractable diffusion likelihoods (D1, WD1, ESPO, SPG). Prior methods focused on improving the importance ratio estimator itself—via mean-field approximations or multi-step MC ELBO sampling—while keeping the standard GRPO update intact. StableDRL instead departs by reformulating the update rule (unconditional upper clipping plus self-normalization) to be robust to noisy ratios, drawing on truncated and self-normalized importance sampling from the statistics literature. It further extends the framework to block diffusion via a novel staircase attention mechanism for leakage-free proxy estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StableDRL is the first method to sustain stable full-parameter RL training on both full-attention and block dLLMs beyond 1,000 steps, which is practically critical because prior methods (ESPO, SPG) required LoRA or early stopping to avoid collapse, limiting how much reasoning capability could be unlocked. The instability-loop diagnosis (noise → spike → drift → amplified noise) provides a reusable diagnostic framework for any RL setting where importance ratios are estimated from noisy proxies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to broader reward models, longer-horizon tasks, and non-language discrete diffusion domains (molecular, polymer), and whether the theoretical conditions (tail envelope, local smoothness) hold in practice at larger scale.
