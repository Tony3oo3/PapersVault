## Problem

SVD-based LoRA variants like PiSSA and MiLoRA that outperform standard LoRA under supervised fine-tuning can underperform or even collapse under RLVR, yet the appropriate initialization for low-rank adaptation in RLVR remained unclear. The paper asks: what initialization is effective for low-rank adaptation in RLVR fine-tuning, and why do SFT-optimized SVD initializations break down when the KL-divergence constraint of RLVR is imposed?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM weight matrix W₀ ∈ ℝ^{m×n} and its singular value decomposition W₀ = UΣVᵀ, then produces LoRA adapter matrices B₀ ∈ ℝ^{m×r} and A₀ ∈ ℝ^{r×n} with r ≪ min(m,n). B₀ is always set to the zero matrix, while A₀ is initialized as the transpose of either the top-r or bottom-r right singular vectors of W₀, ensuring A₀A₀ᵀ = I_r. The fine-tuned model then consumes text prompts (e.g., math problems) and generates text responses scored by a verifiable rule-based reward (exact-match correctness).

## Method

The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2) showing that with B₀ = 0, orthonormal initialization of A₀ minimizes the Frobenius-norm gap between LoRA and full fine-tuning over T RLVR steps, since ‖I_n − A₀ᵀA₀‖₂ ≥ 1 with equality iff A₀ has orthonormal rows. Motivated by this, two geometry-preserving variants are proposed: LoRA-RLPO sets A₀ = V_rᵀ (top-r right singular vectors) and LoRA-RLMO sets A₀ = V_{-r}ᵀ (bottom-r right singular vectors), both with B₀ = 0. The key distinction from PiSSA/MiLoRA is the removal of singular-value scaling (Σ_r^{1/2} or Σ_{-r}^{1/2}), which the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) amplifies first-step updates by a factor ≥ σ_r relative to orthonormal initialization, driving rapid KL-divergence violations that destabilize RLVR training. A complementary proposition (Proposition 4.3) shows orthonormal A₀ bounds the first-step LoRA update by the full fine-tuning gradient norm, preventing abrupt policy shifts.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B fine-tuned with DAPO (cosine LR decay, 500 steps, rank 16), LoRA-RLPO achieves the highest average accuracy of 65.03% ± 0.55% across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, followed by LoRA-RLMO (63.76% ± 1.64%) and standard LoRA (62.40% ± 2.96%). LoRA-RLPO obtains the best scores on MATH500 (87.33%), AIME 2022 (46.67%), AIME 2023 (42.22%), and AIME 2024 (73.33%), while LoRA-RLMO achieves the strongest GSM8K result (76.42%). PiSSA catastrophically collapses to 4.57% average, and MiLoRA trails standard LoRA at 51.71% average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard LoRA (62.40% avg), LoRA-RLPO improves by +2.63 points on average with substantially lower variance (±0.55 vs ±2.96), winning on MATH500 (+5.40), AIME22 (+3.34), AIME23 (+3.33), and AIME24 (+1.11), while LoRA-RLMO gains +1.36 on average, primarily driven by MATH500 (+4.87) and GSM8K (+0.78). Against PiSSA, both proposed methods are dramatically superior: PiSSA collapses to 4.57% average (near-zero on all AIME benchmarks), whereas LoRA-RLPO reaches 65.03% and LoRA-RLMO reaches 63.76%, confirming that singular-value scaling in the principal subspace is the primary source of RLVR instability. Against MiLoRA (51.71% avg), LoRA-RLPO leads by +13.32 points and LoRA-RLMO by +12.05 points, with the gap most pronounced on AIME22 (+17.78 and +13.33 respectively) and AIME24 (+25.55 and +24.44). The paper also compares against OLoRA and OLoRA-tail in training-dynamics ablations (Figure 5), showing that removing singular-value scaling from PiSSA/MiLoRA substantially reduces KL divergence and mitigates reward collapse, but these orthonormal baselines are not included in the final evaluation table. At 7B scale (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GRPO), LoRA-RLPO extends its lead to 35.96% average vs. 30.39% for LoRA, 26.72% for MiLoRA, and 24.74% for PiSSA, confirming the findings generalize across model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based LoRA initialization line (PiSSA for principal components, MiLoRA for minor components) by identifying that their shared singular-value scaling is the key destabilizing factor under RLVR, in contrast to SFT where it aids convergence. It builds on OLoRA's orthonormal initialization concept but departs by keeping B₀=0 (standard LoRA) rather than OLoRA's nonzero B₀, and provides the first theoretical justification (Theorem 4.2) for why orthonormal A₀ minimizes the LoRA-to-full-fine-tuning gap specifically in the RLVR setting. The analysis also draws on Zhu et al. (2025)'s KL-constraint-to-weight-bound result to connect initialization geometry to training stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is straightforward: for LoRA in RLVR, replace singular-value-scaled SVD initialization with orthonormal singular-vector initialization (A₀=V_r^T or V_{-r}^T, B₀=0), which stabilizes training and improves accuracy over standard LoRA. The unified explanation for why PiSSA and MiLoRA collapse in RLVR (subspace geometry + singular value scaling jointly violating the implicit KL budget) is of independent interest for understanding paradigm transfer in PEFT. Limitations include evaluation on only 1.5B–7B models, mathematical reasoning as the primary domain, and rank 16–32; whether the orthonormality principle extends to other RL objectives, much larger models, or non-SVD geometry remains open.
