## Problem

Gradient-variation regret bounds for two-point Bandit Convex Optimization (BCO) incur large dimension dependence (d² for convex, d²/λ for strongly convex) because the non-consecutive nature of coordinate sampling makes consecutive-round gradient differences difficult to analyze. This paper tightens the dimension dependence by nearly √d for convex and d for strongly convex functions, and further derives the first gradient-variance, small-loss, dynamic, and universal gradient-variation regret bounds for two-point BCO.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner queries two points x_t = w_t + δe_{i_t} and x'_t = w_t − δe_{i_t} along a uniformly random coordinate direction i_t ∈ [d], receiving only the scalar values f_t(x_t) and f_t(x'_t); the exploration parameter δ = 1/(2d²LTR) and shrinkage ξ = δ/r keep queries inside the feasible domain. The algorithm outputs decision iterates w_t and theoretical regret guarantees that scale with problem-dependent quantities (gradient variation V_T, gradient variance W_T, small loss F_T) rather than the worst-case horizon T, with the non-consecutive gradient variation V̄_T serving as the intermediate analysis quantity.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a refined analysis of the non-consecutive gradient variation V̄_T = d²Σ(v_t − v_{α_t})², where α_t is the last round the same coordinate was sampled. The key insight connects the maximum non-consecutive sampling gap max_{i∈[d]} ρ_{t,i} to the Coupon Collector's Problem, yielding E[max ρ_{t,i}] = O(d log d) instead of the naive O(d²) bound; this decouples the sampling gap from the gradient difference and removes an extra d factor in the convex case. For the strongly convex case, a deterministic step size η_t = 1/(λt) replaces the stochastic problem-dependent rate, combined with a tight characterization max_t E[‖g_t − g̃_t‖²] ≤ 8dG². For one-point BLO over hyper-rectangular domains, a novel gradient estimator maintains buffer vectors storing the most recent function value per coordinate-sign pair, enabling the first gradient-variation regret bound in that setting via a log-barrier regularizer whose diagonal Hessian aligns eigenvectors with the standard basis.

## Results

For two-point BCO with gradient variation, the paper achieves Õ(d^{3/2}√V_T) regret for convex functions and O(d/λ log V_T) for λ-strongly convex functions, improving the prior best bounds by factors of nearly √d and d respectively. As additional problem-dependent guarantees, the paper establishes O(√(dF_T)+d) small-loss regret for convex functions and O(√(dW_T)+d) gradient-variance regret for linear functions, both of which recover the minimax-optimal O(√(dT)) rate when T ≥ d. For one-point BLO over hyper-rectangular domains, the first gradient-variation bound O(d^{7/2}√(V_T log²T log(dT))) is obtained. The paper also establishes the first gradient-variation dynamic regret bound Õ(√(d³(1+P_T+V_T)(1+P_T))) and universal regret bounds for two-point BCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Chiang et al. (2013), whose two-point BCO gradient-variation bounds are Õ(d²√V_T) for convex and O(d²/λ log V_T) for strongly convex functions. This paper improves both by a factor of nearly √d (convex) and d (strongly convex), respectively, by exploiting the Coupon Collector's Problem structure in the non-consecutive sampling gap to decouple the dependence between the gap and the gradient difference. For dynamic regret, the paper matches the full-information gradient-variation bound of Zhao et al. (2020) in its dependence on P_T and V_T (up to logarithmic factors), but incurs an additional factor of d in dimension compared to the worst-case optimal O(√(dT(1+P_T))) bound of He et al. (2025); the authors note this O(d) gap remains open. The paper also introduces the first gradient-variance and small-loss bounds for two-point BCO, which have no direct prior comparison in the bandit setting but recover the minimax-optimal O(√(dT)) rate as a special case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Chiang et al. (2013), whose Algorithm 1 and coordinate-sampling gradient estimator (Eq. 2.2) form the algorithmic backbone; the key departure is a refined analysis of the non-consecutive gradient variation that connects the maximal sampling gap to the Coupon Collector's Problem, yielding O(d log d) instead of the previously used O(d²) bound. The optimistic online gradient descent framework from Chiang et al. (2012) provides the foundational regret decomposition, while the deterministic step-size schedule for the strongly convex case is borrowed from Chen et al. (2023). For the dynamic and universal regret extensions, the paper adapts the online ensemble architectures of Zhao et al. (2020) and Yan et al. (2024) to the bandit setting, introducing a new Bregman-divergence-based decomposition of the non-consecutive variation to handle unknown curvature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results close the dimension-dependence gap between convex and linear two-point BCO (up to logarithmic factors) and provide the first gradient-variation dynamic and universal regret bounds in the bandit setting, establishing a more complete problem-dependent regret landscape for BCO. The one-point BLO result, while restricted to hyper-rectangular domains, is the first gradient-variation bound in that setting and highlights the versatility of the non-consecutive gap analysis. Open questions include whether the O(d) gap in dynamic regret can be eliminated, whether the one-point BLO result extends to general convex domains, and whether the techniques apply to adaptive (non-oblivious) environments or exp-concave functions.
