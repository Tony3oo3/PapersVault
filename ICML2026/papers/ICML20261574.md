## Problem

Vision-language models generate fluent causal explanations, but existing evaluations cannot distinguish genuine structural causal reasoning from linguistic pattern matching. Prior causal reasoning benchmarks rely on multiple-choice selection or verification of pre-provided chains, which overestimates a model's ability to construct causal structure independently. This paper asks whether VLMs can generate explicit causal abstractions (arrow-notation chains) from visual scenes, and quantifies the gap between their text fluency and structural generation capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes a COCO image paired with a natural-language causal question at one of three levels of Pearl's Ladder of Causation (association, intervention, counterfactual). For intervention and counterfactual questions, the model must first emit a minimal causal chain in arrow notation (e.g., 'strong wind → force on umbrellas → umbrellas topple') before producing a free-text explanation; association questions require text only. The Abstraction Gap score is computed per model as the normalized difference between mean text scores and mean chain scores on a 0–10 scale, with values near 1 indicating severe plausibility-faithfulness divergence.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-probe evaluation framework: the Text-Only Probe elicits unconstrained textual responses to measure plausibility, while the Chain-Text Probe requires the model to generate a lightweight causal chain before the text, measuring faithfulness through structural abstraction. The chain-first ordering mirrors the cognitive sequence of structure-before-expression, and the minimal chain requirements (one link for intervention, two for counterfactual) ensure that failure reflects absent structural understanding rather than representational complexity. The CAGE benchmark provides 49,500 open-ended questions across 5,500 COCO images, generated by GPT-4o and validated through three-stage expert review. Evaluation uses a multi-judge framework (GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet) with human validation on 4,500 responses. Fine-tuning experiments on 45,000 chain-annotated examples test whether explicit chain supervision can close the gap, and ablations isolate the effects of chain format, prompting order, and the independence of structural versus perceptual grounding.

## Results

On CAGE (49,500 questions across 5,500 COCO images), seven of eight evaluated VLMs exhibit an Abstraction Gap (AG) exceeding 0.50, meaning their causal chain generation scores are less than half their text scores. Text-Only Probe scores range from 6.76 to 8.26 (L2) and 6.68 to 8.11 (L3), while Chain-Text Probe chain scores drop to 0.42–4.28. The AG ranges from 0.03 (LLaVA-NeXT) to 0.92 (mPLUG-Owl2). Fine-tuning on 45,000 chain-annotated examples fails to close the gap for low-baseline models (e.g., MiniGPT-4 chain scores actually decrease from 2.05 to 0.54 on L3), while Gemini 2.5 Flash achieves a near-zero AG of −0.006.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-NeXT is the only primary-evaluation model with near-zero AG (0.03), maintaining chain scores of 7.61–7.83 comparable to its text scores, confirming that structural causal generation is achievable within current VLM architectures. Gemini 2.5 Flash (MoE architecture) eliminates the gap entirely (AG = −0.006, chain 8.46 vs. text 8.60), while Qwen3-VL shows moderate improvement (AG = 0.313) over most open-source models. Among the seven failing models, mPLUG-Owl2 exhibits the worst AG (0.92) with chain scores near zero (0.42–0.75) despite text scores of 7.06–7.09, and CogVLM2 (AG = 0.88) shows a stark 8.11 text vs. 1.37 chain disparity on L3. LLaVA-RLHF (AG = 0.84) is notable because its explicit hallucination-mitigation training does not improve structural reasoning, and it produces blank outputs in 76.4% of chain-generation cases. InternVL2-76B (AG = 0.57) demonstrates that model scale alone does not close the gap. Fine-tuning on 45,000 chain examples fails to reduce AG for MiniGPT-4 (chain scores drop from 2.05 to 0.54) and mPLUG-Owl2 (0.75 to 0.82, p = 0.36), while Qwen-VL-Chat (moderate baseline AG = 0.50) shows significant improvement (chain L3: 4.28 → 6.47), consistent with the LIMO hypothesis that fine-tuning activates but cannot create absent capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior causal reasoning benchmarks (CausalVLBench, CELLO, CausalVQA, MuCR) that test whether VLMs can *select* valid causal chains from pre-provided candidates, by instead requiring models to *generate* causal structures from scratch without scaffolding. It operationalizes the plausibility-faithfulness distinction from explainable AI (Agarwal et al., 2024; Lyu et al., 2024) into a concrete dual-probe evaluation framework grounded in Pearl's Ladder of Causation. The cognitive science motivation—that structural representations should precede linguistic expression (Levelt, 1989; Jackendoff, 2002)—informs the chain-first prompting design, distinguishing this work from standard text-only VQA evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 7 of 8 VLMs exhibit Abstraction Gap > 0.50 (text scores 6–8 vs. chain scores <2.5) and that 45,000 chain-annotated fine-tuning examples fail to close the gap challenges the assumption that fluent causal language reflects genuine structural understanding, with direct implications for deploying VLMs in high-stakes domains. The demonstrated independence between perceptual grounding (hallucination) and structural grounding (causal abstraction) means that existing hallucination-mitigation training does not transfer to causal reasoning reliability. Open questions include whether MoE architectures (as in Gemini 2.5 Flash, AG ≈ 0) systematically preserve structural generation, whether alignment methods can be redesigned to avoid suppressing chain outputs, and whether the gap generalizes beyond linear chains and COCO imagery to complex causal structures and specialized domains.
