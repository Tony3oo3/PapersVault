## Problem

Classical optimal transport yields a single deterministic plan from a fixed cost matrix, ignoring the inherent variability and noise in real-world cost observations. While the BayesOT framework provides a principled Bayesian posterior over transport plans given stochastic costs, it relies on constrained MCMC (HMC/NUTS) that is computationally prohibitive and fails to converge at scale. This paper asks how to perform scalable posterior inference over the transport polytope without repeated constrained sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes fixed marginals (μ, ν), an ensemble of N noisy cost matrices (e.g., log-normal perturbations of a base cost), and in application settings, source/target images or PCA cell embeddings from which cost views are derived. It outputs a parametric posterior distribution over the relative interior of the transport polytope, from which one can draw transport plan samples, extract the MAP plan, and compute per-entry variance or credible intervals. The variational family is supported on strictly positive couplings (the relative interior), so all sampled plans automatically satisfy the marginal constraints exactly.

## Method

The key insight is that the Sinkhorn map, when restricted to a gauge-fixed logit subspace (the orthogonal complement of additive row/column offsets), becomes a smooth global diffeomorphism from R^r onto the relative interior of the transport polytope, providing a differentiable reparameterization that enforces marginal constraints by construction. A RealNVP-style normalizing flow maps standard Gaussian noise to a latent code, which is exponentiated and passed through T steps of Sinkhorn scaling to produce a valid coupling. Training maximizes the ELBO, where the pushforward density on the manifold is computed via the area formula; the log-determinant of the Jacobian Gram matrix is estimated efficiently using Hutchinson trace estimation combined with Stochastic Lanczos Quadrature, requiring only matrix-vector products with the Sinkhorn Jacobian. The paper proves that both the truncated Sinkhorn coupling and its Jacobian converge geometrically to their exact counterparts, justifying the use of a fixed finite iteration budget T in the variational objective.

## Results

On Office-31 domain adaptation, SPVI achieves a mean classification accuracy of 90.77%, matching JDOT (90.84%) and slightly exceeding OT-MM (90.48%), while substantially outperforming the source-only baseline (87.18%) and SurK (86.27%). In the high-dimensional scalability experiment, SPVI completes posterior inference in 2,144 s at n=256 (r=65,025), roughly 10× faster than HMC/NUTS (21,533 s), and remains feasible at n=512 (7,933 s) where HMC fails to converge within a 10-hour wall-clock limit. Posterior quality is validated via empirical 2-Wasserstein distances of 0.0017–0.0027 between SPVI and HMC sample sets across scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Office-31, SPVI (90.77% mean) is essentially tied with JDOT (90.84%) and marginally ahead of OT-MM (90.48%), while clearly surpassing SurK (86.27%) and the source-only baseline (87.18%) by 3–4 percentage points. SPVI's advantage over JDOT and OT-MM is task-dependent: it wins on dslr→webcam (96.88 vs 96.56/96.17) and dslr→amazon (89.47 vs 88.59), but trails on webcam→dslr (97.43 vs 99.99/98.55) and amazon→dslr (86.81 vs 87.48). Against the sampling-based BayesOT (HMC/NUTS), SPVI is approximately 10× faster at n=256 (2,144 s vs 21,533 s) and is the only method that completes at n=512, where HMC does not converge within a 10-hour limit. The MAP transport plan extracted from SPVI's posterior achieves these accuracy numbers, demonstrating that the distributional framework does not sacrifice point-estimate quality relative to deterministic OT baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a variational-inference alternative to the BayesOT framework (Mallasto et al., 2021), which first formulated posterior inference over transport plans under stochastic costs but relied on HMC/NUTS sampling that becomes intractable at scale. The key conceptual move is repurposing the Sinkhorn-Knopp operator (Cuturi, 2013; Knight, 2007) not merely as a solver for entropic OT but as a differentiable reparameterization of the transport polytope, combined with a gauge-fixed logit embedding that turns the non-injective Sinkhorn map into a global diffeomorphism. The flexible variational family is instantiated with RealNVP-style normalizing flows (Rezende & Mohamed, 2015; Dinh et al., 2017), and the reparameterization trick (Rezende et al., 2014) is adapted to the constrained polytope setting. The paper also draws on Frogner & Poggio (2019) for MAP inference on the polytope and on Gumbel-Sinkhorn networks (Mena et al., 2018) for the differentiability of Sinkhorn layers, but departs from both by targeting general couplings rather than permutations and by performing full posterior inference rather than point estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPVI provides the first scalable variational framework for Bayesian OT, replacing MCMC with differentiable optimization and enabling posterior inference at n=512 where HMC fails to converge entirely. The gauge-fixed Sinkhorn parametrization is a clean theoretical contribution (global diffeomorphism with geometric convergence of truncated iterates) that could serve as a general template for probabilistic inference over constrained couplings beyond OT. However, the method is currently limited to balanced entropic OT, lacks theoretical guarantees on the optimization landscape of the ELBO, and the domain-adaptation results are competitive with but not clearly superior to deterministic baselines like JDOT. Open questions include extension to unbalanced OT, theoretical analysis of flow optimization on the polytope, and whether the variational approximation faithfully captures the full multimodal posterior in high-dimensional regimes.
