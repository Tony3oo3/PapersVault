## Problem

While the straight-through estimator (STE) is widely used to train quantized neural networks, its learning dynamics remain poorly understood, particularly how quantization hyperparameters such as bit width and quantization range shape the training trajectory. Prior theoretical work focused on surrogate gradient properties and convergence guarantees but did not characterize the influence of these hyperparameters on dynamics, and most analyses addressed either weight-only or activation-only quantization rather than the jointly quantized setting that is more representative of practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a linear Gaussian regression model with jointly quantized weights and inputs, parameterized by bit width b, quantization range ω, learning rate η, and regularization λ, and produces a closed system of two deterministic ODEs governing the macroscopic order parameters (alignment m and norm-squared q of the weight vector). From these ODEs, the paper extracts the full generalization error trajectory, identifies fixed points and their stability boundaries as functions of quantization hyperparameters, and quantifies the asymptotic performance gap relative to the unquantized model. The framework is validated against finite-dimensional STE simulations (d = 100–3000) across multiple bit widths and ranges.

## Method

The paper derives a microscopic stochastic differential equation (SDE) for the empirical distribution of individual weight coordinates in the high-dimensional limit (d→∞), then applies an isotropy assumption in the subspace orthogonal to the ground-truth direction to collapse the infinite-dimensional PDE into a closed 2D ODE system for the macroscopic states m and q. The key methodological novelty is extending the standard high-dimensional concentration framework (Wang et al., 2017; 2019) to handle nonlinear transformations of both parameters and inputs simultaneously, which is necessary because the quantizer ψ acts on both weights and inputs. The resulting ODEs predict a characteristic two-phase trajectory—an extended plateau followed by a sharp drop in generalization error—whose timing depends on the quantization range. A fixed-point analysis then characterizes the long-time limit, revealing that quantization can expand the stable learning region (acting as an implicit regularizer) and that the asymptotic error depends non-monotonically on both bit width and range.

## Results

The derived ODEs predict a characteristic two-phase trajectory: an extended plateau in generalization error followed by a sharp drop, with the plateau duration controlled by the quantization range ω. At b=3, small ranges (ω=0.25) slow convergence and raise the error floor, while moderate-to-large ranges (ω≥1.0) accelerate the transition and lower the final error. Input quantization at b_x=3 introduces non-monotone transients and delays convergence relative to unquantized inputs. The fixed-point analysis reveals that the stability boundary 2(σ_ψ²+λ)/σ_ψ⁴ is non-monotonic in bit width, meaning certain quantization configurations actually expand the stable learning region beyond the unquantized baseline, acting as an implicit regularizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the ODE predictions and finite-dimensional STE simulations, showing close agreement at d=900 (weight-only, b∈{2,3,4,5}) and d=500 (joint, b∈{3,4}, b_x∈{3,4,5}). Against the unquantized linear regression baseline (κ_ψ=σ_ψ²=1), quantization introduces a persistent error floor that decreases with bit width but is sensitive to the range ω. Weight-only quantization at b=5 nearly recovers the unquantized error floor, while b=2 retains a visible gap. Joint weight-input quantization degrades performance more than weight-only quantization at the same bit width, with b_x=3 showing the most pronounced non-monotonic transients. In the fixed-point regime (η=10⁻⁴, τ up to 8×10⁶), the leading correction from weight quantization is O(Δ²) and varies non-monotonically with the fractional position p within the quantization cell, confirming that the optimal ω depends jointly on both weight and input quantizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional learning-dynamics framework from statistical physics (Wang et al., 2017; 2019; Ichikawa & Hukushima, 2024), which previously could not handle nonlinear transformations of both parameters and inputs, to the setting of jointly quantized weights and inputs trained via STE. Prior STE analyses (Yin et al., 2019a,b; Long et al., 2021) focused on activation-only quantization in two-layer networks, while static quantization analyses (Kashiwamura et al., 2024; Baldassi et al., 2016) characterized generalization without modeling the training dynamics. The key methodological departure is the isotropy assumption that closes the ODE system for macroscopic order parameters despite the nonlinearity of the quantizer applied to both weights and inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of how quantization hyperparameters (bit width, range) shape STE training dynamics, revealing a plateau-then-drop trajectory whose timing depends on the quantization range and non-monotonic transients at low bit widths. The finding that quantization can expand the stable learning region—acting as an implicit regularizer rather than mere perturbation—challenges the default assumption that quantization only degrades optimization. However, the analysis is restricted to a single-layer linear model with one-pass SGD and an isotropy assumption; whether these dynamics persist in multi-layer architectures with structured data remains open, and the practical guidance for hyperparameter selection in deep networks is indirect.
