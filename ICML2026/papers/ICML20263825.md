## Problem

Lyapunov functions have been widely used to certify convergence of optimization algorithms a posteriori, but their potential as a *design* tool for nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problems remains largely unexplored. Existing Lyapunov-based algorithm design frameworks target minimization, convex-concave saddle points, or monotone variational inequalities, leaving a gap for the broader class of smooth two-sided PL min-max problems where oscillatory dynamics make standard first-order methods unreliable.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a smooth minimax objective f(x,y) satisfying the two-sided PL condition, an initial point (x₀, y₀), and problem constants (μ_f, L_f, L_xy) that determine the step size η and the contraction parameter r. It outputs an approximate saddle point (x_T, y_T) after T iterations, with the guarantee that the exact primal-dual gap V^Exact(x_T, y_T) ≤ V_T ≤ ε. Unusually, the algorithm maintains four iterates (x, y, x̂, ŷ) rather than the standard two, and at each step it evaluates two candidate updates (a gradient step and a variable swap) before committing to whichever achieves a Lyapunov contraction, making it a single-loop, decision-based procedure.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the intractable idealized Lyapunov function V^Exact (which requires exact best-response oracles) with a computable surrogate V(x, y, x̃, ỹ) that upper-bounds V^Exact using PL-inequality gradient-norm bounds, so that V depends only on function values and first-order gradients. TALDA then enforces Lyapunov descent by construction through a tri-action mechanism: at each iteration it (i) updates approximate best-response points x̂, ŷ via gradient steps on f, (ii) computes a candidate gradient-descent-ascent step on the primary iterates, and (iii) tests whether either the gradient step or a variable swap (exchanging the roles of primary and auxiliary iterates) achieves the required contraction V_{k+1} ≤ (1−r)V_k, accepting whichever succeeds. The contraction rate r is set to balance the two competing bounds from the gradient-step and swap cases, yielding r = 2/(1 + γ(δ)(κ_f + κ_xy²)). This produces a linear convergence rate of Õ((κ_f² + κ_f κ_xy²) log²(1/ε)) that explicitly captures cross-player coupling through κ_xy, improving upon AGDA's O(κ_x κ_y² log(ε⁻¹)) in weakly coupled regimes where κ_xy < √κ_f.

## Results

TALDA achieves a linear convergence rate with iteration complexity $\tilde{O}((\kappa_f^2 + \kappa_f \kappa_{xy}^2)\log^2(1/\varepsilon))$ under the two-sided PL condition (Theorem 1), where $\kappa_f = L_f/\mu_f$ and $\kappa_{xy} = L_{xy}/\mu_f$. In the weakly coupled regime ($\kappa_{xy} < \sqrt{\kappa_f}$), this strictly improves upon AGDA's $\mathcal{O}(\kappa_x \kappa_y^2 \log(\varepsilon^{-1}))$ rate. Empirically, on a quadratic minimax problem ($d_x=50, d_y=100$) with $\kappa=10$, TALDA significantly outperforms AGDA when $\kappa_{xy}=2$ (weakly coupled) and also outperforms it when $\kappa_{xy}=20$ (strongly coupled). On the Robust Least Squares problem ($L_x=5697.3, L_y=4.0, L_{xy}=106.7, \mu_x=169.5, \mu_y=4.0$), TALDA consistently outperforms AGDA across theoretical, adaptive, and fine-tuned hyperparameter settings over 60,000 gradient evaluations.

**Table: Iteration complexity comparison under S2SPL.**

| Method | Iteration Complexity | Log Dependence | Coupling Dependence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AGDA (Yang et al., 2020) | $\mathcal{O}(\kappa_x \kappa_y^2 \log(\varepsilon^{-1}))$ | $\log(\varepsilon^{-1})$ | $\kappa_y^2$ (independent of $\kappa_{xy}$) |
| TALDA (this work) | $\tilde{O}((\kappa_f^2 + \kappa_f \kappa_{xy}^2)\log^2(\varepsilon^{-1}))$ | $\log^2(\varepsilon^{-1})$ | $\kappa_{xy}^2$ (captures cross-coupling) |
| TALDA (exact oracle) | $\mathcal{O}((\kappa_f + \kappa_{xy}^2)\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))$ | $\log(\varepsilon^{-1})$ | $\kappa_{xy}^2$ |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is AGDA (Alternating Gradient Descent Ascent) from Yang et al. (2020), which is the only first-order method with established linear convergence guarantees under the two-sided PL condition. On the synthetic quadratic minimax problem, TALDA significantly outperforms AGDA in the weakly coupled regime ($\kappa_{xy}=2 < \sqrt{10}$), consistent with the theoretical prediction that TALDA's dependence on $\kappa_{xy}^2$ rather than $\kappa_y^2$ yields a sharper rate. In the strongly coupled regime ($\kappa_{xy}=20 > \sqrt{10}$), both methods share the same theoretical guarantee, yet TALDA still empirically outperforms AGDA, attributed to its adaptive Lyapunov-descent design. On the Robust Least Squares problem, TALDA outperforms AGDA across all three hyperparameter settings (theoretical, adaptive backtracking, and grid-search fine-tuned); notably, AGDA exhibits apparent initial divergence in Lyapunov values due to its large $\kappa_x \approx 33.6$, while TALDA maintains monotonic Lyapunov decrease by construction. The paper also shows that in the idealized exact-oracle setting, TALDA's contraction rate strictly exceeds that of gradient descent on the idealized Lyapunov function, confirming the tightness of the analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lyapunov-based algorithm design paradigm—previously applied to minimization (Diakonikolas & Orecchia, 2019), submodular maximization (Du, 2022), and monotone variational inequalities (Upadhyaya et al., 2025)—to the nonconvex-nonconcave min-max setting under the two-sided PL condition established by Yang et al. (2020). It builds directly on the idealized Lyapunov function V^Exact and its smoothness/PL properties from Nouiehed et al. (2019), replacing the intractable best-response terms with computable gradient-norm surrogates. The key departure from AGDA (Yang et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022) is that TALDA's convergence rate explicitly depends on the cross-smoothness constant κ_xy rather than κ_y², capturing the actual coupling strength between players rather than assuming worst-case interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Lyapunov-as-design-principle framework is generalizable beyond two-sided PL, with the authors sketching extensions to stochastic settings, constrained convex-concave problems, and monotone variational inequalities. The explicit κ_xy dependence yields a strictly better rate than AGDA in weakly coupled regimes (κ_xy < √κ_f), which is practically relevant for problems where the min and max players interact only mildly. Limitations include the log²(ε⁻¹) dependence (vs. log(ε⁻¹) for AGDA), the use of simplified constants (κ_f = max{κ_x, κ_y}), and empirical validation limited to one baseline (AGDA) on synthetic quadratics and a single RLS problem. Open questions include whether Catalyst-style acceleration can improve the rate while preserving the κ_xy advantage, and whether the framework extends to settings where the two-sided PL condition does not hold.
