## Problem

Existing financial LLM benchmarks rely on simulated or generic data disconnected from real business workflows and focus exclusively on static offline tasks, creating a significant gap between reported benchmark performance and actual efficacy in real-world financial operations. No prior benchmark integrates authentic user query data from both Chinese and U.S. equity markets with online real-time evaluation capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs span heterogeneous financial data: multi-turn user dialogues (avg. 10,361 tokens for FMP), full financial reports (avg. 19,681 tokens for FRA), stock price histories with technical indicators and news for SPP, and real-time market feeds for PAA. Outputs are task-specific: most tasks yield a verifiable open-ended answer scored by accuracy; SPP and SA require a prediction interval (1% and 10% business tolerance respectively) checked against ground truth; PAA produces investment metrics (total return, Sharpe ratio, max drawdown) from a live trading simulation with hourly decision cycles, transaction fees, dynamic slippage, and liquidity constraints over a multi-month period.

## Method

BizFinBench.v2 is constructed from 28,860 authentic user query-response pairs collected from real financial business platforms covering Chinese and U.S. equity markets, organized into four scenarios (Business Information Provenance, Financial Logic Reasoning, Stakeholder Feature Perception, Real-time Market Discernment) comprising eight offline and two online tasks. A three-level quality control pipeline—platform clustering and desensitization, manual screening by ten frontline professionals, and cross-validation by six senior financial experts—filters and normalizes the data while preserving real-world perturbations. The PAA task introduces a real-time LLM investment simulation system that replicates actual trading rules (bid-ask spreads, dynamic slippage, fill rates, commission) on live market data, with the LLM making hourly buy/sell/hold decisions over a multi-month window. Twenty-one LLMs (proprietary, open-source general, and open-source financial) are evaluated under both zero-shot and Chain-of-Thought settings, and a business-perspective error analysis by domain experts categorizes failures into five typologies: Financial Semantic Deviation, Long-term Business Logic Discontinuity, Multivariate Integrated Analysis Deviation, High-precision Computational Distortion, and Financial Time-Series Logical Disorder.

## Results

On BizFinBench.v2 (zero-shot), GPT-5 achieves the highest average accuracy of 61.5% across nine offline tasks, followed by Gemini-3 (61.3%) and Doubao-Seed-1.6 (59.4%). Among open-source models, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 leads at 53.3%, while the best financial-domain model (Dianjin-R1) reaches only 35.7%. In the online Portfolio Asset Allocation task, DeepSeek-R1 achieves a +47.34% total return with a Sharpe ratio of 1.25, outperforming all other commercial LLMs (which all posted negative returns) and approaching mid-tier quantitative strategies, though its 53% maximum drawdown remains a critical risk concern. Financial experts score 84.8% average accuracy, establishing the practical business threshold that no LLM currently meets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary LLMs, GPT-5 (61.5%) narrowly edges out Gemini-3 (61.3%) and Doubao-Seed-1.6 (59.4%), while Claude-Sonnet-4 (39.5%) and Grok-4 (37.2%) lag significantly, particularly on quantitative computation (FQC: 29.8% and 9.3% respectively). The best open-source general model, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 (53.3%), trails GPT-5 by 8.2 percentage points, and all four financial-domain-specific models (Dianjin-R1, FinX1, Fino1, Fin-R1) underperform even the 32B general-purpose Qwen3-32B (41.3%), with Dianjin-R1 (35.7%) being the best at only 5.6% below Qwen3-32B. In the PAA investment task, DeepSeek-R1 is the sole LLM to generate positive returns (+47.34% TR, SR 1.25), while GPT-5 (−13.80%), Grok-4 (−10.15%), Gemini-3 (−6.61%), Claude-Sonnet-4 (−2.11%), and Qwen3-Max (−3.79%) all underperform the S&P 500 ETF baseline. However, even DeepSeek-R1's 53% maximum drawdown far exceeds that of the equal-weight quarterly strategy (−9.20%) and the momentum Top-5 strategy (−56.89% but with +101.71% return), indicating LLMs still cannot match established quantitative strategies on risk-adjusted returns. Under CoT prompting, most models degrade (GPT-5 drops from 61.5% to 54.6%; Claude-Sonnet-4 collapses from 39.5% to 13.7%), with DeepSeek-R1 being the notable exception, improving from 41.5% to 50.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in financial LLM benchmarking by departing from the dominant paradigm of synthetic or simulated data (FinQA, CFLUE, FinEval, FinBen, FinMaster) and purely static offline evaluation. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes the general LLM-evaluation task by grounding it in authentic user query-response data from live Chinese and U.S. equity trading platforms and by introducing a dual-track framework that pairs offline accuracy tasks with online, real-time investment simulation (stock price prediction and portfolio asset allocation). The expert-informed error taxonomy (five business dilemmas) also represents a methodological departure from prior benchmarks that typically report only aggregate accuracy without domain-specific failure analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark gap—GPT-5 achieves only 61.5% versus the 84.8% practical business threshold—suggesting that current LLMs are far from deployable in real financial operations despite strong scores on existing benchmarks. The finding that Chain-of-Thought prompting degrades performance for most models (Claude-Sonnet-4 drops from 39.5% to 13.7%) challenges a widely assumed benefit of CoT in reasoning-heavy domains. Open questions include whether the five identified error categories (semantic deviation, logic discontinuity, multivariate analysis failure, computational distortion, time-series disorder) can be addressed through targeted fine-tuning or architectural changes, and whether the online PAA task's results will hold across different market regimes and time periods.
