## Problem

Listwise multimodal rerankers for long documents face two compounding latency bottlenecks: (1) concatenating visual tokens from many candidate page images produces extremely long input sequences that dominate Transformer prefill cost, and (2) autoregressive decoding of multi-token ranking outputs (including reasoning traces) adds sequential generation overhead. Existing VLM-based rerankers like MM-R5 achieve strong accuracy but incur 10–50× higher inference latency than is practical for real-world, latency-sensitive retrieval systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and k candidate document page images (RGB, resized so the largest dimension does not exceed 1024 px), each assigned a unique single-token letter identifier (A, B, C, …). Before the main LLM pass, a lightweight query-image early interaction module prunes each page's visual tokens to a configurable keep ratio ρ (e.g., 50%), retaining only the tokens with the highest max-cosine similarity to the query's hidden states. The output is a full permutation of the k pages in descending relevance, produced by reading the identifier-token logits from a single forward pass rather than generating a sequence autoregressively.

## Method

ZipRerank is built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (vision encoder frozen) and is trained in two stages: Stage 1 pretrains general listwise ranking on RankZephyr text passages rendered as 280×280 images using a weighted RankNet loss on first-step identifier logits plus a standard LM loss; Stage 2 finetunes on MMDocIR document images with a soft-ranking loss whose target distribution follows a geometric decay (γ=0.5) derived from a GPT-5-mini teacher's ordering, making it robust to noisy teacher supervision. At inference, two complementary mechanisms eliminate the latency bottlenecks: (i) query-aware visual token pruning computes max cosine similarity between query hidden states (extracted from a short prefix pass) and each visual token, keeping only the top-ρ fraction per image while preserving original RoPE positions; (ii) single-logit decoding replaces autoregressive generation by sorting the k identifier-token logits from one forward pass. The paper also provides a FLOPs scaling law showing ~1/ρ² prefill speedup in the long-context regime, a pruning error bound of O(ε)·V_max in terms of discarded attention mass, and a lemma showing the hard-max pruning score is within log N_q of a smooth log-sum-exp attention-pooling surrogate.

## Results

On MMDocIR with DSE wiki-ss first-stage retrieval, ZipRerank achieves 84.8% Macro Recall@3 and 89.0% Macro Recall@5, surpassing the prior SOTA reranker MM-R5 (79.1% and 83.8%) while reducing cached LLM inference latency from 3.82 s to 0.36 s (~10× speedup). ZipRerank slightly trails MM-R5 on Recall@1 (64.2 vs. 66.1 Macro) but substantially outperforms all other listwise rerankers (LamRA: 77.6% R@3; UniME: 70.9% R@3). On the out-of-domain ViDoRe benchmark, ZipRerank achieves the best NDCG@5 among listwise rerankers (53.4 with DSE, 59.9 with ColQwen), improving over MM-R5 by 4.4 and 4.1 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZipRerank substantially outperforms both first-stage retrievers it is paired with: over DSE wiki-ss it gains +18.2 points on Macro Recall@1 (46.0→64.2) and +15.3 on Recall@3 (69.5→84.8); over ColQwen it gains +5.7 on Recall@1 (60.4→66.1) and +5.9 on Recall@3 (79.0→84.9). Against the zero-shot VLM baselines, ZipRerank dramatically surpasses Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (24.8→64.2 Macro R@1) and approaches GPT-5-mini (70.0) at ~65× lower latency (0.36 s vs. 23.38 s). Among dedicated listwise rerankers, ZipRerank beats LamRA by +7.2 points on Macro Recall@3 (77.6→84.8) and UniME by +13.9 points (70.9→84.8). The closest competitor is MM-R5: ZipRerank trails it by 1.9 points on Recall@1 (64.2 vs. 66.1) but leads by +5.7 on Recall@3 and +5.2 on Recall@5, while running ~10× faster (0.36 s vs. 3.82 s). On the out-of-domain ViDoRe benchmark, ZipRerank outperforms DocReRank (pointwise) by +0.1 NDCG@5 with DSE and +3.4 with ColQwen, and beats MM-R5 by +4.4 and +4.1 respectively, confirming that the efficiency gains generalize beyond MMDocIR.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZipRerank extends the single-token decoding paradigm for listwise reranking (Gangi Reddy et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024b) from text-only settings to long multimodal documents, where the challenge is compounded by thousands of visual tokens per page. It departs from the dominant multimodal reranker MM-R5, which relies on autoregressive chain-of-thought reasoning, by showing that a properly trained model can rank all candidates in a single forward pass without generating any reasoning trace. The query-aware visual token pruning mechanism draws on recent VLM efficiency work (SparseVLM, VisionZip) but applies it in a reranking-specific way: using query hidden states extracted from a prefix pass to score and filter visual tokens before the main LLM forward pass. The two-stage training recipe—text pretraining on rendered passages followed by VLM-teacher-distilled soft-ranking finetuning—combines knowledge distillation and transfer learning in a way not previously applied to multimodal listwise reranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× latency reduction while matching or surpassing SOTA multimodal rerankers addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: current VLM-based rerankers are too slow for real-time M-RAG and document search systems. The finding that autoregressive reasoning is not necessary for competitive multimodal reranking (given the right training objectives) challenges the assumption that explicit CoT is required for complex cross-modal ranking. Limitations include dependence on a strong VLM teacher for Stage 2 supervision, potential loss of fine-grained visual evidence under aggressive token pruning, and evaluation primarily on document-image benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or audio modalities, whether weaker or open-source teachers suffice, and whether the single-logit formulation can be extended to multi-hop or compositional retrieval tasks.
