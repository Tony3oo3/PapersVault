## Problem

Active learning has not been systematically studied under the dual challenges of class imbalance and label noise simultaneously, particularly in the text domain. While foundation models offer rich prior knowledge for data-efficient learning, existing foundation-model-based active learning methods do not explicitly account for imbalanced class distributions or noisy oracle annotations, leaving a gap in realistic deployment scenarios where both issues co-occur.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled pool of RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 2D PathMNIST) or text sequences (TREC, AGNews, SST-2), a frozen pretrained foundation model (CLIP-L14 or Llama-3.1-8B) that generates class probabilities and pseudo labels in a single forward pass, a small trainable model (ResNet-18 or RoBERTa-Base), and a human annotation oracle with a fixed per-round budget B. The output is a trained small classifier plus a partition of the unlabeled pool into a clean set (assigned high-confidence pseudo labels at zero annotation cost) and a noisy set from which the top-B most uncertain samples are queried for ground-truth labels.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-phase pipeline: (1) Prior Labeling extracts class probabilities from both the foundation model and the small model, with pseudo labels derived from the foundation model's argmax; (2) Imbalance-aware Uncertainty Sampling computes a Products-of-Experts (PoE) combined probability by multiplying and normalizing both models' class distributions, then calculates an entropy weighted by the ratio of each class's pseudo-label count to the maximum class count (w_i/w_max), which amplifies the entropy of majority-class samples so that the top-ρ highest-entropy samples form a noisy set while the remainder form a clean set with reliable pseudo labels; (3) Small Model Training fine-tunes the small model on the union of the labeled pool and the clean set, while the top-B samples from the noisy set are selected by the small model's own entropy for oracle annotation. The imbalance-aware weighting is the key differentiator: by scaling entropy proportionally to class frequency, the method preferentially routes majority-class uncertain samples to the oracle (where they are most likely to be noisy) while allowing minority-class confident samples to be pseudo-labeled for free, thereby mitigating both imbalance and label noise without additional annotation cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with merge imbalance, PriorAL achieves 67.86% balanced pool accuracy at 10% annotation cost, surpassing the best baseline (BADGE at 63.28%) which requires 20% of the budget — a >50% reduction in annotation cost. Under long-tailed imbalance on CIFAR-100, PriorAL reaches 66.30% at 11% budget versus 18.58% for BADGE at the same budget. On the long-tailed Trec text dataset, PriorAL attains 96.20% balanced test accuracy, the highest among all methods. With 20% injected label noise on long-tailed CIFAR-100, PriorAL achieves 42.34% balanced pool accuracy at 11% budget, outperforming the best baseline by 17.30 percentage points at 22% budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriorAL consistently outperforms all nine active learning baselines on balanced pool accuracy across the majority of dataset settings, with the largest margins on image datasets. On CIFAR-100 merge imbalance (10% budget), PriorAL (67.86%) exceeds BADGE (48.50%) by 19.4 points and CORESET (44.64%) by 23.2 points; on long-tailed CIFAR-100 (11% budget), the gap widens to 47.7 points over BADGE (18.58%). In the text domain, PriorAL is competitive with CORESET and BADGE on pool accuracy (e.g., 87.12% vs. 87.42% and 89.14% on Trec LT) but achieves the highest balanced test accuracy (96.20% vs. 95.44% and 95.40%). DIRECT and Most Likely are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing PriorAL by 15–30 points on most datasets. The Pretrained Priors baseline (zero annotation cost) achieves high pool accuracy on image data (67.45% on C100 merge) but collapses on text (43.04% on Trec LT), whereas PriorAL maintains strong performance in both domains by combining foundation model priors with uncertainty-based oracle queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the products-of-experts (PoE) framework of Hinton (1999, 2002) from its original role in combining constraint-satisfying distributions to a new purpose: jointly fusing foundation model and small model predictions to guide active learning sample selection. It departs from prior active learning under imbalance (GALAXY, DIRECT, BADGE) by incorporating foundation model priors as a free source of pseudo-labels, and from prior active learning with foundation models (Bhatt et al. 2024, Gupte et al. 2024) by explicitly handling the joint challenge of class imbalance and oracle label noise. The imbalance-aware entropy weighting draws on class-balanced loss ideas (Cui et al. 2019) but applies them to the uncertainty computation rather than the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% annotation savings over the best active learning baseline is practically significant for deployment scenarios where labeling is expensive, and the cross-domain validation (image + text, 21 settings) strengthens generalizability claims. However, the method's reliance on a strong foundation model (CLIP-L14, Llama-3.1-8B) limits applicability in domains where no suitable pretrained model exists, and the imbalance-aware weighting is a simple frequency-proportional scheme that may not capture more complex distributional shifts. Open questions include whether the PoE combination remains effective when the foundation model is significantly weaker than the small model, how the method behaves under distribution shift between the foundation model's pretraining data and the target domain, and whether the clean/noisy set partition (controlled by ρ) can be made adaptive rather than a fixed hyperparameter.
