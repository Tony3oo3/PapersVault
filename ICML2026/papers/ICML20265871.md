## Problem

Existing defenses for large vision-language models have predominantly targeted jailbreak attacks, leaving general visual prompt injection—where imperceptible image perturbations manipulate model behavior toward arbitrary objectives—largely unaddressed. Moreover, prior defenses lack a mechanistic understanding of how adversarial images actually drive model behavior, and they often incur substantial computational overhead or degrade model utility on benign tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265871/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt as input, encodes the image into a sequence of embedding tokens via the LVLM's vision encoder, and produces a safe text response in the same format as normal LVLM generation. The key operational detail is that the defense operates entirely at the embedding-token level: it identifies a small subset (top-k, typically 5% of tokens) of image embedding vectors and replaces them with zero vectors before feeding the sanitized sequence to the language model. The entire defense requires only a single partial forward-backward pass, adding roughly 0.2 seconds of latency over the undefended baseline.

## Method

GTM is built on the empirical finding that adversarial effectiveness in prompt injection images is concentrated in a sparse subset of critical image tokens rather than being uniformly distributed across the embedding sequence. To localize these tokens, GTM computes a Hidden-State Gradient Norm score for each image embedding token: the L2 norm of the gradient of the first hidden-layer representation's L2 norm with respect to that token. This representation-aware metric overcomes a critical failure mode of conventional first-token logit-based attribution, which becomes non-discriminative under token alignment (when the adversarial target's first token coincides with the model's natural output). The paper proves that the Hidden-State Gradient Norm ranking is asymptotically consistent with the full adversarial loss gradient ranking, providing a theoretical guarantee for accurate localization. The top-k highest-scoring tokens are then zeroed out, and the sanitized embedding sequence is passed to the LVLM for generation, effectively disrupting the adversarial attack path while preserving benign visual information.

## Results

On jailbreak attacks (Table 3), GTM reduces ASR to 0.00–0.91% across all perturbation budgets (ε=16 to ∞), compared to 68–97% without defense. Against VMA prompt injection (Table 1), GTM achieves 0–1.64% average ASR across five attack categories on Qwen2-VL, Phi-3-Vision, and LLaVA-v1.5, versus 90–99% undefended. On MM-Vet utility evaluation, GTM preserves model performance with negligible degradation (score ~56.8 vs 62.3 undefended), while DiffPure and Guard suffer substantial utility loss. Inference overhead is minimal: 3.50 s and 16.96 GiB GPU memory versus 3.28 s / 15.66 GiB for the undefended model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTM matches DiffPure in defense effectiveness (both achieve near-0% ASR on VMA and ImgHijack) but with dramatically lower overhead: 3.50 s inference vs. 7.22 s, 16.96 GiB vs. 18.33 GiB memory, and no auxiliary diffusion model required; moreover, DiffPure causes severe utility degradation on text-rich images (MM-Vet OCR subcategory) while GTM preserves performance. Against ECSO, GTM is substantially stronger across all settings (e.g., 0.91% vs. 9.09% ASR at ε=32 on jailbreak; 0% vs. 68% average on VMA for Qwen2-VL). GTM also clearly outperforms ASTRA (0.91% vs. 15.45% at ε=32) and CIDER (0.91% vs. 17.27% at ε=32), both of which degrade sharply as perturbation budget increases. Compared to Guard, GTM achieves equal or slightly better ASR (0.91% vs. 1.82% at ε=32) while running 7× faster (3.50 s vs. 25.35 s) and requiring no additional model or training. Under adaptive attacks (Appendix F.2), GTM still reduces ASR from 82.73% to 4.55–19.09% (ε=32/256), confirming robustness even when the attacker is defense-aware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes gradient-based attribution (well-established in interpretability and adversarial detection) for a new purpose: token-level defense in the embedding space of LVLMs. It departs from prior LVLM defenses (ASTRA, Cider, ECSO, Guard, DiffPure) that target jailbreak attacks specifically, by addressing the broader and underexplored problem of general visual prompt injection. The key conceptual departure is identifying a theoretical failure mode of standard first-token logit-based attribution under token alignment, and replacing it with a hidden-state gradient norm metric backed by a ranking-consistency theorem. The sparse-token-dependence finding connects to the broader adversarial ML literature on non-uniform vulnerability but is the first systematic demonstration in the LVLM image-embedding context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic insight that adversarial behavior in prompt injection images is concentrated in a sparse subset of image tokens provides a principled basis for lightweight defenses, and the practical recipe (single forward-backward pass, 5% token masking, no auxiliary models) is directly deployable in production LVLM pipelines. However, adaptive attack experiments (Table 11) show that a fully defense-aware attacker can recover ASR to ~30%, indicating the defense is not a hard guarantee. Open questions include whether the sparse-token structure persists under stronger adaptive optimization, whether the approach extends to structure-based (non-perturbation) prompt injection, and how it behaves under multi-turn or agentic settings where the attack signal may be distributed across turns.
