## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks trained with BPTT and surrogate gradients are expected to improve with more timesteps, yet in practice they exhibit a counter-intuitive performance degradation known as the timestep scaling paradox (TSP). The paper traces TSP to long-term temporal gradient vanishing in the standard LIF neuron, where the decay factor α<1 causes k-step temporal gradients to shrink as |α|^k, preventing error signals from later timesteps from reaching earlier states and weakening cross-timestep dependencies.

## Input / Output

TS-LIF is a drop-in replacement for the LIF neuron within existing SNN architectures (SEW-ResNet, SpikeYOLO, 3B-Net, 3FC-Net), so it accepts the same modalities as the host network: RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 224×224 ImageNet), event streams (CIFAR10-DVS, Gait), EEG segments (Sleep-EDF), audio spike trains (SHD), and tabular time series (Electricity). Outputs are standard task-specific: class labels for classification, bounding boxes for COCO detection, and scalar predictions for Electricity forecasting. The distinguishing operational detail is that TS-LIF is designed to be evaluated at substantially larger timestep counts (T=4 up to T=200) where vanilla LIF degrades, and it requires storing the membrane potential from two timesteps ago (V[t-2]) in addition to the immediate previous state.

## Method

The TS-LIF neuron augments the standard LIF update with three mechanisms. First, long-term memory reconsolidation injects the membrane potential from two steps ago, β[t]·V[t-2], into the current update, creating a sparse cross-step gradient pathway that bypasses the repeatedly attenuated t-1 chain and raises the spectral norm of the augmented state-transition matrix above |α|^k. Second, a temporal forgetting mechanism periodically (every q=3 steps) zeros out the accumulated V[t-1] contribution, preventing the additional pathway from causing excessive temporal buildup and gradient explosion. Third, a GroupNorm layer is applied after the update for t>2 to stabilize membrane potential scale drift. The decay factor α is made layer-wise learnable, and β[t] is a learnable per-timestep coefficient, so the network adapts the balance between short-range decay and long-term injection in a data-driven manner. Theoretical analysis shows the k-step temporal gradient bound under TS-LIF is governed by the product of augmented transition norms rather than |α|^k, and the forgetting step tightens this bound by replacing the full transition with a smaller reset transition at periodic intervals.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet19, TS-LIF achieves 82.14% accuracy at T=6, outperforming the strongest BPTT baseline (LM-H, 80.31%) by 1.83%. On ImageNet with ResNet34, it reaches 70.11% at T=6, surpassing SLTT (66.19%) and OTTT (65.15%) by 3.92% and 4.96% respectively. On COCO (23.1M params, T=8), TS-LIF attains 57.8% mAP@50 and 41.0% mAP@50:95, exceeding SpikeYOLO (55.6%/39.1%) by 2.2%/1.9%. On CIFAR10-DVS (T=10), it achieves 83.20% accuracy, beating FSTA-SNN (81.50%) and Dspike (75.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-LIF consistently outperforms all 29 baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet19, T=4), it beats the strongest prior BPTT method LM-H (80.31%) by 1.41% and MI-TRQR (77.70%) by 4.02%; at T=6 it further extends the lead to 82.14% vs. TEBN's 76.41%. On CIFAR10-DVS (T=10), TS-LIF (83.20%) surpasses FSTA-SNN (81.50%) by 1.70% and Dspike (75.40%) by 7.80%, and notably matches FSTA-SNN's T=10 result using only T=4 (81.60%). On ImageNet (ResNet34, T=6), it exceeds SLTT (66.19%) and OTTT (65.15%)—both of which explicitly discard temporal gradients—by 3.92% and 4.96%, directly validating that strengthening temporal gradient propagation is beneficial. On COCO (T=8, 23.1M params), TS-LIF (57.8% mAP@50) outperforms SpikeYOLO (55.6%) and Meta-SpikeFormer (51.3%), and unlike SpikeYOLO it monotonically improves as T increases from 4 to 8, avoiding the timestep scaling paradox. On Electricity forecasting, TS-LIF achieves the best average R² (0.970) and lowest RSE (0.308), with the advantage widening at the longest prediction horizon (96 steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BPTT-with-surrogate-gradients training paradigm for SNNs but departs from two dominant strategies for handling temporal gradients: (1) methods that discard temporal gradient paths to simplify optimization (SLTT, OTTT), which the authors argue undermines a core advantage of SNNs, and (2) methods that add auxiliary supervision branches to strengthen gradient flow (DeepTAGE), which increase computational cost. Rather than modifying the training procedure, TS-LIF modifies the neuron model itself—introducing a two-step memory reconsolidation term and a periodic forgetting gate—making it a drop-in replacement for the standard LIF neuron. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–4) provides the first formal characterization of why vanilla LIF temporal gradients vanish as |α|^k and how the augmented state transition matrix in TS-LIF changes the contraction behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and resolving the timestep scaling paradox, this work opens a previously underexplored axis of SNN scaling (temporal depth) that could yield further accuracy gains without increasing spatial model size, which is particularly valuable for energy-constrained neuromorphic hardware where adding timesteps is cheaper than adding layers. The minimal overhead (0.01M extra parameters, comparable energy) makes TS-LIF a practical upgrade. Open questions include whether the two-step reconsolidation window generalizes to even longer temporal dependencies (T > 200), whether the approach extends to other neuron models beyond LIF (e.g., Izhikevich, adaptive exponential), and whether the periodic forgetting period q can be learned end-to-end rather than fixed. The paper also notes that training time and memory increase modestly, which could matter for very long sequences.
