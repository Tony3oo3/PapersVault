## Problem

The leading differentially private OLS method, AdaSSP, achieves near-optimal error bounds but incurs a multiplicative log(1/ϱ) factor that becomes prohibitive when the failure probability is kept exponentially small. Meanwhile, Gaussian-sketching-based DP-OLS methods (Sheffet 2017; Lev et al. 2025) offer a smaller leading multiplier but have unclear regimes where they outperform AdaSSP, and their guarantees depend on λ_min^{XY} (the minimum eigenvalue of the concatenated (X,Y) Gram matrix), which is rarely large in practice. This paper asks: can a sketching-based method retain the smaller multiplier of prior sketching approaches while avoiding their dependence on λ_min^{XY} and matching or beating AdaSSP's eigenvalue dependence on λ_min^X?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a fixed, bounded dataset (X, Y) with X ∈ R^{n×d}, Y ∈ R^n, known per-row bounds ‖x_i‖ ≤ C_X (normalized to 1) and |y_i| ≤ C_Y, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ) and a target failure probability ϱ. It outputs a single private linear predictor θ̂ ∈ R^d. The method requires n ≥ d (overdetermined system) and uses three tunable hyperparameters—sketch size k, number of iterations T, and clipping level C—that the paper shows can be set globally without data-dependent tuning in practice.

## Method

IHM (Iterative Hessian Mixing) adapts the non-private Iterative Hessian Sketch (Pilanci & Wainwright, 2016) to the DP setting by sketching only the design matrix X (not Y) at each of T iterations. At iteration t, it forms a noisy sketched Hessian X̃_t = S_t X + ηξ_t using a fresh Gaussian sketch S_t and calibrated noise η, and a noisy gradient-like term G̃_t = X^T clip_C(Y − Xθ̂_t) − η²θ̂_t + σζ_t with per-coordinate clipping and additive Gaussian noise. The update θ̂_{t+1} = θ̂_t + (k⁻¹X̃_t^T X̃_t)⁻¹ G̃_t mirrors a Newton step on the sketched quadratic. The key insight is that restricting the sketch to X alone makes the privacy noise scale with λ_min^X (the Gram matrix of X) rather than λ_min^{XY} (the concatenated Gram matrix), substantially reducing noise when X is well-conditioned while avoiding the error amplification that arises from sketching Y. Privacy is composed over 2T+1 mechanism calls (one eigenvalue calibration, T sketching steps, T Gaussian-mechanism steps) via Rényi-DP, and the excess empirical risk bound improves over AdaSSP by removing a factor up to √d in the leading multiplier.

## Results

IHM is evaluated on 33 real-world linear regression datasets (from Wang, 2018) plus 2 synthetic datasets, reporting normalized excess empirical risk (divided by n) with 95% confidence intervals across ε ∈ [0.1, 10] and δ = 1/n². Theoretically, IHM's excess risk bound (Theorem 1) improves upon AdaSSP by removing a multiplicative factor of up to √d in the leading multiplier γ. Empirically, IHM with fixed hyperparameters (k = 6·max{d, log(4T/ρ)}, T = 3, C = C_Y) consistently outperforms or matches both AdaSSP and LinMix across all four dataset regimes tested (large residual, large λ_min^X, small ‖θ*‖, and mixed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaSSP (Wang, 2018), IHM achieves a strictly better leading multiplier: γ_h/γ_a = √(max{1, g^X/max{d, log(1/ρ)}}), meaning IHM retains AdaSSP's scale term C_h² = C_a² while gaining a factor of up to √d improvement whenever g^X = O(max{d, log(1/ρ)}). Against LinMix (Lev et al., 2025), IHM retains the smaller sketching-based multiplier (avoiding the √d factor in γ_a) while eliminating the irreducible L(θ*) term that appears in LinMix's guarantee and can dominate the bound when residuals are large. IHM also matches AdaSSP's eigenvalue dependence on λ_min^X (rather than the smaller λ_min^XY used by LinMix), giving it an advantage on well-conditioned designs. Empirically, IHM outperforms both baselines across all 35 datasets in the full suite, with LinMix only beating AdaSSP in the narrow regime where L(θ*) = O(γ_m·C_m²), C_Y²‖θ*‖² = Θ(max{C_Y², ‖θ*‖²}), and λ_min^X ≪ 1 simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the non-private Iterative Hessian Sketch (Pilanci & Wainwright, 2016) to the differentially private setting, making a key structural departure from prior DP-OLS sketching methods (Sheffet, 2017; Lev et al., 2025) that sketch the full (X, Y) concatenation. By sketching only inside the Hessian X^T X and iterating, IHM avoids the irreducible L(θ*) term that plagues single-shot Gaussian sketching approaches and reduces the noise level when X is well-conditioned. The privacy analysis builds directly on the Gaussian mixing mechanism and Rényi-DP calibration from Lev et al. (2025), while the utility analysis is positioned against AdaSSP (Wang, 2018), the established state-of-the-art for bounded-data DP-OLS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IHM removes a multiplicative factor of up to √d from AdaSSP's excess risk bounds and provides a new accuracy analysis (Theorem 2) for prior Gaussian-sketching methods that clarifies their fundamental limitations—specifically, an irreducible L(θ*) term that can dominate in low signal-to-noise regimes. Open questions include whether the g^X term and hidden O~ factors can be tightened, whether the iterative framework extends to general convex losses via a private Newton sketch, and whether structured random projections can improve computational efficiency while preserving DP. The method's practical advantage is that hyperparameters (k, T, C) can be set globally without data-dependent tuning, unlike DP-SGD-style approaches.
