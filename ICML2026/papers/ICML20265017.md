## Problem

DMD-style few-step video diffusion distillation faces two coupled challenges: the auxiliary fake-score network must track a rapidly evolving generator (requiring multiple costly updates per iteration), and the reverse-KL matching objective is mode-seeking, which suppresses motion dynamics in video generation. The paper addresses both by replacing the reverse-KL objective with a stable Fisher-based one and redesigning the fake-score update mechanism to require far fewer inner-loop steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265017/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distilled model takes a text prompt as input and generates an 81-frame video at 480×832 resolution using only 4 denoising steps (NFE=4), compared to the 100-step teacher (Wan2.1-T2V-14B). Training requires only ~200K text prompts without paired ground-truth videos, and the generator and fake-score network are both initialized from the frozen teacher and trained with full parameters at a learning rate of 1e-6.

## Method

SGMD adopts a fake-score perspective: the fake score is treated as the primary target that moves toward the teacher, while the generator acts as a tracker maintaining score-consistency. The distribution-matching objective is a teacher stop-gradient Fisher divergence (½‖s_fake − sg[s_real]‖²), which under ideal tracking is directionally consistent with reverse-KL matching but avoids unreliable teacher input gradients on out-of-distribution states. To handle inevitable tracking lag, SGMD introduces a pair of dual potentials: a negative-residual (NR) term added to the generator update that pulls x₀ toward the fake-score prediction, and a residual-contraction (RC) term in the fake-score update that contracts the tracking residual. This yields a lightweight two-step (two-backward) bilevel update per iteration with a tunable weight λ (optimal at 0.1), reducing fake-score updates from 5 to 1 and achieving a ~3× training speedup while substantially improving motion dynamics (OptFlow 9.29 vs. 4.51 for DMD2) under 4-step distillation.

## Results

Under 4-step distillation of Wan2.1-T2V-14B, SGMD achieves the best FVD (100.3) and highest motion intensity (OptFlow 9.29, near the base model's 9.41) among all distilled methods, while remaining competitive on VBench quality (84.77) and semantic alignment (75.64). By reducing fake-score updates per iteration from 5 to 1, SGMD achieves an approximately 3× training speedup over the DMD2-style baseline. In human evaluation, SGMD is preferred over DMD2 in overall preference (65% vs. 13%) and motion quality (71% vs. 24%), with text-video alignment and visual quality judged as ties (90% and 74% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DMD2 (the strongest reverse-KL baseline), SGMD substantially outperforms on motion-related metrics—OptFlow 9.29 vs. 4.51 and DynDeg 93.06 vs. 80.56—and achieves a lower FVD (100.3 vs. 115.1), while DMD2 retains a small edge on VBench quality (85.05 vs. 84.77) and semantic score (77.46 vs. 75.64); SGMD also requires only 1 fake-score update per iteration versus DMD2's 5, yielding a ~3× training speedup. Against TSG-Fisher (the same Fisher objective without dual potentials), SGMD improves FVD (100.3 vs. 126.7), quality (84.77 vs. 82.98), and semantic score (75.64 vs. 71.50) while reducing fake-score updates from 5 to 1, confirming that the NR/RC dual potentials are critical for stable training at low tracking overhead. Against TSG-SIM, SGMD dominates on every reported metric (FVD 100.3 vs. 193.0, OptFlow 9.29 vs. 3.27, DynDeg 93.06 vs. 59.72), with the paper attributing TSG-SIM's poor performance to its fixed implicit-term weighting that effectively imposes an overly strong tracking correction (analogous to λ=1), causing slow convergence and persistent blurriness.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGMD sits at the intersection of DMD-style distribution matching distillation (DMD, DMD2) and Fisher-divergence-based score matching (SIM, SiD), all targeting few-step video diffusion distillation. The key conceptual departure is a 'fake-score perspective': rather than treating the fake score as a passive tracker that must be repeatedly refit to the generator (the standard DMD/SIM view), SGMD treats the fake score as the primary optimization target moving toward the teacher, with the generator acting as a tracker. This reframing motivates a pair of dual potentials (negative-residual for outer-loop correction, residual-contraction for inner-loop tracking) that replace SIM's fixed implicit-gradient weighting with a tunable, decoupled two-step update under a teacher stop-gradient Fisher objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~3× training speedup (reducing fake-score updates from 5 to 1 per iteration) combined with substantially improved motion dynamics makes SGMD a practically attractive option for deploying few-step video diffusion at scale, particularly for interactive or real-time applications. The fake-score perspective and the gradient analysis of how tracking lag bends the effective update direction provide a reusable analytical lens that could inform other bilevel distillation settings beyond video. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 1-step distillation, whether the λ trade-off generalizes across model scales and architectures, and whether the motion-quality gains persist at higher resolutions or longer durations where temporal consistency becomes harder to maintain.
