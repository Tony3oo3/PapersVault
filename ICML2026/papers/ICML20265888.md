## Problem

Despite rapid gains from scaling and self-supervised pretraining, it remains unclear whether dense geometric predictors perform any inference-time verification of physical plausibility when visually salient edge cues conflict with 3D structure. No controlled diagnostic existed to isolate the effect of physically unsupported edges from generic high-frequency noise, and standard global metrics (RMSE, AbsRel) can even improve under structural collapse, masking the failure entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265888/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The diagnostic protocol takes standard RGB images (480×640 on NYU Depth v2, 1242×375 on KITTI) and produces a perturbed image by extracting top-15 Canny edge segments, applying random affine warps (rotation ±60°, translation ±25% of image dimensions), and darkening them at intensity α=0.8 (~36% mask coverage). Evaluated models consume these perturbed RGB images and output dense depth maps (and derived surface normal maps); the key quantitative outputs are collapse ratios (RMSE deviation from clean prediction normalized by energy-matched noise baseline), Edge F1 for structural fidelity, and oracle-repair recovery percentages that bound global spillover.

## Method

Scrambled Edges is a falsification-style counterfactual that relocates and rotates real edge segments into geometrically smooth regions while darkening them, simultaneously violating surface continuity, illumination coherence, and occlusion causality. Two matched controls isolate the mechanism: energy-matched high-pass noise (frequency control) and edge-shaped noise at original positions (structure control), ensuring that collapse is attributed to physical-prior violation rather than high-frequency energy or edge sparsity. A Prior-Violation Ladder ablates each violation independently, revealing that occlusion causality (direction/T-junction disruption) dominates collapse. Global spillover is quantified via output-level oracle patch repair (Eq. 7), which recovers only 47% of clean predictions even with perfect mask knowledge, proving errors propagate beyond perturbed pixels. Multi-view photometric and depth-depth consistency on KITTI further expose a metric paradox where appearance-based reprojection error decreases while geometric consistency degrades sharply.

## Results

Across four dense depth predictors on NYU Depth v2 (N=1449), Scrambled Edges induce collapse ratios of 1.88× (MiDaS v2.1), 2.34× (MiDaS DPT), 2.02× (DepthAnything v1), and 3.20× (DepthAnything V2) relative to energy-matched high-pass noise. The Direction (occlusion causality) condition drives the strongest single-prior collapse (up to 3.18×), while structure-only Edge-Shaped noise causes minimal degradation (≤1.71×). Oracle output-level repair recovers only 47% of the clean prediction, confirming global error propagation beyond the perturbed mask. Cross-dataset validation on KITTI (N=6852) confirms generalization with slightly lower ratios (1.21×–2.48×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four feed-forward depth regressors fail to quarantine physically unsupported edges: DepthAnything V2 (DINOv2+ SSL) is the most sensitive at 3.20× collapse, followed by MiDaS DPT (ViT) at 2.34×, DepthAnything v1 at 2.02×, and MiDaS v2.1 (CNN) at 1.88×—revealing an SSL paradox where stronger pretraining correlates with higher edge-cue adoption. Generative estimators show attenuated but statistically significant collapse: Marigold (diffusion) reaches 1.55× (Cohen's d=0.88) and DepthFM (flow-matching) 1.11× (d=0.25), suggesting iterative inference delays but does not eliminate unsupported-edge adoption. SAM (segmentation) exhibits no differential sensitivity (0.84× collapse ratio), confirming task selectivity—the failure is specific to edge-to-geometry mapping rather than a general fragility. Notably, all models remain stable under energy-matched high-pass noise (1.00× baseline) and even under structure-matched Edge-Shaped noise (≤1.71×), isolating the failure to physical-prior violations rather than high-frequency content or edge sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of robustness diagnostics and physical vision. It departs from standard corruption benchmarks (ImageNet-C, frequency-shift sensitivity) and adversarial patch literature by intervening on interpretable, physically-motivated edge evidence with energy-matched and structure-matched controls that isolate prior violations from generic high-frequency content. Conceptually, it connects to classical surface-perception priors (Gibson, Marr, Nakayama & Shimojo) and modern geometric-consistency objectives (photometric consistency in self-supervised depth, NeRF multi-view coherence), but reframes the question behaviorally: rather than imposing physical structure through training objectives, it probes whether an end-to-end predictor spontaneously discounts unsupported cues at inference time. The diagnostic protocol (Scrambled Edges) is novel in its falsification-style design, and the Geometric Collapse failure mode is distinct from both noise sensitivity and adversarial fragility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that scaling and SSL pretraining do not yield inference-time physical verification (the 'negative emergence' result) challenges a common assumption in the field and has direct safety implications: phantom walls in depth maps could mislead robotics and autonomous navigation systems. The metric paradox—where standard GT-RMSE actually improves under collapse while boundary fidelity drops by 50–67%—argues that current evaluation protocols are structurally blind to this failure mode, motivating boundary-aware metrics in depth benchmarking. Open questions include whether support-aware cue-selection modules or learned plausibility scoring can close the gap, whether iterative generative inference (Marigold, DepthFM) can be pushed further, and whether the diagnostic generalizes to other boundary-sensitive tasks like optical flow and stereo matching. A key limitation is that Scrambled Edges is a synthetic diagnostic, not a realistic corruption, and the paper does not train new robust predictors.
