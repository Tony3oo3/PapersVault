## Problem

Existing NL-to-Opt benchmarks are dominated by toy-sized or synthetic instances (typically 4–120 variables/constraints), causing LLM performance to saturate and masking the structural challenges of industrial optimization models with 10³–10⁶+ variables and constraints. No benchmark aligns natural-language specifications with solver-validated reference formulations grounded in real industrial MILPs, leaving the true capability of NL-to-Opt systems on realistic problems largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance provides a compact natural-language problem description (abstract form with symbolic parameters) alongside external CSV data files that instantiate index sets and coefficients, following a strict model-data separation format that keeps NL length largely independent of problem scale. The expected output is a mathematical optimization model expressed with explicit loop-based constraint families (model.md) plus solver-executable code (solve.py) that reads the external data and invokes an industrial solver. All 223 instances are triply aligned: NL description, canonical formulation, and solver code must be semantically equivalent to the original MIPLIB 2017 MPS formulation.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage structure-aware reverse construction pipeline that transforms flat MIPLIB 2017 MPS files into NL-to-Opt benchmark instances. Stage 1 (structural abstraction) has OR experts recover indexed variable groups and repeated constraint families from the flattened algebraic rows, producing a compact loop-based scaffold that preserves the generative logic lost in MPS format. Stage 2 (structure-preserving Opt-to-NL generation) uses expert-designed NL blueprints to deterministically translate the scaffold into faithful natural-language descriptions, with optional LLM-based linguistic polishing strictly constrained to surface-level rephrasing. Stage 3 (semantic validation) performs independent NL-to-Opt reconstruction with multiple LLMs under a Pass@8 criterion, followed by expert-driven human-LLM interaction for instances that fail automatic reconstruction, yielding 223 verified one-to-one reconstructions that exactly preserve the mathematical content of their source instances.

## Results

On MIPLIB-NL, the best-performing system (GPT-5.1) achieves only 39.05% Pass@1 accuracy, while the average across all 14 evaluated systems is just 17.85%. In stark contrast, the same systems achieve 78.78% average on NL4Opt, 91.78% on MAMO-EasyLP, and 90.97% on NLP4LP. Fine-tuned models suffer the most dramatic degradation: OptMATH-Qwen2.5-7B drops from 79.91% on NL4Opt to 0.00% on MIPLIB-NL, and SIRL-Qwen2.5-32B drops from 87.90% to 1.43%. Scale analysis (RQ2) shows accuracy declines from small (<500 vars) to very large (≥10000 vars) instances, with modeling-level errors (48.3%) and execution-level errors (45.0%) dominating failures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 14 NL-to-Opt systems (5 fine-tuned models and 9 bare LLMs) on 11 benchmarks, with MIPLIB-NL as the new industrial-scale test. Fine-tuned models (OptMATH, SIRL, ORLM) that achieve 79–99% on toy benchmarks like NL4Opt and MAMO-EasyLP collapse to 0–5.5% on MIPLIB-NL, indicating that supervised training on small-scale data does not transfer to industrial structure. Among bare LLMs, GPT-5.1 leads MIPLIB-NL at 39.05% Pass@1, followed by Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (36.19%) and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 Thinking (30.00%), but all still fall far below their 70–94% performance on existing benchmarks. The gap between best and worst on MIPLIB-NL (39.05% vs 0.00%) is far larger than on any prior benchmark, confirming that MIPLIB-NL exposes structurally grounded failure modes (data/index coupling errors, incomplete constraint families) that are invisible at toy scale. No system exceeds 40% on MIPLIB-NL, whereas the same systems routinely exceed 80% on NL4Opt or NLP4LP, demonstrating that prior evaluations substantially overestimate current NL-to-Opt capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NL-to-Opt benchmarking line initiated by NL4Opt, OptiMUS, and subsequent datasets (IndustryOR, MAMO, ComplexOR, NLP4LP, OptMath, Bench4Opt, etc.), all of which are predominantly toy-scale or synthetically generated. Its key methodological departure is a structure-aware reverse construction pipeline that recovers loop-based constraint families and indexed variable groups from flat MIPLIB 2017 MPS files, then reverse-generates natural-language specifications grounded in that recovered structure. While OptMath (Lu et al., 2025) also performs reverse generation from MIPLIB, MIPLIB-NL emphasizes structural fidelity at industrial scale (10^3–10^7 variables), explicit loop-scaffold recovery, and a unified model-data separation format that keeps NL descriptions compact regardless of instance size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a large and previously hidden performance gap: systems scoring 70–90%+ on existing toy benchmarks drop to roughly 18–39% (and near-zero for several fine-tuned 7B models) on MIPLIB-NL, with error modes shifting from simple constraint-logic mistakes to structurally grounded data/index coupling failures. This reframes the evaluation landscape for NL-to-Opt, suggesting that prior progress may substantially overestimate real-world modeling capability. Limitations include the 223-instance size (vs. 1,300+ in MIPLIB 2017) due to manual structure-recovery effort, restriction to MILP (no NLP/SOCP), and the fact that 23 infeasible/open instances are excluded from quantitative evaluation. Open questions include whether targeted fine-tuning on industrial-scale loop-structured data can close the gap, and whether the reverse-construction methodology can be automated to scale beyond the current 223 instances.
