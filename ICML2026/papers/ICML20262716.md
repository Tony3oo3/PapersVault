## Problem

Extending Spiking Neural Networks to deep language models faces two fundamental obstacles: dead neurons in deep SNNs cause severe gradient vanishing that cripples training, and removing Softmax for energy efficiency eliminates the winner-takes-all competition needed for selective token attention. Existing spiking language models (SpikeBERT, SpikeLM) either trail their ANN counterparts substantially or retain costly floating-point operations, failing to achieve fully spike-driven efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes BERT-style tokenized text sequences (max length 128) and produces task-specific predictions (accuracy, F1, MCC, or correlation) across GLUE tasks. Internally, all activations are constrained to discrete binary spikes (0/1) over T=4 time steps, with computation relying predominantly on addition rather than multiply-accumulate operations. The LIF neurons use a decay factor β=0.5 and threshold V_th=1.0, and the entire pipeline eliminates floating-point intermediate representations, softmax, and GELU activations.

## Method

SpikingLM is a fully spiking BERT architecture (12 layers, 768 hidden, 12 heads) that introduces two key mechanisms. First, Distribution-aware Scaling applies learnable per-channel scaling vectors λ to linear layer outputs before LIF neurons, rescaling activations into an activation-friendly range to prevent dead neurons; at inference, λ is fused into the preceding weight matrix (W̃ = W·diag(λ)), incurring zero additional overhead. Second, Spike2Max restores winner-takes-all attention via γ_t·2^(A−max(A)−1), where A is the integer spike-coincidence matrix QK^⊤; because Q and K are binary, base-2 exponentiation reduces to exact left bit-shifting and max-subtraction replaces the softmax denominator, cutting attention energy by over 95% versus standard softmax. The model is pre-trained with masked language modeling using sigmoid surrogate gradients and fine-tuned end-to-end on GLUE, achieving 77.1 average score at 5.79 mJ per token.

## Results

SpikingLM achieves a GLUE average score of 77.1 with an inference energy of 5.79 mJ, representing a 57.9% energy reduction compared to SpikeLM (13.74 mJ) and an 88.7% reduction compared to BERT_base (51.41 mJ). Among all spiking baselines, SpikingLM attains the best GLUE average, outperforming Spikingformer (66.8) by 10.3 points and SpikeBERT (59.8) by 17.3 points. On individual tasks, SpikingLM scores 90.1 on SST-2, 87.9 on MRPC, and 84.6 on QNLI, while maintaining the lowest energy consumption (5.79 mJ) across all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ANN baselines, SpikingLM (77.1 avg) trails BERT_base (83.2) by 6.1 points but consumes only 11.3% of its energy (5.79 vs. 51.41 mJ); it also outperforms BERT_3L (75.9) and ELMo (70.2) in average score while using roughly half the energy of BERT_3L. Among SNN baselines, SpikingLM clearly dominates: it surpasses Spikingformer (66.8) by 10.3 points with 14.3% lower energy, SpikeBERT (59.8) by 17.3 points with 59.5% lower energy, and SpikeLM (76.5) by 0.6 points with a 57.9% energy reduction (5.79 vs. 13.74 mJ). The closest competitor is SpikeLM, which relies on floating-point spikes and softmax; SpikingLM matches or exceeds it on every GLUE task while being fully spiking and eliminating both softmax and GELU. In the controlled perplexity comparison (Table 3), Spike2Max also reduces perplexity from 24.58 to 19.78 relative to vanilla SSA while lowering energy by 7.8%, confirming that the attention mechanism improvement does not come at a hardware cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spikformer line of work (Zhou et al., 2022), which introduced Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) by replacing softmax with direct spike coincidence counting. SpikingLM identifies two specific failure modes of SSA in deep language models—gradient degradation from dead neurons and loss of token selectivity—and addresses them with Distribution-aware Scaling (a learnable static scaling vector that replaces RMSNorm) and Spike2Max (base-2 exponentiation with max-subtraction to restore winner-takes-all dynamics). It also builds on the broader SNN training literature (surrogate gradients, LIF neurons) and departs from prior spiking language models like SpikeBERT and SpikeLM by eliminating all floating-point operations, achieving a truly fully spiking architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpikingLM demonstrates that fully spiking language models can approach ANN-level GLUE performance (77.1 vs. 83.2 for BERT_base) while reducing energy by 57.9% over the closest SNN competitor, which is meaningful for edge and neuromorphic deployment. However, several open questions remain: energy savings are estimated from operation counts rather than measured on actual neuromorphic hardware (Tianjic, Loihi), the model is limited to BERT-scale (12 layers, 768 hidden) and encoder-only architectures, and the substantial 6-point GLUE gap with BERT_base suggests the approach has not yet closed the performance gap. Whether Distribution-aware Scaling and Spike2Max generalize to decoder-only or generative LLMs, and whether the bit-shift-based attention remains numerically stable at larger sequence lengths, are important directions for future work.
