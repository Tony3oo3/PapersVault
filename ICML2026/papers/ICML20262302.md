## Problem

Existing Bayesian optimization approaches for policy search treat the optimization objective as a black-box function, discarding the sequential structure of returns induced by policy roll-outs. This leads to inefficient learning because Monte-Carlo value estimates have high variance and finite-horizon value iteration methods fail to capture informative temporal correlations in discounted infinite-horizon problems. The paper asks whether the temporal difference structure of value functions can be exploited within a GP-based BO framework to improve sample efficiency while retaining rigorous regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes episodic trajectory data τⁿ = {(s_i, r_i)} of variable length M_n from roll-outs of a linear policy π(s,θ) = θᵀs with θ ∈ [-1,1]^{d_s}, where states live in a compact subset of ℝ^{d_s}. It produces the next policy parameter θ_{n+1} by maximizing an optimistic GP-UCB estimate of the discounted infinite-horizon return J(θ) = E_{s_0}[V(s_0, θ)], along with a full posterior GP over the value function V(s, θ). The GP model is updated every episode with all accumulated trajectory data, and the expectation over initial states is approximated via 10 fixed samples.

## Method

TD-GP-UCB places a GP prior V ~ GP(0, k_V) on the value function and exploits the closedness of GPs under linear operators to derive a structured prior on temporal differences ΔV(s,s⁺,θ) = V(s,θ) − γV(s⁺,θ), yielding the kernel k_Δ(z_i,z_j) = k_V(x_i,x_j) − γk_V(x_i,x_j⁺) − γk_V(x_i⁺,x_j) + γ²k_V(x_i⁺,x_j⁺). Since E_{s⁺}[ΔV] = r(s,π(s,θ)), immediate rewards serve as unbiased regression targets, and the posterior over V is obtained in closed form from the joint GP distribution. Policy selection uses the standard UCB rule θ_n = argmax_θ E_{s_0}[μ_V + β(δ_n)σ_V], with a union-bound schedule δ_n = 6δ/(π²n²). The key theoretical contribution is bounding σ_V²(x_0) in terms of σ_Δ²(z_m) along trajectories plus a residual (M+1)γ^M σ_V²(x_M) that vanishes as M_n grows as O(log N), enabling regret bounds of Õ(√N · Γ_{k_V}(N log N)) that match or improve upon kernelized RL baselines up to a log factor.

## Results

The paper evaluates TD-GP-UCB against three baselines (MC-GP-UCB, kQ-learn, DDPG) on Gymnasium Pendulum (dense and sparse rewards), Gymnasium Cart-Pole (sparse rewards), and an adapted LQR design problem, reporting cumulative regret averaged over 20 random seeds with γ=0.9. TD-GP-UCB achieves consistently lower cumulative regret across all initial-state distributions (random, downwards, upwards) in the dense-reward Pendulum setting, while DDPG requires over 200 episodes before regret decays in non-random scenarios. In the adapted LQR problem, MC-GP-UCB achieves only a slight improvement over the random baseline, whereas TD-GP-UCB significantly outperforms it. The theoretical regret bound is O*(√N · Γ_{k_V}(N log N)), which is worse by only a log(N) factor compared to standard GP-UCB's O*(√N · Γ_{k_V}(N)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-GP-UCB outperforms all three baselines in the dense-reward Pendulum environment across all three initial-state distributions (random, downwards, upwards), where MC-GP-UCB suffers from high return variance in the random scenario and flat early rewards in the upwards scenario, kQ-learn improves only for downwards initial states, and DDPG fails to benefit from structured initial states (requiring >200 episodes for regret decay). In the sparse-reward Pendulum setting, TD-GP-UCB still leads but all methods show increased regret due to discontinuous value functions; in the Cart-Pole variant with discrete actions, TD-GP-UCB is initially worse than MC-GP-UCB due to a steep regret growth before the first hyperparameter tuning, but its regret then grows very slowly. On the adapted LQR problem, TD-GP-UCB significantly outperforms MC-GP-UCB, which barely improves over random within the evaluated episodes. Overall, TD-GP-UCB wins or ties in 5 out of 6 experimental settings, with the sole loss (Cart-Pole sparse) being transient and attributed to the initial hyperparameter-tuning phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Bayesian optimization (GP-UCB, Srinivas et al. 2012), Bayesian RL with Gaussian processes (Engel et al. 2003), and the mathematical machinery of linearly constrained GPs (Jidling et al. 2017). It departs from standard BO for policy search, which treats episode returns as i.i.d. black-box samples (Wilson et al. 2014), by exploiting the temporal difference structure of discounted infinite-horizon value functions within the GP prior. Unlike kernelized RL approaches (Chowdhury & Oliveira 2023) that target finite-horizon value iteration, the paper formulates a closed-form GP posterior for the value function from TD targets and provides the first full regret and information-gain analysis for this structured model in a BO setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a theoretical gap by showing that the temporal difference GP's maximum information gain is asymptotically bounded by that of its base kernel (Theorem 3.9), yielding regret bounds only a log(N) factor worse than vanilla GP-UCB. Practically, TD-GP-UCB consistently outperforms MC-GP-UCB, kernelized Q-learning, and DDPG across dense/sparse reward and varied initial-condition scenarios, demonstrating that the sequential structure of rollouts carries exploitable information that standard BO discards. Key limitations include the need for a global optimization oracle, sensitivity to rollout length, and the curse of dimensionality in both the acquisition maximization and GP learning; the authors identify local-optimality variants, informative priors, and policy fingerprinting as promising directions for scaling to higher-dimensional problems.
