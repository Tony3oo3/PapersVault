## Problem

Collaborative perception systems are vulnerable to adversarial attacks from malicious CAVs that broadcast perturbed feature maps to induce phantom objects or mask real hazards. Existing defenses rely on output-level consensus (detecting statistical outliers in bounding boxes or scores), which fails against stealthy attacks that produce visually plausible outputs and suffers from high false-positive rates under environmental noise such as adverse weather. The paper addresses the gap of detecting adversarial perturbations at the internal feature level, where attacks inevitably violate the intrinsic manifold structure of scene representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes intermediate feature maps (C×H×W tensors) from all neighboring CAVs and the ego vehicle, along with vehicle poses for coordinate alignment via a differentiable warping function. It outputs a benign/malicious classification for each CAV group, and when an anomaly is detected, recursively partitions the group to isolate the specific malicious vehicle. The final output is a filtered set of benign CAVs whose features are fused into 3D detection results, with the decision threshold dynamically adjusted per frame based on ego perception uncertainty.

## Method

Cerberus is an unsupervised, training-free defense framework built on three components. First, Isomorphic View Fusion sorts neighboring CAVs by azimuth angle and interleaves them into two groups with equivalent fields of view, eliminating geometric viewpoint bias that confounds attack detection. Second, Feature-Level Consistency Verification quantifies conflicts between each group's fused features and the ego features across three complementary dimensions: topological integrity via SSIM, semantic direction via cosine dissimilarity, and energy distribution via an exponential L1-norm mapping, aggregated into a joint anomaly score. Third, an Uncertainty-Aware Adaptive Decision module computes ego feature entropy to dynamically modulate the detection threshold, combining a 3-sigma statistical bound from a sliding window of historical benign scores with an uncertainty-driven relaxation that prevents false positives under degraded perception. The key insight is that adversarial perturbations disrupt the structured latent manifold of feature representations (spatial continuity, semantic alignment, activation magnitudes) even when decoded outputs remain plausible, enabling detection at a level invisible to output-level consensus methods.

## Results

On the OPV2V benchmark with V2VAM, Cerberus reduces MOR attack ORR/ASR to 7.87%/0.19% and restores AP@0.5 to 0.88 (AP@0.7 = 0.79), outperforming all baselines. Under the stealthier TOR(In) attack, Cerberus limits ASR to 4.48% while ROBOSAC and CP-Guard remain nearly ineffective (ASR > 92%). Under TOR(Out), Cerberus achieves 38.22% ASR versus 94.83% for CP-Guard and 62.32% for LUCIA. On the V2V4Real dataset, Cerberus reduces AttFusion's MOR ASR from 44.26% to 9.05% and ORR from 88.52% to 29.88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cerberus outperforms all three baselines across every attack type and CP backbone. Against ROBOSAC (ICCV 2023), the gap is most dramatic under TOR attacks: on V2VAM, ROBOSAC's ASR remains above 89% for all TOR variants, while Cerberus suppresses it to 4.48% (TOR-In) and 9.56% (TOR-Random); under MOR, Cerberus also improves ORR from 12.97% to 7.87% and AP@0.5 from 0.77 to 0.88. Against LUCIA (USENIX Security 2025), Cerberus achieves lower ASR under all TOR variants (e.g., 4.48% vs. 14.14% for TOR-In on V2VAM) and higher AP@0.5 (0.88 vs. 0.82 under MOR). Against CP-Guard (AAAI 2025), the advantage is most pronounced under TOR attacks where CP-Guard's ASR stays above 91% while Cerberus keeps it below 10%; under MOR the two are closer (7.87% vs. 8.27% ORR on V2VAM), but Cerberus still edges ahead on AP@0.5 (0.88 vs. 0.87). Overall, the feature-level consistency verification provides a decisive advantage over output-level consensus methods, particularly against stealthy TOR perturbations that evade detection in the bounding-box domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant consensus-based defense paradigm for collaborative perception (ROBOSAC, LUCIA, CP-Guard), which verifies consistency at the output level (bounding boxes, object scores), by shifting verification into the internal feature space. It repurposes the recursive partitioning and sampling logic of prior consensus methods but applies it to multi-dimensional feature metrics (SSIM-based topological integrity, cosine semantic alignment, L1 energy distribution) rather than detection outputs. The isomorphic view fusion module is a novel geometric preprocessing step that addresses a gap in prior sampling-based methods, which ignore viewpoint heterogeneity and thus suffer from high false-positive rates. The uncertainty-aware adaptive threshold draws on standard statistical process control (3-sigma rule) but applies it to ego-feature entropy, a connection not previously explored in CP security.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from output-level to feature-level verification addresses a fundamental blind spot: stealthy attacks (e.g., TOR) that produce plausible outputs while corrupting internal representations are invisible to all prior consensus defenses, as confirmed by the near-100% ASR of ROBOSAC and CP-Guard under TOR. The practical impact is significant for deploying CP in safety-critical autonomous driving, where a single compromised vehicle can mask real hazards. However, the threat model remains white-box and single-attacker, and evaluation is primarily on the simulated OPV2V benchmark; open questions include robustness to coordinated multi-attacker scenarios, black-box attacks, and whether the feature-level consistency assumption holds across fundamentally different sensor modalities (camera, radar) beyond LiDAR.
