## Problem

Existing LLM evaluation metrics focus on final-answer accuracy or surface-level statistics (perplexity, token length, lexical diversity), treating the Chain-of-Thought reasoning process as a black box. There is no lightweight, reference-free metric that diagnoses the structural quality of how a model constructs its argument, making it difficult to pinpoint specific reasoning flaws or use process quality as a training signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262162/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw LLM CoT reasoning block as input, segments it into sentences via spaCy, and produces a composite TRACE score in [0,1] alongside per-sentence multi-label annotations over 8 constructive elements (Claim, Data/Evidence, Warrant, Backing, Qualifier, Rebuttal, Monitoring, Evaluation). The score decomposes into State Validity (local structural soundness) and Transition Coherence (inter-sentence logical flow), both computed via rule-based operations on the label sequence. The classifier is a 184M-parameter DeBERTa-v3-base requiring <2GB VRAM, making it substantially lighter than LLM-judge alternatives.

## Method

TRACE operates as a two-stage pipeline grounded in Toulmin's argumentation model and Flavell's metacognition theory. Stage 1 (TRACE-DeBERTa) is a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-base with an 8-dimensional sigmoid multi-label head, trained on ~100K LLM-annotated reasoning sentences to classify each sentence's constructive elements. Stage 2 applies a rule-based scoring algorithm: State Validity computes the mean Jaccard similarity of each sentence's label set against a predefined set of allowed argumentative states, while Transition Coherence measures the net density of Good Transitions (e.g., Data→Claim) minus Bad Transitions (e.g., Monitoring→Qualifier) across consecutive sentences. The final score is a weighted sum (α=0.7 for State Validity), and the same metric serves as a structural reward component in GRPO-based reinforcement learning, where it complements accuracy and length rewards to guide models toward sounder reasoning processes.

## Results

TRACE achieves a Pearson correlation of r=0.741 (Spearman ρ=0.755) with benchmark accuracy across 26.3K reasoning samples from 7 LLMs on 39 benchmarks, drastically outperforming surface-level baselines (Token Length r=−0.147, Perplexity r=+0.221, MTLD r=−0.207). Per-model correlations remain strong (r>0.78 for all 7 models). As an RL reward signal on GSM8K, TRACE+Accuracy+Length yields 84.69% accuracy (+9.9% over the SFT base) and 59.90% on out-of-distribution ARC-Challenge, compared to 77.86%/57.93% for accuracy-only rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE substantially outperforms all three surface-level baselines on the primary correlation metric: Perplexity (Radford et al., 2019) achieves only r=+0.221 and MTLD (McCarthy & Jarvis, 2010) achieves r=−0.207, both far below TRACE's r=+0.741. On Arena Hard v2.0 judge-prediction, TRACE leads in Math (64.37% vs. MTLD 58.62%, Perplexity 50.57%) and Coding (55.78% vs. MTLD 52.96%), but loses to MTLD in Creative Writing (50.00% vs. 53.45%), consistent with TRACE's design focus on argumentative structure rather than lexical diversity. In the RL setting, adding TRACE to the reward function improves GSM8K accuracy by 6.83 points over the Accuracy+Length baseline (84.69% vs. 77.86%) and ARC-Challenge by 1.97 points (59.90% vs. 57.93%), demonstrating complementary structural guidance beyond accuracy-only training.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE repurposes argumentation mining—originally developed to identify functional units (claims, premises) in human persuasive essays (Stab & Gurevych, 2014)—and transplants it to machine-generated Chain-of-Thought, grounding the label taxonomy in Toulmin's argumentation model and Flavell's metacognition theory. It departs from process-based evaluators like ProcessBench and PRM, which require step-level correctness labels or heavyweight verifier models, by being fully reference-free and relying on a 184M-parameter DeBERTa classifier plus rule-based scoring. It also contrasts with LLM-as-a-judge paradigms (Arena-Hard, FLASK) that remain black-box in their decision-making, by offering interpretable, sentence-level diagnostics of where reasoning structure breaks down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACE provides a lightweight (<2 GB VRAM, ~2 s/sample) reference-free diagnostic that correlates strongly with benchmark accuracy (r=0.74) and functions as a complementary RL reward signal, filling a practical gap between outcome-only metrics and expensive LLM-judge pipelines. However, it explicitly does not capture factual correctness, and its utility is concentrated in deductive domains (math, natural sciences) while degrading on knowledge-retrieval and creative tasks. Open questions include how to incorporate intermediate-step factual grounding, how to handle mixed-format inputs (code, LaTeX), and whether the rule-based transition sets generalize beyond the specific model families tested.
