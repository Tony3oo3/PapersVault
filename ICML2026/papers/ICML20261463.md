## Problem

Scaling video Diffusion Transformers to high-resolution, long-duration generation is bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of self-attention, and existing sparse attention methods (both static-pattern and dynamically learned) suffer pronounced quality degradation—water-ripple artifacts, spatial warping, temporal flickering—when sparsity exceeds moderate levels. The paper identifies that the root cause is not the sparsity ratio itself but the misalignment between the sparse tile mask and the tile-wise geometry of full attention, compounded by head-specific heterogeneity in spatiotemporal dependency patterns.

## Input / Output

Veda operates as a drop-in sparse attention replacement within existing video DiT backbones (Waver-T2V-1B/12B, Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B/14B), taking a text prompt as input and producing videos at resolutions up to 720P with up to 241 frames (sequence length ~220K tokens). The method is evaluated at 480P/81F (~34K tokens), 720P/81F (~76K tokens), and 720P/241F (~220K tokens), with sparsity levels of 90% and 95% reducing per-layer attention FLOPs by roughly an order of magnitude while preserving visual fidelity.

## Method

Veda formulates tile selection as an explicit reconstruction problem from full attention rather than letting sparsity emerge implicitly from the diffusion objective. A lightweight estimator compresses each query/key tile into a TripPool descriptor (concatenated Avg, Max, Min statistics) and projects it through head-specific MLPs to predict tile-level attention scores, supervised by KL divergence against max-pooled full-attention scores with a stop-gradient on backbone features to decouple mask learning from feature learning. A Head-Aware Tiling strategy assigns per-layer-per-head spatiotemporal tile factorizations (p_t, p_h, p_w) under a fixed hardware tile budget of 128, selected offline to minimize full-attention output approximation error, addressing the heterogeneity in local-spatial vs. long-range-temporal attention patterns across heads. A custom tile-skipping kernel built on ThunderKittens with TMA and Warp Specialization on Hopper GPUs materializes the predicted masks, reaching ~80% of FlashAttention-3 MFU and converting theoretical FLOPs reduction into a 5.1× end-to-end speedup at 720P/241F.

## Results

On Waver-T2V-12B generating 720P 10-second videos (241 frames), Veda at 95% sparsity achieves a 5.1× end-to-end GPU speedup (19.4 min → 3.8 min) and a 10.5× self-attention speedup, reducing attention overhead from 92% to 50%. On Wan2.1-T2V-14B (720P, 81 frames), Veda delivers a 2.63× end-to-end speedup with 7.08× self-attention acceleration. VBench scores at 90% sparsity on Waver-T2V-1B remain near full-attention levels (Subject Consistency 0.940 vs. 0.938; Motion Smoothness 0.980 vs. 0.979), and the speedup increases with sequence length, confirming favorable scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VSA (the primary dynamic sparse-attention baseline), Veda at 90% sparsity outperforms VSA at 87.5% sparsity on Waver-T2V-1B in Subject Consistency (0.940 vs. 0.933) and wall-clock time (31.9s vs. 34.3s), while matching or exceeding Motion Smoothness (0.980 vs. 0.978). In human evaluation (Waver-bench 1.0), Veda at 95% sparsity surpasses VSA at 87.5% sparsity in Visual Quality (54% vs. 46% preference), and at equal 95% sparsity dominates VQ (76% vs. 24%) and Overall Quality (39% vs. 16%). Against the FlashAttention-3 dense baseline, Veda at 90% sparsity achieves perceptual parity in blinded side-by-side evaluation (50% vs. 49% VQ preference, 63% tie rate in Motion Quality), confirming no noticeable quality degradation. On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, Veda at 90% sparsity shows a slight drop in Subject Consistency (0.887 vs. 0.940 full attention) but still beats VSA on wall-clock time (37.6s vs. 42.5s) and Dynamic Degree (0.913 vs. 0.795). The Oracle Mask, while preserving quality at 90% sparsity, is impractical (126.3s vs. 31.9s wall time) since it requires computing full attention, underscoring Veda's advantage of achieving near-oracle quality with a lightweight learned estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

Veda extends the trainable sparse attention paradigm introduced by VSA (Zhang et al., 2025b) for video DiTs, but departs fundamentally in how tile selection is learned: rather than letting the diffusion objective implicitly shape sparse patterns, Veda formulates tile scoring as an explicit distillation problem supervised by full-attention tile scores. It also moves beyond the static-pattern approaches (SVG, STA, Sparse-vDiT) and the mean-pooling-based dynamic estimators (VSA, VMOBA) by introducing TripPool statistics and Head-Aware Tiling to address two distinct failure modes—estimation error and structural mismatch—that the paper's empirical study identifies as the true bottlenecks. The hardware tile-skipping kernel, built on ThunderKittens and Hopper TMA/WGMMA primitives, positions Veda as a systems-level contribution alongside the algorithmic one, reaching ~80% of FlashAttention-3 MFU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.1× end-to-end speedup at 720P/241 frames, with speedup increasing as sequence length grows, makes long high-resolution video generation substantially more tractable and positions distilled sparse attention as a composable acceleration layer orthogonal to caching and sampling-distillation methods. The empirical finding that mask quality (alignment with full-attention tile geometry) dominates over sparsity ratio reframes how the community should think about sparse attention design—shifting focus from 'how much to prune' to 'how accurately to select.' Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond 95% sparsity, whether temporal caching of tile scores across diffusion steps can further reduce mask-preparation overhead, and whether the stop-gradient decoupling strategy generalizes to other auxiliary-head architectures.
