## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving models suffer significant performance degradation when deployed across domains (different cities, weather, lighting), largely due to training data imbalance that under-weights rare, safety-critical scenarios. While LLM-based approaches offer open-world knowledge, they provide no inherent cross-domain guarantee and incur prohibitive retraining costs for domain adaptation. RoCA addresses this by learning a compact, probabilistic representation of driving scenarios that supports both robust in-domain training and efficient cross-domain adaptation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view camera images plus ego status (pose, velocity, acceleration) and produces ego trajectory waypoints over a 3-second horizon (2 waypoints/sec), a trajectory class from 48 ego groups (16 per driving command: turn left, turn right, go straight), agent trajectory waypoints and classes from 64 agent groups, and a predictive variance per prediction. The GP module operates on 256-dimensional token embeddings extracted by the base E2E encoder, and can be deployed either as a zero-overhead training regularizer or as an inference-time trajectory predictor adding ~16 ms latency (measured on RTX 3090).

## Method

RoCA learns a codebook of 112 basis token groups (48 ego + 64 agent, each of size 64) that bijectively map to representative trajectories sampled from ground-truth data. A Gaussian Process with an RBF kernel computes pairwise similarity between the current scene's ego/agent tokens and the basis tokens, yielding a closed-form posterior over future trajectories with mean and variance. Training proceeds in three stages: pretraining the base E2E model, learning basis tokens and GP parameters via a reconstruction loss (token recovery from basis) plus variance-weighted trajectory supervision and triplet loss for embedding separation, then finetuning the base model with GP-based regularization that aligns its predictions with the probabilistic GP output. For target-domain adaptation, the frozen GP serves as a teacher signal (enabling unsupervised adaptation without ground-truth labels) and its predictive variance drives uncertainty-based active learning to select the most informative samples for fine-tuning.

## Results

On the 220-route Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, RoCA(ORION) achieves a driving score of 80.38 and success rate of 58.2%, improving over the ORION baseline (77.74 / 54.6%) and raising mean ability from 54.72% to 61.11%. On nuScenes validation (standardized protocol), RoCA with SparseDrive-S as the base model reduces average L2 error from 0.65 m to 0.55 m and collision rate from 0.14% to 0.09%. In zero-shot sim-to-real transfer (Bench2Drive→nuScenes), RoCA(ORION) cuts L2 error from 0.98 m (ORION) to 0.68 m, and after fine-tuning further reduces it to 0.44 m (0.52 m without ground-truth labels).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoCA consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluation setting. On Bench2Drive closed-loop, RoCA(ORION) improves driving score by +2.64 and mean ability by +6.39 percentage points over ORION, while RoCA(SSR) delivers a dramatic +27.8% mean-ability gain over SSR (35.38→48.98). In zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to nuScenes, RoCA(ORION) reduces L2 error from 0.98 m (ORION) to 0.68 m and collision rate from 0.44% to 0.23%; after fine-tuning, it reaches 0.44 m / 0.08%, beating ORION (0.72 m / 0.29%) and DiMA (0.61 m / 0.19%). For cross-city transfer (Boston↔Singapore), RoCA with SparseDrive-S achieves 0.49 m L2 and 0.09% collision after GT adaptation, outperforming direct finetuning (0.55 m / 0.12%) and even matching or exceeding unsupervised baselines. On NAVSIM closed-loop, RoCA-Orion improves PDMS from 84.4 to 87.9 and EP from 78.7 to 82.6 over Orion; on DriveArena, route completion jumps from 17.9 to 33.7. In active learning, RoCA's GP-based uncertainty selection beats both random sampling and Deep Ensemble / MC Dropout baselines at 10% and 15% labeling budgets (e.g., 0.554 m vs. 0.708 m L2 for Singapore→Boston at 10%).

## Relation to Prior Work

RoCA positions itself as a lightweight, plug-and-play alternative to LLM-based E2E driving systems (ORION, DiMA, Senna) that rely on large pre-trained language models for world knowledge. It repurposes Gaussian Processes—previously applied to image restoration and domain adaptation in other vision tasks—into the E2E driving pipeline, introducing a learned codebook of basis tokens that bijectively map to trajectory prototypes. Unlike deterministic anchor-based planners (VAD, SparseDrive, SSR) that perform hard classification followed by residual regression, RoCA models the joint distribution over token embeddings and trajectories, enabling kernel-based similarity lookup and uncertainty-aware inference. The paper explicitly notes that GP integration into E2E autonomous driving is 'underexplored,' carving out a new methodological niche between modular GPs and monolithic LLM-driven planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoCA addresses a critical practical gap: E2E driving models trained in one city or environment degrade significantly when deployed elsewhere, and LLM-based solutions are prohibitively expensive to retrain. By providing a GP module that requires no extra inference cost in its regularization mode and enables unsupervised adaptation (outperforming supervised direct finetuning in several settings), RoCA offers a data-efficient path to cross-domain deployment. The uncertainty estimates also enable principled active learning, reducing annotation costs for target-domain adaptation. Open questions include scalability to more complex multi-agent interactions, the paper's own noted plan to extend to NAVSIM v2, and whether the GP formulation generalizes to sensor suites beyond multi-view cameras (e.g., LiDAR, radar).
