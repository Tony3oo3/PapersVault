## Problem

Automatic AMS circuit sizing is bottlenecked by high-dimensional, nonlinear design spaces with strict performance constraints. Existing EDA methods treat sizing as static black-box optimization, while recent LLM-based agents rely on fixed optimization algorithms and single-stage workflows without iterative self-reflection or feedback-driven search-space refinement, limiting their ability to adapt to complex circuits with sparse feasibility regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265623/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a SKY130 CMOS SPICE netlist with parameterized transistor widths (drawn from a discrete set of 9 values per variable), user specifications expressed as a composite figure-of-merit formula with per-metric constraints, and a testbench template for Ngspice simulation. It produces a set of discrete transistor width assignments that maximize the FoM while satisfying all specification constraints, along with a feasibility assessment. The optimization operates under a fixed 300-sample simulation budget, with the LLM making all meta-level decisions (variable selection, range assignment, algorithm choice) as structured JSON outputs.

## Method

AutoSizer is a reflective two-loop multi-agent LLM framework that separates numerical parameter search from adaptive optimization-problem formulation. The inner loop invokes a configurable optimization engine (LHS, GA, BO, simulated annealing) selected and configured by an LLM orchestration agent based on convergence history and search-space characteristics, evaluating candidates via Ngspice simulation. The outer loop performs reflective search-space refinement: an LLM circuit-understanding agent extracts topology-level sensitivities and trade-offs, a search-space decision agent prioritizes variables and assigns sparse ranges (achieving ~82× reduction in the telescopic OTA example), and a feedback agent analyzes boundary clustering, stagnation, and variable impact to decide whether to expand ranges, unfix variables, or change focus. This progressive, data-driven space adaptation—where dimensions can shrink or expand non-monotonically across outer-loop cycles—enables recovery from suboptimal initial decisions without restarting from scratch.

## Results

On AMS-SIZINGBENCH (24 SKY130 circuits), AutoSizer achieves a 100% success rate across all three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard), outperforming all baselines. On the Hard split, AutoSizer reaches a mean FOM of 4.5±2.7 with only 63.6 evaluations to best solution, compared to LEDRO (FOM 3.8, 140.8 evals, 80% SR) and EE-Sizer (FOM 3.7, 170.8 evals, 56.4% SR). On Easy circuits, AutoSizer requires the fewest evaluations (38.9) while achieving the highest FOM (4.4). The advantage is most pronounced on Hard circuits, where traditional methods (GA, BO, TuRBO) drop to 25–50% success rate while AutoSizer maintains 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoSizer outperforms all five baselines on every difficulty level in both FOM and success rate. Against the strongest LLM-based baseline LEDRO, AutoSizer gains +0.7 FOM on Hard (4.5 vs 3.8), reduces evaluations by 55% (63.6 vs 140.8), and improves success rate from 80% to 100%. Against EE-Sizer, the gap on Hard is +0.8 FOM and +43.6 percentage points in success rate (100% vs 56.4%), with EE-Sizer also requiring 2.7× more evaluations. ADO-LLM, which lacks a self-reflection mechanism, underperforms AutoSizer by +1.0 FOM on Hard and achieves only 41% success rate. Traditional methods (GA, BO, TuRBO) degrade sharply on Hard circuits (25–50% SR) due to their inability to adapt the search space, while AutoSizer maintains 100% SR by iteratively refining variable ranges and algorithm selection. On Easy circuits, the performance gap narrows (AutoSizer 4.4 vs LEDRO 4.1 FOM), confirming that conventional optimization remains competitive on low-dimensional, well-conditioned problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior LLM-based circuit sizing agents (AmpAgent, LEDRO, EE-Sizer, ADO-LLM) that employ fixed optimization algorithms and single-pass workflows, by formulating sizing as a reflective meta-optimization problem where the LLM orchestrates multiple optimization algorithms and iteratively refines the search space based on simulation feedback. Conceptually, it extends the design-space-reduction idea from LEDRO into a closed-loop, multi-cycle process and borrows the self-reflection pattern from general LLM agent frameworks (ReAct, multi-agent systems) but applies it to the specific problem of numerical optimization orchestration. The inner loop subsumes traditional black-box optimizers (LHS, GA, BO, SA) as interchangeable tools, while the outer loop introduces a level of adaptive problem reformulation absent in both classical EDA methods and prior LLM agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of AMS-SIZINGBENCH (24 SKY130 circuits with standardized netlists, specs, and simulation workflows) fills a gap in reproducible evaluation for LLM-driven sizing, enabling fair comparison across methods. The ablation finding that misguided search-space reduction (removing circuit understanding while keeping the reduction step) is worse than no reduction at all is a practical caution for future LLM-agent designs in constrained optimization. Open questions include scalability beyond SKY130 to advanced nodes, PVT-robustness of the sizing solutions, whether the two-loop architecture generalizes to topology synthesis or layout, and whether the 300-sample budget is sufficient for industrial-grade designs with hundreds of variables.
