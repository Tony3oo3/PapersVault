## Problem

In the node-arrival data stream model—where the stream contains raw data objects (nodes) rather than precomputed edges—no prior algorithm could approximate the correlation clustering cost in sublinear space. While edge-arrival streaming algorithms exist, they do not apply when edges must be derived on-demand via a similarity function, which is the natural setting for real-world data such as images or tweets. The paper asks whether a good approximation of the optimal clustering cost can be obtained using o(n) space and O(1) passes in this node-arrival model.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of n nodes (e.g., image embeddings, tweets) over O(1) sequential passes, with access to a pairwise similarity function that can be evaluated only when both endpoints are simultaneously in memory. It outputs a scalar estimate of the correlation clustering cost (number of mismatch pairs) that is an (O(1), n^κ)-approximation of the optimal cost with probability 0.99, using O(n^(1−κ/4) log n) words of space for any constant κ ∈ (0,1]. The similarity function is never stored explicitly; edge labels are computed on-the-fly during the passes.

## Method

C⁴Approx maintains a reference set R of the highest-ranked nodes under a random permutation, which acts as a partial oracle for determining pivot assignments (and hence mismatch status) for a large subset of nodes. Nodes are partitioned into A (pivots resolvable via R) and B (remaining low-degree nodes, guaranteed by a Chernoff bound). The mismatch edge set is split into E_A^mis (at least one endpoint in A) and E_B^mis (both endpoints in B), estimated separately: E_A^mis is approximated via a high-low degree decomposition with reservoir sampling and rescaling, while E_B^mis is approximated by sampling clusters in B and counting intra- and inter-cluster mismatch edges. The entire pipeline runs in O(1) passes (k+4 for parameter k) and leverages Bernstein's inequality for concentration, building on the PrunedPivot algorithm whose clustering cost is an O(1)-approximation of the optimum.

## Results

On three real-world datasets (Wikipedia, LiveJournal, ImageNet-21K), C4Approx achieves a relative error mean that closely matches PrunedPivot while using only sublinear space. At just 2% of the nodes stored, C4Approx achieves roughly 5% standard deviation in relative error, compared to ~150% of the mean for ASW23-Pivot on Wikipedia and ~100% on ImageNet. Increasing space to 10% reduces C4Approx's standard deviation below 2%, and setting the pivot-search depth k=5 is sufficient to achieve under 2% relative error in expectation. The algorithm uses O(1) passes and O(n^{(3+α)/4} log n) words of space, achieving an (O(1), n^{1−α})-approximation of the optimal correlation clustering cost with probability 0.99.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Pivot** (Ailon et al., 2008), C4Approx is evaluated via relative error on the same random permutation π, isolating the error introduced by sampling and pruning; the empirical mean of C4Approx matches Pivot's with little to no bias. Against **PrunedPivot** (Dalirrooyfard et al., 2024), C4Approx's mean tracks PrunedPivot's closely across all space budgets and k values, while its standard deviation converges rapidly as space increases, confirming the theoretical variance reduction. Against **ASW23-Pivot** (Assadi et al., 2023), C4Approx dramatically outperforms in stability: at 2% space on Wikipedia, ASW23-Pivot's standard deviation is about 150% of its mean, whereas C4Approx achieves roughly 5%; on ImageNet the gap is similarly large (~100% vs. ~5%). Against **ASW23-SDD** (Assadi et al., 2023), C4Approx wins on both accuracy and stability, particularly on sparse graphs like Wikipedia where ASW23-SDD's additive error term dominates its output, producing a noticeably higher empirical mean. Overall, C4Approx outperforms all competitors in terms of accuracy and stability while using significantly less memory (as low as 2% of nodes).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pivot/PrunedPivot line of correlation clustering algorithms from the edge-arrival stream model (Ahn et al. 2015; Assadi et al. 2023; Behnezhad et al. 2023) to a new node-arrival stream model where only raw objects arrive and pairwise similarities are computed on-demand. The algorithm C4Approx builds directly on PrunedPivot's recursive pivot-search structure (Dalirrooyfard et al. 2024), adapting it via a restricted reference set R and a high-low degree decomposition to achieve sublinear space. The key conceptual departure from prior streaming work is that edges cannot be enumerated in o(n) space under constant passes, forcing a fundamentally different estimation strategy than the sparse-dense decompositions used in edge-arrival settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The node-arrival model is arguably more natural for real-world streaming applications (images, tweets, documents arrive as objects, not as precomputed pairwise similarities), and this paper provides the first sublinear-space algorithm with matching lower bounds for estimating correlation clustering cost in this setting. The lower bounds cleanly separate two necessary ingredients—multiple passes and additive error—giving a tight characterization of what is achievable. Open questions include whether the high-low degree decomposition and reference-set techniques extend to other graph problems in node-arrival streams, whether the additive error n^κ can be tightened, and whether the approach handles dynamic node insertions/deletions.
