## Problem

Existing diffusion-based molecular generative models produce macrocycles (rings of 12+ heavy atoms) at rates below 1%, because macrocycles are scarce in public datasets and the large ring is a global topological property that local chemical-validity objectives do not enforce. No prior method explicitly generates arbitrary, non-peptidic macrocycles de novo without relying on handcrafted scaffolds or linear precursors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263195/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a random 3D atomic point cloud (unconditional) or a protein pocket with a small set of pharmacophore atoms (conditional) as input, and produces a 3D macrocyclic molecular structure with atomic positions, types, and bonds. Notably, the topological guidance operates exclusively on atomic coordinates—bonds are not used by the guidance itself—and ring size is controlled indirectly through a geometric death-time parameter (in Ångströms) rather than by specifying an atom count, with a theoretical linear mapping between the two (Theorem 3.1).

## Method

MACROGUIDE is a training-free, inference-time guidance mechanism that modifies the score function of a pretrained diffusion model at each denoising step. At every step it constructs a Vietoris-Rips complex from the current atomic coordinates, computes persistent homology, and subtracts the gradient of a composite topological objective F_TDA = F_death^{H1} + F_birth^{H1} + F_death^{H0} from the denoiser's score. The H1 death term penalizes the largest cycle's death time outside a target interval (controlling ring size), the H1 birth term constrains the longest edge in the cycle to a maximum bond length (ensuring connectivity), and the H0 death term keeps all atoms in a single connected component. A gradient-masking scheme on the H0 term prevents an 'unzipping' instability where a distant outlier atom would pull internal atoms out of their local equilibrium. The guidance is applied as a predictor-corrector step after each base denoising update, can be sparsified to every k steps for a 5× speedup with <15% quality loss, and requires no retraining of the base model.

## Results

In unconditional generation (1000 molecules, 30 heavy atoms), MACROGUIDE applied to MolDiff achieves a 99.7% macrocycle rate among successful molecules, compared to 5.3% for unguided MolDiff and 85.1% for a finetuned variant — a nearly 20-fold improvement. In the protein-conditioned setting with MolSnapper, the macrocycle rate jumps from 0.3% to 99.5% (a 300-fold increase), while also improving all PoseBusters tests from 0.440 to 0.575 and internal energy from 0.818 to 0.921. Generated macrocycles also show higher diversity (0.771 vs. 0.707 unguided) and better chemical validity metrics across bond lengths, bond angles, and steric clash checks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACROGUIDE decisively outperforms all baselines on the primary macrocycle generation metric. Against unguided MolDiff (5.3% macrocycle rate), it achieves 99.7% — a ~20× improvement — while maintaining comparable validity (0.989 vs. 0.991) and substantially better connectivity (0.999 vs. 0.958). The finetuned MolDiff baseline reaches 85.1% macrocycle rate but still trails MACROGUIDE by ~15 percentage points, and in the protein-conditioned setting the finetuned model collapses to just 18% versus MACROGUIDE's 99.5%. Naive geometric guidance (forcing a subset of atoms into a ring) plateaus at ~37% regardless of subset size, and torus-shaped noise initialization performs no better than the unguided baseline (5.4%). In the conditional setting, MACROGUIDE also surpasses MolSnapper on quality metrics: all PoseBusters tests improve from 0.440 to 0.575, internal energy from 0.818 to 0.921, and protein PoseBusters from 0.806 to 0.911, indicating that topological guidance simultaneously improves both macrocycle yield and 3D structural quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACROGUIDE sits at the intersection of diffusion-based molecular generation (MolDiff, MolSnapper) and topological data analysis, repurposing persistent homology as an inference-time score guidance mechanism rather than a training-time loss or architectural component. It departs from prior macrocycle generation work—Macformer (linear-to-macrocyclic conversion) and Macro-Hop (scaffold hopping via RL)—by requiring no reference scaffold, linear precursor, or handcrafted constraints, and from cyclic peptide methods by operating on arbitrary atom types and bond patterns. Conceptually, it extends the score-guidance framework of Guo et al. (2024) and the broader line of topological guidance in diffusion (Hu et al. 2024; Wang & Berger 2025; Gupta et al. 2025) to the molecular domain, where the topological objective is the Vietoris-Rips persistence diagram of atomic coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to generate arbitrary (non-peptidic) macrocycles de novo, filling a gap that has limited deep generative modeling's applicability to a class of molecules with 17+ FDA-approved drugs in the last five years. The training-free, plug-in nature means it can be adopted by any 3D point-cloud diffusion model without retraining, and the H0 death guidance as a general large-molecule connectivity fix (80% vs 20% at n=110) has utility beyond macrocycles. Open questions include synthetic accessibility of generated macrocycles, whether bond-level constraints improve chemical realism, and whether the approach extends to other global topological properties (e.g., molecular cages via H2). The paper acknowledges that quality improvements are indirect—MACROGUIDE does not explicitly optimize 3D structure or molecular properties—and that the non-conservative masked gradient field is a practical approximation rather than an exact score.
