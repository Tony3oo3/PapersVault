## Problem

Existing symmetry-based optimal transport accelerations are restricted to cyclic groups and require points to be presented in a specific ordering that exposes the block structure. This paper addresses the broader and practically more important setting where source and target point sets share an arbitrary finite symmetry group (including non-abelian groups such as tetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral rotation groups), and where no prior knowledge of the group or point ordering is available.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a cost matrix C ∈ R^{|S|×|T|} of pairwise distances, a source probability vector p, and a target probability vector q, with no requirement on point ordering or explicit group specification. It produces the full optimal transport plan P ∈ R^{|S|×|T|} and the corresponding objective value. The key constraint is that points within the same orbit must share identical measures (Assumption 3.1), and distinct orbits must yield distinct distance patterns to the other set (Assumption 3.3), which holds generically in R^d.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages: (i) orbit identification by clustering rows and columns of the cost matrix by their distance-pattern multisets, which automatically recovers the orbit decomposition and group order without any prior group knowledge; (ii) reduction to an orbit-reduced problem (OR-SG-ROT) of size m×n (where m and n are the numbers of source and target orbits) by exploiting the group-invariance of the optimal plan (Lemma 3.2) and the algebraic structure of invariant matrices under reducible ordering (Proposition 3.4); (iii) lifting the reduced solution back to the full m|G|×n|G| scale via row-permutation lookups determined by the cost matrix structure. For the linear case, the reduced problem becomes a standard m×n LOT solved by network simplex; for the entropic case, it becomes a Sinkhorn-type iteration on an aggregated kernel L_{ij} = Σ_b exp(−C^{1,b}_{ij}/λ). The overall speedup is roughly |G|² in the dominant iterative term, and the method is invariant to arbitrary input permutations.

## Results

On synthetic data with the octahedral group O (4,800 points, block ordering), SG-LOT runs in 0.111 s versus 3.649 s for direct LOT and 0.329 s for C-LOT (Takeda et al., 2024), a ~33× speedup over the baseline. SG-EROT runs in 0.146 s versus 2.544 s for direct EROT and 0.458 s for C-EROT, a ~17× speedup. All methods produce identical objective values and transport plans (solution distance 0.0000). Under random ordering, SG-ROT still achieves ~2.2× speedup over direct ROT, with the gap widening as group order increases (up to ~4× for the icosahedral group I).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cyclic-symmetry solver C-ROT (Takeda et al., 2024), SG-ROT consistently wins on runtime while matching objective values exactly. For the octahedral group O with 4,800 points under block ordering, SG-LOT is 3.0× faster than C-LOT (0.111 s vs 0.329 s) and SG-EROT is 3.1× faster (0.146 s vs 0.458 s); the gap widens for the icosahedral group I where SG-EROT is 2.9× faster (0.128 s vs 0.377 s). Against the standard Sinkhorn-based EROT baseline (Cuturi, 2013), SG-EROT achieves a ~17× speedup at 4,800 points (0.146 s vs 2.544 s) and ~17× at 9,600 points (0.679 s vs 11.659 s), with identical objective values to machine precision. Under random ordering where C-ROT is inapplicable, SG-ROT still outperforms direct ROT by 2.2–4.4× depending on group size. On real NYU symmetric images with approximate D₂ symmetry, SG-EROT reduces runtime from 12.9 s to 0.86 s (block) and from 14.0 s to 2.18 s (random) while maintaining near-identical objective values (deviation <0.013). On molecular graph data, SG-EROT halves runtime (0.29 ms vs 0.59 ms) with zero solution distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry-aware optimal transport framework of Takeda et al. (2024), which was restricted to cyclic groups and required a specific cyclic ordering of points to expose a circulant block structure in the cost matrix. The key conceptual departure is handling arbitrary finite groups—including non-abelian groups such as the tetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral rotation groups—and recovering the orbit decomposition directly from the cost matrix without any prior knowledge of the group or a predefined point ordering. The orbit-reduced formulation and the group-averaging argument for existence of invariant optimal plans draw on standard representation-theoretic tools, while the numerical solvers (network simplex for LOT, Sinkhorn-type iterations for EROT) reuse algorithms from the prior cyclic-symmetry work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled ~|G|² speedup in the dominant iterative term for entropy-regularized OT, which becomes substantial for large symmetry groups (e.g., the icosahedral group of order 60). Open questions include characterizing the stability of the orbit-reduced solution under approximate symmetry violations, extending the framework to Gromov-Wasserstein transport, and integrating with other structural accelerations (low-rank, sparse, hierarchical). The reliance on Assumption 3.3 (distinct distance patterns for distinct orbits) is a theoretical boundary that, while holding generically, can fail for highly structured configurations, limiting universal applicability.
