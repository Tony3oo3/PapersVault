## Problem

Existing MLLM-based V2V cooperative driving frameworks (e.g., V2V-LLM, V2V-GoT) rely on dense token-level sharing among all agents, incurring prohibitive communication bandwidth and LLM inference costs that limit real-time deployment. Conventional token pruning methods are designed for single-agent 2D vision settings and ignore LiDAR-specific spatial structure (sparsity, geometric consistency) and the multi-agent fusion context, making them unsuitable for cooperative V2V reasoning under strict bandwidth constraints.

## Input / Output

Each agent's LiDAR point cloud is processed by a PointPillars 3D detector to produce scene-level BEV feature maps and object-level detection vectors, which are projected into the LLM embedding space via an MLP as scene tokens and object tokens. A natural language question specifying the cooperative task (grounding, object identification, or planning) is encoded as text tokens. The system outputs a text answer (e.g., object center coordinates for grounding) or six future driving waypoints with associated collision metrics for planning tasks, all generated by the LLM after fusing pruned multi-agent visual tokens with the question.

## Method

V2V-CCM introduces a dual-stage communication paradigm: agents first broadcast two compact request messages—Question Semantic Message (QSM), a single token obtained by mean-pooling the question embeddings to encode global task intent, and Spatial Coverage Message (SCM), a single token compressing the agent's BEV scene tokens to mark already-observed spatial regions—then selectively transmit only pruned visual tokens. Communication-Conditioned Token Pruning (CCTP) operates in three sub-stages: (1) Object-Scene Grouping assigns each object token as a center and clusters nearby scene tokens by LiDAR spatial coordinates into region groups; (2) Group-Level Importance Estimation computes per-group question-relevance (cosine similarity to QSM) and spatial-redundancy (cosine similarity to SCM) scores, combined as I = I_t − γ·I_s; (3) Token Selection with a Global Budget solves a constrained optimization that maximizes the sum of group-importance-weighted token scores (S_t − λ·S_s) subject to a total retained-token budget K, efficiently solved via top-K ranking. The key insight is that the natural language question itself serves as a powerful, highly compressed signal for identifying which visual tokens are redundant, enabling aggressive pruning while preserving question-relevant and spatially complementary information across agents.

## Results

On V2V-QA (V2V-split), V2V-CCM at 70% pruning retains QGr F1 of 38.7 (vs. 40.7 unpruned) and achieves Q5 planning L2 error of 5.21 m with 6.28% collision rate, substantially outperforming all baselines. On V2V-GoT-QA, V2V-CCM at 70% pruning achieves Q9 L2 error of 3.01 m and 2.73% CR, compared to 5.48 m / 6.09% for VisionZip and 5.83 m / 6.42% for DivPruner. At 30% pruning, V2V-CCM nearly matches the unpruned baseline on both datasets (e.g., Q9 L2 = 2.65 m vs. 2.62 m unpruned), while 70% pruning yields a 2.07×–2.10× TTFT speedup and ~70% communication bandwidth reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2V-CCM consistently outperforms all four baselines (Random, ToMe, VisionZip, DivPruner) across every pruning ratio and both datasets. The largest margins appear on planning tasks: at 70% pruning on V2V-QA, V2V-CCM achieves Q5 L2 error of 5.21 m vs. 9.94 m (Random), 7.58 m (ToMe), 6.97 m (VisionZip), and 7.35 m (DivPruner), with collision rates of 6.28% vs. 10.68%, 7.80%, 8.15%, and 7.68% respectively. On V2V-GoT-QA at 70% pruning, the advantage is even more pronounced—V2V-CCM achieves Q9 L2 of 3.01 m and 2.73% CR, while the best baseline (VisionZip) reaches only 5.48 m / 6.09%. On grounding and object-identification tasks (Q1–Q4), V2V-CCM leads by smaller but consistent margins (e.g., QGr F1 of 38.7 vs. 37.5 for VisionZip at 70% pruning). VisionZip is the strongest baseline overall, particularly on grounding tasks at lower pruning ratios, but V2V-CCM's communication-conditioned design gives it a clear edge on planning where spatial redundancy removal is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the V2V-LLM and V2V-GoT frameworks, which introduced dense token-level sharing among agents for MLLM-based cooperative driving. It departs from these frameworks by replacing dense token transmission with a dual-stage communication paradigm inspired by feature-level V2V perception methods like Where2Comm: agents first exchange compact request messages (QSM for question intent, SCM for spatial coverage) and then selectively transmit only pruned tokens. The token pruning strategy itself extends single-agent methods (ToMe, VisionZip, DivPruner) to the multi-agent, LiDAR-specific setting by introducing object-scene grouping and joint question-relevance/spatial-redundancy scoring, rather than relying on attention-based or similarity-based criteria designed for 2D vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-world V2V deployment: 70% token pruning yields ~70% bandwidth reduction and 2× TTFT speedup on both server-grade (H200) and edge (Jetson AGX Orin) hardware while preserving near-baseline task performance. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that LLM inference cost remains high and that inference-stage token optimization is left for future work. Open questions include whether the dual-stage protocol scales gracefully beyond two agents, whether the mean-pooling-based message compression is sufficient for complex multi-intent questions, and whether the approach generalizes to camera-based or mixed-sensor V2V settings.
