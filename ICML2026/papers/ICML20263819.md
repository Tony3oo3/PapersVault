## Problem

Multi-objective discrete optimization (e.g., molecular design) suffers from premature convergence in evolutionary algorithms, and single frozen LLMs cannot internalize search experience through parameter updates. Closed-source LLMs offer strong reasoning but are untrainable, while small open models are trainable yet lack broad knowledge—relying on either alone creates an inherent bottleneck. The paper asks how to enable continual, experience-driven learning in a hybrid LLM system that preserves global exploration while progressively adapting to the search space.

## Input / Output

The system takes a multi-objective problem definition (five drug-likeness objectives: QED, SA, DRD2, GSK3β, JNK3), an initial population of 100 SMILES molecules sampled from ZINC, and two LLMs (a frozen GPT-4o API model and a locally trainable Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). At each generation, parent pairs are fed into a task-specific crossover prompt, and the active LLM outputs exactly two new SMILES candidates per call. The final output is a Pareto-optimal population scored by TDC predictors, plus updated local-model weights; DPO training triplets (prompt, preferred molecule, rejected molecule) are synthesized from the trajectory memory using fingerprint-based similarity filtering.

## Method

MCCE alternates a frozen API LLM (global exploration) and a trainable local LLM (targeted exploitation) as evolutionary operators over an iterative generate–evaluate–learn loop. The key technical contribution is a similarity-based data synthesis pipeline for DPO: it computes global Tanimoto-similarity statistics (μ, σ) over both models' outputs, filters candidates to the μ±σ band, stratifies them into top/bottom score pools (α=0.3 quantile), and assembles preference triplets using progressively relaxed nested similarity intervals (I₁→I₂→I₃) with score-pool fallbacks, ensuring chosen/rejected pairs are structurally comparable and preventing contradictory prompt-response confusion. The local model is refined via DPO with the initial untrained model fixed as reference, using a 50/32 API-to-local call split and updates every 200 candidates. Population maintenance uses a hybrid elite-diversity strategy: the top 50% by aggregate fitness are kept as elites, and the remaining 50% are filled from non-dominated Pareto layers with strict duplicate removal, jointly maximizing hypervolume and uniqueness.

## Results

On the five-objective molecular optimization benchmark (QED, SA, DRD2, GSK3β, JNK3), MCCE achieves the best Top-1 Fitness (4.35±0.17) and Top-10 Fitness (4.28±0.15), outperforming all baselines including GFlowNet (4.24/4.20) and DyMol (4.23/4.16). The Hypervolume (HV) of 0.847±0.138 is competitive but slightly below GFlowNet (0.871) and DyMol (0.868). On combinatorial optimization, MCCE achieves SOTA HV on MOCVRP (1.048843 vs. 1.034541 for ReEvo) and surpasses long-standing community records on Circle Packing for n=26 and n=31.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCCE achieves the highest Top-1 Fitness (4.35) and Top-10 Fitness (4.28) among all molecular design baselines, beating GFlowNet (4.24/4.20), DyMol (4.23/4.16), REINVENT (4.23/4.14), MoLLEO (4.19/4.08), and GB-GA (4.02/3.98). However, MCCE trails GFlowNet (0.871), DyMol (0.868), and MoLLEO (0.860) on Hypervolume, suggesting its Pareto front coverage is slightly narrower despite superior peak solution quality. On the combinatorial optimization front, MCCE achieves SOTA HV on MOCVRP (1.048843), surpassing ReEvo (1.034541), AlphaEvolve (1.031803), and FunSearch (1.032126), while remaining competitive on MOTSP (1.025206 vs. AlphaEvolve's 1.029279). For Circle Packing, MCCE discovers configurations that exceed the previous best-known community records for both n=26 (2.635983 vs. 2.634+) and n=31 (2.889970 vs. 2.889+). The DPO-based co-evolution variant also outperforms SFT (4.24 Top1 F) and RL (4.19 Top1 F) co-evolution ablations, confirming that preference learning is the most effective training paradigm for this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCCE extends the LLM-as-evolutionary-operator paradigm established by ExLLM and MoLLEO, which used a single frozen LLM to generate and refine molecular candidates. The key departure is the introduction of a trainable local model (Qwen2.5-7B) that co-evolves with a frozen API model (GPT-4o) through similarity-filtered DPO training, moving beyond the static-generator assumption that limits prior work (FunSearch, ReEvo, AlphaEvolve, MoLLEO). Unlike multi-model collaboration approaches (CollabLLM, Collab-RAG) that restrict parameter updates to one side, MCCE establishes a mutual-inspiration loop where the large model provides global exploration and the small model internalizes experience, with both improving beyond their standalone performance. The similarity-based data synthesis pipeline for constructing stable DPO triplets is a methodological contribution not present in prior preference-learning or evolutionary-LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that parameter-level adaptation (specifically DPO with similarity filtering) is the right training paradigm for experience-driven LLM optimization, outperforming both SFT (catastrophic forgetting) and RL (training instability) in a controlled comparison. The co-evolutionary design offers a practical path toward continual learning in hybrid LLM systems without requiring access to the large model's parameters, with a remarkably low API cost (~$3.81 per 5,000-candidate run). Limitations include primary validation on molecular optimization (with combinatorial optimization as secondary evidence), and the framework's applicability to continuous or mixed-variable optimization remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the co-evolutionary gains persist over longer horizons, how the approach scales to more than five objectives, and whether domain-specific similarity metrics can further improve the DPO training signal.
