## Problem

Standard MoE routing relies on a shallow linear projection that has limited capacity to capture complex input structure, leading to unstable and imbalanced expert specialization that degrades as the expert pool grows. Prior work addresses load imbalance through auxiliary regularizers or alternative routing paradigms, but leaves unexamined whether the router is actually aware of input structure at all — a complementary axis that determines whether routing decisions reflect meaningful input variation.

## Input / Output

STAR operates on d-dimensional hidden representations (e.g., Transformer encoder outputs) and produces K routing scores from which top-k experts are selected. The method maintains an evolving orthonormal basis V ∈ R^{K×d} that is updated online via m GHA iterations per forward pass, alongside a learnable mixing matrix R ∈ R^{K×K} and interpolation coefficients α ∈ R^K. At test time, GHA updates can optionally be re-enabled to adapt the basis to the test distribution without any supervised signal.

## Method

STAR rethinks MoE routing as a subspace learning problem by augmenting the standard linear gate with an evolving principal subspace estimated online via the Generalized Hebbian Algorithm (GHA). At each forward pass, m GHA iterations incrementally refine an orthonormal basis V whose rows approximate the top-K eigenvectors of the input covariance, without explicitly forming it. A learnable mixing matrix R maps the principal directions into expert-specific routing vectors Z = RV, which the paper proves (via Propositions 4.2–4.3) is essential to decouple expert selection from the PCA variance hierarchy that would otherwise cause routing energy to concentrate on leading components. The final routing logits interpolate between the task-supervised linear gate and the structure-aware subspace gate via per-expert learnable coefficients α, and the full framework remains compatible with standard load-balancing losses. Theoretical analysis characterizes per-expert routing energy as a weighted sum of eigenvalues, showing that no-mixing (R=I) inherits the variance ordering while random orthonormal mixing equalizes energy in expectation.

## Results

On large-scale LLaMA-MoE pretraining (30B tokens on The Pile), STAR achieves the highest zero-shot average accuracy at both scales: 41.31% at 182M active params and 43.93% at 469M active params, outperforming Standard MoE (40.65% / 42.69%), Expert-Choice (40.13% / 42.81%), and ReMoE (39.94% / 43.29%). On GLUE with BERT-large MoE, STAR(8,4) reaches 82.24% average, surpassing Cosine Router(8,4) at 81.77% and DynMoE at 81.64%. On ImageNet-C at severity 5, STAR with test-time adaptation achieves 24.19% top-1 accuracy versus 22.63% for Standard MoE, and on GLUE-X OOD evaluation STAR improves 0.65%p on average over the cosine-router MoE baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard MoE (Fedus et al., 2022), STAR wins on average at both pretraining scales (+0.66% at 182M, +1.24% at 469M) and on GLUE (82.24% vs. no direct Standard MoE row, but STAR outperforms the cosine router which is a stronger variant). Against Expert-Choice routing (Zhou et al., 2022), STAR achieves higher average accuracy at both scales (41.31% vs. 40.13% at 182M; 43.93% vs. 42.81% at 469M), though EC wins on individual tasks like ARC-e and BoolQ at 469M. Against ReMoE (Wang et al., 2025), STAR leads by +1.37% at 182M and +0.64% at 469M on average, with the gap driven primarily by LAMBADA and BoolQ. On GLUE, STAR(8,4) outperforms both Cosine Router(8,4) (Li et al., 2023) by +0.47% and DynMoE (Guo et al., 2025) by +0.60%, with the advantage persisting at E=16 (82.11% vs. 81.69% for Cosine Router). On ImageNet-C OOD robustness, STAR with test-time adaptation beats Standard MoE by +1.56% at severity 5 (24.19% vs. 22.63%), and on GLUE-X OOD, STAR improves 0.65%p on average over the cosine-router MoE baseline across 5 of 7 OOD datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds on two distinct lineages: the Generalized Hebbian Algorithm (Sanger, 1989) for online principal component estimation, and the standard sparsely-gated MoE routing paradigm (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) that uses a shallow linear projection followed by softmax. The key conceptual departure is from the dominant framing of MoE routing as a load-balancing problem (auxiliary losses, expert-choice routing, ReMoE), which STAR treats as necessary but insufficient. Instead, STAR reframes routing as a structure-aware subspace learning problem, arguing that the router must first be aware of dominant input variation before balancing can produce meaningful specialization. The learnable mixing matrix R and interpolation coefficient α are novel architectural additions that decouple expert selection from the PCA variance hierarchy while preserving compatibility with existing balancing losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of MoE routing as online subspace learning offers a principled, data-driven alternative to the ad-hoc linear gate that has persisted across all major MoE architectures (Switch, Mixtral, DeepSeek-MoE, Qwen). The test-time adaptation capability—where GHA updates continue at inference to track distribution shift—is a lightweight mechanism for OOD robustness that requires no additional parameters or labels. However, the subspace is inherently linear (PCA-based), raising open questions about whether non-linear or adaptive-rank structure capture would yield further gains, and whether the approach scales to the 100B+ parameter MoE models where expert pools are much larger. The modest but consistent improvements (0.5–1.3% across settings) suggest the current linear gate is not the primary bottleneck at scale, and the practical impact may be more significant as expert pools grow.
