## Problem

Recovering scene-consistent 4D crowd motion from monocular video in large-scale scenes is ill-posed due to severe depth ambiguity and complex non-planar terrain. Existing methods rely on single-plane ground assumptions that break down on complex terrain, and human and scene reconstructions are typically decoupled, making accurate metric-scale human-scene alignment unreliable. Additionally, per-person temporal smoothness alone is insufficient in dense crowds where frequent occlusions fragment observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video as input and produces a unified world-coordinate reconstruction comprising per-person SMPL motion sequences (pose, shape, orientation, translation over time), a gravity-aligned global camera trajectory, and static scene geometry. The pipeline is entirely test-time optimization (no training), bootstrapped by off-the-shelf components: YOLOX for detection, SAM 2 for instance masks, DWPose for joint confidence, BoostTrack++ for multi-object tracking, π³ for scene/camera reconstruction, and TRAM for per-person motion initialization. A 100-person, 200-frame scene takes approximately 4 hours on a single NVIDIA 4090D GPU.

## Method

Crowd4D introduces the Human-Scene Interaction Proxy (HSIP), an intermediate representation built from Scene Interaction Point Clouds (SIPC) and a Scene Interaction Surface (SIS) that distill dense multi-frame point clouds into a compact, terrain-aware interaction geometry. HSIP constrains each individual's 3D location to a scene-grounded feasible region via horizontal feasible-region alignment, vertical terrain adaptation, and projection consistency losses, while jointly optimizing a residual global scene scale to resolve monocular scale ambiguity. To improve temporal stability under occlusions, Crowd Structural Coherence Regularization (CSCR) enforces soft temporal consistency of pairwise relative displacements and directions within HSIP-defined local crowd neighborhoods using a Charbonnier penalty. The full system is solved via a three-stage optimization strategy: Stage-1 refines scale and root positions using HSIP constraints, Stage-2 refines articulated poses under VPoser priors and reprojection losses, and Stage-3 recovers temporally coherent motion dynamics using learned motion priors, contact-aware regularization, and group-guided motion consistency.

## Results

On the VirtualCrowd benchmark under ground-truth object tracking, Crowd4D achieves PPDS of 91.43, PA-PPDS of 92.38, PCOD of 95.57, MPJPE of 59.35 mm, and ACCEL of 12.93, outperforming the primary baseline DyCrowd by +6.8 PPDS and −9.5 mm MPJPE. Under the more challenging unified tracking-by-detection protocol, the method still leads with PPDS 89.04 and MPJPE 61.83 mm. Against the adapted small-scene method VideoMimic, Crowd4D improves PPDS by +11.4 (91.43 vs. 80.03) and reduces ACCEL by nearly half (12.93 vs. 23.80), demonstrating that scene-aware anchoring is critical for large-scale crowd consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary large-scene video baseline DyCrowd, Crowd4D wins on every global-consistency metric: PPDS improves by +5.8 (unified) and +6.8 (GT tracking), while MPJPE drops by 7.9 mm and 9.5 mm respectively; the method also reduces WA-MPJPE and W-MPJPE by 3.2 mm and 9.2 mm under GT tracking. The single exception is ACCEL under the unified protocol, where DyCrowd edges ahead by 0.38 (15.72 vs. 16.10), though Crowd4D reverses this under GT tracking (12.93 vs. 15.53). Compared to the single-image methods Crowd3D and GroupRec, Crowd4D achieves substantially lower MPJPE (61.83 vs. 122.99 and 89.04 mm) and higher PPDS (89.04 vs. 82.61 and 74.25), confirming that video-based temporal cues are essential for large-scene accuracy. Against the adapted small-scene method VideoMimic, Crowd4D gains +11.4 PPDS and +5.23 PCOD while halving ACCEL (12.93 vs. 23.80), showing that iterative depth alignment alone cannot maintain globally coherent crowd layouts at scale. Under the GT tracking protocol, Crowd4D also outperforms the modified SLAHMR-Large* on all reported metrics, with the largest gap in MPJPE (59.35 vs. 106.35 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

Crowd4D extends the large-scene crowd reconstruction line initiated by Crowd3D and DyCrowd, which rely on HVIP-style 2D-to-3D plane anchoring under a single-plane ground assumption. The key departure is the introduction of the Human-Scene Interaction Proxy (HSIP), built on compact Scene Interaction Point Clouds and a Scene Interaction Surface (SIPC&SIS), which replaces the flat-ground proxy with a terrain-aware feasible region derived from globally consistent scene geometry (via π³). This shifts the problem from decoupled human and scene reconstruction to a tightly coupled joint optimization where a residual global scale is co-optimized with individual root positions, and a new Crowd Structural Coherence Regularization (CSCR) enforces group-level temporal consistency beyond per-person smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap—monocular 4D crowd reconstruction in large, non-planar scenes (plazas, drone footage, street surveillance) where prior flat-ground methods break down. The HSIP formulation as a compact, scene-aware intermediate representation is a conceptually clean contribution that could inform other human-scene coupling problems. However, the method remains an offline optimization requiring ~4 hours for 100 people over 200 frames, and quantitative evaluation is limited to one synthetic benchmark (VirtualCrowd) plus qualitative results on PANDA without 3D ground truth. Open questions include whether feed-forward or learned approximations can match the optimization quality, and how the approach performs on truly diverse real-world terrain with available annotations.
