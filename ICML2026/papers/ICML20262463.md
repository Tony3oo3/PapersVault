## Problem

Attention sinks—where the initial token in a decoder-only transformer disproportionately monopolizes attention scores—are a well-documented but structurally unexplained phenomenon. While prior work offers functional hypotheses (Softmax sum-to-one constraint, positional mechanisms, spectral subspaces), the causal chain explaining why position zero is consistently selected as the structural anchor, and how this anomaly propagates through the network, remains unelucidated.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard text token sequences processed through Llama-2-7B and Llama-3-8B decoder architectures, examining per-layer hidden state representations, dimension-wise variance, attention score distributions, and FFN activation patterns. The proposed head-wise RMSNorm modification takes the per-head aggregated value vectors (of dimension d_k) as input and produces variance-stabilized vectors before the output projection, with a shared learnable scaling vector λ ∈ R^{d_k} across all heads. Pre-training experiments use 152M-parameter models on OpenWebText (20B tokens, 4096 block size) evaluated on WikiText-2.

## Method

The paper identifies a multi-stage causal chain: causal masking causes the first token to skip value aggregation, creating a persistent high-variance outlier; the output projection W_O structurally preserves this discrepancy (verified via Kendall's rank correlation); super neurons in the SwiGLU FFN selectively amplify the outlier through massive activations; and channel-sparse down-projections channel these activations into a few outlier dimensions, creating extreme dimension disparity that locks subsequent QK projections into the sink. Causality is validated via two interventions—blocking aggregation at arbitrary positions via mask modification and directly amplifying token variance by a factor λ—both of which induce attention sinks at non-zero positions. The proposed architectural fix, head-wise RMSNorm, normalizes each head's aggregated value vector by its RMS before W_O, with a learnable per-dimension scaling vector, restoring statistical parity across positions and heads. Pre-training from scratch demonstrates that this intervention suppresses attention sinks, alleviates manifold collapse (higher effective rank), and accelerates convergence compared to both the Softmax baseline and Sigmoid attention alternatives.

## Results

On 152M-parameter models pre-trained on OpenWebText (20B tokens, 40K iterations), Head-wise RMSNorm reduces validation loss from 2.7812 to 2.7421 and train loss from 2.7483 to 2.7073 compared to the Llama-2 baseline. The method also increases the layer-wise mean effective rank from 343.71 to 445.96 (+29.8%) and reduces the layer-wise mean dimension disparity from 82.67 to 33.74 (−59.2%), indicating substantially less manifold collapse. Convergence is visibly faster, with HeadNorm reaching lower validation loss earlier in training than both the baseline and Sigmoid attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Llama-2 Softmax baseline [23], Head-wise RMSNorm wins on every reported metric: it lowers both train and validation loss, raises effective rank by ~30%, and cuts dimension disparity by ~59%, all consistently across four random seeds. Against Sigmoid attention [18] (which removes the sum-to-one constraint to eliminate the first-token variance outlier), HeadNorm also wins: Sigmoid attention converges more slowly and achieves worse validation loss than even the unmodified Softmax baseline, whereas HeadNorm accelerates convergence while retaining the standard Softmax formulation. The paper notes that Sigmoid attention does suppress attention sinks and dimension disparity (comparable to HeadNorm on those structural metrics), but its unnormalized scores cause training instability that HeadNorm avoids by stabilizing variance directly at the value-aggregation step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard self-attention formulation (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the Llama-2/3 decoder architecture, but departs from prior functional explanations of attention sinks (StreamingLLM's sum-to-one constraint, Bondarenko et al.'s no-op hypothesis, spectral subspace accounts) by providing a causal mechanistic chain: variance discrepancy in value aggregation → super neuron activation in FFN → dimension disparity via sparse down-projections → QK projection locking. It also connects to the normalization literature (RMSNorm, gated attention in Qiu et al. 2025, hypersphere normalization in Loshchilov et al. 2024), arguing that their empirical success may be partially attributable to unintended variance stabilization. The head-wise RMSNorm proposal is positioned as a targeted architectural fix that retains standard Softmax while neutralizing the variance outlier at its source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes attention sinks from an unexplained architectural artifact into a controllable property with a clear causal origin, which could inform the design of more stable transformer variants. The head-wise RMSNorm is a minimal, drop-in modification that simultaneously suppresses sinks, alleviates manifold collapse (higher effective rank), and accelerates pre-training convergence—making it practically attractive. However, all validation is at 152M parameters; whether the mechanism and the fix hold at 7B+ scale, or interact well with MoE architectures, remains an open and critical question. The finding that variance (not norm) is the causal factor, and that sigmoid attention actually worsens convergence, challenges the common assumption that the Softmax sum-to-one constraint is the primary culprit.
