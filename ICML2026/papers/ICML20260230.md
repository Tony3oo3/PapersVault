## Problem

A widely held explanation for neural scaling laws is that power-law structures in the data (e.g., Zipf's law in word frequencies) directly cause the power-law decrease in test loss. This paper challenges that account by demonstrating that scaling laws emerge even when the training data contains no power-law correlations whatsoever, and by systematically tracking how scaling exponents evolve as data complexity is dialed from simple random walks up to natural language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260230/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of discrete tokens generated from progressively more complex sources: unbiased or biased random walks on Erdős-Rényi and Barabási-Albert graphs, bigram language models over a 50K vocabulary, sequences sampled from 1/2/4-layer transformers trained on Fineweb-edu, and natural language text itself. Each sequence is of fixed length (50 or 100 tokens), and the model performs next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss. The output is a set of fitted scaling exponents (α_D, β_N for 1D fits; γ, a, b for compute-optimal curves) along with confidence intervals obtained via BCa bootstrap, plus compute-optimal N_opt(C) and D_opt(C) curves extracted from a 2D regression surface.

## Method

The paper trains pre-normalization decoder-only transformers (2 or 4 layers, embedding dimension 128–4096, rotary positional embeddings, tied input/output embeddings) under both standard parameterization (SP) and maximal update parameterization (μP), using 1-epoch training with linear warmup and cosine decay. Scaling exponents are extracted by fitting 1D power laws L(N)_D = E_D + A_D·N^{−α_D} and L(D)_N = E_N + B_N·D^{−β_N} with a Huber-robust objective, where the irreducible loss offset E is critical (omitting it underestimates exponents by ~2×). For compute-optimal curves, the authors replace the standard 2D Chinchilla formula with a 3-layer fully connected neural network regression (or ANOVA-style RBF kernel regression) over (log N, log D), which achieves ~2× lower validation MSE, and then extract L_opt(C), N_opt(C), D_opt(C) by minimizing over a dense grid. The key empirical finding is that α_D decreases monotonically as data complexity increases (from ~1.0 for ER random walks to ~0.35 for natural language with SP), while β_N remains relatively stable around 0.5, and that μP yields larger α_D (~0.5 vs. ~0.35) indicating greater parameter efficiency.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that neural scaling laws (power-law decrease in test loss with N and D) emerge even for random walks on Erdős-Rényi graphs with no power-law structure in the data, with α̅_D = 1.028 ± 0.129 and β̅_N = 0.749 ± 0.014 for an 8K-node/50K-edge ER graph. Systematically dialing down language complexity from natural language (α̅_D ≈ 0.35) through T2L, T1L, to language bigrams (α̅_D ≈ 0.98) reveals a monotonic increase in α̅_D with decreasing dataset entropy, while β̅_N remains relatively stable around 0.5. For natural language with 2-layer transformers, μP parameterization yields α̅_D ≈ 0.50 versus α̅_D ≈ 0.35 for standard parameterization, indicating improved parameter efficiency. Refitting Chinchilla data shows that 1D power-law fits (MSE ≈ 7.8×10⁻⁵) and neural network regression outperform the standard 2D Chinchilla formula (MSE ≈ 4.8×10⁻⁴) by nearly an order of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Chinchilla 2D parametric formula (Hoffmann et al., 2022), the paper shows that neural network regression and kernel ridge regression achieve roughly 2× lower validation MSE on the raw Chinchilla data (245 N-D pairs), while 1D power-law fits with irreducible loss achieve MSE of 7.76×10⁻⁵ versus 4.77×10⁻⁴ for the 2D formula — nearly an order of magnitude improvement. The compute-optimal exponents extracted via NN regression (γ=0.16, a=0.482, b=0.504) are in good agreement with the Chinchilla 2D fit values (γ=0.178, a=0.513, b=0.487), validating the alternative methodology. Against Kaplan et al. (2020), the paper resolves the long-standing discrepancy between Kaplan and Chinchilla scaling laws: when embedding parameters are excluded from N, the compute-optimal exponents become (a,b) = (0.742, 0.386), closely matching Kaplan's (0.73, 0.27), whereas including embeddings gives a/b ≈ 0.94, matching Chinchilla's a/b ≈ 1. This explanation is demonstrated using only 2-layer transformers with context length 100, showing the discrepancy is not an artifact of model depth. The paper also shows that μP parameterization is more parameter-efficient than SP (α̅_D ≈ 0.50 vs. 0.35, and compute-optimal a ≈ 0.42 vs. 0.74), implying that compute-optimal scaling under μP does not occur at constant tokens-per-parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant theoretical narrative that neural scaling laws originate from power laws already present in the data (Zipf's law in language, power-law spectra in images), as formalized in kernel regression and linear model analyses (Bordelon et al., 2020; Bahri et al., 2021; Spigler et al., 2020). It repurposes the well-studied connection between transformers and Markov chains (Perozzi et al., 2014; Edelman et al., 2024; Makkuva et al., 2025) not to study model capability but as a controlled testbed for isolating the origin of scaling exponents. Methodologically, it departs from the widely-used 2D Chinchilla parametric fit (Hoffmann et al., 2022) in favor of standard ML regression (neural network and kernel ridge), and extends the μP parameterization analysis (Yang & Hu, 2021; Dey et al., 2023) to question whether compute-optimal scaling holds at constant tokens-per-parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating scaling laws on Erdős-Rényi random walks with no power-law structure in the data, the paper forces the field to look beyond 'power laws in data' as the sole explanation for scaling exponents, pointing instead toward optimization-landscape or architectural mechanisms. The monotonic evolution of α_D with dataset complexity (bigrams → T1L → T2L → natural language) offers a new axis for studying how data structure shapes scaling behavior, though the trend is based on few data points. The preliminary finding that μP yields larger α_D (more parameter-efficient scaling) than standard parameterization, if it holds at scale, would revise the compute-optimal recipe. Key open questions include: what mechanism produces power-law loss decay in the absence of power-law data correlations, whether the μP parameter-efficiency advantage persists at larger scales, and whether the systematic D-dependence of α_D in language data reflects a deeper structural property.
