## Problem

Latent Action Models (LAMs) face a fundamental trade-off: strong predictive bottlenecks (VQ, variational priors) yield abstract, transferable actions but disrupt the latent manifold and degrade video generation fidelity, while relaxing bottlenecks improves generation but produces entangled representations that capture irrelevant visual details. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-level action abstraction and high-fidelity generation within a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

DiLA consumes 16-frame video clips at 256×256×3, encoded by a frozen DINOv2 backbone into 768-dim embeddings over a 16×16 patch grid. All prediction occurs in latent embedding space (JEPA-style), with no pixel-level reconstruction during training; visual outputs are produced only at inference via a frozen RAE decoder. For downstream planning, the model takes a goal image plus a short video context and outputs an optimal action sequence via MPPI; for action transfer, it takes an initial frame and a sequence of transferred 256-dim continuous latent actions to synthesize a physically consistent rollout.

## Method

DiLA splits video features into two specialized pathways: a structure pathway that compresses tokens into 32-dim per-patch embeddings and learns latent actions from their temporal differences via 3D convolutions (IDM) and a lightweight ST-Transformer with AdaLN-zero (FDM) that predicts the next structure state as a residual update; and a content pathway that compresses tokens into 256-dim embeddings and aggregates historical appearance via a Mamba module mimicking Slow Feature Analysis. The key mechanism is that the temporal-difference bottleneck forces the structure pathway to retain only dynamics-relevant spatial layouts while offloading high-entropy visual details to the content pathway, creating a co-evolving loop where the predictive bottleneck drives disentanglement and the resulting structural purity facilitates more abstract latent action learning. A Fusion Decoder with dual cross-attention (over content memory and the initial frame) recombines predicted structure with content to reconstruct the target DINOv2 embedding. Training is fully self-supervised with a weighted combination of visual prediction, structure prediction, latent action consistency, and geometric regularization (norm, variance, and inverse-temporal symmetry) losses, followed by a short latent-rollout fine-tuning phase for temporal stability.

## Results

On video generation (16-frame autoregressive rollout), DiLA achieves the best LPIPS on both SSv2 (0.356) and RT-1 (0.206), and the best SSIM on RT-1 (0.774), outperforming all baselines. On SSv2 SSIM, DiLA scores 0.660, slightly below AdaWorld's 0.674 (which relies on an external pretrained diffusion model). In visual planning on VP², DiLA achieves an aggregate success rate of 41.44, nearly doubling AdaWorld's 21.54, with the largest gain on the Blue Button task (78.33% vs. 29.17%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiLA outperforms LAPA on all four generation metrics, with the largest margin on RT-1 LPIPS (0.206 vs. 0.595) and SSv2 LPIPS (0.356 vs. 0.565). Against MOTO, DiLA wins on SSv2 (SSIM 0.660 vs. 0.555; LPIPS 0.356 vs. 0.593) and RT-1 LPIPS (0.206 vs. 0.284), while MOTO is competitive on RT-1 SSIM (0.762 vs. 0.774). Compared to AdaWorld, DiLA achieves substantially better LPIPS on both datasets (0.356 vs. 0.521 on SSv2; 0.206 vs. 0.429 on RT-1) and higher RT-1 SSIM (0.774 vs. 0.634), though AdaWorld edges out DiLA on SSv2 SSIM (0.674 vs. 0.660) by leveraging an external pretrained diffusion model. Against Villa-X, DiLA dominates across all metrics, with RT-1 SSIM improving from 0.576 to 0.774. In visual planning (VP²), DiLA's aggregate success rate of 41.44 nearly doubles AdaWorld's 21.54, confirming that the disentangled world model translates into superior downstream control performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiLA sits at the intersection of two research threads: Latent Action Models (Genie, LAPA, Moto, AdaWorld, Villa-X) that infer abstract actions from unlabeled video, and content-structure disentanglement (DSVAE, ContextWM, Dyn-O) that separates static appearance from dynamic layout. It departs from prior LAMs by rejecting both the two-stage paradigm (LAM + separate pretrained diffusion model) and the optical-flow/depth supervision approaches, instead proposing that the predictive bottleneck of the IDM-FDM loop itself acts as the driving force for disentanglement. Architecturally, it repurposes off-the-shelf components (DINOv2 encoder, Mamba memory, AdaLN-zero conditioning) into a dual-pathway design where the structure pathway is constrained to predict only spatial-layout dynamics while the content pathway offloads appearance details, creating a co-evolutionary loop between abstraction and disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the LAM Trade-off within a single self-supervised model, DiLA offers a practical path toward scalable world models that are simultaneously abstract enough for cross-embodiment action transfer and faithful enough for high-quality video generation—two properties that prior work treated as mutually exclusive. The co-evolution principle (predictive bottleneck drives disentanglement, which in turn facilitates better action learning) is a reusable design insight that could inform other representation-learning settings. Key limitations remain: the disentanglement is limited to spatial layout vs. appearance (no multi-object decomposition), autoregressive compounding errors degrade long-horizon rollouts, and the inherent abstraction sacrifices fine-grained control precision, positioning latent actions as high-level rather than low-level control signals.
