## Problem

Existing generative novel view synthesis methods produce semantically implausible and distorted content when the camera moves far from the input view, because they fail to fully leverage the high-level semantics in their conditioning signals and intermediate denoising states. While pre-trained semantic feature extractors like DINO have been used in image generation (e.g., REPA), their integration into multi-view diffusion for NVS remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or a few source RGB views at 576×576 resolution with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, plus a target camera trajectory, and produces a sequence of novel views following that trajectory. Conditioning is provided via Plücker ray maps, warped RGB, and warped DINO features; generation operates in a 72×72 latent space with a window size of 21 frames. Inference follows a two-stage procedure: anchor views are synthesized along the trajectory first, then intermediate views are interpolated between adjacent anchors.

## Method

SemanticNVS builds on the SEVA camera-conditioned diffusion backbone and introduces two complementary semantic conditioning mechanisms. First, DINOv2 features are extracted from input views, geometrically warped into target views via VGGT point-cloud reconstruction, ℓ₂-normalized, and projected to a compact 32-channel representation to anchor object-level semantics in visible regions. Second, at every denoising step, DINO features are extracted from the noise-free one-step estimate x̂₀ᵗ and fused with the warped features using a rendering mask, providing step-wise semantic guidance for unobserved regions. During training, the unavailable intermediate x̂₀ᵗ is approximated by applying a timestep-dependent Gaussian blur to ground-truth frames. The model is fine-tuned from SEVA's checkpoint on ~75k videos from RealEstate10K and DL3DV, with the iterative DINO paradigm enabled after 37k of 60k total iterations.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (in-distribution), SemanticNVS achieves FID of 22.73 (short) and 26.56 (long trajectory), outperforming SEVA (26.38 / 31.34), Uni3C (25.31 / 39.57), and ViewCrafter (32.93 / 37.70). On the out-of-distribution Tanks-and-Temples benchmark, it reaches FID 30.75 (short) and 44.38 (long), with SEVA degrading severely to 44.75 / 46.56. The method reduces image-quality drift by 25–35% over baselines on long trajectories (e.g., 0.364 vs. SEVA's 0.511 on RealEstate10K), and improves PSNR by up to 2.5 dB over SEVA on long trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemanticNVS outperforms all three primary baselines (ViewCrafter, Uni3C, SEVA) across nearly every metric on both datasets. Against SEVA (the closest baseline and the backbone it builds upon), it improves FID by 10.2–15.3% on RealEstate10K and 4.7–14.98% on Tanks-and-Temples, while reducing image-quality drift by up to 35.2% on full long trajectories. ViewCrafter and Uni3C, which lack a sliding-window mechanism, are limited to 20-frame subsampled trajectories and show severe camera drift (RE > 3°) and content collapse on long trajectories; SemanticNVS maintains RE below 0.5° and TE below 0.06 m. Against REPA (which distills DINO features into the diffusion backbone's intermediate layers), SemanticNVS achieves substantially better FID (26.56 vs. 30.58) and PSNR (13.82 vs. 12.79), attributed to decoupling semantic understanding from the generative model rather than injecting it implicitly. Against the concurrent Gen3R baseline, SemanticNVS leads by 4.4–24.4 FID points on long trajectories across both datasets, with the gap widening on the out-of-distribution Tanks-and-Temples set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SEVA, a camera-conditioned multi-view diffusion backbone, and augments its geometric conditioning (Plücker ray maps, warped RGB) with pre-trained DINOv2 semantic features. Conceptually, it departs from the warp-and-inpaint lineage (ViewCrafter, Uni3C) and pure camera-conditioning approaches by arguing that the bottleneck is not geometric control but the model's failure to *understand* its conditioning. The closest prior work is REPA, which injects DINO features implicitly into diffusion intermediate representations; SemanticNVS instead uses them as explicit, decoupled conditioning signals at each denoising step, a design choice the authors argue preserves generative capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and actionable failure mode—semantic degradation under long-range camera motion—and shows that a relatively lightweight addition (warped DINO features + iterative DINO extraction) yields 10–15% FID improvements and ~30% drift reduction without architectural changes to the diffusion backbone. This suggests a broadly applicable pattern: decoupling semantic understanding from generation via pre-trained extractors can strengthen any camera-conditioned diffusion model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, very long trajectories (thousands of frames), and whether the reliance on DINOv2's semantic coverage becomes a bottleneck for object categories it was not trained to recognize.
