## Problem

Current RL pipelines for flow-based image generation suffer from two underappreciated failure modes: (i) a quality-diversity dilemma where improved prompt adherence collapses output diversity, starving RL of the behavioral variation needed for effective exploration, and (ii) prompt overfitting where models memorize superficial lexical patterns and catastrophically degrade on semantically equivalent paraphrases. These issues stem from treating prompts as fixed inputs rather than malleable optimization components, making standard prompt enhancement techniques ineffective or even harmful after RL fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a natural language prompt and produces a generated image at 512×512 (training) or 1024×1024 (evaluation) resolution; for instructional editing, it takes a reference image plus a text instruction and produces an edited image at 1024×1024. The LM component outputs refined prompt variants enclosed in XML <answer> tags, and the jointly trained LM itself serves as a deployable prompt-enhancement module at inference time. Each prompt (original or refined) is paired with an independent Gaussian noise sample for the flow-matching denoising process.

## Method

PromptRL jointly trains a 3B language model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) and a 12B flow-matching model (FLUX.1-dev or FLUX.1-Kontext) within a unified GRPO loop, where the LM generates semantically grounded prompt variants that expand the FM's exploration space while reward signals from generated images guide the LM toward useful reformulations. A prompt retention mechanism keeps m=2 of n=8 samples on the original prompt per group, providing a stable baseline for advantage estimation and preventing the FM from becoming overly dependent on LM refinements. Gradients are architecturally disjoint—only LM-refined samples contribute to the LM's policy gradient, while all n samples update the FM—ensuring selective, complementary learning. For multi-reward training, a per-sample reward tagging scheme assigns each prompt a categorical tag and normalizes advantages within each tag, eliminating the need for manual reward coefficient tuning or multi-stage curricula.

## Results

PromptRL achieves state-of-the-art GenEval of 0.97 (vs. FlowGRPO 0.92, DiffusionNFT 0.88), OCR accuracy of 0.98 on OCR-1k (vs. 0.89 for both FlowGRPO and DiffusionNFT), and PickScore of 24.05 (vs. 23.33/23.63). On instructional image editing, PromptRL lifts FLUX.1-Kontext from 1.19 to 1.43 EditReward with only 0.06M rollouts, surpassing Nano Banana (1.37) and matching ReasonEdit-Think (1.44). Across all objectives, PromptRL reaches comparable or superior performance with approximately 50% fewer rollouts than flow-only RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, PromptRL (0.97) outperforms all non-RL baselines (Qwen-Image 0.87, Seedream 3.0 0.84, HiDream 0.83) and both RL baselines (FlowGRPO 0.92, DiffusionNFT 0.88), with the largest gap over DiffusionNFT (+0.09) driven by superior Counting (0.99 vs. 0.74) and Position (0.99 vs. 0.85). On aesthetic and OCR metrics, PromptRL leads FlowGRPO by +0.72 PickScore and +0.09 OCR-1k, and DiffusionNFT by +0.42 PickScore and +0.09 OCR-1k. On image editing, PromptRL (1.43) surpasses Nano Banana (1.37), FLUX.2-klein (1.34), and ReasonEdit (1.40), trailing only ReasonEdit-Think (1.44) by 0.01; the latter relied on fine-grained data annotations and a multi-stage pipeline. Notably, naively applying prompt enhancement to FLUX.1-Kontext without joint training degrades EditReward from 1.19 to 1.01, confirming that the gains require co-adaptation rather than a static rewriter. Even without test-time PE, PromptRL w/o PE (0.94 GenEval, 1.36 EditReward) exceeds FlowGRPO and all non-RL baselines, indicating the joint training itself instills robustness beyond the LM rewriter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-based RL line (FlowGRPO, DiffusionNFT, DanceGRPO) by identifying two failure modes—generation diversity collapse and prompt linguistic overfitting—that prior work treats as fixed inputs rather than optimization variables. It departs from the prompt-enhancement literature (Promptist, RePrompt, PAE), which uses LMs as static preprocessors, by instead co-training the LM as a dynamic prompt refiner within the same GRPO loop as the flow-matching model, with architecturally disjoint gradients but shared reward signals. The RL algorithm itself is standard GRPO (Shao et al., 2024); the contribution is the joint LM-FM training architecture, the prompt retention mechanism, and the reward-tagging strategy for multi-objective optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete diagnosis of why flow-based RL is sample-inefficient (diversity collapse + prompt overfitting) and shows that a 3B LM co-trained in-loop recovers ~50% of the rollout budget while reaching higher ceilings, a practically significant efficiency gain for an area where compute is the bottleneck. A key open question is the co-adaptation limitation: the FM becomes partially specialized to its training-time LM partner (GenEval drops from 0.97 to 0.84 with a different LM at inference), which constrains modular deployment. Future work on multi-LM training, cross-LM regularization, or disentangling the FM's prompt-conditioning from the specific rewriter's linguistic style would address this coupling.
