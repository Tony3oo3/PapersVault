## Problem

Existing experience learning methods for LLM agents operate entirely in explicit text space: experiences are stored as textual summaries, retrieved via semantic similarity, and concatenated into the context window. This introduces substantial token overhead (1.5–2× more tokens than non-retrieval baselines) and a decoupled architecture where the retriever is trained separately from the generator, preventing joint optimization toward the downstream task objective. The paper asks how to enable LLM agents to learn from experience beyond these text-level constraints.

## Input / Output

For generative tasks (QA, math, coding, chemistry), the input is a text query and the output is a generated textual answer or code. For ranking tasks (recommendation), the input is a sequence of 20 historical user interactions plus 20 candidate items, and the output is a ranked ordering of those candidates. Critically, retrieved experiences are never appended to the prompt; instead they are consumed as dense latent embeddings injected into the model's hidden states at every decoding step, so the context window size remains identical to a non-retrieval baseline.

## Method

ExpWeaver encodes each past trajectory's textual summary into a latent embedding using the LLM's own final-layer hidden state, ensuring on-policy alignment as the policy evolves. At every decoding step, the current hidden state h_t acts as a query to retrieve Top-K experience embeddings via cosine similarity in latent space, eliminating the need for a separate retriever. Retrieved embeddings are aggregated through cross-attention with a single learnable query token, then integrated into the hidden state via a gated residual mechanism (retention and input gates with norm-preserving interpolation) that adaptively balances experience influence against the original representation. The entire pipeline—policy θ and experience parameters ψ_exp (query token, gate matrices, mixing coefficients)—is trained end-to-end with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using a KL-regularized objective, supporting both generative and ranking task heads from the same architecture.

## Results

ExpWeaver achieves state-of-the-art results on 12 out of 13 tasks across ExpBench (Generic, Sci, Rec), outperforming the strongest baseline by over 6.8% on average. On ExpBench-Generic (10 tasks), ExpWeaver attains an average score of 78.25, surpassing the best baseline Search-R1 (73.27) by 5.0 points. On ExpBench-Sci (Chem-TDC), ExpWeaver reaches 69.58 exact match vs. 63.78 for Search-R1, and on ExpBench-Rec it achieves 44.40 NDCG@10 vs. 37.90 for IRanker. ExpWeaver also maintains token efficiency comparable to non-retrieval baselines (only ~10% more tokens than HRPO), while text-based retrieval methods like Search-R1 require 1.5–2× more tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpWeaver outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest LLM-centric baseline Search-R1 (avg 73.27 on ExpBench-Generic), ExpWeaver gains +5.0 points on average, with particularly large margins on reasoning tasks (GSM-Symbolic: 85.00 vs. 77.56; MATH: 65.33 vs. 59.78) and coding (MBPP+: 78.00 vs. 67.50). Among general reasoning baselines, HRPO (74.04) and R1 (73.10) are the closest competitors, but ExpWeaver still leads by 4.2 and 5.2 points respectively. Retrieval-centric methods (ReasoningBank 60.57, ExpeL 60.94, LLM-R 63.52) and other LLM-centric methods (IRCoT 59.82, Search-o1 63.10) trail significantly, with gaps of 14–18 points. On ExpBench-Rec, ExpWeaver (44.40 NDCG@10) surpasses the strongest recommendation baseline IRanker (37.90) by 6.5 points and Search-R1 (28.53) by 15.9 points. On ExpBench-Sci, ExpWeaver (69.58) leads Search-R1 (63.78) by 5.8 points and R1 (62.22) by 7.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExpWeaver departs from both retrieval-centric (ReasoningBank, ExpeL, LLM-R) and LLM-centric (IRCoT, Search-o1, Search-R1) experience learning paradigms by eliminating the separate RAG module entirely. Instead of storing and retrieving experiences as text in an external index, it encodes them as dense embeddings using the LLM's own final-layer hidden states and performs retrieval at every decoding step in latent space. The aggregation and integration mechanisms (cross-attention with a single learnable query token, gated norm-preserving residual) are borrowed from vision-language fusion (BLIP-2/Q-Former) and recurrent gating, but repurposed here to fuse retrieved experience vectors into autoregressive generation. Training unifies policy and experience parameters under GRPO, extending the RL-for-reasoning line (DeepSeek-R1, Search-R1) to jointly optimize what to retrieve and how to use it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that experience-augmented generation no longer incurs the 1.5–2× token overhead of text concatenation, making retrieval-augmented agents viable under tight context budgets. The 16.3% zero-shot cross-domain transfer gain suggests latent experience representations capture more abstract problem-solving structure than surface-level text, which could reduce the data needed to adapt agents to new domains. Limitations include evaluation on a single 3B model (Qwen2.5) with LoRA, a fixed-capacity experience bank with simple LRU eviction, and no analysis of how the method scales to larger models or longer experience horizons. Open questions include whether the on-policy alignment argument holds under off-policy or multi-agent settings, and whether the single-query-token cross-attention remains sufficient as the experience bank grows to millions of entries.
