## Problem

On-policy RL methods like GRPO suffer from mode collapse: once a high-reward trajectory is discovered, the policy concentrates probability mass on it and ceases exploring alternative strategies. The paper identifies this as a direct consequence of reverse KL minimization's inherent mode-seeking behavior, rather than an implementation artifact, and shows that forward KL's mode-covering property is the principled fix—but is intractable globally because it requires sampling from the reward-weighted target distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes either a natural-language problem description or a rendered graph image (nodes, edges, weights) of an NP-hard combinatorial optimization instance, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a structured solution (e.g., a complete tour sequence for TSP, a vertex subset for Vertex Cover, or a color assignment for Graph Coloring). Rewards are computed by a rule-based verifier that checks structural validity (not just scalar outputs) and then scores optimization quality via a Quality Ratio against a heuristic solver's near-optimal reference, making the reward fine-grained rather than binary.

## Method

DMPO augments GRPO with a group-level distribution-matching loss that approximates forward KL minimization. For each group of G trajectories sampled from the current policy, it constructs a Boltzmann target distribution p(o_i|O) ∝ exp(r(o_i)/α) over the group and a length-normalized policy distribution q_θ(o_i|O) from per-token log-likelihoods, then aligns them via an MSE loss (chosen over forward KL for bounded gradients when the policy becomes near-deterministic). The unified objective L_GRPO + λ·L_DM combines exploitation (reward maximization via clipped policy gradient) with exploration (mode-covering via distribution matching), requiring only the trivially computable group-level partition function rather than the intractable global one. Theoretically, DMPO guarantees local mode-covering behavior (high-reward trajectories cannot be assigned zero probability), converges to proportional sampling as λ→∞, and smoothly interpolates between pure GRPO and full distribution matching via the single hyperparameter λ.

## Results

On the primary multimodal benchmark MM-NP-Bench, DMPO achieves 43.1% Quality Ratio (QR) and 61.9% Success Rate (SR), outperforming GRPO (38.4% QR, 55.7% SR) by 12% relative improvement in QR. On text-based NP-Bench, DMPO reaches 43.9% QR versus GRPO's 40.1% (+9% relative). DMPO also improves mathematical reasoning by 2.0% (44.5% vs 42.5% average across six benchmarks) and out-of-domain visual reasoning by 0.5% (39.8% vs 39.3% when combined with general reasoning data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMPO outperforms all seven baselines on the primary MM-NP-Bench metric (QR): it beats GRPO by 4.7 points (43.1 vs 38.4), GSPO by 3.4 points (43.1 vs 39.7), GPG by 6.3 points (43.1 vs 36.8), ClipCov by 4.8 points (43.1 vs 38.3), FlowRL by 4.2 points (43.1 vs 38.9), and both Pass@K variants by 8–10 points. On text-based NP-Bench, DMPO leads with 43.9% QR, surpassing GRPO (40.1%), GSPO (41.2%), and ClipCov (41.3%), while GPG shows severe mode collapse (88.6% SR but only 32.7% QR). On mathematical reasoning, DMPO achieves the best average (44.5%) over GRPO (42.5%), GSPO (42.1%), ClipCov (42.3%), GPG (39.7%), and FlowRL (40.2%). Notably, DMPO is the only method that simultaneously improves both SR and QR across all task categories, whereas GPG and the Pass@K variants trade quality for feasibility or vice versa.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMPO extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by adding a single group-level distribution-matching term that approximates forward KL minimization, addressing what the authors identify as the root cause of mode collapse in on-policy RL: the implicit reverse KL objective's mode-seeking behavior. This positions the work in contrast to FlowRL (Zhu et al., 2025), which also targets reward-distribution matching via GFlowNet principles but still minimizes reverse KL and requires a learned partition function, and to entropy-regularization or Pass@K approaches that address diversity heuristically. The key conceptual departure is operating at the group level with a tractable Boltzmann target and MSE loss, avoiding both the intractability of global forward KL and the need for a backward policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean diagnostic framework (high SR / low QR) for observing mode collapse and a minimal-overhead fix (one MSE term, ~15% wall-clock increase) that yields consistent 9–12% relative gains on optimization tasks and modest 2–2.3% transfers to math reasoning and out-of-domain visual reasoning. MM-NP-Bench fills a gap by offering a multimodal, dual-metric testbed where exploration quality is directly measurable rather than inferred from pass rates. Open questions remain: DMPO requires exact verifiable rewards (limiting applicability to open-ended tasks), the group-level approximation cannot recover global optima in extremely sparse-reward settings, and the transfer gains, while positive, are small enough that it is unclear whether diversity preservation is the true mechanism or a proxy for better reward shaping.
