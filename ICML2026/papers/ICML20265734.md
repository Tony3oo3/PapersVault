## Problem

Maximum entropy RL, the dominant off-policy framework for balancing exploration and exploitation, suffers from two structural bottlenecks: (1) non-stationary Q-value targets caused by the tight coupling of reward and entropy within a single Bellman target, where concurrent temperature updates inject additional variance into value learning; and (2) short-sighted local entropy tuning that enforces a uniform per-step entropy target across all states, ignoring the varying exploration needs of different situations and the long-term cumulative stochasticity of the policy over entire trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observation vectors of varying dimensionality (6 to 376 dimensions across MuJoCo locomotion and manipulation tasks) or 96×96×3 RGB images for the vision-based CarRacing task, and produces a continuous control action vector (1 to 17 dimensions) sampled from a diagonal Gaussian policy. The architecture inherits the distributional Q-network structure from DSAC-T, where critics output a distribution over returns rather than a single scalar, and the policy network is a standard MLP of the same size as the baseline, ensuring identical inference cost.

## Method

The paper proposes the Trajectory Entropy-Constrained RL (TECRL) framework, instantiated as DSAC-E on top of DSAC-T, which replaces the single coupled Q-function with two independent critics: a reward critic Q_r trained with a standard Bellman target free of any entropy or temperature term (Reward-Entropy Separation, RES), and an entropy critic Q_e that estimates the expected discounted cumulative policy entropy from the next time step onward (Policy Introspection). The policy improvement step maximizes Q_r + α(−log π + Q_e), preserving the exact soft policy objective, while the temperature update (TUP) tunes α to match the cumulative entropy (−log π + Q_e) to a trajectory-level budget H_budget = ρH₀/(1−γ) rather than a per-step target. This design yields stationary Q-targets (since α never appears in either Bellman target) and enables state-dependent, strategically allocated exploration: the entropy critic learns to assign higher stochasticity to normal states and lower stochasticity to high-risk states. Theoretical analysis establishes convergence of Q_e via a γ-contraction mapping, derives a closed-form optimal policy as a reward Boltzmann distribution reweighted by exp(Q_e), and provides a performance bound showing that a conservative entropy budget can only improve upon the maximum-entropy optimum.

## Results

DSAC-E achieves the best average final return across all 10 continuous control tasks, with the largest gains on high-dimensional locomotion: Humanoid (12542±280, +15.82% over second-best DSAC-T), Ant (8640±57, +21.93%), and Walker2d (7780±137, +21.11%). On manipulation and driving tasks, DSAC-E also leads: Pusher (-17.7±0.2, +7.33%), Reacher (-2.9±0.1, +6.45%), and CarRacing (918±3, +1.66%). The method ties DSAC-T on InvertedDP (9360±0) and shows modest but consistent advantages on Swimmer (+2.54%) and HalfCheetah (+5.16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSAC-E outperforms its direct predecessor DSAC-T on 9 out of 10 tasks, with the most dramatic gains on Humanoid (+15.8%), Ant (+21.9%), and Walker2d (+21.1%), while tying on InvertedDP. Against the broader maximum-entropy family (DSAC, SAC), DSAC-E consistently leads, e.g., on Hopper it reaches 3901 vs. DSAC's 2135 and SAC's 2483, and on CarRacing it achieves 918 vs. SAC's -12 (a catastrophic failure for SAC on the vision-based task). Compared to deterministic off-policy baselines (TD3, DDPG), DSAC-E dominates on all locomotion tasks (e.g., Humanoid 12542 vs. TD3's 5632) and manipulation (Pusher -17.7 vs. DDPG's -30.5), though DDPG slightly edges DSAC-E on Swimmer (145.6 vs. 149.3 is actually DSAC-E winning; DDPG's 145.6 is lower). Against on-policy methods (TRPO, PPO), DSAC-E is substantially superior on high-dimensional tasks (Humanoid 12542 vs. TRPO's 965) and more stable, with TRPO showing extreme variance (±555 on Humanoid). The only task where DSAC-E does not strictly win is InvertedDP, where it ties DSAC-T, SAC, and PPO at 9360, suggesting the trajectory entropy constraint provides no additional benefit on this simple 1-DOF balancing task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the maximum entropy RL lineage (SAC, DSAC, DSAC-T) by identifying two structural bottlenecks—non-stationary Q-value targets from coupled reward-entropy updates, and short-sighted local entropy tuning—and proposing a trajectory entropy-constrained (TECRL) framework that resolves both. The key architectural departure is reward-entropy separation (RES), which splits the single soft Q-function into independent reward and entropy critics, and the trajectory entropy constraint (TEC), which replaces per-step entropy matching with a discounted cumulative entropy budget enforced via a dedicated entropy critic. Theoretically, the paper builds on soft policy iteration and contraction mapping arguments from Haarnoja et al. (2017) but introduces a new Bellman equation for the entropy critic and a closed-form optimal policy that factors into a reward Boltzmann distribution times an entropy-augmented weight function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-level entropy constraint offers a principled alternative to the ad-hoc local entropy target that has been standard in SAC-family algorithms since 2018, and the error propagation analysis (Appendix C) provides a clear structural explanation for why decoupling reward and entropy critics suppresses cascading instability. The consistent 15–22% improvements on high-dimensional locomotion tasks (Humanoid, Ant, Walker2d) over the strong DSAC-T baseline, combined with the robustness to the budget coefficient ρ, suggest the framework is practically deployable. Open questions include whether the trajectory entropy constraint generalizes to multi-agent or hierarchical settings, how it interacts with model-based or offline RL, and whether the additional entropy critic's ~10–17% training overhead remains acceptable at larger scales. The framework also raises the question of whether the entropy budget should be state-dependent or learned end-to-end rather than set via a fixed ρ.
