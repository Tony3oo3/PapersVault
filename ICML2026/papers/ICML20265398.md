## Problem

Vision-language models trained on passive video data develop strong temporal priors that cause spatiotemporal hallucinations in egocentric, task-oriented settings: they implicitly assume the later frame in a sequence represents the completed task state rather than genuinely reasoning about spatial state changes. This chronological bias leads to severe failures in long-horizon embodied tasks where action failures, partial progress, and state regressions are common, and existing models cannot reliably determine whether an action genuinely advances the task toward completion.

## Input / Output

The model receives two ego-centric video frames (I_a, I_b) sampled at varying temporal intervals (Δt ∈ {5,…,12+}×10 for short tasks; INTRA/INTER/MULTI-TASK windows for long tasks), a natural-language task instruction, and optionally a sequence of orthogonal atomic subtasks for long-horizon evaluation. It outputs a binary completion-state judgment (which frame is closer to task completion) and, in long-horizon mode, a logical plan of atomic subtask steps. Critically, both forward (I_a, I_b) and inverse (I_b, I_a) frame orderings are evaluated to measure and eliminate chronological bias, and the base architecture is Qwen-VL-7B fine-tuned in bf16 on H800 GPUs.

## Method

EgoTSR introduces a three-stage curriculum learning paradigm over 46M samples that mimics human cognitive development: Stage 1 (15M CoT samples) trains explicit chain-of-thought reasoning with fine-grained spatial descriptions to establish a 'Perception-Planning-Decision' pathway; Stage 2 (16M Tag samples) removes intermediate text via weak supervision to internalize reasoning into direct visual-to-judgment mapping; Stage 3 (15M LongTag samples) consolidates generalization using orthogonal atomic subtask sequences as conditioning for long-horizon planning. A Reasoning-Enhanced Task Decomposition mechanism (Subtask Planner) is trained via next-token prediction to decompose abstract task descriptions into sequences of functionally orthogonal atomic subtasks, explicitly modeling causal dependencies between actions and objects. The 'explicit-to-internalized' progression is shown to be essential: sequential curriculum (S1+S2+S3) achieves 92.4% long-horizon accuracy with a 0.1 forward-inverse gap, while mixed non-sequential training (S1-S2-S3) drops to 69.6% with a 4.9 gap, confirming that ordered progression is indispensable for eliminating chronological bias without sacrificing atomic perceptual precision (~88%).

## Results

On the Dual-Level Evaluation Framework, EgoTSR-LongTag achieves 87.5% average accuracy on the Short (atomic perception) level and 92.4% on the Long (logical planning) level, approaching human performance (98.9% Short, 95.2% Long). Critically, the model exhibits near-zero chronological bias with a Forward/Inverse gap of only 0.1 (92.4% vs. 92.3%), whereas all baselines show substantial directional asymmetry. The curriculum progression is visible in the ablation: EgoTSR-CoT reaches 73.6% Short but only 46.9% Long, EgoTSR-Tag improves Short to 86.8% but Long drops to 55.8%, and the full EgoTSR-LongTag consolidates both to 87.5% and 92.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoTSR-LongTag outperforms all baselines by a wide margin on the Long (planning) level: it achieves 92.4% average accuracy versus 69.0% for the best closed-source model (GPT-o3), 62.0% for Gemini-2.5-Pro, 50.6% for the best open-source 2D model (InternVL-8B), and 50.4% for the best open-source 3D model (LL3DA). On the Short (perception) level, EgoTSR-LongTag (87.5%) also leads all models, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (80.2%) by 7.3 points and InternVL-8B (48.9%) by 38.6 points. The most striking finding is the elimination of chronological bias: EgoTSR-LongTag maintains a Forward/Inverse gap of only 0.1, while InternVL-8B shows a catastrophic 97.5-point gap (99.7% forward vs. 2.2% inverse) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B shows an 84.7-point gap, indicating these models rely on input-order heuristics rather than genuine spatial reasoning. Among the 3D-specific baselines (3D-LLM, LL3DA, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLVA, Video-3D LLM), all score near chance on at least one direction of the Long level, confirming that point-cloud representations do not resolve the temporal reasoning challenge. The ablation further shows that the sequential curriculum (S1+S2+S3, 92.4%) dramatically outperforms mixed-stage training (S1-S2-S3, 69.6%), validating that ordered progression is essential for bias elimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) and Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) into the egocentric embodied reasoning setting, building most directly on Progressive Training (Yang et al., 2026a) which also studies curriculum-based VLM training to mitigate spatiotemporal hallucinations. It departs from prior embodied VLM work (RT-2, InstructVLA, Voyager) by reframing the problem as a binary completion-state judgment over frame pairs rather than end-to-end action generation, and from prior temporal reasoning benchmarks by introducing bidirectional evaluation to explicitly diagnose and eliminate chronological bias. The Reasoning-Enhanced Task Decomposition mechanism draws on task planning in robotics (Ahn et al., 2022; Yao et al., 2022) but repurposes it as a training-time conditioning signal rather than an inference-time planner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic chronological bias exposed by the bidirectional evaluation (e.g., InternVL-8B: >99% forward vs. ~2% inverse on long tasks) provides a concrete diagnostic that the field has lacked, revealing that many VLMs exploit input-order shortcuts rather than performing genuine spatiotemporal reasoning. The 'incremental without degradation' property—maintaining ~88% short-task accuracy while reaching 92.4% on long-horizon planning—addresses a practical concern about catastrophic forgetting in multi-stage training. Open questions include whether the curriculum generalizes beyond Qwen-VL-7B and robotic manipulation to other egocentric domains (e.g., human activity understanding), whether the 46M-sample scale is truly necessary, and whether the approach transfers to settings without clean subtask metadata for LongTag construction.
