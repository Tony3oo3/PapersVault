## Problem

Existing methods for adapting pre-trained diffusion models to high-reward objectives either require additional training (RL fine-tuning, DPO) or assume the reward is differentiable, which fails for black-box or discrete rewards common in molecular design and robotics. Moreover, training-free inference-time methods that do handle non-differentiable rewards (SMC, search-based) incur high computational overhead and lack theoretical convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether a training-free, theoretically grounded, and computationally efficient adaptation method exists for generic non-differentiable rewards.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained score network s_θ(x,t) and a scalar reward function r(x) that only needs to be evaluated (not differentiated). In the text-to-image setting, it takes a text prompt and produces images with improved aesthetic or ImageReward scores; in the offline RL setting, it takes state observations and produces action sequences with higher Q-function returns. DOIT-Practical incurs zero additional score-network NFEs beyond vanilla generation, and the reward is queried only at the surrogate terminal states (M one-step lookahead samples per timestep).

## Method

DOIT formulates reward adaptation as measure transport from the pre-trained distribution P_θ to a reward-tilted target P_θ(·|E), realized via Doob's h-transform, which adds a time-dependent correction term ∇log h(x_t, t) to the reverse SDE while keeping the score network frozen. The key technical contribution is a simulation-based approximation of ∇log h that avoids reward differentiability: it unrolls the backward trajectory into a product of Gaussian transition densities φ_θ and differentiates through the transition kernel rather than through the reward, yielding a Monte Carlo estimator over M backward rollouts. DOIT-Practical further replaces full rollouts with one-step lookahead states and Tweedie's formula to estimate terminal states, eliminating all extra score-network calls. The h-function is instantiated as exp(r(x)/τ) (exponential tilting), and two hyperparameters—correction strength γ and time threshold l*—control the magnitude and temporal extent of the Doob correction. A high-probability total-variation convergence bound (Theorem 5.4) is established for DOIT-Proto, decomposing error into pre-training gap, MC approximation error O(M^{-1/6}), and discretization error.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks, DOIT-Practical achieves an average score of 87.6, the best among all inference-time adaptation methods, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art TTS (86.1) and competitive training-based baselines QGPO (86.6) and D-QL (86.3). It wins on 8 out of 9 individual tasks among inference-time methods, with notable gains on Medium Hopper (101.0 vs. TTS 99.5) and Medium-Expert Walker2d (113.2 vs. TTS 111.4). On text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion v1.5, combining DOIT-Practical with BFS yields an ImageReward of 1.115±0.009 (K=8), surpassing BFS alone (1.087±0.031) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among inference-time methods, DOIT-Practical outperforms TTS (previous SOTA) on 8 of 9 D4RL tasks, with the largest margin on Medium-Replay Hopper (101.8 vs. 97.4) and Medium Hopper (101.0 vs. 99.5). It also surpasses TFG (82.1 avg) and DAS (80.2 avg) by substantial margins, with DAS particularly weak on Medium Hopper (71.3) and Medium-Replay Walker2d (63.8). Against training-based baselines, DOIT-Practical (87.6 avg) beats QGPO (86.6), D-QL (86.3), IQL (76.9), and Diffuser (75.3) without any additional training. On text-to-image (Table 1), DOIT-Practical combined with BFS achieves 1.115 ImageReward (K=8), beating BFS alone (1.087), DAS (1.052), TreeG (1.023), FK-Steering (0.926), and SVDD (0.775). The only task where DOIT does not lead is Medium-Replay HalfCheetah (46.9 vs. TTS 47.8), a narrow gap within standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Doob's h-transform framework (Rogers & Williams, 2000) for measure transport and applies it to the VP-SDE diffusion sampling process (Ho et al., 2020). It departs from prior Doob's h-transform adaptations for diffusion (Denker et al., 2024; Chang et al., 2026) that require training a separate neural network for the correction term, and from Nguyen et al. (2025) which is training-free but assumes a differentiable reward. The key conceptual departure is a simulation-based approximation of ∇log h that passes differentiation through the Gaussian transition kernel rather than through the reward function, enabling handling of generic non-differentiable rewards without any additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DOIT provides the first training-free, non-differentiable-reward adaptation method with an end-to-end high-probability total-variation convergence guarantee (Theorem 5.4), filling a gap where prior methods lacked theoretical grounding. The DOIT-Practical surrogate via Tweedie's formula achieves zero additional NFEs over vanilla sampling, making it immediately deployable. A key limitation is sensitivity to the probability of the high-reward event: when the pre-trained model rarely generates high-reward samples (small ρ), the MC approximation variance grows and the 1/ρ factor in the bound degrades. Open questions include whether the O(M^{-1/6}) convergence rate can be improved and how to handle extremely rare target regions without prohibitive sample counts.
