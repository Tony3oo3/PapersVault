## Problem

Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) can generate tokens in parallel but lack an explicit self-correction mechanism to revise erroneous tokens from earlier denoising steps, limiting their practical speed advantage over autoregressive models. Prior self-correction approaches—whether inference-time remasking or post-training fine-tuning—suffer from limited generalization, and the closest pretraining-based method (GIDD) relies on an opaque interpolation pipeline with coupled transition rates and retains a redundant remasking step that halves effective correction capacity.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a length-L sequence of latent variables (each position being either a vocabulary token or [MASK]) at a given denoising time t, and outputs a predicted clean-token distribution per position via a DiT backbone at GPT-2 SMALL scale. During generation, the sequence starts fully masked and is iteratively denoised; unlike standard MDLMs, SCDD can directly overwrite a previously unmasked token with a different one in a single step without first reverting it to [MASK], effectively doubling the correction throughput per denoising step.

## Method

SCDD redesigns the forward noising process with two independently controllable SNR parameters: γ_t governs the rate of absorbing-mask transitions (token → [MASK]) and ρ_t governs the rate of uniform transitions (token → random non-mask token). By enforcing monotonicity of both schedules, [MASK] becomes an absorbing state in the forward chain, which—via Bayes' rule—guarantees that the backward process contains no remasking transitions, so a token can be corrected in one step rather than two. The backward kernel is parameterized by a denoiser x_θ that predicts the clean-token distribution, with the Zero Masking Probabilities constraint retained but the Carry-Over Unmasking constraint relaxed to permit direct token revision. Training minimizes the theoretical continuous-time ELBO without any additional re-weighting, and inference requires no heuristic samplers or hyperparameter tuning beyond the noise schedule.

## Results

SCDD (p_u=0.2) achieves the best generative perplexity across all denoising steps on both LM1B and OWT. On OWT at 1024 steps, SCDD reaches 55.7 Gen PPL, outperforming GIDD+ (p_u=0.1) at 63.8 and MDLM at 88.5. At the critical few-step regime (32 steps on LM1B), SCDD achieves 130.0 Gen PPL versus 157.5 for ReMDM-cap and 146.4 for GIDD+ (p_u=0.1). On validation perplexity, SCDD (p_u=0.1) attains 28.41 on OWT and 39.16 on LM1B, a 9.9% and 3.7% improvement over the best GIDD+ baseline respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024), SCDD (p_u=0.2) achieves substantially lower Gen PPL at every step count (e.g., 55.7 vs. 88.5 on OWT at 1024 steps), though MDLM retains a lower validation perplexity (24.72 vs. 28.41 on OWT), reflecting the cost of learning uniform transitions. Against GIDD+ (von Rütte et al., 2025), SCDD wins on Gen PPL across all steps and datasets (e.g., 55.7 vs. 63.8 on OWT at 1024 steps; 130.0 vs. 146.4 on LM1B at 32 steps), on validation perplexity (28.41 vs. 31.54 on OWT for p_u=0.1), and on correction rate (0.75 vs. 0.40 at 1024 steps). The LLM-as-a-judge evaluation confirms SCDD's advantage with a 60.6% win rate over GIDD+ at 1024 steps, with significant gains in Clarity and Style, though GIDD+ retains a Creativity edge. Against ReMDM-cap (Wang et al., 2026), SCDD shows the largest relative gains in the few-step regime, reducing Gen PPL by 55% at 32 steps on LM1B (130.0 vs. 157.5), demonstrating that pretrained self-correction is far more effective than inference-time remasking heuristics when step budgets are tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCDD extends the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework by incorporating uniform transitions alongside the absorbing [MASK] token in the forward process, following the pretraining-based self-correction paradigm introduced by GIDD. However, it departs from GIDD in three key ways: (1) it formulates the forward process as an explicit discrete-time Markov chain with [MASK] as a true absorbing state, rather than GIDD's continuous interpolation pipeline with opaque interactions; (2) it decouples the two noise mechanisms (uniform mixing and masking) into independently controllable SNR parameters; and (3) it eliminates the remasking step entirely, so the backward process never transitions from a non-[MASK] token back to [MASK]. This positions SCDD as a cleaner, more interpretable reformulation of GIDD's core idea rather than a fundamentally new paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The remasking-free property is a practically significant architectural insight: by making [MASK] absorbing in the forward process, SCDD can correct a token in one backward step rather than two (mask-then-unmask), effectively doubling correction capacity under few-step parallel generation. This is particularly relevant as the field pushes toward faster inference for diffusion LMs, where few-step generation is the primary bottleneck. However, all experiments are at GPT-2-small scale, leaving open whether the self-correction advantage persists at larger scales where autoregressive baselines with speculative decoding become more competitive. The paper also acknowledges that standard zero-shot benchmarks (ARC, BoolQ, etc.) do not capture self-correction ability, and future work on scaling and RL-based enhancement for reasoning tasks is explicitly flagged as needed.
