## Problem

Existing imputation-free incomplete multi-view clustering methods rely on first-order fusion mechanisms (weighted averaging, co-regularization) that act as shallow low-pass filters with limited spectral selectivity, making them unable to separate latent consensus structure from view-specific structural variations amplified by missing views. The paper asks whether a higher-order spectral filtering perspective can refine degraded relational structure under partial observations without explicit data imputation.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view data where each sample has at least one observed view (encoded by a binary availability matrix Π ∈ {0,1}^{N×V}), along with a target cluster count c. It constructs sparse sample-anchor bipartite graphs per view using K-means-selected anchors and top-δ nearest-neighbor connections, so unobserved sample-view pairs simply contribute zero rows rather than imputed features. The output is a cluster assignment matrix Y ∈ {0,1}^{N×c}; the similarity matrix C is never materialized as a dense N×N matrix but is represented through compact r-dimensional anchor-subspace factorizations, keeping memory at O(NVδ + Nrc + M²) for a fixed anchor budget M.

## Method

CARD constructs a unified anchor-induced hypergraph from availability-gated sample-anchor relations and defines a normalized bipartite diffusion operator Q = D^{-1/2}H̃W^{1/2}D_e^{-1/2}, inducing a symmetric PSD sample-to-sample operator A = QQ^T. The core innovation is a high-order resolvent operator (I − αA⊗A)^{−1} that acts as a rational spectral filter: its per-mode gain g_α(λ) = (1−α)/(1−αλ²) amplifies consensus-dominant eigenmodes relative to view-specific residual modes, with tunable selectivity controlled by α. For a fixed clustering Y, the similarity update admits a closed-form compact solution C* = (1−α)S + U_r(Ω_r ∘ (U_r^T S U_r))U_r^T in the r-dimensional anchor subspace (Theorem 3.1), where Ω_r is a PSD matrix with entries α(1−α)σ_i²σ_j²/(1−ασ_i²σ_j²). For a fixed kernel, the assignment update is exactly equivalent to Euclidean K-means on a dynamically constructed embedding Z^{(t)} ∈ R^{N×(rc+c)} (Theorem 3.2), yielding linear scaling in N per iteration without any dense N×N matrix. The full algorithm alternates between compact feature construction and Lloyd K-means iterations, with theoretical guarantees including spectral selectivity, positive path-supported diffusion, conditional local subspace contraction, and first-order stability to anchor perturbations.

## Results

On UCIDigits (small-scale, 2,000 samples), CARD achieves ACC of 0.9580–0.9615, NMI of 0.9075–0.9144, and ARI of 0.9101–0.9168 across missing rates ρ=0.1–0.9, outperforming the strongest baseline FSIMVC-OF (ACC 0.9165–0.9530) by 1.5–4.5 points. On the large-scale NUSWIDEOBJ dataset (30,000 samples, 5 views), several competing methods fail due to out-of-memory errors, while CARD completes all missing-rate settings and achieves the best or competitive results among runnable baselines. On CCV (6,773 samples) and SUNRGBD (10,335 samples), CARD leads in ACC by 2–5 points over the next-best method at ρ=0.5. Runtime scales linearly in N for a fixed anchor budget, making CARD substantially faster than dense graph-based alternatives on large datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARD outperforms all ten incomplete multi-view clustering baselines across every dataset and missing rate tested. The closest competitor is FSIMVC-OF (Du et al., MM 2024), which achieves ACC 0.9330 on UCIDigits at ρ=0.5 versus CARD's 0.9375, and 0.4036 on Scene-15 versus CARD's 0.4762; the gap widens at higher missing rates (e.g., CARD leads by 4.5 ACC points on UCIDigits at ρ=0.9). FIMVC-VIA (Liu et al., TNNLS 2024) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing CARD by 2.6 ACC points on UCIDigits (0.9115 vs. 0.9375) and 8.3 points on Scene-15 (0.3935 vs. 0.4762). CAL (Liu et al., IJCAI 2025) degrades severely under high missingness, dropping to ACC 0.0923 on Scene-15 at ρ=0.5 and near-zero NMI/ARI at ρ≥0.3, while CARD maintains stable performance. On the large-scale NUSWIDEOBJ dataset (30,000 samples), all dense-graph baselines fail due to out-of-memory errors, leaving CARD as the only method that completes all missing-rate settings. Under complete settings (ρ=0), CARD is compared against ten additional multi-view clustering methods (EOMSC-CA, FSSC, FPMVS-CAG, FCAG, UDBGL, EDCAG, BGAE, SSA-MVC, ALPC, AEVC) and achieves the best or competitive performance on most dataset-metric pairs, confirming that the spectral filtering design does not sacrifice performance when data is fully observed.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARD carves new ground by reframing Incomplete Multi-View Clustering from a data-recovery or first-order graph-fusion problem into a high-order spectral filtering problem over observed anchor-induced relations. Conceptually, it repurposes resolvent operators from operator theory as rational spectral filters on a unified anchor hypergraph, departing from both imputation-driven methods (which reconstruct missing views) and first-order fusion approaches (weighted averaging, co-regularization) that the authors interpret as shallow low-pass filters with limited spectral selectivity. The scalability mechanism builds on the anchor-based bipartite graph paradigm (Qin et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026a,b), while the high-order relational structure draws on hypergraph and tensor-based modeling (Yao et al., 2025; Pan & Kang, 2023), but the specific combination of a Kronecker-product resolvent with a compact implicit K-means reformulation is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled explanation for why first-order fusion degrades under high missing rates (limited spectral selectivity) and provides a tunable alternative with provable spectral separation properties. Its practical impact is strongest in large-scale settings: CARD scales linearly in N and completes runs on NUSWIDEOBJ (30K samples) where dense-graph baselines fail with out-of-memory errors. Open questions include whether the spectral-gap and local-basin assumptions needed for the refinement analysis hold in practice for highly imbalanced or noisy clusters, and whether the resolvent parameter α can be adapted per-view or per-cluster rather than set globally. The method's performance gains over the strongest baselines (e.g., FSIMVC-OF) are moderate on most datasets, suggesting the spectral-filtering advantage is most pronounced under severe missingness or at scale.
