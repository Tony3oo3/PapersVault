## Problem

Task-optimized neural networks are the leading in-silico models of sensory cortex, but the field lacks a unified understanding of which modeling choices—pretraining scale, neural supervision, or mapping design—drive improved brain alignment. Prior NeuroAI work is fragmented across datasets and modalities, making it difficult to separate robust scaling trends from benchmark-specific artifacts and to establish a principled resource allocation plan for next-generation brain models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261294/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes static RGB images as input, processes them through a pretrained vision backbone (CNN, ViT, or recurrent architecture), extracts features from a single committed layer per ROI, and maps them to neural responses via ridge-regularized linear readouts or cross-attention probes. Outputs are predicted neural responses (firing rates, BOLD signals, or evoked potentials) and a noise-ceiling-normalized Pearson r alignment score, evaluated across 8 datasets spanning macaque electrophysiology (V1/V2/V4/IT) and human fMRI/EEG/MEG with 135–70,000 stimuli per benchmark.

## Method

The paper systematically disentangles three scaling axes for brain alignment: (1) pretraining scale, where power-law curves S = E − A(X + 10^λ)^(−α) are fitted over 600+ models varying data, parameters, and compute, revealing saturation across all modalities; (2) neural fine-tuning, where a hybrid objective L_total = λ_img·L_img + λ_neural·L_neural optimizes backbone weights against frozen per-ROI linear readouts, yielding monotonic gains with neural sample count and positive cross-dataset/cross-modality transfer; and (3) mapping data scaling, where subsampling stimulus-response pairs shows log-linear alignment gains. The key architectural novelty is a multi-subject cross-attention probe (ATTENTION-MS) that shares a cross-attention block g_φ with M learned queries across all subjects while retaining lightweight subject/ROI-specific linear heads, reducing parameter count by ~10× relative to per-subject linear maps while matching or exceeding their alignment on most benchmarks.

## Results

Across 8 neural datasets (electrophysiology, fMRI, EEG, MEG) and 600+ models, pretraining scale (data, compute, parameters) improves brain alignment but saturates, with fMRI and electrophysiology reaching higher plateaus than M/EEG. The proposed multi-subject cross-attention readout (ATTENTION-MS) achieves the best average noise-ceiled Pearson r of 0.527 while using only 2.06×10⁷ parameters—roughly 11× fewer than per-subject linear mappings (2.30×10⁸, r=0.516). Neural fine-tuning on paired stimulus-response data yields monotonic alignment gains with no saturation at current dataset sizes, improving ViT-S mean alignment from r_NC=0.529 to 0.535 at a small task-accuracy cost (0.783→0.769). Mapping performance scales log-linearly with the number of stimulus-neural pairs used to fit the readout, with the largest absolute impact on alignment among the three scaling axes studied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three readout families: per-subject linear (LINEAR-SS), per-subject cross-attention (ATTENTION-SS), and the proposed multi-subject cross-attention (ATTENTION-MS). ATTENTION-MS outperforms LINEAR-SS on 4 of 6 benchmarks (TVSD-EP, NSD-fMRI, T-fMRI, T-EEG1) and achieves the best overall average (0.527 vs. 0.516), while LINEAR-SS retains an edge on T-EEG2 (0.447 vs. 0.434) and T-MEG (0.453 vs. 0.414). ATTENTION-MS uses an order of magnitude fewer parameters than LINEAR-SS (2.06×10⁷ vs. 2.30×10⁸), making it the most parameter-efficient option. For fine-tuning, ViT-S benefits most from neural supervision (largest alignment gains), while ConvNeXt-S and ResNet-50 show negligible or negative changes; LoRA (r=32) recovers most of the full fine-tuning gain (Δr_NC=0.0042 vs. 0.0059) at a fraction of the trainable parameters. Across architectures, pretraining scale reduces inter-family disparities: at low scale, CORnet-S outperforms ViT, but at high scale all families converge to similar alignment plateaus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-modality scaling analysis of Gokce & Schrimpf (2025), which established power-law trends between pretraining scale and macaque electrophysiology alignment, to a unified multimodal setting spanning fMRI, EEG, MEG, and electrophysiology. It adds two new scaling axes—neural fine-tuning (building on brain-tuning work by Dapello et al. 2023, St-Yves et al. 2023, Khosla et al. 2022) and mapping-data scaling (building on Banville et al. 2025 for decoding)—and introduces a multi-subject cross-attention mapping that builds on attention-based decoders (Ivanova et al. 2022; Adeli et al. 2025; Saha et al. 2025) but adds parameter sharing across subjects. The evaluation pipeline unifies the spvvs controlled scaling suite with external timm models, enabling out-of-family validation of scaling trends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By disentangling three resource axes (pretraining, neural fine-tuning, mapping data) under a single protocol across 8 datasets and 600+ models, the paper provides a concrete resource-allocation framework for the NeuroAI community: pretraining saturates, but neural fine-tuning and mapping data remain data-limited at current scales, suggesting where additional investment yields returns. The multi-subject cross-attention mapping, which matches or exceeds per-subject linear decoding with ~10× fewer parameters, is a practically adoptable technique for data-efficient brain encoding. Key limitations include reliance on static visual stimuli, abstraction of temporal structure, and a single sensory modality; open questions include whether video models, multisensory inputs, and temporally structured mappings shift the observed scaling trends, and whether joint multi-modal training of mappings can further improve sample efficiency.
