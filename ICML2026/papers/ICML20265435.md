## Problem

Existing dynamic graph neural networks embed all nodes into a single geometry (typically Euclidean or hyperbolic), which degrades representation quality when local topologies are diverse—mixing hierarchical, grid-like, and cyclic patterns—and evolve over time. Two practical barriers remain: selecting the appropriate curvature (both type and magnitude) per node per timestamp, and the high computational cost of Riemannian operations (Möbius addition, exponential/logarithmic maps) that makes them infeasible on real-world continuous-time dynamic graphs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous-time dynamic graph as a sequence of timestamped events (u_i, v_i, t_i). For each node at each timestamp, an ordered sequence of its historical first-order interactions is assembled, and the input representation is formed by concatenating node, edge, temporal, interaction (temporal walk matrix), and geometric (degree/betweenness/closeness centrality, clustering coefficients, Gromov δ-hyperbolicity, neighborhood growth rate) features into a matrix of shape L×d. The model outputs node embeddings that are scored for dynamic link prediction (AP/AUC-ROC under transductive and inductive settings with random, historical, and inductive negative sampling) and for dynamic node classification (AUC-ROC).

## Method

DyGMoCE is a two-layer Transformer that embeds a Mixture of Curvature-aware Experts into both the multi-head attention and FFN modules, where each expert operates on a κ-stereographic manifold with a distinct curvature spanning hyperbolic, Euclidean, and spherical regimes. A Geometrically Continuous Expert Router (GCRouter) replaces the standard Top-k router with a sliding-window strategy that selects a contiguous block of k neighboring experts maximizing the aggregated routing score, exploiting the geometric continuity of the ordered curvature spectrum. A ranking loss regularizes the soft routed curvature to preserve the ordering of empirical Gromov δ-hyperbolicity across nodes, with a margin hyperparameter τ for robustness. For efficiency, the paper reformulates Riemannian attention via polar decomposition (r, ρ, u) and the Riemannian law of cosines, eliminating expensive Möbius addition and scalar multiplication in both similarity scoring and value aggregation, yielding a 27.5% average speedup and 52.6% memory reduction over conventional Riemannian attention while preserving the same O(L²d + Ld²) time complexity.

## Results

On transductive dynamic link prediction (AP), DyGMoCE achieves average ranks of 1.00, 1.13, and 1.13 under random, historical, and inductive negative sampling across eight datasets. Under random sampling, it outperforms the second-best method (TPNet) by 5.2% on US Legis. (84.43 vs. 80.27), 4.4% on UN Trade (90.20 vs. 86.37), and 4.1% on UN Vote (78.24 vs. 75.16). For dynamic node classification (AUC-ROC), DyGMoCE achieves 89.19 on Wikipedia and 70.58 on Reddit, both the best among all methods. The fast Riemannian attention module yields a 27.5% average speedup and 52.6% memory reduction over conventional Riemannian attention at L=32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyGMoCE outperforms all eight baselines on average across all three negative sampling strategies. TPNet (Euclidean, temporal walk matrices) is the strongest Euclidean baseline, ranking second under random (rank 2.50) and inductive (rank 3.75) sampling, but DyGMoCE leads it by 4.7–5.2 AP points on the harder datasets (US Legis., UN Trade, UN Vote). STGN^h, the only hyperbolic baseline, ranks second under historical sampling (rank 3.25) but is outperformed by DyGMoCE by 5.8–10.1 AP points on US Legis., UN Trade, and UN Vote, confirming that a single hyperbolic geometry is insufficient. DyGFormer and RepeatMixer are competitive on Wikipedia and Reddit (where topologies are relatively homogeneous) but degrade sharply on UN Trade and UN Vote, where heterogeneous and evolving topologies dominate. TIDFormer and TAMI show high variance and poor performance on several datasets (e.g., TIDFormer drops to 50.88 AP on UN Trade under random sampling), while TGAT and GraphMixer are consistently the weakest baselines. For node classification, DyGMoCE leads RepeatMixer (the best baseline) by 0.93 and 0.84 AUC-ROC on Wikipedia and Reddit respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the κ-stereographic model (Bachmann et al., 2020) from static mixed-curvature GNNs to continuous-time dynamic graphs, and reformulates the Riemannian attention mechanism from STGN^h (Xu et al., 2024) and HGFoRmer (Yang et al., 2025) into a fast variant via polar decomposition and the Riemannian law of cosines. It departs from prior MoE-based mixed-curvature approaches (CAT, GraphMoRE) that coarsely fuse separate Transformer branches by embedding curvature-aware experts within both the attention and FFN modules of a single Transformer, and from standard Top-k routing by introducing a geometrically continuous sliding-window router with a ranking constraint tied to Gromov δ-hyperbolicity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fast Riemannian attention module (27.5% speedup, 52.6% memory reduction) is explicitly noted as broadly applicable to any Transformer with sequence inputs, potentially lowering the barrier to adopting Riemannian geometry in dynamic graph and other sequential tasks. A key open challenge acknowledged by the authors is information redundancy among curvature experts, which they plan to address via knowledge distillation. The paper also notes that extending the approach to graph foundation models is future work, and the current method's reliance on precomputed 3-hop neighborhood hyperbolicity limits its applicability to very large or sparsely connected graphs.
