## Problem

Existing federated independence testing methods, notably FUIT, aggregate client-level covariance matrices in a way that is equivalent to naively concatenating samples in feature space. Under client heterogeneity—where marginal distributions and dependence strengths vary across clients—this naive aggregation can produce spurious dependence from locally independent data or cancel genuine local dependencies, leading to uncontrolled Type I error or severe power loss without any theoretical guarantee.

## Input / Output

Each of K heterogeneous clients holds n_k paired samples (x_i, y_i) and locally computes summary statistics (sums and cross-products of 1D copula-transformed projections) that are transmitted to a central server. The method requires a train/test split per client: the aggregation weights (client subset or continuous mixing coefficients) are learned on training data, while the permutation-based p-value is computed on held-out test data to preserve valid Type I error control. The final output is a binary independence decision at significance level α, with the p-value obtained from B permutation replicates of the aggregated correlation statistic.

## Method

FedIT-CS applies a two-stage copula transformation per client: first, an empirical copula map removes client-specific marginal effects while preserving dependence structure (via Sklar's theorem); second, random Fourier features followed by CCA distill the dominant nonlinear dependence into a single canonical correlation direction, and a second copula transform re-aligns the resulting 1D projections to uniform marginals. Cross-client aggregation then reduces to summing a small set of additive summary statistics (sums and cross-products of the 1D outputs) rather than full h×h covariance matrices, which avoids eigenvalue cancellation and is naturally compatible with additive homomorphic encryption (Paillier) for privacy. A client subset selection step—either exhaustive enumeration (FedIT-CS-M) or a continuous gradient-based relaxation (FedIT-CS-ML)—chooses the aggregation weights that maximize the global correlation coefficient as a surrogate power score. Theoretical guarantees include a population-level soundness theorem (aggregated statistic is zero under H₀ and positive under H₁) and a finite-sample Type I error bound via the permutation test with sample splitting.

## Results

On three synthetic heterogeneous-client settings (Covariance, Frequency, Functional), all FedIT-CS variants consistently outperform FUIT in test power while maintaining Type I error control at the nominal α=0.05 level. On the real-world Sachs dataset (7 clients, 55 variable pairs), all FedIT-CS variants achieve tighter Type I error control while simultaneously reducing Type II error compared to FUIT. Notably, the linear-time variant FedIT-CS-ML outperforms the exhaustive FedIT-CS-M, likely due to the greater flexibility of its continuous weight space. The -F variants (trained on extra data) confirm that sample splitting reduces power, yet FedIT-CS-ML still surpasses FUIT even under this penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is FUIT (Li et al., ICLR 2024), a kernel-based federated independence test that aggregates covariance matrices in random feature space. FedIT-CS outperforms FUIT on test power across all three synthetic settings (Covariance, Frequency, Functional), with the gap widening as sample size grows. On the Sachs real-world dataset, FedIT-CS variants achieve both lower Type I error and lower Type II error than FUIT, indicating superior overall detection power under realistic distributional shift. The paper also shows that FUIT's aggregation is mathematically equivalent to naive sample concatenation in feature space, which explains its vulnerability to the eigenvalue-cancellation problem (e.g., correlations of +0.3 and −0.3 summing to zero). Among the FedIT-CS variants, the linear-time FedIT-CS-ML achieves the best practical performance, outperforming even the exhaustive FedIT-CS-M, while FedIT-CS-S (no subset selection) trades theoretical guarantees for simplicity but still beats FUIT on power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three pillars of centralized independence testing: the Kernel Independence Test (KIT, Zhang et al. 2012) for covariance-based dependence measurement, the Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi coefficient with random Fourier features (Lopez-Paz et al. 2013) for nonlinear dependence capture, and copula theory (Póczos et al. 2012) for marginal alignment. Its key departure is from FUIT (Li et al. 2024), the most recent federated independence testing method, which the authors show is equivalent to naively concatenating client samples in feature space—thereby inheriting all the pitfalls of heterogeneous aggregation. By combining copula-based marginal alignment with CCA-based intra-client dependence distillation and a stacking aggregation strategy, the paper repositions FedIT from a simple federated extension of KIT to a heterogeneity-aware framework with explicit theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in federated causal discovery by providing the first theoretically sound federated independence test that handles client heterogeneity, with provable Type I error control and a practical privacy-preserving (homomorphic encryption) implementation. The assumption of consistent dependence status across clients, while realistic for many multi-institutional settings, limits generality; extending to conditional independence testing and eliminating the sample-splitting power loss are identified as important open directions. For practitioners in federated causal inference, this provides a drop-in replacement for FUIT's independence test primitive with stronger guarantees and better power under distribution shift.
