## Problem

Standard two-sample IV estimators are inconsistent when the number of experimental conditions (instruments) grows while the sample size per condition remains fixed, due to a persistent measurement-error bias in the plug-in denominator. This paper asks whether consistent causal effect estimation is still possible in this many-environments, few-repetitions regime with unpaired data and hidden confounding, and provides both identifiability conditions and a bias-corrected estimator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263983/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two fundamentally unpaired samples: {(I_i, Y_i)}_{i=1}^n from the outcome side and {(Ĩ_j, X̃_j)}_{j=1}^{ñ} from the covariate side, where I ∈ R^m is a (possibly one-hot) instrument encoding experimental conditions and X ∈ R^d is never observed jointly with Y. The key constraint is that m can grow to infinity while n/m → r ∈ (0,∞), and d may exceed m when sparsity is assumed. The output is the causal effect vector β* ∈ R^d, its support S* (under sparsity), and asymptotically valid confidence intervals via post-selection GMM refitting.

## Method

The core insight is that the unpaired causal problem reduces to a two-sample IV moment condition Cov(I,Y) = Cov(Ĩ,X̃)β, but the naive plug-in denominator Cov(Ĩ,X̃)ᵀCov(Ĩ,X̃) is asymptotically biased in the high-dimensional instrument regime because the estimation error in Cov(Ĩ,X̃) does not vanish when ñ/m → r̃. SPLITUP removes this bias by splitting the (Ĩ, X̃) sample into K ≥ 2 independent folds and replacing the denominator with a cross-moment C_XX = (m/K(K−1)) Σ_{k≠h} Cov̂_h(Ĩ,X̃)ᵀ Cov̂_k(Ĩ,X̃), which is unbiased because the fold-specific errors are independent. For sparse β*, an ℓ1 penalty is added to the GMM objective with λ_m ≍ 1/√m, followed by post-selection refitting on the recovered support. The paper proves consistency at rate O_p(√(s*/m)) and support recovery under a restricted eigenvalue and beta-min condition, and shows that a closed-form infinite-split average of the cross-moment equals the plug-in quadratic form minus a diagonal self-inner-product correction.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is that SPLITUP is consistent as m → ∞ with n/m → r ∈ (0,∞), while standard two-sample IV estimators (TS-IV, UP-GMM) are asymptotically biased in this high-dimensional instrument regime due to a measurement-error bias in the plug-in denominator. In the sparse setting (‖β*‖₀ = s*), SPLITUP(ℓ₁) achieves the rate O_p(√(s*/m)) and recovers the true support under a beta-min condition. On the Causal Chambers Light Tunnel real-world experiment (r = 8 observations per environment), SPLITUP's estimation error decreases with m while TS-IV and UP-GMM exhibit persistent bias that does not vanish, confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SPLITUP against TS-IV (Inoue & Solon, 2010) and TS-2SLS (Angrist & Krueger, 1992) across three synthetic regimes and one real-world experiment. In the high-dimensional instrument regime (Setting 2, dense β*), SPLITUP is the only consistent estimator: TS-IV and UP-GMM converge to β*·Q/(Q + b/r̃) ≠ β*, with the bias decreasing only as n/m grows. In the sparse finite-dimensional regime (Setting 1), UP-GMM(ℓ₁) slightly outperforms SPLITUP(ℓ₁) because the bias correction is unnecessary when m is fixed. In the combined sparse + high-dimensional regime (Setting 3), SPLITUP(ℓ₁) is the only method that is both consistent and achieves the smallest error, since TS-IV suffers from both rank deficiency and measurement-error bias, while UP-GMM(ℓ₁) addresses sparsity but still inherits the high-dimensional bias. On the Causal Chambers Light Tunnel data, SPLITUP's error decreases with m while TS-IV and UP-GMM show persistent bias, though UP-GMM can underperform TS-IV in finite samples due to instability in estimating the optimal weighting matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-sample instrumental variables (TS-IV) framework of Inoue and Solon (2010) and Angrist and Krueger (1992) to the high-dimensional instrument regime where the number of environments m grows while the sample size per environment stays constant (n/m → r). It builds on the GMM estimation framework of Hansen (1982) and the ℓ₁-regularized GMM literature (Caner, 2009; Shi, 2016), but departs from prior work by identifying a measurement-error bias specific to the unpaired, many-instrument setting that is distinct from the many-instrument bias in paired IV. The cross-fold sample-splitting solution (SPLITUP) borrows the cross-fitting idea from double/debiased machine learning but applies it to the denominator of the two-sample moment condition rather than to nuisance parameter estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first consistent estimator for causal effects in the practically important regime of many experimental conditions with few repetitions per condition, a setting common in biology (Perturb-seq, drug screening) and molecular medicine where unpaired data is the norm. The identifiability results for sparse causal effects when d > m (via the restricted nullspace condition) broaden the conditions under which causal inference is possible from unpaired data. Open questions include uncertainty quantification in the high-dimensional instrument setting, extending beyond linear structural effects, and relaxing the exclusion restriction and cross-sample moment invariance assumptions that are necessary but may be hard to verify in practice.
