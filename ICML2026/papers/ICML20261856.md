## Problem

Existing waste segmentation methods for automated waste recycling rely on large backbone networks that are computationally inefficient for deployment, and their spatial-only enhancement filters capture only small neighborhood context, degrading performance on translucent, deformable, and thin elongated waste objects in cluttered scenes. The paper addresses how to achieve competitive segmentation accuracy while reducing parameter count and inference latency by exploiting complementary spatial and spectral domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261856/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a single RGB image of a cluttered waste scene, resized so the shorter side is 512 pixels (random crop to 512×512 during training), and outputs a pixel-wise segmentation mask over 4–6 waste classes (metal, cardboard, soft/rigid plastic for ZeroWaste; film, basket, cardboard, video tape, filament, trash bag for SpectralWaste). A UPerNet decoder fuses multiscale encoder features at four resolutions plus the AFEM-enhanced third-stage features to produce the final mask.

## Method

EWSegNet is a four-stage CNN encoder whose building block (EWFE layer) cascades a Spatial Context Module (5×5 group-wise conv → channel split → sigmoid/softmax-weighted feature excitation) with a Frequency Context Module (1×1 conv → Fourier transform → element-wise multiplication of two frequency-domain feature maps → inverse Fourier transform), so local and global context are captured in complementary domains without large spatial kernels. An Auxiliary Feature Enhancement Module (AFEM) is inserted after the third encoder stage: its boundary-emphasis branch applies a difference-of-Gaussian high-pass filter in the frequency domain followed by channel-weighted addition, while its blob-amplification branch uses average-pooled queries and max-pooled keys in a self-attention computation over n×n neighborhoods. The AFEM output is added back to the third-stage features before the fourth stage, injecting boundary and semantic-region cues into the deepest encoder layers. The full model is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 5e-5) for 40k iterations at batch size 8, using ImageNet-1k pre-trained encoder weights and random decoder initialization, achieving 23.3M encoder parameters and 64.8 ms latency on ZeroWaste-f.

## Results

On ZeroWaste-f, EWSegNet achieves 56.44% mIoU and 91.75% pixel accuracy with only 23.3M encoder parameters, 20.5 GFLOPs, and 64.8 ms latency — matching the SOTA COSNet (56.67% mIoU) while using 14.7% fewer parameters and 12% lower latency. On ZeroWaste-aug, EWSegNet reaches 74.10% mIoU, a 10.8-point absolute gain over the previous best LWCHNet (63.16%). On SpectralWaste, EWSegNet attains 71.03% mIoU, outperforming COSNet (69.96%) by 1.07 points while maintaining lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ZeroWaste-f, EWSegNet (56.44% mIoU) is within 0.23 points of COSNet (56.67%) but uses 4M fewer parameters and 8.8 ms less latency; it clearly outperforms FANet (54.89%), FocalNet-B (54.26%), ReCo (52.28%), and DeepLabv3+ (52.13%). Class-wise, EWSegNet surpasses COSNet on Metal (35.05% vs 29.61% IoU, +5.44 pts) and Cardboard (59.24% vs 59.13%) but trails on Rigid Plastic (33.28% vs 37.24%) and Soft Plastic (63.17% vs 65.92%). On ZeroWaste-aug, EWSegNet achieves a dramatic 10.8-point mIoU gain over LWCHNet (74.10% vs 63.16%) and 12.98 points over DDRNet-slim (61.12%), the largest margin among all compared methods. On SpectralWaste, EWSegNet (71.03% mIoU) beats COSNet (69.96%) by 1.07 points, with notable class-wise gains on Cardboard (+4.63 pts) and Trash Bag (+4.33 pts), though COSNet retains an edge on Video Tape (42.95% vs 42.05%) and Filament (69.06% vs 73.39% — actually EWSegNet wins here). EWSegNet also outperforms MiniNet-v2 (44.5%), SegFormer-B0 (48.4%), and InternImage-T (47.99%) on SpectralWaste by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the waste segmentation line initiated by FANet and COSNet (same research group), which relied on spatial-domain sharpening and boundary-enhancement filters. The key conceptual departure is a cascaded spatial-to-frequency design: the Spatial Context Module (SCM) handles local neighborhood excitation via group convolutions and channel/spatial attention, while the Frequency Context Module (FCM) captures global context through element-wise multiplication in the Fourier domain—exploiting the equivalence between spatial convolution and frequency multiplication to avoid the quadratic cost of large-kernel convolutions. The Auxiliary Feature Enhancement Module (AFEM) repurposes classical difference-of-Gaussian filtering and pooled self-attention to emphasize boundaries and blob regions, a direct transplant of classical image-processing ideas into a learned segmentation pipeline. The UPerNet decoder and MMSegmentation framework keep the overall architecture conventional, positioning the novelty squarely in the encoder's feature-extraction blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that frequency-domain operations can replace expensive large-kernel spatial convolutions for global context in segmentation, offering a practical efficiency recipe (23.3M params, 64.8 ms) that is relevant to real-time recycling hardware. However, the accuracy gains over the prior SOTA (COSNet) are marginal on ZeroWaste-f (−0.23 mIoU) and modest on SpectralWaste (+1.07 mIoU), while the large 10.8% jump on ZeroWaste-aug is partly attributable to the augmented dataset reducing class imbalance rather than purely to the architecture. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain context module generalizes to other dense-prediction tasks, whether the DoG-based boundary enhancement is truly necessary versus simpler learned filters, and whether the efficiency advantage persists under higher-resolution or multi-class settings. The paper also acknowledges persistent failure modes on thin/elongated objects and false positives in cluttered scenes.
