## Problem

Large Reasoning Models generate excessively long Chain-of-Thoughts even for trivial queries, inflating inference costs and latency. Existing adaptive reasoning methods rely on discrete mode selection (on/off switching, predefined budget tiers, or external classifiers) rather than a principled, continuous criterion for when reasoning is sufficient. The paper asks whether models can actually achieve higher accuracy with less reasoning, and how to train them to autonomously calibrate reasoning effort along a continuous spectrum.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question or problem as input and produces a structured output consisting of an optional chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory enclosed in <think>... delimiters followed by a final answer. A key design choice is that the CoT can be entirely empty for trivial problems (approximately 40.7% of training samples yield empty MSCs), in which case the model directly outputs the answer. The reasoning length is not fixed or externally controlled but is autonomously determined by the model based on its internal sufficiency assessment of the problem.

## Method

SuCo is a two-stage training framework built around the concept of Minimal Sufficient CoT (MSC): the shortest sentence-level prefix of a CoT trajectory whose geometric-mean answer-token probability exceeds a problem-adaptive threshold δ(x) = δ₀ + α·C(x), where C(x) is the percentile rank of the problem's reasoning length. Stage I (MFT) constructs MSC data from full CoT trajectories generated by a strong LRM, refines them for coherence, and performs SFT to internalize concise-yet-sufficient reasoning patterns. Stage II (SAPO) applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning with two key mechanisms: a dynamic complexity pool that EMA-updates per-question reasoning length estimates as the policy evolves (preventing stale thresholds), and a sufficiency-aware reward that penalizes both over-thinking (reasoning beyond the minimal sufficient prefix plus tolerance ε) and under-thinking (insufficient reasoning on incorrect answers), enabling continuous and autonomous reasoning allocation without discrete modes or external controllers.

## Results

On the 7B scale, SuCo achieves 72.1% average accuracy across 8 benchmarks (math, code, science), outperforming the strongest adaptive baseline LHRMs (68.6%) by 5.1% and the full-CoT baseline DeepSeek-R1-Distill (63.2%) by 14.1%. At the 1.5B scale, SuCo reaches 53.1% average accuracy vs. 50.5% for LHRMs and 45.2% for DeepSeek-R1-Distill. Critically, SuCo reduces average reasoning tokens by 75.8% (7B) and 74.1% (1.5B) compared to DeepSeek-R1-Distill, e.g., 1,267 tokens vs. 5,239 on average at 7B. On the hardest benchmark AIME 2025, SuCo attains 61.7% accuracy at 7B with only 2,679 tokens, a 24.1% accuracy gain over DeepSeek-R1-Distill while using 75.3% fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuCo consistently outperforms all six baselines across both model scales and all eight benchmarks. Against the strongest adaptive baseline LHRMs (Jiang et al., 2025), SuCo gains +5.1% average accuracy at both 1.5B (53.1% vs. 50.5%) and 7B (72.1% vs. 68.6%), while also using fewer tokens (1,483 vs. 2,055 at 1.5B; 1,267 vs. 1,891 at 7B). Compared to the full-CoT baseline DeepSeek-R1-Distill (Guo et al., 2025), SuCo improves accuracy by +17.5% (1.5B) and +14.1% (7B) while reducing token usage by ~75%. Against S-GRPO (Dai et al., 2025), which uses early-exit RL, SuCo leads by +3.9% (1.5B) and +2.7% (7B) in accuracy with substantially fewer tokens. AdaCoT (Lou et al., 2025) and AdaptThink (Zhang et al., 2025) trail SuCo by 5.3–5.9% in average accuracy, with SuCo also halving their token consumption. The gap is most pronounced on hard benchmarks: on AIME 2025 at 7B, SuCo (61.7%) surpasses S-GRPO (58.3%) and LHRMs (57.7%) by 3.4 and 4.0 points respectively, while using roughly half the tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Adaptive Large Reasoning Models (ALRMs) such as AdaCoT, AdaptThink, LHRMs, and S-GRPO, which adjust reasoning effort based on problem difficulty. The key departure is from discrete mode selection (binary on/off, predefined budget tiers, domain-level labels) to continuous, autonomous reasoning control grounded in a principled sufficiency criterion (Minimal Sufficient CoT). The training framework builds on GRPO for the RL stage and standard SFT for the alignment stage, but introduces a dynamic complexity pool and sufficiency-aware reward shaping that have no direct analogue in prior ALRM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that shorter, sufficiency-calibrated reasoning outperforms full CoT in accuracy while reducing tokens by 74–76% challenges the prevailing test-time scaling narrative and offers a practical path to deploying reasoning models in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained settings. Limitations include the dependence on ground-truth answers for MSC construction (restricting direct application to open-ended generation) and reliance on data distilled from strong LRMs. Open questions include whether sufficiency-based control transfers to agentic multi-step tasks where overthinking incurs compounding API costs, and whether the approach generalizes to open-ended evaluation without verifiable answers.
