## Problem

Deep networks built from repeated blocks (transformers, ResNets) develop strong cross-depth structure in their representations and optimization dynamics during training, yet standard optimizers treat each block's update independently. This mismatch between architectural coordination and independent per-layer updates raises the question of whether optimizer updates across depth should themselves be coupled to better exploit the emergent structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263272/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the block-wise update vectors produced by an arbitrary base optimizer (SGD, Adam, AdamW, Muon) at each training step and outputs smoothed update vectors in which each layer's update is replaced by a convex combination of its own and its immediate depth-neighbors' updates. Non-block parameters (input embeddings, output heads, normalization layers) are updated by the base optimizer without smoothing. The only hyperparameter is the smoothing strength α ∈ [0,1), and the operator introduces no additional trainable parameters or architectural changes.

## Method

Gradient Smoothing applies a structured depth-wise preconditioning operator P = S ⊗ I to the stacked block-wise update vector, where S is a symmetric, row-stochastic tridiagonal matrix implementing local window averaging across layer indices. Each layer's update is replaced by (1−α)u_ℓ + (α/2)(u_{ℓ−1}+u_{ℓ+1}), with half-weight boundary handling, so the operation is a convex combination that preserves the row-stochastic property. The approach is fully optimizer-agnostic: it composes with any base optimizer's update rule (including Muon's Newton-Schulz orthogonalization) by simply post-multiplying the update vector. Theoretically, the induced operator on first-differences of updates is a contraction with spectral radius μ⋆ = 1 − α(1 − cos(π/L)) < 1, which yields provable improvements in the alignment of consecutive residual increments and a variance-reduction guarantee for stochastic updates. Empirically, the method consistently improves convergence and generalization across LLM pretraining, RL post-training for reasoning, ViT image classification, and diffusion model training without modifying architectures, objectives, or base hyperparameters.

## Results

On RLVR fine-tuning of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Gradient Smoothing (α=0.1, Rescale, Full) improves average mathematical reasoning accuracy from 53.03% to 57.71%, with the largest gain on AIME24 (30.00% → 43.33%). On CIFAR-100 with ViT-B/4, smoothing raises top-1 accuracy from 74.56% to 75.62% (α=0.2, Directional, Proj). For U-ViT diffusion on CIFAR-10, FID@10k drops from 6.58 to 5.82 and FID@50k from 4.01 to 3.74. In nanochat LLM pretraining, smoothing accelerates validation-loss convergence and improves the DCLM CORE metric at both depth 24 and depth 30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Gradient Smoothing against the standard tuned training baseline (no smoothing) in each of four settings, without modifying any other hyperparameters. In RLVR fine-tuning, the best smoothing configuration (α=0.1, Rescale, Full) outperforms the AdamW baseline by 4.68 points in average accuracy (57.71% vs. 53.03%), with a particularly large 13.33-point gain on AIME24. On CIFAR-100 image classification, smoothing with α=0.2 (Directional, Proj) beats the AdamW baseline by 1.06 points (75.62% vs. 74.56%), and α=0.1 (Rescale, Proj) still improves by 0.88 points. For U-ViT diffusion on CIFAR-10, smoothing reduces FID@10k by 0.76 (6.58→5.82) and FID@50k by 0.27 (4.01→3.74) over the baseline. In nanochat pretraining, smoothing consistently lowers validation loss and raises the CORE metric throughout training at both depths, with the gap widening for the deeper (depth-30) model. All improvements are obtained without additional hyperparameter tuning specific to smoothing, suggesting further gains are possible with dedicated tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is motivated by a body of empirical work (Transformer Block Coupling, Residual Alignment, compression valleys, layer redundancy studies) that has documented strong cross-depth structural regularities in trained networks, but which has not been exploited at the optimization level. Standard optimizers (Adam, Muon, Shampoo) adapt updates per-parameter or per-parameter-group but treat layers independently; Gradient Smoothing introduces a complementary depth-wise axis by applying a structured preconditioner (S ⊗ I) to the stacked block-update vector. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and composable with existing adaptive optimizers, and as a structured preconditioner distinct from curvature-based methods like K-FAC, which operate on per-layer geometry rather than cross-depth correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a few lines of code, no architectural changes, no hyperparameter re-tuning, and consistent (if modest) gains across LLM pretraining, RL fine-tuning, ViT classification, and diffusion. The theoretical contribution (contraction of depth-to-depth update variation, variance reduction bound) provides a clean mechanistic explanation but is limited to one-step bounds under Lipschitz assumptions. Open questions include whether more sophisticated or adaptive coupling operators yield substantially larger gains, whether benefits grow superlinearly with depth at scale, and whether the representation-alignment effects observed at small scale persist in frontier-size models. The single-seed, small-model RLVR result (the most impressive gain) would benefit from replication at larger scale.
