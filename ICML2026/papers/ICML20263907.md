## Problem

Training robust ad-hoc teamwork agents typically requires large pre-trained partner populations or manually tuned mixture parameters, both of which scale poorly as tasks and partner diversity grow. The paper asks whether collaboration partners can be generated cheaply and adaptively—analagous to unsupervised environment design—and whether such a mechanism extends to joint partner-environment curricula in procedurally generated settings where population-based methods are difficult to scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263907/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The ego agent receives grid-state observations from the environment (7×7 LBF, 5×5 or larger Overcooked layouts) plus the partner's past five state-action pairs, which are embedded and fed into an auxiliary partner-modelling network. The output is a discrete action (6 actions in Overcooked: four directions, interact, stay; 6 in LBF: four directions, no-op, load). Partner policies are generated as stochastic mixtures of the ego policy and a Dirichlet-biased random policy, with the mixing coefficient ε sampled uniformly from [0,1] at each generation step rather than fixed.

## Method

UPD constructs an adaptive training distribution over partner behaviours through online generation and learnability-guided selection, eliminating pre-trained partner populations. A partner generator samples ε~U(0,1) and a persistent Dirichlet bias mask to produce diverse partner policies spanning a broad competence and behavioural range. Each candidate partner is scored by the variance of ego-agent returns over N rollouts (ℓ_var), and a buffer retains the top-scoring partners, which are then used for PPO training in the induced single-agent game. When a procedural level generator is available, the same mechanism extends to joint partner-environment selection (JUPD) using a coefficient-of-variation-squared (CV²) score to normalise across levels with different reward ranges. The learnability criterion also induces emergent convention-breaking: partners that violate the ego agent's current coordination conventions produce higher return variance and are preferentially selected, discouraging overfitting to a single equilibrium without explicit convention-breaking components.

## Results

On Overcooked-AI, UPD achieves an average return of 94.4±2.7 across five layouts when paired with diverse unseen partners, outperforming E3T (78.8±9.2) by +19.8%, MEP (75.3±7.8) by +25.2%, FCP (70.0±2.6) by +34.9%, and self-play (41.4±3.2) by +128%. On the Overcooked Generalisation Challenge (5×5), JUPD achieves 58.9±4.4 average return, surpassing DR-DR (49.9±3.2), SFLE3T (44.0±3.0), and CEC (23.9±9.7). In a 12-participant human-AI user study, UPD agents achieve higher returns than SP, MEP, and E3T, and are rated significantly more adaptive, more human-like, and less frustrating (Holm-Bonferroni-corrected p<0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPD outperforms all population-based baselines (FCP, MEP) and the population-free E3T baseline on average across Overcooked-AI layouts, with the largest gains on AA (+53.5 over E3T) and CC (+9.5 over E3T), while slightly underperforming E3T on CRoom (107.5 vs 111.3). Against ROTATE (Wang et al., 2025), UPD achieves a higher average return (94.4 vs 91.7) with a single shared hyperparameter configuration, whereas ROTATE requires per-layout tuning and open-ended iterations; a fine-tuned UPD variant further extends this gap to 102.3. On the Overcooked Generalisation Challenge, JUPD (the joint partner-environment extension) beats DR-DR by +9.0, SFLE3T by +14.9, and CEC by +35.0 in average return, demonstrating that the learnability-based joint curriculum is critical when levels are procedurally generated. In the human-AI study, UPD achieves higher returns than SP, MEP, and E3T and receives significantly better subjective ratings on adaptivity, human-likeness, and frustration (all p<0.05 after Holm-Bonferroni correction). On Level-Based Foraging, UPD consistently outperforms E3T across all tested fixed ε values (0.2–0.8), confirming that adaptive partner generation eliminates the need for manual mixture tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

UPD extends the E3T population-free training paradigm (Yan et al., 2023) by replacing its fixed mixture coefficient with stochastic sampling over ε ~ U(0,1) and adding a Dirichlet-based action bias mechanism, while simultaneously transplanting the learnability-driven curriculum mechanism from unsupervised environment design (SFL, Rutherford et al., 2024; Monette et al., 2025) from the level space to the partner-policy space. This positions UPD as a bridge between two previously separate literatures: AHT methods that require expensive pre-trained partner populations (FCP, MEP, ROTATE) and UED methods that adaptively select training instances. The key conceptual departure is treating each partner policy as an induced single-agent training environment whose return variance serves as a principled proxy for expected policy improvement, enabling adaptive partner selection without any pre-trained population or hand-tuned hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UPD offers a practical path to scalable AHT by eliminating the O(NC) cost of partner population pretraining while maintaining competitive or superior zero-shot coordination performance, and it extends naturally to joint partner-environment curricula in procedurally generated settings where population-based methods struggle to scale. The human-AI study provides rare direct evidence that population-free training yields partners perceived as more adaptive and less frustrating, though the small sample (n=12) limits generalisability. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to richer partner spaces (latent, generative), whether the learnability component's modest marginal gain over stochastic generation alone justifies its overhead in compute-constrained settings, and whether the method scales beyond grid-based, discrete-action environments to continuous or high-dimensional coordination tasks.
