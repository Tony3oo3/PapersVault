## Problem

Finite-time convergence analyses of TD(0) with linear function approximation typically require setting step-sizes using hard-to-estimate problem-dependent quantities such as the minimum eigenvalue of the feature covariance (ω) or the Markov chain mixing time (τ_mix), and often rely on nonstandard modifications like projections or iterate averaging. This creates a gap between theoretical guarantees and practical deployment, where such constants are unavailable and averaging doubles memory overhead. The paper asks whether a standard TD(0) algorithm can achieve optimal convergence rates for the last iterate without any knowledge of these problem-dependent parameters.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes sequential MDP transitions (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) with known linear feature vectors φ(s) ∈ R^d (bounded by ‖φ(s)‖² ≤ 1) and a discount factor γ ∈ (0,1], producing the value-function parameter vector w at the final iterate T. The step-size schedule η_t = η_0 α^t with α = (1/T)^(1/T) requires knowledge of the horizon T, which is removed via a doubling trick. The output is a last-iterate convergence guarantee (not an averaged iterate), with the regularized variant requiring no problem-dependent parameters at all—only the regularization strength λ = 1/√T, which is set from T alone.

## Method

The core mechanism is an exponential step-size schedule η_t = η_0 α^t with α = (1/T)^(1/T), originally designed for SGD on strongly convex objectives, applied to the semi-gradient TD(0) update. Under i.i.d. sampling, the analysis replaces standard strong convexity with a one-point strong-monotonicity property in the value-space ‖·‖_D-norm, showing the exponential schedule achieves the optimal bias-variance trade-off for the last iterate without requiring ω. Under Markovian sampling, a strong induction argument (adapted from Mitra 2025) simultaneously controls Markovian drift and variance terms, proving iterates remain bounded and the last iterate converges without projections. To eliminate the remaining dependence on ω in the Markovian case, a regularization term −λw is added to the TD update (borrowed from Patil et al. 2023 but repurposed here for parameter-freeness rather than constant improvement), with λ = 1/√T yielding a fully parameter-free algorithm whose convergence rate matches prior work up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical convergence guarantees for TD(0) with exponential step-sizes η_t = η_0 α^t, α = (1/T)^(1/T). Under i.i.d. sampling, the method achieves the optimal bias-variance trade-off for the last iterate: Õ(exp(-ωT) + σ²/(ω²T)) without requiring knowledge of ω. Under Markovian sampling, the regularized variant (λ = 1/√T) achieves O(exp(-ω√T/ln³(T)) + ln³(T)/(ω²T) · exp(m/ln(1/ρ))) with no problem-dependent parameters, no projection, and no iterate averaging. Empirical validation on a synthetic MDP (Figures 1–2) confirms the exponential schedule outperforms fixed and polynomially decaying step-sizes in both sampling regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bhandari et al. (2018), the proposed exponential schedule matches or improves the convergence rate while eliminating the need for ω (i.i.d. case) and removing the nonstandard projection step (Markovian case); Bhandari's best i.i.d. last-iterate rate O(σ²/(Tω)) requires knowing ω, whereas the paper's rate Õ(σ²/(ω²T)) is parameter-free at the cost of an extra log factor. Against Samsonov et al. (2024) and Patil et al. (2023), the paper achieves the same optimal bias-variance trade-off but for the last iterate rather than the averaged iterate, which is more practical since it avoids doubling the value-model memory footprint. Against Mitra (2025), the paper removes the dependence on τ_mix for step-size selection (Mitra requires both τ_mix and ω) and proves last-iterate convergence without the impractical case-splitting on step-size comparisons that Mitra's proof requires. Against Haque et al. (2024), both methods are parameter-free under Markovian sampling, but the paper's regularized TD(0) achieves last-iterate convergence (vs. Haque's average-iterate only) and provides an explicit dependence on the mixing parameters (m, ρ), whereas Haque's dependence on mixing time is not made explicit. The key trade-off is that the paper's Markovian rate carries an exp(m/ln(1/ρ)) ≈ exp(mτ) factor, which is a weaker (exponential) dependence on mixing time compared to the linear τ_mix dependence in Srikant & Ying (2019) and Mitra (2025); the authors conjecture this is an artifact of the analysis rather than inherent to the algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the exponential step-size schedule from Li et al. (2021) (originally designed for SGD on strongly convex objectives) to the TD(0) semi-gradient setting, where the contraction mechanism is one-point strong monotonicity in value-space rather than standard strong convexity. It builds on the optimization-style analysis of Bhandari et al. (2018) for progress and variance bounds, the induction technique of Mitra (2025) for controlling Markovian drift, and the regularized TD(0) update of Patil et al. (2023) — but reinterprets regularization not as a constant-improvement tool (as in Patil et al.) but as the mechanism that eliminates dependence on the feature covariance eigenvalue ω. The key departure from prior work is achieving last-iterate, parameter-free convergence without projections, data drop, or iterate averaging, which all competing methods require in some form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that practitioners no longer need to estimate the minimum eigenvalue ω or the mixing time τ_mix to set step-sizes, and can use the standard TD(0) update (plus a simple -λw regularization term) with a self-tuning exponential schedule. Two limitations temper the contribution: the variance term retains a quadratic O(1/ω²) dependence (matching all known parameter-free bounds but worse than methods that use ω directly), and the Markovian rate has an exponential dependence on mixing time (exp(m/ln(1/ρ))) that is weaker than the linear dependence in prior work — the authors conjecture this is an artifact of the analysis. Open questions include whether the extra log factors can be removed, whether unregularized parameter-free Markovian TD is achievable, and whether high-probability or kernel-based extensions are tractable.
