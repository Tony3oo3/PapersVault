## Problem

Standard Gromov-Wasserstein distance is fragile to structural noise because its global quadratic coupling propagates a single corrupted pairwise relation across the entire transport plan. Existing robust variants (UGW, PGW, RGW) inherit node-centric mass relaxation from linear optimal transport, which can discard outlier nodes but cannot selectively filter spurious edges between otherwise valid inliers without discarding useful structural information.

## Input / Output

The method takes two intra-domain distance matrices C ∈ R^{n×n} and D ∈ R^{m×m}, uniform or user-specified marginal distributions μ and ν, and two scalar noise budgets ε₁, ε₂ that cap the total L1 mass of the filters. It produces a transport plan T ∈ Π(μ,ν) with strict marginal constraints (unlike unbalanced variants), plus two entrywise mask matrices α, β ∈ R^{n×m} satisfying 0 ≤ α,β ≤ T and ‖α‖₁ ≤ ε₁, ‖β‖₁ ≤ ε₂. The masks are factorized n×m matrices rather than a full n²m² edge-pair tensor, keeping the filtering tractable while operating at the interaction level.

## Method

The Structurally Robust GW (SRGW) formulation replaces the standard quadratic coupling T_ik·T_jl with (T_ik − α_ik)(T_jl − β_jl), allowing the solver to 'mute' high-distortion edge interactions while preserving strict mass conservation. The Mask-Guided GW algorithm alternates two blocks: (1) an inexact BAPG T-step that bilinearizes the quadratic objective by splitting T into row-marginal and column-marginal variables coupled via a Bregman divergence penalty, solved with multiplicative updates and Bregman projections; and (2) a greedy knapsack solver for α and β that sorts a gain matrix in descending order and allocates the budget to the highest-gain entries until saturation. The α/β subproblems are shown to be Continuous Knapsack Problems with closed-form solutions, and the full algorithm is proven to converge to a critical point under the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz framework with explicit local rates (linear for KL exponent θ ≤ 1/2, polynomial for θ > 1/2).

## Results

On subgraph alignment (ρ=0.5), Mask-Guided GW achieves 52.1% node accuracy on BZR, 52.3% on ENZYMES, 34.1% on DHFR, and 47.5% on Fingerprint, surpassing the best baseline RGW by +9.5%, +10.7%, +11.1%, and +21.6% respectively. Under structural noise (η=0.1, Fingerprint), it reaches 60.8% edge accuracy versus 47.3% for UGW and 16.6% for RGW. Computationally, it is ~15× faster than RGW on ENZYMES (77s vs. 1142s) while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask-Guided GW outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor RGW (Kong et al., 2023), it gains +9.5% on BZR (52.1 vs. 42.6), +10.7% on ENZYMES (52.3 vs. 41.6), +11.1% on DHFR (34.1 vs. 23.0), and a striking +21.6% on Fingerprint (47.5 vs. 25.9), while also being ~15× faster on ENZYMES. UGW (Séjourné et al., 2021) is the strongest on Fingerprint node accuracy (43.3%) and edge accuracy (47.3%) among robust methods, but Mask-Guided GW still leads by +7.2% and +13.5% respectively. BAPG (Li et al., 2023), the underlying transport solver, achieves 30.6% on ENZYMES and 40.2% on Fingerprint, confirming that the dual-mask mechanism adds substantial value over the base solver. Node-centric relaxations (PGW, MD-SRGW) and standard balanced methods (FW, SpecGWL, eBPG, BPG) all trail by 10–40 points, with eBPG being both the slowest (5900s on ENZYMES) and among the least accurate (14.2%), highlighting that entropic regularization alone does not address structural noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust Gromov-Wasserstein line of work (UGW, PGW, RGW, MD-SRGW) by identifying a fundamental limitation of node-centric marginal relaxation: it cannot filter spurious edges between otherwise valid nodes. The key departure is maintaining strict transport polytope constraints T ∈ Π(μ,ν) while introducing sparse, budgeted dual masks (α, β) that operate at the edge-interaction level within the quadratic coupling term. The solver builds on the BAPG Bregman projection framework (Li et al., 2023) for the T-step and reduces the mask subproblem to a continuous knapsack problem, with convergence analyzed via the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz framework for inexact block coordinate descent (Attouch et al., 2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The node-vs-edge noise distinction is a clean conceptual contribution that explains why existing robust GW methods plateau on edge-corruption tasks, and the dual-mask mechanism provides a principled, computationally efficient remedy. However, the noise budgets ε₁, ε₂ remain user-specified hyperparameters (tied to sampling ratio in experiments), convergence is only to critical points of a nonconvex objective, and the quadratic coupling still limits scalability to large graphs. Open questions include data-driven budget selection, low-rank extensions for scalability, and whether the edge-centric filter generalizes to fused or feature-aware GW formulations.
