## Problem

Long-context LLMs are bottlenecked by KV cache size, and existing reduction methods (token eviction, merging, summarization) degrade sharply at high compaction ratios. Cartridges achieves strong latent-space compaction but requires expensive end-to-end gradient optimization (GPU-hours per context). The paper addresses the gap of achieving Cartridges-level compaction quality in latent space without any gradient-based optimization at compaction time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266340/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the full KV cache (K, V ∈ R^{T×d}) for each KV-head and a set of reference queries Q_ref ∈ R^{n×d} extracted by running the model on the context via repeat-prefill (a 'repeat the previous context' prompt) and/or self-study (synthetic Q&A). It produces a compacted cache (C_k, C_v ∈ R^{t×d}) with t < T entries plus per-token scalar attention biases β ∈ R^t that multiplicatively reweight each retained key's contribution to attention mass. The compacted cache retains a logical length T so that newly appended tokens receive correct RoPE phases, and the biases are natively supported by PyTorch SDPA and FlexAttention with negligible memory overhead.

## Method

The key insight is that attention over concatenated blocks decomposes into a mixture weighted by each block's unnormalized attention mass, so matching both the local attention output and the attention mass of the compacted block (the Attention Matching objective) is sufficient to preserve behavior under arbitrary future tokens. This objective decomposes into three closed-form subproblems solved sequentially per KV-head: (1) select compact keys C_k as a subset of original keys via highest-attention scoring under reference queries or via Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) that greedily minimizes mass-matching residual; (2) fit per-token biases β via nonnegative least squares (NNLS) to match attention mass; (3) fit compact values C_v via ordinary least squares to match attention outputs. No gradient descent is required at compaction time. The framework further supports nonuniform per-head compaction budgets (learned once per model from head-sensitivity curves via greedy exchange) and chunked compaction for long contexts, with on-policy query extraction that compacts layers sequentially to reduce distribution shift.

## Results

On QuALITY at 50× compaction (2% cache), AM-HighestAttnKeys achieves 37.3% accuracy on Qwen3-4B, compared to 24.3% for H2O+, 12.4% for KVzip, and 21.5% for KVMerger, while matching or exceeding Cartridges' accuracy at 2 orders of magnitude lower compaction time. On LongBench v2 at 100× compaction (1% cache), AM-HighestAttnKeys retains 61.7% accuracy (vs. 72.3% full context), whereas all baselines collapse toward the no-context score (H2O+: 10.6%, KVzip: 17.0%, KVMerger: 19.2%). On QASPER at 1% cache, AM-HighestAttnKeys achieves 0.338 token-level F1 on Qwen3-4B versus 0.188 for H2O+ and 0.127 for KVzip. The fastest AM variant (AM-HighestAttnKeys-fast) completes 50× compaction in seconds on a single H100, versus ~5 GPU-hours for Cartridges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cartridges (the prior state-of-the-art latent-space method), AM-OMP matches or exceeds accuracy at 50× compaction on QuALITY while being ~100× faster (seconds vs. ~5 GPU-hours per context); however, Cartridges still outperforms AM at extreme 100× compaction on LongHealth, attributed to its ability to optimize over a wider representation space. Against token-eviction baselines (H2O+, SnapKV, PyramidKV, KVzip), AM-HighestAttnKeys consistently outperforms across all compaction ratios on both QuALITY and LongHealth, with the gap widening dramatically at high compaction: at 1% cache on LongBench v2, AM achieves 61.7% vs. 10.6% (H2O+) and 17.0% (KVzip). Against KVMerger (token merging), AM outperforms at all ratios above 5×, with the advantage growing at higher compaction (e.g., 61.7% vs. 19.2% at 1% on LongBench v2). KVzip occasionally matches AM at intermediate ratios (e.g., 10% on QASPER: 0.411 vs. 0.428), which the authors attribute to KVzip's non-uniform global budget outperforming the precomputed per-head schedule at certain ratios. Summarization performs worst at high compaction, matching the no-context baseline on LongHealth, though combining summarization with AM-OMP enables ~200× total compaction with accuracy comparable to summarization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from Cartridges (Eyuboglu et al., 2025), which performs latent-space KV compaction via end-to-end gradient-based prefix-tuning on self-study data, by replacing that expensive optimization with an Attention Matching objective that decomposes into closed-form subproblems (NNLS for per-token biases, least squares for values, and OMP or heuristic selection for keys). It also extends the token-eviction/merging line (H2O, SnapKV, KVzip, KVMerger, PyramidKV) by adding attention-mass correction via per-token scalar biases and value refitting, which prior methods omit and which the paper shows are necessary to preserve the compacted block's contribution under concatenation with future tokens. The OMP key-selection component repurposes classical sparse recovery (Tropp & Gilbert, 2007) in a new context: greedily selecting a subset of original keys to best match the attention mass vector across reference queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: Attention Matching achieves Cartridges-level quality at 50× compaction in seconds-to-minutes rather than GPU-hours, making latent-space compaction feasible in real-time deployment pipelines. The nonuniform head-budget schedule is a reusable, model-specific artifact computed once, and the chunked-compaction design supports arbitrarily long contexts. Key limitations remain: at extreme (100×) compaction, Cartridges' gradient-based search over a wider representation space still wins; the method is restricted to subset selection of original keys rather than free-form compact keys; and query generation (self-study) still dominates runtime. Open directions include online mid-trajectory compaction for long-horizon agents, integration into inference engines (RadixAttention-style prefix caching), and moving beyond subset selection to directly optimized compact keys.
