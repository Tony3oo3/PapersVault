## Problem

GRPO was introduced as an empirical recipe for training LLMs with verifiable binary rewards (as in DeepSeek-R1), but the theoretical behavior of its various practical variants—differing in reward normalization (mean+variance vs. mean-only) and KL regularization target (reference model, previous iterate, or both)—remained uncharacterized. In particular, it was unclear how these choices affect the probability of success (PoS) dynamics, whether the PoS converges, and whether GRPO provably amplifies the reference model's success rate.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text prompts (math questions from GSM8K) paired with a binary verifiable reward (correctness via string matching to a gold answer) and a frozen reference policy (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct). The analysis is conducted in the probability space over policies rather than the parameter space, tracking the per-prompt probability of success p_n(q) as the key scalar quantity. The output is a sequence of policies {π_n} whose PoS obeys a closed-form recurrence; empirically, the trained policy produces text responses with reasoning chains evaluated by Pass@1 on a 1319-question test set using 50 samples per question.

## Method

The paper shows that GRPO's mean+variance whitening of binary rewards induces an adaptive weighted contrastive loss where the weights are nonlinear functions of the previous policy's PoS (ω⁺(p)=√((1-p)/p) for successes, ω⁻(p)=√(p/(1-p)) for failures). Leveraging this equivalence, it derives explicit closed-form policy recursions π_n ∝ π_anc · exp(s_{n-1}/β) for four variants: reference-KL GRPO, mirror-KL GRPO (α=0), two-KL GRPO (α∈(0,1)), and mean-only (Dr. GRPO) normalization. The PoS then satisfies a fixed-point iteration p_n = h(p_{n-1}) whose fixed point p* strictly exceeds p_ref (success amplification, Theorem 3). Mirror GRPO (α=0) yields monotone logit increments logit(p_n) = logit(p_{n-1}) + Ω_ε(p_{n-1})/β with no interior fixed points, guaranteeing p_n ↑ 1; the two-KL case introduces a Rényi divergence correction Δ_R encoding success/failure distribution mismatches between reference and previous policy. A noise model with flip probability 1−λ is analyzed, showing PoS amplification persists for λ > 1/2 but deteriorates for λ < 1/2.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical: it proves that GRPO with verifiable binary rewards induces a PoS recurrence whose fixed point strictly exceeds the reference PoS (Theorem 3, success amplification), and that Mirror GRPO (KL to previous iterate only) guarantees monotonic improvement with convergence to PoS = 1 for any non-degenerate starting point (Theorem 5). Empirically, on GSM8K (1319 test questions, Pass@1 with 50 samples per question) using Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct as reference, Mirror GRPO and Mixed Ref-Mirror GRPO variants achieve the highest success rates in the noiseless setting, with DAPO slightly outperforming all variants. Under moderate reward noise (λ=0.7), the original clipped GRPO with mean-variance normalization and reference KL is the most robust, while DAPO's performance deteriorates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed GRPO variants (Mirror GRPO, Mixed Ref-Mirror GRPO, Dr. GRPO with mean-only normalization) against three baselines: (1) original GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with PPO-style clipping and mean+variance normalization, (2) Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) with mean-only normalization, and (3) DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) which removes reference KL entirely. In the noiseless setting, Mirror GRPO and Mixed Ref-Mirror GRPO slightly outperform both original GRPO and Dr. GRPO on Pass@1, while DAPO achieves the best overall performance. Under moderate noise (λ=0.7), the ranking reverses: original GRPO with clipping and reference KL becomes the most robust, followed by Mixed Ref-Mirror with mean normalization, while DAPO deteriorates significantly. Under heavy noise (λ=0.3), all variants show performance degradation, consistent with the theoretical prediction that Φ_λ < 0 when λ < 1/2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a unified theoretical analysis of GRPO and its variants (original GRPO, Dr. GRPO, Mirror GRPO, DAPO-style reference-free training) by showing that GRPO's mean+variance reward calibration is equivalent to an adaptive weighted contrastive loss on samples from the previous policy. It builds on the GRPO formulation from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), the PPO optimization framework (Schulman et al., 2017), and the Mirror Descent Policy Optimization perspective (Tomar et al., 2021), while also incorporating the mean-only normalization of Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) and the reference-free approach of DAPO (Yu et al., 2025). The key departure from prior work is the derivation of closed-form policy recursions and probability-of-success (PoS) fixed-point dynamics for each variant, revealing that the choice of KL anchor (reference vs. previous iterate vs. both) fundamentally changes the convergence behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear theoretical map of when each GRPO variant is preferable: Mirror GRPO guarantees monotonic PoS improvement to 1, while reference-regularized GRPO converges to an interior fixed point that still exceeds the reference PoS. The equivalence between mean+variance normalization and an adaptive effective β (β_eff = β·σ) is a useful simplification that explains why variance normalization acts as a learning-rate scheduler. Open questions include incorporating clipping into the theoretical framework (the paper analyzes no-clipping variants), extending the analysis to continuous rewards, and understanding whether the PoS dynamics hold under finite-sample and finite-optimization-step conditions in practice.
