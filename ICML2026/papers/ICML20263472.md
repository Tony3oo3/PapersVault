## Problem

Current audio understanding benchmarks suffer from overly simplistic, event-level annotations, monolithic single-perspective descriptions, and heavy data redundancy (reusing AudioSet clips), while existing evaluation metrics (BLEU, FENSE) cannot reliably distinguish between generic vague captions and highly detailed accurate ones. This limits the ability to assess whether large audio-language models truly achieve fine-grained perceptual understanding or merely produce superficial outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263472/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 10-second Creative Commons-licensed audio clips from ACAV100M spanning eight domains (silence, speech, sound events, music, and all four mixed combinations). For each clip, it produces 18 reference captions (6 sub-categories × 3 synonymous variants) covering systemic, content-specific (speech/music/sound), and content-unrelated (acoustic environment) perspectives, plus 5 open-set QA pairs across 6 cognitive sub-categories (perception, analysis, reasoning). The DATE metric outputs a single score in [0,1] per sample, combining TF-IDF-weighted embedding similarity with a cross-sample discriminability rank.

## Method

MECAT's annotation pipeline first classifies each clip into one of eight domains using CED-Base, then routes it through domain-specific expert models (e.g., Whisper + Pyannote + Emotion2Vec for speech; MERT + Music2Emo + ByteSep for music; DNSMOS + NISQA + SHAART for acoustic properties), followed by DeepSeek-R1 Chain-of-Thought reasoning that synthesizes all structured outputs into fine-grained captions and QA pairs with confidence levels. A multi-stage quality control (GLAP audio-text similarity filtering with threshold 6, LLM confidence thresholding, domain consistency checks, and hallucination removal) retains only ~10% of candidates. The DATE metric applies embedding-level TF-IDF weighting to Sentence-BERT token embeddings to penalize generic terms, then computes a harmonic mean of the resulting single-sample semantic similarity and a cross-sample discriminability score (rank of correct match among all candidate-reference pairs), enabling efficient evaluation without LLM-as-judge costs.

## Results

On MECAT-Caption (DATE %), the top-performing models are Gemini-3-Pro (53.1), Qwen3-Omni-Flash-1201 (52.9), and Gemini-2.5-Flash (51.6), while traditional caption-only baselines (Pengi: 29.4, EnClap: 31.9) lag substantially. On MECAT-QA, Qwen3-Omni leads at 52.3%, narrowly ahead of Gemini-2.5-Flash (52.1%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (51.5%), with the gap between proprietary and open-weight models narrowing considerably compared to captioning. A consistent finding across both tasks is that Music and Sound performance degrades by 10–25% when moving from Pure to Mixed domains, and Content-Unrelated (environment/quality) tasks remain the weakest area for all models, with top scores below 32% on captioning and below 40% on QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 15 LALMs substantially outperform the two traditional caption-only baselines (Pengi: 29.4, EnClap: 31.9), with the gap exceeding 10 DATE points even for the weakest LALM (Phi-4-Multimodal: 30.0). Among audio-focused LALMs, Audio Flamingo 3 (40.4) and MiMo-Audio (40.1) lead, while Kimi-Audio-7B (32.8) and Baichuan-Audio (33.7) trail by ~7 points. The Omni LALM family is highly competitive: Qwen3-Omni-Flash-1201 (52.9) nearly matches the best proprietary model (Gemini-3-Pro: 53.1), and Qwen2.5-Omni 7B (42.6) surpasses all audio-focused models. On MECAT-QA, the hierarchy shifts: open-weight Qwen3-Omni (52.3) edges out Gemini-2.5-Flash (52.1) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (51.5), while Gemini-3-Pro drops to 46.0, suggesting its captioning strength does not fully transfer to structured QA. Across all models, Quality Assessment remains the most challenging QA sub-task (best: 39.3 by Qwen3-Omni), and the Pure-to-Mixed degradation in Music (e.g., Qwen3-Omni: 51.2→26.8) and Sound (e.g., Gemini-3-Pro: 55.1→29.9) reveals a persistent speech-centric bias in current architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior audio captioning and QA benchmarks (AudioCaps, Clotho, MMAU, WavCaps-QA) on two axes: data construction methodology and evaluation metric. Rather than relying on manual annotation or single-model LLM labeling, MECAT synthesizes annotations through a multi-expert pipeline (CED, Whisper, Pyannote, Emotion2Vec, MERT, DNSMOS, etc.) followed by CoT-guided LLM reasoning (DeepSeek-R1), drawing from ACAV100M to avoid the AudioSet/Clotho redundancy plaguing existing benchmarks. The DATE metric extends embedding-based evaluation (FENSE) by adding embedding-level TF-IDF weighting and a cross-sample discriminability rank, explicitly targeting the failure mode where generic captions score comparably to detailed ones. Conceptually, the paper positions the evaluation bottleneck—not model architecture—as the primary obstacle to measuring fine-grained audio understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MECAT provides the first large-scale (20k clips, 18 reference captions each, 100k+ QA pairs) fine-grained audio benchmark with extended multi-domain coverage including silence and mixed-domain combinations, filling a gap where prior benchmarks could not distinguish between a model that says 'a dog is barking' and one that specifies 'an excited bark in a park.' The DATE metric offers a practical, low-cost alternative to LLM-as-judge that empirically aligns with human preferences (90.9 vs. 49.3 mean scores). Key open questions remain: the 10-second clip limit precludes long-range temporal reasoning, residual expert-model hallucinations persist in non-speech categories, and the speech-centric bias (10–25% degradation on Music/Sound in mixed settings) and universal weakness on acoustic-property tasks (Quality Assessment <40% even for top models) point to architectural gaps that current LALMs have not addressed.
