## Problem

In safety-critical domains like healthcare and finance, event sequences are sparse, incomplete, and must obey domain-specific logical constraints (ordering, eligibility, timing). Existing neural TPPs encode history into generic statistical summaries that do not track admissible system conditions, while prior neuro-symbolic TPPs apply rules directly on observed sequences rather than maintaining a logically coherent latent state throughout generation. The paper asks how to generate realistic, constraint-satisfying, and controllable event sequences under data scarcity by integrating multi-hop symbolic reasoning into the generative dynamics themselves.

## Input / Output

The model consumes marked event histories as (time, type) pairs, a set of definite Horn-clause logic rules (oracle-provided, LLM-generated, or learned from scratch), and a fixed predicate vocabulary with anchored semantic embeddings initialized from a pretrained language model. It autoregressively produces future inter-event times (from an exponential-family distribution) and event types (from a categorical distribution) in continuous time, with the decoder conditioning exclusively on the rule-completed predicate state v^(H) ∈ [0,1]^K rather than the raw recurrent state. In zero-shot mode, pre-defined rules can be swapped in to steer generation toward novel, out-of-distribution patterns without retraining.

## Method

FC-TPP maintains two coupled latent representations: a GRU recurrent state z_i that absorbs local event evidence, and a predicate-level symbolic state v_i^(H) that encodes high-level system conditions. After each event, PredicateMatch projects z_i into initial predicate scores via cosine similarity with anchored predicate embeddings, and then H-hop ForwardChain applies differentiable soft logic rules—using softmin_τ for logical AND over rule bodies and softmax_τ for logical OR over competing rule heads—to propagate implications and produce an approximately rule-closed state. The key architectural choice is that the TPP decoder conditions only on v^(H), not on z_i, enforcing a 'reasoning-before-generation' paradigm where symbolic structure shapes every sampling step rather than appearing as a post-hoc constraint. Rule embeddings Θ_F are trainable parameters aligned to the anchored predicate set via cosine matching, making the entire forward-chaining operator differentiable and end-to-end trainable via autoregressive log-likelihood. A Symbolic Prior Bank (SPB) initializes predicate anchors and rule candidates from LLM outputs, with optional multi-round LLM refinement during training to enrich the rule set.

## Results

On the synthetic Syn@5 dataset, FC-TPP achieves the best performance across all four metrics: KL=0.42, QQ-RMSE=0.23, MMD=0.35, and RV=0.28. On the real-world MIMIC-IV dataset, FC-TPP attains DS=0.34, GPTScore=0.70, and MMD=0.48, representing relative gains of 11%, 17%, and 14% over the strongest neural baseline (GNTPP). On IKEA ASM, FC-TPP leads on all three metrics (DS=0.34, GPTScore=0.65, MMD=0.62), while on EPIC-100 it is competitive with GNTPP (MMD 0.95 vs. 1.29 in FC-TPP's favor, though GNTPP edges ahead on DS and GPTScore).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FC-TPP outperforms all four neural TPP baselines (THP, AVAE, GNTPP, UFM-TPP) on the majority of metrics across all six datasets, with the most consistent wins on synthetic data (Syn@5, Syn@10) and MIMIC-IV/IKEA ASM. The strongest baseline is LAMP (an LLM-augmented TPP), which is competitive on Syn@5 (MMD 0.43 vs. 0.35) and notably beats FC-TPP on LogiCity MMD (0.73 vs. 0.92), though FC-TPP still leads on LogiCity RV (0.31 vs. 0.47). On EPIC-100, GNTPP slightly edges FC-TPP on DS (0.37 vs. 0.43) and GPTScore (0.62 vs. 0.58), but FC-TPP achieves a substantially lower MMD (0.95 vs. 1.29). The simple LLM baselines (SP, QA) are consistently the weakest across all datasets, with SP particularly poor on MMD (e.g., 1.63 on EPIC-100, 1.38 on IKEA ASM). Overall, FC-TPP achieves the best or near-best result on 11 of 12 metric-dataset combinations, with the sole clear loss being LogiCity MMD to LAMP.

## Relation to Prior Work

FC-TPP extends the neuro-symbolic TPP line initiated by Temporal Logic Point Processes (Li et al., ICML 2020) and Neuro-Symbolic TPPs (Yang et al., ICML 2024), where logic rules directly modulate conditional intensities on observed event sequences. The key departure is architectural: rather than rules shaping intensities or explaining past events, multi-hop forward chaining becomes the latent state transition operator, producing a rule-completed predicate vector that drives a standard TPP decoder. This 'reasoning-before-generation' design contrasts with both purely neural TPPs (where hidden states are generic statistical summaries) and LLM-TPP hybrids (where the LLM acts as an external encoder without maintaining a stable, stepwise-updated internal state).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in safety-critical domains (ICU monitoring, urban simulation, activity recognition) where event data are sparse, incomplete, and must satisfy domain constraints. The rule scrambling test—showing that semantically broken rules perform worse than no rules at all—provides meaningful validation that the model genuinely exploits rule semantics rather than merely injecting structured bias. However, the approach's reliance on LLM-generated or human-specified rule priors limits its applicability to domains where such rules can be articulated, and the performance gains, while consistent, are moderate rather than transformative. Open questions include scalability to very large predicate/rule spaces and whether the fixed-hop approximation to forward chaining can be made adaptive without the gradient saturation issues the authors identify.
