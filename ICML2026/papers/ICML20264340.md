## Problem

Existing anytime-valid sequential tests protect against indefinite monitoring but are unnecessarily conservative when a hard deadline N is known in advance, while fixed-sample-size tests designed for N inflate false-alarm rates under continuous monitoring. The paper asks how betting strategies in the e-process framework should be modified under a strict deadline to maximize rejection probability by time N while preserving anytime-validity, a question that introduces nonstationarity absent from standard sequential inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of i.i.d. observations X_n in [0,1] along with a known null mean m, significance level alpha, and deadline N. At each step t, the DQN receives a 22-dimensional predictable feature vector computed solely from F_{t-1} (empirical mean gap, normalized distance-to-threshold, remaining time fraction, variance proxies, empirical Kelly and endpoint bets, and shape summaries like skewness and a Beta concentration proxy), and outputs one of three discrete actions mapped to bets in Lambda_m = [-1/(1-m), 1/m]. The resulting wealth process W_t(m) yields a stopping time tau_m and, by inverting over a fine grid in [0,1], horizon-aware confidence sequences {C_n : 1 <= n <= N} with uniform coverage guarantee.

## Method

The paper casts horizon-aware betting as a finite-horizon optimal control problem over the state (t, log W_t) with a Bellman recursion, then proves a three-regime phase diagram: Kelly betting is provably near-optimal in an interior 'on-schedule' band (Theorem 3.1), more aggressive than Kelly is better when the bettor is behind schedule (Proposition 3.4, via KL-rate-function conditions), and more defensive than Kelly is better when ahead of schedule (Proposition 3.6). Guided by this structure, a universal Double DQN (MLP 256→128, 3 actions: half-Kelly, Kelly, all-in) is trained on 500,000 synthetic episodes over log-uniform N in [100,350], uniform m in [0.01,0.99], and randomized Beta/Beta-mixture distributions, using a sparse terminal reward equal to the indicator of threshold crossing by N. The learned policy is schedule-aware (adapting as t approaches N and the log-wealth gap changes) and distribution-sensitive (reacting differently across variance and tail regimes), while anytime-validity is preserved by construction since the policy only selects predictable bets within Lambda_m. A hedging baseline over six epsilon-greedy schedules (varying onset fraction and growth trend) provides a strong interpretable alternative, but the DQN typically achieves higher deadline-limited power and tighter confidence sequences.

## Results

The DQN policy achieves the highest deadline-limited rejection probability (P(τ≤N)) across all synthetic and real-data settings evaluated. On real data (N=100, α=0.05), DQN attains the best rejection probability in 5 out of 6 settings (DNA methylation and relative humidity), while maintaining valid Type-I error calibration below α throughout. For confidence sequences at N=100, DQN produces the narrowest intervals at the deadline and remains tight in early steps across three Beta-mixture settings. The theoretical results quantify regime-specific gains: in a 'behind schedule' Bernoulli example, aggressive betting achieves P(τ≤N)≈0.13 versus 8.6×10⁻⁴ for Kelly, and in an 'ahead of schedule' example, defensive betting achieves P(τ≤N)≈0.999 versus 0.970 for Kelly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against STaR-Bets and STaR-Hoeffding (Voráček & Orabona, 2025), which are the only prior horizon-aware betting-based methods, as well as horizon-agnostic baselines including predictable empirical Kelly betting (Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2024), a Linear-ε mixed-strike heuristic, a Hedge-over-ε-schedules variant, and an All-in (λ_max) strategy. DQN consistently outperforms STaR-Bets and STaR-Hoeffding in both rejection probability by the deadline and confidence sequence width across all tested distributions (Beta, Beta-mixture, logit-normal, Bernoulli, and real data). Against the empirical Kelly baseline, DQN improves power by adapting bet aggressiveness to the schedule position (ahead/on/behind), while Kelly remains fixed. The Hedge-over-ε-schedules baseline is competitive but DQN typically performs better by leveraging richer 22-dimensional predictable features to switch state-dependently among fractional-Kelly, Kelly, and all-in actions. On real data (GSE33896 DNA methylation and NOAA USCRN humidity), DQN trained solely on synthetic Beta/Beta-mixture distributions transfers without retraining and achieves the best rejection probability in 5/6 settings while maintaining valid null calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the betting/e-process framework of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2024) and Shafer & Vovk (2019) for constructing anytime-valid tests and confidence sequences for bounded means, but reframes the problem under a hard deadline N as a finite-horizon optimal control problem over the state space (t, log W_t). It departs fundamentally from the only prior horizon-aware betting work, Voráček & Orabona (2025) STaR-Bets, by deriving a three-regime phase diagram (Kelly, aggressive, conservative) via Bellman recursion analysis rather than a target-recalculating heuristic, and by learning a universal DQN policy from synthetic experience rather than hand-designing schedule-dependent bets. The ε-greedy hedging baseline follows the e-process hedging principle of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when to deviate from Kelly betting under a finite deadline, yielding a clean phase diagram that explains the structure of optimal horizon-aware policies. The universal DQN policy's ability to transfer to real data (DNA methylation, humidity) without retraining suggests a practical path toward adaptive sequential testing in resource-constrained settings. Key limitations include the i.i.d. assumption, restriction to continuous distributions in main experiments, and reduced interpretability of the learned policy compared to hand-crafted rules. Open questions include extending to mixed or dependent data, understanding when the DQN's 3-action discretization is sufficient versus when finer action spaces help, and formalizing the phase diagram boundaries for arbitrary distributions.
