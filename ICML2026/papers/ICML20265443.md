## Problem

Existing color equivariant architectures model saturation and luminance as 1D translations on the real line, but these are inherently interval-valued quantities. This approximation requires value clipping at the boundaries, introducing spurious artifacts that make the network only approximately equivariant to saturation and luminance shifts. The paper asks whether a topological construction can eliminate this approximation and yield a network that is perfectly equivariant to all three HSL channels simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265443/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (ranging from 32×32 CIFAR to 96×96 NORB to full-resolution Camelyon17 patches), converts them to HSL color space, and lifts each pixel to a function on the 3D hypertorus group H_N × S_M × L_R. The output is a class label obtained after group pooling over the HSL group at the final layer, which produces an HSL-invariant representation. The lifting cardinality (N, M, R) is a hyperparameter that trades off equivariance resolution against filter depth, with the paper providing an entropy-based criterion for selecting the optimal order per channel.

## Method

The core contribution is a double-cover lifting layer that maps interval-valued saturation and luminance values onto the circle via π(θ) = (c/2)sin(θ), converting them from a non-group interval to a cyclic group C_M (or C_R). This eliminates the clipping artifacts of prior translation-based approaches and makes the group action a pure cyclic permutation, yielding exact equivariance. The full HSL group is the product H_N × S_M × L_R, and the network performs standard group convolution on this hypertoroidal group with a lifting layer that maps each input pixel to its orbit under all group elements. The architecture is a drop-in replacement for conventional ResNet blocks (ResNet-18/44/50 backbones), where each convolution is replaced by an HSL group convolution and filter depth is reduced to maintain matched parameter counts. The same double-cover construction generalizes beyond color to RGB channel shifts and spatial scale transformations.

## Results

T³CEN achieves near-zero saturation equivariance error (4.66×10⁻⁶) compared to LCER's 0.445, and lifting error of 6.33×10⁻⁶ vs. LCER's 8.65. On the HSL-shifted 3D Shapes dataset, T³CEN-H4S4L4 achieves 0.00% classification error versus 9.76% for LCER-H4S3 and 55.40% for ResNet44. On Small NORB luminance shift, T³CEN-L16 reduces out-of-distribution error to 11.09% (low light) and 24.32% (bright light) versus 34.83% and 36.43% for LCER-L3. On the Camelyon17 medical imaging dataset, T³CEN-S4 achieves 12.11% error, outperforming LCER-S3 (16.08%) and ResNet50 (28.91%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LCER (Yang et al., 2024), T³CEN achieves dramatically lower saturation equivariance error (4.66×10⁻⁶ vs. 0.445) and lifting error (6.33×10⁻⁶ vs. 8.65), and wins on saturation-shifted 3D Shapes (0.00% vs. 0.04% on A/C), Small NORB luminance shift (11.09% vs. 34.83% on low-light A/B), and Camelyon17 saturation (12.11% vs. 16.08%), while tying on hue-shifted 3D Shapes (both 0.00%). Against CEConv (Lengyel et al., 2023), T³CEN matches or exceeds performance on hue-shifted 3D Shapes (0.00% vs. 0.02–0.60%) and Camelyon17 (12.11% for T³CEN-S4 vs. 28.76% for CEConv-3). Against plain ResNet baselines, T³CEN consistently achieves substantially lower error on all out-of-distribution color shift tasks (e.g., 11.59% vs. 11.71% on CIFAR-10 saturation shift, 21.00% vs. 30.28% on STL-10). Against data augmentation methods (AugMix, DeepAugment, Planckian Jitter), T³CEN-S4 outperforms all on CIFAR-10 (11.59% vs. 11.80–20.39%), STL-10 (21.00% vs. 23.57–24.17%), and Oxford Pets (48.70% vs. 41.43–51.88%), though augmentation methods retain an edge on Caltech-101 (35.53% for AugMix vs. 42.24% for T³CEN-S4). On the luminance-shifted real-world benchmarks, T³CEN-L4 and T³CEN-H4L4 outperform LCER-L3 on CIFAR-10 (25.33% vs. 25.62%), STL-10 (38.01% vs. 40.13%), and Caltech-101 (50.41% vs. 49.55%), with the largest gap on Stanford Cars (55.55% for T³CEN-H4L4 vs. 66.51% for LCER-L3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the HSL color equivariant network framework of Yang et al. (2024, LCER), which modeled hue as a cyclic group but saturation and luminance as 1D translation groups requiring value clipping. The key departure is replacing the translation-group approximation with a topological double-cover that lifts the bounded interval to the circle, converting saturation and luminance into cyclic groups and enabling perfect (rather than approximate) equivariance. The group convolution machinery itself follows Cohen & Welling (2016), and the broader lineage of color equivariant architectures traces back to Lengyel et al. (2023, CEConv). The paper further generalizes the double-cover construction beyond HSL to RGB channel shifts and spatial scale, positioning it as a general tool for imposing cyclic structure on any interval-valued symmetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from approximate to perfect equivariance eliminates a class of artifacts (clipping-induced) that degraded prior color equivariant networks, and the double-cover lifting provides a principled, reusable mechanism for any interval-valued group action. However, the computational cost of GCNNs (filter orbits scale with group order) remains a significant practical barrier, and the paper acknowledges that higher group orders can reduce effective network capacity and hurt performance when color shift is minimal. Open questions include whether the redundant representations inherent in the double-cover (at most N/2+1 useful values per input) can be mitigated, whether the approach scales to larger group orders without the filter-depth tradeoff, and whether the qualitative scale-equivariance demonstration can be made quantitative on standard benchmarks.
