## Problem

Insertion-based masked diffusion models like FlexMDM generate variable-length sequences by interleaving token insertion with unmasking, but they use fixed, data-independent schedules that force the model to learn to generate every ground-truth sequence in all possible orders. For structured sequences such as graphs and molecules, some generation orders are far more natural than others (e.g., building a star-graph path from endpoints toward the junction), and spreading probability across suboptimal orders wastes capacity. No prior insertion-based diffusion model learns data-dependent generation orders while retaining a tractable, closed-form training objective.

## Input / Output

For de novo molecule generation the model starts from a completely empty sequence and grows a variable-length SAFE bracket string; for fragment-constrained tasks it conditions on a fixed fragment prefix within the SAFE string. For graph traversal, the input is a stringified graph (edge pairs in a fixed random order) concatenated with the start and terminal node identifiers, and the output is the ordered sequence of edges forming the path. Unlike fixed-length masked diffusion, positions are relative (gaps between existing tokens) rather than absolute indices, and the sequence length is not known in advance—the model inserts multiple mask tokens per gap and unmasks them in parallel.

## Method

LoFlexMDM replaces FlexMDM's fixed target schedules with learnable, example-dependent per-token insertion and unmasking rates parameterized by Kumaraswamy CDFs, which induce a distribution over generation orders and admit closed-form likelihoods and inverse-CDF sampling. The authors generalize Discrete Flow Matching to variable-length sequences via projected rate matching on an auxiliary space that tracks alignment (position tuples), deriving a Bregman-divergence training loss that jointly optimizes the generator (a DDiT transformer with insertion-rate, unmasking-rate, and token-probability heads) and the target schedule network. Gradients for the schedule parameters use a REINFORCE leave-one-out estimator over two sampled partial states, avoiding full trajectory simulation. Sampling proceeds with a tau-leaping algorithm that fires multiple Poisson-distributed insertion and unmasking events per time step, optionally with confidence-based position selection for greedy decoding.

## Results

On de novo molecule generation (SAFE dataset), LoFlexMDM achieves 79.5% quality and 99.7% validity, improving quality over FlexMDM (62.0%) by 17.5 percentage points while maintaining near-saturated validity. On fragment-constrained generation, LoFlexMDM achieves the best quality on all four tasks (linker design 51.7%, motif extension 53.6%, scaffold decoration 40.5%, superstructure generation 37.0%), with the largest gain over FlexMDM on motif extension (+6.7 pp). On the star graph traversal task, LoFlexMDM achieves 87.9% exact match on the hard setting versus only 7.4% for FlexMDM, demonstrating that learned generation orders (endpoints toward junction) dramatically reduce task difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoFlexMDM consistently outperforms FlexMDM (Kim et al., 2025a), its direct predecessor, on all evaluated tasks: de novo quality improves from 62.0% to 79.5% (+17.5 pp), and fragment-constrained quality improves by 1.1–6.7 pp across the four SAFE-GPT tasks. Against the insertion-based autoregressive model ARM (Patel et al., 2025b), LoFlexMDM achieves substantially higher quality on molecule generation (79.5% vs. 21.7% on linker design) and exact match on star graphs (87.9% vs. 23.0% on hard). Compared to the fixed-length masked diffusion model GenMol/MDM (Lee et al., 2025), LoFlexMDM improves de novo quality from 53.8% to 79.5% and validity from 96.7% to 99.7%, while also enabling fully unconstrained variable-length generation. Against the autoregressive SAFE-GPT baseline (Noutahi et al., 2024), LoFlexMDM improves quality from 54.7% to 79.5% on de novo generation. The trade-off is a modest reduction in diversity (0.850 vs. 0.890 for FlexMDM), which the authors attribute to learned structurally-informed generation orders reducing randomness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FlexMDM (Kim et al., 2025a), a variable-length masked diffusion model that grows sequences by inserting multiple mask tokens and unmasking them, by replacing its fixed, data-independent target schedules with learnable, example-dependent insertion and unmasking rates. The theoretical foundation builds on Discrete Flow Matching (Gat et al., 2024; Lipman et al., 2024) and the projected DFM framework from Edit Flows (Havasi et al., 2025), which the authors generalize to variable-length sequences with an auxiliary alignment space. The key technical departure is a tractable Kumaraswamy CDF parameterization that admits closed-form likelihoods and inverse-CDF sampling, enabling joint training of the generator and target schedules via a REINFORCE leave-one-out estimator without simulating full trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that learning data-dependent generation orders in insertion-based diffusion models yields substantial quality gains (up to 17.5% on de novo molecules) while preserving tractable training, and the learned orders are interpretable—LoFlexMDM resolves molecular structure (ring closures, fragment separators) before filling in chemistry, and generates graph paths from endpoints toward junctions. Limitations include the shared-backbone setting underperforming the separate-backbone variant (limiting scalability to larger models), persistent idle steps during generation, and the SAFE representation's permutation invariance creating large equivalence classes. Open questions include whether bootstrapping orders from the generator alone (without a separate target network) can close the gap, and whether these gains transfer to text or other long-sequence domains where the shared backbone is essential.
