## Problem

Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) accelerates autoregressive image generation but suffers from low token acceptance rates during verification, a phenomenon previously attributed to 'token ambiguity' without a clear mechanistic explanation. This paper identifies the root cause: fine-grained visual token grids (e.g., 48×48) assign each token to a tiny local patch that cannot unambiguously encode a semantic, making individual tokens interchangeable. Existing SJD variants (GSD, LANTERN) only relax verification strictness without addressing this fundamental granularity mismatch, leaving significant acceleration potential unrealized.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-in inference module that operates on top of frozen autoregressive image models (Lumina-mGPT, Emu3, LlamaGen). It takes a text prompt as conditioning input and produces a high-resolution RGB image, with no changes to the model architecture, tokenizer, or output format. The internal representation is a discrete token sequence (e.g., 2304 tokens for a 48×48 grid), and SJD-SV modifies only the verification logic during the speculative decoding loop, adding negligible overhead (~1–5 ms per iteration).

## Method

SJD-SV shifts speculative verification from individual tokens to semantic-aware token subsequences. First, it partitions the drafted token sequence into variable-length subsequences by grouping consecutive tokens whose conditional probabilities exhibit a non-decreasing trend, identifying coherent local semantic units. Second, it computes a joint acceptance probability over each subsequence rather than per-token, and adaptively truncates the uncertain prefix using a proportion threshold α (default 0.8) so that only the high-confidence suffix contributes to the product. A fallback mechanism progressively shortens the suffix if verification fails, preserving the longest valid prefix instead of rejecting the entire block. Theoretically, the paper proves that subsequence-level acceptance probability P_SV = min(1, ∏q/p) is always ≥ token-level P_SJD = ∏min(1, q/p), and that the output distribution remains identical to the target model's distribution, guaranteeing no quality degradation.

## Results

On Parti-prompt, SJD-SV reduces latency from 36.07s (SJD) to 30.48s and NFE from 1035 to 826, achieving 2.60× latency and 2.89× NFE acceleration over the Lumina-mGPT baseline while maintaining CLIP-Score of 32.13. On MS-COCO 2017, SJD-SV achieves 2.63×/2.74× acceleration with FID 30.76 and CLIP-Score 31.33. When combined with the strongest baseline LANTERN, LANTERN-SV reaches 2.86×/4.33× (Parti-prompt) and 3.75×/4.19× (MS-COCO) acceleration, representing the best overall speedup across all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SJD-SV consistently outperforms the vanilla SJD baseline on both datasets: 2.60× vs. 2.20× latency acceleration on Parti-prompt and 2.63× vs. 2.16× on MS-COCO, with NFE reductions of ~20% and ~18% respectively, while CLIP-Score and FID remain essentially unchanged. Against GSD, SJD-SV achieves comparable or better speedups (2.60× vs. 2.38× on Parti-prompt; 2.63× vs. 2.54× on MS-COCO) with slightly better quality metrics. The largest gains appear when SJD-SV is plugged into LANTERN: LANTERN-SV reaches 4.33× NFE acceleration on Parti-prompt (vs. 3.76× for LANTERN alone) and 4.19× on MS-COCO (vs. 3.63×), representing the best overall speedup in the paper. On the additional baselines Emu3 and LlamaGen (Table 7), SJD-SV further improves over SJD by 1.24× (Emu3: 4.33× vs. 2.26× latency acceleration) and marginally on LlamaGen (1.57× vs. 1.54×), confirming plug-and-play generalizability. All SV-enhanced variants maintain generation quality within 0.02–0.05 CLIP-Score and 0.01–0.03 FID of their non-SV counterparts, indicating no quality degradation from the semantic-level verification shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) and its relaxation-based variants (GSD, LANTERN), which address low token acceptance rates by loosening verification criteria. It departs from this line by identifying the root cause of token ambiguity—fine-grained visual tokens correspond to small, semantically under-specified local patches—and shifting verification from individual tokens to semantic-aware token subsequences. Rather than modifying the verification threshold, SJD-SV changes the granularity of verification itself, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing relaxation strategies as a plug-in module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means SJD-SV can be layered onto any existing SJD variant without retraining, offering a practical path to further acceleration for the growing family of discrete-token AR image generators. The theoretical guarantee (P_SV ≥ P_SJD) and distributional consistency proof provide a principled foundation rather than an ad-hoc heuristic. Open questions include whether the probability-trend-based subsequence partitioning generalizes to non-spatial token orderings (e.g., VAR's coarse-to-fine scales), whether the fixed α=0.8 threshold is optimal across architectures, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where token semantics are even more distributed.
