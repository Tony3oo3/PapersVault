## Problem

Adjoint matching (AM) and the adjoint Schrödinger bridge sampler (ASBS) are highly efficient stochastic optimal control solvers for continuous state spaces, but their extension to discrete spaces has remained unexplored due to the absence of gradients and the lack of an additive noise structure. The paper identifies that a cyclic group structure on the discrete state space is a necessary condition to recover the additive reference kernel that underpins AM, and formalizes the first principled discrete analogue of ASBS.

## Input / Output

The method takes an energy function E: [N]^D → R and inverse temperature β defining the target ν(x) ∝ exp(−βE(x)), along with a first-order oracle (the discrete score, i.e., ratios ν(x^{d←n})/ν(x) for all Hamming-distance-1 pairs) and an initial distribution μ over the discrete state space. It produces samples from ν by running a learned continuous-time Markov chain via τ-leaping, where each dimension's transition is sampled independently using the learned controller matrix Φ_t(x) ∈ R^{D×N}. The state space is a product of D categorical variables each with N states, and transitions are restricted to single-coordinate flips.

## Method

DASBS formulates discrete neural sampling as a Schrödinger bridge problem over CTMCs, derives the optimal transition rate u*_t(y,x) = (φ_t(y)/φ_t(x)) r_t(y,x), and establishes an equivalent SOC formulation with terminal cost log(φ̂_1/ν). The key structural choice is treating the state space as the cyclic group Z_N^D with a uniform reference rate r_t(y,x) = γ_t/N for Hamming-distance-1 pairs, which makes the reference kernel additive (p_{1|t}^r(y|x) = q_t(y−x)) and enables discrete adjoint matching identities. Training alternates between a controller loss (ctrl-AM) that regresses Φ_t(x)_{d,n} onto the target-score ratio φ_1(x_1^{d←·})/φ_1(x_1) using Bregman divergence, and a corrector loss (corr-AM or corr-DM) that regresses Φ̂(x_1)_{d,n} onto the corresponding boundary or transition-kernel ratio, with stop-gradient on the other network. Convergence to the optimal path measure is guaranteed by the iterative proportional fitting structure of the alternating projections, and the method avoids storing full trajectories or computing losses over entire paths, yielding significant training speedups over prior discrete samplers.

## Results

On the 24×24 Ising model, DASBS achieves EW₂ of 5.4 (β_high), 53.7 (β_critical), and 2.5 (β_low) using only 3.0k training steps and 0.2h wall-clock time on 2×A6000 GPUs, compared to 30–50k steps and 2–17h for competing methods. On the 16×16 Potts model (N=4), DASBS reaches EW₂ of 6.6, 14.6, and 1.4 across the three temperature regimes. The key advantage is training efficiency: DASBS converges in roughly 10× fewer steps than LEAPS or MDNS while maintaining competitive or superior sample quality, particularly at critical and low temperatures where UDNS and DFNS fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASBS is compared against four discrete neural samplers (LEAPS, UDNS, DFNS, MDNS) and Metropolis-Hastings (MH). Against LEAPS (the strongest uniform-based baseline), DASBS achieves substantially better EW₂ at β_critical (53.7 vs. 96.5) and β_low (2.5 vs. 176.6) on the Ising model, while LEAPS has a slight edge at β_high (3.1 vs. 5.4); on the Potts model, DASBS dominates LEAPS at β_critical (14.6 vs. 79.9) and β_low (1.4 vs. 90.5). Against MDNS (masked diffusion, the strongest overall competitor), DASBS is competitive at β_high on both models (EW₂ 5.4 vs. 0.1 on Ising; 6.6 vs. 6.4 on Potts) but MDNS retains an advantage at β_critical and β_low on the Potts model (1.9 vs. 14.6; 0.7 vs. 1.4). UDNS and DFNS fail to converge at β_critical and β_low on the Ising model, while DASBS produces meaningful samples in all regimes. The most striking result is training efficiency: DASBS requires only 3.0k steps (0.2h) versus 30–50k steps (2–17h) for all other learned methods, a 10–50× speedup, while MH (which requires no training) achieves the best absolute sample quality at β_high but degrades severely at lower temperatures (EW₂ 293.3 at β_critical).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adjoint Schrödinger bridge sampler (ASBS) and adjoint matching (AM) framework from continuous state spaces to discrete spaces modeled as continuous-time Markov chains. The key conceptual move is identifying that AM's core mechanism is state-space agnostic, requiring only an additive reference noise structure, and then showing that a cyclic group structure on the discrete state space (Z_N^D) restores this additivity. This positions DASBS as a principled discrete counterpart to ASBS, distinct from prior discrete samplers (LEAPS, MDNS, UDNS, DFNS) that rely on escorted transport or different SOC loss formulations, and from So et al. (2026) whose discrete AM attempt yielded complex training objectives due to masked transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DASBS provides the first unified theoretical framework connecting discrete Schrödinger bridge and stochastic optimal control, with a convergence guarantee via iterative proportional fitting. Practically, it achieves competitive sample quality with roughly 10× fewer training steps than LEAPS, attributed to the informative discrete score oracle and memory-efficient loss design. However, limitations remain: training instability persists at critical and low temperatures (mode barriers), evaluation is restricted to synthetic Ising/Potts benchmarks, and the first-order oracle requirement may be costly for expensive energy functions. Open directions include extending to running-cost SOC, non-uniform reference dynamics (e.g., Ehrenfest process), and general state spaces beyond product structures.
