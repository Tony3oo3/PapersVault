## Problem

Existing scalar bijections in normalizing flows face a fundamental trade-off: affine maps are smooth and analytically invertible but lack local expressivity, monotonic splines offer fine-grained local control but are only piecewise smooth (C^k for finite k) and act on bounded domains, and residual flows achieve global smoothness but require numerical root-finding for inversion. This paper resolves the trade-off by constructing bijections that are simultaneously globally C^∞, defined on all of R, analytically invertible in closed form, and expressive enough to support local deformations and global probability-mass redistribution.

## Input / Output

The bijections operate element-wise on scalar coordinates: in coupling layers they receive parameters from a conditioner network applied to passive coordinates, while in the proposed radial flows they act directly on the radial coordinate r=‖x‖ (optionally conditioned on angle via Fourier series). For density estimation the method consumes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), tabular rows (UCI), or 2D synthetic samples and outputs log-likelihood or density values; for the physics application it takes 20×20 lattice field configurations (400-dimensional) and produces improved MCMC samples with higher effective sample size. All bijections are O(1) in memory per coordinate, making them drop-in replacements with runtime comparable to splines at matched expressivity.

## Method

The paper introduces three parametric families of analytic bijections built from two construction principles: (1) algebraic rational functions h(x)=x+λ(x−γ)/(1+(x−γ)²/σ²) whose inverses reduce to Cardano's formula (cubic rational), and (2) conjugation with monotonic maps—sinh or cubic polynomials—that supports both local deformations and global shifts via additional parameters μ,ν. Expressivity is increased by stacking N independently parameterized copies into a composite bijection, which serves as a drop-in replacement for affine or spline transforms in standard coupling and autoregressive architectures. Beyond coupling flows, the paper develops radial flows: a novel architecture that applies scalar bijections to the radial coordinate while preserving angular direction, with optional angular dependence parametrized by truncated Fourier series. Radial flows use direct parametrization (no conditioner networks), enabling learning rates an order of magnitude higher than coupling flows, geometric interpretability, and—on radially structured targets—comparable quality with roughly 1000× fewer parameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, all three analytic bijection variants (cubic rational, sinh conjugation, cubic conjugation) reduce test bits-per-dimension to 3.36–3.37, improving over the published RealNVP affine baseline of 3.49 BPD by ~0.12 BPD. On the 2D spiral coupling benchmark, cubic conjugation at stack depth N=9 achieves forward D_KL ≈ 0.35, outperforming both affine (≈ 0.8) and rational-quadratic spline (≈ 0.45) baselines. On the 20×20 φ⁴ lattice field theory target (400 dimensions), analytic bijections achieve effective sample sizes of 38.5–39.7%, exceeding the spline baseline (34.3%) and affine baseline (31.9%). Radial flows match coupling-flow quality on the spiral with ~1000× fewer parameters (319 vs. ~2,311k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RealNVP affine baseline (Dinh et al., 2017), all three analytic bijection variants consistently win: on CIFAR-10 they reduce BPD from 3.49 to 3.36–3.37; on the 2D spiral coupling flow, cubic conjugation at N=9 achieves D_KL ≈ 0.35 versus the affine baseline's ≈ 0.8; and on φ⁴ lattice field theory, analytic bijections raise ESS from 31.85% to 38.5–39.7%. Against the rational-quadratic spline baseline (Durkan et al., 2019), the analytic bijections win on the 2D spiral (D_KL 0.35 vs. 0.45) and on φ⁴ (ESS 38.5–39.7% vs. 34.3%), while on UCI tabular data the spline+ hybrid (sinh + spline) matches or slightly exceeds the published RQ-NSF(C) numbers on POWER (0.64 vs. 0.64) and BSDS300 (157.57 vs. 157.54), though pure sinh is competitive but not uniformly superior across all five datasets. Against the Albergo et al. (2019) φ⁴ coupling architecture, the analytic bijections provide a clear ~7–8 percentage-point ESS improvement over the affine-only baseline, demonstrating that the smoothness and expressivity gains scale to 400-dimensional physics targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a long-standing trade-off in scalar bijections for normalizing flows: affine maps (Real NVP) are smooth and invertible but lack local expressivity, monotonic splines (Neural Spline Flows) offer local control but are only piecewise smooth on bounded domains, and residual/Gaussianization flows achieve global smoothness but require numerical inversion. The authors construct three analytic bijection families (cubic rational, sinh conjugation, cubic conjugation) that simultaneously satisfy global C^∞ smoothness, unbounded domain, closed-form invertibility, and local deformability, derived from algebraic rational functions solvable via Cardano's formula and conjugation with monotonic maps. The radial flow architecture extends the simple radial transformations of Rezende & Mohamed (2015) by replacing the single fixed map with stacked analytic bijections, multiple learnable centers, and Fourier-series angular dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The analytic bijections provide a principled, drop-in upgrade for coupling and autoregressive flows with guaranteed smoothness, validated on CIFAR-10, UCI tabular data, and 400-dimensional φ⁴ lattice field theory where they outperform affine and spline baselines. The radial flow architecture is a genuinely novel design that achieves comparable density estimation quality to coupling flows with orders of magnitude fewer parameters on geometrically structured targets, while offering geometric interpretability and superior training stability. Key limitations include radial flows being restricted to low dimensions, the modest (~0.12 bpd) CIFAR-10 gain, and the fact that hyperparameters were not extensively tuned per method. Open directions include hybrid radial-coupling architectures, spherical-harmonic angular dependence for 3D, and integration with equivariant or manifold-based flow frameworks.
