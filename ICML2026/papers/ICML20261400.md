## Problem

Obtaining a post-hoc, decomposable evidential view of uncertainty for a frozen GNN remains difficult: sampling-based methods are costly and non-decomposable, standard Evidential Deep Learning requires retraining with specialized losses, and plug-in evidential probes introduce opaque auxiliary networks. The paper asks whether a transparent, parameter-free construction can turn any converged GNN into an evidential predictor that separates epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the frozen GNN's first-layer node embeddings (H ∈ R^{N×d_H}), second-layer logits (z_i ∈ R^K), the normalized adjacency matrix, and a reference set D_ref = V_L ∪ V_val (labeled training plus validation nodes). It outputs class-wise Dirichlet concentration parameters α_i ∈ R_+^K, a scalar epistemic uncertainty score K/S_i^epi, a scalar aleatoric uncertainty score −max_c p̄_ic^new, and a rectified posterior probability p̄_i^new. Notably, the method introduces zero trainable UQ-specific parameters and requires no tuning on OOD or misclassification labels, distinguishing it from baselines like EPN that select hyperparameters on a specific OOD split.

## Method

X-EviProbe operates in three stages. First, Geometric Evidence Extraction computes L1 nearest-neighbor distances in the frozen latent space using a Laplace kernel (bandwidth = median NN L1 distance): epistemic evidence e_i^epi measures proximity to the full reference set, while aleatoric evidence e_i^alea measures proximity to error anchors (misclassified reference nodes), grounded in a Lipschitz excess-risk bound and Neural Collapse reasoning respectively. Second, Holographic Evidence Fusion constructs Dirichlet parameters as α_i = (λ_i · Σ_c softplus(z_ic)) · p̂_i + 1, where the geometric gate λ_i (set to e_i^epi or 1−e_i^alea per stream) modulates logit magnitude, which in turn scales the predicted probability direction—enforcing a Geometry→Magnitude→Direction hierarchy aligned with the model's g→l→φ inference chain. Third, Linear Evidence Strength Propagation diffuses the scalar total evidence S_i over the graph via T steps of lazy smoothing S^(k) = γS^(k−1) + (1−γ)ÂS^(k−1) with γ=0.5, incorporating structural smoothness while remaining fully traceable through the propagation matrix M_γ^T.

## Results

X-EviProbe achieves the best OOD detection AUROC across all seven benchmarks, with improvements of 4.00%–33.43% over the strongest per-dataset baseline (e.g., 94.52 on CoraML vs. 90.77 for VGNN-gnnsafe; 83.30 on PubMed vs. 62.43 for SGNN-GKDE). For misclassification detection, X-EviProbe also leads on all seven datasets with AUROC gains of +0.44% to +8.74% (e.g., 92.07 on CiteSeer vs. 85.40 for VGNN-ensemble; 83.86 on Amazon Computers vs. 77.12 for EPN). All misclassification AUROC improvements are statistically significant (p < 10⁻³) except Amazon Photo (p < 0.051). The method requires no retraining, no auxiliary networks, and no UQ-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-EviProbe outperforms all seven baseline families on every dataset for both OOD and misclassification detection. The strongest OOD baseline is VGNN-gnnsafe (Wu et al., 2023), which X-EviProbe beats by 4.00–18.61% AUROC; the gap is largest on Amazon Computers (93.35 vs. 78.70) and Amazon Photo (97.08 vs. 85.67). EPN (Yu et al., 2025), the closest post-hoc evidential competitor, shows high variance (e.g., 66.72 ± 14.48 on Amazon Computers, 79.90 ± 11.03 on Coauthor CS) due to split-specific hyperparameter tuning, while X-EviProbe remains stable (±0.24–1.96). For misclassification detection, the strongest baselines vary by dataset (EGNN on CoraML, VGNN-ensemble on CiteSeer, EPN on PubMed/Amazon Computers/Coauthor CS/Coauthor Physics), but X-EviProbe leads in all cases, with the largest margin on Amazon Computers (83.86 vs. 77.12, +8.74%). Sampling-based methods (VGNN-dropout, VGNN-ensemble) and energy-based methods (VGNN-energy) consistently underperform, confirming that multi-pass or energy heuristics are less effective than the geometry-grounded evidential construction. SGNN-GKDE (Zhao et al., 2020) and EGNN (Sensoy et al., 2018) are weakest overall, particularly on co-purchase and co-author networks where they drop below 70% AUROC for OOD detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-EviProbe extends the Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) framework of Sensoy et al. (2018) to the post-hoc, frozen-model setting for GNNs, departing from standard EDL (Zhao et al., 2020) which requires retraining with a specialized loss. It contrasts with the closest prior work, Evidential Uncertainty Probes (EPN, Yu et al., 2025), which trains auxiliary black-box networks to predict Dirichlet parameters from frozen embeddings; X-EviProbe instead constructs class-wise evidence directly from latent-space geometry (L1 nearest-neighbor distance to a reference set and to misclassified 'error anchors'), logit magnitude, and predicted probability, fused through a hierarchical gate. The graph-structural component draws on linear smoothing operators related to Personalized PageRank and energy propagation (Wu et al., 2023), but propagates only a scalar evidence strength rather than full Dirichlet vectors, keeping the method parameter-free and traceable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a transparent, parameter-free post-hoc probe can deliver state-of-the-art decomposable uncertainty (epistemic for OOD, aleatoric for misclassification) from a frozen GNN without auxiliary networks or multi-pass sampling, which is practically important for deploying pretrained GNN backbones in safety-critical settings where retraining is infeasible. Key limitations include reliance on a representative reference set (labeled + validation nodes), O(N·N_ref·d) nearest-neighbor computation that may not scale to very large graphs, and evaluation primarily on a two-layer GCN backbone across mid-sized benchmarks. Open questions include adaptive or scalable reference construction, extension to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the geometric probing principle transfers to other structured modalities beyond node classification.
