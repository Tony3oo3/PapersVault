## Problem

Latent Diffusion Models produce photorealistic images but exhibit brittle latent spaces where minor perturbations cause discontinuous semantic jumps, undermining editing, interpolation, and inversion. Existing explanations attribute this to general non-linearity but remain qualitative, failing to quantify where the manifold breaks, why editing directions diverge, or what geometric cost the model pays for high-fidelity output. This paper asks whether the instability has a measurable geometric signature that distinguishes functional curvature (encoding detail) from pathological curvature (wasted on semantic conflict).

## Input / Output

The framework is applied to pre-trained LDMs (SD3.5 Medium, FLUX.1) without retraining. For each generated sample, it consumes the latent code z, a text prompt (normal or OOD), and a small perturbation radius ε to compute a finite-difference subspace Jacobian (P ≪ E random directions). Outputs are a suite of per-sample diagnostic scores—Local Scaling, Local Complexity, PHFE, Spectral Isolation Score—plus spatial heatmaps (LC-Map, PHFE-Map) upsampled to image resolution, and for trajectory experiments, tortuosity, excess length, and extremal increment statistics over slerp paths in noise space. The OOD detection application produces a single scalar anomaly score (LC/PHFE ratio) per image with no ground-truth annotation required.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Riemannian decomposition of the generative Jacobian into capacity (Local Scaling, the log-sum of metric tensor eigenvalues measuring volume expansion) and curvature (Local Complexity, the neighborhood-averaged rotation rate of the principal eigenvector V₁). A key diagnostic probe, Projected High-Frequency Energy (PHFE), applies a Laplacian to the Jacobian-Vector Product P₁ = J_sub·V₁ to measure whether the principal direction actually encodes high-frequency image detail or merely low-frequency global shifts. The central finding is a 'Geometric Decoupling': under OOD prompts, the LC–PHFE Spearman correlation collapses from ~0.41 to ~0.08 while LS–PHFE remains stable at ~0.82, proving that extreme curvature is functionally wasted on resolving semantic conflict rather than encoding perceptible detail. The framework is further validated through Spectral Isolation Score (grounded in Davis-Kahan perturbation theory) showing dimensional collapse, interpolation trajectory analysis revealing 18% path-length inflation and heavy-tailed discontinuities under OOD, and a training-intervention experiment (SD3.5 Base vs. Turbo) demonstrating that the decoupling gap is sensitive to training regime, supporting a structural rather than correlational interpretation.

## Results

The core finding is a 'Correlation Gap': for SD3.5, the Spearman correlation ρ(LC, PHFE) drops from 0.413 (Normal) to 0.083 (OOD), while ρ(LS, PHFE) remains stable at ~0.82–0.84, confirming that curvature decouples from detail encoding under OOD stress. For the practical OOD detection application, the proposed LC/PHFE geometric efficiency ratio achieves an AUROC of 0.816 on 1,000 samples, substantially outperforming raw LC (0.427) and raw LS (0.199). Interpolation trajectory analysis shows OOD paths exhibit 18.3% greater cumulative path length, 15.0% higher tortuosity, and 21.9% greater excess length compared to Normal paths, with upper-tail increments (Δ⁰·⁹⁵) rising from 0.453 to 0.496.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic/analytical paper without traditional method-vs-method baselines; the key comparisons are internal. For OOD detection (Table 9), the proposed LC/PHFE ratio (AUROC 0.816) decisively outperforms raw LC (0.427) and raw LS (0.199), demonstrating that neither curvature nor capacity alone signals failure—only their functional relationship does. The training-intervention comparison (Table 7) shows that ADD distillation (SD3.5 Turbo) reduces the LC-PHFE correlation drop from −80% (Base) to −54%, while the LS-PHFE relationship remains statistically invariant (−1.5% vs −0.6%), providing causal evidence that decoupling is structural rather than correlational. Cross-architecture replication (Table 8) confirms the asymmetric pattern: Δ(LC-PHFE) ranges from −40% (Flux.1) to −80% (SD3.5 Base), while Δ(LS-PHFE) stays within −0.6% to −5.7% across all three architectures. The interpolation trajectory comparison (Tables 4–6) shows OOD consistently exceeds Normal on all path metrics (L, τ, E, Δ⁰·⁹⁵, Δ^max) with probability-of-increase fractions of 0.568–0.728, confirming the effect is systematic rather than sporadic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian geometric analysis of deep generative manifolds—formalized for VAEs, Normalizing Flows, and GANs by Shao et al. (2018) and Chen et al. (2019), and most directly by Humayun et al. (2024, 2025) who introduced Local Scaling (LS) and Local Complexity (LC)—into the specialized latent space of Latent Diffusion Models under Out-of-Distribution stress. The key departure is the introduction of Projected High-Frequency Energy (PHFE) as a functional probe that tests whether curvature is actually encoding perceptible detail, and the application of Davis-Kahan perturbation theory to define a Spectral Isolation Score. While prior diffusion-geometry work (Park et al., 2023; Tang & Yang, 2024) focused on trajectory curvature or adaptation, this paper is the first to diagnose the *functional* role of curvature and show it becomes non-functional under semantic conflict.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative mechanism explaining *why* LDM latent spaces are brittle: under OOD stress, the model's geometric budget (curvature) is misallocated to resolving semantic conflicts rather than encoding detail, a 'Geometric Decoupling' that was previously only observed qualitatively. The practical LC/PHFE ratio as an annotation-free OOD detector (AUROC 0.816) and the sensitivity of the decoupling gap to training interventions (ADD distillation reduces it from 80% to 54%) offer actionable signals for model developers. Key limitations include the computational cost of the matrix-free Jacobian approximation, the fact that the metric only detects *realized* OOD (not latent semantic intent), and the finite set of hand-crafted OOD prompts. Open questions include whether geometric regularization during training can suppress the decoupling, and whether curvature-adaptive sampling can mitigate traversal instability at inference time.
