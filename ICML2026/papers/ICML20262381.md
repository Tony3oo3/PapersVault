## Problem

Existing spatial audio generation methods face a fundamental quality-latency tradeoff: diffusion transformers produce high-fidelity audio but require multi-step global denoising with high first-frame latency, while autoregressive codebook-based models introduce quantization reconstruction errors. Additionally, CLIP-based video encoders used in prior work lack intrinsic acoustic awareness and their global pooling discards the geometric spatial cues needed for accurate sound-source localization in 360° content.

## Input / Output

The model accepts panoramic (360°) video at 4 fps, natural-language spatial captions, or both simultaneously, and produces four-channel First-Order Ambisonics (W, X, Y, Z) audio at 44.1 kHz. Audio is represented in a continuous latent space (d=128 per frame at 21.5 FPS) via a fine-tuned Stable Audio VAE, avoiding discrete codebook quantization. Generation is streaming: a patch of 4 latent frames is produced with a 0.21 s first-chunk latency and a 2-patch causal context window, yielding ~9.13 s total inference for a 10 s clip.

## Method

SwanSphere decouples generation into a causal autoregressive Spatial LM that plans inter-patch semantic/spatial structure (conditioned on VideoMAE features aligned via SVAC and/or FLANT5 text embeddings) and a Local Diffusion Transformer (LocDiT) that performs intra-patch bidirectional flow-matching denoising to render high-fidelity continuous FOA latents. SVAC introduces four physics-aware negative types—semantic mismatch, temporal circular shift, 3D audio rotation, and panoramic video rotation—into a symmetric InfoNCE loss to force the frozen VideoMAE/AudioMAE encoders (via learned projection layers) to encode orientation-sensitive, temporally aligned representations. A multi-objective online DPO stage then refines the generative distribution using a weighted reward combining DoA angular errors (spatial), ImageBind cross-modal similarity (semantic), and Audiobox Aesthetics perceptual distance (fidelity). Curriculum learning pre-trains LocDiT on ~1 M non-spatial samples converted to pseudo-FOA format before fine-tuning on the 165 K-clip SwanSphere spatial dataset, and an MLLM-based pipeline (Gemini 2.5 Pro + acoustic intensity-vector DoA estimation) auto-generates spatial captions for text-conditioned training.

## Results

On the SwanSphere video-to-spatial-audio test set, SwanSphere (1.09B) achieves an FD of 120.28 and KL divergence of 1.36, outperforming the strongest end-to-end baseline OmniAudio (FD 157.67, KL 1.93) by 23.7% and 29.5% respectively. In spatial accuracy, SwanSphere reduces the aggregate angular error to 1.03° (vs. 1.27° for OmniAudio and 1.59° for ViSAGe), while its streaming architecture delivers a first-chunk latency of only 0.21 s compared to 0.85 s for OmniAudio and 20.19 s for ViSAGe. Subjective MOS scores confirm the advantage: MOS-SQ 4.32 and MOS-AF 4.44, both exceeding the nearest baseline (OmniAudio: 4.12 / 4.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwanSphere outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest end-to-end spatial-audio baseline OmniAudio (1.22B), SwanSphere reduces FD by 37.39 points (120.28 vs. 157.67), KL by 0.57 (1.36 vs. 1.93), and aggregate angular error by 0.24° (1.03 vs. 1.27), while also achieving a 4× lower first-chunk latency (0.21 s vs. 0.85 s). Against ViSAGe (0.36B), the gains are even larger: FD drops by 111.89 points and angular error by 0.56°, with a ~96× speedup in initial response (0.21 s vs. 20.19 s). The cascaded baselines MMAudio+AS and Diff-Foley+AS, which rely on a separate spatialization step, trail substantially on both semantic quality (FD 261.65 and 304.03) and subjective scores (MOS-SQ 3.91 and 3.68), confirming the advantage of unified end-to-end spatial modeling. On the text-to-spatial task (Table 2), SwanSphere similarly leads OmniAudio(text) by 31.33 FD points (142.80 vs. 174.13) and 0.40 KL (1.43 vs. 1.83), and surpasses the best cascaded text-to-audio baseline Tango2+AS by 92.91 FD points. An independent SELD-based spatial evaluator (wCS) further validates the spatial advantage: SwanSphere scores 0.63 versus 0.41 for OmniAudio and 0.35 for ViSAGe.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwanSphere sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) video-to-audio generation, where prior work has progressed from two-stage mono-then-spatialize pipelines (Morgado et al., 2018) to end-to-end FOA synthesis (ViSAGe, OmniAudio), and (2) streaming/low-latency audio generation, where SoundReactor demonstrated that a causal transformer with a diffusion head can produce online stereo audio. The key departure is combining an autoregressive semantic planner with a local diffusion renderer (LocDiT) specifically for First-Order Ambisonics from panoramic video, while adding text conditioning via FLANT5 cross-attention. The SVAC contrastive strategy extends CLIP-style alignment by introducing physics-grounded negatives (3D audio rotation, panoramic video rotation, temporal shift), and the multi-objective ODPO stage generalizes Tango2's DPO to simultaneously optimize spatial accuracy, semantic alignment, and perceptual fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.21-second first-chunk latency (vs. 6.47 s for a full-sequence DiT of comparable size) makes real-time, interactive spatial audio in VR/AR and metaverse applications practically feasible, addressing a bottleneck that has kept spatial audio generation offline. The curated 165K-clip SwanSphere dataset with automated spatial captions also alleviates a data scarcity problem that has constrained the field. However, the authors acknowledge that spatial captions describe only dominant sound sources, leaving complex multi-source scenes (e.g., simultaneous instruments in a concert) poorly modeled, and generalization to unseen microphone rigs and recording environments remains an open question. Future work on multi-source spatial disentanglement and robust cross-environment transfer will be needed before the approach can handle the full complexity of real-world immersive audio.
