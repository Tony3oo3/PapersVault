## Problem

Existing neural network expressivity theory (universal approximation, depth-width tradeoffs) characterizes function complexity but ignores how the ambient topological structure of data constrains what narrow networks can compute. This paper asks: when do topological entanglements (e.g., linked class manifolds) create fundamental barriers for classification, and which architectural features—skip connections, attention, nonmonotonic activations—overcome them? By fixing width to d=3, the authors isolate the effects of activation type and depth from the confounding power of dimension expansion.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes two disjoint compact manifolds (class supports) in R^d with complementary dimensions (m+n+1=d) and a width-d network architecture, producing a topological expressivity verdict (can/cannot linearly separate). The empirical linking-detection algorithm takes two finite point clouds, projects them to R^3 via PCA, builds ε-filtered mutual k-NN spatial graphs, extracts fundamental cycle bases via spanning forests, and computes Gauss linking integrals over all basis-cycle pairs to return a linked/not-linked decision with a witness cycle pair. For CIFAR-10 experiments, inputs are 32×32 RGB images processed by width-bounded CNNs (all intermediate layers ≤3072D), producing binary or 10-class labels.

## Method

The core result is an impossibility theorem: width-d feedforward networks with coordinatewise monotonic activations (ReLU, sigmoid, tanh, etc.) cannot linearly separate linked manifolds in R^d, because invertible affine layers preserve linking number as homeomorphisms, monotonic activations preserve it via straight-line link homotopies, and rank-deficient layers force intersections when link≠0. The paper then shows three mechanisms break this constraint while preserving narrow width: (i) nonmonotonic activations (GELU, Swish, Mish) create coordinatewise folds that collapse signed coordinates, (ii) ResNet skip connections synthesize |x|=x+2·ReLU(−x) from monotonic ReLU alone, and (iii) two-token attention with distinct positional encodings produces a smoothed V-shape locally approximating |x|. A linking-detection algorithm for point clouds (k-NN graphs → spanning-forest cycle bases → midpoint-quadrature Gauss integrals) is applied to CIFAR-10 PCA-3D projections, revealing that class pairs with stronger detected linking are harder for topologically less expressive architectures to classify, with the activation gap concentrated near detected link witnesses.

## Results

On the Hopf link classification task (width-3, 30 seeds), ReLU feedforward networks are capped at ~92.8% best-run accuracy regardless of depth, while GELU reaches 100% at depths 5–12 and ResNet (ReLU + skip) reaches 100% at every depth from 3 to 8. On CIFAR-10, all three nonmonotonic activations (GELU, Mish, Swish) outperform all four monotonic activations (ReLU, LeakyReLU, ELU, SELU) by +1.2% on the topologically linked deer–dog pair, but show no such gap (+0.1%) on the unlinked frog–ship control pair. In higher dimensions (S²⊔S² in R⁵), nonmonotonic architectures maintain a 3–4 pp advantage over the best monotonic architecture at k≥10 linked copies, and width expansion to ~5d saturates near 99–100% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes a strict expressivity hierarchy across six architecture families. Invertible/flow-based models (ambient homeomorphisms) are the weakest: they cannot merge components, fill holes, or unlink. Width-d feedforward networks with monotonic activations (ReLU, sigmoid, tanh) can merge and fill but provably cannot unlink linked manifolds, confirmed empirically by the ~92.8% ceiling on the Hopf link. Three mechanisms break this barrier and are shown to be equivalent in topological power: (i) nonmonotonic activations (GELU, Swish, Mish) via coordinatewise folding, (ii) ResNet skip connections via the identity |x| = x + 2·ReLU(−x), and (iii) two-token attention in transformers approximating a local V-shape. Width expansion to d+1 eliminates the obstruction entirely via universal approximation. On CIFAR-10, the linked-pair advantage of nonmonotonic over monotonic activations (+1.2%) disappears when skip connections are added, confirming the theoretical equivalence of ResNet and nonmonotonic feedforward networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant topological-data-analysis line (Carlsson, Naitzat et al.) by studying extrinsic invariants—how manifolds are embedded in ambient space—rather than intrinsic ones like Betti numbers, and from universal-approximation/depth-width literature (Hanin & Sellke, Telgarsky) by fixing width to isolate architectural effects from dimension. It repurposes classical low-dimensional topology tools (Gauss linking integrals, link homotopy, ambient homeomorphism invariance) as a lens for comparing MLPs, ResNets, and transformers at fixed width, a framing the authors explicitly position as absent from prior expressivity work. The width-(d+1) universal-approximation upper bound and the width-d impossibility lower bound together close a gap that prior minimum-width results (Park et al., Cai, Li et al.) addressed only through approximation-theoretic arguments rather than ambient topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a topological rationale for the empirical observation that skip connections, attention, and nonmonotonic activations all outperform plain monotonic feedforward networks: they all implement the same 'folding' operation that breaks linking-number preservation. This gives practitioners concrete design guidance for bottleneck layers (use GELU/Swish, add skips, or leverage attention rather than relying on width expansion). Limitations include the restriction to pairwise linking number (richer invariants deferred to a sequel), the correlational and PCA-3D-projected nature of the CIFAR-10 evidence, and the fact that the theory addresses expressivity rather than optimization dynamics. Open questions include whether the task correspondence between unlinking and classification generalizes beyond binary classification, and whether the linking-detection algorithm scales to higher-dimensional native data manifolds without dimensionality reduction.
