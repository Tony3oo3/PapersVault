## Problem

Existing spectral prediction theories for directed actions (grid-cell models, successor representations) implicitly assume flat action geometry where translations commute and a single Fourier eigenbasis simultaneously diagonalizes all action operators. This assumption breaks in the presence of path-dependent effects—circulation, rotational drift, or topological loops—whose defining signature is nontrivial holonomy, making the relevant algebra noncommutative and scalar diagonalization impossible. The paper asks: what is the correct spectral structure when directed actions compose path-dependently, and what are the minimal representational ingredients required for reusable prediction in such curved action geometries?

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete torus state space G = Z_N^d, a family of action kernels k_a (probability distributions over displacements), a U(1)-valued 2-cocycle σ encoding the holonomy class, a translation-homogeneous stationary policy π, and a discount factor γ. It produces a universal block-diagonalization of all action operators and the successor resolvent via a twisted Fourier transform, where each frequency block is a small d_ρ × d_ρ matrix (with d_ρ determined by the holonomy order) rather than a scalar eigenvalue. The SR computation reduces to inverting these small matrices independently per block, and the holonomy class [σ] serves as a gauge-invariant transfer invariant: environments sharing [σ] admit identical predictive structure up to a local phase reparameterization, while differing classes provably require representational changes of dimension at least q (the flux denominator).

## Method

The core contribution is a twisted harmonic analysis framework that replaces scalar Fourier diagonalization with matrix-valued block spectral decomposition. Directed actions are modeled as a projective (phase-twisted) representation of translations satisfying U_g U_h = σ(g,h) U_{g+h}, where σ is a U(1)-valued 2-cocycle whose commutator bicharacter encodes plaquette holonomy. The twisted translation algebra A_σ is shown to admit a universal block basis formed by matrix coefficients of irreducible σ-projective representations (the twisted Fourier transform), which simultaneously block-diagonalizes every action operator: within each block indexed by an irrep ρ, the operator acts by left multiplication with a small matrix-valued multiplier k̂_a(ρ). This yields closed-form SR blocks (I − γ k̂_π(ρ))^{-1}, a gauge-invariant transfer theorem (same holonomy class → unitary conjugacy of all propagators and resolvents), and a curvature-induced lower bound showing that a primitive commutator phase of order q forces every irreducible block to have dimension at least q. In the constant-flux case, the blocks are explicitly q×q Harper matrices indexed by a reduced Brillouin zone, connecting the theory to magnetic Bloch waves and the Hofstadter butterfly.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The headline results are: (1) Theorem 5.2 proves that the twisted Fourier transform block-diagonalizes all action operators in the holonomy MDP, reducing long-horizon prediction to independent d_ρ × d_ρ matrix inversions per block; (2) Theorem 6.4 establishes that a primitive q-th root-of-unity holonomy forces every irreducible block to have dimension at least q, providing a curvature-induced lower bound on representational dimension; (3) Theorem 6.2 shows that environments sharing the same holonomy class [σ] admit exact basis reuse up to a gauge conjugacy, while Theorem 6.3 proves that nontrivial holonomy obstructs any scalar eigenbasis (Definition 2.1). In the constant-flux model (Corollary 5.5), all blocks are uniformly q×q and the SR computation cost drops from O(|G|³) to O(|G|·q³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no empirical baselines or head-to-head comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. Its closest 'comparison' is to the flat translation-invariant directed-action framework of Yu et al. (2021), which achieves exact scalar eigenbasis reuse (Definition 2.1) via ordinary Fourier characters. The present paper shows this mechanism necessarily fails under nontrivial holonomy (Theorem 6.3) and provides the correct replacement: a twisted block decomposition (Theorem 5.2) that generalizes the Yu et al. result as the σ≡1 special case. The paper also positions itself relative to magnetic-Laplacian methods for directed graphs (Fanuel et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2021), noting that those encode directionality in a single graph operator, whereas here holonomy governs a controlled family of action operators and the successor resolvent simultaneously. No method is shown to 'win' or 'lose' in an empirical sense; the contribution is a structural impossibility/possibility theorem pair (scalar reuse impossible, block reuse possible) with explicit dimension bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the directed-action grid-cell prediction framework of Yu et al. (2021), which showed that a single Fourier eigenbasis supports prediction across many directed actions on flat translation-invariant grids, to the case where action composition around cycles incurs nontrivial phase (holonomy). It draws on the successor representation resolvent formalism of Dayan (1993) and Gershman (2018) as the core prediction object, and borrows the algebraic machinery of twisted group algebras and projective representations (Karpilovsky, 1985; Terras, 1999) to construct the block-diagonalizing transform. The connection to magnetic Laplacian methods for directed graphs (Fanuel et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2021) is acknowledged but positioned as complementary: here holonomy governs a controlled family of operators and multi-step prediction rather than a single graph Laplacian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric criterion—holonomy class—for when reusable spectral prediction codes exist, when they must change form, and what minimal internal dimension they require, which could guide architecture design for navigation and planning agents in environments with drift, circulation, or topological loops. However, all results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the assumptions (translation-homogeneous actions, abelian base group, stationary policies) are restrictive; extending to state-dependent policies, non-abelian motion groups like SE(2), and learning holonomy classes from data are explicitly flagged as open. The practical impact depends on whether real-world environments exhibit the kind of structured, gauge-invariant cycle phases the theory assumes.
