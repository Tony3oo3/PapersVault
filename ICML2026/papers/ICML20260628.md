## Problem

Many margin-based preference optimization objectives (DPO, IPO, CPO) entangle the updates of chosen and rejected responses, so suppressing the loser also drags down the winner, causing likelihood displacement and gradient entanglement. While newer density-ratio objectives (DIL, DDRO, BPO) often avoid this, there is no unified framework explaining when and why different objectives produce entangled versus disentangled likelihood dynamics, nor a general, objective-agnostic way to steer training toward the desired behavior of suppressing the loser while preserving the winner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard preference triplet (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) together with any existing preference objective (DPO, CPO, IPO, DIL-BCE, DDRO, etc.) and produces calibrated likelihood gradients that promote Pathway (iii) dynamics. The forward pass is unchanged (z_rc = z), but the backward pass rescales gradients by factors derived from the score-norm ratio and the incentive ratio. In practice, score norms are approximated using output-layer gradients (the LM head in full fine-tuning, or the final LoRA/QLoRA adapter layers) to avoid a full backward pass, and log-domain exponential moving averages stabilize the ratio estimates under stochastic minibatch training with batch size 1.

## Method

The core contribution is an incentive-score decomposition showing that all preference objectives, whether entangled or disentangled, share the same two update directions (score vectors s_w, s_l) and differ only in scalar incentive coefficients (d_w, d_l) that weight them. From this unified form, the paper derives continuous-time likelihood dynamics (Theorem 3.1) revealing a self-reinforcement term and a coupling drag term, then identifies the Disentanglement Band (DB): a testable interval on the incentive ratio r_t = d_w/d_l, bounded by ρ_t·||s_l||/||s_w|| and (1/ρ_t)·||s_l||/||s_w||, that guarantees Pathway (iii) (suppress loser, preserve winner). The robust center of the DB is log r_t* = log(||s_l||/||s_w||), which balances score vector norms and maximizes tolerance to multiplicative fluctuations. Reward Calibration (RC) is a plug-and-play wrapper that adaptively rescales the chosen and rejected likelihoods via a stop-gradient reparameterization so that the realized incentive ratio tracks the DB center, without modifying the base objective, the optimizer, or the forward pass.

## Results

On Mistral-7B, RC delivers the largest gains for objectives with entangled or unstable dynamics: DPO improves average benchmark score from 24.26 to 36.44 (+12.18), with MMLU-Pro jumping from 11.66 to 30.31 and ARC from 22.70 to 60.92; DDRO recovers from 16.53 to 36.34 (+19.81). For already-strong disentangled baselines (SimPO, BCE, LSIF, TI-DPO), RC is non-disruptive with average changes within ±0.15. On Qwen2.5-7B, gains are more modest (DPO +0.48, BCE +1.33, SimPO +0.26) but consistently non-negative except a tiny DDRO drop (−0.05). On Pythia-2.8B, DPO improves ARC by +3.08 and SimPO by +4.44, while the EMA momentum ablation shows RC is robust across momenta 0.5–0.999.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RC is evaluated as a plug-and-play wrapper applied on top of six base objectives (DPO, SimPO, CPO, BCE, DDRO, LSIF, TI-DPO), so the comparison is each objective with vs. without RC. The largest wins are on entangled or unstable baselines: DPO on Mistral-7B gains +12.18 average (ARC 22.70→60.92, MMLU-Pro 11.66→30.31), and DDRO recovers +19.81 from a near-failed run (MATH/GSM8K going from 0 to ~38). CPO shows a moderate +0.39 improvement, consistent with its tendency to drift toward Pathway (i). For already-disentangled and stable baselines (SimPO, BCE, LSIF, TI-DPO), RC is essentially non-disruptive, with average changes between −0.05 and +0.15, confirming it does not harm objectives that already exhibit Pathway (iii) dynamics. On Qwen2.5-7B the pattern holds with smaller magnitudes (DPO +0.48, BCE +1.33), and on Pythia-2.8B the clearest gains appear on ARC (DPO +3.08, SimPO +4.44). Overall, RC consistently wins or ties across all six baselines, with the magnitude of improvement tracking the degree of entanglement in the uncalibrated run.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Yuan et al. (2025), who identified gradient entanglement in margin-based objectives like DPO, and on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) that spawned the current zoo of preference objectives. The key departure is the incentive-score decomposition, which shows that both entangled (DPO, IPO, CPO, SimPO) and disentangled (DIL, DDRO, KTO) objectives share identical score directions and differ only in two scalar incentive coefficients, unifying analyses that were previously conducted in separate settings. Rather than proposing yet another objective, the paper derives a continuous-time dynamical condition (the disentanglement band) and a plug-and-play gradient rescaling (reward calibration) that can be applied to any existing objective without redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement band provides a simple, locally testable diagnostic for practitioners to understand why a given objective is or isn't achieving the desired 'suppress loser, preserve winner' behavior, and RC offers a lightweight intervention that requires no changes to the base objective or optimizer. The finding that RC is most beneficial for entangled or unstable baselines (e.g., DPO +12.18, DDRO +19.81 on Mistral-7B) while being non-disruptive for already-strong disentangled objectives suggests it functions as a stabilizer rather than a universal booster. Open questions include extending the framework to iterative/online preference optimization, multi-response settings, and whether the first-order continuous-time analysis captures higher-order effects in practice.
