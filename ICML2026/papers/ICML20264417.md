## Problem

Current Visual Document Retrieval (VDR) models are built by repurposing large generative vision-language decoders (causal attention) as embedding models via post-hoc contrastive fine-tuning, yet it remains unclear whether this design is optimal. The paper identifies that causal attention fundamentally limits multi-vector late-interaction retrieval and that no prior work has systematically studied which architectural and training choices (attention masking, modality alignment objectives, data regimes) actually govern VDR performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264417/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text query and a document page image as input. Images are split into 512×512 pixel tiles processed by the SigLIP2 vision encoder, with a downscaled full-image version appended for global context; each tile yields 1024 patch embeddings compressed to 64 tokens via pixel shuffling (ratio r=4). For the multi-vector variant (ColModernVBERT), the output is a set of token-level embeddings matched against document tokens via MaxSim late interaction; the single-vector variant (BiModernVBERT) produces a single mean-pooled embedding. The entire 250M-parameter model runs on consumer CPUs with ~20 ms query encoding latency.

## Method

ModernVBERT is a 250M-parameter early-fusion encoder combining a 150M ModernBERT bidirectional text encoder with a 100M SigLIP2 vision encoder, trained in two phases: (1) modality alignment via Masked Language Modeling on 10B tokens (with a 2048px high-resolution cooldown stage) using LoRA adapters, and (2) contrastive post-training with InfoNCE loss on document-query pairs interleaved with text-only pairs at a 2:1 ratio. The central finding is that bidirectional attention is essential for late-interaction retrieval, yielding +10.6 nDCG@5 over causal decoders in multi-vector settings, while single-vector performance is comparable. This synergy between bidirectional attention and MaxSim matching is what allows the 250M ColModernVBERT to match ColPali (2.92B) on ViDoRe (68.6 vs. 69.2 avg nDCG@5) while achieving 7× faster CPU inference.

## Results

ColModernVBERT, a 250M-parameter multi-vector visual document retriever, achieves an average nDCG@5 of 68.6 on the ViDoRe benchmark suite (81.2 on v1, 56.0 on v2 English), matching ColPali (2.92B, 69.2) within 0.6 points while being 11.7× smaller. On CPU, ColModernVBERT encodes queries in 20 ms versus 155–175 ms for comparable-performing 3–4B models, a >7× speedup. The single-vector variant BiModernVBERT scores 49.7 average, still outperforming all other sub-1B models. On the NanoBEIR text retrieval benchmark, ColModernVBERT achieves 0.589 NDCG@5, competitive with dedicated text retrievers of similar size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColModernVBERT (68.6 avg) nearly matches ColPali (69.2, 2.92B) at 11.7× fewer parameters, trailing by only 0.6 nDCG@5, and decisively beats the closest sub-1B competitor ColFlor (55.9) by 12.7 points. It outperforms all single-vector repurposed VLMs including MoCa-3B (66.9), E5-V (56.1), and VLM2Vec (43.1), as well as all dual-encoder baselines (SigLIP2-FT Large 35.4, BGE Visualized M3 11.3, Jina CLIP 15.8). It falls short of the largest late-interaction models—NemoRetriever-3B (78.7), ColQwen2.5 (75.5), Jina-v4 (75.2), and GME-Qwen2 (75.8)—which are 3–8× larger and trained on billions of tokens. On text-only retrieval (NanoBEIR), ColModernVBERT (0.589) beats Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (0.567) and BGE-M3 (0.590/0.606) but trails the dedicated multi-vector GTE-ModernColBERT (0.669). The key differentiator is latency: at 20 ms CPU query encoding, ColModernVBERT is 7–21× faster than the 3–4B late-interaction models (155–175 ms) that achieve similar or modestly higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VDR paradigm of repurposing large generative VLMs (causal decoders like PaliGemma, Qwen2-VL) as retrieval embedders via post-hoc contrastive fine-tuning (ColPali, ColQwen2.5, E5-V, VLM2Vec). Instead, it builds a retrieval-specific encoder from the ground up by combining the ModernBERT bidirectional text encoder (Warner et al., 2024a; Weller et al., 2025) with the SigLIP2 vision encoder, drawing on the text-only retrieval finding that bidirectional attention outperforms causal attention (Gisserot-Boukhlef et al., 2025). The late-interaction MaxSim scoring is inherited from ColBERT (Khattab & Zaharia, 2020) and the ColPali VDR formulation (Faysse et al., 2025), but the architectural choice of a natively bidirectional encoder is the key departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that bidirectional attention is essential for late-interaction retrieval (+10.6 nDCG@5 over causal counterparts under identical conditions) challenges the assumption that repurposing generative VLMs is the optimal path for VDR, and suggests that most current SOTA visual retrievers are leaving substantial performance on the table. The practical impact is significant: a 250M-parameter model matches ColPali (2.9B) within 0.6 nDCG@5 while achieving 7× faster CPU inference, making visual document retrieval viable in resource-constrained industrial deployments. Open questions include whether the bidirectional–late-interaction synergy persists at larger scales, how multilingual text backbones trade off against English retrieval quality, and whether the encoder design generalizes to token-level tasks beyond retrieval.
