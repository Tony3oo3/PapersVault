## Problem

State-of-the-art differentially private synthetic tabular data methods rely on answering low-order marginal queries, whose count grows exponentially with correlation order, making them fundamentally unable to capture high-order dependencies. The paper demonstrates via XOR stress tests that SOTA methods (AIM, PrivGSD, GEM, etc.) collapse to random-guess performance when full-order correlations are required, yet standard benchmarks (Adult, Bank, Census) are dominated by low-order dependencies, masking this limitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a private tabular dataset containing categorical and numerical features along with class labels, and produces a synthetic tabular dataset of configurable size (typically 10–20% of the original) that satisfies (ε,δ)-DP. It operates per-class, preserving the original class distribution, and requires numerical feature bounds (assumed known or DP-estimated). The synthetic samples are scored against the private data using a mixed-type distance metric that balances categorical mismatches (weighted by λ) against normalized numerical squared differences, enabling full-record nearest-neighbor matching without discretization.

## Method

Tab-PE adapts the Private Evolution framework to tabular data using three lightweight components that require no foundation models: (1) a RANDOM_API that initializes samples by uniform sampling over feature domains, (2) a VARIATION_API implementing a random walk with polynomially decaying mutation rates—resampling categorical features with probability μ_cat and adding Gaussian perturbations to numerical features with scale μ_num·range—and (3) a DP_NN_HISTOGRAM that assigns each private sample to its nearest synthetic candidate and adds Gaussian noise (sensitivity 1) to the resulting histogram. A two-stage selection strategy drives the evolutionary loop: early iterations sample with replacement from the noisy histogram to quickly shift the distribution toward the private data, while later iterations select the top-scoring samples for local refinement. Privacy is guaranteed via the Gaussian mechanism composed over T iterations, and the full-record nearest-neighbor scoring implicitly captures high-dimensional dependencies without enumerating marginal queries.

## Results

On the Artificial Characters dataset (ε=1.0), Tab-PE achieves 49.38% test accuracy and 48.09% macro F1, outperforming the best baseline AIM (23.24% accuracy) by +26.14 points and PrivGSD (40.36%) by +9.02 points. On Person Activity, Tab-PE reaches 63.72% accuracy vs. AIM's 59.53% (+4.19 points). Tab-PE also achieves the best high-order fidelity (5-WD: 0.158 on Artificial Characters, 0.116 on Person Activity) and highest embedding precision (~91–98%), while running 28× faster than AIM and entirely on CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tab-PE consistently outperforms all six baselines on high-order correlation datasets. Against the SOTA marginal-based method AIM, Tab-PE gains +26.14% accuracy on Artificial Characters and +4.19% on Person Activity, while also achieving lower 5/6/7-WD fidelity errors. PrivGSD (the closest evolutionary baseline) is outperformed by +9.02% accuracy on Artificial Characters and +7.25% on Person Activity. On the XOR stress test, all marginal-based baselines collapse to random-guess performance at 5 features, while Tab-PE maintains AUC 0.8. On the flattened MNIST (196 features), Tab-PE achieves 54.05% accuracy versus 12.06% (GEM) and 9.82% (RAP). On low-order datasets (Adult, Breast Cancer), AIM retains a ~1% accuracy edge over Tab-PE, confirming that marginal-based methods remain competitive when high-order correlations are absent. Tab-PE is also 28× faster than AIM and 10× faster than PrivMRF, running entirely on CPU without GPU requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Private Evolution (PE) framework—originally developed for DP image synthesis using foundation model APIs (Lin et al., 2024) and later adapted to text (Xie et al., 2024) and simulator-based generation (Lin et al., 2025)—to tabular data. The key departure is replacing expensive foundation-model or simulator APIs with lightweight heuristic operators (Gaussian perturbation for numerical features, scheduled resampling for categorical features), making the evolutionary loop CPU-only and training-free. It positions itself against the dominant marginal-query paradigm (AIM, PrivGSD, RAP++, PrivMRF), arguing that these methods' strong performance on standard benchmarks is an artifact of those datasets being dominated by low-order correlations, and that they fundamentally cannot scale to high-order dependencies due to exponential query growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant blind spot in the DP tabular synthesis literature: widely used benchmarks (Adult, Bank, Census) do not stress-test high-order correlation capture, masking the failure of SOTA methods on tasks that require it. By introducing new evaluation datasets (Artificial Characters, Person Activity, SCM simulations, XOR stress tests) and demonstrating that a simple evolutionary method with no model training outperforms all marginal-based baselines by up to 10% accuracy while running 28× faster on CPU, the work shifts the field's evaluation focus and offers a practical, scalable alternative. Limitations include a persistent ~30% accuracy gap to the non-private upper bound on high-order datasets, lower embedding-space recall compared to AIM, and only competitive (not superior) performance on low-order datasets. Open questions include whether the heuristic operators can be further refined, whether the approach generalizes to relational or time-series tabular data, and whether the gap to the non-private bound can be closed with more iterations or better scoring mechanisms.
