## Problem

Machine Learning as a Service (MLaaS) deployments are vulnerable to Model Extraction Attacks, where adversaries replicate target models via API queries. Existing ownership verification defenses—watermarking and fingerprinting—lack rigorous theoretical guarantees, making them difficult to deploy in production settings. CREDIT is the first framework to provide certified ownership verification against MEAs with provable error bounds on both false positives and false negatives.

## Input / Output

The method takes d-dimensional embeddings (d=1024 in practice) from a suspicious model h and a defended model g, evaluated on a verification set V (|V|=1000) drawn from the test split, and produces a binary ownership decision (surrogate vs. independent) along with the CREDIT threshold τ and explicit exponential error bounds γ₁ (Type I false alarm) and γ₂ (Type II missed detection). The mutual information statistic is computed via the KSG nearest-neighbor estimator with a fixed neighborhood size k, and the defended model g is constructed by injecting isotropic Gaussian noise Z~N(0,σ²I_d) into the target model's embeddings. The threshold τ depends on the attacker's query budget Q, the verification set size, and the embedding dimension, allowing the owner to calibrate the verification sensitivity to the expected threat level.

## Method

CREDIT builds a defended model g by adding Gaussian noise to the target model's embeddings, which via the Gaussian mechanism yields a mutual information upper bound β = Δ²n/(2σ²) between g and any other model. The CREDIT threshold τ = β[1 − ρ·exp(−Qβ/(ηd|V|))] is then set within [0, β], increasing with the attacker's query budget Q (more queries → closer surrogate → higher threshold needed) and decreasing with verification set size and embedding dimension (more accurate MI estimation → lower threshold suffices). Ownership verification computes the empirical mutual information Î between the suspicious model's and defended model's embeddings on V using the KSG estimator, and flags the model as a surrogate if Î > τ. Rigorous error guarantees follow from McDiarmid's bounded-differences inequality applied to the KSG estimator, yielding exponential concentration bounds: Pr[Type I error] ≤ exp(−2|V|τ²/C²) and Pr[Type II error] ≤ exp(−2|V|(I_sur − τ)²/C²), where C is the bounded-difference constant. The noise parameter σ is selected via a joint optimization balancing utility entropy loss (Gaussian surrogate differential entropy increase) against verification entropy (binary entropy of error rates), with the optimal σ found by grid search.

## Results

On ownership verification (AUC), CREDIT achieves a perfect 100.0 across all four backbone architectures (ResNet-50, VGG-16, DenseNet, GoogLeNet) on CIFAR-10, substantially outperforming all baselines which range from 40.74 to 80.25. In terms of model utility, CREDIT preserves near-vanilla accuracy (e.g., 94.67% vs. 95.70% vanilla on CIFAR-10/ResNet-50), while baselines suffer 5–35% drops. CREDIT also demonstrates superior efficiency: verification takes ~22 seconds versus 73–189 seconds for baselines, and preparation is negligible (0.001 s) compared to 0.28–5.7 s for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CREDIT achieves perfect AUC (100.0) on ownership verification across all four CNN backbones, decisively outperforming every baseline. Among watermarking methods, Backdooring (Adi et al., 2018) reaches at most 80.25 AUC (GoogLeNet) and EWE (Jia et al., 2021) peaks at 62.96 (VGG-16), both far below CREDIT. Among fingerprinting methods, IPGuard (Cao et al., 2021) is the weakest at 40.74 AUC on ResNet-50, while UAP (Peng et al., 2022) is the strongest baseline at 79.63 AUC on GoogLeNet—still a 20-point gap. The integrity-verification method MiSentry (He et al., 2024), when adapted to the extraction setting, performs comparably to other baselines (47.30–61.44 AUC), confirming that integrity fingerprints do not survive model extraction. In utility, CREDIT also leads or is competitive: on CIFAR-10/ResNet-50 it achieves 94.67% accuracy versus 91.08% (IPGuard) and 90.25% (EWE), and on graph datasets (e.g., PROTEINS/GAT) it even exceeds the vanilla model (74.14% vs. 73.09%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Gaussian mechanism from differential privacy (Abadi et al., 2016) as a defense against model extraction, using it to construct a protected model whose embeddings carry a provable upper bound on mutual information with any other model. The KSG nearest-neighbor estimator (Kraskov et al., 2004) serves as the practical tool for computing the mutual information statistic that drives verification. Conceptually, the work departs from prior ownership verification methods (watermarking, fingerprinting) that lack theoretical guarantees, and from certified defenses in robustness or unlearning that do not address intellectual property theft via extraction, by introducing mutual information as a model-agnostic similarity measure with a formally derived threshold and exponential error bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CREDIT fills a recognized gap in MLaaS security by providing the first ownership verification with rigorous probabilistic guarantees, making it potentially adoptable in production settings where watermarking and fingerprinting methods cannot offer such assurances. The approach is notably model-agnostic, extending across CNNs, GNNs, and NLP embeddings, and achieves a 10× efficiency gain in verification time by eliminating auxiliary model training. Open questions include scalability to very large foundation models where embedding dimensions and dataset sizes may challenge the KSG estimator's accuracy, the sensitivity of the threshold to the assumed query budget Q (which may be unknown in practice), and whether adversarially trained surrogates could learn to minimize mutual information while preserving utility beyond the bounded decorrelation attacks tested here.
