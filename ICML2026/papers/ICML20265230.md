## Problem

Existing 'thinking with images' methods for multimodal reasoning optimize answer accuracy but fail to enforce evidence-answer consistency: models frequently produce correct answers grounded in wrong visual regions (spurious correctness), wrong answers despite correct evidence (faithful incorrectness), or both (mislocalized failure). No prior framework explicitly trains models to select the right evidence regions and to abstain when that evidence is absent, leaving a gap between fluent reasoning and faithful visual grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265230/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a free-form text question, and must produce a strictly structured three-field response: a <bbox> field containing a JSON list of [x1, y1, x2, y2] coordinates (or null) for evidence regions, a <think> field with reasoning explicitly tied to those boxes, and an <answer> field with the final prediction. A key behavioral constraint is that when the question-relevant evidence regions are masked (counterfactual setting), the model must output 'Unknown' rather than guessing, making abstention a first-class output mode alongside normal answering.

## Method

DeFacto constructs counterfactual training data via an automated pipeline: Qwen2.5-VL extracts question-relevant descriptors, RPN and OCR generate candidate regions, and DINO-X open-vocabulary detection partitions them into evidence (R+) and irrelevant (R−) sets, yielding ~100K positive/counterfactual/random-masking triplets. Training uses GRPO-based reinforcement learning on Qwen2.5-VL with a composite reward combining three terms: (1) answer correctness that rewards correct answers in positive/random cases, rewards 'Unknown' in counterfactual cases, and applies a stronger penalty when the model outputs the correct answer without evidence access; (2) format consistency for valid structured output; and (3) region selection coherence computed as mean IoU overlap with R+ minus overlap with R−, which is zeroed out in counterfactual cases. The random-masking variant (occluding irrelevant regions while preserving evidence) prevents the model from learning to abstain based on the mere presence of masks rather than the specific absence of question-relevant content.

## Results

On general VQA benchmarks, DeFacto (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) achieves 72.1% on VQAv2, 63.9% on GQA, 61.4% on VizWiz, and 71.0% on VSR, improving over the backbone by +5.1, +10.7, +7.3, and +7.4 points respectively. On document and text-centric benchmarks, it reaches 94.0% on DocVQA, 82.1% on ChartQA, and 82.9% on TextVQA. On the proposed DeFacto-1.5K faithfulness benchmark, DeFacto attains mAP 35.5, AP50 49.8, and 60.8% accuracy, while reducing spurious correctness to 5.6% and achieving 64.1% masked-evidence abstention accuracy—substantially higher than GRIT (11.6% spurious, 46.1% abstention) and DeepEyes (11.0% spurious, 33.1% abstention).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeFacto outperforms all thinking-with-images baselines on the majority of general VQA benchmarks: it beats GRIT by +7.4 on VQAv2, +18.6 on OKVQA, and +22.0 on VizWiz, and surpasses DeepEyes by +21.7 on OKVQA and +29.2 on VizWiz. Against Visual-SR1 (the strongest open-source competitor on the same backbone), DeFacto wins on VQAv2 (+7.4), OKVQA (+12.7), GQA (+11.6), VizWiz (+25.6), and VSR (+4.6), but concedes SciQA (83.6 vs. 88.0, −4.4). On document/text-centric benchmarks, DeFacto leads Visual-SR1 on DocVQA (+9.1), ChartQA (+10.4), InfoVQA (+2.6), TextVQA (+12.8), and AI2D (+7.1), while losing on DeepForm (45.6 vs. 50.8) and WTQ (63.7 vs. 71.3). Against closed-source models, DeFacto-7B achieves 871 on OCRBench (vs. GPT-4o's 736) and 81.2 on MMB1.1 (vs. GPT-4o's 82.2), but trails GPT-4o on MMMU (56.6 vs. 69.2) and is roughly on par on MMStar (63.2 vs. 63.9). On the faithfulness-specific DeFacto-1.5K benchmark, DeFacto reduces spurious correctness from 11.6% (GRIT) and 11.0% (DeepEyes) to 5.6%, and raises masked-evidence abstention from 46.1% (GRIT) and 33.1% (DeepEyes) to 64.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two relatively recent research threads: 'thinking with images' (GRIT, DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus, COGCOM) and counterfactual reasoning in vision-language models (CoCT, C-VQA, CounterCurate, CFMM). While prior thinking-with-images methods use RL to encourage visual tool use (cropping, zooming) or grounded bounding-box generation, they optimize primarily for answer accuracy and do not explicitly enforce that the selected evidence actually supports the answer. DeFacto departs from this by importing the counterfactual data-generation idea from the VLM robustness literature—masking question-relevant regions and requiring abstention—and coupling it with GRPO-based RL and a region-level coherence reward, thereby making evidence-answer consistency a first-class training objective rather than an emergent byproduct of accuracy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The evidence-answer consistency problem is fundamental: a model that answers correctly by latching onto the wrong region (spurious correctness) or answers incorrectly despite finding the right region (faithful incorrectness) is unreliable in safety-critical or diagnostic applications. The DeFacto-1.5K benchmark and its failure-mode taxonomy (Mislocalized, Spurious Correct, Faithful but Wrong) provide a practical evaluation protocol that the field has lacked, and the counterfactual abstention mechanism offers a concrete, low-cost training signal that does not require manual region annotations. Key limitations include reliance on external detectors (RPN, DINO-X, OCR) for region proposals, evaluation restricted to static images, and a training set (~100K) that is modest relative to pretraining corpora; extending the framework to video, embodied settings, and self-generated region proposals are natural open directions.
