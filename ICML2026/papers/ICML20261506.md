## Problem

Existing sharpness-aware optimization methods for domain generalization suffer from two coupled limitations: they apply a uniform perturbation strategy across all source domains, ignoring varying learning difficulties (domain shift), and they probe flatness along a single gradient direction, missing anisotropic sharpness where minima appear flat in one direction but remain sharp in others. Together, these prevent the model from converging to truly robust, isotropically flat minima in multi-domain settings.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes mini-batches of RGB images paired with class labels and domain identifiers from K source domains; the domain labels are used solely to compute per-domain losses for the DAS module and do not alter the forward pass. At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and outputs a class label with zero additional overhead compared to standard SAM or ERM. The method is architecture-agnostic, validated on both ResNet-50 (CNN) and ViT-Base/16 (Transformer) backbones pre-trained on ImageNet.

## Method

DA-SAM introduces two synergistic modules that replace SAM's single uniform perturbation. The Dynamic Adaptive Scaling (DAS) module computes a domain-specific scaling factor σ_k via a reversed sigmoid on the standardized per-domain loss, assigning smaller exploration budgets to high-loss (harder) domains and larger budgets to low-loss (easier) domains, with normalization preserving the average at 1.0. The Adaptive Multi-Directional Flattening (AMDF) module then generates N gradient-biased random probe directions per domain whose variance is controlled by σ_k, computes one-sided sharpness gaps along each direction, and aggregates them into an anisotropic sharpness metric L_AMDF = E[h] + √Var[h] that captures both average curvature (∝ Tr(H)) and eigenvalue spread (∝ ‖λ‖₂). This metric procedurally modulates the perturbation step size via Step_DA-SAM = Step_base / (1 + L_AMDF), shrinking steps in sharp or anisotropic regions. The final perturbation vector is the uniformly averaged probe direction scaled by this adaptive step, and the model is updated using the gradient at the perturbed point—requiring only (2+N) forward passes in inference mode with no Hessian computation.

## Results

DA-SAM achieves the highest average accuracy of 66.0% across five standard DG benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet) using a ResNet-50 backbone under the DomainBed leave-one-domain-out protocol. The most significant gain is on the challenging TerraIncognita dataset, where DA-SAM reaches 50.0%, outperforming the second-best optimizer SAGM (48.6%) by +1.4% and standard SAM (45.7%) by +4.3%. On PACS, DA-SAM scores 87.5%, surpassing the strongest conventional DG method RDM (87.2%) and the best optimizer SAGM (86.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-SAM outperforms all 38 compared baselines on the 5-dataset average. Among conventional DG methods, it surpasses RDM (64.8%) by +1.2%, SagNet (64.2%) by +1.8%, and ERM (63.9%) by +2.1%. Among sharpness-aware optimizers, it beats SAGM (65.7%) by +0.3%, FSAM (65.4%) by +0.6%, FAD (65.3%) by +0.7%, GSAM (65.1%) by +0.9%, and standard SAM (64.1%) by +1.9%. The largest margin is on TerraIncognita, where DA-SAM (50.0%) leads SAGM (48.6%) by +1.4% and SAM (45.7%) by +4.3%, confirming the DAS module's effectiveness on severe distribution shifts. On OfficeHome, DA-SAM (68.4%) trails FSAM (70.2%) and SAGM (69.4%), suggesting the multi-directional probing is less advantageous when domain shifts are moderate. The ablation (Table 4) attributes +1.0% to AMDF alone and a further +0.9% to the DAS–AMDF synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) for domain generalization by addressing two limitations the authors identify in all prior SAM variants: the uniform, domain-agnostic perturbation strategy and the reliance on a single gradient direction. The DAS module draws on Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) to justify domain-specific scaling factors, while the AMDF module borrows from risk-averse optimization (CVaR-style mean-plus-std aggregation) and stochastic trace estimation to connect multi-directional sharpness probing to the Hessian spectrum. Unlike prior DG optimizers (SAGM, FAD, FSAM) that refine the flatness metric or update rule but still treat all domains uniformly and probe in one direction, DA-SAM couples domain-level guidance with multi-directional geometric exploration in a single procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DA-SAM offers a practical, drop-in optimizer for DG that requires no auxiliary networks or domain-invariant feature alignment, making it attractive for practitioners who want robustness gains through optimization geometry alone. The efficiency analysis showing that intermittent probing (k=30) matches or exceeds full probing while adding only ~10% training overhead is practically useful, and the demonstrated complementarity with SWAD suggests DA-SAM can serve as a superior base engine for ensemble-averaging pipelines. Open questions include whether the DAS module can be adapted for single-source DG (where per-domain losses are unavailable), whether the multi-directional probing can be replaced by cheaper second-order approximations, and whether the approach transfers to non-vision DG tasks such as tabular or text domain shifts.
