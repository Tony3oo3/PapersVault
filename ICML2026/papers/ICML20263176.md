## Problem

Training-based GUI grounding methods require large-scale annotated datasets and generalize poorly to unseen interfaces, while existing attention-based alternatives (e.g., TAG) avoid fine-tuning but suffer from low reliability because MLLM attention signals are spatially diffuse and lack explicit, complementary spatial anchors in GUI images. The paper asks whether accurate GUI grounding can be achieved purely from attention mechanisms without task-specific fine-tuning, and argues the bottleneck is not semantic understanding but the absence of localized, modality-specific grounding cues.

## Input / Output

The method takes a GUI screenshot (downsampled to the MLLM's input resolution to fit GPU memory) and a natural language instruction as input, and outputs a single click-point coordinate for the target element. A two-stage zoom-in strategy is applied: the first pass on the full downsampled image yields a coarse region, which is then cropped at half resolution (W/2 × H/2), resized back to input resolution, and re-inferred for fine-grained localization. On OSWorld-G, the benchmark supports a refusal output of (-1,-1) for absent elements, but Trifuse does not implement this and always produces a coordinate prediction from the fused heatmap.

## Method

Trifuse extracts three complementary grounding heatmaps and fuses them via a Consensus-SinglePeak (CS) strategy. For the attention modality, it applies token-level filtering (retaining top-k query tokens ranked by cosine similarity to visual patches) and head-level filtering (retaining top-k heads ranked by spatial entropy computed over connected-component regions), then aggregates via softmax-weighted summation. For the OCR modality, PaddleOCR detects text instances whose semantic relevance to filtered query tokens (via BGE-M3 embeddings) is projected onto the patch grid; for the caption modality, OmniParser detects icons and generates semantic descriptions that are similarly matched to the instruction. CS fusion combines a consensus term (element-wise product of all three heatmaps, amplifying cross-modal agreement) with a single-peak term (confidence-weighted selection of modality-specific peaks exceeding a threshold, where confidence is a sigmoid of the ratio of supporting cross-modal response to the peak's own magnitude), then applies a two-stage zoom-in localization to recover fine-grained coordinates from the coarse patch grid.

## Results

Trifuse (training-free, 3B backbone) achieves 81.1% on ScreenSpot, 82.6% on ScreenSpot-v2, 18.9% on ScreenSpot-Pro, and 38.7% on OSWorld-G, outperforming the prior training-free attention-based method TAG (8.5B) by +23.6, +31.4, +15.9, and +13.4 points respectively. With a 7B backbone, Trifuse reaches 86.2%, 86.9%, 29.7%, and 43.6% on the same benchmarks. When integrated with fine-tuned attention-based models (GUI-Actor, GUI-AIMA), Trifuse adds consistent gains of +0.5 to +3.0 points across all four benchmarks, demonstrating its value as a modular enhancement even for trained systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary training-free attention-based baseline TAG (Xu et al., 2025, 8.5B), Trifuse (3B) outperforms by 23.6–35.7 points across all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on ScreenSpot-v2 (+31.4) and ScreenSpot-Pro (+15.9), where TAG scores only 3.0%. Against training-based attention methods, Trifuse integrated with GUI-Actor (Wu et al., 2025b) adds +2.2 to +3.0 points (e.g., 90.5% vs 88.3% on ScreenSpot 7B), and with GUI-AIMA (Zhou et al., 2025a) adds +0.1 to +2.5 points (e.g., 51.3% vs 49.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro). Trifuse (training-free, 7B) also surpasses several SFT baselines: it exceeds UI-TARS (89.5% on ScreenSpot) and approaches JEDI (91.7%), while outperforming all RL-based methods (UI-R1, GUI-G1, GUI-G²) on ScreenSpot-Pro (29.7% vs 17.7–37.1%). Against general-purpose MLLMs without GUI fine-tuning (GPT-4o at 18.8%, Qwen2.5-VL-3B at 46.9% on ScreenSpot), Trifuse-3B achieves 81.1%, a 34-point improvement over the backbone alone. The one area where Trifuse shows limited gains is OSWorld-G's Refusal subtask, where the method cannot output null predictions, explaining the smaller +0.1 improvement over GUI-AIMA on that benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

Trifuse extends the attention-based GUI grounding paradigm introduced by TAG (Xu et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that MLLM attention maps can localize GUI elements without task-specific fine-tuning. The key departure is the identification of a 'lack of explicit and complementary spatial anchors' as the bottleneck, addressed by fusing three modalities—MLLM attention, OCR-derived text cues, and icon caption semantics—through a novel Consensus-SinglePeak (CS) strategy. Unlike subsequent attention-based methods (GUI-Actor, GUI-AIMA) that reintroduce training via learnable tokens, Trifuse remains entirely training-free while also serving as a modular plug-in that improves already-fine-tuned attention-based models. The two-level filtering (token relevance scoring + spatial entropy head selection) refines TAG's heuristic selection into a more principled mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Trifuse demonstrates that training-free GUI grounding can approach the accuracy of supervised fine-tuned and RL-based methods (e.g., 86.2% vs. 88.6–92.0% on ScreenSpot), substantially reducing the data and compute burden of adapting GUI agents to new platforms. However, the 7–10 second per-image inference time (dominated by the icon caption model at 83–88% of latency) is a significant practical bottleneck that limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether lightweight caption models can close the quality gap, whether the approach can be extended to region-level or refusal predictions, and whether hybrid training-free + lightweight fine-tuning can push past the current performance ceiling of pretrained backbones.
