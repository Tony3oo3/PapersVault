## Problem

Existing LipSync forgery detectors rely on local visual artifacts or explicit audio-visual mismatches that are increasingly neutralized by modern diffusion-based generators producing high-fidelity, well-synchronized outputs. Moreover, no prior work addresses the critical forensic task of attributing a LipSync forgery to its specific source generator. The paper identifies that LipSync synthesis inherently severs the biological coupling between lip articulation and head pose—a signal that persists even when pixel-level artifacts are eliminated.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips as sliding windows of T=5 frames, from which 96×96 lip ROIs and 468-point facial landmark trajectories (via MediaPipe) are extracted, alongside MFCC audio features temporally aligned to the visual stream. Stage I produces a binary real/fake prediction, while Stage II outputs a 5-way source model family label (Transformer, GAN, Diffusion, VAE, or Statistical). During inference, both stages run concurrently to yield a joint (detection, attribution) prediction.

## Method

LipDA is a two-stage framework. Stage I (detection) employs a dual-branch encoder—ResNet on lip patches and a projection head on landmark trajectories—trained with a margin-based contrastive loss that pulls lip and pose embeddings together for authentic videos and pushes them apart for forgeries, combined with a binary cross-entropy classifier on the concatenated features. Stage II (attribution) freezes Stage I and trains two modules: a Temporal Dynamic Module (Temporal-CNN + Bi-LSTM on keypoint sequences) capturing generator-specific motion fingerprints, and a Modality Synchronization Module (3D-CNN on lip ROIs + 1D-CNN on MFCC with multi-head cross-attention) capturing audio-visual alignment patterns; their features are concatenated for a 5-way cross-entropy classifier. The core insight is that LipSync generators treat lip motion (audio-constrained, deterministic) and head pose (probabilistic or transplanted) as independent sub-problems, breaking the joint distribution P(L,P|A,I) that natural speech maintains, and that different architectural families imprint distinct temporal dynamics exploitable for attribution.

## Results

On the primary LipSync-A dataset, LipDA (audio-visual) achieves 95.96% ACC and 99.42% AUC for detection, with an average of 96.26% ACC and 98.91% AUC across all three benchmarks (LipSync-A, AVLips, TalkHeadBench). For source attribution across five generator families, LipDA reaches 97.5% ACC and 93.9% macro F1, surpassing the second-best method (TALL) by over 12 percentage points in F1. The visual-only variant still achieves 94.51% average ACC and 98.69% average AUC, outperforming all 15 baselines in both modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipDA outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and both modalities. In the audio-visual setting, the strongest baseline SpeechForensics (88.80% avg ACC, 90.95% avg AUC) is surpassed by LipDA A-V by +7.46% ACC and +7.97% AUC; AVH-align (71.90/73.83) and FGMDC (78.96/81.05) trail by 14–24 points in AUC. Frequency-domain methods (FreqNet, NPR) perform near or below random (AUC 9–14%), confirming that LipSync forgeries suppress the high-frequency artifacts these detectors rely on. Among visual-only baselines, TALL (65.16/83.03) and LipForensics (79.86/87.48) are the closest, yet LipDA V still leads by 19 and 15 points in average ACC respectively. For the attribution task (Table 3), LipDA A-V achieves 97.5% ACC / 93.9% F1, beating TALL (92.3/81.5) by +5.2% ACC and +12.4% F1, while SpeechForensics (72.4/26.6) and AVAD (74.2/33.4) collapse on GAN- and Transformer-based forgeries with F1 scores below 35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of LipSync detection: (1) artifact-based methods (frequency-domain, spatial anomalies) that assume forgeries leave detectable pixel-level traces, and (2) audio-visual synchronization methods (AVAD, SpeechForensics, LipFD) that detect explicit lip-audio mismatches. Both paradigms are rendered increasingly ineffective by diffusion-based generators that synthesize high-fidelity textures and enforce tight lip-audio alignment. LipDA instead exploits a fundamentally different signal—the disruption of the biological coupling between lip articulation and head pose—that is an inherent structural limitation of decoupled generation architectures rather than a removable artifact. For attribution, it extends the generative fingerprint concept from spatial pixel signatures (ManiFPT, Riemannian-Geometric Fingerprints) to temporal motion dynamics and cross-modal synchronization patterns specific to audio-driven facial synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LipDA establishes the first unified framework for both detecting LipSync forgeries and attributing them to their source generator family, addressing a critical gap in forensic accountability that binary detection alone cannot fill. The LipSync-A dataset (16,000 videos, 15 generators, 7 architectures, with per-generator labels) fills a significant resource gap for attribution-oriented research. Key limitations include degradation under stationary speakers, persistent downward gaze, and prolonged occlusion, as well as audio sensitivity in the attribution module. Open questions include whether the physiological coupling signal extends to full-video generation paradigms (partially validated with Wan-S2V), how to handle open-set attribution for entirely novel architectures, and whether complementary upper-face or full-body cues can close the operational boundary gaps.
