## Problem

Visual foundation models such as DINO produce dense features that degrade substantially under viewpoint change, revealing a lack of true 3D spatial understanding. Existing self-supervised video approaches (TimeTuning, MoSiC) propagate semantic groupings over time but do not directly optimize for viewpoint-robust spatial correspondence, while image-based ordering methods (NeCo) are restricted to static crops and discard the global context that encodes 3D structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264164/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video clips (parsed into sequences of frames at 224×224 resolution) and produces dense patch-level features (p×d per frame) that are viewpoint-invariant. A grid of g×g points is initialized on the first frame and tracked across the sequence by CoTrackerV3, yielding trajectories and visibility masks that define where features are sampled. Downstream, these features support keypoint correspondence (SPair-71k, Navi), depth estimation, and surface normal estimation via lightweight linear probes on frozen representations.

## Method

3DPoV is a post-training strategy that enforces temporal consistency of patch-level similarity rankings via a differentiable sorting loss. A teacher-student ViT architecture processes video frames: the teacher encodes the anchor frame (t=0, guaranteed visible) while the student encodes subsequent frames; features at tracked point locations are resampled to form temporally aligned patch sequences. For each query patch, cosine distances to a shared reference bank (drawn from other batch frames and a non-anchor frame of the same video) are passed through an Odd-Even differentiable sorting network to produce soft permutation matrices. The student's permutation at frame t is supervised by the teacher's permutation at t=0 using a reversed cross-entropy (mode-seeking) loss, weighted by joint visibility to handle occlusion. Only the last two transformer blocks are fine-tuned (~20 GPU-hours on 4×A100) on a mixture of CO3D, DL3DV, and YouTube-VOS, making the approach backbone-agnostic and applicable to DINOv1, DINOv2-reg, and DINOv3.

## Results

On SPair-71k keypoint matching with DINOv2R-B/14, 3DPoV achieves 55.40% recall on the 'All' split, improving over the DINOv2R baseline (53.47%) by +1.93 points, with the largest gains at large viewpoint shifts (54.50% vs 53.41%). On Navi 3D correspondence, 3DPoV reaches 89.18% at θ₀¹⁵ and 31.63% at θ₆₀¹²⁰, consistently outperforming the baseline across all viewpoint bins. For depth estimation (scale-aware δ₁), 3DPoV improves from 57.62% to 59.17%, and for surface normal estimation (11.25° threshold) from 37.10% to 38.29%. All improvements are achieved with only ~20 GPU-hours of fine-tuning on 4×A100s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DPoV outperforms all seven baselines on SPair-71k 'All' recall with DINOv2R-B/14: +1.93 over DINOv2R (55.40 vs 53.47), +0.98 over NeCo (55.40 vs 54.42), +3.68 over MoSiC (55.40 vs 51.72), and +0.76 over 3DCorrEnhance (55.40 vs 54.64). A critical distinction is that NeCo, while competitive on small viewpoint shifts (59.57 vs 60.16), degrades sharply at medium and large shifts (49.06 and 52.35), whereas 3DPoV improves uniformly across all bins (52.79 and 54.50). On Navi 3D correspondence, 3DPoV leads in every viewpoint bin (e.g., 68.98 vs 67.45 at θ₁₅³⁰), while MoSiC only wins at θ₆₀¹²⁰ (31.78 vs 31.63) and NeCo loses at all bins beyond θ₀¹⁵. For depth and surface normal estimation, 3DPoV consistently improves over DINOv2R (δ₁: 59.17 vs 57.62; 11.25°: 38.29 vs 37.10), confirming that the gains extend beyond keypoint matching to broader geometric understanding. The method also transfers to DINOv3, yielding a small but consistent +0.08 on 'All' SPair recall, demonstrating backbone-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

3DPoV extends NeCo's differentiable-sorting-based patch ordering from static images (overlapping crops at a fixed viewpoint) to video by anchoring the ordering supervision on point-track trajectories across frames. It departs from the temporal-propagation paradigm of TimeTuning and MoSiC, which stabilize semantic groupings over time but do not directly optimize viewpoint-robust spatial structure. The method also builds on the DINOv2/DINOv3 teacher-student architecture and the Probe3D benchmark, positioning itself as a lightweight post-training stage that injects geometry-aware signal into already-pretrained ViT backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme efficiency: roughly 20 GPU-hours of fine-tuning on a small video mixture yields consistent gains across all Probe3D tasks and multiple backbones (DINOv1, DINOv2-reg, DINOv3), making it a drop-in upgrade for teams already using these models for correspondence or geometry. A notable open question is the dependence on an external point tracker (CoTrackerV3); while ablations show tracker-agnosticism, tracker failures on repetitive textures or long occlusions still limit supervision quality. The modest but uniform gains on DINOv3 (a highly saturated model) suggest diminishing returns at the frontier, and whether the approach transfers to 3D reconstruction or embodied tasks beyond Probe3D remains unexplored.
