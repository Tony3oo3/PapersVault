## Problem

Existing delta-rule recurrences (DeltaNet, GDN, KDA) perform first-order gradient steps on the test-time regression least-squares objective, ignoring the curvature of the key Gram matrix that governs the regression geometry. Exact solvers like Mesalayer (sequential Sherman-Morrison) and MesaNet (conjugate gradients) are either too slow or numerically unstable for practical training. The paper asks whether a cheap, stable approximation to the exact preconditioner can recover most of the second-order benefit while retaining efficient chunkwise parallelism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token sequences processed into per-token key, value, and query vectors (with a short convolution and SiLU preprocessing). A key structural detail is that the recurrence now maintains a distinct 'write key' \tilde{k}_t (the preconditioned key) separate from the 'read key' k_t used for the state lookup, so the state update becomes S_t = S_{t-1} + β_t(v_t − S_{t-1}k_t)\tilde{k}_t^⊤. The output is a contextual representation o_t = S_t q_t used for next-token prediction, with the preconditioner state A_t ∈ R^{d_k} tracking coordinate-wise second moments of keys via an additive prefix-sum that is trivially parallelizable across chunks.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 3.1) that linear attention and DeltaNet are equivalent under exact preconditioning by the inverse key Gram, unifying them as two realizations of the same least-squares solver. It then replaces the full Gram inverse with a diagonal approximation A_t = A_{t-1} + k_t ⊙ k_t, which admits a chunkwise parallel form because its state update is an elementwise prefix-sum and inversion is elementwise. For trainability, the raw diagonal is reparameterized in log-space with a squashing function that bounds the preconditioner B_t to [1/x, x], ensuring recurrent-matrix eigenvalues stay ≤ 1. This yields Preconditioned DeltaNet (PDN), Preconditioned GDN (PGDN), and Preconditioned KDA (PKDA) via the 'apply-to-key' (ATK) transform, plus Preconditioned Linear Attention (PLA) and P-Mamba-2 via the 'apply-to-query' (ATQ) transform. The authors further propose the DGPS taxonomy (Decay, Gain, Preconditioner, Solve) as a four-axis design space that maps directly to efficient chunkwise parallel kernels, and show empirically that PGDN/PKDA outperform their unpreconditioned counterparts on MQAR recall, S-NIAH, and commonsense reasoning at 340M/1B scale with only ~10% throughput overhead.

## Results

At the 340M scale, preconditioned recurrences consistently outperform their unpreconditioned counterparts: PGDN achieves 49.96% average commonsense accuracy (vs. 48.86% for GDN) and 26.17% average in-context retrieval accuracy (vs. 24.77% for GDN), while PKDA achieves 25.33 LAMBADA perplexity (vs. 31.37 for KDA). At the 1B scale, PKDA reaches 56.64% average commonsense accuracy and 11.86 LAMBADA perplexity (vs. 55.43% and 14.52 for KDA), and PGDN achieves 34.99% average ICR accuracy (vs. 33.87% for GDN). On the S-NIAH long-context retrieval benchmark, all preconditioned variants show consistent improvements over their base recurrences across all three difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares each preconditioned recurrence against its unpreconditioned base: PDN vs. DeltaNet, PGDN vs. Gated DeltaNet, and PKDA vs. Kimi Delta Attention. PGDN consistently outperforms GDN at both scales, with the largest gains on LAMBADA perplexity (28.43 vs. 31.39 at 340M; 13.09 vs. 13.46 at 1B) and in-context retrieval (26.17% vs. 24.77% at 340M; 34.99% vs. 33.87% at 1B). PKDA similarly dominates KDA, most notably on LAMBADA perplexity (25.33 vs. 31.37 at 340M; 11.86 vs. 14.52 at 1B) and commonsense reasoning (49.89% vs. 49.12% at 340M; 56.64% vs. 55.43% at 1B). PDN improves over DN on most metrics, with the clearest gain in ICR (23.84% vs. 22.59%), though it slightly underperforms on HellaSwag (33.83% vs. 34.08%). In throughput, PGDN is more than 20% faster than MesaNet, and the preconditioned kernels incur only ~10% overhead over their unpreconditioned counterparts, while the general DPLR kernel (used by RWKV-7) is significantly slower and OOMs at 32K tokens for KDA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the delta-rule recurrence family (DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, Kimi Delta Attention) by introducing a diagonal preconditioner derived from the test-time regression (TTR) framework. The key theoretical departure is showing that linear attention and DeltaNet are equivalent under exact preconditioning with the inverse key Gram matrix (Theorem 3.1), unifying two previously distinct recurrences as different realizations of the same operator. Practically, the authors replace the intractable exact inverse Gram (used sequentially in Mesalayer) or iterative conjugate gradients (used in MesaNet) with a diagonal approximation analogous to Adam's second-moment accumulator, which uniquely admits an efficient chunkwise parallel form. The paper also proposes the DGPS taxonomy (decay, gain, preconditioner, solve) as a design space that bridges the theory-heavy TTR framework and the implementation-oriented DPLR form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The preconditioner axis provides a principled, low-overhead (~10%) lever for improving linear recurrences that is orthogonal to existing design choices (decay, gain, solve), and the consistent gains across all three base recurrences at both 340M and 1B scale suggest it is a broadly applicable component. The DGPS taxonomy is a useful organizational tool for the rapidly growing space of linear recurrences, though its practical utility depends on whether the community adopts it. Open questions include whether the ATQ (query-side) preconditioning axis can match or exceed ATK (key-side) for approximate preconditioners, whether gains persist at larger scales and longer contexts (only 2K context tested), and whether the diagonal approximation can be replaced by richer low-rank structures without losing the chunkwise parallel property.
