## Problem

SVD-based 4-bit quantization methods (e.g., SVDQuant) rely on rapid singular-value decay to absorb outliers into a small low-rank branch, but LLM weights exhibit much slower spectral decay, creating a dilemma where small rank leaves large residual errors while large rank adds prohibitive memory and compute overhead. Moreover, existing decompositions minimize real-valued residual energy rather than post-quantization error, and naively quantizing the low-rank component to 4-bit introduces large scale-imbalance errors that offset the decomposition's benefits.

## Input / Output

TwinQuant takes pretrained LLM weight matrices and a small calibration set (128 WikiText2 prompts, 2048 tokens each) as input, and produces a fully 4-bit quantized model where both the low-rank (rank r=128) and residual components are stored in INT4 with group-wise scales (group size 128). The learned global orthogonal transforms and layer-specific invertible transforms are folded offline into the weights, so at inference the model consumes only quantized activations and weights through a single fused kernel, producing standard text generation output with no extra runtime overhead beyond the low-rank path.

## Method

TwinQuant decomposes each linear weight into a low-rank component UV and a residual R, then learns two complementary transforms to make both branches quantization-friendly: global orthogonal matrices Q (constrained to the Stiefel manifold) that jointly reshape activation and residual distributions, and layer-specific invertible matrices G (on the general linear manifold) that re-distribute the low-rank factors. A hybrid manifold optimizer updates Q via Cayley SGD to preserve orthonormality, while G is parameterized as a polar decomposition G=PS (orthogonal P times symmetric positive-definite S via Cholesky factorization) to guarantee invertibility, trained with a three-stage schedule (Global Alignment → Invertible Adaptation → Joint Refinement) to avoid gradient conflicts between the coupled components. A fused dual-component CUDA kernel pipelines the two-stage low-rank GEMM entirely on-chip—re-quantizing the intermediate INT32 accumulator to INT4 in shared memory without a global-memory round-trip—and merges both component outputs in a single epilogue with one write-back, eliminating intermediate kernel launches and global-memory traffic.

## Results

Under the most challenging W4A4 setting, TwinQuant achieves 70.9% average zero-shot accuracy and 9.4 WikiText2 perplexity on LLaMA3-8B (vs. 73.5% and 8.6 for FP16), and 73.3% accuracy with 10.4 perplexity on Qwen3-32B (vs. 75.2% and 7.6 for FP16). Under W4A8, TwinQuant reaches 74.3% accuracy and 9.9 perplexity on Qwen3-32B, staying within 0.9 points of FP16 accuracy. In terms of speed, TwinQuant delivers 1.63–1.8× end-to-end throughput over FP16 TensorRT-LLM on an RTX 4090 and 1.32–1.73× on an L20, while the low-rank component adds only 3.5–7.3% memory and 3.8–8.2% latency overhead at rank=128.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under W4A4, RTN and SmoothQuant catastrophically fail on most models (perplexity exceeding 1,000–20,000), confirming that naive rounding and channel-wise scaling cannot handle fully 4-bit quantization. TwinQuant substantially outperforms QuaRot (rotation-based), reducing perplexity by up to 15.8 points on LLaMA3-3B and by 2.9–11.3 points across Qwen3 models, while also improving zero-shot accuracy by 1.6–7.4 points. Against SVDQuant (the closest decomposition-based baseline at rank=32), TwinQuant reduces W4A4 perplexity by 0.6–3.1 points and improves average accuracy by 0.9–2.7 points across all six models, with the gap widening on smaller models (e.g., +2.7 points on LLaMA3-3B). Compared to FlatQuant (affine-based), TwinQuant achieves comparable or better perplexity in most cases (up to 1.6 points improvement) and higher accuracy on LLaMA3-8B (70.9 vs. 70.1) and Qwen3-32B (73.3 vs. 72.8). Against SpinQuant (learned rotations), TwinQuant wins by 0.5–1.9 points in accuracy and 0.5–1.7 points in perplexity under W4A4, with the largest gains on Qwen3-32B (73.3 vs. 72.0 accuracy, 10.4 vs. 12.1 perplexity). Under W4A8, TwinQuant is competitive with the strongest baselines (FlatQuant, SpinQuant), achieving the best perplexity on LLaMA3-8B (8.9) and Qwen3-32B (9.9), while GPTQ and AWQ (W4A16-only) remain strong in the weight-only regime but are not directly comparable to the W4A4 setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TwinQuant extends the SVD-based low-rank decomposition of SVDQuant (Li et al., ICLR 2025) from diffusion models to LLMs, where the paper empirically shows singular-value spectra decay far more slowly, invalidating the small-rank assumption. It also builds on rotation-based quantization (QuaRot, SpinQuant) and learnable affine rescaling (FlatQuant), but departs from these by learning the decomposition subspace itself—via a hybrid Stiefel/General-Linear manifold optimization—rather than applying fixed or single-space transforms to a pre-decomposed weight. The key conceptual shift is that both the low-rank and residual components are quantized to 4-bit (SVDQuant keeps the low-rank branch in FP16), and the transforms are learned to minimize post-quantization error jointly across both branches rather than minimizing real-valued reconstruction error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving near-FP16 accuracy under full W4A4 quantization (both weights and activations at 4-bit) with a 1.8× end-to-end speedup is practically significant for deploying large LLMs on memory-constrained hardware, and the fused dual-component kernel design addresses a real systems bottleneck that prior decomposition-based methods leave unresolved. However, the gains over the strongest single-space baselines (FlatQuant, SpinQuant) are modest—typically 0.5–1.5 accuracy points—and the method requires calibration data and offline optimization (up to ~6.5 hours for 32B models). Open questions include extension to MoE and multimodal architectures, robustness under ultra-long contexts, and whether the learnable transforms can be made data-free or more lightweight.
