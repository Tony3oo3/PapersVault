## Problem

Existing WSI survival prediction methods collapse heterogeneous tissue into a single deterministic representation, providing neither an explanation of which morphological components drive survival risk nor a quantification of predictive uncertainty. This is particularly problematic given that distinct tissue components (tumor epithelium, stroma, necrosis) carry independent prognostic value, and that censored survival data introduces additional uncertainty that point-level estimates fail to convey.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265184/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes gigapixel whole-slide histopathology images segmented into non-overlapping patches, whose embeddings are extracted by a frozen foundation model (UNI2h or CONCH). Rather than outputting a single survival curve, DPsurv produces a survival function bounded by belief and plausibility measures, α-level belief and probabilistic prediction intervals, a component-wise relative risk map over the WSI, and a patch prototype assignment map. Survival times are discretized into quantile bins during training to enable probability-based calibration metrics.

## Method

DPsurv is a three-stage evidential pipeline. First, a patch-prototype-guided GMM decomposes each WSI into C morphological components, each summarized by a mixture weight, mean, and covariance in the patch embedding space. Second, each component embedding is mapped into evidence space via K learned component prototypes that act as local risk experts; each prototype contributes a GRFN whose parameters are weighted by a cosine-similarity discount, and the component-level GRFN is obtained by an unnormalized product-intersection aggregation. Third, the C component-level GRFNs are fused into a slide-level mixture GRFN (m-GRFN) using the evidence mixture mechanism, which preserves uncertainty through aggregation rather than averaging point predictions. Training uses a novel mixture evidential loss that combines censored and uncensored negative log-likelihood with a trade-off parameter, and the dual-prototype structure (patch prototypes for feature decomposition, component prototypes for risk reasoning) yields interpretability at the feature, reasoning, and decision levels simultaneously.

## Results

DPsurv achieves the highest mean C-index of 0.704 across five TCGA cancer cohorts (BRCA 0.720, BLCA 0.625, LUAD 0.667, UCEC 0.766, KIRC 0.741), outperforming the next-best method BayesMIL (0.674) by 3.0 points. On calibration, DPsurv attains the lowest mean IBS (0.310) and IBLL (0.824), substantially ahead of the best non-UA baseline DSMIL (IBS 0.538, IBLL 1.520) and the best UA baseline BayesMIL (IBS 0.542, IBLL 1.475). The ablation confirms that the full dual-prototype evidential pipeline (GMM + GRFN + CP + EM) yields the strongest combined discrimination and calibration (C-index 0.687, IBS 0.209, IBLL 0.604 in the fixed-K ablation setting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPsurv outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all five TCGA cohorts. On discrimination (C-index), it leads the strongest non-UA baseline PANTHER (0.655 avg) by 4.9 points and the strongest UA baseline BayesMIL (0.674 avg) by 3.0 points. On calibration, the gap is even more pronounced: DPsurv's mean IBS of 0.310 is roughly 42% lower than DSMIL (0.538, best non-UA) and 43% lower than BayesMIL (0.542, best UA), while its mean IBLL of 0.824 is nearly half that of DSMIL (1.520) and BayesMIL (1.475). EVREG, the only other GRFN-based method, performs poorly on WSI features (C-index 0.598, IBLL 6.503), confirming that the evidential framework alone is insufficient without the dual-prototype decomposition. Among non-UA MIL baselines, DSMIL and PANTHER are the closest competitors on C-index, but both lack explicit uncertainty modeling and consequently show substantially worse calibration. The feature-extractor robustness experiment (Table 3, UCEC with CONCH) further shows DPsurv generalizes beyond UNI2h, achieving C-index 0.780 and IBS 0.239, again outperforming all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPsurv sits at the intersection of two research threads: prototype-based WSI representation learning (PANTHER, Song et al. 2024) and evidential uncertainty modeling via Gaussian Random Fuzzy Numbers (Denœux, 2023a,b). It departs from prior WSI survival methods (ABMIL, TransMIL, PANTHER) that collapse heterogeneous tissue into a single deterministic slide embedding by instead decomposing each WSI into morphology-aware Gaussian components and assigning component-wise survival evidence. The key conceptual departure is the dual-prototype design: patch prototypes handle feature-level decomposition (GMM), while component prototypes serve as local risk experts at the reasoning level, with GRFNs bridging the two to produce interval-valued survival predictions. The mixture evidential loss (Eq. 14) further extends prior evidential regression losses to handle right-censored survival data, a setting where most uncertainty-aware methods (BayesMIL, UMSA, EVREG) were not originally designed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two underexplored gaps in WSI survival analysis: (1) interpretability beyond attention heatmaps, and (2) principled uncertainty quantification for censored survival outcomes. The multi-level interpretability (feature → reasoning → decision) validated by board-certified pathologists offers a more clinically actionable explanation than patch-level attention weights, and the GRFN-based BPIs provide conservative, well-calibrated prediction intervals that naturally widen under epistemic uncertainty. Limitations include evaluation on only five TCGA cancer types, reliance on a frozen foundation model (UNI2h) for patch features, fixed (non-learned) GMM parameters, and a small pilot clinical coherence study (17 cases). Open questions include whether the dual-prototype structure generalizes to other imaging modalities or multi-institutional settings, and whether the GMM could be made fully differentiable to enable end-to-end training.
