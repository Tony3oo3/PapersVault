## Problem

Exact sparse (ℓ₀-constrained) regression methods such as IHT, CoSaMP, and Subspace Pursuit are computationally efficient and empirically successful for time series modeling, yet their theoretical guarantees were restricted to i.i.d. data. This paper closes that gap by providing the first finite-sample statistical and computational guarantees for these methods under temporal dependence, specifically for stationary Gaussian α-mixing processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265652/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series with regressor X_t ∈ R^p and target Y_t ∈ R^q over T time steps, assumed stationary, Gaussian, and α-mixing. The design matrix is constructed via the Kronecker product X̃ = I_q ⊗ X ∈ R^{Tq × pq}, and the ℓ₀ constraint is imposed on the vectorized coefficient matrix vec(Θ) ∈ R^{pq}, bounding the total number of nonzero entries across all response equations. The output is a sparse coefficient matrix Θ with at most s nonzeros, from which the selected lag support and point predictions are directly read off.

## Method

The core technical contribution is establishing high-probability restricted strong convexity/smoothness (RSC/RSS) for the empirical quadratic loss under α-mixing Gaussian assumptions (Proposition 3.2), by proving concentration of the empirical Gram matrix I_q ⊗ (X^T X/T) over sparse directions using mixing-based quadratic form bounds. Leveraging these conditions, the paper derives geometric convergence rates for IHT (projected gradient descent with hard-thresholding projection) and greedy pursuit methods (CoSaMP, Subspace Pursuit), showing both reach the minimax rate O(√(s log(pq)/T)) in O(κ(Σ_X) log(1/ε)) iterations. The analysis is then specialized to Gaussian VAR(d) models, where the error bound is expressed through interpretable quantities: the companion matrix spectral radius r, the constant C_A governing decay of A^j, and the condition number of the innovation covariance Σ_ε. The RSC/RSS proof builds on Wong et al. (2020) for concentration under mixing, while the algorithmic convergence analysis extends Jain et al. (2014) from the i.i.d. to the dependent setting.

## Results

On a multivariate sparse Gaussian VAR(d) (p=5, d=100, T=10,000, s*=20), exact sparse methods (IHT, SP, CoSaMP) achieve test MSE of 0.0401–0.0402, matching LASSO (0.0403) and near-oracle (0.0401), while enforcing exactly 20 nonzeros versus LASSO's 85. Precision jumps from 0.21 (LASSO) to 0.80–0.85 (exact sparse), and runtime drops from 0.83 s to 0.13–0.20 s. On the NYC ridesharing AR(192) real-data task, SP(s=10) achieves test MSE 0.012 (vs. LASSO 0.015) in 0.04 s (vs. 1.92 s), a ~48× speedup, and recovers the interpretable lags {1, 24, 168} corresponding to short-term, daily, and weekly periodicity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is LASSO (ℓ₁-regularized least squares via cvxpy). On both synthetic VAR settings, exact sparse methods (IHT, SP, CoSaMP) match or slightly beat LASSO on test MSE (e.g., 0.0401 vs. 0.0403 for p=5) while dramatically outperforming it on precision (0.85 vs. 0.21) and sparsity control (exactly 20 nonzeros vs. 85). LASSO retains a small edge on recall (0.90 vs. 0.85) and ℓ₂-error (0.013 vs. 0.010), but at the cost of selecting 4× more coefficients than the true support. Computationally, CoSaMP is ~6× faster than LASSO in the multivariate VAR (0.13 s vs. 0.83 s) and ~48× faster on the NYC ridesharing task (0.04 s vs. 1.92 s). On the real NYC data, SP(s=10) also achieves lower test MSE (0.012 vs. 0.015) than LASSO at the same sparsity level, and its selected support {1, 24, 168} aligns cleanly with daily/weekly periodicity, whereas LASSO scatters mass across less interpretable lags (e.g., 171, 172).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence theory of exact sparse algorithms (IHT, CoSaMP, Subspace Pursuit) from the i.i.d. setting to α-mixing Gaussian time series. The RSC/RSS proof builds directly on Wong et al. (2020)'s concentration inequalities for dependent Gaussian processes, while the geometric convergence analysis for IHT follows the framework of Jain et al. (2014). The key departure from prior work is that all existing guarantees for these greedy/projected-gradient methods assume independent rows in the design matrix, whereas here the design is constructed from overlapping lag vectors with strong temporal dependence. The paper also positions itself against the rich LASSO-for-time-series literature (Basu & Michailidis 2015, Nardi & Rinaldo 2011, Kock & Callot 2015), which provides convex-estimator guarantees but leaves the nonconvex exact-sparse case unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-sample theoretical analysis of exact sparse methods under genuine temporal dependence, closing a gap that has limited practitioners' confidence in applying IHT/CoSaMP/SP to time series. The practical implication is significant: exact sparse solvers are already empirically faster and more interpretable than LASSO, but without theory, their use on dependent data was unjustified. Key limitations include the Gaussianity assumption (extensions to sub-Gaussian or heavy-tailed settings would require stronger mixing conditions) and the need for summable α-mixing to recover the classical O(√(s log(pq)/T)) rate. Open directions include post-selection inference for selected lags, nonlinear dependence structures, and combining exact sparse estimation with uncertainty quantification.
