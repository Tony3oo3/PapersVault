## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors exhibit a critical prediction asymmetry: high overall accuracy is sustained by near-perfect real-image recognition while sensitivity to generated content collapses under common post-processing such as JPEG compression and resizing. The paper attributes this to detectors relying on spurious features—compression artifacts as realness cues and generator-specific shortcuts as fakeness cues—rather than robust forensic signals, causing the model to default to predicting 'real' when fragile cues are disrupted.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolution (the ResNet-50 backbone uses spatially adaptive average pooling, so no fixed input size is required) and outputs a binary real/fake label with a calibrated probability score. During the diagnostic dissection phase, the method additionally consumes inpainted composite images paired with their binary spatial masks to compute per-channel alignment scores. The pruned feature representation is a 2048-dimensional vector (after global average pooling over a 12×12 spatial grid from 96×96 training crops) with a subset of channels zeroed out by the bilateral pruning mask.

## Method

DEAR (Dissect and Prune) is a post-hoc feature selection framework applied to a frozen ResNet-50 backbone in three stages. First, diagnostic inpainted images are generated by compositing Stable Diffusion 1.5 inpainting output into random rectangular regions of real LSUN images, providing known ground-truth masks that spatially separate generated from authentic pixels. Second, the Regional Activation Discrepancy (RAD) metric is computed for each of the 2048 final-convolutional-layer channels as the difference between mean activation in the inpainted region and the background, averaged over ~6,400 diagnostic crops. Third, bilateral pruning removes channels at both extremes of the RAD distribution (strongly fake-aligned channels capturing generator-specific shortcuts and strongly real-aligned channels capturing compression artifacts), then only the linear classifier is retrained on the pruned feature space using a combined loss over original training data and inpaint data. The key insight is that features at both extremes of the alignment spectrum are structurally fragile under perturbation, while intermediate channels encode more robust forensic signals.

## Results

On in-the-wild benchmarks (Chameleon, LOKI, WildRF), DEAR-r achieves the best average AUC of 82.9 (original) and 72.1 (post-processed), while DEAR-c reaches 79.1 and 77.5 respectively. The most dramatic gains appear in fake accuracy under post-processing: DEAR-c maintains an average F.Acc of 63.5 versus 17.4 for Corvi+ and 24.4 for Rajan+, and DEAR-r reaches 52.0 versus 27.5 for Rajan+. On the challenging FLUX generator, DEAR-c recovers F.Acc from 21.5% (Corvi) to 100%, and DEAR-r from 12.0% (Rajan) to 96.6%. Under WEBP compression at quality 90, DEAR-c improves F.Acc by +37.7 points over Corvi (71.5% vs. 33.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAR-c and DEAR-r consistently outperform their base detectors (Corvi, Rajan) and the Stay-Positive variants (Corvi+, Rajan+) on fake accuracy across all in-the-wild benchmarks, both original and post-processed. Under post-processing, the gap is most striking: DEAR-c achieves 63.5% average F.Acc versus 17.4% for Corvi+ and 0.6% for base Corvi, while DEAR-r reaches 52.0% versus 24.4% for Rajan+ and 27.5% for base Rajan. On AUC, DEAR-r leads on WildRF (87.8 original, 86.3 post-processed) and Chameleon (85.6 original), while DEAR-c leads on LOKI (85.9 original). Compared to other CNN baselines, DEAR-r surpasses DRCT (82.9 vs. 63.8 avg AUC original) and all CLIP-based methods (UFD 60.7, C2P-CLIP 61.6, RINE 64.7, ClipDet 70.4 avg AUC). The trade-off is a modest reduction in real accuracy (e.g., DEAR-c drops from 99.6% to 79.9% R.Acc on Chameleon), but the dramatic F.Acc recovery (from 5.5% to 57.9%) directly addresses the prediction asymmetry problem that the paper identifies as the core failure mode of existing detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEAR adapts the Network Dissection framework (Bau et al., 2017) from semantic concept interpretation to forensic feature analysis, replacing IoU with a density-based Regional Activation Discrepancy (RAD) metric inspired by Chan-Vese segmentation. It builds directly on two ResNet-50 detectors—Corvi (ICASSP 2023) and Rajan (ICLR 2025)—as frozen backbones, and positions itself as a principled extension of Stay-Positive (Rajan & Lee, 2025), which only prunes real-associated features via non-negative weight constraints. The key conceptual departure is bilateral pruning: DEAR shows that features at both extremes of the alignment spectrum (strongly fake-aligned and strongly real-aligned) are structurally fragile, whereas Stay-Positive addresses only the real-associated side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies prediction asymmetry as a fundamental reliability gap in deployed AIGI detectors, where near-perfect real accuracy masks catastrophic fake-detection failure under common post-processing—a problem that aggregate metrics like AUC obscure. The bilateral pruning insight has practical implications for anyone deploying CNN-based detectors, and the inpainting-based diagnostic protocol is a reusable tool for auditing feature robustness. Limitations include analysis restricted to the final convolutional layer of two ResNet-50 backbones, the post-hoc nature of the method (no integration into training), and dependence on an inpainting model for diagnostic data generation. Open questions include whether RAD-based pruning generalizes to ViT or CLIP-based detectors, whether multi-layer dissection yields complementary gains, and whether alignment-based supervision can be incorporated during training rather than applied retrospectively.
