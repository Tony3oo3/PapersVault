## Problem

MoE models rely on static routing decisions at inference, which become suboptimal under distribution shifts in deployment. Existing test-time adaptation methods either target dense models or require external reference data (e.g., C3PO needs 100 retrieved samples), making them impractical for data-free, online MoE adaptation. This paper asks whether MoE expert routing can be continuously refined during generation using only the model's own output as a self-supervised signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and the progressively growing generated context, which serves as the self-supervised training signal for routing optimization. It outputs standard generated text alongside a set of compact additive routing vectors δ (e.g., only 1,792 floats for DeepSeek-V2-Lite) that persist across the generation session and can be stored on-device. The method is plug-and-play: it modifies only router logits without altering the generation process, so it composes with other test-time techniques like self-consistency or in-context learning.

## Method

The framework alternates between two phases in a continuous loop: (1) In-Context Routing Optimization, where cross-entropy loss on the already-generated sequence is minimized with respect to lightweight additive vectors δ^(l) added to router logits, and (2) Steered Generation, where text is produced normally under the modified routing until the next optimization cycle (every 96–128 tokens). To prevent over-adaptation, only a subset of MoE layers is updated, selected by an expert-specialization score (negative log of top-k expert probabilities, distinct from Shannon entropy) that identifies layers with concentrated, confident routing. Critically, the method optimizes only the bias-like additive term rather than the full router weight matrix—full linear optimization causes complete performance collapse, while even rank-1 LoRA underperforms the baseline. Each optimization cycle uses just 5 Adam steps with a small learning rate (0.05), keeping the per-cycle overhead to a few additional prefill passes on already-generated text.

## Results

The method achieves consistent improvements across three MoE models (DeepSeek-V2-Lite, OLMoE, Qwen1.5-MoE) and five reasoning benchmarks. On HumanEval, gains of 3.6%, 5.5%, and 6.7% are observed on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, OLMoE, and Qwen1.5-MoE respectively. Average scores improve from 50.89% to 53.59% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite), 33.56% to 36.13% (OLMoE), and 41.75% to 43.66% (Qwen1.5-MoE), outperforming all baselines on every benchmark. When combined with self-consistency (3 paths), the method achieves a 57.09% average on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, a 6% gain over self-consistency alone (54.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all three baselines (ICL 3-shot, ICL 5-shot, and C3PO with 100 reference samples) on every benchmark across all three MoE models. Against ICL, the advantage is most pronounced on OLMoE where 5-shot ICL actually degrades HumanEval from 28.66% to 21.21%, while the method lifts it to 34.17%; on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, ICL(3-shot) drops MMLU from 50.77% to 44.33% whereas the method improves it to 52.40%. Against C3PO, which requires 100 reference samples per dataset, the method achieves superior results without any external data — e.g., on DeepSeek-V2-Lite GSM8K, C3PO drops from 72.10% to 68.80% while the method reaches 73.62%. The method also complements self-consistency: combining rerouting with 3-path self-consistency yields a 57.09% average on DeepSeek-V2-Lite versus 54.88% for self-consistency alone, a 2.2-point gain. In terms of efficiency, the method uses 1.3× fewer FLOPs than C3PO and 1.6× fewer than ICL(5-shot) while delivering the best accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation literature for MoE models by departing from C3PO (Li et al., 2025c), which requires external reference sets and performs a single static routing correction, toward a fully data-free, continuous online framework that alternates between self-supervised routing optimization and steered generation. It also diverges from broader TTT approaches (Hardt & Sun; Hubotter et al.) that target dense models and rely on retrieved neighbors, by operating exclusively on the model's own generated context with no retrieval overhead. The method repurposes the concept of lightweight parameter steering (additive bias vectors on router logits) from the prompt-tuning/steering literature, applying it specifically to MoE gating decisions rather than to hidden states or output layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, data-free nature of the approach makes it directly deployable in production MoE systems where access to reference data or retrieval infrastructure is unavailable, and the minimal parameter footprint (e.g., 1,792 floats for DeepSeek-V2-Lite) enables per-conversation adaptation at negligible storage cost. Key limitations include reliance on self-supervision from the model's own (potentially erroneous) generations, which risks confirmation bias, and the restriction to routing-only updates that cannot correct fundamentally misrouted expert knowledge. Open questions include whether richer objectives (e.g., real-time reward signals) or longer optimization horizons could yield larger gains, and whether the approach transfers to non-top-k routing schemes or multimodal MoE architectures.
