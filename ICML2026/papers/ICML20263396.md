## Problem

Standard XAI methods (LIME, SHAP) attribute MPC control decisions to current-state features, missing the defining characteristic of predictive control: actions driven by anticipated future constraint violations rather than present deviations. No prior framework unifies the three complementary evidence sources required to explain this temporal causality — which optimization constraints bind, what physical mechanisms drive the predicted violation, and when historical disturbance patterns triggered the response.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263396/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires explicit access to the MPC optimization problem (not a black-box controller): it consumes solver-returned KKT multipliers, the optimal action, measured state, and disturbance forecast at each decision instant, alongside a pre-constructed domain knowledge graph (signed directed edges between states, controls, and disturbances) and a PCMCI-learned temporal causal graph built offline from 1–3 months of operational history. Output is a structured four-part explanation — primary hypothesis type (safety/optimization/prediction/economics/history), mathematical evidence (multiplier magnitudes), counterfactual justification (predicted trajectory without the action), and physical context (KG causal pathway) — synthesized into natural language by an LLM that performs fluency assembly rather than causal reasoning.

## Method

HCA integrates three evidence sources through a hierarchical hypothesis-ranking architecture: KKT multipliers identify active constraints and rank them by cost sensitivity (∂J*/∂c_i), knowledge-graph traversal recovers physical causal chains linking forecast disturbances to binding constraints, and PCMCI queries check whether parent variables showed >2σ deviations at their discovered lags. Five candidate hypotheses (safety → optimization → prediction → economics → history) are evaluated in configurable priority order, with the first evidence-supported hypothesis selected as primary. Causal necessity is validated via two counterfactual MPC re-solves: CF-A (force u=0, verify constraint violation) certifies the action was needed, and CF-B (relax the identified constraint, verify the optimal action changes) pinpoints which specific constraint drove the decision. The framework is domain-transferable — PCMCI parameters (τ_max=48, α=0.05) and LLM settings are calibrated once on greenhouse data and reused unchanged across building HVAC and chemical process domains — with optional 2–3 day KKT threshold calibration recovering Answer Correctness from 0.478 to ~0.88.

## Results

HCA achieves Answer Correctness (AC) of 0.478 on Greenhouse, 0.394 on Building HVAC, and 0.406 on Tennessee Eastman Process using a single set of cross-domain hyperparameters (PCMCI τ_max=48, α=0.05, LLM temperature=0.3). This represents a 54% AC improvement over LIME (0.478 vs. 0.311 on Greenhouse) and outperforms all other baselines (SHAP, IOC, RETAIN, LSTM+Attention, MPC-XAI) across all three domains. With domain-specific KKT-threshold calibration (2–3 days), AC further improves to approximately 0.88 on both Building HVAC (0.894) and TEP (0.880). Expert evaluation (155 ratings) yields moderate scores: Clarity 3.43/5.0, Accuracy 3.29/5.0, Trust 3.21/5.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCA outperforms all four primary baselines on AC across all three domains. Against LIME (Ribeiro et al., 2016), HCA achieves 0.478 vs. 0.311 on Greenhouse (+54%), 0.394 vs. 0.225 on Building (+75%), and 0.406 vs. 0.356 on TEP (+14%). Against SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017), the gap is even wider: 0.478 vs. 0.304 (Greenhouse), 0.394 vs. 0.206 (Building), and 0.406 vs. 0.308 (TEP). Against IOC (Porcari et al., 2025), HCA leads on Greenhouse (0.478 vs. 0.354) and Building (0.394 vs. 0.316) but trails slightly on TEP (0.406 vs. 0.335); notably, IOC achieves near-perfect Faithfulness (F≈0.9–1.0) by copying context verbatim, while HCA prioritizes causal depth over surface-level alignment. Against RETAIN (Choi et al., 2016), HCA wins on all three domains (0.478/0.394/0.406 vs. 0.312/0.322/0.350). The paper also reports that on the 38.7% of Greenhouse scenarios involving predicted future violations (temporal causality), LIME and SHAP drop below AC=0.25 while HCA maintains AC>0.45, confirming that the advantage is concentrated precisely where feature-attribution methods are architecturally unable to reason.

## Relation to Prior Work

HCA sits at the intersection of three research threads that have each addressed part of the MPC explanation problem in isolation: KKT multiplier analysis provides mathematical rigor for constraint activity but is opaque to operators; PCMCI-based temporal causal discovery identifies lagged dependencies in operational data but ignores the optimizer's internal logic; and domain knowledge graphs encode physical mechanisms but cannot determine which are active at a given timestep. The paper departs from feature-attribution XAI (LIME, SHAP) by arguing these methods are fundamentally mismatched to predictive control, where actions are driven by anticipated future violations rather than current-state features. Its core architectural contribution is the hierarchical hypothesis-ranking pipeline that fuses all three evidence sources with counterfactual MPC re-solves as a validation oracle, a combination the authors argue no prior work achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, verifiable pathway to explaining safety-critical control decisions, with the KKT/counterfactual oracle enabling ground-truth evaluation of explanation correctness in a way that black-box XAI cannot. The large gap between domain-transferable performance (AC≈0.478) and calibrated performance (AC≈0.88) is practically important: it identifies threshold calibration, not architectural redesign, as the bottleneck for deployment, giving engineers a concrete 2–3 day tuning target. Key open questions include extending the framework to learned controllers (neural MPC, model-based RL) where no explicit optimization oracle exists, enriching counterfactuals beyond null-input to address timing and partial-action questions, and reducing the 12.1% failure rate driven by temporal-attribution mismatches in rare-event scenarios.
