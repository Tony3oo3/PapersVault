## Problem

Existing panel Granger causality tests (DH, HPJ) aggregate unit-level evidence via averaging or quadratic forms, which dilutes strong but localized signals when Granger causality is present in only a small subset of panel units (sparse alternatives). This power loss is particularly severe in high-dimensional panels where N exceeds T, a regime increasingly common in firm-level and user-level data. Additionally, DH and HPJ are valid under different asymptotic regimes, leaving practitioners without a single reliable test across all panel configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a linear dynamic panel with N cross-sectional units over T periods (allowing N > T), a known small lag order K, and covariate/response series x_{i,t}, y_{i,t} per unit. It produces a composite test statistic T_PE-PGCT = T_pivotal + J_PE, a p-value against the null of no Granger causality for all units, and—uniquely—a list of specific panel units whose individual Wald statistics exceed the PE threshold, identifying where causal signals are concentrated. The pivotal test is selected data-adaptively: DH when N < T−K, HPJ when N ≥ T−K.

## Method

The core contribution is a Power Enhancement (PE) component J_PE = √N · M_Wald · I{M_Wald > δ_{N,T}}, where M_Wald = max_i W_i is the maximum of individual unit-level Wald statistics and δ_{N,T} = (K + 2√(K log N) + 2 log N)·max{1.5, log log T} is a calibrated threshold separating estimation noise from genuine signals. This component is added to any existing pivotal panel Granger test, yielding T_PE-PGCT = T_pivotal + J_PE. Under H0, J_PE equals zero with probability tending to one (no size distortion), while under sparse alternatives where at least one unit's signal strength exceeds (η log log T log N)/T, J_PE diverges at rate √N, delivering consistent power. The theoretical foundation rests on non-asymptotic Kolmogorov bounds for the maximum of N Wald statistics via high-dimensional Gaussian approximation, random-design linearization, and studentization error control, establishing that the PE component is provably at least as powerful as the baseline test in all configurations.

## Results

Under the null hypothesis, PE-PGCT maintains correct Type I error (≈0.05) across all (T, N) configurations, while DH inflates to 0.187 at (T=50, N=500) and HPJ inflates to 0.171 at (T=50, N=5). Under sparse alternatives (N₀=1 signal-bearing units), PE-PGCT delivers substantial power gains over both baselines, with HPJ being nearly powerless. In the real-data FDIC application (N=300 banks, quarterly 2006–2019), PE-PGCT achieves p < 10⁻¹⁶ for cost-efficiency → profitability Granger causality in nearly all sub-panels, and the PE component identifies specific banks (e.g., First State Bank of San Diego, Solvay Bank) with strong unit-level causal signals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the Dumitrescu-Hurlin (DH) test, PE-PGCT wins decisively on Type I error control in the high-dimensional regime (N ≥ T−K): DH's rejection rate inflates to 0.187 at (T=50, N=500) while PE-PGCT stays at 0.052. On power under sparse alternatives (N₀=1), PE-PGCT dominates DH across all (T, N) settings; DH's power deteriorates markedly when T ≤ N. Under dense alternatives (large N₀), PE-PGCT is comparable to or slightly below DH, consistent with the theoretical design where averaging-based tests favor dense signals. Compared to the Half-Panel Jackknife (HPJ) test, PE-PGCT wins on Type I error when N is small (HPJ reaches 0.189 at T=100, N=5 vs. PE-PGCT's 0.044) and wins overwhelmingly on power under sparse alternatives, where HPJ is described as 'nearly powerless.' The data-adaptive pivotal test selection (DH when N < T−K, HPJ when N ≥ T−K) ensures PE-PGCT inherits the correct null distribution in each regime, making it robust across the full range of panel dimensions without requiring size correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two established panel Granger causality tests: the Dumitrescu-Hurlin (DH) test, which averages individual Wald statistics across units, and the Half-Panel Jackknife (HPJ) test, which uses a quadratic-form aggregation. It adapts the power enhancement (PE) framework of Fan et al. (2015) from high-dimensional cross-sectional testing to the panel Granger causality setting, introducing a max-of-Wald-statistics component with a carefully calibrated threshold. The key methodological departure from prior PE work is the treatment of the asymptotic distribution of the maximum of N Wald statistics in high-dimensional panels (N potentially exceeding T), requiring new Gaussian approximation and anti-concentration arguments, and the data-adaptive selection of the pivotal test based on the N vs. T regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important limitation: existing PGCTs (DH, HPJ) substantially lose power when Granger causality is present in only a small subset of panel units, a scenario common in firm-level or user-level data. The PE-PGCT provides a plug-in enhancement that is provably at least as powerful as the baseline while delivering consistent power under sparse alternatives, with a clean application to FDIC bank data identifying specific institutions with strong causal signals. Key limitations include the cross-sectional independence assumption, the stationarity requirement, and the growth condition log N = o(T^{1/7}); extending to cross-sectional dependence and non-stationary settings are identified as important open problems requiring substantially different technical tools.
