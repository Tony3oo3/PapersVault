## Problem

Unsupervised and supervised tensor network (TN) learning have developed largely in parallel, and existing attempts to borrow ideas between them remain sub-optimal: most TN structure search methods find only a single structure, lacking the flexibility needed when different layers exhibit varying redundancy, and DL concepts (depth, nonlinearity, width) have been only shallowly incorporated into TN models. The paper asks whether a unified Bayesian framework can simultaneously achieve expressive multi-TN modeling, flexible multi-structure search, and efficient uncertainty quantification.

## Input / Output

For tensor recovery, the input is an incomplete high-order tensor (e.g., multispectral images from CAVE at 85% or 95% missing rates), where each observed element is encoded as an outer product of basis vectors indicating its position, and the output is the completed tensor. For PEFT, the input is a pre-trained LLaMA2-7B model plus DROP dataset fine-tuning pairs, and the output is a fine-tuned model with learned TN structures replacing fixed low-rank factors. For tensor regression, 20×20 Gaussian-distributed matrices serve as inputs, producing point predictions plus predictive variance via MC-dropout sampling.

## Method

MTNL stacks L layers, each containing E SVD-inspired TNs with threshold matrices, connected by activation functions and optional skip connections, unifying unsupervised tensor recovery and supervised learning under one Bayesian model. A Gaussian-GIG prior is placed over the threshold vectors (rather than directly on tensor cores as in prior work), yielding a generalized hyperbolic marginal that flexibly reduces to various sparsity-inducing distributions; Theorem 2.1 proves this design corrects a flaw where prior methods impose sparsity separately on paired cores rather than jointly. Theorem 2.2 shows the MAP objective implicitly enforces an ℓ_{2/T} norm on rank-one coefficient tensors, promoting compact structures with adaptive layer-wise reweighting that allocates more parameters to less compressible layers. Theorem 3.1 establishes that the MAP solution upper-bounds a dropout-regularized objective, enabling lightweight MC-dropout uncertainty quantification that requires only post-hoc Bernoulli sampling on threshold matrices, reducing computation by up to 200× compared to SVGD.

## Results

On tensor recovery over four CAVE multispectral images, MTNL achieves the best average RSE of 0.0414 (85% missing) and 0.1143 (95% missing), outperforming the strongest baseline BayesTNSS (0.0465 / 0.1219) by roughly 11% and 6% respectively. On LLM PEFT (LLaMA2-7B on DROP), the rank-pruned variant MTNL_l reaches an F1 score of 60.1 with only 0.044% of full-tuning parameters, surpassing QuanTA (59.5 F1 at 0.041% params) and LoRETTA (59.1 F1 at 1.254% params). For uncertainty quantification, MTNL-dropout achieves an NLL of 1.984 on 64×64 tensor regression, matching or exceeding SVGD which requires up to 280× more computation to reach comparable NLL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTNL outperforms all six tensor recovery baselines on every MSI at both missing rates. The closest competitor, BayesTNSS (Zeng et al., 2024b), is beaten by 11% in average RSE at 85% missing (0.0414 vs 0.0465) and 6% at 95% missing (0.1143 vs 0.1219); the gap is largest on MSI3 where MTNL achieves 0.0617 vs 0.0732 at 85%. Among non-Bayesian methods, BTT (Xu et al., 2023) and TW (Wu et al., 2022) are the strongest, but MTNL still leads by 25% and 27% in average RSE at 85% respectively. For PEFT on DROP, MTNL_l (F1=60.1, 0.044% params) surpasses QuanTA (Chen et al., 2024a; F1=59.5, 0.041% params) by 0.6 F1 at nearly identical parameter budgets, and dominates LoRETTA (Yang et al., 2024; F1=59.1, 1.254% params) by 1.0 F1 while using ~28× fewer parameters. For uncertainty quantification, MTNL-dropout (NLL=1.984) matches SVGD (Hawkins & Zhang, 2021) which requires 240× more computation to reach NLL=9.836 and 280× to reach −0.083, demonstrating a 200–280× computational advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTNL builds on the SVD-inspired TN decomposition (Zheng et al., 2024) as its core representation and extends the Bayesian TN-SS framework of Zeng et al. (2024b) by replacing the direct Gaussian-GIG prior on tensor cores with a prior over threshold vectors, which Theorem 2.1 shows yields a theoretically more consistent joint sparsity penalty across paired cores. It also departs from prior TN-SS methods that search for a single structure by formulating a multi-TN structure search problem, and from LoRA-style PEFT by replacing fixed low-rank factorization with adaptively learned TN structures. The unification of unsupervised tensor recovery and supervised PEFT under a single Bayesian deep-network-style architecture is the paper's central conceptual contribution, drawing on depth, nonlinearity, and width concepts from modern DL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled bridge between two previously parallel TN research directions, and the implicit structure learning theorem (Theorem 2.2) offers theoretical justification for why the model adaptively allocates parameters across layers—aligning with empirical observations in PEFT. The MC-Dropout-based uncertainty quantification, requiring only 8–9% of training time and achieving comparable NLL to SVGD at up to 200× lower cost, is a practical contribution for large-scale TN learning where full Bayesian posterior estimation is infeasible. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (e.g., ~10% relative RSE improvement in tensor completion, ~0.5 F1 point in PEFT), and the authors acknowledge that the added modeling complexity raises open questions about optimization efficiency and scalability to even larger networks.
