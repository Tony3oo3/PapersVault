## Problem

Bidirectional video diffusion models achieve strong global coherence but are computationally expensive, while autoregressive models enable fast streaming generation but suffer from error accumulation and weak long-range consistency. No existing framework allows a single trained model to flexibly operate under both paradigms, forcing practitioners to choose one inference regime and accept its trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces a 5-second video at 832×432 (81 pixel frames, 21 latent frames) or a 30-second long video, with the chunking configuration (e.g., [15,3,3] or [7,7,7]) serving as a test-time hyperparameter that controls the quality-speed trade-off. For editing, the input is a fully generated video plus a target temporal segment and optional timestep range; the output is a re-edited segment that preserves coherence with unedited neighboring frames, supporting both any-order (editing middle segments without regenerating the full sequence) and any-timestep (restricting edits to refinement steps only) modes.

## Method

Flex-Forcing introduces flexible chunking defined over two orthogonal axes—temporal frames and denoising timesteps—so that autoregressive (chunk size 1) and bidirectional (single chunk) inference emerge as extreme cases of a unified sampling equation. During training, a bidirectional video diffusion model (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) is post-trained via ODE initialization followed by asymmetric DMD distillation, where the chunk partition is randomly sampled each rollout, exposing the model to a continuous spectrum from strictly causal to fully non-causal attention. A key component is K-Projection, a lightweight timestep-conditioned linear layer that projects clean KV-cache states from past chunks into the noisy latent space of the current denoising step, resolving the signal-to-noise mismatch between causal and non-causal attention contexts. At inference, the chunk size can vary per denoising step (e.g., large chunks early for global structure, smaller chunks later for local detail), and the model can condition on both past and future clean tokens to enable any-order, any-timestep video editing without regenerating the full sequence.

## Results

On 5-second video generation (VBench), Flex-Forcing's best configuration (15-3-3 chunking) achieves a total score of 85.07 (Quality 86.33, Semantic 80.02) at 25.8 FPS with 5 NFE, outperforming Self-Forcing (84.31, 24.9 FPS) and the bidirectional Wan2.1 baseline (84.26, 1.3 FPS). The speed-optimized (7-7-7) configuration reaches 84.63 total at 29.4 FPS. On 30-second long-video generation (VBench-Long), Flex-Forcing built on Infinity-RoPE achieves 84.01 total and 85.30 quality at 24.96 FPS, surpassing Self-Forcing (81.49 total, 19.10 FPS) and LongLive (82.80 total, 25.07 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flex-Forcing outperforms Self-Forcing (the primary autoregressive baseline) by +0.76 total VBench (85.07 vs 84.31) and +0.9 FPS (25.8 vs 24.9) in the best configuration, while also beating it on the 30s VBench-Long benchmark (84.01 vs 81.49 total). Against the bidirectional Wan2.1 baseline, Flex-Forcing achieves a higher total score (85.07 vs 84.26) at roughly 20× the inference speed (25.8 vs 1.3 FPS). Compared to CausVid, Flex-Forcing improves total score by +3.87 points and semantic score by +10.22 points. In the few-step distillation comparison (Table 3), Flex-Forcing at 4 steps (85.13 total) surpasses DMD-v (84.60) and rCM (84.43), and at 2 steps (84.20) remains competitive with rCM (84.09). On 30s long-video generation, Flex-Forcing beats LongLive on total (84.01 vs 82.80) and quality (85.30 vs 83.21) but trails on semantic alignment (78.86 vs 81.17), and it outperforms Infinity-RoPE on total and quality while matching its FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the causal distillation pipeline established by CausVid and Self-Forcing, which convert bidirectional video diffusion models into strictly autoregressive ones via ODE initialization and DMD-based asymmetric distillation. The key departure is that instead of committing to a single causal or non-causal attention pattern, Flex-Forcing introduces flexible chunking along both the temporal and denoising axes, treating autoregressive and bidirectional inference as two extremes of a continuous spectrum within one model. A novel K-Projection mechanism addresses the noise-level mismatch that arises when a query attends to both clean (causal past) and noisy (non-causal future) key-value states, a problem absent in purely causal or purely bidirectional settings. For long-video inference, the method builds on Infinity-RoPE's positional encoding strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying bidirectional and autoregressive generation in a single model, Flex-Forcing gives practitioners a test-time dial for the quality-efficiency trade-off rather than forcing a binary architectural choice, which is practically significant for deployment across heterogeneous hardware budgets. The any-order, any-timestep editing capability is a genuinely new application enabled by the hybrid attention, opening a path toward localized video editing without full regeneration. However, the authors acknowledge that training-inference mismatch persists and that the method's effectiveness depends heavily on strong bidirectional priors inherited from pre-training, limiting transferability to models lacking such priors. Open questions include whether the flexible chunking strategy generalizes to architectures trained natively autoregressive without a bidirectional teacher, and whether the residual exposure bias under fine-grained chunking can be further mitigated.
