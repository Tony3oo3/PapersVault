## Problem

Existing model compression methods (quantization, pruning, low-rank factorization) produce fixed-size models or require independently training separate submodels for each deployment budget, making adaptive deployment across heterogeneous hardware costly and impractical. No prior method provides a principled, nested mechanism to extract a family of Pareto-optimal submodels from a single pretrained model's weights without retraining from scratch for each budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265684/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FLEXRANK takes as input a pretrained model (GPT-2, Llama-3.2/3.1, or DINOv3 ViT), a small calibration set for collecting per-layer activations (a few hundred samples suffice), and a set of target deployment budgets. It produces a single set of shared low-rank factorized weights (U_l, V_l per layer) together with a Pareto front of nested rank configurations; at deployment, a budget β selects a rank per layer, and the Gauge-Aligned Reparametrization (GAR) transforms the factors to avoid storing or multiplying the r×r identity block, reducing per-layer inference cost to O((m+n−r)·r) FLOPs.

## Method

FLEXRANK operates in three stages: (1) each linear layer is decomposed via a data-aware SVD (DataSVD) that whitens weights by the activation covariance, yielding ordered, nested rank components; (2) a dynamic-programming procedure (framed as a multi-choice knapsack under an additive-error assumption) selects globally near-optimal rank assignments across layers for each budget in O(L·K) time rather than O(K^L) brute force; (3) all nested submodels are jointly refined via knowledge distillation from the frozen base model, with submodels sampled proportionally to budget weights. The core theoretical contribution proves that nested submodel training (NSL) is necessary for Pareto-optimal elasticity: post-training selection (PTS) yields zero-measure optimal submodels (Thm. 4.1), all-subspaces learning (ASL) incurs a strictly positive optimality gap (Thm. 4.2), while NSL provably recovers the true Pareto front (Thm. 4.3). The GAR reparameterization exploits the non-uniqueness of the (U,V) factorization to eliminate the dense r×r block from inference, enabling computational savings without aggressive rank reduction.

## Results

On ImageNet1K classification with DINOv3 ViTs, FLEXRANK maintains within a 5% accuracy margin of the full model even when pruning up to 70% of parameters, and compressing to 30% of the original model size remains close to full-model performance. On LLM commonsense benchmarks (lm-eval-harness), FLEXRANK exhibits the most graceful accuracy degradation across parameter budgets compared to SVD-based and ACIP baselines, which degrade sharply after removing just 20% of parameters. In post-adaptation settings with LoRA fine-tuning, Llama-3.2-3B submodels retain 40.49% math accuracy at full size (vs. 40.56% base) and 15.54% at 0.4× size, demonstrating meaningful performance even at aggressive compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACIP (Genzel et al., 2025), the current state-of-the-art low-rank elastic method, FLEXRANK achieves more graceful degradation across all parameter budgets on both LLM and ViT models; ACIP's LoRA adapters provide limited gains and can actually hinder full-budget recovery due to ASL-like interference dynamics, while FLEXRANK's nested distillation avoids this. Against LLM-PRUNER (Ma et al., 2023, structured pruning) and LAYERSKIP (Elhoushi et al., 2024, variable depth), FLEXRANK is competitive on Llama-3.2-1B, notably while using only 5B training tokens versus LAYERSKIP's 839B tokens (a 167× reduction). FLEXRANK also slightly outperforms a strong non-elastic baseline that independently trains the same submodels from the same DataSVD initialization at matched total budget, suggesting that weight sharing across nested submodels provides a small but consistent advantage. Pure SVD-based methods (including DataSVD without the DP search and nested training) degrade sharply after 20% parameter removal, confirming the theoretical analysis that post-training selection alone is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLEXRANK builds on data-aware SVD initialization (DRONE, Chen et al. 2021) and low-rank compression methods (SVD-LLM, ASVD, A3), but departs fundamentally from the post-training selection paradigm by introducing nested submodel training as the core mechanism. It also connects to the elastic network literature (FlexTron, LlamaFlex, Once-for-All, Matformer), which constructs multiple model sizes from a shared backbone, but operates entirely in the factorized parameter space rather than varying architectural components. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical proof that both post-training selection and all-submodel learning provably fail to recover Pareto-optimal submodels, while nested training succeeds, combined with a dynamic-programming procedure for Pareto-optimal rank allocation across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'train-once, deploy-everywhere' paradigm with provably nested, Pareto-optimal submodels addresses a practical gap in deploying large models across heterogeneous hardware without retraining per budget. The GAR reparameterization provides a general, drop-in technique for reducing inference cost of any rank-r factorization, applicable beyond FLEXRANK itself. Limitations include roughly 2× memory overhead during training (factors span full rank), no evaluation of adaptive routing policies, and performance still bounded by consolidation training quality. Open directions include combining rank-based elasticity with architectural elasticity (depth, width), input-adaptive routing, and better optimization tailored to jointly adapting nested submodels.
