## Problem

Existing simulation-based inference methods rely on brute-force fusion strategies that ignore the structural disparity between low-dimensional physical parameters and high-dimensional sensor observations, limiting posterior fidelity. While MCMC provides asymptotically exact posteriors, it is prohibitively slow for time-sensitive scientific inference, and current amortized neural approaches (e.g., FMPE) compress observations into static embeddings, failing to faithfully recover complex multimodal posterior structures in real-world inverse problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes heterogeneous inputs: a set of N scalar physical parameters (each individually tokenized into K embedding tokens) and an M-dimensional observation vector (projected into N_x context tokens), plus a forward simulator used at inference time for likelihood scoring. The output is a full posterior distribution over the N-dimensional parameter space, generated as samples from a learned flow-matching transport. Notably, the simulator is invoked during sampling (not just training) to compute intermediate log-posterior rewards that steer particle trajectories, and the observation tokens remain accessible at every integration step rather than being compressed into a single static embedding.

## Method

FUSE employs a dual-track Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) architecture where parameter tokens and observation tokens are processed through modality-specific projections before being concatenated in joint self-attention at each layer, preserving distinct structural features while enabling bidirectional cross-modal interaction. The model is trained with a conditional rectified-flow objective, learning a velocity field that transports Gaussian noise to the posterior via linear interpolation paths. At inference, a Feynman-Kac (FK) steering strategy maintains B parallel particles propagated by a stochastic SDE sampler, resampling them at intermediate timesteps using simulator-based log-posterior rewards evaluated at denoised proxy states (θ_t − t·v_φ). This trajectory-level likelihood guidance concentrates computation on high-density posterior regions, improving mode accuracy and credible-region overlap without sacrificing the speed advantage of amortized inference.

## Results

On the SBIBM benchmark (10 tasks, 10⁵ simulation budget), FUSE achieves the best score on all seven metrics: ℓ-C2ST 0.59 (vs. 0.63–0.66 for baselines), MMD 0.016, KL divergence 0.28, Sinkhorn distance 0.077, PME 0.06, PVR 1.27, and MEDDIST 1.55. On the real-world β Pictoris b orbital characterization task, FUSE with FK-steering improves the 95% credible-region IoU from 0.52 (without FK) to 0.62 and reduces the posterior mode L2 distance from 6.60 to 3.85, while baselines FMPE and NPE yield mode distances of 132.41 and 75.74 respectively. FUSE completes the orbital inference in ~3 minutes versus ~8.5 hours for the reference PTMCMC chain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE (without FK-steering) outperforms all three baselines on every SBIBM metric. Against NPE (Neural Spline Flows), FUSE reduces KL divergence from 0.89 to 0.28 and PME from 0.19 to 0.06, while improving PVR from 9.14 to 1.27, indicating far better variance calibration. Against FMPE (flow matching with ResNet), FUSE halves the MMD (0.016 vs. 0.033) and cuts KL by 70% (0.28 vs. 0.94). Against Simformer (transformer-based), FUSE achieves a lower ℓ-C2ST (0.59 vs. 0.63) and substantially better PME (0.06 vs. 0.20) and PVR (1.27 vs. 1.55). On the β Pictoris b exoplanet task, the gap widens dramatically: FUSE with FK-steering achieves a 95% credible-region IoU of 0.62 and mode L2 distance of 3.85, whereas FMPE and NPE produce mode distances of 132.41 and 75.74, indicating near-complete failure to resolve the sharp orientation-parameter degeneracies (inclination i, longitude of ascending node Ω) that FUSE captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSE adapts the MMDiT dual-track architecture (Esser et al., 2024) from image generation to simulation-based inference, replacing the brute-force observation-to-embedding compression used by FMPE with a modality-preserving joint-attention design that lets parameter tokens dynamically query observation tokens at every layer. The generative backbone is a conditional rectified-flow velocity field (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu, 2022), and the inference-time correction borrows the interval-resampling Feynman-Kac steering framework from Singhal et al. (2025), repurposing it to inject simulator likelihoods into the flow trajectory rather than into a diffusion denoising step. The key conceptual departure from prior SBI methods is treating parameters and observations as structurally distinct modalities with bidirectional interaction, rather than concatenating or compressing them into a single representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-minute inference time versus ~8.5 hours for PTMCMC on the β Pictoris b orbital characterization task makes real-time ephemeris updating feasible as new astrometric data arrive, which is practically significant for time-sensitive astronomy. However, the method remains an amortized approximation without MCMC's asymptotic exactness, the MM-DiT backbone is data-hungry and underperforms lighter models in extremely low-simulation regimes, and tail coverage of the posterior is not formally guaranteed by the finite-particle FK sampler. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-planet systems or high-dimensional gravitational-wave parameter spaces, and whether sequential or observation-specific variants can further close the gap to exact inference.
