## Problem

While AI-generated image (AIGI) detection has advanced substantially, correcting detected images with visible artifacts to restore realistic appearance remains largely underexplored, and existing correction pipelines rely on frozen inpainting models guided by bounding boxes or masks, which introduces seams and limits quality. Crucially, prior work treats detection and correction as isolated tasks, overlooking their natural mutual reinforcement: detection provides diagnostic guidance for targeted repair, while correction's generative prior sharpens sensitivity to subtle artifacts.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 512–1024 px for correction, 378–980 px for detection) plus a text instruction as input. For detection, it autoregressively emits a structured text answer with three tagged fields: an authenticity label (<detect>), an image caption (<caption>), and an evidence-based justification (<reason>). For correction in VCoT mode, the output is an interleaved multi-round sequence of diagnostic text tokens and flow-matched image tokens, terminating when the model generates a stopping diagnosis stating no artifacts remain; the number of rounds is adaptive (typically 2–4).

## Method

GenShield adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture built on BAGEL, with two specialized experts—an AI Image Detection Expert and an Artifact Correction Expert—operating on a shared decoder-style multimodal backbone with shared multi-head self-attention at every layer, enabling bidirectional information flow between understanding and generation. Detection is formulated as autoregressive structured-text generation (next-token prediction), while correction uses a flow-matching velocity network conditioned on diagnostic text to produce the repaired image. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 jointly optimizes detection and instruction-guided correction (explicit artifact description → corrected image) to establish stable generative priors; Stage 2 upgrades correction to iterative Visual Chain-of-Thought self-correction, where the model alternates between generating its own repair diagnosis and applying a targeted edit, re-feeding the result until an explicit termination criterion is met. The key insight is that shared self-attention lets the correction expert inject camera-consistent generative priors that align detection features with real-image statistics, while the detection expert propagates repair-oriented diagnostic descriptions as explicit guidance for more precise, semantically preserving edits.

## Results

On the Holmes-Set AIGI detection benchmark, GenShield achieves a mean accuracy of 98.8% and mean A.P. of 99.8% across 10 generative models, outperforming the best non-LLM detector AIDE (97.0% Acc, 99.7% A.P.) and the best LLM-based detector AIGI-Holmes (95.6% Acc, 99.5% A.P.). On the SynthScars artifact correction benchmark, GenShield's multi-step VCoT correction achieves the lowest mean artifact score (0.15 GPT-assisted, 0.16 human) and the highest objective quality scores (HPSv3 6.20, CLIP-Score 22.12, PickScore 18.86), surpassing all closed-source editors (e.g., GPT-Image: 0.24/0.23 mean artifact, HPSv3 6.09) and open-source editors (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit: 0.53/0.41, HPSv3 5.99). On the broader AIGCDetectBenchmark covering 16 generators, GenShield attains the best mean accuracy of 94.93%, ahead of AIGI-Holmes (93.16%) and AIDE (92.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenShield outperforms all 24 baselines on both detection and correction tasks. In AIGI detection on Holmes-Set, it surpasses the strongest non-LLM detector AIDE by 1.8 points in mean accuracy (98.8% vs. 97.0%) and 0.1 in A.P. (99.8% vs. 99.7%), and beats the best LLM-based detector AIGI-Holmes by 3.2 points in accuracy (98.8% vs. 95.6%), with particularly large margins on FLUX (97.9% vs. 79.5%) and Janus-Pro-7B (99.4% vs. 93.4%). Among non-LLM baselines, RINE (96.2% mean Acc.) and LaRE (85.0%) trail by 2.6 and 13.8 points respectively, while older methods like CNNSpot (72.9%) and NPR (84.0%) show the largest gaps. In artifact correction on SynthScars, GenShield's multi-step VCoT correction achieves a mean artifact score of 0.15 (GPT-assisted) and 0.16 (human), beating the best closed-source editor GPT-Image (0.24/0.23) by roughly 38–31% and the best open-source editor Qwen-Image-Edit (0.53/0.41) by over 3×. On objective quality metrics, GenShield leads HPSv3 (6.20 vs. 6.09 for GPT-Image), CLIP-Score (22.12 vs. 22.07 for FLUX-Pro), and PickScore (18.86 vs. 18.71 for Nano-Banana-Pro). The single-step variant (Ours*) already matches or exceeds all baselines, confirming that the iterative VCoT strategy provides additional gains (0.23→0.15 mean artifact score) rather than being the sole source of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenShield builds directly on BAGEL's Mixture-of-Transformers architecture (Deng et al., 2025), repurposing its dual-expert design with shared self-attention for a forensic task rather than general multimodal understanding/generation. It departs from prior AIGI detection work (AIGI-Holmes, AIDE, UniFD) that treats detection as an isolated classification or VQA task, and from artifact correction pipelines (LEGION, HumanRefiner) that rely on frozen inpainting models guided by bounding boxes or masks. The key conceptual departure is framing detection and correction as a mutually reinforcing closed loop within a single autoregressive model, and introducing a Visual Chain-of-Thought curriculum that decomposes correction into iterative diagnose-then-repair steps with an explicit stopping criterion—neither of which appears in prior synthetic-image forensics literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying explainable detection and mask-free artifact correction in one model, GenShield shifts AI image forensics from passive real/fake verification toward active authenticity restoration, and the GenShield-Set dataset plus its evaluation pipeline fill a concrete gap left by detection-only benchmarks. The demonstrated synergy (joint training outperforming single-task variants on both detection accuracy and correction quality) suggests a broader principle that understanding and generation tasks can be co-optimized for forensic applications. Limitations include dependence on a proprietary editing model for training data quality, difficulty with extreme artifacts (large-scale text corruption, global incoherence), and evaluation on a relatively small correction benchmark (SynthScars); whether the detection-correction synergy generalizes to video, 3D, or weaker base architectures remains open.
