## Problem

While recent post-training methods for Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) have successfully leveraged understanding to enhance generation, the reverse direction—using generation to improve understanding—remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether UMMs' generation capabilities can be harnessed to strengthen their visual understanding, particularly in fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, and hallucination reduction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264264/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed through the UMM's visual understanding encoder) and text prompts as input, and jointly produces four types of output: a reconstructed image, a depth map (normalized to [0,255], 3-channel), a segmentation map (white object boundaries on black background, 3-channel), and a text answer for understanding tasks. Depth targets are precomputed with Depth Anything V2 (ViT-L, 518×518 input) and segmentation targets with SAM (ViT-H, 32×32 point grid, IoU threshold 0.88, stability 0.95). At inference, the model operates identically to a standard UMM with no architectural changes or extra computational overhead.

## Method

UniMRG is an architecture-agnostic post-training method that trains UMMs on four simultaneous tasks: pixel reconstruction, image-to-depth, image-to-segmentation, and standard visual understanding, with a total loss that is a weighted sum of the four individual losses (all weights set to 1). The key insight is that pixel-level reconstruction alone provides weak constraints on intrinsic factors like geometry and structure, so the model is additionally forced to generate depth maps (encoding spatial relations) and segmentation maps (encoding object boundaries and region partitions), which compels it to internalize complementary geometric and structural regularities. These auxiliary generation objectives transfer to stronger understanding without any architectural modifications, working across autoregressive (Show-o), masked-autoregressive (Harmon), and diffusion-based (OpenUni) UMM paradigms. The VQ-VAE and text encoder/decoder are frozen; the understanding encoder is updated only for UMMs with a shared generation-understanding encoder (e.g., Harmon) and frozen otherwise. Training is resource-efficient, requiring only 3–8 hours on 8 H20 GPUs depending on architecture.

## Results

On Harmon-1.5B, UniMRG improves MMBench from 50.43 to 52.23 (+1.80), MMVP from 60.00 to 62.67 (+2.67), HallusionBench from 46.69 to 49.32 (+2.63), and VSR from 60.88 to 61.21 (+0.33), while boosting GenEval from 71.37 to 85.26 (+13.89). On OpenUni-3.6B, the gains are even more pronounced for spatial understanding: VSR jumps from 66.69 to 73.90 (+7.21) and HallusionBench from 60.88 to 64.56 (+3.68). Against SFT, which catastrophically degrades generation (GenEval drops to 0.30 on Harmon), UniMRG preserves and enhances generation while simultaneously improving understanding. On the SOTA comparison (Table 2), OpenUni+UniMRG (2B) achieves 74.67 MMVP, 64.56 HallusionBench, and 73.90 VSR, outperforming all unified models of comparable or larger scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RecA (the closest post-training baseline), UniMRG wins on every understanding metric across all three architectures: on Harmon-1.5B, MMBench 52.23 vs. 49.50, MMVP 62.67 vs. 61.00, HallusionBench 49.32 vs. 47.11, RWQA 51.90 vs. 49.54, and VSR 61.21 vs. 59.00; on OpenUni-3.6B, the VSR gap widens to 73.90 vs. 66.69. On generation, UniMRG slightly trails RecA on DPG for Harmon (85.27 vs. 86.61) but leads on GenEval (85.26 vs. 83.86), and for OpenUni UniMRG leads on DPG (81.78 vs. 81.54) while trailing on GenEval (68.00 vs. 70.07). SFT, which trains only on understanding loss, catastrophically destroys generation (GenEval 0.30 on Harmon) and provides no consistent understanding gains, making it a weak baseline. In the SOTA comparison (Table 2), OpenUni+UniMRG (2B) outperforms all unified models including Janus-Pro (7B: MMVP 74.67 vs. 63.00, VSR 73.90 vs. 71.03), BAGEL (3B: MMVP 74.67 vs. 54.70), UniLIP (2B: Hallusion 64.56 vs. 60.57, VSR 73.90 vs. 65.55), and Emu3-Chat (8B: MMBench 81.44 vs. 64.35, Hallusion 64.56 vs. 52.16). It also surpasses understanding-only models of similar scale: InternVL3 (2B) on MMVP (74.67 vs. 71.67), HallusionBench (64.56 vs. 60.88), and VSR (73.90 vs. 66.69), and Qwen2.5-VL (3B) on both MMBench (81.44 vs. 78.26) and MMVP (74.67 vs. 64.67). The only notable exception is Show-o-1.3B, where gains are marginal (e.g., VSR +0.16) due to its limited 4,096-token VQ codebook constraining representational capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the dominant direction in UMM post-training: while RecA, SRUM, and UniCorn all leverage understanding to improve generation, UniMRG studies the reverse—using auxiliary generation of intrinsic representations (depth, segmentation) to strengthen understanding. It also departs from reconstruction-for-understanding work (DIVA, ROSS) by targeting intrinsic geometric and structural cues rather than pixel-level appearance, and from depth-cue methods (SpatialRGPT, DepthVLA) by requiring no architectural modifications. The method repurposes existing dense-prediction tools (Depth Anything V2, SAM) as generation targets within a multi-task training objective, making it architecture-agnostic across AR, MAR, and diffusion-based UMMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple multi-task post-training recipe (3–8 hours on 8 GPUs) can simultaneously improve fine-grained perception, reduce hallucinations, and strengthen spatial understanding across three distinct UMM architectures is practically valuable for the UMM community. The OOD generalization test on MidjourneyV6 provides evidence that the model genuinely internalizes geometric/structural regularities rather than overfitting. However, gains are moderate (1–7 points), the Show-o VQ-codebook bottleneck reveals a capacity limitation that caps benefits, and only two intrinsic representations are explored—extending to pose, sketches, or video remains open. Whether this generation-to-understanding synergy scales to larger models or more representations is an important open question.
