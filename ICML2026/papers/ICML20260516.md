## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts Transformers are expected to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by localizing updates to sparse expert subsets, yet they still exhibit substantial backward transfer even with well-balanced expert utilization. This paper identifies the root cause as a pre-routing bottleneck: multi-head attention concatenates heterogeneous head-specific signals into a single router input, forcing routing to act on co-occurring feature compositions rather than separable channels, which induces composition collisions that amplify interference across tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token sequences processed through a frozen pretrained Transformer backbone and produces next-token predictions (or generated text for summarization/code tasks). The key structural difference is that the post-attention representation h_t^(l) ∈ R^d is split into H contiguous head-aligned slices of dimension d/H, each of which is independently routed to a private expert bank, yielding a tuple-valued routing decision (S_1, ..., S_H) rather than a single expert selection. Experts map d/H-dimensional slices to full d-dimensional outputs, and the final layer output is the sum over all head contributions.

## Method

MH-MoE replaces the single full-vector routing decision of standard MoE with head-wise routing: the post-attention state is partitioned into H disjoint slices, and each slice m is scored by its own router W_rt^(l,m) against a head-private expert bank {E_k^(m)}, selecting top-k experts independently per head. This factorizes the routing outcome space from K choose k to (K choose k)^H virtual paths, dramatically reducing the effective composition number N_eff (the route-wise diversity of feature compositions sharing an update destination). The paper provides a theoretical link (Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.2) showing that higher N_eff yields a nondecreasing lower bound on route-level old-task loss increase, and empirically confirms that routes with larger N_eff forget more. MH-MoE is task-agnostic, requires no task IDs or replay buffers, and uses identical token-level routing during training and inference, making it compatible with continuous streams of unknown task boundaries.

## Results

On TRACE (8 sequential tasks), MH-MoE achieves OP 52.2 and BWT −2.2 on Llama-3.2-3B, improving over LoRAMoE (OP 46.0, BWT −7.1) by +6.2 OP and +4.9 BWT. On the larger Ministral-3-8B backbone, MH-MoE reaches OP 58.6 and BWT −1.7, versus LoRAMoE's OP 52.7 and BWT −11.0, a gain of +5.9 OP and +9.3 BWT. MH-MoE also outperforms the strongest LoRA-based continual-learning baseline OPLoRA on both backbones (e.g., OP 52.2 vs. 48.1 and BWT −2.2 vs. −5.1 on Llama-3.2-3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MH-MoE wins on both OP and BWT against all five baselines on both backbones. Against the primary MoE baseline LoRAMoE, MH-MoE improves OP by +6.2 (Llama-3.2-3B) and +5.9 (Ministral-3-8B), while reducing forgetting by +4.9 and +9.3 BWT respectively. Against the strongest LoRA-based continual-learning method OPLoRA, MH-MoE still leads by +4.1 OP and +2.9 BWT on Llama-3.2-3B, and by +2.3 OP and +2.2 BWT on Ministral-3-8B. The remaining MoE/LoRA variants (MoLE, LoRA-Mixer, MixLoRA) are consistently weaker, with MoLE and LoRA-Mixer showing the worst BWT (−11.5 to −16.0), indicating that simply composing or routing LoRA modules without head-wise decomposition does not adequately prevent composition collisions. Notably, MH-MoE's advantage is largest on tasks with strong composition-dependent gradient separation (e.g., ScienceQA, NumGLUE-ds, FOMC), while gains are smaller on generation-heavy tasks like MeetingBank and 20Minuten where the within- vs. cross-composition gradient gap is narrower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRAMoE (Dou et al., 2024), replacing its single full-vector routing decision with head-wise routing over post-attention sub-representations while retaining the LoRA-style expert architecture. Conceptually, it departs from the broader MoE routing design literature (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022) by identifying the bottleneck not in the routing mechanism itself but in the router input: the concatenation of multi-head attention outputs that forces routing to act on co-occurring feature compositions. It also positions itself within the continual learning literature (EWC, GEM, adapter-based methods) by providing a structural diagnosis—composition collision quantified via N_eff—rather than another regularization or replay strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, measurable diagnostic (route-wise N_eff) linking a specific architectural choice (head-mixed router input) to catastrophic forgetting, offering a principled explanation for why sparse MoE routing alone fails to prevent interference. MH-MoE's task-agnostic, streamable design makes it applicable to real-world continual deployment without task boundaries or replay buffers. However, the theory is limited to single-step updates and does not characterize multi-step accumulation effects, and all experiments are confined to the 8-task TRACE benchmark; whether the gains persist on longer streams, broader task distributions, or native MoE models (e.g., Mixtral, DeepSeekMoE) remains open.
