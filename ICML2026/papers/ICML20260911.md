## Problem

Previous analyses of LoRA's update dynamics showed that gradients are confined to the subspace spanned by A^T A, but A^T A is not a projection matrix for general A, making gradient compression highly sensitive to the singular values of A. The critical unresolved question is: what structural properties should the low-rank matrix A possess to preserve the maximum information of the gradient ∇f(W) when the gradient itself is unavailable (which is precisely what LoRA aims to avoid computing)? Additionally, existing convergence analyses for LoRA relied on impractical assumptions such as the LoRA rank equaling the full weight dimension.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model weight matrix W0 ∈ R^{m×n}, a rank parameter r ≪ min(m,n), and standard training data, and produces fine-tuned weights W = W0 + (α/r)BA where A ∈ R^{r×n} is initialized as an Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) constructed via Seidel matrices of strongly regular graphs. A key practical detail is that when A is an ETF, the pseudoinverse (AA^T)^† reduces to (r/n)I_r, so no matrix inversion is needed at runtime and the update rule is computationally identical to standard LoRA. The ETF initialization is a drop-in replacement for Gaussian initialization in existing LoRA variants without additional computational overhead.

## Method

The paper first derives the correct update dynamics of LoRA by minimizing the L-smooth quadratic upper bound, showing that the effective gradient step projects ∇f(W) onto the subspace spanned by P_A = A^T(AA^T)^†A, which is a proper symmetric idempotent projection matrix (unlike A^T A used in prior analyses). The central theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) proves that for gradients with s-effective freedom, the ETF structure of A achieves the maximum lower bound Ω = (r/n)(1−ε) on the proportion of gradient energy preserved after projection, where ε = (s−1)√((n−r)/(r(n−1))), by simultaneously maximizing the minimum diagonal entry of the Gram matrix and minimizing coherence via the Welch bound. The paper then establishes O(1/T) convergence of the averaged squared gradient norm for L-smooth losses and exponential convergence under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, both under ETF initialization. Empirically, replacing Gaussian initialization with ETF initialization in LoRA, FLORA, AsymLoRA, ReLoRA, RAC-LoRA, and GaLore yields consistent improvements across GLUE, GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP, and MT-Bench benchmarks with RoBERTa, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B backbones.

## Results

On GLUE benchmarks with RoBERTa (rank 8), ETF initialization consistently improves all LoRA variants by 1–5 points over their default Gaussian initialization. The largest gains appear on CoLA: LoRA improves from 57.95% to 62.18% (+4.23), ReLoRA from 57.14% to 62.11% (+4.97), and RAC-LoRA from 55.35% to 58.84% (+3.49). On NLG tasks with Llama-3.1-8B (rank 8), ETF-initialized LoRA reaches 71.07% on GSM8K (+2.74) and 46.34% on HumanEval (+3.86). ETF-initialized GaLore achieves performance comparable to GaLore with full SVD-based gradient initialization, validating the theoretical guarantee that ETF maximizes the lower bound of preserved gradient information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETF initialization outperforms the default Gaussian initialization of every LoRA variant tested, with gains of +2.10 to +4.97 on CoLA and +0.60 to +2.77 on RTE. The largest relative improvement is seen with ReLoRA (+4.97 on CoLA, +2.37 on MNLI), suggesting that methods with weaker default subspaces benefit most from ETF. Compared to LoRA-GA (which uses gradient approximation for initialization) and PiSSA (which uses SVD of pre-trained weights), ETF still achieves higher or equal accuracy, indicating that gradient-free ETF construction is more effective than these partial-information approaches. Against GaLore, which performs full SVD on the actual gradient at each step, ETF-initialized GaLore achieves comparable performance (e.g., 78.71 vs. 76.21 on GSM8K for Llama-3.1-8B), confirming that ETF's worst-case guarantee matches the best-case gradient-aware initialization in practice. The improvements are consistent across models (RoBERTa, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) and ranks (4, 8, 16), confirming robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the gradient-compression interpretation of LoRA established by FLORA (Hao et al., 2024) and He et al. (2025a), which showed that LoRA confines updates to the subspace spanned by A^T A. The key departure is recognizing that A^T A is not a true projection matrix for general A, and deriving the correct projection operator P_A = A^T(AA^T)^†A that emerges from the L-smooth descent analysis. The paper then imports frame theory—specifically the Welch bound and Equiangular Tight Frames—to prove that ETF initialization is the optimal choice for gradient preservation when the gradient is unavailable, in contrast to the heuristic Gaussian or Stiefel-manifold initializations used by LoRA, FLORA, AsymLoRA, ReLoRA, and RAC-LoRA. Prior convergence analyses (Malinovsky et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2025) required impractical assumptions (full-rank LoRA or positive restricted strong convexity), which this paper avoids by leveraging the ETF structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a drop-in initialization replacement (ETF for Gaussian) that requires no gradient access and no additional computation, yet consistently improves all major LoRA variants by 1–4 points across NLU and NLG benchmarks. The theoretical contribution—proving ETF achieves the maximum lower bound on gradient information preservation under an s-effective-freedom assumption—gives practitioners a principled justification for a previously heuristic choice. Open questions include: (1) ETFs do not exist for all (r, n) pairs, requiring approximate constructions in practice; (2) the s-effective-freedom assumption on gradients, while empirically supported by recent work showing low-rank gradient structure, has not been formally verified for all architectures; (3) whether the optimality result extends to multi-layer or multi-task settings where gradient structure may differ across layers.
