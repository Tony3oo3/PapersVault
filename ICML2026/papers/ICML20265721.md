## Problem

No existing method can test whether Euclidean covariates contribute to the conditional mean of a manifold-valued response. Distance covariance (dCov) captures global distributional dependence and cannot isolate mean effects, while martingale difference divergence (MDD/FMDD) relies on linear vector-space structure and cannot be applied to non-Euclidean data. This leaves a critical gap for regression diagnostics and variable screening when responses live on spheres, SPD matrices, or Wasserstein spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. pairs (X_i, Y_i) with X_i ∈ R^p and Y_i on a Riemannian manifold M, along with a translation-invariant kernel K (Gaussian or Laplace) and bandwidth h. It first computes the empirical Fréchet mean μ̂, projects each Y_i onto T_μM via the logarithmic map, and then produces a single scalar test statistic T_n = n·MMDD_n(Y|X). Inference is via a wild bootstrap critical value (B=499 replicates), yielding a p-value; the same statistic also serves as a covariate-impact score for ranking individual predictors.

## Method

The Manifold Martingale Difference Divergence (MMDD) replaces the linear inner product in MDD/FMDD with the tangent-space inner product ⟨Log_μ(Y), Log_μ(Y')⟩_{T_μM}, weighted by a positive semi-definite kernel K(X−X'). Proposition 2.3 establishes that MMDD(Y|X)=0 if and only if E(Log_μ(Y)|X)=0 a.s., making it a valid characterization of conditional mean independence. The empirical estimator is a second-order degenerate U-statistic whose null limit is a weighted sum of chi-squared variables (Theorem 2.6), with nontrivial power against n^{-1/2}-local alternatives (Theorem 2.7). Because the null distribution is non-pivotal, a wild bootstrap with i.i.d. zero-mean, unit-variance weights consistently approximates the critical value (Theorem 3.3), and the resulting test maintains correct size even when the variance of Y depends on X—a setting where dCov suffers severe size distortion.

## Results

Under the null with variance-dependent noise (DGP1, n=100, S²), MMDD maintains correct size with rejection rates of (0.112, 0.051, 0.008) at nominal levels (0.1, 0.05, 0.01) even at the strongest variance dependence (s²=0.7), while dCov inflates to (0.987, 0.967, 0.871). For power, MMDD achieves 0.997–1.000 at r²=0.5, n=200, α=0.05 under linear alternatives (DGP2) and 0.994–0.998 under nonlinear alternatives (DGP4), with only modest degradation under heteroscedastic noise (DGP3/DGP5). On the NOAA wind direction dataset, MMDD identifies geographic covariates (ALBERS_X, ALBERS_Y, ELEVATION) as the strongest drivers of mean wind direction, and the post-fit residual test yields p=0.956, confirming model adequacy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the distance covariance (dCov) test of Székely et al. (2007), which measures global distributional dependence rather than conditional mean dependence. MMDD wins decisively on the critical size-control metric: under DGP1 where the conditional mean is independent of X but the variance depends on X, dCov's rejection rate inflates from ~0.05 to 0.967 at α=0.05 as s² increases from 0 to 0.7, while MMDD stays within 0.001–0.012 of nominal across all three manifolds (S², W₂(R), P_d). On power, the paper does not directly report dCov power numbers in the tables, but the wind direction application illustrates the practical divergence: dCov ranks wind-speed covariates (WSF2, AWND) highest because it captures variance dependence, whereas MMDD correctly prioritizes geographic covariates (ALBERS_X, ALBERS_Y, ELEVATION) that drive the conditional mean. Additionally, MMDD detects a significant diurnal effect from PGTM (p<0.05) that dCov misses entirely (p>0.05), confirming that MMDD isolates regression-relevant mean dependence that global dependence measures dilute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the martingale difference divergence (MDD) framework of Shao & Zhang (2014) and its functional-data generalization FMDD of Lee et al. (2020) to manifold-valued responses by replacing linear operations (subtraction, inner product) with the logarithmic map and tangent-space inner product at the Fréchet mean. It departs from the distance covariance (dCov) line of work (Székely et al., 2007; Lyons, 2013), which tests global distributional dependence on metric spaces, by isolating the regression-relevant conditional mean component. The key geometric insight is that the tangent space at the Fréchet mean provides a local Euclidean structure where the MDD construction becomes well-defined, bridging Riemannian geometry and kernel-based dependence testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine methodological gap: before this work, no test existed for conditional mean independence with manifold-valued responses, a setting increasingly encountered in neuroscience (SPD matrices), meteorology (spherical wind directions), and distributional data (Wasserstein space). The dramatic dCov size distortion (rejection rates reaching 0.987 under the null with moderate variance dependence) underscores a practical risk for practitioners who might default to dCov for manifold data. Open questions include extending the framework to conditional quantile independence, high-dimensional covariate screening, and computational scaling to very large n, as well as adapting the test to non-translation-invariant kernels or manifolds without a global Fréchet mean.
