## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are constrained by the scarcity of high-quality, domain-specific training data, and annotating complex reasoning tasks is prohibitively expensive. While recent text-to-image generators like Nano Banana Pro show promise for multimodal data synthesis, directly applying them produces data with multimodal misalignment, factual inconsistencies, limited diversity, and trivial difficulty—making them insufficient for driving meaningful model improvement on complex real-world tasks.

## Input / Output

The synthesis pipeline takes seed multimodal samples (image + question + answer) or textual task descriptions as starting points, and a panel of four MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-4-Sonnet) collectively generates and judges new instances. The output is MMSynthetic-20K: 20K synthesized multimodal instances (each comprising a generated image, a textual question, and a ground-truth answer) spanning 8 domains, with Logic Reasoning (58.9%) and Math (28.9%) as the largest categories. The trained model R1-SyntheticVL (Qwen2.5-VL-7B base) then takes an image and text question as input and produces a text answer.

## Method

CADS (Collective Adversarial Data Synthesis) operates in two cyclic phases. CAD-Generate leverages collective knowledge from multiple MLLMs through three steps: (1) rationale analysis to extract domain and reasoning logic from seeds, (2) synthesis strategy generation using four meta-strategies (numerical variation, logic reversion, auxiliary extension, isomorphic scenario transfer) dynamically adapted per seed, and (3) visual prompt generation that encodes precise spatial and geometric constraints for Nano Banana Pro. CAD-Judge then employs the same MLLM panel as judges, computing a consensus score C (number of models matching the ground truth) to filter unsolvable instances (C=0). The key differentiator is Adversarial Context Optimization: instances with inter-model disagreement (1 ≤ C < K) are identified as high-value adversarial samples, and insights from their disagreement patterns are fed back into the generation context to progressively encourage more challenging, boundary-level data. The final dataset is used to train R1-SyntheticVL via GRPO reinforcement learning on Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

## Results

R1-SyntheticVL, trained exclusively on the synthetic MMSynthetic-20K dataset via GRPO, achieves an average score of 52.0 across six benchmarks, outperforming all open-source 7B MLLMs trained on real data. On MathVista, it reaches 75.6, surpassing the strongest open-source competitor ThinkLite-VL-7B (75.1) and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (68.2) by 7.4 points. On the highly challenging MMMU-Pro (Std-10), R1-SyntheticVL scores 42.0, leading all open-source models by a substantial margin (next best: OpenVLThinker-7B at 39.1). On CharXiv reasoning, it achieves 47.8, again the best among open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R1-SyntheticVL is trained solely on synthetic data (MMSynthetic-20K) and outperforms all open-source 7B MLLMs trained on real data across the majority of benchmarks. On MathVista, it edges out ThinkLite-VL-7B (75.6 vs. 75.1) and Vision-R1-7B (75.6 vs. 73.5), while on MMMU it leads all open-source models (56.3 vs. 55.5 for ThinkLite-VL-7B). The most striking margin is on MMMU-Pro Std-10, where R1-SyntheticVL scores 42.0 versus 39.1 for OpenVLThinker-7B and 37.5 for ThinkLite-VL-7B, a gap of 2.5–4.5 points. On CharXiv reasoning, it achieves 47.8, surpassing the next best (ThinkLite-VL-7B at 38.7†) by over 9 points. However, it trails closed-source GPT-4o on MMMU (56.3 vs. 70.7) and MMMU-Pro (42.0 vs. 54.0), and is slightly behind ThinkLite-VL-7B on MathVerse (51.2 vs. 52.1) and MathVision (29.1 vs. 32.9), indicating that synthetic data alone has not yet fully closed the gap on certain reasoning-intensive subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data synthesis paradigm established for LLMs (Self-Instruct, ScaleQuest, WizardMath) to the multimodal setting, but departs from prior MLLM data synthesis work that either synthesizes only text for existing images (Oasis, ECD) or relies on programmatic engines like Python plotting packages for visual content (TR-CoT, ECD). The key conceptual departure is using a text-to-image generative model (Nano Banana Pro) to synthesize both visual and textual content jointly, wrapped in a collective adversarial framework where multiple MLLMs collaboratively generate, judge, and adversarially refine the synthesis context. The training side builds on the GRPO-based RL paradigm popularized by R1-VL, Vision-R1, and MMR1, but applies it to a fully synthetic training set rather than real or teacher-generated data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 20K fully synthetic multimodal instances can outperform models trained on real data across multiple benchmarks, offering a concrete pathway to mitigate the data scarcity bottleneck for MLLMs. However, the framework's reliance on four expensive proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-4-Sonnet) for the collective generation and judgment phases limits immediate accessibility, and the open-source judge ablation shows a ~1-point drop. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 20K samples without quality degradation, whether the adversarial context optimization generalizes to domains not represented in the seed data, and whether the method remains effective as open-source generators close the gap with Nano Banana Pro.
