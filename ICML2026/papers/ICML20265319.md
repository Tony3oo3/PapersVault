## Problem

Existing privacy canary designs for membership inference auditing rely on ad hoc heuristics—mislabeled examples, out-of-distribution points, or adversarial perturbations—that may underestimate true privacy leakage and produce inconsistent detection signals. No principled formulation exists for constructing canaries that are provably maximally detectable, leaving auditors without a rigorous foundation for quantifying training-data leakage in deployed models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265319/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base training dataset D (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB, MNIST at 28×28 grayscale), a target model architecture (e.g., ResNet-9), and an initial canary image with its correct label. It produces an optimized canary image (same spatial resolution as the dataset) paired with its original label, along with a privacy leakage estimate reported as TPR@0.1%FPR from LiRA membership inference. Notably, the optimized canary transfers across architectures (ResNet-9 → ResNet-50, WideResNet-16) without re-optimization, enabling third-party auditing without access to the model provider's training infrastructure.

## Method

OptiFluence formulates canary design as a bilevel optimization: the inner loop trains a model on D ∪ {canary}, and the outer loop maximizes the privacy loss ℓ_priv = f(θ_{D∪{x}}; x) − f(θ_D; x), where f is the hinge loss (correct-class logit minus max incorrect-class logit) following LiRA. The two-stage pipeline first uses IF-Init, which selects the training sample with the highest normalized self-influence (ratio of self-influence to max cross-influence, computed via EK-FAC-approximated inverse Hessian-vector products over an ensemble of 20 models) as the initial canary. The second stage computes the canary gradient ∇_x ℓ_priv by unrolling T SGD updates of the model and backpropagating through the trajectory, made memory-efficient via rematerialization (gradient checkpointing) and truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT with window K=2–4). This avoids the convergence and convexity assumptions of influence-function-based gradient approximations, yielding exact (or near-exact) gradients that drive the canary toward near-perfect detectability (99.6% TPR@0.1%FPR on CIFAR-10).

## Results

OptiFluence achieves near-perfect canary detectability across all four datasets: 100.0% TPR@0.1%FPR on MNIST, 99.6% on CIFAR-10, 100.0% on CIFAR-100, and 100.0% on HAM10K. On CIFAR-10, this represents a 4× improvement over in-distribution baselines (24.6%) and a 10× improvement over the strongest heuristic baseline (adversarial examples at 89.9%). The method also transfers across architectures: canaries optimized on ResNet-9 achieve 100% TPR@0.1%FPR when inserted into ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and WideResNet-164 training without re-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiFluence outperforms all heuristic canary baselines on every dataset. Against the adversarial example baseline (Nasr et al., 2021), OptiFluence gains +2.2 points on MNIST (100.0 vs. 97.8), +9.7 on CIFAR-10 (99.6 vs. 89.9), +27.1 on CIFAR-100 (100.0 vs. 72.9), and +93.7 on HAM10K (100.0 vs. 6.3). Against the mislabeled canary baseline (Steinke et al., 2023; Aerni et al., 2024), the gap is even larger on CIFAR-10 (+27.6 points) and HAM10K (+99.0 points), though on MNIST the mislabeled baseline is already strong (92.1%). The most dramatic comparison is with Nasr et al. (2023, Algorithm 3), whose gradient-orthogonality canary achieves only 0.2–7.4% TPR@0.1%FPR on MNIST versus OptiFluence's 100%, a gap the authors attribute to the fixed-model proxy ignoring how canary inclusion changes trained parameters. Notably, OptiFluence also exhibits far lower variance (±0.2 on CIFAR-10) compared to the high-variance heuristic baselines (±22.3 for mislabeled, ±22.1 for adversarial on CIFAR-100), indicating more reliable auditing signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on influence functions (Koh & Liang, 2017) for canary pre-selection and on the LiRA membership inference framework (Carlini et al., 2022) to define a formal privacy loss objective, then departs from prior heuristic canary constructions (mislabeled, OOD, adversarial) by formulating canary design as a bilevel optimization problem solved via unrolled training dynamics. It explicitly contrasts with the gradient-orthogonality condition of Nasr et al. (2023) and Maddock et al. (2023), showing those treat the model as fixed and ignore how canary inclusion changes trained parameters, and positions itself as subsuming Boglioni et al. (2025)'s metagradient approach as a special case. The memory-efficient optimization (rematerialization + TBPTT) repurposes techniques from RNN training and gradient checkpointing to the novel setting of differentiating through a full training trajectory with respect to an input sample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The transferability of optimized canaries across architectures (ResNet-9 → WideResNet-164) with near-perfect TPR is practically significant: it enables third-party regulatory auditing where auditors need only black-box access and a mandated canary in training data, without requiring the model provider's infrastructure. Key limitations include that canaries may not resemble realistic training samples (a realism constraint is noted but not imposed), that the method requires access to the training data distribution for IF-Init, and that it does not yield tight DP bounds for DP-SGD. Open questions include scaling to LLMs and federated settings, and whether the bilevel framework can incorporate distributional realism constraints without degrading detectability.
