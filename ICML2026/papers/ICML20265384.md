## Problem

Existing 2-bit trellis-coded quantization (TCQ) pipelines for LLMs face two deployment-critical gaps: they either reintroduce micro-LUTs or lookup tables into the dequantization inner loop (causing irregular on-chip memory access), or they suffer from overlap-amplified artifacts because global incoherence-based Gaussianization does not guarantee the overlap-local joint geometry that the bitshift-trellis decoder actually operates on. This paper asks whether a strictly lookup-free TCQ can simultaneously achieve overlap-robust neighbor-set coverage and competitive 2-bit accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes FP16 linear weights, a small calibration set, and a Hessian curvature proxy (stored as a packed lower-triangular second-moment matrix plus mean vector). It outputs a 2-bit packed trellis bitstream Z, per-layer discrete generator parameters (a_ℓ, s_ℓ) chosen from a fixed 3-candidate pool, per-layer affine statistics (μ_ℓ, σ_ℓ), and per-channel diagonal scales (S_in, S_out). At inference, the decoder is a fixed bitshift-trellis kernel that performs zero runtime codebook or LUT loads; only the packed indices and per-layer constants are stored, and the channel scales can be folded into adjacent normalization gains.

## Method

PROTEUS's core is MUL-BAL, a compute-only generator that applies modular 32-bit multiplication followed by an XOR-with-shift (high-to-low feedback) to mix shared trellis bits into the low-order bytes before a byte-sum Gaussianizer and per-layer affine normalization, explicitly targeting joint reachable-set isotropy rather than marginal Gaussianity alone. The framework decouples compression into three stages: Stage I enumerates a tiny 3-candidate pool of pre-vetted (multiplier, shift) pairs and solves a Hessian-weighted TCQ via a Viterbi-style dynamic-programming solver inside a BlockLDLQ blockwise procedure to select the best per-layer generator and pack trellis indices; Stage II freezes the discrete structure and learns only per-channel diagonal scales via output-matching on calibration data; Stage III optionally performs a global few-shot KL-distillation pass that tunes only the per-layer affine parameters (μ_ℓ, σ_ℓ) while keeping indices and the decoder unchanged. The key design insight is that under bitshift-trellis overlap, the generator must disrupt correlation in the shared-bit suffix/prefix so that the joint neighbor set {(y_t, y_t+1)} fills the plane isotropically, a property that marginal distribution tests alone cannot certify.

## Results

At 2-bit weight-only PTQ on Llama 2, Proteus achieves WikiText2 PPL of 5.85 (7B), 5.10 (13B), and 3.69 (70B), improving over QTIP (5.91/5.26/3.78) and QuIP# (6.19/5.35/3.91) across all model sizes. On C4, Proteus reaches 7.68/6.82/5.46 PPL versus QTIP's 7.76/6.99/5.56. In zero-shot evaluation, Proteus leads on ARC-Challenge (36.1% at 7B vs. 35.3% QTIP, 34.6% QuIP#) and PIQA (81.2% at 70B vs. 80.1% QTIP). On the 70B model, Proteus reduces observed decode memory bandwidth from 1020 GB/s (QTIP) to 740 GB/s at comparable throughput (36.28 vs. 36.29 tok/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proteus consistently outperforms QTIP (its closest TCQ competitor) on PPL across all three Llama 2 sizes (e.g., 3.69 vs. 3.78 on 70B WikiText2) and on most zero-shot tasks (e.g., 49.0% vs. 48.1% ArcC at 70B; 71.7% vs. 70.6% ArcE at 13B), while also reducing decode memory bandwidth by ~27% at 70B (740 vs. 1020 GB/s). Against QuIP#, Proteus gains 0.34 PPL on 7B WikiText2 and 0.25 PPL on 70B WikiText2, with similar margins on C4. On zero-shot benchmarks, Proteus beats OmniQuant, QuIP, and AQLM across nearly all tasks and model sizes (e.g., 49.0% vs. 47.9% ArcC at 70B vs. AQLM; 36.1% vs. 33.6% ArcC at 7B vs. AQLM). The two lattice/entropy-coded baselines (NestQuant, EntroLLM) diverge entirely at 2-bit (PPL > 10³), confirming they operate outside their design regime at this extreme bit-width. At batch size 32, Proteus achieves a 1.05× throughput advantage over QTIP (2940 vs. 2797 tok/s) due to eliminating shared-memory codebook traffic, while at BS=1 both methods are HBM-bound and comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROTEUS extends the QTIP/QuIP# lineage of Gaussianized TCQ for LLM PTQ by replacing QTIP's computed or hybrid codebooks with a strictly lookup-free generator (MUL-BAL) that explicitly targets the joint reachable-set geometry induced by bitshift-trellis overlap, rather than merely matching marginal Gaussianity. The Hessian-weighted quadratic proxy and incoherence transform follow the GPTQ/OBC and QuIP# recipes, while the three-stage decoupling (discrete generator selection → channel-scale compensation → few-shot affine distillation) is a new instantiation strategy that keeps the bitshift-trellis decoder and packed indices frozen at deployment. The key conceptual departure from QTIP is the recognition that incoherence improves aggregate Gaussianity but does not certify overlap-local joint isotropy, and that small LUTs (HYB) do not fully remedy this under the shared-bit constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck—irregular on-chip memory access from codebook/LUT loads in the dequantization inner loop—that prior TCQ work acknowledged but did not eliminate, yielding measurable bandwidth savings (740 vs. 1020 GB/s at 70B) without sacrificing accuracy. The diagnostic framework separating marginal Gaussianity from overlap-induced joint dependence (Table 1, Fig. 3) provides a reusable lens for evaluating any compute code under trellis constraints. Open questions include whether the three-candidate pool and Hadamard-preprocessing assumption transfer to MoE or very-long-context architectures, whether the efficiency advantage persists on ALU-constrained accelerators where INT32 decode arithmetic cannot overlap tensor-core matmul, and whether the batch-size crossover (BS ≥ 32) limits practical benefit in single-stream serving.
