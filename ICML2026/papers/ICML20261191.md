## Problem

RL fine-tuning of LLMs causes diversity collapse, where the model converges to a narrow set of solutions and Pass@K degrades. Existing countermeasures (entropy bonus/penalty, temperature tuning, early stopping) are ad hoc, task-dependent, and often trade correctness for diversity—some tasks benefit from entropy maximization while others from minimization. The paper asks whether this correctness-diversity tradeoff is inherent or can be resolved by a principled, universally applicable method.

## Input / Output

The method operates within the GRPO framework: for each text prompt, a group of G completions is sampled from the current policy, each receiving a binary reward from a verifier. The key input signal beyond the reward is the log-probability of each trajectory under the old policy π_θ_old, which is used to compute the differential advantage modification. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy that generates reasoning trajectories; evaluation uses Pass@1 (correctness) and Pass@K for K up to 64 (diversity) across math reasoning and arithmetic benchmarks.

## Method

The paper first proves that RL fine-tuning causes diversity collapse through two compounding biases: selection bias (high-probability correct trajectories are more likely to be reinforced) and reinforcement bias (they receive disproportionately larger probability gains). Building on this, the key insight is that diversity only matters among correct trajectories, so the reward modification should be differential rather than global. DS-GRPO modifies the GRPO advantage: for correct completions it subtracts γ_p·log π_θ_old(y_i|x) (an entropy bonus that flattens the distribution over correct solutions), and for incorrect completions it adds γ_n·log π_θ_old(y_i|x) (an entropy penalty that sharpens away from errors). Theorem 4 proves that under the same KL-divergence constraint, this differential reward simultaneously achieves higher correctness C(π) and lower normalized variance σ(π) over correct trajectories than any global entropy regularization, resolving the apparent tradeoff. Empirically, DS-GRPO improves both Pass@1 and Pass@K across models from 1.5B to 8B parameters, with up to 11.4% gains on AIME25 and a ~4× inference speedup at matched Pass@64.

## Results

DS-GRPO achieves Pass@1 gains of up to 1.1% and Pass@64 gains of up to 11.4% (on AIME25 with Mistral-8B) over vanilla GRPO, with the largest improvements concentrated on the harder AIME 2024/2025 benchmarks. On the Countdown task, DS-GRPO improves Pass@K by approximately 4% across all K values while delivering a 4× inference speedup (matching vanilla GRPO's Pass@64 with only k=16 samples). Gains are consistent across models ranging from 1.5B to 8B parameters (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Ministral-8B-Instruct) and across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, OlympiadBench, AMC23, AIME24, AIME25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS-GRPO consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO on both Pass@1 and Pass@K across all five math benchmarks and Countdown, with the largest Pass@64 gain of 11.4% on AIME25 (Mistral-8B). Against the rank-based unlikeliness reward of He et al. (2025a), DS-GRPO wins across all K values because it simultaneously improves correctness (via the negative-trajectory penalty) rather than sacrificing Pass@1 for diversity. Compared to PKPO and GR-PKPO (Chen et al., 2025b; Walder & Karkhanis, 2026), DS-GRPO avoids the instability and Pass@1 degradation that those methods exhibit, particularly on Countdown. Against CISPO (Chen et al., 2025a), DS-GRPO outperforms at most K values across all four math reasoning datasets, with the largest gaps at large K. Finally, DS-GRPO dominates both global entropy bonus and entropy penalty baselines (Agarwal et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2025) regardless of task: the entropy bonus helps only on high-solution-multiplicity tasks (Countdown) while the penalty helps only on low-multiplicity tasks (MATH500), whereas DS-GRPO's differential approach wins on both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its training backbone and addresses the well-documented diversity collapse problem in RL fine-tuning of LLMs. It departs from prior ad hoc heuristics—global entropy bonuses/penalties (Agarwal et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2025), Pass@K reward shaping (Chen et al., 2025b; Walder & Karkhanis, 2026), and rank-based unlikeliness rewards (He et al., 2025a)—by grounding the solution in a formal decomposition of diversity collapse into selection bias and reinforcement bias. The key conceptual departure is the 'differential' principle: applying an entropy bonus only to correct trajectories (to preserve diversity) and an entropy penalty only to incorrect ones (to sharpen correctness), rather than a uniform global adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical resolution to a genuinely confusing area of the literature where prior work reported contradictory effects of entropy maximization vs. minimization, explaining them via a Solution Multiplicity metric. The practical 4× inference speedup (matching Pass@64 with only k=16 samples) is a notable efficiency gain for test-time compute scaling. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees hold under the practical approximation of using π_θ_old instead of π_base, whether the method extends to non-binary reward settings, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales beyond 8B. The formal analysis of the entropy principle and its task-dependent effects is explicitly left as future work.
