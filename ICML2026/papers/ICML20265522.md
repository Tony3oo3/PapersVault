## Problem

Knowledge-intensive VQA systems retrieve noisy or contradictory external passages, but existing conflict-detection approaches rely on rigid entity or keyword matching that produces false positives in multi-hop reasoning chains, and prevailing decoding strategies lack intra-model constraints to selectively suppress conflicting evidence without disrupting valid reasoning. The paper identifies that a valid conflict should be defined only as a logical contradiction within the same reasoning-pivot (an atomic node or edge in the reasoning chain), rather than any surface-level mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265522/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image, a natural-language question, and k=5 retrieved Wikipedia passages as input. In Stage 1, the RPA-SFT discriminator outputs reasoning-pivots wrapped in special <RPivot> tokens followed by a binary conflict label; in Stage 2, the RPGD decoder consumes the pivot token set K to produce a single-word or phrase VQA answer. The discriminator is trained exclusively on the 4,149-sample REAL-VQA set and evaluated for generalization on E-VQA, ScienceQA, and MMKC.

## Method

REAL introduces the Reasoning-Pivot concept—an indispensable atomic unit (node or edge) in a multi-hop reasoning chain whose absence makes the answer unreachable—and defines conflict strictly as mutual logical incompatibility within the information set of a single pivot. RPA-SFT fine-tunes an MLLM with special <RPivot> tokens and a three-stage target (question pivot extraction → paragraph pivot extraction → binary conflict verification) to train a generalizable discriminator that localizes conflict sources rather than memorizing dataset patterns. RPGD is a training-free contrastive decoding strategy that constructs a conflict-dominant pathway via patch shuffle (randomly permuting visual patch embeddings to destroy object-level topology while preserving feature magnitude), applies an adaptive token-wise gate that scales suppression strength based on conflict-dominant logits at identified pivot tokens, and uses Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to geometrically project and selectively subtract only the conflict-aligned logit component, preserving robust reasoning structure independent of the conflicting knowledge.

## Results

On InfoSeek, REAL with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 44.1% overall accuracy (43.1% Un-Q, 45.1% Un-E), surpassing the best retrieval-augmented baseline VLM-PRF (42.5%) by +1.6%. On E-VQA, REAL reaches 41.4% overall (45.5% Single-Hop), outperforming VLM-PRF (39.2%) by +2.2%. On A-OKVQA, REAL (LLaVA-1.5-7B) achieves 80.3% MC and 68.3% DA accuracy, exceeding the previous best QACap (76.7% MC, 66.3% DA) by +3.6% and +2.0% respectively. The conflict discriminator (RPA-SFT) improves MCC by up to 14.68% over zero-shot baselines across four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REAL with Qwen3-VL-8B outperforms all 18 baselines on both InfoSeek and E-VQA. Against the strongest retrieval-augmented baselines, REAL surpasses VLM-PRF (InternVL3-8B) by +1.6% on InfoSeek All (44.1 vs. 42.5) and +2.2% on E-VQA All (41.4 vs. 39.2), and beats mKG-RAG by +3.6% on InfoSeek All. On A-OKVQA, REAL (LLaVA-1.5-7B) achieves 80.3% MC / 68.3% DA, outperforming QACap (Claude 3.5) by +3.6% MC and +2.0% DA, and SKP (LLaVA-1.5-7B) by +15.0% MC and +3.0% DA. Among decoding strategies, RPGD outperforms VCD and CAD by +4.9% and +5.4% on InfoSeek All (Qwen3-VL-8B), confirming that the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization and adaptive gating provide more precise conflict isolation than linear logit subtraction. The conflict discriminator (RPA-SFT) also generalizes to unseen benchmarks, achieving 52.9 MCC on MMKC and 87.9 MCC on ScienceQA, substantially exceeding few-shot CoT baselines (42.4 and 67.4 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG-for-VQA paradigm (EchoSight, Wiki-LLaVA, VLM-PRF) by identifying and formally addressing knowledge conflicts arising from noisy open-domain retrieval, a gap that prior retrieval-optimization and reranking work largely overlooks. It departs from existing conflict-detection approaches that rely on entity or keyword mismatch by introducing the Reasoning-Pivot formalism, which scopes conflicts to logically indispensable units in multi-hop chains. On the decoding side, it builds on contrastive decoding (VCD, CAD) but replaces their uniform linear logit subtraction with a structured pipeline of patch-shuffle perturbation, pivot-conditioned adaptive gating, and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, and pairs this with a newly trained pivot-aware discriminator (RPA-SFT) rather than relying on heuristic prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reasoning-pivot formalization provides a principled, generalizable criterion for distinguishing true logical contradictions from harmless entity/keyword variation in multi-hop KI-VQA, which prior schema-based or large-scale-training methods struggle with. The training-free RPGD component is architecturally agnostic and adds only ~1.3× inference latency, making it a practical drop-in for existing RAG-VQA pipelines. Key limitations remain: performance is contingent on retrieval quality and pivot-extraction accuracy, the approach adds computational overhead, and generalization to very long reasoning chains or multi-image settings is untested. Open questions include whether the pivot concept transfers to agentic or iterative retrieval settings and whether the conflict-discrimination signal can be distilled into the base model to eliminate the two-stage dependency.
