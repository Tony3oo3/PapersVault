## Problem

Existing generalization measures face a trilemma: sharpness-based metrics (e.g., Hessian eigenvalues) are unreliable predictors across training configurations, while output-based measures like inconsistency require training multiple models at high cost. No prior measure is simultaneously computable from a single model, differentiable for direct optimization, and estimable from unlabeled data alone—limiting its use in label-scarce settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/F-MNIST/SVHN, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels via softmax. The key distinction is that the local inconsistency regularizer operates in parameter space: it computes KL divergence between the model's output distribution and that of a parameter-perturbed copy, evaluated on unlabeled inputs only. This means the regularization signal requires no ground-truth labels, enabling plug-and-play integration into semi-supervised (FixMatch) and self-supervised (SimCLR) pipelines where labels are sparse or absent.

## Method

The paper defines local inconsistency S_ρ(θ) as the worst-case expected KL divergence between a model's output distribution and its perturbed counterpart within an ℓ₂ ball of radius ρ in parameter space. Under a second-order Taylor approximation, S_ρ(θ) reduces to (1/2)ρ²λ_max(F(θ)), where F is the Fisher Information Matrix, linking it to both the FIM and the loss Hessian via the Gauss-Newton approximation. Two optimization variants are proposed: IAM-D adds βS_ρ(θ) directly to the training loss, while IAM-S minimizes the loss at the adversarially perturbed point θ+δ* (analogous to SAM but with KL-based rather than loss-based perturbation). The perturbation δ* is estimated via K steps of normalized gradient ascent (power iteration) on the KL objective, requiring only K extra forward-backward passes per update. A theoretical generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) shows that minimizing S_ρ lowers the upper bound on true risk, and the label-agnostic computation of S_ρ from unlabeled data is what enables IAM to improve semi- and self-supervised training where SAM cannot operate effectively.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, IAM-S achieves 16.82% test error, outperforming SAM (17.63%) by 0.81% and ASAM (17.15%) by 0.33%. On ImageNet with ResNet-50 (200 epochs), IAM-D reaches 21.36% Top-1 error, beating SAM (21.80%) by 0.44%. In semi-supervised learning, FixMatch+IAM-D reduces CIFAR-10 test error from 6.26% (250 labels) to 5.30%, and CIFAR-100 from 32.84% (2500 labels) to 28.95%. In self-supervised learning, SimCLR+IAM-D achieves higher linear-probe accuracy than vanilla SimCLR on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM, IAM-S achieves the best overall result on CIFAR-100 (16.82% vs. 17.63%, a 0.81% gain) and ties on CIFAR-10 (3.28% vs. 3.31%), while IAM-D clearly outperforms SAM on SVHN (3.13% vs. 3.47%) and ImageNet Top-1 (21.36% vs. 21.80%). Against ASAM, IAM-S wins on CIFAR-100 (16.82% vs. 17.15%) and F-MNIST (4.10% vs. 4.11%), but ASAM retains a slight edge on CIFAR-10 (3.15% vs. 3.28%). In the semi-supervised setting, FixMatch+IAM-D improves over FixMatch alone by 0.96% on CIFAR-10 (250 labels) and 3.89% on CIFAR-100 (2500 labels), whereas FixMatch+SAM actually degrades performance (9.90% vs. 6.26% on CIFAR-10 with 250 labels). In self-supervised learning, SimCLR+IAM-D achieves higher linear-probe accuracy than SimCLR+SGD on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, confirming that the label-free local inconsistency regularizer benefits representation learning even without any explicit supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sharpness-aware optimization (SAM, ASAM) and information-geometric generalization measures. It departs from SAM's reliance on the loss Hessian's largest eigenvalue by instead using the Fisher Information Matrix's largest eigenvalue, accessed through KL divergence between output distributions of a model and its perturbed counterpart. The key conceptual departure from Johnson & Zhang's (2023) inconsistency measure is that local inconsistency is single-model, differentiable, and computable from unlabeled data alone, avoiding the multi-model training cost. The theoretical grounding via the Gauss-Newton/FIM equivalence and the FIM-based generalization bound (building on Luo et al., 2024) positions it as a complementary signal to traditional sharpness rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-agnostic nature of local inconsistency makes IAM a plug-and-play regularizer for semi-supervised (FixMatch) and self-supervised (SimCLR) pipelines, where SAM cannot operate on unlabeled data without pseudo-labels. The finding that S_ρ retains positive correlation with the generalization gap (τ=0.37) in settings where λ_max(H) turns negative (τ=−0.12) for WRN28-2 suggests output-based sensitivity is more robust to hyperparameter-induced scale effects than Hessian-based sharpness. Open questions include whether the near-interpolation assumption in the theoretical bound holds in practice for under-trained models, whether the 2× computational overhead is justified at very large scale, and whether the heuristic connection to Johnson & Zhang's inconsistency can be made rigorous without the isotropic-covariance assumption.
