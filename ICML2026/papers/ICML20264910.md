## Problem

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) have emerged as a structured alternative to MLPs, yet principled theory for their training dynamics, generalization, and privacy properties remains largely absent. Existing analyses are either algorithm-independent (expressivity/approximation) or require polynomially wide networks under strong positive-definiteness assumptions, leaving open whether gradient descent on KANs can achieve fast optimization and generalization rates at modest widths, and how differential privacy constraints interact with the nonconvex KAN landscape.

## Input / Output

The model takes a d-dimensional input vector x with ||x||₂ ≤ 1 and a binary label y ∈ {−1, +1}, producing a scalar prediction f_Θ(x) via a two-layer KAN whose edge functions are parameterized by B-spline bases (p basis functions per edge) with a bounded activation σ (e.g., tanh). The theoretical outputs are explicit bounds: an optimization risk of Õ(1/T) after T GD iterations, a population risk of Õ(1/n) under NTK separability, and a DP utility bound of Õ(√d/(nε)) for the trajectory-averaged population risk under (ε,δ)-DP. The 1/√d and 1/√m normalizations in the first and second layers respectively stabilize pre-activations and output scale as d and m grow.

## Method

The paper develops a unified reference-point framework for analyzing GD and DP-GD on two-layer B-spline KANs. The core technical tool is a double induction that simultaneously bounds the cumulative training loss and the parameter distances ‖Θ(k)−Θ(0)‖₂ and ‖Θ(k)−Θ*‖₂, establishing a 'stay-in-a-ball' property that certifies local smoothness and almost-convexity of the loss along the trajectory despite its trajectory-dependent curvature. Under an NTK-separability assumption (weaker than Gram-matrix positive-definiteness), polylogarithmic width m ≳ polylog(n,T) suffices for GD to achieve Õ(1/T) optimization and Õ(1/n) generalization via on-average argument stability. For the private setting, the authors design a projected DP-GD with explicit Gaussian noise calibration and a trajectory-wise sensitivity analysis, proving that polylogarithmic width is both sufficient and essentially necessary to attain the utility rate Õ(√d/(nε)), matching the classical convex Lipschitz lower bound and revealing a qualitative gap between nonprivate and private training regimes.

## Results

The paper's headline results are theoretical bounds for two-layer KANs trained by GD and DP-GD under NTK separability with margin γ: an optimization rate of Õ(1/(γ²ηT)), a fast-rate generalization bound of Õ(1/(γ⁴n)), and a DP utility bound of Õ(√d/(γ⁴nε)) for the trajectory-averaged population risk, matching the classical convex Lipschitz lower bound. Empirically, on MNIST with ε=2, private utility peaks at m≈8 and T≈64, while on synthetic logistic data the critical width is m_crit≈24 and critical iterations T_crit≈256 beyond which test accuracy saturates. In the NTK margin validation (Table 1), KANs achieve consistently larger margins than parameter-matched ReLU networks across three nonlinear datasets, with the gap widening at larger widths (e.g., 0.1027 vs 0.0197 on Multi-interval at m=512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no standard benchmark comparisons against competing training methods. The closest empirical comparison is between KANs and parameter-matched two-layer ReLU networks on NTK margin (Table 1): KANs achieve 2–5× larger margins than ReLU across all three nonlinear synthetic datasets (Multi-interval, XOR, Checkerboard), with the advantage growing with width. Theoretically, the optimization bound improves upon Gao & Tan (2025), which requires polynomially scaling width for linear convergence, whereas this work achieves sublinear Õ(1/T) convergence with only polylogarithmic width under the weaker NTK-separability assumption. For the DP utility bound, the paper matches the classical convex Lipschitz lower bound of Bassily et al. (2019) in its dependence on d, n, and ε, and is the first such analysis for KANs. No direct head-to-head accuracy comparisons against other KAN training methods or private training baselines are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GD convergence analysis for two-layer KANs initiated by Gao & Tan (2025), who required polynomial width under a strong NTK positive-definiteness assumption, by replacing it with the weaker NTK separability condition to achieve polylogarithmic width. For generalization, it departs from the algorithm-independent uniform-convergence bounds in prior KAN work (Zhang & Zhou, 2025; Li et al., 2025b) by using the on-average argument stability technique of Lei & Ying (2020) to derive the first algorithm-dependent generalization bound for GD-trained KANs. The differential privacy analysis is entirely new for KANs, adapting the projected DP-GD framework from the MLP literature (Wang et al., 2025a; Shi et al., 2026) to the B-spline-parameterized edge-function architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical framework simultaneously covering optimization, generalization, and differential privacy for KAN training, yielding concrete practical guidance (polylogarithmic width suffices, early stopping helps under privacy). The key conceptual contribution is the qualitative gap: polylogarithmic width is sufficient for non-private GD but essentially necessary for DP-GD to match the classical convex Lipschitz lower bound, revealing that privacy fundamentally tightens the admissible architecture regime. Limitations include restriction to two-layer KANs, smooth activations (excluding ReLU), and full-batch GD rather than SGD, leaving open whether these results extend to deeper or stochastic settings.
