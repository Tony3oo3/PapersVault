## Problem

Diffusion-based anomaly detection methods inherit multi-step noise schedules, timestep conditioning, and iterative reverse processes designed for high-fidelity generation, yet anomaly detection only requires assessing local manifold compliance. This paper asks whether the generative machinery of diffusion is actually necessary for tabular one-class anomaly detection, or whether a single fixed-noise denoising step with a different inference-time scoring mechanism is sufficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261571/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standardized d-dimensional tabular vectors as input, trained exclusively on normal (uncontaminated) samples in a strict one-class setting. At inference, each test sample is perturbed K=15 times with independent Gaussian noise at a fixed scale σ²=0.2 (SNR=5), and the output is a scalar anomaly score equal to the mean squared deviation between each denoised output and the original clean input — not the denoising loss itself. The score is threshold-free and comparable across datasets with varying anomaly prevalence (0.03%–39.9%).

## Method

DenoiserAD trains a ResMLP backbone under a fixed-noise denoising objective (no schedule, no timestep embedding) using EDM-style preconditioning that decomposes the denoiser into an explicit linear reference channel c_skip·x plus a learned nonlinear correction c_out·g_θ(c_in·x). The key architectural choice is the reference channel: it forces the network to learn a residual correction rather than a direct reconstruction, providing a fixed baseline against which perturbation-induced deviations are measured. At inference, the anomaly score A(y) = E_n[‖f_θ(y+n) − y‖²] is computed over K stochastic perturbations, which is deliberately decoupled from the training loss. A first-order Taylor expansion shows this score decomposes into a bias term (‖f_θ(y)−y‖²) and a stability term (σ²‖J_f(y)‖_F²), so it probes local functional sensitivity of the denoising field rather than merely measuring reconstruction error. Ablations confirm that both the reference-channel parameterization and the input-referenced stability score are necessary: removing either degrades performance, and a direct Jacobian-norm baseline underperforms the finite-neighborhood score.

## Results

On the ADBench benchmark (121 sub-datasets), DenoiserAD achieves an average AUCROC of 83.34, improving over the strongest prior baseline (NCSBAD, 82.34) by 1.22% in AUCROC and 1.13% in AUCPR. The method also outperforms DTE (79.77) and DDPM (70.73) by substantial margins. Performance is stable across a broad noise-scale plateau (σ² ∈ [0.12, 0.35]) and saturates at K=15 perturbations, with inference cost remaining near-constant due to parallel evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenoiserAD outperforms all 17 baselines on ADBench across AUCROC and AUCPR. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitors, it beats NCSBAD (82.34) by ~1.0 AUCROC points, DTE (79.77) by ~3.6 points, and DDPM (70.73) by ~12.6 points. Among classical tabular methods (kNN, LOF, KPCA, GMM, EIF, ABOD, PCA, KDE), the paper reports consistent wins but defers per-dataset numbers to Appendix G; the box-plot rank distribution (Figure 4) shows DenoiserAD achieving the best mean rank across all 121 sub-datasets. Deep one-class and self-supervised baselines (DROCC, GOAD, ICL, LUNAR, SLAD) are also outperformed, with the gap most pronounced for reconstruction-style methods that suffer from the identity-mapping failure mode. The MCM baseline (Yin et al., 2024) is likewise surpassed. Notably, the gains are attributed to the stability-based scoring formulation rather than architecture: applying the fixed-noise stability formulation to DDPM, DTE, and NCSBAD backbones consistently improves each (Table 3), while simply swapping in the ResMLP backbone without the stability score yields only marginal or negative changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the denoising objective from diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song & Ermon, 2019) and the EDM preconditioning scheme (Karras et al., 2022) for tabular one-class anomaly detection, but deliberately strips away the generative machinery—noise schedules, time conditioning, and multi-step reverse processes—that prior tabular diffusion AD methods (Livernoche et al., 2024; Hirth & Kasneci, 2025) retain. It also departs from self-supervised AD methods (Golan & El-Yaniv, 2018; Shenkar & Wolf, 2022) that typically reuse the auxiliary training loss as the anomaly score, instead decoupling training (fixed-noise denoising) from inference (input-referenced stability under repeated perturbations). The explicit linear reference channel in the denoiser parameterization is a structural addition not present in standard DAEs or diffusion denoisers, forcing the network to learn a residual correction rather than a direct reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the computationally expensive multi-step diffusion pipeline is unnecessary for tabular anomaly detection, offering a simpler, faster alternative with competitive performance. The stability-based score addresses a known failure mode of reconstruction-based methods—near-manifold but unsupported anomalies that DAEs reconstruct well—by probing local functional sensitivity rather than pointwise error. Key limitations include sensitivity to training-set contamination (requiring clean one-class data), restriction to tabular/feature-space inputs, and the inference cost of K stochastic perturbations per sample. Open questions include whether the stability formulation transfers to raw image, text, or time-series modalities, and how to make the method robust to mixed training distributions without the trimmed-loss heuristic.
