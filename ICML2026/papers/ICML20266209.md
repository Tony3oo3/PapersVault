## Problem

Existing federated learning frameworks either assume static local datasets with fixed distributions or adopt an overly pessimistic adversarial perspective (online FL), leaving a gap for streaming settings where client data evolves over time with structured, partially predictable patterns (e.g., UAVs following scheduled routes). The paper asks whether partial predictions about temporal distribution shifts can be leveraged to jointly optimize client-side data sampling and server-side aggregation in a principled, theoretically grounded manner.

## Input / Output

Each of N clients continuously receives new samples from one of M latent states (modeled via Dirichlet class distributions) over T time steps per round, while maintaining a fixed-capacity local buffer. A prediction oracle supplies an estimated state distribution π̂_n per client (e.g., via Bayesian Dirichlet updates from historical observations), which drives both the per-state sampling ratios α_{n,m} and the server-side aggregation weights p_n. The final output is a global classification model trained over R communication rounds, evaluated on standard image classification benchmarks (32×32 to 224×224 RGB images).

## Method

SFedPO models each client's data as governed by a latent state space and introduces two coordinated modules derived from a convergence bound (Theorem 4.5). Distribution-guided Data Sampling (DDS) computes per-state sampling ratios via a score function that balances the global objective weight w_m, the state-specific gradient heterogeneity bound d_m, and the client's predicted state probability π̂_{n,m}, then updates the local distribution as a convex combination of the previous buffer and the current state's distribution. Shift-aware Aggregation Weights (SAW) assigns each client a heterogeneity score s_n capturing the combined effect of its state distribution and sampling strategy, then sets aggregation weights inversely proportional to s_n (with a 1/q_n fairness term). A robustness theorem (Theorem 5.1) shows that convergence degrades only linearly in the L1 prediction error of the oracle, and the framework is demonstrated to be plug-and-play, improving FedAvg, FedProx, FedCurv, FedNTD, and FedEXP under streaming conditions.

## Results

SFedPO achieves the best test accuracy across all four datasets (Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, HAM10000) in both full-access and partial-access (extreme heterogeneity) scenarios. On CIFAR-10, SFedPO reaches 67.45% (full) and 63.53% (partial), surpassing the strongest baseline (Flash) by 3.58% and 7.45% respectively. On HAM10000, SFedPO attains 68.63% (full) and 64.23% (partial), outperforming the next best method (DRSR) by 8.91% and 11.81%. The performance gap widens under partial access, confirming that SFedPO's distribution-guided sampling and shift-aware aggregation are most beneficial under severe client heterogeneity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFedPO outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and scenario. The closest competitor is Flash (Panchal et al., 2023), a concept-drift adaptation method that adjusts learning rates based on update magnitudes; SFedPO exceeds Flash by 3.58% on CIFAR-10 (full) and 7.45% on CIFAR-10 (partial), and by 3.93% on HAM10000 (full). Among data-selection baselines, DRSR (Wang et al., 2023a) is the strongest, yet SFedPO still leads by 9.81% on CIFAR-10 (full) and 15.11% on CIFAR-10 (partial). Online FL methods (FedOGD, FedOMD) and concept-drift methods (AdapFedAvg, FedDrift) cluster tightly together (within ~1% of each other) and are consistently 10–16% below SFedPO on CIFAR-10, indicating that adversarial or reactive drift-adaptation strategies are insufficient for structured temporal shifts. Federated continual learning baselines (FLwF-2T, FedEWC) perform comparably to the online FL group, confirming that task-sequential forgetting mitigation does not address the streaming distribution-shift problem. The gap between SFedPO and all baselines is notably larger in the partial-access (extreme heterogeneity) scenario, validating that the prediction-oracle-guided sampling and shift-aware aggregation provide the greatest benefit when clients have highly restricted and heterogeneous state access.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFedPO positions itself as a principled middle ground between two extremes in federated learning: static-data FL (FedAvg and variants) that assumes fixed distributions, and online FL (FedOMD, FedOGD) that adopts a fully adversarial perspective. It builds on the FedAvg training loop but introduces a prediction oracle over a latent state space to capture temporal distribution shifts, then jointly optimizes client-side data sampling (DDS) and server-side aggregation weights (SAW) guided by a convergence analysis. Conceptually, it draws from concept drift adaptation (reactive methods like Flash, FedDrift), federated continual learning (FedEWC, FLwF-2T), and data selection in FL (ODE, DRSR), but departs from all of these by explicitly modeling and predicting the temporal evolution of distributions rather than merely reacting to detected shifts or assuming a long-term stationary distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, theoretically grounded path for streaming FL in settings where data dynamics are structured but non-stationary (UAVs, mobile sensing, periodic sensor data), filling a gap that neither static FL nor adversarial online FL addresses well. The plug-and-play modularity—consistently improving FedAvg, FedProx, FedCurv, FedNTD, and FedEXP without architectural changes—makes it broadly applicable. Open questions include: how well the prediction oracle assumption holds in truly unpredictable environments, whether the latent state space (M=60 in experiments) scales to open-ended drift, and whether the Dirichlet-based Bayesian oracle can be replaced by lightweight learned predictors without degrading the convergence guarantees. The robustness to prediction errors (linear degradation in L1 error) is reassuring but the practical oracle design remains somewhat idealized.
