## Problem

Looped transformers like TRM achieve strong reasoning on ARC-AGI but rely on Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (TBPTT) with deep supervision to handle long-horizon training instability. Standard diffusion avoids this by training a single-step denoiser on noised targets, but suffers a train-test mismatch: intermediate states at test time are self-generated rather than noised ground truth, leading to compounding errors and greedy updates. The paper asks whether one can retain diffusion's curriculum of intermediate difficulty states while preserving the train-test alignment and multi-step credit assignment of recursive backward-training.

## Input / Output

Each ARC-AGI task provides 2–5 few-shot input-output grid pairs (each grid up to 30×30 with 10 colours) as context, and the model must predict the output grid for a held-out input grid. The model is non-autoregressive: at every recursive step it produces a complete fixed-size prediction in parallel (one token per cell position). At inference, the grid is initialized as fully MASKed and the model iteratively predicts a full grid followed by stochastic re-masking of a shrinking subset of cells until no masks remain (16 denoising steps). A learned task embedding shared across all examples of the same task conditions the recursion, and a scalar Q-head predicts exact-match correctness for early exit and candidate selection.

## Method

Denoising Recursion Models (DRMs) combine the forward corruption process of diffusion with multi-step recursive denoising. During training, a corruption level τ is sampled uniformly, the ground-truth grid Y is corrupted via a discrete masking schedule (cosine schedule for the masking fraction) to produce Ỹ_τ, and the model's latent state is initialized as H_0 = Embed(Ỹ_τ). The shared transition operator Φ_θ is then unrolled for k recursive steps with full backpropagation through all k steps, and the loss is the negative log-likelihood of the clean target Y given the final state H_k. This requires optimizing only a single recursive window (no TBPTT detachment schedule), yet the k-step backpropagation provides multi-step credit assignment that incentivizes forward-looking, non-greedy latent updates. At inference, the model starts from the maximally corrupted state (all tokens MASKed) and iteratively applies the denoiser over a decreasing sequence of corruption levels, predicting the full grid and stochastically re-masking a shrinking subset at each step. A secondary variant, SPRM, instead injects Gaussian noise into the latent hidden states at TBPTT truncation points during backward-training, increasing trajectory diversity without changing the initialization.

## Results

On ARC2-Eval (120 hard tasks), the 14M-parameter DRM with NVARC pretraining achieves 24.9% pass@2 exact-match accuracy, outperforming the TRM baseline (21.8%) and the 4B-parameter NVARC LLM (22.6%). On the easier ARC-Easy set (1000 tasks), DRM (7M, no pretraining) reaches 50.5% versus TRM's 45.7%. Standard masked diffusion without recursive weight sharing collapses to ~0% accuracy even at 70M parameters, confirming that iterative refinement via shared weights is the primary driver of performance. On Sudoku, TRM (87.9%) substantially outperforms DRM (20%), while on Maze DRM (81%) edges out TRM (78%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TRM baseline (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025), DRM consistently wins on ARC-AGI: 50.5% vs 45.7% on ARC-Easy (7M, no pretraining) and 24.9% vs 21.8% on ARC2-Eval (14M, NVARC pretraining), with gains of 3–5 points across all pretraining scales. However, on Sudoku TRM (87.9%) far outperforms DRM (20%), which the authors attribute to Sudoku's need for long sequential search that DRM's shorter gradient windows do not support. Against the 4B-parameter NVARC LLM (Sorokin & Puget, 2025), the 14M DRM achieves 24.9% vs 22.6% on ARC2-Eval with matched NVARC pretraining data, demonstrating roughly 285× parameter efficiency. Against ViARC (Hu et al., 2025), DRM (14M, +reARC) reaches 21.0% on ARC2-Eval versus ViARC's 8.3%, though the two use different architectures and the comparison is not perfectly controlled. Standard masked diffusion (the forward-training analogue) fails almost entirely (~0%), confirming that the recursive inductive bias is essential and that the one-step denoising objective alone is insufficient for ARC-AGI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: iterative-refinement looped transformers (Lee et al. 2018 → HRM → TRM) and denoising diffusion models. It builds directly on the TRM architecture (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025) and borrows the forward corruption process from diffusion (Ho et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2022 for discrete masking), but recombines them in a novel way: instead of training a single-step denoiser (forward-training) or unrolling from pure noise with TBPTT (backward-training), DRMs initialize from a corrupted target and backpropagate through k recursive denoising steps. This hybrid addresses a specific train-test mismatch identified in pure diffusion (the model is trained on noised targets but tested on self-generated trajectories) while avoiding the long-horizon instability of pure backward-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 14M-parameter looped transformer can outperform a 4B-parameter LLM system (NVARC) on ARC-AGI-2 when trained on identical data, reinforcing that training objective design—not just scale—is a critical bottleneck for algorithmic reasoning. The complete failure of standard masked diffusion (~0%) even at 70M parameters, contrasted with strong looped-model performance, provides a useful diagnostic for why iterative refinement via shared weights is essential for discrete, exact-match reasoning tasks. Key open questions include why DRM underperforms TRM on tasks requiring long sequential search (Sudoku: 20% vs 87.9%), whether the gap closes with longer gradient windows, and whether the approach generalizes beyond grid-based puzzles to other discrete reasoning domains.
