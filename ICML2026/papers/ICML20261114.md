## Problem

Generative policies for offline RL face a stark trade-off: diffusion-based policies capture complex multi-modal behaviors but require slow iterative sampling, while consistency-based policies enable fast one-to-two-step generation at the cost of degraded policy quality. This paper asks whether a single policy class can achieve both high expressiveness and computational efficiency, and identifies the missing piece as a principled framework that unifies these seemingly disparate generative approaches.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a continuous environment state s as conditioning input and produces a continuous action by starting from pure Gaussian noise a_T ~ N(0, T²I) and iteratively applying the learned flow map Φ_θ over K=5 timesteps to yield the final denoised action a_0. Unlike standard diffusion policies that require hundreds of steps or consistency policies limited to 1–2 steps, GTP achieves high-fidelity generation with a small fixed number of deterministic steps, making it practical for real-time control while retaining the expressiveness of the full trajectory.

## Method

GTP unifies diffusion, flow matching, and consistency models under a single continuous-time ODE framework where the policy learns the full solution map Φ(x_t, t, s) rather than just a denoiser to t=0. Training combines two complementary objectives: an Instantaneous Flow Loss (local anchor enforcing correct infinitesimal dynamics) and a Trajectory Consistency Loss (global regulator enforcing self-consistency across multi-step jumps). Two key adaptations make this practical for offline RL: (1) a score approximation that replaces the true vector field with the closed-form surrogate (x_t − x)/t, eliminating costly ODE solving and breaking the self-referential instability of early training (proven O(h^p)-equivalent to the ideal objective); and (2) an advantage-weighted generative objective that multiplies both loss terms by exp(η·max(0,A)/std(A)), derived from KL-regularized policy optimization, to shift probability mass toward high-value actions while remaining within dataset support. The full algorithm pairs this generative actor with a standard double Q-network critic in an actor-critic loop.

## Results

On D4RL Gym tasks, GTP achieves an average normalized score of 89.0, outperforming the previous best generative policy D-QL (87.9) and non-generative methods like χ-QL (83.7) and IQL (77.0). On the notoriously difficult AntMaze suite, GTP reaches 80.6 average, surpassing IDQL-A (79.1), QGPO (78.3), and D-QL (69.6), and achieves a perfect 100.0 score on antmaze-umaze. In the pure behavior cloning setting (Table 1), GTP-BC averages 82.3 on Gym and 66.3 on AntMaze, substantially exceeding D-BC (76.3/41.2) and C-BC (69.7/44.1), confirming the expressiveness advantage of learning the full ODE solution map.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTP outperforms all 12 baselines on the AntMaze average (80.6 vs. next-best IDQL-A at 79.1 and QGPO at 78.3), with particularly large margins over D-QL (69.6) and CQL (50.6). On Gym tasks, GTP leads with 89.0 average, edging out D-QL (87.9) and BDM (87.3), while substantially surpassing value-based methods like CQL (77.6) and IQL (77.0). Among generative-policy competitors, GTP beats D-QL by 1.1 on Gym and 11.0 on AntMaze, and outperforms C-AC (85.1/–) on Gym. In the BC-only setting, GTP-BC (82.3/66.3) dominates D-BC (76.3/41.2) and C-BC (69.7/44.1), with the largest gap on AntMaze where long-horizon multimodal planning is critical. GTP achieves a perfect 100.0 on antmaze-umaze, a task where no other method reaches full score. The ablation confirms that the score approximation and advantage-weighted objective are both essential: removing the approximation drops hopper-me from 112.2 to 99.7, and replacing variational guidance with linear Q-loss causes training divergence at typical λ values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies diffusion models, flow matching, consistency models, and consistency trajectory models (CTMs) under a single continuous-time ODE solution-map framework, then adapts this framework to offline RL as a new policy class (GTP). The flow-map parameterization is directly inspired by CTMs (Kim et al., 2024), the trajectory consistency loss generalizes the self-consistency objective from Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023), and the instantaneous flow loss subsumes both diffusion denoising and flow-matching velocity regression. For the RL side, the advantage-weighted training objective follows the standard KL-regularized policy improvement formulation used in AWAC and CQL, while the score approximation technique is a novel practical adaptation that replaces costly ODE solver calls with a closed-form surrogate anchored to offline data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTP resolves the long-standing expressiveness-efficiency trade-off in generative policies for offline RL by learning the full ODE solution map, enabling high-quality generation in just 5 steps without the iterative cost of diffusion or the quality loss of one-step consistency policies. The unified ODE framework provides a principled design space that clarifies how prior generative models relate to each other, potentially guiding future policy architectures. However, the paper acknowledges that training time remains substantial (4+ hours per task), and evaluation is limited to standard D4RL benchmarks; extending to more complex observation spaces (visual, high-dimensional) and reducing training cost are important open questions. The practical impact is significant for offline RL practitioners seeking expressive policies without the inference overhead of diffusion.
