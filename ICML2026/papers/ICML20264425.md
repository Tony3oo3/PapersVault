## Problem

Training visual editing models typically requires massive paired datasets of source and edited outputs, which is prohibitively expensive for long-tail edits and especially for video where aligned pairs barely exist. This paper asks whether external paired supervision is necessary at all, given that a pretrained generative model already possesses deep knowledge of visual content and can guide edits toward target descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264425/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image or video, a text edit instruction, and source/target captions (describing the scene before and after editing) as input, and produces an edited image or video. No ground-truth edited target is ever used during training. For images, the model is a LoRA-adapted FLUX.1-dev (rank 64) operating in VAE latent space with 20 inference steps; for video, it is a LoRA-adapted Wan2.2 text-to-video model at 480×832×81 resolution using the UniPC sampler. Training data consists of unpaired images from OpenImages with VLM-generated edit specifications, or unpaired videos from VideoUFO wrapped in style templates (cartoon or photo-realistic).

## Method

The framework decomposes editing into instruction following and source preservation, addressing each with a distinct loss derived from unpaired data. For instruction following, a frozen text-to-image model is queried with both source and target captions on the same noisy input; the editing model's velocity is supervised to align with the directional difference (v_tgt − v_src) via a cosine loss, plus an MSE anchor to regulate magnitude. For source preservation, a cycle-consistency loss requires that applying the inverse instruction to the one-step edited prediction reconstructs the source. A key technical contribution is gradient routing via Straight-Through Estimation: the reverse pass conditions on a clean multi-step EMA prediction (matching inference) while backpropagating gradients through the noisy one-step prediction, bridging the train-inference gap. A frozen EMA copy of the editing model bootstraps its own noisy training inputs by running n denoising steps from pure noise to the current timestep, eliminating the need for ground-truth edited targets.

## Results

On the long-tail style-editing benchmark (6 unseen styles, 335 images), the method achieves an Overall VIEScore of 8.30 (Style→Photorealistic) and 6.33 (Photorealistic→Style), outperforming the best supervised baseline FLUX-Kontext (7.85 / 6.00) and the zero-shot FlowEdit (6.20 / 3.64). On video editing, the method achieves a 75.3% ± 2.2% user-preference win rate against Ditto (trained on 1M paired videos), with 85.0% wins on out-of-distribution 3D-CGI inputs. On GEdit-Bench general editing, the method is competitive with FLUX-Kontext overall, notably outperforming it on motion change (6.88 vs 4.53), human edits (6.59 vs 3.94), and style change (6.95 vs 5.90), while trailing on subject removal (1.91 vs 6.94) and text editing (2.10 vs 5.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ditto (Bai et al., 2025), a supervised video editing model trained on 1M paired videos, the unpaired method wins decisively: 75.3% user-preference win rate overall (70.0% cartoon, 80.5% photo-realistic), and leads on CLIP directional similarity (0.104 vs 0.091), DINO source preservation (0.718 vs 0.536), and Motion Fidelity (0.715 vs 0.616). Against FLUX-Kontext (Labs et al., 2025), a supervised image editor trained on millions of pairs, the method wins on all six long-tail styles in both directions (e.g., 8.30 vs 7.85 Overall for Style→Photo) and on GEdit-Bench categories of motion, human, and style edits, but loses on subject removal (1.91 vs 6.94) and text editing (2.10 vs 5.44) where explicit paired supervision provides a clear advantage. Against Qwen-Image-Edit (Wu et al., 2025), the method again leads on all long-tail styles in both directions. Against the zero-shot FlowEdit (Kulikov et al., 2025), the method dominates across every benchmark and metric, with the largest gaps on semantic consistency (e.g., 5.22 vs 1.46 on Photorealistic→Style), confirming that the learned unpaired objective substantially exceeds what zero-shot flow inversion can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as the generative backbone, adapts Straight-Through Estimation (Bengio et al., 2013) from discrete bottlenecks to continuous denoising for gradient routing, and extends cycle consistency from CycleGAN (Zhu et al., 2017) from two-domain GAN translation to open-ended instruction-based editing in a flow-matching setting. It departs from supervised editing methods (FLUX-Kontext, Ditto, Qwen-Image-Edit) that require millions of paired examples, and from NP-Edit (Kumari et al., 2025) which relies on external VLM feedback, by instead extracting instruction-following supervision from the frozen base model's own velocity field via a directional regularization that isolates the semantic edit direction. The key conceptual departure is treating the pretrained T2I model as an internal teacher whose knowledge of target captions provides a training signal without any paired data or external reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for paired training data, this framework directly addresses the scalability bottleneck that limits editing to narrow domains and makes video editing prohibitively expensive to train. The strong generalization to unseen styles and out-of-distribution 3D-CGI inputs (85% win rate) suggests the unpaired paradigm may be more robust than supervised training on synthetic pairs, which can propagate artifacts. Key limitations include weaker performance on object removal (caption-based supervision lacks explicit absence signals) and inheritance of base-model biases. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex multi-step or compositional edits, and how it scales to 4D or interactive editing settings where even unpaired data collection becomes non-trivial.
