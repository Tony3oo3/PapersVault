## Problem

Most continual RL benchmarks are restricted to 3–15 sequential tasks because CPU-bound environments make longer sequences computationally impractical, obscuring long-horizon failure modes such as plasticity loss and over-regularization. Meanwhile, continual learning in cooperative multi-agent settings—where inter-agent dependencies can amplify individual forgetting into team-level failures—remains largely unexplored. MEAL addresses both gaps by providing the first GPU-accelerated, procedurally generated benchmark for continual multi-agent RL, enabling 100-task sequences on a single GPU in a few hours.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a grid-based observation of shape (H_max, W_max, 26) encoding tile types and states (walls, agents, onions, plates, pots, delivery stations, cooking progress, held items), padded to a fixed maximum size across the sequence; a partially observable variant restricts this to a direction-aware egocentric window (2×3 to 3×5) that scales with difficulty. The agent outputs one of six discrete actions (up, down, left, right, stay, interact) per timestep. Evaluation produces three normalized metrics—average soup delivery (score), an exponentially weighted forgetting score, and a forward-transfer AUC ratio—computed against a single-agent optimal cook-deliver cycle bound specific to each layout.

## Method

MEAL is built entirely in JAX on top of JaxMARL's Overcooked implementation, using JIT compilation and vectorized GPU execution to train 2048 parallel environments simultaneously (~2 min per 10⁸-step task). A procedural kitchen generator produces solvable layouts on the fly by sampling grid dimensions, stamping interactive tiles, injecting walls to match a target obstacle density, and validating via BFS reachability checks, yielding a continuous space of variable-difficulty tasks across three levels. The core MARL algorithm is IPPO (independent PPO with shared reward), paired with a multi-head actor-critic architecture where each task receives a dedicated output slice selected by task index, isolating per-task policies while sharing a common backbone. The benchmark supports three sequence regimes (fixed-level, curriculum, repetition), diverse frozen partners for ad-hoc teamwork, partial observability, and additional non-stationary dynamics (variable pot size, soup timer, sticky actions, slippery tiles). Key empirical findings include that multi-head outputs are the single most critical architectural component, that standard EWC saturates around 20 tasks while Online EWC sustains learning over 100, and that adding agents beyond a layout-dependent optimum sharply increases forgetting.

## Results

On the primary Overcooked-based sequences, PackNet achieves the highest average normalized soup delivery (S) at every difficulty level—1.75±0.13 (Level 1), 1.38±0.11 (Level 2), and 1.29±0.04 (Level 3)—while maintaining near-zero forgetting (F≈0.00). AGEM attains the best forward transfer (FT=+0.04 on Level 1, the only positive value in the table) but pays a steep forgetting cost (F=0.41). A critical finding is that conclusions change with sequence length: standard EWC scores 1.60±0.08 on 10 tasks but collapses to 0.23±0.03 on 100 tasks, whereas Online EWC retains 1.45±0.05, demonstrating that long-horizon dynamics invisible at small scale can reverse method rankings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PackNet dominates on average score (S) at all three difficulty levels, outperforming EWC by 0.75 (L1), 0.40 (L2), and 0.67 (L3), and beating MAS by 0.47, 0.31, and 0.48 respectively, while keeping forgetting at exactly 0.00. AGEM is the only method achieving non-negative forward transfer (+0.04 on L1, 0.00 on L2), but its forgetting is 20–40× higher than PackNet's (0.41 vs 0.00 on L1), making it unsuitable when retention matters. Online EWC is the strongest regularization-based method, scoring 1.61 on L1 and 1.13 on L3—substantially above cumulative EWC (1.00 and 0.62)—and the gap widens dramatically over 100-task sequences where EWC collapses to 0.23 while Online EWC holds 1.45. ER-ACE sits in the middle, forgetting less than FT/AGEM but retaining less plasticity than the regularizers. The paper further shows that the choice of MARL algorithm interacts with the CL method: HAPPO lifts Online EWC from 1.61 to 1.80 on L1, while PackNet peaks under IPPO (1.75) and gains nothing from more complex algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEAL sits at the intersection of two established benchmarking paradigms: CRL benchmarks that organize existing environments into task sequences (CORA, COOM, Continual World, Continual Bench) and JAX-based MARL platforms that enable hardware-accelerated multi-agent simulation (JaxMARL, SocialJAX, Craftax, Kinetix). It builds directly on JaxMARL's Overcooked implementation and extends it with procedural kitchen generation, scalable difficulty, and continual learning evaluation. The key departure from prior CRL benchmarks is the shift from CPU-bound, 3–15 task sequences to GPU-accelerated 100-task sequences, and the key departure from prior MARL benchmarks is the addition of continual learning with partner adaptation. A small number of prior CMARL works exist (Lifelong Hanabi, MACPro, RPG, COMAD), but none provide a general, scalable, hardware-accelerated benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically important finding is that method rankings in CRL can change substantially as task sequences lengthen: standard EWC appears competitive on 10 tasks but collapses on 100 tasks (score 1.60 → 0.23), while Online EWC sustains performance (1.45), suggesting that many prior CRL conclusions drawn from short sequences may not generalize. The benchmark also establishes that continual cooperation is a distinct challenge from single-agent retention—adding agents worsens both learning and forgetting in ways that single-agent CL cannot capture. Open questions include methods purpose-built for CMARL that jointly handle partner- and environment-level non-stationarity, credit assignment under partial observability across long sequences, and whether the observed stability-plasticity trade-offs persist with more sophisticated MARL algorithms or communication mechanisms. A limitation is the primary focus on Overcooked, though the authors demonstrate extensibility to JAXNAV, SMAX, and MPE.
