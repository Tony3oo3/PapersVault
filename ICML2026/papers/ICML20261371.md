## Problem

Existing LLM-based automated heuristic design (AHD) methods rely on fixed evolutionary operators and static prompt templates, treating heuristic generation as a sequence of isolated sampling steps without memory of how heuristics were derived. This leads to premature convergence, redundant evaluations, and similar heuristic rediscovery, as neither population-based nor tree-based frameworks maintain a stateful, semantic representation of derivation history or adapt their search strategy to evolving dynamics.

## Input / Output

The system takes a COP specification (instance space, solution space, cost function), a search framework (step-by-step constructive, ACO, or GLS), a training dataset of problem instances, and an LLM backbone (GPT-4o-mini or GPT-5-nano at low/medium reasoning). It outputs an evolved heuristic as executable code whose form depends on the framework: a selection function for constructive, a heuristic information matrix for ACO, or a penalty function for GLS. The entire process is training-free—no LLM parameters are updated—and the heuristic is evaluated under a fixed budget of 500 evaluations with 60-second execution time per heuristic on the training set.

## Method

PathWise formulates heuristic discovery as a sequential MDP over an entailment graph, where each node stores a tuple of (heuristic code, natural-language derivation rationale, algorithmic description, performance score, compressed parent metadata) and directed edges encode how a child heuristic was derived from its parents. A coordinated multi-agent system operates at each entailment step: a Policy Agent selects parent nodes and generates a natural-language derivation rationale (the action), a World Model Agent executes the action by producing heuristic code rollouts, and two Critic Agents (Policy Critic and World Model Critic) synthesize routed reflections from performance contrasts to condition the policy and world model at the next step. The system runs on two timescales—an outer loop maintaining a compact population of root nodes and an inner loop incrementally expanding the entailment graph—balancing exploration and exploitation through a leaf-first population selection strategy. Prompt-level diversity is enforced via a decaying exploration rate that injects role-specific exploratory phrases and via random state shuffling to mitigate positional bias, ensuring the policy explores diverse parent selections and the world model generates contrasting rollouts.

## Results

On the ACO framework across 5 COPs (TSP, CVRP, MKP, OP, offline BPP), PathWise achieves mean relative gap improvements (MRGI) of 60.22%, 38.73%, 89.35%, 54.81%, and 44.86% respectively over LLM-based AHD baselines, averaged across test sets and LLM backbones. On the step-by-step construction framework, PathWise achieves 20.38% average MRGI on TSP and up to 65.20% on KP (GPT-5-nano medium), while also outperforming the trained neural solver POMO on TSP (N=200) and KP. Notably, PathWise uses only 500 heuristic evaluations versus 1000 for baselines in convergence comparisons (Figure 1), yet achieves stronger performance with lower variance and faster convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PathWise consistently outperforms all five LLM-based AHD baselines (FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, MCTS-AHD) across all 10 test sets in the ACO framework and all 7 test sets in the constructive framework, for every LLM backbone tested (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5-nano low, GPT-5-nano medium). Against the trained neural solver DeepACO, PathWise achieves better gaps on MKP (0.16% vs 1.82% at N=100) and OP (8.60% vs 0.94% at N=200 is a loss, but PathWise wins at N=50 with 3.04% vs 0.00%—actually DeepACO wins there; the paper states PathWise outperforms DeepACO on OP N=200 and MKP). Against POMO (trained NCO), PathWise achieves lower gaps on TSP N=200 (12.14% vs 21.52%) and all KP test sets. Against the original ACO heuristic, PathWise reduces gaps by 10–40+ percentage points on CVRP and TSP. In convergence comparisons (Figure 1), PathWise reaches better best-so-far performance with half the evaluation budget (500 vs 1000) and lower run-to-run variance. The largest relative gains appear on MKP (89.35% MRGI) and CVRP, where baselines struggle most with capacity constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

PathWise extends the LLM-based AHD lineage (FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, MCTS-AHD) by replacing their stateless population or tree-based search with a sequential decision process over an entailment graph, borrowing the graph structure from Dalvi et al. (2021) and the world-model planning framing from Hao et al. (2023). The critic agents' verbal-gradient feedback mechanism is directly adapted from Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023), but is routed to distinct policy and world-model agents rather than applied monolithically. The key conceptual departure is from fixed evolutionary operators and static prompt templates to a policy agent that dynamically invents derivation rationales in natural language, making the search state-aware rather than statistically driven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a training-free, multi-agent framework with structured derivation memory can match or exceed baselines at half the evaluation budget across six COPs and three search frameworks, PathWise offers a practical recipe for more sample-efficient LLM-based heuristic design. The consistent gains across GPT-4o-mini and GPT-5-nano (low/medium) suggest the approach is not tied to a specific model's capabilities. Open questions include whether the entailment graph's compact state representation degrades on problems with very long derivation chains, whether the multi-agent overhead (four LLM calls per entailment step) remains cost-effective at scale, and whether the framework transfers to domains beyond combinatorial optimization such as neural architecture search or prompt optimization.
