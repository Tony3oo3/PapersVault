## Problem

Despite the success of scaling paradigms in protein structure prediction and language modeling, cell foundation models for perturbation prediction consistently fail to outperform simple linear baselines. This position paper argues the primary failure mode is not insufficient model capacity or data volume, but inadequate coverage of diverse biological contexts — a causal transportability problem where the perturbation-to-response mechanism itself shifts across contexts, making additional data from the same contexts yield diminishing returns.

## Input / Output

The task takes a single-cell gene expression profile (G genes), a perturbation identifier (e.g., CRISPRi knockdown), and biological context covariates (donor identity, activation timepoint) as input, and produces a perturbed gene expression distribution as output. Evaluation uses both aggregate Δ-based metrics (Pearson correlation, cosine distance, L2 distance between predicted and true perturbation effects) and biologically grounded DEG-F1 (F1 score of differentially expressed gene recovery), with the paper demonstrating these metrics can dissociate substantially. The empirical analysis uses a 22M-cell genome-scale Perturb-seq dataset of primary human CD4+ T cells spanning ~12,000 gene knockdowns across 4 donors and 3 activation timepoints (resting, 8 hr, 48 hr post-stimulation).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a theoretical framework connecting perturbation prediction to causal transportability: when biological context modulates the mechanism linking perturbation P to response Y (a mechanism shift rather than covariate shift), predictions from source contexts cannot transfer to unseen contexts without target-domain interventions or explicit invariance assumptions. Empirically, they benchmark scLDM (a transformer autoencoder followed by a latent flow-matching diffusion model jointly conditioned on donor, timepoint, and perturbation embeddings) alongside CPA, GEARS, and perturbation-mean baselines on a cross-context transfer task where models train on a subset of donor-timepoint combinations and predict effects in held-out contexts. The central finding is that DEG-F1 improves substantially with the number of distinct training contexts in which a perturbation was observed (mean DEG-F1 rising from ≤0.1 to ~0.19 going from ≤3 to 8 contexts) but shows only weak association with training cell count (Spearman's r = 0.10), while aggregate L2-Δ correlates more strongly with cell count (r = −0.52) — demonstrating that scale improves standard scores without consistent gains in biologically meaningful recovery.

## Results

On a 22M-cell CD4+ T cell Perturb-seq dataset (4 donors × 3 timepoints × ~12,000 CRISPRi knockdowns), scLDM with explicit context conditioning outperforms mean baselines on Δ-based aggregate metrics (Pearson correlation, cosine distance, L2 distance) and achieves median discrimination scores >0.9 for held-out donor-timepoint contexts. However, DEG recovery is poor: scLDM achieves median precision ≈0.67 but median recall ≈0.09, recovering only ~9% of true differentially expressed genes. The central finding is that DEG-F1 improves from ≤0.1 to ~0.19 as training context coverage increases from ≤3 to 8 donor-timepoint combinations, while showing only a weak association with cell count (Spearman's r = 0.10); a controlled comparison at matched cell counts (700–1700) confirms the context-diversity benefit is significant (p = 0.002) while the L2-Δ difference is not (p ≥ 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks scLDM against two established deep perturbation-prediction models—CPA (Lotfollahi et al., 2023) and GEARS (Roohini et al., 2022)—and simple mean-based baselines (perturbation-mean, context-matched mean). On aggregate Δ-based metrics, scLDM outperforms both CPA and GEARS and the mean baselines, with CPA being competitive only on L2-Δ. However, on biologically meaningful DEG recovery, both CPA and GEARS fail: CPA achieves average precision and recall below 0.02, and GEARS could only be evaluated on a subset of test perturbations it supported. Critically, neither CPA nor GEARS consistently outperforms the simple mean baselines across aggregate metrics, consistent with the broader benchmarking literature (Ahlmann-Eltze et al., 2025). The paper's central comparison is not scLDM vs. CPA/GEARS per se, but rather a controlled analysis showing that context diversity (number of donor-timepoint combinations in which a perturbation was observed) drives DEG-F1 improvement far more than cell count, a finding that holds for scLDM and shows a weaker trend for CPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant scaling paradigm in single-cell perturbation prediction (scGPT, Geneformer, UCE, CPA, GEARS) by reframing the core challenge as a causal transportability problem rather than a data-volume or model-capacity problem. It draws on the causal inference literature (Pearl & Bareinboim's transportability framework) and decades-old statistical genetics results (GTEx tissue-specific eQTLs) to argue that the perturbation-to-response mapping is context-dependent, making it fundamentally different from context-invariant problems like protein folding. Empirically, it uses scLDM (a latent diffusion model) as a state-of-the-art test case on a 22M-cell CD4+ T cell Perturb-seq dataset to validate the hypothesis that context diversity, not cell count, drives cross-context generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's prevailing 'just scale up' narrative with a clean controlled experiment showing that observing the same perturbation across more biological contexts improves DEG recovery far more than adding cells from the same context. The dissociation between aggregate Δ-based metrics and biologically meaningful DEG recovery (models scoring well on correlation while recovering only ~9% of true DEGs) has immediate practical implications for how the community evaluates and optimizes perturbation models. Key open questions include whether conclusions extend beyond a single cell type and transcriptomic modality, how much context diversity is sufficient, and how to handle continuous rather than discrete biological contexts. The paper's recommendations for breadth-over-depth dataset design and multi-metric benchmarking could reshape how large-scale perturbation screens (e.g., Tahoe-100) are structured.
