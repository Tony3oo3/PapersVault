## Problem

Comparisons between autoregressive (AR) and masked diffusion model (MDM) paradigms for language modeling are confounded by their typical architectural pairings—AR with decoder-only causal attention and MDM with encoder-only full attention—making it impossible to isolate whether performance differences stem from the modeling formulation or the underlying architecture. This paper decouples these factors by implementing MDMs (as Any-Order AR) within a decoder-only framework, enabling equitable comparison of both the AR vs. MDM formulation and the encoder-only vs. decoder-only architecture choice for likelihood modeling and generation efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of up to 1024 tokens, where during training each token is paired with its original positional encoding plus an explicit target position encoding indicating which original position to predict next. During generation, the model starts from a fully masked sequence and iteratively unmask tokens over T diffusion steps, with a specialized attention mask ensuring that concurrently predicted masked tokens do not attend to each other. The output is a fully unmasked text sequence, and the model can be configured to generate in any token order (left-to-right, fixed random, or block-wise random).

## Method

AO-GPT is a decoder-only GPT architecture that extends the σ-GPT baseline with three key enhancements: adaptive LayerNorm (adaLN) conditioned on target position for richer position injection, Exponential Moving Average (EMA, decay 0.9999) for training stability, and 10% left-to-right data mixing to exploit language's inherent sequential structure. The training objective is the MDM/AO-AR loss, which averages cross-entropy over all n! token permutations and is shown to be mathematically equivalent to the masked diffusion ELBO. For efficient generation, the model leverages KV-caching combined with a two-stage sampling procedure (a Bernoulli trial for mask retention followed by vocabulary sampling only when unmasking) that restricts computation to tokens being unmasked, reducing total generation complexity to O(n) versus O(n²) for encoder-only MDMs. The paper demonstrates that with appropriate temperature annealing (top-p 0.95, temperature 0.7), AO-GPT achieves comparable generation perplexity to encoder-only SEDD while delivering a ~25× inference speedup.

## Results

On zero-shot validation perplexity (GPT-2-medium, ~350M), AO-GPT with 10% L2R data achieves 33.73 on WikiText2 and 32.38 on WikiText103 under Any-Order evaluation, compared to SEDD (31.04, 29.98) and RADD (29.96, 28.51). Context-order ensembling (64 permutations) narrows the gap substantially, bringing AO-GPT to 30.16 and 28.98 respectively. On generation perplexity with appropriate annealing (Top-p 0.95, Temp 0.7), AO-GPT reaches 4.6 at 1024 steps versus SEDD's 5.1, while achieving a ~25× generation speedup over SEDD. Under Left-to-Right evaluation, AO-GPT (31.52 WikiText2, 30.86 WikiText103) outperforms GPT-2 (31.80, 31.39) and is competitive with SEDD (30.29, 30.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SEDD (encoder-only MDM), AO-GPT (decoder-only MDM) shows higher Any-Order validation perplexity without ensembling (e.g., 33.73 vs 31.04 on WikiText2), but context-order ensembling with 64 permutations nearly closes the gap (30.16 vs 31.04). Against RADD (encoder-only MDM), the gap is similar: AO-GPT scores 32.38 vs 28.51 on WikiText103 without ensembling, narrowing to 28.98 with 64× ensembling. Against GPT-2 (standard decoder-only AR), AO-GPT with 10% L2R data actually outperforms GPT-2 on most Left-to-Right benchmarks (e.g., 31.52 vs 31.80 on WikiText2, 50.23 vs 55.72 on 1BW), while GPT-2 retains an edge on LAMBADA (35.66 vs 42.44). Against σ-GPT (decoder-only any-order baseline), AO-GPT's architectural improvements (adaLN, EMA, 10% L2R mixing) yield substantial gains, reducing WikiText2 Any-Order perplexity from 43.28 to 33.73. On generation, AO-GPT achieves a ~25× speedup over SEDD while matching its generation perplexity under appropriate temperature annealing (4.6 vs 5.1 at 1024 steps, Top-p 0.95, Temp 0.7), though without annealing AO-GPT's generation perplexity is notably worse (136.4 vs 81.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on σ-GPT (Pannatier et al., 2024) as its architectural foundation for decoder-only any-order autoregression, and on SEDD (Lou et al., 2023) for the masked diffusion objective and training protocol. Its central conceptual move is decoupling the generative formulation (AR vs. MDM/AO-AR) from the attention architecture (causal decoder vs. full-attention encoder), a confound that prior comparisons (e.g., LLaDA, Dream vs. GPT) have not addressed. The theoretical equivalence between MDM and AO-AR objectives (via RADD and NADE) is leveraged to frame the comparison as one over the distribution of token prediction orders, while the order-invariant vs. order-dependent conditional probability space analysis (n·2^{n-1} vs. e·n!) is a new combinatorial characterization not present in prior any-order or diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25× generation speedup for decoder-only MDMs (via KV-cache plus the two-stage sampling lemma) is a concrete, practically significant efficiency gain that could make masked diffusion a viable alternative to autoregressive inference at scale. However, the study is limited to ~350M parameters on a single training corpus, with no downstream task evaluation, leaving open whether the perplexity and speed trade-offs persist at the 7B+ scale where LLaDA and Dream operate. The finding that a mere 10% left-to-right data mixture improves any-order generalization suggests non-uniform order distributions may be a productive direction, but the paper does not explore this beyond a single ablation. Broader applicability to non-linguistic discrete modalities (where left-to-right structure is weaker) remains untested.
