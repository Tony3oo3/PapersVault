## Problem

Network pruning is widely expected to improve efficiency while preserving performance, yet pruned language models retain strong accuracy on non-generative tasks (retrieval, multiple-choice) while frequently collapsing on generative tasks. The root cause of this task-dependent discrepancy has remained unexplained, with prior work attributing pruning effects primarily to embedding-space perturbations without accounting for how those perturbations transform through the full inference pipeline.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a pruned transformer model (with specific attention/MLP layers dropped or intra-layer weights sparsified via Wanda/SparseGPT) and a text prompt, then measures representation deviations at each layer by swapping only the target layer with its pruned counterpart while keeping all other layers at their dense values. Outputs include per-layer cosine similarity and KL divergence in three sequential spaces (embedding, logit, probability), task-level accuracy on generative benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP) and non-generative benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag, BEIR retrieval), and step-wise perturbation trajectories across autoregressive decoding steps.

## Method

The paper decomposes LLM inference into three sequential representation spaces—embedding (hidden states), logit (pre-softmax), and probability (post-softmax)—and derives second-order Taylor approximations characterizing how pruning-induced perturbations evolve across them. Theorem 1 shows embedding and logit deviations are governed by the orthogonal component of the perturbation relative to the signal norm, explaining their stability. Theorem 2 reveals that the softmax nonlinearity amplifies deviations into a weighted variance of logit perturbations (weighted by p_i²/‖p‖²), which is substantially larger than the orthogonal magnitude ratio, especially in deeper layers. Theorem 3 connects this to KL divergence, showing distributional shift scales as Var_p(Δz)/(2T²). A multi-scale analysis further shows that during autoregressive decoding, errors compound through the key-value cache and diverging generated-token histories, while non-generative tasks remain robust because they depend only on a small categorical-token probability subspace where shifts are mild.

## Results

Under inter-layer pruning (dropping 8 attention or 8 MLP layers) on Mistral-7B-Instruct, non-generative multiple-choice performance is largely preserved (average 69.3 → 69.8 for Drop-8A, 64.3 for Drop-8M), while generative performance collapses dramatically (average 22.3 → 13.2 for Drop-8A, 0.8 for Drop-8M; GSM8K drops from 48.4 to 0.0 under Drop-8M). In the representation hierarchy, embedding-space cosine similarity remains high (deviation < 0.05 for most layers) and logit-space similarity is comparable, yet probability-space angular deviation and KL divergence are substantially larger, confirming that the softmax nonlinearity amplifies pruning-induced perturbations. The theoretical second-order approximations (Theorems 1–3) closely match ground-truth measurements across layers and decoding steps, with the weighted variance of Δz under r_i = p_i²/‖p‖² identified as the dominant factor governing probability-space deviation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an analysis study rather than a method proposal, so it uses four pruning methods as tools to demonstrate its representation-hierarchy framework. Wanda (Sun et al., 2024) and SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023) serve as intra-layer baselines: both show mild degradation on HellaSwag (non-generative) but sharp collapse on GSM8K (generative) as sparsity increases from unstructured 50% to 2:4 structured. Layer Drop / Attn-MLP Drop (He et al., 2026) and ShortGPT (Men et al., 2025) serve as inter-layer baselines: dropping 8 attention layers (Drop-8A) preserves multiple-choice accuracy nearly exactly (69.3 → 69.8) but reduces GSM8K from 48.4 to 36.2, while dropping 8 MLP layers (Drop-8M) is more destructive (GSM8K → 0.0). The key finding is that no pruning method, regardless of granularity, can avoid the generative-task collapse caused by softmax amplification; the discrepancy is intrinsic to the representation hierarchy, not to a specific pruning algorithm. E5-Mistral retrieval (a non-generative embedding task) confirms robustness: average BEIR score drops only from 58.9 to 56.8 under Drop-8M, consistent with the embedding-space stability predicted by the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the layer-redundancy line of work initiated by Gromov et al. (2025) and He et al. (2026), which established that individual transformer layers can be removed with limited impact on single-step representations. It departs from prior pruning studies (Wanda, SparseGPT, ShortGPT, Layer Drop) that have predominantly reported success on non-generative benchmarks by introducing a representation-hierarchy decomposition (embedding → logit → probability) and a Taylor-based theoretical analysis showing that the softmax nonlinearity is the critical amplification point. Unlike prior work that treats pruning as a uniform compression operation, this paper explicitly separates the effects across three sequential spaces to explain task-dependent robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, representation-level explanation for the well-observed but previously unexplained discrepancy between pruning success on classification/retrieval and failure on generation, giving practitioners a clear decision framework for when pruning is safe. The theoretical results (Theorems 1–3) are clean and empirically validated across multiple models and pruning strategies, and the analysis naturally extends to quantization. Key limitations include the focus on training-free pruning (post-training recovery is left open) and the reliance on a localized Taylor approximation that breaks down at the first and last layers. An open question is whether the softmax amplification mechanism can be mitigated architecturally (e.g., temperature scheduling or probability-space regularization) to make pruning viable for generative tasks.
