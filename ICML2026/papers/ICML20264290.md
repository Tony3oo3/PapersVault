## Problem

Standard RAG retrievers and rerankers optimize exclusively for query-document relevance, creating two systematic failure modes: they surface trivially answer-revealing passages that encourage shortcut learning, and they cannot distinguish genuinely unsolvable evidence from challenging yet sufficient evidence. This relevance-only objective creates a severe train-test mismatch, as generators trained on curated evidence face noisy, incomplete retrieval at deployment, leading to a persistent gap between retrieval recall and end-to-end QA accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and a fixed pool of top-25 candidate documents retrieved by a dense retriever (E5-base-v2) over a Wikipedia corpus. During training, the selector outputs a subset of exactly k=5 documents from this pool, and the generator produces a structured answer containing a reasoning chain with document citations in a  block followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. At inference, the selector is discarded entirely and the fine-tuned generator operates directly on top-k documents from a standard retriever, incurring no additional inference cost.

## Method

BAR-RAG reframes the reranker as a boundary-aware evidence selector trained via a two-stage reinforcement learning pipeline using GRPO. In Stage 1, the selector is trained with a composite reward that includes a triangular boundary reward peaking at a target correctness rate c=0.5 (the 'Goldilocks Zone'), a relevance reward, a format gate, and a count penalty, guiding it toward evidence sets that are challenging yet solvable for the frozen generator. In Stage 2, the selector is frozen and the generator is fine-tuned under the induced evidence distribution using accuracy (F1 + EM), format, and citation rewards. The two stages alternate for T iterations so that evidence selection progressively tracks the generator's evolving competence boundary. A lightweight training-time filtering step removes queries that are trivially solvable or fundamentally unanswerable to ensure informative RL signal variance. All training uses LoRA (rank 32) with the backbone frozen, and the selector is discarded at inference time.

## Results

BAR-RAG achieves an average Exact Match (EM) of 39.1 on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and 40.7 on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct across seven QA benchmarks, yielding an average gain of 10.3% over the strongest RAG and reranking baselines. The largest improvements appear on multi-hop tasks: +12.5 EM on Bamboogle and +7.2 on 2WikiMultiHopQA (Qwen-2.5-7B), confirming that boundary-aware evidence selection is most beneficial when multi-step reasoning is required. Performance improves consistently across training iterations (Iter 1 → Iter 2 → Iter 3), with diminishing returns after the second round, indicating convergence within a few iterations of selector-generator co-adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAR-RAG outperforms all six compared baselines across all three backbone models and all seven QA benchmarks. Against the strongest RAG-based baseline (RAG SFT), BAR-RAG (3 Iter) gains +7.3 EM on average for Qwen-2.5-7B (39.1 vs. 31.8) and +6.2 for LLaMA-3.1-8B (40.7 vs. 34.5), with the largest single-dataset margin being +14.6 on PopQA (46.9 vs. 32.3, Qwen-2.5-7B). Standard RAG (REPLUG) and RAG w/ Reranker are consistently weaker, with BAR-RAG exceeding them by 11–12 EM on average. IRCoT, which interleaves retrieval with chain-of-thought, underperforms even plain RAG on most datasets (e.g., 22.6 avg vs. 26.9 for RAG on Qwen-2.5-7B), suggesting that iterative retrieval without competence-aware evidence selection is counterproductive. Among agentic RAG methods, BAR-RAG surpasses Search-o1 by a wide margin (39.1 vs. 20.6 avg on Qwen-2.5-7B) and edges out Search-R1 (39.1 vs. 38.5), though Search-R1 uses substantially different multi-round retrieval settings on multi-hop datasets. Against DynamicRAG (RL-based reranking), BAR-RAG achieves +4.3 EM on NQ and +7.0 on HotpotQA for LLaMA-3.1-8B, while incurring no additional inference-time cost since the selector is discarded at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its optimization backbone but departs sharply from prior RL-based reranking work like DynamicRAG (Sun et al., 2025), which uses generator feedback to optimize relevance or answer quality. The key conceptual departure is reframing the reranker's objective from maximizing relevance to targeting the generator's competence boundary — selecting evidence that is challenging yet solvable (the 'Goldilocks Zone') rather than trivially answer-revealing or fundamentally unsolvable. This positions BAR-RAG as orthogonal to Self-RAG (which controls retrieval timing) and to agentic search methods like Search-R1 and Search-o1 (which add multi-round retrieval at inference), since BAR-RAG operates entirely at training time and incurs no inference overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized practical failure mode of RAG systems — brittleness under noisy retrieval even when relevant evidence is present — and offers a clean, implementable solution that requires no architectural changes at inference time. The iterative selector-generator co-training recipe is a practical pattern that could be adapted to other retrieval-grounded settings beyond QA. Open questions include whether the fixed target correctness c=0.5 generalizes across generator scales and task difficulties, whether the approach extends to open-ended generation or summarization tasks, and how it interacts with increasingly capable base models whose competence boundaries shift rapidly.
