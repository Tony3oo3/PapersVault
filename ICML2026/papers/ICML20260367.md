## Problem

Scaling video generation from seconds to minutes is fundamentally harder than scaling image resolution: a one-minute video is a temporal extrapolation requiring new events and causal structure, not an interpolation of a short clip. The common practice of training on mixed-length video 'soups' forces the model to relearn high-fidelity short-video dynamics from a data-constrained long-video regime, causing outputs to lose local sharpness and motion. This creates a fidelity-horizon gap where long-range coherence and local realism cannot be simultaneously optimized under a single mean-seeking objective.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as conditioning and generates 30- to 60-second videos in the latent space of a video VAE (Wan 1.3B or 14B). At inference, only the Distribution Matching head is used with 4 function evaluations (NFE), producing end-to-end bidirectional long videos without autoregressive rollouts. During training, the model consumes real long videos (10 s to 61 s, ~100k clips from Sekai, MiraData, and filtered internet sources) for the FM head and on-policy student rollouts cropped into ~5-second sliding windows for the DM head, with a frozen short-video teacher queried on each window.

## Method

The core architecture is a Decoupled Diffusion Transformer with a shared long-context encoder and two lightweight velocity heads that resolve the mathematical conflict between mean-seeking flow matching and mode-seeking distribution matching. The Flow Matching head is trained with supervised rectified-flow loss on real long videos to learn minute-scale narrative structure, while the Distribution Matching head is trained via a DMD/VSD-style reverse-KL gradient surrogate that aligns every sliding-window segment of the student's own rollouts to a frozen short-video teacher, inheriting local realism without additional short-video data. At inference, the FM head is discarded and the DM head alone serves as a fast few-step sampler, yielding videos with both sharp local dynamics and consistent long-range context. A practical detail handles the image/video latent mismatch at sliding-window boundaries by re-encoding the preceding frame into an image latent before computing the DMD loss.

## Results

On the VBench-Long 30-second evaluation with 4 NFE, the proposed method achieves the best Dynamic Degree (0.9453), Image Quality (0.6982), and Motion Smoothness (0.9863) among all few-step methods, while also attaining the highest VLM Consistency (75.42) — surpassing even the 50-NFE Mixed-length SFT baseline (74.63). In the 60-second stress test (Table 3), it further leads on Dynamic Degree (0.9297), Image Quality (0.7015), and VLM Consistency (73.08). The user study (Table 4) confirms these gains, with the method receiving the highest Overall Quality (3.68/5 at 30 s; 3.59/5 at 60 s) and Global Consistency ratings while substantially reducing static artifacts (1.86 vs. 4.12 for InfinityRoPE at 30 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two SFT-only baselines (Long-context SFT and Mixed-length SFT, both at 50 NFE), the proposed method matches or exceeds their Dynamic Degree (0.9453 vs. 0.9375/0.8906) and Image Quality (0.6982 vs. 0.6303/0.6683) while using only 4 NFE, and achieves comparable VLM Consistency (75.42 vs. 77.28/74.63). Among the teacher-only / rollout-based methods, the proposed method decisively outperforms CausVid (VLM Consistency 75.42 vs. 39.30; Dynamic Degree 0.9453 vs. 0.8594) and Self-Forcing (75.42 vs. 37.60; 0.9453 vs. 0.9063), which suffer from autoregressive error accumulation and over-saturation over long horizons. Compared to InfinityRoPE, the proposed method achieves substantially higher Dynamic Degree (0.9453 vs. 0.7188) and VLM Consistency (75.42 vs. 68.61), indicating that InfinityRoPE's sink-based context extension produces overly static content. Against LongLive, the proposed method leads on Dynamic Degree (0.9453 vs. 0.6422) and VLM Consistency (75.42 vs. 62.92), while LongLive's frame-level AR design yields slightly higher Subject Consistency (0.9681 vs. 0.9682, essentially tied). Overall, the decoupled mean-seeking + mode-seeking design wins on the metrics that capture both local fidelity (Image Quality, Dynamic Degree) and long-range coherence (VLM Consistency) simultaneously, closing the gap that SFT-only or teacher-only approaches leave open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Decoupled Diffusion Transformer (DDT) architecture, which separates a shared encoder from multiple lightweight decoder heads, and adapts the DMD/VSD reverse-KL gradient formulation for distribution matching to the video domain. Its key conceptual departure is from prior long-video generation approaches that either rely solely on supervised fine-tuning over mixed-length clips (mean-seeking) or distill from a frozen short-video teacher via autoregressive rollouts (mode-seeking). The paper argues these two objectives are mathematically incompatible when applied to a single velocity predictor—mean-seeking flow matching pulls toward conditional expectations while mode-seeking reverse-KL pulls toward sharp teacher modes—and resolves the conflict by routing them to disjoint heads. The sliding-window DMD implementation also borrows the image/video latent boundary handling from LongLive to address a practical mismatch in modern video VAEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled mean-seeking/mode-seeking training paradigm offers a principled recipe for scaling video generation from seconds to minutes under the fundamental constraint that high-quality long-form data is orders of magnitude scarcer than short clips. By inheriting local realism from a frozen expert teacher without requiring additional short-video data, the approach reduces the data burden for long-video training while the 4-NFE inference capability makes minute-scale generation practically deployable. However, the paper acknowledges that the method does not solve compositional temporal planning (e.g., action-state transitions, revisitation of landmarks), and the interaction with causal autoregressive frameworks remains an open direction. The broader question of whether decoupled objectives generalize beyond the flow-matching/distribution-matching pairing to other divergences or modalities is left unexplored.
