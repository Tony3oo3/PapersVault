## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) cannot natively process structured, non-visual data such as human skeleton sequences, and existing bridging methods either compress motion into a single lossy feature vector for text alignment or quantize it into discrete tokens that generalize poorly across heterogeneous skeleton topologies (e.g., 25-joint Kinect vs. 22-joint SMPL vs. 17-joint COCO). This creates a 'format silo' where a model trained on one skeleton format cannot be applied to another without costly retraining, and both paradigms fail to leverage the MLLM's pre-existing visual understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a skeleton sequence of arbitrary topology (17–25 joints, 2D or 3D coordinates, with or per-joint orientation quaternions) and a text prompt as input. DrAction renders 12 uniformly sampled frames at 448×448 resolution, which are processed by the MLLM's ViT vision encoder into visual tokens. Outputs are generated autoregressively: action labels via multiple-choice question answering (pattern-matched from generated text), free-form motion captions, natural-language question answers, and step-by-step causal reasoning chains describing body-part dynamics.

## Method

DrAction models each pose as K = J + |E|×10 deformable 3D Gaussian primitives (joint-anchored plus bone-interpolated), deformed per-frame via Linear Blend Skinning with SO(3)-projected rotations, and modulated by a Neural Feature Modulator (GRU-based) that adjusts color and opacity from local position/velocity cues to highlight motion-salient regions. The entire rendering pipeline is differentiable, so MLLM task gradients propagate back to shape the visual representation end-to-end. A four-stage cooperative training curriculum—(1) frozen-MLLM render warm-up, (2) discriminative finetuning on confusable action pairs, (3) causal reasoning distillation from a GPT-4o teacher, (4) recognition refinement—progressively activates renderer, projector, and LoRA-adapted LLM parameters to avoid the chicken-and-egg optimization problem. The backbone is InternVL3-8B with LoRA (rank 32), and action recognition is cast as MQA with no separate classification head.

## Results

On NTU-60, SkeletonLLM achieves 87.37% top-1 accuracy on the standard 55/5 split, surpassing the best traditional alignment method TDSM (86.49%) by 0.88%. The advantage grows dramatically under data scarcity: on the extreme 30/30 split, SkeletonLLM reaches 37.84% versus TDSM's 25.88% (+11.96%). On NTU-120, the 60/60 split shows a 7.73% gap (34.94% vs. 27.21%). For cross-format transfer (NTU-60→NW-UCLA), SkeletonLLM achieves 60.38% without any finetuning, outperforming TDSM (43.19%) and MotionGPT (10.35%) by 17.19% and 50.03% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkeletonLLM outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest traditional alignment method TDSM (ICCV'25), the gap widens from +0.88% on the easy 55/5 split to +11.96% on the extreme 30/30 split of NTU-60, and from +1.90% to +7.73% on NTU-120 (110/10 to 60/60), indicating superior generalization under data scarcity. Tokenization-based LLM methods (MotionGPT, MotionLLM) are substantially outperformed—by 57.49% on NTU-60 55/5 and 27.49% on 30/30—confirming that discrete motion tokens create a severe information bottleneck. Compared to the same MLLM backbone (InternVL3-8B) with fixed rendering, SkeletonLLM gains +7.71% on 55/5 and +9.69% on 30/30, isolating the contribution of differentiable, task-optimized rendering. On cross-format transfer (NTU-60→NW-UCLA), SkeletonLLM achieves 60.38% versus TDSM's 43.19% and MotionGPT's 10.35%, and on cross-format captioning (HumanML3D) it reaches 37.28 BertScore versus MotionLLM's 23.44 and InternVL3-8B's 29.45. Even when paired with frontier closed-source models, GPT-5.4+DrAction (53.18% on 48/12) still trails jointly-trained SkeletonLLM (64.72%) by 11.54%, demonstrating that end-to-end co-optimization is irreplaceable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms for skeleton understanding: feature-text alignment (PURLS, TDSM) that compresses spatiotemporal dynamics into a single vector, and tokenization-based LLM methods (MotionGPT, MotionLLM) that quantize motion into discrete codebook tokens. Instead, it repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting and Linear Blend Skinning—techniques from real-time graphics rendering—as a differentiable, format-agnostic front-end that translates arbitrary skeleton sequences into image sequences consumable by a pre-trained MLLM (InternVL3-8B). The key conceptual departure is treating the MLLM's native visual modality as the universal interface, allowing task-specific gradients to flow back through the renderer rather than forcing the model to learn an abstract 'pose language' from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The format-agnostic rendering approach directly addresses the long-standing 'format silo' problem in skeleton-based action recognition, enabling a single model to process Kinect, MoCap, and 2D pose data without architectural changes or retraining. The cooperative training strategy (causal reasoning distillation + discriminative finetuning) provides a practical recipe for instilling structured reasoning into MLLMs for non-visual inputs. Open questions include whether the rendering-to-image paradigm generalizes to other structured non-visual modalities (e.g., point clouds, sensor data), whether the approach scales to longer sequences or multi-person interactions beyond the 12-frame, single/two-person setting tested here, and whether the ~192ms end-to-end latency is sufficient for latency-critical deployment scenarios.
