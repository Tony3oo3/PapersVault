## Problem

Existing relational deep learning (RDL) methods for entity classification on relational databases ignore the class imbalance problem, causing minority entity representations to collapse into majority-dominated message passing and rendering models useless for critical rare-event detection (e.g., fraud, churn). Prior class-imbalance remedies (SMOTE, GraphSMOTE, GraphSHA) are designed for homogeneous graphs and fail to preserve the complex relational consistency inherent in heterogeneous entity graphs derived from RDBs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a relational database structured as a heterogeneous entity graph, where nodes are table rows with multi-modal features (numeric, categorical, timestamp, text, images) and edges are foreign-primary key linkages. It outputs binary entity class labels and synthetic minority samples, each carrying both a learned representation vector and a relational signature (entity-type histograms of 1-hop/2-hop neighbors plus fan-in/fan-out distributions per relation type). A FIFO memory bank of size U (default 1024) stores historical minority representations and signatures for the synthesis step.

## Method

Rel-MOSS comprises two relation-centric modules. Rel-Gate applies a cross-attention mechanism per relation type to estimate the likelihood that neighborhood messages lean toward the minority class, then element-wise modulates each relation's message to amplify minority-discriminative signals and suppress majority-dominated noise, preventing the exponential decay of minority information across layers (formalized in Proposition 4.1). Rel-Syn extends SMOTE-style interpolation by conditioning on relational signatures: it selects the nearest minority neighbor using a combined L2 distance over both representations and signatures (weighted by ω), then interpolates both the representation and the signature to produce structurally faithful synthetic samples. Training jointly optimizes a binary cross-entropy classification loss over all samples and an MSE reconstruction loss that forces the model to recover relational signatures from gated representations, with a weighted hyper-parameter γ balancing the two objectives.

## Results

On 12 RDB entity classification datasets from RelBench, Rel-MOSS achieves an average improvement of 2.46% in Balanced Accuracy (B-Acc) and 4.00% in G-Mean over the best baseline. On the severely imbalanced f1-driver-top3 dataset (imbalance ratio 4.86), Rel-MOSS reaches 0.8098 B-Acc and 0.8014 G-Mean, compared to 0.7521/0.7426 for the best baseline (GraphSMOTE), a 7.67%/7.92% relative gain. On avito-user-clicks (imbalance ratio 24.83), Rel-MOSS improves G-Mean by 10.03% over GraphSMOTE. On mildly imbalanced datasets (e.g., event-user-repeat, ratio 1.04), Rel-MOSS still leads by 4.26% B-Acc, confirming robustness across imbalance severities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rel-MOSS outperforms all 8 baselines across the 12 evaluated datasets. Against the native RDL pipelines (RDL, RDL-HGT, RelGNN), the gains are most dramatic on severely imbalanced datasets: on f1-driver-top3, native RDL collapses to B-Acc 0.5000 / G-Mean 0.0000, while Rel-MOSS reaches 0.8098 / 0.8014. Among the classic imbalance-handling baselines, SMOTE and GraphSMOTE are the strongest competitors (e.g., GraphSMOTE achieves 0.7521 B-Acc on f1-driver-top3), yet Rel-MOSS still leads by 5.8–10% relative. GraphSHA and ReVar are highly unstable on heterogeneous RDB graphs, frequently collapsing to B-Acc ≈ 0.50 on avito-user-clicks and avito-user-visits, whereas Rel-MOSS maintains consistent performance. On mildly imbalanced datasets (event-user-repeat, ratio 1.04; amazon-user-churn, ratio 1.66), Rel-MOSS's advantage narrows to 0.16–4.26%, and on amazon-user-churn the B-Acc improvement is slightly negative (−1.42%), indicating that the relational signature constraint can marginally over-constrain when imbalance is negligible. Overall, Rel-MOSS delivers an average 3.22% B-Acc and 6.49% G-Mean improvement over the best classic imbalance method (SMOTE/GraphSMOTE) across all 12 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: relational deep learning (RDL), which recasts relational databases as heterogeneous entity graphs for GNN-based prediction (Fey et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2020), and class-imbalance handling in graph data mining (GraphSMOTE, GraphSHA, ReVar). It departs from prior imbalance methods by recognizing that heterogeneous RDB entity graphs introduce two unique challenges—relation-type-specific information dominance and structurally-determined entity labels—that homogeneous-graph methods cannot address. The proposed Rel-Gate (cross-attention-based relation-wise gating) and Rel-Syn (relational-signature-conditioned SMOTE-like synthesis) are novel architectural components, but each draws on well-known primitives (attention, interpolation, memory banks) repurposed for the relational setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of class imbalance in the RDL paradigm, filling a gap that is practically critical since real-world RDB applications (fraud detection, churn prediction, clinical trial outcomes) are inherently imbalanced. The paper's limitation to binary entity classification on 12 RelBench datasets, and its reliance on a relatively simple interpolation-based synthesizer, leave open whether the approach extends to multi-class settings, entity regression, or more expressive generative models (diffusion, flow-based). The authors also note that foundation models pre-trained on large-scale relational data may be inherently robust to imbalance, an unexplored direction that could reshape the problem.
