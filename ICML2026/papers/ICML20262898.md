## Problem

Existing non-stationary time series forecasting methods suffer from a failure mode the authors term Phase Amnesia: normalization-based approaches assume future statistics can be restored from historical moments, while structure-based approaches assume periodicity persists rigidly into the future. Both paradigms lose the 'coordinates' of the evolving global context, making them unable to distinguish genuine structural shifts from temporary stochastic noise. The paper asks whether a physics-informed inductive bias grounded in dynamical systems principles can outperform raw architectural complexity for non-stationary forecasting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate window X ∈ R^{T×C} (T=96 fixed) plus task-specific timestamp features (e.g., hour-of-day, day-of-week) and produces a future forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{H×C} over horizons H ∈ {96,192,336,720} for long-term or {12,24,48,96} for short-term tasks. Unusually, the raw input is first decomposed into a deterministic Phase Anchor and a stochastic residual; only the residual undergoes RevIN normalization, while the anchor is preserved as a structural prior. The final output is reconstructed by denormalizing the predicted residual and re-adding a *generated* future anchor, so the affine rescaling acts exclusively on fluctuations rather than the full signal.

## Method

PULSE follows a Disentangle-Evolve-Simulate design. (1) Phase-Anchored Disentanglement builds a dynamic reference frame from a learnable codebook indexed by global phase (t_end mod W) plus a TimeEncoder, then applies RevIN only to the residual R_x = X − A_x, provably restoring gradient sensitivity from O(σ_A^{-1}) to O(σ_R^{-1}) and eliminating 'Variance Masking.' (2) A Dual-Stream Evolution feeds the anchor-preserved input through a lightweight MLP backbone to obtain a latent future Y_0, while a Generative Phase Router (two-stage cross-attention over fixed-size phase tokens of length P ≤ 24) actively synthesizes the future anchor A_y from A_x and Y_0, breaking the static-periodicity assumption. (3) Statistic-Aware Mixup (SAM) regularizes training by interpolating inputs while explicitly linearly interpolating the residual decoding statistics (μ_R, σ_R) rather than recomputing them from the mixed waveform, provably preventing anti-phase 'Scale Collapse.' Training uses a Frequency-Domain MAE (FFT-based ℓ₁) loss, and the entire framework is plug-and-play: the Phase Router's O(P²) attention is independent of sequence length, yielding asymptotically O(T) total complexity.

## Results

PULSE with a simple MLP backbone achieves 1st place on 10 out of 12 datasets in MSE and 8 out of 12 in MAE (18 first-place entries out of 24 total). Headline MSE values include 0.412 on ETTh1, 0.361 on ETTh2, 0.160 on Electricity, 0.194 on Solar, and 0.103 on PEMS03. The only losses are on Traffic (iTransformer: 0.428 vs. PULSE 0.460 MSE) and PEMS07 (TimeXer: 0.085 vs. PULSE 0.094 MSE), both dense multivariate benchmarks where cross-channel attention provides an advantage. PULSE also substantially outperforms the most recent baseline CFPT across all 12 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PULSE (MLP backbone) outperforms all 9 baselines on 10 of 12 datasets in MSE, with the two exceptions being Traffic (where iTransformer's inverted attention over 862 channels wins 0.428 vs. 0.460) and PEMS07 (where TimeXer's exogenous-variable modeling wins 0.085 vs. 0.094). Against the strongest recent baseline CFPT, PULSE leads on every dataset, with margins ranging from 0.003 (ETTh2: 0.361 vs. 0.364) to 0.040 (PEMS03: 0.103 vs. 0.143). The gap widens substantially against structure-based methods: on Solar, PULSE (0.194) beats TimesNet (0.301) by 35% and MSGNet (0.263) by 26%; on PEMS03, it beats DLinear (0.278) by 63%. In the plug-and-play evaluation (Table 2), PULSE consistently improves host architectures—DLinear gains 33.3% on ETTh2 and 34.5% on Solar, iTransformer gains 9.9% on ETTh1 and 17.2% on Solar—confirming that the phase-disentanglement mechanism is architecture-agnostic. The one notable exception is PatchTST on Traffic (−17.5%), attributed to an inductive-bias mismatch between PatchTST's local-patch assumption and the more stochastic residual left after anchor absorption.

## Relation to Prior Work

PULSE builds on the normalization-based paradigm (RevIN, Non-stationary Transformer, Dish-TS) and the structure-based paradigm (TimesNet, CycleNet, MSGNet) but departs from both by formalizing non-stationarity through three physical hypotheses (Wold decomposition, dynamical phase evolution, heteroscedastic residual manifold) rather than relying on static statistical restoration or rigid periodicity. It repurposes RevIN by applying it exclusively to the stochastic residual rather than the full signal, and extends Mixup with a statistic-aware variant (SAM) that prevents the scale-collapse failure mode identified in Theorem 3.2. The Generative Phase Router introduces a novel mechanism for actively synthesizing future deterministic trajectories via cross-attention over fixed-resolution phase tokens, moving beyond the 'direct historical copying' assumption shared by most decomposition-based forecasters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight MLP with correct physics-informed inductive bias can match or exceed complex Transformer-based forecasters across 12 benchmarks, suggesting that appropriate structural priors matter more than raw architectural capacity for non-stationary forecasting. Its plug-and-play design (validated on iTransformer, PatchTST, TimesNet, DLinear) makes it immediately applicable as an accuracy booster for existing pipelines. A key limitation is performance degradation on densely correlated multivariate benchmarks (Traffic, PEMS07) where cross-channel interactions dominate, pointing to an open question about integrating graph-aware or channel-aware mechanisms within the phase-evolution framework. The theoretical results (Proposition 3.1 on gradient sensitivity, Theorem 3.2 on scale collapse) provide actionable guidance for practitioners designing normalization and augmentation strategies under distribution shift.
