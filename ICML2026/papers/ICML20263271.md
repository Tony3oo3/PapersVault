## Problem

Deepening PINNs to capture high-frequency PDE solutions is blocked by a derivative instability distinct from standard vanishing/exploding gradients: the product of layer-wise Jacobians causes first-order derivatives to vanish (MLPs) or explode (ResNets) as depth grows, corrupting the higher-order gradients needed to train the PDE residual loss. Existing frequency-aware tricks (Fourier features, sinusoidal activations) do not resolve this architectural pathology, and ResNet skip connections only delay it.

## Input / Output

The network takes spatial coordinates (1D or 2D) and optionally temporal coordinates as input, optionally elevated through a Fourier feature embedding into a higher-dimensional space. It outputs the scalar (or vector) PDE solution value at those coordinates. Training is entirely self-supervised: the loss is the sum of a PDE residual term (computed by automatic differentiation of the network output against the governing equation at interior collocation points) and a boundary-condition term, with no labeled solution data required.

## Method

CoupledNet replaces standard MLP/ResNet hidden layers with stacked CoupledBlocks adapted from normalizing flows (NICE/RealNVP). Each block applies a fixed random permutation, splits the vector into two halves, and computes y₁=x₁, y₂=s⊙x₂+t where s=exp(LayerNorm(σ(W₂σ(W₁x₁+b₁)+b₂))) and t=W₄σ(W₃x₁+b₃)+b₄. The triangular Jacobian structure combined with zero-bias LayerNorm guarantees det(J_l)=1 per block, which provably enforces a spectral-norm lower bound of 1 on the composite Jacobian, eliminating derivative vanishing at any depth. A two-pronged upper-bound controller (fixed LayerNorm standard deviation derived from log-normal tail analysis, plus a dynamic output-layer rescaling by max(s_i)·L) prevents derivative explosion. The paper proves both spectral control and universal approximation for the architecture, and demonstrates that CoupledNet's relative L₂ error decreases monotonically with depth up to 50 layers on high-frequency ODEs, outperforming PirateNet and Jaxpi on high-frequency, high-dynamic-range, and 40-dimensional HJB benchmarks.

## Results

On the high-frequency 2D PDE (Eq. 6), CoupledNet (8-layer) achieves a relative L₂ error of 0.0117, substantially outperforming PirateNet (0.0334) and Jaxpi (0.0324) by roughly 2.8×. On the 40-dimensional HJB equation, CoupledNet reaches 0.137 relative L₂ error versus 0.400 for PirateNet and 0.240 for MLP. On the Jaxpi conventional PDE benchmark, CoupledNet achieves the best result on Advection (3.64e-4) but trails PirateNet on Allen-Cahn, Burgers, Grey-Scott, and KdV, confirming its strength lies in challenging high-frequency and high-dimensional settings rather than simple PDEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PirateNet (Wang et al., 2024a), CoupledNet achieves a 2.8× lower relative L₂ error on the high-frequency PDE (0.0117 vs. 0.0334) and a 2.9× lower error on the 40D HJB equation (0.137 vs. 0.400), while also converging faster in training. On the Jaxpi conventional benchmark, however, PirateNet outperforms CoupledNet on four of five PDEs (e.g., Allen-Cahn: 2.24e-5 vs. 9.09e-5; Burgers: 4.03e-5 vs. 1.22e-4), with CoupledNet winning only on Advection (3.64e-4 vs. 4.88e-4). Against Jaxpi (Wang et al., 2023), CoupledNet similarly dominates on the high-frequency PDE (0.0117 vs. 0.0324) and Advection (3.64e-4 vs. 6.88e-4) but underperforms on Allen-Cahn (9.09e-5 vs. 5.37e-5) and KdV (1.30e-3 vs. 1.96e-3). The paper explicitly positions CoupledNet as an architecture whose inductive bias yields the largest gains on difficult, high-frequency, or high-dimensional PDEs, while remaining competitive (not always best) on simpler benchmarks where PirateNet's NTK reweighting and adaptive residual connections provide an edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the coupling-layer architecture from normalizing flows (NICE, RealNVP, Glow) to solve a fundamentally different problem: stabilizing automatic differentiation in deep Physics-Informed Neural Networks. While prior deep PINN work (PirateNet, Jaxpi) relies on ResNet skip connections or Fourier feature embeddings to handle depth and frequency, this paper identifies the root cause as uncontrolled Jacobian spectral norms and addresses it through a determinant constraint (det=1 per block) inherited from invertible flow design, combined with LayerNorm-based spectral containment. The theoretical analysis builds on Wang et al. (2024a)'s probabilistic bounds on MLP derivative vanishing and Yang et al. (2024)'s spectral condition for feature learning, but departs from the ResNet-based deepening strategy that those works implicitly endorse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled architectural answer to why deep PINNs fail and how to fix it, moving the field beyond ad-hoc training tricks (NTK reweighting, causal training, Fourier tuning) toward a structural solution that scales with depth. However, the output-layer constraint restricts applicability to value-range-limited PDEs, the per-step computational overhead is non-trivial, and CoupledNet does not dominate on all conventional benchmarks (e.g., Allen-Cahn, Grey-Scott). Open questions include whether the spectral containment strategy generalizes to unbounded solution spaces, whether the permutation matrix's role can be made more theoretically rigorous, and whether the architecture's efficiency can be improved to make deep PINNs practical for large-scale industrial PDEs.
