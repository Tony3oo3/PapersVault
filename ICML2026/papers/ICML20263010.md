## Problem

Existing training-free image editing pipelines implicitly use the model's scale coordinate (noise level or flow time) as both a granularity knob and an edit-progress clock, coupling how far the edit has progressed to which noise regime is visited. This coupling forces stronger edits into high-noise states where the differential field is spatially underdetermined, causing drift and background artifacts in non-edited regions. The paper asks whether decoupling progress from scale and reallocating a fixed step budget as density within an effective scale window can break the editability-fidelity trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source image, a source prompt, and a target prompt, and produces a single edited image. It operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained flow or diffusion model (SD3, SD3.5, FLUX.1, SD1.4, SDXL-Turbo) with exactly K model evaluations and fresh Gaussian noise at every step. No external masks, training, or inversion are required; the optional feasible-region gate is extracted from the model's own internal representations during the same forward pass, so no additional model calls are incurred.

## Method

NaviEdit is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time controller that introduces an explicit progress axis s ∈ [0,1] independent of the model's scale coordinate u ∈ [0,1], turning editing into a controlled ODE integration where scale is a control input rather than a clock. At each step, it constructs co-located source and target anchors via residual-shift mixing with fresh noise, queries the pretrained flow model once to obtain a differential velocity field, and applies a self-consistent Euler update where mixing, querying, and the update increment all share the same scale u_k (Theorem 4.4). Under a fixed step budget K, the controller allocates compute by increasing sampling density within a fixed effective scale window U_eff (a tail window on the scheduler path) rather than expanding the visited range into high-noise regimes, a principle formalized by Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 showing that coupling forces an irreducible risk floor while density refinement reduces discretization error. An optional feasible-region gate M(u) projects the differential field to suppress leakage outside the target region, and an online speed controller can adapt the next step's density using per-step leakage and oscillation diagnostics already computed during editing.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, Navi-FlowEdit + gate (SD3) achieves the best background preservation among all compared methods: PSNR 27.94, SSIM 93.85, LPIPS 48.74, and Dist 10.67, while also attaining the highest CLIP-Whole (26.18) and CLIP-Edited (22.72) scores. Navi-FlowAlign (M≡1) achieves the best Dist (5.40), PSNR (28.33), and MSE (2.09) across all rows. In the controlled cross-backbone ablation (Table 2, K=28), decoupling improves SSIM by ~5–10 points and PSNR by ~5 points over coupled allocation on SD3, SD3.5, and FLUX.1 [dev] while keeping CLIP scores comparable or slightly higher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Navi-FlowEdit + gate substantially outperforms its direct baseline FlowEdit (SD3) on every PIE-Bench metric: PSNR improves from 22.46 to 27.94 (+5.5), SSIM from 84.08 to 93.85 (+9.8), LPIPS from 103.00 to 48.74 (−54), and Dist from 14.64 to 10.67 (−4.0), while CLIP-Whole/Edited also improve slightly (25.91→26.18, 22.50→22.72). Navi-FlowAlign (M≡1) beats FlowAlign (SD3) on all metrics: Dist 5.40 vs 6.21, PSNR 28.33 vs 27.78, SSIM 93.40 vs 92.41, CLIP-Whole 26.15 vs 25.44, and CLIP-Edited 22.44 vs 21.80, with LPIPS essentially tied (34.49 vs 34.47). Against diffusion-based baselines (DiffEdit, PnP, MasaCtrl, SYE, TiNO-Edit, TurboEdit), the Navi methods dominate on background preservation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) while also matching or exceeding their CLIP semantic scores. The ungated Navi-FlowEdit (M≡1) already improves over FlowEdit on all metrics, isolating the decoupled controller's contribution from the optional gate; the gate further boosts preservation (PSNR 22.54→27.94, LPIPS 92.47→48.74) with negligible CLIP change. On ImgEdit-Bench, both ungated Navi variants (Navi-InfEdit, Navi-FlowAlign) improve Basic and UGE averages over their respective base editors, with the largest gains in background, action, and replace categories where trajectory drift is most impactful.

## Relation to Prior Work

NaviEdit builds directly on the training-free, inversion-free editing paradigm established by SDEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, DiffEdit, and Plug-and-Play, and is instantiated primarily on FlowEdit's rectified-flow ODE framework. It departs from prior schedule-modification work (TiNO-Edit, Schedule Your Edit, Dual-Schedule Inversion) by identifying a specific structural conflation—using the model's noise/scale coordinate simultaneously as a granularity knob and an implicit edit-progress clock—and explicitly separating the two axes. The self-consistency contract (Theorem 4.4) and the rollout-level semantic granularity functional provide a formal lens absent from prior editors, which treat scale allocation as a byproduct of step count rather than a controllable design decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle is portable: it requires no training, no external masks, no inversion, and no modification of the underlying model, and it transfers across flow backbones (SD3, SD3.5, FLUX.1) and compatible editors (FlowEdit, FlowAlign, InfEdit) with consistent gains. This makes it a practical plug-and-play controller for the growing ecosystem of drift-based training-free editors. Open questions include whether the principle extends to less aligned editing interfaces (e.g., instruction-based or multi-turn editing), whether it remains effective for trained editors that learn the allocation tradeoff end-to-end, and how to handle edits requiring strong inter-object geometric consistency that the current controller does not enforce.
