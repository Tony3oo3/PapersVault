## Problem

LLMs exhibit severe length volatility in long-form generation: when given the same instruction multiple times, output lengths vary dramatically, leading to unpredictable token consumption and unreliable deployment. Prior work has focused almost exclusively on single-generation quality and unstructured tasks (e.g., story writing), systematically overlooking cross-run stability and lacking mechanistic explanations for why models fail to maintain consistent output lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt specifying a target structure (e.g., M chapters with N minimum words each, or M Python functions) in English or Chinese, with complexity ranging from simple to fine-grained constraints (character-level patterns, keyword presence, specified themes). It produces long-form text up to 100k words in either unstructured format (stories, dialogues, diaries) or structured format (Python code libraries, JSON profiles, LaTeX formulas), evaluated across 5 independent sampling runs per instruction to quantify both macro-level length volatility and chapter-by-chapter consistency.

## Method

GLoBo (Stable Generation via Logits Boosting) is a training-free, decoding-stage intervention that modifies logits in real time via two composed components. First, Hybrid Structural Enforcement manages section transitions: once a section reaches its target length, the system enters a soft-wait state monitoring for natural interruption tokens (periods, newlines) before boosting logits for the next section title; if no pause occurs within a grace period (δ=100 tokens), a hard trigger forces the transition regardless of context. Second, Proactive Failure Prevention sets logits to −∞ for tokens associated with known failure modes (conversational filler phrases like 'I hope these…' and the EOS token before the final section is complete), directly suppressing premature termination and persona reversion. The approach is grounded in attention-trace analysis that identifies two internal failure signatures—Attention Collapse (attention to constraint tokens dropping to near-zero, causing repetition loops) and Attention Instability (abnormal attention spikes preceding section skipping)—which GLoBo's periodic logit boosting effectively counteracts by acting as a resynchronization signal at each section boundary.

## Results

On the 100-section VOLTBench task (English, simple difficulty), GLoBo applied to Qwen2.5-7B achieves a Length Variation Coefficient (LVC) of 14.02%, a 69% reduction compared to LongWriter-8B (45.4%), and a Mean Length Accuracy (MLA) of 78.25% versus 31.6% for LongWriter-8B. The mean output length reaches 15,651 words, more than double LongWriter-8B's 6,320 words and over 15× that of other baselines (<1,000 words). On generation quality, GLoBo scores 100% Structured Content Accuracy (SCA) and 86.7% Unstructured Content Accuracy (UCA), outperforming LongWriter-8B (32.6% SCA, 66.7% UCA) and all other evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLoBo (on Qwen2.5-7B) decisively outperforms LongWriter-8B, the strongest dedicated long-form model, on every metric: LVC drops from 45.4% to 14.02%, MLA rises from 31.6% to 78.25%, SCA jumps from 32.6% to 100%, and mean output length more than doubles from 6,320 to 15,651 words. Against proprietary models, GLoBo surpasses GPT-4o mini (MLA 78.25% vs 4.8%; mean length 15,651 vs 959 words) and DeepSeek-R1 (MLA 78.25% vs 6.0%) on length adherence, though DeepSeek-R1 retains a slight edge on UCA (93.3% vs 86.7%). Compared to the base Qwen2.5-7B without GLoBo, the method improves MLA from 2.2% to 78.25% and mean length from 445 to 15,651 words while keeping LVC comparable (17.0% → 14.02%). On external benchmarks, GLoBo achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 85.3 on LongBench-Write (vs Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 80.7) and 8.43 on Writing-Bench (vs base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct's 7.4), confirming generalization beyond the structured VOLTBench setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-form generation benchmarking line (HelloBench, LIFEBench, LongGenBench, LongInOutBench) by introducing multi-sampling length volatility as a first-class evaluation dimension, which prior work systematically overlooked in favor of single-generation quality. Conceptually, it builds on Li et al. (2025)'s link between attention to constraint tokens and instruction-following, extending that analysis to long-form generation to identify Attention Collapse and Attention Instability as internal precursors of failure. It departs from length-control methods like Hansel and LongDPO, which modify model weights, by proposing GLoBo—a training-free, decoding-stage logit intervention—while drawing on the attention-sink phenomenon (Xiao et al., 2024) to frame stability as a matter of preserving reliable attention anchors over extended generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even LongWriter-8B, specifically fine-tuned for long generation, exhibits 45.4% length variation coefficient reveals that current long-form capabilities are far less reliable than single-sample evaluations suggest, with direct implications for cost predictability and deployment. GLoBo's training-free nature and cross-architecture generalization (Qwen, Llama, Mamba) make it immediately adoptable, and the attention-trace diagnostic framework offers a reusable tool for identifying generation failures. Open questions include whether the attention patterns are truly causal (the paper explicitly frames them as correlational), whether the method transfers to genuinely open-ended generation without section structure, and whether the representational-drift analysis (using cosine similarity as a CKA proxy) holds under stricter measurement.
