## Problem

Large-scale pretraining has enabled LLMs to adapt rapidly to new tasks with minimal supervision, but no equivalent foundation model exists for the visual domain where compositional understanding, sample efficiency, and general-purpose problem-solving remain unsolved. This paper asks whether video pretraining, with its inherent spatiotemporal inductive biases, can serve as a complementary foundation for structured visual intelligence, and whether VDMs adapted with lightweight LoRA adapters outperform equally adapted LLMs on visually grounded reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the VDM, each grid-based task instance is rendered as an RGB image (3×H×W) and paired with a fixed neutral text embedding; the input-output pair is reframed as a short transition video using discrete interpolation (input frame held for the first half, output frame for the second half), and the model generates the full video conditioned on the first frame, with the final frame serving as the predicted output grid. For the LLM baseline, the same instances are serialized as compact JSON strings in a text-to-text translation format. Both models receive identical training/evaluation splits and use LoRA adapters of comparable rank, isolating the effect of pretraining modality.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified framework that reframes image-to-image visual prediction as conditional video generation: each (input, output) pair becomes a short F-frame video where frame 1 is the input image, frame F is the output image, and intermediate frames use discrete interpolation to avoid introducing spatial biases. A pretrained VDM (e.g., CogVideoX1.5-5B) is fine-tuned with LoRA adapters while keeping the backbone frozen, trained with the standard denoising objective conditioned on the first frame and a fixed text embedding. At inference, the model performs reverse diffusion to generate the transition video, and the final frame is extracted as the prediction. The LLM counterpart (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) is adapted with an equivalent LoRA setup on a JSON-to-JSON sequence-to-sequence objective. This symmetric protocol—matched model scale, identical adapter architecture, and shared data splits—enables a controlled head-to-head comparison of how video pretraining versus text pretraining shapes data efficiency on tasks spanning ARC-AGI, ConceptARC, visual games, route planning, and cellular automata.

## Results

On ARC-AGI (two-attempt protocol), CogVideoX1.5-5B achieves 16.75% accuracy, more than doubling Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (8.00%) and approaching commercial LLMs like GPT-4o (9.00%) and Gemini 1.5 (8.00%) in single-attempt settings. On ConceptARC, CogVideoX1.5-5B reaches 0.33 average accuracy across 16 visual concepts, outperforming Qwen3-4B (0.24), Qwen3-8B (0.24), Llama3.1-8B (0.18), and GPT-4[IC] (0.19), while Wan2.1-14B leads at 0.41. On Shortest Path, the VDM achieves 67% Path Success Rate with only 30 training samples versus 1.5% for the LLM, demonstrating up to a 10× data-efficiency gap in low-sample regimes. In 2D cellular automata (Game of Life, Langton's Ant), the VDM reaches 90%+ accuracy with 30–100 samples while the LLM requires 300+ samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (the primary LLM baseline), CogVideoX1.5-5B wins decisively on ARC-AGI (16.75% vs 8.00% two-attempt), ConceptARC (0.33 vs 0.24 average), 4 of 5 visual games (Sudoku, Sudoku Mini, Hitori, Connect 4), and all route-planning tasks (up to 10× fewer samples needed for equivalent performance). The LLM retains an edge only on Chess Mate-in-1, attributed to abundant chess notation in its pretraining corpus, and on 1D Elementary Cellular Automata where sequential processing suffices. Against larger LLMs (Qwen3-8B, Llama3.1-8B) on ConceptARC, the VDM advantage widens (0.33–0.41 vs 0.18–0.24). Compared to commercial LLMs from the ARC Prize 2024 report, CogVideoX1.5-5B's 12.50% single-attempt score exceeds GPT-4o (9.00%) and Gemini 1.5 (8.00%) but trails o1-preview and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (21.00%). The Gemma-4B VLM ablation (Appendix E.1) shows that adding image input to a VLM provides no measurable benefit over text-only on Sudoku (0.06 vs 0.06 accuracy), and the image-only variant performs worse than a trivial baseline, justifying the use of pure LLMs rather than VLMs as the language-side baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pretrained Video Diffusion Models (CogVideoX, Wan2.1, LTX-Video) as visual problem-solving agents by reframing image-to-image tasks as short temporal transitions, a conceptual move that departs from prior work treating VDMs purely as generative tools. It draws on the LLM in-context learning and LoRA fine-tuning literature (Brown et al., 2020; Hu et al., 2022) to construct a symmetric comparison protocol, and builds on the visual in-context learning line (Wang et al., 2023a,b; Geng et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025) while explicitly contrasting with those approaches by using a frozen VDM backbone with only lightweight adapters. The paper carves new ground primarily through its controlled head-to-head design—identical LoRA budgets, identical task splits, modality-matched inputs—rather than through a novel architecture or training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide the first systematic evidence that spatiotemporal video pretraining confers inductive biases for structured visual reasoning that are qualitatively different from (and in many settings more data-efficient than) those from text pretraining, with VDMs achieving up to 10× fewer training samples on 2D spatial tasks like mazes and Life-like cellular automata. However, the evaluation is confined to synthetic grid-based benchmarks, and the continuous-latent precision bottleneck the authors acknowledge limits extrapolation to high-resolution or naturalistic visual problems. Open questions include whether these advantages persist on non-grid, embodied, or open-vocabulary visual tasks, and whether the VDM's generative nature can be harnessed for planning and simulation in robotics without the discretization losses observed here.
