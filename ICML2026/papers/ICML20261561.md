## Problem

Diffusion language models achieve sub-linear generation latency by sampling multiple tokens in parallel, but they do so by drawing each token independently from its conditional marginal distribution. This conditional token independence assumption breaks down for local structures such as multi-token words and syntactic code blocks, producing incoherent outputs where individual tokens are valid in isolation but fail to form coherent sequences. The paper asks how to enforce local joint dependencies among concurrently generated tokens without sacrificing the parallelism and bidirectional capabilities that make DLMs attractive.

## Input / Output

The method takes a code or text prompt and a partially masked sequence as input, where masked positions are represented by a dedicated [MASK] token. The DLM computes per-token conditional marginal probability vectors over the vocabulary for each masked position within a block. These marginals are projected into the auxiliary AR model's embedding space as soft (expected) embeddings, encapsulated between special boundary tokens, and fed to the AR model which outputs a coherent sequence of B discrete tokens per block. The final output is a fully unmasked code sequence or infilled code span, with the unmasking schedule (static, dynamic, or verification) controlling how many blocks are resolved per denoising iteration.

## Method

CoDiLA partitions the token sequence into contiguous blocks of size B and applies masked diffusion at the block granularity rather than the token level, proving that this block-level factorization strictly reduces the irreducible NELBO compared to token-wise independence (Theorem 3.2). Since directly modeling the joint distribution over |V|^B macro-tokens is intractable, the method decomposes the problem: a frozen bidirectional DLM backbone (Dream-Coder-Instruct-7B) provides global context and per-token marginals, while a small auxiliary AR model (Qwen3-0.6B) refines local structure within each block. The interface between the two is a soft-conditioning mechanism that computes the expected AR embedding under each DLM marginal (Equation 4), projecting the full probability distribution into the AR model's pretrained representational space without token-ID remapping. The AR model is trained end-to-end to minimize the block-level cross-entropy loss while the DLM remains frozen, and generation proceeds via entropy-based unmasking schedules (static full-block, dynamic partial-block, or AR-based coherence verification) that select which block to decode next based on average conditional entropy.

## Results

Under static parallelism on HumanEval, CoDiLA (B=4) achieves 51.8% Pass@1 at 33 tokens/sec, compared to 24.4% for the Dream-Coder-Instruct-7B baseline at the same parallelism level (K=4), a 27.4 pp improvement. At maximum parallelism (K=8), CoDiLA reaches 29.9% versus 5.5% for the baseline. On MBPP, CoDiLA (B=4) scores 55.6% vs. 33.9% for the baseline. CoDiLA also reduces syntax error rates by up to 56 pp (from 70% to 16% at K=8 on HumanEval) and, under dynamic parallelism with B=4, bridges the gap to sequential sampling while maintaining >2× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Dream-Coder-Instruct-7B base DLM, CoDiLA dominates at every parallelism level above K=1: at K=4 on HumanEval it gains 27.4 pp (51.8% vs. 24.4%), and at K=8 it gains 24.4 pp (29.9% vs. 5.5%), while maintaining comparable throughput. Against ADJUST (Bansal & Sanghavi, 2025), CoDiLA achieves higher accuracy at equivalent throughput; the paper attributes ADJUST's inferiority to its unbounded full-sequence attention overhead and limited single-layer verifier capacity. On the HumanEval-Infilling task, DreamOn+CoDiLA (τ=0.2) matches the sequential DreamOn(K=1) baseline at 62.5% P@1 while generating 1.3 tokens/step versus 1, and outperforms all pure AR baselines (DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B at 45.7%, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B at 58.7%). On ParallelBench waiting-line tasks, CoDiLA as a verifier improves over Dream-Instruct-7B by 10.5 pp at K=8 (63.1% vs. 52.6%) and 7.0 pp at K=16 (50.3% vs. 43.3%). On graph traversal planning, CoDiLA (B=8) consistently exceeds the MGDM baseline, e.g., 95.4% vs. 94.4% at 2 decoding iterations and 99.4% vs. 99.3% at 20 iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDiLA extends the masked diffusion language model framework (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024) by addressing the conditional token independence problem that limits parallel decoding coherence. It departs from prior coherence-enforcement strategies—ADJUST (Bansal & Sanghavi, 2025) which uses a single-layer DLM verifier over the full sequence, and left-to-right AR verification methods (Israel et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025b) that strip DLMs of their non-causal capabilities—by restricting an auxiliary AR model to bounded blocks and soft-conditioning it on the DLM's marginal distributions. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.2) generalizes prior NELBO lower-bound analyses for token-level independence (Huang et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2025a; Kang et al., 2026) to arbitrary block sizes B > 1, while Theorem 3.3 provides a Fréchet-class argument for why full soft-conditioning is necessary over top-k truncation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoDiLA offers a practical recipe for making DLMs competitive with AR models on code generation while preserving the unique bidirectional, infilling, and planning capabilities that motivate DLM adoption in the first place. The finding that a 0.6B AR model suffices—larger models provide no consistent gain—suggests the AR component acts as a lightweight local n-gram readout rather than a strong language model, which keeps the overhead minimal and the approach broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the fixed block-size partition can be replaced by semantically grounded dynamic boundaries, whether the approach generalizes to non-code domains, and how it interacts with emerging DLM scaling efforts (100B+ parameters). A limitation is that a separate AR model must be trained per block size, and the method is specific to masked (absorbing-state) DLMs.
