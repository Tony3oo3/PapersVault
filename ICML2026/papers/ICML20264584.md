## Problem

Reward models used in RLHF can exhibit systematic biases (e.g., favoring length, formatting, or hallucinated content) that cause downstream models to reward-hack, but prior work has only identified specific, pre-specified biases or adversarial examples. This paper formalizes the problem of automatically discovering *novel* reward model biases as natural language descriptions, without imposing prior restrictions on what biases to search for, and provides a black-box pipeline that requires only score access to the RM and preference access to a judge LLM.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes synthetically generated user prompts (from 20 handwritten topic summaries), a reward model accessed only via scalar scores, a frontier LLM judge accessed via binary pairwise preferences, and sampled responses from six open-weight LLMs of varying sizes. It produces natural language bias descriptions (e.g., 'Triple spaces between words'), quantified as RM bias strength (mean reward difference on counterfactual pairs) and judge winrate (fraction of pairs where the judge prefers the attribute-absent response). Counterfactual pairs are generated by minimally rewriting responses with three independent rewriter models, and final outputs must pass Bonferroni-corrected one-sided t-tests plus a partial conjunction test requiring significance in at least 2 of 3 rewriters.

## Method

The core contribution is an evolutionary search over natural language attribute descriptions, framed as a two-objective optimization: maximize RM bias strength R(A) while minimizing judge winrate J(A). The pipeline begins with hypothesis generation, where an LLM inspects sampled responses sorted by (reversed) RM reward and proposes candidate attributes, which are then semantically clustered. Subsequent iterations evaluate each candidate's bias strength via counterfactual pairs (responses minimally rewritten to add/remove the attribute), select survivors near the Pareto frontier, and mutate them via LLM prompting. A key design choice is using three independent rewriter models to generate counterfactuals, reducing the risk that correlated side-changes confound the bias measurement. The paper shows this evolutionary depth-5, branching-4 configuration outperforms flat best-of-N search at equal candidate budget, and validates recall by injecting known regex-detectable biases into a reward model.

## Results

The evolutionary pipeline identified 17 bias candidates across 7 of 20 user prompt topics for Skywork-V2-8B, of which 10 were statistically significant (p<0.01) for both the RM and the LLM judge. The most striking finding is the triple-space bias: the RM assigns ~1.2 higher reward to responses with triple spaces between words, while the judge winrate is only 0.02 (i.e., the judge almost never prefers the triple-spaced version). The hallucinated-details bias shows an RM strength of 2.52±0.51 (p=1.9e-6) with a judge winrate of 0.156, corroborating prior evidence that RMs reward convincing-sounding but false content. On the format-bias sanity check, the RM shows a strong preference for bold text on broad prompts and additionally for list formatting on narrow how-to prompts, consistent with Zhang et al. (2025b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other bias-discovery methods; its primary comparison is between pipeline configurations. In the controlled study (Section 3.3), the depth-5/branching-4 evolutionary run outperforms both the depth-3/branching-8 run and the depth-1 (flat best-of-N) run on both the visualization metric (more points in the bottom-right quadrant) and the diversity-adjusted bias strength (DABS) score, though only a single run per configuration was performed. As a sanity check, the pipeline's format-bias results align with Zhang et al. (2025b): the RM favors bold text on broad prompts and additionally favors lists on narrow how-to prompts, matching prior findings. Recall experiments (Section 3.4) show that the hypothesis-generation step can still propose relevant attributes even at very low signal-to-noise ratios, but detection rate drops sharply when the attribute is present in fewer than ~10% or more than ~90% of sampled responses, suggesting evolutionary iteration is important for discovering rarer biases. The authors note that more independent runs would be needed for rigorous conclusions on the evolutionary-vs-flat comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the evolutionary population-mutation-selection loop from Guo et al. (2023) to the domain of reward model interpretability, where prior work (Sharma et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025b; Kumar et al., 2025) identified specific biases like sycophancy or formatting preferences through targeted analysis rather than open-ended search. The counterfactual-pair evaluation methodology is closely related to Jiang et al. (2024), who perturb preference data along 15 pre-written attributes; the key departure here is that attribute hypotheses are generated and refined by an LLM without any prior restriction on what biases to look for. The two-objective formulation (high RM preference, low LLM-judge preference) formalizes what prior RM interpretability work handled ad hoc, and the use of a frontier LLM-as-judge as a human-proxy extends the model-diffing setup of Lindsey et al. (2024) to the RM auditing context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a lightweight, black-box audit that model developers could run routinely during RM development, potentially catching issues like the triple-space or hallucination biases before they propagate through RLHF into downstream models. However, the study is limited to a single RM family (Skywork-V2-8B), synthetic user prompts, and relies on LLM rewriters whose counterfactuals cannot be guaranteed fully disentangled from other changes. Open questions include whether the method scales to generative RMs, whether the discovered biases actually manifest during PPO/DPO optimization (the authors note the generated-text distribution may be far from the reference policy), and whether agentic or more capable models would substantially improve recall beyond the 7/20 topic coverage achieved here.
