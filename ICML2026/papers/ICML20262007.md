## Problem

GNNs trained on real-world graphs are severely degraded by noisy labels, yet existing graph-specific methods either rely on simple prediction-consistency criteria that ignore rich structural information for noise detection, or employ hard neighbor-voting corrections that suffer from confirmation bias under class imbalance. There is no principled mechanism that explicitly quantifies how graph topology and node attributes jointly contradict a node's label, nor a soft correction strategy that avoids over-committing to potentially wrong neighbor predictions.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a semi-supervised regime where only a small fraction of nodes (1% for large graphs, 5% for small graphs) carry labels, and those labels are corrupted at a specified noise rate (default 20%, tested up to 80%). The graph diffusion matrix is computed once from the full adjacency structure, while the attribute-level diffusion is recomputed each epoch from K=5 nearest-neighbor representations. Output is a class prediction for every unlabeled node, with the training loss combining corrected soft labels for labeled nodes and pseudo-labels for unlabeled nodes.

## Method

ICGNN detects noisy labels via an Influence Contradiction Score (ICS) that measures, through a Personalized PageRank diffusion matrix, how much influence a labeled node receives from nodes of *other* classes at both the structural level (graph topology) and the attribute level (KNN representation affinity graph), then fits a two-component GMM via EM to obtain a soft per-node confidence of being clean. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) shows that under a joint homophily assumption with constant δ, clean nodes have ICS ≤ 1−δ while noisy nodes have ICS ≥ δ, providing a principled separation. For correction, ICGNN replaces hard label flipping with a convex combination of the original one-hot label and a diffusion-weighted aggregation of neighbors' softmax predictions, weighted by the GMM confidence, thereby avoiding confirmation bias. Pseudo-labels for the abundant unlabeled nodes are generated using the same neighbor-aggregation formula, and the total cross-entropy loss over both corrected labeled and pseudo-labeled nodes drives end-to-end GNN training (GCN backbone by default, but GNN-agnostic).

## Results

Under 20% uniform noise, ICGNN achieves the best accuracy across all six benchmark datasets: 87.4% on Coauthor CS, 87.3% on Amazon Photo, 80.9% on Cora, 80.3% on Pubmed, 80.1% on DBLP, and 71.5% on Citeseer. Under 20% pair noise, ICGNN similarly leads with 85.9%, 86.3%, 79.4%, 80.6%, 80.2%, and 70.7% respectively. On the large-scale OGBN-Arxiv dataset, ICGNN reaches 28.3% accuracy while NRGNN and RTGNN run out of memory, and on the heterophilous Cornell dataset it achieves 44.7% versus 38.3% for DND-NET. Wilcoxon rank-sum tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.1) over runner-up methods in most cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICGNN achieves the highest accuracy on all 12 dataset–noise-type combinations, consistently outperforming the strongest graph-specific baselines. The closest competitors are DND-NET (best on Coauthor CS uniform at 86.2 vs. 87.4, and Citeseer uniform at 70.4 vs. 71.5), RTGNN (best on Coauthor CS uniform at 86.7 vs. 87.4 and Pubmed pair at 80.4 vs. 80.6), and NRGNN (best on Cora uniform at 80.0 vs. 80.9 and DBLP uniform at 79.3 vs. 80.1). Classic GNNs (GCN) and vision-domain noisy-label methods (Forward, Coteaching+) lag substantially, with GCN trailing ICGNN by 7–13 points depending on the dataset. On the large-scale OGBN-Arxiv, NRGNN and RTGNN fail entirely (OOM), while ICGNN (28.3%) surpasses DND-NET (25.9%) and CGNN (21.8%). On the heterophilous Cornell graph, ICGNN (44.7%) leads DND-NET (38.3%) by 6.4 points, confirming robustness beyond homophilous settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICGNN extends the line of graph-specific noisy-label methods (NRGNN, RTGNN, CGNN, DND-NET, ProCon) by replacing their detection mechanisms—prediction consistency, small-loss criteria, or neighbor voting—with a graph-diffusion-based Influence Contradiction Score (ICS) that captures both structural and attribute-level contradictions. The diffusion matrix from Klicpera et al. (2019) is repurposed as the core tool for quantifying cross-class influence, and a two-component GMM with EM provides soft-threshold noise confidence rather than hard binary decisions. For correction, ICGNN departs from CGNN's neighbor voting (which suffers from class-imbalance confirmation bias) in favor of a convex combination weighted by GMM confidence, and adds pseudo-labeling for unlabeled nodes in the spirit of NRGNN and DND-NET.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (Theorem 3.1) and empirically consistent approach to noisy-label node classification that works across GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, GIN) and scales to large graphs (OGBN-Arxiv), making it a practical tool for real-world graph data where annotation errors are common. However, the method still relies on a homophily assumption (albeit softened by the attribute-level ICS), and performance degrades substantially at extreme noise rates (>60%), suggesting that beyond a certain noise threshold, label information becomes net-negative and representation learning from features alone would be preferable. Open questions include whether the ICS framework can be extended to instance-dependent noise patterns, dynamic graphs, or multi-task settings, and whether the fixed α=0.5 trade-off generalizes to graphs with very different structural densities.
