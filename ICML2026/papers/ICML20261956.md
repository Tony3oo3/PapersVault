## Problem

LLM-based agents can produce unsafe behaviors from benign instructions because small deviations in intermediate thoughts propagate into harmful actions, yet existing guardrails either operate only on final outputs, require intrusive model modifications, or terminate execution entirely. There is no lightweight, model-agnostic mechanism that intervenes on the agent's internal reasoning to correct unsafe thoughts before they trigger tool actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261956/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step of the agent's think-act-observe loop, Thought-Aligner receives the original user instruction, the trajectory history (all prior thoughts and observations, with actions excluded since they are deemed redundant), and the current raw thought as a single concatenated text prompt. It outputs a single corrected safe thought, which is then concatenated back into the base agent's context so the agent regenerates its action and parameters from the corrected reasoning rather than the original unsafe one.

## Method

Thought-Aligner is a lightweight (1.5B/7B, Qwen2.5-based) plug-in language model that performs causal intervention on intermediate agent thoughts, inserted between thought generation and action execution without modifying the base agent's architecture, prompts, or tool configuration. It is trained via a two-stage supervised fine-tuning strategy: a warm-up stage on ~33,000 I-T-T pairs (safe thought → itself) to stabilize benign reasoning and prevent over-correction, followed by a core stage on ~41,000 I-T-C pairs (unsafe thought → minimal human-validated correction) to learn precise safety-aligned edits. The training dataset of 74,000+ paired thoughts spans ten risk scenarios and is generated by four SOTA LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B, GPT-4.1, Claude-Sonnet-4) with multi-reviewer manual validation. Both stages optimize the same conditional likelihood objective, minimizing divergence between the model's output and the curated safe-thought distribution. The key design choice is operating at the thought level rather than the output level, which makes the module model-agnostic, adds under 100 ms latency, and preserves task helpfulness by steering reasoning rather than blocking execution.

## Results

On ToolEmu, Thought-Aligner-7B achieves an average safety rate of ~94.7% across six LLMs (ranging from 92.2% on DeepSeek-V3 to 97.9% on o3), improving behavioral safety by ~23% on average over the best baseline guardrail and ~40% over the undefended setting, while also increasing helpfulness by roughly 5%. On Agent-SafetyBench, it improves behavior safety by ~22% on average (e.g., 85.6% vs. 74.5% for Athena on GPT-4.1). Cross-benchmark validation on AgentHarm, AgentDojo, and InjecAgent shows consistent gains of ~15%, 12%, and 19% respectively, with safety rates above 90% on the latter two. The 1.5B variant adds under 100 ms per-step latency, making it deployable in resource-constrained settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Thought-Aligner-7B outperforms all four baselines on every evaluated LLM and benchmark. Against Self-Reflection, it gains 15–35 percentage points in ToolEmu safety (e.g., 95.2% vs. 73.6% on GPT-4.1), demonstrating that internal introspection alone is insufficient for catching subtle reasoning risks. Against GuardAgent, the gap is 10–25 points (95.2% vs. 84.7% on GPT-4.1; 95.1% vs. 84.7% on Claude-Sonnet-4), showing that outer-layer rule-based detection misses risks that thought-level correction catches. Against ShieldAgent, the advantage is even larger (95.2% vs. 56.9% on GPT-4.1), as rule-based blocking is brittle in dynamic settings. Against Athena (the strongest baseline in most settings), Thought-Aligner still leads by 10–23 points (95.2% vs. 80.6% on GPT-4.1; 95.1% vs. 76.4% on Claude-Sonnet-4), while also avoiding Athena's reliance on a commercial LLM critic. Notably, Thought-Aligner improves helpfulness over Athena on Claude-Sonnet-4 (44.4% vs. 48.6% is a slight loss) but matches or exceeds it on most other models, and substantially outperforms blocking-based guardrails (GuardAgent, ShieldAgent) that terminate tasks and reduce utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of agent safety guardrails (Athena, ShieldAgent, GuardAgent, AgentSentinel) that operate at the output level, use external LLM critics, or rely on hand-crafted rules, by intervening directly on intermediate reasoning thoughts within the ReAct loop. Methodologically, it adopts the two-stage learn-to-correct fine-tuning strategy from Ji et al. (2024, Aligner), repurposing it for the specific task of mapping unsafe agent thoughts to minimally edited safe alternatives. The key conceptual departure is framing safety as a causal intervention on an upstream variable (the thought) rather than a post-hoc filter on the action or output, which the authors argue enables generalization to unseen unsafe intents that rigid rule-based defenses miss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Thought-Aligner offers a practical, model-agnostic safety module (<100 ms latency at 1.5B scale) that can be slotted into existing agent frameworks without architectural changes, addressing a real deployment gap where current guardrails are either costly (API-based critics) or brittle (rule-based). The main limitation is that it still depends on the base agent faithfully translating the corrected thought into a safe action, and it requires the agent to explicitly generate thought traces, excluding architectures that do not. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger or more specialized agent models, how it performs in truly open-ended (non-benchmark) environments, and whether adversarial attackers could learn to craft thoughts that evade the corrector.
