## Problem

Simultaneous speech-to-speech translation requires learning a fine-grained policy for when to listen and when to speak, but state-of-the-art systems depend on word-level aligned interpretation data that is virtually non-existent in the wild. Current workarounds use synthetic alignments built with language-specific heuristics, making the pipeline complex, suboptimal, and difficult to scale to new languages with different grammatical structures.

## Input / Output

The model receives a monophonic source speech waveform at 24 kHz, encoded into discrete tokens by the streaming Mimi codec at 12.5 Hz with 16 residual quantization levels. It produces both a translated speech waveform in the target language and a parallel text translation (Inner Monologue) in a single forward pass, operating in streaming mode where output tokens are generated incrementally as input tokens arrive. Source audio tokens are forced at inference time while the model autoregressively generates target text and audio tokens across a multistream of concatenated sequences.

## Method

Hibiki-Zero is a decoder-only RQ-Transformer (2B Temporal Transformer + 6-layer Depth Transformer per codebook, 3B total parameters) that jointly models source audio, target text, and target audio as discrete token streams. Training proceeds in two phases: first, supervised learning on sentence-level aligned data where coarse timing is established by inserting random silences between sentences (no word-level alignment required); second, reinforcement learning using GRPO with a novel process-reward scheme that computes BLEU scores at intermediate frames (every 8 input words) and combines them with the final BLEU via a tunable weight α, yielding fine-grained local advantages across a group of 4 sampled translations. This single BLEU-based reward unifies translation quality and latency optimization without the hyperparameter complexity of multi-component reward systems, and the model is further distilled with codebook weight sharing to reduce inference cost.

## Results

On the long-form Audio-NTREX-4L benchmark, Hibiki-Zero achieves ASR BLEU scores of 28.7 (French), 31.5 (Spanish), 31.3 (Portuguese), and 28.3 (German), exceeding Seamless by 4.8–4.3 points and surpassing Hibiki (French-only) by 2.3 points. Speaker similarity scores reach 61.3–66.0, outperforming Seamless by 16.9–18.2 points. On the short-form Europarl-ST benchmark, Hibiki-Zero attains BLEU of 35.0 (French), 33.8 (Spanish), 33.6 (Portuguese), and 28.7 (German) with end offsets of 1.9–2.4 s, beating Hibiki by ~3 pts ASR BLEU and Seamless by >30 pts speaker similarity. Human evaluation confirms superior audio quality (MOS 62–73.5 vs. 10.7–15.6 for Seamless) and speech naturalness (66–76 vs. 21–27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Seamless (Barrault et al., 2023), Hibiki-Zero wins decisively on speaker similarity across all four languages and both settings (e.g., 61.3 vs. 44.4 on long-form French, a 16.9-pt gap; 49.5 vs. 19.1 on short-form French, a 30.4-pt gap). On long-form ASR BLEU, Hibiki-Zero leads by 4.3–4.8 points (e.g., 28.7 vs. 23.9 French, 28.3 vs. 24.0 German), while also achieving lower end offset (1.9–2.6 s vs. 2.5–3.2 s) and comparable or lower LAAL. On short-form, Hibiki-Zero matches or slightly exceeds Seamless on BLEU (35.0 vs. 33.8 French) while being faster (2.1 s vs. 2.4 s end offset). Against Hibiki (Labiausse et al., 2025, French-only), Hibiki-Zero improves ASR BLEU by ~3 points (34.6 vs. 31.8 short-form), reduces end offset (2.1 vs. 2.5 s), and more than doubles speaker similarity (49.5 vs. 35.7). Human MOS evaluations confirm these objective gains: Hibiki-Zero scores 64.5/70.0/67.2 (audio quality/speaker sim/naturalness) on French vs. 11.4/21.1/21.2 for Seamless and 62.9/44.7/57.0 for Hibiki.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hibiki-Zero extends the multistream RQ-Transformer architecture from Défossez et al. (2024) and the simultaneous S2ST pipeline of Hibiki (Labiausse et al., 2025), but fundamentally departs from the supervised training paradigm by replacing word-level aligned synthetic interpretation data with sentence-level alignment followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning using BLEU-only process rewards. This contrasts with Seed LiveInterpret 2.0 (Cheng et al., 2025), which applied PPO with a complex multi-component reward (quality + latency) and required chunk-level alignment plus human-annotated finetuning, and with Seamless (Barrault et al., 2023), which relies on language-specific alignment heuristics. The key conceptual shift is casting the translation policy (when to listen vs. speak) as an RL optimization problem solvable with a single, language-agnostic reward signal rather than as a supervised regression target requiring hand-crafted temporal alignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for word-level alignment heuristics, Hibiki-Zero removes a major bottleneck that has limited simultaneous speech translation to a handful of well-resourced language pairs, and demonstrates that a new input language can be supported with under 1000 hours of speech while matching or exceeding Seamless on quality, latency, and speaker identity preservation. The released Audio-NTREX-4L benchmark (45h multilingual long-form data) and open model weights lower the barrier for future research. Open questions include whether the quality/latency trade-off can be controlled at inference time (currently fixed at training), whether accent intensity in the output can be steered, and whether the BLEU-only reward generalizes to non-English target languages where BLEU is less reliable.
