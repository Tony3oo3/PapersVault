## Problem

Mixtures of distributions satisfying approximate tensorization of entropy (ATE) do not themselves satisfy ATE, leaving a gap in theoretical guarantees for multimodal distributions in both sampling and identity-testing. Prior work on data-based initialization for sampling only handled the weaker Poincaré inequality, and the identity-testing question for mixtures of ATE distributions was left open by Blanca et al. (2023).

## Input / Output

For sampling, the method takes a mixture μ = Σ ρ(a)μ_a with each component satisfying c*-ATE (or the weaker 1/(c*n)-MLSI) plus m = O(k/ε + log(1/δ)/ε) i.i.d. samples from μ as a data-based initialization, and produces approximate samples via Glauber dynamics mixing in time Õ(c*n). For identity-testing, the algorithm consumes two oracles: a General Oracle yielding i.i.d. samples from an unknown π, and a Coordinate Oracle yielding samples from π|x\i (conditioning on all coordinates except the i-th), along with known mixing weights ρ and descriptions of the ATE components. The output is an accept/reject decision with guarantees in KL-divergence.

## Method

The core technical tool is a Φ-entropic generalization of weak Sobolev inequalities for mixtures, combined with the chain rule for entropy to decompose global divergence into intra-component and inter-component parts. For sampling, the key insight is that the inter-component entropy of a data-based initialization concentrates around zero: the authors derive a moment-generating-function bound on the KL-divergence of the empirical posterior distribution from the true mixing weights, avoiding standard Chernoff bounds that would introduce an undesirable dependence on the minimum mixing weight ρ*. For identity-testing, the algorithm has two steps: Step 1 performs local coordinate-conditional KL-tests to detect intra-component discrepancies (leveraging ATE of individual components via Lemma 19), while Step 2 estimates the effective posterior mixture weights ρ_π from samples of π and runs a base KL identity-tester against the true weights ρ to detect inter-component discrepancies. The chain rule guarantees that if D_KL(π‖μ) ≥ ε, at least one of the two terms is ≥ 0.5ε, ensuring rejection.

## Results

For sampling from mixtures of ATE distributions via Glauber dynamics with data-based initialization, the paper achieves optimal sample complexity m = O(k/ε + log(1/δ)/ε) with mixing time c*·n·O(log log(1/min_x μ(x)) + log(1/ε)) in KL-divergence (Theorem 4). For identity-testing under coordinate conditional access, the algorithm requires O(c*·n/ε)·log²(c*·n/ε)·log(1/η)·log log(1/η)·√|Σ| + O(√k·log(1/ρ*)/ε) oracle calls (Theorem 5). The sample complexity is tight: learning a distribution on k elements to ε KL-divergence requires Θ(k/ε + log(1/δ)/ε) samples, matching the lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Koehler et al. (2024), this work replaces the Poincaré inequality assumption with the stronger modified log-Sobolev inequality (MLSI), which follows from ATE, and eliminates the log(k/δ) factor in the sample complexity, achieving O(k/ε²) versus O(k log(k/δ)/ε²). Compared to Huang et al. (2024), the key improvement is removing the dependence on the minimum mixing weight min_a ρ_a, which can be exponentially small in natural mixture settings; this is accomplished by using a moment-generating-function bound on the KL-divergence of the empirical distribution rather than standard Chernoff bounds. Both prior works also suffer from suboptimal mixing times due to their reliance on Poincaré inequalities, whereas this work achieves optimal mixing times in TV distance for product distributions by leveraging MLSIs. The identity-testing result (Theorem 5) answers an open question from Blanca et al. (2023), extending their single-distribution ATE tester to the mixture setting with only a √k·log(1/ρ*)/ε overhead for the inter-component weight estimation step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-based initialization framework for multimodal sampling from Koehler & Vuong (2024) and the discrete-mixture results of Koehler et al. (2024) and Huang et al. (2024), which required the weaker Poincaré inequality, to the stronger and more natural setting of modified log-Sobolev inequalities (MLSI) implied by approximate tensorization of entropy (ATE). For identity testing, it directly answers an open question from Blanca et al. (2023), who proved efficient testers for single ATE distributions under coordinate conditional access but left the mixture case unresolved. The technical core is a Φ-entropic generalization of the weak functional inequality approach from Huang et al. (2024), combined with the chain rule for entropy to decompose the problem into intra-component and inter-component contributions, with the latter handled via posterior weight concentration (sampling) or posterior weight estimation (testing).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multimodal distributions are the central challenge in modern generative modeling, and this paper provides the first theoretical guarantees showing that data-based initialization overcomes the multimodality barrier in the ATE/MLSI regime with optimal sample complexity, bridging a gap between the unimodal ATE literature and practical multimodal settings. The identity-testing result is notable for answering a specific open question and for showing that the Coordinate Oracle's full power is not needed (Remark 6), suggesting broader applicability. Limitations include the restriction to discrete product spaces (though the authors note extension to continuous domains via Langevin dynamics), and the identity-testing algorithm's dependence on η-balancedness. An open question (Remark 36) is whether the 1-homogeneity requirement of KL-entropy can be bypassed to obtain testers for other divergences.
