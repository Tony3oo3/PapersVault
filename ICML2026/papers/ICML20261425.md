## Problem

Standard Euclidean Flow Matching assumes flat geometry, but Spatial Semantic Pointers (SSPs) reside on a Clifford Hypertorus embedded in the unit hypersphere S^{d-1}. Linear interpolants between noisy and clean states cut through the hypersphere's interior, collapsing vector magnitude and destroying the phase relationships required for accurate spatial decoding. No prior method addresses denoising of high-dimensional structured representations under these non-Euclidean geometric constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy SSP vector on S^{d-1} (d up to 1015 dimensions) corrupted by a mixture of Wrapped Normal phase drift, Gaussian cross-talk from bundled memories, and spiking variability, and produces a cleaned SSP vector that preserves the Fourier phasor structure for accurate spatial decoding. In the SLAM application, the input is the drifting recurrent output of a spiking path integrator (1000–3000 LIF neurons), and the output is a stabilized position estimate that can be reliably bound into the allocentric map. The source distribution for training is isotropic Gaussian noise projected onto S^{d-1}, approximating maximally corrupted states.

## Method

Geodesic Flow Matching (GFM) redefines the probability path as a great-circle arc on S^{d-1} using Riemannian Log and Exp maps, ensuring all intermediate states remain on the manifold by construction. A 3-block Residual MLP with sinusoidal time embeddings (bottleneck schedule 2d→d, 4d→d, 2d→d) regresses the parallel-transported tangent velocity field along the geodesic, trained with a cosine flow loss that aligns predicted and true tangent directions. Inference integrates the learned field via K geodesic steps (Exp map with Δt·v_k), with a numerical re-normalization for stability. The key distinction from Euclidean CFM is that the velocity is always tangent to the hypersphere, preserving unit norm and phase structure throughout transport, which prevents the magnitude collapse that corrupts spatial semantics in the Euclidean baseline.

## Results

In the Spiking Semantic SLAM benchmark (Table 1), Geodesic Flow Matching achieves a 72% reduction in path-integration RMSE over the Grid baseline at N=1500 neurons (0.076 m vs. 0.249 m) and a 63% reduction over Euclidean Flow Matching (0.204 m). A 1,500-neuron system with Geodesic cleanup (0.076 m) matches the accuracy of a 2,500-neuron baseline (0.083 m), yielding a 40% reduction in required spiking neural resources. In the static denoising benchmark (d=1015), GFM maintains high cosine similarity across all noise levels while Euclidean CFM degrades sharply at high noise; the geodesic advantage over Euclidean grows from low to moderate dimensions (d≈50–200) and stabilizes at a ~10% plateau for d>500.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Euclidean Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022), Geodesic FM wins decisively at every neuron count in the SLAM task: at N=1000 the gap is 0.449 m vs. 0.162 m (64% lower RMSE), and at N=1500 it is 0.204 m vs. 0.076 m (63% lower). Strikingly, Euclidean FM degrades *worse* than no cleanup at higher neuron counts (e.g., 0.599 m at N=2000 vs. 0.061 m for Grid), indicating that phase-destroying chordal corrections actively destabilize the recurrent integrator. Against the Grid Lookup baseline (Dumont et al., 2023), Geodesic FM dominates in the resource-constrained regime (N≤1500: 0.162 vs. 0.586 and 0.076 vs. 0.249 m) but is marginally outperformed at N≥2000 (0.067 vs. 0.061 m at N=2000), where the grid's discrete prototypes are sufficiently fine-grained to approximate the manifold. In the static denoising benchmark, GFM also surpasses both Grid and L-BFGS-B optimization in high-noise regimes (signal strength < 0.15), where discriminative baselines snap to random prototypes while the learned geodesic vector field provides a continuous geometric prior for recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Riemannian Flow Matching framework of Chen & Lipman (2024) to a domain it was not originally designed for: high-dimensional (d > 1000) Spatial Semantic Pointer representations constrained to a Clifford Hypertorus on the unit hypersphere. Prior Riemannian FM work targeted low-dimensional data with Gaussian-like target distributions, whereas here the target distribution is a structured, non-Gaussian manifold arising from hexagonal grid-cell encodings. The paper also departs from the VSA cleanup literature (grid lookup, L-BFGS optimization, Hopfield-style attractors) by reframing denoising as a continuous generative transport problem rather than a discrete search or local optimization, and validates the approach in a spiking neural SLAM system where all three noise sources (phase drift, spiking variability, cross-talk) act simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 1,500-neuron spiking path integrator with geodesic cleanup matches a 2,500-neuron baseline (40% resource savings) is practically significant for neuromorphic deployment, where neuron count directly maps to hardware cost. The demonstration that Euclidean flow matching produces valid-looking but spatially displaced outputs (rather than obvious failures) highlights a subtle failure mode that could affect any hyperspherical embedding pipeline. Open questions include extending the formulation to other VSA topologies (Boolean hypercube, complex Fourier-HRRs), converting the Residual MLP velocity field to a fully spiking network for true neuromorphic hardware, and whether the geometric constraint remains critical at even higher dimensions or with more complex target distributions.
