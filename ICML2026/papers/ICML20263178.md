## Problem

MARL-based traffic signal control struggles to scale to large heterogeneous networks because existing grouped methods (e.g., GPLight) cluster intersections by traffic-embedding similarity and then assign them to policies that are irrelevant to those clusters, leading to behaviorally mismatched sharing. The root cause is the missing connection between intersection representations and the control policies they are assigned to, compounded by passive reassignment that tracks feature-space proximity rather than policy suitability as traffic regimes evolve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each intersection receives a local observation vector (one-hot active phase, per-lane effective running vehicle counts, and per-movement efficient pressure) plus a sliding window of τ recent (observation, executed-phase, reward) triples that encode how traffic has evolved under the current controller. The method outputs a discrete phase selection (one of 8 non-conflicting movement sets) per intersection per control interval, along with a dynamic group assignment that routes the intersection to one of K shared Q-networks; the assignment is recomputed at every step via a compatibility score rather than fixed at training time.

## Method

SLight decouples policy learning from intersection grouping through three coupled components. A policy-aware LSTM encoder consumes the (observation, action, reward) interaction window to produce a traffic-query embedding that reflects policy-conditioned temporal evolution rather than snapshot similarity. A CVAE trained on replayed policy-aware interactions learns K latent control-principle prototypes that capture group-level action-selection tendencies; these prototypes are updated by momentum-averaging the CVAE posterior means of samples routed to each group, yielding slowly-varying behavioral references. An adaptive assignment module computes a bilinear compatibility score between each intersection's traffic query and every principle prototype (with a one-hot identity term for routing stability) and selects the group via ε-greedy sampling during training or argmax at evaluation, so that intersections are matched to the policy whose learned behavior best fits their current traffic regime rather than to the nearest traffic-similarity cluster.

## Results

SLight achieves the best ATT across all six evaluation settings in Table 1: 246.14 (JN1), 234.01 (JN2), 230.90 (JN3), 272.12 (HZ1), 304.11 (HZ2), and 870.41 (NY1). On the 196-intersection NY1 network, SLight reduces ATT by 6.4% over GPLight (929.43) and by 18.8% over Advanced-CoLight (1071.43), while also achieving the highest throughput (8642 vs. 8079 for Advanced-CoLight). On the 2,510-intersection NY2 network (Table 2), SLight attains 856.94 ATT, outperforming CoSLight (1053.90) by 18.7% and MaxPressure (1441.23) by 40.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLight outperforms all 12 baselines in Table 1 on every setting. Against rule-based methods, SLight reduces ATT by 44% over FixedTime and by 15% over MaxPressure on NY1. Among unstructured RL baselines, the closest competitor is Advanced-MaxPressure (1059.88 ATT on NY1), which SLight beats by 18.1%; CoLight and MPLight are further behind at 1115.76 and 1653.93 respectively. The grouped RL baseline GPLight, which is the most directly comparable method, achieves 929.43 ATT on NY1—6.4% worse than SLight—and degrades severely on HZ1 (758.17 vs. SLight's 272.12), confirming that similarity-only grouping produces behaviorally mismatched policies. In the supplementary Table 4, SLight also beats TransformerLight (1097.33 on NY1), CoSLight (1404.99), Proactive-CoLight (1024.62), GPLight+ (1181.42), and all LLM-based baselines (Llama3.3-70B, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1, Traffic-R1-3B) on the shared JN/HZ settings, with the largest margin on NY1 where LLM methods are not evaluated. The consistent pattern is that SLight's advantage grows with network scale and heterogeneity, being smallest on JN2/JN3 (margins of 1–5 s over Advanced-MaxPressure) and largest on NY1/NY2 (margins of 180–570 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

SLight extends the grouped MARL-TSC line initiated by GPLight, which itself builds on CoLight's global parameter-sharing framework. The key departure is a decoupling: rather than clustering intersections by traffic-representation similarity and then assigning policies to clusters (GPLight's approach), SLight learns stable 'control principle' prototypes via a CVAE over replayed state-action trajectories and adaptively routes each intersection to the most compatible principle using a policy-aware traffic-pattern embedding. This shifts the grouping logic from passive similarity-based clustering to an active, policy-conditioned matching process, addressing what the authors identify as three fundamental limitations of GPLight (insufficient observations, irrelevant grouping, and passive assignment).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a small set of learned shared policies (K=4) can absorb substantial network heterogeneity at metropolitan scale (2,510 intersections), which is practically important for cities where per-intersection independent learning is infeasible. The interpretability analysis showing that learned principles correspond to meaningful traffic regimes (NS-dominant, balanced, mixed-load) provides a degree of transparency absent in prior grouped MARL approaches. However, the work remains simulator-based with a fixed control interval, and open questions include cross-city transfer, variable green-time allocation, and validation under real-world deployment constraints with navigation-system interactions.
