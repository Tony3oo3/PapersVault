## Problem

Most vision-language manipulation benchmarks and methods target rigid robotic arms with reliable joint proprioception and low-dimensional kinematics, leaving deformable embodiments largely unexplored. Soft continuum arms offer shape adaptability for cluttered or confined spaces but introduce challenges of unreliable proprioception, distributed low-level actuation, and high-dimensional coupled action spaces that existing benchmarks do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264952/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy model receives a 514×514 RGB image from a fixed camera viewpoint and a natural-language instruction as input, and outputs per-segment external torque commands (τ_e ∈ R^{N×3}) plus a binary end-effector gripper state (S ∈ {0,1}). Critically, no proprioceptive state (joint angles, segment positions) is provided to the policy; the model must infer the soft arm's full configuration and deformation purely from the visual observation, reflecting real-world sensing limitations of soft robots.

## Method

ManiSoft couples Elastica's Cosserat-rod soft-body dynamics with MuJoCo's rigid-body contact simulation through an elastic force constraint (a zero-rest-length virtual spring with translational stiffness k_F and rotational stiffness k_M), enabling contact-rich manipulation with continuous deformation. Expert trajectories are generated hierarchically: a task-specific rule-based planner produces a sequence of 6-DoF SE(3) waypoints, and a SAC-trained RL executor (MLP policy, 160M samples) converts each waypoint into torque commands using a reward combining SE(3) pose-difference and a stability term that penalizes oscillation near the target. The benchmark comprises 6,300 scene-trajectory pairs across four tasks (Collecting, Alignment, Stacking, Arrangement) in both clean and randomized settings, with 263 annotated 3D objects and diverse language instructions. Three representative VLA policies (Diffusion Policy, RDT-1B, OpenVLA-OFT) are evaluated, revealing that performance drops substantially under randomization and that failures stem from inaccurate visual proprioceptive estimation and under-exploitation of soft-arm compliance for obstacle avoidance.

## Results

On ManiSoft's clean setting, Diffusion Policy (DP) achieves the highest average success rate of 31.6% (520 steps), closely followed by OpenVLA-OFT at 30.4% (527 steps), while RDT-1B lags far behind at 9.2% (496 steps). Under the randomized setting, performance drops sharply for all models, but OpenVLA-OFT retains 27.0% average success (554 steps) whereas DP collapses to 2.2% (613 steps) and RDT to 1.6% (368 steps). The COLL task is consistently the easiest across all models, while ARR and STK are the most challenging, particularly under randomization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP and OpenVLA-OFT are the two competitive baselines, both substantially outperforming RDT-1B (9.2% vs. 31.6%/30.4% average in clean). In the clean setting, DP wins on COLL (63.0% vs. 45.4%) but OpenVLA-OFT leads on ALN (25.0% vs. 18.3%), STK (20.0% vs. 15.0%), and ARR (31.3% vs. 30.0%), suggesting DP excels at simpler tasks while OpenVLA-OFT's pretrained VLM backbone aids spatial reasoning. Under randomization, the gap reverses dramatically: OpenVLA-OFT maintains 27.0% average success while DP drops to 2.2% (a 29.4% absolute decline), indicating that DP's from-scratch training is highly sensitive to scene and instruction variation. RDT-1B, despite its ~1B parameters, underperforms both ~400M-parameter models across all tasks, likely due to overfitting on the limited training data. The authors attribute DP's poor generalization to its lack of pretrained language understanding and OpenVLA-OFT's robustness to its VLM prior, while also noting a 'stop-moving' failure mode in OpenVLA-OFT that inflates step counts on COLL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vision-language manipulation benchmarking line (RLBench, ManiSkill, CALVIN, LIBERO, RoboTwin) from rigid arms to soft continuum arms, a setting where reliable proprioception is absent and actuation is distributed and low-level. Technically, it builds on Elastica (Naughton et al., 2021) for Cosserat-rod soft-body dynamics and couples it with MuJoCo via an elastic force constraint to handle contact-rich interactions — a hybrid simulation approach not previously applied to manipulation benchmarking. The hierarchical trajectory generation (rule-based waypoint planner + SAC-trained torque executor) repurposes the Elastica-RL reward structure but adapts it with a stability term to produce stable demonstrations at scale. The evaluated baselines (Diffusion Policy, RDT-1B, OpenVLA-OFT) are all originally designed for rigid-arm kinematic control, making their application here a direct stress test of existing VLA architectures on a fundamentally different embodiment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ManiSoft is the first benchmark to systematically expose how current VLA models fail on soft arms, identifying two concrete bottlenecks: visual estimation of proprioceptive state and under-exploitation of deformability for obstacle avoidance. The dramatic performance collapse under randomization (DP: 31.6% → 2.2%) suggests that existing policies have not learned robust deformable-body reasoning and are highly sensitive to scene variation. Open questions include how to infer soft-arm configuration from vision alone, how to incorporate physical priors about compliance into policy learning, and whether sim-to-real transfer is feasible given the current tabletop-only scope. The benchmark could catalyze research toward compliant interaction in service and medical robotics, but its impact will depend on whether the community can close the gap between clean and randomized performance.
