## Problem

Standard RL policies fail to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, and existing meta-RL baselines (MAML, ANIL, CAVIA, PEARL, VariBAD, Meta-DT) degrade when deployment tasks deviate from the meta-training distribution. The paper asks whether a simple structural constraint—freezing a shared backbone and adapting only lightweight linear layers—can enable robust adaptation to OOD tasks where these methods fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263586/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes MuJoCo state vectors (continuous, low-dimensional) and outputs continuous control actions for locomotion tasks (Cheetah velocity/direction, Ant goal/direction). During adaptation, the input passes through a task-specific linear pre-layer, a frozen nonlinear backbone, and a task-specific linear post-layer; only the two linear layers (a few thousand parameters, ~3–14% of the full network) are updated, while the backbone and a task-specific SAC critic are trained from scratch or loaded from meta-training.

## Method

APB parameterizes the policy as π = h ∘ f ∘ g, where f is a shared nonlinear backbone and g, h are task-specific linear maps placed before and after it. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.3) shows that under isomorphic MDPs, any target policy can be expressed as a linear transformation of a source policy, motivating the linear pre/post structure. During meta-training, the backbone is learned across tasks via SAC while task-specific linear layers are trained per task; during OOD adaptation, the backbone is frozen and only the linear layers (plus a fresh critic) are optimized. Notably, even a randomly initialized backbone yields competitive performance, indicating the architectural constraint itself acts as a strong inductive bias, while a meta-trained backbone provides additional benefit under restricted-data regimes such as behavior cloning.

## Results

On OOD tasks (e.g., Cheetah-vel with target v=−2.0, Ant-goal/Dir at θ=1.5π), APB achieves strong adaptation returns while all meta-RL baselines (MAML, ANIL, CAVIA, PEARL, VariBAD, Meta-DT) show negligible or near-zero returns, indicating complete failure to leverage pre-trained knowledge under distribution shift. In the random-backbone setting (no meta-training), APB matches standard RL on Cheetah-vel and Cheetah-dir, slightly degrades on Ant-dir, and unexpectedly outperforms the baseline on Ant-goal. APB requires only 3.14%–14.13% of the trainable parameters of the full baseline (e.g., 6,906 vs. 219,606 params on Cheetah-vel), reducing per-iteration training FLOPs by ~30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard RL (end-to-end training, same network scale), APB with a randomly initialized frozen backbone matches performance on Cheetah-vel and Cheetah-dir, slightly underperforms on Ant-dir, and outperforms the baseline on Ant-goal, suggesting the structural constraint itself acts as a beneficial inductive bias. On OOD tasks, APB decisively outperforms all parameterized policy-gradient baselines (MAML, ANIL, CAVIA), which show negligible return improvement despite gradient-based fine-tuning, and all black-box/context-encoder baselines (PEARL, VariBAD, Meta-DT), which fail to adapt because their context encoders cannot process OOD observations. Against the linear probing baseline (Kumar et al., 2022; post-backbone layer only), APB (adapting both pre- and post-backbone linear layers) achieves stronger adaptation, confirming that the input-side linear transformation contributes meaningfully. In the behavior cloning setting with narrowly concentrated OOD expert demonstrations, the meta-pretrained frozen backbone (APB-BC) outperforms both the random-backbone variant and a fully unfrozen random model, indicating that meta-learned representations provide a critical prior when interactive data collection is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

APB sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), multi-task learning with shared backbones, and meta-RL. It draws structural inspiration from D'Eramo et al. (2020), which uses a shared backbone with task-specific nonlinear modules, but replaces those modules with linear layers and provides a theoretical justification via policy-matrix decomposition (Lemma 4.1, Theorem 4.3) showing that under state-permutation isomorphism, the target policy is a linear transformation of the source policy. Unlike prior meta-RL methods (MAML, CAVIA, PEARL, VariBAD, Meta-DT) that update the full network or rely on context encoders, APB freezes the backbone entirely and adapts only pre- and post-backbone linear layers, explicitly targeting OOD tasks where those baselines fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a randomly initialized backbone paired with task-specific linear layers matches standard RL performance suggests the architectural constraint itself is a powerful inductive bias, potentially simplifying deployment pipelines by removing the need for careful meta-training in some settings. However, the theoretical analysis is restricted to isomorphic MDPs (permutation assumption), experiments are limited to MuJoCo state-based control, and the paper does not systematically disentangle the contributions of architecture choice versus meta-parameter quality. Extending APB to pixel-based observations, more complex task distributions, and providing tighter theoretical guarantees beyond the permutation setting remain important open questions.
