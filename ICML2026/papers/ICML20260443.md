## Problem

Negative Prompting (NP) in diffusion models simply negates the CFG guidance vector, corresponding to sampling from an unbounded inverted distribution p(x)/p(c⁻|x)^γ that pushes samples into low-density regions or outside the data support, degrading image quality. Existing fixes (Perp-Neg, SLD, DNG) are empirical heuristics that require positive prompts to be applicable or still fail to thoroughly remove overlapping concepts. The paper asks whether a principled, self-regulating negative guidance can be derived that repels samples from unwanted conditions while automatically vanishing when the sample is already irrelevant to that condition.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free modification to the reverse sampling loop of a pre-trained diffusion or flow-matching model. At each denoising step it consumes the standard conditional and unconditional noise predictions ε_θ(x_t, c⁺), ε_θ(x_t, c⁻), ε_θ(x_t, ∅) and produces a single modified noise estimate that is fed into the usual DDIM or flow-matching update. Both positive and negative prompts are optional; when only one is provided the other's weighting coefficient is set to zero. The output is a single generated image at the model's native resolution (e.g., 512×512 for SD1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL), with no additional network forward passes beyond the standard CFG-style dual evaluation.

## Method

CCFG reformulates conditional guidance as a single-step gradient descent on a Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) loss over the denoising direction ε. For positive guidance, the conditioned noise prediction ε̂_c is treated as the 'data' distribution and the unconditional ε̂_∅ as the 'noise' distribution; minimizing the NCE loss at ε = ε̂_∅ yields a guidance term ε̂_∅ + (2ω_t / (1 + e^{−τ‖ε̂_∅ − ε̂_c‖²}))(ε̂_c − ε̂_∅), which approximates standard CFG when the condition is strongly relevant. For negative guidance the roles are swapped: the unconditional is 'data' and the conditioned is 'noise', producing a repelling force weighted by 2ω_t·e^{−τ‖ε̂_∅ − ε̂_c‖²} / (1 + e^{−τ‖ε̂_∅ − ε̂_c‖²}), which saturates to zero as the sample moves away from the unwanted concept—directly resolving the unbounded-divergence failure mode of NP. The temperature τ_t (a constant in practice) controls the transition width of the sigmoid gate, and the overall strength ω_t plays the role of the guidance scale. The final sampling step combines both positive and negative weighted terms additively into a single modified noise estimate, requiring only the norm of the difference between existing noise predictions and no extra model evaluations.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with fine-grained class pairs, CCFG achieves 91.0% positive accuracy, 99.3% negation ratio, and 90.4% overall accuracy, outperforming all baselines (best competitor: Perp-Neg at 85.1% overall). CCFG also attains the best aesthetic score (4.254) and lowest FD-DINOv2 (190.71). On the PIE-Bench-Neg text-to-image benchmark (SD1.5), CCFG scores 0.854 VLM positive-prompt alignment, 0.089 VLM negative-prompt alignment (lower is better), and 0.778 overall VLM accuracy, with the highest aesthetic score (5.621). On GenEval and DPGBench (positive prompts only), CCFG matches or slightly exceeds standard CFG (e.g., 0.432 vs. 0.407 GenEval overall on SD1.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCFG decisively outperforms NP (negative prompting) on ImageNet-1k: NP's inverted probability weighting collapses positive accuracy to 49.6% (near random) and inflates FD-DINOv2 to 456.78, while CCFG maintains 91.0% positive accuracy with the best sample quality. Against DNG (dynamic negative guidance), CCFG achieves a higher negation ratio (99.3% vs. 98.9%) and substantially better overall accuracy (90.4% vs. 82.1%), as DNG's complementary-condition formulation under-repels when classes overlap. Perp-Neg attains the highest negation ratio (99.6%) but sacrifices positive accuracy (85.4% vs. CCFG's 91.0%), while SLD lags on both alignment and quality metrics. On the PIE-Bench-Neg T2I benchmark (SD1.5), CCFG achieves the best overall VLM score (0.778) and aesthetic score (5.621), edging out SLD (0.770, 5.605) and Perp-Neg (0.766, 5.602), while NP degrades both positive alignment (0.798 VLM) and aesthetic quality (5.531). For positive-prompt-only generation (GenEval/DPGBench), CCFG matches or marginally exceeds CFG and CFG++ (e.g., 0.565 vs. 0.555 GenEval overall on SDXL), confirming that the contrastive weighting does not degrade standard guidance behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates both positive and negative classifier-free guidance as a contrastive optimization problem, drawing on Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) to derive a bounded, self-attenuating guidance term that replaces the unbounded negation used in standard Negative Prompting (NP). It builds directly on the guided-sampling reinterpretation of CFG from CFG++ (Chung et al., 2024a), which showed that CFG can be viewed as gradient descent on a denoising-direction loss, and extends this framework to the negative-prompt setting where prior methods (NP, DNG, Perp-Neg, SLD) rely on ad-hoc heuristics or complementary-condition approximations. The key conceptual departure is that NCE's logistic structure naturally produces a sigmoid-weighted guidance coefficient that vanishes when the sample is far from the unwanted condition, eliminating the distributional distortion that plagues the inverted-probability formulation of NP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Negative prompting is a near-universal feature in production text-to-image pipelines, yet its theoretical foundation (sampling from an unbounded inverted posterior) is known to degrade sample quality; CCFG offers a principled, training-free drop-in replacement with minimal computational overhead that works across DDPM, DiT, and flow-matching architectures. The introduction of PIE-Bench-Neg, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate simultaneous positive alignment and negative removal with overlapping (non-conflicting) prompts, fills a gap in evaluation methodology. A stated limitation is the absence of an analytic closed-form sampling distribution for CCFG, leaving open whether a probabilistic interpretation can be recovered while preserving the bounded-guidance advantage. The method's reliance on a single hyperparameter τ (temperature) for controlling the guidance attenuation range is practical but lacks a principled auto-tuning mechanism.
