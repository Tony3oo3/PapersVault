## Problem

Finetuning VLA models with reinforcement learning is constrained by the high cost and safety risks of real-world interaction, while existing world model-based approaches for VLAs suffer from three fundamental challenges: pixel-level world modeling from limited offline data, maintaining multi-view consistency for fine-grained manipulation, and compounding model errors that are severely amplified under sparse reward signals. No prior method has provided a practical, unified framework that addresses all three simultaneously for VLA finetuning.

## Input / Output

The world model consumes multi-view RGB observations (head and wrist cameras), a language instruction, and an action chunk of k steps where continuous joint positions and gripper states are discretized into integer tokens in [0,256] and mapped to the UMM vocabulary. It outputs the next chunk-level pixel observations for both camera views (generated via interleaved decoding: head view first, then wrist view conditioned on the predicted head view) and a binary chunk-level reward. The policy outputs action chunks via flow matching (3 denoising steps), and the full system operates at chunk granularity rather than individual timesteps, reducing the effective rollout horizon by a factor of 1/k.

## Method

VLA-MBPO integrates three key components into a practical offline model-based RL pipeline. First, a pretrained unified multimodal model (Bagel) serves as the world model backbone, enabling joint prediction of future pixel observations and binary rewards within a single model without expensive video rollouts, with a frame-skipping scheme that directly predicts the k-step-ahead observation. Second, interleaved view decoding enforces cross-view consistency by predicting the head view from both current views plus actions, then predicting the wrist view conditioned on the current wrist view and the predicted head view, implemented via a structured causal attention mask. Third, chunk-level branched rollout mitigates compounding errors by initiating rollouts from random states in the offline dataset with a short horizon of n chunks (typically n=2), rather than full-horizon rollouts from the initial state. Policy optimization uses Flow-Noise (a PPO variant for flow-matching policies) with chunk-level GAE, and the theoretical analysis shows the model-error dependency drops from O(kγ^k/((1−γ)(1−γ^k))) to O(n/(1−γ)), enabling a single set of hyperparameters across all tasks without conservative regularization.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (4 suites, 10 tasks each), VLA-MBPO achieves an average success rate of 85.9%, improving over the SFT-initialized π₀.₅ policy by +9.1 points. The largest gain appears on LIBERO-Long (+12.2 points, 66.8% vs. 54.6%), the most challenging long-horizon suite. In the world model evaluation (Table 1), UMM-World outperforms the video-generation baseline Ctrl-World on all pixel-fidelity metrics (e.g., wrist-view LPIPS 0.254 vs. 0.435) while running 2× faster (10s vs. 21s inference), and matches the specialist VLM Qwen3-VL-8B on reward prediction (98.4% ACC, 0.861 F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLA-MBPO outperforms all four policy baselines on every LIBERO suite. Against the online RL baseline π_RL (Chen et al., 2025a), which uses an equivalent real-world interaction budget, VLA-MBPO gains +3.3 points on average (85.9 vs. 82.6), with the gap widening to +5.6 on LIBERO-Long. Against the offline model-free RL method IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023), the improvement is +8.4 points on average, confirming that model-based imagination provides a substantial advantage over purely offline policy optimization. The BC-on-world-model baseline (BC WM) actually underperforms the SFT policy on LIBERO-Long (48.6 vs. 54.6), suggesting that naive behavior cloning on generated trajectories is insufficient without RL. For world model quality, UMM-World surpasses the video-generation model Ctrl-World (Guo et al., 2025) on all pixel-fidelity metrics (e.g., head-view LPIPS 0.094 vs. 0.150; wrist-view SSIM 0.751 vs. 0.680) and is 2× faster in inference, while matching the specialist VLM Qwen3-VL-8B (Bai et al., 2025) on reward prediction accuracy (98.4% vs. 97.0% ACC). Ablations confirm that removing interleaved view decoding degrades wrist-view SSIM from 0.751 to 0.559, and removing pretraining drops reward F1 from 0.861 to 0.496.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MBPO paradigm of short branched rollouts (Janner et al., 2019) from low-dimensional state spaces to pixel-level VLA settings, and adapts unified multimodal models (Bagel) as a single backbone that jointly predicts future observations and rewards—replacing the two-model (video generator + VLM) architecture used by prior VLA world-model work (WMPO, World-Env). It builds on Flow-Noise (Chen et al., 2025a) for PPO-style policy optimization with flow-matching policies, and on Park et al. (2025) for action-chunked dynamics, but departs by operating in pixel space with multi-view inputs and by introducing interleaved view decoding to enforce cross-view consistency. The theoretical analysis generalizes standard value-gap bounds to the chunk-level, branched-rollout setting, showing how the model-error term shifts from quadratic-in-horizon to linear-in-branch-length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLA-MBPO offers a practical, single-hyperparameter-set recipe for post-training VLA models with limited real-world interaction, which is critical for safe and scalable robotic deployment. The finding that a finetuned UMM achieves sufficient accuracy to eliminate the need for conservative regularization (unlike MOPO/MOBILE) simplifies the pipeline considerably. Key limitations remain: the world model still requires task-specific finetuning (no zero-shot generalization), sample generation is computationally expensive (~7-8 GPU-hours on 8×H100), and partial observability or large physical motions still cause generation failures. An open direction is pretraining the UMM on action-labeled robotic data to enable one-shot or zero-shot world model transfer across tasks.
