## Problem

Normalized Laplacian operators are central to spectral graph theory due to their robustness to degree heterogeneity, yet no dynamic embedding method based on them has provable cross-sectional and longitudinal stability guarantees. Naive unfolding of per-snapshot normalized Laplacians induces spurious temporal drift because degree normalization varies across time even when a node's connectivity behavior is unchanged, and existing alternatives (perturbation, dilation, supra-Laplacian constructions) fail to enforce node-level longitudinal stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T undirected graph snapshots as symmetric binary adjacency matrices on a common vertex set of n nodes, with no node arrivals or departures. It produces a shared time-invariant anchor embedding X ∈ R^{n×d} and T intrinsically aligned dynamic embeddings Y^(t) ∈ R^{n×d} via a single truncated SVD of an n × nT unfolded matrix, so that cross-snapshot comparisons require no post-hoc Procrustes alignment. The embedding dimension d is set to K−1 for ULSE-n1 or K for ULSE-n2 under the DSBM, or selected via an eigengap heuristic on real data.

## Method

ULSE horizontally concatenates normalized Laplacian-type operators across all T snapshots and computes a rank-d truncated SVD, yielding jointly aligned anchor and dynamic embeddings. Two variants address distinct sources of instability: ULSE-n1 uses the standard per-snapshot normalized Laplacian L = I − D^{(t)−1/2} A^{(t)} D^{(t)−1/2} but subtracts a node-specific shift UΣ^{−1/2} from each dynamic embedding to remove the identity-component drift that breaks cross-sectional stability; ULSE-n2 replaces the left normalization with a common time-aggregated degree matrix D^{(1:T)−1/2}, fixing node-wise scaling across snapshots and thereby guaranteeing longitudinal stability without requiring snapshot-specific degree equality. Under a dynamic stochastic block model, the paper proves that both variants converge to well-defined population limits at rate O((ρn)^{−1/2}) and satisfy the desired stability properties. Additionally, a dynamic Cheeger-type inequality links the spectrum of the unfolded normalized Laplacian to worst-case k-way conductance over time, connecting dynamic cuts, spectral values, and node embeddings within a single framework.

## Results

On the balanced Synthetic 1 dataset, ULSE-n1 achieves perfect clustering (ACC = 1.000, NMI = 1.000, ARI = 1.000, F1 = 1.000), while ULSE-n2 reaches 0.997 ACC. Under degree heterogeneity (Synthetic 2), ULSE-n2 attains 0.983 ACC and 0.966 F1, substantially outperforming UASE (0.772 ACC) and all deep baselines. On real-world datasets, ULSE-n1 leads on Brain (0.376 ACC vs. 0.324 for UASE) and Stock (0.453 ACC vs. 0.389 for UASE), while ULSE-n2 achieves near-perfect clustering on School (0.997 ACC). ULSE variants consistently outperform supra-Laplacian methods (TempCut-N/S) by large margins across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULSE-n1 and ULSE-n2 outperform UASE (the prior state-of-the-art with stability guarantees) on all real-world datasets: Brain (0.376 vs. 0.324 ACC), Stock (0.453 vs. 0.389 ACC), and Synthetic 2 (0.980 vs. 0.772 ACC), with the largest gap under degree heterogeneity where Laplacian normalization is most beneficial. Both ULSE variants dramatically surpass the supra-Laplacian baselines TempCut-N and TempCut-S on every dataset (e.g., 0.376 vs. 0.129 ACC on Brain; 0.980 vs. 0.563 on Synthetic 2), confirming that naive temporal Laplacian constructions fail to preserve node-level stability. Against deep temporal-graph methods (JODIE, DyRep, TGN, DyGFormer), ULSE achieves higher clustering accuracy on Brain, Stock, and both synthetic datasets, while on School the gap is small (ULSE-n2 0.997 vs. Node2Vec 0.978). The key differentiator is that ULSE is the only method combining provable cross-sectional and longitudinal stability with strong empirical performance, particularly in the degree-heterogeneous regime where adjacency-based spectral methods suppress low-degree node information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unfolded spectral embedding paradigm of Gallagher et al. (2021) (UASE) from adjacency matrices to normalized Laplacian operators, filling a gap where stability guarantees had remained out of reach. It departs from supra-Laplacian constructions (Silva et al., 2018; Froyland et al., 2024) and context-aware perturbation methods (Zhao et al., 2021), which the paper shows fail to enforce node-level longitudinal stability, and from dilation-based approaches (Davis et al., 2023), whose permutation argument is shown to be invalid. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that per-snapshot degree normalization introduces spurious temporal drift, and resolving it through either an explicit correction term (ULSE-n1) or a time-aggregated left normalization (ULSE-n2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable cross-sectional and longitudinal stability guarantees for normalized-Laplacian-based dynamic embeddings, which are central in spectral graph theory due to their robustness to degree heterogeneity. The dynamic Cheeger-type inequality offers a new structural link between unfolded Laplacian spectra and worst-case conductance over time. Limitations include restriction to undirected graphs with fixed node sets and fixed T; extensions to directed/weighted graphs, node arrivals/departures, and degree-corrected SBMs are left as future work. The practical impact is strongest in degree-heterogeneous regimes where adjacency-based spectral methods degrade substantially.
