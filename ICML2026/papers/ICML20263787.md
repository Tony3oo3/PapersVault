## Problem

Muon has become a leading optimizer for LLM pretraining, yet most publicly available models are pretrained with Adam, and naively switching optimizers for fine-tuning causes a performance degradation known as optimizer mismatch. This mismatch is poorly understood and severely limits Muon's practical utility for fine-tuning, which remains the dominant training paradigm for adapting pretrained models to downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263787/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pretrained model weights (from either Adam or Muon pretraining) and a fine-tuning dataset, producing fine-tuned weights evaluated on downstream benchmarks. Experiments span three model families—T5-Base (NLU), Llama 2-7B/13B (NLG), and CLIP ViT-B/32 (image classification)—with LoRA applied at rank r=8, α=16 to all linear layers except embeddings and the LM head. Muon is applied only to 2D weight matrices while 1D parameters (biases, layer-norm weights) and embeddings are always optimized with Adam, following standard Muon practice.

## Method

The paper first proves that Adam (via SignGD proxy) and Muon (via exact orthogonalization) converge to fundamentally different solutions on an underdetermined linear regression problem: Adam minimizes max-norm while Muon minimizes spectral norm, establishing distinct implicit biases that produce structurally different pretrained weights (confirmed by higher stable rank in Muon-trained models). The key insight is that mismatch makes the model more sensitive to update strength during fine-tuning, actively disrupting pretrained knowledge rather than merely learning less. To mitigate this, the paper shows that LoRA constrains updates through two mechanisms—freezing pretrained weights exactly and limiting updates to a low-rank subspace—and provides a theoretical bound showing the worst-case mismatch inflation scales with rank r (vanishing at r=1, recovering full fine-tuning at r=n). Extensive experiments across language and vision benchmarks confirm that LoRA-Muon matches or outperforms LoRA-Adam, with the benefit diminishing at higher ranks as updates increasingly resemble full fine-tuning.

## Results

On GLUE (T5-Base), LoRA-Muon-PE achieves the best average accuracy of 89.20%, surpassing Full-Adam (89.14%) and LoRA-Adam (88.93%), while Full-Muon lags behind Full-Adam (88.77% vs. 89.14%), confirming the optimizer mismatch under full fine-tuning. On Llama 2-7B NLG tasks, the full fine-tuning gap is larger (Full-Muon 53.10 vs. Full-Adam 54.92 average), but under LoRA the gap reverses: LoRA-Muon (52.15) outperforms LoRA-Adam (51.62). On CLIP ViT-B/32 image classification, LoRA-Muon-PE (84.71%) and LoRA-Muon (84.48%) both outperform LoRA-Adam (84.17%), while the full fine-tuning gap is small (86.05% vs. 86.55%). A random-effects meta-analysis across all tasks confirms the gap reduction is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under full fine-tuning, Muon consistently underperforms Adam across all three task families: on GLUE the gap is 0.37% (88.77 vs. 89.14), on Llama 2-7B NLG it widens to 1.82% (53.10 vs. 54.92, driven by a 4.29-point drop on GSM8K math), and on CLIP image classification it is 0.50% (86.05 vs. 86.55). Under LoRA, this pattern reverses: LoRA-Muon matches or beats LoRA-Adam on every benchmark (GLUE 88.97 vs. 88.93; NLG 52.15 vs. 51.62; CLIP 84.48 vs. 84.17), and LoRA-Muon-PE achieves the best overall score on GLUE (89.20%), exceeding even Full-Adam. Against LoRA variants (Table 6), LoRA-Muon-PE (89.20%) outperforms rsLoRA-Adam (89.11%), LoRA-One-Adam (89.16%), PiSSA-Adam (88.95%), DoRA-Adam (89.01%), and LoRA-Pro-Adam (89.11%), while the same variants applied to Muon (rsLoRA-Muon-PE 89.12%, LoRA-One-Muon-PE 89.09%, PiSSA-Muon-PE 89.12%) do not improve over plain LoRA-Muon-PE, suggesting these Adam-tuned variants do not transfer to Muon. On catastrophic forgetting (Table 5), Full-Muon forgets more than Full-Adam (55.4 vs. 56.8 average commonsense accuracy after math fine-tuning), but LoRA-Muon (56.9) recovers closer to the pretrained baseline (63.5) than Full-Muon, confirming that the mismatch actively disrupts pretrained knowledge rather than merely slowing learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: the Muon optimizer line (Jordan et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2025; Amsel et al. 2026) that has demonstrated ~2× compute efficiency over Adam for pretraining, and the LoRA family (Hu et al. 2022) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Liu et al. (2025) first identified the optimizer mismatch problem but left it unexplained; this paper provides the first systematic analysis, connecting it to the distinct implicit biases of Adam (max-norm) and Muon (spectral norm) as characterized by Bernstein & Newhouse (2024), Zhang et al. (2024), and Fan et al. (2025). The key departure is reframing LoRA's low-rank constraint not merely as a parameter-efficiency mechanism but as an update-strength limiter that specifically mitigates optimizer mismatch, and showing that existing LoRA variants (rsLoRA, PiSSA, LoRA-One) designed to make updates closer to full fine-tuning actually amplify the mismatch under Muon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since most publicly available pretrained models use Adam, the optimizer mismatch problem has been a significant barrier to adopting Muon's efficiency gains for fine-tuning. This paper shows that LoRA-Muon serves as a practical drop-in replacement for LoRA-Adam while saving 50% of optimizer-state memory, with the mismatch gap reduced by 39–78% depending on the pretraining optimizer. Open questions remain: a theoretical characterization of mismatch in deep (non-linear) networks, understanding what task/model factors determine mismatch severity, whether other update-constraining techniques (regularization, EWC-style methods) also help, and the need for Muon-specific LoRA variants. Experiments are limited to 7B (13B preliminary) for Adam-pretrained models and 561M for Muon-pretrained models, leaving open whether the findings hold at frontier scale.
