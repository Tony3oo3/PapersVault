## Problem

Existing work on reasoning trace evaluation lacks a unified framework that jointly addresses what constitutes high-quality reasoning, how to reliably evaluate long implicitly structured traces, and how to leverage such signals for optimization. Process reward models rely on absolute step-level scoring that cannot capture complex non-linear structures, while outcome reward models target final response quality rather than internal reasoning. This paper tackles all three gaps simultaneously by decoupling reasoning quality from answer correctness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265832/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw free-form reasoning traces (up to 8192 tokens) generated by various LLMs. Traces are first partitioned into atomic steps using model-specific prefix keywords, then structured as DAGs via LLM-guided parent selection from a simplified attachment pool (main branch + branch endpoints). The TRM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with a scalar value head) outputs a single unbounded reward score per trace. For pairwise evaluation, the pipeline produces four dimension-wise judgments (macro/micro × efficiency/effectiveness) with natural-language rationales, aggregated into a single preference label, evaluated under both forward and reversed orderings to mitigate position bias.

## Method

The paper introduces the ME² principle, which characterizes reasoning quality along two orthogonal axes: granularity (macro-level structural organization vs. micro-level step validity) and objective (efficiency vs. effectiveness). Reasoning traces are abstracted as DAGs by incrementally selecting parent nodes for each step from a simplified attachment pool, then compressing linear chains into super-nodes; macro-level evaluation uses a structural narrative over the DAG while micro-level evaluation focuses on the dominant path from root to final answer. The Thinking Reward Model (TRM) is trained with Bradley-Terry loss on 103K pairwise preference pairs constructed exclusively from verified-correct traces, making the reward orthogonal to answer-based verifiable signals. In RL training, the thinking reward is integrated via gated reward shaping r = r_v · (1 − α + α·Sigmoid(r_t)) within GRPO, with theoretical guarantees of optimal policy invariance and a lower bound on policy improvement driven by TRM-induced reward variance among correct solutions.

## Results

On the main RL training results (Table 2), TRM consistently outperforms both the Verifier-only baseline and ReasonFlux-PRM-7B across three policy models (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and 10 benchmarks. The largest gains over the Verifier baseline are 3.9% on STEM (Llama-3.1-8B: 31.8% vs. 27.9%) and 3.8% on Math (Llama-3.1-8B: 24.2% vs. 20.4%). At test time, Best-of-N selection with TRM yields up to 19.3% accuracy gain on AIME24 (44.7% → 64.0% with Qwen3-8B at N=16), surpassing both PRM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRM is compared against two baselines: (1) Verifier, which uses only the binary verifiable reward r_v, and (2) ReasonFlux-PRM-7B, a step-level process reward model. TRM wins on all three policy models across both STEM and Math average scores. Against ReasonFlux-PRM-7B, TRM gains +1.0% STEM and +1.5% Math on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (31.1% vs. 30.1%; 46.0% vs. 44.5%), and +2.0% STEM and +1.0% Math on Llama-3.1-8B (31.8% vs. 29.8%; 24.2% vs. 23.2%). Against the Verifier baseline, the gap is larger: up to +3.9% STEM and +3.8% Math on Llama-3.1-8B. On the pairwise preference validation set (Table 1), TRM achieves 88.6% accuracy versus 62.5% for ReasonFlux-PRM-7B and 46.3% for Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B. In test-time Best-of-N scaling (Fig. 4), TRM also outperforms both PRM baselines, reaching 64.0% on AIME24 (Qwen3-8B, N=16) where ReasonFlux-PRM-7B plateaus at a lower accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously fragmented research threads: process reward models (PRMs) that score individual reasoning steps with absolute labels (Lightman et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2025), outcome reward models (ORMs) that use pairwise preferences but target final-response alignment rather than internal reasoning (Stiennon et al., 2020), and structural modeling of reasoning as trees or graphs (Jiang et al., 2025; Minegishi et al., 2025). The key departure is combining the pairwise-preference supervision paradigm of ORMs with DAG-based structural abstraction of reasoning traces, while explicitly decoupling reasoning quality from answer correctness by training only on verified-correct traces. For optimization, it integrates the resulting Thinking Reward Model into GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as an auxiliary signal alongside binary verifiable rewards in the RLVR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified, principled characterization of reasoning trace quality (ME² principle) that jointly accounts for global structure and local step validity, filling a gap where prior criteria were fragmented across step correctness, coherence, and verbosity. The practical impact is demonstrated through a reusable dataset (TRM-Preference, 103K pairs) and a lightweight 8B reward model that improves both test-time selection (up to 19.3%) and RL training (up to 3.9%) across diverse benchmarks. Open questions include whether the LLM-based DAG construction generalizes to much longer or more adversarially structured traces, whether the ME² dimensions are truly exhaustive, and whether the modest RL gains (2–4%) scale further with larger models or longer training horizons.
