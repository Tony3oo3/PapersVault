## Problem

In sparse-reward offline MARL, reward shaping is brittle: dense intrinsic rewards can shift the optimal joint policy, and world-model rollouts can amplify model bias into 'guidance under hallucinated dynamics.' The missing piece is a principled mechanism to decide *when* shaping is beneficial (i.e., only after statistically validated learning plateaus) and *how much* to trust it (i.e., only in low-uncertainty model regions), while preserving the original task optimum.

## Input / Output

SOLAR consumes a fixed offline dataset of ~1M transitions per map (joint states, per-agent vector observations, joint actions, shared scalar rewards, terminal flags) collected by an unknown behavior policy, stratified into Good/Medium/Poor quality tiers. It outputs decentralized cooperative policies under CTDE: each agent maps its local observation to an action at test time, while a centralized critic Q(s, a) is used only during training. Shaped rewards are applied exclusively to simulated rollouts from the learned world model; real offline transitions always retain their original extrinsic reward.

## Method

SOLAR implements a Simulate-Evaluate-Shape loop built on a Transformer multi-agent world model with cross-agent attention that supports low-cost rollouts and ensemble-based uncertainty estimation. At periodic checkpoints, a paired-bootstrap hypothesis test determines whether the current policy has entered a statistically supported plateau; only then is potential-based shaping (r + γΦ(s′) − Φ(s)) activated, preserving the set of optimal policies. Shaping strength is further modulated by an uncertainty-aware throttle λ = exp(−κu) that suppresses guidance in high-uncertainty model regions, and a meta-learner (Augmented Random Search) adaptively reweights three cooperative feature families (coordination, proximity, exploration) at each plateau event. The critic is trained with a CQL-style conservative objective on the union of real and simulated transitions, and the overall two-timescale dynamics (fast policy/critic updates, slow shaping-weight updates gated by plateau detection) is shown to be stable under standard stochastic-approximation conditions.

## Results

On SMAC benchmarks across 6 maps and 3 data quality tiers (Good/Medium/Poor), SOLAR achieves the highest median win rate in all 18 settings. The advantage is most dramatic on Poor-quality data: on 3m (Poor), SOLAR reaches 87.71% vs. 51.2% for the next-best method (MADIFF), a 36.5-point gap; on 5m_vs_6m (Poor), SOLAR attains 58.17% vs. 44.2% (MADIFF). Even on Good data, SOLAR sets new ceilings (e.g., 99.86% on 3m, 99.24% on 8m), surpassing the strongest offline baseline MAICQ (98.1% on 8m) and the model-based MARIE (89.4% on 8m). Ablations confirm the ARS module contributes >11 points and the world model adds ~8 points over model-free training alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOLAR outperforms all 10 baselines across every map and data-quality combination. Against the strongest offline value-based methods (MAICQ, OMAR, MACQL), SOLAR leads by 4–14 points on Good data and by 14–36 points on Poor data (e.g., 87.71% vs. 45.8% on 3m Poor). The sequence-modeling baseline MADT and diffusion-based MADIFF are competitive on Good data (within 3–5 points) but degrade sharply on Poor data, where SOLAR's plateau-triggered shaping provides a 14–36 point advantage. Online methods QMIX and MAPPO, forced into the offline setting, perform poorly on heterogeneous maps (near 0% on 3s5z_vs_3s6z) and are outperformed by SOLAR by 50+ points in those settings. The model-based MARIE baseline is the closest competitor on Good data (within 1–4 points on several maps) but still trails SOLAR by 5–14 points on Medium and Poor data, confirming that SOLAR's adaptive shaping mechanism adds value beyond model-based rollouts alone. In the OOD value-landscape analysis (Sec. 4.4), SOLAR's CQL backbone assigns significantly lower Q-values to out-of-distribution actions compared to a standard IQL baseline, validating its extrapolation-error suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOLAR sits at the intersection of three established threads: conservative offline value learning (CQL, Kumar et al. 2020), potential-based reward shaping with policy-invariance guarantees (Ng et al. 1999), and learned world models for model-based RL (Hafner et al. 2024). Its key departure is repurposing the world model not merely as a data generator but as a low-cost validator of learning progress, and coupling this with a statistical plateau gate that activates shaping only upon detected stagnation. Prior offline MARL methods (MABCQ, MAICQ, OMAR, MADT, MADIFF) address OOD overestimation or sequence modeling but treat reward sparsity as a fixed constraint; SOLAR instead introduces a dynamic, event-triggered intervention that densifies the learning signal adaptively while preserving the task optimum via the potential-based form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plateau-triggered, uncertainty-throttled shaping mechanism offers a principled answer to the 'always-on' failure mode of dense intrinsic rewards in offline settings, and the two-timescale stability analysis provides theoretical grounding for the coupled fast/slow update dynamics. However, all experiments are confined to SMAC/SMACv2 (StarCraft II), and the 2c_vs_64zg results (≤34% win rate even on Good data) suggest the approach struggles at very large scale. Open questions include whether the statistical plateau gate and ensemble-based uncertainty calibration transfer to continuous-control or communication-based MARL domains, and whether the ARS meta-learner's sample efficiency holds when the validation objective is noisier than a binary win indicator.
