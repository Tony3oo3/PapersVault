## Problem

Multimodal agents still suffer from inefficient tool use and inflexible tool orchestration in open-ended settings, and frozen post-trained backbones lack an effective mechanism for continual improvement on unseen tasks. Existing learning-from-experience approaches rely predominantly on textual trajectory logs for knowledge extraction and retrieval, which is fundamentally inadequate for multimodal settings where critical decision signals are grounded in visual observations. No prior framework unifies visually-grounded task-level skills and action-level experiences in a single training-free architecture for general multimodal agentic reasoning.

## Input / Output

The system takes a task instance (natural language query plus a set of relevant images) and an accumulated knowledge base consisting of a structured Markdown skill document (with metadata, workflow sequences, and reusable code templates) and a JSON experience bank (condition-action pairs each ≤64 words, indexed by semantic embeddings). It produces a final task answer along with updated knowledge artifacts. A key design choice is the separation into two MLLM instances — MLLM_exec for tool-use inference and MLLM_kb for all knowledge-base operations — which enables cross-model knowledge transfer where knowledge accumulated by one backbone (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash) can be directly consumed by another (e.g., GPT-5-mini) without re-accumulation.

## Method

XSKILL is a dual-stream, training-free framework that maintains two complementary knowledge types: skills (task-level structured workflows stored as Markdown) and experiences (action-level condition-action tips stored as JSON with embeddings). During accumulation (Phase I), the agent performs N multi-path rollouts per training task, then MLLM_kb performs visually-grounded rollout summarization (analyzing interleaved image observations jointly with trajectory text) and cross-rollout critique (contrastive analysis between successful and failed trajectories) to extract skill fragments and experience updates, followed by hierarchical consolidation that merges semantically similar entries (cosine similarity > 0.70) and prunes low-quality items. During inference (Phase II), the system decomposes the test task into 2–3 abstract subtasks, retrieves top-k experiences per subtask via embedding similarity, rewrites each experience to the current visual context, adapts the global skill document by pruning irrelevant sections and integrating rewritten experiences, and injects the result as a non-prescriptive reference into the agent's system prompt. The usage history from inference is fed back into accumulation, forming a continual learning loop that progressively refines the knowledge base without any parameter updates.

## Results

Across four backbone models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Flash, GPT-5-mini, o4-mini) and four benchmarks, XSKILL achieves average gains of 2.58–6.71 points in Average@4 over the tool-only baseline. The largest single-benchmark improvement is on TIR-Bench with Gemini-3-Flash, where XSKILL reaches 47.75% Average@4 versus 36.62% for the strongest baseline (Agent-KB), a gap of 11.13 points. Overall Average@4 scores range from 23.19 (GPT-5-mini) to 40.34 (Gemini-3-Flash), consistently outperforming all learning-based baselines across every model–benchmark combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XSKILL outperforms all three learning-based baselines (AWM, Dynamic CheatSheet, Agent-KB) across every model–benchmark combination in Average@4. Against Agent-KB (the strongest baseline), XSKILL leads by 3.64 points overall with Gemini-2.5-Pro (28.63 vs. 24.99), 5.46 points with Gemini-3-Flash (40.34 vs. 34.88), 2.32 points with GPT-5-mini (23.19 vs. 20.87), and 3.17 points with o4-mini (23.72 vs. 20.55). The margin is most pronounced on TIR-Bench, where XSKILL surpasses Agent-KB by 11.13 points (47.75 vs. 36.62) with Gemini-3-Flash, indicating the dual-stream design is particularly effective for complex visual reasoning requiring multi-step tool composition. Against AWM and DC, the gaps are smaller but consistent (typically 2–6 points), with XSKILL also leading on Pass@4 in nearly all settings, suggesting improved upper-bound capability rather than merely more reliable single-rollout performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on non-parametric experience learning for agents (AWM, Dynamic CheatSheet, Agent-KB, EvolveR, ReasoningBank) by transplanting it into the multimodal agentic setting and introducing two key departures: (1) grounding both knowledge extraction and retrieval in visual observations rather than relying solely on textual trajectory logs, and (2) unifying two complementary knowledge streams—structured task-level skills and concise action-level experiences—into a single dual-stream architecture. Prior multimodal memory attempts (EchoTrail-GUI, Transductive Visual Programming) are confined to specialized tasks like GUI navigation or spatial reasoning, whereas XSKILL targets general multimodal tool-use reasoning. The POMDP formulation and the separation of MLLM_exec from MLLM_kb for cross-model knowledge transfer are architectural choices not present in the cited baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XSKILL provides a practical, training-free mechanism for multimodal agents to accumulate and reuse knowledge across tasks, which is important as toolsets and task distributions evolve faster than retraining cycles. The demonstrated cross-model transferability (GPT-5-mini and o4-mini benefiting from Gemini-3-Flash's accumulated knowledge) suggests that externalized knowledge bases could serve as a lightweight alternative to fine-tuning for adapting agents to new domains. However, the evaluation covers only a single accumulation-then-test cycle rather than truly iterative refinement, and the open-source model results reveal that externally accumulated knowledge can degrade Average@4 on weaker backbones, raising open questions about when and how knowledge transfer is beneficial versus harmful. The paper also flags bias propagation risks in cross-model knowledge sharing that remain unaddressed.
