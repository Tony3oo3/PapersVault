## Problem

Semantic editing with pretrained text-to-image models remains challenging because existing inversion-based pipelines either produce new sampling paths independent of the source image (losing structural fidelity) or rely on heuristic attention manipulations that distort fine details. The paper asks whether a principled, training-free coupling of the stochastic sampling dynamics can simultaneously achieve high prompt adherence and near-pixel-level consistency with the source image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265894/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB source image, a source prompt describing it (generated via BLIP and manually refined), a target prompt specifying the desired edit, and a pretrained text-to-image model (all experiments use Flux.1[dev], a rectified flow model). It outputs a single edited image at the same resolution. The method is architecture-agnostic in principle—any model samplable via an SDE (diffusion or rectified flow) can be used—and requires no retraining, test-time optimization, or auxiliary networks. A tunable starting time t₀ (default 1/7 of the sampling schedule) controls the trade-off between edit strength and source preservation.

## Method

sync-SDE couples the reverse-time SDEs of the source and target processes by reusing the same backward Brownian motion path, constructed via Anderson's (1982) time-reversal theorem from the forward SDE path that evolves the source image to noise. Concretely, the algorithm (1) samples a forward Brownian path to evolve the source image to a noisy state, (2) computes the structured backward Brownian increments from that path using the source-prompt score, and (3) integrates the target-prompt reverse SDE from the noisy state driven by those same backward increments. The two processes are thus synchronously coupled: they share all stochastic fluctuations and differ only through their prompt-induced drifts. The paper provides an optimal-transport interpretation showing that synchronous coupling (Q_t = I_d) is the greedy minimizer of the local quadratic deviation under bicausal Monge transport, and introduces a resampling-ODE variant that tracks the difference process for improved stability at the cost of fine-grained detail generation.

## Results

The paper evaluates sync-SDE on a custom benchmark of 306 source-target prompt pairs (1024×1024 images) using L1 distance, LPIPS distance, and CLIP score as metrics, with 28 sampling steps on Flux.1[dev]. Results are presented as trade-off scatter plots (Figure 2) rather than a single numerical table; across all three guidance strengths tested ({1.0, 1.5, 2.5}), sync-SDE consistently occupies the upper-left region of the plots, indicating higher CLIP scores (better prompt adherence) at lower L1 and LPIPS distances (better source fidelity) than all baselines. Additional evaluations on the Div2K and PIE-Bench datasets (Figures 20–21) confirm the same dominance. All methods run in comparable time (15–25 s per 1024×1024 image on an H100 GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sync-SDE outperforms all five inversion-based editing baselines—SDEdit, FlowEdit, FireFlow, RF-Inv, and RF-Edit—on the primary 306-pair benchmark, achieving simultaneously higher CLIP scores and lower L1/LPIPS distances, meaning it follows the target prompt more faithfully while altering the source image less. On the Div2K and PIE-Bench datasets, the same pattern holds: sync-SDE dominates the trade-off frontier. Against the instruction-based models Flux Kontext and Qwen Image Edit, sync-SDE also achieves a more favorable CLIP-vs-distance trade-off, and combining sync-SDE with either instruction model further reduces off-target changes while maintaining competitive CLIP scores (Figure 22). Qualitatively, sync-SDE produces more localized edits: in the coffee-saucer task it is the only method preserving saucer texture, in the marble-bust task it alters only the facial expression without distorting material or lighting, and in the glasses task it preserves eye color—whereas competing methods frequently modify unrelated regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Anderson's (1982) time-reversal theorem for SDEs and the SDE equivalence of rectified flow established by Rout et al. (2025a) to propose a fundamentally different editing mechanism: instead of the dominant paradigm of attention sharing, heuristic noise manipulation, or test-time optimization (as in SDEdit, FlowEdit, FireFlow, RF-Edit, NTI), sync-SDE couples two reverse-time SDEs pathwise by reusing the same backward Brownian motion. The paper further provides a novel bicausal optimal transport interpretation, showing that synchronous coupling is the greedy minimizer of a local quadratic cost among all orthonormal matrix process couplings, positioning the method within the broader optimal-control-for-guided-sampling literature while remaining computationally lightweight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

sync-SDE offers a training-free, optimization-free, and largely architecture-agnostic editing tool that works out-of-the-box with any SDE-samplable generative model, lowering the barrier to high-fidelity semantic editing. The bicausal optimal transport framing opens a principled path toward finding the globally optimal coupling (the orthonormal matrix process Q_t), which the authors leave as future work. Key limitations include sensitivity to source prompt accuracy (misspecified prompts degrade results), occasional seed-dependent artifacts, and a tendency to over-preserve structure when the edit requires radical changes. The method's reliance on a good source prompt and its greedy (non-globally-optimal) transport nature suggest room for improvement in more complex editing scenarios.
