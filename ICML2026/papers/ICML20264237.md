## Problem

Gradient-based MPC with learned world models has consistently underperformed gradient-free, sampling-based planners like CEM and MPPI, despite the theoretical efficiency advantage of backpropagating gradients through differentiable models. Prior works attributed this gap to gradient quality issues but did not analyze the root causes or propose effective remedies, leaving gradient-based planning impractical for high-dimensional continuous control.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either proprioceptive state vectors (dimensions ranging from 5 to 223 across tasks) or 64×64 RGB images, which are encoded into a 512-dimensional latent representation. At each time step it outputs a single optimized action (dimension 1–61 depending on the task) by selecting the first action from the best-scoring candidate trajectory out of N=5 candidates optimized over a 3-step horizon. The entire planning procedure requires only 15 world-model evaluations per step, compared to 9,216 for MPPI with 512 candidates and 6 iterations.

## Method

Dream-MPC generates a small set of candidate action sequences by rolling out a stochastic policy prior in latent space, then refines each via a single gradient-ascent step through the learned latent dynamics model. Two key mechanisms overcome the limitations of prior gradient-based MPC: (1) uncertainty regularization, which penalizes trajectories with high epistemic uncertainty estimated from an ensemble of Q-functions (coefficient λ_unc=0.01), and (2) action reuse, which amortizes optimization across time steps by initializing each candidate as a mixture of the previously optimized action and a fresh policy sample (reuse coefficient ρ=0.1). The method is integrated into TD-MPC2/BMPC by replacing the MPPI planner and into Dreamer by replacing the reactive policy at inference, achieving state-of-the-art performance on 24 continuous control tasks while being roughly 600× cheaper in model evaluations than MPPI.

## Results

Across 24 continuous control tasks spanning DeepMind Control Suite, HumanoidBench, and Meta-World, Dream-MPC(BMPC) achieves the best aggregate performance, improving IQM by 26.7% and mean by 20.5% over BMPC alone. On the DeepMind Control Suite, Dream-MPC(BMPC) reaches a mean score of 725±181 vs. 711±181 for BMPC and 657±225 for TD-MPC2; on HumanoidBench it reaches 874±1326 vs. 769±1277 (BMPC) and 761±1617 (TD-MPC2). Inference time is comparable to MPPI-based methods (~18 ms per step on an RTX 4090), while requiring only 15 world-model evaluations per step versus 9,216 for MPPI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dream-MPC(BMPC) outperforms all five baselines (SAC, Dreamer-v3, TD-MPC2, BMPC, and Dream-MPC(TD-MPC2)) on every domain, with the largest gains on HumanoidBench (+13.5% mean over BMPC) and Meta-World (+36% success rate over BMPC). Against the gradient-free MPPI planner, Dream-MPC(BMPC) wins on all three domains, confirming that gradient-based optimization with a strong policy prior can surpass sampling-based methods. Dream-MPC(TD-MPC2), however, consistently underperforms TD-MPC2's MPPI planner (e.g., 433 vs. 657 on DeepMind Control), which the authors attribute to the weaker policy prior in TD-MPC2 providing less informative warm-starts for gradient ascent. On inference time, Dream-MPC(BMPC) is marginally faster than BMPC (18.15 ms vs. 18.77 ms) and TD-MPC2 (20.83 ms), while being orders of magnitude faster than prior gradient-based methods like Grad-MPC (195.75 ms). In the Dreamer integration (Table 3), Dream-MPC(Dreamer) outperforms both Grad-MPC and Policy+Grad-MPC on all four tested tasks, e.g., 836±49 vs. 438±81 on Cheetah Run.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TD-MPC2/BMPC line of model-based RL that uses a learned latent world model with policy-guided MPC, replacing the gradient-free MPPI planner with a gradient-ascent-based optimizer. It departs from prior gradient-based MPC attempts (Bharadhwaj et al., 2020; S V et al., 2023) that consistently underperformed their sampling-based counterparts, by introducing two key mechanisms: epistemic uncertainty regularization in the planning objective and amortization of optimization iterations via action reuse across time steps. The method also integrates into Dreamer, demonstrating compatibility with generative world models beyond the control-centric TD-MPC2 architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~600× reduction in world-model evaluations per time step (15 vs. 9216 for MPPI), which could enable gradient-based MPC on embedded or real-time robotics systems where sampling-based methods are intractable. A key open question is the strong dependence on a high-quality policy prior: without it, gradient-based MPC degrades substantially, limiting applicability in settings where the policy is weak or unavailable. The authors also note that dynamic adaptation of optimization parameters (step size, iterations, horizon) and extension to multi-task world models remain unexplored, as does combining gradient-based MPC with imitation learning during training.
