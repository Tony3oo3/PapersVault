## Problem

Existing Polyak-type step sizes for stochastic subgradient methods in the non-smooth regime either require the interpolation assumption, need oracle knowledge of component-wise optimal losses f_i(x*), or in practice collapse to a constant learning rate because an upper bound γ_b is selected in the majority of iterations. No prior rule provides O(T^{-1/2}) convergence for convex Lipschitz objectives without any of these limitations while remaining genuinely adaptive in deep-network training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-sample loss values f_i(x^t), a stochastic subgradient g_i^t ∈ ∂f_i(x^t), a user-chosen safeguard constant M > 0, and per-sample lower bounds ℓ_i* (e.g., 0 for cross-entropy). It outputs a single scalar step size γ_t = (f_i(x^t) − ℓ_i*) / max{‖g_i^t‖², M} that is applied to the subgradient update. Unlike SPS_max which hard-caps the step size with min{·, γ_b}, SPS_safe never reduces to a constant update regardless of M; the safeguard acts only on the denominator, so the step size remains a function of the current loss gap at every iteration.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the denominator ‖g_i^t‖² in the stochastic Polyak step size with max{‖g_i^t‖², M}, preventing the reciprocal 1/‖g_i^t‖² from blowing up when subgradients vanish while keeping the numerator (loss gap) intact. This single safeguard M simultaneously eliminates the need for oracle values f_i(x*) and the step-size ceiling γ_b, yielding O(T^{-1/2}) convergence to a σ²-neighborhood for convex G-Lipschitz objectives without interpolation. The rule is extended to the Iterate Moving Average (IMA) momentum framework as IMA-SPS_safe, for which both Cesàro-average and last-iterate O(T^{-1/2}) guarantees are proven. A key structural insight is that SPS_safe with M = c² is algebraically equivalent to clipped SSM with an adaptive step size, providing the first stochastic convergence guarantee for Polyak-style clipped subgradient descent. Empirically, the step size never collapses to a constant (unlike SPS_max where 98% of steps use the bound), gradient norms remain larger than under smoothed baselines, and test accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-20/32 is competitive with Adam.

## Results

On ResNet-20/CIFAR-10, SPS_safe achieves 90.39% test accuracy, outperforming SPS_max (87.88%) and Smooth SPS_max (89.79%) from Loizou et al. (2021). With the smoothed adaptive safeguard M_t (β=0.9), ResNet-20 reaches 90.97% on CIFAR-10 and 90.93% on CIFAR-100, while ResNet-32 reaches 90.94% and 91.11% respectively. On the synthetic SVM benchmark, SPS_safe consistently outperforms both fixed-step SSM and SPS* (Garrigos et al., 2023) in final error, and IMA-SPS_safe outperforms IMA-SPS (Gower et al., 2025) in the momentum setting. The theoretical guarantee is O(T^{-1/2} + σ²) for convex Lipschitz objectives without interpolation or oracle knowledge of f_i(x*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPS_max (Loizou et al., 2021), SPS_safe improves ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 accuracy from 87.88% to 90.39%, and critically avoids the practical failure mode where 31.8% (or 98.45% with smoothing) of iterations collapse to a constant step. Against SPS* (Garrigos et al., 2023), SPS_safe achieves lower final error on the SVM benchmark (Figure 2c) while eliminating the need for oracle values f_i(x*). Against IMA-SPS (Gower et al., 2025), IMA-SPS_safe is superior on the SVM momentum experiment (Figure 2d) and removes the dependence on f_i(x*). Against Adam, the paper reports that SPS_safe and IMA-SPS_safe exhibit superior generalization performance in both SSM-based and IMA-based settings on CIFAR-10/100 (Figures 3, 5–7), though exact Adam numbers are only available in the figures. The smoothed adaptive safeguard M_t (β=0.9) matches or slightly exceeds the best fixed M across all architectures (e.g., 90.97% vs 90.84% for ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10), suggesting the tuning-free variant is competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stochastic Polyak Step Size (SPS) framework of Loizou et al. (2021) and the IMA-SPS momentum variant of Gower et al. (2025) to the non-smooth convex regime, where prior analyses either required the interpolation assumption or oracle access to component-wise optimal losses f_i(x*). The key departure is a structural change: rather than capping the step size from above (as in SPS_max) or requiring f_i(x*), the proposed SPS_safe caps the denominator from below via max{||g_i^t||², M}, which simultaneously eliminates the need for oracle values and prevents exploding steps when subgradients vanish. The IMA-SPS_safe rule further extends this safeguard to the Iterate Moving Average (equivalently, stochastic heavy ball) update of Sebbouh et al. (2021), providing the first Polyak-type momentum guarantees in the non-smooth setting without strong assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that SPS_max with the standard smoothing trick selects its constant bound in 98.45% of iterations reveals that a widely-used 'adaptive' optimizer is effectively a fixed learning-rate schedule, motivating the need for genuinely adaptive alternatives. The proposed SPS_safe with M=1 as a default achieves competitive accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 while maintaining larger gradient norms (avoiding vanishing-gradient collapse), and the algebraic equivalence to clipped SSM (Proposition 2.1) provides the first stochastic convergence guarantee for Polyak-style clipped subgradient methods. Open questions include extending the convex-Lipschitz theory to non-convex or (L₀,L₁)-smooth objectives, eliminating the σ²-neighborhood term, and evaluating the approach in distributed, federated, and LLM training settings where adaptive step sizes may offer the largest practical gains.
