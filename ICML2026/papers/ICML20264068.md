## Problem

Embodied navigation agents built on VLMs suffer from a severe scarcity of aligned real-world vision and robot control data, while RL-based approaches that attempt to bridge this gap are hampered by Sim2Real transfer failures and extreme sample inefficiency. The core dilemma is that VLMs possess rich high-level reasoning but cannot learn continuous low-level control, whereas RL provides a learning mechanism but cannot bootstrap effectively from scratch in noisy real-world environments.

## Input / Output

During navigation, the agent receives RGB-D observations (1280×1280, 120° HFOV, 1.5 m sensor height) and a natural language query, which are abstracted into two discrete visual buffers: a Memory Buffer of semantic landmark images and a Frontier Buffer of unexplored boundary nodes. The VLM policy outputs a discrete sub-goal selection (a specific frontier or memory node), which a geometric planner converts into a continuous path with a maximum step of 1.0 m; for QA tasks the agent additionally produces a natural language answer. In the sandbox, observations are rendered at 256×256 with three egocentric views per keypoint (0°, ±120°), and the action space is the selection of the forward view among these three.

## Method

SAGE operates in three phases. Genesis synthesizes 14,526 diverse navigation tasks in a physics-grounded sandbox (HM3D + InteriorGS) by computing A* geodesic trajectories, discretizing them into multi-view observation sequences, and using a VLM to generate natural language instructions plus IF-THEN experience rules stored in a vector database. Evolution optimizes the VLM policy via GRPO with two key mechanisms: (1) hybrid prompt-augmented sampling that injects retrieved experience with a validation-dependent annealing schedule (η_t from 0.8 to 0.0), and (2) Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping (AAC) that relaxes the upper clipping bound (ε_exp=1.0) for experience-augmented samples while enforcing a conservative lower bound (ε_std=0.2) universally, enabling aggressive knowledge absorption without policy collapse. Navigation deploys the evolved policy with retrieval-augmented prompting, where the VLM selects sub-goals from frontier/memory buffers and a geometric planner (Habitat-Sim follower or ROS nav stack) executes the resulting waypoints.

## Results

On A-EQA, SAGE (Qwen3-2B) achieves 53.2% LLM-Match SR† and 37.1% SPL†, surpassing the identical-backbone 3D-Mem baseline (44.3% SR†) by +8.9% and even exceeding the closed-source 3D-Mem (GPT-4o) at 52.6% SR†. Scaling to Qwen3-4B, SAGE sets a new SOTA of 60.2% SR† and 47.2% SPL† on A-EQA. On GOAT-Bench, SAGE (4B) reaches 64.8% SR and 44.9% SPL, narrowing the gap with GPT-4o (69.1% SR) to within 4.3%, while SAGE (2B) nearly doubles the SPL efficiency of the 3D-Mem (Qwen3-2B) baseline (38.9% vs. 20.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE (Qwen3-2B) decisively outperforms both RL baselines (SenseAct-NN Skill Chain at 24.7% and Monolithic at 20.6% A-EQA SR†) by over 28 percentage points, demonstrating that the VLM+RL hybrid paradigm far exceeds pure RL for semantic navigation. Against the same-backbone 3D-Mem (Qwen3-2B), SAGE gains +8.9% on A-EQA SR† and +10.3% on GOAT-Bench SR, with SPL nearly doubling (37.1% vs. 19.4% on A-EQA). Remarkably, SAGE (2B) surpasses the closed-source 3D-Mem (GPT-4o) on A-EQA SR† (53.2% vs. 52.6%), proving that internalized sandbox priors enable a 2B open-source model to rival a proprietary giant. On GOAT-Bench, SAGE (2B) also outperforms the larger LLaVA-7B-based 3D-Mem (56.7% vs. 49.6% SR). The 4B variant narrows the remaining gap to GPT-4o to just 4.3% on GOAT-Bench SR (64.8% vs. 69.1%) and 2.4% on A-EQA SR† (60.2% vs. 52.6%—actually exceeding it), validating the scalability of the Genesis-Evolution-Navigation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing an Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping mechanism that differentiates update aggressiveness between experience-augmented and standard samples, and on the 3D-Mem navigation architecture (Yang et al., 2025b) which provides the frontier-buffer/memory-buffer decision structure. Conceptually, the paper departs from both the RL paradigm (which struggles with Sim2Real transfer and sample inefficiency) and the zero-shot VLM paradigm (which lacks continuous learning) by proposing a 'generative experience-driven learning' loop: a physics-grounded sandbox synthesizes tasks and IF-THEN experience rules offline, which are then distilled into a compact VLM policy via modified GRPO. The framing as 'mental simulation'—rehearsing plans in simplified physics abstractions before execution—positions the work at the intersection of self-evolving agents and embodied planning, though the individual components (retrieval augmentation, frontier exploration, occupancy mapping) are drawn from prior embodied AI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in embodied navigation: the scarcity of aligned vision-control data and the brittleness of Sim2Real transfer. Demonstrating that a 2B open-source model can match or exceed GPT-4o on A-EQA (53.2% vs 52.6% SR†) while running locally is practically significant for edge deployment, and the successful physical robot validation, though limited to indoor settings, provides encouraging evidence of transfer. However, the sandbox still relies on high-fidelity simulators (HM3D, InteriorGS) and the real-world evaluation is narrow in scope; open questions include whether the physics-grounded abstraction generalizes to outdoor or highly dynamic environments, whether the experience database scales without quality degradation, and whether the GRPO modification (AAC) transfers to other RL-for-VLM settings beyond navigation. The data-efficiency result (44.75% SR† at 12.5% data) suggests a scalable path, but the diminishing returns curve implies that further gains will require more than simply adding sandbox trajectories.
