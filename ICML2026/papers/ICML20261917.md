## Problem

Scaling high-quality GUI planning trajectories for Computer-Use Agents remains a critical bottleneck: existing data synthesis methods rely heavily on human annotations, suffer from step redundancy as trajectories repeat early functional entry points, and produce insufficiently diverse long-tail exploration due to model bias toward high-frequency behaviors. This paper tackles the challenge of efficiently scaling GUI planning data without prohibitive human cost while maintaining both trajectory diversity and quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261917/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes high-resolution screenshots of desktop/web/mobile interfaces along with a natural-language task instruction, and outputs a sequence of executable GUI actions (typed as coordinate-based clicks, text inputs, waits, etc.) accompanied by a structured reasoning chain covering observation, planning, and impact assessment. Training data is organized at three granularities—708k step-level samples, 101k sub-trajectories (~3.3 steps each), and 50k long-horizon trajectories (~18 steps each)—all derived from a single tree-structured exploration process.

## Method

TreeCUA organizes exploration into a tree where nodes encode GUI states and edges encode actions, enabling deterministic node replay (reset to s₀ and re-execute action history with RMS visual consistency checking) to amortize the cost of revisiting shared prefixes. A multi-agent pipeline—exploration, verification, summarization, and evaluation agents—operates under an adaptive branching strategy that decays width over depth (discovery → development → convergence phases), is guided by world-knowledge initialization from structured documentation, and uses global prefix memory to enforce inter-tree novelty. Training follows a two-stage SFT protocol (foundational exploration learning, then cognitive intent alignment with human-refined tasks) followed by TreeCUA-DPO, which exploits the tree's branching topology as a free counterfactual generator: at each branching node, two physically valid but semantically distinct actions form dual preference pairs conditioned on their respective goals, requiring no additional human annotation.

## Results

On the OSWorld-Verified benchmark, TreeCUA-7B achieves 34.6% overall success rate, outperforming the strongest open-source offline baseline UltraCUA-7B (28.9%) by 5.7 points. TreeCUA-DPO-7B further improves to 36.6% overall, with particularly large gains on logic-intensive domains such as TB (+20.0 pts) and Code (+13.1 pts) over the SFT-only model. On the constructed 306-task OOD benchmark, TreeCUA-7B reaches 26.1% success rate versus 0.7% for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, and TreeCUA-DPO-7B reaches 28.1%, demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeCUA-7B (34.6%) surpasses all open-source offline baselines on OSWorld-Verified: UltraCUA-7B (28.9%), UI-TARS-1.5-7B (25.1%), OpenCUA-7B (24.3%), ScaleCUA-7B (15.0%), and the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base (5.5%). TreeCUA-DPO-7B further extends the lead to 36.6%, with the largest domain-specific gains on TB (+20.0 pts over SFT) and Code (+13.1 pts), confirming that DPO from tree-branching data is most effective on logic-intensive and sequential tasks. On the OOD benchmark, TreeCUA-7B (26.1%) and TreeCUA-DPO-7B (28.1%) dramatically outperform the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base (0.7%) and the combined OpenCUA+ScaleCUA data (12.1%), indicating that tree-structured exploration yields substantially better generalization than linear-chain human-annotated trajectories. Against the closed-source Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (58.1%), TreeCUA-DPO-7B still trails by ~21.5 points, but narrows the gap considerably compared to prior open-source efforts. The data-scale comparison (Table 6) shows that replacing OpenCUA+ScaleCUA with TreeCUA data on the same Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone more than doubles both ID (20.2→34.6) and OOD (12.1→26.1) success rates, attributing the gain to the tree-structured synthesis pipeline rather than model architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CUA data synthesis line (OpenCUA, ScaleCUA, OS-Genesis) by replacing linear trajectory collection with a tree-structured exploration topology that amortizes repeated prefix exploration via deterministic node replay. It builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) but repurposes the tree branching structure as a zero-cost counterfactual generator for preference pairs, eliminating the need for human-annotated corrective actions. The multi-agent verification and world-knowledge-guided exploration framework departs from prior random-walk or human-demonstration approaches by introducing adaptive branching depth, global memory backtracking, and step-level (rather than trajectory-level) verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the critical bottleneck of scaling GUI *planning* trajectories (as opposed to the more-studied grounding data), and the tree-structured methodology offers a principled path to large-scale, diverse trajectory synthesis without proportional human annotation cost. The 50k+101k trajectory dataset and the DPO-from-topology trick are practically useful for the open-source CUA community. However, the 7B model still trails closed-source systems by ~25 points on OSWorld, DPO introduces regressions on visually-continuous domains (Impress −10.6%), and it remains open whether the tree-structured data advantage persists at 70B+ scale or whether the replay-based exploration engine generalizes to environments with heavier stochasticity.
