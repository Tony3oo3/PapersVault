## Problem

Current LLM scientific reasoning research focuses on scaling training data and extending reasoning chains to improve QA benchmark accuracy, but neglects the logicality that constitutes the rational foundation of valid scientific reasoning. This paper makes the first systematic investigation into the internal logicality of LLM scientific reasoning, asking whether it can be formally quantified and whether enriching it in training data improves both reasoning quality and final task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes physics questions (multiple-choice, expression computation, numeric computation, and proof types across four difficulty levels) paired with ground-truth logical nexuses — discrete, importance-weighted reasoning steps extracted from academic paper derivations. The model produces a sentence-segmented reasoning trajectory and a final answer; evaluation computes three logicality scores (Fidelity, Causal Connection, Inferential Progress) by embedding both nexuses and reasoning steps with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and performing greedy cosine-similarity matching with a threshold τ=0.3.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-dimensional logicality assessment framework: Logical Fidelity (weighted F-score from greedy one-to-one matching of nexus embeddings to reasoning-step embeddings), Causal Connection (weighted proportion of nexus pairs preserving correct temporal order via positional centroids), and Inferential Progress (average conceptual novelty across the reasoning path, penalizing circular or oscillatory reasoning). Two logic-aware SFT sampling methods are proposed for training data: Reasoning Style Transfer (RST), which prompts a powerful LLM to synthesize a coherent narrative reasoning path from discrete weighted nexuses, and Logic-Distill, which scores native LLM reasoning trajectories using the three metrics (z-normalized, sigmoid-bounded, weighted combination with δ_O=0.50 dominant) and retains only the top-κ percentile. The underlying dataset is constructed by extracting 80k QA instances and 864 benchmark problems from 118k theory-centric physics papers (filtered from 380k arXiv/journal papers) using DeepSeek-R1 for question generation, solution derivation, and nexus extraction, followed by multi-stage quality control (rule-based filtering, LLM-based filtering, and human evaluation with Brennan-Prediger agreement ≥0.85).

## Results

On the in-domain PHYSLOGIC benchmark, RST-sampled SFT data yields the highest average logicality across all three backbones (45.94 on Llama-3.1-8B, 45.06 on Qwen2.5-7B, 45.98 on R1-7B), outperforming the best baseline by 3.59%, 1.94%, and 3.30% respectively, while also improving final-answer accuracy by up to 13.65%. On out-of-domain public benchmarks, Logic-Distill (using only 40k samples) achieves the best average accuracy on Qwen2.5-7B (45.04%) and R1-7B (53.42%), surpassing the strongest external baseline (SCP-116k) by 10.32% and 6.08% respectively. RST (80k) ranks second on Qwen2.5-7B (41.41%) and R1-7B (52.26%). On Llama-3.1-8B-base, Direct-Distill (80k) leads at 37.98% average, attributed to scaling-law effects on base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four public physics-QA training datasets: NaturalReasoning, MegaScience, Sci-Instruct, and SCP-116k, all sampled to 80k instances for fair comparison. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Logic-Distill (40k) achieves 45.04% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline SCP-116k (34.72%) by 10.32 points and MegaScience (29.39%) by 15.65 points, while using only half the training data. On R1-7B, Logic-Distill leads at 53.42% versus SCP-116k's 47.34% (+6.08) and MegaScience's 43.74% (+9.68). RST (80k) consistently ranks second among the proposed methods, beating the best baseline by 5.12% on Qwen2.5-7B and 4.92% on R1-7B. On Llama-3.1-8B-base, Direct-Distill (80k) outperforms Logic-Distill (37.98% vs. 35.14%), which the authors attribute to scaling-law effects on base models; however, at equivalent data volumes (20k), Logic-Distill surpasses Direct-Distill, confirming that logicality enrichment is more effective than raw data scale. All four baselines are substantially outperformed on the in-domain PHYSLOGIC logicality metrics, with RST achieving the highest average logicality (45.94–45.98) versus 38.19–42.35 for the best baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the epistemic activities taxonomy of Fischer et al. (2014) to formalize 'logical nexuses' as the structural units of scientific reasoning, then departs from the dominant paradigm in scientific-reasoning LLM training (NaturalReasoning, MegaScience, Sci-Instruct, SCP-116k) that emphasizes data scale and reasoning-chain length while neglecting the logical coherence of the reasoning process. It also extends process-oriented evaluation work (REVEAL, ProcessBench, PRM-Bench, PRISM-Physics) by proposing a multi-dimensional logicality framework—fidelity, causal connection, inferential progress—rather than assessing only step validity or a single topological structure. The two SFT sampling strategies (Reasoning Style Transfer and Logic-Distill) repurpose standard distillation and style-transfer ideas but ground them in the nexus-based logicality scores as an explicit supervisory signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PHYSLOGIC is the first benchmark to evaluate the *process* of physics reasoning across multiple logicality dimensions rather than only final-answer correctness, and the logic-aware sampling methodology offers a concrete recipe for improving reasoning quality without simply scaling data. The finding that Causal Connection (ordering) is the most critical dimension, and that 40k logic-filtered samples outperform 80k unfiltered ones, suggests a data-quality-over-quantity principle for scientific reasoning training. Key limitations include the physics-only scope, the circularity of using R1 to both generate and extract logical nexuses, and the reliance on a lightweight sentence encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) for structural alignment, which may conflate semantic similarity with genuine logical dependency. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to disciplines with less formal deductive structure (e.g., biology, social sciences) and whether the embedding-based metrics can be replaced by more principled logical representations.
