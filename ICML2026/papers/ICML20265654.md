## Problem

Existing MLLM architectures face a fundamental trade-off: embedding space alignment (e.g., LLaVA) is training-efficient but inference-inefficient due to quadratic self-attention over hundreds of visual tokens, while cross-attention space alignment (e.g., Flamingo) is inference-efficient but training-inefficient due to large numbers of new parameters. The paper asks whether attention from visual tokens is truly essential, and whether an architecture can achieve both training and inference efficiency simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or more RGB images (or video frames) along with a text prompt, and generates a free-form text response. Unlike standard MLLMs where visual tokens are concatenated with text tokens into a single sequence, SAISA feeds visual features as per-layer key/value inputs to each attention block while only text tokens flow through the full transformer stack (attention + FFN). The projector produces n separate projections (one per LLM layer) rather than a single embedding-space projection, and visual tokens never pass through FFNs or serve as attention queries.

## Method

The paper introduces NAEViT (No AttEntion from Visual Tokens), an attention mechanism where visual tokens serve only as keys and values but never as queries, reducing attention complexity from O((v+t)²) to O(t(v+t)). Building on this, SAISA (Self-Attention Input Space Alignment) aligns visual features directly with the input spaces of each NAEViT block via per-layer 2-layer MLP projectors, eliminating FFN computation on visual tokens entirely. A KL-divergence analysis demonstrates that attention from visual tokens is highly redundant, and a pilot experiment shows NAEViT outperforms vanilla self-attention. Training uses a two-stage procedure: pre-training a shared MLP across all layers on 558K samples, then replicating it into per-layer MLPs for full fine-tuning. This reduces inference FLOPs by over 66% and training budget by 26% compared to LLaVA-1.5 while achieving superior benchmark performance.

## Results

SAISA reduces inference FLOPs by 66% (from 8.53T to 2.86T for the 7B setting) while improving average benchmark score from 57.4 to 58.3 over LLaVA-1.5-7B. At the 13B scale, SAISA achieves 60.3 average score with 5.52T FLOPs versus 60.2 and 16.61T for LLaVA-1.5-13B. Against EAGLE-X5-7B (14.79T FLOPs, 63.8 avg), SAISA reaches 64.1 average with only 5.54T FLOPs. Training budget is reduced by 26% compared to LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5-7B, SAISA improves average score by 0.9 points (58.3 vs 57.4) while cutting FLOPs by 66% (2.86T vs 8.53T); it also outperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B (60.3 vs 60.2 avg) at 67% lower FLOPs, and beats EAGLE-X5-7B (64.1 vs 63.8 avg) at 63% lower FLOPs. Among efficiency-focused baselines in Table 3, SAISA (2.86T) achieves the best overall performance: it surpasses FastV (4.90T, 56.4 avg on reported benchmarks), VTW (4.70T, with severe OCRBench drop to 5.0), TopV (4.35T, missing several scores), SparseVLM (2.84T, 56.1 avg on reported), PAR (4.00T, 73.6 POPE), VisiPruner (3.93T, 55.2 TextVQA), and CDPruner (2.51T, 56.2 TextVQA) on most benchmarks, with CDPruner being the only method with lower FLOPs but incomplete evaluation. On multi-image and video tasks (Table 4), SAISA applied to Mantis-CLIP improves NLVR2 (84.8 vs 84.7), Mantis-Eval (54.6 vs 53.0), LongVideoBench (45.7 vs 45.1), and NExTQA (73.2 vs 72.7), while slightly dropping on LVBench (35.1 vs 35.5). Ablations confirm SAISA generalizes across LLMs (Vicuna, Mistral, Llama3) and visual encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, ConvNeXt), consistently outperforming the corresponding LLaVA-1.5 baselines by 0.5–3.6 average points while reducing FLOPs by 60–77%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two dominant MLLM architecture families: embedding space alignment (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InternVL) and cross-attention space alignment (Flamingo, OpenFlamingo, Otter), which have been understood to involve a training-vs-inference efficiency trade-off. The key departure is the empirical finding that attention *from* visual tokens (i.e., visual tokens acting as queries in self-attention) is highly redundant, enabling NAEViT to eliminate this computation while using only the pre-trained LLM's existing attention blocks. SAISA then extends this by also removing FFN computations on visual tokens, directly aligning visual features with per-layer attention input spaces via lightweight MLPs — a design that avoids both the long concatenated sequences of LLaVA and the large parameter overhead of Flamingo-style cross-attention blocks. Unlike token-pruning methods (FastV, VTW, TopV, SparseVLM, CDPruner), SAISA preserves the full number of visual tokens, achieving efficiency through architectural restructuring rather than information loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of training and inference efficiency (26% training budget reduction, 66% inference FLOPs reduction) while *improving* benchmark performance challenges the prevailing assumption that these two forms of efficiency are in tension for MLLMs. The conceptual finding that visual tokens do not need to function as attention queries — and that this removal can even improve performance — opens a broader question about what computations are truly necessary for cross-modal interaction in transformer backbones. Limitations include validation primarily on 7B/13B models, the addition of n per-layer MLPs in the projector (which increases parameter count relative to LLaVA's single projector), and the need for full retraining rather than plug-and-play deployment. Open questions include whether the redundancy finding holds at frontier scale (70B+), for very long video sequences, and whether the per-layer projector design can be further compressed.
