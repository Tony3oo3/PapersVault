## Problem

Current de novo epitope generation methods treat the MHC receptor as a frozen structural template, neglecting the induced-fit conformational changes essential for high-affinity binding. Additionally, they rely on positive-only learning that maximizes likelihood exclusively on binders, leaving decision boundaries undefined and failing to suppress non-binding regions. These two limitations—static receptor conditioning and absence of negative-informed discriminative signals—prevent reliable generation of high-specificity, high-affinity epitopes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an Apo-MHC (epitope-free) 3D structure predicted by AlphaFold3 as a scaffold, along with a continuous affinity scalar s∈[0,1] derived from experimental IC50 values via log-linear normalization. At inference, the MHC residues are initialized with their predicted coordinates while the epitope region is pure Gaussian noise; both are jointly denoised over 1000 timesteps, allowing the MHC backbone to undergo conformational adjustments. The output is a full pMHC complex comprising the epitope amino acid sequence (as continuous one-hot vectors decoded to discrete residues), 3D Cartesian coordinates for all residues, and edge topology defining the peptide chain.

## Method

EpiCoCo is an E(3)-equivariant diffusion model (12-layer NodeEdgeNet with Highway Gating) that operates on the joint pMHC complex state, treating both epitope and MHC as co-adaptive entities rather than conditioning on a rigid receptor. During training, Affinity Soft Prompting (ASP) encodes the continuous IC50-derived scalar via Fourier Positional Embeddings into a conditioning vector injected into the denoising network, enabling the model to learn a tunable affinity landscape rather than a binary binder/non-binder split. At inference, Contrastive Affinity Guidance (CAG) computes the gradient difference between the high-affinity and low-affinity conditional score functions, using the abundant negative data as a repulsive boundary that prunes the generative search space and steers trajectories toward a sharpened high-affinity manifold. The training objective is a weighted sum of MSE losses on coordinates, continuous amino acid features, and edge types, with the MHC initialized from AlphaFold3 predictions and the epitope from pure noise at t=T.

## Results

EpiCoCo achieves a mean binding free energy (ΔG) of −45.20 REU, a 23% improvement over the strongest baseline RLpMIEC (−37.55 REU, restricted to 13 alleles), while generalizing across all 100 MHC alleles. It also attains the highest structural confidence scores: ipTM of 0.9334 and plDDT^p of 0.8884. On sequence quality, EpiCoCo records 100% uniqueness, 66.76% diversity, and the best naturalness MAE of 1.09, indicating generated sequences closely match natural epitope distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EpiCoCo outperforms all 10 baselines on binding free energy (ΔG = −45.20 REU vs. best baseline RLpMIEC at −37.55 REU) and structural confidence (ipTM 0.9334 vs. next-best PepFlow at 0.8500; plDDT^p 0.8884 vs. PepFlow at 0.8500). It also achieves the best naturalness (MAE 1.09 vs. PepPrCLIP's 1.27) and 100% uniqueness, matching PepMLM, PepPrCLIP, PepTune, and MOG-DFM. EpiCoCo trails on diversity (66.76% vs. PepGLAD's 75.41% and PepPrCLIP's 71.78%) and instability (44.22 vs. PepTune's 17.68), though the authors argue this trade-off is biologically expected since high-affinity anchors require hydrophobic residues that reduce intrinsic peptide stability. Against the closest structure-aware competitor PepFlow, EpiCoCo gains ~21 REU in binding energy and ~18 percentage points in uniqueness, demonstrating the benefit of dynamic MHC co-modeling over static scaffold conditioning. In the few-negative-shot setting (10 alleles with no positive training data), EpiCoCo achieves ΔG values of −37 to −71 REU, substantially outperforming PepPPO (−23 to −37 REU) and PepPrCLIP (−31 to +9 REU), validating its negative-informed generation capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

EpiCoCo departs from the dominant paradigm in computational epitope design where the MHC receptor is treated as a static geometric or sequence condition (PepFlow, PepGLAD, DPLM, PepPrCLIP) by instead co-generating the epitope and MHC conformation jointly on an E(3)-equivariant graph, capturing induced-fit dynamics. It also moves beyond binary binder/non-binder conditioning (used by RL-based methods like PepPPO and RLpMIEC, and two-stage optimizers like PepTune) toward Full-Spectrum Affinity Learning with continuous IC50-derived soft prompts. The Contrastive Affinity Guidance mechanism repurposes the classifier-guidance idea from diffusion literature but inverts its logic: rather than attracting toward a positive classifier, it uses the gradient of the abundant negative distribution as a repulsive boundary, a formulation not previously applied to pMHC generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward patient-specific neoantigen vaccine design by generating high-affinity epitopes across 100+ MHC alleles without allele-specific fine-tuning, and the negative-informed generation capability (producing binders from only non-binder data) could be transformative for rare alleles where positive assay data is unavailable. However, all validation remains computational (Rosetta ΔG, tFold-TCR, in silico MD); no wet-lab binding or immunogenicity assays are reported, leaving open whether the predicted -45 REU energies translate to functional T-cell recognition. Broader questions include whether the co-modeling advantage persists at larger complex sizes (MHC-II, TCR-pMHC) and whether the CAG guidance generalizes to other receptor-ligand systems beyond pMHC.
