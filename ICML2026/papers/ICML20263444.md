## Problem

Deep time-series forecasters suffer from compounding errors during autoregressive (chunk-based) inference, where predictions are recursively fed back as inputs, causing accuracy to degrade over long horizons. Classical Error Correction Models (ECMs) from econometrics are inapplicable to high-dimensional deep architectures, and existing deep-learning error correction methods either require costly joint training with the backbone or are tied to specific model designs. No architecture-agnostic, post-hoc corrector exists that can reliably improve a broad range of modern forecasters without retraining.

## Input / Output

The corrector receives two tensors per autoregressive step: the historical window X_{τ-W+1:τ} ∈ R^{W×D} (W=96 by default) and the backbone's predicted chunk X̂_{τ+1:τ+L} ∈ R^{L×D}. It outputs a correction vector ΔX̂ ∈ R^{L×D} that is added to the forecast scaled by a scalar strength β∈[0,1]. In the UEC-STD variant, the prediction is first decomposed via a 1D centred moving average (kernel size 25) into trend and seasonal components, and the network produces two separate correction vectors (ΔX̂^t and ΔX̂^s) that are summed. Training examples are constructed from the validation split (not the training split) to reflect realistic generalization errors of the frozen backbone.

## Method

UEC-STD is a lightweight two-stage MLP (hidden size 32, dropout 0.5) that explicitly models seasonal and trend errors separately. Subnetwork 1 reshapes the concatenated input (historical window + trend + seasonal) to (B×D, T) and applies a two-layer temporal MLP; Subnetwork 2 permutes back to (B, T, D) and applies a feature-aggregation MLP, splitting the output into trend and seasonal correction vectors. Training uses a weighted Huber loss λ_t·l_ec(ΔX̂^t, ΔX^t) + λ_s·l_ec(ΔX̂^s, ΔX^s) where the ground-truth error is also decomposed into trend/seasonal via the same moving average. The backbone forecaster is entirely frozen (black-box), and only UEC parameters are optimized over 100 steps with batch size 64 (~1 minute GPU time). The correction strength β is auto-selected via a balanced validation strategy that mixes a portion of seen training data with held-out validation data to avoid over- or under-correction.

## Results

UEC-STD achieves an average MSE reduction of 2.32% and MAE reduction of 0.94% across 7 common benchmark datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Traffic, Weather, Electricity) averaged over 3 backbones (TimeMixer, TimesNet, TimeXer). On the Monash datasets (US Births, Saugeen River Flow, Sunspot), improvements are substantially larger: 10.05% MSE and 9.13% MAE on average, with up to 20.98% MSE reduction on US Births. On ETTm1, UEC-STD delivers 4.06% MSE and 1.58% MAE reduction. Training UEC-STD takes approximately 1 minute of GPU time versus ~10 minutes for the backbone, introducing negligible computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UEC-STD is the only method that achieves a negative (improving) error reduction across all 10 datasets simultaneously, whereas every other UEC variant degrades performance on at least one dataset. Classical ML correctors (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost) completely fail on high-dimensional datasets like Traffic and Electricity (N/A) and produce catastrophic errors on Monash data (e.g., +103% MSE on Births for Logistic). Among neural baselines, UEC-MLP is the closest competitor but still lags UEC-STD by 1–3% MSE on most datasets; recurrent models (LSTM, GRU) and CNNs are inconsistent, often worsening predictions (e.g., LSTM adds +16.64% MSE on ETTm2). The Transformer-based corrector is competitive on some datasets but still trails UEC-STD by 1–5% MSE on average. The seasonal-trend decomposition in UEC-STD is the key differentiator: ablations show that jointly correcting both trend and seasonal components (Full setting) outperforms correcting either alone, with the full model improving MSE by 5.99% on ETTh1 versus 2.7% (trend-only) or 5.3% (seasonal-only).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical econometric Error Correction Model (ECM) concept—adjusting predictions for deviations from equilibrium—but transplants it into the deep learning autoregressive forecasting setting in a fundamentally different way: rather than relying on cointegration across multivariate series, it learns correction vectors from a forecaster's own input-output pairs. It departs from prior deep-learning error correction work (Liu et al. 2020, Zhang et al. 2021, Li et al. 2024) that requires joint training or architecture-specific integration, by treating the backbone as a frozen black box and training a lightweight corrector post-hoc. The seasonal-trend decomposition applied to both the forecast and the error target is a novel architectural choice not present in prior ECM or residual-learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that UEC-STD offers a near-zero-cost plug-in (≈1 min training vs. ≈10 min for the backbone) that consistently reduces autoregressive error accumulation across six different forecasters and ten datasets, making it immediately deployable alongside existing pipelines. However, the improvements on the primary benchmarks are modest (2–4% MSE), and the paper itself acknowledges that current SOTA forecasters are approaching saturation, which limits the ceiling of post-hoc correction. Open questions include whether adaptive or input-dependent correction strengths could yield larger gains, how the approach behaves under non-stationary real-world deployment, and whether the seasonal-trend decomposition generalizes to multivariate settings with strong cross-variable coupling.
