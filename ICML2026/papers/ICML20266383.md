## Problem

Mainstream human pose and body pipelines enforce a fixed skeletal topology with an indexed joint inventory and fixed adjacency, which cannot represent structural absence: for individuals with limb deficiencies, canonical joints become anatomically non-existent rather than merely occluded, yet the fixed schema forces template completion or collapses absence into unlabeled visibility flags. This mis-specification propagates through supervision, inference, and evaluation, producing hallucinated structure, metric blind spots in OKS-style scoring, and topology-inconsistent downstream reasoning in 3D fitting, tracking, and clinical motion analysis.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a standard RGB image or video clip as input and produces a variable-size instance-specific skeleton graph rather than a fixed-length keypoint vector. Each predicted node carries a type from a published vocabulary, an existence probability (a semantic claim about anatomical presence, not a visibility proxy), and a location or region support; the edge set is instance-specific and may be directly predicted or deterministically derived from predicted node types and existence states. A deterministic canonicalization procedure maps the variable-size output into a shared evaluation space, preserving comparability across instances with different joint inventories.

## Method

The paper proposes an instance-adaptive skeletal topology as a minimal output contract: a model jointly predicts a variable-size typed node set with per-node existence probabilities and an instance-specific edge set (or equivalent routing rule), making both node existence and connectivity input-conditioned prediction targets rather than constants. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requiring only that the body-interface head expose type-level existence and an explicit edge policy, while the visual backbone may be any CNN, transformer, or foundation model. The measurement protocol introduces tri-state per-type labels (present-visible, present-occluded, non-existent) to separate anatomical absence from occlusion, and defines topology-aware metrics including node existence F1, edge F1 in a canonical typed space, and an absent-type false positive rate (FPR_abs) that directly penalizes hallucination under absence. A minimal ablation suite compares fixed topology, fixed topology with masking, existence-only heads, and full instance-adaptive topology to isolate the contribution of each structural component.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not report quantitative experimental results. Instead, it proposes a minimal measurement protocol (node existence F1, edge F1, and absent-type false positive rate FPR_abs) and a falsifiable representation-level claim: that fixed skeletal topology makes supervision, inference, and evaluation insensitive to structurally invalid predictions under anatomical absence. The paper outlines a five-condition ablation suite (fixed topology, fixed topology + masking, existence head only, existence + endpoint extension, full instance-adaptive topology) as the required evidence to validate or refute the position, but no such experiments are conducted here.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not run empirical comparisons between methods. Section 4 presents four alternative views (A: more data + limb-def state; B: superset + masking; C: existence heads + small extension; D: practicality favors fixed schemas) and, for each, specifies the single decisive evidence condition that would change the authors' minds (e.g., fixed-schema + state matching instance-adaptive topology on existence and edge correctness across limb strata). Table 1 summarizes these as a 'debate map' with responsible actors and deliverables, but no method is benchmarked against another. The closest empirical anchors cited are the authors' own prior works (LDPose, ResiHMR, InclusiveVidPose), which demonstrate the practical failures of fixed-topology pipelines on limb-deficient individuals, but no head-to-head numbers are provided in this paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant fixed-skeletal-topology paradigm embodied by COCO, SMPL, OpenPose, ViTPose, and HRNet, which all treat the joint inventory and adjacency as constants shared across all individuals. It draws on the authors' own prior work on limb-deficient pose estimation (LDPose, ResiHMR, InclusiveVidPose) as motivation, and on graph/relational learning literature (GAT, Neural Relational Inference) as technical justification that connectivity can be learned rather than assumed. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper reframes the body-interface layer: it repurposes the existing graph-representation machinery by elevating node existence and edge validity from implicit assumptions to explicit, scoreable prediction targets, and it borrows measurement frameworks from datasheets-for-datasets and model-cards literature to define a minimal evaluation contract.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a representation-level gap that affects a large population (WHO estimates >2.5 billion people need assistive products) and argues that scaling data or model size cannot fix a mis-specified output schema. Its most concrete contribution is a measurement protocol—existence F1, edge F1, and hallucination-under-absence FPR—that makes structural errors visible to evaluation, addressing the OKS blind spot where absent-joint slots contribute nothing to headline scores. Key open questions include interoperable type vocabularies across tasks, canonicalization that preserves meaningful anatomical variation, and whether the proposed instance-adaptive contract can be validated at scale before becoming a benchmark standard. As a position paper with no empirical validation, its impact hinges on whether dataset curators and benchmark organizers adopt the absence-explicit semantics and topology-aware metrics it prescribes.
