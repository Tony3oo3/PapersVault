## Problem

Existing approximate attention methods (GQA, MLA, MQA) reduce parameters through specialized low-rank factorizations of individual weight matrices, but it remains unclear what objects they actually approximate and how their low-rank behavior should be interpreted. No unified framework existed to compare these methods or to identify which tensor modes they leave unexploited, leaving exploitable redundancies—particularly in the head and output modes—unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard transformer inputs: text token sequences (context lengths 256–4096 for GPT-2, Shakespeare, and LLaMA-3-1B) or RGB images resized to 224×224 for ViT variants. Internally, the attention layer operates on compressed latent representations: key and value projections are reduced to rank r₃ (e.g., 64 or 128) rather than the full d_model dimension, and the head mode is compressed to rank r₁ (e.g., 8 or 32). The KV cache stores these compressed N×r₃ representations, and the output is standard next-token logits or class scores, with no change to the external interface of the model.

## Method

The paper reformulates multi-head attention by collecting per-head pre-softmax weights (W_i = W_i^Q W_i^{K⊤}) and post-softmax weights (W̃_i = W_i^V W_i^O) into order-3 tensors over the head, query/key, and value/output modes, then applies a learnable Tucker decomposition to each tensor. This yields Tucker Attention, where independent ranks (r₁, r₂, r₃) control compression along the head, query/key, and value/output modes respectively—enabling compression in the head and output modes that GQA and MLA leave at full rank by construction. The paper proves that MHA, MQA, GQA, and MLA are all special cases of Tucker Attention at specific rank settings, and introduces 'latent RoPE,' which moves rotary position embeddings from the per-head dimension into the shared latent key space, simultaneously simplifying MLA's decoupled RoPE and enabling full inference-time weight fusion. The formulation is compatible with Flash-Attention (appearing as MQA from the kernel's perspective since all query heads share one KV pair) and standard KV caching, with the KV cache size directly controlled by the key/value rank r₃.

## Results

On LLaMA3-1B (d_model=2048, n_H=32, N=4096) trained on OpenWebText2, Tucker Attention [32,64,64] uses only 12.6 MB of attention parameters (95% fewer than MHA's 268 MB) while achieving a validation loss of 2.69 and PPL of 14.76, compared to MHA's 2.55 and 12.87. It reduces KV cache by 32× (8.38 MB vs 268 MB), cuts training iteration time by 22% (4.05 ms vs 5.19 ms), and improves decode latency by 8% (1.274 ms vs 1.379 ms). On GPT-2 (d_model=768, N=1024) trained on OpenWebText, Tucker [8,128,64] uses 10.24 MB params (82% fewer than MHA) with a mean HellaSwag accuracy of 46.23% vs MHA's 48.20%. On ViT transfer learning (ImageNet-1k), Tucker Attention requires roughly an order of magnitude fewer parameters for comparable top-5 accuracy than GQA and MLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MHA, Tucker Attention [32,64,64] trades a modest 0.14 increase in validation loss (2.69 vs 2.55) and 1.89 PPL increase (14.76 vs 12.87) for a 95% parameter reduction (12.6 vs 268 MB), 32× smaller KV cache, 22% faster training iterations, and 8% faster decode latency. Against the LLaMA3 default GQA [n_KV=8], Tucker achieves 92% fewer parameters and 8× less KV cache at only 0.08 higher validation loss (2.69 vs 2.61), while training 17% faster. Against MLA [d_C=64], Tucker uses 73% fewer parameters (12.6 vs 79.2 MB) with essentially identical validation loss (2.69 vs 2.69) and PPL (14.76 vs 14.77), while training 7% faster and decoding 3% faster. Against MQA [n_KV=1], Tucker matches the KV cache size (8.38 MB) but uses 91% fewer parameters (12.6 vs 138 MB) at a comparable validation loss (2.69 vs 2.64). The paper also demonstrates that MLA with d_C^Q=768, d_C^K=128 and separate KV down-projection is expressively equivalent to GQA [n_KV=2], confirming the unifying Tucker rank framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the parameter-efficiency problem in multi-head attention by treating the pre-softmax (query×key) and post-softmax (value×output) weight matrices as unified third-order tensors, then applying Tucker decomposition to obtain a single parametrization that subsumes MHA, MQA, GQA, and MLA as special cases at specific Tucker ranks. It builds directly on the low-rank structure observations of Voita et al. (2019) and Brunner et al. (2020), the grouped/latent attention designs of Ainslie et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2024), and concurrent tensor-attention work (Zhang et al., 2025; Gu et al., 2025), but departs from all of these by compressing the head mode—a dimension none of the prior methods exploit—and by introducing 'latent RoPE,' which moves rotary position embeddings from the head dimension to the latent key space, thereby eliminating MLA's need for decoupled RoPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying tensor view yields up to ~9× parameter reduction at comparable validation metrics on GPT-2, LLaMA-3-1B, and ViT benchmarks, while remaining fully compatible with Flash-Attention, KV caching, and RoPE—making it a drop-in replacement that simultaneously reduces training memory, inference KV-cache, and GPU cache pressure. The practical simplification of MLA (removing decoupled RoPE) is an immediate win for implementers. Key open questions include whether the low-rank head-mode assumption holds at much larger scales (70B+), whether custom kernels exploiting the factorized core tensor can close the remaining latency gap versus optimized MHA/GQA kernels, and whether the SVD/HOSVD initialization strategy generalizes to from-scratch training at scale without the transfer-learning warm-start used in the ViT experiments.
