## Problem

Existing learning-based branching policies for MILP solvers rely on expensive expert demonstrations (e.g., strong branching) and optimize surrogate objectives (imitation accuracy) that do not reliably translate to end-to-end solver performance. Reinforcement learning, while theoretically aligned with solver metrics, suffers from sparse delayed rewards and extreme sample inefficiency in the branch-and-bound setting. The paper asks whether LLMs can automate the discovery of branching policies that directly minimize solver cost without any expert teacher.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the discovered policy consumes a 91-dimensional feature vector per candidate variable (normalized to [0,1]) and a small parameter vector (typically <10 values), producing a score vector from which the highest-scoring variable is selected for branching. The policy is a compact executable Python function using at most 10 of the 91 features, enabling purely CPU-based execution with minimal feature-extraction overhead. For policy discovery, only 8 MILP instances per benchmark are required (4 for parameter optimization, 8 for evaluation), with no expert demonstrations needed.

## Method

LLM4Branch represents the branching policy as an executable program p=(s,θ), decomposing discovery into structure and parameters. An LLM (DeepSeek-R1) generates the program skeleton s via in-context learning over a structured prompt containing feature semantics, historical program-performance pairs, and format constraints, operating within a 200-iteration evolutionary loop with island-based sampling for diversity. Underperforming skeletons are rapidly filtered against a solver baseline, and surviving skeletons undergo zeroth-order Bayesian optimization (50 iterations via scikit-optimize) to find θ* that minimizes the geometric mean of B&B nodes on a 4-instance subset. The key design choice is directly optimizing end-to-end solver cost rather than a surrogate imitation objective, which the ablation confirms is critical: replacing solver feedback with strong-branching accuracy degrades performance substantially (e.g., 45.21s vs. 10.91s on Indset). The resulting policies are human-readable, use simple arithmetic and NumPy vectorized operations, and generalize across difficulty levels and even across benchmarks.

## Results

LLM4Branch establishes a new state-of-the-art among CPU-based branching policies across all six MILP benchmarks. On the four standard benchmarks (Medium difficulty), it achieves 1-shifted geometric mean solving times of 37.64s (Setcover), 13.12s (Cauctions), 117.94s (Facilities), and 62.45s (Indset), outperforming the next-best CPU method (Hybrid) by 6–40%. On the two challenging benchmarks, it achieves the best primal-dual gap (0.7196) on Item Placement and the best solving time (34.54s) on NNVerify, both surpassing the GPU-based GNN-GPU baseline. Notably, these results are obtained using only 8 training instances per benchmark and no expert demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM4Branch decisively outperforms all CPU-based baselines on solving time and win count across every benchmark. Against the strongest CPU baseline Hybrid, it reduces solving time by 6.4% on Setcover (37.64s vs 40.18s), 8.4% on Cauctions (13.12s vs 14.38s), 4.5% on Facilities (117.94s vs 123.50s), and 44.5% on Indset (62.45s vs 112.48s), while also achieving substantially more wins (e.g., 66/80 vs 5/75 on Indset Medium). Against Symb4CO, which shares the same 91-dim feature input, LLM4Branch is 17% faster on Setcover and 88% faster on Indset Medium (62.45s vs 521.35s). Compared to the GPU-based GNN-GPU, LLM4Branch is competitive on Setcover (37.64s vs 34.38s) and Cauctions (13.12s vs 12.92s) but clearly wins on Indset Medium (62.45s vs 110.88s) and NNVerify (34.54s vs 44.82s). On the hardest Item Placement benchmark, LLM4Branch achieves the best primal-dual gap (0.7196) versus 0.8073 for GNN-GPU, indicating superior progress toward optimality within the time limit. The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that the end-to-end solver cost objective is critical: replacing it with imitation accuracy (LLM4Branch-IL) degrades Indset time from 10.91s to 45.21s despite higher expert-matching accuracy (39.44% vs 24.72%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) learning to branch in MILP solvers, which has progressed from imitation learning (Gasse et al. 2019, Gupta et al. 2020) through RL (SORREL, Parsonson et al. 2023) to symbolic policy discovery (Symb4CO, Kuang et al. 2024), and (2) LLM-driven program evolution for algorithmic discovery (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, and more recently LLM4Solver, EvoCut for solver components). The key departure is representing the branching policy as a lightweight executable program whose skeleton is generated by an LLM in an evolutionary loop, while its parameters are tuned via zeroth-order optimization against direct solver feedback—bypassing both the expert-teacher dependency of IL and the sample inefficiency of RL. The evolutionary infrastructure builds on OpenEvolve and adopts AlphaEvolve's island-based sampling strategy for maintaining program diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LLM4Branch achieves SOTA among CPU-only methods across six benchmarks using only 8 training instances and no expert demonstrations, producing human-readable policies that are trivially deployable in production solvers without GPU infrastructure. The clear ablation showing that optimizing for strong-branching imitation accuracy (LLM4Branch-IL) yields substantially worse solving performance than end-to-end optimization validates the objective-mismatch concern that has limited IL-based branching. Open questions include whether the compact program representation can capture structural information (e.g., bipartite graphs), how to formalize the choice of end-to-end metric (nodes vs. time), and whether the framework extends to other solver components like node selection or cut generation.
