## Problem

Conventional dynamic SLAM treats moving objects as outliers to be masked, creating perceptual blind spots that erase physical obstacles from the map. While integrating 4D Gaussian Splatting enables holistic scene representation, a fundamental Optimization Paradox arises: without categorical priors, flexible dynamic primitives rapidly overfit static residuals during naive joint optimization, extinguishing the self-supervised signal needed to distinguish motion (uncertainty collapse). The paper addresses how to achieve category-agnostic, holistic 4D reconstruction from monocular video while preserving robust camera tracking.

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular RGB video and internally leverages DINOv2 features (384-channel, H/14 × W/14 grid), dense optical flow, and Metric3D monocular depth priors as auxiliary signals. It produces SE(3) camera poses, a persistent static 3D Gaussian map, a short-lived dynamic 4D Gaussian map (with per-primitive velocity vectors and temporal opacity windows), a pixel-wise uncertainty map β ∈ ℝ₊^{H×W}, and composited rendered RGB and depth images. The uncertainty map serves a dual role: it gates which regions activate dynamic primitives in the mapping backend and modulates the information matrix in the tracking frontend's dense bundle adjustment. The full pipeline runs at 0.47 FPS on a single V100 GPU.

## Method

De4D-SLAM employs a Gradient-Isolated Dual-Stream Architecture that partitions the scene into a rigid static 3DGS stream and a dynamic 4DGS stream (FreeTimeGS-style linear velocity + Gaussian temporal opacity). A Spatially-Aware Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (SA-KAN), combining a 5×5 depthwise convolution for spatial context with a two-layer Linear KAN using learnable B-spline activations, maps frozen DINOv2 features to a pixel-wise uncertainty map β. The critical training mechanism applies a stop-gradient operator to the static reconstruction residual when supervising SA-KAN, preventing the dynamic stream's overfitting from collapsing the uncertainty signal (the Optimization Paradox). Newly activated dynamic primitives receive a Flow-Induced Initialization that lifts 2D optical flow into 3D velocity by backprojecting with metric depth from both current and previous frames, resolving the non-convex cold-start problem. Finally, the uncertainty map β modulates the tracking frontend's information matrix as (1+β)^{-1}·C^{-1}, adaptively down-weighting dynamic regions in the dense bundle adjustment objective.

## Results

On the Bonn RGB-D benchmark, De4D-SLAM achieves the best average ATE of 1.95 cm in tracking, outperforming the closest competitor WildGS-SLAM (2.02 cm) and substantially exceeding static-scene methods like MonoGS (36.18 cm) and SplaTAM (77.56 cm). For holistic 4D reconstruction on Bonn, it attains PSNR 26.70 dB, SSIM 0.903, and LPIPS 0.200, surpassing 4DGS-SLAM (PSNR 22.60, LPIPS 0.279) by a wide margin. On TUM RGB-D, De4D-SLAM achieves 1.27 cm average ATE (vs. 1.32 cm for WildGS-SLAM) and 27.59 dB PSNR / 0.164 LPIPS in reconstruction, the best across all evaluated methods. On the WildGS MoCap split, tracking is essentially tied with WildGS-SLAM (0.47 vs. 0.46 cm ATE), while De4D-SLAM additionally reconstructs dynamic content that WildGS discards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

De4D-SLAM (1.95 cm avg ATE on Bonn) beats all eight baselines in tracking, with the closest margin being WildGS-SLAM (2.02 cm) and a large gap over 4DGS-SLAM (12.84 cm), DROID-SLAM (9.53 cm), DynaSLAM (5.84 cm), RTG-SLAM (43.85 cm), MonoGS (36.18 cm), and SplaTAM (77.56 cm). On TUM RGB-D, it similarly leads with 1.27 cm ATE versus WildGS-SLAM's 1.32 cm and DROID-SLAM's 2.56 cm. In reconstruction quality on Bonn, De4D-SLAM achieves PSNR 26.70 dB and LPIPS 0.200, substantially outperforming 4DGS-SLAM (22.60 dB, 0.279 LPIPS) and MonoGS (20.14 dB, 0.360 LPIPS); WildGS-SLAM cannot render the full scene (N/A) because it masks dynamic regions entirely. D4DGS-SLAM reports strong PSNR on a few available sequences (e.g., 31.18 on Per.2) but provides incomplete sequence-level results, preventing a full comparison. On the WildGS MoCap generalization split, De4D-SLAM (0.47 cm) is essentially tied with WildGS-SLAM (0.46 cm) in tracking, while uniquely preserving dynamic geometry in the output map.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DROID-SLAM's dense bundle adjustment for tracking, 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation, and FreeTimeGS's velocity-based 4D parameterization for dynamic primitives, while introducing a Spatially-Aware KAN (combining depthwise convolutions with Kolmogorov-Arnold spline activations) as a novel uncertainty predictor. Its key conceptual departure from prior dynamic SLAM is identifying the 'Optimization Paradox'—that naive joint optimization of static and dynamic Gaussian streams causes uncertainty collapse due to capacity mismatch—and resolving it via gradient isolation (stop-gradient) rather than the exclusion-based masking paradigm of DynaSLAM/WildGS-SLAM or the external-prior-dependent approach of 4DGS-SLAM/D4DGS-SLAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-isolated decoupling strategy offers a principled, category-agnostic solution to the fundamental tension in unsupervised dynamic SLAM: static residuals must supervise motion detection, yet flexible dynamic primitives can overfit those same residuals. This reframes dynamic SLAM from a trajectory-estimation tool with blind spots into a holistic 4D spatial modeling engine, which is important for embodied agents that need to perceive (not just ignore) moving obstacles. However, the system runs at 0.47 FPS, relies on optical flow quality and sufficient static structure, and has not been validated on long sequences or heavily occluded scenes—key open questions for real-world deployment.
