## Problem

Backward Conformal Prediction (BCP) inverts the standard conformal prediction paradigm by fixing prediction set size and estimating the resulting coverage bound, but the application of Markov's inequality to e-variables introduces excessive looseness, creating a large gap between the estimated coverage bound and empirical coverage. This over-conservatism is practically harmful, e.g., forcing safe medical cases into unnecessary manual review. The paper addresses this gap by reshaping the non-conformity score distribution to better align with the tightest regime of Markov's inequality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained image classifier (e.g., ResNet-50), a calibration set of n labeled (X, Y) pairs, a single unlabeled test feature X_{n+1}, and a size constraint rule T that maps (D, X) to a maximum prediction set cardinality. The non-conformity score is the cross-entropy loss S(X, y) for each candidate label y. The output is a prediction set C(X_{n+1}) of candidate labels satisfying |C| ≤ T, together with a leave-one-out estimated coverage bound α̂^{LOO}(h). The size constraint T can be constant, feature-dependent (via softmax entropy), or feature-and-calibration-set-dependent (via PCA-projected k-NN label entropy), and the transformation parameters are computed per candidate label to handle the unknown true label.

## Method

ST-BCP introduces a data-dependent, monotonically non-decreasing transformation h(s; D, X) applied to non-conformity scores before constructing e-variables, with a symmetric parameterization strategy (combining leave-one-out calibration with a pseudo-test point) that preserves exchangeability. The key theoretical result is that under monotonicity, the prediction set is invariant to the choice of h, so the optimization reduces to minimizing E[α̃_{n+1}(h)], which is equivalent to minimizing E[h_i / h(w_{n+1}; D, X)]. The theoretically optimal solution is a step function h*(s;D,X) = a·I(s ≥ w(D,X)) / √P(S(X,Y) ≥ w(D,X) | D,X), but since the conditional probability is unknown, the authors apply an idempotent improvement operator G to the identity transformation, yielding the computable approximation I_w = w(D,X)·I(s ≥ w(D,X)). This step-function structure aligns with the probability-theoretic insight that Markov's inequality is tightest for two-point distributions. The LOO estimator is proven consistent at rate O_P(1/√n) under boundedness and concentration conditions, and experiments show the coverage gap drops from 4.20% to 1.12% on average across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50 (n=200), ST-BCP reduces the average coverage gap (GAP) from 4.21% to 1.12% across all size constraint rules, with the most dramatic improvement under the constant rule T=2 where GAP drops from 5.38% to 0.72% (a ~7× reduction). The empirical miscoverage (MisCov) remains identical before and after transformation, confirming the prediction-set invariance predicted by Theorem 3.2. ST-BCP also consistently lowers the standard deviation (STD) and mean squared error (MSE) of the LOO estimator, e.g., STD drops from 1.28% to 0.89% and MSE from 1.63×10⁻⁴ to 0.79×10⁻⁴ under T=2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BCP with the identity transformation (h=s) from Gauthier et al. (2025b). ST-BCP (h=I_w) wins on every reported metric across all size constraint rules and all three datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet). The headline improvement is in GAP: the average drops from 4.21% to 1.12%, with the largest single-rule gain at T=2 on CIFAR-10 (5.38% → 0.72%). MisCov is exactly preserved in all settings, empirically validating the prediction-set invariance theorem. STD and MSE are also uniformly lower under ST-BCP, indicating a more stable and precise LOO estimator. The advantage is consistent across model architectures (MobileNet, ResNet, EfficientNet, DenseNet) and non-conformity score functions (cross-entropy, APS, rank, threshold), with the most dramatic gains when the baseline score is poorly calibrated (e.g., APS GAP drops from 48.2% to 0.84%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Backward Conformal Prediction (BCP) by Gauthier et al. (2025b), which inverts the standard conformal prediction paradigm by fixing prediction set size and estimating the resulting coverage bound. The mathematical foundation rests on Conformal e-Prediction (Vovk, 2025), which uses e-variables and Markov's inequality to derive coverage guarantees. The key departure is introducing a data-dependent, monotonic transformation of non-conformity scores to reshape their distribution so that Markov's inequality becomes tighter, thereby narrowing the gap between the estimated and empirical coverage. The paper also situates itself within the broader conformal prediction literature on adaptivity, score design, and conditional guarantees, but its specific contribution—the score transformation as a functional optimization problem with a provably superior computable approximation—is novel within the BCP framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is compelling: in safety-critical applications like medical diagnosis, over-conservative coverage bounds force unnecessary human intervention, and ST-BCP's 7-fold reduction in coverage gap directly addresses this. The theoretical contribution (deriving the optimal transformation, proving an improvement operator, and establishing LOO estimator consistency under data-dependent transformations) provides a principled framework that could inform future score design in conformal methods more broadly. However, the method's applicability is currently limited to the BCP setting, the theoretical assumptions are complex (particularly when size constraints depend on both features and calibration data), and the Taylor approximation underlying the coverage guarantee can fail under strict size constraints—issues the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the transformation approach generalizes to other conformal variants and whether the estimator can be shown to achieve strictly lower variance than the baseline.
