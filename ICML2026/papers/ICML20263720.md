## Problem

Classical approximation algorithms for combinatorial optimization provide worst-case guarantees but are distribution-agnostic and non-differentiable, while learned solvers can exploit instance structure but typically require expensive supervision, reinforcement learning, or brittle gradient estimators, and offer no quality guarantees. This paper asks whether a fully differentiable, unsupervised message-passing neural network can simultaneously embed approximation-algorithmic structure (yielding provable guarantees) and adapt to the input distribution (yielding empirically better solutions).

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite metric space (X, d) with |X| = n, encoded as an edge-weighted graph G_S where an edge (u,v) exists iff d(u,v) ≤ 1 (edges with weight > 1 are removed since opening a facility is cheaper than serving from farther than 1), with self-loops added to every vertex. The MPNN outputs a per-vertex opening probability p_v ∈ [0,1] computed via local message passing; these probabilities are then used in a stochastic post-processing step (independent facility opening plus a fallback that opens a facility at any unserved point) to produce the final facility set F ⊆ X.

## Method

The architecture mirrors the distributed SimpleUniformFL algorithm: it discretizes the radius range (0,1] into bins, uses local ReLU-based aggregation over neighbors to estimate which bin each vertex's radius r_x falls into (recovering the Mettu–Plaxton radius characterization), and computes opening probabilities as min(1, c·log n·r̂_x) via a constant-depth FNN head. Training is fully unsupervised via an analytically derived expected-cost loss (Equation 5) that sums expected opening costs (p_f plus a product-of-(1−p_x) fallback term) and expected connection costs (distance-weighted products over closer vertices), requiring no solver calls or discrete relaxations. The key theoretical insight is that specific parameter initializations recover the O(log n) approximation guarantee of the non-learned algorithm, and a finite training set with a differentiable regularizer provably yields parameters that generalize to all UniFL instances of the same size. A recursive variant (UniformFLRecursionStart) extends the guarantee to a constant factor by iteratively applying the probability-based opening to unassigned points.

## Results

On synthetic geometric graphs (1000 vertices, 2D/5D/10D), the MPNN achieves optimality ratios of 1.002–1.009 relative to the ILP solver, consistently outperforming all non-learned baselines (SimpleUFL: 1.069–1.166, RecursiveUFL: 1.010–1.112, O(1)-UFL: 1.030–1.263) while running in ~4 ms per inference. On real-world city-map graphs (Paris, London, Shanghai, LA) where the ILP solver fails to converge within 24 hours, the MPNN achieves the lowest total cost across all four cities, reducing cost by ~41% on Paris versus the solver's incumbent. Size generalization is strong: a model trained on 1000-vertex graphs maintains a ratio of 1.002–1.003 when tested on graphs up to 10,000 vertices without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The MPNN outperforms all non-learned approximation baselines across every dataset. Against SimpleUFL (the non-learned distributed algorithm), the MPNN reduces the optimality gap from 1.069–1.166 to 1.002–1.009, a 6–16% improvement in total cost. Against the stronger RecursiveUFL baseline, the MPNN still wins by 1–11% in optimality ratio (e.g., 1.009 vs. 1.112 on Geo-1000-2). Against O(1)-UFL (Gehweiler et al., 2014), the MPNN is consistently better, with the gap most pronounced on dense 10D graphs (1.003 vs. 1.263). The MPNN also reproduces the solver's opening/connection cost balance (e.g., 381/394 vs. solver's 370/403 on Geo-1000-5), confirming the unsupervised loss captures the correct tradeoff. In the k-Means variant, the MPNN's connection cost (inertia) beats k-Medoids++ by 5–12% but remains slightly behind k-Means++ (which can place centroids in continuous space rather than on graph nodes), and using MPNN-predicted locations as initialization improves both k-Means and k-Medoids by 4–13%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper embeds the radius-based decision rules from classical distributed approximation algorithms for UniFL (Mettu & Plaxton 2003; Badoiu et al. 2005; Gehweiler et al. 2014) directly into a fully differentiable MPNN architecture, departing from prior learned CO solvers that rely on supervision, reinforcement learning, or surrogate gradient estimators for discrete objectives. It is most closely related to Yau et al. (2024), who showed MPNNs can recover SDP-based approximation algorithms for Max-Cut and related problems, but here the algorithmic structure (radius estimation, probabilistic facility opening) is mirrored in the network's computation graph rather than learned end-to-end from solver outputs. The unsupervised training via an analytically derived expected-cost loss (Equation 5) avoids the expensive solver calls required by GNN-augmented branch-and-cut pipelines (Gasse et al. 2019; Khalil et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unsupervised, fully differentiable neural architecture for UniFL with provable worst-case approximation guarantees and provable size generalization, bridging the long-standing gap between distribution-agnostic approximation algorithms and distribution-adaptive learned heuristics. The empirical near-optimality (ratios of 1.002–1.009 vs. O(log n) theoretical bound) and robust generalization to 10× larger graphs without retraining suggest the framework could be a template for other combinatorial problems where algorithmic structure is known. Key limitations include restriction to uniform opening costs and the need for non-trivial redesign to extend to capacitated, non-uniform, or constrained variants; open questions include tighter instance-dependent analyses and characterizing the full class of problems amenable to this algorithmic-structure-embedding approach.
