## Problem

Classical first- and second-order stochastic modified equations (SDEs) used to model discrete optimizers fail to capture discrete-time stepsize stability restrictions, particularly under (L0,L1)-smoothness where no universal constant stepsize guarantees stability uniformly over initializations. The joint interaction of batch noise, communication compression, and adaptive normalization in distributed stochastic optimization under realistic (non-L-smooth) landscapes and general noise regimes (affine variance, heavy tails) remains poorly understood, with no unified continuous-time framework addressing all three factors simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an (L0,L1)-smooth objective (Hessian norm bounded affinely by gradient norm), a per-client gradient noise model (affine (σ₀²,σ₁²)-variance for DCSGD; Student-t with unbounded mean for DSignSGD), an unbiased compressor with rate ω, and a Robbins-Monro stepsize schedule ηη_t. It produces first-order weak-approximation SDEs whose drift includes a curvature-dependent correction term, from which explicit learning-rate stability constraints and convergence bounds are derived. Unlike classical SDEs that impose no stepsize restriction in the noiseless case, the new models recover the correct η < 2/L₀ threshold and reveal geometry-dependent normalization requirements that couple compression rate, noise structure, and landscape regularity.

## Method

The key insight is that the sign of the Hessian correction in the SDE drift must be flipped relative to the classical second-order modified equation: instead of −(η/2)∇²f∇f, the new models use +(η/2)∇²f∇f, making them first-order weak approximations (not second-order) that nonetheless recover the exact discrete-time stability threshold. This is derived via an ansatz approach: matching the continuous-time loss drift df(X_t) to the discrete-time generator expansion [f(x_{k+1})−f(x_k)]/η up to O(η), which uniquely determines the coefficient α=1/2. The resulting stability-faithful SDEs are then applied to DCSGD (with unbiased compression and affine-variance noise) and DSignSGD (with heavy-tailed Student-t noise), yielding convergence bounds where the admissible stepsize region explicitly depends on (L0,L1)-geometry, compression rate ω, dimension d, and noise parameters. For DCSGD, the bound shows that the required normalization strength is jointly determined by compression, variance growth, and landscape curvature; for DSignSGD, the sign update provides implicit normalization that ensures convergence under heavy tails with standard diminishing schedules.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical convergence and stability bounds. For DCSGD (Thm. 4.2), the stability condition is ηη_t < 2ε / [G(1 + (ω̄ + d(σ̄₁²ω + σ̄₁²))/N) + L₁d(σ̄₀² + σ̄₀²ω)/N], where G = L₀ + L₁·E[‖∇f(X_t)‖₂], which in the noiseless, uncompressed, L-smooth limit recovers the classical η < 2/L₀ restriction. For DSignSGD (Thm. 4.3), convergence holds under Student-t heavy-tailed noise (even ν=1, where the mean is unbounded) with a diminishing schedule η_t = (1+t)^{-a}, a=1/2, and the stepsize bound ηη_t < ℓ_ν/K where K includes an additional baseline term √d(L₀+L₁)M_ν absent in classic SDE analyses. Empirical sanity checks on MLPs (N=8 clients, CIFAR-10) confirm that the predicted normalization (Eq. 15) stabilizes DCSGD under affine-variance Gaussian noise with sparsification at all compression levels ω, while DSignSGD with η_k = 1/√(k+1) converges across Student-t noise scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional empirical baseline comparisons; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The central comparison is between the paper's stability-faithful SDEs and the classic first- and second-order SDEs from Li et al. (2017): the classic models predict unconditional convergence for all stepsizes (and even accelerated convergence at large η for the second-order model), while the paper's models correctly recover the discrete-time stability threshold η < 2/L₀ in the quadratic case and the initialization-dependent restriction in the quartic (L₀,L₁)-smooth case. In terms of theoretical coverage (Table 1/3), the paper is the first to simultaneously handle (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, affine-variance noise, heavy-tailed noise, and gradient compression within a continuous-time SDE framework; prior discrete-time works (e.g., Khiriat et al. 2024 for compression, Faw et al. 2023 for affine variance) each cover only a subset of these factors. The empirical sanity checks (Figure 1) are explicitly framed as mechanism validation rather than competitive benchmarking, confirming that the predicted instability of unnormalized DCSGD and the stabilization via the Thm. 4.2 normalization prescription appear in controlled MLP training with N=8 clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDE-based modeling framework introduced by Li et al. (2017) for stochastic optimizers, but identifies a fundamental flaw: both the classic first-order and second-order modified equations fail to capture discrete-time stepsize stability restrictions, particularly under (L0,L1)-smoothness. The authors correct this by flipping the sign of the Hessian correction in the drift (from negative to positive), yielding new first-order SDEs that are formally proven to be first-order weak approximations while recovering the correct stability thresholds. The distributed compression analysis builds directly on Compagnoni et al. (2025a), and the (L0,L1)-smoothness condition follows Zhang et al. (2020b), but no prior work had combined SDE modeling, (L0,L1)-smoothness, unbiased compression, and general noise (affine variance or heavy-tailed) in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used SDE surrogates can predict unconditional convergence or even accelerated convergence where the discrete optimizer actually diverges has broad implications: learning-rate schedules, batch-size control schemes, and scaling laws derived from these SDEs may push practitioners outside the true stable regime. The paper provides concrete, interpretable stability regions that make explicit how compression rate, noise structure, and landscape geometry jointly determine the required normalization level. Limitations include homogeneous clients, server-aggregated topology, unbiased/signed compression only, and guarantees stated for the continuous-time surrogate rather than the discrete algorithm; extensions to heterogeneous clients, error-feedback, biased compressors, and momentum/adaptive optimizers remain open.
