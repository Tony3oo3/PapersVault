## Problem

Data pricing mechanisms for ML training assume the buyer can evaluate the value of each data source exactly, but in practice this requires repeated retraining that is infeasible at frontier scale. Prior work studies either compute-aware data selection without strategic sellers, or strategic procurement assuming exact evaluation; combining the two is non-trivial because approximate or early-stopped allocation rules can break monotonicity, destroying the existence of truthful payments. This paper makes compute cost a first-class constraint in the mechanism-design problem and characterizes the resulting compute–truthfulness–utility tradeoff.

## Input / Output

The buyer receives k data distributions D_i (with known second-moment matrices Σ_i), reported per-sample costs ĉ_i, a data budget B, and a cost-of-compute parameter λ that converts oracle compute units to monetary units. The mechanism outputs a Poissonized allocation vector π ∈ Δ^k (fraction of budget per source, yielding random sample counts n_i ~ Poisson(Bπ_i/p_i)), per-sample payments p_i, an oracle accuracy schedule {ε_t}, and a stopping time T. Unusually, the buyer never retrains on the full dataset; instead it queries a data attribution oracle that returns an ε-approximate gradient of the expected loss w.r.t. the mixture at cost γ(ε), and payments are computed with a single call to the allocation rule rather than the k/ε re-runs that Myerson's integral formula would naively require.

## Method

The paper introduces a data attribution oracle abstraction that uniformly captures proxy training, deduplication, influence functions, and Shapley-based attribution as maps O: Δ^k × R_{>0} → R^k with cost γ(ε), then instantiates it for well-specified OLS using Hutchinson's trace estimator combined with conjugate gradient to compute ∇f(π) to precision ε at cost O(kd²√κ/ε). A key result shows the optimal oracle accuracy schedule is back-loaded: because early gradient errors are geometrically forgotten (contracted by ρ = 1 − μη per step), the buyer should use coarse/cheap oracles early and fine/expensive oracles late, with a closed-form geometric schedule when γ follows a power law. The paper then shows that even one step of projected gradient descent with an ε-approximate oracle can produce a non-monotone allocation rule (breaking truthfulness), and restores ξ-approximate monotonicity by adding a strongly convex regularizer αR(w) whose gradient is free to compute, improving both the condition number and the effective oracle accuracy at no extra cost. Finally, adapting Babaioff et al.'s implicit payment computation, the mechanism computes both the allocation and Myerson-consistent payments with a single call to the (approximately monotone) allocation rule, achieving ξ-truthfulness in expectation with zero additional compute overhead.

## Results

The paper is purely theoretical and presents no empirical benchmarks. Key quantitative results include: (1) a gradient oracle (Algorithm 1) with error bound |ĝ_i − ∇_i f(π)| ≤ ε + 1024σ_x⁴d²/(m³B³) + 32Md(1+δ)/(m²B) under subgaussian and spectral conditions; (2) convergence of oracle-driven gradient descent at rate f(π̄_t) − f(π*) ≤ O((1/t)·Σ_s ε_s); (3) a threshold condition λ ≥ η‖v‖² − η²‖H‖_F²/M³ below which the compute-optimal policy is to forgo data selection entirely; and (4) a closed-form geometric schedule ε_{t+1}* = ρ^{1/(p+1)}·ε_t* for power-law oracle costs γ(ε)=aε^{−p}, confirming that compute should be back-loaded (coarse oracles early, fine oracles late).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or head-to-head method comparisons. The paper positions itself relative to two foundational results it extends: Myerson's (1981) monotonicity characterization, which the paper shows holds for the exact optimal procurement rule (Theorem 1) but breaks under compute-constrained suboptimal solutions (a concrete 2-seller counterexample in Appendix D.2); and Babaioff et al.'s (2015) implicit payment computation, which the paper adapts to the data-procurement setting to achieve zero-overhead truthful payments (Informal Theorem 3). The paper also contrasts its compute-aware framework with prior data-selection literature (Kolossov et al., 2023; Hashimoto, 2021) that assumes exact evaluation, showing that the compute-fidelity tradeoff introduces a new performance–truthfulness tension absent in those settings. No numerical comparisons against competing mechanisms or allocation algorithms are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate literatures: compute-aware data selection (optimal experimental design, data Shapley, influence functions) and mechanism design for data procurement (Myerson 1981; Agarwal et al. 2019; Cong et al. 2022). Prior work on data pricing assumes the buyer can evaluate allocations exactly, while prior work on compute-efficient data selection ignores strategic sellers. The paper's key departure is making the compute cost of the attribution oracle a first-class constraint in the mechanism-design problem, and it builds on Babaioff et al. (2015) to achieve zero-overhead payment computation. The gradient oracle for linear regression (Hutchinson estimator + conjugate gradient) repurposes standard numerical linear algebra tools in a new context, and the Poissonized acquisition parameterization connects to optimal A-design theory (Pukelsheim 2006).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine pathology: in the presence of compute constraints, even a single step of gradient descent with a valid ε-approximate oracle can break monotonicity of the allocation rule, which in turn precludes any truthful payment scheme. This is practically relevant for frontier AI labs that must procure data under tight compute budgets and cannot afford to re-run expensive optimization pipelines for payment computation. The strong-convexification fix and the zero-overhead payment mechanism offer concrete remedies, but the analysis is restricted to linear regression (convex, well-specified) and a single-buyer Poissonized model. Open questions include extending to nonconvex learners (e.g., neural networks), multi-buyer or competitive market settings, and empirical validation of the back-loaded compute schedule in real data-mixture optimization pipelines.
