## Problem

Existing VLM-based video understanding methods operate as 'naive empiricists,' mapping video input directly to output without any mechanism to introspect or challenge the model's own inherent biases. Even recent RL approaches (Video-R1, VersaVid-R1) remain within this paradigm, passively responding to external evidence and relying on costly manually crafted adversarial samples to expose deficiencies. The paper asks whether video reasoning can be reframed as a Bayesian cognitive process where the model first generates and then critically integrates its own prior hypotheses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy model receives three inputs: the video (16/32/64 frames at 128×28×28 during training, 256×28×28 at inference), the text query, and K=8 self-prior hypotheses (each containing a reasoning path and answer) sampled from a frozen base model (Qwen2.5-VL). The output is structured into two programmatically extractable components: a reasoning path enclosed in <think> tags and a final answer in <answer> tags (single letter for multiple-choice, numerical value, or free-form text). This structured separation is essential because DUS operates on the answer block while PTS computes similarity on the think block.

## Method

Video-BCI implements a two-stage Bayesian cognitive process: a base sampler π₀ generates K=8 prior hypotheses from the video and query, and a trainable policy model π_θ is trained via GRPO to critically integrate these priors with external evidence. Training is guided by a composite Cognitive Utility Function (CUF) with two intrinsic signals. The Dialectical Uncertainty Signal (DUS) rewards correct answers that challenge the prior majority consensus, computed as the product of normalized entropy (cognitive conflict among priors) and challenge strength (complement of the answer's empirical frequency), activated only when the answer is verified correct. The Process Tracing Signal (PTS) performs self-driven reasoning distillation by computing a weighted similarity between the current reasoning path and superior prior reasoning paths, where weights are proportional to the accuracy improvement each prior path offers over the current answer. No SFT or CoT annotations are required; the entire framework is trained purely with RL on the Video-R1-260k dataset.

## Results

On six video understanding benchmarks, Video-BCI-R-7B (64 frames) achieves 39.4 on VSI-Bench, 54.4 on VideoMMMU, 69.6 on MMVU, 67.8 on MVBench, 73.8 on TempCompass, and 62.9 on VideoMME, setting new SOTA across all six. The headline gain is a 3.8-point absolute improvement on MMVU over the previous best (Video-RTS-7B at 66.3) and a 5.8-point gain over Video-R1-7B (63.8). At 16 frames, Video-BCI-7B already surpasses Video-R1-7B by 3.8 points on MMVU (68.0 vs 64.2) and 1.6 points on VSI-Bench (36.2 vs 34.6). The full model (DUS+PTS) consistently outperforms the DUS-only variant, e.g., 54.4 vs 53.0 on VideoMMMU and 62.9 vs 62.6 on VideoMME at 64 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source RL baseline Video-R1-7B (64 frames), Video-BCI-R-7B wins on every benchmark: +2.3 on VSI-Bench (39.4 vs 37.1), +2.0 on VideoMMMU (54.4 vs 52.4), +5.8 on MMVU (69.6 vs 63.8), +3.0 on MVBench (67.8 vs 64.8), +0.6 on TempCompass (73.8 vs 73.2), and +1.5 on VideoMME (62.9 vs 61.4). It also surpasses Video-RTS-7B by 3.3 points on MMVU (69.6 vs 66.3) and 1.7 on VideoMMMU (54.4 vs 52.7), and GRPO-CARE-7B by 3.8 on MMVU (68.0 vs 65.8 at 32 frames) and 2.8 on VideoMMMU. Against SFT-based models, the gains are even larger: Video-BCI-R-7B exceeds Kangaroo-8B by 6.7 on MVBench (67.8 vs 61.1), 11.3 on TempCompass (73.8 vs 62.5), and 6.9 on VideoMME (62.9 vs 56.0). Against proprietary models, Video-BCI-R-7B surpasses GPT-4o on VSI-Bench (39.4 vs 34.0) and VideoMMMU (54.4 vs 53.4), but trails on MMVU (69.6 vs 75.4) and VideoMME (62.9 vs 71.9), indicating that the gap to closed-source models remains on general-purpose tasks. The DUS-only variant (Video-BCI-7B) already beats all RL baselines, while adding PTS (Video-BCI-R-7B) provides a consistent 0.5–1.4 point boost on VideoMMMU and VideoMME, confirming the complementary contribution of both signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm for video understanding (established by DeepSeek-R1 and applied to video by Video-R1) by introducing a two-stage self-reflective mechanism: the model first samples K prior hypotheses from a base VLM, then a policy model critically integrates these priors with external evidence. It departs from prior RL-for-video work (Video-R1, VersaVid-R1, GRPO-CARE) that the authors characterize as 'naive empiricist'—direct input-to-output mapping—by adding two novel intrinsic reward signals (DUS and PTS) that operate on the model's own sampled hypothesis space rather than on external adversarial data. The sampling template and training data are directly inherited from Video-R1, making this a methodological extension rather than a new data pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical value lies in eliminating the need for manually crafted CoT annotations or adversarial diagnostic datasets, relying instead on the model's own prior samples as both training signal and reasoning scaffold. The inference efficiency result (policy generation at 44s vs. 70s for the base model, with 8-prior sampling adding only ~34% overhead via vLLM batching) makes the approach deployable. However, the 'Bayesian cognitive' framing is somewhat loose—the mechanism is essentially reward shaping over self-sampled hypotheses—and the evaluation is limited to a single 7B backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) at up to 64 frames, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or longer videos. A key open question is whether the DUS/PTS signals transfer to non-video multimodal tasks or to settings where the base model's priors are systematically uninformative.
