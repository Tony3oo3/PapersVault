## Problem

Existing analyses of Chain-of-Thought reasoning focus on local properties—individual steps, layers, or state transitions—leaving the global dynamics of how reasoning evolves from exploration to convergence uncharacterized. Without understanding when a model has reliably converged, inference systems cannot safely terminate early or prioritize high-quality trajectories, leading to substantial computational waste from redundant token generation after the answer is already determined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265224/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and an LLM, and at each reasoning step T_i it prompts the model with an answer-inducing suffix (e.g., 'The answer is \boxed') to obtain an intermediate answer A_i whose token-level predictive entropy H_i is computed. Two pre-calibrated entropy distributions (f_0 for the Uncertainty Region, f_1 for the Confidence Region) are estimated via histogram density estimation on 100 trajectories from Bespoke-Stratos-17k, along with a single CUSUM threshold h. In early-exit mode the output is a truncated CoT trajectory and final answer; in test-time scaling mode the output is a CUSUM-weighted vote across N sampled trajectories, where each trajectory's weight is its final CUSUM statistic S_final.

## Method

The paper discovers that CoT reasoning follows a two-phase entropy structure: a high-entropy Uncertainty Region of stochastic exploration followed by an abrupt transition to a low-entropy Confidence Region of deterministic convergence, characterized by high answer reliability (>60% accuracy) and high redundancy (>30% of tokens generated post-convergence). Detection of this transition is formalized as a sequential change-point detection problem with a minimax objective that minimizes worst-case detection delay subject to a false-alarm constraint. The CUSUM algorithm is applied as the asymptotically optimal detector: at each step, a log-likelihood ratio Z_i = log(f_1(H_i)/f_0(H_i)) is computed, accumulated recursively as S_i = max(0, S_{i-1} + Z_i), and generation stops when S_i exceeds threshold h. The approach is entirely training-free—no model fine-tuning is required—and the single hyperparameter h directly controls the reliability-efficiency trade-off, with theoretical guarantees of exponential false-alarm control and near-minimal detection delay proportional to 1/D_KL(f_1||f_0).

## Results

On early exit, CUSUM achieves 63.06% average accuracy with 11.1% token reduction across three models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, Qwen3-14B) and three benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, GPQA-Diamond), outperforming DEER (59.78%) and Dynasor (58.70%) by 3.28 and 4.36 percentage points respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on GPQA with DeepSeek-7B, where CUSUM reaches 40.40% accuracy versus 35.73% (DEER) and 19.32% (Dynasor). For test-time scaling on AIME25, CUSUM-weighted voting consistently surpasses self-consistency, with the gap widening from a slim margin at N=2 to a 3.33% lead at N=64 trajectories for Qwen3-4B. CUSUM also achieves a superior Pareto frontier over both baselines across all three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DEER (Yang et al., 2025a), a single-step high-confidence exit method, CUSUM wins on accuracy across all three models while achieving comparable or greater token reduction: on DeepSeek-7B, CUSUM gains 4.47 points (44.44% vs. 39.97%) with 15.5% vs. 14.7% token savings; on Qwen3-14B, CUSUM gains 4.35 points (71.45% vs. 67.10%) with 11.1% vs. 10.3% savings. Against Dynasor (Fu et al., 2025a), a repetitive-answer-pattern detector, CUSUM's advantage is even larger on DeepSeek-7B (44.44% vs. 36.37%, a 8.07-point gap) and Qwen3-14B (71.45% vs. 68.83%), while matching token reduction on Qwen3-4B (5.6% for both). The gap is most dramatic on GPQA with DeepSeek-7B, where Dynasor collapses to 19.32% accuracy versus CUSUM's 40.40%. For test-time scaling, CUSUM-weighted voting outperforms Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) across all models and trajectory counts (N=2 to 64), with the advantage growing as N increases—reaching 3.33% on Qwen3-4B at N=64—because the final CUSUM score effectively separates correct from incorrect trajectories, unlike uniform majority voting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical CUSUM change-point detection algorithm (Page, 1954; Lorden, 1971) — a 70-year-old tool from sequential statistical analysis — to monitor Chain-of-Thought reasoning, framing early exit as a minimax-optimality problem over predictive entropy. It departs from prior heuristic early-exit methods (DEER, Dynasor, EAT, HALT-CoT) that rely on per-step confidence thresholds or answer-stability checks, which the paper argues are vulnerable to transient entropy fluctuations in the Uncertainty Region. For test-time scaling, it extends self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) by replacing uniform majority voting with CUSUM-score-weighted aggregation, using the final cumulative statistic as a trajectory quality indicator. The empirical observation of a two-phase entropy structure (abrupt Uncertainty→Confidence transition) is positioned as the first systematic analysis of global entropy dynamics across the full CoT trajectory, complementing prior local analyses of individual steps or layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, training-free framework with provable false-alarm control and asymptotic minimax optimality for CoT early exit — properties that heuristic baselines lack — and demonstrates that a single hyperparameter (threshold h) governs the reliability-efficiency trade-off. The finding that 63% of trajectories exhibit oscillating entropy (re-entering uncertainty after an initial convergence signal) explains why simple threshold methods fail and motivates cumulative evidence accumulation. Limitations include the need for per-model calibration on ~100 trajectories, evaluation on only three models (4B–14B), and moderate absolute gains (3–4 points over DEER/Dynasor). Open questions include whether the two-phase structure persists in larger frontier models, multi-turn agentic settings, or non-mathematical reasoning tasks, and whether the histogram-based density estimation of f₀ and f₁ remains robust under distribution shift.
