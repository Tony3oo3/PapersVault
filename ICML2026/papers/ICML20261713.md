## Problem

TextGrad-style textual backpropagation fails in deep compound AI systems because feedback signals mix local critiques with upstream context (Semantic Entanglement), causing three distinct failure modes: Signal Blockage (upstream root causes go unaddressed), Downstream Over-correction (downstream components hallucinate fixes for upstream errors), and Upstream Pollution (local reasoning errors leak backward and destabilize correct components). Existing methods treat the forward pass as lossy rewriting and the backward pass as a flat conversation, making precise credit assignment structurally impossible as chain depth increases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a compound AI system defined as a DAG of LLM agents (each with a learnable prompt variable), a task dataset with input-output pairs, and a global reward function. The forward pass maintains a shared key-value context dictionary where each component receives only its declared input_fields (a projection Π_ℓ of the context) rather than the full trajectory, and returns outputs merged by key-wise update. The backward pass produces structured two-section feedback (LOCAL and UPSTREAM) per component, where UPSTREAM can be the literal token STOP_GRADIENT to halt propagation, and the scheduler outputs a single component selection per step via Boltzmann sampling over accumulated local-error counts.

## Method

TEXTRESNET reformulates textual optimization as a structured semantic routing problem via four coupled mechanisms. In the forward pass, each agent produces an Additive Semantic Delta (Δ_l) that is concatenated with the preserved upstream context (h_l = h_{l-1} ⊕ Δ_l), creating an Identity Highway that keeps historical context explicitly accessible for attribution at any depth. In the backward pass, a Semantic Projector (a Backward LLM performing causal attribution) decomposes each feedback signal into causally independent local and upstream subspaces, enabling Causal Routing: purely local errors emit STOP_GRADIENT to upstream (preventing pollution), purely upstream errors flow directly along the identity highway without triggering local updates (preventing over-correction), and mixed faults are split. A Density-Aware Scheduler then tracks the accumulated count of locally-projected feedback per node (Gradient Density ρ) and selects which component to optimize at each step via Boltzmann sampling, concentrating compute on true bottlenecks. The theoretical contribution includes a proof that under a linear noise model, Causal Routing bounds noise variance by (1-p)/p · σ² independent of depth L, versus linear growth Lσ² without routing.

## Results

TEXTRESNET achieves the best score across all four benchmarks: 46.23 F1 on HotpotQA, 37.86% pass rate on BigCodeBench, 60.31% accuracy on PubMedQA, and 41.75 MRR on STARK-PRIME. The largest gain is on HotpotQA (+21.37 F1 over TextGrad), where the 5-component pipeline's depth exposes TextGrad's signal blockage. On BigCodeBench, TEXTRESNET improves over DSPy by +4.05 Pass% and over TextGrad by +2.15 Pass%. The method also consumes approximately 3× fewer tokens than TextGrad (21k vs. 63k) while achieving nearly double the HotpotQA F1 score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEXTRESNET outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark. Against the primary baseline TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), the gains range from +0.44 MRR on STARK-PRIME to +21.37 F1 on HotpotQA, with the largest advantage appearing where multi-hop dependency chains are longest. Against DSPy/MIPRO (Khattab et al., 2022), TEXTRESNET leads by +1.33 F1 on HotpotQA, +4.05 Pass% on BigCodeBench, +0.05 Acc on PubMedQA, and +0.35 MRR on STARK-PRIME, confirming that structured signal routing complements rather than replaces programmatic prompt compilation. The TextGrad+Sum variant (Chen et al., 2025), which compresses feedback via summarization, consistently underperforms vanilla TextGrad, indicating that naive compression worsens semantic entanglement. CoT (Wei et al., 2022) and HBC (Le et al., 2018) serve as unoptimized and imitation-learning lower bounds respectively; TEXTRESNET exceeds both by wide margins on all tasks, with HBC performing worst overall (e.g., 21.16 F1 on HotpotQA). Notably, the paper excludes OPTIMAS from direct comparison due to its reliance on RL training and auxiliary reward modeling, arguing the supervision paradigms are fundamentally different.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the textual backpropagation paradigm introduced by TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025) by importing geometric principles from residual learning—specifically Deep Delta Learning and Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections—into the discrete semantic space. Where TextGrad treats the forward pass as lossy rewriting and the backward pass as a flat conversation, TEXTRESNET restructures both: the forward pass becomes an additive identity-plus-delta operation preserving an 'Identity Highway,' and the backward pass becomes a structured routing problem via a Semantic Projector that decomposes feedback into causally independent local and upstream subspaces. The approach is positioned as orthogonal to RL-based methods like OPTIMAS, which require training auxiliary reward models, whereas TEXTRESNET is a training-free architectural constraint on how textual gradients flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying compound AI systems: as pipelines grow deeper, standard textual optimization degrades due to signal entanglement, and the proposed routing mechanism offers a training-free fix that also cuts token consumption by roughly 3×. The emergent phase-transition behavior (upstream errors resolved first, then local refinement) suggests the architecture implicitly learns a curriculum, which could inform future scheduling strategies. Open questions include whether the simplified linear variance model (Proposition 4.2) holds under non-linear semantic interactions, whether the LLM-based Semantic Projector can be replaced by more principled or cheaper attribution mechanisms, and how the approach scales to systems with branching (non-DAG) topologies or hundreds of components.
