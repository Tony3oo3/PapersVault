## Problem

Existing PTQ methods that combine learning-based rounding (e.g., AdaRound) with Hessian-based error compensation (e.g., GPTQ) suffer from intrinsic spatiotemporal misalignment in their decoupled 'compensate-then-learn' paradigm: compensation updates invalidate the RTN assumption that adaptive rounding later overturns (temporal conflict), and local rounding decisions ignore global loss-landscape correlations (spatial conflict). This paper identifies these conflicts as the fundamental bottleneck preventing the synergistic combination of fast compensation and accurate learned rounding.

## Input / Output

STLA consumes pre-trained FP16 LLM weights (OPT, LLaMA2, LLaMA3 families) and 128 unlabeled calibration segments of 2048 tokens each (sampled from C4) to compute the per-layer Hessian. It outputs group-wise (group size 256) or per-channel 2-bit/3-bit quantized weights, with the cluster size matched to the group size to keep quantization parameters (scale, zero-point) temporally consistent. The entire quantization pipeline runs on a single GPU (RTX 5090 for ≤7B models, H100 for 70B), requiring no retraining or labeled data.

## Method

STLA reformulates PTQ as a unified cluster-wise optimization that simultaneously performs quantization parameter search, differentiable rounding, and error compensation. Spatial alignment is achieved through a lookahead global objective derived via the Schur complement of the inverse Hessian, which projects the full reconstruction loss onto the current cluster subspace by anticipating the optimal future compensation of remaining weights; this is computed stably via Cholesky-factored triangular solves rather than explicit matrix inversion. Temporal alignment is achieved by setting cluster size equal to group size, so the aggregate quantization error of an entire cluster is propagated to remaining weights in a single update, preventing the weight-distribution drift that invalidates pre-computed quantization parameters. A Hessian-guided SpinCluster algorithm reorders weights into clusters that maximize intra-cluster error cancellation by exploiting both diagonal sensitivity and off-diagonal correlation structure, assigning opposing rounding 'spins' to strongly coupled weights. The per-iteration cost drops from O(d_in²·d_out) (full-space) to O(d_in·d_out·c) (subspace), and convergence requires only ~200 iterations versus ~2000 for the decoupled baseline.

## Results

On 3-bit group-wise weight quantization (group size 256), STLA achieves the lowest PPL across all five evaluated models. For LLaMA2-7B, STLA reaches 6.09 WikiText-2 / 7.99 C4 PPL, outperforming GPTQ (6.54/8.42), AWQ (6.44/8.49), and GPTAQ (6.24/8.22). The advantage is most dramatic at 2-bit, where STLA achieves 10.02 WikiText-2 PPL on LLaMA2-7B versus 30.10 for GPTQ and 18.86 for GPTAQ, while AWQ collapses entirely (2.29×10⁵). On zero-shot accuracy (3-bit, LLaMA2-7B), STLA attains 61.88% average, the closest to the FP16 baseline of 64.16%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STLA consistently outperforms all three primary baselines (GPTQ, AWQ, GPTAQ) on both PPL and zero-shot accuracy across every model and bit-width tested. At 3-bit, the gains over GPTQ are largest on smaller models (e.g., 12.88 PPL reduction on OPT-125M WikiText-2) and more modest but still consistent on larger models (0.45 PPL on LLaMA2-7B). At 2-bit, the separation becomes extreme: STLA achieves 10.02 WikiText-2 PPL on LLaMA2-7B while GPTQ reaches 30.10, GPTAQ 18.86, and AWQ diverges to 2.29×10⁵. Against the decoupled learning-based baseline AESPA (NeurIPS 2024), STLA matches or beats PPL while reducing GPU runtime by 1.7×–4.6× (e.g., 40.6 min vs. 2.6 hr on LLaMA2-7B). Compared to the vector-quantization method QuIP# (PTQ), STLA achieves lower PPL (10.02 vs. 12.30 on LLaMA2-7B WikiText-2 at 2-bit) using only scalar quantization, though QAT-based QuIP# and QTIP still lead by a margin. STLA also integrates cleanly with rotation-based preprocessing (QuaRot, SpinQuant), consistently improving over GPTQ/GPTAQ within the same rotation pipeline (Table 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

STLA sits at the intersection of two PTQ lineages: learning-based adaptive rounding (AdaRound, BRECQ, FlexRound) and second-order error compensation (OBQ, GPTQ, GPTAQ). Rather than following the decoupled 'compensate-then-learn' pipeline (as in AESPA), STLA colocates both phases within a cluster-wise loop, using a Schur-complement-derived lookahead objective to unify the loss landscape across rounding and compensation. The SpinCluster strategy is a novel Hessian-guided reordering that departs from the natural weight ordering used by GPTQ and prior learning-based methods, explicitly grouping strongly correlated weights to maximize intra-cluster error cancellation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-bit results are particularly impactful: STLA achieves 10.02 PPL on LLaMA2-7B WikiText-2 versus 30.10 for GPTQ, a gap that suggests the spatiotemporal misalignment diagnosis captures a real failure mode in existing low-bit PTQ. The method is practical (single 32GB GPU, ~40 min for 7B models) and composes cleanly with rotation-based preprocessing (QuaRot, SpinQuant), making it a drop-in upgrade for existing deployment pipelines. Open questions include whether the scalar-quantization ceiling can be further lowered by combining STLA's cluster-wise optimization with vector quantization, and whether the calibration-data dependency (128 C4 segments) can be reduced or eliminated for truly data-free deployment.
