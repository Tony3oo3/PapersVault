## Problem

Existing VAU evaluation metrics (N-gram, embedding-based, and LLM-based) conflate anomaly semantics with expression style and normal content, causing scores to drift when correct predictions are paraphrased or embedded in lengthy normal context. Compounding this, the field lacks a meta-evaluation benchmark to validate whether any given metric actually reflects anomaly understanding, leaving reported model improvements potentially attributable to metric artifacts rather than genuine progress.

## Input / Output

CG-CoE takes a prediction text and a reference label text (both free-form descriptions of video anomalies) and produces event-level Precision, Recall, and a bipartite match relation M ⊆ Eâ × Eˡ. Each extracted event is stored with a verbatim evidence span from the source text and an evidence-anchored rewrite, and label-side events carry a lightweight class hint (JSON field) used only to gate Stage-B matching candidates. The full output is a structured JSON conforming to a fixed schema, making the evaluation traceable and verifiable.

## Method

CG-CoE is a three-step chain-of-evaluation instantiated with DeepSeek-V3 as the backbone LLM. Step 1 extracts de-duplicated minimal anomalous events from both texts (splitting multi-act sentences, merging paraphrases) and rewrites each into a uniform style with a verbatim evidence span, while also producing a per-event class hint from the label. Step 2 builds the match relation in two stages: Stage A performs strict evidence-span entailment matching (same minimal act/outcome with consistent key arguments), and Stage B applies only to still-unmatched label events, using the class hint to gate candidates and allow flexible matching within a class-defined tolerance boundary (partial matches from split/merge, no cross-class substitution, compositional support via minimal supporting sets). Step 3 computes event-level Precision and Recall using the same counting convention as the human annotation protocol, where each prediction event contributes at most once to Precision regardless of how many label events it supports. The class-guided tolerance boundary is the key differentiator: it bounds semantic flexibility to class-relevant semantics, preventing mismatched substitutions while recovering events that differ in granularity or detail.

## Results

On the AEA validity benchmark, CG-CoE achieves the highest Spearman rank correlations with human event-level judgments: ρ_Prec = 0.800 and ρ_Rec = 0.813, outperforming the strongest LLM-based baseline (VAU-R1 classification, 0.731/0.735) by +0.069 and +0.078 respectively. On the CVP robustness benchmark, CG-CoE(Recall) achieves the lowest mean within-group score variation E[Δ] = 0.051 among all LLM-based metrics, reducing variation by 29%–39% compared to the best LLM baseline. Notably, BARTScore achieves the lowest overall CVP variation (0.008) but at the cost of very poor AEA validity (ρ_Prec = 0.313), illustrating that robustness alone does not guarantee anomaly-event faithfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-CoE decisively outperforms all N-gram-based (BLEU, ROUGE-L) and embedding-based (BERTScore, BARTScore, UniEval) baselines on AEA validity, with the gap ranging from +0.37 to +0.57 in Spearman correlation, confirming that holistic lexical or semantic similarity cannot capture anomaly-event alignment. Among LLM-based baselines, CG-CoE beats HAWK (consistency/reasonability: 0.710/0.706) and all three VAU-R1 dimensions (best: 0.731/0.735) on both ρ_Prec and ρ_Rec. On CVP robustness, CG-CoE(Recall) achieves the lowest E[Δ] = 0.051 among LLM-based metrics, reducing variation by ~39% versus HAWK (0.099) and ~38% versus VAU-R1 classification (0.083). BARTScore is an outlier: it achieves the lowest CVP variation (0.008) but the second-worst AEA validity (0.313), demonstrating that an overly insensitive metric can appear robust while failing to reflect actual anomaly understanding. The paper further validates that CG-CoE's advantage is backbone-agnostic, consistently outperforming HAWK and VAU-R1 across Claude-Sonnet-4-6, DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini-3.1, and GPT-5.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) the VAU task itself, advanced by HAWK and Holmes-VAU, which use MLLMs to describe and reason about video anomalies, and (2) meta-evaluation benchmarks from summarization (SummEval), machine translation (WMT Metrics Shared Task), and open-domain QA (QAEval), which validate metrics against human judgments and controlled perturbations. Rather than extending a specific prior metric, CG-CoE repurposes the human annotation workflow—extracting minimal anomalous events and matching them under class-specific tolerance—as an automated evaluation procedure, departing from the holistic similarity paradigm of N-gram, embedding, and prompt-based LLM metrics. The CVP subset adapts the controlled-perturbation robustness testing from translation and summarization meta-evaluation to the anomaly-specific setting, where the invariant to preserve is anomaly semantics rather than overall meaning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a foundational reliability problem: if VAU metrics are sensitive to expression style and normal content, then reported model improvements may be artifacts rather than genuine progress. The finding that BARTScore achieves near-zero CVP variation (0.008) yet poor AEA validity (ρ=0.313) is a useful caution that robustness alone does not guarantee anomaly-event faithfulness. Key limitations include the benchmark's reliance on a single source dataset (Holmes-VAU), the concentration of human annotations at the (0,0) and (1,1) extremes which limits discriminative power for multi-anomaly scenarios, and the text-only evaluation setting that cannot handle underspecified references. Open questions include whether CG-CoE generalizes across diverse VAU datasets with different anomaly taxonomies, and whether incorporating direct video access would resolve the reference-completeness limitation.
