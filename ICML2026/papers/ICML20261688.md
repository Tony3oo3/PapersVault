## Problem

Theoretical results show that Transformers can simulate RASP programs, and the RASP length-generalization conjecture posits that length-generalizing models implement short RASP algorithms internally. However, no method existed to actually extract such interpretable programs from trained Transformers: circuit discovery yields computation graphs tied to specific input templates and lengths, not length-uniform algorithms, and prior theoretical translations produce one formula per hidden dimension, which is not individually interpretable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained GPT-2-style Transformer with absolute positional encodings (satisfying the Linear Layer Norm Assumption) and produces a D-RASP program whose variables live in interpretable spaces (token basis of dimension |Σ| or position basis of dimension T) rather than the model's hidden dimension d. Faithfulness is measured as match accuracy—the fraction of input sequences (up to length 150) where the extracted program makes the same next-token prediction as the original model at all positions receiving training signal. Decompilation is declared successful when match accuracy reaches ≥90%.

## Method

The pipeline has two steps. Step 1 (faithful reparameterization) translates the Transformer into a D-RASP program under the Linear Layer Norm Assumption, where each path through the residual stream becomes a variable and attention heads become select+aggregate operations; this program exactly preserves input-output behavior but is exponential in depth. Step 2 (simplification) applies causal pruning using trainable gates with a KL-divergence-plus-sparsity objective (based on Li & Janson, 2024) to remove causally irrelevant components in a multi-stage procedure that avoids unfolding the full exponential program, then replaces learned matrices and nonlinear functions with interpretable primitives from a library (e.g., identity matrices, hard-max, off-by-one shifted identity). The key distinction from circuit discovery is that the output is a length-uniform symbolic program operating over sequences, not a computation graph tied to a specific input template, enabling direct inspection of the algorithm the model implements.

## Results

On eight algorithmic tasks that length-generalize, decompilation recovers D-RASP programs with match accuracy ≥ 90% (the success threshold), while all three non-length-generalizing algorithmic tasks fail to decompile. For formal languages, decompilation succeeds on 9 of 14 length-generalizing languages and fails on all 3 non-generalizing ones. The most striking quantitative result is the program-size reduction: a 4-layer 4-head transformer on Most Frequent yields a 16,201,616-line D-RASP translation that prunes to an equivalent 3-line program, and a 1-layer 4-head model's 56-line translation also reduces to 3 lines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing decompilation or interpretability methods in a quantitative head-to-head sense; its validation is internal, showing that decompilation succeeds precisely on length-generalizing models and fails on non-generalizing ones, which aligns with the RASP length-generalization conjecture. Qualitatively, the authors contrast their approach with circuit discovery (Conmy et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023), noting that circuits are tied to specific input templates and lengths, whereas the extracted D-RASP programs are length-agnostic and apply uniformly across the full input space. They also distinguish their method from Tracr-based reverse-engineering (Baker, 2023; Thurnherr & Riesen, 2024), which recovers programs from models specifically compiled from RASP, whereas here the models are trained with standard methods. The comparison to prior theoretical translations (Merrill & Sabharwal, 2023; Yang et al., 2025) highlights that D-RASP programs have size independent of the hidden dimension, unlike per-dimension logic formulas.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RASP language family (Weiss et al., 2021) and causal ablation methods (Li & Janson, 2024) to address a gap identified in the transformer interpretability literature: while Tracr (Lindner et al., 2023) compiles RASP programs into transformers and circuit discovery (Conmy et al., 2023) finds sparse computation subgraphs, neither recovers a compact symbolic program from a standard-trained transformer. The key methodological departure is the introduction of D-RASP, a softmax-based RASP dialect whose program size is independent of the hidden dimension (unlike prior logic-based translations such as Merrill & Sabharwal, 2023), combined with a two-step pipeline of faithful reparameterization followed by causal pruning. The Linear Layer Norm Assumption (LLNA) connects to Baroni et al. (2025), who showed layer norms can be linearized with minimal performance loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the most direct empirical evidence to date for the RASP length-generalization conjecture: length-generalizing transformers on synthetic tasks do internally implement short, interpretable RASP programs, and these can be extracted automatically. The clean separation—decompilation succeeds for length-generalizing models and fails for non-generalizing ones—suggests that interpretability and generalization are mechanistically linked, not merely correlated. Key limitations include reliance on small GPT-2-style models with absolute positional encodings trained on individual synthetic tasks, and the LLNA assumption; whether the method extends to sub-programs within large language models (e.g., induction heads) remains an open and important question for future work.
