## Problem

Existing diffusion model unlearning methods universally minimize an MSE loss between denoiser outputs conditioned on a target and anchor concept, which implicitly corresponds to the KL divergence between two Gaussians. This restricts the optimization landscape to a single divergence, preventing practitioners from tuning the trade-off between unlearning aggressiveness and generative fidelity. The paper asks whether generalizing the objective to arbitrary f-divergences yields meaningfully different gradient dynamics and convergence behavior that can be exploited for better unlearning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263485/cropped_page6_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (tested on SD v1.4, v1.5, v2.1, SDXL, SD v3, and FLUX), a target text prompt to erase, and an anchor text prompt (which can be a null concept, superclass, semantically close, or semantically distant concept). It produces fine-tuned model weights by optimizing the denoiser network at multiple timesteps. The framework is architecture-agnostic, operating on the denoiser output distributions rather than specific attention layers, so it applies to any diffusion or flow-based generative model without modification.

## Method

The paper formulates unlearning as minimizing an f-divergence between the original model's output distribution conditioned on the anchor concept and the unlearned model's output conditioned on the target concept, generalizing the KL-based objective from Concept Ablation. For a subset of f-divergences (Jeffreys, squared Hellinger, Pearson χ², and the broader α-divergence family), closed-form Gaussian expressions yield simple MSE-like losses that differ only in a per-sample exponential weighting of the gradient: H² scales gradients by e^{-MSE} (bounded, smaller than MSE), while χ² scales by e^{+MSE} (unbounded, larger than MSE). For all other f-divergences, the variational (Fenchel-conjugate) representation is used to construct a min-max game between the generator and a discriminator network. Theoretical analysis via nonlinear dynamical systems shows the min-max system is locally exponentially stable, with convergence speed governed by f''(1), implying H² converges fastest near equilibrium. Empirically, H-DMU (closed-form Hellinger) consistently outperforms MSE across object erasure, style ablation, and nudity removal, with better prior preservation and robustness to adversarial prompts.

## Results

On Van Gogh erasure (SD 2.1), H-DMU (closed-form Hellinger) achieves the best erasure with CS=0.624 and CA=0.2 while maintaining strong prior preservation (KID=0.027, CA_preserved=0.78), outperforming CAbl (CS=0.635, KID=0.028) and matching RECE's erasure strength (CS=0.631) with better preservation. For nudity erasure on SD 1.5, H-DMU achieves the lowest nudity generation rate across all benchmarks: 0.063 on I2P, 0.035 on MMA target prompts, and 0.049 on MMA adversarial prompts, substantially outperforming CAbl (0.120/0.066/0.118) and RECE (0.176/0.158/0.209). The variational Hellinger (Hv-DMU) achieves the best preserved-concept scores (CS=0.702, CA=0.88) among all variational methods but at the cost of higher KID (0.051), reflecting a trade-off between aggressive erasure and generative fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-DMU (closed-form Hellinger) achieves the lowest CS (0.624) and CA (0.2) among all methods for Van Gogh erasure, beating CAbl (0.635/0.2), RECE (0.631/0.3), and SPEED (0.642/0.4) on erasure strength while maintaining comparable KID (0.027 vs. 0.028/0.029/0.029). For nudity erasure, H-DMU dominates all baselines: it halves CAbl's I2P rate (0.063 vs. 0.120) and MMA adversarial rate (0.049 vs. 0.118), and outperforms RECE by 3–4× on all MMA benchmarks. The variational Hellinger (Hv-DMU) outperforms DoCo (the prior SOTA variational method) on preserved-concept CS (0.702 vs. 0.691) and CA (0.88 vs. 0.86), though with higher KID (0.051 vs. 0.033), reflecting the paper's observation that variational losses trade generative fidelity for aggressive erasure. SPM and MACE achieve the best KID (0.021) but fail to erase the target concept effectively (CS=0.714/0.690, CA=1.0/0.8), confirming that closed-form weight-editing methods sacrifice erasure quality for preservation. Overall, H-DMU consistently outperforms MSE-based methods (CAbl, ESD) across all evaluated scenarios, with the gap being most pronounced on adversarial robustness metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the KL-divergence-based concept erasure paradigm established by Concept Ablation (Kumari et al., 2023) and Erased Stable Diffusion (Gandikota et al., 2023) to arbitrary f-divergences, recovering MSE as the KL special case. It leverages the variational representation of f-divergences (Nguyen et al., 2010) to handle divergences without closed-form Gaussian expressions, framing unlearning as a min-max game. The authors show that DoCo (Wu et al., 2025), a recent state-of-the-art variational method using Jensen-Shannon divergence, is a special case of their framework, and they position f-DMU as the first unified f-divergence-based DM unlearning approach, subsuming both closed-form and variational loss families under one umbrella.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a theoretically-grounded 'menu' of loss functions, each with distinct gradient scaling and convergence properties, enabling explicit trade-offs between unlearning aggressiveness and generative fidelity. The consistent empirical dominance of the Hellinger closed-form loss over MSE across object erasure, style ablation, and nudity removal is practically significant, as it requires no architectural changes or additional computation. Open questions include whether the variational (min-max) formulation can be stabilized to match closed-form quality at scale, whether the framework transfers cleanly to flow-matching or other non-diffusion generative architectures, and how the divergence choice interacts with sequential multi-concept erasure over many iterations.
