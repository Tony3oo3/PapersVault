## Problem

Expressive generative policies (diffusion, flow-matching) achieve high multimodal returns in offline RL but have been used only in risk-neutral settings, while existing risk-averse offline RL methods enforce safety through value pessimism or restricted policy classes that limit expressiveness. The paper asks whether tail-risk control (CVaR) can be achieved without sacrificing the multimodal capacity of generative actors, and provides an objective-level analysis showing that behavior regularization is the key mechanism suppressing out-of-distribution actions that drive catastrophic lower-tail failures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples from an unknown behavior policy over continuous MuJoCo state spaces, with no further environment interaction. The generative actor takes a state s and latent noise z ~ N(0, I) and produces an action a = ψ_θ(s, z) along a differentiable diffusion SDE or flow-matching ODE trajectory. The distributional critic outputs the full return distribution parameterized as quantiles Z_φ(s, a; τ) for τ ∈ (0,1), from which the lower-tail CVaR_α signal is extracted and backpropagated through the actor's generative path.

## Method

RAMAC couples a Double IQN distributional critic (trained with quantile-Huber loss and deterministic midpoint quadrature) with an expressive generative actor (diffusion with T=5 denoising steps, or flow-matching with 10 Euler steps) and optimizes a single composite objective L_π = L_BC + η·L_Risk. The BC term is the generator's native loss (denoising or flow-matching), anchoring the policy to dataset support, while the risk term maximizes CVaR_α computed as the average of the lowest-α fraction of critic quantiles, with gradients flowing through the entire differentiable generative trajectory. The paper provides two key theoretical results: (i) a KL-based bound showing that shrinking forward KL to the behavior policy suppresses per-state OOD action probability, and (ii) a TV/Pinsker-based bound showing that CVaR is amplified by 1/α under distribution shift, motivating explicit behavior regularization. A geometric analysis (Lemma 4.1) further shows that prior-anchored perturbation methods (ORAAC) can retain off-support leakage even with expressive priors when the perturbation ball overlaps the support complement, a failure mode absent in RAMAC's on-manifold BC+CVaR update.

## Results

On Stochastic-D4RL (6 MuJoCo tasks, 5 seeds), RADAC achieves the best CVaR₀.₁ on all six tasks, with headline values of 805.25 (HalfCheetah-M-E), 573.22 (Walker2d-M-E), and 800.64 (Hopper-M-E), while also attaining the highest mean return on every task (e.g., 916.64, 1708.68, 1277.74 respectively). The strongest risk-neutral baseline, FQL, trails RADAC on CVaR by 51–800 points depending on the task. RADAC also maintains a state-conditioned OOD action rate of ~1%, compared to 5–35% for ORAAC variants, empirically confirming the behavior-regularization mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADAC outperforms all six baselines on both Mean and CVaR₀.₁ across all six Stochastic-D4RL tasks. Against the strongest risk-neutral baseline FQL, RADAC gains 72–936 points on CVaR (e.g., 805 vs. 754 on HalfCheetah-M-E; 801 vs. −9 on Hopper-M-E) while also matching or exceeding FQL's mean return. The anchor-perturbation risk-aware baselines ORAAC and ORAAC-Diffusion are competitive on MEDIUM-EXPERT means (e.g., ORAAC 796 vs. RADAC 916 on HalfCheetah-M-E) but fall well behind on CVaR and collapse on MEDIUM-REPLAY tasks (e.g., ORAAC CVaR −204 vs. RADAC 145 on Walker2d-M-R). Value-conservative methods CQL and CODAC are consistently near-zero or negative on all tasks, confirming that global pessimism is insufficient for multimodal risk-aware settings. DiffusionQL, despite using a diffusion actor, produces negative returns on most tasks due to risk-blind bootstrapping under stochastic hazards. On OOD action rates (Table 2), RADAC maintains ~1% across tasks while ORAAC reaches 5–29% and ORAAC-Diffusion 18–35%, validating the geometric leakage analysis in Lemma 4.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMAC sits at the intersection of two largely separate threads in offline RL: expressive generative policies (DiffusionQL, FQL, IDQL) that achieve high mean returns but operate in risk-neutral settings, and risk-averse offline methods (ORAAC, CODAC) that enforce safety through anchor-perturbation or value pessimism at the cost of policy expressiveness. The paper departs from the prior-anchored perturbation paradigm (ORAAC, ORAAC-Diffusion) by applying behavioral cloning directly to the deployed generative policy rather than learning a bounded residual on top of a frozen anchor, and extends risk-neutral diffusion/flow actors by backpropagating CVaR gradients from an IQN distributional critic through the generative trajectory. The BRAC template and distributional Bellman operator provide the structural backbone, while the composite BC+CVaR objective is the novel coupling that unifies tail-risk shaping with on-manifold constraint in a single loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension in safety-critical offline RL: expressive policies that capture multimodal behavior tend to be risk-blind, while risk-averse methods sacrifice expressiveness. The theoretical contribution—showing that CVaR is amplified by 1/α under distribution shift and that BC regularization suppresses per-state OOD probability via a clean KL bound—gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer direct behavior regularization over anchor-perturbation when optimizing tail objectives. Limitations include the absence of finite-sample guarantees, restriction to CVaR (no mean-variance or constrained-safety formulations), and evaluation limited to MuJoCo locomotion with a small number of denoising steps (T=5). Open questions include whether the framework extends to partial observability, dynamic risk measures, and offline-to-online fine-tuning that preserves on-manifold exploration.
