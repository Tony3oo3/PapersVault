## Problem

Large language models suffer from representation collapse as context length grows, but existing countermeasures (attention sinks, local/sliding-window attention) each fix one failure mode at the cost of another: homogenization collapse (low effective rank) or isolation collapse (low spectral gap). The paper identifies an intrinsic spectral trade-off between mixing efficiency and information capacity in the attention transition operator that standard mechanisms cannot balance simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264403/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TRSP operates as a non-invasive side-path plugin on a standard transformer: at each layer ℓ, it takes the token hidden states X ∈ R^{T×d} and adds a gated triBox output back to the residual stream without modifying the attention computation. The method is evaluated on text sequences at context lengths up to 8× the training window (e.g., trained at 1K, tested at 8K on NoLiMa; trained at 4K, tested at 32K on RULER), producing standard next-token predictions or task-specific answers.

## Method

TRSP introduces a parameter-free Triangular Box (triBox) operator implemented as a cascaded causal box filter with exponentially expanding bandwidth b_ℓ = 2^ℓ across layers, creating a sparse Cayley graph topology on Z_T that provides proximal coupling in shallow layers (preserving effective rank) and distal shortcuts in deep layers (supporting global mixing). A lightweight long-context gate (~50 learnable parameters) scales the side-path injection based on the coverage ratio r_ℓ = b_ℓ/T via a small MLP and a learned decay exponent, making the gain input-agnostic and length-aware. The composite layer operator becomes M = I + A_attn + g_ℓ M_tri^(ℓ), where the triBox branch carries rigorous rank and gap guarantees: a length-independent lower bound on algebraic connectivity (μ₂ ≥ 2) prevents isolation, while the triangular kernel's Frobenius energy scaling prevents rank collapse even under sink-dominated attention. The entire mechanism adds only ~50 trainable parameters and O(Td) per-layer cost, making it composable with existing architectures like LoRA.

## Results

On NoLiMa (from-scratch, 1K training), TRSP retains 83.2% accuracy at 8× the training length, surpassing the Differential Transformer (53.9%) by ~30 points and Gated Attention (33.6%) by ~50 points. On RULER (post-training, 4K training), LoRA+TRSP achieves 60.38% at 8× (32K), outperforming LoRA (57.51%) and Gated Attention (40.29%). On general capabilities (MMLU/HellaSwag), LoRA+TRSP reaches 37.76%/29.26% with only 50 extra parameters, beating LoRA (36.01%/27.97%) and Gated Attention (23.19%/24.79%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (ICLR 2022), TRSP consistently wins: +1.75 pts on MMLU (37.76 vs 36.01), +1.29 on HellaSwag (29.26 vs 27.97), and +2.87 pts on RULER at 8× (60.38 vs 57.51), while adding only 50 parameters versus LoRA's 5.6M. Against Gated Attention (NeurIPS 2026), TRSP dominates across all settings: MMLU 37.76 vs 23.19, RULER 8× 60.38 vs 40.29, and NoLiMa 8× 83.2 vs 33.6, with Gated Attention also requiring 67.2M extra parameters in the post-training setup. Against the Differential Transformer (ICLR 2025), TRSP achieves a decisive 29.3-point margin on NoLiMa at 8× (83.2 vs 53.9) and an 8.6-point margin at 4× (98.8 vs 90.2), while matching at 2× (both 100%). The Differential Transformer is the strongest baseline overall but still degrades substantially at long context, whereas TRSP maintains near-perfect accuracy through 4× and retains 83% at 8×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of analysis—depth-wise rank collapse (Dong et al., 2021; Noci et al., 2022) and length-wise over-mixing/attention-sink phenomena (Barbero et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024)—under a single spectral trade-off between effective rank and spectral gap of the transition operator. Rather than modifying the attention computation itself (as Differential Transformer or Gated Attention do), TRSP draws on graph-theoretic tools (Cayley graphs, algebraic connectivity) borrowed from the GNN over-smoothing literature to inject a parameter-free topological regularizer as a parallel side-path. The dyadic bandwidth schedule and triangular kernel are novel architectural choices, but the underlying idea of using structured mixing to counteract spectral degeneration is a repurposing of known graph-regularization principles applied to transformer token interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50-parameter overhead and non-invasive design make TRSP an attractive drop-in for existing pipelines, and the spectral diagnostic framework (stable rank, SPR, flatness, anisotropy) offers a principled way to monitor representation health during training. However, all evidence comes from small-scale models (1B post-training, 109M from scratch) and a single long-context benchmark family; whether the spectral balance argument and the specific Cayley-graph topology transfer to 7B+ models or to architectures with grouped-query attention remains open. The theoretical guarantees apply to the side-path branch in isolation, with the composite operator's spectrum subject to data-dependent attention interactions—a gap the authors acknowledge but do not close. A key open question is whether the dyadic schedule is optimal or whether learned bandwidths could further improve the rank-gap trade-off.
