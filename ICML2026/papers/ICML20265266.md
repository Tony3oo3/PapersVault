## Problem

Sequential test-time scaling (CoT, RLVR) increasingly plateaus as models lock into rigid inferential trajectories, and while parallel reasoning has shown promise for text LLMs, extending this paradigm to the visual domain remains an open problem due to the unique structural challenges of visual attention allocation across concurrent reasoning paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (up to 16,384 visual tokens within a 32K context window) and a text instruction, then produces four parallel reasoning paths—each structured with a <think_i> tag and guided by a specific visual partitioning strategy (block-based quadrants or scan-order trajectories)—followed by a <summary> block that synthesizes all paths into a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. The visual partitioning determines how the image's spatial information is distributed across paths: block-based assigns each path a spatial quadrant, while scan-order assigns each path a distinct scanning trajectory (left-to-right, top-to-bottom, etc.).

## Method

Visual Para-Thinker is a two-stage parallel reasoning framework for MLLMs built on Qwen-2.5-VL. In the Parallel Reasoning stage, four concurrent paths are generated under visual partitioning, with Pa-Attention enforcing strict path isolation by restricting each token to attend only to its own path and the shared multimodal context. In the Summary stage, LPRoPE injects learnable path-specific embeddings into key and value vectors before RoPE rotation, ensuring that tokens at the same relative depth across paths share identical position IDs (unbiasedness) while remaining geometrically distinguishable in the attention space (discriminability). The inference engine extends vLLM with beam-search forking for parallel decoding and KV-cache reuse via paged attention, reducing total inference time to within 15% of single-pass baseline. Training uses supervised fine-tuning on 163K QA pairs generated by a Qwen3-VL-235B teacher model under a hybrid partitioning scheme, with the special <think_i> tokens masked so the model relies on LPRoPE for path identification.

## Results

Visual Para-Thinker-7B achieves an overall average of 74.8% across counting, fine-grained perception, and hallucination benchmarks, surpassing GPT-4o (62.6%), Gemini 2.5 Pro (66.7%), and even the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (71.1%). On the V* visual search benchmark, the 7B model reaches 74.0% (vs. 70.5% for GPT-4o and 74.7% for Gemini 2.5 Pro), while on HallusionBench it attains 71.0% (vs. 61.4% for GPT-4o). The 3B variant improves over its Qwen2.5-VL-3B base by +9.5% on average, with the largest gain of +12.6% on V*. On grounding (RefCOCO series), Visual Para-Thinker-3B achieves 88.5% Acc@0.5, a 3.7-point improvement over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against frontier closed-source models, Visual Para-Thinker-7B outperforms GPT-4o by +12.2 points on average (74.8 vs. 62.6) and Gemini 2.5 Pro by +8.1 points (74.8 vs. 66.7), while trailing GPT-5-mini by a narrow −0.6 margin (74.8 vs. 64.2 on the reported subset; note GPT-5-mini's CountBench score of 84.4 is lower than VPT-7B's 85.4). Compared to the same-size Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, VPT-7B gains +7.6 points on average, with the largest improvement on V* (+2.7) and HallusionBench (+5.0). Against internal baselines, VPT-7B surpasses Sequential CoT (+7.1 avg), GRPO (+2.1 avg), and Majority Voting@4 (+7.0 avg), confirming that structured parallel reasoning with path isolation outperforms both deeper sequential thinking and naive multi-sample voting. On grounding (Table 2), VPT-3B leads all baselines by 2.8–5.9 points across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, demonstrating consistent gains in spatial localization tasks as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parallel thinking paradigm introduced by Para-Thinker (Wen et al., 2025) from text-only LLMs to the multimodal domain, addressing what the authors identify as an open research gap. The architectural contributions—Pa-Attention for path isolation and LPRoPE for path discriminability—are built directly on the RoPE positional encoding framework (Su et al., 2023), with the key departure being the injection of learnable path-specific embeddings before rotation to resolve positional collisions at merge time. The engineering implementation extends vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) with fork-sequence operations and KV cache reuse to achieve near-single-path inference latency for multi-path decoding, distinguishing it from prior parallel generation approaches that either abandon the decoder-only architecture (diffusion LLMs) or lack the visual partitioning mechanism needed for path diversity in the image domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to bring structured parallel reasoning to MLLMs, and the practical finding that 4-path parallel inference costs only ~17% more wall-clock time than single-path inference (312s vs 266s) while outperforming sequential CoT (481s) and majority voting (1197s) challenges the assumption that test-time scaling in vision must trade off latency for accuracy. The complementary strengths of Block-based vs. Scan-order partitioning across task types (spatial-locality tasks vs. diffuse-attention tasks) suggests a principled routing strategy for future systems. Open questions include whether the gains persist at 70B+ scale where base performance saturates, how parallel reasoning interacts with RLVR training (the authors note this as future work), and whether the visual partitioning strategies generalize to video or 3D inputs.
