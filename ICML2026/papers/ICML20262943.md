## Problem

Unified medical foundation models (MFMs) that apply a single contrastive learning objective across heterogeneous imaging modalities suffer from 'information ambiguity': the uniform mutual-information maximization forces the model to learn modality-shared redundancy, blending representations into one embedding space and degrading both inter-modality specificity and intra-modality fine-grained discrimination. Directly removing the modality-shared redundancy term requires estimating a trivariate mutual information quantity, which is intractable at MFM scale in high-dimensional spaces.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, M-IDoL consumes unlabeled 224×224 medical images from five modalities (X-ray, fundus, OCT, dermoscopy, pathology) in shuffled minibatches of 64 per GPU, generating M=8 augmented views per image (2 global + 6 local crops with DINO-style augmentations). The model outputs a visual representation embedding from the teacher Swin-B encoder; the MoE projectors and student network are discarded for downstream use, where the frozen encoder feeds a task-specific head producing class labels (classification) or pixel-wise masks (segmentation) at 224×224 or 512×512 resolution.

## Method

M-IDoL decomposes the intractable objective max I(X;Y) − I(X;Y;Z) into two tractable entropy terms: maximizing inter-modality conditional entropy H(X|Z) and minimizing intra-modality predictive uncertainty H(X|Y,Z). It implements this via a DINO-style student-teacher Swin-B architecture augmented with a Tutel-based MoE projector (5 experts, top-1 routing) placed after the encoder. A routing-consistency loss (L_route) with Sinkhorn-Knopp balancing enforces that augmented views of the same image activate the same expert while images from other modalities route to different experts, dispersing representations into modality-separated subspaces (maximizing H(X|Z)). An intra-modality contrastive loss (L_cst) in InfoNCE form then pulls same-image view pairs closer within each expert subspace, reducing augmentation noise without collapsing cross-modality distinctions (minimizing H(X|Y,Z)). The two losses are optimized jointly (L = L_route + L_cst) over 100 epochs on 1.15M images, with L_route stabilizing early to establish subspace assignments before L_cst drives fine-grained intra-modality discrimination.

## Results

M-IDoL is pre-trained on 1.15M unlabeled medical images across 5 modalities and evaluated on 21 downstream datasets. Against modality-specific SOTA models (each pre-trained on 1–2M images within a single domain), M-IDoL achieves 93.43% ACC on APTOS (vs. RETFound 92.17%), 97.13% ACC on OCTID (vs. MIRAGE 96.66%), 93.00% ACC on BreakHis-Binary (vs. UNI 92.64%), and 90.09% AUC on CheXpert (vs. AFiRe 88.92%). Against unified MFMs (Unimiss+, LVM-Med, UniMed), M-IDoL achieves an average improvement of over 6% across all modalities, and surpasses continual-learning-based MFMs (MedCoss, CoSMIC) by over 4% on OCT and pathology tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against modality-specific models, M-IDoL consistently outperforms RETFound on all four fundus datasets (e.g., +1.26% ACC on APTOS, +3.42% on Retina) and MIRAGE on all four OCT datasets (e.g., +0.47% on OCTID, +1.50% on TVHL-DR), despite being pre-trained on a shared multi-modality corpus rather than domain-specific data. In pathology, M-IDoL edges out UNI on BreakHis-Binary (+0.36%) and Mitosis (+0.26%) but trails slightly on MHIST (81.78% vs. 82.09%, p>0.05). In dermoscopy, M-IDoL is marginally behind Panderm on ISIC2016 (86.39% vs. 87.88%) but surpasses it on HAM10000 (97.10% vs. 96.13%) and ISIC2017 (86.68% vs. 85.87%). In X-ray, M-IDoL leads AFiRe on CXP (+1.17% AUC) and ZhangCXR (+0.69% ACC) but is slightly behind on SIIM-ACR segmentation (91.21% vs. 91.68% Dice, p>0.05). Against unified MFMs, M-IDoL achieves >6% average improvement over Unimiss+, LVM-Med, and UniMed, and >4% over MedCoss and CoSMIC on OCT and pathology, demonstrating that information decomposition via MoE routing is more effective than continual learning for modality-specific representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

M-IDoL builds on the DINO student-teacher EMA pre-training framework and the Tutel MoE infrastructure, but departs from both in purpose: rather than using MoE for cross-modal fusion (as in MedMoE, FuseMoE, or M⁴oE), it repurposes MoE as a modality-adaptive projector that adaptively partitions unlabeled images into latent modality subspaces during self-supervised pre-training. The key conceptual departure from unified MFMs (LVM-Med, UniMed, Unimiss+) is the information decomposition framework, which reformulates the multivariate mutual information objective I(X;Y)−I(X;Y;Z) into two tractable entropy terms—maximizing H(X|Z) for inter-modality specificity and minimizing H(X|Y,Z) for intra-modality diversity—thereby explicitly removing modality-shared redundancy that prior unified CL objectives inadvertently maximize. Compared to continual-learning approaches like MedCoss and CoSMIC that achieve modality specificity through multi-stage pre-training, M-IDoL accomplishes this in a single unified pre-training pass via the routing-consistency and intra-modality contrastive losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formally addresses a concrete limitation (information ambiguity) in unified medical foundation models, providing both an information-theoretic explanation and a practical mechanism (MoE projector with two losses) to mitigate it. The >6% average improvement over existing unified MFMs and competitive performance with modality-specific models across 21 datasets across five imaging modalities is practically significant for clinical deployment where a single model must handle heterogeneous inputs. Open questions include: whether the learned MoE subspaces truly correspond to anatomical modalities or merely to arbitrary statistical clusters, how the approach scales beyond five modalities or to 3D volumetric data, and whether discarding the MoE projector at inference time loses any of the modality-specific structure learned during pre-training. The fixed number of experts (one per modality) also limits flexibility for open-world modality discovery.
