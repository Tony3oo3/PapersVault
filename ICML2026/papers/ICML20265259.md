## Problem

No prior work addresses finding differentially private second-order stationary points (SOSP) for stochastic nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax optimization. Existing DP minimax methods target only first-order stationarity, while DP-SOSP results are limited to single-level minimization; the implicit value function Φ(x) = max_y f(x,y) and the nested gradient-access pattern make it nontrivial to certify local minima of Φ under privacy constraints without explicit Hessian access.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a dataset of n samples, a minimax objective f(x,y) that is L-smooth and nonconvex in x and μ-strongly-concave in y, and a global privacy budget (ε, δ). It outputs a point x_out satisfying both ‖∇Φ(x_out)‖ ≤ α and λ_min(∇²Φ(x_out)) ≥ −√ρ_Φ·α, with α = Õ((√d/(nε))^(2/3)) for empirical risk and Õ(n^(−1/3) + (√d/(nε))^(1/2)) for population risk. All data access is through privatized gradient queries only—no Hessian or function-value evaluations of Φ are used during optimization.

## Method

DP RGDA combines three mechanisms: (1) a SPIDER-style recursive gradient tracker that refreshes full-batch estimates every q outer iterations and updates via small-batch gradient differences in between, reducing the number of expensive privacy-noise injections; (2) an inner loop of K projected ascent steps on y at each fixed x_t, which converts inner-loop stationarity into a quantitative bound on ‖y − y*(x)‖ and hence on the value-gradient bias; and (3) a perturb-and-monitor escape rule that, upon entering a low-gradient region, applies a random perturbation of radius r and monitors the cumulative squared movement D_t over t_thres steps—large movement certifies escape from negative curvature, while persistently small movement implies near-PSD Hessian at the anchor. The analysis is organized into q-length periods aligned with the refresh schedule, preventing privacy noise from accumulating over the full T-step horizon and yielding the stated rates without any private model-selection step.

## Results

On the synthetic low-rank matrix sensing minimax instance (p=q=20, rank r=3, n=400, T=400), DP-RGDA achieves a final value-function objective Φ(x₃₉₉)≈0.912, gradient norm ‖∇Φ(x₃₉₉)‖≈0.425, and minimum Hessian eigenvalue λ_min≈−4.85×10⁻². Theoretically, the method attains α = Õ((√d/(nε))^{2/3}) for empirical risk and α = Õ(1/n^{1/3} + (√d/(nε))^{1/2}) for population risk, matching the best-known DP-SOSP rates for ERM while using only first-order (gradient) information. The population rate matches Liu & Talwar (2024), who require second-order Hessian access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-RGDA decisively outperforms both minimization-only DP-SOSP baselines (Sto-SPIDER and Ada-DP-SPIDER) on all three metrics, even though those baselines are given the strictly easier oracle of directly optimizing the explicit value function Φ rather than tracking the inner maximizer y*(x). Specifically, DP-RGDA achieves Φ≈0.91 versus 13.70 (Sto-SPIDER) and 8.08 (Ada-DP-SPIDER), with gradient norms roughly 8× and 5× smaller respectively. Against PrivateDiff (Zhang et al., 2025b), a first-order DP minimax method, PrivateDiff achieves a slightly lower objective (0.65 vs. 0.91) and gradient norm (0.33 vs. 0.43) in this single run, but it targets only first-order stationarity and lacks an explicit saddle-escape mechanism; curvature diagnostics are comparable (λ_min ≈ −4.4×10⁻² vs. −4.9×10⁻²). DP-SGDA (Yang et al., 2022) is shown as a single-loop trajectory baseline in Figure 1 but is not included in the final-metric table. Overall, DP-RGDA is the only method in the comparison that simultaneously targets second-order stationarity of the value function under the full minimax oracle model with a purely first-order (Hessian-free) algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: (1) DP-SOSP for single-level minimization (Liu et al., 2023; Liu & Talwar, 2024; Tao et al., 2025), which provides the SPIDER variance-reduction backbone and the perturb-and-monitor escape paradigm; (2) DP minimax optimization (Yang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025b), which handles the nested descent-ascent structure but only certifies first-order stationarity; and (3) non-private SOSP for minimax (Luo et al., 2022; Xian et al., 2025), which motivates the value-function viewpoint and saddle-escape criteria. The key departure is that prior DP-SOSP methods require Hessian access (or expensive private Hessian estimation), while this work shows a purely first-order nested SPIDER recursion with a movement-based escape test suffices to match the best-known population rate, and that the period-wise (q-period) analysis is essential to prevent privacy noise from accumulating over the full horizon in the nested setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithmic framework providing (ε,δ)-DP guarantees for second-order stationary points of the minimax value function, which is directly relevant to privately training GANs, adversarially robust models, and multi-agent RL where first-order stationarity is insufficient. The matching of state-of-the-art ERM and population rates with a purely first-order method (no Hessian) is practically significant since privatizing Hessian information is prohibitively expensive. Limitations include the strong-concavity requirement in the inner variable, validation on a single small synthetic matrix-sensing instance with one seed, and the absence of user-level privacy analysis. Open questions include extending to non-strongly-concave inner problems, scaling to real-world minimax architectures, and whether the period-wise analysis can be tightened to improve the privacy exponent.
