## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods face severe time and space bottlenecks when scaling to large graphs: constraint-based algorithms require exponentially many conditional-independence tests, score-based methods solve a fresh optimization per dataset, and prior amortized approaches like AVICI suffer from attention mechanisms whose memory scales quadratically with the number of variables. No prior method can train on or infer over graphs with hundreds to thousands of nodes within practical compute budgets.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a standardized observational matrix D ∈ R^{m×n} concatenated with a binary intervention indicator I ∈ {0,1}^{m×n} (forming D̃ ∈ R^{m×n×2}), plus a statistical graph prior ρ ∈ R^{n×n} computed as the inverse sample covariance of D. It outputs a probabilistic directed adjacency matrix Ĝ ∈ R^{n×n} via a decomposed pairwise head that assigns softmax probabilities over three states (no edge, i→j, j→i) for each unordered node pair. The architecture scales inference to n=1000 nodes and training to n=500 nodes, with sample sizes m ranging from 1,000 (synthetic) to 20,000 (single-cell GRN).

## Method

CauScale employs a two-stream transformer with a data stream (m×n×d) and a graph stream (n×n×d), processed by alternating data-graph blocks and reduction units. Each data-graph block contains a data axial-attention layer, a data2graph layer that distills pairwise relational evidence from the data stream into an n×n graph message via outer-product of pooled node embeddings, and a graph axial-attention layer that injects this message into the graph stream. For time efficiency, a reduction unit applies average pooling along the observation dimension every k blocks (compressing m by factor r), exploiting the fact that m ≫ n in typical causal discovery settings. For space efficiency, tied attention weights (from MSA-Transformer) share attention scores across rows, reducing stored attention maps from O(RHC²) to O(HC²). The model is trained in two stages (10–100 nodes, then 150–500 nodes) on synthetic SCMs with linear and neural-network mechanisms, enabling zero-shot inference on unseen graph sizes and out-of-distribution causal functions.

## Results

On synthetic data with n=100 nodes, CauScale achieves 99.6% mAP and 15.2 SHD on linear mechanisms, 89.0% mAP on non-additive NN mechanisms, and 84.4% mAP on OOD sigmoid mechanisms, consistently outperforming all baselines. On large-scale graphs (n=1000, unseen during training), CauScale maintains 96.6% mAP on linear data while AVICI collapses to 0.2% mAP. On the SERGIO-GRN single-cell benchmark (n=100, 20,000 samples), CauScale achieves 54.0% mAP versus 5.6% for the next-best method (SEA-gies), while AVICI fails with an Out-of-Memory error. Inference time is 0.0384s at n=100 and 0.8288s at n=1000, yielding 4×–13,000× speedups over prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CauScale achieves the best mAP across all evaluated settings, with the largest margins on in-distribution data: 99.6% vs. 92.1% (SEA-gies) and 42.3% (SDCD) on linear n=100, and 89.0% vs. 65.7% (SDCD) and 51.2% (SEA-gies) on non-additive NN. On OOD sigmoid mechanisms, CauScale leads with 84.4% mAP versus 72.7% (SEA-gies) and 61.9% (SDCD). At n=1000 (beyond training size), CauScale reaches 96.6% mAP on linear data while AVICI drops to 0.2% and SEA-gies to 66.3%, indicating strong extrapolation. On the SERGIO-GRN benchmark, CauScale (54.0% mAP) outperforms SEA-gies (5.6%), SDCD (4.0%), and NOTEARS (4.1%) by a wide margin, and is the only method that avoids Out-of-Memory failure at n=200 with 20,000 samples. In terms of speed, CauScale is 4× faster than AVICI (0.038s vs. 0.297s at n=100), 229× faster than SEA-gies, and 56,000× faster than NOTEARS; at n=1000 the speedup over NOTEARS reaches 13,000× (0.83s vs. 10,896s). The only setting where CauScale does not lead is polynomial OOD at n=1000, where SDCD achieves a slightly lower AID, though SDCD produces 100% cyclic predictions in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

CauScale extends the amortized causal discovery paradigm pioneered by AVICI (Lorch et al., 2022) and SEA (Wu et al., 2025), which pretrain a shared model to perform zero-shot graph prediction, but departs from both in its architectural design. It repurposes the tied-attention mechanism from the MSA Transformer (Rao et al., 2021) — originally developed for protein sequence modeling — to eliminate axis-specific attention maps and reduce memory from O(RHC²) to O(HC²). The two-stream data-graph block with a reduction unit is novel in this context, addressing the specific bottleneck that m (samples) ≫ n (variables) in causal discovery datasets, a structural property that prior amortized methods did not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CauScale makes neural causal discovery practically feasible at scales (1000 nodes, 20,000 samples) where prior amortized methods like AVICI simply run out of memory, opening the door to causal structure learning in large-scale genomics and systems biology. Key limitations remain: the model is trained entirely on synthetic SCMs and shows significant degradation under OOD noise distributions and mechanism functions (e.g., 50.3% mAP on polynomial at n=100), and performance drops sharply under severe latent confounding (h=64). Evaluation on real-world observational datasets and training on more diverse data-generating processes are important open directions before the method can be trusted in high-stakes scientific inference.
