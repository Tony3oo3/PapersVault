## Problem

Existing multimodal deep-research MLLMs formulate visual search as a single full-image or entity-level query, which fails under the hit-rate variability of real-world search engines where visual noise and scale differences cause unreliable retrieval. Additionally, prior methods are constrained to short trajectories (fewer than five retrieval rounds), limiting their ability to aggregate evidence from diverse sources for complex multi-hop questions.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a text answer after performing dozens of reasoning steps and hundreds of search-engine interactions. During inference, the model iteratively generates multi-scale cropped image regions for visual search, issues text queries, visits and summarizes webpages, and integrates evidence across modalities over up to 50 turns with a 64K context window. The output is a single factual answer grounded in retrieved visual and textual evidence.

## Method

Vision-DeepResearch introduces a multi-entity, multi-scale visual cropping-and-search (CIS) strategy where the MLLM localizes multiple bounding boxes at varying scales to probe visual search engines, treating retrieval as an iterative trial-and-error process rather than a one-shot operation. A text-bridging mechanism converts the visual trajectory into a text-only context via image descriptions, enabling a strong text-based deep-research LLM to generate subsequent text-search trajectories that are merged into a unified multimodal trajectory. Training data is synthesized through a verified factual VQA pipeline with interleaved answer and entity obfuscation (random walks over real webpages) to produce complex multi-hop questions, followed by rejection sampling of trajectories. The model is trained in two stages: SFT on 30K multimodal deep-research trajectories to instill multi-turn tool-use patterns, followed by GRPO-based RL on 15K VQA instances with a high-throughput asynchronous rollout architecture that achieves over 10× throughput versus synchronous rollouts, using LLM-as-Judge accuracy rewards and trajectory masking for anomalous rollouts.

## Results

Vision-DeepResearch-30B-A3B achieves a 56.9% average across six multimodal factual benchmarks (VDR, FVQA, MMSearch-Plus, MMSearch, LiveVQA, BC-VL), surpassing the strongest proprietary agent workflows: GPT-5 (48.3%), Gemini-2.5 Pro (50.7%), and Claude-4-Sonnet (48.5%). The smaller 8B variant reaches 50.5% average, already exceeding all prior open-source multimodal deep-research MLLMs (WebWatcher-32B, MMSearch-R1-7B). On document-based VQA benchmarks, the model attains 80.6% average (SlideVQA 93.2, InfoVQA 90.4, MMLongBench-Doc 58.3), a 14.1-point absolute improvement over the strongest baseline ViDoRAG (66.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary agent workflows, Vision-DeepResearch-30B-A3B (56.9% avg) outperforms GPT-5 (48.3%) by +8.6 points, Gemini-2.5 Pro (50.7%) by +6.2, and Claude-4-Sonnet (48.5%) by +8.4, with the largest margins on VDR (+17.4 over GPT-5) and MMSearch-Plus (+11.3 over Gemini-2.5 Pro). Among open-source multimodal deep-research MLLMs, the 30B model surpasses WebWatcher-32B by +14.3 on MMSearch (69.6 vs 55.3), +18.9 on LiveVQA (77.6 vs 58.7), and +27.0 on BC-VL (53.7 vs 26.7), while the 8B model beats MMSearch-R1-7B by +10.9 on FVQA (64.7 vs 58.4) and +28.3 on LiveVQA (76.7 vs 48.4). On document-based VQA (Table 5), the model achieves 80.6% average, exceeding ViDoRAG (66.5%) by 14.1 points and VDocRAG (39.6%) by 41.0 points, with a particularly large 20.4-point gain on MMLongBench-Doc (58.3 vs 37.9). The 8B model (50.5% avg) also surpasses the 30B-A3B base model in agentic mode (40.9% avg) by +9.6 points, demonstrating that the learned deep-research behavior compensates for the parameter gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ReAct reasoning-then-tool-call paradigm (Yao et al., 2022) to the multimodal domain by combining multi-entity, multi-scale visual cropping with text-based deep-research trajectories, bridged via image-description-based context sharing. It departs from prior multimodal deep-research MLLMs (WebWatcher, MMSearch-R1, DeepMMSearch-R1) in two key ways: replacing single-shot full-image or entity-level retrieval with iterative multi-scale visual search, and scaling trajectories from fewer-than-five rounds to dozens of reasoning steps with hundreds of engine interactions. The RL training builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and the rLLM asynchronous rollout framework (Tan et al., 2025), while the data synthesis pipeline draws on text-only deep-research LLMs (Tongyi DeepResearch, WebDancer, WebSailor) to transfer long-horizon ReAct behavior into the visual domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 30B-A3B (active 3B) model, after specialized SFT+RL training, can outperform agent workflows built on GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Claude-4-Sonnet on six multimodal factual benchmarks, suggesting that task-specific long-horizon training can close the gap with much larger proprietary models. The ablation clearly isolates multi-scale visual cropping as the critical differentiator over whole-image search, a finding with practical implications for any system relying on visual search engines. Key limitations include reliance on non-reproducible real-world search engines, evaluation on a single model family (Qwen3-VL), and RL training that the authors acknowledge is not fully scaled. Open questions include whether the multi-scale cropping strategy generalizes to other MLLM architectures and whether the performance gap over proprietary models persists as those models improve their native agentic capabilities.
