## Problem

Existing time-series reasoning approaches encode the full sequence into a fixed representation before inference, forcing the model to answer from a static view regardless of which temporal regions are actually relevant to the question. This is suboptimal because relevant segments are query- and step-dependent: a clinical deterioration query may first need a baseline window, then a narrower interval to test a hypothesis, yet no prior method lets the model adaptively decide what to inspect during multi-step reasoning.

## Input / Output

ARTIST takes a natural-language question paired with a univariate time series (length H, encoded via a 5-layer MLP over patch-based inputs) and produces a multiple-choice or true/false answer along with an interleaved reasoning trace and a list of selected temporal segments. Rather than consuming the full sequence, the model iteratively retrieves contiguous segments via a tool-calling mechanism, typically using only 30–70% of the input per query, and terminates when the controller judges the accumulated evidence sufficient.

## Method

ARTIST equips a single LLM policy (Qwen3-4B) with two roles invoked via prompting: a high-level controller that selects the next temporal segment and decides when to stop, and a low-level reasoner that generates a reasoning trace and answer conditioned on the accumulated segment set. Training proceeds in two stages—SFT on GPT-5-generated interleaved traces, followed by collaborative self-play RL with hierarchical policy optimization. The controller is optimized with a trajectory-level reliability reward (mean correctness over N resampled reasoner rollouts) and group-relative advantages propagated across all interaction rounds, while the reasoner is optimized myopically at the final round using variance-guided group sampling to prioritize informative rollouts. This separation enables precise credit assignment: the controller learns to build an informative segment set over multiple steps, and the reasoner learns focused, segment-conditioned answering without being confounded by selection variance.

## Results

ARTIST (Qwen3-4B backbone) achieves an average accuracy of 69.26% and F1 of 56.61% across six time-series reasoning benchmarks (ETI, RCW, ECG-QA, Sleep-QA, TSQA, TRQA), improving by +6.46 percentage points in accuracy and +6.60 in F1 over the strongest per-dataset baseline. Supervised fine-tuning alone yields 63.61% average accuracy, and the subsequent collaborative self-play RL stage adds a further ~5.65 points. The largest single-dataset gain is +12.50 points on TSQA accuracy, where multi-segment reasoning over short sequences is most challenging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against text-based LLMs (GPT-5, LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen3-8B/14B), ARTIST dominates on every benchmark; even GPT-5 with statistics achieves only 34.48% average accuracy versus ARTIST's 69.26%. Among fine-tuned time-series encoder baselines, ITFormer-4B+ SFT is the strongest on ETI (84.62%) and TSQA (80.12%), while OpenTSLM-4B+ SFT leads on ECG-QA (69.50%); ARTIST surpasses both on average (69.26% vs. 62.08% and 51.01% respectively), with the largest margin on TSQA (+12.50 points over ITFormer). Against vision-language baselines (Table 2), ARTIST+RL beats TimeMaster+RL on five of six datasets (ETI 87.03 vs. 49.00, RCW 77.00 vs. 76.99, ECG-QA 69.81 vs. 69.31, TSQA 62.00 vs. 61.22, TRQA 83.34 vs. 72.08) but trails on Sleep-QA (36.63 vs. 72.55), a gap the authors attribute to the tokenized-input modality rather than the segment-selection mechanism (a VLM-backbone variant of ARTIST reaches 65.00% on Sleep-QA). In the ablation, removing the controller (static full-series input) drops accuracy by 9.30 points, and removing the reliability reward causes a 21.44-point collapse, confirming that adaptive, consistency-verified segment selection is the primary driver of gains. ARTIST also outperforms the adapted PixelReasoner visual-search method on ECG-QA (69.81 vs. 60.15 accuracy) and matches or exceeds TimeX/TimeX++ explainability baselines on segment-level recall and F1 for temporal localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARTIST extends the time-series reasoning line (ChatTS, OpenTSLM, ITFormer, TimeMaster) by replacing static full-sequence encoding with iterative, question-adaptive segment selection during inference, drawing an analogy to dynamic visual search methods (Chain-of-Focus, Pixel-Reasoner) that zoom into image regions. It builds on the GRPO group-relative advantage framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the ChatTS pretraining procedure, but departs from adversarial self-play (AZR, SPICE, SPELL) by introducing collaborative self-play with a hierarchical, role-specific optimization: the controller receives trajectory-level credit across all interaction rounds while the reasoner is optimized myopically on the final round. The reliability reward (consistency across repeated reasoner rollouts) is a novel learning signal not present in prior self-play or RL-for-reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that ARTIST achieves higher accuracy while consuming only 30–70% of the input time series suggests that selective temporal analysis is both more accurate and more efficient than full-sequence processing, a result with direct implications for long-sequence clinical and financial applications. The 21.44% accuracy drop when removing the reliability reward highlights that single-shot correctness is a misleading signal for segment selection, a subtlety likely relevant to any iterative information-acquisition setting. Key limitations include restriction to univariate series, higher inference cost from multi-round interaction, and a modality gap on EEG (Sleep-QA) where tokenized inputs underperform plotted representations. Extending to multivariate and irregularly sampled signals, and reducing inference overhead, are the most pressing open directions.
