## Problem

In collaborative personalized federated learning, the aggregation kernel that governs inter-client mixing is left unconstrained during training, causing the dynamics to drift into two degenerate regimes: overly contractive collaboration flattens inter-client diversity toward a single consensus (standard FL), while similarity-driven weight updates create a positive feedback loop that makes the collaboration graph nearly reducible and induces spontaneous self-clustering (K-clustered FL). The paper asks how to design the collaboration geometry so that the strict expressivity advantage of collaboration over clustering is actually realized in practice rather than erased by these collapse modes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two types of input per client: (i) local training data used for standard supervised optimization, and (ii) a lightweight, client-local descriptor ζ_i (e.g., data-distribution statistics, feature summaries, or initial model-update directions) that is available a priori and stable across rounds. From these descriptors, a static pairwise affinity template Ψ is computed once (via JS divergence of data distributions or cosine similarity of initial updates) and never updated during training. The output is a personalized model θ_i per client, with the collaboration matrix W^t at each round being a valid Markovian kernel (nonnegative, row-stochastic, with stationary distribution equal to the dataset-size proportions q) that governs how each client's next-round initialization is formed as a weighted mix of all clients' locally updated models.

## Method

pFedCCG constructs the time-varying collaboration kernel {W^t} through four coupled design choices. First, a feedback-free affinity template Ψ is fixed from static client descriptors, breaking the similarity-to-weights feedback loop that drives self-clustering. Second, the target stationary distribution of W^t is set to q (the dataset-size proportions) so that the collaboration geometry aligns with the q-weighted personalization objective. Third, at each round the scaled template α_tΨ is projected onto the reversible Markov set {W ≥ 0, W1 = 1, q^T W = q^T, D_q W = W^T D_q} via a convex quadratic program solved by projected gradient descent with closed-form Euclidean projections, yielding a valid kernel that tracks the affinity structure as closely as constraints allow. Fourth, a sigmoid schedule on α_t implements stage-wise control: strong global mixing early (small α_t, kernel close to 1q^T) keeps trajectories in a shared region and prevents premature fragmentation, while weaker mixing later (large α_t, kernel adhering more to Ψ) preserves personalization and avoids consensus collapse. The theoretical foundation shows that under the same K-anchor budget, collaboration achieves a K^{-4/r_int} approximation rate versus K^{-2/r_int} for clustering, and derives disagreement upper bounds that quantify both the consensus-collapse and multi-point-collapse failure modes the scheduling is designed to prevent.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-10), pFedCCG achieves 85.20% mean personalized accuracy at β=0.2, 80.36% at β=0.5, and 78.35% at β=1, outperforming the best baseline (FedAGHN) by 1.23, 1.80, and 2.96 points respectively. On Yahoo Answers (TextCNN), pFedCCG reaches 79.76% at β=0.2 and 70.61% at β=0.5, leading the strongest baseline by 0.90 and 0.74 points. On MovieLens (NCF), pFedCCG attains 88.81 Recall@20 and 92.50 HR@20 at β=0.5, and 86.31/90.69 at β=1, consistently topping all 12 baselines. On the Aya LLM-SFT task, pFedCCG achieves the lowest PPL (6.84 for k=2, 6.35 for k=3) and highest Acc@5 (80.29, 80.12) across 20 multilingual clients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pFedCCG consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. On CIFAR-10, the gap over the strongest baseline (FedAGHN) grows with heterogeneity: +1.23 pts at β=0.2, +1.80 at β=0.5, and +2.96 at β=1, indicating the controlled geometry is most beneficial under severe non-IID conditions. Cluster-based methods (CFL, FedGWC, CFLGP, pFedCS) trail by 2–6 points on CIFAR-10, confirming the expressivity gap predicted by Theorem 3.1. Similarity-driven collaborative methods (pFedGraph, FedAMP, GraphRx, FedAGHN) show the largest degradation under high heterogeneity (e.g., pFedGraph drops to 75.40% at β=1 vs. pFedCCG's 78.35%), consistent with the self-clustering collapse mechanism in Theorem 3.3. On MovieLens, pFedCCG leads by 0.97 Recall@20 over pFedGraph at β=0.5 and by 0.27 over CFLGP at β=1. On the Aya LLM-SFT task, pFedCCG achieves the best PPL (6.84 vs. 6.90 for pFedGraph at k=2; 6.35 vs. 6.41 for FedAGHN at k=3) and highest Acc@5, demonstrating transfer to multilingual fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of clustered PFL (CFL, FedGWC, CFLGP) and collaborative/graph-based PFL (pFedGraph, FedAMP, FedCollab, GraphRx, FedAGHN) by providing a unified dynamical analysis that was previously absent: it formally proves that collaboration strictly dominates clustering under the same K-anchor budget (Theorem 3.1) while simultaneously identifying two degeneration mechanisms—consensus collapse and similarity-induced self-clustering—that erase this advantage in practice. Rather than building on a single prior method, pFedCCG repurposes ideas from Markov chain theory (prescribed stationary distribution, reversible parameterization) and convex projection (Euclidean projection onto the Markov set) to design a feedback-free, stage-scheduled collaboration kernel, departing from the similarity-feedback paradigm that dominates existing collaborative PFL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two collapse theorems provide a principled diagnostic for why existing collaborative PFL methods underperform: the expressivity advantage of fine-grained mixing over hard clustering is real but fragile, and can be silently destroyed by either over-contractive aggregation or positive similarity feedback. Practically, pFedCCG offers a lightweight (0.04 s overhead for 50 clients) drop-in replacement that consistently improves personalization across four modalities without requiring oracle-quality data statistics. Open questions include whether the fixed affinity template can be made adaptive without reintroducing feedback loops, how the framework scales to thousands of clients, and whether the local-linearization theory extends to the non-convex regimes typical of deep training.
