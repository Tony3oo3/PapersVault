## Problem

Existing federated prototype distillation methods suffer from three interrelated failures under non-IID data: coupled bidirectional distillation creates non-stationary teacher targets that induce gradient conflicts, simple prototype averaging distorts global feature geometry under skewed client distributions, and output-space point-wise alignment collapses each class to a single centroid, ignoring cross-client differences in intra-class dispersion. These issues collectively degrade both global and personalized accuracy, especially as heterogeneity increases.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32x32 for SVHN and CIFAR-10/100) and, from round 2 onward, a set of global class prototypes (32-dim vectors), per-class variance vectors, and global soft predictions broadcast by the server. The client's ResNet-18/50 backbone produces 32-dimensional feature vectors that are aligned to the global prototype space; the classifier head outputs class logits and temperature-scaled soft predictions. Beyond standard model parameters, clients upload per-class prototype means, element-wise variance vectors, and averaged soft predictions, making the communication payload slightly larger than vanilla FedAvg but still compact relative to full model weights.

## Method

FedCDWA decouples knowledge transfer into three coordinated mechanisms. First, the Decoupled Distillation Strategy (DDS) separates client-side personalized prototype distillation, where each client uses its own local classifier to infer soft predictions from fixed global prototypes so gradients never flow back to server knowledge, from server-side bidirectional mutual distillation that refines the global model against aggregated local predictions. Second, Hierarchical Wasserstein Aggregation (HWA) replaces naive averaging with a two-level optimal-transport scheme: intra-class prototypes are aggregated as a Wasserstein barycenter of Dirac measures with distribution-aware adaptive weights penalizing clients with high label skewness via a ReLU-gated term, and inter-class prototypes are refined by fusing information from top-k semantically nearest classes identified through Sliced Wasserstein Distance. Third, Prototype-Variance Dual Alignment (PVDA) jointly minimizes L2 distances between local and global feature means (prototype alignment) and between local and global per-class element-wise variances (variance alignment), matching both first- and second-order statistics to prevent feature collapse and preserve intra-class structure. The paper provides a convergence bound quantifying the trade-off between local personalization and global consistency under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On non-IID partitions (Dirichlet β=0.5) with 10 clients, FedCDWA achieves 87.56% local / 86.67% global accuracy on SVHN, 61.25% / 51.38% on CIFAR-10, and 20.36% / 18.01% on CIFAR-100, outperforming all nine baselines in every setting. Gains over FedAvg range from +2.29% to +10.50% in local accuracy across datasets, and over the strongest prototype-based baselines (FedProto, FedGMKD) from +1.06% to +4.24%. Per-round wall-clock time per client is 2.49 s (SVHN), 3.35 s (CIFAR-10), and 3.39 s (CIFAR-100), comparable to or lower than FedProx, FPL, and FedGMKD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCDWA outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and client-count configuration for both local and global accuracy. On CIFAR-10 (10 clients), it leads FedAvg by +10.50% local / +2.26% global, FedProto by +4.24% / +1.71%, FPL by +9.11% / +1.47%, and FedGMKD by +5.06% / +1.67%. On SVHN (10 clients), gains over FedProto and FedGMKD are +1.64% and +1.84% local respectively, while on CIFAR-100 (10 clients) it beats FedGMKD by +4.63% local and +1.75% global. In terms of runtime, FedCDWA (2.49–3.39 s/round) is faster than FedProx, FPL, and FedGMKD on all three datasets, and requires no public data (unlike FedMD). The advantage is most pronounced under the most heterogeneous setting (10 clients, β=0.5), where the gap over the nearest prototype-based competitor (FedGMKD) reaches 4–5% in local accuracy on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCDWA extends the federated prototype distillation line initiated by FedProto and FedGMKD, which share class-level feature statistics across clients, and the hyper-knowledge distillation approach of FedHKD. It departs from these methods in three specific ways: (1) decoupling client-side personalized distillation from server-side mutual distillation to break the gradient-conflict feedback loop identified in coupled distillation (Lee et al., 2022); (2) replacing simple or Gaussian-mixture-based prototype aggregation with a Wasserstein-barycenter formulation and Sliced Wasserstein inter-class refinement; and (3) augmenting first-order prototype alignment with second-order variance matching. The distribution-aware weighting scheme (penalizing clients with skewed class distributions) is a practical refinement over the uniform or cardinality-only weighting used in FedProto and FedGMKD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled distillation design offers a clean architectural principle for federated knowledge transfer: treating server-issued targets as fixed during client updates eliminates a known source of optimization instability without sacrificing personalization. However, the Wasserstein-barycenter aggregation for Dirac measures reduces in closed form to a weighted average (Eq. 11), so the optimal-transport framing is largely theoretical rather than algorithmically distinct from weighted averaging with distribution-aware weights. Experiments are limited to three small-scale image datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10/100) with ResNet-18/50; scalability to larger backbones, ImageNet-scale data, or non-vision tasks remains untested. The inter-class SWD refinement becomes the dominant server-side cost on CIFAR-100 (2.94 s), raising open questions about efficiency at higher class counts or in dynamic-client settings the authors flag for future work.
