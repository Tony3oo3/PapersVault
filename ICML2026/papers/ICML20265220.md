## Problem

Existing video understanding agents can retrieve what happens in a video but cannot decode why it happens in terms of persuasion strategy. End-to-end LMMs hallucinate when explaining marketing intent, and general-purpose agents like DVD lack the cognitive machinery to map low-level visual facts (e.g., a scene fading to black) onto high-level abstract intents (e.g., creating suppression to trigger pain points). This paper tackles the specific gap of grounding abstract persuasion logic in pixel-level evidence for advertising videos.

## Input / Output

The system takes an advertising video (decoded at 1 FPS into 5-second clips) and a natural language query. Offline, the video is decomposed into a structured multimodal database containing VLM-generated captions, Whisper ASR transcripts, OCR text, and a subject registry indexed with BAAI/bge-m3 embeddings. The output is a concise text answer (compressed to under 30 tokens when visual anchoring is required) that must be supported by concrete visual anchors extracted from retrieved frames, with a verification gate that rejects answers lacking pixel-level evidence.

## Method

AD-MIR is a training-free, two-stage multimodal agent built on a frozen LMM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or o1) governed by structured in-context prompts. Stage 1 (Structure-Aware Memory Construction) discretizes the video into a hybrid semantic-lexical index combining dense cosine similarity with exact keyword matching, and maintains a context-anchored subject registry that dynamically activates relevant characters via a composite query-plus-narrative anchor. Stage 2 (Structured Reasoning Agent) runs a ReAct Think-Act-Observe loop over a stratified toolset: perception tools (Global Browse, Clip Search with Temporal Fusion, dual-mode Frame Inspect) for factual grounding, and a Communication Expert that projects 64 frames into 2×2 spatio-temporal grids to decode persuasion logic using marketing theory priors (AIDA staging, Cialdini's principles, central/peripheral route analysis). A Visual Anchor Verification gate checks every candidate answer against extracted frame-level evidence, triggering forced backtracking when grounding is insufficient, and an evidence-gated source hierarchy prevents sparse literal inspection from rejecting valid abstract persuasion labels while still requiring concrete anchors for factual claims.

## Results

On the AdsQA benchmark, AD-MIR with the o1 backbone achieves 38.1% strict accuracy and 60.0% relaxed accuracy, setting new state-of-the-art results across all five evaluation dimensions (VU, ER, TE, PS, AM). With the open-source Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, AD-MIR reaches 30.7% strict and 52.1% relaxed accuracy, surpassing the commercial o1 baseline (33.1% strict, 52.4% relaxed) on relaxed accuracy while remaining competitive on strict. The framework improves over its base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model by +4.5 strict and +3.1 relaxed points, and over the strongest general-purpose agent DVD by +1.8 strict and +9.5 relaxed points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-MIR(o1) outperforms the strongest general-purpose video agent DVD(o1) by +1.8 strict and +9.5 relaxed accuracy points, with the relaxed gap driven primarily by the Persuasion Strategy (PS) and Audience Modeling (AM) dimensions where structured expert reasoning provides the largest gains. Against the commercial o1 baseline used as a zero-shot reference, AD-MIR(o1) improves by +5.0 strict and +7.6 relaxed points, while AD-MIR(Qwen2.5-VL-7B) matches o1's relaxed accuracy (52.1% vs. 52.4%) despite using a 7B open-source model. Compared to the RL-optimized ReAd-R baseline, AD-MIR(Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves +5.7 strict points (30.7% vs. 25.0%) while maintaining a comparable relaxed score (+0.6), highlighting that ReAd-R's implicit optimization struggles with rigid output constraints. Over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, the agentic architecture adds +4.5 strict and +3.1 relaxed points, confirming that the gains come from structured tool orchestration rather than parametric knowledge. All open-source video LLM baselines (VideoLLaMA2-7B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, LLaVA-Video-7B) are substantially outperformed, with AD-MIR(Qwen2.5-VL-7B) exceeding the best of them (LLaVA-Video-7B) by +14.6 strict and +11.1 relaxed points.

## Relation to Prior Work

AD-MIR extends the ReAct reasoning-and-acting paradigm (Yao et al., 2022) into the advertising video domain by coupling a frozen LMM controller with domain-specific tools, departing from general-purpose video agents like DVD (Zhang et al., 2025) that focus on retrieval and visual grounding without decoding persuasive intent. It addresses a specific gap identified in RL-optimized baselines such as ReAd-R (Long et al., 2025), which align retrieval with QA through implicit data fitting but lack explicit structural constraints for persuasion analysis. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a Communication Expert module grounded in classical marketing and persuasion theory (AIDA staging, Cialdini's principles, Elaboration Likelihood Model) alongside a visual-anchor verification mechanism that enforces evidence-first reasoning, neither of which appears in prior video agent frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured agentic scaffolding with domain-specific expert modules can close the proprietary–open-source gap on advertising video QA (7B Qwen2.5-VL matching o1 at 52.1% vs. 52.4% relaxed accuracy), suggesting that architectural design can partially substitute for parameter scale in intent-centric video understanding. The visual-anchor verification and evidence-gated source hierarchy offer a practical, training-free recipe for hallucination control that could transfer to other domains requiring grounded abstract reasoning. However, the ~2× latency overhead, dependence on upstream captioning/ASR quality, and cultural specificity of advertising conventions limit immediate real-time or cross-market deployment, leaving open whether the structured approach generalizes beyond the AdsQA benchmark to broader long-form video reasoning tasks.
