## Problem

Existing active feature acquisition (AFA) methods are task-specific, requiring per-task retraining, and struggle with retrospective missingness in historical data, which induces acquisition bias and poorly calibrated uncertainty estimates. No prior approach combines generalizable, in-context AFA with principled uncertainty quantification under arbitrary missingness patterns without relying on intractable latent-variable models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262183/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a context dataset of m partially observed samples (each encoded as [masked features ⊙ missingness mask, missingness mask, observed label]) and M query samples with partially observed features (encoded as [masked features ⊙ acquisition mask, acquisition mask, zero-padded target]). It sequentially outputs a categorical distribution over the d candidate features to acquire next (restricted to a blocked policy that excludes unavailable features), and after k acquisition steps, produces a posterior predictive distribution over the target Y for each query. The sequence model is permutation-invariant over context samples, so input ordering is irrelevant.

## Method

L2M is a transformer-based sequence model with a target-prediction head and a policy head, pretrained autoregressively over a diverse prior of synthetic tasks with varying label distributions and missingness mechanisms. The predictor approximates pointwise posterior predictive distributions via autoregressive sequence modeling with context-invariance and target-equivariance inductive biases (no positional embeddings, custom attention masks), avoiding latent-variable approximations. The acquisition policy is trained end-to-end by minimizing a one-step predictive loss surrogate for conditional mutual information, using a straight-through Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to make the discrete action selection differentiable. Training proceeds in two stages: first the predictor is pretrained on random feature subsets, then the policy is trained with the predictor frozen, using blocked policies that mask unavailable actions. Theoretically, any joint minimizer of the sequence loss recovers Bayes-optimal predictions and CMI-maximizing acquisition actions (Theorem 4.2), and the framework extends to k-step lookahead planning via the same pathwise gradient estimator.

## Results

The main results (Figure 4) report log loss (lower is better) across acquisition steps on synthetic (RBF GP, Matérn GP, BNN-Plan) and real-world (Metabric, MiniBooNE, MIMIC-IV) tasks. L2M consistently achieves lower log loss than all task-specific baselines (GDFS, DIME, DQN) and its own random-acquisition ablation across all datasets. Coverage plots (Figure 3) show L2M maintains near-perfect calibration across 1–10 acquisition steps, while the task-specific MLP baseline degrades significantly. The one-step lookahead variant (Figure 5) further outperforms the greedy variant, with the largest gains at later acquisition steps on tasks with pairwise feature synergies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2M is compared against three task-specific AFA baselines: GDFS (Covert et al., 2023, greedy CMI via MLP), DIME (Gadgil et al., 2024, value-network CMI estimation), and DQN (Shim et al., 2018, RL-based acquisition). On log loss, L2M consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated dataset (RBF GP, Matérn GP, BNN-Plan, Metabric, MiniBooNE, MIMIC-IV), with the advantage growing as more features are acquired because task-specific baselines degrade under limited data. DQN is excluded from synthetic tasks due to prohibitive computational cost of training on hundreds of test tasks. L2M also outperforms a task-specific MLP in uncertainty calibration (coverage), with L2M remaining closer to the ideal diagonal across the full acquisition trajectory. The paper notes that L2M's advantage is largest under short contexts and high retrospective missingness rates, where task-specific methods struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2M sits at the intersection of active feature acquisition (AFA) and sequence-modeling-based meta-learning. On the AFA side, it inherits the conditional mutual information (CMI) acquisition objective from greedy methods (Covert et al., 2023; Gadgil et al., 2024) and the sequential decision-making framing of RL-based AFA (Shim et al., 2018), but departs from both by replacing task-specific generative or value-network models with a single transformer sequence model that performs in-context inference. On the sequence-modeling side, it builds directly on the formal connection between autoregressive sequence models and Bayesian posterior predictive distributions (Nguyen & Grover, 2022; Ye & Namkoong, 2024; Mittal et al., 2026), extending those invariance-inducing inductive biases to the AFA setting where the model must also output an acquisition action. The key conceptual departure from prior AFA is the elimination of per-task retraining and explicit latent-variable imputation: L2M handles retrospective missingness natively through the sequence model's uncertainty propagation and learns a differentiable acquisition policy via Gumbel-Softmax straight-through gradients rather than REINFORCE or Q-learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The meta-AFA formulation addresses a practical gap in healthcare and other cost-sensitive domains where features are expensive, data is retrospectively missing, and labeled data is scarce—conditions under which task-specific AFA baselines degrade. The clean identification theorem (CMI recoverable under MAR + no direct effect + positivity) and the surrogate-optimality result (Theorem 4.2) provide theoretical grounding that the in-context sequence model recovers CMI-optimal actions, which is a useful guarantee absent in prior heuristic AFA methods. Key open questions include: whether the MAR and positivity assumptions can be relaxed or diagnostically monitored, whether the approach scales to large tabular datasets (the authors suggest TabPFN-style architectures), whether time-varying feature dynamics can be accommodated, and whether pretrained language-model priors can substitute for the synthetic task priors used here. The current scope is limited to time-invariant, medium-scale tabular settings, and the full cost-sensitive MDP formulation with long-horizon planning remains unaddressed.
