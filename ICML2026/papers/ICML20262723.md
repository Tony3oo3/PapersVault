## Problem

Despite extensive research on post-training quantization (PTQ) of LLMs, no information-theoretic analysis of optimality has been attempted for linear layer quantization or its algorithms. The paper shows that the widely used GPTQ algorithm can have an arbitrarily large gap to the information-theoretic (IT) limit, and asks whether a practical algorithm can provably approach this limit without requiring expensive rotations into the PCA basis of the activation covariance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262723/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense weight matrix W ∈ R^{a×n} and calibration-derived statistics (activation covariance Σ_X, cross-covariances for drift correction, and residual stream statistics) as input, and produces a variable-length compressed bitstream encoding integer codes Z_SIC along with per-column grid spacings α_i and diagonal rescalers T, Γ. Unlike fixed-bitwidth quantizers, the output rate is determined by entropy coding of the integer matrix, so the actual bits-per-weight varies per column and per layer, with a global rate controller (secant method on the scale parameter c) matching a target average bitrate to within 0.005 bits.

## Method

WaterSIC's key innovation is allocating different quantization rates to different columns (in-features) of W, mimicking the classical waterfilling solution from information theory. Concretely, it computes the Cholesky factor L of the activation covariance (Σ_X = LL^T), sets the i-th column grid spacing to α_i = c/|ℓ_ii| (inversely proportional to the Cholesky diagonal, i.e., the conditional standard deviation of X_i given past coordinates), then applies successive interference cancellation (ZSIC) with this non-uniform spacing and entropy-codes the resulting integer matrix. Theoretically, this achieves a rate gap of at most 0.255 bits to the IT limit uniformly over all covariance matrices, whereas GPTQ (which uses uniform spacing α_i = α) can be arbitrarily far from optimal. Practical extensions include LMMSE shrinkage correction, activation drift correction (accounting for quantized-model inputs), residual stream compensation, alternating diagonal rescaler optimization, dead-feature erasure for near-singular covariances, and attention-weighted calibration for QKV projections.

## Results

On Llama-3.2-1B, WaterSIC-FT achieves WikiText-2 PPL of 30.47 at 1.00 bits, 13.59 at 2.00 bits, 10.45 at 3.00 bits, and 9.91 at 4.00 bits (unquantized: 9.76), consistently outperforming all baselines at comparable bitrates. On Qwen3-8B, WaterSIC-FT reaches PPL of 10.18 at 2.125 bits, 9.73 at 3.125 bits, and 9.67 at 4.125 bits (unquantized: 9.73), establishing new state-of-the-art at every evaluated rate. The theoretical analysis shows WaterSIC is within 0.255 bits of the information-theoretic limit uniformly over all input covariance matrices, while standard GPTQ can have an arbitrarily large gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaterSIC-FT dominates all five baselines across every evaluated bitrate on both Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen3-8B. Against Huffman-GPTQ (Chen et al., 2026), the closest entropy-coded competitor, WaterSIC-FT achieves 3.1–4.5 PPL lower at comparable rates (e.g., 13.59 vs. 17.74 at ~2.5 bits on Llama-3.2-1B; 10.18 vs. 13.97 at 2.125 bits on Qwen3-8B), with the gap widening at lower bitrates where Huffman-GPTQ exceeds 1000 PPL at 1.52 bits. Against QTIP (Tseng et al., 2024), WaterSIC-FT wins by 1.7–5.1 PPL (e.g., 13.59 vs. 18.67 at 2 bits; 9.91 vs. 10.12 at 4 bits). Against NestQuant (Savkin et al., 2025), WaterSIC-FT is 0.3–0.5 PPL better at matched rates (10.08 vs. 10.38 at 3.5 bits). Against AWQ (Lin et al., 2024), the gap is largest at low rates (10.08 vs. 16.74 at ~3.25 bits) and narrows at 4 bits (9.91 vs. 10.84). Against standard GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) on Qwen3-8B, WaterSIC-FT achieves 5.8–47.3 PPL lower (10.18 vs. 57.51 at 2.125 bits; 9.67 vs. 10.10 at 4.125 bits), demonstrating that the waterfilling rate allocation provides substantial gains even without the more sophisticated codebooks used by QTIP or NestQuant.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaterSIC builds directly on the GPTQ/Huffman-GPTQ lineage (Frantar et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2026), which already identified the Cholesky-based successive interference cancellation structure and added entropy coding. The key departure is the application of classical waterfilling rate allocation (Zamir et al., 2006) to assign different quantization rates to different columns of the weight matrix, whereas all prior PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ, QTIP, NestQuant) use a uniform rate across columns. The paper also provides the first information-theoretic rate-distortion analysis of the linear-layer quantization problem, showing that GPTQ's uniform-rate choice can yield an arbitrarily large gap to the IT limit, while WaterSIC's waterfilling allocation closes this gap to a universal 0.255-bit constant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous IT characterization of the weight-only quantization problem, giving the field a concrete optimality benchmark (0.255-bit gap) and explaining *why* GPTQ is suboptimal in a way that was previously unquantified. The waterfilling insight is conceptually simple and could be retrofitted into other quantization pipelines, potentially improving their rate-distortion tradeoffs without architectural changes. Limitations include evaluation only on 1B–8B models, layerwise (not end-to-end) loss optimization, and no hardware throughput benchmarks; scaling to 70B+ models and integrating with learned codebooks or activation quantization remain open.
