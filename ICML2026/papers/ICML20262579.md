## Problem

Existing best-of-both-worlds algorithms for online tabular MDPs achieve only first-order data-dependent regret bounds in the adversarial regime, despite the broader online learning literature offering second-order and path-length guarantees. No single algorithm simultaneously adapts to multiple data-dependent complexity measures in the adversarial regime and to variance-based measures (both gap-independent and gap-dependent) in the stochastic regime. This paper asks whether one can design a unified algorithm that achieves all of these refined guarantees for tabular MDPs with known transitions.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a finite-horizon episodic tabular MDP with known transition kernel P, horizon H, and |S| states × |A| actions, and observe losses only at visited state-action pairs along each realized trajectory (bandit feedback). At each episode the learner outputs a policy (an action distribution per state) and receives a single trajectory of H state-action-loss tuples. The regret guarantee adapts to data-dependent quantities—first-order L*, second-order Q_∞, path-length V_1 in the adversarial regime, and occupancy-weighted variance V or V^c in the stochastic regime—without requiring these quantities to be known in advance.

## Method

Both algorithms are built on optimistic follow-the-regularized-leader (OFTRL) with a log-barrier regularizer and adaptive, data-dependent learning rates. The global-optimization variant (Algorithm 1) optimizes over the occupancy-measure polytope Ω(P), uses an optimistic importance-weighted loss estimator, and applies a loss-shifting function to enable self-bounding regret analysis in the stochastic regime. The policy-optimization variant (Algorithm 2) runs OFTRL independently at each state with a dilated exploration bonus, virtual episodes to enforce learning-rate conditions, and a novel optimistic Q-function estimator that propagates the loss prediction through the Q-recursion and subtracts a margin γ_t·H/q_t(s) to guarantee controlled optimism. Two loss-prediction schemes are provided: a gradient-descent predictor (yielding path-length bounds) and an empirical-mean predictor (yielding variance-aware gap-dependent bounds that are polylogarithmic in T). The paper also establishes matching lower bounds showing the global-optimization guarantees are nearly optimal in L*, Q_∞, V_1, and V.

## Results

The paper establishes the first second-order (Q∞) and path-length (V₁) regret bounds for online episodic tabular MDPs, achieving Õ(√(SA·min{L*, HT−L*, Q∞, V₁})) in the adversarial regime via global optimization (Theorem 4.1). In the stochastic regime, the same algorithm simultaneously yields a variance-aware gap-independent bound of Õ(√(SA·VT)) and a variance-aware gap-dependent bound of U_Var + √(U_Var·C) that is polylogarithmic in T (Theorem 4.2). Policy optimization (Theorems 5.2–5.3) achieves the same data- and variance-dependent adaptivity up to an additional factor of H. Lower bounds of Ω(√(SAL*)), Ω(√(SAQ∞)), Ω(√(HV₁)), and Ω(√(SAVT)) confirm near-optimality of the global-optimization guarantees up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Zimin & Neu (2013) and Jin et al. (2021), which only achieve the worst-case √(HSAT) adversarial bound, this paper's global-optimization algorithm adapts to first-order (L*), second-order (Q∞), and path-length (V₁) complexity simultaneously, recovering √(HSAT) only in the worst case. Against Lee et al. (2020), which provides only a first-order √(SAL*) bound, the new results strictly improve by adding Q∞ and V₁ adaptivity and by extending to the stochastic regime with variance-aware guarantees. Relative to Jin et al. (2021), the gap-dependent stochastic bound removes their additional 1/min_{s,a}Δ(s,a) dependence and replaces the gap-only variance with the sharper occupancy-weighted variance V^c(s). For policy optimization, the paper matches Dann et al. (2023a) on the leading adversarial term √(H²SAL*) but adds Q∞ and V₁ adaptivity, and improves the lower-order stochastic term from H³S²A²log²(T) to H^{5/2}S^{3/2}A·log²(T). All guarantees are achieved by a single best-of-both-worlds algorithm, whereas prior results required separate algorithms for adversarial and stochastic regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OFTRL framework (Chiang et al., 2012; Rakhlin & Sridharan, 2013) and the best-of-both-worlds paradigm for episodic MDPs (Jin & Luo, 2020; Jin et al., 2021; Dann et al., 2023a) by introducing second-order (Q_∞) and path-length (V_1) complexity measures that were previously only available in multi-armed bandits (Wei & Luo, 2018; Ito, 2021), along with occupancy-weighted variance measures (V, V^c) that are H²-sharper than the Var_max and Var_max^c used in model-based stochastic MDP algorithms (Zhou et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025). The global optimization algorithm adapts the loss-shifting technique of Jin et al. (2021) to the OFTRL setting, while the policy optimization algorithm builds on the dilated bonus and virtual episode machinery of Luo et al. (2021) and Dann et al. (2023a), introducing a novel optimistic Q-function estimator with a controlled optimism margin to handle bias from loss predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish second-order and path-length regret bounds for online episodic tabular MDPs, and the matching lower bounds (Ω(√(SAL*)), Ω(√(SAQ_∞)), Ω(√(HV_1)), Ω(√(SAT))) confirm that the global optimization approach is nearly optimal in terms of these data-dependent measures. The H-factor gap between policy optimization and global optimization in the leading term remains an open problem, as does extending any of these data-dependent or variance-dependent guarantees to the unknown-transition setting, where transition-estimation errors would need to be controlled in a data-dependent way. For practitioners, the known-transition assumption limits direct applicability, but the theoretical framework and the OFTRL-with-log-barrier design provide a template for future extensions.
