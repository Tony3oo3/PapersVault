## Problem

Existing multi-modal dataset distillation methods rely on trajectory matching, which requires storing dozens of teacher model checkpoints (over 30 GB for LoRS) and performs costly bi-level optimization over synthetic data. This makes MDD memory-intensive and limits scalability to larger synthetic budgets. The paper asks whether the inner-loop optimization can be replaced by a closed-form computation that preserves distillation quality while eliminating both the checkpoint storage and the nested optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real multi-modal dataset (image-text pairs from Flickr30k/MS-COCO or audio-text pairs from AudioCaps), a pre-trained CLIP-style teacher model with frozen encoders, and a small set of synthetic pairs (100–1000) initialized from real data. Synthetic images are optimized in pixel space and captions as learned embeddings rather than raw text; for audio, log-mel-spectrograms of shape [1, 128, 1000] are synthesized directly. The output is a compact synthetic dataset whose analytic projector parameters (two cached matrices per modality, ~0.5 GB total) replace the full trajectory storage, and a teacher-generated similarity matrix used for knowledge distillation at evaluation time.

## Method

APM derives closed-form analytic solutions for the linear modal projectors under the InfoNCE loss by recasting contrastive learning as a multi-class classification problem (Lemma A.1), yielding W_I* = (|D|/2τ)(H_I^T H_I)^{-1} H_I^T V(V^T V)^{-1} and its text-side counterpart. The key insight is that these analytic parameters decompose into intra-modal whitening terms (H_I^T H_I)^{-1} and inter-modal alignment terms V(V^T V)^{-1}, so matching them simultaneously captures both within-modality diversity and cross-modality correspondence—something CovMatch's cross-covariance matching alone misses. The APM loss minimizes the Frobenius-norm gap between real and synthetic analytic parameters, stabilized by three normalizations: mean subtraction (embedding shift), 1/|D| scaling (scale explosion), and αI regularization (matrix inversion for low-rank synthetic data). The overall training objective combines the InfoNCE contrastive loss on synthetic pairs with η·L_APM (η=0.01) plus a knowledge-distillation loss on teacher-generated similarity matrices, all optimized in a single forward-backward pass with no bi-level optimization, achieving 65× storage reduction and 9.6× speedup over LoRS.

## Results

On Flickr30k with 100 synthetic pairs, APM achieves 12.8 IR@1 and 17.8 TR@1, outperforming the strongest trajectory-matching baseline RepBlend (11.5 IR@1, 16.2 TR@1) by +1.3 and +1.6 respectively. On the larger MS-COCO dataset (123k images), APM leads all baselines at every budget, reaching 7.1 IR@1 and 8.0 TR@1 with 500 pairs. On AudioCaps, APM achieves 8.3 AR@1 with 100 pairs, nearly doubling LoRS (2.7) and surpassing RepBlend (4.1). In terms of efficiency, APM reduces offline storage from 32.6 GB (LoRS) to 0.5 GB (65× reduction) and cuts per-iteration distillation time from 11.50 s to 1.20 s (9.6× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APM consistently outperforms all trajectory-matching baselines on Flickr30k and MS-COCO. Against the strongest TM method RepBlend, APM gains +1.3 IR@1 and +1.6 TR@1 at 100 pairs on Flickr30k, and +1.0 IR@1 at 500 pairs on MS-COCO (7.1 vs. 6.2), showing better scalability. Against CovMatch, which only matches inter-modal cross-covariance, APM improves IR@1 by +2.7 (12.8 vs. 10.1) at 100 pairs on Flickr30k, confirming the benefit of incorporating intra-modal covariance. Against LoRS, APM achieves +4.5 IR@1 (12.8 vs. 8.3) and +6.0 TR@1 (17.8 vs. 11.8) at 100 pairs on Flickr30k, while also reducing storage by 65× and distillation time by 9.6×. On AudioCaps, APM nearly doubles LoRS in audio retrieval (8.3 vs. 2.7 AR@1 at 100 pairs) and surpasses RepBlend by +4.2 AR@1. In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 9), APM distilled on NFNet+BERT transfers best to ResNet+BERT (6.9 IR@1, +2.7 over RepBlend) and RegNet+BERT (5.4 IR@1, +1.5 over RepBlend), indicating architecture-agnostic modality alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the trajectory matching (TM) paradigm that dominates multi-modal dataset distillation (MTT-VL, LoRS, RepBlend) by replacing the inner-loop optimization with closed-form analytic solutions for modal projectors under the InfoNCE loss. Theoretically, it extends the logistic-regression analytic solution (Lemma A.1) to the contrastive learning setting, and practically builds on CovMatch's cross-covariance matching by additionally incorporating intra-modal covariance terms. The similarity mining component adapts LoRS's sample-correspondence idea but replaces its trained LoRA-like matrix with a teacher-generated one, eliminating an additional optimization variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 65× storage reduction and 9.6× speedup make MDD substantially more practical for large-scale multi-modal datasets, removing the need to store dozens of model checkpoints. However, the method is currently restricted to CLIP-style architectures with linear projectors (non-linear activations like ReLU lack inverses), and requires a pre-trained teacher on the full dataset, which limits its applicability to settings where the full data is already available. Open questions include whether the analytic formulation extends to deeper non-linear projectors, whether it generalizes beyond pairwise (image-text, audio-text) to multi-modal (audio-visual-text) settings, and whether the performance gap versus RepBlend at larger budgets (500 pairs) indicates a fundamental ceiling of the analytic approach.
