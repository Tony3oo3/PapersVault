## Problem

Gradient- and attention-based attribution methods are highly sensitive to imperceptible adversarial perturbations, yet existing robustness techniques (explicit curvature regularization, adversarial training, SAM) incur roughly 4× computational overhead and are difficult to scale. It remains theoretically unclear whether standard SGD training dynamics can implicitly improve attribution robustness without any explicit penalty, and whether such gains transfer from gradient-based to attention-based attribution under softmax normalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images (Imagenette, CIFAR-10, STL-10) with ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16 classifiers. Attributions are computed with respect to negative log-probabilities (rather than the more common logit gradients), which ensures nonzero second-order gradients even in ReLU networks and eliminates the need for post-hoc activation replacement at evaluation time. Robustness is measured by a directional sensitivity metric R that captures worst-case angular deviation between normalized attribution vectors under bounded adversarial perturbations, complemented by validation accuracy, training time, and hyperparameter count.

## Method

The paper proposes Implicit Curvature Regularization (ICR): training with a sufficiently large learning rate near the edge of stability so that SGD's implicit bias toward flatter parameter minima propagates to lower input-space curvature, thereby improving gradient-based attribution robustness at zero additional computational cost. Proposition 1 formalizes this transfer by combining the SGD trace bound tr(G_θ) ≤ 2/η with a parameter-to-input curvature coupling constant c(x) (a layerwise signal-to-noise ratio), showing that higher learning rates provably reduce the expected maximum eigenvalue of the input Hessian. For attention-based attribution, Proposition 2 proves that softmax normalization imposes a minimum-entropy floor that monotonically bounds attribution sensitivity, preventing curvature-based robustness from transferring; this is confirmed empirically. Replacing softmax with unnormalized kernelized attention (GELU activation) removes the entropy constraint and restores ICR's effectiveness for attention-based methods. For ViTs, a differential learning rate variant (ICR†) applies a high backbone learning rate near the edge of stability while keeping the classifier head at a conservative rate to prevent training instability from high-norm [CLS] embeddings.

## Results

On Imagenette, ICR achieves 83±2% validation accuracy on ResNet-50 and 73±1% on ViT-B/16, while reducing the adversarial sensitivity metric R to 0.10±0.2 (ResNet-50) and 0.07±0.0 (ViT-B/16), compared to 0.61±0.5 and 0.11±0.1 for the unregularized baseline. ICR attains the best overall rank (1) across accuracy, robustness, computational cost, and hyperparameter sensitivity, matching the computational cost of standard training (one forward + one backward pass) with a single hyperparameter (learning rate). ECR achieves the highest accuracy (84% ResNet-50, 74% ViT-B/16) but at roughly 4× the training cost, while PAR achieves the lowest R (0.00) but collapses accuracy to 10% on ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICR outperforms all explicit baselines on the overall ranking. Against ECR (Jacobian regularization), ICR trades a marginal 1% accuracy drop (83% vs 84% on ResNet-50) for a ~4× reduction in training cost, while matching ECR's sensitivity R (0.10 vs 0.11). Against ATR (adversarial training), ICR improves both accuracy (83% vs 78%) and robustness (R=0.10 vs 0.21 on ResNet-50) at lower computational cost. Against SAM, ICR achieves higher accuracy (83% vs 79%) and substantially lower sensitivity (R=0.10 vs 0.30 on ResNet-50), while SAM requires two backward passes. Against activation-based methods (AAR/PAR), ICR matches or beats their robustness (R=0.10 vs 0.10 for AAR; R=0.10 vs 0.00 for PAR) while maintaining competitive accuracy (83% vs 75% for AAR; 83% vs 10% for PAR on ResNet-50). The key advantage of ICR is that it requires no additional hyperparameters beyond the learning rate and no architectural or loss-function modifications, making it the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads: (1) the edge-of-stability analysis of SGD showing that higher learning rates implicitly regularize parameter curvature (Wu & Su, 2023; Lee et al., 2023), (2) existing bounds linking parameter-space and input-space curvature (Dherin et al., 2022; Gamba et al., 2023a), and (3) the attribution-robustness literature connecting input Hessian eigenvalues to adversarial sensitivity of gradient-based explanations (Ghorbani et al., 2019; Dombrowski et al., 2019). The key conceptual move is composing these results to show that standard SGD dynamics alone—without explicit curvature penalties, adversarial training, or architectural changes—induce a free inductive bias toward more robust gradient-based attributions. The paper departs from prior work by proving that this transferability fundamentally breaks for attention-based attribution under softmax normalization due to an entropy floor (Proposition 2), and by using unnormalized kernelized attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) as a controlled diagnostic to confirm the normalization is the bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that practitioners can improve gradient-based attribution robustness at zero additional computational cost simply by training near the edge of stability, avoiding the ~4× overhead of explicit curvature regularization or adversarial training. The negative result—that softmax normalization creates an entropy constraint that blocks curvature-based robustness improvements for attention-based attribution—is an important caveat for anyone expecting gradient-robustness techniques to transfer uniformly across attribution families. However, the paper's scope is limited: experiments are training-from-scratch on moderate datasets (Imagenette, CIFAR-10, STL-10), the kernelized-attention fix is explicitly framed as a diagnostic rather than a deployable solution, and the authors acknowledge that pretrained-model compatibility, variable-length inputs, and attention collapse at high learning rates remain open. Scaling to ImageNet-scale pretrained transformers and developing normalization-aware robustness mechanisms are the most pressing next steps.
