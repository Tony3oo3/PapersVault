## Problem

RL-based post-training for explicit chain-of-thought (e.g., GRPO) substantially improves reasoning in multimodal large reasoning models but simultaneously degrades safety alignment, inflating jailbreak success rates by 30–50 percentage points. Existing inference-time defenses either suppress the reasoning process entirely (hurting utility) or prepend fixed safety prefixes (yielding only marginal ASR reductions), leaving no lightweight mechanism that recovers safety without sacrificing the reasoning gains that motivated post-training in the first place.

## Input / Output

SAFETHINK consumes the same adversarial multimodal query as the base model (an RGB image paired with a text prompt, drawn from jailbreak benchmarks such as HADES, FigStep, JailbreakV-28K, and MM-SafetyBench) and produces the model's native output format: an explicit chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. The only modification is that, at selected intermediate reasoning steps, a short corrective prefix ("Wait, think safely") is injected into the conditioning context before the next step is sampled, so the downstream trace and answer are generated under a safety-reconditioned distribution while retaining the original step-by-step structure.

## Method

SAFETHINK is a two-step, inference-only steering procedure that treats safety as a satisficing threshold constraint rather than a maximization objective. At each reasoning step t, a safety reward model (Llama-Guard-3, normalized to [−1,1]) scores the partial trace; if the score falls below a threshold τ = 0, the step is rejected and a pre-selected steering token s* = "Wait, think safely" is appended to the conditioning context before resampling. This token was chosen offline from a GPT-4-generated candidate set by maximizing Monte-Carlo-estimated safety success probability while minimizing KL divergence from the unsteered policy, ensuring minimal distributional shift. The central empirical finding is that steering need only activate within the first 1–3 reasoning steps: once the trajectory is redirected into a safe region, subsequent steps remain safe without further intervention, and the cumulative KL deviation stays bounded by the sum of per-step deviations over those few steps, preserving reasoning accuracy (MathVista: 65.20% → 65.00%) while cutting attack success rates by 30–60%.

## Results

On JailbreakV-28K, SAFETHINK reduces average Attack Success Rate (ASR) from 44.01% (undefended) to 5.30% across six MLRMs, while preserving MathVista reasoning accuracy at 63.46% (vs. 63.51% undefended). On Hades, R1-Onevision's ASR drops from 69.07% to 5.65%, and on FigStep, OpenVLThinker drops from 49.20% to 4.40%. The method introduces minimal latency overhead (0.1–0.9 s per query relative to ZS-SafePath) and requires steering only the first 1–3 reasoning steps to redirect the full generation toward safe completions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFETHINK outperforms all four baselines on both safety and utility metrics. Against ZeroThink and LessThink (Jiang et al., 2025), which suppress or truncate the reasoning trace, SAFETHINK achieves a 38.71-point lower average ASR (5.30% vs. 31.09%/31.78%) while preserving MathVista accuracy (63.46% vs. 54.41%/52.98%), showing that truncation-based defenses sacrifice reasoning capability without adequately closing the safety gap. Against ZS-SafePath (Jeung et al., 2025), which prepends a fixed 8-token safety prefix, SAFETHINK reduces ASR by an additional 26.45 points (5.30% vs. 31.75%) with comparable latency, demonstrating that input-level steering is insufficient when unsafe intent is embedded in visual inputs. Against AdaShield (Wang et al., 2024b), which uses adaptive shield prompting, SAFETHINK achieves substantially lower ASR across all models (e.g., 5.74% vs. 52.79% on LlamaV-o1 for JailbreakV-28K; 5.65% vs. 43.78% on R1-Onevision for Hades). The consistent pattern across all benchmarks is that early-step reasoning steering (SAFETHINK) dominates both reasoning-suppression and input-prompting strategies, confirming that the critical intervention point is within the first 1–3 chain-of-thought steps rather than at the input or output boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant strategies for defending reasoning-tuned multimodal models against jailbreaks: truncation-based methods (ZeroThink, LessThink) that suppress the reasoning trace entirely, and input-level defenses (ZS-SafePath, AdaShield) that prepend fixed safety prompts. Rather than following either approach, SAFETHINK repurposes the inference-time steering idea—reconditioning generation on an auxiliary signal—by making it conditional (triggered only when a safety reward model detects a threshold violation) and temporally localized (applied only to the first 1–3 reasoning steps). The satisficing framing, drawn from bounded rationality (Simon, 1956; Chehade et al., 2025), is the key conceptual departure: safety is treated as a constraint to be met rather than an objective to be maximized, which the authors argue avoids the over-conservatism that plagues safety-maximizing defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 4-token prefix injected at the right moment can reduce attack success rates by 30–60% across six open-source MLRMs while preserving reasoning accuracy, offering a drop-in defense that requires no retraining and adds under 1 second of latency. The 'few steps suffice' finding is operationally significant: it means safety monitoring need not be persistent throughout generation, reducing both compute and the risk of disrupting benign reasoning. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the safety reward model (Llama-Guard-3 or Qwen-Guard-3) and the fact that the steering token was selected offline on a held-out set, raising open questions about robustness to novel attack distributions or models whose safety-relevant behavior has been more thoroughly erased by RL fine-tuning.
