## Problem

Online differentially private query answering provably incurs exponentially larger error than offline mechanisms because the curator cannot exploit correlations among future queries. Real-world workloads are highly predictable (over 60% of production jobs recur on fixed schedules), yet no existing DP mechanism leverages workload predictions to close the online–offline utility gap while retaining worst-case robustness when predictions fail.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives a stream of S linear counting queries arriving in uniformly random order, an oracle prediction set P of queries likely to appear, a database x ∈ ℝⁿ, and a single global privacy budget (ε, δ). Each query must be answered immediately upon arrival with a private scalar value; the overall interaction across all S releases plus one offline precomputation must satisfy (ε, δ)-DP. The prediction set P may contain false positives (queries predicted but never realized), and the overlap ratio ρ = |P ∩ S| / S controls the regime from fully predictable to fully adversarial.

## Method

LAPRAS partitions queries into 'good' (in P) and 'bad' (not in P). Good queries are answered from a precomputed Matrix Mechanism release on P using budget ε_MM, exploiting workload correlations at zero marginal privacy cost at query time. Bad queries are answered online with independent Analytic Gaussian noise drawn from a residual pool. The key technical contribution is Smooth Allocation: using the random-order assumption, LAPRAS forms an unbiased stopping-time estimator B̂ = S(T−1)/(L−1) from the arrival position L of the T = ⌈log²S⌉-th bad query (proven unbiased via Negative Hypergeometric inverse factorial moments), then continuously recalibrates per-query spend as ε_rem / (B̂_rem + 1), so that noise adapts to the realized density of bad queries rather than budgeting pessimistically against S. A reserve budget with geometric decay provides a safety floor, and basic composition over at most S+1 releases yields the overall (ε, δ)-DP guarantee.

## Results

On the Adult and Gowalla datasets (S=100, ε=1.0), LAPRAS with the matrix-heavy split achieves median MAE of 14.3 (Adult) and 17.1 (Gowalla) at 100% prediction overlap, approaching the OfflineMM lower bounds of 6.96 and 10.8 respectively, while reducing error by over an order of magnitude compared to Online Independent Noise (193.4 and 181.2). At 0% overlap, LAPRAS (query-heavy) degrades gracefully to MAE 201.8 (Adult) and 213.9 (Gowalla), remaining within ~8% of the Online Independent Noise baseline (186.5 and 204.1). On the realistic IDEBench flights workload, LAPRAS Smooth at 50% overlap with matrix-heavy achieves MAE 50.4 versus 129.7 for Static, a 61% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CacheDP (Mazmudar et al., 2022), LAPRAS dominates by a wide margin: at 0% overlap on Adult, LAPRAS Static records NMAE 0.0019 versus CacheDP's 0.2076, a roughly 100× advantage, because LAPRAS pre-optimizes noise globally via the Matrix Mechanism while CacheDP distributes a single budget linearly across queries. Against the OfflineMM reference (Li et al., 2010), LAPRAS does not surpass it (as expected, since OfflineMM sees the full workload in advance) but approaches it closely at high overlap: 14.3 vs. 6.96 MAE on Adult and 17.1 vs. 10.8 on Gowalla at ρ=1.0. Against the Online Independent Noise baseline, LAPRAS wins decisively at overlap ≥ 0.3 (e.g., 66.8 vs. 207.0 on Adult at ρ=0.6) and remains within ~8% at ρ=0, confirming the intended consistency–robustness trade-off. The matrix-heavy budget split is essential for high-overlap gains, while query-heavy is preferable when predictions are unreliable (overlap ≤ 0.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

LAPRAS sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines: offline-optimal DP mechanisms (Matrix Mechanism, Li et al. 2010) and online DP query answering (PMW, Bun et al. 2019). It departs from the online literature by introducing a learning-augmented oracle that predicts which queries will appear, enabling a hybrid strategy that applies the offline Matrix Mechanism to predicted queries and paces a residual budget over unpredicted ones. The Smooth Allocation's stopping-time estimator, grounded in the Negative Hypergeometric Distribution, is a new theoretical tool not present in prior DP or learning-augmented work. In contrast to reactive caching approaches like CacheDP (Mazmudar et al., 2022), which exploit historical redundancy, LAPRAS is proactive, exploiting predicted future workload structure before the stream arrives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the well-documented predictability of production query workloads (60%+ recurring jobs) can be formally exploited to close the provable online-offline utility gap in DP, offering a practical path toward near-offline accuracy in streaming settings. Key limitations include the reliance on a random-order arrival assumption (adversarial bad-first ordering degrades Smooth Allocation by ~1.6×) and the SDP runtime scaling as O(|P|^1.5) for the Matrix Mechanism precomputation. Open questions include extending the estimator to partially adversarial arrivals, replacing the Matrix Mechanism with more scalable alternatives like HDMM, and integrating with adaptive composition tools for truly interactive settings.
