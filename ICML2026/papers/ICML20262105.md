## Problem

Value-based alignment of LLMs suffers from three persistent gaps: extraction methods ignore the hierarchical structure of value theories, evaluation measures presence rather than calibrated intensity, and steerability at controlled intensity levels remains poorly characterized. Preference-based approaches capture only surface-level choices, while existing value-based tools are fragmented across extraction, evaluation, and steering with no unified infrastructure for pluralistic, intensity-aware control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For extraction, the system consumes open-ended text and produces a value profile with per-value intensities on a [-10, 10] scale, weighted by embedding distance in the HIVES space. For steering, a query is conditioned on (value, intensity) pairs at four discrete levels {-2, -1, +1, +2}, yielding a steered response. For evaluation, a model response is scored by ranking it against k=6 anchor texts sampled from VIDB over m=3 iterations, with a judge LLM (Gemma-3-27B) producing total orders that are aggregated via Plackett-Luce to yield a calibrated intensity score. The VIDB itself contains 10K texts per value across 32 values and four theories (SVT, MFT, Rights, Duties), with scores normalized to [-10, 10] per value.

## Method

VALUEFLOW integrates three components into a single pipeline. First, HIVES is a hierarchical value embedding model built on Qwen3-embedding-0.6B, trained in two stages: Stage 1 uses a hierarchical contrastive loss that pulls together texts sharing ancestry up to level v and the same direction label, while Stage 2 aligns across heterogeneous theories via InfoNCE objectives against 274 cross-theory anchors and curated user-friendly instances. Second, VIDB is constructed by having a seven-LLM panel produce pairwise rankings of 10K texts per value, aggregated via Plackett-Luce maximum-likelihood estimation to yield stable latent intensity scores robust to model-specific rating biases. Third, the ranking-based evaluator estimates a response's intensity by fixing anchor utilities to their VIDB scores and solving for the response utility through PL log-likelihood over observed rankings, then mapping to [-10, 10] via per-value bounded monotone calibration. The key insight is that relative rankings across models are far more consistent than absolute scalar ratings (reducing variance from 12.6 to 2.1 and sign-flip rate from 48% to 29%), making ranking-based evaluation a more reliable foundation for intensity measurement and steering control.

## Results

On OpinionQA behavior prediction, the proposed Profile method achieves 59.1% average accuracy for Qwen3-32B and GLM-4-32B, and 55.7% for Phi-4-14B, outperforming the Default prompt (56.6%, 56.8%, 51.2%) and Modular Pluralism (39.3%, 47.7%, 43.2%) across all demographic groups. The largest single-attribute gain is Phi-4 Religion, rising from 44.5% (Default) to 57.4% (Profile), a >12-point improvement. For the embedding model, HIVES improves hierarchical ranking accuracy by over 20% and similarity correlation by over 50% relative to both UniVar and the base Qwen3-embedding-0.6B. The ranking-based VIDB evaluator achieves a mean deviation of 1.4 from human ratings and 60–79% win rates against rating-based baselines, while reducing inter-model variance from 12.6 to 2.1 and sign-flip rate from 48% to 29% compared to direct scalar ratings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniVar (Cahyawijaya et al., 2025) and the base Qwen3-embedding-0.6B, HIVES achieves over 20% higher hierarchical ranking accuracy and over 50% higher similarity correlation on both SVT and MFT, while also producing more orthogonal (disentangled) value vectors. On OpinionQA, the Profile method consistently outperforms Modular Pluralism (Feng et al., 2024) by 10–16 points in average accuracy across all three tested models (e.g., Qwen3: 59.1% vs. 39.3%; GLM-4: 59.1% vs. 47.7%), indicating that value-profile conditioning is far more informative than separately trained community LLMs. Profile also beats the Default (attribute-only) prompt by 2.5–4.5 points on average, with the largest gains on Religion and Race attributes where value orientation most strongly predicts group behavior. The ranking-based VIDB evaluator outperforms direct scalar rating baselines on all stability metrics (variance 2.1 vs. 12.6, sign-flip 29% vs. 48%) and on human-alignment metrics (pairwise ranking accuracy 84.2% vs. 77.4%), confirming that relative comparison is a more reliable evaluation paradigm than absolute scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously fragmented components of value-based LLM alignment—hierarchical value extraction, intensity evaluation, and steerable generation—into a single end-to-end framework. It builds on CLAVE-style concept pooling (Yao et al., 2024b) for cross-theory anchor construction, hierarchical contrastive learning (Zhang et al., 2022) for intra-theory embedding alignment, and value-anchor prompting (Rozen et al., 2025) for steering. The key methodological departure is replacing unstable rating-based LLM-judge evaluation with a Plackett-Luce ranking-based approach anchored to a large-scale reference database (VIDB), and extending steerability from binary directional control to calibrated multi-level intensity control across four heterogeneous value theories simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VALUEFLOW provides the first scalable infrastructure for pluralistic value alignment, with VIDB (10K texts per value across 32 values) and HIVES offering reusable resources for the community. The empirical findings—three distinct steerability regimes across models, asymmetric dose-response per value, and strong-anchor dominance in multi-value steering—give practitioners concrete guidance on which models and values can be reliably controlled and under what conditions. Key limitations include modeling intensity as a single scalar per value (losing multi-dimensional nuance), the additional LLM-call overhead of the ranking-based evaluator, and reliance on prompt-based steering that may not transfer to fine-tuned or agentic settings. Open questions include whether richer context-sensitive value representations and more efficient evaluation protocols can preserve the stability gains without the computational cost.
