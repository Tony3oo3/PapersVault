## Problem

Multimodal LLMs applied to time-series anomaly detection rely on coarse visual heuristics (e.g., 'eyeballing' obvious spikes) and fail to produce numerically grounded, fine-grained reasoning for subtle anomalies such as frequency shifts or small trend drifts. Standard reinforcement learning with globally verifiable rewards (e.g., GRPO) is insufficient to instill the detailed temporal analysis needed, as it only reinforces correct/incorrect outcomes without guiding the reasoning process toward structured, quantitative evidence.

## Input / Output

The model receives a rendered line-plot image of a univariate time series (805×124 pixels, no shaded or highlighted regions) alongside a text instruction prompt, and produces a structured three-part output: step-by-step analytical reasoning within <think> tags, a predicted anomaly category (global point, contextual point, trend, seasonal, or shapelet) within <class> tags, and precise anomalous interval(s) within <answer> tags. At inference, the system operates fully end-to-end with no external detectors, ExpCoT traces, or additional token overhead beyond the generated response.

## Method

ANOMSEER couples two components in a pure RL post-training pipeline (no SFT cold-start) on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct. First, an Expert Chain-of-Thought (ExpCoT) generator produces structured, quantitatively verifiable reasoning traces per instance by applying classical TSAD techniques (histogram-based outlier scores, FFT periodogram, smoothed gradient analysis, Matrix Profile discord detection) in a disciplined three-stage path: Observation → Reasoning & Validation → Conclusion. Second, TimerPO extends GRPO by computing a time-series reasoning advantage via optimal transport: token-level embeddings of the model's response and the ExpCoT trace are compared through a cosine-distance cost matrix, solved with entropic-regularized Sinkhorn-Knopp to yield a semantic alignment distance, which is converted into a normalized reasoning reward. This auxiliary advantage is then orthogonally projected against the main outcome-aware advantage (removing the component parallel to the detection objective) before being added with weight α=0.3, ensuring the reasoning signal refines fine-grained temporal analysis without interfering with the primary detection gradient.

## Results

On the AnomLLM benchmark, ANOMSEER-3B achieves an average Affinity F1 of 79.3% and 62.8% anomaly-type classification accuracy, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (41.2% F1, 17.2% accuracy) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (58.5% F1, 12.6% accuracy). The 7B variant further improves to 84.4% average F1 and 65.0% classification accuracy. The largest gains appear on frequency anomalies (58.9% F1 for 3B vs. 10.9% for GPT-4o) and trend anomalies (84.9% vs. 43.5%), where coarse visual heuristics fail most severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SigLLM (GPT-3.5, numerical input), ANOMSEER-3B achieves 79.3% vs. 34.8% average Affinity F1, a 44.5-point gap driven by superior frequency (58.9 vs. 15.8) and trend (84.9 vs. 19.6) detection. Compared to the Qwen2.5-VL family used as zero-shot prompting baselines, ANOMSEER-3B (79.3% F1) surpasses even the 72B variant (50.2% F1) by 29 points, demonstrating that targeted RL post-training on 3.2k samples far exceeds scaling model size alone. Against TimeMaster-3B (SFT+GRPO, the closest trained baseline), ANOMSEER-3B leads by 7.4 points in average F1 (79.3 vs. 71.9), with the largest margin on frequency anomalies (58.9 vs. 51.4 F1), confirming that vanilla GRPO's globally verifiable rewards are insufficient for subtle temporal patterns. Against the classical Matrix Profile detector (48.9% average F1, location-only), ANOMSEER-3B wins on average while also providing classification and natural-language reasoning; however, Matrix Profile retains a slight edge on point anomalies (87.4 vs. 87.8 F1 is essentially a tie, with ANOMSEER marginally ahead).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and extends the TimeMaster line of work (Zhang et al., 2025b), which combined SFT and GRPO for time-series classification in MLLMs. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing SFT+GRPO with pure RL augmented by an optimal-transport-based reasoning advantage (TimerPO) that aligns the model's intermediate reasoning tokens with expert traces (ExpCoT) derived from classical TSAD primitives (FFT, Matrix Profile, gradient analysis), and (2) introducing an orthogonal projection in advantage space to prevent the auxiliary reasoning signal from interfering with the primary detection objective. Conceptually, the paper repurposes OT from its typical role in distribution alignment or preference modeling to a process-level supervision signal for temporal reasoning, and positions classical statistical TSAD methods not as competitors but as knowledge sources to be distilled into MLLM reasoning during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 3B model trained with pure RL on 3,200 synthetic samples substantially outperforms GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro on TSAD is practically significant, suggesting that targeted RL with structured reasoning supervision can be more effective than scale for domain-specific temporal tasks. The finding that vanilla GRPO (outcome-only rewards) systematically fails on subtle anomalies like frequency shifts, while OT-based reasoning alignment fixes this, provides a concrete recipe for when and why process-level supervision matters in RL for LLMs. Key limitations include restriction to univariate series, persistent failure on boundary/short-duration anomalies, and reliance on ground-truth annotations to generate ExpCoT traces. Open questions include whether the OT+orthogonal-projection framework generalizes to multivariate settings, whether ExpCoT can be generated without ground truth (e.g., via self-play or iterative refinement), and how to incorporate external domain knowledge about real-world events that drive anomaly dynamics.
