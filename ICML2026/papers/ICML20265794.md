## Problem

Standard-cell characterization traditionally requires slow R/C extraction followed by exhaustive circuit simulation (days per library), while existing fast ML predictors either ignore layout geometry entirely (netlist-only GNNs/GTNs) or fuse layout and netlist only loosely, missing the fine-grained coupling between specific electrical nets and their surrounding metal geometry. The key unaddressed challenge is how to explicitly align netlist topology with layout geometry so the model interprets spatial details under correct electrical connectivity rather than learning them independently.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two inputs: (1) a 3-channel routed layout tensor (M0/M1/M2) rasterized at fixed H×W resolution, where pixels are populated with normalized Net IDs rather than binary occupancy, and 3D vias are spatially expanded onto adjacent 2D metal planes to preserve vertical connectivity without sparse channels; and (2) a heterogeneous netlist graph with device and net nodes connected by typed edges (device-net connectivity and net-net correlation). The output is a 6-dimensional vector of z-score-normalized values: signal rise/fall delay, rise/fall transition (10%–90% slew), and rise/fall dynamic power.

## Method

FusionCell encodes the layout with a DeiT backbone (pretrained, patch16-224) producing spatial layout tokens, and encodes the netlist with a 2-layer graph transformer that uses a structural connectivity mask as attention bias to respect circuit topology while maintaining global receptive field. The core innovation is an asymmetric topology-guided cross-attention module where graph tokens act as queries and layout tokens as keys/values, forcing each electrical node to actively retrieve the specific metal geometry that implements it—mimicking the R/C extraction process. Correlation edges in the heterogeneous graph short-circuit distance between structurally related nets, enabling the model to capture crosstalk and global signal-flow context. The fused graph tokens are mask-aware pooled and passed through an MLP regression head; the model is trained end-to-end with MSE loss on z-score-normalized targets, and the directed (non-symmetrical) query path is shown via ablation to be critical for preventing modality collapse and vision-only shortcuts.

## Results

On the ASAP7 7nm dataset (>19.5k cells, 149 types), FusionCell achieves an average MAPE of 0.92% and R² of 0.977 across six delay/power targets (rise/fall delay, transition, power), with the best individual target being Fall Delay at 0.64% MAPE. For ranking correlation, FusionCell attains average Spearman's ρ = 0.86 and Kendall's τ = 0.76, substantially outperforming all baselines. Inference over the full 19.5k-cell dataset completes in ~67 seconds on a single GPU, versus >5 days for golden simulation (≈10⁴× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FusionCell outperforms all four baselines on every target. Against ProtoCellLayout (ICCAD 2025), the closest prior multimodal method, FusionCell reduces average MAPE from 4.16% to 0.92% (4.5× lower) and improves average Spearman's ρ from 0.30 to 0.86, indicating that ProtoCellLayout's heuristic projection of layout onto graph edges discards critical spatial coupling information. Compared to the internal ablation baselines, Symmetrical Fusion (bidirectional cross-attention) achieves 2.19% MAPE and Late Fusion (concatenation of pooled vectors) achieves 2.33%, both roughly 2.4× worse than FusionCell's 0.92%, confirming that the directed graph-query → layout-key mechanism is essential. Vision-only (DeiT without netlist) is worst among the internal baselines at 3.16% MAPE and ρ ≈ 0.56, demonstrating that layout texture alone cannot resolve electrically distinct but visually similar routing. The ablation removing correlation edges (FusionCell w/o Corr.) degrades MAPE to 1.45% and ρ to 0.82, showing that net-net correlation edges contribute meaningfully beyond simple device-net connectivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FusionCell departs from two dominant lines in standard-cell characterization: netlist-only graph surrogates (GNNs, GTNs) that capture connectivity but discard layout geometry, and vision-only layout regressors that ignore electrical identity. The closest prior work, ProtoCellLayout (Luo et al., 2025), injects layout cues into graph edges via heuristic projection, but FusionCell instead treats layout and netlist as separate modalities fused through an explicit asymmetric cross-attention mechanism where netlist tokens query layout tokens. Architecturally, it repurposes off-the-shelf DeiT and Graph Transformer backbones, with the novelty residing in the topology-guided fusion strategy and the heterogeneous device-net graph construction with typed correlation edges rather than in any single encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^4× speedup (67 s vs. >5 days for 19.5k cells) makes exhaustive DTCO design-space exploration practical, and the 0.92% MAPE with ρ=0.86 ranking correlation positions FusionCell as a viable pre-screening surrogate before expensive sign-off simulation. However, the evaluation is confined to a single open-source 7nm PDK (ASAP7) and within-family layout-variant generalization; whether the topology-guided fusion transfers to multi-corner characterization, more advanced nodes with complex DRCs, or cross-family generalization remains open. The released dataset (>19.5k cells, 149 types) is a useful community benchmark, but the single-PDK scope limits immediate industrial applicability.
