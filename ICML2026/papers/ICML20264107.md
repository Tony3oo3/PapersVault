## Problem

Current safety interventions for large foundation models rely on the locality hypothesis, suppressing isolated neurons or features to block harmful concepts. However, harmful semantics are distributed across multiple layers and propagate through complex cross-layer computation paths, making localized interventions brittle: they fail to prevent semantic leakage under adversarial attacks while simultaneously degrading general utility by disrupting polysemantic neurons. The paper asks whether intervening at the causal path level—rather than at individual nodes—can achieve robust safety without compromising model capabilities.

## Input / Output

For diffusion models, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image; for MLLMs, it takes an image plus text prompt and produces a text response. The intervention operates entirely at inference time on internal activations, requiring a one-time offline calibration phase where paired sensitive/non-sensitive prompts are used to identify the sensitive onset layer and a Top-K SAE is trained on intermediate features (expansion factor 4, hidden dim 3072). At deployment, the model processes inputs normally but applies selective path-level scaling (factor λ) to identified causal circuits, adding only ~3.28 seconds of overhead per image generation.

## Method

TraceRouter is a three-stage 'Discover-Trace-Disconnect' framework. In the discovery stage, it localizes the sensitive onset layer by computing a sensitivity score (SS) that measures attention divergence from sensitive modifiers to target nouns minus contextual disturbance, then uses a Top-K SAE to disentangle dense activations into sparse features and selects sensitive neurons via differential weighted frequency scores (ΔWFS) between sensitive and non-sensitive samples. In the tracing stage, it back-projects SAE features into the dense latent space and applies zero-out interventions to source neurons, quantifying downstream causal impact via Feature Influence Scores (FIS) that combine activation frequency, conditional mean magnitude, and expected activation shift. In the disconnection stage, it decomposes each downstream layer's activation into a sensitive pathway component and an orthogonal component using a binary mask derived from high-FIS neurons, then selectively scales only the sensitive pathway by factor λ (optimal at λ=2) while leaving orthogonal routes intact. The key insight is that harmful semantics form sparse, tree-structured causal circuits across layers, and physically severing these circuits—rather than suppressing individual neurons—prevents adversarial detour routes while preserving general utility.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 1.4, TraceRouter achieves 99.2% DSR on I2P nudity and 93.6% on I2P violence, with adversarial robustness of 74.8% (P4D) and 98.7% (Ring-A-Bell), while maintaining the best generation fidelity (CLIP Score 31.27, FID 16.24). On LLMs, it reaches 99.6% average DSR on LLaMA3-8B and 98.8% on Mistral-7B across GCG, AutoDAN, Template, and Prefill jailbreaks. On MLLMs, it attains 99.60% average safety on LLaVA-1.5 and 98.40% on MiniGPT-4, with negligible utility degradation (≤0.8% drop on Global-MMLU-Lite and MM-Bench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraceRouter outperforms all 15 baselines across every reported metric. Among DMs methods, it surpasses the strongest baseline SNCE [He 2025] by +0.7% on I2P(N), +11.3% on I2P(V), +17.4% on P4D, and +5.0% on Ring-A-Bell, while also achieving the best CLIP Score (31.27 vs. 30.87) and FID (16.24 vs. 16.64). Earlier weight-level methods (ESD, UCE, CA, MACE) trail by 9–17% on I2P(N) and degrade fidelity substantially (FID up to 23.45). For LLMs, TraceRouter edges out HumorReject [Wu 2025] (99.6% vs. 98.8% on LLaMA3-8B; 98.8% vs. 96.6% on Mistral-7B) and Circuit Breaker [Zou 2024] (99.6% vs. 97.4%; 98.8% vs. 90.6%), with the largest margin on Mistral-7B where the original model scores only 6.6%. On MLLMs, it beats SafePTR [Chen 2025] (99.60% vs. 98.40% on LLaVA-1.5) and clearly outperforms ESCO [Gou 2024] and CoCA [Gao 2024] on MiniGPT-4. The consistent pattern is that path-level intervention yields both higher adversarial robustness and better utility preservation than neuron-level or weight-level suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'locality hypothesis' in LFM safety, which assumes harmful semantics can be suppressed by targeting isolated neurons or features (e.g., SNCE, SLD-Med, ESD). It builds on mechanistic interpretability findings (Nanda et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025b) that complex behaviors arise from distributed cross-layer circuits, and repurposes Top-K Sparse Autoencoders (Cunningham et al., 2023) not merely for interpretability but as a feature-disentanglement tool for causal tracing. The key methodological contribution is the Feature Influence Score (FIS) via zero-out interventions, which bridges SAE-identified features to downstream causal pathways — a step beyond prior neuron-level or representation-steering approaches that never reconstruct the propagation topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that path-level intervention achieves near-perfect safety (99.2% DSR) with negligible utility cost (0.07 CLIP drop) across seven architectures spanning DMs, LLMs, and MLLMs, the paper provides a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for safety that avoids the catastrophic forgetting of weight-level editing and the semantic leakage of neuron-level suppression. The random-suppression control (safety *degrades* to 68.7%) and path-amplification counterfactual (safety drops to 72.2%) offer strong causal evidence that the identified circuits are genuinely necessary, not merely correlated. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is whether the Discover-Trace-Disconnect framework generalizes beyond safety to modulating other functional circuits (reasoning, knowledge retrieval) without disrupting multi-purpose pathways.
