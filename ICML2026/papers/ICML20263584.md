## Problem

Class imbalance in federated learning degrades minority-class accuracy, but standard centralized remedies (reweighting, synthetic augmentation) are infeasible under privacy constraints. Per-Class Normalization (PCN) addresses gradient imbalance by normalizing each class gradient to unit norm, yet in multi-class settings it suffers from directional noise amplification and unstable scaling when summing many unit vectors. Additionally, selecting optimal per-client resampling rates in FL is a combinatorial search problem that existing methods handle only via fixed uniform rates or independent per-client tuning, ignoring cross-client interactions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on heterogeneous client data spanning RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 224×224 chip micrographs), 104-dimensional tabular features (Adult Income), and 196-length network-flow sequences (UNSW-NB15), producing a single class label per sample. A distinguishing aspect is that each client applies a local resampling rate r_k that reweights class frequencies in its mini-batches before gradient computation, and the server jointly tunes these K rates across clients rather than fixing them a priori. The federated protocol aggregates client model deltas via weighted averaging, with the server broadcasting both the global model and per-client sampling rates each round.

## Method

FedCGNM is a client-side optimizer that partitions C classes into H (typically 2) contiguous groups by minimizing within-group variance of sorted class proportions, then maintains an exponential-moving-average momentum per group and normalizes each group momentum to unit length before summing them as the update direction. This reduces the number of normalized vectors from C to H, mitigating the scaling mismatch of PCN while equalizing majority/minority influence, and the momentum attenuates directional noise from rare-class gradients. A convergence theorem (Theorem 3.6) shows O(T^{-1/2}) stationary-point rate with an additional O(V_T·T^{-1/2}) term capturing cumulative variation of the resampling-rate schedule, establishing that rates must stabilize for convergence. FedHOO, an auxiliary X-armed-bandit tuner for small-K federations, exploits FL's linear aggregation: each client trains with two probe rates per round, and the server synthesizes all 2^K joint rate combinations by linearly combining the returned deltas, evaluating them on private validation splits to select the best model and shrink the search interval—achieving exponential coverage at linear per-client cost.

## Results

On the small-client (K=5, IID) setting, FedCGNM+FedHOO achieves the best F1 across all eight benchmark configurations, reaching 0.8628 on CIFAR-10-LT20, 0.7485 on CIFAR-100-LT100, and 0.3183 on CIFAR-100-LT100, outperforming FedAvg+U by up to 29% relative (0.3183 vs. 0.2466 on CIFAR-100-LT100). On the proprietary Chip-Defect-Detection dataset (K=5, Non-IID), FedCGNM+FedHOO attains a weighted accuracy of 90.87, compared to 72.83 for FedAvg and 85.37 for the strongest baseline (FedCGN with uniform rate). In the moderate-client (K=20) regime, FedCGNM+U still leads all baselines, e.g., 0.8010 vs. 0.7128 (FedAvg) on CIFAR-10-LT20, with noticeably milder degradation than FedCGN as client count increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCGNM+FedHOO outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and federation regime. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), the improvement is most dramatic under extreme imbalance: 0.3183 vs. 0.2466 F1 on CIFAR-100-LT100 (K=5), a 29% relative gain. FedProx (Li et al., 2020) and Weighted CE (Aurelio et al., 2019) trail FedCGNM by 1–8 points on image benchmarks and by up to 5 points on UNSW-NB15. Ratio Loss (Wang et al., 2021) is the closest competitor on mild-imbalance tabular data (Adult-3.17: 0.8428 vs. 0.8463) but falls behind by 3–7 points on all image benchmarks. CLIMB (Shen et al., 2022) performs competitively on CIFAR-10-LT20 (0.8518) but degrades sharply on CIFAR-100-LT100 (0.2681 vs. 0.3183). FedGraB (Xiao et al., 2023) is consistently the weakest among the FL-specific baselines, losing 2–10 points to FedCGNM across settings. On the industrial CDD dataset, FedCGNM+FedHOO (90.87 weighted accuracy) surpasses the best baseline FedCGN (85.37) by 5.5 points and FedAvg (72.83) by 18 points, confirming that the grouped-momentum optimizer and joint rate search transfer to real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Per-Class Normalization (PCN, Francazi et al., 2023) from per-class gradient normalization to a grouped, momentum-augmented variant (FedCGNM), addressing PCN's scaling-mismatch and directional-noise problems in multi-class settings. For hyperparameter search, it adapts the X-armed bandit / Hierarchical Optimistic Optimization framework (Bubeck et al., 2011) to the federated setting (FedHOO), exploiting FL's linear aggregation to evaluate 2^K joint rate combinations from only two local runs per client. Unlike prior FL imbalance methods (CLIMB, FedGraB, Ratio Loss) that operate at the loss or model level, FedCGNM intervenes directly at the gradient/optimizer level, and unlike FAST (Wang et al., 2023) which tunes per-client rates independently, FedHOO explicitly searches the combinatorial joint space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical validation on a 780k-image industrial chip-defect dataset (1.7% defect rate) with a 16% improvement over the best baseline demonstrates real-world relevance beyond public benchmarks. The convergence analysis that explicitly incorporates time-varying resampling-rate schedules (requiring V_T/√T → 0) provides a theoretical foundation for practitioners choosing dynamic sampling strategies. Key limitations include FedHOO's restriction to small federations (K ≤ 5–6) due to exponential validation cost, and the grouping rule's reliance on H=2 which, while empirically optimal, lacks a general theoretical justification for when more groups might help. Open questions include whether the approach extends to partial participation, very large client counts, or non-classification tasks.
