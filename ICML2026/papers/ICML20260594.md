## Problem

Existing LLM alignment methods (RLHF, inference-time steering) bind value preferences directly to the backbone's residual stream, where value representations are fragile, low-dimensional, and continually distorted by task-driven feature interactions. This structural mismatch causes alignment to fail under adversarial or distributionally shifted inputs, as value signals shift unpredictably alongside contextual dynamics. The paper asks how to achieve stable, context-independent value alignment without disrupting the backbone's internal representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen backbone LLM and extracts hidden states from a designated middle-to-late layer (e.g., layer 20 of 28 for Llama-3.2-3B), operating in a low-dimensional value subspace (d_v = 128–256) decoupled from the full hidden dimension. It produces K learnable bridge tokens (K = 5–10) that are inserted into the KV cache at positions immediately following the prompt, serving as attention targets for all subsequent layers. During autoregressive generation, bridge tokens are dynamically refreshed every R steps (default R=5) via EMA momentum (β=0.8), and the final output is the backbone's aligned text generation with the backbone parameters entirely frozen.

## Method

SVGT introduces an independent value module with two core mechanisms. First, a dual-pathway value encoder (unconditional projector + conditional cross-attention over the prompt) maps hidden states into a dedicated value space, where a scalar discriminator assigns alignment scores and its gradient yields a directional correction Δz. Second, the Latent Value Bridge (LVB) transduces this correction into bridge tokens via cross-attention retrieval from a context-value manifold, constrained to the model's learned representation manifold and integrated through a zero-initialized gated residual connection anchored at the prompt's terminal state. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) unconditional discrimination on standalone toxic samples, (2) conditional discrimination on paired prompt-response data with asymmetric learning rates, and (3) LVB training with a multi-objective loss (cross-entropy + dense safety supervision + manifold regularization) while the backbone, encoder, and discriminator remain frozen. The key architectural insight is that providing values as external attention targets (bridge tokens) is structurally superior to invasive residual injection, preserving generation fluency while enabling dynamic, context-sensitive steering throughout decoding.

## Results

Across four backbones (GPT-2, Qwen2-1.5B, Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B), SVGT reduces HarmBench Attack Success Rate by 72–80% relative to unguided generation and raises refusal rates to 75–92%, while reducing WildGuard harmful scores by 72–89%. Critically, SVGT preserves or slightly improves generation perplexity (e.g., GPT-2 PPL 24.53→23.89; Mistral-7B 5.60→5.52), whereas ITI and RE-Control inflate PPL by 20–40%. On OOD benchmarks (ToxicChat, XSTest), SVGT still cuts ASR by 48–66% without dataset-specific tuning, and general reasoning drops are minimal (MMLU −2 pp, GSM8K −3 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVGT outperforms all seven baselines on every safety metric across all four backbones. Against DPO (LoRA), SVGT achieves substantially lower ASR (e.g., 18.5% vs. 25.5% on Llama-3.2-3B) and lower harmful scores, while DPO inflates PPL by 37% (6.71→9.21) versus SVGT's modest 9% increase. ITI and RE-Control, the two inference-time steering baselines, reduce ASR by only 35–63% and degrade PPL by 20–40%, whereas SVGT cuts ASR by 72–80% with near-neutral PPL. Among decoding-time methods (PPLM, GeDi, DExperts), SVGT achieves the lowest ASR (18.5% vs. 48.5%, 32.0%, 29.0% respectively on Llama-3.2-3B), attributed to its value-space-grounded attention targets versus token-level logit manipulation. Prefix Tuning, the closest mechanistic analogue, reaches 31.5% ASR but lacks SVGT's dynamic refresh and value-space conditioning, yielding a 13 pp ASR gap. The consistent pattern is that SVGT uniquely combines the strongest safety enforcement with the best capability preservation, a trade-off no other baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVGT departs from both training-time alignment (RLHF, DPO, Constitutional AI), which embeds values implicitly across billions of parameters, and inference-time steering (ITI, RE-Control, CAA), which directly injects vectors into the residual stream. Its closest mechanistic relative is prefix-tuning, but SVGT extends this by grounding the inserted tokens in a learned value space and dynamically refreshing them via EMA during generation. The dual-pathway encoder (unconditional prior + conditional cross-attention) draws on curriculum learning and the separation of context-invariant from context-dependent signals, while the gradient-based correction in a separate value space adapts the PPLM paradigm without requiring backpropagation through the full backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode: value representations in the residual stream are fragile and shift under adversarial or novel inputs, causing alignment to break precisely when it is most needed. The plug-and-play, frozen-backbone design makes SVGT practically attractive for deploying safety on existing models without retraining, and the perplexity preservation (even slight improvement) over activation-steering baselines is a meaningful practical advantage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond safety to broader value domains (fairness, honesty), how it scales to 70B+ models, and whether the single-layer linear discriminator is sufficient for more nuanced normative judgments. The +52–65% latency overhead also limits real-time deployment in latency-sensitive settings.
