## Problem

Unimodal diffusion-based adversarial purification struggles to preserve semantic label consistency under strong or adaptive attacks, while existing multimodal variants (e.g., OSCP) rely on computationally expensive adversarial training or distillation for cross-modal alignment. Both families also lack theoretical guarantees on purification effectiveness and convergence, hindering principled design and real-time deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes an adversarially perturbed RGB image (resized to 256×256 to match Stable Diffusion's input resolution) and produces a purified image in pixel space that lies close to the clean data manifold. Purification is performed via 5–20 projected gradient steps on x₀ with a single randomly sampled (t, ε) pair per iteration, using a frozen miniSD-diffusers backbone and a pre-learned class-agnostic prompt as the sole conditioning signal. No class label or attack-specific information is required at inference time.

## Method

MultiDAP operates in two stages. First, a class-agnostic prompt (M learnable context tokens of dimension 768) is optimized from clean image-text pairs by minimizing the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss while keeping the entire Stable Diffusion backbone frozen, yielding a universal semantic prior that tightens the ELBO on the unconditional data likelihood. Second, at test time, the adversarial input is injected into the forward diffusion process at a mid-range timestep (t ∈ [400, 600]), and purification proceeds by minimizing a regularized DDPM loss — the noise-prediction MSE conditioned on the learned prompt plus an ℓ₂ proximity term to the adversarial input — via projected SGD on x₀ for only 5–20 steps. The key architectural choice is conditioning the U-Net denoiser on the learned prompt through cross-attention, which injects high-level semantic guidance that stabilizes the short optimization trajectory and prevents semantic drift. Theoretical analysis provides an ELBO-improvement guarantee for prompt learning (Theorem 3.1) and an O(1/K) expected-descent bound for the purification loop under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions (Theorem 3.5), explaining why a small fixed number of steps suffices.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, MultiDAP with learned prompts achieves 72.38% robust accuracy under AutoAttack (ℓ∞, ε=8/255) and 76.05% under AutoAttack (ℓ2, ε=0.5) using only 5 purification steps, outperforming DiffPure (44.53% and 72.65%) and Likelihood Maximization (71.68% and 75.00%). On ImageNet-1K, MultiDAP reaches 41.26% AutoAttack robust accuracy with 20 steps, surpassing DiffPure (40.93%) and Strong PGD Adversarial Training (37.89%). On CIFAR-100, MultiDAP obtains 38.15% AutoAttack robustness, matching the strongest adversarial-training baseline AT-DDPM (Wang et al., 2023) at 38.83% while requiring no adversarial training. In terms of efficiency, MultiDAP achieves the lowest FLOPs (38.8G on CIFAR-10) and fastest inference latency (371 ms) among all compared purification methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffPure, MultiDAP achieves a substantial 27.85-point gain in ℓ∞ AutoAttack robustness (72.38% vs. 44.53%) while remaining competitive on ℓ2 (76.05% vs. 75.59%), all with only 5 gradient steps versus DiffPure's dozens of Langevin iterations. Compared to Likelihood Maximization (LM), MultiDAP edges ahead on all three attack metrics (72.38% vs. 71.68% on AA-ℓ∞, 76.05% vs. 75.00% on AA-ℓ2, 68.21% vs. 65.22% on PGD-20), demonstrating the benefit of learned semantic prompts over unimodal likelihood maximization. Against CLIPure-Diff, MultiDAP wins by 16.64 points on AA-ℓ∞ (72.38% vs. 55.74%) but trails by 3.97 points on AA-ℓ2 (76.05% vs. 80.02%); versus CLIPure-Cos, MultiDAP leads on both AA-ℓ∞ (+8.17) and AA-ℓ2 (+10.11). OSCP (CAP-only) remains the strongest ℓ2 baseline at 82.02%, exceeding MultiDAP by 5.97 points, but MultiDAP surpasses it by 5.93 points on AA-ℓ∞ and 9.61 points on PGD-20. On ImageNet-1K, MultiDAP (41.26%) outperforms DiffPure (40.93%), LM (38.27%), and all adversarial-training baselines (≤37.89%), confirming scalability to large-scale natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiDAP extends the diffusion-based adversarial purification paradigm (DiffPure, Likelihood Maximization) by replacing unimodal denoising with prompt-conditioned latent diffusion, drawing on Stable Diffusion's text-to-image architecture and the continuous prompt-learning idea from CLIP-based VLMs (CoOp). It departs from recent multimodal purification work (OSCP/ControlNet) by eliminating the need for adversarial training or distillation of the diffusion backbone, instead optimizing a small set of class-agnostic prompt tokens (<0.02% of model parameters) on clean data. The theoretical contribution connects the DDPM noise-prediction loss to ELBO maximization and provides convergence guarantees for the few-step purification, filling a gap that prior multimodal purification methods leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-robustness trade-off: MultiDAP matches or slightly exceeds DiffPure's robustness while requiring 5–20 gradient steps versus dozens of Langevin iterations, cutting FLOPs by roughly 5× on CIFAR-10. This makes diffusion-based purification more deployable in latency-sensitive settings without sacrificing the generalization to unseen attacks that purification offers over adversarial training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to lightweight diffusion backbones (the current reliance on Stable Diffusion/miniSD is a notable compute cost), whether it generalizes to domain-shifted or out-of-distribution attacks, and whether richer multi-modal prompts (beyond class-agnostic tokens) could further improve semantic fidelity.
