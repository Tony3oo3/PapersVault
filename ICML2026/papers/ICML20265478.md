## Problem

Existing Neural Granger Causality methods rely on local importance measures (weight magnitudes, input-output Jacobians) to extract causal graphs from trained networks, which fail to capture global feature interactions and saturation effects inherent in nonlinear architectures. This creates a fundamental misalignment: the model can perfectly encode temporal dependencies, yet the inference mechanism cannot identify them. The paper identifies the importance measure as the underexplored cornerstone of the causal discovery triad (importance measure, predictive model, penalty) and addresses this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional multivariate time series of length T and a lag order K, constructing a lagged feature vector of dimension Kd as input to a neural network (Residual MLP by default). It produces two outputs: continuous Info-Shap importance scores for every (target series i, source series j, lag k) triple, and a binary Granger causal adjacency matrix obtained via a knockoff-style data-adaptive thresholding procedure that controls a target FDR level q. The regularizers are model-agnostic and integrate into the training loss of any differentiable architecture.

## Method

The paper defines the Information-Theoretic Shapley value (Info-Shap) by constructing a coalition value function from the Conditional Generative Information Matrix (CGIM) under a Gaussian conditional assumption, yielding a closed-form expression that decomposes into an individual effect E[|∂_i g|²] and a pairwise interaction effect ½Σ_{j≠i} E[|∂_i g ∂_j g|]. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) establishes that zero Info-Shap is equivalent to Granger non-causality via the dummy axiom of cooperative game theory. Two model-agnostic regularizers are derived: Shap (exact ℓ₁ penalty on Info-Shap values) and F-Shap (a scalable surrogate replacing the element-wise ℓ₁ norm with a squared Frobenius norm, approximated via Hutchinson's trace estimator with random projections to avoid full Jacobian materialization). The training objective combines MSE prediction loss with the chosen regularizer, and a residual-bootstrap knockoff filter provides data-adaptive thresholding for binary graph recovery.

## Results

On the CausalTime benchmarks, F-SRNGC achieves the best AUROC of 0.961 on AQI (d=36) and 0.820 on Traffic (d=20), with AUPRC of 0.929 and 0.682 respectively. On the high-dimensional DREAM3 gene regulatory networks (d=100, T=21), SRNGC attains AUROC of 0.700–0.702 on Ecoli sequences, outperforming all baselines. On the nonlinear Lorenz-96 system (F=40, T=1000), the exact Shap regularizer reaches AUROC 0.984 versus 0.941 for the Jacobian-ℓ1 baseline, demonstrating the advantage of global over local importance measures for complex dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-SRNGC outperforms the closest NGC baseline JRNGC (Zhou et al., 2024) by 3.3 AUROC points on AQI (0.961 vs 0.928) and 9.1 points on Traffic (0.820 vs 0.729), while also surpassing CUTS+ (Cheng et al., 2024) by 6.8 points on AQI. On DREAM3, SRNGC leads Rhino (Gong et al., 2023) by 1.5–2.2 AUROC points and JRNGC by 3.4–3.5 points across Ecoli sequences. The method loses to eSRU (Khanna & Tan, 2020) on the Medical dataset (0.736 vs 0.756 AUROC), which the authors attribute to predictive model inadequacy rather than the importance measure. On the nonlinear Lorenz-96 system, the Shapley-based regularizer consistently outperforms its Jacobian counterpart (e.g., 0.984 vs 0.941 AUROC at F=40, T=1000), confirming that global attribution captures interactions that local sensitivity misses. On the linear VAR(3) benchmark, all four penalty variants perform comparably (AUROC ≥ 0.88), consistent with the theoretical expectation that the Jacobian fully characterizes linear dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the Neural Granger Causality (NGC) paradigm (Shojaie & Fox, 2022) but identifies a fundamental misalignment: existing NGC methods rely on local importance measures (Jacobian entries, input-layer weights) that are insufficient for deep nonlinear models. It builds on the Shapley value from cooperative game theory (Shapley, 1953) and the generative information framework (Cheng & Tong, 2024) to define a closed-form global importance measure (Info-Shap), and generalizes Jacobian regularization (Hoffman et al., 2019) into a two-component penalty (F-Shap). The knockoff filter (Barber & Candès, 2015) is adapted for data-adaptive thresholding of causal edges. The key departure is making the inference mechanism model-agnostic, decoupling causal graph extraction from architectural constraints that prior methods (cMLP, GVAR, SENN-based approaches) require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence between zero Info-Shap and Granger non-causality (Theorem 3.2) provides a clean axiomatic foundation that prior local-measure approaches lack, and the model-agnostic regularizers can be plugged into any differentiable architecture without structural compromises. However, the Gaussian assumption on the conditional distribution, while noted as analytically convenient rather than essential, limits the generality of the closed-form result. The knockoff-style thresholding lacks formal FDR guarantees under time-series dependence, and the F-Shap approximation incurs a measurable performance cost relative to exact Shap, particularly in low-sample regimes. Open questions include extending the framework to latent confounders, contemporaneous effects, and exploring whether the 2-additivity assumption on the value function can be relaxed for higher-order interactions.
