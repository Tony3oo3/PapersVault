## Problem

Integrating GNNs and LLMs on text-attributed graphs remains challenging because prior alignment methods rely on coarse-grained output-stage matching (logit mixing or concatenation), use insufficient training constraints (proxy token losses or synthetic pseudo-pairs), and ignore global distributional consistency between the two modalities. These shortcomings cause representation drift in intermediate layers and limit generalization under distribution shift and label scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265719/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text-attributed graph (adjacency matrix plus per-node text such as paper titles/abstracts, user profiles, or product descriptions) and produces either node class labels (via a linear softmax head on GNN embeddings or via verbalizer log-likelihood on LLM outputs) or binary link-prediction scores (via affirmative/negative verbalizer probability differences). A distinctive aspect is the bidirectional information flow: intermediate LLM layer outputs are injected into GNN message passing at four paired layer indices, while final GNN embeddings are projected into B=10 soft-prompt tokens prepended to LLM instructions, all operating in a shared 512-dimensional latent space.

## Method

ERAlign projects 4-layer GraphSAGE and 32-layer LLaMA2-7B (LoRA-tuned) intermediate representations into a common 512-dim space via learnable MLP projectors, then defines a set EBM whose energy is the empirical Cramér distance (cross-modal pairwise distances minus intra-modal dispersions) to enforce distributional rather than point-wise alignment. Instead of the expensive Langevin sampling required by Contrastive Divergence or the nearsightedness of Score Matching, training uses an Energy Discrepancy (ED) objective that contrasts the energy of data samples against Gaussian-perturbed samples at S=4 multi-scale noise levels, with a w-stabilization term to bound the log-estimator and prevent numerical divergence. Theoretically, ED interpolates between score matching (t→0) and maximum likelihood (t→∞), and its multi-scale form equals the integral of the score-matching loss over noise scales, providing both global mode bridging and local structure preservation. Two output variants are supported: ERAlign_GNN uses a linear classifier on the final GNN layer, while ERAlign_LLM injects GNN embeddings as soft prompts into the LLM for verbalizer-based prediction, enabling zero-shot cross-task transfer to link prediction.

## Results

Under the full-supervised setting, ERAlign_GNN achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy across all eight TAG benchmarks, with headline results of 90.75% on Cora, 92.17% on PubMed, 78.07% on Arxiv, 68.66% on Instagram, 65.58% on Reddit, 89.38% on Photo, and 90.07% on Computer. In the semi-supervised setting (20% labeled nodes), ERAlign_GNN further demonstrates strong label efficiency, reaching 52.33% on Cora and 63.27% on Photo. For zero-shot cross-task transfer to link prediction, ERAlign_LLM attains the highest AUC across five datasets (e.g., 71.17% on PubMed, 66.91% on Arxiv), outperforming the strongest transfer baseline TEA-GLM by 1.2–3.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERAlign_GNN outperforms all GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) by 2–12% across datasets, and surpasses text-only PLMs (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa) by 5–18%, confirming that neither structure nor text alone suffices. Among GNN-LLM enhancers, ERAlign_GNN beats the strongest (SimTeG at 88.75% on Cora, ENGINE at 86.79%) by 2.0% and 4.0% respectively, and outperforms GraphAdapter (88.95% on Cora) by 1.8%. Against GNN-LLM predictors, ERAlign_GNN exceeds LLaGA (85.54%) by 5.2% on Cora and GraphGPT (85.12%) by 5.6%, while ERAlign_LLM also surpasses TEA-GLM in zero-shot link prediction AUC by 1.2–3.2% across five datasets. In the semi-supervised (20% labels) setting, ERAlign_GNN leads the best enhancer GLEM (49.01% on Cora) by 3.3% and ENGINE (42.32%) by 10.0%, demonstrating superior label efficiency. Against the most recent LLM-based baselines LLM4NG (87.24%) and Locle (86.52%), ERAlign_GNN achieves 90.75% on Cora, a 3.5% margin, indicating that explicit energy-based distribution alignment provides gains beyond pseudo-label or contrastive strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: GNN-LLM integration for text-attributed graphs and energy-based representation learning. Prior GNN-LLM methods (TAPE, ENGINE, LLaGA, GraphAdapter, TEA-GLM) typically align representations only at the output stage via logit mixing or concatenation, relying on proxy signals like autoregressive token loss. ERAlign departs by formulating alignment as a distribution-matching problem in a shared latent space, using a set EBM with the Cramér distance as the energy function. The training scheme (Energy Discrepancy) is theoretically derived from the score-matching/maximum-likelihood framework of Song & Kingma (2021), positioning it as a sampling-free alternative to Contrastive Divergence that interpolates between local score matching and global likelihood gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap in GNN-LLM integration: enforcing global distributional consistency between structural and textual representations rather than relying on point-wise or output-level matching. The ED training scheme is practically valuable because it eliminates MCMC sampling while retaining theoretical guarantees, making EBM-based alignment feasible at the scale of 7B LLMs. Limitations include dependence on a specific LLM backbone (LLaMA2-7B with LoRA), the need to tune multiple hyperparameters (noise scales, perturbation count, fusion strength), and evaluation limited to node classification and link prediction on eight relatively small-to-medium graphs. Open questions include whether the alignment framework scales to billion-node graphs, whether it generalizes to other modalities (e.g., image-attributed graphs), and whether the Cramér distance choice is truly optimal or merely convenient.
