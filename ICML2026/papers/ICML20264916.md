## Problem

Existing reference-guided video editing methods face a fundamental tension: decoupled encoders (T5 + CLIP) cannot bridge the semantic gap between text instructions and visual content, while unified VLM encoders that rely solely on final-layer representations discard the fine-grained spatial details needed for precise, faithful editing. The paper identifies that VLM layers encode complementary information hierarchically—early layers localize spatial details while deeper layers capture global semantics—but no prior method exploits this multiscale structure for video editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a text editing instruction, and a reference image (the first frame of the source video edited by an external model such as FLUX.1 Kontext) and produces a full edited video at 720P resolution with 81 frames. The reference image serves as an appearance anchor that is prepended temporally to the latent sequence, and the model must propagate the visual changes specified in the reference across all frames while preserving original motion and unedited regions. Inference on a single H100 takes approximately 6.5 minutes end-to-end with 50 GB peak GPU memory.

## Method

MiVE repurposes Qwen3-VL-8B as a multiscale feature extractor, harvesting hidden states from both the first and last Transformer layers and projecting them through independent RMSNorm + Linear adapters into the DiT latent space, where they are concatenated and fused into stationary conditional tokens fixed at diffusion timestep t=0. A reference-aware VAE encoding prepends the clean reference latent to both the noisy target and clean source branches before channel-wise concatenation, preserving a consistent appearance anchor throughout denoising. All tokens—conditional context and visual latents—are then processed jointly within a unified self-attention DiT backbone (initialized from Wan2.1-T2V-14B) using per-token AdaLN that modulates clean tokens at t=0 and noisy tokens at the current timestep, trained with a flow matching objective. This unified self-attention design replaces conventional cross-attention, enabling bidirectional interaction between conditional and visual tokens in a shared representation space.

## Results

On the Complex Subset (holistic editing without masks), MiVE achieves the highest scores across all six VLM evaluation dimensions—IA 9.23, CC 8.05, TS 8.27, PR 8.18, VA 8.09, SC 9.22—and ranks first in human preference (3.75/5), outperforming both Wan-Animate (3.03) and Kling O1 (3.61). On the Simple Subset (localized editing), MiVE leads in Instruction Adherence (9.30), Visual Aesthetics (8.83), Style Coherence (9.46), and user study (4.18/5), while Kling O1 edges ahead on Content Consistency (9.03 vs. 8.65) and Physical Realism (8.68 vs. 8.08). Cross-evaluator validation with InternVL3.5-8B confirms identical top-3 rankings on the Complex Subset, and paired Wilcoxon tests show significance (p < 0.01) against all baselines except Kling O1 on Complex (p = 0.247).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiVE outperforms all five baselines on the Complex Subset across every VLM metric and in human preference, with the largest margins over LucyEdit (+2.01 IA, +2.52 VA) and VideoCof (+1.85 IA, +3.10 VA). Against the strongest commercial competitor Kling O1, MiVE gains +0.55 IA, +0.34 CC, +0.16 TS, +0.40 PR, +0.35 VA, and +0.08 SC on Complex, plus +0.14 in user study (3.75 vs. 3.61, though not statistically significant at p = 0.247). On the Simple Subset, MiVE leads Kling O1 in IA (+0.82), VA (+0.32), SC (+0.15), and user study (+0.49), but trails in CC (−0.38), TS (−0.10), and PR (−0.60), a gap the authors attribute to the subset's simpler, slower-motion nature. Against Wan-Animate (Complex only), MiVE wins all six VLM dimensions (e.g., +0.36 IA, +0.37 CC, +0.44 VA) and user study (+0.72). VACE, which requires explicit masks, is evaluated only on Simple and trails MiVE by 1.5–2.4 points across most dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiVE builds on the Wan2.1-T2V-14B DiT backbone and the per-token AdaLN formulation from Z-Image, but departs fundamentally from the dominant decoupled-encoder + cross-attention paradigm used by VACE, LucyEdit, and VideoCof. Its key conceptual departure is repurposing a VLM (Qwen3-VL) as a multiscale feature extractor—drawing from both early (spatial) and final (semantic) layers—and injecting those features via unified self-attention rather than asymmetric cross-attention. This is motivated by a diagnostic analysis of cross-modal attention patterns across VLM layers, which quantifies the hierarchical shift from localized to global representations, a property previously noted anecdotally but not exploited for video editing conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multiscale VLM feature extraction combined with unified self-attention offers a practical recipe that other video generation/editing pipelines could adopt to improve instruction following and spatial fidelity without architectural overhauls. However, the method still requires an external image editor (FLUX.1 Kontext) to produce the reference frame, and inference at 720P/81 frames takes ~6.5 minutes on a single H100, limiting real-time applicability. The human-study advantage over the strongest commercial baseline (Kling O1) on the Complex Subset is not statistically significant (p=0.247), leaving open whether the gap widens with more diverse or longer sequences. Whether the hierarchical VLM layer findings generalize beyond human-centric content and to other VLM families remains untested.
