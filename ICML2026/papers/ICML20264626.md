## Problem

Conformal prediction sets provide distribution-free coverage guarantees but do not specify how to act on them for downstream decisions. Existing max-min rules (Kiyani et al., 2025) only consider worst-case loss inside the set, ignoring the α fraction of out-of-set probability mass that can incur catastrophic losses in high-stakes settings. This paper asks: how should one make minimax-optimal decisions from a prediction set under expected loss, and how should prediction sets themselves be designed to minimize downstream decision risk?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test covariate (e.g., a chest X-ray or driving image), a black-box probabilistic model f mapping inputs to label distributions, a held-out calibration set, and a miscoverage level α. It outputs two coupled objects: a conformal prediction set C_ROCP(X_test) with finite-sample marginal coverage ≥ 1−α, and a robust action a_ROCP(X_test) chosen by minimizing the worst-case expected loss over all distributions consistent with that coverage. The set and action are jointly optimized—the set is constructed to minimize the decision risk that the action will face, rather than optimizing set size or top-k mass as in standard conformal methods.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form minimax robust loss L_S(a;α) = ℓ_S^in(a) + α(ℓ_S^out(a) − ℓ_S^in(a))_+ for any fixed prediction set S, showing the optimal action hedges against out-of-set catastrophic losses in addition to the in-set max-min criterion. For set design, they formulate a population problem minimizing expected robust risk subject to a coverage constraint and solve it via Fenchel-Rockafellar duality in the normal-integrand framework, yielding a one-dimensional scalar parameter β that indexes the optimal coverage assignment t*(x) = argmin_t {V_x(t) − βt}. The practical ROCP algorithm (Algorithm 1) estimates the oracle quantities using a black-box model f, then calibrates β on a held-out set via standard conformal splitting to guarantee finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability without requiring f to be correct. The key distinction from prior work is that expected-loss minimization (as opposed to quantile objectives) fundamentally changes the minimax structure by introducing the α-weighted out-of-set penalty, which becomes decisive when severe mismatches carry disproportionately high cost.

## Results

The paper evaluates ROCP on two benchmarks (medical diagnosis on the COVID-19 Radiography Database and a toy autonomous-driving task on BDD100K), reporting average realized worst-case risk, average realized loss, empirical miscoverage, and critical mistake rates as a function of miscoverage level α. Under a 10× severe-mismatch loss matrix, ROCP substantially reduces worst-case risk and realized loss compared to RAC as α grows, nearly matching the best-response baseline on realized loss while almost eliminating critical mistakes. On the BDD100K driving task, ROCP matches or outperforms all baselines in both worst-case risk certificate and realized loss across all α values, with RAC's realized loss degrading after α ≈ 0.03 while ROCP remains stable. All results are averaged over 20 random splits with ±1 standard error bars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAC (Kiyani et al., 2025), ROCP ties on worst-case risk under the baseline loss matrix Λ₀ but wins decisively under the 10× severe-mismatch matrix Λ₁, where RAC's worst-case certificate and realized loss deteriorate sharply at larger α while ROCP stays stable and nearly matches best-response on realized loss; ROCP also almost eliminates critical mistakes for all critical labels where RAC remains comparable to the baseline setting. Against the Best-Response baseline (Zhao et al., 2021), best-response achieves the lowest average realized loss but incurs much higher critical mistake rates, making it unsafe in high-stakes regimes; ROCP closes most of the realized-loss gap under Λ₁ while retaining far fewer critical errors. Against the three decision-agnostic conformal baselines (LAS, APS, SOCOP), applying the ROCP decision rule a_ROCP to their prediction sets yields nearly identical performance to the in-set max-min rule a_RA under the mild loss matrix Λ₀, but for α ≥ 0.05 under Λ₁, a_ROCP consistently produces lower worst-case risk certificates and realized losses than a_RA on the same sets. On the BDD100K driving task, ROCP outperforms RAC in critical mistake rates across all hazardous states while maintaining comparable realized loss, and pairing conformal sets with a_ROCP improves over a_RA once α ≥ 0.03.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the expected-loss counterpart to Kiyani et al. (2025), which established the prediction-action interface for risk-averse agents optimizing a quantile (value-at-risk) objective and proved the in-set max-min rule minimax-optimal for that criterion. The key conceptual departure is that expected loss forces the decision maker to account for the α fraction of probability mass outside the prediction set, yielding an additional out-of-set penalty term in the optimal policy and a fundamentally different set-design problem. The duality machinery also differs: where Kiyani et al. reduce to an infinite-dimensional LP, this paper invokes Fenchel-Rockafellar duality through a randomized-kernel relaxation and derandomization to handle the integral-functional objective. The conformal calibration step (Algorithm 1) follows the same split-conformal structure as RAC, LAS, APS, and SOCOP, but the scoring rule is tailored to the robust risk rather than set size or top-k mass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap: conformal prediction sets guarantee coverage but leave the downstream action unspecified, and the standard max-min rule is shown to be brittle precisely in high-stakes regimes where rare out-of-set errors carry disproportionate cost. The (2−α) approximation guarantee bridging the conditional-coverage oracle to the marginal-coverage reality is a useful but loose bound, and no formal approximation guarantee to the oracle optimum is established for the finite-sample ROCP algorithm. Open questions include whether group-conditional or label-conditional coverage guarantees can be incorporated, whether the method extends to continuous outcome spaces beyond the discrete setting used in experiments, and whether the plug-in model quality requirement can be relaxed. For safety-critical applications (medical diagnosis, autonomous driving), the explicit accounting for out-of-set catastrophic loss offers a principled upgrade over decision-agnostic conformal sets.
