## Problem

In disaggregated LLM serving, transmitting raw KV caches between a producer and a weight-mismatched consumer (e.g., base vs. fine-tuned model) creates a severe bandwidth bottleneck that dominates time-to-first-token, while direct cache reuse causes semantic drift that compounds across layers and degrades generation quality. Existing compression methods (quantization, sparsification) assume identical producer-consumer weights, and selective recomputation approaches like DroidSpeak enforce a rigid binary trade-off between quality and latency without a smooth intermediate regime.

## Input / Output

The producer transmits compact semantic codes: low-rank projections of deRoPE'd keys and raw values for REUSE layers, and compressed normalized pre-attention hidden states for PATCH layers. Keys are deRoPE'd before encoding and reRoPE'd after decoding; K, V, and H states are compressed independently with separate branch-specific ranks (r_K, r_V, r_H ≪ d). The consumer reconstructs a full native-space KV cache ready for immediate decoding, skipping its entire local prefill. All translator parameters (linear encoders/decoders and MLP aligners) are frozen offline artifacts loaded as a static routing table keyed by layer index, so online execution involves no per-request layer search.

## Method

SCD integrates two complementary mechanisms: REUSE reconstructs KV caches for the majority of layers via joint truncated SVD on paired producer/consumer tensors to identify a shared low-rank subspace, followed by ridge-regression encoders/decoders that map producer observations to compact codes and back to consumer-native space; PATCH, applied at a sparse set of k critical transition layers (selected offline via cost-aware greedy search on marginal F1 gain), uses an MLP aligner to map compressed pre-attention hidden states into the consumer's normalized space, after which native KV pairs are regenerated using the consumer's own projection weights. The key theoretical insight is that layer-wise reconstruction errors propagate multiplicatively (governed by Lipschitz constants α_ℓ, β_ℓ), but each Patch layer acts as a spectral truncation operator that resets accumulated error to a small local term ε_patch, preventing downstream drift. This yields a segmented error bound (Lemma 5.1) showing that a few well-placed Patches reduce the worst-case error from a product over all L layers to a product over the longest inter-Patch segment, formalizing the Reuse-heavy, Patch-sparse design.

## Results

On the canonical MistralLite → Mistral-7B split-inference setup at 200 Gbps, SCD achieves a 2.65× TTFT speedup over the oracle consumer prefill (125.2 ms vs. 331.9 ms) while maintaining F1 of 0.7850 (vs. Oracle 0.8105) and PPL of 4.65 (vs. Oracle 4.15). On the larger Qwen-32B pair, SCD delivers a 2.07× speedup with F1 0.847 (Oracle 0.876). Standard compression baselines (Raw KV, 4-bit Quantized) collapse in quality (F1 0.13–0.27, PPL >100), confirming that weight-mismatched transfer requires semantic alignment rather than mere bit-rate reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCD decisively outperforms DroidSpeak-style Selective Recompute (Liu et al., 2024a) on both latency and quality: SCD achieves 2.65× speedup vs. 1.67× and F1 0.7850 vs. 0.7052, while also transmitting less data (87.31 MB vs. 127.34 MB per request). Against the adapted compression baselines in Appendix E, SCD dominates on the quality-latency Pareto frontier: SVD-LLM-style adaptation retains only 70.6% quality at 0.83× speedup (i.e., slower than oracle), SliceGPT-style reaches 85.0% quality at 0.95× (still slower), and TensorLLM-style collapses to 0.0% quality at 0.16×. SCD achieves 95–97% quality retention at 1.98–2.65× speedup, the only method that simultaneously improves both axes. The key insight is that DroidSpeak's binary reuse/recompute decision creates a rigid trade-off, whereas SCD's continuous low-rank reconstruction plus sparse Patch correction smooths the frontier, enabling higher compression than raw reuse and lower latency than recomputation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCD carves out a specific niche at the intersection of disaggregated LLM serving and cross-model state transfer. It builds on the KV-transfer bottleneck identified by DistServe, Splitwise, and Mooncake, and on the cross-model reuse problem formalized by DroidSpeak (Liu et al., 2024a), which makes a binary reuse-vs-recompute decision per layer. SCD departs from DroidSpeak by replacing this binary selection with learned continuous transformations: low-rank SVD-based encoders/decoders (borrowed from matrix approximation) for most layers, and MLP aligners (borrowed from knowledge distillation) at sparse transition layers. It explicitly positions itself as orthogonal to intra-model compression (quantization, sparsification) and to semantic-communication/C2C frameworks, targeting the narrower but practical case of shared-architecture, weight-mismatched producer-consumer pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As disaggregated serving becomes the default architecture for large-scale LLM inference, the ability to amortize expensive prefix computation across a family of fine-tuned specialists without full recomputation is a concrete deployment need. SCD's 2.65× TTFT speedup at near-oracle quality addresses this directly, and the finding that standard 4-bit quantization catastrophically fails (F1 0.13 vs. 0.81) for cross-model transfer is a useful caution against applying intra-model compression tools to heterogeneous settings. Key limitations include the per-pair calibration cost (9.74 hours, 1.58 GB of Patch artifacts), restriction to shared-architecture pairs, and untested behavior beyond 32K context; open questions include bandwidth-adaptive rank selection, cross-architecture transfer, and whether the sparse Patch budget generalizes across model families without re-calibration.
