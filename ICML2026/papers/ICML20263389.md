## Problem

Existing MLP-Mixer architectures for time series lack explicit temporal causality mechanisms (unlike transformers' attention masking), allowing future information to leak into each time step's representation. Additionally, multivariate time series with heterogeneous inter-channel correlations suffer from spurious correlations when all channels share a single embedding layer, and point-based anomaly scoring ignores the temporal continuity of anomalous segments. These limitations collectively degrade online anomaly detection performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sliding look-back window of L=24 timesteps across C channels (ranging from 25 to 123 depending on the dataset), min-max normalized using training-set statistics. It reconstructs only the latest observation x_t, and the reconstruction loss is converted into a sequential anomaly score via empirical p-values against a held-out validation loss distribution. The final output is a binary anomaly label per time step, produced entirely online—each prediction depends only on past and present inputs, with no access to future data or full-test-set statistics.

## Method

CCM-TAD combines three components: (1) a causal temporal mixer that applies an upper-triangular mask (with 1/j normalization for optimization stability) to the weight matrices of two stacked linear layers, ensuring each output position depends only on inputs at or before that position; (2) a cluster-aware multi-embedding module that groups channels via spectral clustering on cosine similarity of their absolute Pearson correlation profiles, then embeds each cluster through a dedicated linear layer with dimension proportional to cluster size, reducing spurious inter-channel correlations; (3) a sequential anomaly scoring method that converts reconstruction loss to an empirical p-value, computes anomaly evidence β_t = log(α/(p_t+ε)), accumulates evidence over time with a reset after δ consecutive negative evidences, and refines anomaly segment boundaries via backward search. The model is trained unsupervised on normal data only, minimizing MSE reconstruction loss, and operates in a fully online inference mode.

## Results

CCM-TAD achieves the best F1 score on 5 out of 6 single-entity datasets: WADI (0.761), PSM (0.716), SWAT (0.883), and leads on all three multi-entity datasets under the per-entity protocol (MSL 0.638, SMD 0.602, SMAP 0.551). The most notable gains are +8.71% on WADI (0.761 vs. 0.700 for SensitiveHUE-Offline) and +8.65% on PSM (0.716 vs. 0.659 for OracleAD). On MSL under protocol 3, CCM-TAD improves over the previous best (SensitiveHUE-Offline, 0.452) by +41.2%. The only dataset where CCM-TAD does not achieve the absolute best F1 is SWAT, where SensitiveHUE-Offline (0.911) slightly edges out CCM-TAD (0.883), though CCM-TAD still surpasses the online variant (0.874).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCM-TAD outperforms all 23 baselines on WADI (0.761 vs. 0.700 for SensitiveHUE-Offline) and PSM (0.716 vs. 0.659 for OracleAD), with margins of +6.1% and +5.7% respectively. On SWAT, SensitiveHUE-Offline (0.911) narrowly beats CCM-TAD (0.883), but CCM-TAD surpasses the online variant (0.874) and all other methods including SAT (0.842) and NPSR (0.839). On multi-entity datasets, CCM-TAD dominates under all three evaluation protocols: on MSL (protocol 3) it achieves 0.638 vs. 0.452 for SensitiveHUE-Offline (+41.2%) and 0.551 for NPSR (+15.8%); on SMD (protocol 2) it reaches 0.612 vs. 0.572 for PCA-Error and 0.535 for NPSR; on SMAP (protocol 3) it scores 0.551 vs. 0.523 for SensitiveHUE-Online. The PR_AUC results (Table 3) confirm consistent superiority, with CCM-TAD leading on WADI (0.720), SWAT (0.911), and all multi-entity datasets under protocol 2 (MSL 0.610, SMD 0.587, SMAP 0.552). The ablation in Table 5 further shows that the causal temporal mixer and channel clustering are complementary: removing either degrades F1, while the non-causal temporal mixer performs similarly to having no temporal mixer at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLP-Mixer architecture for time series (TSMixer, WPMixer) by introducing a causal masking mechanism into the temporal mixing layers, analogous to how transformers enforce causality via attention masks but absent in prior MLP-Mixer designs. It further departs from standard single-embedding approaches by proposing cluster-aware multi-embedding that uses spectral clustering on correlation profiles to partition channels into groups with dedicated embedding subspaces, reducing spurious inter-channel correlations. The sequential anomaly scoring method with statistical p-value grounding and boundary refinement builds on the nominality-score idea from NPSR but generalizes it to an online, cumulative-evidence framework that does not require access to the full test set, unlike SensitiveHUE's offline robust normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: MLP-Mixers are competitive with or superior to transformers for time series tasks but lack the causal structure that makes them suitable for online, real-time anomaly detection. The demonstrated ability to perform online detection with consistent F1 improvements across six benchmarks (up to +41% on MSL) makes this relevant for deployment in critical infrastructure monitoring. Open questions include whether the offline clustering step degrades under significant distributional shifts in inter-channel correlations over long operational periods, and whether the causal masking design generalizes to longer look-back windows or streaming settings with variable-length inputs.
