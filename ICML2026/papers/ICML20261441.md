## Problem

CASSI compressive hyperspectral measurements impose incompatible local and global constraints on the latent signal: coded apertures preserve pixel-wise spatial-spectral statistics, while spectral dispersion and integration entangle distant spatial-spectral components. Existing CNN-based methods lack the receptive field to resolve global entanglement, while vanilla Transformers assume globally comparable token semantics that are violated by the measurement process, amplifying spurious correlations under noise and undersampling. No prior architecture explicitly reconciles these competing constraints in a manner aligned with the hierarchical structure of the CASSI forward operator.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D CASSI snapshot measurement of size H×(W+d(B−1)) (e.g., 256×310 for simulated 28-band scenes, 660×714 for real scenes) together with the coded aperture mask, and produces a reconstructed 3D hyperspectral cube of size H×W×B (e.g., 256×256×28). The coded mask is not merely a sensing parameter but is actively consumed as a physics-guided conditioning signal inside the architecture (via Physics-Guided Feature Reweighting) to gate attention over physically admissible interactions. Training uses 512×512 CAVE scenes (32 indoor scenes, 28 bands in 450–650 nm) with 256×256 crops; testing uses 10 KAIST outdoor scenes up to 2704×3376 with 660×660 crops.

## Method

HSRA decomposes the learned regularizer into three scale-aligned components that mirror the CASSI forward operator A = S∘D∘M: (i) Multi-Kernel Perception Token Mixing (MK-PTM) uses parallel depth-wise convolutions with 3×3 and 9×9 kernels to enforce local sufficiency aligned with coded-aperture modulation; (ii) a Latent Window Interaction Module (LWIM) mean-pools features into a compact latent grid and applies window-based self-attention there, capturing dispersion-induced coupling at a fraction of full-resolution attention cost; (iii) Hierarchical Multi-Granularity Spatially-Shifted Attention (HM-SSA) progressively expands attention windows (w→2w) with alternating spatial shifts and conditions features via the coded mask to enforce global spectral-spatial coherence. These modules are arranged in a U-Net hierarchy and can be deployed either as an end-to-end regressor or as the Z-update (learned proximal prior) within a Half-Quadratic Splitting deep unfolding framework, where the data-fidelity step enforces CASSI physics and HSRA provides a scale-aware, physically grounded regularizer. A theoretical analysis in the appendix formalizes scale-conditional identifiability, showing that applying regularization in increasing spatial scale order monotonically suppresses null-space error components.

## Results

On the KAIST test set (10 scenes, 28 bands), HSRA-L achieves the best E2E performance with 37.49 dB PSNR / 0.965 SSIM at only 27.34 GFLOPs, outperforming DWMT (36.82 dB / 0.958 SSIM, 46.71 GFLOPs) by 0.67 dB while using roughly half the compute. In the deep-unfolding setting, HSRA-DUM-3stg reaches 39.40 dB / 0.978 SSIM, surpassing the previous best SSR-3stg (39.01 dB / 0.974), and HSRA-DUM-4stg attains the overall best of 39.81 dB / 0.982 SSIM. The ablation study further confirms that each hierarchical component (MK-PTM, LWIM, HM-SSA) contributes incrementally to both global PSNR/SSIM and scale-specific metrics (SAM, LPSNR, ERGAS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the E2E category, HSRA-L (37.49 dB / 0.965) outperforms all 11 baselines, with the largest margins over DWMT (+0.67 dB, +0.007 SSIM) and S²-Tran (+1.01 dB), while using only 27.34 GFLOPs versus DWMT's 46.71 GFLOPs. The lighter HSRA-S variant (9.01 GFLOPs) still surpasses CST-L+ (36.12 dB) and approaches DWMT's accuracy at less than one-fifth the compute. In the DUM category, HSRA-DUM-3stg (39.40 dB / 0.978) beats the strongest prior DUM, SSR-3stg (39.01 dB / 0.974), by 0.39 dB and 0.004 SSIM, and outperforms RDLUF-3stg (37.56 dB) and DAUHST-3stg (37.22 dB) by 1.84 and 2.18 dB respectively. HSRA-DUM-4stg extends this lead to 39.81 dB / 0.982, establishing a new state of the art. Notably, HSRA-S achieves competitive E2E accuracy (36.08 dB) with the lowest GFLOPs (9.01) among all compared methods, including transformer-based baselines like CST-S (11.67 GFLOPs) and MST-S (12.96 GFLOPs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding (HQS) framework for CASSI reconstruction by replacing generic learned priors with a hierarchical architecture whose components are explicitly aligned to the three physical operators of the CASSI forward model (coded aperture modulation, spectral dispersion, spectral integration). Conceptually, it builds on Swin Transformer's shifted-window attention for the global coherence stage and on CNN-based local feature extraction for the mask-aligned stage, but departs from prior hybrid CNN-Transformer designs by formalizing the 'local-global dissonance' as a principled problem and enforcing a strict scale-ordered hierarchy (local → mid-scale → global) rather than loosely coupling local and global branches. The theoretical appendix provides a scale-conditional identifiability sketch that motivates why this ordering matters, though it remains a proof sketch rather than a full convergence guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local-global dissonance framing offers a useful diagnostic for why existing CNN and Transformer methods struggle with CASSI: local priors lack global context to disambiguate dispersed signal, while global priors ignore mask-induced spatial boundaries. The demonstrated noise-robustness advantage (graceful degradation vs. homogeneous baselines) is practically important for real CASSI systems operating under non-ideal sensing conditions. Open questions include whether the physics-aligned hierarchical decomposition generalizes to other compressive imaging modalities (e.g., coded diffraction, multi-frame CS), whether the theoretical framework can be tightened into a rigorous convergence bound, and whether the approach scales to higher spectral resolutions (beyond 28 bands) and larger spatial dimensions without the U-Net hierarchy becoming prohibitive.
