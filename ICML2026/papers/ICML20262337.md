## Problem

Deciding how much labeled data to collect from each of multiple sources to reach a target performance at minimal cost requires modeling unknown, source-specific learning curves. Prior methods assume fixed parametric forms (e.g., power laws) that fail in multi-source settings, while GP-based monotonicity enforcement either demands derivative observations or yields intractable posteriors. Additionally, the failure-probability penalty used in existing objectives produces exponentially vanishing gradients when performance is far below target, leaving the optimizer without useful signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of observed (data-count vector, performance) pairs from prior training runs across K=5 sources, a target performance threshold V*, and a per-source cost vector. It outputs a T-stage data collection plan as a sequence of cumulative per-source sample-count vectors (optimized over a continuous relaxation with projected gradient descent for integrality and monotonicity), along with closed-form predictive mean and variance of the learning curve at any query point in R^K. The GP operates over the continuous relaxation of the integer count space, with virtual-derivative locations placed on a grid between the minimum observed count and per-source caps.

## Method

MOVE couples two components: (1) a monotonic variational GP that places a GP prior with RBF kernel over the multi-source learning curve and enforces coordinate-wise monotonicity via virtual-derivative factors—latent partial derivatives at J grid locations per coordinate, linked to fixed binary variables through a probit likelihood weighted by λ. Stochastic variational inference with inducing variables for both function values and directional derivatives yields a tractable ELBO, and the resulting predictive distribution is Gaussian with closed-form mean and variance. (2) An expected-shortfall terminal penalty S(n) = (V*−μ(n))Φ(α(n)) + σ(n)φ(α(n)) that replaces the failure-probability penalty, combined with per-stage acquisition costs weighted by the failure probability R(n). The key theoretical result is that ∇S(n) → −∇μ(n) as the standardized margin α(n)→∞, providing non-vanishing gradient signal in the under-target regime, whereas the failure-probability gradient decays exponentially in |α(n)|.

## Results

Across classification (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), segmentation (VOC-Seg), and detection (VOC-Det) benchmarks with T∈{3,5} planning rounds, MOVE achieves accuracy ratios closest to zero (i.e., tightest target attainment) while reducing the normalized data collection cost ratio by approximately 33% compared to LOC. On CIFAR-100 (T=5), MOVE reaches an accuracy ratio of 0.07 with a cost ratio of 0.0334, versus LOC's 0.16/0.0525 and ExactGP's 0.20/0.0592. On ImageNet (T=5), LOC fails to meet the target (accuracy ratio −0.04), while MOVE achieves 0.01 with a cost ratio of 0.0099. On VOC-Det (T=5), MOVE attains an accuracy ratio of 0.21 at a cost ratio of 0.0614, roughly halving LOC's cost ratio of 0.1159.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LOC (Mahmood et al., 2022), which fits additive power-law learning curves, MOVE consistently achieves lower cost ratios across nearly all settings (e.g., 0.0334 vs. 0.0525 on CIFAR-100 T=5; 0.0614 vs. 0.1159 on VOC-Det T=5), while also reaching the target more tightly (smaller accuracy ratio). Critically, on ImageNet LOC fails to meet the target at all (negative accuracy ratio), whereas MOVE achieves a positive accuracy ratio of 0.01–0.02. Against ExactGP, which uses the same expected-shortfall objective but replaces the monotonic variational GP with an unconstrained exact GP, MOVE wins on cost ratio in 8 of 10 settings (the sole exception being VOC-Det at T=3, where ExactGP achieves 0.0587 vs. MOVE's 0.0633), confirming that coordinate-wise monotonicity via virtual derivatives is essential for reliable extrapolation and cost-efficient collection. In the learning-curve ablation (Figure 3), MOVE also outperforms both DSVGP (Padidar et al., 2021) and standard SVGP (Hensman et al., 2015) on MSE and NLL in an extrapolation regime, demonstrating that even a minimal monotonicity weight (λ=0.001) yields consistent predictive improvements over unconstrained variational GPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOVE extends the virtual-derivative monotonic GP construction of Riihimäki & Vehtari (2010) from one-dimensional shape-constrained prediction to a multi-source (K-dimensional) data collection setting, and couples it with the stochastic variational GP framework of Hensman et al. (2013) to handle the non-Gaussian probit likelihood that arises from the monotonicity constraints. The data collection objective follows the target-driven multi-stage formulation of Mahmood et al. (2022, LOC), but replaces their parametric power-law learning curves with a nonparametric GP and swaps the failure-probability terminal penalty for an expected-shortfall penalty, drawing on the probit-link construction of Hernandez-Lobato et al. (2014). The key methodological departure is the combination of coordinate-wise virtual-derivative monotonicity with variational inference, which prior constrained-GP methods (Jidling et al., Lange-Hegermann) handle via hard operator constraints that are fragile under noisy observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The expected-shortfall penalty provides a clean theoretical fix for a practical failure mode: when the model is far below target, the failure-probability gradient vanishes exponentially, leaving the optimizer blind, whereas the shortfall gradient remains informative. This makes gradient-based multi-source data collection planning more reliable in the under-target regime that is most common in practice. Limitations include evaluation only on standard vision benchmarks with K=5 sources, uniform within-source sampling (no active learning), and a relatively small number of planning rounds (T=3,5). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger source counts, whether coupling with within-source active selection yields further gains, and how robust the GP extrapolation is under severe distribution shift beyond the tested benchmarks.
