## Problem

AI developers face a dual-use dilemma: capabilities that enable beneficial applications (e.g., vaccine design) also enable harmful ones (e.g., bioweapons). The gold standard for access control—serving separately trained, data-filtered models to different users—is prohibitively expensive at frontier scale, while cheaper alternatives like refusal training or post-hoc unlearning leave capabilities intact and vulnerable to adversarial elicitation. The paper asks whether a single training run can approximate N data-filtered models, each with a different capability profile, at the cost of one.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes text batches annotated with a capability label (core or one of N auxiliary domains), which determines which modules are active in the forward pass and which receive gradient updates. At inference, the input is a standard text prompt plus a capability profile specifying which auxiliary modules to activate; the output is generated text with only the retained capabilities available. Ablating a module (setting its contribution to zero) removes the corresponding capability without retraining, and the model supports 2^(N-1) distinct profiles from a single checkpoint.

## Method

GRAM augments each MLP block of a dense transformer with N-1 small auxiliary MLP modules (one per dual-use capability) alongside an always-active core MLP. Rather than using a learned router or per-token routing as in mixture-of-experts, GRAM applies batch-level gradient routing: on auxiliary data from domain i, the core MLP and module i are active in the forward pass, module i is always updated in the backward pass, and core parameters are updated only with probability p_as; on core data, the core is always updated and a random auxiliary module is activated with probability p_cr to stabilize core performance across module configurations. Each parameter partition (core vs. each auxiliary module) is optimized by a separate AdamW optimizer, and a heterogeneous gradient-accumulation scheme ensures that mixed-routing micro-batches do not leak gradients into the wrong modules. At inference, a capability profile is served by activating the core plus selected auxiliary modules and ablating the rest, localizing each capability to ablatable parameters and approximating a data-filtered model in a single training run.

## Results

On the Simple Stories synthetic benchmark (26M params), GRAM achieves a retain compute ratio of 0.952, closely matching data filtering (0.962) while requiring only 1× training FLOPS versus 5× for filtering across five capability profiles. On the realistic 800M-parameter dual-use setting (virology, cybersecurity, nuclear physics, Lisp code), GRAM and FT-LoRA both approximate data filtering on core, retain, and forget metrics while adding at most 10% more active MLP parameters. Across Chinchilla-optimal scaling from 50M to 5B parameters, GRAM tracks data filtering closely at every scale, with the suppression gap on removed capabilities widening with model size. Under partial labeling (50% of data unlabeled), GRAM achieves a forget compute ratio of 0.60, substantially below both filtering (0.85) and FT-LoRA (0.85).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FT-LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), GRAM matches or slightly exceeds retain performance in the realistic 800M setting, but demonstrates superior composability: when multiple auxiliary modules are activated simultaneously, GRAM's retain compute ratio stays constant while FT-LoRA degrades across all categories (Figure 5). Under partial labeling, GRAM's forget compute ratio of 0.60 substantially outperforms FT-LoRA's 0.85. Against MaxEnt (Yuan et al., 2025), GRAM is far more robust to adversarial elicitation: MaxEnt's forget compute ratio of 0.84 nearly recovers to 0.98 after finetuning, whereas GRAM's 0.77 rises only modestly to 0.86, indicating the capability is architecturally removed rather than merely suppressed. Against DEMix (Gururangan et al., 2022), GRAM dramatically outperforms on both core (0.94 vs. 0.25) and retain (0.95 vs. 0.18), which the authors attribute to DEMix's lack of a shared always-active core module under class imbalance. Overall, GRAM matches the gold-standard data filtering method on capability retention and removal while reducing training cost by 5× in the five-profile setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAM synthesizes three prior threads—domain-specific MLP modules (DEMix, Gururangan et al. 2022), data-dependent selective gradient masking (Gradient Routing, Cloud et al. 2024), and per-partition optimizers (Shilov et al. 2025)—into a single pretraining procedure that approximates N data-filtered models in one run. It explicitly departs from mixture-of-experts by using no learned router or per-token routing; instead, data labels at the batch level determine which auxiliary modules receive forward activation and gradient updates. The paper positions GRAM as a compute-efficient alternative to the gold-standard approach of training separate data-filtered models (O'Brien et al. 2025) and as a more robust replacement for post-hoc unlearning (MaxEnt, Yuan et al. 2025), while also comparing against branched LoRA finetuning (Hu et al. 2022) as a parameter-efficient baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAM offers a 5× training-cost reduction over data filtering for a 5-profile setting while matching its capability-removal robustness across Chinchilla-optimal scales from 50M to 5B parameters, making differentiated access control for dual-use AI capabilities practically feasible at frontier scale. The finding that GRAM outperforms data filtering under partial labeling (50% of tokens unlabeled) is a genuine reversal of the expected ordering and suggests gradient-routing-based modularization may be more robust to imperfect supervision than the current gold standard. Key open questions include whether these trends persist at true frontier scale, how GRAM interacts with post-training stages (RLHF, instruction tuning), whether the composability advantage over FT-LoRA has a principled explanation, and how entangled capabilities limit clean separation regardless of method.
