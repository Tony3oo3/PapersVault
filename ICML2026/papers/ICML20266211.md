## Problem

Existing tabular adversarial attacks rely on symmetric geometric constraints or undirected generative models that fail to capture the asymmetric, directed dependencies governing tabular data generation. This produces perturbations that are statistically plausible yet semantically inconsistent, and the resulting optimization frequently stagnates due to gradient masking in heterogeneous feature spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mixed-type tabular sample (continuous features in ℝ and categorical features as probability vectors on the simplex Δ^{K_j−1}), a target class label, a differentiable target classifier, and a training dataset from which the SCM ensemble is learned. It outputs a structurally admissible adversarial sample: continuous features are perturbed within an ℓ_p ball, categorical features are optimized in relaxed simplex space and discretized to the argmax category post-optimization, and the final perturbation must satisfy both a geometric budget ε and a structural likelihood budget γ derived from the learned SCM ensemble.

## Method

LCSA is a two-phase white-box framework. Phase I trains an ensemble of M heterogeneous neural SCMs, each with type-specific MLP mechanisms (regression for continuous, softmax for categorical) and a learnable weighted adjacency matrix enforcing DAG structure via a log-determinant acyclicity penalty; exogenous noise variances are estimated from residuals. Phase II introduces a structure-aware ripple perturbation that recursively propagates updates downstream through the learned DAG in topological order, so that perturbing a parent feature induces coherent adjustments in all descendants. This ripple reparameterization makes the perturbation Jacobian lower-triangular with unit diagonals, enabling 'gradient borrowing' from downstream nodes to escape local optima where standard PGD gradients vanish. The attack is solved via a two-stage augmented Lagrangian (warm-start gradient ascent followed by dual-ascent constraint correction) with simplex projection for categorical features and soft-embedding to maintain differentiability through discrete lookups.

## Results

Under the standard evaluation setting (L2=0.5, γ=0.5), LCSA achieves the lowest robust accuracy in 23 out of 25 dataset-model configurations, and 22 out of 25 under L∞=0.5. On the URL dataset, LCSA reduces TabTransformer's robust accuracy from 93.6% (clean) to 2.1% (L2) and 3.0% (L∞), compared to 11.3% and 20.8% for the strongest baseline CAPGD. Across all five real-world datasets and five architectures, LCSA outperforms all six baselines in 45 of 50 evaluated configurations, with particularly large margins on URL and LCLD where robust accuracy drops below 10% for multiple models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCSA dominates all six baselines in the vast majority of configurations. Against the strongest gradient-based baseline CAPGD (Simonetto et al., 2024a), LCSA achieves substantially lower robust accuracy on URL (e.g., 2.1% vs. 11.3% for TabTr.) and LCLD (e.g., 0.2% vs. 0.9% for RLN), with the gap narrowing on high-dimensional MALWARE where both methods struggle. The generative-manifold baseline Adv-VAE (He et al., 2025c) is consistently outperformed, particularly on URL and LCLD where LCSA's structural constraints yield 10–20 percentage point advantages. Heuristic methods BF* (Kulynych et al., 2018) and MOEVA (Simonetto et al., 2021) are the weakest baselines, often failing to reduce robust accuracy below 50% on URL while LCSA drops it below 20%. CaFA (Ben-Tov et al., 2024) and PGD (Madry et al., 2017) occupy the middle tier; LCSA outperforms both in nearly all settings except LCLD-STG where CAPGD and LCSA are closer (56.4% vs. 46.3%). The one notable exception is LCLD-STG, where LCSA (46.3%) still leads but the margin over CAPGD (56.4%) is the smallest across all 25 L2 configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCSA carves new ground at the intersection of structural causal modeling and adversarial robustness for tabular data. It departs from two dominant paradigms: (1) dependency-mining and handcrafted-constraint attacks (CaFA, CAPGD, BF*) that enforce sparse, manually specified rules, and (2) generative-manifold attacks (Adv-VAE, TabDDPM) that define feasibility via undirected, symmetric correlations. By learning an ensemble of heterogeneous neural SCMs with directed edges and introducing a recursive 'ripple' reparameterization that propagates perturbations downstream along the DAG, LCSA repurposes causal structure learning (Bello et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2018) and augmented Lagrangian optimization (Bertsekas, 2014) as a structural preconditioner for adversarial gradient flow—a use of SCMs in the adversarial loop that prior tabular-attack literature had not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete evaluation blind spot: attacks that achieve the lowest robust accuracy (e.g., CAPGD at 8.3% on Chain) simultaneously produce the most structurally invalid perturbations (SCE > 1.5), meaning they exploit the classifier by violating the data-generating process rather than finding genuine vulnerabilities. This reframes how tabular robustness should be measured and defended. Open questions include whether structure-aware validation pipelines can be integrated into deployment to reject structurally plausible manipulations, how the approach scales to truly high-dimensional settings (the MALWARE dataset with 24K features is the stress test), and whether the white-box differentiability requirement can be relaxed for black-box or non-differentiable targets beyond the GBDT transfer experiments.
